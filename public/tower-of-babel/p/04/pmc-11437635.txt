A retrospective study on the outcome of pulpotomy with iRoot BP plus in primary molars and its relationship with hemostasis time
Authors: Guangyun Lai, Kai Sheng, Jin Zhao, Ning Ding, Shimin Zhao, Jun Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulpotomy is a crucial method to preserve primary teeth until natural exfoliation. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical and radiographic outcomes of pulpotomy with iRoot BP Plus in primary molars and to explore the association between hemostasis time and these outcomes.MethodsPrimary molars that underwent iRoot BP Plus pulpotomy and were followed for at least 12 months were selected for this study. Clinical and radiographic data were collected, and the success rate was analyzed in relation to factors such as hemostasis time, tooth type, and arch type. The tests of significance used were the chi-square test, Fisher’s exact test, or Kruskal-Wallis test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.ResultsA total of 183 teeth in 106 patients were included in the analysis. The follow-up period fell into a range of 1–3 years, with a mean of 1.6 years. The clinical and radiographic success rates were 96.7% and 92.9%, respectively. The earliest time to observe the radiographic failures was half a year after the treatment, and the latest time was two years after the treatment. Among all the teeth, 130 were recorded with hemostasis time before the application of iRoot BP Plus. Compared to teeth with a hemostasis time of 5 min or less, teeth with a hemostasis time exceeding 5 min showed no significant differences in clinical and radiographic success (P = 1.000 and 0.879). Additionally, neither arch nor teeth type showed a relationship with the pulpotomy success rate (P > 0.05).ConclusionsPulpotomy using iRoot BP Plus in primary molars achieved favorable results. The hemostasis time may not significantly impact the outcomes of pulpotomy using iRoot BP Plus in primary molars.

Background

Primary teeth are the valuable assets of a child, playing a pivotal role in eating, phonetics, and esthetics and maintaining space for permanent teeth [1]. Premature loss of primary teeth, especially primary molars, can cause orthodontic problems, including crowding, ectopic eruption, and tooth impaction, resulting in malocclusion [2]. Thus, it is essential to maintain primary teeth in function until natural exfoliation.

Vital pulp therapy (VPT) encompasses a range of treatment methodologies aimed at preserving dental pulp vitality and maintaining tooth integrity. Treatment options for VPT include direct pulp capping, indirect pulp capping, as well as partial and complete pulpotomy procedure [3]. Pulpotomy is indicated in a primary tooth when caries removal results in a pulp exposure in a tooth with a normal pulp or reversible pulpitis or after a traumatic pulp exposure, and there is no radiographic sign of infection or pathologic resorption [4]. The operative procedure generally consists of amputating coronal pulp, controlling pulpal hemorrhage, and finally treating the remaining radicular pulp tissue surface with a long-term clinically successful medicament [4].

The commonly used pulpotomy agents are calcium hydroxide(CH), formocresol(FC), ferric sulfate(FS), and mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) [5]. In 2020, the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) recommended only MTA and FC as the medicament choice for teeth expected to be retained for 24 months or more [4]. FC has been popular since its introduction by Sweet in 1930 [6]. However, FC, containing formaldehyde, has some adverse effects, such as potential carcinogenicity [7], mutagenicity [8], and cytotoxicity [9], and damage to permanent teeth [10]. It can be distributed systemically via the passage from the root [11]. Many studies have been focusing on finding alternatives to replace FC.

In recent years, calcium silicate-based materials have been considered optimum medications for primary teeth pulpotomy due to their properties, including high biocompatibility and hard tissue formation [12]. MTA is the first calcium-silicate-based material introduced to the market. It has excellent biocompatibility, promotes tissue regeneration, and provides good marginal integrity without microleakage, leading to a high success rate of pulpotomy in primary teeth [13]. However, it has drawbacks such as difficult manipulation, a long setting time, and tooth discoloration [14].

iRoot BP Plus (Innovative Bioceramix Inc, Vancouver, Canada) is a relatively newly ready-to-use premixed calcium silicate-based bioceramic paste initially developed for permanent root canal repair and surgical applications. It is an insoluble, radiopaque, and aluminum-free material. It needs water to set and harden. Previous studies have demonstrated its excellent biocompatibility [15], sealing ability [16], antibacterial activity [17], and the ability to promote cell proliferation, adhesion, migration, and dentinal bridge formation [18], suggesting that it could be a favorable pulpotomy agent. Until now, iRoot BP Plus has exhibited a significantly higher success rate of pulpotomy than CH [19] and a similar success rate of pulpotomy to MTA for permanent teeth with complicated crown fractures [20]. But still, data on the performance of iRoot Bp Plus for primary teeth pulpotomy are rare, which requires clinical investigations.

Long-term outcomes are a concern in research on pulpotomy in primary teeth using calcium silicate-based materials. Many studies on MTA and Biondentine pulpotomy had follow-up periods ranging from 12 to 24 months [5, 12, 21, 22]. Additionally, various systematic reviews and meta-analyses have reported treatment success rates at 12 months and more extended follow-up [23–25]. The 2024 guidelines on vital pulp therapies in primary teeth, released by AAPD, provide recommendations based on outcomes of at least a 12-month follow-up [26]. A minimum 12-month follow-up is necessary to evaluate the outcomes of irootBp Plus pulpotomy in primary teeth.

Controlling hemorrhage is crucial for successful outcomes of pulpotomy. The time needed for hemostasis has been considered a vital indicator in vital pulp therapy [27]. The AAPD guideline recommends pulpectomy if bleeding cannot be controlled following pulp amputation after a few minutes [4]. However, no further detailed hemostasis time was discussed. Until now, there has been no agreement on the relationship between hemostasis time and pulpal status or pulpotomy outcomes in either primary or permanent teeth. Therefore, this retrospective study aimed to evaluate the outcomes of pulpotomy with iRoot BP Plus in primary molars and explore whether a relationship existed between the pulpotomy outcome and hemostasis time. The null hypothesis was that there was no significant association between pulpotomy outcomes using iRoot BP Plus and hemostasis time.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval

The study was conducted with approval from the Ethical Committee of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (No.SH9H-2024-T152-1). Since this is a retrospective study, the hospital’s ethical committee waived the need for informed consent.

Sample selection

The records of patients who underwent dental treatment under general anesthesia between November 2020 and September 2022 were reviewed. Patients who met the following criteria were included: those who received iRoot BP Plus pulpotomy on at least one of their primary molars; those who had no clinical signs or symptoms of irreversible pulpitis or periapical diseases prior to treatment; those in whom preliminary radiographs revealed absence of pathological root resorption and inter-radicular or periapical radiolucency; those who had at least a follow-up period of 12 months. Patients lacking radiographic examinations with at least 12 months of follow-up were excluded from the study.

Treatment procedures

According to the AAPD guidelines [4], pulpotomy is performed when caries removal results in pulp exposure in a primary tooth with normal pulp or reversible pulpitis or after traumatic pulp exposure and when there are no radiographic signs of infection or pathologic resorption. When the coronal tissue is amputated, the remaining tissue must be judged to be vital, and hemorrhage can be controlled.

In this study, all the pulpotomy procedures were performed due to caries. Rubber dam isolation was used in all cases. After the caries removal and the amputation of the coronal pulp, moistened sterile cotton pellets were placed over the orifices of the root canals under moderate pressure to stop the bleeding. Once hemostasis was achieved, a small amount of iRoot BP Plus was placed over the pulp floor and the canal orifices and gently condensed with moist cotton pellets. Then, the Fuji II glass ionomer base (GC Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) was applied over iRoot BP Plus. Stainless steel crown (SSC) restoration was the first treatment of choice after pulpotomy. In a patient whose parents did not want SSC restoration, the tooth was restored using resin composites. All the operators in the present study had more than five years of experience as dentists working in the Department of Pediatric Dentistry at Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital.

Data collection

Information on patients and treatment was collected, including sex, age, arch type (maxilla or mandibular), tooth type (first primary molar or second primary molar), restoration type (SSC or composite), clinical findings, radiographic findings, hemostasis time, and follow-up period from the electronic record system. The hemostasis time was defined as the time taken from placing a moist cotton pellet into the pulp chamber to the complete cessation of pulpal bleeding. All radiographs were evaluated by two experienced examiners (G.Y.L. & J.Z.). Before the study, intra- and interexaminer reliability was tested by evaluating 30 radiographs. First, the two examiners independently read the radiographs in a random order. The evaluation was repeated two weeks later.

Evaluation of treatment outcome

All the included teeth were evaluated based on clinical records and radiographs obtained during the follow-up visits. Clinical symptoms and signs of pulpotomy failure included spontaneous pain, tenderness to percussion/palpation, pathologic mobility, abscess, and fistula formation in the adjacent soft tissues. Findings indicating radiographic failure included periapical radiolucency, furcal radiolucency, internal root resorption, and external root resorption not related to a normal exfoliation process. Pulp canal obliteration and dentin bridge formation were not considered to indicate treatment failure. Once both clinical and radiographic failures were excluded by the time of the last follow-up assessment, the treatment was considered successful.

Statistical analysis

All data were processed using SPSS Statistics software Version 25.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages (%). The tests of significance mainly used were the chi-square test. If the expected frequency did not meet the conditions for chi-square tests, a Fisher’s exact test or Kruskal-Wallis test was utilized. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. Cohen’s kappa statistic was applied to assess the level of agreement between the two readers. Both the intra- and interexaminer reliability tests yielded values of 1.0, indicating an almost perfect level of agreement.

Results

A total of 183 teeth in 106 patients were included in the study (Table 1). The pulpotomy was mostly carried out among boys (63.2%) and children less than six years old (84%). The mean age of children was 4.8 years. The most commonly involved teeth were lower second primary molars (32.8%). Treated teeth were more frequently located in the mandibular (59%) than in the maxillary (41%). All the teeth except one were restored with SSC. We calculated the follow-up period according to the date of treatment and the last visit. In our department, patients are recommended to have a regular checkup every six months. As this is a retrospective study design, we could not control patients’ exact follow-up intervals. The follow-up period fell into a range of 1–3 years, with a mean of 1.6 years.

Among all the teeth, 130 had the records of hemostasis time. Furthermore, only whether the time exceeded 5 min was recorded in some cases. As a result, it was impossible to calculate the average time for controlling the bleeding. We divided these teeth into two groups based on a cut-off value of 5 min. Discussion

The study assessed the outcomes of primary molar pulpotomy with iRoot BP Plus and found a similar success rate to those using MTA and Biodentine reported previously [5, 12]. Moreover, all the analyzed factors, including hemostasis time, were not associated with the outcome of pulpotomy based on this retrospective study design. The null hypothesis was accepted.

With respect to the caries-involved teeth, the main indications of pulpotomy are teeth without spontaneous pain and evidence of radicular pathology, in other words, teeth with normal pulp or reversible pulpitis [4]. Nevertheless, it is frequently challenging for pediatric dentists to elicit an accurate pain history in children, which is an essential piece of clinical information in the American Association of Endodontists (AAE) criteria used to differentiate between reversible and irreversible pulpitis [28]. On the one hand, due to limited language proficiency and comprehension, the ability of young children to express symptom details is uncertain, and a caregiver proxy often provides their pain history [29]. This invariably compromises the value of pain experience in making a clinical pulpal diagnosis, as history taken from parents is more or less subjective [30]. On the other hand, a throbbing pain associated with an inflamed dental papilla owing to food impaction may be misinterpreted by dentists as an irreversible pain condition [31]. Therefore, although all the cases had no spontaneous pain history, the failures in this study may be partly attributed to the unreliable history recall of parents.

Besides the pain history, the radicular pulp status is pivotal for the success of pulpotomy in primary teeth. It is diagnosed based on the color and volume of blood and the achievability of hemostasis, all of which, with the exception of hemostasis, are subjective criteria [32]. Witherspoon et al. [33] suggested that a hemostatic period of 10 min, achieved through irrigation with 1% Sodium Hypochlorite (NaOCl), results in a remaining pulp in a reversible inflammatory state. Taha et al. [34] reported a 100% success rate in stopping blood flow in 6 min before vital pulp therapy by directly contacting the exposed pulp with a cotton pellet moistened with 1% NaOCl. On the contrary, Qudeimat et al. [35] found that vital pulp therapy with a hemostatic period of up to even 24 min was effective in teeth with irreversible pulpitis. High blood flow may indicate immense pulpal blood supply and be a necessity for pulp vitality and repair/regeneration [27].

Among studies regarding pulpotomy in primary teeth, a 5-minute hemostasis time is a common parameter [12, 22]. For this study, we divided all cases into two groups based on whether hemostasis was achieved within 5 min. The outcomes between the two groups did not show any significant difference, which may support the finding reported by Waterhouse et al. [36] that there was no significant difference between inflamed and non-inflamed pulps regarding time required for hemostasis. One case in the study failed with a hemostasis time of 2 min. We speculated that the short time, such as 2 min, to control the bleeding in some cases may be because part of the pulp was necrosis rather than in a healthy status, especially when the pain history of children was unreliable. Due to insufficient data in the present study, it was impossible to calculate the average time spent controlling the bleeding. Therefore, the time needed for hemostasis, or the duration/intensity of bleeding, still needs further study in primary teeth pulpotomy.

The 2024 guideline on vital pulp therapies in primary teeth, released by AAPD, recommended the use of calcium silicate cement pulpotomies due to their higher success rate [26]. It ranked MTA first and Biodentine second. In China, Biodentine is not accessible because it has not entered the market, which we only learn from publications. Regarding MTA, due to its difficult manipulation and discoloration property, it is increasingly being replaced by iRoot Bp Plus. iRoot Bp plus primarily consists of tri-calcium silicate, bi-calcium silicate, calcium phosphate, tantalum oxide, and zirconium oxide. Once hydration reactions happen, calcium silicate hydrogel and calcium hydroxide are produced, and then, calcium hydroxide reacts with calcium phosphate to precipitate hydroxyapatite [37]. A previous study has shown that it has a similar property in stimulating calcified dentine bridge formation as a pulp capping material to MTA [38]. Although there was no comparison between MTA and iRoot Bp Plus, the success rate of pulpotomy using iRoot Bp Plus was similar to that of MTA pulpotomy published previously, implying a favorable effect of iRoot Bp Plus as pulpotomy medication.

Steel stainless crowns are traditionally recommended as the final restoration following pulp therapy to ensure a biological seal, which is critical for long-term success [39]. Despite the findings of Kuo et al. [40] and Kim et al. [41] that SSC showed a higher success rate for primary teeth treated by pulpotomy than other restorations, the latest AAPD guideline reported that the certainty of the evidence for the restoration’s effect on MTA pulpotomy success after 12 months was very low. It recommended that clinicians may choose the type of restorations based on clinical expertise and shared decision-making [26]. In this study, all the teeth except one received SSC. Besides the ability to ensure a biological seal for the residual pulp tissue, SSC can provide exceptional prevention from further decay, especially for children at high risk of caries, which can indirectly influence the success of pulpotomy. And this is the main reason we chose SSC as the first choice in clinical practice.

In this study, the success rates of iRoot Bp Plus pulpotomy showed no association with arch type. On the contrary, both Strange et al. [42] and Kim et al. [41] found that primary mandibular molars showed a significantly lower success rate than maxillary molars. Strange et al. attributed their findings to the difficulty of reading radiographs taken for the primary maxillary molars since maxillary sinuses obscured the radiographic changes [42]. Kim et al. [41] proposed that as it is more difficult to obtain complete rubber dam isolation of mandibular teeth, the possibility of coronal leakage is high, leading to a lower success rate of pulpotomy. Nevertheless, further evidence is needed on the relationship between the success rate of pulpotomy in primary molars and arch type.

Radiographic failures were higher than clinical ones in this study, which is consistent with previous findings. For this reason, it is crucial to perform radiographic evaluations regularly after the treatment, even in the absence of clinical signs. Additionally, half of the failures appeared one and a half years or two years after the treatment. This suggests that a more comprehensive evaluation of pulpotomy outcome in primary molars necessitates a long-term observation for two years or more.

However, the study also has notable weaknesses. The retrospective design may introduce biases and limit the ability to control for all variables, potentially affecting the validity of the results. Additionally, as a single-center study, the generalizability of the findings to other settings or populations is limited. The reliance on panoramic images for follow-up evaluations may compromise the accuracy of radiographic assessments, as these images are not ideal for detailed evaluation of pulp therapy outcomes. The lack of standardized follow-up intervals could affect the consistency of data collection and the timing of observed outcomes. Furthermore, the study predominantly used stainless steel crowns for restoration, with only one case using resin composites. This lack of variety limits the ability to compare the effectiveness of different restoration types on pulpotomy outcomes. Therefore, regarding the pulpotomy performed in primary teeth, additional studies utilizing a prospective design with more extended evaluation periods and more medications, as well as standardized treatment and radiographic examination protocols, are required to clarify the findings of the present study.

Conclusions

Despite the limitations, this study showed that iRoot BP Plus could be a suitable medicament for primary molars pulpotomy. Hemostasis time may not significantly impact the success of pulpotomy using iRoot BP Plus in primary molars, which requires further research.

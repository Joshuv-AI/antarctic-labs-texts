Design, synthesis, and biomedical evaluation of Ag/Cu bimetallic MOFs for controlled drug delivery and synergistic therapeutic applications
Authors: Iqra Saleem, Zia Ul Haq Khan, Ibrahim A. Alsayer, Fida Ullah, Rabia Naz, Mahmood M. S. Abdullah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cancer and antibiotic resistance are major global health concerns due to high mortality rates, frequent treatment failures, and the development of resistance to conventional therapies. In the proposed research, a multifunctional silver/copper (Ag/Cu) bimetallic metal–organic framework (MOF) is described as a biomedical platform. Its integrity was ensured by structural characterization, and in vitro tests on MCF-7 breast cancer cells showed that the Ag/Cu MOF had intrinsic antitumor activity, with cytotoxicity and cell viability of 33.9% and 66.1%, respectively. The suggested MOF had PCM and CUR drug-loading capacities of 57.7% and 28.9%, respectively, with full release of PCM in 48 h and partial release of 40–60% of CUR in three days. Besides, the Ag/Cu MOF showed potent antimicrobial activity against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and Escherichia coli (E. coli), antifungal activity against Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus niger, and high antioxidant activity. These results indicate that the Ag/Cu MOF can serve as a useful multifunctional nanoplatform for drug delivery and other biomedical applications.

Introduction

Cancer remains the deadliest disease, encompassing a wide spectrum of conditions marked by uncontrolled and abnormal cell proliferation. The most common is breast cancer worldwide.1 The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) has reported that the global incidence is 1 per 20 women, while in Pakistan, it is about 1 per 9 women throughout their lives, which is twice the global risk.2 During the past decades, radiotherapy, surgery, chemotherapy, photodynamic therapy, and immunotherapy have been used on their own or in combination.3 However, persistent challenges, including multidrug resistance (MDR), off-target toxicity, and suboptimal pharmacokinetics, explain the necessity of advanced drug delivery systems to achieve more precise and tolerable therapeutic responses.4 The application of nanotechnology has significantly transformed oncology by enabling the use of nanoparticles to address off-target drug release.5

Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) are among the leading classes of porous, highly ordered crystalline materials formed by coordinating metal ions with organic linkers. They have a large surface area, tunable porosity, and diverse functionalities, which make them very promising for efficient drug loading and release.6 Many monometallic MOFs, such as UiO-66 (Zr), initially prepared by the University of Oslo group, and ZIF-8 (Zn), by Yaghi et al., have laid the foundation for more advanced drug delivery systems, enabling higher loading capacity and controlled release. This role has recently changed to an active participant by incorporating a bioactive metal center.7 The formation of bimetallic MOFs leverages heterometallic nodes, leading to increased catalytic activity and cooperative metallic interactions, e.g., Fe–Cu MOFs in the Fenton reaction and Zn–Co MOFs in photodynamic therapy.8 This approach effectively addresses drug resistance by inducing oxidative stress and underscoring the need for personalized therapeutics.9 This work presents the first systematic integration of Ag/Cu bimetallic metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) that are engineered simultaneously for controlled drug delivery and multimodal therapeutic functions on a single platform.10 Unlike most existing studies, which primarily focus on monometallic MOFs or investigate bimetallic systems for isolated purposes such as antimicrobial activity or catalysis, the present study exploits the synergistic interplay between Ag and Cu nodes to achieve controlled drug loading/release alongside combined therapeutic effects (e.g., antibacterial, anticancer, and ion-mediated therapeutic responses). The novelty lies in the rational design–property–performance correlation, demonstrating how bimetallic coordination enhances structural stability, drug–MOF interactions, and biological efficacy beyond what has been reported for single-metal MOFs.11 Consequently, this work advances current MOF-based biomedical research by offering a multifunctional, tunable, and biologically effective theranostics platform, rather than a single-function delivery system. Silver (Ag) and copper (Cu) are promising agents for anticancer therapy due to their cytotoxic properties. Ag+ ions induce cell death by generating reactive oxygen species (ROS), leading to DNA damage and apoptosis, and also reduce the risk of infection in immunocompromised patients.12 Cu2+ ions disrupt redox homeostasis and inhibit angiogenesis more effectively than Fe. The combination of these metals in an Ag/Cu MOF leverages their complementary mechanisms to enhance ROS generation, facilitate controlled drug delivery, and mitigate multidrug resistance (MDR).13 Despite these advantages, research on bimetallic MOFs for cancer drug delivery remains limited, particularly for Ag/Cu MOFs. In the present study, an Ag–Cu bimetallic MOF was synthesized via solvothermal synthesis, and its anticancer activity was evaluated against the MCF-7 breast cancer cell line. Its antibacterial, antifungal, and antioxidant properties, as well as its drug-loading capacity, were also investigated.

Chemicals and synthesis

Silver nitrate (AgNO3), copper nitrate trihydrate (Cu(NO3)2·3H2O), N,N′-dimethylformamide (DMF), 1,4-benzenedicarboxylic acid (BDC), ethanol (C2H5OH), 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH), cisplatin (CIS), paracetamol (PCM), curcumin (CUR), suramin (SUR), dodecyl sulfate (SDS), distilled water (dH2O) and phosphate buffer saline (PBS) were purchased from Merck, Pakistan.

Synthesis of Ag/Cu MOF

The Ag/Cu MOFs were synthesized via a solvothermal method, following a previously reported protocol with slight modifications.14 Stoichiometric amounts of AgNO3, Cu (NO3)2·3H2O, and 1,4-benzenedicarboxylic acid (BDC) in a 1 : 2 : 3 molar ratio were utilized as precursors. Ag and Cu salts were each dissolved in DMF and stirred magnetically until completely dissolved. Separately, BDC was dissolved in DMF under constant stirring to obtain a homogeneous solution. Subsequently, the Ag and Cu salt solutions were combined and stirred to ensure thorough mixing, then gradually added to the BDC solution under continuous stirring to promote uniform nucleation. The resulting mixture was stirred for an additional 1 h to ensure homogeneity, then transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and subjected to solvothermal treatment at 140 °C for 22 h. Upon completion of the reaction, the autoclave was allowed to cool naturally to room temperature. The resulting precipitate was isolated by filtration, thoroughly washed with dH2O and ethanol (EtOH) to remove unreacted species and impurities, and subsequently dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 6 h to yield the final Ag/Cu bimetallic MOF product. Drug loading and release study

The drug loading capacity (DLC) and release profiles of the proposed sample were evaluated using two models: PCM and CUR. For the DLC study, 0.3 g of PCM was dissolved in 10 mL of deionized water (dH2O), then 0.1 g of Ag/Cu MOF powder was added to the solution. The mixture was continuously stirred for at least 3 days to achieve maximum drug loading. Subsequently, the drug-loaded MOF was separated by centrifugation, and the absorbance of the supernatant was measured at 243 nm using a UV-visible spectrophotometer to determine the remaining drug concentration.15 The amount of loaded dug was determined from the difference between initial drug concentration and the concentrations left in the supernatant after loading, as measured by UV-visible spectroscopy. Based on UV-visible spectroscopy measurements, the amount of PCM incorporated into the Ag/Cu MOF was found to be 136.3 mg (0.1363 g). The DLC was calculated using eqn (1).1

Using 0.1363 g of loaded PCM and 0.1 g Ag/Cu MOF the DLC was calculated to be 57.7 wt%.

For CUR loading, the same procedure was followed: 0.3 g of CUR was dissolved in EtOH, and then 0.1 g of the prepared MOF solution was added. After 72 h of stirring, the drug-loaded MOF was separated by centrifugation, and the concentration of unbound CUR remaining in the supernatant was measured by UV-visible spectroscope at a wavelength of 428 nm. Based on the absorbance measurements, 40.6 mg (0.0406 g) of CUR was loaded into the framework. The DLC for CUR was calculated using eqn (1) in the same manner as for PCM and was found to be 28.9 wt%.15,16 For drug release studies, the Ag/Cu MOFs loaded with PCM and CUR were dispersed in 25 mL of two different buffer solutions: acetate buffer (pH 5.0) and phosphate buffer (pH 7.4), and shaken at 100 rpm at 37 °C. The release medium was collected in 1 mL aliquots at predetermined time intervals, and the concentrations of released drugs were determined by UV-Vis spectrophotometry (PCM: 243 nm; CUR: 428 nm). Cytotoxicity, anti-bacterial and antifungal activity

Cytotoxicity

The cytotoxicity of Ag/Cu MOF was evaluated using the MTT assay against the MCF-7 breast cancer cell line (Pajwani Center, University of Karachi, Karachi-Pakistan). Cells were seeded at a density of 10 000 cells per well in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 to ensure the proper cell attachment and stabilization.15 Following incubation, the medium was replaced with test compounds, including the positive control compounds CIS and SUR at 100 µM. The plates were then incubated for 3 h at 37 °C until the formazan crystals became visible.16 Formazan crystals were dissolved in SDS (10% in isopropanol), and absorbance was measured at 570 nm and 630 nm using a microplate reader to determine the cell viability and cytotoxicity.17–19

Antibacterial activity

The antibacterial efficacy of the designed material was evaluated against E. coli and MRSA using the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and zone of inhibition techniques. Bacterial inoculum was prepared in nutrient broth for the MIC test, and 5 mL of fresh nutrient broth was combined with 1 mL of bacterial suspension and 1 mL of the Ag/Cu MOF solution. The test tubes were then subjected to 24 h incubation.20 Turbid tubes demonstrated proliferation, but clear tubes indicated bacterial growth inhibition.

For the zone of inhibition test, nutrient agar plates were prepared, and a bacterial inoculum was spread over their surfaces. Wells were created in the agar, and 100 µL of Ag/Cu MOF solution at different concentrations (1 mg, 2.5 mg, and 5 mg) were added to each well. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 24 h, and the zone of inhibition was measured to evaluate the antibacterial activity of Ag/Cu MOF against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.20,21

Antifungal activity

The antifungal activity of Ag/Cu MOF was tested against freshly cultured Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus niger species. To carry out this test, potato dextrose agar (PDA) was prepared and autoclaved at 120 °C for 1 h. Then, under the laminar hood, PDA media mixed with different concentrations of Ag/Cu MOF were poured into each sterilized plate. Then all the plates were left aside for a few minutes to solidify. After this, inoculate the plates with both strains and carry out the inoculation period for 4 to 5 days.22,23

Radical scavenging activity

To evaluate the antioxidant activity of Ag/Cu MOF, a DPPH radical scavenging assay was performed. For this purpose, a 100 mL DPPH solution was prepared in ethanol (EtOH), while Ag/Cu bimetallic MOF solutions were prepared in deionized water (dH2O). Equal volumes of the DPPH and MOF solutions were then mixed and incubated at 37 °C for 30 minutes in the dark to prevent photodegradation of DPPH.24 After incubation, the absorbance of each mixture was recorded at 517 nm using a spectrophotometer. Results and discussion

Materials characterization

FTIR analysis

The FTIR spectrum analysis of the Ag/Cu MOF and the associated reagent in Fig. 2 and S1a, b revealed key functional groups within the 4000–500 cm−1 range. A broad band at 3615 cm−1 indicates O–H stretching, confirming the presence of –OH groups. A weaker band around 3000 cm−1 corresponds to C–H vibrations from the DMF ligand.24 Bands at 1596 cm−1 and 1775 cm−1 relate to the stretching of coordinated –COO− groups, evidencing the coordination of the BDC linker with Ag+ and Cu2+ centers.25 The observed Δν values indicate that the –COO− linker may be bidentate or bridging, thereby stabilizing the framework. Bending vibrations of C–H and C–N bonds at 924 cm−1, 1042 cm−1, and 1313 cm−1 confirmed linker integrity.26 Additionally, bands at 713 cm−1 and 956 cm−1 confirm Ag–O and Cu–O stretching, respectively.27 The result validates the successful synthesis of the Ag/Cu MOF and reveals essential M–O and M–COO− interactions, indicating their suitability for drug-loading and controlled-release applications.

XRD analysis

The XRD pattern of Ag/Cu MOF validates successful synthesis and crystallinity, showing distinct peaks associated with Ag and Cu phases, as shown in Fig. 3. Characteristic diffraction peaks at 38.26° (111), 44.48° (200), 64.19° (220), and 77.27° (311) are attributed to the Ag phase,28 indicated by comparison to (JCPDS:00-087-0717). Such peaks reveal the existence of Ag NPs predominantly oriented along the (111) plane, suggesting the crystalline nature of Ag in the MOF structure.29 Additionally, peaks at 43.25° (111) and 50.375° (200) evidence the presence of Cu, as confirmed by the JCPDS card (00-003-1005). Furthermore, peaks at 17.61° (101), 28.09° (112), and 30.61° (200) correspond to Cu–O,30 confirming its existence within the structure (JCPDS: 00-033-0480*). The presence of Ag and Cu supports the formation of the bimetallic MOF, thereby enhancing its stability and catalytic activity.

SEM/TEM analysis

SEM and TEM analyses were used to evaluate the morphology and structure of Ag/Cu MOF. Fig. 4a–c and S2a–c of SEM images at different magnifications show that the plate-shaped crystallites are aggregated with a visible surface roughness, facilitating drug interaction, while their significant porosity promotes heavy drug loading.31 Also, as seen by TEM images in Fig. 4d–f and S2d–f, a hierarchical architecture is confirmed; well-dispersed Ag/Cu NPs are present in the MOF matrix, optimized for effective drug loading and controlled release.32

Elemental analysis of the Ag/Cu MOF was performed using EDX spectroscopy, as shown in Fig. 5. The analysis revealed the presence of primary elements, including C, O, S, Cu, and Ag, as evidenced by characteristic peaks in the spectrum. Strong peak intensities of Cu and Ag show their substantial concentration, which confirms the bimetallic morphology of the MOF,33 further confirmed by the mass distribution (%), which is Cu: 12.63% and Ag: 17.23%. DLS analysis

Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis was used to evaluate the hydrodynamic size, distribution, and dispersity of the synthesized Ag/Cu MOF nanoparticles. The intensity distribution plot (Fig. 6a) shows the relative scattering intensity, indicating the presence of primary nanoscale populations. The volume distribution (Fig. 6b) demonstrates how different particle fractions contribute to the total sample volume, while the number distribution (Fig. 6c) reveals the dominant particle sizes, indicating a narrow and uniform size distribution. Furthermore, the autocorrelation function, shown as LN(G2(τ) − 1) versus delay time τ (Fig. 6d), offers insights into particle diffusion and hydrodynamic diameter. Overall, these results confirm that the Ag/Cu MOF is nanoscale in size and exhibits uniform dispersion, making it suitable for controlled drug delivery and various biomedical applications.

Applications study

Antitumor activity

The in vitro cytotoxicity of free CIS, free SUR, and Ag/Cu MOF was assessed against the MCF-7 cell line. The results reveal that free CIS and SUR exhibit cell viability of 32.4% and 49.3%, respectively, and cytotoxicity of 67.6% and 50.7%, respectively, against the MCF-7 cell line, while Ag/Cu exhibit 66.1% cell viability and 33.9% cytotoxicity, as displayed in Fig. 7a and b, respectively.35 These MTT assay results suggest that Ag/Cu MOF exhibits good biocompatibility and moderate cytotoxicity towards cancer cell lines. This cytotoxicity stems primarily from the synergistic release of Ag+ and Cu2+ from the framework upon internalization by cancerous cells.36–38 These metal ions elevate ROS through redox-active cycle and Fenton-like chemistry. This elevated ROS level overwhelms the cell's antioxidant defenses, catalyzing lipid peroxidation, protein structural modifications, and DNA fragmentation, ultimately driving tumor cells toward apoptosis. Beyond direct molecular damage, oxidative stress may also induce mitochondrial dysfunction and initiate apoptosis-related signaling.39–41 Ag+ may also interact with protein thiol groups, resulting in enzymatic inhibition and disrupting essential metabolic pathways.42,43 These synergistic pathways induce an irreversible state of stress, leading to programmed cell death. Proposed anticancer mechanism of action of Ag/Cu MOF.

The mechanistic pathway underlying the biological activity of nanomaterials, such as Ag- or Cu-based systems, can be described as a cascade of interconnected intracellular events. Upon cellular uptake, these nanoparticles or released metal ions (Ag+/Cu2+) interact with mitochondria and redox-active biomolecules, leading to excessive generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), including superoxide anions (O2˙−), hydroxyl radicals (˙OH), and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2).44 This oxidative stress disrupts the mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm), leading to mitochondrial dysfunction characterized by the opening of the permeability transition pore and the release of cytochrome c into the cytosol. The released cytochrome c activates the intrinsic apoptotic pathway by forming the apoptozole and subsequently activating initiator caspase-9, followed by executioner caspases-3 and -7, ultimately leading to DNA fragmentation and programmed cell death. Concurrently, ROS-mediated signaling alters the balance of Bcl-2 family proteins by upregulating pro-apoptotic Bax and downregulating anti-apoptotic Bcl-2, thereby promoting mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP).45 In parallel, liberated Ag+/Cu2+ ions further amplify oxidative stress by catalyzing Fenton-like reactions and binding to thiol-containing proteins, thereby disrupting enzymatic systems and antioxidant defenses, including glutathione. The combined effects of ion toxicity, ROS overproduction, mitochondrial collapse, and caspase activation synergistically drive apoptosis and cellular death pathways in a tightly regulated but irreversible manner.46

Cytotoxicity and safety considerations

Although Ag and Cu ions are well known for their strong antimicrobial and therapeutic potential, their cytotoxic effects are also well documented and are highly dependent on concentration, exposure duration, and cell type.47 Elevated levels of these metal ions may induce oxidative stress and adversely affect normal cellular functions. In the present study, the release of Ag and Cu ions is intended to be controlled and sustained, thereby minimizing potential toxicity while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.48,49 However, it is important to note that a comprehensive evaluation of toxicity to normal (non-cancerous) cell lines, along with long-term in vivo biocompatibility studies, was outside the scope of this work. Therefore, while the current findings demonstrate promising biomedical potential, further systematic investigations are necessary to fully establish the safety profile and support future clinical translation.50

Antifungal activity

The antifungal activity of the synthesized Ag/Cu MOF was systematically evaluated against two pathogenic fungal strains, Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus niger, at three different concentrations (0.001 g, 0.0025 g, and 0.005 g), and the results are summarized in Table 1. A pronounced concentration-dependent antifungal response was observed for both fungal species, indicating that the inhibitory efficiency of the Ag/Cu MOF increases with increasing dose. Overall, a progressive reduction in fungal colony diameter and growth intensity was observed as the MOF concentration increased, demonstrating its strong bioactivity and potential as an effective anti-fungal agent. For Aspergillus niger, the control plate (Fig. S3a) showed complete, unrestricted fungal growth, confirming optimal conditions in the absence of treatment. However, treatment with 0.001 g of Ag/Cu MOF significantly suppressed A. niger growth, reducing the colony diameter to approximately 4 mm and resulting in 56% inhibition of mycelial growth compared to the control (Fig. S3b), indicating initial antifungal efficacy of the material. Increasing the concentration to 0.0025 g resulted in a marked suppression of fungal proliferation, reducing the colony diameter to 1.2 mm and achieving 87% inhibition of mycelial growth (Fig. S3c). Remarkably, at the highest tested concentration (0.005 g), complete inhibition of fungal growth was achieved, corresponding to 100% mycelial growth inhibition, suggesting a strong fungicidal effect of the Ag/Cu MOF at higher concentrations (Fig. S3d). A similar trend was observed against Aspergillus flavus (Fig. S5). The control plate (Fig. S4a) exhibited dense and fully developed fungal growth, representing normal fungal propagation in the absence of the MOF. In contrast, treatment with 0.001 g of Ag/Cu MOF significantly reduced fungal expansion, limiting the colony diameter to approximately 5 mm and resulting in 44% inhibition of mycelial growth (Fig. S4b). Further increasing the concentration to 0.0025 g led to a marked suppression of fungal growth, with the colony diameter reduced to 3.6 mm, corresponding to 60% inhibition of mycelial growth (Fig. S4c). At the highest tested concentration (0.005 g), the colony diameter was further reduced to 2.5 mm, achieving 72% inhibition of mycelial growth, demonstrating the concentration-dependent antifungal activity of the Ag/Cu MOF (Fig. S4d). This consistent inhibitory trend across both fungal strains highlights the strong antifungal efficiency of the Ag/Cu MOF system. The observed dose-dependent antifungal behavior can be attributed to the synergistic action of Ag and Cu metal centers within the MOF structure, which generate reactive oxygen species (ROS), disrupt fungal cell membrane integrity, and interfere with essential enzymatic and metabolic processes. Consequently, the Ag/Cu MOF demonstrates excellent potential as a broad-spectrum antifungal material for biomedical and environmental applications.

Antibacterial activity

The antibacterial activity of the Ag/Cu MOF was evaluated against both E. coli and MRSA at different concentrations (0.001 g, 0.0025 g, and 0.005 g), as summarized in Table 2 and illustrated in Fig. S5a–d and S6a–d. The assessment was carried out using both minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) determination and agar well diffusion (zone of inhibition) assays to obtain a comprehensive understanding of antibacterial efficiency. In the MIC assay, serial concentrations of the Ag/Cu MOF were tested to evaluate their ability to inhibit visible bacterial growth. Among the tested concentrations, only the most effective three were selected for detailed analysis. The results clearly demonstrated a concentration-dependent antibacterial response. At lower concentrations (0.001 g and 0.0025 g), partial bacterial growth was still observed, indicated by turbidity in the culture tubes, confirming incomplete inhibition. However, at the highest tested concentration of 0.005 g, complete inhibition of bacterial growth was observed, as evidenced by clear solutions in the tubes, indicating no visible bacterial proliferation. This confirms that 0.005 g represents the most effective concentration within the tested range for both bacterial strains. Further confirmation was obtained through the zone of inhibition (ZOI) assay, which demonstrated the diffusion-based antibacterial effectiveness of the Ag/Cu MOF. The results revealed that the synthesized MOF exhibited significant antibacterial activity against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria; however, a comparatively stronger effect was observed against E. coli. This is clearly reflected in the larger diameter of the inhibition zone for E. coli compared with that for MRSA. The enhanced susceptibility of E. coli may be attributed to differences in cell wall structure, in which the thinner peptidoglycan layer and the outer membrane composition of Gram-negative bacteria facilitate greater interaction with metal ions (Ag+ and Cu2+) released from the MOF. These ions are known to induce oxidative stress, disrupt membrane integrity, and interfere with essential cellular processes, ultimately leading to bacterial cell death.

Antioxidant activity (radical scavenging)

In this study, the antioxidant (free-radical-scavenging) potential of the synthesized Ag/Cu MOF was evaluated using the standard DPPH assay. A clear and visible color change was observed upon addition of the MOF to the DPPH solution, where the deep violet color of DPPH gradually faded, indicating the reduction of DPPH radicals to the corresponding non-radical form (DPPH-H).49,50 This visual decolorization is a primary qualitative indicator of radical scavenging activity and suggests that the Ag/Cu MOF effectively donates hydrogen atoms or electrons to neutralize free radicals, as illustrated in Fig. 9a. To quantitatively assess this activity, UV-visible spectrophotometric analysis was performed at the characteristic absorption wavelength of DPPH (517 nm). A concentration-dependent decrease in absorbance was observed as MOF dosage increased, confirming enhanced radical-scavenging efficiency at higher concentrations. Specifically, at MOF concentrations of 100, 150, 200, and 250 µg mL−1, the recorded absorbance values progressively decreased to 1.3, 0.9, 0.7, and 0.4, respectively. Based on this calculation, the Ag/Cu MOF exhibited scavenging efficiencies of 45.83 ± 2.07%, 62.5 ± 1.90%, 70.83 ± 2.32%, and 83.33 ± 2.44% at concentrations of 100, 150, 200, and 250 µg mL−1, respectively. A concentration-dependent increase in antioxidant activity was observed with scavenging efficiency rising from 45.83 ± 2.07% at 100 µg mL−1 to 83.3 ± 2.44% at 250 µg mL−1. This enhanced performance at elevated concentrations may be attributed to greater availability of active sites capable of donating electron or hydrogen atoms to neutralize DPPH radicals. Statistical analysis revealed significant difference among all the tested concentrations (P < 0.05) as donated by distinct superscript letters in Fig. 9b. This pronounced activity suggests that the Ag/Cu MOF possesses significant potential for applications in antioxidant-based biomedical and environmental systems.

Conclusion

This study reports the successful synthesis of a bimetallic Ag/Cu MOF via solvothermal synthesis, yielding a highly ordered porous structure with uniformly incorporated Ag and Cu ions. The material's structural, morphological, and physicochemical features were thoroughly characterized using FTIR, XRD, SEM, TEM, DLS, and ZP. FTIR confirmed metal–organic coordination; XRD showed crystalline phase purity; SEM and TEM detailed particle morphology and nanoscale features; PSA indicated a narrow particle size distribution; and ZP demonstrated good colloidal stability, vital for biomedical applications. Biological tests revealed the MOF's multifunctionality: moderate cytotoxicity against MCF-7 breast cancer cells from Ag+ and Cu2+ ion release, which generate ROS, causing oxidative stress, mitochondrial damage, DNA injury, and apoptosis, illustrating its therapeutic potential. The MOF also showed high capacity for carrying PCM and CUR, owing to its high surface area, tunable porosity, and dual active sites, making it suitable for various drug-delivery applications. Additionally, it exhibited potent antimicrobial activity against Gram-negative bacteria (E. coli), resistant strains such as MRSA, and fungi such as A. flavus and A. niger, primarily through membrane disruption, protein inactivation, and oxidative stress induced by released metal ions. The MOF also exhibited significant antioxidant activity, capable of neutralizing free radicals and reducing oxidative stress linked to many chronic diseases. Despite these promising findings, limitations remain, notably the lack of in vivo validation, long-term toxicity, pharmacokinetics, and biodistribution studies, which are critical for safety and therapeutic assessment. Future work will focus on enhancing multi-drug loading, controlled release, and targeted delivery, paving the way for the clinical application of Ag/Cu MOFs as multifaceted therapeutic platforms.

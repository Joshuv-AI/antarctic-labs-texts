A commentary on ‘A new designed full process coverage robot-assisted total hip arthroplasty: a multicenter randomized clinical trial’
Authors: Zheng Yan, Yan Xu, Lianguo Wu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We are interested in the article entitled ‘A new designed full process coverage robot-assisted total hip arthroplasty: a multicenter randomized clinical trial,’ which was published in the International Journal of Surgery 1. Total hip arthroplasty (THA) is an effective method for the treatment of advanced hip joint disease. With the increasing types of prostheses, THA has significantly grown in complexity over time. Due to differences in surgical experience, improper placement of the prosthesis can increase the risk of complications following THA and may lead to biomechanical alterations, affecting the longevity of the prosthesis2. In this study, a new type of haptic robotic-assisted system (TRex-RS, Longwell Corp., Shanghai, China) based on computed tomography (CT) was designed to assist the implantation of femoral prosthesis. This study was a multicenter, randomized controlled study that reported a comparison of the effect of a new complete robot-assisted total hip arthroplasty (RA-THA) with that of the manual total hip arthroplasty (MTHA). The authors collected 148 patients, including 74 patients who underwent RA-THA and 74 patients who underwent MTHA. This study concluded that the femoral angulation and leg length discrepancy in the RA-THA group were significantly lesser than those in the MTHA group. Although these findings have certain clinical significance, there still remain several issues worthy of in-depth discussion.

Firstly, this study adopts a randomized, prospective, single-blind grouping method to control bias, which is commendable. However, with only 74 patients included in each group, the sample size was relatively small, resulting in a lower statistical power for the outcomes. Increasing the sample size in future studies would enhance the robustness and reliability of the findings.

Secondly, the utilization of a multicenter study design in this research can significantly enhance research efficiency, mitigate single-center bias, and foster communication among researchers across different regions. This approach holds valuable benefits for all clinical researchers. However, it is important to note that variations in surgical procedures and the experience level of the surgeons can have a considerable impact on patient recovery and, consequently, influence the outcomes of the study.

Thirdly, the study’s handling of potential confounding factors is commendable, as it effectively adjusts for several critical covariates, including gender, age, BMI, and preoperative VAS (visual analog scale) score. Nevertheless, considering the multitude of other potential confounders that could influence the outcomes, such as patients’ comorbidities, the level of experience of the surgeons, and patients’ adherence to postoperative care instructions, the robustness of the study’s results could be further enhanced.

Additionally, the study’s follow-up period of only 6 months is inadequate for a comprehensive evaluation of the long-term survival of the prostheses. Extending the follow-up duration would likely yield more dependable results. Furthermore, incorporating patient-reported outcome measures, including aspects of quality of life, would enrich the assessment and provide a more holistic comparison of the effectiveness between the two surgical methods.

Overall, the robot-assisted hip arthroplasty utilized in this study represents a revolutionary advancement that establishes a robust foundation for enhancing precision in the treatment of hip conditions. Our recommendations are intended to refine an already exemplary study. We look forward to additional groundbreaking contributions from the authors in their future work.

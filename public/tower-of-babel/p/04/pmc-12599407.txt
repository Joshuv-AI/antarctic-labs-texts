Comparative effectiveness of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor treatment intensification for metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer in real-world patients
Authors: Chan Ho Lee, Soodong Kim, Ja Yoon Ku, Kyung Hwan Kim, Byeong Jin Kang, Hyeok Jun Goh, Won IK Seo, Hong Koo Ha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study compared the real-world effectiveness of androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs)—abiraterone acetate, apalutamide, and enzalutamide—as treatment intensification in patients with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC).Materials and MethodsThis multicenter retrospective cohort study analyzed 219 patients with mHSPC who received first-line ARPIs combined with androgen deprivation therapy. We evaluated progression-free survival to metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (PFS to mCRPC) and prostate-specific antigen (PSA) response within 12 weeks and investigated the prognostic impact of early PSA response on clinical outcomes.ResultsThe cohort comprised patients receiving abiraterone acetate (n=94, 42.9%), apalutamide (n=91, 41.6%), and enzalutamide (n=34, 15.5%) with an 18.8-month median follow-up. The 2-year PFS to mCRPC rates demonstrated no significant difference between the groups (abiraterone acetate: 74.1%; apalutamide, 81.4%; enzalutamide, 85.6%; p=0.482). However, apalutamide and enzalutamide achieved superior rates of PSA decline to ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks compared with abiraterone acetate (44.0% and 55.9% vs. 25.5%, respectively) and significantly shorter median time to PSA nadir (7.2 and 7.5 months vs. 12.2 months; p<0.001). A PSA reduction of ≥90% within 12 weeks was observed in 87.2%, 94.5%, and 97.1% of patients receiving abiraterone acetate, apalutamide, and enzalutamide, respectively. Multivariate analysis identified early PSA response as an independent prognostic factor for improved PFS to mCRPC regardless of ARPI selection.ConclusionsAbiraterone acetate, apalutamide, and enzalutamide showed comparable PFS to mCRPC outcomes, while significant differences in early PSA kinetics were observed, with early PSA response serving as a crucial prognostic factor in mHSPC patients.

INTRODUCTION

Over the past decade, the therapeutic landscape of metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) has undergone a paradigm shift following the integration of androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs) into standard treatment protocols [12]. The introduction of abiraterone acetate (ABI), enzalutamide (ENZA), and apalutamide (APA) has fundamentally transformed therapeutic approaches, with multiple landmark phase III trials demonstrating significant survival benefits when these agents are combined with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [3456]. This treatment intensification strategy has consistently shown overall survival (OS) improvements of 18–24 months compared with ADT monotherapy, establishing a new standard of care for patients with mHSPC [2].

Despite compelling evidence from randomized controlled trials, considerable implementation disparities remain in clinical practice. Real-world evidence indicates that approximately 30% of patients with mHSPC continue to receive ADT monotherapy rather than combination therapy [78]. This treatment gap reflects patient-specific characteristics (advanced age and multiple comorbidities), physician-related considerations (concerns regarding treatment tolerability), healthcare system constraints (financial accessibility), and the evolving understanding of optimal treatment sequencing.

Emerging data from pivotal clinical trials have suggest potential differential efficacy profiles among ARPIs based on disease burden stratification. Recent meta-analyses have suggested that ABI can confer superior outcomes in patients with high-volume metastatic disease, whereas APA and ENZA exhibit improved efficacy in low-volume disease settings [910]. However, as clinical trial participants frequently represent a selected cohort, the generalizability of these findings to heterogeneous real-world patient populations remains uncertain. Furthermore, the lack of direct head-to-head comparative trials between ARPIs creates uncertainty regarding treatment selection for specific patient subgroups.

Considering these clinical challenges and knowledge gaps, comprehensive real-world comparative effectiveness research is needed to evaluate ARPI performance across diverse patient populations. Such investigations are crucial for identifying predictive factors for treatment response, optimizing drug selection algorithms for specific patient subgroups, and developing evidence-based tailored treatment strategies that can be implemented in routine clinical practice. Therefore, by conducting a comprehensive comparative effectiveness analysis of different ARPIs in real-world patients with mHSPC, this multicenter retrospective cohort study was designed to address these critical knowledge gaps. This study focused on key clinical outcomes, including prostate-specific antigen (PSA) response kinetics, oncological outcomes, and safety profiles, with emphasis on identifying patient subgroups that may derive differential benefits from specific ARPI selections.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Patients

This multicenter observational retrospective study encompassing human participants adhered to the ethical standards of institutional and national research committees and the Declaration of Helsinki principles. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Inje University Busan Paik Hospital (approval number: BPIRB 2025-06-030). The requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the study’s retrospective nature. Before data collection, all centers obtained approval from the local research ethics board. We identified 255 patients with mHSPC who started first-line treatment with ARPIs (ABI, APA, or ENZA) combined with ADT from November 2019 to December 2024 at four South Korean urologic cancer centers. Patients without baseline data, including Gleason grading and PSA, follow-up oncological outcome data, PSA response, insufficient follow-up, and ARPI treatment of >3 months following mHSPC diagnosis or <3 months of ARPI treatment, were excluded. The final cohort comprised 219 patients.

2. Treatment protocols and follow-up

All patients received standard ADT using a luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone agonist or antagonist per physician’s discretion. ARPIs were orally administered: ABI 1,000 mg daily with prednisone 5–10 mg daily, APA 240 mg daily, and ENZA 160 mg daily. ARPI selection and dose modifications were at the treating physician’s discretion based on comorbidities, adverse events, and drug availability. Treatment was continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal, or death. Patients underwent clinical and laboratory evaluations at baseline and every 2–3 months, with imaging studies performed at 3-month intervals. PSA was measured at baseline and each follow-up visit.

3. Data collection and definitions

Clinical data, including demographics, ECOG-PS (Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status), baseline PSA at mHSPC diagnosis, Gleason grade group, metastatic sites and burden, laboratory parameters, adverse events during ARPI administration, and ARPI administration details, were retrospectively collected from electronic medical records. Disease volume was classified according to the CHAARTED criteria: high-volume disease indicated visceral metastasis and/or ≥4 bone lesions (with at least one outside vertebral column and pelvis) [11]. High-risk disease was defined according to the LATITUDE criteria: at least two of Gleason score ≥8, ≥3 bone metastases, or measurable visceral metastasis [6]. Early PSA response was defined as ≥90% decline of PSA level from baseline PSA within 12 weeks after ARPI initiation or PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks. Early PSA response was defined as ≥90% decline in the PSA level from baseline within 12 weeks following ARPI initiation or a PSA level of ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks. mCRPC was defined as castrate testosterone (<50 ng/dL) with either biochemical progression (three consecutive PSA increases, two ≥50% increases over nadir, and PSA level >2 ng/mL) or radiographic progression (new lesions on bone scan or soft tissue progression by RECIST 1.1) [12].

4. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test or one-way analysis of variance, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to determine factors independently associated with progression-free survival to metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (PFS to mCRPC), including baseline clinical characteristics and early PSA response. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp.) and MedCalc version 22.0 (MedCalc Software). A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

1. Patient characteristics

A cohort of 219 patients with mHSPC receiving ARPIs was analyzed (median follow-up, 18.8 months [IQR, 10.8–28.5]; median age, 74.2 years [IQR, 68.3–79.8]). ARPI treatments were distributed as follows: ABI (n=94, 42.9%), APA (n=91, 41.6%), and ENZA (n=34, 15.5%). The ABI (30.4 months) group exhibited a significantly longer median medication duration than the APA (13.7 months) and ENZA (14.1 months) groups (p<0.001). The ABI group (202.0 ng/mL) showed an elevated baseline median PSA level compared with the APA (117.5 ng/mL) and ENZA (72.7 ng/mL) groups (p=0.019). The ABI group demonstrated higher rates of bone metastases (95.7% vs. 79.1% and 73.5%; p<0.001), ≥5 bone metastases (89.4% vs. 62.6% and 44.1%; p<0.001), CHAARTED high-volume disease (89.4% vs. 63.7% and 38.2%; p<0.001), and LATITUDE highrisk disease (98.9% vs. 57.1% and 41.2%; p<0.001) (Table 1).

2. PFS to mCRPC

mCRPC progression occurred in 40 patients (18.3%): 28/94 (29.8%), 8/91 (8.8%), and 4/34 (11.8%) in the ABI, APA, and ENZA groups, respectively (p<0.001). Median PFS to mCRPC was not reached in any treatment arm. The 2-year PFS to mCRPC rates were comparable across groups (ABI, 74.1%; APA, 81.4%; ENZA, 85.6%; p=0.482), including patients with LATITUDE high-risk disease (73.8%, 73.7%, 80.0%, respectively; p=0.850) (Fig. 1A, B).

3. PSA response to ARPI treatment

The ABI group (median, 12.2 months [IQR, 6.3–25.6]) exhibited a significantly prolonged time to PSA nadir compared with the APA (median, 7.2 months [IQR, 4.7–11.6]) and ENZA (median, 7.5 months [IQR, 4.9–12.7]) groups (p<0.001). PSA decline to ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks was achieved in 25.5%, 44.0%, and 55.9% of patients receiving ABI, APA, and ENZA, respectively (p=0.002). A PSA reduction of ≥90% within 12 weeks was observed in 87.2%, 94.5%, and 97.1% of patients receiving ABI, APA, and ENZA, respectively (p=0.094) (Fig. 1D).

4. Early PSA response and PFS to mCRPC in patients with CHAARTED high-volume disease

CHAARTED high-volume disease was identified in 155 patients (70.8%), 84 (54.2%) and 71 (45.8%) of whom were receiving ABI and APA or ENZA, respectively. In this high-volume cohort, the medication duration was significantly longer in the ABI group (p<0.001), which also had a higher proportion of patients with elevated Gleason grade (p=0.013) (Supplementary Table 1). The 2-year PFS to mCRPC rates were comparable across the groups (ABI, 73.4%; APA+ENZA, 77.2%; p=0.510) (Fig. 1C). The APA+ENZA group exhibited superior rates of PSA decline to ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks (40.8% vs. 25.0%; p=0.036) (Fig. 1E) and shorter time to PSA nadir (median, 9.29 months [IQR, 5.02–12.43] vs. 11.96 months [IQR, 5.44–25.34]; p=0.003).

5. Prognostic factors for progression to mCRPC

Multivariable analysis identified Gleason grade group 5 (hazard ratio [HR], 1.29; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.07–1.55; p=0.006) and liver metastasis (HR, 9.36; 95% CI, 2.87–30.44; p<0.001) as significant predictors of mCRPC progression. Failure to achieve PSA reduction of ≥90% within 12 weeks (HR, 3.10; 95% CI, 1.37–7.01; p=0.006) and failure to reach PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks (HR, 3.27; 95% CI, 1.23–8.66; p=0.017) were associated with increased mCRPC progression risk (Table 2). Comparable prognostic factors were observed in the CHAARTED high-volume disease subgroup. Consistent risk factors included Gleason grade group 5 (HR, 1.33; 95% CI, 1.09–1.63; p=0.004) and liver metastasis (HR, 7.21; 95% CI, 2.05–25.2; p=0.002). Additionally, failure to achieve PSA reduction of ≥90% within 12 weeks (HR, 3.57; 95% CI, 1.05–12.1; p=0.041) or inability to reach PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks (HR, 4.74; 95% CI, 1.93–11.6; p=0.001) remained significantly associated with increased progression to mCRPC in this high-volume disease subset (Supplementary Table 2).

Achievement of ≥90% PSA reduction within 12 weeks was correlated with superior 2-year PFS to mCRPC in both the overall cohort (77.9% vs. 42.1%; p<0.001) and patients with CHAARTED high-volume disease (80.1% vs. 32.1%; p<0.001). Similarly, PSA decline to ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks was associated with improved 2-year PFS to mCRPC in both the overall cohort (77.9% vs. 70.6%; p=0.001) and patients with CHAARTED high-volume disease (92.6% vs. 67.7%; p=0.003) (Fig. 2).

6. Adverse events

Treatment-related adverse events were documented in 25.1% of patients, with significant variation across treatment groups (ABI, 7.4%; APA, 45.1%; ENZA, 20.6%; p<0.001). Cutaneous reactions were predominant (17.8%), primarily occurring in the APA group (40.7% vs. 1.1% ABI and 2.9% ENZA; p<0.001). Dose reductions (4.6% overall) were exclusively observed in the APA group (11.0%; p<0.001). Permanent treatment discontinuation (4.1%) demonstrated no significant intergroup differences (Table 3).

DISCUSSION

This real-world study offers valuable insights into the comparative effectiveness of ARPIs for treatment intensification in mHSPC. Our findings demonstrated that the 2-year PFS to mCRPC rates remained similar across all three ARPIs in the overall or high-volume disease subgroups. These findings suggest that ABI, APA, and ENZA provide comparable clinical benefits in preventing mCRPC progression, despite significant differences in the early PSA response kinetics.

Our analysis revealed that APA and ENZA achieved significantly higher rates of deep PSA response (≤0.2 ng/mL) within 12 weeks than ABI, while showing similar ≥90% PSA reduction rates across all three ARPIs. Furthermore, APA and ENZA demonstrated shorter median time to PSA nadir than ABI. This distinction between PSA response metrics underscores the significance of clearly defining endpoints in comparative effectiveness research. Our findings of comparable oncologic outcomes are consistent with recent studies demonstrating similar PFS among ARPIs despite differences in PSA kinetics [1314]. However, our ≥90% PSA reduction results differ from previous reports of APA superiority over ABI [15], whereas other studies have shown mixed findings [141617]. Early PSA kinetic differences did not translate into differences in PFS to mCRPC outcomes, contradicting clinical trial meta-analyses suggesting ABI advantages in high-volume disease [10]. Discrepancies between real-world data and clinical trial results probably reflect variations in patient populations and methodological approaches [818].

Notably, our multivariate analysis identified that achievement of early and deep PSA response was a strong prognostic factor for improved PFS to mCRPC outcomes, independent of the specific ARPI used. Patients achieving PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks demonstrated significantly superior 2-year PFS to mCRPC rates in both the overall cohort and high-volume CHAARTED subgroup. Similarly, patients achieving ≥90% PSA reduction within 12 weeks demonstrated superior 2-year PFS to mCRPC in both the overall cohort and high-volume subgroup. These findings highlight the significance of early PSA response as a predictive biomarker of long-term outcomes, regardless of the ARPI agent selected. Furthermore, although the ABI group demonstrated significantly higher baseline PSA levels at mHSPC diagnosis than the other treatment groups, no significant difference in PFS to mCRPC was observed between ARPI agents. These findings are consistent with recent real-world evidence suggesting that early PSA response to treatment is a more significant prognostic factor than baseline PSA levels at diagnosis [1920]. Consistent with our findings, other Phase III clinical trials and real-world data studies have demonstrated the prognostic significance of early deep PSA response [1321222324].

The predictive value of early and deep PSA response for ARPI treatment efficacy has driven initiatives toward mHSPC treatment optimization. Ongoing and upcoming clinical trials are exploring innovative treatment strategies based on PSA response patterns. Representative approaches encompass de-escalation strategies, in which patients demonstrating early and deep PSA response may discontinue ARPI or ADT after a defined treatment period, and treatment intensification protocols, in which patients with suboptimal early PSA response during ARPI therapy receive additional agents, such as docetaxel [2526]. These adaptive treatment paradigms show promise for tailored therapy in mHSPC, and their findings require careful evaluation in the coming years.

Regarding safety and tolerability, our findings revealed differences in adverse event profiles among the three ARPIs. APA demonstrated significantly higher dose reduction rates due to adverse events than ABI and ENZA, aligning with previous real-world studies reporting varying tolerability profiles among ARPIs [1427]. Although permanent treatment discontinuation rates due to adverse events were documented across all three groups, they did not reach statistical significance in our analysis. The observed safety differences highlight the significance of individualized ARPI selection in clinical practice, considering patient-specific factors, comorbidities, and anticipated tolerability profiles to optimize treatment outcomes and maintain therapeutic benefit in patients with mHSPC [2829303132].

This study had several strengths, including providing real-world data from a Korean regional cohort and evaluating comparative early PSA kinetics using multiple metrics and their association with PFS to mCRPC among three frequently used ARPIs. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the retrospective study design and relatively short median follow-up period (18.8 months) may limit the generalizability of our findings and precluded analysis of long-term outcomes, such as OS and cancer-specific survival. Second, non-randomized treatment selection could introduce selection bias, as demonstrated by significant baseline differences among the treatment groups, particularly higher disease burden in the ABI cohort. Lastly, the heterogeneous timing of PSA measurements in real-world practice may affect the accuracy of early response assessments. To address these limitations, we performed subgroup analyses focusing on patients with high-volume disease and used multiple PSA response metrics to comprehensively evaluate treatment efficacy evaluation. Despite these efforts, unmeasured confounding factors may still influence our results, emphasizing the significance of prospective comparative studies with long-term follow-up.

CONCLUSIONS

This real-world analysis suggests that although ABI, APA, and ENZA demonstrate comparable PFS to mCRPC outcomes, variations in early PSA response are noted, with APA and ENZA exhibiting significantly higher rates of achieving PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL within 12 weeks than ABI, despite similar rates of ≥90% PSA reduction across all three ARPIs. The identification of early PSA response as a prognostic factor suggests the significance of developing strategies to improve treatment outcomes in mHSPC and predict treatment intensification failure.

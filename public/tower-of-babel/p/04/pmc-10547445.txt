Elevated serum fatty acid-binding protein 4 level predicts all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in peritoneal dialysis patients: a five-year study
Authors: Sijia Zhou, Xiaoxiao Wang, Qingfeng Han, Lian He, Wen Tang, Aihua Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo explore the prospective role of serum fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4) in the outcomes of peritoneal dialysis (PD) patients.MethodsA prospective observational study was conducted with 159 patients on PD. Demographic and clinical data at baseline were collected from medical records. Biochemical data were recorded based on blood samples measured in a central laboratory. Serum FABP4 concentrations were determined using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Body composition was measured using a Body Composition Monitor. Abdominal lateral plain radiography was used to evaluate vascular calcification. The primary endpoints were all-cause and cardiovascular death.ResultsThe median of serum FABP4 concentration was 154.6 ng/mL (interquartile range, 132.8–269.7 ng/mL). Increased serum FABP4 was associated with increased vascular calcification proportion, time on dialysis, body mass index, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH), triglycerides, body fat mass, and body fat percentage (p < 0.05). Increased serum FABP4 was associated with decreased residual kidney Kt/V urea (p < 0.05). Patients with hs-CRP≥ 3 mg/L had significantly higher serum FABP4 than those with hs-CRP< 3 mg/L (p < 0.05). Patients with vascular calcification had significantly higher serum FABP4 than those without vascular calcification (p < 0.05). During a median follow-up of 58.0 months, 58 all-cause deaths and 26 cardiovascular deaths occurred. High serum FABP4 levels were independently predictive for all-cause [hazard ratio (HR), 1.003; 95% confidence interval (CI), 1.001–1.005; p = 0.016] and cardiovascular death (HR, 1.005; 95% CI, 1.001–1.008; p = 0.006) in PD patients.ConclusionsIncreased serum FABP4 levels can independently predict all-cause and cardiovascular death in patients on PD.Keywords: FABP4, adipokine, peritoneal dialysis, mortality, cardiovascular disease

Introduction

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is an effective treatment for patients with kidney failure. According to recent estimates, more than 272,000 people are receiving PD worldwide, representing 11% of the global dialysis population [1]. In China, PD patients have increased over the last two decades [2] and represent nearly a quarter of the worldwide PD population, although this may be an underestimation. For patients with kidney failure, PD could be the optimal choice for improving prognosis. However, cardiovascular events are still common and remain the leading cause of death in patients on PD. In Australia and New Zealand, the incidence rate of cardiovascular mortality in patients on PD is approximately 10 per 100 patient-years, approximately 20-fold greater than that in healthy controls [3]. As reported in some studies, traditional risk factors for cardiovascular death in dialysis patients include malnutrition, inflammation, atherosclerosis, and vascular calcification (MIAC) [4].

Fatty acid-binding protein (FABP4), also known as adipocyte fatty acid-binding protein (AFABP) or adipocyte P2 (aP2), is a relatively novel adipokine with a molecular mass of 14.6 kDa. FABP4 is highly expressed and secreted by adipocytes and plays a key role in intracellular lipid transport and homeostasis [5]. FABP4 is also expressed in macrophages and endothelial cells [6]. Recently, the bioactive effects of FABP4 have received widespread attention, including the mediation of lipolysis, inflammation, foam cell formation, vascular dysfunction, and hepatic glucose production [7]. Yeung et al. [8] found that circulating FABP4 levels were positively associated with increased MIAC in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). In our previous study, serum FABP4 was positively associated with vascular calcification, hyperlipidemia, and inflammation biomarkers, such as serum high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), in patients on PD [9]. A series of studies have shown the correlations between serum FABP4 and the prognosis of patients with metabolic syndrome or established cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases [10,11]. Serum FABP4 levels were found to be predictive of short- and long-term mortality, as well as poor functional outcomes in patients with ischemic stroke [12,13]. High levels of circulating FABP4 were independently associated with cardiovascular mortality in patients with established coronary artery disease [14] or T2DM [15]. Studies on the role of FABP4 in the prognosis of patients with chronic kidney disease were limited. Furuhashi et al. [16] reported that patients undergoing hemodialysis with high serum FABP4 levels had increased cardiovascular mortality. These findings indicate that FABP4 may be involved in cardiovascular risks and clinical outcomes of patients on PD, which requires further study. With this background, we conducted a study to investigate the prognostic role of serum FABP4 among patients on PD.

Materials and methods

Patients and study design

Patients aged between 18 and 80 years who had been stably maintained on PD treatment for > 3 months were recruited from the PD Center of the Peking University Third Hospital from May 2018 to July 2018. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with acute coronary syndrome, acute heart failure, or other symptoms of cardiovascular instability within 3 months; (2) patients with peritonitis or other active infections; (3) patients with active tumors, severe liver insufficiency, or systemic inflammatory diseases; and (4) patients who refused to provide consent. This was a prospective cohort study with no intervention conducted by the investigators. The participants were prospectively followed up until 30 April 2023.

Data collection

The following demographic and clinical data were collected at baseline: age, sex, primary cause of kidney failure, time on dialysis, presence of diabetes mellitus, blood pressure, and a history of cardiovascular disease (CVD). CVD was defined as the presence of coronary artery disease (myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, percutaneous coronary angioplasty, and coronary artery bypass surgery), congestive heart failure, stroke, and peripheral vascular disease (angioplasty, vascular surgery, and amputation). Medication data (aspirin and statin) were also recorded. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated by dividing the body mass in kilograms by the square of height in meters. Serum biochemical parameters were recorded based on blood samples measured in a central laboratory during routine clinic visits. Biochemical data were collected close to the day of body composition assessment.

Measurements of serum FABP4 concentration

When patients underwent routine biochemical tests during clinic visits, their blood samples were taken and serum was extracted by centrifugation and stored in the −80 °C refrigerator for the FABP4 measurement. FABP4 and biochemistry data are from the same serum source. The serum FABP4 concentration was determined using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit (Phoenix Pharmaceutica, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Body composition assessment

Body composition was measured using a Body Composition Monitor (BCM, Fresenius Medical Care AG&CO., KGaA D-61346 Bad Homburg, Germany). Data on parameters such as muscle mass (kg), body fat mass (kg), body fat percentage (%), lean body mass (kg), and overhydration (L) were collected. Overhydration ≥2 L could be defined as volume overload. All the body composition tests were performed by well-trained physicians.

Vascular calcification assessment

Vascular calcification was evaluated using the abdominal aortic calcification (AAC) score, which was graded using a semi-quantitative scoring system described by Kauppila et al. [17]. AAC was measured using abdominal lateral plain radiography. Calcification status of the abdominal aorta adjacent to the first to fourth lumbar vertebrae was evaluated. The AAC score varies from 0 to 24, and vascular calcification is defined as an AAC score of ≥4. Trained investigators analyzed all lateral lumbar radiographs.

Follow-up procedure and primary endpoint definitions

All subjects were followed up prospectively from study entry until death, switching to hemodialysis, receiving a kidney transplant, or to the end of the study (30 April 2023). The primary endpoint events were all-cause and cardiovascular death. Cardiovascular death was defined as death caused by myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, sudden cardiac death, stroke, or peripheral artery disease. The primary endpoint events were confirmed and recorded by trained physicians.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were expressed as mean ± SD for normally distributed variables or median with 25th and 75th percentiles for non-normally distributed variables. Absolute values and percentages were used to represent the categorical variables. The patients were stratified into three groups according to tertiles of serum FABP4 levels. One-way analysis of variance, Kruskal–Wallis test, or χ2 test was performed for comparisons across the three groups. The Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test were used to generate cumulative survival curves. Hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were summarized using Cox proportional hazard regression models to explore the predictors of all-cause and cardiovascular death. Variables with a p-value <0.1 in the univariate Cox analysis were selected for the multivariate Cox regression analysis. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 22.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

The characteristics of the study participants are presented in Table 1. A total of 159 patients on PD were enrolled in the study, with a median follow-up length of 58.0 months, of whom 77 (48.4%) were male. The mean age was 58.5 ± 13.3 years and the median length of dialysis was 50.0 (20.0–88.0) months. The primary kidney diseases were chronic glomerulonephritis (n = 55, 34.6%), diabetic kidney disease (n = 41, 25.8%), hypertensive nephropathy (n = 20, 12.6%), chronic interstitial nephritis (n = 18, 11.3%), other diseases (n = 9, 5.7%), and unknown causes (n = 16, 10.0%).

Parameters between different tertiles of serum FABP4 level

Patients were divided into three groups according to the serum FABP4 concentration, namely those with serum FABP4 < 139.1 ng/mL (lower tertile, n = 53), those with serum FABP4 139.1 − 193.6 ng/mL (middle tertile, n = 53), and those with serum FABP4 > 193.6 ng/mL (upper tertile, n = 53). The demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients across the three tertiles of serum FABP4 levels are detailed in Table 2. Increased serum FABP4 was associated with increased vascular calcification proportion, time on dialysis, BMI, hs-CRP, intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH), and triglycerides (p < 0.05). Increased serum FABP4 was also associated with decreased residual kidney Kt/V urea (p = 0.001). Furthermore, patients in the upper FABP4 tertile had lower total Kt/V urea and muscle mass than those in the lower FABP4 tertile (p < 0.05). In addition, patients in the upper FABP4 tertile were more likely to be female and had higher alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and total cholesterol than those in the lower FABP4 tertile (p < 0.05). As for other body composition parameters, patients with serum FABP4 concentration >193.6 ng/mL had higher body fat mass and body fat percentage than their counterparts (p < 0.05).

Correlation between serum FABP4 and inflammation, CVD, and vascular calcification

Inflammation was defined as a hs-CRP level ≥ 3 mg/L. Eighty-three (52.2%) patients had hs-CRP levels of ≥ 3 mg/L at baseline. Serum FABP4 levels in patients with hs-CRP levels ≥ 3 mg/L were significantly higher than those in patients with hs-CRP < 3 mg/L (p < 0.001) (Figure 1). In this study, 72 (45.3%) and 80 (50.3%) patients had a history of CVD and vascular calcification, respectively. Patients with vascular calcification had significantly higher serum FABP4 levels than those without vascular calcification (p = 0.005) (Figure 1). Serum FABP4 levels did not differ significantly between patients with and without a history of CVD (p > 0.05).

Association between serum FABP4 level and all-cause mortality on PD

Patients were followed up until 30 April 2023. The follow-up period ranged from 1.0 to 59.0 months, with a median value of 58.0 months. Nine (5.7%) patients received kidney transplants, 13 (8.2%) switched to hemodialysis, and 58 (36.5%) patients died. Among them, 26 (16.4%) patients experienced cardiovascular death, and the other causes of death were infection, cancer, multiorgan failure, and gastrointestinal bleeding (Table 3). Eighteen (25.0%) cardiovascular deaths occurred in patients with established CVD, and 8 (9.2%) cardiovascular deaths occurred in patients without CVD at baseline.

According to Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, a significant increase in all-cause mortality (log-rank test, p = 0.012; Figure 2) was observed across the three tertiles of increasing serum FABP4. Association between serum FABP4 level and cardiovascular mortality on PD

Cardiovascular death was the leading cause of death in the present study. According to Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, patients in the upper FABP4 tertile had significantly higher cardiovascular mortality compared with patients in the other two tertiles (log-rank test, p < 0.001; Figure 3). Univariate Cox regression analysis showed that serum FABP4 concentrations were significantly associated with cardiovascular mortality (p = 0.002). In the multivariate Cox regression analysis, serum FABP4 level (HR, 1.005; 95% CI, 1.001 − 1.008; p = 0.006) and history of CVD remained independent predictors of cardiovascular death after adjusting for age, time on dialysis, vascular calcification, diabetes mellitus, total cholesterol, and hs-CRP in PD patients (Table 5).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective study showing that serum FABP4 levels could be an independent predictor of all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in PD patients. Patients with serum FABP4 concentrations in the highest range of three tertiles (i.e., higher than 193.6 ng/mL) had a significantly higher risk of all-cause and cardiovascular death than those with lower serum FABP4 concentrations.

Our study showed that a history of CVD is an independent risk factor for cardiovascular mortality in patients with PD. Although serum FABP4 concentrations did not differ significantly between patients with and without a history of CVD at baseline, it was found that cardiovascular mortality was lower in the lower FABP4 tertile compared to the upper FABP4. Meanwhile, it was also found that patients in the middle FABP4 tertile had lower cardiovascular mortality, which could be partially explained by the lower incidence of CVD and diabetic mellitus in the middle FABP4 tertile. Patients in the middle FABP4 tertile had higher body fat mass and BMI than those in the lower FABP4 tertile at baseline, and that always means a better nutritional status and a lower risk of cardiovascular death. Circulating FABP4 levels were reported to be closely associated with the progression and poor outcomes of CVD [18,19]. FABP4 was suggested to be a biomarker of heart failure [20] and could induce a cardiodepressant effect in isolated rat cardiomyocytes [21]. In addition, FABP4 may act as an important motivator for inflammation, atherosclerosis, and vascular calcification in patients with or without established CVD. MIAC has been suggested to strongly predict all-cause and cardiovascular death in patients on PD [4]. Atherosclerosis plays a crucial role in the development of CVD. Circulating FABP4 has been positively associated with atherosclerotic plaque burden in patients with coronary artery disease, T2DM, and cerebral infarction [22]. Furuhashi et al. observed that baseline serum FABP4 levels could predict the progression of carotid plaques in patients with carotid atherosclerosis [23]. Furthermore, epicardial and perivascular fat-derived FABP4 may also be involved in the development of coronary atherosclerosis in a paracrine manner [24].

The pathogenesis of atherosclerosis includes the infiltration of macrophages, proliferation and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) from the media into the intima, and dysfunction of endothelial cells [25]. Fu et al. [26] found that FABP4 regulated lipid metabolism and accelerated the formation of macrophage-derived foam cells. Consistently, macrophage-specific FABP4 deficiency alleviated atherosclerosis in mice with apolipoprotein E deficiency [27]. Basic studies have revealed that reduced expression of FABP4 in the visceral fat of mice with apolipoprotein E deficiency could alleviate atherosclerotic lesions [28], suggesting that both macrophage-derived and adipocyte-derived FABP4 might play an important role in the development of atherosclerosis.

Abnormal proliferation and migration of VSMCs in atherosclerotic lesions is triggered by the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway. Treatment with recombinant FABP4 could contribute to the proliferation of human coronary smooth muscle cells by reducing the phosphorylation of pro-angiogenic molecules, such as endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS), and this effect was attenuated in the presence of anti-FABP4 antibodies [29]. In addition, exogenous FABP4 induces VSMC proliferation and migration in vitro, mainly through a MAPK-dependent pathway that activates the nuclear transcription factors c-jun and c-myc [30].

Wu et al. [31] reported that FABP4 can lead to endothelial dysfunction by activating the signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT-1) signaling pathway, which impairs the phosphorylation of eNOS and stromal cell-derived factor 1 (SDF-1) and increases atherosclerosis. Although the molecular mechanisms that regulate FABP4 cellular uptake are still poorly understood, cytokeratin 1 (CK1) has been suggested to facilitate the exogenous FABP4 cellular uptake in endothelial cells [32].

Vascular calcification is another important risk factor for cardiovascular mortality in patients on PD [33]. In patients with chronic kidney disease, vascular calcification is characterized by medial arterial calcification [34]. This study showed that serum FABP4 was positively correlated with corrected calcium, Ca × P, ALP, and iPTH, which are all bone metabolism biomarkers in patients with kidney failure. In vitro, Garaikoetxea et al. [35] found that FABP4 levels were positively correlated with calcification markers, including bone morphogenetic protein (BMP)-2, interlukin-6, and BMP-4 in aortic valves from patients with aortic stenosis, while pharmacological blockade of FABP4 exerted beneficial effects against calcification. The MAPK pathways are also known to play crucial roles in modulating the osteoblastic phenotype transition of VSMCs [36,37], which is the most important mechanism of medial arterial calcification. Vascular calcification in kidney failure rats fed a high-fat diet was aggravated by obesity-related inflammation through activation of the MAPK pathway [38], and inhibition of p38 MAPK decreased VSMC calcification stimulated by inorganic phosphate [39]. FABP4 exerts pro-inflammatory, pro-oxidative stress, and pro-apoptotic effects on bronchial epithelial cells by activating the p38 MAPK pathway [40]. We speculate that FABP4 may upregulate the osteogenic differentiation of VSMCs and deteriorate vascular calcification by activating the MAPK pathways. However, further studies are required to validate this hypothesis.

Inflammation is the core mechanism underlying many diseases, including atherosclerosis, vascular calcification, acute infection, and even cancer. Emerging data have demonstrated that FABP4 may promote the progression of chronic inflam­mation in patients with metabolic syndromes [41]. The up-regulation of FABP4 exerted a pro-inflammation effect on macrophages [42] and bronchial epithelial cells [40]. In contrast, FABP4 inhibitors attenuated inflammation and endoplasmic reticulum stress in the islets of rats with metabolic syndrome [41]. Accordingly, FABP4 knockout mice exhibit reductions in myocardial infarct size, apoptotic index, oxidative stress, and inflammation after myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury [43]. In addition, FABP4-deficient macrophages display reduced IκB kinase and NF-κB activity, which further leads to suppression of inflammatory functions [44]. Severe infections and cancer are the leading causes of death in patients on PD. Recent studies have shown that Chlamydia pneumoniae infection induces endoplasmic reticulum stress and increases FABP4 secretion in murine adipocytes [45]. Furthermore, Liang et al. [46] found that macrophage-derived FABP4 was required for neutrophil recruitment and bacterial clearance in Pseudomonas aeruginosa pneumonia. More data are needed to clarify the role of FABP4 in peritonitis, which is relatively common and specific in patients undergoing PD. Recently, it has been suggested that FABP4 is involved in the progression and poor outcomes of several major human cancers. FABP4 expression was found to affect immune cell infiltration in colon cancer [47]. Exogenous FABP4 significantly increased the proliferation and migration of human hepatocellular cancer cells [48]. Increased FABP4 expression was associated with an increased risk of residual disease in serous ovarian cancer [49].

This study had several limitations that cannot be ignored. First, a single time point measurement of serum FABP4 levels was performed and may not reflect changes over time. Second, the study had a relatively small sample size consisting of Chinese patients on PD from a single center, and thus, had limited generalizability. Third, patient selection bias could not be ruled out. For example, patients who were unable to undergo body composition measurements, which are usually more clinically unstable, were not enrolled.

In summary, this prospective study showed that increased serum FABP4 levels can independently predict all-cause and cardiovascular death in patients on PD. The effects of FABP4 on the development of atherosclerosis and inflammation may be possible mechanisms that require further study.

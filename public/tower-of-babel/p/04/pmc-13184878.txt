Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) remains a leading cause of hospitalization and rehospitalization worldwide, contributing substantially to health care use, morbidity, and mortality. Despite advances in guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), rehospitalization rates for patients with HFrEF remain high, often reflecting gaps in postdischarge care, medication adherence, lifestyle modification, and follow-up compliance.1 Thirty-day readmissions for HFrEF are associated with worse outcomes and increased health care costs, prompting hospitals to pursue targeted quality improvement (QI) initiatives aimed at mitigating preventable rehospitalizations.Take-Home Messages•Patient-centered education should be a foundational component of comprehensive HFrEF care.•Structured inpatient education can significantly reduce 30-day and 90-day rehospitalization rates.

Patient education plays a central role in HFrEF management, influencing medication adherence, self-care behaviors, lifestyle modification, and follow-up compliance.2 However, educational efforts during hospitalization are often inconsistent and inadequately standardized. This QI project sought to evaluate whether a structured, standardized patient education intervention could reduce rehospitalization rates among patients admitted with HFrEF at a single institution.

What Prompted the Project Launch

Our facility experienced a concerning increase in rehospitalizations among patients admitted for HFrEF exacerbations. Internal quality metrics and administrative data demonstrated frequent repeat admissions within a short time after discharge. In response, hospital leadership established a multidisciplinary heart failure task force to identify modifiable contributors to this trend and to propose actionable interventions aimed at reducing rehospitalizations.

Project Rationale

A root-cause analysis performed by the heart failure task force revealed that one of the most significant contributors to rehospitalization was patient nonadherence. Specifically, deficiencies were noted in adherence to prescribed medications, understanding of HFrEF as a chronic disease, compliance with dietary and lifestyle recommendations, and attendance at posthospitalization follow-up appointments.

Existing literature supports the role of patient education in improving adherence, self-care behaviors, and outcomes in HFrEF populations.2, 3, 4 However, educational efforts are often fragmented or inconsistently delivered. This QI project was designed to assess whether a simple, standardized educational intervention could meaningfully reduce HFrEF rehospitalizations in a real-world inpatient setting.

Project Description

This single-center, retrospective, before-and-after QI initiative evaluated rehospitalization rates among patients admitted for HFrEF exacerbation before and after implementation of a structured patient education intervention. Preintervention baseline data were collected from patients hospitalized for HFrEF exacerbation during January and February 2025. After implementation of the intervention, postintervention data were collected from patients hospitalized during May and June 2025. All patients in both cohorts were monitored for rehospitalization for a total of 90 days after index hospital discharge. Rehospitalizations occurring within the first 30 days and those occurring between 31 and 90 days postdischarge were captured and analyzed.

The educational intervention consisted of a standardized, 1-page patient education document provided during hospitalization before discharge. Educational content focused on understanding HFrEF as a chronic disease, the role and importance of GDMT, medication adherence, lifestyle modifications (including dietary sodium restriction, fluid management, and physical activity), and the importance of compliance with posthospitalization follow-up appointments (Figure 1). The educational sheet was developed by the resident leading the project and was reviewed by the attending cardiologist for accuracy. Education was provided by the resident physician leading the project when available, or by bedside nursing staff as part of routine discharge planning when the resident was unavailable. Project Deliverables

The primary deliverable of this QI initiative was the development and implementation of a standardized, 1-page patient education document designed to improve understanding of congestive heart failure, GDMT, medication adherence, lifestyle modifications, and the importance of postdischarge follow-up. In addition, clinical staff were educated on the consistent delivery of this intervention to ensure uniform patient counseling before discharge. The project also facilitated integration of structured patient education into routine discharge planning workflows for patients admitted with congestive heart failure. Finally, systematic collection and comparison of rehospitalization data before and after implementation of the intervention served as a key deliverable, enabling assessment of the intervention's impact on 30-day and 90-day HFrEF-related rehospitalization rates.

Impact

During the preintervention period (January-February 2025), 109 patients were hospitalized for HFrEF exacerbation and were monitored for rehospitalization for up to 90 days after discharge. Of these patients, 41 were rehospitalized for HFrEF, yielding an overall 90-day rehospitalization rate of 37.6%. During the postintervention period (May-June 2025), 115 patients hospitalized for HFrEF received structured patient education and were similarly monitored for 90 days after discharge. Among these patients, 18 were rehospitalized for HFrEF, corresponding to an overall 90-day rehospitalization rate of 15.7%. Notably, only 1 patient experienced a 30-day readmission, with the remaining rehospitalizations occurring between 31 and 90 days after discharge (Table 1).

These findings demonstrate a substantial reduction in both overall 90-day HFrEF rehospitalizations and early (30-day) readmissions after implementation of a structured patient education intervention, consistent with prior evidence supporting education-focused transitional care interventions.3, 4, 5 The marked decrease in 30-day rehospitalizations suggests improved transition of care, enhanced patient understanding of HFrEF and GDMT, and better adherence to medications, lifestyle recommendations, and postdischarge follow-up.

Future steps include expanding the educational intervention to incorporate caregiver participation, reinforcing education during outpatient follow-up visits, and using teach-back methodologies to confirm patient comprehension. Additional planned initiatives include assessing medication adherence and appointment compliance as well as exploring the use of multilingual or digital educational tools to enhance accessibility and sustainability of the intervention.

Discussion

This QI initiative demonstrates that a simple, low-cost educational intervention can be associated with a meaningful reduction in HFrEF rehospitalizations. The findings align with existing evidence highlighting the importance of patient engagement, health literacy, and adherence in chronic disease management.2, 3, 4

Notably, the intervention required minimal resources and was easily integrated into existing discharge workflows, enhancing its sustainability. By addressing knowledge gaps related to GDMT, lifestyle modification, and follow-up care, the project targeted modifiable drivers of rehospitalization identified during root-cause analysis.

During the educational encounters, several patients expressed concerns regarding barriers to postdischarge care, including difficulty scheduling follow-up appointments, limited transportation to outpatient visits, and challenges affording prescribed medications. When these concerns were identified, additional guidance and support were provided. Case management services were engaged to assist patients with scheduling follow-up appointments, identifying transportation resources for outpatient visits, and accessing medication assistance programs or coupons to help reduce medication costs.

Limitations of this study include the single-center design, lack of randomization, and potential confounding factors such as secular trends or concurrent system-level improvements. Additionally, the project did not assess patient comprehension or long-term adherence beyond rehospitalization rates. Despite these limitations, the magnitude of improvement supports the value of patient education as a key component of congestive heart failure care.1, 2, 3

Conclusions

A structured, standardized patient education intervention was associated with a significant reduction in HFrEF rehospitalizations at our institution. Educating patients about their disease, GDMT, lifestyle factors, and follow-up compliance represents an effective and scalable strategy to improve outcomes in patients with HFrEF. This QI initiative underscores the importance of patient-centered education as a foundational element of high-quality heart failure care.

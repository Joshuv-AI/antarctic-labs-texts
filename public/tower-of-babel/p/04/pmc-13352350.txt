Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Teresa Chavez Capilla, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Mariano Lopez Romano, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Oriol Magrans, Iris Mangas, Silvia Mazzega, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea, Juan Manuel Parra Morte, Simone Rizzuto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authority of the rapporteur Member State The Netherlands for the pesticide active substance Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative uses of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 as a fungicide and nematicide for potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and applied by drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module treatment on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnel) protected structures). The reliable endpoints, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment, are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed. Concerns are identified.Keywords: Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001, fungicide, nematicide, peer review, pesticide, risk assessment

SUMMARY

Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council, the rapporteur Member State (RMS), The Netherlands, received an application from FMC Agricultural Solutions A/S on 30 November 2018 for approval. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 4 September 2019.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was provided by the RMS in the draft assessment report (DAR) and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The following conclusions are derived.

The uses of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 according to the representative uses as a fungicide and nematicide for potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and applied by drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module treatment on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnel) protected structures), as proposed at EU level result in a sufficient fungicidal and nematicidal efficacy against the target fungi and nematodes.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to identity, biological properties of the microbial active substance and physical, chemical and technical properties of the representative formulation and analytical methods.

In the area of mammalian toxicology and non‐dietary exposure of humans, there are no issues that could not be finalised or critical areas of concern.

For pesticide residues, a data gap for qualitative and quantitative occurrence of bacitracin in edible commodities under conditions of the representative uses was identified. In consequence, the consumer risk assessment cannot be finalised.

In the area of environmental fate and behaviour, further information might be necessary pending outstanding information on the levels of bacitracin not yet excluded from being present in the manufactured active substance.

In the area of ecotoxicology, issues that could not be finalised or critical area of concerns were not identified.

BACKGROUND

Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council 1 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) lays down, inter alia, the detailed rules as regards the procedure and conditions for approval of active substances. This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States and the applicant(s) for comments on the initial evaluation in the draft assessment report (DAR), provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS), and the organisation of an expert consultation, where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether an active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation (also taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation) within 120 days from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of 30 days where an expert consultation is necessary, and a further extension of up to 150 days where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 12(3).

Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of the Regulation, the RMS, The Netherlands (hereinafter referred to as the ‘RMS’), received an application from FMC Agricultural Solutions A/S on 30 November 2018 for approval of the active substance Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 4 September 2019.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 in the DAR, which was received by EFSA on 13 February 2023 (The Netherlands, 2023). The peer review was initiated on 20 April 2023 by dispatching the DAR to the Member States and the applicant, FMC Agricultural Solutions A/S, for consultation and comments. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the DAR. The comments received were collated by EFSA and forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of a reporting table. The applicant was invited to respond to the comments in column 3 of the reporting table. The comments and the applicant's response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicant in accordance with Article 12(3) of the Regulation were considered in a tele‐conference between EFSA and the RMS on 15 March 2024. On the basis of the comments received, the applicant's response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicant, and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of potential effects on human/mammalian health, and potential effects on non‐target organisms.

The outcome of the tele‐conference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments, is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA should adopt a conclusion on whether Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation, taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment took place with Member States via a written procedure in April 2026.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 as a fungicide and nematicide for potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and applied by drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module treatment on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnel) protected structures), as proposed by the applicant. THE MICROBIAL ACTIVE SUBSTANCE AND THE FORMULATION FOR REPRESENTATIVE USES

The microbial active substance is currently identified as B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001, which was considered to be an acceptable update of the previous nomenclature Bacillus licheniformis FMCH001.

B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 is a bacterium deposited at Leibniz Institute DSMZ‐German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbH under the deposit number DSM 32154.

There is not any area of concern with respect to the identity of the microorganism.

The formulated product for the representative uses for the evaluation was ‘F4018‐4’, a suspension concentrate (SC) containing 2.3 × 1010–2.3 × 1011 colony forming units (CFU)/mL of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 and 2.3 × 1010–2.3 × 1011 CFU/mL of Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002.

The information on the microbial active substance and the formulation for representative uses, including the co‐formulants in this formulation, was considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. None of the co‐formulants is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 2 however a co‐formulant is a currently approved active substance, and two co‐formulants are not approved active substances under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009. 3 , 4 A data gap for the composition of a co‐formulant was identified (see Section 10). 5

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was considered as a second active substance pending approval in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. Details on the composition of the formulations cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, however this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review. A proposal for classification of the formulation(s) according to Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 was provided by the applicant and assessed by the RMS (please see Volumes 3 CP of the RAR).

The representative EU uses evaluated were potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) application on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module application on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnels) protected structures) to control soilborne diseases (e.g. Rhizoctonia solani, Pythium ultimum, Phytophthora capsica, Sclerotinia sclerotiorum, Fusarium sp., Phoma lingam, Plasmodiophora brassiceae) and nematodes (e.g. Meloidogyne sp., Heterodera schachtii, Globodera pallida, Globodera rostochiensis). Full details of the GAP can be found in the list of end points in Appendix A.

Data were submitted to conclude that the use of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 according to the representative uses proposed at EU level results in sufficient fungicidal and nematocidal efficacy against the targeted diseases and nematode pests, following the guidance document SANCO/2012/11251‐rev. 4 (European Commission, 2014).

CONCLUSIONS OF THE EVALUATION

General aspects

With regard to the mammalian toxicity information available for the formulation for representative uses ‘F4018‐4’, no acceptable acute toxicity studies were provided. With regard to the co‐formulants contained in ‘F4018‐4’, sufficient toxicological data was available for all components, but four (three present in limited amounts – below 1% – and one present well below 10%). For these four co‐formulants, the experts considered that the available toxicological information did not sufficiently address the genotoxicity and/or general toxicity potential of ‘F4018‐4’ over the short‐ and long‐term and that they might be considered for further assessment. The collected information (not covering all endpoints), including information from the existing uses other than plant protection products, under other regulated EU frameworks, did not highlight any concern (see Section 10).5

The availability of ecotoxicity data with the formulation for representative uses (‘F4018‐4’) was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168 6 (refer to Section 6). The experts also discussed the data retrieval search and the available data for the individual components. Considering the reasoning agreed by the experts, no concerns were identified. Nevertheless, pending on the outcome on identified missing data for toxicology for some components in the formulation for representative uses, further consideration to non‐target organisms may be necessary.

SPECIFICATION OF THE MICROORGANISM, PHYSICAL AND TECHNICAL PROPERTIES AND METHODS OF ANALYSIS

The following guidance documents were followed in the production of this conclusion: European Commission (2012) and EFSA FEEDAP Panel (2014, 2018).

The minimum and maximum content of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 in the microbial pest control agent as manufactured (MPCA‐AM) was 7.5 × 1011–7.5 × 1012 CFU/g. A clarification is needed at which manufacturing process scale the representative batches have been produced (e.g. pilot scale or industrial scale production) (data gap see Section 10). A data gap was identified for details on the chemical identity and composition of some of the materials used in the manufacturing process (see Section 10). Bacitracin and subtilisin were concluded as metabolites of potential concern (see Section 2). Data gaps for their analysis in the MPCA‐AM, together with description and validation of the analytical methods for analysis were identified (see Sections 2 and 10). The content of microbial contaminants in MPCA and MPCP were below the limits defined in the Working Document on Microbial Contaminant Limits (European Commission, 2012).

Multilocus sequence analysis was used to identify the microorganism as belonging to B. paralicheniformis species. PCR amplification combined with restriction digestion were used to obtain strain‐specific markers. It was not sufficiently demonstrated that the developed strain‐specific markers could distinguish B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 from other closely related strains (data gap, see Section 10). It is noted that the genome of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was completely sequenced, however the raw data were not submitted or deposited in an international database/archive by the applicant.

It was demonstrated that the formulation for representative uses is stable when it is stored for 24 months at 25°C in its commercial packaging. A data gap for assessing suspensibility in a low temperature stability study was identified (see Section 10). It is noted that pourability of the formulation was outside of the acceptable limits (5%) therefore data on the residue after recommended rinsing procedures is required (data gap, see Section 10). In addition, a data gap for data on the adherence and distribution of the formulation to seeds was identified (see Section 10).

Acceptable methods were available for the determination of the microorganism in the MPCA and MPCP and for determination of the content of contaminating microorganisms in MPCA‐AM and MPCP. Residue definitions were not set for B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 (see Sections 4 and 6), therefore, monitoring methods are not required.

BIOLOGICAL PROPERTIES OF THE MICROORGANISM

Historical background: B. licheniformis is the former species name of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001. B. licheniformis has been used for many years in human probiotics and as a feed additive. The species also has a history of safe use as a veterinary probiotic in the United States and Europe and has been evaluated and approved by the European Food Safety Authority for use in feed supplements in turkeys, chickens and associated minor avian species, pigs, piglets, sows and calves.

Natural occurrence: B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 is a wild‐type strain which was originally isolated from soil in USA. Bacillus licheniformis is a commonly occurring soil microorganism which has a global distribution. It is also a common commensal organism in humans, having been isolated from the skin (based solely on 16S rRNA sequence data) the gastrointestinal tract (based on 16S rRNA sequence data and phenotypic data) and nasal mucosa (with unknown reliability of identification method).

Target organisms: The target organisms for the representative uses are plant pathogenic fungi (such as R. solani, P. ultimum, P. capsica, S. sclerotiorum, Fusarium spp., P. lingam, P. brassiceae) and nematodes (such as G. pallida, G. rostochiensis, Meloidogyne chitwoodi, Heterodera schachtii).

B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 does not exhibit strict host specificity, as its growth is not dependent upon a host organism, but upon the availability of organic matter.

Mode of action: B. paralicheniformis supports plants in defending against pathogens through multiple mechanisms: direct antifungal and antimicrobial activity, induction of systemic resistance, production of volatile organic compounds that enhance plant defences and production of indoleacetic acid, which promotes plant growth. The direct antifungal effect is primarily attributed to the production of lipopeptides that damage cell membranes, leading to cell lysis. However, it is acknowledged that the mode of action has not yet been fully elucidated.

Life cycle: The life cycle of B. paralicheniformis comprises several stages: spore germination into a metabolically active cell, subsequent outgrowth and multiplication, and sporulation when environmental conditions become unfavourable. Endospores represent highly resistant resting forms. Strain‐specific data on growth temperature were not provided; however, literature indicates that the vast majority of the species exhibit optimal growth between 25 and 40°C, typically around 30°C. It should be noted that growth temperatures can vary considerably among strains and between species.

Genetic stability: Genetic transfer is considered unlikely for B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001. Although horizontal gene transfer is a common feature among Bacillus species, literature indicates it occurs at low frequency and the strain FMCH001 does not contain any plasmids.

Screening for acquired antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes in B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 revealed only hits corresponding to genes regarded as intrinsic to the species and therefore transferable at very low frequency. This is consistent with evidence that certain antibiotic resistance genes in B. paralicheniformis species are intrinsic and part of their ancient resistomes, rather than acquired through horizontal gene transfer. However, some limitations were noted in the whole genome sequencing (WGS) analysis, as the raw data were neither submitted nor deposited in an international database/archive, which the experts considered to introduce some uncertainty. 7

In a phenotypic study, B. paralicheniformis FMCH001 showed resistance to erythromycin and sensitivity to all other tested antibiotics (chloramphenicol, clindamycin, gentamycin, kanamycin, streptomycin, tetracycline, vancomycin).

Relationships to pathogens: B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 is not closely related to known pathogens. With the exception of the well‐known human pathogens Bacillus anthracis and Bacillus cereus, most members of the genus Bacillus are only rarely associated with human infection (see also Section 3.1).

Information on metabolites of (potential) concern:

The identification of (secondary) metabolites of (potential) concern for B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was based on a preliminary literature search, followed by a WGS analysis on the strain and by a subsequent literature search to corroborate the (eco)toxicological information for (potential) metabolites.

WGS analysis indicated that B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 has the potential to produce lichenysin, bacitracin, bacillibactin, fengycin, amyloliquecidin GF610 and subtilisin. In contrast, it was found to lack the gene cluster associated with amylosin production and the cytK gene responsible for B. cereus enterotoxin production. Other metabolites reported in the literature and not associated to significant adverse effects, could not be excluded based on the WGS data. Additional PCR analysis of the strain FMCH001 also confirmed that it does not appear to possess genes for biosurfactant production (including mycosubtilin, surfactin, fengycin and plipastatin).

Regarding bacitracin, known to have antibiotic properties, based on WGS and the proposed bioassay for bacitracin production, it has not been possible to exclude its production in the MPCA‐AM. Therefore, bacitracin levels should be analytically determined in the MPCA‐AM (data gap, see Section 10), considering that to prevent the induction of antimicrobial resistance, an amount in the environment below a threshold of 8 μg bacitracin/L has been reported as needing to be ensured. The levels of subtilisin (respiratory sensitiser category 1) should also be analytically determined in the MPCA‐AM and demonstrated to be below the threshold for classification (1%) in the MPCP (data gap, see Sections 3.2 and 10). A more detailed discussion on the relevance to mammalian health is presented in Section 3.2.

POTENTIAL EFFECTS ON HUMAN/MAMMALIAN HEALTH

B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' TC 165 in March 2025. Effects of potential concern for human/mammalian health have not been identified from the available data on B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001, including medical data and acute toxicity studies. Metabolites of potential concern are discussed in Section 3.2.

Mammalian infectivity and pathogenicity of the microorganism and sensitisation effects

In humans, B. licheniformis is typically a common commensal organism, though it can act as a weak opportunistic pathogen under certain circumstances (e.g. physical trauma or severe immunosuppression). The rare cases of foodborne diseases where B. licheniformis was suspected were likely due to misidentifications of B. cereus, as the accuracy of identification is not always clear.

B. paralicheniformis and B. licheniformis are included in the EFSA qualified presumption of safety (QPS) list (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2023) with the following qualifications: absence of acquired antimicrobial resistance genes to clinically relevant antimicrobials, absence of toxigenic potential and absence of bacitracin production. In vitro cytotoxicity studies (not GLP) were provided to demonstrate the absence of toxigenic potential, in accordance with the recommendation of the EFSA FEEDAP Panel (2014); no cytotoxic effect was observed in a Vero cells assay, in a boar sperm motility inhibition assay or in a haemolysis study on blood agar. It is noted that under Commission Regulation (EU) No 283/2013, 8 all studies conducted with microorganisms related to their potential effects on human health must be carried out in compliance with GLP standards. Details on the screening for acquired AMR genes and bacitracin production are provided in Section 2.

As the currently available methods for testing dermal sensitisation are not suitable for testing microorganisms, and suitable methods for testing respiratory sensitisation are also not available, all microorganisms are regarded as potential sensitisers. Therefore, the non‐specific labelling with the warning phrase ‘Contains B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001. Microorganisms may have the potential to provoke sensitising reactions’ is recommended.

B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 is phylogenetically clearly separated from human pathogens belonging to the B. cereus group.

Acute toxicity, pathogenicity and infectivity studies in rats (via oral, inhalation and intravenous routes) were conducted with B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001, and clearance was sufficiently demonstrated. Based on the available information (i.e. history of use, no reports on adverse findings in the literature, QPS status and results from acute studies) it can be concluded that this microorganism is neither infectious nor pathogenic, and no further investigations into infectivity or pathogenicity are required.

Mammalian toxicity of the metabolites of (potential) concern

The identification and toxicological assessment of (secondary) metabolites of potential concern for B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was based on a preliminary literature search, followed by a WGS analysis on the strain and by a subsequent targeted literature search to corroborate the toxicological information for (potential) metabolites (see also Section 2). The outcome was discussed in Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting. 9

B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 has the potential to produce lichenysin, bacillibactin, fengycin and amyloliquecidin GF610. On the basis of no significant adverse effects for human health reported in the literature reviews for these metabolites, the experts agreed that they can be considered of no concern.

The metabolite bacitracin is known to have antibiotic properties and its production in the MPCA‐AM/MPCP cannot be excluded (see Section 2). Data to demonstrate that bacitracin in the environment after application will be below levels that prevent the induction of antimicrobial resistance of possible concern to human and animal health were not provided (see Section 2 and data gap in Section 10).

Subtilisin, classified as respiratory sensitiser Category 1, should be analytically determined and demonstrated to be below the threshold for classification (1%) in the MPCP (see Section 2 and data gap in Section 10).

Although neither levels below certain thresholds in the MPCA‐AM or MPCP (see Section 2) nor exposure to operators, workers, residents and bystanders during and after application have been sufficiently demonstrated for these two metabolites, the use of personal protective equipment by operators and workers is considered a sufficient risk mitigation measure. The exposure of residents and bystanders resulting from representative uses is considered unlikely to be of concern. The RMS is of the view that the concentration of subtilisin in the MPCA‐AM is only relevant for determining whether classification for respiratory sensitisation (H334) is required and will not impact on the non‐dietary risk assessment.

RESIDUES

The assessment in the residue section is considering criteria outlined in the following guidance document: European Commission (2015).

B. paralicheniformis strain FCMH001 is not approved as a basic substance under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (Criterion I of the guidance), nor it is listed in Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (Criterion II).

Bacitracin and subtilisin were identified as metabolites of potential concern for B. paralicheniformis strain FCMH001 (see Sections 2 and 3.2). Since the strain can produce enzymes used in food (such as subtilisin), subtilisin is not considered of potential concern for dietary exposure.

Considering the representative uses, dietary exposure to B. paralicheniformis strain FCMH001 and to its (secondary) metabolites cannot be excluded. Therefore, Criterion V of the guidance cannot be considered to be met.

The available information on viable counts in or on the relevant crops after treatments according to the representative uses was not sufficient. Consequently, it remains unclear how natural exposure to B. paralicheniformis FCMH001 compares with exposure resulting from its use as a plant protection product. Consequently, Criterion IV of the guidance cannot be considered to be met.

A potential in‐situ production of the metabolite of potential concern bacitracin cannot be excluded. Uses involving drip irrigation up to BBCH 89 on tomato, eggplant, pepper, cucumber, melon and lettuce are considered as more critical than uses involving a single application at BBCH 00. Treatments at BBCH 00 include applications on tomato, aubergine, pepper, cucumber, melon and lettuce performed at pre‐planting, in‐furrow treatments for potatoes and seed treatments for sugar beet, sunflower and rape seeds and maize.

With regards to non‐viable residues, information on occurrence and expected quantities of bacitracin in edible commodities under the GAP‐directed conditions of the representative uses, and particularly at harvest, was not provided (data gap, see Section 9.1).

Therefore, in view of the uncertainties related to the toxicological profile of the non‐viable residues (bacitracin) of B. paralicheniformis strain FCMH001 and particularly regarding potentially anticipated amounts (qualitative and quantitative information) in edible commodities, particularly at harvest, the consumer risk assessment cannot be finalised (see Section 9.1). According to the RMS, the consumer risk assessment can be finalised, as the identified data gap (see above) is not considered necessary because no foreseeable risks were identified.

None of the criteria of the above‐mentioned guidance can be concluded as met.

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

Satisfactory information has been provided in relation to potential interference of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 with the analytical systems for the control of the quality of drinking water provided for in Directive 98/83/EC (see specific Annex VI decision‐making criteria in Part II Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011). As these methods require pathogenic bacteria to be identified and confirmed as absent, it was considered unlikely that B. paralicheniformis would grow on the selective media employed or give a false positive result for the specific reactions used to indicate the presence of E. coli, enterococci or Clostridium perfringens that are used for analysis when controlling the microbial quality of drinking water. Hence B. paralicheniformis will not interfere with methodologies used for such determinations.

Fate and behaviour in the environment of the microorganism

Specific studies on the persistence and multiplication in soil of B. paralicheniformis FCMH001 were not available. Information was included in the dossier indicating that Bacillus species occur in soil at levels significantly above the levels that would result from the proposed use pattern of the product ‘F4018‐4’ also when considering multiple applications of the product over consecutive crop growing seasons. Bacilli primarily occur in bulk soil in their inactive endospore stage, and B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 endospores will persist under these conditions. Its vegetative cells (present when nutrients availability is sufficient, such as in the rhizosphere) are unlikely to compete effectively with other soil microorganisms and therefore are not expected to persist.

In specific investigations where the MPCP ‘F4018‐4’ was added to water (suspended in heat‐sterilised Dutch Standard Water with and without supplementary glucose and incubated in the dark or under UV light for 10 days at 12°C), the CFU of B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 declined to 0.32%–0.45% of their initial measured values after 10 days. This provides evidence that persistence and multiplication in natural water systems (where competition with other organisms absent from the test system would occur) are unlikely.

In air, B. paralicheniformis FCMH001 will not multiply due to the absence of nutrients and will become non‐viable as a result of solar radiation. During and shortly after application, endospores and any vegetative cells present in the product may disperse through the air, which is considered the primary mechanism for the organism mobility. Vertical leaching through the soil column is expected to be limited based on evidence from B. paralicheniformis species.

The predicted environmental densities (PED) in soil and surface water covering the representative uses assessed are provided in Appendix A of this conclusion.

Fate and behaviour in the environment of secondary metabolite(s) of (potential) concern produced by the microorganism under relevant conditions

Information on the rate and degradation in soil or water and adsorption to soil of the metabolites of potential concern bacitracin and subtilisin (as discussed in Sections 2 and 3) was not available. However, as the hazard identified for subtilisin is just sensitisation, this can be considered not relevant for environmental risk assessment where population effects are the protection goal.

Considering the information in Section 5.1 on the ability of vegetative cells and endospores to compete and grow in the bulk soil and natural water systems, in‐situ formation of bacitracin and subtilisin is unlikely except in the rhizosphere where higher nutrient levels are present. The possible levels of these two compounds that might be present in the MPCA‐AM (if produced during manufacture) are currently data gaps (see Section 10).

POTENTIAL EFFECTS ON NON‐TARGET ORGANISMS

Some aspects of the ecotoxicological risk assessment were discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168 in March 2025 (EFSA, 2026).

Birds and mammals

One study was available for birds with the active substance. The available information was considered sufficient to finalise the assessment of infectivity and pathogenicity of B. paralicheniformis strain FCMH001 to birds. The available data do not indicate that the active substance is infective or pathogenic for birds.

Acute toxicity studies on rats were available with the active substance. The available information does not indicate that B. paralicheniformis FCMH001 is either infective or pathogenic for wild mammals (see Section 3).

The available data and WGS analysis indicate the potential for B. paralicheniformis strain FCMH001 to produce various secondary metabolites (see Section 2). While the in‐situ formation of these metabolites is reported to be unlikely (see Section 5.2), it cannot be excluded that bacitracin might be present in the MPCA‐AM (see Section 3.2 and 5.2). Further information is needed regarding the levels of secondary metabolites of potential concern (bacitracin) present in the test materials (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for birds and mammals.

Aquatic organisms

No study was available with the active substance for any aquatic organisms. Studies with the representative formulation ‘F4018‐4’ were available for fish and aquatic invertebrates. Literature studies were available for algae. No study was available for macrophytes.

No inhibitory effects were observed at the highest concentration tested in studies on fish and aquatic invertebrates. A risk assessment based on a margin of safety (MoS) approach for the representative product was available. The assessment concluded that the margin of safety for fish and aquatic invertebrates was relatively large. Considering the study data, the absence of reported adverse effects in the literature, and the MoS approach, the available information was deemed sufficient, and no concerns regarding infectivity or pathogenicity were identified. 10

The risk to algae and macrophytes for infectivity and pathogenicity was considered low in a weight‐of‐evidence approach. This conclusion was supported by the following lines of evidence: (i) the application methods limit the exposure to water, (ii) bacteria surviving in water showed rapid decline, (iii) no adverse effects in green algae were reported in two separate literature searches covering the last 16 years and (iv) potential beneficial effects to algae were reported in the literature. 11

No concerns were identified regarding the production of secondary metabolite of potential concern to aquatic organisms for any representative use (see Section 5). However, additional information is needed on the levels of secondary metabolites present in the test materials used in the ecotoxicology studies (see Sections 1 and 10).

Bees

25 day‐contact and 12 day‐oral studies were available with the active substance. Both studies were limit tests with at least 100 times the maximum field application rate. No study was available with the representative formulation. Additional peer‐reviewed open literature studies together with B. subtilis strain FMCH002 were also available. For wild bees, it was noted that no regulatory or peer‐reviewed literature data were available to perform a risk assessment.

A quantitative risk assessment indicated a high risk from oral exposure based on a margin of safety approach. At the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168 12 the experts considered that the contact exposure route from seed treatment cannot be entirely excluded for the representative uses in‐furrows, although it is considered less relevant than oral exposure.

Therefore, considering the information above, the limited exposure due to the application method (i.e. seed treatment, drip application and pre‐transplant applications), the high application rate in the oral study (i.e. 100 times the maximum field application rate), the evidence from the peer‐reviewed studies identified in the literature search, and the absence of reported adverse effects, a low risk for bees can be concluded for all intended uses.

No concerns regarding secondary metabolite to honey bees were identified for any representative use. Nevertheless, further information is needed regarding the levels of secondary metabolites present in the test materials used in the ecotoxicology studies (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for bees.

Non‐target arthropods other than bees

Studies with the standard indicator species Typhlodromus pyri and Aphidius rhopalosiphi are not available. Instead, chronic studies were available with the active substance and the representative formulation ‘F4018‐4’ for Hypoaspis aculeifer and Folsomia candida. Survival was not significantly affected when F. candida was exposed to the active substance, but reproduction was significantly reduced after 28 days. For H. aculeifer, significant effects were observed on mortality and on reproduction in tests with the active substance and sterile filtrate controls. No effects were observed in either of the tests conducted with the representative formulation.

At the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168, 13 the results comparison of the test with the active substance and with the representative formulation were discussed. The experts agreed that the different results of the tests are likely due to metabolites, either absent or present at lower concentration in the formulation.

A quantitative risk assessment using a MoS approach indicated a low margin of safety. However, considering (i) that the PEDsoil represents a worst‐case scenario (see Section 5), based on 20 applications simultaneously and (ii) that no effects on juveniles or adults of F. candida and H. aculeifer were observed in the studies with the formulation ‘F4018‐4’, the experts at the meeting agreed that a low risk can be concluded for non‐target soil‐dwelling arthropods for all intended uses.

A concern was not identified regarding in‐situ secondary metabolite of potential concern production to non‐target arthropods other than bees for any representative use (see Section 5). Nevertheless, further information is needed regarding the levels of secondary metabolites present in the test materials (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for non‐target arthropods other than bees.

Soil organisms

No regulatory studies for earthworms were provided. Instead, literature data and a risk assessment based on a weight‐of‐evidence approach were presented.

Considering that (i) B. paralicheniformis occurs naturally in soil, (ii) Bacillus spp. are part of the natural microbiome of earthworms and (iii) no evidence of infectivity or pathogenicity relevant to field populations is reported in literature, waiving earthworms studies for B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was deemed acceptable, and a low risk was concluded for all intended uses.

Additionally, no concerns were identified for soil organisms regarding in‐situ secondary metabolite production (see Section 5) for all representative uses. However, further information is needed regarding the levels of secondary metabolites of potential concern present in the test materials (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for non‐target arthropods other than bees.

No studies on the effects of the active substance or the representative formulation on soil microorganisms were provided. During the peer review, it was agreed that studies with B. paralicheniformis FMCH001 are not necessary, and a low risk for soil microorganisms can be concluded for all intended uses.

Additional information

It is noted that the presence of a functional bacitracin biosynthetic gene cluster cannot be excluded. This might indicate a capacity to produce bacitracin, which might lead to microbial selection in the environment. Nevertheless, due to limited information on whether the bacitracin biosynthetic gene cluster is expressed under environmental conditions, the relevance of in‐situ production of bacitracin is unclear.

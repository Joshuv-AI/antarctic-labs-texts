A Case of Angiolymphoid Hyperplasia with Eosinophilia Associated with Bowen’s Disease
Authors: Jong Heon Jeong, Hui Young Shin, Yu Jeong Park, Jong Soo Hong, Ai-Young Lee, Seung Ho Lee
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Angiolymphoid hyperplasia with eosinophilia (ALHE) is a rare benign vascular neoplasm with debated etiology. It was first reported by Wells and Whimster in 19691,2. It appears as persistent and recurrent erythematous papules or nodules, typically occurring on the face, scalp, and ear. Histopathologic findings show characteristic “cobblestone” appearance of enlarged, epithelioid endothelial cells projecting into the lumina of vessels with perivascular infiltrate of lymphocytes and eosinophils1,2. Here, we report a case of ALHE associated with Bowen’s disease (BD).

A 55-year-old Korean man presented with a slowly enlarging, 1.0×0.6 cm sized, erythematous to brownish plaque on the penile shaft with a 6-month duration (Fig. 1). A punch biopsy revealed hyperkeratosis, parakeratosis, acanthosis, and atypical keratinocytes throughout the epidermis with ‘windblown’ appearance. In addition, there were proliferation of vascular structures lined by enlarged epithelioid endothelial cells showing cytoplasmic vacuoles and perivascular infiltrate of numerous lymphocytes and eosinophils in the dermis (Fig. 2A). Based on histopathologic findings, he was diagnosed with ALHE associated with BD. ALHE was confined to the area of BD in the specimen from complete excision (Fig. 2B). After excision, there was no recurrence at follow-up of 9-month.

There has been a case report of ALHE associated with squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). In that case, lesions of ALHE occurred on the anterior aspect of one ear with a SCC on the posterior aspect of the same ear simultaneously3. However, we could not find any report in which ALHE occurred in the exactly same site with squamous cell malignant lesion like in our case. ALHE and BD might have occurred coincidentally without having any relationship with each other. However, we think that one lesion might have induced the other one rather than they have occurred independently based on the following facts: 1) Both ALHE and BD are rare; 2) the penis is not common site of ALHE in this case, and 3) two lesions occurred on the precisely same area.

It is unclear whether ALHE is a vascular neoplasm or a reactive process1,4. Significant percentage of ALHE was attributed to a reactive basis, probably secondary to damage and repair of vessels5. In the forementioned case, after surgical removal of the SCC, ALHE resolved spontaneously. The authors suggested that ALHE might be secondary to the SCC and that the release of angiogenic factors by the SCC or vascular damage by infiltration of the malignant neoplasm was a possible explanation for the development of ALHE. ALHE was associated with BD in our case. The fact that BD commonly presents on the genitalia supports that BD might precede ALHE. BD is limited within the epidermis. Therefore, it could induce ALHE by releasing some factors such as angiogenic ones rather than causing dermal vascular damage.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first case of ALHE occurring beneath BD. This case supports that ALHE is a reactive process rather than a vascular neoplasm. Neoplasm could be one of stimuli that induce ALHE.

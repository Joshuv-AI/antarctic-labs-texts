Pathological response and metabolites’ prognostic role in HER2-positive breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant pyrotinib, trastuzumab, nab-paclitaxel, and carboplatin: a single-arm phase II trial
Authors: Mei Liu, La Zou, Ningning Zhang, Wenqi Zhou, Xianjun Pan, Yongchun Deng, Yeli Yue, Jing Wu, Xinrui Liang, Maoshan Chen, Xiaohua Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of pyrotinib in combination with trastuzumab, nab-paclitaxel, and carboplatin (TCbHPy) in the neoadjuvant setting (ChiCTR2100043523), as well as the role of metabolites in predicting the efficacy of neoadjuvant therapy (NAT).MethodsIn this study, treatment-naive women with HER2-positive early or locally advanced breast cancer (II–III) were enrolled and received six neoadjuvant cycles of pyrotinib (400 mg) once daily, plus trastuzumab (8 mg/kg loading dose, followed by 6 mg/kg), nab-paclitaxel (260 mg/m2), and carboplatin (area under the curve [AUC] = 6) every 3 weeks. Plasma samples from 26 patients were collected at baseline, and serum metabolites were analyzed based on liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry data. The primary endpoint was the total pathological complete response (tpCR; ypT0/is and ypN0) rate. Secondary endpoints include objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), breast pCR (bpCR; ypT0/is) rate, safety, and the predictive effect of metabolites on NAT.ResultsAmong the 35 patients enrolled, the tpCR rate was 55.9% (19/34). For women with hormone receptor–negative and–positive tumors, the tpCR rates were 64.7% and 47.1% (P = 0.5034). The bpCR, ORR, and DCR were 61.8%, 91.2%, and 100.0%, respectively. The most common grade 3–4 adverse events were diarrhea (17.6%), vomiting (17.6%), and alopecia (11.8%), with 2 patients experiencing neutropenic fever. There were significant differences in plasma metabolic profiles between tpCR patients and non-tpCR patients at baseline. These differentially expressed metabolites were markedly enriched in the ABC transporter pathway. The area under the curve values for discriminating the tpCR and non-tPCR groups from the NAT of the single potential metabolite [spermidine and l-isoleucine] or combined panel of the two metabolites were greater than 0.875, implying that spermidine and l-isoleucine could be useful biomarkers for distinguishing between non-sensitive and sensitive individuals.ConclusionsNeoadjuvant TCbHPy is an effective and safe treatment option for HER2-positive breast cancer. Serum-metabolomics could be a powerful tool for exploring informative biomarkers for predicting efficacy, and facilitate the development of new treatment targets for insensitive patients. A randomized controlled trial is necessary to validate our findings.

Introduction

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer (BC) is a highly aggressive subtype associated with a poor prognosis and accounts for approximately 15–20% of all BC [1]. Anti-HER2 therapy significantly improves outcomes in these patients. Until now, trastuzumab combined with pertuzumab and chemotherapy was the approved dual anti-HER2 regimen for neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) in patients with HER2-positive early BC [2–4]. In addition, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors may provide more new options for NAT of HER2-positive BC.

TKIs exhibit robust anti-tumor activities against BC. In the NeoALTTO study, patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy with trastuzumab plus lapatinib and chemotherapy had significantly higher pathologic complete response (pCR) (51.3% vs. 29.5%) than patients receiving trastuzumab alone combined with chemotherapy [5]. A meta-analysis of 1410 patients disclosed that in comparison with trastuzumab monotherapy, the combination of lapatinib and trastuzumab significantly enhanced recurrence-free survival (HR = 0.62, 95% CI 0.46–0.85) and OS (HR = 0.65, 95% CI 0.43–0.98) when combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy [6]. Those findings suggested combining trastuzumab with TKIs might be a promising neoadjuvant strategy. Also, compared with monoclonal antibodies, TKIs possess the advantages of an oral administration route, the inhibition of both ligand-dependent and independent signaling, and a low risk of cardiac toxicity.

The efficacy of ADC in advanced HER2-positive BC is remarkable, encouraging further research and exploration of its value in NAT. PREDIX HER2 clinical trials demonstrated that neoadjuvant Trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) alone produced equivalent pCR with a better safety profile [7]. However, in the KRISTINE trial, T-DM1 + pertuzumab NAT for HER2-positive BC had a lower pCR rate than the TCbHP (Paclitaxel, carboplatin, trastuzumab, and pertuzumab) regimen. Long-term follow-up revealed that the group receiving T-DM1 and pertuzumab had a lower 3-year EFS than the group receiving TCbHP [8]. Trastuzumab Deruxtecan (T-DXd) is a novel anti-HER2-targeted ADC drug that has shown promising results in patients with advanced HER2-positive BC [9–10]. DESTINY Breast11 explores the efficacy and safety of T-DXd monotherapy versus T-DXd-sequenced THP (paclitaxel, trastuzumab, and pertuzumab) and ddAC (Doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide)-THP NAT in high-risk, locally advanced or inflammatory HER2-positive early BC patients [11]. However, the results of the study have not yet been published. Furthermore, several studies have explored the use of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in HER2-positive NAT. According to the Neo-PATH research, which assessed the effectiveness of atezolizumab in combination with chemotherapy and anti-HER2 double target as NAT for HER2-positive BC, the tpCR of the atezolizumab group was 61.2% [12]. Further trials of neoadjuvant immunotherapy for early-stage HER2-positive BC, such as NeoHIP [13] and APTneo [14], are ongoing.

Pyrotinib is an irreversible TKI of the pan-ErbB receptor. By binding covalently to the ATP-binding site of the intracellular kinase domain of HER, pyrotinib can inhibit the autophosphorylation of the homodimers/heterodimers of HER, thereby blocking the Ras/Raf/MEK/MAPK and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways [15]. It has shown good tolerance and antitumor activity in advanced and metastatic HER2-positive BC [16–19]. However, its role in neoadjuvant therapy is still being explored. Several trials of pyrotinib combined with other anti-tumor agents have been conducted in the neoadjuvant phase [20–23], and the results show that pyrotinib can significantly improve the pCR and ORR of patients with neoadjuvant therapy. However, chemotherapy regimens in combination with trastuzumab and pyrotinib vary. How to optimize the chemotherapy regimen and balance the efficacy and toxicity of neoadjuvant therapy is a key issue that needs to be further explored.

TCb in combination with anti-HER2 targeted therapy proved effective for the (neo)adjuvant treatment of HER2-positive BC, with a lower incidence of long-term cardiac toxicity than anthracycline-containing chemotherapy regimens [4, 24, 25]. Furthermore, studies have shown that in neoadjuvant therapy, nab-paclitaxel has a higher pCR rate and longer-term efficacy than paclitaxel, making it an attractive partner for combination therapy [26]. As a result, in this trial, we used neoadjuvant pyrotinib in combination with trastuzumab, albumin-paclitaxel, and carboplatin (TCbHPy) to treat HER2-positive early or locally advanced BC and assess its efficacy and safety.

The resistance of cancer cells to HER2-targeted drugs remains an important clinical challenge. Even with anti-HER2 dual-targeted neoadjuvant therapy, around 30–50% of patients are unable to achieve pCR during surgery, and such patients frequently have a poor prognosis [2–4, 5, 20]. Another clinical problem is that there is no identified useful indicator for predicting the pathological response to NAT. Exploring biomarkers for assessing pathologic responses to NAT is therefore crucial, as it will aid in the development of new ways for overcoming resistance and have a substantial impact on personalized therapy for HER2-positive BC.

The metabolome represents the downstream output of the genome, transcriptome, and proteome, as well as the metabolic response to external environmental disturbances. It is the closest to the phenotype of the omics, but also the ultimate embodiment of the overall function or state of the biological system [27, 28]. Metabolic changes in tumor cells are considered a significant feature of tumor cells adapting to changes in the microenvironment to maintain survival and are a well-known marker of malignant tumors [29]. In fact, HER2-mediated signaling is associated with the activation of certain metabolic pathways, underscoring the importance of metabolic dysregulation in maintaining uncontrolled growth, proliferation, and therapeutic resistance in HER2-positive BC cells [30]. Metabolite panels are frequently used in the diagnosis, categorization, evaluation of treatment response, and prognosis of BC [31–39]. However, research into biomarkers for assessing the therapeutic response of HER2-positive BC patients to NAT via metabolomics is relatively limited. Furthermore, the present research are primarily concerned with single-targeted therapy rather than dual-targeted therapy. So far, there has been no study on the relationship between the efficacy and metabolomics of the combination regimen based on pyrotinib and trastuzumab.

In this trial, we aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of TCbHPy in the neoadjuvant setting, as well as the role of metabolites in predicting the efficacy of NAT. The results showed that TCbHPy neoadjuvant therapy is an effective and safe option for the treatment of HER2-positive BC. Furthermore, we discovered that there were significant differences in plasma metabolic profiles between tpCR patients and non-tpCR patients, and spermidine and l-isoleucine could be useful biomarkers for discriminating between non-sensitive and sensitive individuals.

Methods

Study design and patient population

This single-arm, single-center phase II clinical trial was conducted at the Chongqing University Cancer Hospital. All procedures followed the Declaration of Helsinki and the Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice (ChiCTR2100043523). The study protocol received approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of Chongqing University Cancer Hospital (CZLS2021022-C). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion in the study.

The key inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients (ages 18–75) with pathologically confirmed HER2-positive (According to 2018 ASCO/CAP guidelines, HER2-positive expression is associated with IHC 3 + or/and FISH (+)) invasive BC (stages II-III), who had not received prior therapy for invasive BC; (2) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0–1; (3) the presence of a measurable primary breast tumor suitable for evaluation; and (4) adequate organ functions. Exclusion criteria included pregnancy or breastfeeding, a second primary malignancy, mental illness, or any condition that might impact compliance.

Study treatment and procedures

All enrolled patients received 6 neoadjuvant cycles of oral pyrotinib (400 mg) once daily, plus intravenous trastuzumab (8 mg/kg loading dose, followed by 6 mg/kg), nab-paclitaxel (260 mg/m2), and carboplatin (area under the curve [AUC] = 6) every 3 weeks. Before initiation of neoadjuvant therapy, the primary tumor site should be physically marked using skin tattooing or metallic clip for appropriate surgical excision in case of tumor regression after neoadjuvant therapy. To evaluate clinical response, a baseline magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan was obtained within 4 weeks before the first dose of NAT, followed by MRIs every 6 weeks. The Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST version 1.1) were employed to evaluate the efficacy.

Evaluation of the pathological response

Pathological response was assessed using the semi-quantitative Miller-Payne (MP) grading method. Based on the pathological assessment of surgical materials, this calculates the percentage reduction of invasive tumor volume and cellularity after NAT [40]. In this investigation, the total pathologic complete response (tpCR) was defined as the absence of residual invasive disease after surgery in both the breast (MP grade 5) and the axillary lymph nodes (ypT0/is ypN0). The breast pathologic complete response (bpCR) was defined as an MP grade of G5, with the possibility of concurrent regional lymph node metastases.

Safety assessment

Adverse events were tracked from the time of informed consent to 28 days after the final NAT and graded using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (NCI-CTCAE 5.0), with the incidence and severity of all adverse events calculated.

Non-targeted metabolomic analysis

Sample collection

A total of 26 blood specimens of patients were collected before the first dose of neoadjuvant therapy. Blood samples were taken from the elbow vein in the fasting state in the morning, kept in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid vacuum tubes (BD Vacutainer, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA), and centrifuged for 10 min at 3000 rpm at 4 °C. Immediately after separation, the serum was kept at -80 °C until detection by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC–MS).

LC–MS detection

Plasma samples from patients with pCR (N = 12) and non-pCR (N = 14) at baseline were designated as the A and C groups, respectively. These groupings served as the basis for the metabolomic analyses. The sample preparation and LC–MS detection followed known techniques as delineated in the previous literature [41].

Data processing

The raw data were firstly converted to mzXML format by MSConvert in ProteoWizard software package (v3.0.8789) [42] and processed using R XCMS(v3.12.0) [43] for feature detection, retention time correction and alignment. Key parameters settings were set: ppm = 15, peakwidth = c(5, 30), mzdiff = 0.01, method = centWave. The batch effect was then eliminated by correcting the data based on QC samples. Metabolites with RSD > 30% in QC samples were filtered and then used for subsequent data analysis.

The metabolites were identified by accurate mass and MS/MS data, which were matched with HMDB (http://www.hmdb.ca), massbank (http://www.massbank.jp/), KEGG (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/), LipidMaps (http://www.lipidmaps.org), mzcloud (https://www.mzcloud.org), and the metabolite database built by Panomix Biomedical Tech Co., Ltd. (Suzhou, China). The molecular weight of metabolites was determined according to the m/z (mass-to-charge ratio) of parent ions in MS data. The molecular formula was predicted by ppm (parts per million) and adduction and then matched with the database to realize MS identification of metabolites. At the same time, the MS/MS data from the quantitative table of MS/MS data were matched with the fragment ions and other information of each metabolite in the database, to realize the MS/MS identification of metabolites.

Then two different multivariate statistical analysis models, unsupervised and supervised, were applied to discriminate the groups (PCA; PLS-DA; OPLS-DA) by R package (v1.22.0). The statistical significance of P value was obtained by statistical test between groups. Finally, combined with P value, VIP (OPLS-DA variable projection importance) and FC (multiple of the difference between groups) to screen biomarker metabolites. By default, when P value < 0.05 and VIP value > 1, we think that metabolites were considered to have significant differential expression [44]. To determine and appreciate the biological importance of these differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs) between the tpCR and non-tPCR groups, the enriched pathway analysis of changed metabolites was performed using MetaboAnalyst, which combines results from powerful pathway enrichment analysis with the pathway topology analysis. The identified metabolites in metabolomics were then mapped to the KEGG pathway for biological interpretation of higher-level systemic functions. The metabolites and corresponding pathways were visualized using KEGG Mapper tool.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was the total pathological complete response (tpCR; ypT0/is and ypN0) rate. Secondary endpoints include objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), breast pCR (bpCR; ypT0/is) rate, safety, and the predictive effect of metabolites on NAT.

Statistical analysis

Simon’s 2-stage design [45] with a 2-sided α of 5% and a power of 80% was applied for sample size calculation. The null hypothesis of the pCR rate with TCbH was 40%, the pCR rate with TCbHP was about 40 ~ 70% [2–4], and the alternative hypothesis with the addition of pyrotinib to TCbH was 62%. That is, in the first stage, if 8 or more of 20 patients had a pCR, another 12 would be accrued to the second stage, otherwise the study will be terminated. With the assumption of a 10% dropout rate, 35 patients were needed.

Continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (range), and categorical data were presented as frequency, percentage, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Chi-square test was used to compare dichotomous variables in other clinicopathological features analyses. The significance of metabolites in the two groups was calculated using Wilcoxon test and Kruskal–Wallis test, respectively. Metabolites with VIP > 1.0 and P < 0.05 between two groups were identified as differentially expressed. To identify the key metabolites associated with the treatment efficacy, random forest (RF) was employed to assess candidate metabolites for predicting treatment response to NAT using DEMs from pCR and non-pCR patients. The area under receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) (R package pROC) was applied to evaluate the performance of the predictive model based on the metabolites chosen through RF analysis. Statistical analysis and result visualization were performed using SPSS 22.0, R (v3.2.0), and GraphPad Prism software (v8.0). Unless otherwise specified, variables were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

From March 4, 2021, to May 20, 2024, a total of 35 patients were enrolled. Among them, 34 patients (94%) completed the entire NAT. 1 patient refused follow-up treatment due to their own reasons after the 2 cycles of NAT, and was automatically discharged from the group to receive surgery directly. All 34 patients were included in the efficacy and safety data analysis. The baseline characteristics of participants are shown in Table 1. The median age was 52 years (range, 29–63 years). 58.8% of patients were postmenopausal (n = 20); the proportion of patients with clinical stage III disease was 64.7% (n = 22); the proportion of patients with N1-3 was 91.2% (n = 31), and over half were cN2-3 (n = 21; 61.8%). Hormone receptor (HR)-positive patients accounted for 50.0% (n = 17), and patients with Ki-67 index ≥ 30% accounted for 64.7% (n = 22).

Efficacy

In the first stage, pCR was reached in 9 (45%) of 20 patients and the trial continued to full accrual. Overall, postoperative pathological evaluation showed that 55.9% (19/34) of patients achieved tpCR(Table 2). Subgroup analyses revealed that, compared to Stage Ⅲ BC, tpCR was more frequent in Stage Ⅱ (91.7% vs. 36.4%, P = 0.0321). For women with HR-negative and HR-positive BC, the tpCR rate was higher in HR-negative BC (64.7% vs. 47.1%), but the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.5034). There was no significant difference between tpCR and non-tpCR groups in terms of age (P = 0.1253), clinical status of lymph nodes (P = 0.9173), or Ki-67 index (P = 0.7579) (Table 3).

Safety

All 34 patients were included in the safety group. Table 4 lists treatment-related AEs occurring in 10% or more of the 34 patients. Generally, the most common grade 3–4 AEs were diarrhea (17.6%), vomiting (17.6%), and alopecia (8.8%), with 2 patients experiencing neutropenic fever. There were no deaths during the period, and no patients experienced grade 4 diarrhea, as well as grade 3 or higher cardiac events. No LVEF < 50% or > 10-point decrease was observed from baseline to pre-surgery.

LC–MS metabolomic analysis

Screening and identification of differential metabolites

LC–MS detected 5879 and 6657 peaks respectively in positive and negative ionization modes. By interacting with databases, 279 metabolites were identified after comparative analysis. The results of OPLS-DA (Fig. 2A) and volcano plot analyses (Fig. 2B) reveal significant differences in the metabolic features between tpCR and non-tpCR groups, involving changes in 19 metabolites. To fully demonstrate the variations in metabolites between the tpCR and non-tPCR groups, we used a hierarchical cluster analysis based on all 19 changed metabolite expressions. Figure 2C shows substantial changes in metabolites between the non-tPCR and tpCR groups. These differences may be connected to the treatment response of breast cancer patients to the neoadjuvant therapy regimen of TCbHPy.

Analysis of differential metabolites and metabolic pathways

The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on the DEMs showed that these DEMs involved 26 metabolic pathways, and the bubble chart (Fig. 3A) showed the top 20 significantly different pathways. The most common enrichment factors were found to be connected with the ABC transporter (P = 0.036), phospholipase D signaling (P = 0.058), and Biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids (P = 0.059) pathways. Figure 3B depicts the metabolite network, which displays the DEMs participating in these three pathways. However, differences involving only the ABC transporter pathway were statistically significant. These data suggest that changed metabolites and pathways may be related to treatment responses.

Screening of key DEMs

Finally, two key metabolites spermidine (S) and l-isoleucine (L) were selected by RF analysis. The predictive accuracy of the two metabolites and the combined panel of these metabolites were evaluated for their ability to predict tpCR. The AUC for the single metabolite of S and L reached 0.875 (95% CI 0.736–1.000) and 0.97 (95% CI 0.916–1.000) respectively, and the combined panel of L and S reached 0.988 (95% CI 0.960-1.000) (Fig. 4A). Among the two metabolites, the concentrations of spermidine and l-isoleucine were significantly higher in tpCR group than in non-tpCR group (Fig. 4B). These findings suggested that the two metabolites could be useful biomarkers for discriminating between non-sensitive and sensitive individuals.

Discussion

In the present study, our data showed that neoadjuvant TCbHPy is an effective and safe treatment option for HER2-positive early BC. In line with earlier research [21, 23], our study showed that pyrotinib resulted in high pCR when combined with trastuzumab, nab-paclitaxel, and carboplatin. We also discovered that the neoadjuvant TCbHPy used in this trial can contribute to rapid tumor shrinkage, with an ORR rate of 76.5% after two cycles, 88.2% after four cycles, and 91.2% after six cycles. Subgroup analyses revealed a numerically greater pCR in HR-negative patients, although there was no statistically significant difference (64.7% vs. 47.1%, 0.5034), which is comparable with the NeoATP trial and the trial by Shi Q et al. [21, 46]. Ample evidence has unveiled that complex crosstalk between HER2 and ER signaling lays the foundation for poor response to current HER2-targeted combination chemotherapy in patients with HR + HER2 + BC and for a rationale to block HER2 and ER signaling simultaneously [47, 48]. For HR + HER2 + BC subtylpes, some studies try to use anti-HER-2 dual-target combined endocrine and CDK4/6 inhibitors for NAT, providing a new idea for the treatment of this type of BC [49].

Several studies have demonstrated the efficacy [20–23, 46] of pyrotinib-based dual-targets in NAT. However, How to optimize the chemotherapy regimen while balancing the efficacy and toxicity of NAT is a critical problem that requires additional investigation. The Phase III PHEDRA study showed that the addition of pyrotinib to trastuzumab plus docetaxel significantly increased the pCR rate compared with the placebo group. However, the use of four cycles of single-agent chemotherapy (docetaxel) in the PHEDRA study was not consistent with current clinical practice. And the pCR rate was only 41.0%, which had no advantage compared with another chemotherapy regimen. Furthermore, Shi Q et al. [46] investigated the application of AC-T chemotherapy regimen combined with Pyrotinib and trastuzumab (ECPy-THPy) in neoadjuvant setting, and the findings revealed that the ECPy-THPy regimen produced a high pCR rate (68.6%). Despite the promising outcomes, dual HER2 blockade was only utilized in the second half of the NAT, which may limit its efficiency. Although it is not a head-to-head study, the data showed that during the first half of NAT, ORR (66.4%) was lower than in other studies [20, 21, 23], which is undoubtedly unfavorable in terms of rapid tumor shrinkage and enhancing patient confidence. Second, the incidence of grade ≥ 3 diarrhea (54.3%) was also higher in this study. These may be the reasons for the high rate of patients coming off protocol and permanently discontinued neoadjuvant pyrotinib. The TRAIN-2 study evaluated the efficacy and safety of 9 cycles of TCbHP versus FEC (5-fluorouracil, epirubicin, and cyclophosphamide) + HP (3 cycles) -TCbHP (6 cycles) as neoadjuvant therapy for HER2-positive BC. The results indicated that the pCR of TCbHP was comparable to that of the FEC + HP-TCbHP group (68% vs.67%, P = 0.95) [4]. There was no difference in EFS (93.5% vs. 92.7%) or OS (98.2% vs. 97.7%) at three years. In terms of safety, the TCbHP regimen exhibits lower hematological and cardiovascular toxicity than the regimen containing the anthracycline regimen [50]. Additionally, several trials have supported the efficacy and safety of the TCbHP regimen in early HER2-positive BC [8, 51] .Furthermore, studies have shown that in NAT, nab-paclitaxel has a higher pCR rate and longer-term efficacy than paclitaxel [26]. Therefore, in this study, we adopted 6-cycle TCb (nab-paclitaxel and carboplatin) combined with HPy regimen for NAT. Considering that pyrotinib is prone to cause diarrhea, we adopted loperamide for primary prevention. The results showed that the tpCR rate was 55.9%, and the bpCR rate was 61.8%. The incidence of grade ≥ 3 diarrhea was only 17.6%, which was much lower than other studies [20–23]. Similarly, in the NeoATP study and the study of Liu Z et al. [21, 23], the pCR rates of TCbHPy regimen were 69.81% and 55.1%, respectively. In summary, TCbHPy is an effective and safe option for the treatment of HER2-positive BC, and diarrhea can be effectively controlled through timely management.

Exploring biomarkers to predict pathologic responses to NAT is critical, as it will help develop new ways to overcome drug resistance and have a major impact on personalized treatment of HER2-positive BC. Several studies have explored the association of sTILs and PIK3CA mutation with pCR. Shi Q et al. [52] found that PIK3CA mutation has a significant relationship with the treatment response. However, in other trials, no association was found between TILs [21, 23, 46] and tPIK3CA [21, 23] mutation and pCR. Obviously, contradicting data do not provide useful clinical direction.

In this study, we looked into metabolites’ predictive role in NAT. Importantly, we discovered there were significant differences in plasma metabolic profiles between tpCR patients and non-tpCR patients, and these metabolites were markedly enriched in the ABC transporter pathway. Moreover, we discovered that spermidine and l-isoleucine could be useful biomarkers to distinguish between non-sensitive and sensitive persons, allowing us to detect insensitive individuals before NAT. To our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the predictive role of metabolites in the neoadjuvant therapy of pyrotinib.

ABC transporters are superfamilies of membrane proteins that have ATPase activity and are capable of transmembrane transport of substrates, including hormones, vitamins, fatty acids, sugars, nucleotides, amino acids, peptides, and xenobiotic compounds [53, 54], which play an important role in lipid homeostasis, molecular transport, and signal transmission [55]. Unsurprisingly, ABC transporters hold tremendous biomedical and pharmacological relevance. Continuous efflux of chemotherapy medications mediated by ABC transporters reduces the cumulative concentration of intracellular pharmaceuticals, leading to drug resistance to chemotherapy agents, which is an important cause of multidrug resistance (MDR) [56]. Multiple ABC transporter superfamily members have been linked to MDR of chemotherapy drugs [53]. Among them, ABCB1, ABCG2, and ABCC1 are the most closely related to multidrug resistance in tumors [57], and they can transport various antitumor drugs such as paclitaxel, olaparib, vinblastine, etoposide, and doxorubicin to the outside of cells, reducing their concentration in tumor cells and leading to drug resistance [58, 59]. Interestingly, some TKIs, including lapatinib, erlotinib, and gefitinib, are substrates for ABC transporters. They have been revealed to restore sensitivity in chemotherapy-resistant cancer cells by decreasing the activity of ABC transporters [60–63]. Furthermore, Zhang N et al. investigated potential biomarkers related to the efficacy of neoadjuvant therapy with the TCbHP regimen using metabolomic detection. The findings revealed that there were significant metabolic differences between pCR and non-pCR patients. Moreover, the enriched amino acids are strongly associated with pathways such as ABC transporter [41]. Our findings demonstrate that the ABC transporters pathway is intimately linked to the sensitivity of BC to the neoadjuvant TCbHPy. These results contribute to our understanding of the fundamental mechanisms behind the response of HER2-positive BC patients to NAT therapy.

Spermidine, as a natural component from polyamine members, is originally isolated from semen and also exists in many natural plants, and can be responsible for cell growth and development in eukaryotes [64]. Recent studies indicate that spermidine regulates tumor cells by regulating the cell cycle, polyamine metabolism, anti-cancer immune surveillance, autophagy, and apoptosis [65, 66]. Spermidine and its metabolites have shown potential as biomarkers of effectiveness in several kinds of cancers [67–70]. In addition, their expression levels are closely related to the sensitivity of tumors to anti-tumor drugs. Akyol Z et al. [71] demonstrated that N1,N11-diethylnorspermine overcame Bcl-2 mediated resistance against Paclitaxel treatment in MCF-7 breast cancer cells via activating polyamine catabolic machinery. Velenosi TJ et al. found that diacetylspermine and other spermidine-related metabolites were significantly elevated in doxorubicin-sensitive TNBC patient-derived xenotransplantation. They also discovered that the effect of doxorubicin on Diamine acetyltransferase 1 (SAT1) expression and activity is the mechanism of increased diacetylspermine [72]. In addition, Miolo G et al. discovered that spermidine is a possible indicator pCR to trastuzumab-paclitaxel neoadjuvant therapy in HER-2 positive breast cancer through metabolomics detection [36]. In this study, we discovered that the tpCR group had a noticeably greater spermidine content. Additionally, the AUC values of spermidine for discriminating the tpCR and non-tPCR groups were 0.875, implying that spermidine could be a useful biomarker for distinguishing between non-sensitive and sensitive individuals.

Furthermore, we discovered that l-isoleucine (an isomer of isoleucine) was overexpressed in the tpCR group and was an excellent predictor of the efficacy of TCbHPy neoadjuvant therapy, either alone or in combination with spermidine. Isoleucine is a kind of branched-chain amino acid (BCAAs), a necessary amino acid, which plays an important role in regulating metabolic balance in the body and maintaining normal life activities. BCAAs are imported into the cell by L-type amino acid transporters (LATs) [73–75] and SLC25A44 is responsible for BCAA transport into the mitochondria [76]. Intracellular BCAAs are then converted to branched-chain α-keto acids (BCKAs) including α-ketoisocaproate (KIC), α-keto-β-methylvalerate (KMV), and α-ketoisovalerate (KIV) by branched-chain amino acid transaminases (BCATs), which meanwhile transfer the amino group from BCAAs to α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) to produce glutamate. Glutamate contributes to a number of metabolic fates in proliferating cancer cells, including protein synthesis and synthesis of nucleotides and other nonessential amino acids [77, 78]. Therefore, BCAAs are important nitrogen donors in cancer cells. Accumulating evidence suggests that BCAAs metabolism plays an important role in tumorigenesis and progression. Studies have shown that BCAAs metabolism is directly involved in the acetylation of histones by producing acetyl CoA [79], or indirectly regulates cellular epigenetics by altering the abundance of other metabolites [80–82], and in turn, BCAAs metabolism is also regulated by epigenetic inheritance. The significance and essentiality of BCAA metabolic reprogramming are recently highlighted in many types of human cancers [79, 83–85], Various models have been proposed to elucidate the role of altered BCAA metabolism in tumor progression, stem cell maintenance, and drug resistance. In addition, under stress conditions such as hypoxia, BCAAs metabolism regulates tumor growth by affecting tumor microenvironment (TME) and immune cell activity [86–90]. In several clinical trials, BCAAs supplementation has been reported to improve outcomes in patients with certain types of tumors [91–94]. In our study, we found that l-isoleucine has a high prediction accuracy for NAT efficacy, and this helpful data will serve as a good reference for clinical screening of the population sensitive to the neoadjuvant TCbHPy.

Our study has some limitations. First, this is a single-arm Phase II trial, which gives a wealth of useful information, but additional randomized controlled studies are required to validate our findings. Second, we discovered that HR status might be associated with the efficacy of the neoadjuvant TCbHPy regimen, however, the small sample size may result in the underdiscovery of this predictor. Third, while we discovered that several metabolites may be related to TCbHPy efficacy, this protocol included four different medicines, making it impossible to pinpoint the exact correlation of potential biomarkers to a specific agent. Fourth, the current study does not explain the underlying molecular mechanisms of metabolite changes, which will be addressed in future research.

Conclusion

In the present study, our data showed that neoadjuvant TCbHPy is an effective and safe treatment option for HER2-positive early BC. Importantly, we discovered there were significant differences in plasma metabolic profiles between tpCR patients and non-tpCR patients, and these metabolites were markedly enriched in the ABC transporter pathway. Moreover, we discovered that spermidine and l-isoleucine could be useful biomarkers to distinguish between non-sensitive and sensitive persons. These helpful data will serve as a good reference for clinical screening of the population sensitive to the neoadjuvant TCbHPy, and facilitate the development of new treatment targets for insensitive patients. A randomized controlled trial is necessary to validate our findings.

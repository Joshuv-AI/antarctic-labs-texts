A new quadratic step-up DC-DC converter with low voltage and current stresses
Authors: Sara Hasanpour, Tohid Nouri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a new coupled-inductor-based high step-up DC-DC converter characterized by low voltage and current stress. The use of a coupled inductor (CI) in combination with voltage multiplier cells enhances the voltage gain of the proposed structure. In this topology, the vertical structure results in very low voltage stress across all semiconductor components. Moreover, current sharing between the coupled inductor and the input inductor reduces power dissipation in the magnetic components of the proposed converter. In this circuit, a quasi-resonant cell is employed to reduce switching power loss during turn-off. Additionally, regenerative passive clamp circuits absorb and recycle the energy stored in the leakage inductance of the coupled inductor (CI), thereby limiting the voltage stress on the switches, which operate under simultaneous switching conditions. Therefore, the proposed circuit provides a high voltage gain ratio, low voltage stress, and low current stress, contributing to high efficiency. Detailed presentations of the operational principle and steady-state analysis of the introduced structure are included. The topology’s effectiveness is validated by experimental results obtained from a 200 W laboratory prototype operating between 25 V and 400 V.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23433-w.Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In recent years, high step-up DC-DC converters have been increasingly utilized in applications involving low-voltage sources such as renewable energy sources (RES) (e.g., photovoltaic(PV) panels and fuel cells (FC)) and batteries. {R1-4} Fig. 1 shows the conceptual diagram of RES integration to the load/AC grid. These converters help reduce the number of series-connected RES panels required1. Such topologies function as interface circuits, boosting and regulating the low input DC voltage to a higher, stable output voltage. In addition, high step-up converters are employed in various other applications, including electric vehicles, HID lamps, satellites, robotics, data centers, uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), medical devices, and server power systems. However, these step-up structures must meet several critical requirements, including a high voltage conversion ratio, continuous input current with low ripple, low voltage and current stress, and high efficiency1.

{R1-4} Conceptual diagram of integration of RES to load/AC grid.

Conventional high-gain DC-DC converters with simple structures such as Boost, and SEPIC, suffer from low voltage gain and high voltage stress on the active switch. To address these limitations, various boosting techniques have been employed, including voltage-lift strategies, voltage multiplier cells, switched capacitors/inductors, and cascading methods, to enhance the voltage gain ratio1–3. These approaches help reduce the voltage stress on the active switch while significantly increasing the voltage gain. However, to achieve higher voltage gains with a reduced number of components, magnetic devices, particularly coupled inductors (CI), are often used4,5. CI-based converters offer higher voltage gains through two degrees of freedom: the duty cycle and the magnetic turns ratio, thereby improving overall circuit performance. Nevertheless, voltage spikes across the active switches, primarily due to the leakage inductance of the CI, are commonly suppressed using passive or active clamp circuits6–9. Magnetic components are frequently combined with other voltage-boosting techniques to further increase the voltage gain. As a result, numerous CI-based high-voltage gain structures have been proposed to date.

In10–12, new types of two-winding CI-based step-up DC-DC converters with low input current ripple have been proposed. However, these converters experience high voltage stress across the output diode and output capacitor. Additionally, recent designs involving three-winding Cis13,14 and combined CI/built-in transformer (BIT) structures15 have been introduced to achieve ultra-high voltage gain. Although these converters offer ultra-high voltage gain through three degrees of freedom, they still suffer from high current stress on the magnetic components and high voltage stress across the output diode and capacitor.

Among various configurations, modified quadratic converters, based on cascaded conventional boost topologies, can achieve ultra-high voltage conversion ratios with a relatively small number of components. In16–18, three new types of CI-based step-up DC-DC converters featuring quadratic structures have been proposed. However, the series connection between the input DC source and the primary side of the coupled inductor leads to high input current ripple, limiting their applicability. In contrast, other quadratic CI-based topologies with high voltage gain and low input current ripple have been presented in6,7,19–21. Nevertheless, these circuits suffer from significant power dissipation in their input diodes. Furthermore, high current and voltage stress on the high-voltage side components are additional drawbacks of these designs.

Using voltage multipliers in a vertical (stacked) structure effectively reduces the maximum voltage stress across the high-voltage components22,23. This configuration decreases the voltage stress on the capacitors, thereby improving the power density of the circuit. In24–26, three types of CI-based step-up DC-DC converters with vertical structures are introduced. Although these converters exhibit low voltage stress, they are unable to provide high voltage gains. Additionally, new low-voltage stress step-up DC-DC structures with active clamp circuits are proposed in27,28. However, a high input current ripple remains a major disadvantage of these converters. To address this issue, new CI-based stacked-structure DC-DC topologies are presented in29,30. Despite improvements, these circuits still suffer from high current flow through the input inductor and power switch. To further increase the voltage gain, three quadratic CI-based stacked converters with high voltage gain are suggested in31–33. However, these topologies experience high current stress in the input diodes and input inductor. Moreover, a new double-switch step-up DC-DC converter using a CI in combination with voltage multiplier (VM) cells in a stacked configuration is introduced in34. This circuit benefits from current sharing between the input inductors, which alleviates power losses. However, the use of three magnetic cores increases both the volume and cost of the topology.

Conclusion

This paper presents a high step-up DC-DC converter with a coupled inductor (CI) and a voltage multiplier cell. By adjusting the turns ratio of the CI and implementing a stacked capacitor architecture on the output side, the voltage gain is significantly increased in a quadratic manner. To reduce current stress on the components and improve conversion efficiency, the input power is shared between two paths, each equipped with magnetic devices. Another key feature of the proposed converter is the provision of low Reverse Recovery (LRR) for the diodes, achieved through quasi-resonant operation. During operation, one of the MOSFETs turns on with Zero Current Switching (ZCS) due to the leakage inductance of the CI. This results in reduced switching losses and improved operational performance. A comprehensive comparison study demonstrates that the proposed converter, with its high voltage gain, low voltage stress across switches and diodes, common source to load ground, continuous input current, and high conversion efficiency, is a suitable candidate for high step-up applications. The steady-state analysis of the converter has been validated through experimental results from a 200 W, 25 V to 400 V prototype, achieving a conversion efficiency of 96.5%.

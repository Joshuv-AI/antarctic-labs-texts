Unraveling flavor formation in Jiuqu: Source pathways, influencing factors, and regulatory strategies
Authors: Shiming Shen, Boyuan Hu, Jia Zheng, Shuang Xing, Gailing Shi, Boqin Zhang, Liangcai Lin, Cuiying Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Jiuqu serves as the traditional fermentation starter for Chinese alcoholic beverages, primarily consisting of Daqu, Xiaoqu, and Fuqu. Its diverse flavor characteristics stem from variations in production techniques, raw materials, and environmental conditions. As the key source of flavor compounds in alcoholic beverages, Jiuqu fundamentally determines product quality. However, while the inherent open fermentation process generates flavor complexity, it simultaneously poses significant quality control challenges. To enhance alcoholic product quality and flavor stability, investigation of Jiuqu's major flavor compounds, their formation pathways, influencing factors, and control strategies is essential. This review summarizes key flavor components across major Jiuqu varieties, identifies their primary source pathways, analyzes critical factors affecting flavor formation, and provides effective flavor regulation approaches. This review establishes a foundation for developing robust quality control measures to ensure consistency in Jiuqu production and improves traditional alcoholic beverage quality, while also providing guidelines for researchers exploring flavor regulatory mechanisms in other fermented foods.

Introduction

Jiuqu is a traditional Chinese fermentation starter culture produced through controlled cultivation of microbial communities on grain substrates (typically wheat, barley, or rice), primarily comprising Daqu, Xiaoqu, and Fuqu (Kang et al., 2022). Its diverse flavor characteristics stem from variations in production techniques, raw materials, and environmental conditions (Luo et al., 2024; Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2024b). The production process involves: (i) grain crushing and add moisture, (ii) natural or directed microbial inoculation, (iii) temperature-controlled incubation, and (iv) post-processing including drying and aging (Kang et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2021). The development of Jiuqu-making technology, a seminal advancement in microbial biotechnology, emerged from millennia of empirical optimization in fermentation practice, marking humanity's earliest known transition from passive microbial utilization to active microbial cultivation (Kang et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2021). Although Jiuqu is a natural fermentation process, it can be controlled to capture the desired microorganisms needed for subsequent fermentation, while also obtaining enzymes and flavor compounds, thereby forming a complex fermentation starter. The Jiuqu produced via this technology is a composite fermentation starter of enzymes, microorganisms, and flavor compounds, making possible the production of a variety of traditional beverages, including Huangjiu and Baijiu (Jin et al., 2017). As the fermentation starter for alcoholic beverage production, Jiuqu primarily determines product quality through three key mechanisms: (i) Its rich microbial community serves as the primary source of fermentation microorganisms, significantly influencing both the fermentation process and metabolite production; (ii) The abundant enzymatic systems (including amylases, proteases and esterases) play crucial roles in substrate saccharification and utilization; (iii) Jiuqu contains a large number of flavor compounds and flavor precursors, some of which can enter the liquor through distillation, while others provide precursors for the formation of flavor compounds. Additionally, the abundant nitrogen and carbon sources in Jiuqu provide nutrition for subsequent fermentation (Kang et al., 2022; Tang et al., 2024). In addition, Chinese fermented alcoholic beverages employ Jiuqu as a natural fermentation starter, presenting a distinctive contrast to Western fermentation practices that predominantly utilize isolated enzyme preparations and pure yeast cultures (Wang, Hao, & Ren, 2023; Zhu & Tramper, 2013).

In recent years, researchers have devoted considerable time and resources to unraveling the mysteries of Jiuqu. The microorganisms and flavor compounds of Jiuqu were examined using a multi-omics approach, which included macro-genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and flavoromics (Gan et al., 2019; Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2021; Kang et al., 2022). Meanwhile, more researchers have been paying more attention to the study of the flavor profile in Jiuqu. However, due to the influence of raw materials, production methods, environment, climate, and other factors, there are variations in the flavor of different types of Jiuqu (Liang et al., 2022). Typically, Daqu contains a greater variety of flavor compounds compared to other types of Jiuqu. This is likely attributed to its more intricate production process, higher microbial diversity and longer fermentation period, thereby allowing more time for flavor development (Sakandar et al., 2020; Xia et al., 2023). The flavor composition of the Jiuqu could also be affected by the environment (Jin et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2011). For instance, Shi et al. detected the flavor compounds of HTDQ in different environments spatial zones during fermentation, specifically the upper, middle, and lower layers of the fermentation room (Shi et al., 2022). They found that the environmental heterogeneity of various locations in the Qu-room during fermentation resulted in the final production of Daqu with three different kinds (white, yellow, and black Daqu) (Shi et al., 2022).

Currently, regulating the flavor profile in Jiuqu to improve the quality of liquor has been the highlight of the research. For example, Ali et al. review a range of the most common and powerful methods, including fortification, bioaugmentation, development of microbial consortia, and omics techniques, to give insight into the latest state of knowledge in this field, including the significance of modified Daqu and its influence on the quality of Baijiu (Ali et al., 2024). To enhance alcoholic product quality and flavor stability, further summarization of major flavor compounds, their formation pathways, influencing factors, and control strategies is necessary. To understand the advances in the field of Jiuqu research, we collected a dataset of all studies in the field over the past decade (2014–2024) using the Web of Science and extracted key terms from the title and abstract fields of the dataset using the VOSviewer software tool (Van Eck & Waltman, 2010). Key search terms included Jiuqu, Daqu, Xiaoqu, Fuqu, Maiqu and Hongqu. The co-occurrence network of key terms related to the Jiuqu research is shown in Fig. 1b. The data in Fig. 1b revealed that there were 89 terms with 3 clusters, 3319 links, and 14,560 total link strengths. These clusters demonstrate the research overview of flavor substance diversity in Jiuqu. This review first overviews the production processes, classifications, and flavor profiles of various Jiuqu types. It then summarizes key flavor components across major Jiuqu varieties, identifies their primary sources, analyzes critical factors affecting flavor formation, and proposes effective flavor regulation strategies. The aim of this review is to establish a foundation for developing robust quality control measures to ensure consistency in Jiuqu production and enhance traditional alcoholic beverage quality, while also providing guidelines for researchers exploring flavor sources and regulatory mechanisms in other fermented foods.

Daqu

Daqu is a traditional grain-based fermentation starter primarily made from wheat or barley (sometimes including peas or other grains), widely used in China liquor production (Fig. 3). The manufacturing process involves crushing the raw materials, mixing with water, and pressing into brick-shaped form either mechanically or manually. These molded Daqu undergo fermentation in Qu-room for over one month, followed by a storage period for maturation before use (Ao et al., 2010; Shen et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2012). The diversity of Daqu production methods has led to the development of various Daqu types, which are systematically classified through two principal categorization systems. Based on distinctive flavor profiles, Daqu is categorized into four primary types: Maotai-flavor Daqu, Luzhou-flavor Daqu, Fen-flavor Daqu, and Mixed-flavor Daqu. (Zheng et al., 2011). Alternatively, classification according to peak fermentation temperature yields three categories: high-temperature Daqu (60–70 °C, HTDQ), medium-temperature Daqu (50–60 °C, MTDQ), and low-temperature Daqu (40–50 °C, LTDQ) (Zheng et al., 2011).Fig. 3Geographical distribution of traditional Chinese Jiuqu.Fig. 3

The aroma profile of Daqu is rich and complex, characterized by a unique foundational “qu aroma” that harmonizes multi-layered floral, fruity, and roasted notes (Hu, Huang, et al., 2021). This distinctive sensory character arises from a diverse array of volatile compounds, primarily including esters, alcohols, acids, pyrazines, and phenols. Specifically, compounds such as butyl acrylate and (2E)-2-octenal contribute floral and fruity nuances, while tetramethylpyrazine significantly imparts the characteristic roasted and nutty aroma (Cai et al., 2021; Tang et al., 2024; Yang, Fu, et al., 2024). The core “qu aroma” has been attributed to the synergistic effects of several key flavor compounds, including isovaleric acid, 1-hexanol, isovaleraldehyde, 2-octanone, trimethylpyrazine, γ-nonalactone, and 4-vinylguaiacol (Hu, Huang, et al., 2021). The flavor characteristics of Daqu exhibit spatial heterogeneity, which is fundamentally determined by the production processes (Chen et al., 2025; Mu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). For example, during the fermentation of HTDQ, the combined effects of the two-stage turning process (turning the Daqu blocks at day 8 and day 16 of fermentation) and variations in the microenvironment shape unique flavor profiles within the Daqu blocks, leading to the formation of white Qu, yellow Qu, and black Qu with distinct flavor characteristics at different spatial positions (Shi et al., 2022). This differentiation is closely related to local temperature and humidity gradients (Shi et al., 2022). The spatial heterogeneity in Daqu fermentation contributes to the diversity of Daqu's flavor characteristics, which is beneficial for the production of liquors with different flavor profiles. However, this heterogeneity may also affect the consistency of flavor characteristics across different production batches of Daqu, potentially influencing standardized production. Therefore, achieving a balance between flavor diversity and product consistency while preserving the unique flavor characteristics of traditional Daqu fermentation is a challenge for the future.

Xiaoqu

Xiaoqu represents one of the earliest forms of Jiuqu with historical origins dating back to the Zhou Dynasty, which gained widespread application during the Tang Dynasty (618–907 CE) and remains prevalent in southern China's liquor production today (Fig. 3). As a crucial fermentation starter, Xiaoqu plays dual functional roles in both saccharification and microbial inoculation, making it essential for the production of various Baijiu and Huangjiu varieties (Kang et al., 2021; Sakandar et al., 2020; Tang et al., 2019). The manufacturing process utilizes rice (including rice bran), wheat bran, and sorghum as primary substrates, which undergo inoculation with traditional “Qumu” starter cultures or defined microbial consortia followed by controlled fermentation at 25–30 °C (Sakandar et al., 2020). When compared to Daqu, Xiaoqu exhibits several distinctive characteristics: lower cultivation temperatures, smaller physical dimensions, reduced microbial diversity, and proportionally smaller usage quantities in liquor production batches (Kang et al., 2022; Su et al., 2020; Tong et al., 2024). These technical specifications contribute to Xiaoqu's unique biochemical properties and flavor development patterns in traditional Chinese alcoholic beverages.

Compared to Daqu, Xiaoqu exhibits relatively mild aromatic characteristics with a less diverse profile of flavor compounds. It contains higher concentrations of volatile compounds such as 1-octen-3-ol, 2,3-butanediol, pHenylacetaldehyde, benzaldehyde, octanoic acid, and nonanoic acid (Tang et al., 2019). Among these, 1-octen-3-ol contributes notably to its herbaceous, peppermint-like, and mushroom-like notes (Tang et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2025). Xiaoqu can be systematically classified based on two fundamental parameters: raw material composition and production methodology. In terms of raw materials, Xiaoqu can be categorized into two types, namely, Herbal Xiaoqu (with herbs) and White Xiaoqu (no herbs). The herbs, including vaob loaf, tangerine peel, and licorice, can reduce undesirable microorganisms, enhance the growth of positive microorganisms, and contribute the unique herbal flavor to the liquor (Tang et al., 2019). According to the production method of Xiaoqu, it can be categorized into three types, namely, traditional Xiaoqu, purebred Xiaoqu, and mechanical Xiaoqu (Dong et al., 2020). Traditional Xiaoqu, made by natural inoculation in an open environment, have a lot of microorganisms of both positive and undesirable and result in a low output of liquor when it is used in liquor production. To reduce the influence of undesirable microorganisms, herbs, like vaob loaf, are usually added to the production of traditional Xiaoqu. With the advance of technology in microbial culture, purebred Xiaoqu is made by inoculating special microorganisms, like Rhizobium. Purebred Xiaoqu is extensively employed in liquor fermentation, typically in conjunction with yeasts, and has significantly contributed to improving liquor production output.

With the rapid development of the economy and technological advancements, the Baijiu industry is progressing toward mechanization and intelligence. To further reduce labor costs, a new type of Xiaoqu (mechanized Xiaoqu, produced using mechanical technology) has been developed (Hu, Yang, et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2024; Wang, Li, et al., 2023). Traditional Xiaoqu serves as a microbial starter in conventional production, which relies on natural fermentation (uncontrolled room temperature) and requires substantial manual labor. In contrast, mechanical Xiaoqu incorporates wheat bran into the raw materials, resulting in reduced viscosity. In addition, the entire production process is automated, remain untouched by workers, including steaming, cooling, inoculating with starter cultures, mixing, temperature- and humidity-controlled cultivation, mechanical ventilation drying, and storage (Hu, Yang, et al., 2021). The mechanization of Xiaoqu production represents an advancement over traditional methods. Given its high stability, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness, mechanized production is becoming the future trend for both Qu-making and the broader Baijiu industry.

Fuqu

Fuqu was invented to save grain, and currently has been one of the most important starters for brewing Baijiu. Fuqu were made from brans of grains (steamed wheat husk), which were then inoculated with microorganisms (mainly molds and yeasts) and cultured at a certain temperature. In Fuqu Baijiu production, molds present in the Fuqu first hydrolyze starch from raw materials into fermentable sugars, which are subsequently converted into alcohol by yeast during fermentation. Meanwhile, a lot of flavor compounds were produced by microorganisms through metabolism (Xia et al., 2023).

Compared to Daqu, Fuqu exhibits a milder aromatic profile characterized by a distinct wheat bran note (Ying et al., 2024). Its characteristic flavor is closely associated with the specific microorganisms inoculated during its production. For instance, inoculation with Bacillus amyloliquefaciens promotes high yields of tetramethylpyrazine, contributing to baked and roasted aromas (Zhang et al., 2019). Additionally, bacterial strains such as Pseudomonas sp. YF-58 are capable of producing guaiacol compounds—including guaiacol, 4-vinylguaiacol, 4-methylguaiacol, and 4-ethylguaiacol—which impart spicy and smoky notes to Fuqu (Deng et al., 2024). This is likely attributed to its single raw material (wheat bran) and lower microbial diversity, which consequently generates fewer aroma compounds during fermentation (Xia et al., 2023). However, it enhances raw material utilization, increases yield, and significantly shortens the production cycle (Zheng & Han, 2016; Kang et al., 2022). In addition, Fuqu can be used to brew sesame-flavor Baijiu (SFB) together with HTDQ (Xia et al., 2023). Compared to Daqu Baijiu, the aroma of Fuqu Baijiu is usually more moderate, which may be caused by the lesser diversity of microorganisms in Fuqu and the shorter fermentation period. Consequently, current researchers are also more concerned about developing functional Fuqu (Ai et al., 2019; Sakandar et al., 2020). For instance, Zhu et al. screened Bacillus amyloliquefaciens with high production of tetramethylpyrazine and applied it to the production of functional Fuqu (Zhu et al., 2010). They found that the application of the functional Fuqu significantly increased the contents of tetramethylpyrazine in the SFB. By inoculating the brans with specific microorganisms, it is possible to control the flavor compounds of Baijiu, but excessive use of Fuqu may give Baijiu the “bran of grains” flavor and should be controlled.

Other Jiuqu categories

Maiqu represents one of the earliest traditional fermentation starters for Huangjiu production, manufactured through a process involving wheat binding, controlled hydration and precise molding (Hou et al., 2025). During fermentation, Maiqu develops a wine-like aroma, while the finished product exhibits a fresh and pleasant fragrance. In contrast, poor-quality Maiqu may develop a raw and musty odor (Wang, 2004). Key aroma compounds, including 1-nonanol, phenethyl alcohol, 2-methylbutanal, isovaleric acid, 2-octanone, guaiacol, 2,3-dimethylpyrazine, 5-ethyl-2,3-dimethylpyrazine, tetramethylpyrazine (TTMP), camphor, and α-terpineol (Fig. 2), not only define the characteristic aroma profile of Maiqu samples but also serve as key volatile markers for distinguishing between different types of Maiqu (Han et al., 2025). Aspergillus, Rhizoctonia, and Trichoderma are the dominant microorganisms in Maiqu, which also contains small amounts of bacteria and yeast. These microorganisms provide amylase and protease enzymes, which have the role of hydrolyzing and saccharifying raw materials.

Hongqu serves as a fundamental fermentation starter for Huangjiu, with widespread application in Southeast Asian brewing traditions (Kang et al., 2022). It is produced through sequential processing of rice, including immersion, steaming, controlled cooling, and inoculation with Monascus purpureus, followed by solid-state fermentation under optimized conditions (Ai et al., 2019). Hongqu develops distinctive alcoholic and bitter almond aromas during fermentation, contributing to its unique aroma profile (Lin, 2020). The resulting Hongqu imparts distinctive qualities to Huangjiu characterized by vibrant red pigmentation, refined taste profile and complex aromatic bouquet, with Gutian Hongqu and Wuyi Hongqu recognized as geographical indications of Chinese varieties (Chen, Li, et al., 2023; Lv et al., 2013). The resulting Hongqu imparts distinctive qualities to Huangjiu, characterized by vibrant red pigmentation, a refined taste profile, and a complex aromatic bouquet. Gutian Hongqu and Wuyi Hongqu are recognized as geographical indications of Chinese varieties (Chen, Li, et al., 2023; Lv et al., 2013). Characteristic flavor compounds identified during the Huangjiu brewing process with these two types of Hongqu include 3-methylbutan-1-ol, isobutanol, ethyl lactate, ethyl acetate, octanoic acid, diethyl succinate, phenethyl alcohol, hexanoic acid, and n-decanoic acid (Chen, Yuan, et al., 2023). However, quality consistency remains challenging due to technological limitations in traditional production methods, high environmental sensitivity during fermentation and incomplete quality standardization systems, prompting current research initiatives focusing on implementing modern fermentation control technologies (Ai et al., 2019; Ma et al., 2022).

Major flavor compounds and their aroma characteristics in traditional Chinese Jiuqu.

Esters

Esters, one of the most important flavor compounds of Jiuqu, usually have a pleasant aroma profile and confer a degree of health attributes to the food. Table 1 summarizes the 30 major ester compounds in the different Jiuqu. The esters were reported to be higher in HTDQ, followed by MTDQ, and lowest in LTDQ (Jin et al., 2019). Ethyl palmitate and methyl linoleate are the main volatile compounds common to all three macrobiotics. From Table 1, it can be seen that ethyl palmitate and methyl linoleate are the main volatile compounds common to all three kinds of Daqu. In addition, ethyl acetate, ethyl butyrate, and ethyl caproate are also key flavor components of the Jiuqu (Mu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022).

The content and type of esters change during the fermentation of the different Jiuqu. For instance, Yang et al. detected the flavor compounds of MTDQ in different fermentation stage an found that 22 compounds such as ethyl acetate, ethyl butyrate, and ethyl hexanoate showed a tendency of first increasing and then decreasing during the maturation process of Daqu (Yang et al., 2022). Ethyl valerate, ethyl formate, and ethyl heptanoate were only produced in the middle stage; ethyl isobutyrate and ethyl isovalerate were only produced in the final stage (Yang et al., 2022). Yu Mu et al. detected volatile flavor compounds in three different processes of MTDQ and found that methyl caproate, methyl oleate, and methyl linoleate accounted for more than 50 % of the flavor compounds in MTDQ (Mu et al., 2023).The high-content ester of Jiuqu was also high in liquor. For instance, ethyl valerate, ethyl caprylate, methyl linoleate, and ethyl hexadecanoate are not only the main flavor compounds in MTDQ but also are found at relatively high content in Strong flavor Baijiu (SFB). This suggested there may be a potential association between the flavor compounds of Jiuqu and liquor.

Alcohols

Alcohols are not only the important aroma-presenting compounds in the Jiuqu but also the important flavor precursors. During the maturation process of the Jiuqu, alcohols react with acids to form esters, which give the Jiuqu a certain “wine aroma” (Yang et al., 2022). Alcohols typically exhibit low olfactory thresholds (generally <1 ppm) and impart distinctive aromatic notes to Jiuqu, including fruity and floral characteristics. Notably, their sensory profiles demonstrate significant concentration-dependent variations. As shown in Table 1, the 15 main alcohols are summarized. Among them, 2,3-butanediol (0.095 ppm), isoamyl alcohol (0.004–0.25 ppm), 1-octen-3-ol (0.001–0.0014 ppm), and phenylethanol (0.564–1 ppm) were highly significant aroma compounds in the different Jiuqu (Li et al., 2023; Mu et al., 2023). 2,3-butanediol contributes to the production of fruity and oniony aromas and can be interconverted with ethylene glycol and 2,3-butanedione. Moderate amounts of isoamyl alcohol give a nail oil and floral aroma to Jiuqu and contribute to sweetness and set off the ester aroma, but excessive amounts of isoamyl alcohol bring out a pungent flavor and a bitter taste. Isoamyl alcohol significantly contributes to the aroma profiles of MTDQ, LTDQ, and Maiqu, while also serving as an important bittering agent in Maotai flavor Baijiu. 1-octen-3-ol, which has aroma profiles of herbs, lavender, and mushrooms, and phenyl-ethanol, which has aroma profiles of honey, rose, clove, and pungency, contribute significantly to the aroma profiles of different Jiuqu. In addition, n-hexanol, heptanol, 1-nonanol, and furfuryl alcohol were also important aroma compounds in the MTDQ, and contributed significantly to the fruity, floral, herbaceous, and pungent aromas.

Acids

Acids usually have a vinegary and sweaty odor, and a moderate amount of acid makes people feel comfortable, but an excessive amount may bring irritating odors such as acid and fat stink. Acids are also precursors of other flavor compounds, with high content in the earlier stage of Daqu and decreasing in later stages (Wang et al., 2022). As shown in Table 1, nine major acid compounds in different Jiuqu were summarized. Among them, 3-methylbutyric acid had ripe fruity, sweaty, and cashew odor characteristics and had additive and folding effects with 3-methylbutanol and 1-octen-3-ol, respectively, which contributed significantly to the aroma profiles of the MTDQ and LTDQ (Mu et al., 2023). Caproic acid has strong fat odor, sweaty odor, and other unpleasant odor characteristics and is an important aroma substance for both Xiaoqu and Maiqu. 3-methylbutyric acid and caproic acid were higher in MTDQ, whereas linoleic acid, stearic acid, arachidic acid, nonanoic acid and other fatty acids were found to be higher in the Xiaoqu, which may be related to their respective microbial composition and can impart a vinegary, sour, and fatty flavor to the Xiaoqu (Mo et al., 2010).

Aldehydes

The key aldehydes in the different Jiuqu were summarized in Table 1. Hexanal was the main flavor substance and key aroma substance in MTDQ, Xiaoqu, and Maiqu, with fishy, herbaceous, and medicinal aromas, which may be one of the sources of herbal aromas in liquor (Wang et al., 2022). Benzaldehyde and phenylacetaldehyde were important aroma compounds in different Jiuqu, among which benzaldehyde had bitter almond, woody, burnt, and cherry sweetness, which contributed to the aroma of MTDQ and Xiaoqu (Mu et al., 2023). Phenylacetaldehyde has strong aromas such as rose and hyacinth flower aroma, which can be reduced to phenylethanol. Compared with other compounds, aldehydes are less abundant and diverse in liquor, mainly containing trace amounts of acetaldehyde, which may be generated through the oxidation of lipid and make an important contribution to the flavor of liquor. In addition to the odors of the aldehydes themselves, the complex aromas of these aldehydes may also have an important effect on the flavor of Daqu (Wang et al., 2022). In addition, aldehydes have a very important contribution to the flavor of foods such as yogurt and cheese (Al-Khalili et al., 2025).

Ketones

Ketones, which are less numerous in type and content in Jiuqu, can be produced by Bacillus (Jiang et al., 2025). As a multifunctional microorganism, Bacillus contributes significantly to brewing through material breakdown, flavor production, and ecosystem regulation. For instance, Bacillus subtilis secretes cellulases that demonstrate synergistic effects when combined with ligninolytic enzymes such as laccase. This enzymatic cooperation significantly enhances the depolymerization of lignin, a structurally complex polymer critical for Jiuqu quality. The resulting breakdown products serve as precursors for diverse flavor compounds, including various ketones that contribute to the characteristic aroma profile of fermented products. Bacillus further enhance flavor compound production through cooperative interactions with other microbiota. Tu et al. investigated the synergistic relationship between Pediococcus and Bacillus, revealing that the initial fermentation phase is marked by competitive niche partitioning, while later stages demonstrate a clear transition toward mutualistic cooperation (Tu et al., 2024). This microbial shift collectively enhances thermal output and accelerates temperature elevation during fermentation. The heating capacity is critical for sustaining the enzymatic activities, Maillard reaction processes, and flavor precursor synthesis required for Baijiu fermentation. In addition, under thermophilic fermentation conditions, Bacillus populations served as primary producers of acetoin (buttery notes), pyrazines (roasted aromas), and guaiacol (savory characteristics), with species-specific metabolic pathways generating distinct volatile markers and unique sensory profiles. Zhu et al. identified dual genetic-environmental control of Bacillus flavor metabolism (Zhu et al., 2025). There were differences in volatile flavor and sensory characteristics between different Bacillus species, and the main genes influencing flavor contribution included padC, alsD and acoA/B, etc. (Zhu et al., 2025). This study advances comprehension of Bacillus aroma biosynthesis diversity, enabling data-driven strain optimization for industrial fermentation. Table 1 summarizes the six key ketones in the different Jiuqu. 3-Hydroxy-2-butanone and geranylacetone are important aroma compounds in HTDQ, which can give HTDQ a creamy, floral, and honey aroma (Tang et al., 2024). 2-octanone is the main flavor substance in HTDQ, MTDQ, Xiaoqu, and Maiqu, and it has the characteristics of grassy and floral aroma (Zhang et al., 2022). Acetophenone has almond aroma characteristics and is the main flavor substance in HTDQ.2-heptanone is the main flavor substance in Maiqu and Xiaoqu, with a fruity and pear aroma. 1-octen-3-one is an aroma-active substance in Maiqu, and Xiaoqu, with mushroom odors (Tang et al., 2019). 2-Octen-3-one was the main flavor substance in HTDQ, MTDQ, Maiqu, and Xiaoqu, with floral, grassy, and woody odors (Zhang et al., 2022).

Pyrazines

Pyrazines usually have aroma characteristics such as roasted, nutty, burnt, sweet, and chocolate aromas, which can be produced by means of Maillard reaction and metabolism by microorganisms such as Bacillus and Actinomyces (Jin et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2021). Table 1 summarizes the key pyrazines in the different Jiuqu. Among them, tetramethylpyrazine was first isolated as a pharmacologically active ingredient from the traditional Chinese medicine Ligusticum sinense ‘Chuanxiong’, which could confer pleasant aromas such as charred and baked aroma to HTDQ and the liquor. 2,3-dimethylpyrazine, 2,5-dimethylpyrazine, 2,6-dimethylpyrazine, and 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine are important aroma compounds in HTDQ and MTDQ (Jin et al., 2019; Mu et al., 2023). These pyrazines mixed with 2,6-dimethylpyrazine and 2-ethyl-3,3-dimethylpyrazine could lower the threshold of each other. The content of pyrazines changed during the storage stage of Jiuqu (Wang et al., 2022). Wang et al. detected the flavor compounds of MTDQ at different stages of maturation and found that the content of pyrazines gradually increased during the storage process (Wang et al., 2022). Especially the content of tetramethylpyrazine increased the fastest. Pyrazines are primarily generated through Maillard reactions (which initially produce aldehydes and ketones before forming pyrazines in later stages) and microbial metabolic pathways. Compared to other flavor compounds, pyrazine formation requires substantially longer reaction times. Furthermore, the characteristic post-storage elevation in pyrazine concentrations likely results from ecological niche differentiation induced by dynamic environmental shifts (including temperature, humidity, pH, and nutrient availability) during the fermentation-to-storage transition, which may drive microbial community reorganization toward pyrazine-producing populations (Sun et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2025).

Others flavor compounds

Phenols, terpenoids and sulfur-containing compounds also play an important role in the Jiuqu flavor profile (Table 1). Guaiacol and 4-vinylguaiacol significantly contribute to the smoky aroma and characteristic sauce fragrance in HTDQ, with notably higher concentrations observed in premium-quality Daqu (Tang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2022). β-Caryophyllene, exhibiting distinctive clove and pine resin aromatic notes, is consistently detected in both MTDQ and Xiaoqu (Mu et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2019). Sulfur compounds are often the source of off-flavors (such as pickle-like odor) in Baijiu. However, the odors of sulfides such as dimethyl trisulfide can be masked by sorghum alcohol solubilizing proteins. For instance, Zhu et al. conducted a comprehensive investigation of kafirin's inhibitory effects on pickle-like (sulfurous off-odors) in Maotai flavor Baijiu through an integrated analytical approach combining instrumental analysis, sensory evaluation, and computational modeling (Zhu et al., 2023). They found that after the addition of kafirin, the aroma profile of the pickle-like Maotai flavor Baijiu changed and approached that of the normal Maotai flavor Baijiu. Further analysis demonstrated the formation of kafirin-VSCs complexes through spontaneous molecular interactions that concurrently reduced compound volatility by 22.05 to 64.28 % while increasing odor detection thresholds by 87.29 to 232.57 %. These quantitative changes confirm the dual mechanistic action of kafirin in both physically restricting vapor phase release and perceptually masking sulfurous off-odors through molecular complexation. In conclusion, kafirin exhibited a significant suppressing effect on the pickle-like off-odor of Maotai flavor Baijiu and this effect could be used to reduce the off-odor of Maotai flavor Baijiu and Maotai flavor Baijiu -derived alcoholic beverages (Zhu et al., 2023).

The primary sources pathways of flavor compound formation in Jiuqu

Jiuqu serves as the primary source of flavor compounds in traditional alcoholic beverages, critically determining final product quality. A comprehensive understanding of its flavor formation mechanisms is essential for targeted metabolic regulation throughout production, particularly given the remarkable diversity of Chinese Jiuqu varieties and their distinct brewing processes. Current research classifies flavor compound derivation pathways into three principal domains (Table 2): first, intrinsic compounds from raw grains such as terpenes and fatty acids; second, microbially driven biosynthetic processes including esterification reactions, pyruvate metabolism exemplified by the PDC ADH cascade, the Ehrlich pathway converting amino acids to fusel alcohols, and amino acid transamination; third, non enzymatic reactions such as Maillard reactions, caramelization, and oxidative transformations including lipid peroxidation. Flavor compounds from grain materials

It is clear that Jiuqu is composed of a variety of grain materials, mainly including wheat, barley, peas and sorghum (Fig. 5). These grain materials are rich in amylum, protein, lipids, and a variety of flavor compounds, all of which form the essential foundation for the unique aromatic characteristics of Jiuqu. For instance, Wanli Qiang et al. analyzed the flavor composition of different varieties of wheat using GC-IMS (Qiang et al., 2024). They had obtained 48 VOCs, of which alcohols were the most abundant, followed by aldehydes. And the content of butanol, butyraldehyde, nonanal, ethyl butyrate, ethyl caproate, and limonene was high in all wheats. Barley is also abundant in various aldehyde compounds (Zhang et al., 2016). Zhang et al. conducted an in-depth analysis of the aroma components in barley and identified a total of 37 different aroma compounds. Peas contain 2-methyl-3-ethyl pyrazine, 2-methoxy-3-isobutyl pyrazine, and other pyrazines; these pyrazines have an extremely strong nutty aroma and sweet caramel aroma. Peas are also rich in proteins and vitamins, which are the precursors for the formation of the flavor compounds (Mei et al., 2023). Sorghums are also rich in flavor compounds. An investigation led by Jiao Hu et al. thoroughly detected the flavor compounds of sorghum by combining liquid-liquid extraction with gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (Hu et al., 2024). This research found 37 flavor compounds. In particular, 2-heptenal, hexanal, (Z)-2-decenal, and 2-pentylfuran stand out due to their elevated concentrations. In addition, “low quantity, high aroma” compounds were detected, including 1-octen-3-ol, nonanal, and γ-nonanolactone, which can significantly affect the aroma profile of the grains.

Biosynthesis of flavor compounds in Jiuqu production

During Jiuqu production, microorganisms generate flavor compounds through individual metabolism and synergistic interactions (Wang, Quan, et al., 2023). Ester biosynthesis occurs via dual pathways: direct ester synthesis and facilitation of ester precursor formation. Direct enzymatic catalysis by ester synthases derived from fungi (Thermoascus aurantiacus, Aspergillus niger, A. chevalieri, and Rhizopus microspores) (Tie et al., 2025). In an integrated multi-omics study, Wang, Quan, et al., 2023 combined metatranscriptomic and metaproteomic analyses with experimental validation to identify Aspergillus as the core genus driving ester biosynthesis in MTDQ (Wang, Quan, et al., 2023). Aspergillus dominated microbial activity at transcriptional and proteomic levels, while a synergistic consortium of Aspergillus, Bacillus, Leuconostoc, and Pediococcus orchestrated the ester-synthesis metabolic network from raw material degradation to ester formation. These genera exhibited significantly elevated expression of ester-forming enzymes. Recombinant fermentation confirmed Aspergillus's indispensable role: microbiotas containing Aspergillus produced ester/precursor profiles nearly identical to native MTDQ, whereas Aspergillus-deficient microbiotas showed polar metabolic divergence. Fungi such as Pichia also play an important role in ester production and have been implicated in the production of phenylethanol and ethyl acetate, 3-hydroxy-2-butanone (Wang et al., 2011).

The synthesis of ester precursors is closely associated with bacteria. Bacterial consortia exhibit type-specific roles—Sphingomonas, Pantoea, and Lactobacillus correlate with short-chain acid/alcohol precursors in LTDQ, while Apiotrichum, Pseudomonas, and Cutaneotrichosporon link to aromatic alcohols in MTDQ, as evidenced by LC-MS detection of 50 ester-related compounds (Quan et al., 2023).In addition, Bacteria, such as Bacillus, not only promote the production of esterase and amylases but also produce flavor compounds such as 3-methyl-1-butanol, diacetyl, 2-butanone, and ethyl diphosgene (Pang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2014).

Acid synthesis is similarly mediated by microorganisms, particularly lactic acid bacteria in traditional fermentation processes. Lactic acid is produced by lactic acid bacteria in traditional MTDQ, while acetic acid is generated via ethanol oxidation by Acetobacterium balch in mechanical MTDQ, which may contribute to increased acidity during storage (Guan et al., 2021; Krusong et al., 2020). A study by He et al. showed that acetic acid content in Daqu correlates with the microbial community, exhibiting a positive association with Pichia, Acetobacter, Lactobacillus, Weissella, and Pseudoalbicans (He et al., 2023). These taxa were identified as the functional acid-producing microbiota in MTDQ. Furthermore, PICRUSt2 analysis revealed enriched gene abundances encoding enzymes involved in glycolysis and pentose phosphate pathways for acetic acid synthesis in MTDQ, corresponding to its elevated acetic acid levels. This study provides microbial evidence explaining the high ethyl acetate and acetic acid content during strong flavor Baijiu production and establishes a theoretical basis for baijiu fermentation control via functional microorganisms. Molds also contribute to acid production. For example, Yin et al. found that Aspergillus not only produces lactic acid but also exhibits positive correlations with metabolites including α-ketoglutaric acid, citric acid, malic acid, succinic acid, and isobutyric acid (Yin et al., 2020).

Aldehydes, alcohols, and other flavor compounds are formed during Daqu fermentation through collaborative interactions among bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms (Fig. 5e-5f). In the initial stages of HTDQ and MTDQ fermentation, fungi (Filamentosa) and molds (including Aspergillus thermophilus and Thermus thermophilus)) convert raw starch and proteins into small-molecule sugars and amino acids. These products serve as substrates for non-enzymatic reactions such as the Maillard reaction (Deng et al., 2020; He et al., 2022). Sugars, particularly cellobiose, play a pivotal role in microbial metabolism and flavor formation. Weissella hydrolyzes cellobiose into reducing sugars, which then undergo glycolysis to generate pyruvate. Pyruvate is decarboxylated by pyruvate decarboxylase (PDC) to form acetaldehyde, which is subsequently reduced to ethanol by alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH). Concurrently, critical flavor compounds such as 2-methyl-1-propanol, 2-methyl-1-butanol, and 3-methyl-1-butanol are synthesized via the Ehrlich pathway (Huang et al., 2017). Alternatively, α-acetolactate synthase (ALS) converts pyruvate to α-acetolactate, which is then transformed to acetoin via two distinct pathways. First, non-enzymatic oxidative decarboxylation of α-acetolactate yields diacetyl, which is subsequently reduced to acetoin by diacetyl reductase. Second, direct decarboxylation catalyzed by α-acetolactate decarboxylase (α-ALDC) produces acetoin (Cui et al., 2020) (Fig. 5g).

Nonenzymatic reactions for the formation of flavor compounds

Non-enzymatic reactions (chemical transformations occurring independent of enzyme catalysis) constitute a critical mechanism in flavor development during Jiuqu production (Fig. 5b-d). Three primary reaction classes dominate this process: Maillard reactions, caramelization, and various oxidative transformations. These reactions are critically influenced by thermal processing conditions and storage duration, collectively shaping the final flavor profile through generation of volatile organic compounds.

The Maillard reaction serves as a principal route for melanoidin formation, involving condensation and polymerization between carbonyl compounds (aldehydes, ketones, reducing sugars) and nitrogenous compounds (amino acids, peptides, proteins) (Fig. 5d). Highly reactive α-dicarbonyl intermediates (e.g., 2,3-butanedione, glyoxal) act as crucial precursors for both melanoidins and diverse flavor compounds. This pathway generates multiple flavor compounds including pyrazines (nutty, roasted notes), aldehydes (fruity, floral aromas), and heterocyclic compounds (meaty, savory flavors) (Zhang et al., 2021).

Esterification in Daqu constitutes a thermally-driven equilibrium process that significantly enhances flavor complexity. Under conditions of elevated temperature and acidity, ethanol and organic acids undergo condensation to form characteristic esters, including ethyl lactate (fruity, creamy notes) and fatty acid ethyl esters. The reversible nature of these reactions (esterification and hydrolysis) creates dynamic flavor evolution throughout production (Fig. 5c) (Xiao, 2022).

Thermal degradation, which occurs in both essential and non-essential compositions, produced lots of flavor compounds (Zhuang, 2007). Essential compositions, including sugars, proteins, and lipids, are degraded and transformed into different flavor compounds at high temperatures. For example, furans (caramel, toasted bread, nutty, baked aromas), lactones (creamy, coconut, sweet), and cyclic diketones (buttery, caramel, sweet) will be produced through dehydration reactions between sugars. When the fermentation temperature is raised, a series of important flavor compounds are generated through the oxidation of fats, including β-enal (grassy, melon), dienal (fatty, fried), lactone, and short-chain fatty acids (sour, rancid) (Tang et al., 2024; Zhuang, 2007). Non-essential compositions could also be converted into flavor compounds by thermal degradation in Jiuqu. It was reported that thiamine was converted into furans and thiophenes in peas, including methyl-3-furan disulfides (meat flavor) and 2-methyl-3-ketotetrahydrothiophene (roasted chestnut flavor) (Zhuang, 2007).

Abiotic factors shaping the flavor formation in Jiuqu

Process parameters and manufacturing

The distinctive flavor profiles of various Jiuqu types are shaped by their unique production parameters and manufacturing techniques. Research indicates that the flavor composition of Jiuqu is governed by two critical factors: first, key process parameters such as peak fermentation temperature，moisture content, and flipping Daqu operations; second, the production method employed, including both traditional manual techniques and modern mechanized processes, all of which influence the entire production workflow. For example, HTDQ contains elevated levels of pyrazine compounds, imparting characteristic baked aroma. MTDQ had a high content of guaiacol and 4-ethyl-2-methoxyphenol, while ethyl butyrate was high in LTDQ (Wang et al., 2022). Moisture content is another key factor that affects the fermentation process of Daqu. It provides the necessary environmental support for microbial growth, regulates temperature and humidity, and affects metabolic efficiency, thereby influencing the flavor characteristics of Daqu (Wang et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2025; Yao et al., 2025). In addition, flipping Daqu is an essential craft in traditional Daqu production. Turning the Daqu blocks helps to lower the fermentation temperature and promotes the evaporation of moisture. Therefore, the timing and frequency of flipping have a significant impact on the flavor formation of Daqu (Wen et al., 2025). Wen et al. systematically compared microbial communities and metabolites in MTDQ under flipping (FD) and non-flipping regimes (NFD) (Wen et al., 2025). They found that the flipping process substantially influences microbial community development and metabolic formation during MTDQ fermentation (Wen et al., 2025). Compared to NFD, FD samples showed a 19.8 % increase in total volatile organic compound (VOC), with significant enrichment of key aroma-active substances including 2-phenylethanol (rose-like aroma) and 2-octanone (cheesy note). These effects can be attributed to the control of temperature and moisture throughout Daqu fermentation. These findings emphasize the contribution of the flipping process in balancing environmental conditions and facilitating flavor formation, while providing viable approaches for enhancing traditional Daqu production, maintaining quality consistency, and directing mechanized fermentation processes (Wen et al., 2025).

Notably, distinct production parameters between manual and mechanized methods also significantly impact flavor profile. For example, in the Fen-flavor Baijiu, the soaked temperature of grain is higher in mechanical production than in manual production, and there are fewer turnings in mechanical. The mechanical production inhibits the growth of yeast and provides a more suitable temperature for Bacillus, which promoted the production of esters and alcohols (Luo et al., 2023). It was also reported that the different crushing degree of the raw material between mechanical and manual production could also change the flavor composition of Jiuqu (Liu, Zhou, et al., 2023). With technological advancements, mechanical production is becoming predominant. However, there are significant differences in the flavor compositions and microbiome community between the mechanical and traditional Jiuqu. For instance, it has been reported that Staphylococcus may come from artificial steps on HTDQ promoting the production of 3-methyl-1-butanol and 2-butanone (Wang et al., 2021).

In summary, the flavor characteristics of Jiuqu are the result of the combined effects of multiple factors, including key process parameters (such as fermentation temperature, moisture content, and turning operations) and production methods (manual versus mechanized). These factors directly influence the formation of flavor compounds by altering microbial community structures, metabolic activities, and enzymatic reaction rates. Future research should focus on how to maintain the traditional flavor quality of Jiuqu in mechanized production and improve the overall quality of Baijiu by optimizing production process parameters. This will not only help standardize production processes but also provide a scientific basis for targeted flavor design in Jiuqu manufacturing.

Geography, climate, and seasons

Jiuqu produced under different environmental conditions exhibits distinct flavor profiles, with significant variations in physicochemical properties, enzymatic activities, microbial communities, and characteristic aroma compounds, ultimately leading to substantial differences in liquor quality and style. As illustrated in Fig. 2, these environmental factors primarily include geographic region, climatic conditions, and production seasons. Notably, flavor profiles vary significantly across production regions. Yu et al. conducted a comparative study of flavor compounds in LTDQ between northern and southern China, revealing that northern LTDQ contained higher pyrazine content while southern LTDQ showed elevated alcohol levels. These findings provide a scientific foundation for optimizing the quality of Baijiu production across different geographical regions in China (Yu et al., 2024).

Daily temperature, as a key climatic variable exhibiting significant seasonal and regional variations, exerts profound influences on flavor diversification during Jiuqu production. Liang et al. conducted a comprehensive investigation into the effects of climatic parameters on aroma compound profiles in Daqu fermentation (Liang et al., 2022). They found that when the mean daily temperature and relative humidity reached optimal thresholds of 18.89 °C and 49.21 %, respectively, the aromatic complexity peaked with a remarkable 70.69 % enhancement in compound diversity index compared to suboptimal conditions. Subsequent research by Wang et al. further elucidated that the average daily temperature of the external environment was the main determinant of the changes in the composition of the microbial and flavor compounds, and there were higher contents of ethyl lactate and isooctanol and lower microbial community diversity at higher daytime temperatures (Wang et al., 2020). These findings provide mechanistic insights into climate-driven quality formation in traditional Daqu fermentation systems.

Ambient air conditions (such as airborne microorganisms) in production workshops in different regions also have a great influence on the flavor profile of Jiuqu (Qin et al., 2025; Wang, Shen, et al., 2025). Continuous exposure to air during the maturation and storage stages leads to dynamic changes in its aromatic composition (Wang et al., 2021). Research has demonstrated that Streptomyces present in workshop air can produce crucial enzymes including lipases, alkaline phosphatases, and phosphohydrolases, which play vital roles in forming the characteristic flavor compounds of Maotai flavor Baijiu (Wang et al., 2021). Furthermore, seasonal climatic variations substantially impact the composition of flavor compounds in Daqu. Guan et al. investigated the flavor components and microbial community structure of MTDQ across different seasons, revealing that variations in precipitation and temperature between seasons caused shifts in microbial populations, resulting in distinct seasonal flavor profiles. Spring Daqu contained fewer varieties and lower concentrations of flavor compounds, summer Daqu was rich in pyrazines, while winter Daqu predominantly contained esters (Guan et al., 2024). However, Jiang et al. found that spring-produced MTDQ contained significantly higher levels of polyphenols (such as guaiacol and 4-vinylguaiacol) compared to summer batches (Jiang et al., 2023). These findings suggest that strategic blending of Daqu produced in different seasons during actual production processes could potentially enhance overall product quality. This discovery provides important theoretical foundations for standardized Daqu production and flavor modulation.

In summary, climatic conditions including daily temperature, air, humidity and precipitation vary significantly across production seasons and geographic regions. These variations influence Jiuqu flavor formation through multiple interconnected mechanisms. First, they alter microbial community structures and metabolic activities. Second, they modify enzymatic reaction rates and efficiencies. Third, they directly affect the kinetics of flavor compound formation through chemical reactions. Together, these climate mediated biochemical processes constitute the fundamental basis for regional and seasonal variations in Jiuqu flavor profiles. Future research should focus on quantitatively characterizing how these climatic factors interact and identifying their critical thresholds. Such investigations will provide the scientific foundation needed to standardize production processes and enable targeted flavor design in Daqu manufacturing.

General regulation strategies to improve flavor profile

The formation of flavor compounds in Jiuqu involves multiple sources and is influenced by a series of factors during the production process. Therefore, in order to improve the flavor profile, the regulation strategies should be conducted with a high degree of specialization, and we give some strategies for regulating the flavor profile from the following perspectives. Most critically, safety considerations must be incorporated throughout the production process, including but not limited to systematic monitoring of biogenic amines (BAs) and ethyl carbamate (EC) (Ge et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2022).

Modify grain matrix for enhanced flavor production

Grains are one of the initial sources to form the flavor compounds of Jiuqu. The formation of these flavor compounds has a close relationship with nutritional composition in grains, including protein, fat, starch, and carbohydrates (Liu, Li, et al., 2023). It was reported that protein content was significantly and positively correlated with β-phenylethanol and 3-hydroxy-2-butanone content, but too much protein content led to the rancidity of fermentation (Shi et al., 2025). In addition, fat content was significantly and negatively correlated with acetic acid and ethyl acetate content; starch content was significantly and positively correlated with β-phenylethanol and acetaldehyde content; branched-chain starch content was significantly and negatively correlated with β-phenylethanol and 3-hydroxy-2-butanone content; tannin content was significantly and negatively correlated with acetaldehyde content. Ethyl acetate and ethyl caprylate content were positively correlated with tannin content when tannin content was <1.94 % (Han et al., 2017). As shown in the results of the above studies, it is feasible to improve the flavor quality by regulating the content of nutritional composition from grains. These approaches include choosing high-quality raw materials, developing new combinations of different types of raw grains, or adding nutritional composition from exogenous sources. Similar raw material optimization strategies have proven effective in various fermented products (Baltaci et al., 2024; Kilic & Sengun, 2023). However, comprehensive assessments are imperative when implementing such modifications, as alterations in raw material composition may impact multiple parameters that influence product safety. As demonstrated by Kilic and Sengun, variations in tea fermentation substrates resulted in significant differences in both microbial populations and sensory characteristics (Kilic & Sengun, 2023). Correspondingly, Baltaci et al. demonstrated that modifications in vinegar production materials induced concurrent alterations in sensory profiles (taste and aroma) and physicochemical properties (including acidity, sugar content, and mineral composition), thereby confirming cherry laurel fruit's viability as the fermentation material from both safety and aroma perspectives (Baltaci et al., 2024).

Choosing high-quality raw materials to make Jiuqu

Since the formation potential of Daqu flavor compounds depends on the nutritional composition in grains, by regulating the grain matrix for making Daqu, researchers intend to overcome constraints of traditional production processes and achieve more efficient, diverse, and consistent Daqu fermentation methods. It has been reported that the use of wheat with a high protein content can increase the tetramethylpyrazine content in MTDQ because these wheats can be enriched with Bacillus (including Halobacillus and Virgibacillus), which promoted the production of tetramethylpyrazine and pointed a way to improve the content of pyrazine compounds production in MTDQ (Sun et al., 2022; Hou et al., 2023; Hou et al., 2024). Mei et al. had analyzed the substrate of raw material for LTDQ and found that the protein content of black peas was higher (23.92 g/100 g), followed by hemp peas, and the lowest by white peas (Mei et al., 2023). The selection of black peas in the production is conducive to improved flavor quality of the LTDQ. Proteins serve as essential nutrients for the fermentation processes involved in Jiuqu and Baijiu production. However, excessive protein content can lead to increased formation of fusel oils and accumulation of acidic substances, adversely affecting fermentation (Han et al., 2017; Lu et al., 2025). Research indicates that high-protein wheat yields 1.5–2 times higher ethyl carbamate (EC) concentrations in the resulting liquor compared to low-protein wheat (Xue et al., 2023). Further studies by Hou et al. demonstrated that compared to strong-gluten wheat (crude protein content: >30 %), medium-strong gluten (crude protein content: >28 %) and medium-gluten wheat varieties (crude protein content: >26 %) are more suitable for producing MTDQ (Hou et al., 2023). Similarly, tannin levels in raw materials must be carefully regulated, as excessively high concentrations can also impede fermentation efficiency (Han et al., 2017; Lu et al., 2025). For instance, Han et al. compared sorghum varieties with different tannin contents and found that an appropriate tannin concentration promotes the synthesis of esters such as ethyl acetate and ethyl octanoate (Han et al., 2017). The study showed that ethyl acetate content peaked at a tannin level of 1.94 %, while a higher tannin content of 2.69 % led to a reduction. The optimal tannin content for brewing sorghum is recommended to be within the range of 1–2 %, which may also serve as a useful reference for selecting raw materials in Jiuqu production. Therefore, raw material selection should not rely solely on any single component but must consider a balanced composition of multiple nutrients. It is important to note that specific nutritional requirements may vary depending on the type of Jiuqu and the target end product.

Selecting stable raw grain origins for flavor consistency

The consistency of flavor among different production rounds of Daqu can be improved by selecting grains with a stable yield. For example, Huang et al. analyzed the effect of wheat from different origins on the flavor of HTDQ. They selected 21 soft wheat samples from 12 locations across five provinces (Guizhou, Hubei, Anhui, Henan, and Sichuan). They found significant differences in the flavor composition of Jiuqu made from wheat of different regions. The flavor profiles were more similar when using the same wheat variety or geographically similar wheat, as their microbial compositions were more alike (Huang et al., 2021). Therefore, in practical production, when facing insufficient raw material supply from selected origins, alternative materials from geographically proximate locations with similar grain varieties can be used to maintain Jiuqu flavor consistency. If the same variety is unavailable, alternative raw materials with comparable starch and protein content may be substituted (He et al., 2023; Hou et al., 2023). This study provides systematic guidance for improving Jiuqu flavor consistency through comprehensive selection criteria including raw material origin, grain variety, and nutritional components.

A reasonable combination of different types of grains

As the protein, starch, tannin, and other matrix compositions vary significantly among different grain varieties and quality grades, employing a reasonable combination of grains for Jiuqu fermentation can effectively enhance the flavor profile of the final product. For example, protein content in sorghums was 9.18 % ∼ 10 %, in wheats it was 12.31 % ∼ 14.2 %, and in peas it was 20.70 % ∼ 23.92 % (Shi et al., 2025; Hou et al., 2024; Mei et al., 2023). The Fen-flavor Daqu and the strong flavor Daqu are made from sorghum and with a different certain proportion of peas. The difference in the proportion of peas can lead to the difference in the content of protein, which may be one of the reasons for the different flavor of the two types of Daqu. Xu et al. had also studied the impact of adding different contents of fenugreek to raw materials for making different Daqu to improve the flavor quality of Daqu (Xu et al., 2024). Daqu samples were made by adding different contents of fenugreek, respectively, 5 % fenugreek and 20 % fenugreek, and using Headspace Solid-Phase Microextraction Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) to determine VOCs, and calculating the odor activity value (OAV) values of the VOCs to analysis the change on key flavor compounds. As a result, the Daqu with 5 % fenugreek had a higher content of VOCs, especially alcohols, aldehydes, acids, esters, and pyrazines, which were significantly increased. OAV analysis showed there were 10 compounds that became the key flavor compounds (OAV > 1) in the fenugreek-added Daqu, including n-hexanol, ethyl caprylate, acetic acid, caprolactone, trimethylpyrazine, n-octanol, phenylethanol, hexanoic acid, 2-ethyl-6-methylpyrazine, and 2-octanone. This study highlighted the significant impact of fenugreek on the flavor composition of Daqu.

Exogenous addition of specific nutrients

Amino acids, as one of the important nutritional compositions, have a significant impact on the growth, carbon consumption, and flavor production of yeast (Wang et al., 2024). They serve as precursors for higher alcohols, esters, and other aroma compounds. Notably, branched-chain amino acids (BCAA) and aromatic amino acids are metabolized via the Ehrlich pathway, significantly enhancing the formation of higher alcohol acetates (HAAs) (González et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2016). For instance, adding BCAA such as valine, leucine, and isoleucine significantly increased the content of the corresponding higher alcohols and HAAs in wine (Wang et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2025). These amino acids undergo degradation to α-keto acids, which yeast subsequently assimilate through the Ehrlich pathway to produce higher alcohols during fermentation. These higher alcohols are crucial precursors for the synthesis of HAAs, such as isobutyl acetate (fruity) and 2-phenylethyl acetate (floral), through enzymatic reactions with acetyl-CoA catalyzed by alcohol acetyltransferase (AATase). In addition, It was previously reported that the formation of esters could be promoted by the addition of arginine during the stable growth phase of yeast (Gutiérrez et al., 2015). The content of isoamyl alcohol, phenylethanol, acetic acid, and propionic acid in fruit wines can be increased by adding threonine, while the content of isobutanol is decreasing. This metabolic regulation occurs because these amino acids serve as precursors for flavor compound biosynthesis. Through enzymatic degradation, they are converted into α-ketoglutarate, thereby promoting the synthesis of various esters and higher alcohols (Liu & Pilone, 1998). For example, under the action of arginine deiminase, arginine is converted to ornithine, which can be further metabolized to putrescine and glutamate-5-semialdehyde, thereby influencing α-ketoglutarate levels and indirectly regulating the availability of acetyl-CoA—the key precursor for ester synthesis. Meanwhile, threonine is converted to α-ketobutyrate (α-KB) by threonine dehydratase, which then enters the Ehrlich pathway to influence the formation of flavor compounds (Jiang et al., 2025). These studies have shown that the content of specific flavor compounds in Daqu can be controlled by exogenous addition of specific nutrients, which provides a guide to improve the characteristic flavor of Daqu and thus facilitates the flavor diversity of Daqu. (Luo et al., 2022). Furthermore, the incorporation of nutritional components requires careful evaluation of their potential safety implications. For instance, excessive histidine supplementation, the primary metabolic precursor to histamine, may promote biogenic amine accumulation and thereby introduce potential health hazards (Luo et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2025).

Optimization of Jiuqu production parameters

In addition to modifying the fermentation grain matrix, optimizing fermentation and storage parameters such as temperature, humidity, and moisture at different production stages further enhances the flavor profile of Jiuqu (Du et al., 2023;Wang, Du, et al., 2023). A study by Du et al., combining metabolomics with high throughput sequencing, revealed the metabolic profiles and microbial communities linked to metabolism during the thermophilic stage of Nongxiangxing Daqu fermentation (Du et al., 2023). The Daqu samples, transferred to a chamber at 60 °C controlled for the thermophilic stage after the heating phase, were taken from the 0th, 5th, and 9th days of the thermophilic stage. Daqu DNA was extracted and sequenced by high-throughput sequencing technology, and volatile organic compounds were extracted, separated, and identified by combining the use of HS-SPME-GC–MS (Du et al., 2023). Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography tandem Fourier transform mass spectrometry was used to detect the non-volatile compounds. The results showed that amino acid and carbohydrate metabolism were highly active during the thermophilic phase, and the thermophilic phase promoted the formation of pyrazines, while ester formation was minimized, suggesting that the longer thermophilic phase would promote the expression of amino acid-related metabolic pathways by microbes, but not the accumulation of tetramethyl pyrazine (Du et al., 2023). This study showed that an appropriate temperature and duration could promote the formation of aroma compounds and their precursors in the thermophilic stage, pointing a way to optimize the flavor compounds production in Daqu. Wang et al. also analyzed the volatile ester compounds during the whole production process of two kinds of Daqu and investigated the potential ester-synthesizing enzymes as well as their dependent environmental factors (Wang, Du, et al., 2023). HTDQ and MTDQ were randomly taken from three different locations in their own same Qu room at each time point, including fermentation days 0, 2, 6, 10, 24, and 40; stored for 1 month, 2 months, and mature starters (stored for two and a half months) (Wang, Du, et al., 2023).The volatile ester compounds of different treatments Daqu were analyzed by using HS-SPME-GC–MS, and physicochemical properties and enzyme activities were determined according to the general analytical method QB/T 4257–2011 of brewing Daqu in the light industry of the People's Republic of China. The results showed that the contents of short-chain esters (C4-C8) and medium-chain esters (C9-C13) in MTDQ were overall higher than those in HTDQ, and the temperature of Daqu was directly correlated with the short-chain esters and the activity of alcohol acyltransferase (AAT) (Wang, Du, et al., 2023). In addition, the content of short- and medium-chain esters was overall higher than that of the other treatment Daqu under the treatment closer to the claimed optimal temperature of 40–50 °C of AATs reaction (Wang, Du, et al., 2023). This study revealed that the appropriate control of temperature could facilitate AAT reaction, which was the primary enzymatic way to produce the short- and medium-chain esters throughout the Daqu production process, pointing a way to optimize the ester compounds production in Daqu.

Optimization of environment conditions in Jiuqu production

Modifying the environmental conditions in fermentation and storage is also one of the ways to modulate the flavor profile in Jiuqu, especially for Daqu (Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2024a; Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2024b). A study conducted by Yang et al. investigated the microbial succession of HTDQ under different storage seasons to explain the adaptation mechanism of microbial communities to the storage environment and to elucidate the microbial features of different seasonal Daqu (Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2024a). Remarkably, in these stored Daqu in different seasons, the seasonal effect (especially the temperature difference) on the microbial functional expression in Daqu ultimately resulted in differences in function and flavor composition, while modifying the level of moisture during the Daqu storage phase was found to decrease microecological differentiation due to seasonal temperature variation. This research pointing a way to reduce the differentiation of flavor compound in different seasonal Daqu stored. Another study by Yang et al. also showed that differences among the microbial communities under different environmental conditions are the major reason for microecological differentiation in Daqu (Yang, Fan, & Xu, 2024b). The primary environmental factors that regulate the assembly of microbial communities in the top and middle piles were pH and temperature, acidity and moisture, which resulted in the changes in flavor compounds of Daqu. They also found that an environment with high temperature and high humidity can improve the functional abilities of the microbial community in Daqu, thus enhancing the highly efficient utilization of grain materials, which provide a foundation to promoting the formation of flavor compounds. In summary, a high-temperature and high-humidity environment can be selected during Daqu fermentation to enhance the utilization of raw materials. During storage, maintaining consistent moisture levels in the environment can reduce the impact of seasonal temperature differences on the flavor differentiation of Daqu.

Biotechnological approaches

The strong microbial-flavor relationship in Jiuqu has enabled targeted biotechnological strategies to optimize flavor compound production and improve Baijiu quality. A key approach involves microbial inoculation during Jiuqu production, where fortified starter cultures are introduced to enhance enzymatic activity and metabolic output. Xu et al. demonstrated that the total content of flavor compounds can be significantly increased by inoculating Bacillus licheniformis and Bacillus pombe to MTDQ, with the content of phenylglyoxal and phenyl-ethanol being raised by 31.2 % and 19.5 %, respectively (Xu, Xu, et al., 2022). Another study demonstrated that Lactobacillus inoculation during Fen-flavor Baijiu production leads to substantial increases in key flavor compounds, with Lactobacillus acetotolerans and Lactobacillus plantarum supplementation elevating ethyl lactate and ethyl acetate concentrations to 13.29 kg/mL and 97.47 kg/mL, respectively, while simultaneously boosting acetic acid production (Pang et al., 2021).

Bioaugmentation is another way to improve the flavor compounds of Jiuqu. Mu et al. developed three types of Daqu (space Daqu, fortified Daqu, and traditional Daqu) and then determined the microbiological and flavor compounds of the three types of Daqu (Mu et al., 2023). The results showed that there were more volatile flavor compounds and aroma active compounds in space Daqu (inoculated with space-mutated Muqu) compared with traditional Daqu (Mu et al., 2023). And the further analysis revealed that the formation of non-volatile metabolites was regulated by the entire microbiota, yet the formation of volatile flavor compounds was strongly associated with the bacterial community (Mu et al., 2023). These results demonstrated the potential of spatial mutagenesis and bioaugmentation techniques in fermentation, which can contribute to the achievement of targeted regulation of flavors and microbial communities. Additionally, one method to enhance flavor is to tweak current strains or create new ones with particular traits, such as creating ethanol-tolerant strains (Ali et al., 2024). Daqu's flavor is enhanced not just by inoculation with single microbial strains but also by the formation of microbial consortia utilizing many metabolically compatible strains. I has been shown that inoculation with Bacillus subtilis, Staphylococcus epidermidis, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae increased the amount of Pantoea, Weissella, Staphylococcus, and Thermoacidophilus in Jiuqu and resulted in the succession of microbial communities and a significant increase in the levels of esters and aromatic compounds (Xu, Wu, et al., 2023). Thereby, the optimization of Daqu flavor involves more than just strain-specific improvements. It is important to consider the overall dynamics of the microbial community and how it affects the quality of Jiuqu.

However, microbial inoculation may introduce product safety concerns. For instance, certain microorganisms possess amino acid decarboxylase activity that can convert histidine to histamine through decarboxylation (Luo et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2025). If such microorganisms are introduced, they may compromise product safety. Therefore, when applying biotechnological approaches to modulate Jiuqu flavor profiles, comprehensive safety assessments of inoculated microorganisms and their potential impacts must be conducted. For instance, Xu et al. isolated a Staphylococcus strain with high esterase activity from MTDQ and conducted comprehensive safety evaluations. They found that this strain exhibited no detectable virulence factors, hemolytic activity, antibiotic resistance, or biofilm formation capability (Xu, Yang, et al., 2023). Therefore, the strain could be considered as a good functional starter and could be used in the Jiuqu-making industry. In addition, Tian et al. inoculated Saccharomyces cerevisiae (urea-utilizing strain) and Lactobacillus fermentum (producing EC-degrading enzymes) into wheat Qu for Huangjiu production (Tian et al., 2022). They found that this fortified starter culture not only enhanced the concentration of key volatile compounds, sweetness, and umami amino acids but also significantly reduced urea and EC levels in the final product. This safety assessment system is equally applicable to the selection criteria for inoculated microorganisms in other fermented products. For instance, Tong et al. screened a multifunctional Lactiplantibacillus plantarum strain from Daqu and applied it in yogurt fermentation (Tong et al., 2025). Using genomic and metabolomic approaches to assess its safety and functional properties, they demonstrated the strain's histamine-degrading capability and confirmed its ability to synthesize over 40 flavor compounds.

Machine learning-enabled dual optimization of food flavor and safety

In recent years, the integration of machine learning (ML) and biotechnology has significantly advanced bioengineering, with broad applications in the food, chemical, and related industries (Fang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Liao et al., 2023; Moreno-Paz et al., 2024). Key areas include functional microorganism screening, fermentation condition optimization, metabolic mechanism analysis, and pathway design. For instance, Liao et al. employed an integrated experimental design approach to optimize blueberry juice fermentation using three bacterial strains (Liao et al., 2023). Through regression modeling and genetic algorithm optimization of inoculation ratios, they achieved optimal phenolic enrichment. This study provides a methodological framework for optimizing mixed-culture fermentation and enhancing product quality with ML. Li et al. developed a dual-stage fermentation system for mead production and identified essential rate-limiting steps in VOC formation by employing combined fermentation monitoring with machine learning analysis. This study provides a theoretical foundation for enhancing mead and other food brewing processes through data-guided fermentation refinement methods (Li et al., 2025). In addition, Moreno-Paz et al. developed a ML-enhanced Design-Build-Test-Learn (DBTL) framework for p-coumaric acid production in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. By integrating one-pot libraries, targeted sequencing, and SHAP analysis, they achieved a 68 % higher titer (0.52 g/L) in two cycles, demonstrating ML's efficacy in metabolic engineering (Moreno-Paz et al., 2024).

ML enables precise flavor modulation during Jiuqu fermentation, offering new strategies for quality control. As evidenced by Fang et al.'s study on HTDQ fermentation, the integration of ML with microbiome analysis successfully identified six functional amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) from Saccharopolyspora, Bacillus, Lactobacillus, and Virgibacillus. These ASVs exhibited high enzymatic activity in amino acid metabolism during the flipping stages, with their ensemble machine learning mode providing precise predictions of amino acid concentrations and offering a novel metabolic control strategy for fermented food (Fang et al., 2025). Nevertheless, current applications of ML in Jiuqu flavor regulation are still scarce, underscoring an important avenue for future research. Drawing upon these research findings, machine learning technology can be employed to screen key aroma-producing microorganisms and implement targeted modifications of existing microbial strains. These functionally enhanced microorganisms can then be inoculated into Jiuqu starters to precisely regulate flavor profiles. Furthermore, by monitoring production parameters and flavor compound profiles of premium Jiuqu, machine learning-based predictive models can be developed to correlate process parameters with flavor characteristics, thereby optimizing fermentation conditions—particularly temperature and humidity control—which plays a critical role in Jiuqu flavor regulation (Zhang, Wang, et al., 2025).

The application of machine learning in the food industry has also significantly advanced food safety (Wang, Zeng, et al., 2025). Cui et al. developed an integrated machine learning and multi-omics bioinformatics pipeline for systematic mining of novel antimicrobial agents from Shanxi aged vinegar microbiomes (Cui, Wang, Liu, et al., 2024; Cui, Wang, Sun, et al., 2024). This high-throughput approach successfully identified new bacteriocins and antimicrobial peptides. The methodology demonstrates broad applicability for discovering antimicrobial compounds from diverse fermented food microbiomes, offering significant potential for enhancing food safety. Collectively, the combination of ML and biotechnology is driving the transformation of the fermented food industry toward intelligent and precision-oriented development.

Conclusion and perspective

Jiuqu varieties exhibit fundamental variations in three key aspects: (1) raw material composition, (2) production methodology, and (3) environmental conditions, which collectively shape their distinctive flavor profiles. This review examines three representatively Jiuqu (Daqu, Xiaoqu, and Fuqu), focusing on their production processes, classification, and characteristic flavor compounds, including alcohols, esters, aldehydes, ketones, and pyrazines, each contributing unique aroma profile. The formation of these flavor compounds occurs through three principal pathways: (i) direct derivation from raw materials, (ii) microbial biosynthesis, and (iii) non-enzymatic chemical reactions. Critically, the final flavor profile of each Jiuqu type emerges from the complex interplay between specific production parameters, artisanal techniques, and environmental factors (including geographic location, climatic conditions, and seasonal variations). The established relationships between production variables and flavor composition provide actionable targets for flavor profile optimization through targeted manipulation of key formation pathways and influencing factors.

In summary, the remarkable diversity, inherent complexity, and cascade interactions of flavor sources, formation pathways, and influencing factors present significant challenges in Jiuqu flavor modulation. However, advancing research has yielded progressively deeper understanding of Jiuqu flavor profile. Within this context, future investigations should prioritize three critical research directions: (1) Elucidating the fundamental relationships between basic physicochemical components, primary metabolites, and flavor compounds in Jiuqu, with particular emphasis on delineating the specific roles of microbial communities, enzymatic activities, and environmental parameters in flavor formation pathways. (2) Developing advanced flavor regulation technologies through integration of ML with modern biotechnology. (3) Establishing quantitative models to assess the contribution of Jiuqu flavor compounds to Baijiu sensory characteristics, thereby enabling the development of controlled flavor production systems. These strategic approaches will facilitate the transition from empirical practice to precision control in traditional fermentation processes.

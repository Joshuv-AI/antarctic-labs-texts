Hexagonal Zn1-xMgxS sheds light on the lattice dynamics of atomic alloys
Authors: Abdelmajid Elmahjoubi, Mala N Rao, Alexandre Ivanov, Andrei V Postnikov, Alain Polian, Toni Alhaddad, Samrath L Chaplot, Andrea Piovano, Sébastien Diliberto, Stéphanie Michel, Alain Maillard, Karol Strzałkowski, Olivier Pagès
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCubic pseudo-unary A1-xBx high-entropy metallic alloys and pseudo-binary A1-xBxC disordered semiconductor alloys set a benchmark to explore how physical properties are impacted by disorder. Through its diversity, the lattice dynamics offers a unique playground to assign the relevant length scales at which operate various kinds of disorders induced by alloying. (i) In high-entropy metallic alloys, the overdamping of the bond-collective (multi-bond→1-mode) acoustic modes at short wavelength originates from force-constant fluctuations. (ii) In semiconductor alloys, the lattice mismatch splits, at any wavelength, the bond-specific (1-bond→1-mode) optical modes in duos distinguishing “same” from “alien” environments, as explained by the percolation model. Zn1-xMgxS is ideal to test both univocal assignments. Its force-constant disorder is small, reducing the cause for overdamping of the acoustic modes. Its local strain is inverted, the lighter substituent being the larger one and forming the longer bond. This forecasts a dramatic inversion of the mode-duos. Further, its wurtzite structure enables (iii) to test whether/how the percolation model for the mode-duos transfers under lowering the crystal symmetry from cubic to hexagonal. The triple acoustic-(i)/optical-(ii-iii) test on Zn1-xMgxS, combining inelastic neutron scattering with first-principles simulations, is positive. Due to their simple (cubic) structure and constituents (atoms), the pseudo-unary high-entropy A1-xBx metallic alloys1 and pseudo-binary disordered A1xBxC semiconductor alloys2,3 are model systems to explore how physical properties are impacted by disorder in a percolation context4. In particular, the lattice dynamics, governed by the bond force constant, i.e., a local physical property, is potentially sensitive to the alloy disorder down to the atom scale where the A↔B substitution takes place.

On this basis, the lattice dynamics of cubic high-entropy metallic alloys with face centered cubic structure attract considerable studies at various wavelengths spanning the Brillouin zone (BZ) using inelastic neutron scattering (INS) and/or inelastic X-ray scattering5–7. This structure corresponds to a unique atom per primitive cell, and thus provides only acoustic () phonons bearing a “cumulative” (bond-collective: multi-bond→1-mode) message about the alloy disorder. By offering an access to clear -modes, such high-entropy metallic alloys are exceptional among metals, at the frontier of glasses and complex/nanostructured alloys, in which the -insight is generally biased at short length scale. In glasses, this is due to the absence of a regular lattice. The positional disorder dramatically affects short wavelength vibrations, the propagating character being lost near the BZ-boundary8. In complex metallic alloys with multiple atoms per primitive cell (the number going to infinity for aperiodic crystals9the -modes are screened by numerous optical modes (defined below) near the BZ-boundary.

INS and inelastic X-ray scattering measurements were recently done on Fe20Co20Cr20Mn20Ni20, a prototype face centered cubic high-entropy metallic alloy7 involving a quintuple substitution on the unique site of the primitive cell at maximum alloy disorder (equal proportion of substituents). The composition is customized so as to minimize the contrasts in covalent radius and atomic mass (the substituents are immediate neighbors on the same line of the periodic table), thus emphasizing the remaining contrast in force constant involved in the metallic bonding (20%). The -modes nicely disperse across the BZ, with a progressive attenuation manifested by overdamping towards the BZ-boundary, imputed to the sole existing -contrast.

A1-xBxC pseudo-binary disordered semiconductor alloys with zincblende structure grown as bulk crystals by the Bridgman method are choice systems to further test such direct imputation, for two reasons. First, the growth under equilibrium conditions guarantees a close to random A↔B substitution, verified by solid-state nuclear magnetic resonance10,11 or by extended X-ray absorption fine structure spectroscopy12–16like in high-entropy metallic alloys. Second, the bond force-constant put forward to explain the attenuation of the -modes of high-entropy metallic alloys plays a central role in the lattice dynamics of disordered semiconductor alloys, due to their strong covalent bonding.

Further, with two (cation + anion) atoms per primitive cell, the zincblende-A1-xBxC disordered semiconductor alloys do not only exhibit -modes, due to in-phase cationic and anionic motions, but also optical () modes, due to out-of-phase cationic and anionic displacements, reflecting effective A-C and B-C bond-stretchings. The -modes are interesting in case of alloying because they offer a bond-specific (A-C- or B-C) insight into the alloy disorder in the spirit of a 1-bond→1-mode paradigm, beyond the bond-collective (A-C and B-C) insight provided by the -modes. Besides, as AB substitution occurs either on the cationic or anionic site, the number of -modes achieves minimum, i.e., merely two (A-C and B-C), reducing the risk for mutual screening between - and -modes near the BZ-boundary. Despite the number of elements is less in diamond-type A1-xBx disordered semiconductor alloys, such as Si1-xGex, the dynamics is more complicated since the AB substitution occurs on both sites of the primitive cell. Hence, three bond species coexist, giving rise to as many -modes.

Altogether, the zincblende-A1-xBxC disordered semiconductor alloys offer a unique playground to address the /-lattice dynamics of atomic alloys at all length scales. Notably, in contrast with the -modes, the -modes propagate near the BZ-center. A convenient access is achieved in-house by far-infrared absorption or by inelastic visible-light (Raman) scattering which operate near the BZ-center by force of the vertical dispersion of the light probe17. The literature on this topic is abundant since the emergence of disordered semiconductor alloys in the 1960s2. Such -insight is not possible for high-entropy metallic alloys due to the total reflection of far-infrared and visible lights by metals, redirecting the study of their lattice dynamics towards large facilities (using, e.g., INS and/or inelastic X-ray scattering).

Our pioneering, and so far unique, INS study of a zincblende-type disordered semiconductor alloys, namely Zn0.67Be0.33Se18 with highly-mismatched bond-related physical properties19,20 exalting the lattice dynamics, e.g., substituent’s covalent radius (13.5%), bond length (9.0%), bond reduced mass (77%) and bond force constant (24.5%; here, the values are estimated from multiplied by the squared frequency of the transverse optical modes of the parent compounds probed near the BZ-center, i.e., ~25.6 and ~62.1 meV for cubic ZnSe and cubic BeSe, respectively21, evidenced increasingly damped -modes towards the BZ-boundary18. This conforms to expectations by analogy with the -modes of the similarly -contrasted Fe20Co20Cr20Mn20Ni20 high-entropy metallic alloy7. More surprisingly, the INS data revealed a dual -mode for Be-Se (1bond→2-mode) scaling 1:2 in intensity, in proportion to the Be and Zn fractions, also observed by Raman scattering18 (Fig. S1, with prefix S standing for Supplementary information). This Be-Se -duo exists throughout the BZ, from quasi-infinite wavelengths near the BZ-center, probed by Raman scattering, through finite wavelengths all the way down to the shortest ones achieved by INS near the BZ-boundary22.

The Be-Se -duo in Zn0.67Be0.33Se was fruitfully assessed within an original percolation model (PM)23in which the -modes distinguish, beyond bond species, “same” (homo) from “alien” (hetero) environments of a given bond (1-bond→2-mode), and thus “see” the local fluctuations in composition inherent to random alloying. This deviates from the historical modified-random-element-isodisplacement (MREI) model24 in which the -modes are “blind” to the local alloy disorder, the like bonds of a given species vibrating at the same frequency at a given alloy composition (1-bond→1-mode), irrespectively of their local environment. Prior to the PM, an attempt to formalize (beyond the MREI model) a capacity of the -modes to “see” the local environment was done within the cluster model, suggested for cubic25 and hexagonal26 alloys in the 1960’s. However, the cluster model was recently ruled out as a realistic model for the paradigmatic case of cubic GaAs1xPx for which it was developed27. Successful applications of the PM to all so far explored cubic alloys by Raman scattering, with zincblende (a non-exhaustive list is given in Ref28. or diamond29 structures, suggested the model’s universality.

Within the PM, various kinds of disorder have separate roles in shaping the -duos of A1xBxC zincblende-type disordered semiconductor alloys, our reference in this work. The mass disorder () governs the frequency gaps between -duos due to different bonds along a basic rule that, generally, lightweight/heavyweight bonds vibrate at high/low frequency, respectively. The -duo is best resolved for the short bond that involves the small- substituent, generally the lightweight one that vibrates at high frequency. This is because in its cage of C nearest-neighbors the small substituent has more room than the large one to accommodate the local strain due to the lattice mismatch (), with concomitant impact on its vibration frequencies, being more diversified. Generally, the -duo clarifies in ()-mismatched systems. In this line, the MREI model represents a limit PM case for well-matched () disordered semiconductor alloys, the PM-doublet degenerating into a MREI-singlet. Experimental (Raman scattering30 and ab initio (phonon dispersion31,32 data indeed support the MREI scheme for the well-matched () zincblende-Al1xGaxAs disordered semiconductor alloy (40%, 0.8%, %, 1.6%)19 in which the lattice regularity is hardly challenged by alloying, the bond lengths remaining similar within a few percent33.

In a nutshell, the attenuation of the bond-collective -modes observed in cubic high-entropy metallic alloys and disordered semiconductor alloys at short length scale originates from the -mismatch, while the dual -mode typically associated with the short bond of zincblende-type disordered semiconductor alloys, that involves the small- substituent, originates from the -mismatch. Accordingly, the attenuation of the -modes at short length scale should disappear in absence of -mismatch, and the -duos should be turned upside down in case of disruptive ()-mismatches, to be tested.

Zn1 − xMgxS ( 36%, 4.0%, 7.8%,15.0%)19,20 involving two substituents far apart in the periodic table is an ideal disordered semiconductor alloy to complete the test on an experimental and/or ab initio basis, i.e., in a model-free approach. First, in Zn1−xMgxS , estimated from the frequencies of the transverse optical modes near the BZ-center of cubic ZnS, i.e., 33.8 meV34, and cubic MgS, i.e., 40.5 meV35 (considering that the phonon frequencies remain close in zincblende- and wurtzite-type ZnS36 and MgS37, is small, comparable to that observed in the well-matched Al1xGaxAs disordered semiconductor alloy (see above). This reduces the presumed cause for overdamping of the -modes at short length scales. Zn1xMgxS is further interesting for assessing the predictive power of the PM for the -duos of disordered semiconductor alloys in a non-standard situation: the lighter (Mg) substituent has the larger covalent radius and hence forms the longer bond, a unique case among all disordered semiconductor alloys tested so far23,28. This double -inversion invites to anticipate a dramatic PM “inversion”, a situation in which the PM scheme for Zn0.67Mg0.33S would make a fair “mirror image” in frequency and intensity of that for Zn0.67Be0.33Se (Fig. S1). Accordingly, the triple {, minor-, dominant-} signal of Zn0.67Be0.33Se with a Be-Se -duo scaling 1:2 in intensity would transform into a {dominant-, minor-, } signal for Zn0.67Mg0.33S – in this order of frequency – with an inverted minor vs. dominant Zn-S -duo scaling 2:1 in intensity, using the PM-notation of an optical mode () due to a given bond (subscript) in a given environment (superscript). Hence the double ()-inversion would generate a double -duo inversion: visible for the heavyweight bond instead of for the lightweight one, with a switch of the minor and dominant submodes. Moreover, Zn1xMgxS crystallizes in the wurtzite structure, offering an opportunity to test how/whether the PM for the -duos transfers under lowering the crystal symmetry from cubic to hexagonal.

In this work, the planned triple-test on the (origin of attenuation at short length scale) and (origin of the -duo zincblende→wurtzite extension of the PM) modes of high-entropy metallic alloys and disordered semiconductor alloys is performed on wurtzite-Zn0.67Mg0.33S (Fig. 1) used as a case study. The selected wurtzite-type disordered semiconductor alloys ideally matches in composition the reference zincblende-Zn0.67Be0.33Se one, which facilitates comparison. A dispersion of the {, }-duo of wurtzite-Zn0.67Mg0.33S created by the double ()-inversion is searched for by INS on the IN8 triple axis spectrometer at Institut Laue Langevin across the BZ along the and high-symmetry reciprocal lattice directions. An additional wurtzite-Zn0.94Mg0.06S alloy with minimal Mg content preserving the wurtzite structure is used as a “negative” PM-control, meaning that the -duo should not show up in this case, because alloying tends to zero. To investigate the -duos we focus on the non polar -modes that assimilate with purely mechanical (mass + spring) oscillators. The reason is that such oscillators hardly couple and thus preserve the natural diversity of the phonon pattern of a complex system like an alloy27. The discussion of the INS data is supported by ab initio phonon calculations done by applying the SIESTA code38 to large (up to 360-atom) disordered wurtzite-type Zn1xMgxS supercells at high (=0.33) and minimal (~0,1) alloying. The ab initio insight is used as a “positive” PM-control for the -modes – meaning that any presumed -duo seen by INS should replicate ab initio if intrinsic to random ZnMg alloying (=0.33), and also to track down a possible origin of the -duo in wurtzite-Zn1xMgxS at the microscopic scale at minimal alloying (~0,1). Besides, the -modes are studied by INS along both and directions. It is a matter to see whether in absence of -mismatch the -modes are overdamped at short length scale, or not.

Our ambition is twofold through the triple-test: First, to achieve a unified understanding of the -modes of disordered semiconductor alloys under the shift of paradigm from the MREI to the PM, beyond cubic and onto hexagonal crystal structures, with the PM set as a robust tool able to manage bond specificities, related to and in this case. Second, to test the presumed -origin of the attenuation of the -modes of atomic alloys at short length scales7 on a system that exhibits a small -mismatch. Besides, as we are not aware of any INS study of a hexagonal alloy, the current INS experiment planned for wurtzite-Zn1xMgxS is interesting per se. Altogether, this fixes the scope of this work.

The (Zn; Mg; S) composition is well suited for INS due to small neutron absorption cross sections = (0.9; 0.1; 0.5) barn39 and high coherent neutron scattering lengths = (5.2; 5.7; 2.8) fm40.

Conclusion

Zn1xMgxS, characterized by a small mismatch in force-constant () and a special lattice mismatch (), is used to test two major assertions about the lattice dynamics of high-entropy metallic alloys and disordered semiconductor alloys, i.e., that and are the prime causes for attenuation of the acoustic modes at short length scale and for dual optical modes at all length scales, respectively. Moreover, as a wurtzite crystal, Zn1xMgxS offers an opportunity to test whether/how the percolation model used to explain the optical-duos transfers under lowering the crystal symmetry from cubic to hexagonal. The triple test, combining inelastic neutron scattering with ab initio calculations at moderate (33 at%) and minor (6 at%) Mg-alloying of ZnS, is decisive in the positive sense. This illustrates how and manifest in shaping the lattice dynamics of disordered atomic alloys across the Brillouin zone. Additional INS/ab initio insights at large Mg-contents of Zn1-xMgxS would be useful to complete and to further test the current picture set at low and moderate Mg contents.

Methods

Crystals

The INS experiment on Zn1-xMgxS is carried out on two large (Fig. 1) homogenous (checked by Raman mapping across the ingots) free-standing single crystals with wurtzite structure verified by powder X-ray diffraction, grown using the Bridgman method46 at high (Zn0.67Mg0.33S, half-cylinder 158 mm2 in lengthdiameter) and low (Zn0.94Mg0.06S, cylinder 68 mm2 alloying. High quality of crystal structure is attested by sharp peaks in the powder X-ray diffractograms (Fig. 1). The single crystals are oriented by conoscopy ( axis), X-ray diffraction and neutron diffraction in situ ( and axis). The composition is determined with accuracy 0.1% by the inductively coupled plasma method47.

Raman scattering

Raman measurements are done on wurtzite-Zn0.67Mg0.33S near the BZ-center at room temperature using the 632.8 nm HeNe-laser. Both the phonon and phonon-polariton regimes are addressed by implementing backward and near-forward scattering geometries17respectively, at normal incidence/detection onto/across a ~ 2 mm-thick wurtzite-Zn0.67Mg0.33S single crystal with parallel faces extracted by cleavage from the ingot used for INS, with in-plane -axis tilted by ~ 45° from the ingot axis and out-of-plane -axis (inset of Fig. 1).

Inelastic neutron scattering

INS experiments are conducted at the thermal neutron three-axis spectrometer IN848,49 at the high flux reactor of the Institut Laue-Langevin. The 2D-focusing copper monochromator with reflection planes Cu-(200) and pyrolytic graphite (PG) analyser with reflection planes PG002 provide optimal spectral resolution of the -modes using scattered neutron wave vector 2.662 Å[- [1. Higher order contaminations in the monochromatic beams were practically entirely removed with a 10 cm thick oriented pyrolytic graphite filter. The INS spectra are measured at a temperature of 12 K in the scattering plane at various phonon wave vectors across the BZ along and . Both the - and -modes are probed for Zn0.67Mg0.33S, whereas only the non-polar -modes, of main interest, for Zn0.94Mg0.06S. Experiment was guided by dynamical structure factor computations carried out within the shell model using the program DISPR developed at Bhabha Atomic Research Center43, that helps to target special BZ in the periodic zone scheme (shifted from the first BZ by a reciprocal lattice vector ) as the best compromises between maximal neutron scattering cross section and optimal symmetry (transverse or longitudinal) access, taking into account a selection rule that only the modes polarized along the transferred wavevector are detected by INS22.

Ab initio calculations

Ab initio - and -phonon dispersions of wurtzite-Zn0.67Mg0.33S are calculated with the SIESTA code38 based on the density functional theory (DFT), using specifically the local density approximation, by the finite displacement technique. Two large (360-atoms) special quasirandom structures50 are used, optimized to a random Zn↔Mg substitution with the help of the Alloy Theoretic Automated Toolkit (ATAT51. The supercells are elongated in a sequence of ten base-slices cut normal to [001] and [100] directions (Fig. S2). Each slice is one primitive cell thick and has lateral size of 33 primitive cells. Once the finite displacement calculation is performed for a supercell, different projections of the set of 3 N (N: number of atoms) vibration eigenvectors are applied in order to reveal different trends – as explained in Sec. N.3 of Ref38. In this work, combined projections onto wavevectors and onto irreducible presentations of the wurtzite space group enabled extraction of the “spectral functions”, resolved in frequency and wavevector, to be directly confronted with the INS wurtzite-Zn0.67Mg0.33S phonon dispersions. For practical reasons, the “sharpest” projections of this kind come out for wavevectors being divisions of the reciprocal lattice vector by the supercell multiplication factor. Apart from this, simulations at the onset of the PM-splitting of the non-polar -modes near the BZ-center have been done on 72-atom supercells containing one isolated Mg or Zn impurity. In its current version, the used SIESTA code does not take into account the long-wavelength electric field carried by the polar longitudinal or transverse (phonon-polariton) modes, and thus assimilates with the Raman/INS signal of the related non-polar -modes.

Raman – INS – ab initio situations near the BZ-center

By changing the crystal structure from zincblende to wurtzite, the sequence of close-packing planes changes from abcabc. to abab. corresponding to a doubling of the primitive cell (including two atoms for zb and four for w) along the [111] zincblende-direction or axis of the wurtzite-type crystal. A crude insight into the wurtzite-type phonon dispersion can thus be obtained by folding the zincblende-type phonon dispersion from the midpoint () between the BZ-center and the BZ-boundary52. Folding halfway the zincblende-type dispersions of the acoustic/bond-collective and zincblende-type modes generates the bond-collective and wurtzite-type dispersions. These exhibit a propagative character at the BZ-center, and hence consist of -modes. A similar folding process applied to the optical/bond-specific and zincblende-type dispersions creates the and wurtzite-type dispersions of optical/bond-specific modes, with non-polar character (the net dipole moment per primitive cell is zero – Fig. 2). In addition, the lowering of crystal symmetry from a zincblende-type isotropic crystal to a wurtzite-type uniaxial one splits the polar optical zincblende-type mode, distinguishing between vibrations along () and perpendicular () to the singular axis of the wurtzite-type crystal.

At the BZ-center, the INS vs. ab initio comparison in the polar and symmetries is critical in various respects, as detailed below. A basic selection rule for INS is that only the modes polarized along the transferred wavevector are detected by INS22using the notation introduced in the main text.

Remarkably, by adopting a neutron scattering geometry meant to address the TO modes near the BZ-center (), the and modes transiently show up strictly at (=0). This is because at this limit the distinction between the transverse (perpendicular to ) and longitudinal (parallel to ) vibrations disappears17. As soon as departing from , and immediately vanish; only the genuine transverse and survive. We emphasize that the TO modes in question are non polar, i.e., purely mechanical in character.

The polar TO modes, i.e., the phonon-photon coupled modes known as phonon-polaritons, cannot be detected by INS. Indeed, by force of the quasi vertical dispersion of a photon, the phonon-polaritons are confined extremely close to the () point of the first BZ (), i.e., within the first few 10−4 of the BZ size17. Generally, the INS experiments are operated in BZ remote from the first BZ where the neutron scattering cross section achieves maximum and/or is best resolved for a given mode. No chance to detect phonon-polaritons is given there. Even by placing the INS study near the () point of the first BZ (), the phonon-polariton would remain out of reach by INS. It is not a mere technical problem of a limited -resolution by INS. The limitation is inherent to INS, due to the cited INS selection rule above that the probed vibrations by INS are polarized along . By force of the latter rule, near the () point of the first BZ () where the photon-polariton coupling develops, the only vibrations that can be probed by INS are polarized along , reducing to in this case, hence longitudinal in character and not transverse. In brief, an INS experiment done near the () point of the first BZ () would probe the LO modes only, not the phonon-polaritons.

Neither the phonon-polaritons nor the LO modes can be addressed ab initio near the center () of any BZ ( or ). This is because in its current version the used SIESTA code does not handle the macroscopic () transverse (phonon-polariton) or longitudinal (LO) electric fields resulting from the ionicity of the chemical bonding in a wurtzite crystal17. Hence, strictly at the BZ-center a polar LO mode manifests in ab initio data as the corresponding non-polar TO mode. Immediately out of the BZ-center, the electric field carried by the polar and modes is restored ab initio. The cited LO modes then become visible as distinct features in the ab initio data.

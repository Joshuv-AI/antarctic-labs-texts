Nutritional-immunological assessment by CONUT score predicts surgical outcomes in tuberculous empyema: development of a multivariate risk model
Authors: Liangliang Chen, Wenfeng Yu, Likui Fang, Jing Zhen, Fangming Zhong, Guocan Yu, Pengfei Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTuberculous empyema remains a challenging complication of Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection, with high recurrence rates and poor long-term prognosis. This retrospective cohort study evaluated the prognostic utility of the Controlling Nutritional Status (CONUT) score—a composite index integrating serum albumin, total cholesterol, and lymphocyte count—in predicting postoperative recurrence among 325 patients undergoing decortication (87.7% via VATS) at Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital between 2017 and 2024. Univariate analysis identified high CONUT scores (OR = 2.37, 95% CI:1.53–3.71, p < 0.001), elevated CRP, prolonged operative time, and open surgery as significant risk factors. A multivariate nomogram incorporating these variables demonstrated moderate predictive accuracy (AUC = 0.70), with CONUT score outperforming traditional nutritional indices like BMI. The study highlights malnutrition-immunodeficiency interplay in recurrence pathogenesis, advocating for preoperative CONUT-based risk stratification to guide nutritional optimization and surgical planning. Integrating the CONUT score into perioperative assessment may improve stratification, guide targeted interventions, and ultimately enhance surgical outcomes for patients with tuberculous empyema.Keywords: Tuberculous empyema, Decortication, CONUT score, Postoperative recurrence, Nutritional status

Introduction

Tuberculous empyema represents a particularly severe and challenging sequela of Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection, characterized by the accumulation of chronic, purulent pleural exudate and extensive pleural thickening or fibrosis. Although once considered a relatively uncommon endpoint of pulmonary tuberculosis, tuberculous empyema has gained increased epidemiological significance over the past decades. Global trends reflect not only the resurgence of tuberculosis in developing regions but also shifts in its burden among industrialized countries, particularly due to greater numbers of immunocompromised and elderly individuals, as well as the global spread of drug-resistant TB strains [1].

According to the latest World Health Organization (WHO) data, tuberculosis remains a leading infectious cause of morbidity and mortality, with an estimated 10.8 million new cases and 1.25 million deaths globally reported in 2023. While most develop classical pulmonary or extrapulmonary TB, tuberculous empyema can occur in untreated, clinically neglected, or drug-resistant cases, leading to chronic pleural cavity infection and marked impairment in pulmonary function. The combination of local destruction, persistent inflammation, and host immune dysregulation results in a high incidence of prolonged disability, hospital readmission, and elevated healthcare costs [2, 3].

The management of tuberculous empyema presents considerable clinical challenges. Conventional antituberculosis chemotherapy is often ineffective due to the avascular, fibrotic nature of the pleural rind. Thus, surgical intervention—whether via open decortication or increasingly via video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS)—remains the definitive treatment option in many cases, especially for patients with chronic organized empyema or those failing medical management [4]. However, even after technically successful surgery, postoperative recurrence rates remain alarmingly high, with some studies reporting rates around 4% depending on patient demographics, microbiological characteristics, and perioperative care [5]. Recurrence not only complicates postoperative recovery but also impacts patients’ long-term lung function, quality of life, and overall prognosis.

A growing body of evidence highlights the central role of host factors—particularly nutritional and immunologic status—in modulating the risk of tuberculosis progression, surgical outcomes, and recurrence [6]. Tuberculosis has long been recognized to precipitate a catabolic state due to chronic inflammation and cytokine-mediated cachexia. Malnutrition, as defined by loss of body protein and energy reserves, directly impairs cellular and humoral immunity, diminishing host capability to contain or eradicate infection. Malnourished patients demonstrate altered T-cell responses, decreased cytokine production, and impaired phagocytosis, which together facilitate persistent infection and poor surgical wound healing [5, 7]. Meanwhile, acute-phase proteins and other inflammatory mediators may influence pleural membrane permeability and reparative capacity, compounding the risk of postoperative complications.

Numerous objective indicators have been proposed to assess nutritional and immunological status, including serum albumin, total lymphocyte count, body mass index (BMI), and C-reactive protein (CRP). However, these parameters individually suffer from limitations such as lack of specificity, susceptibility to acute-phase reactions, or poor reflection of functional immune status. The Controlling Nutritional Status (CONUT) score, a relatively novel prognostic tool, integrates three laboratory indices—serum albumin (for protein reserve), total cholesterol (for caloric intake), and total peripheral lymphocyte count (for immune competence)—and yields a composite score reflecting both nutritional deprivation and reduced immunological reserve [8].

Several recent studies have demonstrated the predictive value of the CONUT score in diverse clinical scenarios, including cancer surgery, cardiovascular disease, and tuberculosis, with elevated scores consistently associated with increased morbidity and mortality [9–11]. Notably, in the context of TB, high CONUT scores correlate with slower sputum conversion, prolonged hospitalization, and increased relapse rates, especially among patients with multidrug-resistant or advanced disease [12, 13].

Despite these advances, little is known about the relevance and predictive accuracy of the CONUT score in the setting of surgical treatment for tuberculous empyema. Given the unique pathophysiological profile of this condition—where both local surgical trauma and systemic catabolism play a role—there is a pressing need to identify reliable markers for postoperative recurrence. This study, therefore, aims to systematically evaluate the prognostic significance of the CONUT score in patients undergoing surgical resection for tuberculous empyema, to compare its utility against individual laboratory markers, and to incorporate it into a multifactorial model for individualized risk prediction.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital in Zhejiang Province. Written informed consent from patients has been waived.

Patient cohort

This retrospective cohort study enrolled patients diagnosed with tuberculous empyema in the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital, from January 1, 2017 to November 30, 2024. The initial cohort included 351 consecutive cases. Applying predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 325 eligible patients were selected for final analysis.

Surgical and postoperative management

Preoperative management comprised a comprehensive assessment of pulmonary function and nutritional status, optimization of comorbidities, and the continuation of anti-tuberculosis therapy [14]. The choice of surgical approach—either open thoracotomy or video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS)—was made according to anatomical considerations and surgeon expertise. All patients received standardized intraoperative management, postoperative pain control, physiotherapy, appropriate antibiotic and anti-tuberculosis regimens, as well as nutritional support. Decortication was primarily indicated in cases of thickened pleural cortex resulting in lung entrapment, empyema (with or without bronchopleural fistula), recurrent or progressive disease following anti-tuberculosis treatment, and symptomatic bronchiectasis. Consistent with best practice, all patients underwent anti-tuberculosis therapy for a minimum of six weeks prior to surgical intervention. For chronic tuberculous empyema (CTE), thoracoscopic surgery was generally preferred; however, conversion to open thoracotomy was performed if complete decortication could not be achieved via VATS.

The procedure was performed under general anesthesia using double-lumen endotracheal intubation, with the patient positioned in the lateral decubitus posture. A 10-mm port was created at the seventh intercostal space along the mid-axillary line for lung deflation, with additional ports placed as required. Local anesthesia with 0.5% bupivacaine was administered at port sites for postoperative pain control. The thickened parietal pleura was meticulously dissected from the endothoracic fascia, and adhesions on the visceral pleura were separated to restore pulmonary mobility, with particular care taken to minimize air leaks. The pleural cavity was irrigated thoroughly, and all fluid was aspirated for laboratory analysis, while excised tissues were sent for histopathology. Meticulous repair of air leaks was achieved using absorbable sutures. A chest tube was inserted and subsequently removed when daily drainage was less than 100 mL and no air leak was evident. Patients were discharged upon stabilization and symptom resolution [15].

Follow-up protocol

All patients were scheduled for outpatient clinical visits and imaging (chest CT) every 3–6 months within the first two years post-surgery, and then annually. Recurrence was defined as radiological evidence of renewed pleural effusion, cavity, or atelectasis, independently confirmed by two board-certified thoracic surgeons. During follow-up, chest X-rays were performed at discharge and again at 1, 3, and 6 months postoperatively to assess lung re-expansion. A satisfactory outcome was defined as at least 75% lung re-expansion; residual cavities were diagnosed if lung expansion remained below this threshold at six months. The follow-up period ends when the postoperative anti-tuberculosis treatment has been completed or has not yet been completed with 6 months, the follow-up period should be at least 6 months.The median follow-up period was 19 months.

Data collection

Demographic and clinical data (age, gender, BMI, comorbidities), preoperative laboratory parameters (serum albumin, lymphocyte counts, total cholesterol, CRP, WBC, neutrophil and platelet indices), intraoperative factors (operative time, blood loss, surgical technique), and anti-TB regimens were systematically collected. The CONUT score, calculated as specified in the original scoring system, using serum albumin, total lymphocyte count, and total cholesterol. Serum albumin (g/dL) is scored as: ≥3.5 = 0 points, 3.0–3.49 = 2 points, 2.5–2.9 = 4 points, < 2.5 = 6 points; total lymphocyte count (/mm³): >1600 = 0 points, 1200–1599 = 1 point, 800–1199 = 2 points, < 800 = 3 points; total cholesterol (mg/dL): >180 = 0 points, 140–180 = 1 point, 100–139 = 2 points, < 100 = 3 points, categorized nutritional status as normal (0–1), mild (2–4), moderate (5–8), or severe (9–12) [9, 10]. Derived ratios, such as albumin-to-globulin ratio, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), were also analyzed. The age-adjusted Charlson Comorbidity Index (ACCI) was used for comorbidity quantification.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD or median (IQR), and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Between-group differences were assessed using Student’s t-test, ANOVA, or Mann–Whitney U test (for non-parametric data). Categorical comparisons used Pearson’s chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Correlation and multicollinearity were evaluated by Pearson correlation matrix (R 4.1.2). Univariate logistic regression identified factors associated with recurrence (p < 0.20), with significant variables entered into multivariate logistic regression models via forward and backward selection. Predictive performance was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis (AUC), calibration curves, decision curve analysis (DCA), and clinical impact curves (CIC). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed in R (4.1.2) and SPSS (23.0).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among the 325 included patients, 231 were male (70.1%) and 94 female (29.9%), with a median age of 47 years (IQR, 35–58). More than two-thirds (66.5%) were aged under 50 years. The majority underwent VATS (87.1%), whereas 12.9% required thoracotomy due to extensive pleural rind or dense adhesions. The mean operative time was 3.1 ± 1.7 h, with average intraoperative blood loss of 130.7 ± 216.0 ml. Postoperative complications—such as new-onset atelectasis, persistent air leak, or ICU admission—were noted in 16 patients (5.0%). The distribution of ACCI scores showed that nearly half had a score of 0, with 22.2% presenting a score greater than 3, indicating significant comorbidity. All patients received quadruple first-line anti-TB therapy for at least 2 months preoperatively and continued for 9–12 months postoperatively. Positive nucleic acid amplification tests for M. tuberculosis were found in 84.9%, and positive T-spot tuberculosis tests in 92.3%. Laboratory data revealed a mean CRP of 18.2 ± 30.7 mg/L and an average BMI of 20.1 ± 3.4. Detailed data are presented in Table 1.

Nutritional and immunological profiles

The preoperative CONUT scores ranged from 0 to 7, with the majority classified as mild to moderate malnutrition. Detailed visualization is provided in Fig. 1, there was a moderate positive correlation between elevated CONUT scores and advanced age, comorbidities, and parameters reflecting disease severity or recurrence, supporting its value as a comprehensive predictor of postoperative outcomes. By contrast, traditional nutritional indicators such as BMI had weak predictive value for recurrence. Serum albumin, while a classic marker, is highly susceptible to acute inflammatory changes and perioperative fluid shifts, limiting its standalone utility. Furthermore, perioperative parameters—such as intraoperative blood loss and the occurrence of postoperative complications—were found to be positively correlated, indicating that increased operative bleeding may lead to a higher risk of postoperative adverse events. Variables associated with molecular tuberculosis testing (e.g., TB-DNA, TB-RNA, TB-LIRA) showed strong interrelationships, reflecting their shared diagnostic relevance.

Univariate and multivariate predictors of recurrence

Univariate logistic regression (Fig. 2) revealed several factors significantly associated with postoperative recurrence: high CONUT score (OR = 2.37, 95% CI: 1.53–3.71, p < 0.001), elevated CRP (OR = 1.01, 95% CI: 1.00–1.02, p = 0.03), higher ACCI score (OR = 1.45, 95% CI: 1.05–1.99, p = 0.02), increased intraoperative blood loss (p = 0.005), and prolonged operative time (p = 0.02). Use of the VATS approach demonstrated a protective effect (OR = 0.47, 95% CI: 0.24–0.97, p = 0.04), while BMI showed no significant association.

In the multivariate model incorporating operative time, WBC, surgical approach, and CONUT score, these variables remained independent predictors. The nomogram, refer to Fig. 3; Table 2, facilitates individualized risk assessment by assigning scores to each predictor, with higher total points corresponding to an elevated probability of postoperative recurrence. Notably, a higher CONUT score and prolonged operative time were associated with significantly increased recurrence risk, each 1-point increase in CONUT score conferred a significant elevation in recurrence risk, even after adjustment for confounders, indicating that both impaired nutritional status and surgical complexity are key determinants of adverse outcomes. Elevated WBC further contributed to higher risk, underscoring the relevance of perioperative inflammatory burden. Conversely, the choice of surgical method exerted a protective effect, as reflected by a lower total score and reduced recurrence probability for optimized surgical approaches.

Predictive model performance

A clinical nomogram incorporating key clinical, surgical, and laboratory parameters was generated, facilitating individualized prediction of recurrence risk. ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.70 (95% CI: 0.63–0.77), indicating moderate discriminatory ability, is depicted in Fig. 4. The calibration plot (Fig. 5 for complete dataset) demonstrated good agreement between observed and predicted probabilities. A logistic regression model was developed to predict the risk of recurrence after surgical treatment for tuberculous empyema. The calibration plot showed good agreement between predicted and observed recurrence rates in both training and test cohorts, as indicated by Hosmer-Lemeshow test p-values of 0.55 and 0.24, respectively. However, the model displayed slight fluctuations at the lower predicted probability end and tended to underestimate recurrence risk in moderate-to-high probability groups, suggesting that calibration could be further improved. Analysis of the validation curve identified that a classification threshold between 0.3 and 0.4 provided the best balance of sensitivity and specificity, offering the highest overall predictive accuracy. This threshold range could serve as a reference for clinical risk stratification. In conclusion, the model serves as a valuable quantitative tool for assessing postoperative recurrence risk in tuberculous empyema patients, but further optimization of the threshold and recalibration may be needed to improve its predictive performance and adaptability across different patient populations. As quantitative results are displayed in Fig. 6, the Decision curve analysis(DCA) demonstrated that the recurrence risk prediction model provides a clear net clinical benefit across a wide range of high-risk thresholds and CIC suggested that clinical decision-making based on this model would provide net benefit across relevant threshold probabilities, supporting its clinical utility. As shown in the decision curve, the net benefit of the prediction model consistently exceeds both alternative strategies at clinically relevant threshold probabilities, particularly below a threshold of approximately 0.4. This indicates that applying the model for clinical decision-making can improve the balance between overtreatment and undertreatment, ultimately resulting in better patient outcomes. The standardized net benefit curve further confirms the added value of the model: over a broad range of threshold probabilities, utilizing the prediction model offers a higher standardized net benefit than indiscriminate treatment or no intervention at all. The reduction in net benefit observed at both extremes of the threshold probability is expected and consistent with clinical utility theory.

Taken together, these results support the clinical utility of the proposed prediction tool. Clinicians may use this model to guide individualized treatment and follow-up strategies for patients at risk of postoperative recurrence, thereby enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of perioperative management in tuberculous empyema.

Discussion

The present study demonstrates, for the first time in this setting, the prognostic value of the CONUT score—an integrated marker of both nutritional and immunological status—in predicting postoperative recurrence among patients with tuberculous empyema. Our results indicate that a high preoperative CONUT score is a robust, independent risk factor for early recurrence following surgical decortication, outperforming traditional nutritional indicators such as BMI or isolated albumin measurements. The significant association between the Controlling Nutritional Status (CONUT) score and postoperative outcomes highlights the critical role of nutritional assessment in managing complex respiratory infections [15].

This is biologically plausible: protein-energy malnutrition, hypoalbuminemia, lymphopenia, and hypocholesterolemia together contribute to impaired T-cell function and tissue repair, thus increasing the risk of persistent infection and poor wound healing [16]. Similar findings have been reported among TB patients with advanced disease or coinfection, where nutritional supplementation and immune reconstitution may yield clinical benefit [17]. Our study also highlights the relative shortcomings of single-parameter indices: BMI is frequently confounded by edema or sarcopenic obesity, and serum albumin may drop acutely due to surgical stress or systemic inflammation.

The protective effect of minimally invasive VATS, as evidenced here, underlines its growing role in modern thoracic practice. By reducing operative trauma, preserving chest wall integrity, and minimizing blood loss, VATS may decrease both surgical complication rates and immune/nutritional disruption, thereby translating to reduced recurrence risk. Notably, surgical factors should not be considered in isolation: the interaction between surgical stress, host reserves, and ongoing inflammation is complex and mandates an integrated perioperative management approach.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the study’s retrospective, single-center nature may introduce unmeasured confounders and limit generalizability. Second, while the sample size is robust for a rare entity, external multi-center validation will be essential to confirm the nomogram’s performance across different populations. Third, despite contemporary management, it is possible that variation in perioperative nutritional and anti-TB support exists and may affect outcomes.

Nonetheless, our findings support the routine assessment of composite nutritional and immunologic status—using tools such as the CONUT score—in all patients considered for surgery for tuberculous empyema. Early identification of high-risk patients may facilitate timely nutritional interventions (e.g., high-protein diets, micronutrient supplementation), immune support, and tailored surgical strategies to mitigate recurrence. Future prospective trials are warranted to explore whether targeted perioperative nutritional or immunologic therapies can further reduce risk.

Conclusion

In summary, the CONUT score emerges as a valuable, pragmatic, and easily accessible marker for risk stratification in patients with tuberculous empyema undergoing surgical resection. A multifactorial, individualized approach integrating host nutritional/immunological status, operative details, and perioperative care may optimize outcomes and reduce recurrence. Broader validation will inform its prospective application and facilitate the development of targeted perioperative protocols for this high-risk population.

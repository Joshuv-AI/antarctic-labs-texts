Distal Femoral Varus Osteotomy in Valgus Knee With Lateral Compartment Osteoarthritis: A Systematic Review
Authors: Hamidreza Yazdi, Ali Torkaman, Reza Sahrai, Mohammad Javad Raygani, Amir reza Makhmalbaf, Mohsen Fathi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:The precise role of distal femoral varus osteotomy (DFVO) in managing lateral compartment gonarthrosis remains incompletely defined, particularly concerning the extent of joint degeneration that supports or contraindicates the procedure at different stages of arthritis. This systematic review synthesizes recent comparative studies to evaluate the indications, functional outcomes, survivorship rates, and complication profiles associated with both closing wedge and opening wedge techniques. Given the scarcity of controlled trials and direct comparative analyses, this review aims to provide evidence-based insights into the clinical efficacy of DFVO.Methods:This systematic review evaluated studies primarily focused on DFVO for valgus knees with lateral compartment osteoarthritis, published in PubMed, Medline, Embase, or Cochrane databases since 2000. Only English-language studies reporting clinical outcomes with a minimum follow-up of 2 years in human patients were included. After duplicate removal and screening—based on title, abstract, and full-text review, following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses methodology—the methodologic quality of the included studies was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale.Results:A total of 18 studies met the inclusion criteria, with six evaluating closing wedge DFVO and 12 focusing on opening wedge DFVO. Among these studies, the highest reported conversion rate to total knee arthroplasty was 48.4%, with lateral cortex injury identified as the most common complication. Two studies reported no minor or major complications. Long-term clinical and radiologic follow-up, along with functional assessments, demonstrated substantial efficacy and acceptable safety for DFVO. The reported survival rates at 5, 10, and 15 years ranged from 74% to 88%, 74% to 90%, and 45% to 79%, respectively.Discussion:This systematic review indicates that DFVO provides acceptable long-term survival, favorable functional outcomes, and a manageable complication profile, supporting its role as a viable treatment option for valgus knees with lateral compartment osteoarthritis. Recent studies suggest that when osteotomy is done at the precise site of the deformity, both closing wedge and opening wedge techniques achieve comparable survivorship rates.Conclusion:Current evidence supports that DFVO yields favorable functional outcomes and acceptable survival rates in the management of valgus knee osteoarthritis affecting the lateral compartment. When performed at the precise site of deformity, both closing wedge and opening wedge techniques exhibit comparable survivorship and functional results. Additional randomized studies are necessary to refine surgical techniques and optimize patient selection criteria.

Osteoarthritis (OA) most commonly affects the hip and knee, with the knee being the second most frequently involved joint. Isolated lateral compartment OA is often accompanied by valgus knee deformity, which increases stress on the lateral compartment, exacerbating joint degeneration and potentially accelerating disease progression. Over time, worsening angulation may lead to joint incongruity.1

A range of treatment options exists for knee OA, including bracing treatment, pharmacologic interventions, arthroscopic débridement, osteotomy, unicompartmental knee arthroplasty, and total knee arthroplasty (TKA).2-4 Correcting mechanical alignment through osteotomy can slow or even halt disease progression, potentially delaying or preventing the need for TKA. Moreover, femoral osteotomy in cases of genu valgum optimizes joint alignment, facilitating future TKA if required.1

Although arthroplasty is highly effective, it is generally less preferred in younger patients because of concerns regarding prosthesis longevity. As a result, realignment osteotomy—a joint-preserving procedure—serves as an appealing alternative.5 Although studies have reported favorable osteotomy outcomes for varus deformities, with success rates of up to 92% at 5 years,6-8 evidence regarding its effectiveness in valgus knees with lateral compartment OA remains scarce. In light of these considerations, this systematic review aims to clarify the indications, functional outcomes, survivorship rates, and complications associated with distal femoral varus osteotomy (DFVO) in this patient population.

Methods

This study was conducted as a systematic review. Articles focusing primarily on DFVO for valgus knees with lateral compartment osteoarthritis published in PubMed, Medline, Embase, or Cochrane databases since 2000 were reviewed. Only English-language studies reporting clinical outcomes in human patients with a minimum follow-up of 2 years were included.

Review articles

Review articles

Search strategies incorporated various combinations of terms, including Medical Subject Headings (MeSH): “distal” AND (“femur” [MeSH Terms] OR femur OR femoral) AND (“osteotomy” [MeSH Terms] OR osteotomy) AND (valgus OR valgum).

After removing duplicates through EndNote, articles were screened for relevance based on title, abstract, and full-text review. The study selection process is illustrated in a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses flowchart.14 Two independent reviewers assessed eligibility, with discrepancies resolved by consensus with a third reviewer.

The methodologic quality of included studies was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale.5 Extracted data included study design, number of patients, female-to-male ratio, number of knees, mean age, mean follow-up duration, preoperative and postoperative valgus angles, treatment efficacy, complications, and knee survival rates. Microsoft Excel was used for data organization and calculations. Qualitative data are presented as percentages and frequency counts, while quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Ethical considerations, including unbiased article selection and accurate referencing, were strictly observed.

Results

The initial search identified 1082 articles. After removing 539 duplicates through EndNote, 543 records remained. Title and abstract screening excluded 419 articles. During the retrieval phase, two articles were unobtainable from the remaining 124. A full-text review of 122 articles led to the exclusion of 77 for irrelevance and 27 due to unavailable full texts. Ultimately, 18 articles1,7,15-30 were included (Figure 1).

Among these 18 studies, six investigated closing wedge DFVO, while 12 focused on opening wedge DFVO. Publication dates ranged from 2000 to 2019. Eight studies were case series,1,17-19,21,24,26,28 and 10 were retrospective analyses.7,15,16,20,22,23,25,27,29,30 Sample sizes varied from 6 to 41 patients (total: 427 patients) and 7 to 45 knees (total: 461 knees). The mean patient age ranged from 23.7 to 57 years, with follow-up durations spanning 2.2 to 15.1 years. Additional demographic and study characteristics were extracted for comparative analysis.

The highest reported TKA conversion rate was 48.4%.17 Forkel et al15 identified lateral cortex injury as a common complication in 10 patients, whereas two studies20,28 reported no minor or major complications. Across all studies, functional scores and long-term follow-ups demonstrated significant efficacy and acceptable safety for DFVO. The 5-, 10-, and 15-year survival rates ranged from 74% to 88%, 74% to 90%, and 45% to 79%, respectively (Table 1). Recent studies comparing closing wedge and opening wedge DFVO techniques have highlighted distinct technical challenges and variations in complication profiles, underscoring the importance of standardized patient selection and meticulous preoperative planning.2,3,5 In addition, emerging evidence suggests that comprehensive preoperative assessment—particularly regarding knee alignment and patient age—can markedly enhance long-term outcomes, reinforcing the need for additional randomized controlled trials with standardized criteria.4 Furthermore, findings indicate that when osteotomy is done at the precise site of the deformity, both techniques yield comparable survivorship rates. For instance, Kaplan-Meier analyses across multiple studies report an average 10-year survival rate of approximately 80%.31-35

Complications varied considerably. Total knee arthroplasty conversion rates ranged from 5% (Zarrouk et al30) to 48.4% (Kosashvili et al17). Other reported complications included loss of correction angle (Forkel et al15), lateral cortex injury (10 cases; Forkel et al15), medial hinge compression fractures (two cases; Ekeland et al22), nonunion (one case; Cameron et al24), and loss of fixation requiring revision (one case; Stahelin et al19). Additional postoperative complications included deep vein thrombosis, hematoma, knee contracture, and delayed union requiring bone grafting. One study29 reported implant-related plate irritation. Overall, postoperative complications were observed in 14% of cases (Madelaine et al23). Notably, the complication profile varied between techniques: Opening wedge DFVO frequently required bone grafting to prevent delayed union, while closing wedge DFVO carried a higher risk of lateral cortex fractures.

Functional outcomes were inconsistently reported. Most studies indicated notable improvement in functional scores, including SF-36, Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score, International Knee Documentation Committee, Oxford Knee Score, and Knee Society Scores.1,7,15-17,20-23,25-30 Patient satisfaction ranged from 73% to 86%.17,23,25,28 However, some studies noted no notable changes in specific metrics, such as Knee Society Score23 or physical activity levels.25 Long-term follow-up suggested a decline in function at 10 to 15 years postoperatively in certain cases.1,17 Collectively, the functional outcome data support DFVO's efficacy while highlighting variable long-term benefits.

Critical Appraisal of Methodologic Quality

Although the methodologic quality of the included studies was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, several limitations were identified on closer appraisal. Many studies were retrospective or case series, often characterized by small sample sizes, heterogeneous inclusion criteria, and variable follow-up durations. In addition, inconsistencies in the reporting of key outcomes—such as survivorship rates, deformity correction accuracy, and complication profiles—introduce notable heterogeneity across studies. These methodologic shortcomings affect the internal validity of the findings and warrant cautious interpretation when comparing outcomes between closing wedge and opening wedge DFVO techniques.

Discussion

This systematic review demonstrates that DFVO offers acceptable long-term survival, satisfactory functional outcomes, and a manageable complication profile, making it a viable treatment option for valgus knees with lateral compartment osteoarthritis. Recent studies (31-35) indicate that when osteotomy is done at the site of the deformity, both closing wedge and opening wedge techniques yield comparable survivorship rates, with Kaplan-Meier analyses reporting an average 10-year survival of approximately 80%.

Comparative studies of closing wedge and opening wedge DFVO techniques highlight distinct technical challenges and complication profiles, reinforcing the need for standardized patient selection and preoperative planning.2,3,5 In addition, growing evidence suggests that thorough preoperative evaluation—including knee alignment and patient age—markedly enhances long-term outcomes, advocating for additional randomized controlled trials with standardized selection criteria.4

Precision in deformity correction is crucial. The opening wedge technique allows for incremental adjustments using instruments such as laminar spreaders, whereas the success of closing wedge osteotomy is heavily dependent on the accuracy of bone resection. Despite these technical differences, both methods achieve postoperative alignment values within physiologic ranges. Moreover, comparative analyses of leg-length maintenance indicate that while closing wedge DFVO may slightly decrease femoral length and opening wedge DFVO may increase it, the overall leg length remains virtually unchanged due to the “straight-lengthening effect” associated with alignment correction.7

Nevertheless, technical differences influence complication profiles—opening wedge procedures often require bone grafting to prevent delayed union, whereas closing wedge procedures carry a higher risk of lateral cortex fracture. Additional randomized studies are essential to refine surgical techniques and optimize patient selection criteria.

Limitations in Performing Meta-analysis

Although a meta-analysis could have strengthened the statistical power and rigor of our conclusions, substantial heterogeneity in study designs, outcome measures, and reporting standards prevented a formal quantitative synthesis. Variations in surgical techniques, patient populations, and follow-up durations compromised the ability to meaningfully combine data across studies. Future research using standardized methodologies and uniform outcome reporting is critical to enabling robust meta-analyses that yield definitive, statistically significant comparisons of DFVO outcomes.

Study Limitations

This review is limited by heterogeneity in study design, follow-up duration, and outcome measures. Future studies should prioritize randomized controlled trials with standardized patient selection criteria and long-term follow-up. In addition, incorporating age-adjusted survivorship data may facilitate optimized technique selection, ensuring more precise treatment recommendations.

Summary

Current evidence indicates that DFVO yields satisfactory functional outcomes and acceptable survival rates in the management of valgus knee osteoarthritis with lateral compartment involvement. When performed at the appropriate site of deformity, both closing wedge and opening wedge techniques demonstrate comparable survivorship and functional results.

Applying Wonheug soybean-sourced meal in broiler diets improves growth performance, nutrient digestibility, carcass yield, and tibia characteristics
Authors: Jing Yang, Weihong Guo, Qingyu Zhang, Zhenyu Xu, Lei Zhang, Shengwei Wu, Shaobo Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluated the potential of Wonheug, a novel soybean cultivar, as an alternative soybean meal (SBM) source in broiler diets for improving growth performance, nutrient digestibility, carcass yield, and tibia characteristics. Wonheug soybeans were processed via hexane solvent extraction to produce SBM for experimental diets. Two SBM types, a commercial SBM and Wonheug-sourced SBM, were compared over a 42-day feeding trial. A total of 200 day-old Arbor Acre broilers (mean initial body weight, 43.90 ± 0.37 g) were randomly assigned to two groups, each with five replicates of 20 birds. Compared with commercial SBM, Wonheug-sourced SBM contained higher crude protein (P = 0.028), phosphorus (P = 0.004), and methionine (P = 0.002) contents. Broilers fed the Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet exhibited greater body weight gain (P = 0.001), improved feed efficiency (P = 0.020), enhanced digestibility of crude protein (P = 0.044), calcium (P = 0.008), and phosphorus (P = 0.002), increased breast (P = 0.004) and leg meat (P = 0.027) yields, as well as better tibia characteristics, including weight (P = 0.020), length (P = 0.033), weight-to-length ratio (P = 0.019), and medullary canal diameter (P = 0.006). Therefore, the results obtained in the present study suggest that Wonheug-sourced SBM is a high-quality and functional feed ingredient which can enhance growth performance, nutrient digestibility, carcass yield, and tibia characteristics of broilers, thereby supporting more sustainable and efficient broiler production.Keywords: Feed formula, Alternative feedstuff, Tibia development, Meat yield, Feed efficiency, Mineral digestibility, Functional feed ingredient

Introduction

Soybeans, rich in high-quality protein and essential amino acids, have long been cultivated for oil extraction (Guo et al., 2022; Nasir et al., 2023). Oil extraction from soybeans generates soybean meal (SBM) as a by-product, which has become the most widely used plant-based protein source in poultry nutrition (J. A. Cristobal et al., 2025). The balanced amino acid profile, high digestibility, and consistent nutrient content of SBM make SBM a reliable component of broiler feed formulations (Aguirre et al., 2022; Husnain et al., 2025). Increasing demand for high-quality feed and fluctuating market prices of SBM have driven interest in identifying alternative SBM sources capable of improving performance and reducing production costs in the broiler industry (Aini et al., 2023; Munawar et al., 2025).

Recent research indicates that dietary SBM quality can substantially influence poultry performance (Cristobal et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2020). Supporting these findings, Hemetsberger et al. (2021, 2022) reported that broiler growth performance, carcass traits, and nutrient digestibility were affected by dietary SBM quality, particularly the protein content, amino acid composition, presence of anti-nutritional factors, and physical and chemical properties of the SBM. Consequently, applying SBM sourced from different soybean cultivars may result in distinct outcomes in broiler performance.

Wonheug is a small-seeded soybean variety characterized by a black seed coat and high protein content (Fig. 1; Dhungana et al., 2021). Wonheug soybeans also contain bioactive compounds, including anthocyanins and isoflavones, which may provide additional health benefits (Li et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2025). Despite the nutritional potential of Wonheug soybeans, research on the application of Wonheug-sourced SBM in poultry feed remains limited. Increasing emphasis on feed innovation highlights the potential of Wonheug-sourced SBM to improve broiler performance and possibly provide functional benefits. The objective of this study was to evaluate Wonheug-sourced SBM as an alternative SBM source in broiler diets for improving growth performance, nutrient digestibility, carcass yield, and tibia characteristics. The hypothesis of this study proposed that broilers receiving diet formulated with Wonheug-sourced SBM will exhibit improved growth performance, nutrient digestibility, carcass yield, and tibia characteristics. Findings from this study are expected to provide valuable information for optimizing broiler feed formulations and expanding the use of diverse soybean cultivars in poultry nutrition.

Materials and methods

Broiler feeding experimental design

Wonheug soybean was cultivated in Anseong (Gyeonggido, Korea) and processed at a local soybean oil processing facility using the solvent extraction method to produce SBM for this study (Supplementary material 1, 2; Gasparetto et al., 2022). A total of 200 day-old Arbor Acre broilers (mean initial body weight, 43.90 ± 0.37 g) were randomly assigned to two groups and provided diets formulated with either commercial SBM (Table 1) or Wonheug-sourced SBM (Table 2). Diet formulations followed the recommendations of the National Research Council and were optimized according to the Aviagen nutrition guide for Arbor Acre broilers (Aviagen, 2014). Each group contained five replicate cages, with 20 birds per replicate. The experimental period lasted 42 days. Broilers were housed in three-tier battery cages under commercial production conditions, with ad libitum access to feed and water (Dang et al., 2022). All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Dankook University (Cheonan, Korea) and were conducted in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Agricultural Animals in Research and Teaching.

Soybeans, SBM, and feed nutritional content evaluation

Representative commercial soybean (Daehan feed Co., Ltd., Incheon, Korea), Wonheug soybean, commercial SBM (Daehan feed Co., Ltd., Incheon, Korea), Wonheug-sourced SBM, and experimental feeds formulated with each type of SBM were divided into six replicates for nutritional composition analysis. Samples were dried in an oven at 70 °C for 72 h, ground to pass through a 1-mm sieve, and collected. Powdered samples were analyzed for crude protein (nitrogen × 6.25; method 968.06), calcium (method 984.01), phosphorus (method 965.17), and crude fat (method 954.02) following the procedures of the Association of Official Analytical Chemists (AOAC, 2000). Amino acid contents, including lysine, methionine, cysteine, threonine, and tryptophan, were determined using an amino acid analyzer (Beckman 6300; Beckman Coulter, Inc., Fullerton, CA). The energy content of feed samples was measured by combustion heat determination using a bomb calorimeter (Parr 6100; Parr Instrument Co., Moline, IL, USA).

Growth performance analysis

The body weight of broilers was recorded on days 1, 21, and 42 to calculate body weight gain (BWG). Cage-based feed intake was measured weekly, and feed efficiency was calculated as the ratio of BWG to feed intake.

Apparent total tract digestibility analysis

On day 35, chromium oxide (2 g/kg) was added to the diet as an inert marker to determine the apparent total tract digestibility of dry matter, energy, crude protein, calcium, and phosphorus, following the method described by Dang et al. (2022). Representative feed samples were collected after thorough mixing and stored at −20 °C. From days 33 to 35, excreta samples were collected in duplicate from each replicate cage and stored at −20 °C until analysis. On day 42, feed and excreta samples were thawed, dried at 60 °C for 72 h, finely ground to pass through a 1-mm sieve, and collected for analysis. According to the procedures of the Association of Official Analytical Chemists (AOAC, 2000), diet and excreta samples were analyzed for dry matter (method 930.15), crude protein (nitrogen × 6.25; method 968.06), calcium (method 984.01), and phosphorus (method 965.17). Gross energy content was determined using a bomb calorimeter (Parr 6100; Parr Instrument Co., Moline, IL, USA). Hematology parameters analysis

On day 42, fifteen birds per group (3 birds per replicate cage) were randomly selected for blood sampling. Blood was collected from the wing vein and centrifuged at 3000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C to obtain serum. Serum concentrations of total protein and albumin were determined using colorimetric methods and globulin levels were calculated as the difference between total protein and albumin values. Additional biochemical analyses, including cholesterol, triglycerides, calcium, and phosphorus, were performed using a fully automated biochemical analyzer.

Tibia characteristics analysis

The left and right tibiae of each bird were excised and immersed in boiling water (100 °C) for 10 min, then cooled to room temperature. Drumsticks were manually defleshed, and the patella was removed. Bones were air-dried at room temperature for 24 h before measurement. Tibia length and weight were recorded. After breaking, the medial and lateral walls thicknesses at the midpoint were measured using a dial caliper. Medullary canal diameter was calculated by subtracting the sum of medial and lateral wall thicknesses from the diaphyseal diameter. The tibia weight-to-length ratio was obtained by dividing tibia weight by tibia length. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the independent-samples t-test in SAS software (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Normality was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test and QQ plots. The replicate cage served as the experimental unit. Results are presented as means ± standard deviation, and differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results and discussion

SBM, a by-product of soybean oil extraction, is a major protein source in poultry diets (J. A. Cristobal et al., 2025). The nutritional value of SBM, particularly protein content, largely depends on the composition of the original soybean feedstock (Pope et al., 2023). In general, soybeans with higher protein content produce SBM with correspondingly higher protein levels (Aguirre et al., 2022). This relationship was confirmed by Karr-Lilienthal et al. (2004), who reported that low-protein soybeans yield SBM with reduced protein concentrations. In the present study, the Wonheug soybean cultivar contained significantly higher levels of crude protein (P = 0.012), phosphorus (P < 0.001), and methionine (P = 0.003) compared with commercial soybean varieties (Table 3). Furthermore, SBM processed from Wonheug retained higher concentrations of crude protein (P = 0.028), phosphorus (P = 0.004), and methionine (P = 0.002) than SBM sourced from conventional soybeans (Table 3). This nutrient advantage observed in Wonheug-derived SBM was reflected in the feed formulations (Table 1, 2), underscoring the superior nutritional profile of diets incorporating Wonheug-sourced SBM. Elevated methionine concentration is of particular importance, as methionine is recognized as the first limiting amino acid in poultry diets (J. A. Conde-Aguilera et al., 2025). Methionine plays a central role in protein synthesis, immune regulation, and feather development, making methionine indispensable for optimal broiler growth and health (Dang et al., 2022). Incorporating of Wonheug-sourced SBM into broiler diets is therefore expected to promote enhanced growth and production performance.

As anticipated, inclusion of Wonheug-sourced SBM in broiler diets resulted in marked improvements in growth performance, particularly BWG and feed efficiency. Broilers receiving diets containing Wonheug-sourced SBM exhibited significantly greater BWG during both the grower phase (days 22–42; P = 0.002) and the overall rearing period (days 1–42; P = 0.001) compared with broilers receiving diets containing commercial SBM (Table 4). Feed efficiency was also significantly higher in broilers fed Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet during days 22–42 (P = 0.030) and across the entire feeding period (P = 0.020) relative to broilers fed commercial SBM-based diet, indicating more effective conversion of feed into body mass (Table 4). Reported outcomes align with previous studies. Galkin et al. (2020) observed reduced live weight in broilers consuming diet formulated with SBM characterized by low crude protein content, whereas Janocha et al. (2022) demonstrated superior weight gain in broilers fed a high-protein SBM-based diet, underscoring the pivotal role of SBM quality in supporting broiler growth. In poultry nutrition, BWG is strongly influenced by the efficiency of nutrient absorption and metabolism (Hafeez et al., 2025). Feed efficiency, the standard measure of nutrient absorption and metabolism process, represents a crucial determinant of both economic and biological performance in broiler production. Because feed constitutes the largest expense in poultry production, even modest improvements in feed efficiency can substantially reduce production costs (Alhotan, 2021). Enhanced feed efficiency also decreases crop input requirements and reduces excreta output, thereby lowering nutrient waste and mitigating the environmental impact of intensive poultry farming (Zampiga et al., 2021). Superior BWG and feed efficiency observed in broilers consuming Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet appear to be primarily associated with elevated crude protein content. Furthermore, a further distinguishing feature of Wonheug-sourced SBM is higher methionine content. Methionine, the first limiting amino acid in conventional corn-soy diets, is indispensable for protein biosynthesis, immune system development, and methyl group metabolism in broilers (J. A. Conde-Aguilera et al., 2025; Dang et al., 2022). Although direct evaluation of methionine-rich SBM on broiler performance has not yet been conducted, dietary methionine supplementation has been shown to improve BWG, feed intake, and carcass yield (Chen et al., 2013). High crude protein concentration combined with elevated methionine levels in Wonheug-sourced SBM therefore likely underpins the improvements in BWG and feed efficiency observed in the present study, reinforcing the potential of Wonheug-sourced SBM as a superior protein source in broiler nutrition.

The relative weight of breast and leg muscles is a critical economic trait in broiler production, directly affecting market value and consumer preference (Santoso et al., 2015). Breast and leg muscles are highly valued in poultry markets due to their high protein content and favorable texture, and muscle development is strongly influenced by dietary nutrient composition (Hailemariam et al., 2022). A key determinant of meat yield is the bird’s capacity to digest and utilize dietary protein. Crude protein digestibility provides a reliable measure of this process, reflecting how effectively ingested protein is hydrolyzed and absorbed. In the present study, broilers fed diets containing Wonheug-sourced SBM exhibited significantly greater crude protein digestibility (P = 0.044; Table 5). Higher digestibility likely facilitated more efficient amino acid absorption, supporting protein synthesis and muscle accretion. Improved protein digestibility was accompanied by elevated serum albumin (P = 0.042) and total protein levels (P = 0.032) (Table 8), biomarkers widely recognized as indicators of protein metabolism and nutritional status (Niu et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2024). Increases in serum total protein and albumin parameters suggest enhanced systemic protein availability in broilers consuming Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet. Fortuoso et al. (2019) similarly reported that greater protein absorption was positively associated with increased breast muscle deposition, reinforcing the link between nutrient bioavailability and meat yield. Carcass evaluation in the current study further demonstrated significantly greater relative breast muscle weight (P = 0.004) and leg muscle weight (P = 0.027) in broilers receiving Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet (Table 6). The nutrient-dense composition of Wonheug-sourced SBM promoted superior muscle development. Elevated methionine concentration, a key amino acid for muscle protein synthesis, likely contributed to enhanced tissue accretion in breast and leg regions that demand high levels of dietary protein (Dang et al., 2022). Overall, Wonheug-sourced SBM improved protein digestibility, increased systemic protein status, and enhanced economically valuable carcass traits, demonstrating strong potential as a high-quality protein source for optimizing broiler meat production and profitability.

Tibia health is a critical determinant of broiler welfare and productivity. The tibia functions as a primary weight-bearing bone, providing structural support necessary for locomotion, standing, and perching behaviors. Strong and well-developed tibiae reduce susceptibility to skeletal disorders, physical injuries, and secondary infections, thereby supporting both animal welfare and production efficiency (Kwon et al., 2024; Takase et al., 2020). Bone quality can be evaluated through anatomical and physiological markers, among which the bone weight-to-length ratio serves as a widely recognized surrogate of bone density, with higher ratios reflecting superior mineral deposition and structural robustness (Sanchez-Rodriguez et al., 2019; Seedor et al., 1991). The diameter of the medullary canal also provides valuable insight into bone maturity and mechanical strength, with larger diameters indicative of healthy bone formation and remodeling processes (Guglielmi et al., 2010). Adequate dietary intake and efficient absorption of calcium and phosphorus are fundamental to skeletal development, as both minerals are essential for bone mineralization and systemic homeostasis (Couce & Saenz de Pipaon, 2021). In the present study, broilers fed diets containing Wonheug-sourced SBM exhibited significantly improved digestibility of calcium (P = 0.008) and phosphorus (P = 0.002) (Table 5), likely attributable to the higher phosphorus content and overall nutrient density of Wonheug-sourced SBM. Hematological analyses further confirmed elevated serum calcium (P = 0.001) and phosphorus (P = 0.019) concentrations in broilers receiving the Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet (Table 8). Enhanced mineral digestibility and increased circulating mineral levels provide critical substrates for hydroxyapatite synthesis, the primary mineral complex responsible for bone rigidity (Dallmann et al., 2023; Rizzoli et al., 2021). Consistent with the improvements in circulating mineral levels, tibial morphology assessments demonstrated significant improvements in tibia weight (P = 0.020), tibia length (P = 0.033), tibia weight-to-length ratio (P = 0.019), and medullary canal diameter (P = 0.006) (Table 7) in broilers receiving Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet compared to broilers receiving commercial SBM-based diet. Similar outcomes have been reported in studies where inadequate calcium and phosphorus intake impaired tibia characteristics (Olgun et al., 2024), while optimized dietary calcium and phosphorus utilization enhanced skeletal properties (Osunbami & Adeola, 2024). Collectively, the improvements in tibial metrics observed in broilers receiving Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet indicate greater bone mass, superior mineralization, and enhanced structural maturity. Such skeletal benefits can be attributed to the favorable phosphorus profiles of Wonheug-sourced SBM, underscoring the potential of nutritionally enriched soybean cultivars to strengthen bone development, improve broiler mobility, and enhance resilience in production environments.

Conclusions and applications

The Wonheug soybean cultivar produced SBM with higher crude protein, phosphorus, and methionine content than commercial soybean varieties. Broiler diets formulated with Wonheug-sourced SBM demonstrated an improved nutritional profile, particularly in protein, amino acid, and mineral contents. Feeding trials showed significantly greater BWG and feed efficiency in broilers receiving Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet throughout the experimental period. Nutrient digestibility, especially for crude protein, calcium, and phosphorus, was enhanced in broilers receiving Wonheug-sourced SBM-based diet. Improved crude protein digestibility reflected in elevated serum albumin and total protein levels, contributing to greater breast and leg meat yields. Tibia characteristics, including weight, length, and medullary canal diameter, were positively influenced by diets containing Wonheug-sourced SBM. Incorporation of Wonheug-sourced SBM into broiler feed represents a practical nutritional approach to improve growth performance, nutrient digestibility, carcass traits, and bone quality.

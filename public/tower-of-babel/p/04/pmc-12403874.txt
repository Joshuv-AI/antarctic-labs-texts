Immunological balance skewing Th1,Th17 towards Th2,Treg in OSCC of hamster induced by 7,12- dimethylbenz[a]anthracene
Authors: Qi Shao, Hongyuan Li, Yuanye Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIt has been reported that CD4 + helper T cells, such as Th1, Th2, Th17 and Treg, play crucial roles in the immunological balance especially when the immune system is invaded by the tumor. Oral squamous cell carcinoma undergoes a process from normal epithelium to dysplasia. However, the dynamic equilibrium of Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg remained unclear during the process of epithelial malignant transformation. The present study was performed to describe the changes of Th1, Th2, Th17, Treg, Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg in golden hamsters induced Oral Potentially malignant Disorders (OPMDs) and oral squamous cell carcinoma.MethodsUsing the carcinogenic agent 7,12-dimethylbenz [a]anthracene to smear the Syrian golden hamster cheek pouch mucosa every 3 days, OPMDs and oral squamous cell carcinoma models had been induced. Samples were obtained surgically from the hamster, grind into single cell suspension, and tested by flow cytometry.ResultWith the increasing of Th2 and Treg has been the decreasing of Th1 and Th17 from normal epithelium to OPMDs and oral squamous cell carcinoma. Th1/Th2 ratio and Th17/Treg ratio were changing in the different lesion.ConclusionOur findings suggested that the shift of Th1/Th2 balance to Th2 might involve in the occurrence and development of normal mucosal carcinogenesis, while the shift of Th17/Treg balance to Treg might involve in the occurrence of normal mucosal cancerization.Keywords: OSCC, OPMDs, Helper t cell, Immunological balance

Introduction

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is a highly malignant disease that threatens global human health. There are more than 300,000 new OSCC patients in the world each year, accounting for 2.1% of the world’s tumors [1]. OSCC generally develops from normal oral mucosa to dysplasia before finally developing into OSCC. The immune microenvironment played a crucial role in maintaining the homeostasis of oral mucosal epithelial cells. A dysregulated microenvironment could promote normal oral mucosa to dysplasia, and this dysregulated microenvironment would make OPMDs more easily converted to OSCC [2]. Recently, studies had shown that in patients with OSCC, the ratio of Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg decreases, and tumor immunity was inhibited [3]. However, there is no relevant study on the dynamic changes of Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg in the continuous process of dysplasia like the progress from normal oral mucosa to OPMDs or to OSCC [4]. The Syrian Golden Hamster Buccal Pouch Cancer (SHBPC) model is one of the most classic oral squamous cell carcinoma models. Dimethylbenzanthracene (DMBA)-induced Syrian golden hamster cheek pouch carcinoma shows broad similarity with human oral squamous cell carcinoma and could be used to study the morphology, histology, and OPMDs of OSCC, as well as changes in biochemistry, molecular targets, gene expression, and epigenetics [5]. The Syrian golden hamster has a keratinized squamous epithelium resembling to human oral cavity and gingiva in the cheek pouch, which also could be continuously induced from normal mucosa, mucosal ulcer, atypical hyperplasia, and squamous cell carcinoma. SHBPC model has high reproducibility and stable canceration rate, and is suitable for studying the pathology and intervention of buccal oral cancer tissues [6, 7]. Therefore, this study used flow cytometry to study the SHBPC model changes of ratio of Th1/Th2, Th17/Treg from normal mucosa to OPMDs and OPMDs to oral cancer.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals

40 healthy male specific pathogen free (SPF, Specific Pathogen Free) grade Syrian golden hamster, about 6 to 8 weeks old, weighing 80 ~ 100 g, provided by the Department of Zoology, Jilin University (production license number: SCXK (Beijing) 2016-0011). During the whole animal experiment modeling process, all animals were kept in the independent ventilation cage of the Zoological Department of the Department of Zoology, Jilin University. The experiment was approved by the Ethics Committee of Baiqiuen School of Stomatology, Jilin University. The whole operation process was strictly based on the rules of Department of Experimental Animals of Jilin University.

Reagents

DMBA was purchased from Sigma, USA; Percp-cy5.5 anti-mouse IFN-gamma, Alexa fluor 647 anti-mouse IL-17a, Alexa fluor 647 anti-mouse CD4, PE anti-mouse CD25, PE anti-mouse IL-4, Alexa fluor 647 anti-mouse Foxp3 antibody were purchased from Biolegend, USA; cell membrane disrupting agent, nuclear membrane disrupting agent, interleukin stimulant were purchased from BD, USA; acetone was purchased from Guangdong Chemical Reagent Factory, China; RPMI 1640 was purchased from Shanghai Haoran Biotechnology, China.

Grouping/modeling and histological examination

A total of 40 Syrian golden hamsters were randomly assigned to one of four experimental groups: blank control group, negative control group, OPMDs group (characterized by high-grade dysplasia), and oral cancer group, with each group containing 10 animals. Each animal was raised independently and the cages were randomly placed. For the OPMDs and oral cancer groups, 1 ml of 0.5% DMBA acetone solution was applied to the left cheek pouch using an oil brush for a duration of 5 s. The negative control group received 1 ml of pure acetone applied in the same manner. Animals in the blank control group received no treatment. Left and right sides cheek pouches checked by visual observation every 1 weeks over a period of 5 months and biopsy were examined every 4 weeks over a period of 5 months. At each observation time point, 2 animals from each group were humanely sacrificed following pentobarbital anesthesia. Cheek pouch biopsy specimens were collected and processed for histopathological analysis. Paraffin-embedded sections were deparaffinized using xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. Sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Two senior pathologists conducted independent light microscopic examinations using an Olympus DP72 microscope (Olympus Microsystems, Tokyo, Japan). They were not clear about the groupings. Pathological diagnoses were categorized according to established oral histopathology criteria into four classifications: normal mucosa, OPMDs, carcinoma in situ, and invasive squamous cell carcinoma [8, 9].

Preparation of lymphocyte suspension

Flow cytometry

After intraperitoneal injection pentobarbital anesthesia then the specimen extraction was operated, the mucosa was separated and immediately rinsed with PBS solution, placed in a Petri dish and milled with a 40 μm filter to prepare a single cell suspension, respectively labeled Th and Tr tubes. The Th tubes were firstly added with interleukin stimulant for 4 h in RPMI 1640 for 30 min, and then incubated with CD4, IFN-gamma, IL-4, IL-17a antibody for 30 min, and centrifuged for 100 µL. The Tr tubes were first added to CD4 and CD25 antibodies reagent, and the nuclear membrane was broken for 50 min. Then, the Foxp3 antibody was added to incubate for 30 min. After centrifugation, 100 µL was left on the machine for detection. The initial data was analyzed and processed by FlowJo_V10 software.

Statistical analyses

The data obtained were statistically processed using SPSS 18.0 software. Data of each group were expressed as mean ± standard error (mean ± SEM). Paired t-test was used to compare the data between the two groups. The test level was α = 0.05, P < 0.05 was statistically significant; the statistical chart was drawn by GraphPad Prism 6.

Results

Syrian hamster buccal mucosa gross changes

The Syrian golden hamster in the blank and negative control group, the intraoral mucosa did not change significantly during the whole experiment. The submucosal blood vessels were clear and the light transmission was good. There was no significant difference between the contralateral and adjacent tissues Fig. 1(A) and Fig. 2(A). In the OPMDs group and the oral cancer group, after one week of smearing DMBA, by visual observation we found the hamster’s buccal mucosa showed congestion and edema. Two weeks after smearing DMBA, the swelling of the buccal mucosa of the hamster gradually subsided, and some epithelial erosion and pus appeared. The buccal mucosa of the hamster showed a little thickening, the blood vessels were not obvious. After six weeks of application of DMBA, the buccal mucosa was significantly thickened, showing white patches that could not be erased, like homogeneous white spots; after eight weeks of DMBA application, the surface of the buccal mucosa was rough and granules or nodules appeared Fig. 3(A). Twelve weeks after the application of DMBA, more than 90% of the hamster mucosa showed papillary neoplasms; after 16 weeks of DMBA application, exogenous tumors appeared on all hamster mucosa, and most of the surface was ulcerated Fig. 4(A).

Syrian hamster buccal mucosa histological changes

The mucosa of the blank control group is composed of several layers of keratinized stratified epithelial cells with obvious keratinized layer, the boundary between the basement membrane and the submucosa is clear, and there is no obvious epithelial spike Fig. 1(B). The negative control group was generally the same as the blank group, while some of the epithelial keratin rupture or shedding was observed Fig. 2(B). DMBA induced experimental group, the keratinized layer was not obvious, the epithelial surface was mostly 3–4 granular layer cells, no epithelial nailing was observed, the basal layer was densely stained, and the acanthosis was unclear. The ninth weekend biopsy: the stratum corneum is separated from the granule cell layer, the granular layer and the spinous cell layer are thickened, the epithelial surface was mostly 5–6 granular layer cells and 8–9 spinous cell layer. The nucleus is stained lightly, the basal layer is densely stained, and the interspinous layer is unclear. The basement membrane and the submucosa are clearly defined as atypical hyperplasia Fig. 3(B). At the end of the sixteenth week, the epithelial level was disordered, and the epithelium and submucosa could not be distinguished. A large number of cancerous nests were seen in the tissue, and the cancer cells infiltrated the blood vessels, showing invasive squamous cell carcinoma Fig. 4(B).

Alteration of CD4 + helper T cells immune balance

In the process from normal mucosa to OPMDs to oral cancer, Th1CD4+IFN−γ+, decreased with the progress of the disease (P < 0.01) Fig. 5(A). With the progressive increase of epithelial malignancy, Th1 cells continued to decrease, and this decrease mainly occurred from the normal mucosa to the OPMDs. In the process from OPMDs to oral cancer, this reduction is not obvious. Meanwhile, Th2CD4+IL−4+ increased with the progression of normal mucosa to OSCC, Th2 type immunity is significantly improved, especially the increase of Th2 cell mainly occurred in the process from OPMDs to oral cancer (P < 0.05) Fig. 5(B), although the increase during normal mucosa to OPMDs was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). As the disease progresses, the ratio of Th1/Th2 became smaller, the immunological balance skewing Th1 towards Th2 (P < 0.05) Fig. 5(C). Th17CD4+IL−17+ decreased with the progression of the disease, especially the decrease of Th17 cell mainly occurred in the process from normal mucosa to OPMDs (P < 0.01) Fig. 5(D). Meanwhile, TregCD4+CD25+FoxP3+ increased with the progression of normal mucosa to OSCC, Treg type immunity is significantly improved (P < 0.05) Fig. 5(E). As the disease progresses, the ratio of Th17/Treg generally became smaller, the immunological balance skewing Th17 towards Treg (P < 0.05) Fig. 5(F). Intriguingly, although in the comparison of blank control group and oral cancer group the ratio of Th17/Treg decreased, there was an increase during OPMDs towards oral cancer (P < 0.05).

Discussion

The process of OSCC had gone from normal mucosa to high dysplasia and eventually to oral cancer. The accumulation of lesions from normal mucosal epithelium to OSCC comes from chromosome abnormalities, DNA mutations, and epigenetic changes such as DNA methylation [10]. These abnormalities are mainly caused by exposure to risk factors such as tobacco, alcohol, betel nut and human papillomavirus [11–13]. The DMBA was used to construct a Syrian golden hamster cheek pouch cancer model, which could replicate human oral mucosa from normal mucosa to OPMDs, and finally developed into a pathological process of oral cancer, thus allowing researchers to study the dynamic changes in the process of malignant transformation in different stages. After visual observation and pathological biopsy, the OPMDs model and the oral cancer model were successfully established when the DMBA was applied for eight weeks and sixteen weeks.

Immune microenvironment plays a crucial role in maintaining the homeostasis of oral mucosal epithelial cells [14]. In the 1970 s, Noone et al. first described the prognosis of OSCC and its relationship with T lymphocytes in the tumor microenvironment [15]. Since then, the concept of microenvironment had been introduced into the process of transformation of OPMDs into OSCC. OPMDs mainly include oral leukoplakia, oral lichen planus, and oral submucous fibrosis [11, 16]. From the normal oral mucosa to the epithelial atypical hyperplasia to the carcinoma, the process involved the increasing heterogeneity of the gene leads to the process of mutation of the proto-oncogene or tumor suppressor gene. At the same time, with the activation of the oncogene-signaling pathway, the microenvironment will change accordingly [17, 18]. A dysregulated microenvironment can promote normal oral mucosa to produce OPMDs, while patients with OPMDs are more likely to be converted to OSCC, especially if they addicted to some carcinogenic things (e.g. tobacco, alcohol, betel nut, etc.). The oral mucosa will gradually develop into OPMDs, and the number of lymphocytes in the microenvironment will change [19, 20]. The body’s immune system recognized tumors and blocked tumor growth through immune surveillance mechanisms. The antigen presenting cells, after recognizing the xenogeneic antigen in vivo, presented it to the original T cells (Th0CD4+). Th0 then differentiated into kinds of different types of effector T cells. Among them, Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg play an immune defense role in the development of tumors [21]. Th1 is primarily responsible for the immune defense and response mechanisms of intracellular pathogens. Th1-mediated immune response is considered an important link in cell-mediated killing immunity. Th2 is primarily responsible for the immune defense and immune response of extracellular pathogens by activating an independent extracellular pathogen phagocytic mechanism [22]. Inhibition of Th1 and Th2 or dysfunction may result in the destruction of anti-tumor immunity, leading to tumor progression. Studies had shown that the lower the ratio of Th1/Th2, the negative the tumor’s size, invasive ability, metastatic ability, and prognosis when drifting in the Th2 direction. In tumors with a higher ratio of Th1, patients have longer survival time and the probability of tumor recurrence is greatly reduced [23]. In present study, the shift of Th1/Th2 balance to Th2 was mainly found at the OPMDs and OSCC, which might suggest that the shift might involve in the occurrence and development of normal mucosal carcinogenesis. The developmental pathways of Th17 and Treg had much in common with each other, and the two were closely related. There was an immune balance between the two, and the breaking or tilting of the balance will result in different immune response results [24]. Th17 played an important role in clearing pathogens of cells such as Candida albicans and Klebsiella, but under certain immune imbalances, Th17 was involved in many autoimmune diseases and inflammatory reactions. Treg was mainly involved in immunosuppressive action, it secreted cytokines with immunosuppressive function, and the reduction of Treg could enhance the body’s immune response to tumors [25, 26]. In present study, the shift of Th17/Treg balance to Treg was mainly found at the OPMDs, which might suggest that the shift might involve in the occurrence of normal mucosal cancerization.

In conclusion, immunoregulation is an important factor in maintaining the homeostasis of the body. Our study found that oral cancers and OPMDs were closely related to the immune regulation disorder of the body. At different stages of the disease, the two balances of Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg showed varying degrees of immune imbalance. Thus, in the future, immune drugs might reverse the two balances to delay the progression of OPMDs and cancerous changes.

Role of Computed Tomography Angiography Enhancement Ratio in Predicting Prognosis in Patients With Stroke Undergoing Endovascular Treatment
Authors: Anyu Liao, Lijun Huang, Kangmo Huang, Liangyuan Pan, Feiluola Kasaer, Fang Wang, Changsheng Zhou, Yangyang Jiang, Zhiruo Song, Xijing Zhu, Wenya Lan, Lulu Xiao, Xiaoqing Cheng, Wusheng Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe computed tomography angiography enhancement ratio (CTA‐ER), calculated through the net water uptake formula, is a valuable predictive tool for detecting adverse events after stroke. However, limited research has investigated the correlation between CTA‐ER and the prognosis of patients receiving endovascular treatment. Therefore, we aimed to examine whether CTA‐ER could predict favorable outcomes in patients undergoing endovascular treatment.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study enrolling patients with acute anterior circulation large‐vessel occlusive stroke undergoing endovascular treatment between January 2016 and January 2023. All patients received noncontrast computed tomography and computed tomography angiography scans upon admission. Multivariate logistic regression analysis and receiver operator characteristic curve were used to assess the predictive efficacy in determining favorable outcomes. The favorable outcome at 90 days was a modified Rankin scale score of 0 to 2.ResultsA total of 206 participants (median age, 68 [60–77] years; 77 women) were included in the study, with 82 (39.8%) individuals demonstrating favorable outcomes. Patients with favorable outcomes had lower noncontrast computed tomography net water uptake (6.42% versus 7.76%; P < 0.001) and CTA‐ER (8.31% versus 10.56%; P < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified that CTA‐ER was a significant predictor for favorable outcomes (P<0.05 in all models). Furthermore, the receiver operator characteristic analysis revealed that CTA‐ER exhibited superior predictive performance, surpassing noncontrast computed tomography net water uptake (area under the curve, 0.707 versus 0.636, Delong test; P = 0.046).ConclusionCTA‐ER is a significant predictor of favorable outcomes in patients with acute large‐vessel occlusion stroke undergoing endovascular treatment.Keywords: computed tomography angiography, ischemic stroke, net water uptake, prognosis, thrombectomy

In recent years, several randomized controlled studies have provided evidence that endovascular treatment (EVT) has emerged as the primary therapeutic approach for patients with acute large‐vessel occlusion stroke within the 6‐hour time window. 1 , 2 The DAWN (DWI or CTP Assessment With Clinical Mismatch in the Triage of Wake Up and Late Presenting Strokes Undergoing Neurointervention With Trevo) and DEFUSE3 (Endovascular Therapy Following Imaging Evaluation for Ischemic Stroke) trials have effectively expanded the allowable time window for EVT implementation to 24 hours. 3 , 4 , 5 Nevertheless, despite the early adoption of EVT, a significant proportion of treated patients continue to face unfavorable outcomes. 6 , 7 Therefore, effective early warning indicators are imperative for patients undergoing EVT.

Neuroimaging plays a pivotal role in the early stratification of patients with acute ischemic stroke, thereby effectively enhancing the favorable prognosis rate among individuals undergoing EVT. 5 , 8 Net water uptake (NWU) is an innovative method based on noncontrast computed tomography (NCCT) density alterations, which is employed to quantify water absorption in ischemic tissue and quantitatively evaluate the extent of brain edema and tissue injury. 9 , 10 NWU can effectively predict adverse events following stroke, such as malignant infarction and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. 11 , 12 However, identifying ischemic changes in computed tomography (CT) density is challenging during the initial acute phase after a stroke. The computed tomography angiography enhancement ratio (CTA‐ER) is a quantitative neuroimaging index calculated using the NWU formula based on the attenuation changes of CTA source images, 13 and it plays a pivotal role in the early stages following a stroke. Previous studies have demonstrated that CTA‐ER outperforms NWU based on NCCT in predicting malignant cerebral edema and hemorrhagic transformation. 12 , 14 Moreover, the correlation between CTA‐ER and prognosis has received limited attention in previous research. Therefore, our objective is to examine the potential of CTA‐ER as a neuroimaging biomarker for predicting favorable outcomes in individuals diagnosed with acute ischemic stroke undergoing EVT.

Methods

The data are accessible from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Population

Patients with acute ischemic stroke undergoing EVT were retrospectively investigated at Nanjing Jinling Hospital from January 2016 to January 2023. Patients were screened according to the following predetermined inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥18 years; (2) large‐vessel occlusion in the anterior circulation confirmed by CTA, magnetic resonance angiography, or digital subtraction angiography; (3) EVT within 24 hours of stroke onset; and (4) underwent CT and CTA scans before EVT. The exclusion criteria were (1) missed clinical and imaging data; (2) software segmentation errors due to old cerebral infarction, motion artifacts, and poor image quality; (3) prestroke modified Rankin scale score >2; and (4) lost to follow‐up.

Baseline Characteristics

The patient characteristics were collected from medical records, including age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, stroke or transient ischemia attack history, coronary heart disease, atrial fibrillation, smoking history, and drinking status. Baseline clinical data, such as baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, glucose, and intravenous thrombolysis, were obtained. Time information encompasses onset to puncture time, time from puncture to reperfusion, or the end of the procedure without recanalization. The occlusion sites were confirmed by digital subtraction angiography, encompassing the internal carotid artery M1 segment, and proximal M2 segment of the middle cerebral artery. The grading of collateral status was conducted using the American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology scale. 15 Collateral circulation status was divided into poor, moderate, and good, defined as a American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology score of 0 to 1, 2, and 3 to 4, respectively. The reperfusion status was categorized on the based of the modified Treatment in Cerebral Infarction scale. 16 Successful recanalization was defined as a modified Treatment in Cerebral Infarction score of 2b or 3.

The favorable outcome was defined as modified Rankin scale score of 0 to 2 at 90 days, assessed by a proficient neurology physician via telephone or during an outpatient follow‐up. According to the prognosis, we divided the patients into a favorable outcome group (modified Rankin scale score, 0 to 2) and an unfavorable outcome group (modified Rankin scale score, 3–6).

Image Acquisition and Protocols

Patients underwent NCCT and a multiphase CTA on dual‐source CT scanners at admission (Somatom Definition, Siemens Healthineers, Forchheim, Germany). The NCCT scan ranged from the skull base to the cranium, with the following parameters: 120 kV, 250 mA, and 5.0 mm layer thickness. CTA images were obtained after 70 mL single bolus contrast material in combination with physiological saline 40 mL injected intravenously at a rate of 4.0 mL/s using a double‐tube high‐pressure syringe. The parameters of CTA were 120 kV and 280 mA with 0.5 mm slice thickness.

Image Analysis

The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) was acquired through an automated software–based analysis (syngo. via Research Frontier, ASPECT Score‐Tool version 2.0.3; Siemens Healthineers, Forchheim, Germany). The software uses a brain atlas with probabilistic maps of the ASPECTS regions to perform region segmentation and automate atlas fitting. 17 , 18 The average Hounsfield unit (HU) value was then calculated for each area, including only voxels that might belong to soft tissue in this process. HU values that are too high (eg, bone, calcifications) or too low (eg, cerebrospinal fluid, ventricle) are not considered. The affected and unaffected regions are subsequently determined on the basis of the preset threshold. 14 , 19 To ensure accurate segmentation and identification of ASPECTS areas, 2 experienced neuroradiologists validated all affected ASPECTS areas. Statistical Analysis

The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test assessed the normality of the data. Measurement data that exhibited a normal distribution were presented as mean (standard deviation), while data that displayed a nonnormal distribution were represented using the interquartile range. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (percentages). The comparison of the numerical variable between the 2 groups was using either the 2‐independent‐samples t test or the Mann–Whitney U test. For categorical variables, either the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test was used. Variables with a significance level of <0.1 in the univariate logistic regression analyses were included in the multivariable model. Model 1 adjusted for clinical variables, while model 2 expanded upon model 1 by incorporating additional imaging variables. In model 3, a forward selection procedure was used to select variables with a univariate P value of <0.1.

Additionally, we evaluated the predictive ability of NCCT‐NWU and CTA‐ER using the receiver operating characteristic curve. The Delong test was used to compare the area under the curve (AUC) of NCCT‐NWU and CTA‐ER. Subsequently, we further compared the predictive efficacy of model 3+NCCT‐NWU with that of model 3+CTA‐ER for favorable outcomes.

Subgroup analyses were performed for each stratified group on the basis of onset to puncture time (onset to puncture time<360 minutes or ≥360 minutes), NIHSS score (<16 or ≥16), ASPECTS (<6 or ≥6), and successful recanalization (yes or no). The statistical analyses were conducted using R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) or SPSS (IBM, Armonk, NY).

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 206 patients fulfilling the inclusion and exclusion criteria were recruited for our study (Figure 2), comprising 77 female participants (37.4%) with a median age of 68 (60–77) years. The median baseline NIHSS score was 16 (12–20). Of the total patients, 64 (31.1%) received intravenous thrombolysis. The median NCCT‐NWU was calculated to be 7.16% (5.27%–8.83%), while the median CTA‐ER was 9.01% (7.56%–11.66%). Successful recanalization was achieved in 161 (78.2%) patients. A total of 82 patients (39.8%) exhibited favorable outcomes. The comparison between the 2 groups revealed that patients with favorable outcomes showed younger age (65 versus 71 years; P < 0.001), lower baseline NIHSS score (15 versus 17; P < 0.001), lower glucose levels (6.6 versus 7.4 mmol/L; P < 0.001), procedure without recanalization (64 versus 88 minutes; P = 0.002), and higher ASPECTS (7 versus 6; P < 0.001). Moreover, statistically significant differences were observed between the 2 groups in relation to sex, stroke or transient ischemia attack history, occlusion site, collateral circulation, and successful recanalization (all P<0.05). Additionally, the favorable outcome group exhibited lower NCCT‐NWU (6.42% versus 7.76%; P < 0.001) and CTA‐ER (8.31% versus 10.56%; P < 0.001; Table 1).

Model 1 was adjusted for clinical variables such as age, sex, stroke or transient ischemia attack history, atrial fibrillation, NIHSS, glucose, and procedure without recanalization. Model 2 expanded upon model 1 by including additional variables, including ASPECTS, occlusion site, collateral circulation, and successful recanalization. Model 3 was determined through a forward selection procedure, including age, NIHSS score, glucose, ASPECTS, occlusion site, and successful recanalization. After adjusting for confounding variables, there is no significant correlation between NCCT‐NWU and a favorable outcome (P>0.05 in all models). Conversely, CTA‐ER consistently emerged as a significant predictor for a favorable outcome across all models (all P<0.05; Table 2).

The receiver operating characteristic analysis revealed that NCCT‐NWU exhibited an AUC of 0.636 (95% CI, 0.559–0.713) in predicting a good prognosis, with a sensitivity of 0.707 (95% CI, 0.601–0.795) and a specificity of 0.581 (95% CI, 0.493–0.664) at the optimal cutoff value of 7.46%. CTA‐ER demonstrated an AUC of 0.707 (95% CI, 0.637–0.778), a sensitivity of 0.829 (95% CI, 0.734–0.895), and a specificity of 0.556 (95% CI, 0.469–0.641) at the optimal cutoff value of 10.20%. Compared with NCCT‐NWU, CTA‐ER displayed superior predictive performance (Delong test, P = 0.046; Table 3, Figure 3). Furthermore, when model 3 was combined with NCCT‐NWU, the AUC was 0.824 (95% CI, 0.767–0.881), sensitivity was 0.927 (95% CI, 0.849–0.966), and specificity was 0.653 (95% CI, 0.566–0.731). Similarly, after adding CTA‐ER in model 3, the AUC was found to be 0.828 (95% CI, 0.773–0.883), sensitivity was 0.902 (95% CI, 0.819–0.950), and specificity was 0.677 (95% CI, 0.591–0.753). However, it is worth noting that the predictive efficacy of CTA‐ER+model 3 is comparable to that of NCCT+model 3 (Delong test, P = 0.561; Table S1, Figure S1).

Discussion

Our study aimed to investigate the potential utility of CTA‐ER as a quantitative imaging biomarker for predicting favorable outcomes. The main finding of this study was that CTA‐ER serves as a reliable predictor of favorable outcomes in patients with acute anterior circulation large‐vessel occlusive stroke following EVT, surpassing the predictive efficacy of NCCT‐NWU.

NWU, a quantitative imaging parameter, is employed to evaluate the water absorption capacity of ischemic brain tissue through the measurement of density alterations. 9 The observed changes in NCCT density are hypothesized to be associated with cytotoxic edema, characterized by cerebral cell swelling caused by ischemia. 20 Thereby, as the CT density decreases, there is a corresponding increase in the NWU value, suggesting compromised viability of the brain tissue. 20 , 21 , 22 , 23 NWU can serve as a “tissue clock” for estimating the age of ischemic lesions and is strongly associated with malignant infarction and malignant edema. 10 , 11 , 24 Several studies have investigated the correlation between NWU and prognosis. Nawabi et al observed a positive correlation between elevated NWU values and futile recanalization after successful recanalization. 10 Broocks et al demonstrated the predictive value of NWU in patients undergoing EVT within an extended time window (4.5–24 hours after stroke onset) or with low ASPECTS scores (ASPECTS<6). 25 , 26 In addition, the study conducted by Lu et al confirmed a significant correlation between the automated ASPECTS software–calculated NWU and favorable prognosis in patients with large‐vessel occlusion. 27 However, our findings differ from theirs, possibly due to using different imaging analysis software and differences in the study population, as we included patients with more severe stroke and only those who underwent EVT. Moreover, a study conducted by the same research group on patients undergoing reperfusion therapy with the same software did not find a significant association between NWU and positive outcomes, 28 similar to our results. In our study, the AUC value of NCCT‐NWU was comparatively lower than that reported in previous studies. 10 We attribute this discrepancy to several potential factors. When the infarct area is small, the calculated value may underestimate the actual value due to the averaging of HU values by automated ASPECTS software for each ASPECTS region. Conversely, CTA‐ER may improve performance in patients with small infarcts owing to its more precise depiction of ischemic areas and earlier detection of changes. 29 , 30 , 31 , 32 Successful recanalization may influence NWU's predictive effect by changing edema progression. With unsuccessful recanalization, patients may experience faster edema progression, suggesting dynamic monitoring of edema changes may be a more useful approach than analyzing at a single time point. 33

The results of our study indicate that CTA‐ER exhibits superior predictive efficacy compared with NCCT‐NWU. Furthermore, it consistently demonstrates reliable predictive performance across all models, establishing itself as an independent prognostic indicator of favorable outcomes. This phenomenon can potentially be attributed to the enhanced ability of CTA to delineate the distinction between normal and pathological tissue in a more distinct manner than NCCT, thereby facilitating a more precise determination of the ultimate infarct area. 29 , 30 , 31 , 32 The alteration in NCCT density is contingent upon the water content of ischemic tissue, while the attenuation of the CTA signal is predominantly influenced by the diminished ability of ischemic brain tissue to uptake contrast agents. 34 Consequently, CTA‐ER can accurately depict the ischemic alterations in brain tissue within a brief timeframe, and it can compensate for the lack of significant ischemic modifications during the initial acute phase following a stroke in NCCT. 13 , 29 Furthermore, it is observed that CTA hypoattenuation exhibits a stronger correlation with cerebral blood flow compared with cerebral blood volume, and it is suggested that the ASPECTS based on CTA may also serve as an indicator of the ischemic penumbra region. 29 , 35 , 36 Based on the findings above, we consider that the ASPECTS region derived from CTA exhibits a higher degree of similarity to the ischemic region. 35 , 37 This observation explains why CTA‐ER can serve as a more practical alternative tool to NWU based on CTP.

Presently, most studies about NWU rely on semiautomated software and necessitate CTP for assistance in demarcating the infarct region, thereby constraining the utilization of NWU in emergency or primary stroke centers. This study uses fully automated ASPECTS software to autonomously segment the infarct area and compute the NCCT‐NWU and CTA‐ER, obviating the need for advanced imaging aid, rendering the process more straightforward, expeditious, and applicable across more centers. Moreover, this study encompasses a broader array of patients, thereby facilitating the identification of more individuals who would benefit from EVT.

There were several limitations to our study. First, our study was a single‐center retrospective study that exclusively enrolled Han Chinese. This may restrict the extrapolation of our findings, necessitating large‐scale prospective trials to validate. Second, we solely included patients who underwent EVT and adhered strictly to guideline‐based screening criteria for identifying potential beneficiaries of this intervention, which might introduce inherent selection bias. Third, our image analysis relied on software to divide the infarct area automatically, which may be affected by midline deviation or motion artifact interference, leading to incorrect segmentation. Finally, the NCCT‐NWU and CTA‐ER values calculated by the software in our study may exhibit a slight underestimation compared with the actual value, likely since the actual infarct area is only a component of the area defined by ASPECTS.

Conclusions

CTA‐ER is a significant predictor of 90‐day favorable outcomes in patients with acute anterior circulation large‐vessel occlusive stroke undergoing EVT. The application value of CTA‐ER in the treatment decision making for patients undergoing EVT needs to be further validated through prospective studies.

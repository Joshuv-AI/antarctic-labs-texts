Extracts of Black Glutinous Rice Influenced Memory Impairment and Anxiety-Like Behavior associated with Kidney Stones in Rats
Authors: Parinyaporn Nuurai, Nantapong Pea-arun, Nattawee Kuntawee, Racha Muengsuwan, Jirasuda Nakeim, Sri-arun Iamjan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the effects of “Luem Pua” black glutinous rice extract on behavior and neuronal integrity by assessing memory impairment, anxiety-like behavior, histology, as well as expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (Bdnf) and caspase-3 (Casp3) genes in a rat model of kidney stone disease. Male Sprague-Dawley rats were divided into five groups (n=6): a control group, an ethylene glycol (EG)-induced kidney stone group, two EG groups receiving either a low or high dose of rice extract, and an anthocyanin-administered EG group. Memory impairment and anxiety-like behavior were assessed by applying the Morris Water Maze (MWM) and the open field test. Neuronal damage was determined using hematoxylin & eosin. Hippocampal Bdnf and Casp3 expression levels were ascertained using SYBR Green qPCR. The EG rats showed a significantly longer MWM escape latency and spent less time in the open field center than controls. However, low doses of rice extract or standard anthocyanin markedly reduced escape latency and enhanced the time spent in the center. Moreover, prefrontal pyknotic nuclei were increased in EG rats, a phenomenon suppressed by the two doses of rice extracts and anthocyanins. Casp3 gene expression was elevated in the EG group but declined after rice extract treatment. Bdnf expression did not alter remarkably. These findings suggest that a limited dose of black glutinous rice extract, as well as anthocyanin, alleviates memory impairment, anxiety-like behavior, and neuronal damage in a rat model of kidney stone disease.Keywords: anthocyanins, anxiety, memory, rice, urolithiasis

INTRODUCTION

Kidney stone formation, or nephrolithiasis, is a common urological disorder with frequent recurrence and high global prevalence (Alelign and Petros, 2018). The formation of calcium oxalate (CaOX) crystals is the most common cause of renal tissue injuries and kidney stone development (Alelign and Petros, 2018). Such a condition is associated with various systemic health issues. Central and peripheral nervous system effects are potential consequences of kidney disease (Jabbari and Vaziri, 2018). The accumulation of urine-derived toxins (Vanholder et al., 2018) due to decreased renal clearance of nitrogenous waste products, along with metabolic and hemodynamic disorders, inflammation, oxidative stress, and impairment of the blood-brain barrier (BBB), may be associated with the development of neurological disorders (Jabbari and Vaziri, 2018). Additionally, clinical evidence reports that patients with acute kidney injury are more prone to mild cognitive impairment (Nowak et al., 2024). Depression and anxiety symptoms are more prevalent in patients with bilateral kidney stones compared to those with unilateral ones (Raizenne et al., 2023).

Oryza sativa L. black glutinous rice (BGR) cultivar “Luem Pua” holds great economic value in Thai agriculture. The seeds contain high amounts of proteins and fatty acids; vitamins B1, B2, and E; gamma-oryzanol; omega-3 and 6; and zinc, iron, manganese, and calcium (Rice Department, 2016). Additionally, it also contains anthocyanins and phenolics, which demonstrate excellent antioxidant properties (Khawsuk et al., 2022). Consumption of black rice anthocyanins at a low dose of 50 mg/kg has the potential to prevent and protect against kidney injury by attenuating oxidative stress and proinflammatory responses (Sun et al., 2025), while suppressing blood glucose levels in diabetic rats (Tantipaiboonwong et al., 2017). However, kidney protection levels are dose-dependent, requiring a high dose of black rice anthocyanins at 100 mg/kg (Sun et al., 2025). The contents of uric acid and proinflammatory cytokines－interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor-α－were also reduced in hyperuricemia mice after treatment with high-dose black rice anthocyanins (Feng et al., 2022). The lipophilic extracts of BGR seeds at a high concentration (200 μg/mL) also inhibited CaOx crystal aggregation to the maximum (Khawsuk et al., 2023). Moreover, anthocyanins also protect against neurodegenerative diseases (Zhong et al., 2023). Free radicals associated with oxidative stress play a vital role in the pathogenesis of neurodegenerative disorders, which are major causes of cognitive impairment and dementia, especially Alzheimer’s disease (Pooja et al., 2025). Ma et al. (2024) demonstrated that anthocyanins at 200 mg/kg improved activities of key antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase), and cognitive function. Therefore, the consumption of anthocyanin-enriched BGR may offer an alternative approach that ameliorates or prevents neurodegeneration and memory impairment in patients with kidney injury.

However, evidence regarding the associations between neurological conditions and kidney diseases, as well as the underlying mechanisms, remains scarce. Therapeutic strategies for kidney disease-related neurological complications have not yet been designed. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the plausible ameliorative influence of BGR extracts on cognitive impairment and anxiety-like behaviors in rats with ethylene glycol-induced kidney stones. A secondary objective was to determine its impact on certain neural integrity indicators using histology and expression markers of genes associated with neuronal vulnerability and protective pathway (brain-derived neurotrophic factor, Bdnf), as well as neuronal cell death (caspase-3, Casp3).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Luem Pua BGR extraction

Luem Pua rice cultivated in the Sa Kaeo Province was used as the study material. In total, 5 kg of rice seeds were extracted using 75% food-grade ethanol and incubation at room temperature for 24 h. The extract was then filtered, and the supernatant was dried using a vacuum rotary evaporator and then a freezer dryer. The final extract was stored at −20°C for further experimentation.

Animals and grouping

A total of 30 male Sprague-Dawley rats, 6 weeks old, weighing 180－200 g, were used. They were obtained from Nomura Siam International Co., Ltd. The rats were acclimatized for one week under a 12:12-h light/dark cycle, with ad libitum access to water and food, before the commencement of experiments. All procedures were approved by the Animal Research Committee of Burapha University, Saen Suk, Thailand (Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee; No. 009/2567).

The rats were randomly divided into five groups (n=6): (1) control group: rats received a normal diet and water; (2) ethylene glycol (EG) group: kidney stones were induced in rats by providing drinking water containing 1% EG (v/v) and 0.75% NH4Cl (v/v) for 7 days, and then drinking water containing only 1% EG for 21 days; (3) EG+low-dose rice extract group: kidney stone-afflicted rats received oral rice extract at 50 mg/kg body weight once daily; (4) EG+high-dose rice extract group: kidney stone-afflicted rats received oral rice extract at 200 mg/kg body weight once daily; and (5) EG+anthocyanin group: kidney stone-afflicted rats received oral food-grade anthocyanin at 200 mg/kg body weight once daily. The doses－50 and 200 mg/kg－were chosen based on previous studies. The low dose of 50 mg/kg protected kidneys, whereas the high dose of 200 mg/kg reduced kidney stone formation and improved brain function, with no reported toxicity (Khawsuk et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025). These doses were chosen to explore potential dose-dependent effects, with 50 mg/kg representing a physiologically relevant low dose and 200 mg/kg reflecting the higher end of effective doses as reported previously.

During treatment, the Morris Water Maze (MWM) test was performed, and locomotor activity was assessed during the last 7 and 2 days of treatment, respectively. Following EG treatment, the rats were euthanized using CO2. After that, urine and kidney tissue samples were collected to investigate crystallopathy as a validation of kidney stone induction. MWM test

The spatial memory of rats was assessed using the MWM test during the last five days of EG treatment. Briefly, a circular pool, 153×60 cm, was divided into four quadrants. The walls of each quadrant bore visual symbols, including a circle, a triangle, and a square. Water maintained at room temperature was filled to a depth of 30 cm, and then surface-dusted with a nontoxic powder. A hidden platform, 28 cm in height, was placed in a symbol-less quadrant. Animal behavior was video recorded. The MWM test protocol applied was a modification of the one reported by Lalert et al. (2022) and was composed of three stages. First, a platform visibility pretest was conducted to check for sight problems and motor impairment: a platform equal in height to the water level was placed, so that the rats could easily see it. Each rat was placed at a different starting point and allowed 60 s of free movement to find the platform. Second, a hidden platform test was conducted to measure the learning ability and memory capacities of rats. This session began 24 h after the first stage and continued for 5 days. The platform was submerged 2 cm below the water level and remained stationary. Each rat was allowed 60 s to find the platform; the time taken by the rats to find the platform was considered the “escape latency.” If the rats were unsuccessful in finding the platform within 60 s, they were guided to the platform and allowed to stay on it for 10 s. Lastly, a probe trial was conducted 24 h after the second stage to test memory retention and spatial memory ability. The platform was removed, and the rats were allowed to swim freely for 60 s. The time the rats spent in the zone where the platform was originally placed, and the number of target crossings, were recorded. After completion of each trial day, all rats were taken out of the pool, dried, and returned to their cages.

Open field test (OFT)

This experiment assessed the anxiety-like behavior of rats by testing their locomotor activity during the final two days of EG treatment. The rats were placed inside a square box－65×65×42 cm－with its floor divided into 25 equal-sized rectangles; the box was placed in the middle of an open field. On the day before testing, they were allowed to habituate to the open field via free movement for ∼10 min. A day later, the rats were allowed to move freely in the open field for 10 min. During the experiment, the activities of the rats within the open field were video recorded. The number of squares that the rats crossed with all four paws during the final 5 min was scored manually. Additionally, the time spent by the rats within four rectangles at the center of the open field was measured.

Quantitative real-time PCR assay for Bdnf and Casp3 genes

The total RNA of 30 mg of hippocampal tissue was extracted using the TRIzolTM reagent (Invitrogen). TRIzol, 1 mL, was added to the tissue sample, and was homogenized until completely dissolved; the homogenate was incubated on ice for 5 min. Next, 200 μL of chloroform was added, the mixture was incubated at room temperature for 3 min, and centrifuged at 13,400 g for 15 min at 4°C. The upper colorless aqueous phase was carefully collected into a fresh 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. Then, 500 μL of 100% isopropanol was added, the tube was incubated for 10 min at room temperature, and centrifuged at 13,400 g for 10 min at 4°C to precipitate the RNA. The supernatant was discarded, the RNA pellet was washed with 1 mL of 75% ethanol, and then centrifuged at 5,200 g for 5 min at 4°C. The supernatant was discarded, and the RNA pellet was dried at room temperature for 20 min. The pellet was resuspended in 25 μL of RNase-free water and incubated at 55°C for 15 min. RNA was quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. Then, 2 μg of RNA was used as a template to obtain cDNA by employing the RScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (BioHelix Co., Ltd.) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The sequences of the quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) primers are shown in Table 1. The qRT-PCR reaction mix had a total volume of 20 μL; it comprised 2 μL of cDNA, 2X PanGreenTM Universal SYBRⓇ Green Master Mix (BioHelix), 300 nM of each of the forward and reverse target gene-specific primers, and nuclease-free water. The reaction was performed on a QuantStudioTM 5 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The reaction program included predenaturation at 95°C for 15 s, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 30 s, annealing at 58－60°C for 20 s, and extension at 95°C for 30 s. Each reaction was performed in duplicate or triplicate. The comparative threshold (Ct) cycle of each reaction was auto-determined by the program, and the expression levels of Bdnf and Casp3 were calculated using the “2−ΔΔCt” method with Gapdh as the internal control. The gene expression levels are shown as relative mRNA expression.

Hematoxylin & eosin (H&E) staining

The right hemisphere of each rat brain was dissected and fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin. After processing the tissue and embedding it in paraffin wax, 5 μm coronal sections were placed on a slide. The frontal cortex and hippocampal sections were selected for H&E staining, performed using an automated stainer. Briefly, following deparaffinization with xylene and rehydration with a graded alcohol series, the sections were immersed in Mayer’s hematoxylin for 3 min and subjected to bluing under tap water for 10 min. The sections were then immersed in eosin stain for 30 s, dehydrated employing a graded alcohol series, cleared in xylene, and mounted using a histological mounting medium.

Image analysis

Brain sections were visualized at 20× using a Nikon upright microscope (Nikon Corp.) with the NIS-Elements software (https://www.microscope.healthcare.nikon.com/en_AOM/products/software/nis-elements). Histology of the pyknosis nuclei within the frontal cortex and dentate gyrus (DG) subregions of the hippocampus was analyzed. For this analysis, three nonoverlapping visual fields, ∼6,000×3,984 pixels, were selected. The number of pyknotic nuclei per unit area was counted using the ImageJ software version 1.48h3 (https://imagej.net/ij/). The mean cell density values for pyknotic nuclei within each section for each animal were calculated.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as the mean±standard error of the mean. The statistical significance of the differences was analyzed by one-way analysis of variance, followed by the least significant difference or independent sample t-test using SPSS version 26 (IBM Corp.). P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Urinary CaOX crystal deposition, a validation of kidney stone induction, was significantly increased in EG rats (2.833±0.173 crystals/power field) compared to controls (0.667±0.121 crystals/power field) (P<0.001; independent sample t-test). Representative images of kidney and urinary CaOX crystals are provided in Supplementary Fig. 1.

MWM test

The results of the three stages of the MWM test are presented in Fig. 2－4, respectively. On day 1 of the visible platform test, no significant inter-group differences in the escape latency time were observed (Fig. 2). During the hidden platform test, escape latency in the EG group was markedly increased. However, in EG rats treated with rice extract at a low dose or standard anthocyanin, latency time and distance to the target declined significantly compared to the EG group (Fig. 3). During the probe trial phase, in which the platform was removed from the pool, the number of target quadrant crossings and time spent within the missing-platform zone was reduced in EG compared to control rats; although the alterations were statistically insignificant. EG rats that received a low or high dose of the rice extract, or standard anthocyanin, showed an increase in the number of target quadrant crossings and time spent within the missing-platform zone compared to the untreated EG group rats. However, these variations did not reach a statistically significant level (Fig. 4).

OFT

The time spent at the center of the open field apparatus was employed to assess anxiety-like behavior in rats. The locomotion patterns (number of squares crossed) and time spent at the center by rats of all groups did not vary remarkably; the results are presented in Fig. 5. However, the EG rats spent markedly less time in the center than the control group. EG rats that received low or high doses of rice extract, or standard anthocyanin, spent more time at the center than the untreated EG group animals; however, the results were statistically insignificant.

Bdnf and Casp3 expression levels

The relative Casp3 and Bdnf mRNA contents of the hippocampus are presented in Fig. 6, respectively. In the EG group, Casp3 expression was elevated when compared to the control group rats, but these increased levels did not reach statistical significance. Moreover, in rats that received BGR extract, especially at a low dose (EG+low dose), Casp3 expression declined compared to the untreated EG rats. However, Bdnf expression did not differ significantly between the EG and control groups. However, in rats that received an anthocyanin standard (EG+anthocyanin), Bdnf expression was enhanced insignificantly compared to the control and EG group animals.

H&E staining

The prefrontal cortex (PFC) and hippocampal DG tissue staining results are presented in Fig. 7 and 8, respectively. The neurons and brain tissue of the control rats were normal in appearance (Fig. 7A and 8A). The pyknotic nuclei, characterized by chromatin condensation and dark appearance, were markedly greater in the PFC (Fig. 7B) and DG (Fig. 8B) of EG rats. In the EG low- or high-dose rice extract (Fig. 7C, 7D, 8C, and 8D) or anthocyanin (Fig. 7E and 8E) groups, pyknotic nuclei could still be observed in both brain regions. However, their density decreased when compared with that of the untreated EG rats. The number of pyknotic nuclei per unit area of the two brain regions was determined, and the results are shown in Fig. 9. The EG rats demonstrated a significantly more robust increase in the number of pyknotic nuclei than controls. Treatment with either a low or high dose of the extracts or anthocyanin remarkably suppressed the number of pyknotic nuclei in the PFC. However, the number of pyknotic nuclei did not increase significantly in the hippocampal DG of EG rats. In summary, treatment with extracts or anthocyanins insignificantly reduced the number of pyknotic nuclei.

DISCUSSION

The results of this study demonstrate memory impairment and anxiety-like behavior (reduction in time spent at the center of the locomotor apparatus) in rats in whom kidney stones were induced by EG. Our findings provide the very first evidence of an association between kidney stone disease and memory deficits as well as anxiety-like behavior in an animal model. Moreover, the number of pyknotic nuclei in the PFC increased significantly along with the enhanced expression of the hippocampal Casp3 in rats with EG-induced kidney stones. Pyknotic nuclei are irreversible nuclear changes observed in apoptotic or necrotic cells, and caspase-3 is a key effector enzyme of the apoptotic process. These results indicate the possible pathology underlying memory deficits and anxiety-like behavior in kidney stone-afflicted rats via the induction of neurodegeneration.

These results are consistent with clinical studies, which reported a higher risk of cognitive impairment in patients with any stage of chronic kidney disease compared to the general population (Li et al., 2024; Yan et al., 2024; Fang et al., 2025). Concerning anxiety-like behavior, the findings are consistent with a previous study, which reported anxiety and depression prevalences of 50.5% and 58.3%, respectively, among 103 chronic kidney disease patients (Alshelleh et al., 2022). Alaoui et al. (2024) observed that 22.2% of kidney disease patients had anxiety, and that the anxiety score markedly correlated with the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Furthermore, these results also support evidence that the presence of CaOX and mixed calcium phosphate crystals in kidney stones induces the production of reactive oxygen species, oxidative stress, localized inflammation, and several forms of regulated death, including apoptosis, ferroptosis, necrosis, necroptosis, and pyroptosis, in renal cells (Khan and Alli, 2025).

However, the pathophysiology of kidney disease-related neurological dysfunction has not been fully elucidated. Moreover, changes in eGFR and albuminuria, indicators of abnormal kidney function, may also serve as possible predictors for cognitive impairment, depression, and anxiety (Kangwan et al., 2015; Alaoui et al., 2024; Yan et al., 2024). A severe decline in eGFR correlated with an increased risk of cognitive impairment (Kangwan et al., 2015). Albuminuria has also been implicated in cognition-associated symptoms of Alzheimer’s disease (Yan et al., 2024).

A disruption of the bidirectional communication between the brain and kidney (brain-kidney axis) mat contribute to neurological disorders, particularly cognitive impairment during the natural course of chronic kidney disease, through a complex network comprising inflammation, uremic toxins, oxidative stress, vascular damage, BBB permeability, and neurodegeneration (Yan et al., 2024; Fang et al., 2025). Moreover, Zimmermann et al. (2024) revealed a novel mechanistic link between cognitive impairment and kidney disease, in which uremic toxins arising from abnormal kidney function can disrupt the inter-endothelial cell barrier and BBB, contributing to altered brain vascular permeability. Uremic conditions affect calcium homeostasis within the microglia, enhancing potassium efflux and IL-1β production. Therefore, together, it can be suggested that abnormal behavior and neuronal cell death are associated with kidney stone-induced uremic conditions. These conditions may activate microglia-mediated potassium homeostasis and neuroinflammation, as well as apoptosis, contributing to a potential mechanistic linkage with nephrolithiasis.

Furthermore, a low dose of rice extract and standard anthocyanin potentially attenuated memory deficits in rats with EG-induced kidney stones. However, the extract only relieved anxiety-like behavior in this model. These findings align with a reduction in brain neuronal death and the decreased Casp3 expression levels in EG rats treated with glutinous rice extract or a standard anthocyanin compared to untreated EG animals. The current study was designed based on the results of a previous study by Kangwan et al. (2015), which suggested that consumption of BGR－rich in anthocyanin functional compounds－improved memory performance during the MWM test in cerebral ischemic rats by reducing brain lipid peroxidation. Similarly, Lee et al. (2020) demonstrated that black rice extract prevented cognitive and memory impairment in a Aβ25-35-induced Alzheimer’s disease mouse model through its protective influence on lipid peroxidation and nitric oxide production in the brain. Moreover, Natthee et al. (2024) reported that BGR improved anxiety, depression, and stress-related symptoms in 46 adult volunteers with mild-moderate stress levels by suppressing oxidative stress and inflammation. The anthocyanin-rich fraction of black rice bran extract exerted neuroprotective effects against Aβ25-35-induced apoptosis in the human neuroblastoma cell line (SK-N-SH) via a reduction in reactive oxygen species levels and inhibition of the mitochondria-dependent caspase pathway (Sivasinprasasn et al., 2024). Additionally, the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects of the anthocyanin-rich fraction from black rice inhibited lipopolysaccharide-induced lung cell inflammation in mice by reducing the blood levels of proinflammatory cytokines: IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-18 (Umsumarng et al., 2025). In addition to these findings, the present study suggests that the improvements in learning, memory, anxiety-like behavior, and histological outcomes observed in rats with EG-induced kidney stones may be attributed to the antioxidant, antioxidative stress, anti-inflammatory, and antiapoptotic properties of anthocyanin-rich BGR extract.

Furthermore, both low and high doses of rice extract reduced the number of pyknotic nuclei within the brain, suggesting an antiapoptotic effect. However, the low dose improved memory performance, whereas the high dose impaired it. These findings suggest that reducing neuronal apoptosis alone is insufficient for cognitive improvement. A previous study by Sangkitikomol et al. (2010) reported the antioxidant effects of a low dose of anthocyanin-rich black sticky rice extract; however, a high dose could induce cytotoxicity (Calabrese, 2008).

This study has a few limitations. We evaluated the effects of crude BGR extract, but did not quantify the other bioactive rice components. However, we examined the impacts of a bioactive compound, anthocyanin, in isolation and observed outcomes similar to those induced by a low dose of the crude extract. Additionally, improvements in memory and anxiety were assessed only through behavioral tests and histopathology; full mechanistic insights were not included. Therefore, future studies must include investigations into the molecular mechanisms underlying the cognitive benefits of rice extract in kidney stone-induced models.

The results indicate that Luem Pua BGR extract has relieving effects on memory impairment and anxiety-like behavior in a rat model of kidney stone formation induced by EG via a reduction in neurodegeneration.

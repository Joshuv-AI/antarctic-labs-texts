Randomised controlled study investigating standard dose continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) versus low-dose CRRT in critically ill patients with acute kidney injury (AKI): study protocol for a prospective, randomised, controlled, international, multicentre trial (the ‘Ketzerei’ trial)
Authors: Christian Strauß, Mahan Sadjadi, Thilo von Groote, Hendrik Booke, Ludwig Maximilian Schöne, Clara Hegner, Carola Wempe, Melanie Meersch, Joachim Gerss, Alice Bernard, Helene Anna Haeberle, Peter Rosenberger, Tim Rahmel, Matthias Unterberg, Michael Adamzik, Christian Arndt, Hinnerk Wulf, Stefano Romagnoli, Manuela Bonizzoli, Raffaele Mandarano, Vedran Premuzic, Lucia Andrade, Igor Smolentzov, Sean M Bagshaw, Lakhmir Chawla, Alexander Zarbock
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe only supportive therapy for patients with severe acute kidney injury (AKI), a common complication among the critically ill, is dialysis. Based on the literature and current guidelines, continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) with a total effluent dose of 20–25 mL/kg/hour and adjustments to ensure such dose is delivered despite down time (eg, due to surgical procedures) is recommended. However, experimental and clinical studies suggest that azotaemia, which can be induced by lowering the effluent dose, may accelerate renal recovery. This clinical study investigates whether a lower effluent dose (10–15 mL/kg/hour) for a maximum of 7 days or until successful (>24 hours) liberation of CRRT in critically ill patients with a dialysis-dependent AKI accelerates renal recovery and reduces time on CRRT compared with guideline-directed standard dose (25–30 mL/kg/hour).Methods and analysisThe Ketzerei trial is an international, multicentre randomised, controlled trial, designed to investigate if a lower effluent dose (10–15 mL/kg/hour) accelerates renal recovery and reduces the time on CRRT compared with the guideline directed standard effluent dose (25–30 mL/kg/hour). The study aims to enrol 150 critically ill patients with a dialysis-dependent AKI. Eligible patients will be randomised to receive either a standard effluent dose (control group, 25–30 mL/kg/hour) or lower effluent dose (interventional group, 10–15 mL/kg/hour). The primary endpoint is the number of days free from CRRT and alive (from randomisation through day 28). Key secondary endpoints include the number of (serious) adverse events due to potential uremia, the duration of RRT and intensive care unit survival.Ethics and disseminationThe Ketzerei trial has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Chamber of Physicians Westfalen-Lippe (2023–343 f-s), the University of Muenster and subsequently by the corresponding Ethics Committee of the participating sites. Results will be disseminated widely and published in peer-reviewed journals, presented at conferences and will guide patient care and further research.Trial registration numberclinicaltrials.gov (NCT06021288).Keywords: Intensive & Critical Care, Acute renal failure, Adult intensive & critical care

Introduction

Acute Kidney IAcute kidney injury (AKI) is a major cause of morbidity and mortality, affecting more than 10% of all hospitalised patients1 and more than 50% of patients admitted to intensive care units (ICUs).2 Renal replacement therapy (RRT) is still the only therapeutic option in the treatment of the consequences of severe AKI and is required in approximately 10–12% of all critically ill patients.3 Despite the fact that these patients show high mortality rates, those patients who survive such an episode have an increased risk of developing chronic kidney disease or end-stage renal disease.4 5 Consequently, it is of utmost importance to improve clinical management.

Preclinical models have demonstrated that an uraemic milieu enhances renal recovery and decreases kidney fibrosis6 by suppressing macrophage activation7 and modulating myofibroblast activation.8 Contralateral nephrectomy prior to unilateral kidney injury decreases fibrosis and enhances renal recovery in the injured kidney, presumably by inducing retention of uremic solutes.7 8 Delayed contralateral nephrectomy has shown similar effects.9 In addition to less fibrosis, significant decreases in monocyte/macrophage infiltration, complement levels, oxidative stress and other markers of inflammation could be observed in the injured kidney. This suggests that an impaired renal clearance leading to a mild uraemic milieu may be an effective treatment for severe AKI.7,9 These experimental data are indirectly supported by clinical data and show that higher intensity RRT does not affect mortality but appears to delay renal recovery.10 The proposed hypothesis is that the mild uraemic milieu is a strong stimulus for renal cellular regeneration and repair.6 Patients with severe dialysis-dependent AKI may therefore benefit from an initial prescription that fosters a permissive azotaemia, defined as blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels of 100–150 mg/dL for a maximum of 7 days. The aim of this pilot trial is to test the effects of low-dose CRRT aimed at inducing permissive azotaemia while sufficiently treating fluid overload, on renal recovery.

Objectives and aims

The aim of this study is to investigate in critically ill patients with a dialysis-dependent AKI whether the prescription of a low effluent dose (ED) during CRRT (10–15 mL/kg/hour vs 25–30 mL/kg/hour) increases the number of days alive and free of CRRT up to day 28 following randomisation.

Methods and analysis

Design and setting

The Ketzerei trial is a randomised, controlled, international, multicentre trial conducted in seven centres, in Germany, Italy, Croatia and Brazil. A definitive list of the participating centres will be provided with the final report of the trial. The trial was planned and initiated by the department of anesthesiology, intensive care and pain medicine of the University Hospital Muenster in Germany. The protocol follows the 2010 updated principles of Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials11 and is conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.12 The Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials reporting guidelines were used in the creation of this protocol.13

Patient and public involvement

Patients and public were not directly involved in designing the study, but patient feedback was sought and considered in the planning process. If desired, patients or their representatives will be informed about the trial results distributed via the local site.

Participants

All critically ill patients with a dialysis-dependent AKI, based on a clinical indication, will be screened on a daily basis by the local study team. Patients will be included if they meet all the following inclusion criteria: (1) critically ill patients with a dialysis-dependent AKI. The patient has a dialysis-dependent AKI, if one of the following criteria occurred: (a) diuretic resistant volume overload, (b) signs of organ oedema (eg, pulmonal oedema), (c) anuria/oliguria (eg, <200 mL/12 hours), (d) steadily increasing serum creatinine (SCr) and thus AKI worsening, (e) severe azotaemia with a BUN >150 mg/dL, (f) severe hyperkalaemia with a serum potassium ≥6.0 mmol/L, (g) severe acidosis with a pH level <7.20 or serum bicarbonate level ≤12 mmol/L. (2) Age ≥18 years, (3) written informed consent. Patients receiving CRRT for non-AKI related aetiology (eg, intoxication, CO2 elimination, hyperammonaemia etc) will be excluded from a study inclusion/randomisation. Patients are also excluded if they meet any of the following exclusion criteria: (1) pre-existing CKD with a glomerular filtration rate (GFR) <30 mL/min/1.73 m2, (2) chronic dialysis-dependent end-stage kidney disease, (3) severe liver cirrhosis (Child-Pugh C), (4) severe acidosis (pH <7.20 at time of screening), (5) severe hyperkalaemia (potassium >6.0 mmol/L), (6) pregnancy or breast feeding, (7) patients with any kind of dependency on the investigator or employed by the sponsor or investigator, (8) patients held in an institution by legal advice. Patients meeting all the above-mentioned inclusion and none of the exclusion criteria will be deemed eligible, randomised and thus included in the study.

Consent process

A physician of the local study team will approach all eligible patients and inform them about the study. The physician will explain the nature of the study, including the risks and benefits, to the potential participant and answer all questions regarding the study. Potential participants must be informed that their participation is voluntary. This conversation will be documented and the patient will be asked to give his/her written informed consent (see the attached informed consent form (ICF)). The medical record must include a statement that written informed consent was obtained before the participant was enrolled in the study. The authorised physician obtaining the informed consent must also sign the ICF. It is obligatory for all sites to use this form or an approved translation. During the monitoring visits, special attention lies on the informed consent process. Every patient will retrieve a copy of the informed consent file. However, emergency conditions, such as sepsis and septic shock, often occur in critically ill patients, most of whom are not capable of providing informed consent. For unconscious emergency patients or patients who lack capacity, the informed consent process has to follow the legal local-specific and national regulations (in accordance with the instruction of the local ethics committee). Based on the presumed will of the patient, a legally authorised representative may consent instead of the participant as soon as possible. Once participants regain the capability to give informed consent, they must consent to participate in the trial. A signed copy of the ICFs must be provided to the participant.

Either the patient or its representative can terminate the study participation at any time, without the need to justify this decision. Based on the participant’s wishes, all collected data can be anonymised and all gathered samples destroyed. All patients are covered by participation insurance throughout the study (insurance policy: 5701031903017).

Randomisation and data collection process

All eligible patients will be randomly allocated to one of the two treatment arms (control or intervention), using a web-based system (RandIMI plug-in for RedCAP, Research Electronic Data Capture, V.10.6.22, Vanderbilt University, Nashville, Tennessee, USA)14 with the use of computer generated variable-block sequences in a 1:1 random proportion.

All clinical data concerning the patient, its treatment and all applied study interventions will be entered, in pseudonymised form, in the electronic case reporting file RedCap, powered by the Vanderbilt University.15 All gathered data will be encrypted, sent to and stored by the University of Muenster. Only local investigators will have access to identifiable data, gathered at their site. All sites will be monitored on a regular basis by physicians from the investigator team Muenster who are not involved in the trial. Monitoring will include source data verification, as well as audits on the local intervention application and safety measurements. The data collection process will be overseen by a data safety monitoring board on a regular basis.

Intervention period

Following the randomisation, all patients will receive a CRRT regime according to their allocated arm. All patients assigned to the interventional arm will receive an initial low dose CRRT, with a prescribed ED of 10–15 mL/kg/hour. All patients assigned to the control arm will receive a standard dose CRRT, with an ED of 25–30 mL/kg/hour. The intervention will be administered for 7 days following the randomisation (the day of randomisation counts as day 0) or until the patient can be successfully weaned from the CRRT, whichever occurs first. We use a 7-day period, because most of the patients who still need dialysis after day seven very likely will not benefit from any further intervention.16 Switching CRRT to intermittent RRT is allowed once the patient is haemodynamically stable (defined as receipt of vasopressors in the equivalent of <0.2 µg/kg/min norepinephrine) and no other reasons exist to use CRRT. In order to standardise the termination of RRT, we used the following criteria: (1) haemodynamic stability (need for vasopressors <0.2 µg/kg/min norepinephrine equivalent), (2) urine output >400 mL/day and (3) no signs of fluid overload. These signs include visible oedema, either during a clinical inspection or in imaging procedures, such as ultrasound, X-ray or CT or a manifest fluid overload detected via the use of Pulse Index Continuous Cardiac Output (PiCCO). Patients are considered successfully weaned if they remain free of RRT for at least three consecutive days after termination of RRT. All sites have to follow these specifications. If a successful liberation from the CRRT was not possible until day 7, the low dose regime (10–15 mL/kg/hour) will be transferred to a standard dose regime (25–30 mL/kg/hour). All decisions concerning the CRRT settings (eg, ultrafiltration, etc) are the subject of the treating intensivist or nephrologist, as long as the ED meets the targeted area. If the BUN levels go above 150 mg/dL, then the treating intensivist can either continue with the prescribed dose (low or standard dose regime) or adjust the dose in order to decrease BUN levels. If the treating intensivist considers a continuation of the low dose regime as detrimental for the further trajectory, the physician is allowed to adjust the treatment regime and even increase the ED. Any decision to do so requires information from the study initiator, as well as a detailed justification. We established a 24/7 hotline for treating physicians so that they can discuss each substantial treatment change related to RRT with the principal investigator. All participating sites are required to assess all enrolled patients on a daily basis during their ICU stay to detect potential (serious) adverse events ((S)AEs). For these assessments, any tests deemed necessary by the local investigator to identify potential (S)AEs should be applied at the local investigator’s discretion.

All drug dosing will be adjusted to the prescribed clearance, per standard ICU procedures of the recruiting centre. Aside from the intervention, all patients will receive treatments according to the local standards.

Outcomes

The primary outcome is the number of days alive and free of RRT from randomisation till day 28. The endpoint is defined as the number of RRT-free days between randomisation and day 28. For each patient, one point is given for each calendar day that a patient is both alive and free of RRT, assuming that the patient survives and remains free of the technique for at least three consecutive calendar days after RRT weaning, whatever the vital status at day 28. A zero RRT-free day value is assigned to patients who die before weaning or who remain dependent on RRT until day 28.

Exploratory endpoint

In a subgroup of patients, involving patients from different centres, blood and urine samples from recruited patients will be collected and analysed (‘reverse translation’). To understand the mechanisms of illness and recovery and how the different treatments affect them, we are testing whether the application of a lower ED (10–15 mL/kg/hour) or a guideline-directed standard dose (25–30 mL/kg/hour) changes the expression of markers of illness and recovery (eg, NGAL, CCL14, immunophenotyping, post RRT BUN concentrations and pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines).

Data quality

Each study team member will be trained in all aspects of the study protocol and data entry prior to the respective site initiation.

Sample size

Based on the statistical considerations, we expect a dropout rate of about 6%. Therefore, a total of 150 patients will be recruited and randomised (75 patients per group), so that at least 70 patients with evaluable data can be expected per group.

The statistical sample size calculation is based on the study ‘Regional Citrate vs Systemic Heparin Anticoagulation for Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy in Critically Ill Patients with Acute Kidney Injury trial’ (RICH trial).17 In the RICH trial, the number of days alive and free of RRT up to day 28 in 28.0% of patients was zero, and the median number of days across all patients was eight. In the control group of the Ketzerei trial, the primary endpoint is expected to be equally distributed to the RICH trial. The Ketzerei trial aims to detect a clinically relevant effect of the intervention with sufficient power (1−β > 80%). The effect of the intervention is clinically relevant if the number of patients with zero days alive and free of RRT up to day 28 is reduced by 33%, and if in patients with >0 days alive and free of RRT up to day 28, the number of days alive and free of RRT up to day 28 is increased by 33%.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses will be based on the primary hypothesis:

‘Controlled permissive azotemia in patients with AKI requiring dialysis will improve renal recovery.’

All statistical analyses will be performed in accordance with the International Council on Harmonisation (ICH guideline E9 (statistical principles for clinical trials). All randomised patients will be included in the primary statistical analysis (‘full analysis set’) and the analysis will be carried out according to the ‘intention-to-treat’ principle.

The primary endpoint (number of dialysis-free and survived days between randomisation and day 28) will be compared between the two randomised groups using a two-tailed Mann-Whitney U test (α=0.05, power >80%). A statistical analysis plan will be generated and signed before the last patients are recruited. No interim analysis is planned.

Blinding

Neither the patient nor the study personnel can be blinded to the administered treatment and thus the result of the randomisation. All individuals involved in the adjudication of endpoints or complications will be blinded to the result of the randomisation and the applied treatment.

Ethics and dissemination

The Ketzerei trial has been evaluated and approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Muenster (2023-343-f-s), including two non-substantial study amendments (mainly concerning updates in the informed consent files to improve understandability) and an amendment regarding the transition from a monocentric to a multicentre design (additional details are listed in the supplementary appendix). The corresponding ethics committees of each cooperation partner have also approved the ethics proposal, including all amendments (see table 2 for a list of all participating centres and corresponding ethics committees). The trial has been registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT06021288) prior to the start of the study. All study-related updates (eg, amendments), as well as all documents suited for public display, are accessible via the clinicaltrials.gov website. The study is supported by the interdisciplinary centre for clinical research (IZKF) Muenster. Detailed funding information is listed further below. All results will be presented at international conferences. The final manuscript will be published in a peer-reviewed journal. Results will be used to guide clinical practice and further research. Trial status

This is the first published version of the study protocol. Recruitment started in October 2023 and is estimated to be completed by December 2025. As we have observed a stable recruitment rate over the past few months, no additional sites are currently planned. However, should an unforeseen decline in recruitment occur, we remain open to including additional sites, if necessary. If insurmountable challenges arise, leading to the conclusion that recruitment cannot be completed or if significant safety concerns are identified by the initiator, the study may be discontinued.

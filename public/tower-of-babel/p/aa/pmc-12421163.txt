The Design for Implementation: The Future of Trauma Research and Clinical Guidance (DFI) Conference Series Editorial
Authors: Kelly L Lang, Pamela J Bixby, Michelle A Price, Cynthia Lizette Villarreal, Ashley N Moreno, Lacey N LaGrone
Published: 2025

The first meeting of the Design for Implementation: The Future of Trauma Research and Clinical Guidance (DFI) Conference Series occurred at the American College of Surgeons (ACS) in Chicago, Illinois, on February 21–22, 2024. This 3-year conference series aims to improve the development and dissemination of trauma clinical guidance nationally and beyond. Conducted in collaboration with the Coalition for National Trauma Research (CNTR) and supported in part by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ), a diverse group of multidisciplinary partners gathered to begin collaboratively redesigning the national approach to trauma clinical guidance development and dissemination with a focus on improved equity and effectiveness. The endeavor was marked by an emphasis on the patient voice and featured 36 presenters ranging from trauma surgeons and public health experts to software developers and others with unique expertise to develop innovative solutions. The collective aim of the attendees is clear: better care for patients, less burnout for providers, and a future where trauma clinical guidance is collaboratively developed and designed for implementation (figure 1).

Map highlighting the locations of the authors of this article.

The present issue of Trauma Surgery & Acute Care Open contains five papers. The conference proceedings are summarized and presented in narrative format in the first paper.1 The next paper provides a case series of rural trauma providers’ experiences with current trauma clinical guidance.2 The third paper provides an expert commentary on the general ‘implementability’ of guidance.3 The fourth paper is a systematic review of the current state of trauma clinical guidance globally.4 Finally, the last paper describes the novel context inquiry and consensus-building techniques, and their output, used during the conference.5

We invite everyone to be an agent of change toward transforming trauma clinical guidance by attending the next meeting of the Design for Implementation conference series scheduled for February 19 and 20, 2025. Your participation is welcome to ensure that trauma remains at the forefront of medical advancements, delivering the highest quality of care to every patient, every time .

Running exercise alleviates chronic heart failure by promoting cardiomyocyte autophagic flux through the NEAT1–QKI affecting Beclin1/LC3B mRNA stability
Authors: Ying Zhang, Rui Song, Lei Zhuang, Ying Zhao, Zhuo Chen, Yongjie Wang, Shouling Mi, Jiaxin Liu, Wenlin Tai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs the end-stage manifestation of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), chronic heart failure (CHF) is associated with high morbidity and mortality. Our previous study showed that Running exercise could improve CHF by inducing autophagy, but the underlying mechanisms and applicability remain unclear. This study investigated the roles of NEAT1 and RNA-binding protein Quaking (QKI) in the cardioprotective effects of running exercise initiated during the early stage after abdominal aortic coarctation (AAC) surgery. NEAT1 and QKI were overexpressed in vivo or NEAT1 and QKI were knocked down in vitro. The effects of running exercise on the myocardial damage of CHF rats or Ang II-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis, autophagic flux, autophagy-related protein expression, and the expression of the PI3K/ AKT pathway by regulating NEAT1 and QKI were assessed using echocardiography, tissue staining, immunofluorescence, western blotting, and confocal microscopy. Furthermore, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP), RBPmap prediction, actinomycin D (ActD) chase experiments and Dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed to explore the post-transcriptional regulation of autophagy-related genes by QKI. Running exercise reduced myocardial tissue damage in CHF rats and up-regulated the expression of autophagy-related proteins. NEAT1 was up-regulated in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats, and Ang II-damaged cardiomyocytes. Whereas QKI expression showed the opposite trend. Additionally, running exercise inhibited NEAT1 while restoring QKI expression, particularly during weeks 2–3 of exercise intervention. Mechanistically, NEAT1 directly interacted with QKI and negatively regulated its expression. Simultaneously, QKI bound to the 3’untranslated regions (3’-UTRs) of Beclin1 and LC3B mRNA and enhances the stability of Beclin1 and LC3B transcripts, thereby promoting autophagy. Functionally, NEAT1 inhibition or QKI upregulation suppressed the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, and attenuated cardiomyocyte apoptosis under pathological conditions. Collectively, the current findings revealed the regulatory mechanism by which running exercise alleviates CHF, and clarified that running promotes cardiomyocyte autophagy and alleviates CHF through the NEAT1-QKI-Beclin1/LC3B regulatory axis, highlighting a novel mechanism underlying exercise-mediated cardioprotection. Given that exercise intervention was initiated during the early stage following AAC surgery, the observed benefits may include early remodeling-modifying and disease-progression-modifying effects, rather than solely therapeutic effects on established decompensated CHF.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13062-026-00880-7.Keywords: Running exercise, lncRNA NEAT1, QKI, Beclin1/LC3B mRNA, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Chronic heart failure, Autophagy

Introduction

CVDs remain the leading cause of mortality worldwide [1], and chronic heart failure (CHF) is the end-stage manifestation of most CVDs [2]. CHF is myocardial damage that results from a complex physiological syndrome caused by changes in myocardial structure or function due to myocardial infarction, hemodynamic overload, inflammation and other pathological stimuli [3]. In addition to pharmacological therapies and instrument therapies, non-pharmacological strategies management, particularly cardiac rehabilitation, have attracted increasing attention in recent years.

Exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation is considered to be one of the important therapeutic strategies for patients with CHF [4]. For the past few years, studies have confirmed that exercise training can improve exercise tolerance and the life quality of patients with heart failure [5]. In experimental models of heart failure, aerobic exercise, including running exercise can be effective in improving the deterioration of left ventricular contraction in rats with heart failure and alleviating mitochondrial dysfunction in the myocardial tissue of rats with heart failure [6]. Moreover, aerobic exercise has been shown to regulate the expression of mitochondria-associated proteins, thereby reducing myocardial hypertrophy and improving cardiac function in CHF models [7]. Therefore, exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation is considered a cost-effective and promising non-pharmacological approach for the management of CHF. However, the protection mechanism of cardiac exercise rehabilitation has not yet been entirely elucidated. In our previous study, we demonstrated that running exercise improved cardiac function in rats with CHF induced by abdominal aortic constriction (AAC) [8], Nevertheless, the underlying molecular mechanisms require further investigation. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play a key roles in cardioprotection and alleviation of CHF [9–11]. Different lncRNAs have different expression profiles in CHF. Key lncRNAs are likely to act as biomarkers of pathology or even targets for therapeutic intervention. As a highly abundant lncRNA in the nucleus of mammalian cells, lncRNA-nuclear-enriched abundant transcript 1 (NEAT1) is closely related to the protection of myocardial damage and the improvement of myocardial function [12]. Studies have shown that NEAT1 is involved in the process of myocardial infarction, and inhibiting NEAT1 alleviates cardiomyocyte damage [13, 14]. Meanwhile, NEAT1 is up-regulated in CHF patients, and the expression of NEAT1 is also increased in the left ventricular tissue of heart failure model mice established by abdominal aortic coarctation (AAC). Silencing NEAT1 alleviates myocardial fibrosis in CHF mice [15]. Moreover, it is worth noting that the decreased expression of NEAT1 could be controlled by exercise training [16]. Nevertheless, whether exercise training improves CHF by regulating the expression of NEAT1 and its specific mechanisms remains unclear.

In this study, it was found that running exercise induced autophagy to alleviate myocardial damage in CHF rats, particularly during the third week of the exercise intervention. Mechanistically, running exercise downregulated NEAT1 expression, thereby restoring QKI expression. Furthermore, QKI enhanced the stability of Beclin1 and LC3B mRNAs through direct binding to their 3’UTRs, ultimately promoting autophagy and suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation. Collectively, these findings reveal a novel NEAT1/QKI/Beclin1-LC3B regulatory axis underlying exercise-mediated cardioprotection in CHF.

Materials and methods

Animal and reagents

All Rats (170 ± 20 g) were brought from Beijing Huafukang Biological Technology Co. Ltd (Beijing, China; certificate number SCXK (Jing) 2019-0008). Ang II was purchased from MedChem Express (MCE, HY-113948). Antibodies of LC3, Beclin1, p62, Bax were purchased from HuaAn Biotechnology (ET1701-65, HA721216, HA721171, ET11603-34, HuaAnBio, Hangzhou, China). Antibodies of BNP, QKI, PI3K/p-PI3K were purchased from ABclonal (A21799, A0193, A0265, AP054, Wuhan, China). Antibody of Bcl-2, caspase-3, QKI were purchased from Abcam (ab194583, ab184787, ab103563, MA, USA). Antibodies of AKT/p-AKT, mTOR/p-mTOR were purchased from Proteinech (10176-2-AP, 80455-1-RR, 28273-1-AP, 28879-1-AP, Wuhan, China). S63845 was purchased from Selleck (USA). Hematoxylin-Eosin (H&E) and Masson’s Trichrome (Masson) kits were purchased from Solarbio (G1120, G1340, Beijing, China).

Rats of CHF model

Rat abdominal aortic coarctation (AAC) was used to induce myocardial hypertrophy and establish a CHF model. The rats were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (60 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection) and fixed on the operating table in supine position. After anesthesia, A small incision (approximately 3–4 cm) was made under the xiphoid process. intestinal tissues were placed out of the abdominal cavity and lavaged with saline. The pulsating abdominal aortic sheath was visible behind the upper right side of the left kidney, exposing the abdominal aorta. About 2–3 cm above the intersection of the abdominal aorta and renal vein, place the No. 7 syringe needle next to the aorta, ligate the abdominal aorta and the No. 7 syringe needle together with No. 4 silk thread, and then remove the needle to form the abdominal aortic coarctation. After the operation, the abdomen was sutured. In the sham control group, the abdominal aorta was sutured without the abdominal aorta ligation after surgical rupture into the abdominal cavity.

The experimental design and implementation plan were submitted to the Animal Experiment Committee of Kunming Medical University for approval (approval number: Kmmu20240308). All experimental plans were carried out in accordance with the “Guidelines for Animal Experiments of Kunming Medical University”.

CHF rats treated of run exercise and transfection

We build the CHF model according to the previous modeling method [8]. Rats were placed on the treadmill in their respective lanes undisturbed for 15 min for their acclimated to the treadmill prior to exercise [17]. Rats were warmed up for 5 min at a slow initial speed of 6 m/min. Adjust the rat treadmill speed from 10 m/min to 20 m/min, observe the rats’ reaction on the treadmill, and find that most rats cannot keep up with the treadmill speed when it exceeds 16 m/min. Therefore, it was determined that the rats in the exercise group ran continuously on the treadmill at a speed of 16 m/minute for 5 days/week. The running time was 20 min/day in the first week, 30 min/day in the second week, and 40 min/day in the third week. 50 min/day in the fourth week. The control group was the heart failure sedentary group (SET) group for the same period of time.

Under the guidance of cardiac ultrasound, a micropump syringe was used for in situ injection of transfected NEAT1 overexpressing adenovirus (HBAD-Adeasy-Neat1-Null-Null, 1.58*10^10 PFU/mL; purchased from HanHeng Trading Co. Ltd.) or QKI overexpressing adenovirus (Adeasy-QKI, 2.21*10^10 PFU/mL; purchased from QinHuai Bio Co. Ltd.) into the myocardium. Use a 25 µL micropump syringe to extract a total of 20 µL of overexpressed adenovirus. Under the guidance of cardiac ultrasound, insert the needle from under the xiphoid process of the rat. Under ultrasound, the needle reaches the left ventricular myocardial tissue. With the apex of the heart as the midpoint, select the left, right, upper and lower four parts respectively. Inject 5 µL into each point. Every 2 weeks/1 time.

Cardiomyocyte isolation and treatments

ventricles from newborn (24 h) rats were washed and minced in PBS. The tissues were then digested in PBS containing 0.06% collagenase and 0.05% trypsin for 10 min at 37 °C. The above steps were repeated 4–5 times until the tissues were completely digested. After differential adhesion, the cardiomyocytes were then plated in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 10 mM bromodeoxyuridine (1:80), After 48 h, the culture medium was changed, and observed the morphology and beating status of cardiomyocytes.

Cardiomyocytes (5 × 104 cells/ml) were cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% fetal calf serum for 48 h and then seeded in a 96-well plate. 1 µM Ang II was added to each well and incubated for 48 h to construct a cell model [18].

Echocardiography

Following the same method used for echocardiography as before [8]. Briefly, transthoracic M-mode echocardiography was performed with a 12-MHz probe on anesthetized rats. Vevo2100 (VisualSonics) small animal ultrasound machine was used for ultrasonic detection with MS-550D probe.

Cell transfection

cardiomyocytes were seeded in six-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells in culture medium. NEAT1-shRNA lentiviral (HBLV-Neat1 shRNA1-ZsGreen-PURO, 1.5*10^8 TU/mL), QKI-shRNA lentiviral (HBLV-Qki shRNA1-ZsGreen-PURO, 1.5*10^8 TU/mL), QKI-OE lentiviral (HBLV-Qki-3xflag-ZsGreen-PURO, 2*10^8 TU/mL) and negative control lentiviruses were purchased from HanHeng Trading Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The lentiviral were transfected into cardiomyocytes using Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. At 48 h after transfection, cells were selected with 5 µg/mL puromycin (Sigma) to obtain stably transfected cells. Detection was performed by RT-qPCR and western blotting. In addition, the autophagic influx was monitored by infecting cardiomyocytes with adenoviral vector GFP-mRFP-LC3 (HanHeng Trading Co. Ltd, Shanghai, China), and the culture medium was replaced and then observed by confocal microscope (Nikon AXR).

Cell viability assay

The CCK8 assay was employed to determine the viability and toxicity of cardiomyocytes cells treated with Ang II or transfected with sh-NEAT1/sh-QKI/OE-QKI. Briefly, the primary cardiomyocytes were seeded in 96-well plates at approximately 1 × 104 cells per well, cells were treated with Ang II or transfected with sh-NEAT1/sh-QKI, CCK8 reagent was added to each well (10 µL) and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C in the dark. Next, the 450-nm absorbance was measured with automatic microplate reader (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

Western blotting assay

Rats myocardial tissue proteins and cardiomyocytes proteins were extracted, separated by 10% SDS PAGE gel and transferred to PVDF membrane (Merck Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA). Next, the membranes were incubated with primary antibody (including: BNP, ANP, QKI, LC3-II/LC3-I, Beclin1, p62, Bax, Bcl-2, caspase-3 and PI3K/p-PI3K, AKT/p-AKT, mTOR/p-mTOR) overnight at 4 °C. The next day, the membrane was incubated with secondary antibody for 1 h. Subsequently, the bands were visualized by enhanced chemiluminescence detection system (Millipore Corporation, Billerica, MA, USA), and the Immunoblots were analyzed by Image J software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Quantitative real-time PCR

The TRIzol reagent (Nanjing vazyme Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) was used to extract the total RNA from cells with different treatments and rat myocardial tissue. The extracted total RNA was reverse-transcribed to cDNA using the cDNA reverse-transcription kit (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Next, the cDNA samples were subjected to real-time PCR using SYBR Green (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The primer information was shown in Table 1. The 2−ΔΔCt method was adopted to quantify the fold change, and GAPDH as an internal reference.

H&E staining and masson staining

Hearts were cut at the horizontal short-axis plane, and the heart tissue from the cardiac apex of rats was collected and fixed in 4% polyformaldehyde at room temperature for 24 h. The fixed tissues were embedded in paraffin after dehydration and diaphanization. Next, the Sect. (4-µm-thick) were stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) and Masson staining according to kit instructions, Finally, the stained sections examined under a microscope.

Immunofluorescence

Cardiomyocytes were washed with PBS and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, and were then permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100. Next, the Cardiomyocytes were blocked with 1% BSA for 1 h and incubated with primary antibody (LC3B and p62) at 4 °C overnight. On the next day, cells were washed with PBS, and incubated with fluorescent secondary antibodies and DAPI. Finally, the cells were observed under a fluorescent microscope (Nikon T2, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

RNA pull-down assay

We used H9C2 cells to perform RNA pull down experiments. RNA pull-down was performed using a Magnetic RNA Protein Pull-Down Kit (Pierce, Thermo, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, after extracting and purifying biotin RNA, the Biotinylated NEAT1 RNA and negative control RNAs were incubated with 50 µL prewashed streptavidin-agarose beads at 4 °C. After washing, protein was obtained from beads. The proteins were eluted and resolved by SDS-PAGE, and analyzed by western blotting.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays

We used H9C2 cells to perform RIP experiments. RIP experiment was using a Magna RIP RNA Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were lysed, collect the lysate, prepare QKI magnetic beads-antibody, and pre-incubate the magnetic beads and antibodies at room temperature for 30 min. Next, QKI-conjugated magnetic beads were incubated with cell lysis buffer. RNA was extracted and analyzed by RT-qPCR.

Measurement of cell surface area

We followed the method of Liu et al. to measure the surface area of cardiomyocytes [19]. Briefly, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min. Next, they were incubated with 0.1% rhodamine-phalloidin (Invitrogen) for 90 min. After washing with PBS and staining with DAPI, immunofluorescence was observed under a fluorescence microscope (Nikon T2, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) to estimate the cell surface area.

Actinomycin D (ActD) assays

To assess Beclin1 and LC3B mRNA stability, cardiomyocyte cells were different treat. After that, cells were treated with 5 µg/mL Actinomycin D (APExBIO) to halt transcription. Total RNA was extracted at 0,6,12, and 18 h post-treatment, and qPCR was performed. Beclin1 and LC3B mRNA transcript stability was determined by comparing its degradation kinetics over time.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

To investigate whether QKI affects the mRNA expression of Beclin1/MAP1LC3B by regulating the 3’-UTR of Beclin1/MAP1LC3B through QRE (ACUAAY), we performed dual-luciferase reporter assay. Briefly, Primers were designed and synthesized to amplify fragments containing the QRE in the 3’UTR of Beclin1/MAP1LC3B. The amplified fragments were then cloned into the pmirGLO vector and named Beclin1 3’UTR-WT and MAP1LC3B 3’UTR-WT (QinHuai Bio Co. Ltd. China). Next, the two QRE segments of the 3’-UTR of Beclin1 and MAP1LC3B were mutated to obtain Beclin1-mut1 (ACTAAT mutated to ACGCGT), Beclin1-mut2 (CTTAAC mutated to CTCGGC), and MAP1LC3B-mut1 (ACTAAT mutated to ACGCGT), MAP1LC3B-mut2 (ATTAAC mutated to ATCGGC). The primers were listed in Table S1.

The pcDNA vector (OE-NC) or pcDNA-QKI (OE-QKI) expression vector, Beclin1 3’UTR-WT, MAP1LC3B 3’UTR-WT, Beclin1-Mut1, Beclin1-Mut2, MAP1LC3B-Mut1, MAP1LC3B-Mut2, Beclin1-Mut1 + 2, and MAP1LC3B-Mut1 + 2, were co-transfected into HEK293T cells. After transfection relative luciferase activity was measured as described of Nazarali et al. [20] and Jin et al. [21]. In the pmirGLO dual-luciferase reporter system (QinHuai Bio Co. Ltd. China), Relative luciferase activity = firefly luciferase values / Renilla luciferase values.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as the mean ± SD. t-test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used to compare cardiomyocytes and Rats with different treated. A value of p < 0.05 or p < 0.01 were considered as statistically significant.

Results

Running exercise alleviates AAC-induced CHF in rats

We established AAC-induced CHF model (Fig. S1A-C) and conducted running exercise on CHF rats for 4 weeks (Fig. 1A). We obtained Left Ventricular Internal Dimension in Systole (LVIDs), left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVIDd), ejection fraction (EF) and fractional shortening (FS) data by echocardiography. The results showed that the LVIDs and LVIDd value were significantly increased, whereas the EF and FS values were significantly decreased in the CHF group compared with the normal group. CHF rats then underwent weekly running exercise training, and echocardiographic analysis revealed that the EF and FS values in the RUN group gradually increased compared with those in the SET group, reaching peak levels during the third week of exercise intervention (Fig. 1B). Meanwhile, the LVIDs and LVIDd value gradually decreased in the RUN group. In addition, we measured the heart weight/body weight ratio (HW/BW). Compared to the normal group, the CHF group had a higher HW/BW value. Following running exercise, the HW/BW value was significantly reduced compared with the SET group, with the most pronounced decrease observed during the third week of exercise intervention (Fig. 1B). H&E staining manifested that, compared with the normal group, the CHF group showed myocardial tissue rupture, fiber arrangement disorder, edema and even necrosis, high inflammatory cell infiltration. Masson staining results showed that the CHF group rats had a large amount of blue stained collagen tissue in their myocardial tissue. All the above phenomena were significantly reversed after running exercise, particularly during the third week of the exercise intervention (Fig. 1C and D). The echocardiographic results and histological examinations of the AAC rats supported the pathological relevance of the successful CHF model. Natriuretic peptide family genes can serve as biomarkers for the severity of heart failure. We examined the expression changes of BNP and ANP within the natriuretic peptide family. Western blotting results showed that, compared with the normal group, the expression of BNP and ANP in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats was significantly upregulated. Compared with the SET group, the expression of BNP and ANFP in the RUN group was gradually downregulated (Fig. 1E). All the above phenomena indicate that running can alleviate myocardial injury in CHF rats, especially with the most significant improvement observed in the third week of exercise intervention.

Running exercise promotes autophagy, inhibits apoptosis, and inhibits PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway

Autophagy is critical in maintaining cardiovascular function [22]. Studies have found that for CVD caused by insufficient autophagy, exercise training can restore normal autophagy function, delay and improve the progression of CVD [22, 23]. We detected the expression of autophagy-related proteins in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats after running exercise, and the results showed that compared with the normal rats, the LC3-II/LC3-I protein ratio and Beclin1 expression in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats were decreased, and p62 protein was increased. However, after exercise, the LC3II/I protein ratio and Beclin1 expression levels gradually increased in CHF myocardial tissue, and p62 expression levels gradually decreased, especially with the most significant improvement observed in the third week of exercise intervention (Fig. 2A-B). PI3K is a known negative regulator of autophagy [24]. We detected the expression of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in exercise or non-exercise myocardial tissue of CHF rats. Western blotting results showed that compared with the normal rats, the expression of p-PI3K, p-AKT and p-mTOR in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats were increased. However, p-PI3K, p-AKT, and p-mTOR levels were decreased after running exercise (Fig. 2E-F). In addition, running exercise also down-regulated the expression of pro-apoptotic proteins Bax and caspase-3 and up-regulated the expression of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 in the heart tissue of CHF rats (Fig. 2C-D). These results showed that running exercise promotes autophagy, inhibits apoptosis in CHF rat myocardial tissues, especially in the third week of exercise intervention.

Overexpression of NEAT1 reverses the alleviating of running exercise on AAC-induced CHF in rats

We further examined the mechanisms of running exercise in alleviating CHF in rats and found that lncRNA NEAT1 was highly expressed in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats compared with the control group, and NEAT1 was gradually downregulated after running exercise (Fig. 3A). We injected an adenoviral vector overexpressing NEAT1(pcDNA-NEAT1) into the left ventricle of rats under cardiac ultrasound guidance (Fig. 3B). The results showed that pcDNA-NEAT1 in myocardial tissue upregulated the expression of NEAT1 compared with running rats (Fig. 3C). At the same time, we detected changes in CHF-related indicators. Echocardiography results showed that, compared with the RUN group, the NEAT1 overexpression group had significantly increased LVIDs, LVIDd and HW/BW values, and decreased EF and FS values (Fig. 3D). H&E and Masson staining results showed that, compared with the running group, the NEAT1 overexpression group exhibited more pronounced myocardial tissue rupture, disordered tissue fiber arrangement, and greater blue collagen staining (Fig. 3E and F). Simultaneously, Western blotting results indicated significantly upregulated BNP and ANP expression (Fig. 3G). All these results suggest that NEAT1 overexpression reversed the alleviating effect of running on myocardial damage in CHF rats.

Overexpression of NEAT1 reverses the promoting effect of running exercise on autophagy in CHF rats

We detected the expression of autophagy-related proteins in the myocardial tissue of running exercise CHF rats after injected pcDNA-NEAT1. The results showed that compared with the exercise CHF group, the LC3-II/LC3-I protein ratio and Beclin1 expression in the pcDNA-NEAT1 group of CHF rats were decreased, and p62 protein was increased (Fig. 4A and B). Expressions of PI3K/Akt pathway related proteins were further detected by western blot. The results showed that compared with CHF rats in the exercise group, the expression of p-PI3K, p-AKT, and p-mTOR in the myocardial tissue of exercised CHF rats after transfection with pcDNA-NEAT1 was relatively increased (Fig. 4E and F). In addition, apoptotic protein detection results showed that compared with the exercise CHF group, transfection of pcDNA-NEAT1 in exercised CHF rats up-regulated the expression of Bax and caspase-3 and reduced the expression of Bcl-2 in myocardial tissue (Fig. 4C and D). These results indicate that transfection of pcDNA-NEAT1 affects the expression of autophagy/apoptotic-related proteins and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway induced by exercise in CHF rats.

Knockdown of NEAT1 inhibits ANG II -induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis and promotes autophagy

Activation of the renin-angiotensin system plays a key role in the pathogenesis of heart failure, and cardiac enzymes promote heart failure by generating angiotensin II (Ang II) from angiotensin-(1–12) [25]. However, Ang II will induce myocardial cell autophagy in the early stage, and the autophagy level will increase and then gradually decrease [26]. In order to be closer to the phenomenon that exercise-induced autophagy in CHF rats alleviates heart failure in vivo, we chose to induce autophagy during the process of inhibiting myocardial cell autophagy [18, 27]. To verify the role of NEAT1 in heart failure, we conducted in vitro experiments. We followed the method of previous studies to construct a cell model by stimulating neonatal rat cardiomyocytes with Ang II [28] (Fig S2A) (1µM; 48 h [29]). CCK8 results showed that Ang II treatment significantly down-regulated the viability of cardiomyocytes (Fig S2B). After Ang II treatment, the surface area of cardiomyocytes cells was enlarged (the characteristics of CHF) (Fig. 5A). Rt-qPCR results showed that the expression of NEAT1 was up-regulated in the Ang II group. Next, we transfected sh-NEAT1 in cardiomyocytes and then treated them with Ang II. The results showed that compared with the Ang II group, NEAT1 downregulated in the sh-NEAT1 group (Fig. 5B). In addition, the expression of autophagy-related proteins, apoptosis-related proteins and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway in the Ang II group was consistent with the myocardial tissue of CHF rats. Compared with the normal group, the LC3-II/LC3-I protein ratio and Beclin1 expression were decreased in the Ang II group, the p62 was increased; the expression of Bcl2 was decreased, the Bax and caspase-3 were increased; the expression of p-PI3K, p-AKT and p-mTOR were increased. Similar results were also observed by immunofluorescence analysis of LC3B expression and p62 expression. However, these phenomena were reversed by NEAT1 shRNA transfection (Fig. 5C and E and Fig S2C).

Cardiomyocyte autophagic flux is related with NEAT1

NEAT1 is involved in the autophagy of cardiomyocytes. To further determine the effect of NEAT1 on the autophagic flux of cardiomyocytes, we transfected sh-NC/sh-NEAT1 and OE-NC/OE-NEAT1 into cardiomyocytes. We observed that the p62 expression was downregulated and LC3-II expression was upregulated after transfected with sh-NEAT1, whereas OE-NEAT1 showed opposite effects (Fig. 6A). In addition, Using GFP-mRFP-LC3 adenovirus to infect cardiomyocytes, autophagic flux was monitored by tracking LC3. When the autophagosome (yellow fluorescence) fuses with the lysosome, only the red fluorescence of mRFP is presented due to the quenching of GFP. Therefore, when autophagy is induced, the lysosome fuses with the autophagosome to form an autophagolysosome, and the red fluorescence is enhanced. When the autophagic flux is inhibited, it is weakened. Our results showed the numbers of GFP and mRFP-positive dots were both significantly increased after the downregulation of NEAT1. When NEAT1 is upregulated, it is reduced (Fig. 6B). These data indicated that sh-NEAT1 facilitates the induction of autophagy in cardiomyocytes. We used the POSTAR3 database to predict RBP for NEAT1(Fig. 7A and B), and performed Rt-qPCR detection on the five predicted proteins UPF1, ELAVL1, MBNL2, Fmr1, and QKI that are closely related to myocardial damage. The results showed that compared with the expression of other proteins, QKI expression were the most obvious. The Rt-qPCR results showed that QKI was down-regulated in the myocardial tissue of CHF rats, and upregulated after exercise (Fig. 7C). We further verified the relationship between NEAT1 and QKI through RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) and RNA pull-down assays (in H9c2 cells). The results showed that NEAT1 pulled down QKI and there is an interaction between NEAT1 and QKI (Fig. 7D and E). Western blotting detected the expression of QKI in rat myocardial tissues, the results of western blotting were consistent with the results of Rt-qPCR (Fig. 7G). In parallel, compared with the exercise CHF rats, transfection pcDNA-NEAT1 in exercise CHF rats, the QKI expression was decreased (Fig. 7F). Furthermore, QKI expression was reduced in Ang II-stimulated cardiomyocytes compared with normal cardiomyocytes. Compared with the Ang II group, QKI expression increased after transfection with sh-NEAT1 (Fig. 7H). These results indicated that the mechanisms by which exercise alleviates myocardial damage by downregulating NEAT1 was related to QKI expression. Next, we further conducted rescue experiments in CHF rats by overexpressing QKI. Based on our previous findings, we selected CHF rats subjected to either 3 weeks of RUN or 3 weeks of SET for these experiments. The rats were divided into the following groups: Control, CHF + SET-3 W, CHF + RUN-3 W, pCDNA-NEAT1 + RUN-3 W, and pCDNA-NEAT1 + OE-QKI + RUN-3 W. Concurrently, Western blotting results indicated that the simultaneous overexpression of NEAT1 and QKI significantly downregulated the expression of BNP and ANP (Fig. 7K).

QKI regulates the stability of Beclin1/ MAP1LC3B mRNA

We further investigated the interaction between QKI and Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA. According to the NCBI database, the 3’-UTR of Beclin1 mRNA (NM_003766.5) is located between nucleotides 1506 and 2131. The 3′UTR of MAP1LC3B mRNA (NM_022818.5) is located between nucleotides 471 and 2147 (Fig. 9A and B). We predicted the binding of QKI to Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA using the RBPmap database. QKI preferentially recognizes QKI response elements within the 3’-UTR of the target mRNA. Based on the QKI motif sequence (ACUAAY) and Screening criteria (Z-score ≥ 2.0; P-value < 0.05), the results showed that multiple potential QKI binding sites exist within the 3’-UTR of Beclin1 and LC3B mRNA (Fig. 9A and B). In the RIP experiment, Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA was also precipitated using an anti-QKI antibody, indicating that QKI can enrich Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA (Fig. 9C-D). Furthermore, ActD assays showed that QKI knockdown reduced Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA levels and shortened its half-life (Fig. 9F and J), while QKI overexpression in cardiomyocytes produced the opposite effect (Fig. 9E and I). To determine whether QKI is essential for sh-NEAT1 induced upregulation of Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA, we co-transfected sh-NEAT1 plasmid with sh-QKI into cells. Rt-PCR analysis showed that QKI knockdown reversed the sh-NEAT1-induced increase in Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA expression (Fig. 9G and K). Simultaneously, we examined the effect of Ang-II treatment on Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA. The results indicated that Ang-II further shortened the half-life of Beclin1/MAP1LC3B mRNA (Fig. 9H and L). To further investigate whether QKI regulates the expression of Beclin1/ MAP1LC3B through the QRE elements, the dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed, and the sequence information was depicted in Fig. 9M and N. Dual-luciferase reporter assay showed that Overexpression of QKI resulted in an increased luciferase activity of Beclin1 3’-UTR and MAP1LC3B 3’-UTR (Fig. 9O and P). Mutation of Beclin1-Mut2 and MAP1LC3B-Mut2, also resulted in increased luciferase activity indicating QKI was still able to interact with Beclin1 3’-UTR and MAP1LC3B 3’-UTR despite the mutation to QRE. In contrast, mutation of Beclin1-Mut1 and MAP1LC3B-Mut1, or double mutation in Beclin1-Mut1 + 2 and MAP1LC3B-Mut1 + 2, failed to increase the luciferase activity. These results indicate that the nucleotides in Beclin1-Mut1 (ACTAAT mutated to ACGCGT) and MAP1LC3B-Mut1 (ACTAAT mutated to ACGCGT) are critical for the functional interaction between QKI and Beclin1/MAP1LC3B (Fig. 9O and P). These findings support that QKI regulate the mRNA stability of Beclin1/ MAP1LC3B by binding to QRE elements within its 3’UTR.

Discussion

CHF remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [30]. Despite the difference in therapeutic efficacy due to patient compliance and exercise tolerance, exercise training remains an effective strategy for cardiac rehabilitation that alleviates CHF and improves heart function [31, 32]. Nonetheless, the role of exercise training in alleviating CHF is still unclear. In the present study, we demonstrated that running exercise alleviates myocardial injury during the progression of CHF by enhancing cardiomyocyte autophagic flux through a NEAT1–QKI regulatory axis. Importantly, our findings further revealed that QKI functions as a key RNA-binding protein that stabilizes the mRNAs of essential autophagy-related genes, Beclin1 and LC3B, thereby promoting autophagic flux and suppressing cardiomyocyte apoptosis under pathological conditions. We found that NEAT1 was markedly upregulated in CHF and Ang II–damaged cardiomyocytes, whereas QKI expression was suppressed, Running exercise reversed these pathological changes, with the most pronounced protective effects observed during the third week of exercise intervention. Moreover, overexpression of NEAT1 in vivo partially abolished the beneficial effects of running exercise, while sh-NEAT1 in vitro restored QKI expression, enhanced autophagy, and attenuated cardiomyocyte apoptosis. In summary, these results indicate that the NEAT1-QKI-Beclin1/LC3B axis is a key mediator of exercise-induced cardioprotection in CHF.

Exercise training is one of the major therapeutic modalities for heart failure. The ESC Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute and Chronic Heart Failure indicate that exercise training can improve exercise tolerance and health-related quality of life in patients with heart failure [33]. Zhuang et al. [34] and Pearson et al. [35]’s clinical trials and meta-analyses on clinical patients showed that exercise improves diastolic function, exercise capacity and quality of life in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF). However, clinically, the therapeutic effect of exercise training is often limited by patient adherence and physical capacity. Among CHF patients who are capable of and willing to participate in exercise rehabilitation, high-intensity interval training (HIIT) has been shown to improve peak oxygen consumption (VO2), thereby improving overall health status [36, 37]. In several animal studies, exercise training has been reported to reduce cardiac hypertrophy in CHF mice [7]. In CHF rats, exercise training promotes cardiac remodeling and improves cardiac function [38, 39]. Our findings are consistent with these previous studies. In this study, we found that running exercise gradually reduced myocardial fibrosis and myocardial damage in CHF rats. At the same time, the expression of BNP and ANP, an important detection indicator of myocardial damage in CHF, also gradually decreased after running. It is worth noting that running exercise exerted significant protective effects on myocardial tissue in CHF rats during weeks 2–3 of the intervention period, particularly during the third week of exercise intervention. However, when the exercise duration was extended to the fourth week, the therapeutic effect of running exercise weakened and myocardial fibrosis increased. These findings suggest that the exercise intensity and duration used in our protocol may have exceeded the exercise tolerance of the rats during the fourth week. Excessive exercise training may therefore exert detrimental effects on cardiac recovery. This observation suggests the existence of an “exercise therapeutic window”. This phenomenon is highly relevant to the individualized implementation of exercise prescriptions in clinical practice.

Autophagy has been widely reported to exert protective effects against myocardial damage and myocardial fibrosis [40]. In autophagy-deficient mice, restoring autophagy alleviates diabetes-induced myocardial damage [41]. Similarly, in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion, induction of autophagy can alleviate myocardial damage caused by I/R [42]. Consistent with these findings, we found that the LC3-II/LC3-I protein ratio and Beclin1 expression were reduced, p62 expression was increased in CHF myocardial tissues, indicating the autophagy was inhibited. In addition, for myocardial damage caused by insufficient autophagy, exercise training up-regulates autophagy, restore regular autophagy function, and delay the progression of CVD [22]. Our study also verified this result, in our experiments, running exercise induced autophagy in CHF rats and inhibited the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway.

NEAT1 is one of the most abundant lncRNA transcripts in mammalian cells and has been identified as a biomarker in multiple diseases, including heart failure [43]. Studies have found that NEAT1 expression was significantly increased in heart failure patients and CHF. In addition, our results further found that running exercise gradually down-regulated the expression of NEAT1, especially after 2–3 weeks of exercise intervention. At the same time, knockdown of NEAT1 significantly alleviated Ang II-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis in vitro, whereas overexpression of NEAT1 partially reversed the protection of running exercise on myocardial damage in CHF rats under in vivo. These findings indicate that NEAT1 plays a role in the malignant progression of CHF, and running can act as a Neat1 inhibitor to a certain extent, downregulating Neat1 to protect CHF-induced myocardial damage. More interestingly, these effects were closely associated with the regulation of autophagy. Autophagy is a dynamic process during heart failure progression. Previous studies have found that NEAT1 was involved in the regulation of autophagy, promoting or inhibiting the development of autophagy [44, 45]. In this study, we found that NEAT1 overexpression inhibited running-induced autophagy in CHF rats, whereas NEAT1 knockdown significantly enhanced autophagy in Ang II-treated cardiomyocytes.

To clarify the specific mechanism of running exercise-induced autophagy in CHF through NEAT1, we first predicted RNA-binding proteins associated with NEAT1 using the POSTAR3 database and identified QKI as a potential target. QKI is an RNA-binding protein, belonging to the signal transduction and activation of RNA (STAR) family [46, 47]. There is evidence suggested that QKI dysregulation is involved in the development of multiple CVD [48]. QKI is also an important regulator of the contractile apparatus of cardiomyocytes. Esther et al. reported that QKI knockout adult mice developed heart failure associated with severe sarcomere disruption [49]. In contrast, Overexpression of QKI could inhibit myocardial damage and alleviate cardiomyocyte apoptosis [50]. Consistent with these findings, our data demonstrated that the expression of QKI was significantly decreased in AAC-induced CHF rats and in Ang II-damaged cardiomyocyte. On the other hand, QKI, as an RNA-binding protein, can bind to upstream non-coding proteins. In lncRNAs, Feng et al. demonstrated that QKI can bind to and be regulated by NEAT1 [51]. Consistent with this finding, we confirmed that QKI directly interacts with NEAT1. Notably, silencing QKI reversed the pro-autophagic and anti-apoptotic effects induced by NEAT1 knockdown, establishing QKI as a critical mediator of NEAT1-regulated autophagy. Furthermore, using RBPmap predictions, RIP assays, Actinomycin D chase experiments and Dual-luciferase reporter assays, we demonstrated that QKI directly binds to the 3’-UTR untranslated regions of Beclin1 and LC3B mRNAs and enhances their stability, thereby promoting autophagic flux. Collectively, these findings reveal a NEAT1–QKI–Beclin1/LC3B regulatory axis that links running exercise to post-transcriptional control of autophagy in cardiomyocytes, providing a mechanistic explanation for the beneficial effects of exercise in CHF.

Despite revealing the therapeutic mechanism of exercise and NEAT1 in CHF, this study has several limitations.

A major limitation of the present study is that running exercise was initiated at an early stage after AAC surgery. Therefore, the beneficial effects observed in this study may reflect not only therapeutic actions, but also the ability of exercise to attenuate adverse cardiac remodeling and modify disease progression during CHF development. Future studies in which exercise intervention is initiated after confirmation of established decompensated heart failure are needed to further determine the therapeutic efficacy of exercise in advanced CHF.

Although CHF rats exhibited improved recovery under the exercise intensity and duration used in this study, it remains unclear whether alternative exercise intensities or training durations could produce superior therapeutic outcomes. Therefore, the existence and applicability of the proposed “exercise therapeutic window” require further validation in clinical studies.In summary, the present study demonstrates that running exercise alleviates myocardial damage in CHF by inducing cardiomyocyte autophagy. Mechanistically, this effect is mediated, at least in part, through a NEAT1–QKI–Beclin1/LC3B regulatory axis, which enhances autophagic flux and suppresses apoptosis by modulating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. Notably, running exercise downregulates NEAT1, thereby restoring QKI expression and promoting the stability of autophagy-related mRNAs, providing a post-transcriptional mechanism for exercise-induced cardioprotection. These findings highlight the NEAT1–QKI axis as a potential therapeutic target, and suggest that appropriately prescribed running exercise could offer a novel adjunctive strategy for improving cardiac function in CHF.

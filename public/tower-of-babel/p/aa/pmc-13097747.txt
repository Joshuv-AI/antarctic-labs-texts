A study of the mediating role of coping styles on social support and caregiving burden among family caregivers of older adults with disabilities in China
Authors: Yuwei Zhang, Li Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn the context of global population aging and advanced aging, family care with “warmth of affection” has always been an indispensable form of care that matches the wishes of the majority of older adults with disabilities to enjoy their later life. Caregiving burden is prevalent among family caregivers of older adults with disabilities. This study aims to analyze the direct association between social support and caregiving burden, as well as the indirect pathway mediated by coping styles.MethodsThe study used a random cluster sampling method to recruit 464 family caregivers of older adults with disabilities in Guangzhou. On-site questionnaires were utilized to gather information about respondents’ social support, caregiving burden, coping styles, and basic demographics. Multiple linear regression and Bootstrap sampling were used to investigate the correlations between the variables.ResultsFamily caregivers of older adults with disabilities reported a caregiving burden score of 43.47 ± 17.09. Social support and positive coping were negatively associated with caregiving burden, and negative coping was positively associated with caregiving burden. Social support had an indirect influence on caregiving burden through coping styles, with positive coping styles explaining 15.40% of the variance and negative coping styles accounting for 22.61% of the variance.ConclusionGreater social support was associated with lower caregiving burden, with indirect effect through coping; programs that strengthen social support and adaptive coping may relate to lower burden, pending confirmation in longitudinal studies.Keywords: Social support, Coping styles, Caregiving burden, Family caregivers

Introduction

A group of older adults known as older adults with disabilities are those who, because of physical dysfunction or cognitive impairment brought on by aging, illness, or accidental injury, have lost some or all of their ability to take care of themselves and require care or assistance from others in order to maintain a normal life. Scholars typically assess self-sufficiency using the six Activities of Daily Living (ADL) indicators, nevertheless, an aged individual will be classified as disabled if they are unable to fulfill any one of the six indicators, older adults with disabilities have longer care times and care cycles, more urgent care needs, a wider variety of care content and methods, and higher demands placed on the long-term care service system than other groups of older adults. Those who are tasked with caring for family members who are unwell or unable to care for themselves are known as family caregivers. Therefore, the majority of older adults with disabilities look to their families for care, and family care, with its warmth of familial love, is always an irreplaceable model of care that fulfills the wish of most older adults with disabilities to enjoy their old age. Relief from the caregiving burden for this population is therefore desperately needed. Guangzhou city, a symbol of China’s economically developed areas, has a big and rapidly aging old population, and there is an even greater need for family care for older adults with disabilities due to the city’s earlier aging demographic structure. Because of this, the study’s focus is on the Guangzhou city metropolitan region. Its findings are indicative and prospective, and they are especially important for the theoretical evaluation of shifts in family caregiving.

According to research, more than 80% of older people worldwide want their children to care for them [1], and in the United States, more than half of older people over the age of 65 prefer the family care model, with 40% to 60% providing care to family members, relatives, or friends, and nearly 30% providing care at least once a week in practice [2]. In China, over 90% of older adults with disabilities use family care, the most common caregivers are their spouses or children, older people who face challenges to self-care prefer family care over independent living, and as time goes on and social development occurs, the trend in China toward family care for the older adults is growing [3–7]. Due to social role conflicts and insufficient social support policies, the majority of family members today work in productive jobs and must balance a number of social responsibilities. This has left family caregivers of older adults with disabilities with a heavy physical and mental burden. It is therefore imperative that the caregiving burden for this population be reduced as soon as possible.

Coping style is a strategy for individuals to consciously regulate their cognition, emotion, and behavior when confronted with pressure or factors that are difficult for them to cope with [8, 9]. Coping style is a dynamic process, individual coping behaviors will be adjusted accordingly as the stressor develops, and the responses made by different individual thoughts and behaviors will reflect the individual’s inertia and tendency. Individuals tend to use a positive coping method to objectively deal with an issue and actively take steps to fix it, actively resolve unpleasant feelings, and so on, whereas negative coping is used to ignore the situation or to vent. In general, positive coping with the reduction of caring obligations is more likely to be beneficial. Caregiving burden is directly impacted by coping techniques and styles, with problem-oriented, sensible, and constructive coping styles being linked to a reduced caregiving burden. According to Engler et al., coping methods have an impact on the caregiving burden experienced by unpaid HIV caregivers, with blaming-withdrawal coping having a positive correlation with caregiving burden [10], Iavarone et al. found that emotionally oriented coping increases caregiving burden, whereas task or avoidance oriented coping decreases caregiving burden [11]. According to Alnazly’s study, the majority of family caregivers of haemodialysis patients practiced positive problem-focused coping, which helped to alleviate the burden of their care [12].

When Zarit et al. studied the caregiving burden of individuals with Alzheimer’s disease in 1980, they introduced the concept of caregiving burden to the scientific community in the 1960s. They proposed that caregiving burden is defined as the “varying degrees of negative impacts on family members’ emotional, physical health, social life, and economic status in order to provide kinship care” [13]. Long-term caring duties can increase a caregiver’s risk of sadness, anxiety, and physical discomfort, according to research by Rezende et al. [14]. According to Han et al. [15], family caregivers of older adults with disabilities bear heavy responsibilities, which call for interventions to assist them cope. These burdens are linked to external sources. According to Li et al. [16], role conflict tends to increase the strain on family caregivers, and the stress on caregivers is directly correlated with the level of infirmity in the older adults.

The material and emotional support that a person can receive in their social surroundings can be interpreted as social support, and it is a measure of how strongly a person is connected to society [17–19]. Studies have indicated that the degree of stress experienced by family caregivers of older adults with disabilities is largely influenced by social support, and more has to be done to raise this level of support [20]. The study conducted by Vrabec et al. [8] revealed that the degree of social support has an effect on how much of a strain caregivers have to bear. Del-Pino-Casado et al. [21] demonstrated that perceived social support can lessen the subjective caregiving burden, García-Mochón et al. [22] emphasized the need for social support to lessen the burden and suggested the establishment of an all-encompassing social support system. Chiou et al. [23] concluded that social support and family functioning have a significant impact on the burden of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities. They also suggested that social support resources should be optimized while giving full play to the family care function. Haya et al.‘s research [24] shown how family assistance and formal caregiving can effectively lessen the load placed on family caregivers of older adults with disabilities.

The ecosystem theory and the stress process model [25, 26] state that the primary stressors are challenges directly related to caregiving, such as the cared-for person’s ability to take care of himself/herself, the state of his/her health, the state of his/her behavioral ability, etc. The burden of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities is affected by various systems, both by the direct effect of the micro-environment, such as the family environment and the community, as well as by the external macro-social support. The activities involved in providing care, such as the employment and income of the family caregiver, are secondary stressors. Few studies have examined the mediating role of coping styles in the relationship between social support and caregiving burden, and the majority of them have stayed at the parallel level of the role of single and multiple factors on caregiving burden, which is the outcome of the interaction between subjectivity and objectivity rather than coming solely from external objective factors. These studies have a significant impact on the burden of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities.

As shown in Fig. 1, the buffering model proposes that support is primarily associated with the well-being of individuals experiencing stress. This is termed the buffering model because it posits that support “buffers” (protects) persons from the potentially pathogenic influence of stressful events, although stressful events may elicit needs for multiple resources, it is reasonable to assume that specific events elicit particular salient coping styles. We posit that there must be a reasonable match between the coping styles and the available support in order for buffering to occur [20]. First, a certain level of social support can help people reduce the perceived stress of a future stressful event and increase their perceived ability to cope. This is because there is a relationship between the potential stressful event and the perceived appraisal of stress. Second, social support gives people coping mechanisms to deal with the perceived significance of stress in between subjective experience and negative physiological responses [27]. Based on this theory, the buffering model incorporates clear mediating hypotheses. This study empirically tests the core proposition of the buffering model by examining whether social support reduces negative perceptions of burden through the mediating pathway of positive coping.

The process of social support on caregiving burden

Family caregivers of older adults with disabilities have a caregiving burden, social support for the effective integration of a variety of resources, by providing caregivers with emotional, informational, and instrumental resources, this approach promotes more positive coping styles such as cognitive restructuring and stress reappraisal, ultimately aiming to alleviate their physical and mental burdens. Therefore, coping is not merely an adaptive response by the individual but may also serve as a crucial mediating factor between social support and caregiving burden. Specifically, social support promotes positive coping among caregivers by adjusting cognition and reevaluating stressful events, thereby weakening psychological or physiological reactions and indirectly alleviating their perceived burden [28], theoretical model shown in Fig. 2. The majority of research that has already been done focuses on the parallel levels of the roles that single and multiple elements play in caregiving burden. However, the interaction between subjectivity and objectivity is what ultimately shapes caregiving burden, rather than it coming from external objective factors alone.

Theoretical hypothetical model

Based on prior research, theoretical frameworks, and hypotheses, we attempt to link the relationships among social support, coping styles, and caregiving burden. As illustrated in Fig. 2, our hypothetical path is: social support and coping styles directly influence caregiving burden. Social support indirectly affects caregiving burden through coping styles, while coping styles mediate the effect of social support on caregiving burden. Therefore, this study aims to validate the direct effects of social support and coping styles on caregiving burden, and to analyze and quantify the mediating role of coping styles among family caregivers of older adults with disabilities between social support and coping styles.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

During the pre-survey phase, a pre-survey was carried out in order to ascertain whether the questionnaire was operational based on the survey’s real findings, whether any of the entries were unclear or poorly articulated, and to promptly address any shortcomings. Additionally, investigators received standardized pre-survey training to ensure they fully understood the study’s content and objectives, and were proficient with the questionnaire and procedures. During the formal survey phase, one-on-one interviews were conducted. Investigators explained the content to respondents, accompanied them on-site to complete the questionnaire, and collected the forms immediately. The completion time ranged from 10 to 20 min. Questionnaires completed in under 10 min or over 20 min were deemed invalid and excluded. Responses completed in under 10 min are highly likely to indicate that the respondent failed to fully comprehend the question wording or the implications of the options, or did not undergo the necessary recall and judgment process. Such an unusually short response time is typically associated with systematic answering patterns. This pattern is more likely mechanical clicking than reflecting genuine attitudes or behaviors. Responses exceeding 20 min clearly surpass the normal timeframe for this survey, typically indicating the respondent encountered interruptions, multiple breaks, or scattered attention during completion. This may lead to inconsistent answers or reduced validity. Prudent responses are characterized by slightly longer time spent on critical or complex questions, yet remain within a reasonable overall timeframe, with the total duration still falling within the normal range. Conversely, abnormally prolonged completion times often stem from external distractions that disrupt the integrity and consistency of the response process. Consequently, the authenticity of such data cannot be guaranteed. Therefore, responses falling into either of these two categories are deemed invalid. The scale with the highest number of variables (22 items total) was used to calculate the required sample size, which was 275 ~ 550 for this study after accounting for a 20% loss in interview rate. The required sample size was determined based on the assumption that the sample size should be at least 10–20 times the study variables. In this study, a total of 490 questionnaires were gathered; 464 of those had a valid recovery rate of 94.7%. In six central Guangzhou City urban areas, communities were chosen for the survey using the stratified whole cluster random sampling method. Specifically, one-sixth of the communities were chosen through random sampling, with each administrative district serving as a sampling stratum. In Guangzhou 2023, the community boards of 18 Haizhu, 22 Liwan, 21 Tianhe, 24 Baiyun, 18 Yuexiu, and 13 Huangpu were selected for the survey. Community health officers will collect and register health information of elderly residents within the community. We will use Activities of Daily Living (ADL) assessments to further screen eligible individuals as the sampling frame. Finally, we will conduct questionnaire surveys with all family caregivers of older adults with disabilities meeting the inclusion criteria in the selected survey communities.

A cross-sectional study was conducted in Guangzhou, China from May to August 2023. Standardized, trained interviewers administered face-to-face surveys. Participation was voluntary and anonymous, with inclusion based on expressed informed consent. The study’s participants were family caregivers of older adults with disabilities living in Guangzhou City’s urban areas. The study’s inclusion criteria included the following: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) spouse, children (including their spouses), parents, siblings, and other family members of older adults with disabilities who are related to the older adults; (3) providing care for longer than three months in a row, taking on the majority of the care duties, and providing the longest duration of care; (4) willingly and deliberately taking part in this survey with informed consent There was no communication or comprehension issue in this poll. The exclusion criteria were: (1) cognitive or mental impairments; (2) accepting remuneration. The whole cluster random sampling method was used to select communities in six central urban areas of Guangzhou City, with each administrative district as a sampling stratum. The communities selected for the survey were randomly sampled by one-sixth, and questionnaires were administered to all family caregivers for older adults with disabilities within the jurisdiction that met the inclusion criteria, as provided by the neighbourhood committees of the selected communities.

Instruments

Social support

The Perceived Social Support Scale (PSSS) was adopted. This scale was developed by Chinese scholar Jiang Qianjin based on the Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support (MSPSS) developed by Zimet et al. to meet the needs of localization [17]. The scale has 12 items, 7-point response options, three subscales–Family, Friends, Other Support; higher score means greater support. A total score between 12 and 36 is considered low support, 37 and 60 is considered moderate support, and 61 and above is considered high support [29].

Coping style

The Simplified Coping Style Questionnaire (SCSQ) was used, which consists of 20 items with two dimensions of positive coping and negative coping [30]. The positive coping dimension has a total score of 36 and the negative coping dimension has a total score of 24.

Caregiving burden

The Zarit Caregiver Burden Interview (ZBI) was used, which is a 22-item scale divided into two dimensions: personal burden and burden of responsibility, and all the entries were scored on a 5-point Likert scale [31]. Based on the value of the score, it can be divided into four levels, with 0–19 representing no burden or little burden, 20–39 being mild burden, 40–59 being moderate caregiving burden, and 60 and above being severe burden.

Data analysis

The data was processed using SPSS 25.0 and ProcessV4.0. This study used the Harman single-factor test to examine whether all items related to the study variables exhibited common method bias. The Harman single-factor test is a statistical method that uses exploratory factor analysis to detect common method bias. In the descriptive statistics section, variables were summarized using composition ratios and mean±standard deviations, this analysis served solely to present the basic characteristics and distribution of the sample.In inferential statistics and modeling analysis, to examine differences and relationships among variables, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare differences in caregiving burden across groups. Pearson correlation analysis was employed to assess associations between social support, coping styles, and caregiving burden. Subsequently, the factors influencing the caregiving burden were examined through multiple linear regression analysis. Mediation effects were tested using Bootstrap sampling (Process v4.0) to validate the mediating roles of social support and coping styles within the theoretical model. (Given that the research framework focuses solely on testing mediating pathways to validate the specific hypotheses proposed in this paper, moderation effects analysis was not incorporated. Consequently, statistical diagnostics specific to interaction terms—such as multicollinearity and residual normality tests—were not conducted).

Mediation analysis

This study employed the process plugin in SPSS 25.0 for validation, conducting 5,000 bootstrap sampling analyses to calculate 95% confidence intervals and output non-standardized coefficients. The model examines the hypothesized pathway where social support serves as the independent variable and caregiving burden as the dependent variable, testing whether the independent variable influences caregiving burden through the mediating influences of positive coping and negative coping. Variables associated with caregiving burden identified in the differential testing of sociodemographic characteristics among research subjects are incorporated as covariates for both pathways. As shown in Models 1, 2, and 3 of Table 6, social support is associated with caregiving burden, positive coping, and negative coping. (β = -0.721, P < 0.001, β = 0.185, P < 0.001, β = -0.132, P < 0.001). Social support is negatively associated with caregiving burden, positively associated with positive coping, and negatively associated with negative coping. As shown in Model 4 of Table 6, when coping styles were included as a mediating variable in the regression model, the negative association between social support and caregiving burden remained significant (β = -0.447, P < 0.001). Social support and positive coping were negatively associated with caregiving burden (β = -0.447, P < 0.001, and β = -0.601, P < 0.001), while negative coping was positively associated with caregiving burden (β = 1.231, P < 0.001). As shown in Table 7, the mediating influence of coping style was 38.01% of the total indirect association between social support and coping style, with a 95% confidence range of [-0.349, -0.191]. The proportion of positive coping was 15.40%, with a 95% confidence interval of [-0.216, -0.114], and the proportion of negative coping was 22.61%, with a 95% confidence interval of [-0.165, -0.055]. Since none of these intervals included zero, a significant mediating influence was confirmed.

Discussion

This study investigated the interrelationships between social support, coping styles, and caregiving burden among family caregivers of older adults with disabilities in metropolitan locations, and provided a model for coping styles’ mediation function in the link between social support and caregiving burden. In this study, the caregiving burden of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities was moderate, with more than 90% of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities having a caregiving burden to varying degrees, which is basically consistent with the findings of Liu et al. [32], and similar to the findings of the study of caregivers of advanced cancer patients at home in China [33]. This shows that caregiving burden is pervasive, and that older adults with disabilities rely heavily on their family caregivers, who are under enormous pressure as they try to manage job and caregiving. The caregiving burden scores of family caregivers exhibited statistically significant differences across marital status, chronic disease status, kinship relationships, per capita monthly household income, duration of caregiving, coping styles, and social support. Additionally, the burden of caregiving is associated with the baseline condition of caregivers, encompassing complex and variable external factors.

According to the study’s findings, 47.4% of people had social support at the medium support level, and family caregivers of older adults with disabilities had generally better social support status, with the understanding that social support was primarily derived from family support. These findings were consistent with those of a study by Zhou et al. [34]. The “hierarchical complementary delivery” from near too far and from close to distant, according to Fei Xiaotong’s “differential order pattern” theory, characterizes the individual help-seeking sequence. As a result, there is a distinct division of labor among family caregivers, family caregiving generally follows the sequence of close relatives, distant relatives, friends, and society. Spouses and children serve as primary family caregivers, while distant relatives, friends, government agencies, and community organizations typically function as secondary family caregivers, the caregiver’s family offers more easily accessible support [35]. Chinese LTCI program aims to relieve the financial and caregiving burdens, yet its effectiveness in reducing caregiving burden remains an area for further exploration. As of November 30, 2021, 2,776,800 urban and rural inhabitants who were at least 18 years old, as well as 8,954,000 employees, were covered by Guangzhou’s LTCI system [36]. The hypothesis theory states that social support has a significant negative impact on caregiving burden. This finding has gained widespread recognition. A study by Kramer et al. [37] found that an individual’s social support, or access to outside resources, is a major factor in the care outcomes of informal caregivers for older adults. Different forms of social support significantly reduce the caregiver’s burden, according to research by Han et al. [38]. As a result, social support is associated with the stress levels of family caregivers.The study’s findings indicate that coping styles are not only directly associated with caregiving burden but also mediate the relationship between social support and caregiving burden, with the mediating effect accounting for 38.01%, which is consistent with the findings of the study on primary caregivers of patients with chronic heart failure. It has been demonstrated that caregiving burden for family caregivers of older adults with disabilities does not fall directly on the individual, but rather on how the individual responds and how to make effective use of social support, which can help to control and alleviate the level of caregiving burden if a positive and objective response is taken, but may pose a threat to physical and mental health if a negative response is chosen. Training programs that enhance problem-solving skills and emotional resilience could help family caregivers adopt positive coping styles, thereby reducing their perceived burden. This, in turn, encourages the individual to adopt a positive coping styles [39]. Consequently, family caregivers of older adults with disabilities may find that adopting positive coping styles help reduce feelings of burden. The purpose of this study’s findings is to offer empirical references and lessons for enhancing family care quality, optimizing policies, and long-term dealing with population aging. The caregiving burden of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities could be reduced by focusing on improving social support and altering coping mechanisms. A national policy framework should provide direct financial assistance, paid caregiver leave, and flexible work policies to support family caregivers [40]. In order to support family care policies, resources must be made available in a number of areas, such as employment support and systemic adjustments to household registration. The latter is exemplified by paid nursing leave and flexible work schedules. Based on national conditions in China, it is imperative to strengthen the bonds between family members, raise awareness of gender equality in family care, foster a peaceful home environment, emphasize love and respect among family members as well as mutual assistance, and strike a balance between the rights and interests of multigenerational family members [41]. At the community level, there is a need to strengthen the building of community cohesion and reinforce the sense of identity of the same community. In addition, the community can target interventions and guidance according to their family situation and educational background, so that they can agree from the bottom of their hearts that negative coping does not help to reduce caregiving burden, and motivate individuals to adopt relatively positive coping styles [42].

However, this study has certain limitations that require future improvement. As a cross-sectional design, it examines only the relationship between variables at a specific point in time and cannot establish strict causal relationships or temporal sequences between variables. The respondents filled out the questionnaire themselves, which may have resulted in subjective bias. Since the questionnaire survey was only conducted in the Guangzhou region and did not cover the entire country, the study’s findings cannot be applied to other regions, and more research is required to see whether the findings can be applied in other contexts. In data analysis, statistical adjustments for design characteristics of stratified cluster random sampling (such as cluster effects) were not performed, this may result in an excessively small standard error, potentially leading to an overestimation of the p-value and confidence interval in hypothesis testing. Therefore, the relevant findings should be regarded as preliminary or exploratory conclusions. Additionally, while we employed the Harman single-factor test for preliminary screening, this test is generally regarded as a basic screening tool that cannot fully eliminate common method bias.

Despite these limitations, the preliminary findings of this study provide insights for future research. This study, from the viewpoint of family caregivers of older adults with disabilities uses the social support buffer model to highlight the mediating effect of coping styles and refocuses attention on the support that the family care and social support system needs. This approach yields more comprehensive and in-depth research findings, with results demonstrating representativeness and forward-looking value within Guangzhou’s sampling framework. It provides an analytical perspective and basis of considerable reference value for future studies on social support systems for family caregivers of elderly individuals with disabilities in similar contexts, such as other relevant cities or regions within China.

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicate that family caregivers of older adults with disabilities in central urban areas of Guangzhou, China, bear varying degrees of caregiving burden. Social support is not only directly associated with caregiving burden but also indirectly linked through coping styles. Strong social support helps alleviate caregiving burden and promotes the adoption of positive coping styles. Therefore, to alleviate the caregiving burden on family caregivers of older adults with disabilities, efforts must be undertaken from governmental, community, and family perspectives. This approach aims to reduce the burden on caregivers while simultaneously enhancing the level and quality of family care.

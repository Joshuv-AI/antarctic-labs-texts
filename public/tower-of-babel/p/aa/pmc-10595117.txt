Maternal healthy lifestyle score and offspring birth outcomes: secondary analyses of the PEARS RCT
Authors: K Philippe, F M McAuliffe, C M Phillips
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe influence of maternal lifestyle behaviours during pregnancy on offspring outcomes has been investigated individually. A composite Healthy Lifestyle Score (HLS) comprising low-risk protective lifestyle behaviours can be useful for studying links between overall lifestyle and health outcomes. We aimed to study links between HLS and its components in early and late pregnancy with birth outcomes among women with overweight and obesity (OWOB), as their offspring may be at increased risk for adverse birth outcomes.MethodsSecondary analyses were conducted with data from the PEARS (Pregnancy Exercise And nutrition Research Study) trial, a RCT of a diet and exercise lifestyle intervention to prevent gestational diabetes. HLSs (scored 0-5) based on information about maternal diet (AHEI-P), exercise (MET-minutes), alcohol consumption, smoking, and sleep habits were created for 334 and 289 mothers in early (14-16 weeks gestation) and late pregnancy (28 weeks gestation), respectively. Higher HLSs indicate a healthier overall lifestyle. Regression analyses examined associations of HLSs and individual lifestyle factors with adverse birth outcomes; low birthweight (BW), macrosomia and being born small or large for gestational age (SGA or LGA).ResultsMean HLS was 3.38 in early, and 3.19 in late pregnancy. Only 12% and 10% of mothers had the maximum HLS in early and late pregnancy. Preliminary results indicated no strong links between composite HLSs and birth outcomes. Examination of the individual factors revealed that not smoking in early and late pregnancy was linked to a decreased risk for SGA (OR: 0.23, 95% CI: 0.07-0.74, p < 0.01; OR: 0.12, 95% CI: 0.04-0.35, p < 0.01) and low BW (OR: 0.11, 95% CI: 0.02-0.62, p = 0.01; OR: 0.10, 95% CI: 0.02-0.57, p = 0.01) (unadjusted results).ConclusionsAdherence to a healthy lifestyle among pregnant women with OWOB can be improved. Our results highlight the importance of smoking cessation in pregnancy to avoid adverse birth outcomes.Key messages• Many pregnant women with OWOB do not adhere to a healthy lifestyle.• Smoking cessation in pregnancy is important to reduce the risk of adverse birth outcomes.

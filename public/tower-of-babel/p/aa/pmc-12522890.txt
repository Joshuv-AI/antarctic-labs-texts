Three intronic variants altering RNA splicing were identified in the CLCN5 gene by minigene assay
Authors: Dan Qiao, Xuyan Liu, Irene Bottillo, Ran Zhang, Leping Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Dent disease 1 is a rarely inherited renal tubular disease caused by variants in the CLCN5 gene. Increasing evidence suggests that many intronic variants can affect the normal splicing of pre-mRNA by altering various splicing regulatory signals. Therefore, this study aims to provide novel insights into the impact of intronic variants of CLCN5 on pre-mRNA splicing.MethodsWe analyzed intronic variants of the CLCN5 gene from the Clinvar database, selected four candidate variants using bioinformatics analysis, and identified candidate variants that may alter the splicing of pre-mRNA through minigene assays.ResultsOur study revealed that, among four candidate variants, three intronic variants in CLCN5 resulted in splicing alterations(c.603 + 4 A > G, c.727–3 C > A, and c.933 + 3 A > C). Intronic variants c.603 + 4 A > G and c.727–3 C > A induce aberrant splicing that result in partial exon deletions by activating cryptic donor and acceptor splice sites, respectively. In contrast, the c.933 + 3 A > C variant disrupts the classical donor splice site of exon 7, leading to complete exon skipping.ConclusionsTo our knowledge, this is a comprehensive study regarding alterations in pre-mRNA of intronic variants in the causal gene CLCN5 of Dent disease. In addition, we propose the importance of assessing the effect of intronic single nucleotide substitutions on CLCN5 at the mRNA level in the absence of patient RNA samples and show that minigenes functional analysis is valuable for assessing the effect of splicing in vitro.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-025-02230-4.

Background

Dent disease is a rare X-linked recessive hereditary renal tubular disease characterized by proximal tubular dysfunction [1, 2]. The main manifestations include low molecular weight (LMW) proteinuria, hypercalciuria, nephrolithiasis and progressive renal disfunction, etc. Dent disease is mainly caused by mutations in the CLCN5 gene that accounts for about 60% of cases and is known as Dent disease 1 [3]. CLCN5 gene encodes the chloride channel Cl−/H+ exchanger ClC-5, a transmembrane protein involved in the regulation of cell volume, membrane potential, and interepithelial transport [4, 5]. Variants in the CLCN5 gene can result in the loss of CLC-5 protein function, further contribute to a range of clinical abnormalities [6].

Precursor messenger RNA (pre-mRNA) splicing is a crucial process for ensuring the accuracy of gene expression [7]. An increasing number of human diseases caused by RNA mis-splicing have been identified with serious consequences [8]. The majority of mutation splicing analyses of the CLCN5 gene concentrated on exons and classical splice sites in previous studies [9]. However, the impact of intronic mutations on the pre-mRNA splicing process has been largely ignored [10], which also play a significant role in pathogenicity of inherited diseases. In this study, we explored the effect of intronic mutations in CLCN5 on pre-mRNA splicing using bioinformatic tool and the minigene assay.

Methods

Variant collection and filtering

Variants in the CLCN5 gene (NM_001127898.4) reported as Dent disease 1 were collected from the Clinvar database and literature reports [11, 12]. The variants were analyzed using the bioinformatics software - BDGP (http://www.fruitfly.org), which was used to investigate the potential effects of variants on classic 5′ donor sites or 3′ acceptor sites and/or to predict the generation of new sites and/or activation of cryptic sites. The variants located outside 5′ and 3′ splice site (5′ss and 3′ss) and decreased the scores of either 5′ss or 3′ss were selected.

Minigene construction and mutagenesis

The construction of the minigene has been described previously [13]. The target DNA fragments were amplified using specific primers (Table S1). Then the target fragments were connected to the pSPL3 splicing vector to construct the WT minigene (Figure S1). Site-directed mutagenesis was carried out using Prime STAR Max premix as described in the manufacturer’s instructions (TaKaRa).

Analysis of minigenes

HEK 293 T and HeLa cells both were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, USA). These two kinds of cells were grown in DMEM medium (Procell, China) supplementing with 10% fetal bovine serum (Procell, China), penicillin (100 U/L, Procell), and streptomycin (100 mg/L, Procell). Cell cultures were incubated at 37 ℃ and 5% CO2 in a humidified incubator. One day before transfection, both cells were transferred to a 12-well culture plate, where they grew to approximately 70–80% fusion in an antibiotic-free medium. Plasmids (empty pSPL3 control (EV), pSPL3-WT and pSPL3-Mutation) were transfected to Human embryonic kidney 293 T (HEK293T) and HeLa cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, United States). After 48 h, we extracted total RNA using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). RNA is reversely transcribed into cDNA by Superscript II Reverse Transcriptase (Invitrogen Corporation). The targeted region was amplified by PCR. The PCR products were separated by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis. Each band was quantified by Image J software and analyzed by Sanger DNA sequencing. In the Sanger-sequencing approach, amplified DNA or cDNA is extended by DNA polymerase. When ddNTPs are incorporated, the addition of the limiting concentration of ddNTPs will stop the elongation reaction, thereby generating distinguishable DNA fragments of different lengths. Then, they were compared and analyzed with the human reference genome. All experiments were run in triplicate in HEK293T and HeLa cells, respectively. The variant was considered to affect splicing only when the splicing pattern was different from the WT minigene in both cell Lines. Finally, the structure of the mutated proteins was predicted using AlphaFold 3, and all structures were visualized in PyMOL (PyMOL Molecular GraphicsSystem, Version 3.0.2, Schrödinger LLc, New York, NY, USA).

Statistical analysis

The percentage of aberrant band (%) = (aberrant band/all bands) × 100. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism (Version 8.0.1; GraphPad Software). The results were analyzed using the two-tailed Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA test. Error bars represent SEM (n = 3). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The single-nucleotide variants we collected were analyzed using bioinformatics analysis, and we eliminated variants located in the first and last exon/intron because they could not be analyzed by minigene assays. Analyzes were performed using BDGP to select splice variants with potential effects on cis-acting elements such as 5’ donor or 3’ acceptor splice sites and further minigene analyzes were performed to predict whether they affect splicing [14].

In summary, four candidate variants were enrolled in the study: c.416–6 A > T, c.603 + 4 A > G, c.727–3 C > A, and c.933 + 3 A > C, as shown in Table 1 and Figures S1. The minigenes were successfully constructed. 3 of 4 variants were verified to alter pre-mRNA splicing by minigene splicing assays in HEK293T and Hela cells. All RT-PCR products were sequenced by sanger sequencing (Fig. 1).

Variant c.603 + 4 A > G caused skipping of exon 5

The variant c.603 + 4 A > G, positioned at the intronic region downstream of exon 5, was predicted by BDGP to weaken the canonical donor splice site strength (reducing the prediction score from 0.91 to 0.48) (Table 1). In order to validate the splicing alterations in variant c.603 + 4 A > G, we constructed the wild-type minigene (572 bp) containing exon 5 and surrounding intron sequences, and variant minigenes containing the single nucleotide substitution c.603 + 4 A > G. Minigene assays showed that variant c.603 + 4 A > G produced two different fragments of 320 bp and 263 bp, the larger fragment containing an incomplete exon 5 with a 131 bp deletion at the 3’ end of exon 5, and the smaller skipping exon 5 (Fig. 2A). The final sequencing analysis revealed that the variant c.603 + 4 A > G disrupted the normal splicing double strand (DS), and it could simultaneously activate two recessive 5’ss located upstream of the natural DS, which is situated upstream of the natural splicing site, leading to a frameshift deletion of the last 131 bp of exon 5 (44.65%/42.30% in Fig. 2A) and a complete skipping of exon 5 (55.35%/57.70% in Fig. 2A), resulting in generating truncated protein (p.Ile159Ser fs*40 and p.Ala142Cys fs*38, Table 1).

Variant c.727–3 C > A alter pre-mRNA splicing of exon 7

The single nucleotide substitution c.727–3 C > A is located on intron 3 bp upstream of exon 7 in the CLCN5 gene, which was predicted by BDGP severely impair splice site recognition, with the acceptor site score decreasing from 0.82 (wild-type) to 0.07 (Table 1).

The RT-PCR products revealed that the wild-type plasmid produced a single 470 bp fragment containing of completed exon 7, while the variant c.727-3C > A consisted of two distinct bands of 438 bp and 382 bp. The larger band included an incomplete exon 7 with a 32-bp deletion at the 5’ end of exon 7, while the smaller one contained an 88-bp deletion at the 5’ end of exon 7 (Fig. 2B). After the final sequencing results, we analyzed and found that the transcripts produced by this variant contained a frameshift deletion of the first 32 bp of exon 7 (71.49%/61.90% in Fig. 2B) and a frameshift deletion of the first 88 bp of exon 7 (28.51%/38.10% in Fig. 2B) by activating different acceptor sites, resulting in generating truncated protein (p.Ile243Gly fs*5 and p.Ile243Cys fs*5, Table 1).

Variant c.933 + 3 A > C resulted in complete skipping of exon 7

The variant c.933 + 3 A > C in CLCN5 gene, which is positioned at the terminal intron of exon 7, was predicted by BDGP to significantly reduce the score of the donor splice site from 0.85 to 0.04 (Table 1). The results of minigene splicing assays confirmed the mutant plasmid generated a product distinct from the wild-type but matching the size of EV (Fig. 2B). Direct sequencing indicated that the variant c.933 + 3 A > C resulted in complete skipping of exon 7 (Fig. 2B) and contributed to an in-frame deletions (codon 243–311) (Table 1).

Variant c.416–6 A > T did not alter pre-mRNA splicing

The variant c.416–6 A > T is located at the − 6 position of the acceptor splice site preceding exon 5, and BDGP analysis showed slightly reduction in the acceptor splice site score from 0.85 to 0.81 (Table 1). However, the RT-PCR products of the minigene containing the variant c.416–6 A > T matched in size with the corresponding WT (Fig. 2C). The single nucleotide mutation c.416–6 A > T may not disturb 3′ss of intron 4 and not affect splicing according to minigene assays.

Discussion

Precise pre-mRNA splicing is essential for DNA transcription and protein translation [15]. Genetic mutations resulted in aberrant splicing patterns are considered to be an important cause of some inherited diseases [16]. It is critical to evaluated whether exonic mutations or intronic mutations disrupted the correct splicing patterns. So far, many exonic mutations affecting splice sites have been identified by conducting mRNA-level analysis, while intronic mutations should also be confirmed. Moreover, majority of reported sequence variants are rare, and their pathogenicity is based only on bioinformatic prediction which is limited for variants outside coding regions. Prediction of the functional impact is straightforward for frameshift mutations, stop mutations and canonical splice-site mutations resulting in truncation of protein but is more difficult for variants outside the conserved sequences at exon–intron junctions with an unclear functional effect [17]. The minigene assay has proven to be a reliable tool for testing variant-splicing outcomes in the absence of patient RNA [18, 19], which has been widely verified in our previous studies [20, 21].

In the CLCN5 gene, according to the records in the public database, missense mutations account for approximately 50%, while splice site mutations account for about 10% [22], and our ability to predict whether a variant will affect pre-mRNA splicing remains limited. In the process of pre-mRNA splicing, some specific sequences play an important role in determining the location of splicing. In our study, variants c.603 + 4 A > G and c.933 + 3 A > C was respectively located close to the 5′ss of intron 5 and intron 7, which all destroyed the normal splice DS. The recognized intensity of proximal cryptic splice site was slightly stronger than that of distal one. This may be related to the mechanism of the preferential selection in the proximal DS [23, 24]. Variant c.727–3 C > A positioned close to the 3′ss of intron 6 may affect splicing by breaking original splice AS and wiping out the recognition of the 3′ss, concurrently activating two cryptic acceptor sites. This mutation created two new 'AG' dinucleotides and could compete with the natural one. Generally, the upstream polypyrimidine tract and branch point sites (BPS) were used to the competing 3′-AGs in close proximity. The first 'AG' dinucleotides located downstream of the BPS is discerned first and gained a dominant position in the competition [25]. Our result of study further verified that splicing regulation of the mutants is mainly dominated by the first 'AG' dinucleotides created newly.

The results of bioinformatics analysis showed that the four variations, c.416–6 A > T, c.603 + 4 A > G, c.727–3 C > A, and c.933 + 3 A > C, significantly altered the splicing strength of the 5'ss or 3'ss. The minigene analysis indicated that C.603 + 4 A > G, C.727-3c > A and C.933 + 3 A > C affected the splicing process of the CLCN5 gene. These variations might cause partial or complete skipping of exons. This led to changes in protein length or the formation of truncated proteins, which impacted the stability of the complex (Fig. 3). The c.603 + 4 A > G variant was associated with a characteristic clinical phenotype in the patients, specifically low molecular weight proteinuria (LMWP). Unfortunately, other potential clinical manifestations could not be definitively assessed. In the original study, further analysis could not be conducted because the RNA samples were missing. Therefore, our minigene splicing assay demonstrated that the c.603 + 4 A > G mutation causes the complete or partial skipping of the fifth exon, and leads to the production of a truncated protein (Table 1), which may ultimately result in the patient developing LMWP and other symptoms. Patients carrying the c.727–3 C > A variant exhibit a clinical phenotype characterized by LMWP, hypercalciuria, renal impairment, nephrocalcinosis and nephrolithiasis. The RNA sequencing analysis of this patient indicated that this variation led to a complete exon skipping, which might result in the truncation of CLC-5 and the loss of its function. Our experimental findings demonstrate that the c.727–3 C > A variant induces a frameshift deletion in exon 7, leading to the production of a truncated CLC-5 protein. Our results show some differences, but they are similar to those of Tomohiko Inoue [26]. This discrepancy may be derived from the difference between in vivo and in vitro minigene assays using cultured cells. Functional analysis at the protein level showed that partial or complete skipping of exon 5 or exon 7 resulted in protein truncation (Table 1), which affected the CLC-5 protein to lack many important structural domains, including the CBS structural domain and other parts of the CLC-5 protein (Fig. 3b, c), and subsequently preventing CLC-5 from performing its normal physiological functions in the kidney.

Regarding the c.933 + 3 A > C and c.416–6 A > T variants, limited clinical data are available, and both their molecular mechanisms and definitive pathogenicity remain unclear. Our experimental results demonstrate that the variant c.933 + 3 A > C will result in the deletion of the 7th exon, causing the loss of 69 amino acids from position I243 to G311(Fig. 3d). This will affect the transfer of protons from the external aqueous phase to the interior of the protein, and will impact the transport of H+ and Cl−. In contrast, the c.416–6 A > T variant does not appear to disrupt the consensus 3′ss of intron 4 and is not expected to affect splicing. Thus, our research further determined the pathogenicity of the variation. Accurate characterization of the mutation provides critical insights into the disease’s pathogenesis, progression, and prognosis.

Notably, we cannot ignore the limitations of the minigene assay. The minigene assay may not fully mimic situation in vivo due to the complexity of the regulatory mechanism [27, 28]. Additionally, if the measurement is conducted in cells expressing CLCN5, our results may also vary. It is recommended to analyze RNA samples from patients, further to determine the pathogenicity of variants. Unfortunately, this study lacked RNA sample analysis from patients with these variants. Furthermore, splicing is complex and in some cases cell type specific. Present results of the minigene assay are dependent on the HEK293 and Hela cell systems, and more variants might have functional effects if another cell system were to be used.

As a fundamental step in human gene expression, RNA splicing can generate different mRNA isoform [29], but also affect the process of protein biosynthesis. Therefore, we recommend that more attention be paid to their pathogenicity and potential therapeutic approaches. So far, antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapies targeting exon skipping/restoration have shown potential therapeutic efficacy for a variety of genetic diseases [30, 31]. One of the methods is ASOs targeting the mutated pre-mRNAs and rendering them for degradation. Therapeutics with ASOs has also been promising for splice mutations by blocking the recognition sequence of aberrant splice sites. The research and application of ASOs in the urinary system will be highly anticipated in the near future [32, 33].

Conclusions

In conclusion, our results demonstrated that the three intronic variants including c.603 + 4 A > G, c.727–3 C > A and c.933 + 3 A > C were confirmed to alter pre-mRNA splicing probably due to disturb the 5′ss or 3′ss. This study emphasized the importance of assessing effects of DNA variants on the RNA level, as well as the effectiveness of minigene analysis, which help us better understand the pathogenicity of Dent disease 1. Therefore, according to the standards and guidelines for the interpretation of sequence variants [34]. The experimental proof that CLCN5 variants in Dent patients affect protein function is crucial for their inclusion to future clinical trials. This information is also valuable for genetic counselling of the families with intronic mutations present. For the same purpose, functional testing of all CLCN5 intronic variants associated with Stargardt disease is desirable.

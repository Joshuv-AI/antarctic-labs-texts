Combining chicory root pulp with yeast selenium: a novel strategy for improving egg quality, enhancing immune function, and promoting gut health in laying hens
Authors: Shuyuan Wang, Yanfang Peng, Xiaoguang Liu, Siyu Wang, Mengyuan Feng, Ruijie Wang, Haixiang Qin, Hanqi Gu, Jingwen Hu, Xiaoguo Qian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium improved egg quality.•5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium increased the nutritional content of eggs.•5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium enhanced immune function.•5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium optimized the gut microbiota.•5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium reduced the harm of virulence factor.

Introduction

Against the backdrop of the global poultry and egg industry's transition towards efficiency and greenness, laying hens farming is facing multiple challenges in improving egg quality, enhancing physical health, and reducing environmental burden. As a core means of regulating the physiological functions of laying hens, the balance between safety and functionality of feed additives has become a research focus.

Chicory is rich in active ingredients such as inulin, polyphenols, and dietary fiber. Among them, inulin can proliferate beneficial bacteria in the intestine to regulate the microbial balance, enhance the absorption of minerals such as Ca, Mg, and Fe by the body, and also have immunomodulatory effects (Perović et al., 2021). Polyphenols have antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial properties (Birsa and Sarbu, 2023). Dietary fiber can promote intestinal peristalsis and maintain gut health (Janda et al., 2021). The chicory root pulp used in this experiment is the residue after extracting inulin from chicory, which still contains some active ingredients and has high potential utilization value.

Selenium is one of the essential trace elements for animal organisms. Yeast selenium, as an organic selenium source, is an easily absorbable organic form formed through yeast biotransformation, which can improve the bioavailability of selenium and avoid the toxicity of inorganic selenium (Sun et al., 2023a). Research has shown that yeast selenium can improve egg quality, increase selenium content in food, regulate gut microbiota, and have detoxifying effects on mycotoxins and heavy metals (Liao et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023, 2023a; Sun et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024). Therefore, yeast selenium may play an important role in laying hens farming.

Based on this, this study systematically analyzed the effects of different doses of chicory root pulp and yeast selenium combination on the production performance, egg quality, egg nutrition, immune function, gut microbiota, antibiotic resistance, and virulence factors of laying hens through feeding experiments, screened the optimal compatibility scheme, and provided theoretical basis for the development of "natural plant extracts + organic trace elements" composite additives. The research results can not only enrich the theoretical system of nutritional regulation for laying hens, but also provide scientific guidance for breeding enterprises to optimize feed formulas and enhance the added value of egg products, helping laying hens farming to transform and upgrade towards environmental friendliness and quality safety. At the same time, it meets the policy needs of "reducing resistance and replacing resistance" and the development trend of functional poultry egg market, which has important theoretical value and practical significance.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

The experiment was conducted according to the guidelines of the National Act on the Use of Experimental Animals (People's Republic of China) and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Hebei Minzu Normal University.

Experimental design and feeding management

Chicory root pulp, the residue obtained from extracting inulin from chicory, taken from Fengning Ping'an High tech Industrial Co., Ltd., Chengde, China. Powdered yeast selenium (selenium ≥ 2000 ppm) was purchased from Shanghai Yuanye Bio-Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China. The chicory root pulp was first crushed into particles of approximately 2 ± 0.01 mm using an integrated crushing and mixing machine (500 kg 15 kw + 3 kw, Xinxiang Nongda Feed Machinery Co., Ltd., Xinxiang, China), and then proportionally mixed with powdered yeast selenium and basal diet for feeding laying hens. In September 2024, the feeding experiment was conducted at Chengde Muzhan Breeding Co., Ltd., Chengde, China. A total of 96 22-week-old Hy-Line Sonia laying hens with good physical condition and similar weight were selected and randomly divided into 6 groups with 4 replicates each, housing 4 chickens per cage. Based on the results of the preliminary pre-experiment, the control group was fed a basic diet. Groups 0, 5, 10, 15, and 20 were fed with a basic diet and 0.5 mg/kg powdered yeast selenium, followed by 0 %, 5 %, 10 %, 15 %, and 20 % granular chicory root pulp, respectively. The experimental diet with nutritional composition is shown in Table 1. The experimental diets were formulated according to the NRC (1994) recommendations for laying hens. The poultry house was provided with 16 h of natural daylight per day, with the temperature ranging from 16 ± 0.50 °C to 22 ± 0.50 °C and relative humidity ranging from 45 ± 0.50 % to 55 ± 0.50 %. Laying hens were raised in separate cages and immunized according to routine procedures, with free access to food and water. The experiment lasted 56 days. After fasting for 12 h, laying hens were euthanized for sample collection at week 4 (groups control-Week 4, 0-Week 4, 5-Week 4, 10-Week 4, 15-Week 4, and 20-Week 4) and week 8 (groups control-Week 8, 0-Week 8, 5-Week 8, 10-Week 8, 15-Week 8, and 20-Week 8), respectively. Technical roadmap.

Production performance

The number of eggs and egg weight were recorded every day to determine the average egg weight. The defective egg rate was calculated by dividing the number of defective eggs by the total number of eggs. The egg production rate was calculated based on the number of eggs divided by the number of laying hens. The feed conversion rate (FCR) was calculated by dividing feed intake by egg mass. Feed intake was recorded every day. Egg mass was calculated by multiplying the egg production rate by the average egg weight and divided by 100 (Han et al., 2023).

Egg quality

Eight eggs from each group were collected for egg quality assessment. The egg shape index was calculated from the short/long diameter of the egg (Gunjevic et al., 2023). Eggshell strength was determined using an eggshell strength tester (DET 6000, Nabel, Kyoto, Japan) (Sun et al., 2023b). After the inner membrane was removed from the eggshell, a vernier caliper (N15P, Mitutoyo, Kawasaki, Japan) was used to measure the thickness at the blunt, middle, and sharp ends of the egg. The average of these measurements was calculated to obtain the eggshell thickness (Sun et al., 2023b). Egg yolk colors ranging from level 1 (light yellow) to level 15 (deep red) were obtained by Roche Yolk Colour Fan (2019, Robotmation, Tokyo, Japan) (Romero et al., 2024). Haugh units = 100·Log (H - 1.7W+7.57), where H = albumen height (mm) and W = egg weight (g). Albumen height and egg weight were measured using a height gauge (1086R-HR, Mahr, Esslingen, Germany) and an electronic balance (LA204E/A, Mettler toledo, Greifensee, Switzerland), respectively (Romero et al., 2024).

Egg Nutritional Content

Eight eggs from each group were collected. The albumen and yolk of each egg were thoroughly homogenized for nutritional content analysis. The protein content of whole eggs was determined by Kjeldahl method. The sample (10 ± 0.01 g) was digested with copper sulfate, potassium sulfate, and sulfuric acid on an adjustable hot plate (KDN-12C, Shanghai Xinjia Electronic Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The digestate was then subjected to distillation, absorption, and titration using a Kjeldahl apparatus (KDN-08C, Xinrui, Shanghai, China) (Araújo et al., 2022).

The fat content was determined by Soxhlet extraction. The sample (5 ± 0.01 g) was subjected to thorough drying at 105°C, crushed, and then wrapped with filter paper. The wrapped powder was put into a Soxhlet extraction tube for crude fat extraction with anhydrous ether (Gao et al., 2024).

Calcium content was detected by EDTA titration method according to the previous research with little modifications (Kim and Vipulanandan, 2003). In brief, the sample (1 ± 0.01 mL) was digested with nitric acid and perchloric acid on an adjustable hot plate (KDN-12C, Shanghai Xinjia Electronic Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) at 180°C for 6 h. One drop of 10 g/L sodium sulfide and 0.1 ± 0.01 mL of 0.05 mol/L sodium citrate were added into 0.5 ± 0.01 mL of 100.0 mg/L calcium standard stock solution, then 1.5 ± 0.01 mL of 1.25 mol/L potassium hydroxide solution and 3 drops of calcium red indicator were added. After that the diluted 10 times of EDTA solution was used to titrate immediately until the indicator changes from purple-red to blue, and record the consumed volume of the EDTA. Based on the titration result, calculate the milligrams of calcium in the sample.

Phosphorus content was quantified utilizing molybdenum blue spectrophotometry method. The sample (1 ± 0.01 mL) was digested with a mixture of nitric acid, perchloric acid, and sulfuric acid on an adjustable hot plate (KDN-12C, Shanghai Xinjia Electronic Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The digestate was then reduced with hydroquinone and sodium sulfite solution, and its absorbance was finally measured by a visible spectrophotometer (722S, Jinghua, Shanghai, China) (Lavedrine et al., 2000).

The selenium content was determined by fluorescence spectrophotometry method. The sample (2 ± 0.01 mL) was digested with a mixture of nitric acid and perchloric acid (V: V = 9: 1) for 12 h, followed by sequential digestion with nitric acid and hydrochloric acid on an electric hot plate. Selenium content was determined by fluorescence spectrophotometry (F-7000, Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) (Gao et al., 2024).

Immunoglobulin and cytokine levels

5 ± 0.01 mL of wing vein blood was collected from six laying hens per group. The blood sampled were centrifuged at 3500 x g (Sorvall™ Legend™ Micro 21R, Thermo Scientific, Shanghai, China) at 4 °C for 15 min to obtain serum. The levels of immunoglobulin G (IgG), immunoglobulin M (IgM), interleukin-2 (IL-2), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) in serum were measured using ELISA kits (JL21625-48T, JL21628-48T, JL10953-48T, JL13475-48T, Shanghai Jonlnbio Industrial Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) by microplate reader (Multiskan FC, Thermo Scientific, Shanghai, China).

Metagenomic sequencing

The collected cecal chyme was placed in a 2 mL sterile EP tube and sent to Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for library construction and metagenomic sequencing. The raw reads were subjected to quality evaluation, reads filtering, and effective reads statistics using Fastp 0.36 to remove contaminated sequences from both the human body and the provided host genome, resulting in relatively accurate clean reads. Clean reads were assembled and relatively complete contigs were obtained. Open reading frames of contigs were predicted, and genes with a length greater than or equal to 100 bp were selected as the candidate gene set. A non-redundant gene set was constructed by clustering the predicted gene sequences from all samples with CD-HIT-2.60 and selecting the longest sequence within each cluster as the representative. The clean reads of each sample were compared with the non-redundant gene set to obtain the number of reads of the aligned genes and calculate the abundance of each gene in the sample based on its length. The abundance and diversity of microbial communities were assessed via alpha diversity analysis. The gene set was compared with the ARDB and VFDB database to obtain the corresponding antibiotic resistance gene and virulence factor information (Wang et al., 2022).

Data analysis

The experimental data was preliminarily processed using Microsoft Excel, and then subjected to significance analysis using SPSS 26.0 (P < 0.05), and plotted using Origin 8.5.

Egg nutritional content

As depicted in Fig. 2, the protein content in groups 0-Week 4, 5-Week 4, 10-Week 4, 15-Week 4 and 20-Week 4 was significantly higher than in group control-Week 4 (P < 0.05). Similarly, group 5-Week 8 exhibited a significantly higher protein content compared to groups control-Week 8 and 0-Week 8 (P < 0.05). Furthermore, the phosphorus content in group 5 was also significantly elevated relative to groups control and 0 in both results of week 4 and week 8 (P < 0.05). These results implied that the combined feeding of 5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium was most effective in enhancing both the protein and phosphorus content of eggs. The fat content in group 5-Week 4 was significantly lower than in group control-Week 4 (P < 0.05), indicating that short-term combined feeding of 5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium reduced the fat content of eggs. Compared with other groups, the fat content in group 10-Week 8 increased (P < 0.05), while that in group 15-Week 8 decreased (P < 0.05). These results demonstrated that long-term combined feeding of 10 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium increased the fat content of eggs, whereas feeding 15 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium reduced it. In both results of week 4 and week 8, the calcium and selenium content in groups 0, 5, and 10 was significantly higher than in group control (P < 0.05). Among these, group 0-Week 8 showed the highest calcium content across all groups (P < 0.05). Meanwhile, group 5 exhibited higher selenium content than group 0 (P > 0.05). Discussion

The production performance of laying hens directly affects the economic benefits of laying hens farming to a large extent. The better the production performance, the higher the economic benefits (Gunjevic et al., 2023). However, long-term feeding yeast selenium alone or in combination with chicory root pulp resulted in a decrease in the egg production rate of laying hens. Kopacz et al. (2021) found that feeding fermented rapeseed cake can improve the egg production rate of laying hens compared to feeding raw rapeseed cake. Therefore, it may be possible to try feeding laying hens with fermented chicory root pulp to improve their production performance in the future. Moreover, this study found that long-term combined feeding of 15 % or 20 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium resulted in the lowest egg production rate. This is likely due to the high dietary fiber content in chicory root pulp, which is difficult for laying hens to digest and utilize efficiently when provided in excess.

Egg laying hens generally start laying eggs at around 20 weeks of age and reach their peak egg production at 26-45 weeks of age (Huang et al., 2025). Therefore, the increase of egg production rate in group control from week 4 to week 8 was likely a result of the natural transition from the early laying phase to the peak laying period. Additionally, this study showed that the FCR was 2.64-2.86 g/g, the egg production rate was 94.64 %−99.11 %, and the average egg weight was 54.60-57.39 g at week 4, and the FCR was 2.73-3.07 g/g, the egg production rate was 90.48 %−99.40 %, and the average egg weight was 55.83-58.86 g at week 8. Maharjan et al. (2023) found that feeding 36-week-old White shavers laying hens with full-fat high-oleic soybean meal resulted in a FCR of 1.601-1.727 g/g, an egg production rate of 92.71 %−96.35 %, and an average egg weight of 60.20-63.16 g. Chen et al. (2023a) pointed out that the FCR of 34-week-old Lohmann laying hens fed with tea residues-fermented was 2.08-2.23 g/g, the egg production rate was 87.7 %−91.9 %, and the average egg weight was 55.46-56.83 g. Liu et al. (2024) revealed that 30-week-old Wenchang breeder hens fed with Alpiniae oxyphyllae had a FCR of 3.24-3.51 g/g, an egg production rate of 63.52 %−66.36 %, and an average egg weight of 43.77-44.82 g. The above results suggested that compared to other feed formulas, the feed formula used in this experiment was beneficial for improving the egg production rate of laying hens in the early laying period, but had a mediocre effect on FCR and average egg weight, which may be related to the colder weather during the feeding period. Li et al. (2020) found that low temperature reduced the average egg weight of 18-week-old Hy-Line Brown laying hens.

Eggshell strength refers to the ability of eggshells to resist external force (Gunjevic et al., 2023). The Haugh unit is an important indicator for measuring the freshness of eggs (Dedousi et al., 2022). This study found that long-term combined feeding of 5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium improved eggshell strength and Haugh unit, which was beneficial for reducing the damage rate of eggs during collection, transportation, and storage, and prolonging the shelf life of eggs. Eggshell thickness is one of the important indicators for measuring egg quality as it affects the preservation and hatchability of eggs (Dedousi et al., 2022). This study revealed that the combined feeding of 15 % or 20 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium led to a decrease in eggshell thickness. It is often assumed that an increase in eggshell thickness leads to greater eggshell strength. However, this study expressed that there was no proportional relationship between eggshell thickness and strength, which was consistent with the research of Xin et al. (2021) and Xiao et al. (2019). The egg shape index is mainly used to evaluate the uniformity of egg shape (Gunjevic et al., 2023). In this study, the egg shape index ranged from 76.55 to 78.92 at week 4 and from 76.66 to 79.98 at week 8. Similarly, Gunjevic et al. (2023) showed that the egg shape index of Lohmann Brown laying hens fed with maize hybrid ranged from 78.14 to 81.4. Dedousi et al. (2022) found that the egg shape index of Isa Brown laying hens fed with dried olive pulp ranged from 76.07 to 78. These results proved that feeding different diets has almost no effect on the egg shape index of laying hens.

Protein, fat, calcium, phosphorus, and selenium are important nutrients in eggs that are beneficial to human health (Wallace et al., 2023). This study demonstrated that the combined feeding of 5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium increased the protein, phosphorus, calcium, and selenium content of eggs. This improvement contributes to superior egg quality, ultimately generating greater economic benefits for the layer industry. It was also found that the nutrient content of eggs was unrelated to the depth of their yolk color. Therefore, the common belief that yolk color depth can be used to assess nutritional value is clearly unreliable. For instance, Kojima et al. (2022) showed that yolk color was affected by the pigments in the feed. Furthermore, this study revealed that long-term combined feeding of 10 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium increased the fat content of eggs, whereas feeding 15 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium reduced it. These results indicated that eggs produced by long-term combined feeding of 15 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium fulfilled the requirements of consumers aiming to reduce body fat.

Immunoglobulin IgG and IgM, as well as immune factors IL-2 and IL-6, play important roles in immune responses. Among them, IgM is the first antibody that appears upon initial contact with the antigen. IL-2 is a cytokine produced by activated T lymphocytes that promotes the activation, proliferation, and differentiation of immune cells. IL-6 plays an important role in host defense, acute phase response, and immune response (Liu et al., 2021). This study implied that the combined feeding of 5 % or 10 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium enhanced the levels of IgM and IL-2 in laying hens, and feeding yeast selenium alone enhanced IL-6 level. Similarly, Sun et al. (2020) revealed that adding selenium-enriched earthworm powder to the diet increased the IL-2 level of laying hens. This result suggested that yeast selenium may contribute to enhancing the immune function of laying hens. Dilawar et al. (2021) found that phenolic compounds and flavonoids can increase the immunoglobulin level in poultry and enhance immune response. Therefore, we speculated that in addition to yeast selenium, the phenolic compounds in chicory root pulp may also played a role in enhancing the immune function of laying hens. Furthermore, the correlation analysis between the level of immune indicators and the relative abundance of gut microbiota implied that the regulation of immune function in laying hens by chicory root pulp and yeast selenium was closely related to gut microbiota composition, especially phyla Thermodesulfobacteriota, Chordata and Bacillota, as well as genera Prevotella, Mediterraneibacter and unclassified_Bacteroidales_genus.

The Chao and Ace indices are commonly used to indicate the total number of species (Zhong et al., 2023). This study found that short-term feeding yeast selenium alone or in combination with 10 % or 15 % chicory root pulp increased the total number of gut microbiota species, while long-term combined feeding of chicory root pulp and yeast selenium reduced the total number of gut microbiota species. Yi et al. (2023) reported that the development of gut microbiota in early life has significant long-term impacts on health later in life. Based on the results of the relative abundance of gut microbiota, we speculated that these impacts may primarily influence the dominant gut microbiota in later stages, rather than the total number of gut microbiota species.

Previous studies have shown that the cecal microbiota of laying hens was mainly composed of Bacteroidota and Bacillota at the phylum level, accounting for over 85 % of the entire microbiota (Guo et al., 2022). This study had similar findings. Among them, Bacteroidota not only promotes host digestion and absorption, but also maintains gut microbiota balance, enhances intestinal barrier function, and resists pathogen invasion by competing with harmful bacteria for nutrients and attachment sites, as well as producing antibacterial substances (Li et al., 2024a). Bacillota can promote host digestion, produce vitamins and other nutrients for host utilization, and maintain gut microbiota balance by interacting with other microbial communities (Hidalgo-García et al., 2023). This study revealed that feeding yeast selenium alone or in combination with 5 % chicory root pulp increased the relative abundance of dominant gut microbiota phyla Bacteroidota and Bacillota in laying hens, which was beneficial for their gut health.

Phocaeicola belongs to the phylum Bacteroidota, and is an important component of gut microbiota, playing a crucial role in maintaining gut health (Fernandez-Cantos et al., 2024). Bacteroides and Prevotella belong to the phylum Bacteroidota, and Mediterraneibacter and Faecalibacterium belong to the phylum Bacillota. They are important short-chain fatty acid-producing bacteria, benefical for maintaining gut health (Huang et al., 2024; Jan et al., 2023). This study clarified that feeding yeast selenium alone or in combination with chicory root pulp increased the relative abundance of dominant gut microbiota genera Phocaeicola, Bacteroides, Prevotella, Mediterraneibacter and Faecalibacterium in laying hens, which was beneficial for maintaining gut health. Similarly, Li et al. (2024b) shown that supplementing selenium regulated the composition of the cecal microbiota, making the intestine healthier. Seidavi et al. (2021) evidence​d that chicory had the effect of improving gut microbiota. These studies confirmed the positive role of yeast selenium and chicory root pulp in regulating the gut microbiota of laying hens.

In poultry farming, antibiotics are mainly used to prevent and treat diseases and promote growth. However, overuse of antibiotics may lead to increased bacterial resistance, and residual antibiotics in poultry products may pose a threat to human health through the food chain (Rivera-Gomis et al., 2021). The laying hens raised in this experiment had high resistance to both tetracycline antibiotic and aminoglycoside antibiotic. Similarly, Farooq et al. (2022) found that laying hens raised without antibiotics had higher resistance to tetracycline antibiotic. We speculated that this may be due to the repeated use of these antibiotics in various production activities, which led to the emergence of drug-resistant bacteria in the feeding environment, and the laying hens were exposed to these drug-resistant bacteria during the cyclic production process. In addition, this study identified that feeding yeast selenium alone or in combination with 15 % chicory root pulp reduced the resistance of laying hens to aminoglycoside antibiotics, which was beneficial for the green development of the poultry industry.

Virulence factors refer to molecules with invasive and toxic properties produced by the metabolism of bacteria, viruses, fungi, etc. When microorganisms infect the host, they enter and exit the host's tissue cells by inhibiting or evading the host's immune response, and obtain nutrients from the host for their own proliferation and growth (Chen et al., 2023b). Cps4L is a gene encoding the virulence factor Streptococcus pneumoniae TIGR4. Streptococcus pneumoniae TIGR4 can resist to complement deposition and mask cell wall-associated complement from being recognized by the complement receptors on phagocytes, thereby achieving antiphagocytosis (Abdellah et al., 2023). This study reflected that feeding yeast selenium alone or in combination with chicory root pulp reduced harm to laying hens caused by the virulence factor Streptococcus pneumoniae TIGR4, which was beneficial for the healthy and sustainable development of the poultry industry.

Conclusions

In summary, the combined feeding of 5 % chicory root pulp and yeast selenium improved egg quality, increased the nutritional content of eggs, enhanced immune function by optimizing the gut microbiota, and reduced harm to laying hens caused by virulence factor. These findings provide new feed application strategies and theoretical basis for the healthy farming of laying hens and the production of high-quality eggs.

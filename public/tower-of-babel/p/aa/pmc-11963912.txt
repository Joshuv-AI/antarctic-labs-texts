Burden of Heart Failure in Patients With Tricuspid Regurgitation and Effect of Transcatheter Repair on Different Subdimensions of Quality of Life
Authors: Thomas J. Stocker, Saskia Sommer, David J. Cohen, John A. Spertus, Lukas Stolz, Philipp M. Doldi, Ludwig T. Weckbach, Michael Nabauer, Steffen Massberg, Jörg Hausleiter
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRight‐sided heart failure (HF) due to severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is associated with reduced quality of life (QoL). Here, we analyzed the impact of TR on specific QoL dimensions and the effect of transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI) on individual QoL items.Methods and ResultsIn this study, we included 174 patients with HF (49% women; median age, 79 years; 97% New York Heart Association ≥3) with baseline QoL assessment undergoing TTVI by transcatheter edge‐to‐edge‐repair at our center between April 2016 and March 2022. QoL was assessed by the standardized Minnesota Living With HF Questionnaire. QoL change after TTVI and correlation to functional end points were analyzed. In addition, all QoL domains and the 21 individual items of the Minnesota Living With HF Questionnaire were analyzed. TTVI significantly reduced TR (TR ≥3: baseline 95%, 1‐year‐follow‐up 7%; P<0.001). Total Minnesota Living with HF Questionnaire score improved from 37 (interquartile range, 26–50) points to 31 (interquartile range, 17–42) points (median follow‐up‐interval, 355 days; P<0.001). QoL improvement was associated with positive New York Heart Association class, 6‐minute walking distance, and actigraphy changes (all P<0.05). The detailed analysis revealed that all items of the physical‐related QoL dimension were impaired at baseline and strongly improved after TTVI. In contrast, the emotional and “social” Minnesota Living With HF Questionnaire dimensions were largely unaffected at baseline, yet specific items improved with TTVI.ConclusionsIn this single‐center study, we delineate the QoL‐associated disease burden of TR and identify specific QoL items that improved after TTVI. Our findings support TTVI in patients with reduced QoL and may add to the development of specific tools assessing the functional status of an increasing patient population undergoing TTVI.

Right‐sided heart failure (HF) with underlying tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is a serious and progressive disease. 1 , 2 Until recently, patients with severe TR were relatively neglected and severely undertreated, 3 particularly because isolated tricuspid surgery remains associated with high mortality rates such that patients with severe TR are often deemed inoperable due to severe comorbidities and frailty. 1 , 4 Within the past years, several percutaneous transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (TTVI) techniques were introduced, of which tricuspid transcatheter edge‐to‐edge‐repair (T‐TEER) is currently the most frequently performed intervention.

Besides the association of TR with excess death, diseased patients report impaired quality of life (QoL), leading to severe limitation of their coping in everyday life. While studies of isolated tricuspid surgery largely focus on clinical outcomes, 5 studies of TTVI used several QoL instruments including the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ), the 36‐Item Short Form Health Survey, and the Minnesota Living With Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ). 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 Improved overall QoL as assessed by the 36‐Item Short Form Health Survey and the MLHFQ was described after TTVI. 8 , 9 , 10 The results of the recently published TRILUMINATE (Trial to Evaluate Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients Treated With the Tricuspid Valve Repair System) pivotal trial demonstrated a significant treatment effect of T‐TEER on the KCCQ 1 year after the intervention. 6 , 11 However, the impact of severe TR on different areas of QoL (eg, physical, emotional, and social) is largely unknown. Furthermore, data regarding the treatment effect of TTVI on individual QoL components in patients of clinical practice is unknown.

In this study, we used the MLHFQ consisting of physical, emotional, and social dimensions in a large prospective population of patients with significant TR undergoing TTVI and (1) analyzed the total MLHFQ score and the association with different functional end points before and after TTVI, (2) assessed the impairment of individual QoL items at baseline, and (3) assessed the long‐term change of individual QoL items 1 year after TTVI. The results of this study will add to a better understanding of the impaired QoL areas in patients with right‐sided HF and help to estimate the treatment effect of TTVI on different dimensions of QoL. Furthermore, the results will support the development of specific QoL questionnaires for patients with TR to improve the QoL assessment of an increasing patient population undergoing TTVI.

Methods

Patient Enrollment and Study Design

Between April 2016 and March 2022, all consecutive patients with right‐sided HF and severe underlying TR scheduled for TTVI at the university hospital of the LMU Munich in Germany and available preprocedural baseline MLHFQ assessment were included in this study. All patients received T‐TEER. We also enrolled patients with significant TR and concomitant but less severe mitral regurgitation (MR), who underwent simultaneous treatment of both valves and additional mitral transcatheter edge‐to‐edge repair. The patient enrollment in the study was biased by the selection of patients for planned TTVI. In the final analysis set, we included patients with baseline MLHFQ assessment and available long‐term follow‐up MLHFQ data. The data analysis of patients treated with TTVI at our site was approved by an institutional review committee. QoL data acquisition was performed during clinical routine practice without additional informed consent. The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology cohort reporting guidelines were used. 12 The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

QoL Assessment

QoL was assessed at baseline (within 2 weeks before TTVI) and at a prespecified 1‐year follow‐up visit. QoL was estimated using the MLHFQ. The questionnaire consists of 21 different questions asking patients if HF prevented them from living as they wanted during the past month in different situations. Each item is described on a 6‐level Likert scale ranging from 0 to 5 (0=no, 1=very little, 5=very much). A total score is obtained by summing the scores from individual questions (total range, 0–105 points), with higher scores indicating poorer QoL. The primary outcome of the MLHFQ is the overall score and includes all 21 items. A change of ≥5 points is considered as clinically meaningful. 13 , 14 The MLHFQ contains a physical subdomain consisting of 8 QoL items and an emotional subdomain consisting of 5 QoL items. 15 , 16 , 17 The remaining 8 nonphysical and nonemotional items of the MLHFQ describe a “social” dimension in QoL. 15 In this study, we defined significant baseline impairment of an individual QoL item as median baseline score of >1 point, which can be translated into a more than “very little” prevention of the patient from living as she/he wants during the past month.

HF Examination

In addition to the QoL assessment, we performed a structured HF assessment before TTVI and at the follow‐up visit. This included the New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification, a 6‐minute walking distance test (6‐MWDT), measurement of different laboratory parameters including NT‐proBNP (N‐terminal pro‐B‐type natriuretic peptide) (), and actigraphy analysis in a subset of patients, as described previously. 18 Echocardiography was performed with documentation of the left and right ventricular function as well as evaluation of MR and TR severity according to US and European guidelines for quantification of native valve regurgitation. 19 , 20 TR severity was assessed with grades of 0+ (none or trace), 1+ (mild), 2+ (moderate), 3+ (severe), or 4+ (massive/torrential). The reduction of TR after TTVI was evaluated at discharge from the hospital and at the planned follow‐up visit.

Statistical Analysis

Variables are expressed as counts with percentages for categorical variables or as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) for continuous variables without normal distribution. The Shapiro–Wilk normality test was used to test for normality of distribution of continuous variables. Differences in QoL before and after TTVI were tested using the Wilcoxon matched‐pairs signed‐rank test. Differences in QoL change after TTVI between patient groups as stratified by NYHA, 6‐minute walking distance test, or actigraphy changes (worsened/unchanged versus improved) were tested using the Wilcoxon rank‐sum test. A P value <0.05 was considered as statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R version 3.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Study Cohort

In this study, we included 201 consecutive patients with HF undergoing TTVI due to underlying significant TR in our heart valve center between April 2016 and March 2022 with available QoL assessment before transcatheter intervention. After TTVI, 21 patients died before follow‐up QoL assessment (median time to death after TTVI, 140 days), and 6 patients were lost to follow‐up (follow‐up rate, 96.7%; Figure S1). Accordingly, we included 174 patients with baseline and follow‐up QoL data in the current analysis.

The baseline characteristics of the study cohort are detailed in Table 1. Patients had a median age of 79 years and 49% were women. All patients were symptomatic for chronic heart failure (97% NYHA class III or IV). Most patients were hospitalized for heart failure within 1 year before TTVI (75%). Many patients had significant chronic kidney disease (70%), and 8 patients were on dialysis at baseline. TR was identified as the clinically leading cause for HF in all patients of this study (52% TR grade 3+, 43% TR grade 4+). Detailed echocardiographic parameters are listed in Table 2. Left ventricular ejection fraction was preserved (≥50%) in 69% of the enrolled patients with HF. Moderate to severe MR (grade 3+) was present in 25% of patients and severe MR (grade 4+) was observed in 6% of patients at baseline. Median estimated systolic pulmonary artery pressure was 39 mm Hg in the patients of the analysis set.

Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Intervention

All patients were deemed inoperable for conventional heart surgery due to high individual perioperative risk for death, and the heart team decided for TTVI. All patients received T‐TEER as the treatment of choice. A combined procedure with additional mitral transcatheter edge‐to‐edge repair before tricuspid repair was performed in 36% of all patients. A median of 2 clips (IQR, 2–2) was implanted in the tricuspid valve.

The intervention led to significant reduction of TR (≤2+: baseline, 5%; postprocedural, 93%; P<0.001; Figure 1). Echocardiographic and clinical follow‐up was performed after a median follow‐up interval of 352 days. At the follow‐up visit, TR reduction maintained (grade≤2+: follow‐up, 86%; P<0.001 compared with baseline; Figure 1).

All assessed functional parameters improved 1 year after TTVI (Table 3). The fraction of patients in NYHA class ≤II increased by 60% (NYHA class ≤2: baseline, 3%; follow‐up, 63%; P<0.001), the median result of 6‐MWDT improved by 24% (baseline, 204 [IQR, 138–297] m; follow‐up, 260 [IQR, 185–359] m; P<0.001), and the median physical activity as assessed by activity tracking devices to the wrist improved by 19% (baseline, 2834 [IQR, 1157–4924] steps per day; follow‐up, 3230 [IQR, 1562–6282] steps per day; P<0.05). Global QoL Improvement After TTVI as Estimated by the MLHFQ

QoL was estimated using the MLHFQ score. At baseline, the total median MLHFQ score was 37 (IQR, 26–50) points. After a median QoL follow‐up interval of 355 days, the median total MLHFQ score significantly improved by 19% to 31 (IQR, 17–42) points (P<0.001; Figure 2A). After TTVI, 96 of 174 patients (55%) showed clinically significant MLHFQ improvement of at least 5 points, and 78 of 174 patients (45%) experienced a strong MLHFQ improvement of ≥10 points. The completeness of answered QoL items was 96% at baseline and 95% at follow‐up (Table S4). The QoL change was similar in 111 patients undergoing isolated T‐TEER without intervention at the mitral valve (Tables S1 through S3). Diuretic therapy was similar in patients with QoL improvement, when compared with patients without QoL improvement at the time of follow‐up 1 year after TTVI. Furthermore, the diuretic dose remained unchanged between baseline and follow‐up in patients with and without QoL improvement (change of loop diuretic equivalent dose, 0 [IQR, −10 to 20] mg versus 0 [IQR, −10 to 10] mg; P=0.52).

In an additional analysis, all patients were stratified according to their baseline QoL level into minimal impairment (patients<quartile 1 of baseline MLHFQ score), moderate impairment (patients within IQR of baseline MLHFQ score) and severe impairment (patients>quartile 3 of baseline MLHFQ score; Figure 2B). Patients with severe impairment experienced a strong 29% QoL improvement after TTVI (median MLHFQ score: baseline, 61 points; follow‐up, 41 points; P<0.001), when compared with patients with moderate impairment (14% QoL improvement; P<0.001) and patients with minimal impairment (no significant MLHFQ change after TTVI; Figure 2B).

Furthermore, the QoL improvement as estimated by the MLHFQ was associated with the procedural result at the tricuspid valve (Figure 3). In patients without TR reduction following TTVI (n=18), median change of MLHFQ was −3.5 points, while improvement of TR by 1 grade, 2 grades, or ≥3 grades at follow‐up was associated with median improvement (numerical reduction) of MLHFQ by −4.0 points, −8.5 points, or −13.0 points, when compared with baseline.

Change of MLHFQ Subdomains and Individual QoL Items After TTVI

The physical subdomain of the MLHFQ consists of 8 different items (Figure 5). This allows a total range of 0 to 40 points. At baseline, the median score of the physical subdomain was 24 (IQR, 18–29) points (60% of the maximum 40 points). Thus, the relative disease burden in the physical‐related QoL dimension is relatively higher, when compared with the total MLHFQ score (median, 37 points; 35% of the maximum 105 points). In more detail, the median score of all MLHFQ items of the physical subdomain were impaired at baseline (questions 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 13; Figure 5 in bold letters). Impairment of the QoL item was defined as median baseline score of >1 point (more than “very little” prevention of the patient from living as she/he wants during the past month). After TTVI and at 1‐year follow‐up, the median score of the physical subdomain significantly improved (numerically reduced) by 20% from 24 (IQR, 18–29) points to 18 (IQR, 10–25) points (P<0.001). In detail, the median score of the individual items significantly improved in 6 of 8 impaired items (Figure 5).

Discussion

The improvement of QoL is increasingly recognized as major target in the treatment of HF. 21 The overall disease burden from HF is broader than hospitalizations and death. For some patients, improved functional status and QoL is of greater importance than longevity, especially patients with right‐sided HF with underlying TR, who represent an HF entity of fragile patients with high morbidity; thus, the improvement of QoL might be of particular importance in this patient population. 6 , 8 , 22 This large prospective study evaluates the change of individual QoL components in a population of patients with right‐sided HF undergoing TTVI. QoL analysis was performed at baseline to assess the disease burden and was repeated at a follow‐up visit 1 year after TTVI. The four main findings of this study were: T‐TEER was associated with clinically relevant QoL improvement, and patients with severely impaired QoL at baseline benefited most from the intervention.The overall QoL improvement correlated with the magnitude of TR reduction and with changes of functional end points including NYHA class, 6‐MWDT, and actigraphy.The detailed analysis revealed strong impairments of all physical‐related QoL components in patients with right‐sided HF with underlying TR, while emotional and social aspects were largely unaffected.Almost all individual QoL components that were impaired at baseline significantly improved 1 year after TTVI.

In this study, T‐TEER was associated with an improvement of QoL in a large real‐world cohort of patients with right‐sided HF with underlying severe TR. In the recently published TRILUMINATE pivotal trial, significant impact of T‐TEER on QoL was described in a highly selected study cohort. In the TRILUMINATE pivotal trial, the KCCQ was used as a QoL assessment tool, and the overall KCCQ score improved by 12.3±1.8 points compared with medical therapy 1 year after T‐TEER. 6 Almost 50% of patients who received T‐TEER improved by ≥15 points in the KCCQ score. In comparison, this improvement was observed in 26% of patients of the control group. 6 T‐TEER significantly improved QoL 1 month after the intervention, with a small additional improvement after 1 year (KCCQ difference between interventional and control group, 10.4 points). 11 Here, we confirm the findings in patients with advanced HF in a real‐world setting. Compared with the TRILUMINATE pivotal trial cohort, we included patients with a higher rate of previous hospitalizations (75% versus 25%), a higher rate of kidney disease (70% versus 35%), and advanced NYHA classification (NYHA class ≥III, 97% versus 57%). Importantly, clinically significant improvement of the MLHFQ score (≥5 points) was observed in 55% of patients. Even more, a strong MLHFQ improvement of ≥10 points was detected in 45% of patients. Interestingly, patients with a strong QoL impairment at baseline benefited most from TTVI. In contrast, QoL change was negligible in patients with mild symptoms at baseline. Thus, the impact of T‐TEER on the functional status seems to be the highest in severely morbid patients.

It is worth noting that the rate of technical success and TR reduction was high in this study. In 90% of patients, TR was reduced 1 year after T‐TEER, when compared with the baseline TR severity. Nevertheless, we were able to identify a numerical association of the magnitude of TR reduction and QoL improvement. This suggests that the QoL improvement was truly facilitated by the improvement of TR. QoL improvement after T‐TEER was significantly associated with various functional end points including NYHA class, 6‐MWDT, and actigraphy analysis. This suggests that the changes in QoL of patients with TR, as assessed by the MLHFQ, were closely coupled to subjective and objective measures of physical capacity. Patients who benefit from T‐TEER in terms of physical activity report a simultaneous improvement in the QoL. In a previous study, we demonstrated that TTVI had the highest impact on physical activity in patients with low baseline activity before the intervention, suggesting that this patient cohort with advanced HF might be associated with the strongest QoL benefit following TTVI. 18

In the detailed QoL analysis on the level of the individual QoL items, we identified that right‐sided HF with underlying TR mainly impairs the physical dimension. In this regard, all 8 items of the physical subdomain of the MLHFQ were impaired with a median baseline value of ≥2 points on the 6‐point Likert scale. In the physical MLHFQ dimension, the median baseline value was 24 of 40 points. This score can be translated to a relative value of 60% from the maximum in this dimension. In contrast, social and emotional QoL items were largely unaffected in the investigated population of patients with TR. The median value of the complete MLHFQ was 37 of 105 points. This can be translated to a relative value of 35% from the maximum MLHFQ score, which is well below the relative value of the isolated physical dimension. This suggests that the disease burden of TR is mainly driven by impairments in the physical capacity explaining the association of changes in the QoL to physical functional end points such as 6‐MWDT and actigraphy after TTVI. The results also suggest that the emotional and social life is mostly unaffected, even in the presence of significant right‐sided HF due to underlying TR. Solely, the median value of 1 emotional item (“Did your HF prevent you from living as you want during the past month by making you worry”?) and 1 social item (“Did your HF prevent you from living as you want during the past month by making your recreational pastimes, sports, and hobbies difficult”?) were impaired with a median baseline value of ≥2 points. However, some aspects of the emotional, psychological, social, or spiritual life might not be covered by the MLHFQ; therefore, future studies are required to further investigate impairments in these dimensions.

After T‐TEER, almost all affected individual QoL items from the baseline assessment significantly improved 1 year after the intervention. Improvement was observed in 8 of 10 individual items that were impaired at baseline. The remaining QoL items of the MLHFQ were without pathological value at baseline and could not be further improved after T‐TEER. In this study, none of the individual QoL items worsened after T‐TEER. This further demonstrates the broad and consistent improvement of QoL after TTVI. The results from this study could be used for the design of a novel QoL questionnaire focusing on the specificities of patients with right‐sided HF. The available QoL assessment tools were originally created for patients with left‐sided HF, and several items seem insensitive in right‐sided disease. This deserves extra attention since an increasing patient population is considered for transcatheter therapy in the emerging field of TTVI.

Several limitations need to be considered when interpreting the findings. Although comprehensive analysis of QoL changes after TTVI were performed in the largest patient cohort to date, the included patients were highly selected, and the overall number of patients is still limited. Furthermore, the study is based on a single‐center experience without a control arm and a limited follow‐up interval of 1 year. While all patients received T‐TEER due to clinically leading severe TR, several patients received concomitant mitral transcatheter edge‐to‐edge repair, however, results were similar in a subgroup with patients undergoing isolated TTVI. All included patients from this study were initially selected by an interdisciplinary heart team for TTVI, and thus clinical considerations in the TTVI selection process might have led to the exclusion of certain patients.

In conclusion, the results suggest that TTVI significantly improves QoL in patients with TR with right‐sided HF, especially in patients with markedly reduced functional status. Particularly, the physical subdomain of the MLHFQ was largely affected in patients with TR and significantly improved after TTVI. Therefore, the physical subdomain should be analyzed and may be reported separately in future TTVI trials. The potential impact of right‐sided HF on psychological, social, and spiritual aspects of QoL should be further investigated in future studies since these dimensions might not be entirely assessed in the existing QoL tools. Consequently, the results of this study might add to the development of novel QoL instruments that are required for an increasing TR population undergoing TTVI strategies.

Correlation analysis between the collapsibility of loess and physicomechanical indices in Longdong region and prediction of the collapsibility coefficient based on machine learning
Authors: Deren Liu, Kaiqiang Wang, Yicheng Bian, Xu Wang, Yanjie Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Against the backdrop of booming engineering construction in loess areas, achieving rapid and accurate prediction of the loess collapsibility coefficient is of great significance. This study takes the loess in the Longdong area as the research object. Based on analyzing the correlation between the collapsibility coefficient and various soil property indices, a rapid determination standard for the collapsibility of loess in this area is proposed. Prediction models between the loess collapsibility coefficient and void ratio, depth, saturation, and compression coefficient are established using three machine learning algorithms: random forest (RF), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and support vector machine (SVM). The prediction results are subjected to interpretability analysis using the Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) technique. The research results show that loess with a void ratio less than 0.960 and a soil layer depth greater than 16 m can be used as the rapid determination standard for non-collapsible loess in this area. The XGBoost model has higher prediction accuracy and applicability, with coefficients of determination (R2) of 0.910 and 0.900 for the training set and testing set, respectively. The prediction accuracy for identifying whether loess is collapsible or nocollapsible reaches 92.7%. SHAP analysis reveals that the influence trends of various factors on model predictions are consistent with theoretical cognition, and soils with a depth less than 11 m and a void ratio greater than 1.02 have a high collapsibility tendency. The results and conclusions of this study can provide a theoretical basis for subsequent engineering construction in the Longdong area.

Introduction

With the advancement of the Western Development Strategy and the construction plan of the “Silk Road Economic Belt”, the number of engineering construction projects in loess regions is gradually increasing, and the problems of loess collapsible deformation encountered are becoming increasingly severe1,2. Loess possesses unique geotechnical engineering properties such as regionality, macroporosity, structural property, and collapsibility3–6. Collapsible loess refers to the type of loess whose soil structure is damaged upon being soaked by water under the combined effect of self-weight stress and additional stress, leading to significant additional deformation7–9. The Longdong region is located in the hinterland of the Loess Plateau. Loess is widely distributed in this area and has a relatively large sedimentary thickness10,11. The Bailongjiang Water Diversion Project passes through multiple collapsible loess areas.The foundations of many buildings are composed of loess. The collapsibility of loess can pose many potential risks to engineering construction, such as, uneven settlement, foundation instability, and subgrade settlement12–17. Therefore, studying the correlations between the collapsibility coefficient of loess and various soil property indices in the Longdong region, and further establishing a rapid judgment criterion for loess collapsibility and a prediction model for the collapsibility coefficient, is of great reference value for subsequent engineering designs in this area.

At present, many scholars have adopted mathematical statistics and machine learning methods to explore the relationship between loess collapsibility and soil property indices as well as the prediction of the loess collapsibility coefficient. In the early studies, many scholars applied regression analysis to examine the relationship between soil property indices and the collapsibility coefficient using linear and multiple nonlinear regression methods. Zheng et al.18 established a prediction model for the loess collapsibility coefficient, taking dry density, water content, void ratio, and pressure as independent variables and the collapsibility coefficient as the dependent variable. Nie et al.1 used the resistivity of loess as a prediction parameter and established an evaluation method for loess collapsibility based on dry density, moisture content, and resistivity through regression analysis. Ren et al.19 improved the regression analysis method in MATLAB and established a fitting model between the loess collapsibility coefficient and seven physical property indices in the Luoyang region, China. Shao et al.20 combined factor analysis and multiple linear regression analysis to reduce the correlation among soil property indicators, thus optimizing the parameter selection for the model.

As technology advances and research deepens, a growing number of scholars have begun introducing machine learning algorithms to establish more accurate and rapid prediction models. Ma et al.21 considered the influence of particle composition and integrated the radial basis function neural network (RBF) based on analyzing the relationship between the basic physical indices of soil and the collapsibility coefficient to develop a collapsibility coefficient prediction model. Chen et al.22 adopted the RBF neural network theory to establish loess collapsibility coefficient prediction models for Gongliu County and Xinyuan County in the Yili river valley region. Zhu et al.23 respectively employed three representative machine learning algorithms, namely multi-expression programming (MEP), random forest (RF), and support vector machine (SVM). Focusing on factors such as initial water content, initial void ratio, plasticity index, soil depth, and compression modulus, they established three prediction models for the self-weight collapsibility coefficient of loess.

SHAP interpretability analysis is of great significance for the reasonable construction of machine learning prediction models. It can clearly clarify the decision-making logic of the model and effectively improve the interpretability and transparency of machine learning prediction results. Hamdaoui et al. (2025) established a machine learning prediction model for the compression index of cohesive soil based on SHAP interpretability analysis. Diza et al. (2024) applied the SHAP method to the interpretation of tree-based models and proposed a unified interpretation framework for tree-based model outputs. By quantifying the importance of input variables, they verified the significant advantages of this method over other interpretation approaches.

Scholars have achieved accomplishments in the research on loess collapsibility and the collapsibility coefficient, particularly in relation to physical property indicators. However, existing studies have not conducted interpretability analysis on the established models, failing to provide sufficient theoretical support for the prediction results. This study takes the loess of the Longdong region as the research object. Based on indoor test data, it applies mathematical statistics analysis methods to investigate the relationship between the collapsibility coefficient and physicomechanical indices, and to establish a rapid judgment criterion for the non-collapsibility of loess along with a prediction model for the collapsibility coefficient. Meanwhile, the SHAP interpretability analysis method is introduced to realize full-dimensional visual interpretation of the model prediction results, providing a scientific basis for the prediction of loess collapsibility coefficient and the evaluation of loess site collapsibility in this area. Engineering overview and characteristics of regional loess

Engineering overview and engineering geological conditions

The research area is situated in the Longdong region, China, primarily covering eight counties and districts, namely Xifeng District, Zhenyuan County, Heshui County, and Ning County in Qingyang City. The average annual precipitation in this area ranges from 400 to 600 mm, while the annual evaporation varies from 1210 to 1691 mm, and the precipitation is mainly concentrated between July and September. The average annual temperature is 8.4 °C, and the terrain is higher in the north and lower in the south. The geomorphic types include loess tablelands, ridges, mounds, and river terraces. The strata is predominantly consist of collapsible loess, especially the Malan loess of the upper pleistocene (Q3). Exploration data indicate that the soil layer 1 to 2 m beneath the surface is uneven and contains large pores and plant roots. Within the range of 3 to 13 m, the soil quality is relatively uniform, and a 2 to 3 m-thick paleosol layer is present at the bottom. Relationship between collapsibility coefficient and void ratio

The void ratio comprehensively reflects the compactness of loess particles. When the value of the void ratio increases, it indicates that the soil becomes looser and contains a greater number of pores. Under such conditions, the loess is more susceptible to deformation under relatively small external forces. Research has shown that loess exhibits a metastable soil structure28–30. Among the various pore types in the soil mass, the presence of overhead pores (medium pores) is the fundamental cause of the collapsibility characteristic of loess31.The scatter plot of the collapsibility coefficient and the void ratio is shown in Fig. 5a. All points are well distributed, and a positive correlation exists between the collapsibility coefficient and the void ratio. In addition, when the void ratio of loess in the research area exceeds 0.960, the loess demonstrates significant collapsibility,in contrast, it generally lacks collapsibility. Therefore, e = 0.960 can be considered as the “limiting void ratio” for loess collapsibility in the research area. The provided fitting relationship formula between the collapsibility coefficient and the void ratio demonstrates an exponential relationship. R2 = 0.809, indicating a relatively high degree of fit.Fig. 5Scatter plots of collapsibility coefficient and physical indicator: a void ratio; b dry density; c porosity; d depth.

Relationship between collapsibility coefficient and dry density

Density is one of the key indicators utilized to measure the compactness of loess. High-density loess has a more compact structure with relatively fewer pores. It is difficult for water to penetrate the interior of the soil, and there is limited space available for the loess to deform. Therefore, the potential for collapsibility is reduced. Figure 5b illustrates the relationship between the collapsibility coefficient and dry density. It indicates that there is clear negative correlation between the collapsibility coefficient and dry density. That is, the higher the dry density of the loess , the lower its collapsibility . When the dry density of the loess in the research area ranges from 1.20 to 1.38 g/cm3, the loess exhibits clear collapsibility. When the dry density exceeds 1.38 g/cm3, the loess generally lacks collapsibility. Therefore, ρd = 1.38 g/cm3 can be considered the “limiting dry density” for loess collapsibility in the research area. The fitted relationship between the collapsibility coefficient and dry density is shown in the figure, where R2 = 0.812, indicating a relatively high degree of fit.

Relationship between collapsibility coefficient and porosity

Figure 5c presents the scatter plot between the collapsibility coefficient and porosity. From the figure, the relationship between porosity and the collapsibility coefficient is consistent with that between the void ratio and the collapsibility coefficient described previously. As porosity gradually increases, the collapsibility coefficient also shows a gradual increasing trend. The critical porosity of loess, n = 49%, is presented in the figure. A fitting analysis between the collapsibility coefficient and the porosity is performed, yielding a fitting accuracy index R2 = 0.786, indicating a good fit between the two variables.

Relationship between collapsibility coefficient and depth

Soil has a specific unit weight, and as the burial depth increases, the overlying self-weight load also rises. With continuous increases in burial depth, the arrangement of soil particles becomes denser. Simultaneously, the inter-particle pores and large voids within the soil decreases progressively, and the pore structure gradually transforms into a mosaic micropore structure. This structural transformation can constrain the collapse of the soil particle framework, reducing the soil’s collapsibility. Figure 5d indicates that the collapsibility coefficient of loess generally exhibits a negative correlation with depth. In addition, the collapsibility of the loess is mainly concentrated within the 2–16 m depth range, with the degree of collapsibility predominantly falling between slight and moderate.

Rapid judgment criterion and verification of loess collapsibility

After analyzing the relationships between the collapsibility coefficient and various physicomechanical indices, a methodology is proposed for rapidly assesing whether the loess in the research area exhibits collapsibility. Among the 259 sets of loess geotechnical test data, 155 sets of soil samples have a collapsibility coefficient not less than 0.015, while 104 sets of samples lack collapsibility, accounting for 40% of the total data. If it is possible to quickly determine that loess lacks collapsibility using one or two indicators, considerable time can be saved during preliminary work. This is highly significant for reducing the test period and accelerating project progress.

Two-parameter prediction model for the loess collapsibility coefficient

Based on the analysis of the relationship between the collapsibility coefficient and various physicomechanical indices, a two-physical-parameter prediction model for the loess collapsibility coefficient is established. After examining numerous combinations of dual-index parameters for the soil mass, the optimal prediction combination is ultimately identified as dry density and void ratio, as depicted in Fig. 6. The two-parameter regression model for predicting the loess collapsibility coefficient is presented in Eq. (3). Machine learning prediction models for the loess collapsibility coefficient

As shown in the previous analysis, when single-parameter and two-parameter indicators are utilized to represent the relationship between the collapsibility coefficient of loess and various soil property parameters in the study area, the fitting accuracy remains relatively low and insufficient. This phenomenon indicates that a single factor does not determine the collapsibility coefficient of loess in the study area but results from the synergistic effect of multiple factors. Therefore, it is necessary to construct a more comprehensive prediction model to relate the collapse coefficient to the existing physicomechanical indices of loess in the laboratory. In this study, three algorithms, random forest (RF), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and support vector machine (SVM), are introduced to establish prediction models for the collapsibility coefficient. However, due to the high linear correlation among various physical indicators, excessive redundancy can occur during the model prediction process, affecting the model’s prediction efficiency. In light of this, cluster analysis is first conducted among the various indicators to rationally select physical parameters.

Clustering analysis of the correlation coefficients among diverse indices.

Using the cluster analysis approach, the various physicomechanical indices of the aformentioned soil mass are categorized into eight groups. Water content and saturation are grouped into one category; wet density, dry density, void ratio, and porosity are classified into another; and the remaining six physical indicators each from separate categories. Depth is a key parameter for describing the spatial position of soil, which indirectly reflects the mechanical state under the current soil’s self-weight. It serve as the external pressure load condition influencing the collapsibility of loess. Saturation characterizes the water content state of the soil, and acts as the external wetting water content condition for loess collapsibility. The void ratio and compression coefficient reflect the internal structural characteristics of loess and serve as internal structural conditions for collapsibility. These four indices cover both internal and external factors affecting loess collapsibility and can comprehensively reflect the collapsibility properties of soil. Based on this, this study finally selects depth (H), saturation (Sr), void ratio (e), and compression coefficient (as) as the input indices for the machine learning model to construct a prediction model for the collapsibility coefficient of loess. Data division and evaluation indicators

In the process of model prediction, the entire dataset was divided into a training set and a testing set in a ratio of 7:3. SHAP interpretability analysis

As revealed by the above analysis, Machine learning models exhibit better predictive performance. However, compared with the two-parameter model, their higher complexity leads to reduced model interpretability. To address the interpretability issue of prediction models, this study introduces SHAP for visual analysis of prediction results, which not only explains the model’s prediction outcomes but also provides support for the reliability of the model results.

SHAP value

Based on game theory, measures the impact of each feature on the prediction result by calculating the contribution value of each feature to the prediction outcome, thereby achieving both local and global interpretability39. Conclusions

Based on correlation analysis and clustering analysis, this study adopts a two-parameter model and three machine learning algorithms (RF, XGBoost, and SVM) to establish a model for the relationship between loess collapsibility coefficient and void ratio, depth, saturation, and compression coefficient in Qingyang City, Longdong region. The prediction results are verified using SHAP interpretability analysis. The main conclusions are as follows:The loess collapsibility coefficient exhibits an extremely strong correlation with the void ratio, a strong correlation with depth, and a moderate correlation with saturation and compression coefficient.The conditions in which the void ratio of loess is less than 0.960 and the soil layer depth exceeds 16 m can serve as a rapid judgment criterion for the non-collapsibility of loess in the study area.The XGBoost model exhibits superior predictive performance compared to the RF, SVM, and two-parameter models. In terms of the coefficient of determination (R2), the training set performance ranks as XGBoost (0.910) > RF (0.895) > SVM (0.874) > two-parameter model (0.799); the testing set performance ranks as XGBoost (0.900) > SVM (0.861) > RF (0.856) > two-parameter model (0.835). Additionally, the XGBoost prediction model achieves a classification accuracy of 92.7% for distinguishing between non-collapsible and collapsible loess, indicating a relatively high prediction performance.The SHAP algorithm was used to perform an interpretability analysis on the established prediction model. The results indicate that void ratio, depth, and compression coefficient are key factors influencing the prediction of collapsibility coefficient, and the influence trends of each factor on model predictions are consistent with theoretical cognition. Through the analysis of the dependence plot of the XGBoost model, it is found that soils with a depth of less than 11 m and a void ratio greater than 1.02 have high collapsibility tendency (with positive SHAP values).-

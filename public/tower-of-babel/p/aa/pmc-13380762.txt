Quantifying vegetation canopy interception drivers and runoff effects using a coupled RS-Gash-WEP model
Authors: Xiangyang Zhang, Boya Gong, Sheng Yan, Zening Wu, Wei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe canopy interception driving mechanism and its watershed-scale runoff influence are urgent research priorities. The WEP model was improved by coupling it with the RS-Gash canopy interception model and incorporating remote-sensing vegetation cover and leaf area index. The coupled model was applied to the upper Pihe River Basin, and structural equation modeling was used to identify interception drivers and runoff effects. Results showed the improved WEP model boosted simulation accuracy (NSE = 0.79, R2 = 0.80), with the canopy retention module raising NSE by 0.07–0.17 in upstream and downstream regions. Canopy saturated rainfall (path coefficient = 0.69), regulated by rainfall intensity, leaf area index, and vegetation cover, dominated interception; vegetation cover exerted weak direct effects (path coefficient = 0.20). Enhanced interception reduced runoff intensity, mitigating hydrological cycles by decreasing surface runoff (impact coefficient = 0.47). This study provided a scientific basis for revealing the regulatory mechanisms of canopy interception on watershed hydrological processes and improving watershed-scale hydrological modeling methods.

Introduction

Canopy interception, a critical component of the soil-plant-atmosphere continuum and a key link in hydrological cycles, generally functions to moderate rainfall processes and provides essential ecological services such as climate regulation, erosion reduction, flood peak attenuation, and water conservation.1,2,3 Research on canopy interception focuses on the processes of interception and evaporation of rainwater on the vegetation canopy, which has significant impacts on aspects such as the redistribution of rainfall, the regulation of microclimate, and the replenishment of soil moisture.4,5,6 The dynamic changes of canopy interception are not only affected by biological factors, such as tree species characteristics, stand structure, and canopy density, but also jointly influenced by abiotic factors, including rainfall characteristics (e.g., rainfall intensity, raindrop size, etc.), meteorological conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity, wind speed, etc.), and topographic factors (e.g., slope gradient, slope aspect, slope position, etc.).7,8,9,10,11 Cebulski and Pomeroy12 recently confirmed the significant coupling effect of meteorological factors and canopy density on interception processes, which further highlights the complexity of the driving mechanism of canopy interception. Studying the mechanisms of canopy interception processes and their spatiotemporal variations is of great significance for identifying the causes of runoff generation processes and improving the accuracy of distributed hydrological models.13,14,15

Research on the driving mechanisms of canopy interception and its impacts on runoff generation faces pressing needs for model improvement and scale expansion.

On one hand, canopy interception modules in physically based distributed hydrological models often rely on generalized calculations, limiting high-precision characterization. In recent years, a series of relevant studies have been conducted successively. Vlček et al.16 discussed multiple interception simulation methods and their effects on the HBV (Hydrologiska Byråns Vattenballansavdelning)-light model, showing that subtracting a constant from each rainfall event improved model efficiency and accuracy. This highlights that even simple canopy interception models can enhance specific runoff simulations. Zhao et al.17 enhanced the Biome-BGC (biogeochemical) model’s temporal scale to hourly and added a theoretical canopy interception module. At the Qianyanzhou site, the modified model better matched measurements, with lower annual GPP (gross primary productivity) and ET (evapotranspiration) than the original daily-scale model. While canopy interception correction didn’t boost GPP and ET accuracy much, it notably improved canopy interception, soil moisture, and runoff simulations. A pressing priority lies in advancing the canopy interception module within large-scale hydrological models or in coupling existing canopy interception models, so as to precisely characterize the intricate links of the canopy interception process within the hydrological cycle, and to further explore the driving mechanisms of canopy interception at the watershed scale and its impacts on runoff generation.

On the other hand, studies on canopy interception have mostly focused on small spatial scales, such as the interception capacity of individual tree species or single forest stands, while there is a lack of research on variability in canopy interception across larger spatial scales, especially under different slope positions. Most canopy interception models are based on the large scale, such as the Horton model,18 Rutter model,19 and Gash analytical model,20 which have been widely utilized in canopy interception. In recent years, researchers have combined remote sensing technology to modify some parameters in canopy interception models, thereby achieving scale conversion. Cui et al.21 constructed the RS-Gash (remote-sensing-based Gash) model based on the Gash model and verified it in the small watersheds of Dayekou and Pai Lu Gou in the upper reaches of the Heihe River. Zagyvai-Kiss et al.22 further optimized the physically based canopy interception model by integrating high-resolution remote sensing data, which provides a new technical support for the accurate characterization of canopy interception at grid scale. The RS-Gash model combines remote sensing vegetation canopy parameters with meteorological data, extending the original Gash model from the plot scale to the regional scale. It not only reveals the mechanism of canopy interception but also simulates the interception situation of each pixel and explores its spatial heterogeneity.21,23 The simplified parameter set of the RS-Gash model not only reduces the complexity of the model but also decreases the computation time and data demand, enhancing the practical application value for rapid assessment and monitoring. However, the quality and accuracy of remote sensing data directly affect the model output. Cloud cover, satellite image resolution, and data processing errors may impact the accuracy of the results. Although the RS-Gash model has been well applied at the regional scale, its applicability at the local or micro scale still needs further verification. Local climatic conditions, vegetation types, and their interactions may pose challenges to the universality and accuracy of the model. In the context of agricultural and ecological water management, canopy interception exerts a profound influence on irrigation planning and efficiency.24,25 By altering rainfall redistribution and modifying the proportion of effective precipitation that infiltrates the soil, interception directly determines the amount of water available in the crop root zone. High interception rates in forested or mixed vegetation areas can substantially reduce throughfall and soil moisture replenishment, leading to underestimated irrigation demand if not properly accounted for in irrigation quota calculations. Conversely, areas with low interception capacity, such as shrublands or cultivated vegetation zones, exhibit greater rainfall infiltration and slower runoff generation, which can extend soil moisture retention and reduce irrigation frequency.

To address the above limitations, this study takes the upper Pihe River Basin as the study area and seeks to improve the representation of canopy interception in distributed hydrological modeling. The specific targets are as follows: (1) couple the RS-Gash model with the WEP (water and energy transfer processes) model to build a grid-scale coupled hydrological model, (2) quantify dominant drivers and interactions of canopy interception through structural equation model (SEM), and (3) reveal the regulatory effects of canopy interception on runoff generation. To achieve these objectives, a coupled RS-Gash-WEP distributed hydrological model is developed and applied in the study basin. The improved model is intended to provide a more accurate description of land-surface hydrological processes and a useful methodological reference for hydrological simulation and water resource management in mountainous watersheds.

Results

Simulation verification accuracy and results

In this study, a WEP nested model with modified watershed canopy interception was developed to simulate the runoff variations at the Huangwei River station, the Mozitan station, and the Foziling station during the period from 2000 to 2015. The modified RS-Gash-WEP model was subjected to simulation (2000–2013), validation (2013–2016), and operational testing (2016–2021) across three hydrological stations. This multi-stage verification (16 years of simulation and validation +5 years of operation) effectively captures the hydrological cycle’s response to long-term canopy interception dynamics. Figures 1A–1C show the simulation performance and operational continuity of measured hydrological data at the Huangwei River station, the Mozitan station, and the Foziling station, respectively. As indicated in the figures, the runoff simulated by the model developed in this study is in close agreement with the observed runoff, which is further validated during the verification period. The model operation results from 2016 to 2020 show that the basin runoff has increased significantly after 2016. The simulation (2000–2013), validation (2013–2016), and operational (2016–2021) periods were selected to ensure sufficient temporal coverage of hydrological processes while maintaining data completeness. During this period, no major hydrological infrastructure changes occurred in the basin. The comprehensive direct impact results show that the loadings of the two observation indicators of canopy saturated rainfall in this area are 0.68 and 0.50 respectively. From the analysis of the influence results of path coefficients, in this area, the dominant factor controlling canopy interception is canopy saturated rainfall, with a path coefficient of 0.693, followed by rainfall and evapotranspiration during rainfall, which are 0.405 and 0.198, respectively. The impact coefficient of potential evapotranspiration on evapotranspiration during rainfall reaches 0.749. The results show that the impact degree of canopy saturated rainfall increases with the improvement of regional vegetation coverage. The regional rainfall is relatively abundant, and canopy interception is easy to reach saturation. Li et al.26 focused on the Loess Plateau under large-scale afforestation, finding that vegetation factors dominate rainfall interception (RI) changes with a significant upward RI trend, while Wang et al.27 studied the arid-semiarid Qilian Mountains, revealing shrub as the highest interception vegetation type with RI accounting for 14.69% of rainfall. Compared with these two studies, our research in the subtropical humid Upper Pihe River Basin confirms the universal dominance of canopy saturated rainfall as the core driver, but differs in that: (1) coniferous forests are the dominant interception vegetation with a higher interception ratio (20%–25%) than the Qilian Mountains (14.97% for forests) and the Loess Plateau and (2) unlike the Loess Plateau where afforestation-driven vegetation coverage is the key regulating factor, rainfall intensity and LAI play a more prominent role here due to stable vegetation patterns.

Impact of canopy interception on irrigation management in the upper Pihe River Basin

Against the background of the upper Pihe River Basin’s distinct vegetation spatial distribution (coniferous forests in the central and western regions, shrubs in the north-central area, and scattered cultivated vegetation in the south and northeast) and 1505.4 mm multi-year average annual rainfall, canopy interception plays a crucial regulatory role in the regional water cycle. It reshapes natural rainfall redistribution in forest ecosystems and interacts with artificial irrigation systems, directly affecting the formulation of irrigation strategies, calculation of irrigation quotas, and optimization of scheduling schemes. SEM results highlight canopy saturated rainfall (whole-basin path coefficient 0.693) as the dominant driver of interception; in upper coniferous forest areas and lower mixed vegetation areas, high interception reduces throughfall, especially in the dry season, lowering soil moisture replenishment (e.g., effective rainfall to the soil layer in coniferous forests above the Huangweihe station decreases by 12%–18% per improved WEP model simulations) and increasing irrigation demand. In midstream shrub-dominated areas, lower canopy interception capacity (model NSE improvement only 0.02–0.04 after incorporating the RS-Gash module) enhances its rainfall buffering effect, delaying rainfall arrival, reducing surface runoff intensity (impact coefficient 0.47), and extending 0–60 cm root-zone soil moisture retention by 3–5 days, thus cutting irrigation frequency.

The coupled RS-Gash-WEP model (the Foziling station simulation accuracy R2 up to 0.80), which integrates remote sensing-derived parameters like vegetation coverage (FVC) and LAI, enables dynamic simulation of canopy interception’s spatiotemporal variations, addressing the flaw of traditional irrigation quota calculations that ignore interception losses. For upper coniferous forests (growing season LAI = 1.8–2.5, canopy saturated rainfall Pi = 8–12 mm), monthly interception loss accounts for 20%–25% of total rainfall, requiring a 15%–20% increase in irrigation amount; for southern cultivated vegetation areas (FVC = 0.4–0.6, Pi = 4–6 mm), interception loss is 8%–12% of rainfall, allowing irrigation quotas to be adjusted by grid-by-grid model-calculated monthly rainfall intensity (5%–8% reduction in high-intensity months like July-August and slight increase in low-intensity months like May and September). In areas with high canopy interception, the increased efficiency of water use due to accurate interception modeling allows for better irrigation scheduling, reducing water wastage and optimizing resource use. Meanwhile, canopy interception’s spatial heterogeneity (significant model accuracy improvement in upper and lower reaches, minor in midstream) supports differentiated scheduling: temporally, avoid irrigation during canopy saturation (24–36 h post >50 mm daily rainfall in lower reaches, to prevent 10%–15% more surface runoff) and irrigate in early morning/late evening in the dry season (to reduce evaporation loss, with potential evapotranspiration’s impact coefficient on rainfall-period evapotranspiration at 0.749); spatially, use “small-amount, frequent” drip irrigation in upper coniferous forests (ΔNSE = 0.11–0.17), sprinkler irrigation (5–8 mm/h intensity) in midstream shrubs, and surface irrigation with real-time interception data adjustment in southern cultivated areas.

As an important water conservation area of the upper Huaihe River Basin, the upper Pihe River Basin relies on canopy interception research to balance ecological protection and water utilization. For central coniferous forest ecological reserves, the improved WEP model simulates vegetation water demand under interception, showing that maintaining 150–200 mm annual canopy interception ensures both coniferous forest growth and stable runoff regulation (impact coefficient 0.47). The optimized irrigation strategy enabled by the improved model not only improves irrigation efficiency but also ensures that ecological needs are met without overexploiting water resources. In water allocation between agricultural and ecological irrigation, interception loss differences (with coniferous forests experiencing 2–3 times the loss of cultivated vegetation) are considered to appropriately increase ecological water, safeguarding ecosystem stability while supporting agricultural production. In summary, this research enriches the understanding of regional ecohydrological processes and provides a scientific basis for precision, water-saving, and ecological irrigation management. Integrating coupled model results into decisions achieves the balance of improving irrigation efficiency, protecting the ecological environment, and optimizing water resource allocation.

The proposed irrigation scheme is ecologically and economically feasible; it aligns with the basin’s water conservation goals without disturbing native vegetation and uses portable water-saving facilities that reduce water use by over 30% at lower costs compared with traditional methods. This balance of ecological protection and cost-effectiveness ensures the scheme is practical and scalable for real-world implementation in mountainous watersheds like the upper Pihe River Basin.

Broader implications and transferability

Although this study focuses on the upper Pihe River Basin, the proposed RS-Gash-WEP framework has potential applicability to other humid mountainous watersheds where vegetation strongly affects rainfall redistribution and runoff generation. By incorporating remote sensing-derived vegetation coverage and LAI, the model provides a practical way to represent spatially heterogeneous canopy interception in distributed hydrological simulations. The results indicate that canopy saturated rainfall is a key link between rainfall characteristics, vegetation structure, interception loss, and runoff response, suggesting that explicit consideration of canopy interception can improve the estimation of effective rainfall, soil moisture replenishment, and surface runoff in similar forested or mixed vegetation basins.

The findings can also inform watershed water-resource management beyond the present study area. With appropriate local calibration of canopy parameters and validation using observed hydrological data, the framework could support precision irrigation scheduling, ecological water allocation, and runoff regulation in regions with comparable vegetation and rainfall conditions. However, because the empirical parameters used in this study reflect the specific climatic, vegetation, and terrain characteristics of the upper Pihe River Basin, further testing across different basins and environmental settings is needed to confirm the broader transferability of the approach.

Discussion

This study developed a coupled RS-Gash-WEP distributed hydrological model to improve the simulation of vegetation canopy interception and its hydrological effects in the upper Pihe River Basin. By integrating remote sensing-derived vegetation coverage and LAI into the WEP model, the improved framework provided a more explicit representation of rainfall redistribution before runoff generation and enhanced runoff simulation accuracy across different sub-basins. The model achieved good performance, with NSE and R2 reaching 0.79 and 0.80, respectively, at the Foziling station, indicating that the incorporation of canopy interception can effectively improve watershed-scale hydrological simulation.

The results show that canopy saturated rainfall is the dominant factor controlling canopy interception dynamics, with a basin-scale path coefficient of approximately 0.69. Rainfall intensity and LAI jointly regulate canopy saturated rainfall, whereas vegetation coverage exerts a relatively weak direct effect on interception changes. These findings indicate that canopy interception is not controlled by vegetation coverage alone but by the combined influence of rainfall characteristics, canopy storage capacity, and vegetation structure. The study further confirms that canopy interception significantly affects runoff generation by reducing effective rainfall reaching the ground surface and weakening surface runoff intensity. Among the runoff-generation drivers, canopy interception showed the strongest influence, with a path coefficient of approximately 0.47–0.48, and its effect became more pronounced from upstream to downstream areas.

In addition, incorporating canopy interception into the distributed hydrological model improved the estimation of effective rainfall and soil moisture replenishment, providing practical implications for precision irrigation and ecological water-resource management. For example, monthly interception loss in coniferous forest regions accounted for approximately 20%–25% of total rainfall, suggesting that irrigation quotas may need to increase by 15%–20% in these areas. Overall, the coupled RS-Gash-WEP framework provides a scalable approach for integrating vegetation interception processes into watershed-scale hydrological simulations and offers useful support for runoff regulation, irrigation scheduling, and water-resource management in similar subtropical mountainous regions.

Limitations of the study

In the study, a coupled RS-Gash-WEP model was proposed to simulate canopy interception and its impact on runoff, which optimizes the interception module with remote sensing parameters and effectively improves hydrological simulation accuracy. However, there are certain limitations, discussed as follows.

Existing studies have shown that canopy component differences (e.g., leaves and branches) and seasonal structure dynamics affect interception processes. This study simplifies canopy structure parameters and does not distinguish component-specific interception contributions when constructing the model. In the future, different canopy components and seasonal dynamics will be incorporated to refine the interception simulation framework. In addition, the proposed model was developed and validated only in the upper Pihe River Basin with specific vegetation and climatic conditions, and therefore, its applicability to other climate zones needs further verification. In future, we will conduct model validation across multiple basins with different environmental conditions and integrate climate change scenarios to improve the model’s applicability and predictive ability.

Method details

Study area

This study selects the Pihe River Basin above the Foziling Reservoir in the mountainous area of the upper Huaihe River Basin as the research area. The geographical coordinates of the basin range from 30°55′N to 31°23′N and 115°46′E to 116°30′E, with a total area of approximately 1817 km2. The altitude varies between 84 m and 1740 m, with an average slope of 17.13° (Figure S1). The multi-year average annual rainfall in the upper reaches of the study area reaches 1505.4 mm. The surface relief in the upper Pihe River Basin is relatively large, ranging from 0 to 198 m, with the main spatial feature being high relief in the central part and lower in the surrounding areas, and the slope ranging from 0 to 69.55°. In terms of the spatial distribution of different vegetation types, coniferous forests are mainly distributed in the central and western parts of the upper Pihe River Basin, while shorter shrubs occupy the north-central part. The total area of these two vegetation types accounts for more than 60% of the total basin area. Broad-leaved forests are interspersed among the shrubs, and cultivated vegetation is scarce, scattered in the southern and northeastern parts of the basin, with significant differences in the quantity of different vegetation types. A natural gradient of canopy interception capacity has formed among its sub-basins, providing an ideal natural research carrier for exploring the large-scale spatiotemporal variation characteristics of canopy interception and its correlation with runoff generation.

Data

The data utilized in this paper are divided into in-situ observation data and remote sensing data. The in-situ observation data come from the Danlongsi Field Experimental Station in Huoshan County from 2018 to 2023, including canopy interception data and maximum leaf water-holding capacity. Remote sensing and meteorological data, including rainfall (2000–2019), Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI, 2000–2019), average temperature (2000–2019), and other key environmental parameters, were sourced from long-term datasets (e.g., National Tibetan Plateau Data Center, Earth Resource Data Cloud), ensuring temporal continuity and representativeness of interannual variations. Numerical simulation based on coupled WEP model and modified RS-Gash Model

The WEP distributed hydrological model is a distributed model based on the interaction of “water-energy-atmosphere-vegetation-soil”, which uses the basic equations of energy conservation and mass conservation to simulate hydrological and energy cycles, and is particularly suitable for hydrological research under complex terrain and basin conditions.28,29,30 The RS-Gash model is an extension of the Gash model at the regional scale, integrating remote sensing data to evaluate the impact of vegetation cover on rainfall interception. By introducing the Vegetation Area Index (VAI) and Fractional Vegetation Cover (FVC), the RS-Gash model can comprehensively consider the role of vegetation elements in the rainfall interception process, making it more applicable in large-scale ecohydrological research while maintaining the core advantages of the Gash model, i.e., a simplified parameter set that is easy to apply and interpret.31,32 The WEP model was implemented at a 1000 m grid resolution. All input data with different original resolutions were resampled to 1000 m to ensure spatial consistency.

In the WEP model, vegetation coverage (Veg) is mainly used to reflect the degree of differentiation in canopy interception evaporation (Ep). The specific calculation is as follows:(Equation 1)Ei=Veg×δ×Epwhere Eiis the modified canopy interception evaporation, δ is the proportion of wet leaf area to the total leaf area. In the model, vegetation coverage is basically given as a constant parameter, and the basin or study area is uniformly set.

In this study, the RS-Gash model is used to directly determine the canopy interception amount instead of reversely determining it through the difference in forest evaporation. Considering the periodic changes of canopy unsaturated and saturated periods and the dynamic setting in each hydrological response unit in space, when the rainfall is lower than the canopy saturated rainfall Pˆ, it is mainly corrected by vegetation coverage (Veg); when the rainfall exceeds the canopy saturated rainfall, it is corrected by fully considering the characteristics such as monthly rainfall intensity and average evaporation rate during the canopy saturated period.(Equation 2)Iwep={FVC×P,P<PˆiFVC×[Pˆi+E¯c/R¯×(P−Pˆi)],P≥Pˆiwhere Iwep is the vegetation canopy interception amount at a certain moment, P is the total regional rainfall at that moment, Pˆi is the canopy saturated rainfall in areas with different vegetation types (in this study, based on the observation results of the experimental station, empirical parameters affecting the maximum interception capacity of the canopy are set for different vegetation types, which fully consider the degree of differentiation in time and vegetation types). FVC is the regional vegetation coverage, E¯c is the average evaporation rate during the canopy saturated period, R¯ is the monthly rainfall intensity, calculated on a grid-by-grid scale.(Equation 3)FVC=(NDVIl−NDVImin)/(NDVImax−NDVImin)where NDVIi is the NDVI value of each grid pixel, NDVImax is the maximum NDVI value of vegetation pixels in the basin, NDVImin is the minimum NDVI value of vegetation pixels in the basin.

Canopy saturated rainfall Pˆ represents the critical rainfall value when the regional vegetation canopy interception reaches saturation. After reaching this rainfall value, throughfall in the forest begins to occur. The specific calculation process is as follows:(Equation 4)Pˆ=−R¯/E¯c×Sc×ln[1−(E¯c/R¯)](Equation 5)Sc=n×Smax(Equation 6)Smax=aLAI+bwhere C is the canopy closure, Sc is the adsorption and interception capacity of forest canopy leaves and branches per unit coverage area, i.e., Sc=S/c, Among them, S for arbor forests is obtained according to the fitting empirical formula (Sarbor = 0.688 × P - 1.43), and S for bamboo forests is obtained according to the fitting empirical formula (Sbamboo = 0.567 × P + 0.78), n is the number of rainy days per month in the grid pixel, Smax is the maximum interception capacity of regional vegetation canopy.

The empirical parameters for canopy leaf and branch adsorption capacity were derived from field observations at the Danlongsi Experimental Station and calibrated for the Pihe River Basin. These parameters reflect local vegetation characteristics and rainfall regimes, and are therefore site-specific for this study. The fitting process considered observed maximum interception capacity of each vegetation type and the temporal variability of rainfall, ensuring accurate representation of canopy interception dynamics at the basin scale.(Equation 7)C=max[S(1−exp(−λPd/Smax))]where C is the water adsorption capacity of regional vegetation canopy during precipitation (mm); λ is the forest rainfall interception coefficient (which can be represented by vegetation coverage), Pd is the regional daily rainfall (mm).(Equation 8)E=C/Sc×Ep1×nwhere E is the evaporation amount during rainfall (mm), Sc is the maximum monthly water-holding capacity of the vegetation canopy (mm), Ep1 is the potential evaporation amount (mm).

Based on the optimal parameters of the WEP model with the modified RS-Gash canopy interception module, the runoff results of the modified WEP model R1 and the original WEP model R0 are calculated respectively to determine the impact degree of the modification of the canopy interception module Cd. Driving Mechanism and Attribution Identification Based on Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Model

The Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Model (PLS-SEM) is a complementary method to the traditional structural equation model, which does not require the data to satisfy normal distribution, reducing the requirements of the traditional structural equation model on data.33,34 In addition, since PLS-SEM constructs latent variables by compressing original variables, it can reduce the direct dependence between variables. At the same time, the PLS method aims to extract the principal components of latent variables rather than directly estimate the relationships between latent variables, which makes PLS-SEM less sensitive to data collinearity than traditional SEM, and focuses on the explanatory power (R2) of the model’s latent variables.35,36,37

Analysis of canopy interception driving mechanism

Based on the canopy interception calculation process of the RS-Gash model, this study constructs structural equation models for canopy interception drivers in the upper reaches of the Huangwei River, from Huangwei River to Mozitan, from Mozitan to Foziling, and the entire basin. The observed variables selected include input data of the RS-Gash model such as rainfall, rainfall intensity, vegetation coverage, leaf area index, potential evapotranspiration, and output data such as evapotranspiration during rainfall, canopy interception amount, canopy interception rate, and canopy water adsorption capacity, and runoff volume are selected to construct six latent variables: temperature, precipitation, evapotranspiration, vegetation coverage, canopy interception, and runoff generation, and the influence paths are planned.

Figure S2 is the conceptual diagram of the Conceptual Diagram of the Structural Equation Model (SEM) for Canopy Interception Driving Mechanisms Based on the RS-Gash Model. As shown in the conceptual model, rainfall intensity directly impacts the calculation of canopy saturated rainfall amount, while the leaf area index also feeds into this calculation. Canopy water adsorption and potential evaporation are the drivers for evapotranspiration during rainfall. Vegetation coverage and rainfall, along with the previously calculated canopy saturated rainfall amount and evapotranspiration during rainfall, influence the canopy water storage, which in turn affects the canopy interception amount. Eventually, the canopy interception amount and rainfall determine the canopy interception rate. Meanwhile, evapotranspiration during rainfall, which is calculated from potential evaporation, also has a connection with the overall process. This conceptual model visually presents the driving mechanism of canopy interception through these interrelated factors and their influence paths.

Impact of canopy interception on runoff generation process

Based on the canopy interception mechanism and water cycle theory in the upper Pihe River Basin, the driving factors are determined according to the occurrence process of canopy interception and the surface runoff generation mechanism. Observed variables such as air temperature, rainfall amount, rainfall intensity, vegetation coverage, leaf area index, potential evapotranspiration, evapotranspiration during rainfall, canopy interception amount, canopy interception rate, canopy water adsorption capacity, and runoff volume are selected to construct six latent variables: temperature, precipitation, evapotranspiration, vegetation coverage, canopy interception, and runoff generation, and the influence paths are planned.

Figure S3 is the conceptual diagram of Conceptual Diagram of the Structural Equation Model (SEM) for Basin Canopy Interception-Runoff Generation. In the path planning between latent variables, temperature and precipitation factors, as the main drivers of environmental changes, have a direct impact on vegetation growth. Temperature affects the physiological processes of vegetation, influencing its development and coverage, while rainfall provides the necessary water for vegetation. At the same time, both rainfall and vegetation directly affect canopy interception. Rainfall, with its amount and intensity, determines the potential for interception by the vegetation canopy, and the structure and coverage of vegetation, reflected by factors like vegetation coverage and leaf area index, dictate the capacity of the canopy to intercept rainfall. The above factors jointly act on evapotranspiration. Temperature influences the rate of evapotranspiration, rainfall supplies the water source, and vegetation, through its canopy and physiological activities, modulates the evapotranspiration process. Moreover, canopy interception also plays a role in evapotranspiration, as the water intercepted by the canopy can be evaporated back to the atmosphere. Finally, all these factors, through their interactions in the water cycle, contribute to the generation of runoff, which is the final variable representing the output of the water cycle process in this conceptual model.

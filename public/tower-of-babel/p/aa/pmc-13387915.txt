ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe pathophysiology of idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) is poorly understood and disease‐specific biomarkers are lacking. We aimed to shed light on IIH pathophysiology and identify disease‐specific biomarkers.MethodsThis prospective cross‐sectional cohort study included patients with new‐onset IIH and age‐, body mass index‐, and sex‐matched healthy controls from 2 tertiary Danish Headache Centers (discovery cohort). Liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry analysis was used to measure reproducible proteomic profiles for paired serum and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples. Results were validated using 3 validation cohorts comprising patients with IIH (pwIIH), cerebral sinus venous thrombosis (pwCSVT), and normal pressure hydrocephalus (pwNPH).ResultsWe included 53 pwIIH and 35 controls. PwIIH presented with papilledema and increased median lumbar puncture opening pressure (interquartile range [IQR]) (38.0 [33–46] vs 19.0 [17.0–23.0] cmH2O; p < 0.001) compared to controls. Machine learning analysis identified 20 IIH‐predicting proteins for serum and CSF segregating pwIIH, but neither pwCSVT nor pwNPH, from controls with areas under the curve (AUC) of 0.84 and 0.92 (pwIIH, discovery cohort), 0.99 and 0.90 (pwIIH, validation cohort 1), 0.63 (plasma; pwCSVT), and 0.67 (CSF; pwNPH), respectively. Serum carbonic anhydrases (CA) 1 and 2 were upregulated and among the most IIH‐predicting proteins as were amyloid precursor protein (APP), S100P, and S100A12.InterpretationWe identified a panel of independently validated IIH‐specific candidate biomarkers. The serum candidate biomarkers pointed toward CA2 driven CSF hypersecretion in IIH providing a possible explanation for the therapeutic efficacy of CA inhibitors. The regulation of markers associated with neuronal impairment (APP, S100P/S100A12) confirmed the non‐benign character of IIH. ANN NEUROL 2026;100:255–267

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) primarily affects obese young women with increasing incidence because of pandemic obesity. 1 IIH is characterized by increased cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) pressure without an identifiable cause. Main symptoms are headache, cognitive decline, and visual disturbances because of papilledema, which may result in blindness. 1 The diagnosis is made according to the revised 2013 criteria 2 based on papilledema, elevated lumbar puncture opening pressure (LPOP) (≥25cm H2O), and normal CSF composition, neurological examination, and cerebral magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) apart from 4 defined IIH‐associated MRI characteristics. If elevated intracranial pressure (ICP) is caused by drugs or medical conditions known to raise ICP, the condition is classified as secondary pseudotumor cerebri syndrome (PTCS). 2 Recently, an evidence‐based update of the current diagnostic criteria, still based on ICP‐related rather than disease‐specific characteristics, was published. 3

The pathophysiology behind IIH is still obscure. IIH is linked to young females with truncal obesity and adipose tissue dysfunction. 4 , 5 Accordingly, in contrast to body mass index (BMI), the truncal fat mass correlated with LPOP in IIH patients 5 , 6 and has been suggested to function as an endocrine organ secreting proinflammatory cytokines, adipokines, and hormones involved in IIH pathogenesis. 7 , 8 Altered glucocorticoid regulation, hyperleptinemia, androgen excess, 9 and insulin resistance have also been linked to IIH pathogenesis, 10 , 11 and a recently published work suggested that an interplay of inflammatory, metabolic, and neurodegenerative processes 12 was involved in IIH. Dysfunction of the glymphatic system driven by aquaporin dysfunction, neuro‐glio‐vascular unit disruption, a pro‐inflammatory CSF profile, and impaired glymphatic outflow have been proposed as underlying mechanisms. 13 Finally, these perturbations may result in increased CSF production, reduced CSF drainage, and increased venous sinus pressure as key mechanisms of the disease. 14

Diagnostic biomarkers that could assist clinicians in differentiating IIH from clinical mimics and help understanding the yet unknown underlying pathophysiology of IIH are urgently needed. Blood testing is routinely used in the clinic because of its easy accessibility and minimal invasiveness and would be a preferred source for novel markers. During the past decade, mass spectrometry (MS) based proteomics has become a technology of choice in biomedical research and now allows in‐depth characterization of bodily fluids such as serum or CSF. 15 , 16 , 17 To identify molecular disease mechanisms and potential treatment targets, proteomics, 12 , 18 , 19 and metabolomics 20 , 21 profiles have been investigated in IIH. The results suggest that the renin‐angiotensin system, 18 inflammatory, neuroendocrine and brain plasticity proteins, 12 urea, acetate, lactate/pyruvate ratio 20 and the glucocorticoid metabolism 22 may be linked to the pathophysiology of IIH. However, consistent, reproducible, and IIH‐specific patterns indicative for driving pathways are still lacking.

Therefore, we applied high‐throughput MS‐based proteomics analysis to profile the paired serum and CSF samples from treatment naïve patients with new‐onset IIH compared to age‐, sex‐, and BMI‐matched controls to identify disease‐specific biomarkers. We validated our results in 3 different validation cohorts comprising patients with IIH (pwIIH) from an independent cohort from an Austrian tertiary headache center, patients with cerebral sinus venous thrombosis (pwCSVT), and patients with normal pressure hydrocephalus (pwNPH).

Material and Methods

Participants

Danish IIH‐Patients (Discovery Cohort)

Between January 2018 and August 2021, patients were consecutively recruited at 2 tertiary headache centers in Denmark with multidisciplinary IIH expertise covering a total population of 3.9 million people (Danish Headache Center, Rigshospitalet‐Glostrup and Odense University Hospital). Inclusion criteria for the cohort were 18 years of age or older, ability to give written informed consent, and clinical suspicion of IIH. IIH suspicion was raised by a senior neurologist, or a senior ophthalmologist based on objective findings (eg, papilledema, abducens nerve palsy), clinical symptoms (eg, blurry vision, headache, and pulsatile tinnitus), and phenotype (young female with obesity). IIH was diagnosed according to the diagnostic criteria from 2013. 2 Only treatment‐naïve patients with definite IIH (“patients with IIH”) were included for proteomics analysis. Exclusion criteria for proteomics analysis were the exclusion of an IIH diagnosis (‘non‐IIH’), previous known IIH or IIH relapse, pregnancy, breastfeeding, male sex, secondary PTCS, and missing data, if these were essential to confirm or exclude an IIH diagnosis. Previous known IIH and IIH relapse were excluded to assure that diagnosis was made according to the same standardized diagnostic work‐out and diagnostic criteria, 2 to standardize disease stage, and confine biomarker discovery to the acute new‐onset stage. Moreover, patients were excluded if their serum or CSF samples did not fulfill quality criteria (see ‘Sample collection’). All patients underwent a standardized diagnostic work‐up comprising a semi‐structured medical interview, neurological and ophthalmological examination, neuroimaging, LPOP measurement, routine analysis of blood and CSF, and collection of serum and CSF samples for research purposes. Neuroimaging was evaluated by an experienced senior neuro‐radiologist and ophthalmic measures evaluated by a senior neuro‐ophthalmologist, both masked for the diagnosis of the patients. Parts of the cohort have been published elsewhere. 3 , 23 , 24 , 25 For proteomics analysis, paired serum and CSF samples were collected at time of diagnosis.

Healthy Controls

We recruited 35 healthy controls matched by sex, age, and BMI. Inclusion criteria were ability to give written informed consent, 18 to 45 years of age, BMI of 25 to 60, and 1 or less headache day per week. Papilledema was excluded by confocal color fundus imaging (Compass). Blood and CSF samples were collected in conjunction with the diagnostic work‐up. Controls were neither allowed to suffer from significant comorbidities or conditions associated with elevated ICP (eg, severe anemia) nor to take medications known to cause elevated ICP. Paired serum and CSF samples were collected for each healthy control patient and excluded if samples did not fulfill the quality criteria (see ‘Sample collection’).

Austrian pwIIH (Validation Cohort 1)

We confirmed our results using 27 patients with new‐onset IIH from the Vienna University Hospital as a validation cohort. The patients had to be diagnosed with IIH according to the diagnostic criteria from 2013. 2 The only exclusion criteria for proteomics analysis were pregnancy, breastfeeding, male sex, secondary PTCS, and missing data, if these were essential to confirm or exclude an IIH diagnosis. Moreover, patients were excluded if their serum or CSF samples did not fulfill quality criteria (see ‘Sample collection’). For proteomics analysis, paired serum and CSF samples were collected at time of diagnosis. Seven patients had started treatment with acetazolamide at the time point of blood and CSF sampling. Daily doses of 500mg (n = 4), 750mg (n = 1), 1,000mg (n = 1), or 1,500mg (n = 1) were given.

For PwCSVT and pwNPH (validation cohort 2 and 3) see supporting information and for protocol approvals, registrations, and patient consents see supporting information.

Lumbar Puncture

Measurement of LPOP was standardized, with the relaxed patient placed in left lateral decubitus position, legs, and neck extended. A standard issue manometer was used (cm CSF). CSF composition was analyzed. 3

Cerebral Imaging

Standardized radiological diagnostics were evaluated by an experienced neuroradiologist masked for the results of the diagnostic work‐up and patient identity and comprised 1.5 T or 3 T MRI without contrast, with or without thin‐cut, fat‐suppressed orbital sequences. Choice of modality for cerebral venography depended on contraindications and availability (contrast‐enhanced computed tomography venography or MRI time‐of‐flight without contrast). 3 , 23

Ophthalmological Investigation

The ophthalmological investigation included measurements of visual acuity, visual fields (by automated perimetry), fundus examination, peripapillary, and macular optical coherence tomography (OCT; Heidelberg Spectralis OCT), and Ishihara color plate testing. All assessments were performed at the Departments of Ophthalmology at the Odense University Hospital and the Copenhagen University Hospital‐Rigshospitalet, using a standardized protocol and were independently re‐evaluated by 2 senior neuro‐ophthalmologists experienced in diagnosing papilledema, optic disc edema for other reasons than elevated ICP, and pseudopapilledema. Pseudopapilledema was identified by morphological features (eg, optic disc drusen, small disc with no physiological cup, and pathological vessel alterations) using enhanced depth OCT, supplemented by autofluorescence imaging and B‐scan ultrasonography. Optic disc edema because of other diseases than elevated ICP was distinguished by disease‐specific characteristics. 3

Sample Collection

The samples were collected as described above and stored in a biobank. CSF samples were preferentially taken at the time of diagnostic lumbar puncture, but samples within 30 days of initial diagnosis were accepted. CSF samples were excluded when the time to freeze was more than 3 hours. CSF (5ml) was centrifuged for 10 minutes at 400g at room temperature. The supernatant was aliquoted into 10 portions of 0.5ml and stored at −80°C in polypropylene tubes, Sarstedt, Nuernbrecht, Germany. Whole blood (6ml) was centrifuged for 10 minutes at 2,000g at room temperature. Serum was aliquoted into portions of 0.5ml and stored at −80°C in polypropylene tubes, Sarstedt, Nuernbrecht, Germany. Samples showing serum hemolysis, microscopic erythocytes in CSF, or insufficient quality (eg, deviation of the time to freeze) were considered not to meet the quality criteria and excluded.

Serum Depletion and Digestion

For every serum sample, 300μL of High Select Top 14 Abundant Protein Depletion Resin, Thermo Scientific, Rockford, Illinois, USA was taken in a 96‐well filter plate (Captiva ND Plate 0.45μm, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, California, USA) and centrifuged at 1 k × g for 2 minutes to remove the resin buffer. A total of 190μL of Tris hydrochloride, pH 7.5 buffer, and 10μL of plasma sample were added to the drained resins. The sample solutions were left for 1 hour of incubation with shaking. Post incubation, the Captiva plate was mounted on top of 96‐deep well plates and centrifuged for 2 minutes at 1 k × g to collect a clear solution of depleted plasma. To each sample solution, 50μL of 5% sodium deoxycholate (SDC) and 25mM dithiothreitol (DTT) were added, mixed, and incubated for 1 hour at 37°C. A total of 5μL 1 M IAA was added and incubated for 30 minutes at RT in the dark. Subsequently, 5μL of 260mM DTT was added to quench iodoacetamide (IAA), followed by addition of trypsin (10μL of 0.35μg/μL). Protein digestion proceeded overnight at 37°C. Digested protein samples were moved to a new Captiva plate and 5μL of 100% formic acid was added to each sample. Post thorough mixing, a clear solution of peptides was retrieved in 96‐well plate after centrifugation.

CSF Sample Preparation

For every sample, 50μL of CSF were added to 200μL solution consisting of 1.25% SDC, 6.25mM DTT in 100mM tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (Tris), pH 7.5, mixed and incubated for an hour at 37°C. Afterward, 5μL of 1 M IAA was added, mixed, and incubated at RT for 30 minutes. A total of 5μL of 260mM DTT was added afterward to quench the unreacted IAA. Proteins were digested with trypsin (10μL of 0.15μg/μL was added) overnight at 37°C. Post digestion, 5μL of 100% formic acid was added, the sample was mixed and centrifuged at 20 k × g for 10 minutes, and a clear solution was withdrawn for liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) analysis.

LC–MS/MS Analysis

Nano EASY LC 1000 (Thermo Scientific, Germering, Germany) pumps were used to load and elute peptides from AURORA ELITE, 15cm × 75μm, C18, and 1.6μm column (ionopticks, Collingwood, Victoria, Australia). A total of 200ng of each sample in solvent A (0.1% FA) were loaded. Peptides were eluted with increasing concentration of solvent B (95% ACN, 0.1% FA): 7% B at start, which increased linearly to 20% in 20 minutes, to 35% in 7 minutes, and to 100% in 5 minutes, at which it stayed for 7 minutes, and returned to 0% in 3 minutes. Flow rate was 300nl/minute. Column oven was set to 50°C. Peptides were analyzed by DIA (dia‐parallel accumulation‐serial fragmentation [dia‐PASEF]) using a timsTOF HT (Bruker) with the following settings: MS scan 100–1700 m/z, positive polarity; TIMS: 1/K0 start–end 0.6–1.6 V × s/cm2; 100ms accumulation time; and ramp time 100ms. dia‐PASEF: cycle time 1.7 seconds; 15 MS/MS ramps; 26 MS/MS windows; mass range: 339.9–1119.9Da;and mobility range: 0.66–1.4 1/K0.

Quality Controls

Please see the supporting information for quality controls. Both discovery and validation experiments were conducted in the DIA (dia‐PASEF) mode. Specifically, an independent cohort was analyzed in validation phase to confirm the fidelity of candidate biomarkers using same technology and similar cohort size.

Data Analysis

The mass spectrometry raw data were analyzed using Spectronaut software, primarily using the standard settings with the following adjustments: Cleavage: Trypsin, specific, peptide length 7–52 AA; Modifications: fixed carbamidomethylation of Cys, variable oxidation of Met. Identification: directDIA+ (Deep), Protein‐Q value cutoff (experiment): 0.01. Quantification: MS level: MS2, “Area” type, cross‐run normalization applied. List of identified and quantified proteins was then exported and used to perform downstream analysis using Hiplot, infernoRDN, Python, Quickomics, and Rv4.4.1. 26 , 27 , 28

Pearson correlational analyses were performed to identify the association between top features and BMI and ICP. This analysis was carried out to assess whether any correlations were related to the pathogenesis of IIH. The correlation was considered significant when Pearson correlation coefficient value was at least 0.5 and p value <0.05.

Functional Analysis

Functional enrichment analysis for the panel proteins was performed to identify underlying biological pathways linked with the disease process. Specifically, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways enrichment analysis were carried out using a web‐based Enrichr tool. The analyzed data was visualized to display associated networks and terms using SRplot web‐based tool. 29

Identification IIH‐Specific Proteins

OmicLearn (v1.4) 30 was used for performing data analysis, model execution, and creation of plots and charts. Machine learning was done in Python (3.9.1). Feature tables were imported via the Pandas package (1.5.1) and manipulated using the Numpy package (1.23.4). The machine learning pipeline was used using the scikit‐learn package (1.1.3). The Plotly (5.9.0) library was used for plotting. The Spectronaut protein group matrix table was used as an input for OmicLearn. No normalization and imputation were performed on the data. Features were selected using an ExtraTrees (n_trees = 500) strategy with the maximum number of 20 features. During the training, normalization and selection of features were performed individually using the data of each split. For classification, we used a XGBoost‐Classifier (random_state = 23 learning_rate = 0.3 min_split_loss = 0 max_depth = 15 min_child_weight = 1). We used a repeated (n_repeats = 10), stratified cross‐validation (RepeatedStratifiedKFold, n_splits = 5) approach to classify control versus IIH. This leads to a total of 50 iterations for the training of the model, each time with 35 features calculating the 20 most IIH‐predicting proteins with their importance score assigned by a classifier. 31

Other Statistical Analysis

The statistical analysis for metadata was performed using SPSS (ver. 29.0; IBM, Arhus, Denmark). The groups were compared using the chi‐squared for categorical variables and the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables. The significance level used for all analyses was p < 0.05.

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 144 Danish patients with clinically suspected IIH were included and 53 patients fulfilled the eligibility criteria. Ninety‐one patients were excluded because of disproven IIH diagnosis (n = 54), secondary PTCS (n = 22), suggested IIH without papilledema (n = 2), previous IIH (n = 4), pregnancy (n = 1), male sex (n = 2), language barrier (n = 1), and acetazolamide treatment (n = 5). Figure 2 describes patient identification and selection. Moreover, we included 27 Austrian pwIIH as validation cohort 1 (Fig S1). Patient characteristics are shown in Table 1. All included subjects were female. Danish pwIIH, Austrian pwIIH, and healthy controls did not differ in terms of sex (100% females in all groups), median age (interquartile range [IQR]) (28 [23–35] vs 32 [24–39] vs 29 [24–33] years) or median BMI [IQR] (36 [32–40] vs 35 [31–41] vs 36 [31–44] kg/m 2 ). Danish and Austrian pwIIH showed significantly higher median LPOP [IQR] (38.0 [33–46] and 34.0 [31.0–37.5] vs 19.0 [17.0–23.0] cm H2O; p < 0.001) and presented more frequently with papilledema (44/44 [100%] and 27/27 [100%] vs 0/32 [0%]) compared to healthy controls.

Proteomics

For proteomics analysis of the discovery cohort, we included paired serum and CSF samples from Danish pwIIH and age‐, sex‐, and BMI‐matched healthy controls (serum proteomics: n = 43 patients and n = 31 controls; CSF proteomics: n = 44 patients and n = 32 controls). Reproducible proteome profiles were recorded for the paired serum and CSF samples. The LC–MS/MS proteomics analysis resulted in the identification of 2250 (CSF) and 1289 (serum) proteins. The high dynamic range of serum proteome resulted in the identification of fewer protein groups compared to CSF despite the immunodepletion approach. The overlap for protein group identifications was 34.8% between CSF and serum (Fig S2).

Serum Proteome

The principal component analysis (PCA) revealed some segregation between Danish pwIIH and healthy controls as shown in Fig S3. Machine learning analysis identified the 20 most IIH‐predicting proteins in the serum of Danish pwIIH compared to healthy controls (Fig S4A, Table S1), which segregated pwIIH from controls with an area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC) curve value of 0.84 (Fig S4B). Figure 3 shows the 20 most IIH‐predicting proteins in the serum of Danish pwIIH compared to controls in a heat map. Of the 20 most IIH‐predicting serum proteins, only F9 was found to be positively correlated with ICP (Pearson correlation analyses r = 0.5; p = 6.3e−06) (Fig S5). Subsequently, KEGG pathway analysis further highlighted the interconnected functions of subsets of the 20 proteins, RELN, COL6A2, COL6A1, FN1, HSPG2, CA1, CA2, and MMP2 in the complex pathophysiology of IIH by pointing toward dysregulated extracellular matrix (ECM) homeostasis, fluid dynamics, and inflammation (Fig S6, Table S2). In detail, KEGG pathway analysis revealed an involvement of bicarbonate related metabolism (proximal tubule bicarbonate reclamation, collecting duct acid secretion, nitrogen metabolism carbonic anhydrases 1 [CA1] and carbonic anhydrases 2 [CA2]) (Fig S6, Table S2) corresponding to the upregulation of carbonic anhydrases 1 and 2 (CA1: log2FC = 1.10, p = 0.0002; CA2: log2FC = 1.02, p = 4.35E−05) in IIH patients. Moreover, amyloid precursor protein (APP) was found to be upregulated (log2FC = 0.40, p = 0.01), and S100P and S100A12 were among the most IIH‐predictable proteins, too (Table S1).

CSF Proteome

The PCA analysis of CSF proteome data identified differences between pwIIH and controls at time of diagnosis (Fig S7). To identify a panel of putative biomarkers, we applied machine learning identifying the 20 most IIH‐predicting proteins in the CSF of Danish pwIIH compared to healthy controls (Fig S8A), which segregated pwIIH from controls with an AUROC curve value of 0.92 (Fig S8B, Table S5). Figure 4 shows the 20 most IIH‐predicting proteins in the CSF of Danish pwIIH compared to controls in a heat map. Glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase 1 (GOT1; Pearson correlation analysis: r = 0.53; p = 2.9e−07), chromogranin‐A (CHGA; Pearson correlation analysis: r = 0.52; p = 1.4e−06), and profilin‐2 (PFN2; Pearson correlation analysis: r = 0.58; p = 3.6e‐08) were found to be positively correlated with ICP, whereas apolipoprotein D (APOD) was found to be negatively correlated with ICP (Pearson correlation analysis: r = −0.47; p = 5.5e−06) (Fig S9). The KEGG enriched proteins from CSF data—TF, HEPH, ALDOA, GPX3, SOD1, PFN2, GOT1, CA4, and LAMC1—were linked to altered oxidative mechanisms and metabolic regulation, involvement of bicarbonate metabolism / pH homeostasis related pathways, and amino acid metabolism (Fig S10, Table S3; FC and p‐values see Table S5).

To investigate the extent to which our IIH‐predictive proteins were specific for IIH, we evaluated the IIH‐predictive serum proteins in a cohort consisting of individuals with CSVT (n = 10) and healthy controls (n = 16), whose plasma proteomics—comprising 10 of 20 IIH‐predictive serum proteins—were available online. 32 Moreover, we evaluated the IIH‐predicting CSF proteins in a cohort of pwNPH (n = 68) and patients suspected to suffer from NPH, in which NPH was refuted (non‐NPH; n = 33) comprising 18 of 20 IIH‐predictive CSF proteins. Demographic data of the validation cohort 3 is shown in Table S7. However, neither the IIH‐predictive serum proteins nor the IIH‐predictive CSF proteins were able to distinguish pwCVST (AUROC, 0.63) or pwNPH (AUROC curve value 0.67) from their respective controls (Figs 6 and 7).

Discussion

We identified proteomic profiles of serum and CSF in treatment‐naïve Danish patients with new‐onset IIH compared to age‐, sex‐, and BMI‐matched healthy controls (discovery cohort). Afterward, the serum and CSF proteomic profiles were applied on Austrian patients with new‐onset IIH compared to age‐, sex‐, and BMI‐matched healthy controls (validation cohort 1). The proteomic profiles of serum and CSF segregated Austrian pwIIH from control subjects with an AUROC of 0.99 and 0.86, respectively, confirming their validity, but were not able to differentiate pwCSVT and pwNPH from their respective controls confirming their IIH‐specificity.

Until now, the reliability of the results of previous proteomics studies was limited by small sample sizes, a lack of control groups matched for age, BMI, and sex as well as lack of independent validation cohorts, and diagnostic uncertainties in pwIIH. 12 , 18 , 19 Moreover, control subjects were not necessarily healthy, but suffered from other diseases, which may have affected their proteomic profiles 19 and the treatment status of the pwIIH (treatment‐naïve or treated with ICP‐lowering drugs, eg, acetazolamide/topiramate at time of CSF collection) was not provided. 19 Another study used pooled CSF samples, which neglects substantial individual biological variability while also reducing the statistical power of the data. 33

Therefore, one of the major strengths of our study is its design comparing proteomic profiles of paired serum and CSF samples from treatment‐naïve Danish patients with new‐onset IIH at diagnosis with age‐, BMI‐, and sex‐matched healthy controls and their validation in pwIIH, confirming their validity, but also in pwCSVT and pwNPH, confirming their disease‐specificity. Our proteomic profiles describe, in line with already published data, multiple metabolic disturbances including amino acid and lipid metabolism. 20 , 21 , 34 However, some of our additional findings deserve to be discussed in the following.

Serum Proteome

A major finding is the significant predictive impact of CA1 and CA2 in the serum of pwIIH, which both were found to be consistently upregulated in pwIIH from both cohorts. CA2 is expressed in the choroid plexus and plays a crucial role in CSF production by converting CO2 + H2O = >HCO3 − + H+. 35 Because patients treated with acetazolamide or topiramate were excluded from the analyses, the results cannot be ascribed to a treatment‐related iatrogenic effect but rather provide evidence that CA2 may contribute to increased ICP in pwIIH. This would also explain why treatment with CA inhibitors is relevant and effective. Given that IIH is a disorder of androgen excess, previous findings that testosterone increased CA2 expression in choroid plexus could suggest that the androgen excess in IIH could drive the increased levels of CA2 and therefore CSF secretion. 9 CA1 is of interest too, as it is expressed in the motor neurons in the human spinal cord 36 and it is overexpressed in hippocampal astrocytes and linked to depressive disorders 37 that also are common in IIH. 24 The observed pathway enrichment in nitrogen metabolism may explain previous findings of deranged amino acid metabolism in the serum of newly diagnosed, treatment naïve pwIIH. 38

We observed down‐regulation of ECM proteins in pwIIH, especially collagen VI (COL6A1/2), reelin (RELN), HSPG2, and FN1, which is consistent with metabolic dysregulation and chronic inflammation previously linked to obesity associated with IIH.

We observed up‐/down‐regulation of markers that were previously associated with neuronal impairment stressing the non‐benign nature of IIH. For instance, we found down‐regulation of RELN. In Alzheimer's disease, disruption of the RELN pathway was linked to tau‐hyperphosphorylation, amyloid beta accumulation and cognitive decline. 39 We found upregulation of APP, which becomes upregulated in response to stress, for example, at sites of axonal injury 40 in pwIIH. Finally, S100P and S100A12 were among the most IIH‐predicting proteins in pwIIH. S100P has been linked to tumor progression and metastasis, 41 but was also shown to be upregulated after traumatic brain injury, 42 whereas S100 A12 was linked to inflammatory diseases and neurite growth, 43 neuronal apoptosis, 33 and poor outcome after acute primary intracerebral hemorrhage 44 and ischemic stroke. 45

CSF Proteome

We observed downregulation of CA4 in pwIIH. CA4 is a membrane anchored enzyme located on the luminal surface of cerebral capillaries, associated with the blood–brain barrier, and expressed in layers III and VI in the cortex, thalamus, and hippocampus. 46

The proteome profile of CSF indicated altered carbon and amino acid metabolism, as well as alterations in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle—a central oxidation pathway that supplies energy to all cells. Increased proportions of pathological mitochondria have previously been found in brain biopsies from pwIIH 47 suggesting impaired cerebral energy homeostasis. The increased expression of CSF GOT1 and ALDOA in pwIIH is indicative of dysfunction of CNS energy metabolism. GOT1 is a cytosolic enzyme that interconverts aspartate to oxaloacetate and ALDOA is a glycolytic enzyme placing them both at the interface of glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, and the TCA cycle. Both proteins are crucial for bioenergetic homeostasis and metabolic regulation. The CSF proteome matches and could explain previous findings of impaired carbohydrate metabolism and amino acid metabolism in newly diagnosed treatment naïve IIH CSF. 38 Elevated CSF GOT1 levels have been linked to uncontrolled intracranial hypertension and cerebral ischemia directly after subarachnoid hemorrhage where it was associated with a poor neurological outcome. 48 In line, we found CSF GOT1 levels correlating with ICP in our pwIIH. 49 Notably in pwIIH, APOD correlated negatively with ICP. We observed positive correlation of CHGA with ICP. CHGA regulates metabolic processes with its derived peptide, catestatin, found to be significantly elevated in obesity related complications. 50

The study contains some important strengths as we used very strict inclusion criteria, newly diagnosed and untreated patients from 3 different headache clinics, and related their biomarkers to a unique healthy BMI matched control group. Additionally, the 3 external validation cohorts confirmed the findings from the exploratory cohort as such and their IIH‐specificity. Limitations include the relatively small sample size and the lack of generalizability to other ethnicities. Furthermore, the precise onset of IIH is unknown as symptoms often are subtle and unspecific so disease duration before diagnosis may have influenced the findings. In the future, prospective monitoring of these biomarkers in relation to weight loss and treatment may add important insights to IIH‐pathophysiology.

Conclusion

We identified a panel of candidate biomarkers in paired serum and CSF samples from newly diagnosed pwIIH. These candidate biomarkers differentiated patients with new‐onset IIH from age‐, sex‐, and BMI‐matched healthy controls. Their validation in 3 different validations cohorts confirmed the findings and their IIH‐specificity. Upregulation of serum CA2 could point toward CSF hypersecretion in IIH providing a possible explanation for the therapeutic efficacy of CA inhibitors. The upregulation of markers associated with neuronal impairment confirmed the non‐benign character of IIH.

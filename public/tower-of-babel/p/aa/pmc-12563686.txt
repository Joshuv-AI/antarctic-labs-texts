Isolation, Production and Optimization of Gelatinase from Organic Waste
Authors: Snehal A. Masurkar, Girish R. Pathade, Shivani B. Gujar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction and Aim:Investigation aimed to isolate and characterize gelatinase from an organic waste sample. Screening for gelatinase production was performed by using gelatin agar plates, and confirmation of gelatinase production was achieved through the Ninhydrin method.Materials and methods:A total of seven bacterial isolates derived from organic waste exhibited a clear zone surrounding the colony, indicative of gelatinase activity.Results and conclusion:The OWS4 isolate exhibited the highest gelatinase activity and was tentatively identified as Serratia marcescens based on morphological and biochemical assessments. Additionally, various physiological parameters were examined, including pH and temperature. The gelatinase exhibited peak activity at a pH of 6.6, with the optimal temperature for gelatinase activity determined to be 20°C. The Bacterial isolated OWS4 was a good source of gelatinase as it showed high gelatinase activity. It showed a largest clear zone on gelatin agar medium plate of 1.9 mm in diameter. The isolate OWS4 produced amount of gelatinase enzyme 0.77/per 50 mL medium unit after the incubation of 24 to 48 hrs.

INTRODUCTION

All living organisms produce enzymes; however, microbial enzymes provide distinct advantages compared to those derived from plants or animals. These benefits include a wide spectrum of catalytic activity, reduced production costs, and increased stability.[123456] As a result, gelatinases are regarded as important targets for the development of therapeutic drugs[78910] (Deryugina EI and Quigley JP).[2]

MATERIALS AND METHOD

Collection of organic waste sample

Organic waste was collected from dumping areas located at Banawadi, karve Naka, Aagashiv Nagar, Karad. The portion of collected to the organic waste samples were transferred to the sterile polythene bags and brought to laboratory.

1. Isolation of Bacteria:

The isolation of bacteria was carried out by serial dilution method. The plates were incubated at 37°C for 24–48. and screened for Gelatinase activity.[6]

2. Detection of gelatinlytic bacteria (Primary screening):

Gelatinase producing bacteria were screened out by using Gelatin agar medium and incubated at 37°C for 24 to 48 hours. The diameters of zone of growth of colonies and zone of hydrolysis (clearance) were measured and recorded.

S/R value = dimeter of zone of clearance (mm)/diameter of growth (mm)

Identification of isolates

a. Microscopic observation:

The bacterial isolates underwent gram staining and a motility assessment was performed to evaluate the morphology and motility characteristics of the cells (Sharma K et al., 2007).

b. Biochemical characterization:

The bacterial isolates underwent biochemical characterization through a series of biochemical tests.[3]

Production of Gelatinase Enzymes from the isolates [Figure 1]:

Standard graph of gelatinase assay

Production of Gelatinase was carried out in 100 mL fermentation medium. 5 mL of inoculum was added into fermentation medium by McFarland’s standards and flask was incubated at 37°C for 72 h. for crude enzyme extraction.[11]

Purification of gelatinase

The crude extract was subjected to partial purification through ammonium sulfate precipitation followed by dialysis.[49]

Enzyme assay

Gelatinase hydrolysis is quantified in micromoles of leucine equivalent per minute per milliliter of the culture filtrate.[1213]

PH

The study aimed to investigate the impact of culture media pH, which was adjusted using various pH buffers at levels of 6.6, 6.8, 7.0, 7.2, and 7.4. The samples were incubated at 37°C for a duration of 48 hours. Enzyme activities were determined using a standard enzyme assay (Balan S. S et al., 2012).

Temperature effect

Substrates combined with crude enzyme were subjected to various temperature conditions, specifically at 20°C, 25°C, 32°C, 37°C, and 45°C. Enzyme activity was determined by standard enzyme assay.[5]

CONCLUSION

Organic waste samples were collected from waste dumping area nearby karad. Seven isolates were obtained. These isolates were further screened for Gelatinase production. The primary screening showed that one isolates were highly efficient for Gelatinase production (OWS4). The OWS4 was tentatively identified as Serretia marcescens by using Bergey’s manual of determination bacteriology volume I and volume II. The yield obtained of Gelatinase was 0.77 unit/mL obtained. Enzyme activity reaches its maximum at pH 6.6 and enzyme temperature at 20°C, according to research on the impact of pH and temperature on enzyme activity.

Correlation Between Non-Invasive Biomarkers and Histological Healing in Children with Celiac Disease: Role of Cytokines, Intestinal Fatty Acid–Binding Protein, and Tissue Transglutaminase Antibodies
Authors: Rajni Kumari, Sadhna B Lal, Satya V Rana, Kaushal K Prasad, Pramod K Gupta, Arun K Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeData on treatment response in children with celiac disease (CeD), particularly with respect to changes in tissue transglutaminase IgA antibodies (tTGA-IgA), cytokines, intestinal fatty acid–binding protein (I-FABP), and quantitative histology, remain limited. To evaluate the utility of non-invasive parameters (NIPs), including I-FABP, tTGA-IgA, and cytokines, as potential markers of mucosal recovery in pediatric CeD.MethodsThis single-center, prospective, observational follow-up study included children with newly diagnosed CeD. Blood and duodenal biopsy samples were obtained at baseline and after 1 year of follow-up.ResultsSeventy-nine children who underwent follow-up biopsy at 1 year were analyzed. Significant improvements were observed in anthropometric measures, histological parameters, tTGA-IgA levels, cytokine profiles (tumor necrosis factor-alpha [TNF-α], interferon-gamma [IFN-γ], interleukin [IL]-15, IL-6, and IL-10), and I-FABP levels. tTGA-IgA showed strong correlations with histological indices, including intraepithelial lymphocyte (IEL) count (r=0.554, p<0.001), villous height (r=−0.557, p<0.001), crypt depth (r=0.553, p<0.001), and villous-to-crypt ratio (VCR; r=−0.561, p<0.001). TNF-α demonstrated weak correlation with crypt depth (r=0.24, p=0.04) and VCR (r=−0.22, p=0.05). IL-15 levels (r=0.34, p=0.003) and Δcycle threshold (Ct) scores correlated with IEL counts (r=–0.22, p=0.048). IFN-γ ΔCt scores also correlated with IEL counts (r=–0.34, p=0.001).ConclusionAmong the NIPs evaluated, tTGA-IgA showed the strongest correlation with mucosal recovery at 1 year. TNF-α, IL-15, and IFN-γ demonstrated weaker but significant correlations and may have the potential as adjunctive NIP of mucosal healing in pediatric CeD.

INTRODUCTION

Celiac disease (CeD) is an autoimmune enteropathy of the small intestine triggered by ingestion of gluten and related proteins present in wheat, barley, and rye in genetically susceptible individuals. It is one of the most common lifelong disorders, with an estimated global prevalence of approximately 1% [1]. A lifelong strict gluten-free diet (GFD) remains the cornerstone of effective management; however, adherence poses a major clinical challenge [23].

Mucosal recovery is considered the key indicator of successful treatment. The goals of therapy extend beyond symptom resolution to include assessment of dietary adherence, monitoring of disease activity, and surveillance for long-term complications such as autoimmune diseases and malignancies, which may develop despite minimal or absent symptoms if gluten exposure persists [45].

Data on mucosal recovery in CeD are limited and inconsistent, and follow-up practices vary widely [678]. Current international guidelines recommend regular clinical and serological monitoring, with repeat duodenal biopsy reserved for selected patients, particularly those with persistent or recurrent symptoms [910]. Although achieving mucosal healing is a desirable therapeutic goal, histological recovery often lags behind clinical and serological improvement and differs between children and adults [11]. While tissue transglutaminase IgA antibodies (tTGA-IgA) are widely used for screening and reflect disease activity [12], their reliability in predicting mucosal healing has been questioned [1314]. Moreover, repeat biopsy during follow-up is invasive and impractical for routine use, highlighting the need for reliable non-invasive predictors of mucosal recovery.

Several biomarkers have been evaluated for monitoring CeD, including serological markers, cytokines, markers of enterocyte damage (intestinal fatty acid–binding protein [I-FABP], plasma citrulline, regenerating gene 1α), gluten immunogenic peptides, and markers of intestinal permeability [151617]. Emerging evidence suggests that biomarkers such as I-FABP and cytokines may predict villous recovery. However, further validation is required before these markers can be incorporated into routine clinical practice.

Prospective follow-up studies assessing treatment response through changes in cytokine and I-FABP levels in children from India—particularly from northwestern regions where CeD prevalence is high—are limited. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the role of non-invasive biomarkers as predictors of mucosal recovery in children with CeD.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study patients

This observational follow-up study was based on prospectively collected data of 128 children newly diagnosed with CeD who were evaluated at the Division of Pediatric Gastroenterology and Hepatology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, between February 2017 and January 2020. Children aged 5–12 years with subtotal villous atrophy (STVA) on baseline small bowel histology were enrolled. Written informed consent and assent, as appropriate, were obtained from parents, primary caregivers, and participants. At enrollment, detailed clinical history—including demographic characteristics, presenting symptoms, and routine laboratory investigations—was recorded. Adherence to GFD was evaluated at each follow-up visit using tTGA-IgA antibody serology and structured interviews with patients and their parents. This study protocol was approved by the Institute Ethics Committee (INT/IEC/2017/171).

Collection of patient samples and follow-up

Duodenal biopsy samples were obtained during upper gastrointestinal endoscopy at baseline and after 1 year of gluten withdrawal for quantitative histological analysis. Three to five biopsy specimens were collected in 100 µL of RNA stabilization solution (Sigma; Lot no. MKBT6996V) and stored at −80°C until RNA extraction. Blood samples (3–4 mL) were collected at baseline and at 3, 6, and 12 months of follow-up for measurement of tTGA-IgA, cytokines, and I-FABP. Plasma and serum samples were stored at −80°C until evaluation to ensure sample stability. To minimize confounding effects, patients were confirmed to be free of intercurrent illnesses for at least 3 weeks prior to and 1 week following sample collection.

Anti-tTGA-IgA antibody, cytokine, and I-FABP quantification

Serum anti-tTGA-IgA antibodies and plasma levels of cytokines and I-FABP were measured at baseline and each follow-up visit using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), strictly according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Anti-tTGA-IgA levels were measured using a commercial ELISA kit (XEMA, KQ V108; Russia, cutoff <20 U/mL). Cytokines (tumor necrosis factor-alpha [TNF-α], interferon-gamma [IFN-γ], interleukin [IL]-15, IL-6, and IL-10) were quantified using kits from Diaclone (France), and I-FABP levels were measured using Qayee-Bio ELISA kits. The analytical sensitivities of the assays were 8 pg/mL for TNF-α, 5 pg/mL for IFN-γ, 18.3 pg/mL for IL-15, 2 pg/mL for IL-6, 4.9 pg/mL for IL-10, and 9.3 ng/mL for I-FABP.

Biopsy processing, histology, and quantitative histology (QH)

At least four biopsy specimens were obtained from the second part of the duodenum in each participant. Specimens were oriented on filter paper, fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin wax, sectioned at 3–4 µm thickness, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Only well-oriented tissue sections were included in the analysis. Histological evaluation was performed by a single experienced pathologist, who was blinded to the clinical data. The assessed parameters included intraepithelial lymphocyte (IEL) count, villous height (Vh), crypt depth (Cd), and Vh-to-Cd ratio (VCR). IELs at the villous tips were counted per 20 epithelial cells. Five villous units were analyzed, and IEL density was expressed as the number of cells per 100 epithelial cells (normal <25 per 100 enterocytes) [18]. QH was performed using Olympus CellSens Standard morphometry software (version 1.17; build 16030) on an average of five well-oriented biopsy fragments. Vh and Cd were measured from adjacent villous–crypt units, and mean values calculated from at least five such units. The average VCR was calculated accordingly [19].

RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) total RNA extraction

Total RNA was extracted from duodenal biopsy samples using TRIzol reagent (Sigma-Aldrich) according to the standard protocol described by Chomczynski (1993) [20] (Supplementary Fig. 1). The RNA pellet was resuspended in 20 µL of RNase-free water (Exiqon; lot no. 68104). RNA concentration and purity were assessed by measuring absorbance at 260/280 nm using a NanoDrop2000/2000c spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific), with RNase-free water as the blank or control.

cDNA synthesis (reverse transcription)

Reverse transcription was performed using 3 µg of total RNA as template with the PrimeScriptTM First-strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara; Cat No. 6110A) in a total reaction volume of 20 µL. The reaction was performed using an Eppendorf ProS thermal cycler (Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, as detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

Quantitative RT-PCR

Relative gene expression of cytokines was analyzed using a 96-well RT-PCR system (Applied Biosystems StepOneTMPlus, version 2.3) with SYBR® Premix Ex TaqTM (Tli RNaseH Plus; Takara). Reactions were performed in duplicate in a total of 10 µL, comprising 5 µL of 1x SYBR Green master mix, 0.25 µM of each forward and reverse primer, and 1 µL of cDNA template. Primer sequences are summarized in Supplementary Table 2. Thermal cycling conditions included 95°C for 10 minutes (pre-heating), followed by denaturation at 95°C for 15 seconds, annealing for 40 seconds, and extension at 72°C for 40 seconds. Product specificity and amplification of the internal control gene (β-Actin) were confirmed by melting curve analysis. Delta cycle threshold (ΔCt) values were calculated and used for comparative analyses.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0 (IBM Co.), and GraphPad Prism version 5 (GraphPad Software Inc.) was used for graphical presentation. Continuous variables were presented as mean±standard deviation or median with interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Paired comparisons were performed using the paired t-test or the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, depending on data distribution. Comparisons of variables across multiple time points (baseline, 3, 6, and 12 months) were conducted using repeated-measures analysis of variance or the Friedman test. Associations between categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test. The Mann–Whitney U-test was used to compare non-normally distributed continuous variables between groups. Correlations were assessed using Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficients, as appropriate. Associations between histological parameters and other study variables were evaluated. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Anti-tTGA-IgA antibodies

Following initiation of the GFD, anti-tTGA-IgA seronegativity (cutoff <20 U/mL) was achieved in 38 patients (48.1%) at 3 months, 46 patients (58.2%) at 6 months, and 58 patients (73.4%) at 1 year.

Plasma cytokines and I-FABP levels

Plasma concentrations of cytokines, including TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-6, and IL-10, before and after initiation of a GFD are shown in Fig. 2A, with detailed values summarized in Supplementary Table 4. Overall, significant reductions in plasma cytokine levels were observed following gluten withdrawal. Median TNF-α levels decreased significantly from 41.50 (20.75–120.25) pg/mL at diagnosis to 22.0 (9.0–42.5) pg/mL at 1 year (p<0.001). IFN-γ levels also declined significantly, from 16.50 (10.0–27.12) pg/mL at baseline to 8.0 (5.0–15.0) pg/mL at 1 year. Similarly, medians levels of IL-15, IL-6, and IL-10 decreased from 74.0 (45.75–164.0) pg/mL, 3.50 (2.5–7.5) pg/mL, and 8.50 (5.5–15.5) pg/mL at baseline to 45.0 (20.0–69.75) pg/mL, 2.0 (2.0–4.25) pg/mL, and 5.0 (4.9–7.0) pg/mL, respectively, after 1 year (p<0.001 for all). As illustrated in Fig. 2A, levels of TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-15, and IL-10 declined rapidly within the first 3 and 6 months of GFD (p<0.05), whereas no significant additional reductions were observed between 6 months and 1 year (p>0.05).

Cytokine gene expression profile before and after GFD

Cytokine gene expression analysis was performed in the 79 patients who underwent repeat duodenal biopsy after 1 year. Total RNA was extracted from biopsy samples, and relative gene expression of TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-6, and IL-10 was quantified using 96-well RT-PCR with specific primers (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). Mean ΔCt values were calculated for each cytokine. A significant increase in mean ΔCt values of cytokines was observed after 1 year compared with baseline (Fig. 2B), indicating reduced cytokine gene expression following GFD. This reduction paralleled the decline in peripheral blood cytokine levels (TNF-α, p=0.007; IFN-γ, p=0.001; IL-15, p=0.023; IL-6, p=0.049; and IL-10, p=0.001) (Table 2).

However, comparison of percentage change at 1 year (Supplementary Table 7) demonstrated significantly greater improvement in Vh (p<0.01), Cd (p<0.01), and VCR (p<0.001) in children with VCR >2.0 (n=17) compared with those with VCR <2.0 (n=62). Changes in IEL counts were comparable between the two groups (p=0.21).

DISCUSSION

In this prospective longitudinal study, 128 children with newly diagnosed CeD were enrolled and followed for 1 year after initiation of GFD. Clinical parameters, serology (tTGA-IgA), cytokines (TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-6, IL-10), I-FABP, and QH were assessed at baseline and during follow-up to evaluate the association between NIPs and histological recovery.

Improved nutrient absorption following initiation of a GFD results in normalization of anthropometric parameters, typically within 3 months to 1 year [21222324]. The extent of catch-up growth depends on factors such as age at diagnosis, severity of growth impairment, dietary adherence, and population characteristics [2526272829]. Consistent with our previous findings, we observed a significant and steady improvement in weight, height, and BMI z-scores over 1 year (p<0.001), with the greatest gains occurring within the first 3 months of treatment [21].

Strict adherence to a GFD is essential for clinical, serological, and mucosal recovery but remains a major challenge in disease management. Reported adherence rates range from 42% to 91%, with younger children demonstrating better compliance due to parental supervision [3031]. Current guidelines recommend a combination of dietary assessment and serological testing for monitoring dietary compliance in patients with CeD [323334]. In our cohort, tTGA-IgA became negative in approximately 72% of patients at 1 year, likely reflecting frequent dietary counseling during the quarterly follow-up visits. However, dietary transgressions may have been underestimated, as fecal gluten immunogenic peptides were not assessed [35].

Both cell-mediated (Th1) and humoral (Th2) immune responses play central roles in CeD pathogenesis by modulating pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Activation of gliadin-specific T cells results in the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-15, IL-6, and IL-10, which promote IEL activation, epithelial damage, and villous atrophy [3637]. These cytokines upregulate T-cell functions and induce other cells to produce cytokines. TNF-α and IL-1β have been implicated in matrix metalloproteinase activation and villous destruction [3839], while IL-15, through IFN-γ–mediated pathways, enhances IEL cytotoxicity via NKG2D receptor upregulation [4041]. IL-6, a pleotropic cytokine, contributes to immune regulation and inflammation through regulation of T-cell functions [42]. Furthermore, IL-10 is thought to have a complex immunomodulatory role in CeD, which is mediated by IEL [43].

In the present study, baseline plasma levels of TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-6, and IL-10 were significantly elevated in children with newly diagnosed CeD, confirming active immune-mediated inflammation associated with pediatric CeD. These levels declined markedly during follow-up, accompanied by reduced mucosal cytokine mRNA expression at 1 year, indicating true resolution of mucosal inflammation following a GFD rather than nonspecific peripheral changes (Supplementary Table 4).

Previous studies have reported comparable TNF-α levels in newly diagnosed patients with CeD, patients with CeD on a GFD for at least 2 years, and healthy controls [1744]. However, such cross-sectional comparisons across heterogeneous patient groups are inherently limited. Genetic polymorphisms in cytokine genes may significantly influence cytokine expression, rendering the cross-sectional analyses less informative [45]. In contrast, our longitudinal assessment in the same cohort demonstrated a steady and significant decline in TNF-α concentrations at each follow-up to 1 year. Additionally, we evaluated TNF-α gene expression at baseline and follow-up. Baseline duodenal biopsy specimens collected from patients with CeD showed high TNF-α mRNA expression, consistent with previous reports [46]. Notably, tissue TNF-α mRNA expression decreased significantly at 1 year of GFD. Confirmation by intestinal RT-PCR suggests that circulating cytokine levels reflect mucosal inflammation rather than peripheral nonspecific inflammation.

Similarly, IFN-γ levels declined significantly and steadily during the first 6 months of GFD, with corresponding reductions in mucosal mRNA expression at 1 year (p<0.05), in agreement with previous studies [46474849]. Di Sabatino et al. [50] reported higher IFN-γ levels in supernatants of ex-vivo cultured baseline mucosal biopsies from patients with CeD compared with healthy controls and treated patients. Abed et al. [51] also demonstrated significantly elevated IFN-γ levels in untreated patients with CeD, with a marked decline after 6 months of GFD. Conversely, one study reported a nonsignificant decrease in IFN-γ levels after 1.5 years of GFD [52], potentially reflecting suboptimal dietary adherence.

Elevated IL-15 levels in untreated patients with CeD have been consistently reported [5051], although follow-up data are scarce. In our study, IL-15 levels declined steadily and significantly at each follow-up (p<0.05), accompanied by a parallel reduction in IL-15 mRNA expression (p<0.05).

Increased IL-6 expression in the intestinal mucosa of patients with active CeD has been demonstrated using immunohistochemistry and RT-PCR [3946]. In our longitudinal cohort, both serum IL-6 levels and duodenal mRNA expression decreased significantly after 1 year (p<0.05). In contrast, earlier cross-sectional studies reported comparable IL-6 levels among healthy controls, untreated patients with CeD, and patients on GFD for at least 2 years [17]. A previous study similarly reported a significant reduction in IL-6 after 3 years of GFD [53].

Research has shown that IL-10 is produced in significantly higher amounts by IELs from patients with active CeD compared with controls or patients on GFD [4354]. However, other studies have reported increased IL-10 production in silent CeD, controls, and patients on GFD [5556]. Some investigators have demonstrated a significant decline in IL-10 levels following GFD [5152]. In our study, both IL-10 levels and mRNA expression showed a significant decrease after GFD (p<0.001). Notably, previous studies did not assess longitudinal changes in IL-10 gene expression [4649]. Further research is required to elucidate the dual pro-inflammatory versus regulatory roles of IL-10 in CeD.

Collectively, our findings indicate that patients with active CeD exhibit elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-15, and IL-6). These cytokines decline steadily throughout follow-up, suggesting resolution of immune-mediated inflammation with mucosal healing following GFD. Concordant changes in cytokine mRNA expression further confirm that circulating cytokine levels primarily reflect intestinal inflammation, although not all mRNA transcripts are translated into proteins.

Our results underscore the value of measuring cytokines longitudinally in patients with CeD before and after treatment. Unlike most previous studies, which relied on inter-group comparisons, we evaluated cytokine responses in the same cohort over 1 year, thereby minimizing inter-individual variability related to genetic or immunologic differences. This approach provides a more accurate assessment of disease- or treatment-related cytokine dynamics.

I-FABP, released into the peripheral circulation following enterocyte injury, is significantly elevated in patients with untreated CeD [5758]. A reduction in I-FABP levels after 6 months of GFD has led to its proposal as an NIP of mucosal recovery [59]. In our study, I-FABP levels declined significantly after 1 year (p=0.03). However, no correlation with histological parameters was observed at 1 year. These results likely reflect ongoing, incomplete mucosal recovery at 1 year.

A meta-analysis reported complete mucosal recovery in only 36% of children after 1 year of GFD, potentially due to ongoing undetected gluten exposure [11]. Higher baseline antibody levels, severe mucosal damage, and classical malabsorption at diagnosis are independent predictors of incomplete recovery [60]. Our cohort included children with STVA at diagnosis, which may explain the high rate of persistent histological abnormalities at 1 year.

QH, which provides an objective measure of histopathological severity and response to GFD, has been increasingly used in recent years [1961]. Although histological improvement occurs in upto 94% of children after 1 year, complete normalization is uncommon in those with STVA at baseline, even after 3 years of GFD [32]. Similarly, in our study, complete histological normalization was not achieved after 1 year of GFD, as the mean VCR remained <2.0. This finding may reflect severe baseline mucosal damage and inadvertent gluten exposure despite apparent dietary compliance.

In our study, 73% of children were seronegative for tTGA-IgA at 1 year after GFD. Significant improvements in IEL count, Vh, Cd, and VCR were observed in both TGA-positive and TGA-negative groups; however, improvements were significantly greater in seronegative children.

We explored correlations between histological parameters, TGA, cytokines, and clinical parameters. Moderate correlations were observed between histological parameters and tTGA-IgA. TGA correlated positively with IEL count and Cd and negatively with Vh and VCR. TGA also showed negative correlations with BMI and weight z-scores (r=0.25) and a positive correlation with IL-15 (r=0.25). Anthropometric parameters demonstrated only weak correlations with VCR (weight [r=0.22] and height [r=0.25]).

TNF-α correlated negatively with BMI (r=−0.28) and height z-score (r=−0.23). Other cytokines did not show any correlations with anthropometric indices. Cytokines, IL-15, and IFN-γ upregulate NKG2D receptor on IELs and activate their cytotoxicity, culminating in villous blunting, as a result of T cell-mediated inflammation. Weak associations were observed between IELs, IL-15, and IFN-γ, consistent with their role in upregulating NKG2D expression on IELs and promoting cytotoxicity. TNF-α correlated positively with Cd (r=0.243) and negatively with VCR (r=−0.229). IL-15 levels correlated positively with IELs (r=0.34), whereas IL-15 ΔCt (r=−0.22) and IFN-γ ΔCt (r=−0.34) scores correlated negatively with IELs.

Despite elevated baseline cytokine levels and significant reductions after 1 year, correlations with histological recovery remained modest, likely attributable to incomplete mucosal healing, as reflected by persistently low mean VCR (<2.0). Baseline IL-6 and IL-10 elevations were less pronounced than those of TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-15, which may explain their weaker associations with histological parameters [62]

Repeat biopsy could not be performed in all enrolled patients, resulting in follow-up histology for 79 of the initial 128 children. Complete villous recovery was not observed at 1 year, indicating the need for longer histological follow-up. The strengths of this study lie in its prospective longitudinal design, a considerable number of follow-up biopsies, objective assessment using QH, paired cytokine analyses in blood and tissue at baseline and follow-up, and minimizing confounding due to inter-individual variability.

The results of the present study reaffirm the importance of a GFD in improving clinical symptoms and growth parameters in children with CeD, with measurable benefits observed as early as 3 months. Elevated concentrations of TGA, cytokines, and I-FABP at diagnosis support their role in disease pathogenesis and inflammatory activity in CeD. The significant reduction in these markers following GFD further underscores their potential utility in monitoring disease activity in individual patients with CeD. Objective QH suggests that histological recovery is slow and remains incomplete at 1 year.

Among NIPs evaluated, tTGA-IgA showed the strongest correlation with mucosal recovery at 1 year, whereas cytokines (TNF-α, IL-15, and IFN-γ) demonstrated only weak correlation. Nonetheless, the significant improvement in these NIPs following GFD suggests their potential role in tracking histologic recovery at the individual patient level. A larger sample size and longer follow-up duration prior to repeat biopsy may have yielded stronger correlations. I-FABP appears to be of limited value for monitoring mucosal recovery at 1 year, as most patients do not achieve villous recovery early or even over longer durations [35].

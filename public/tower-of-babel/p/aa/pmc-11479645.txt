Study on the characteristics particles of coarse grain material in high bench dump
Authors: Bo Cui, Yuanlong Bi, Tianfeng Gu, Kai Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Particle shape is an important factor affecting the strength and deformation of coarse materials in dumps. However, conducting research on particle shape in current laboratory experiments, and there exists no classification standard for the particle shape of the dump. Considering the project on high-bench dump in Jiangxi Province, based on the random particle construction method, this study determined the characteristic particles of coarse grain material in the dumping site by combining field investigation results, large-scale direct shear tests, and numerical analysis. Further, the influence mechanism of coarse grain content and characteristic particles on the mechanical characteristics of the dump were analyzed. The results showed that the increase in coarse grain content enhanced the irregularity of particle shape, increased the internal friction angle between particles, and reduced the cohesion of the dump. Moreover, with increase in the normal stress, the shear strength changed from insignificant to gradually enhanced, although the increasing trend slowed down. The constructed random particle model can well solve the effects of occlusion, nesting, and friction between particles that is neglected by the standard spherical particle model, and the fitting degree is better. The particle characteristics of coarse-grain materials in the dump were primarily block, strip, and flake. The three types of shape characteristics under low normal stress did not affect the mechanical properties of the dump. Further, with the increase in normal stress, the stress-strain curve reflected the shape characteristics and content of the dominant characteristics in the dump. Specifically, the block sample exhibited the dominant characteristics and the most content under the same normal stress, followed by the strip sample, with the flake sample exerting the least influence. The research results provide a theoretical basis for long-term safe operation, disaster monitoring, and early warning of high bench dump.

Introduction

Coarse-grained soil is extensively distributed in nature and serves as the primary material for mine dumps and various natural slopes. It exhibits excellent compaction performance and high shear strength1,2. As the content of coarse grains increases, the mechanical properties and particle morphology of coarse-grained soil diverge significantly from those of continuous materials. The strength of this soil primarily relies on the frictional forces and interlocking mechanisms between coarse particles, with particle shapes displaying notable non-uniformity and randomness.

The distribution of coarse particle content varies across different locations due to factors such as primary joint cutting, production blasting, and discharge methods. Therefore, their impact on the mechanical properties and strength deformation of discharged materials cannot be overlooked. Relevant scholars have analyzed and summarized the mechanical characteristics inherent to coarse-grained soils3. In terms of shear behavior, it has been observed that shear strength markedly increases with rising coarse grain content when it ranges from 30 to 70%. Beyond a threshold of 70%, shear strength is predominantly influenced by frictional interactions among coarse particles. Furthermore, varying levels of coarse grain content also affect the shear characteristics exhibited by these soils. Both volume strain and stress-strain relationships demonstrate distinct behaviors under differing confining pressures. Confining pressure emerges as a critical factor influencing the strength attributes of coarse-grained soils: at low confining pressures, their shear strengths adhere to Coulomb’s equation but exhibit nonlinear behavior at elevated confining pressures. However, conducting large-scale direct shear tests to investigate the shear behavior in these soils poses significant challenges. Therefore, integrating discrete element analysis methods becomes essential for achieving more accurate assessments.

In studies concerning coarse-grained soil, it has been identified that irregularities in particle shape are a principal factor affecting its mechanical properties. Currently, certain results have been achieved in the numerical analysis of particle shape. Cundall4 first introduced the discrete element method into the analysis of rock slope, and proposed the two-dimensional Ball (disk program) and three-dimensional program Trobal (sphere program) with Strack5. This resulted in the formation of the calculation prototype of the discrete element model. Subsequently, many scholars conducted studies on the mechanical properties of rock-soil mass based on the discrete element method. Most of the early irregular particles used ellipse, polygon, or ellipsoid models6–10. Although the simulation test of this type of model can analyze the influence of particle shape on the mechanical properties of materials, the shape parameters such as roundness and roughness are considerably different from the actual particles. The test results obtained can only be used as a reference for regular characteristics, and cannot be used as the final actual results. To render the simulation results closer to the reality, certain scholars have attempted to establish a method wherein the basic circles or spheres are bonded into rigid bodies through a certain arrangement, and the roughness and outer contour of the particles are characterized by shape coefficients and friction factors between particles to simulate actual particles of different shapes. This approach is closer to the real particle morphology but still has a certain error value.

In recent years, digital image processing technology has been widely used in the field of rock and civil engineering through CT scanning technology, laser scanning method, etc11–18. Compared with manual methods, data image processing technology can obtain more particle information, and construct the model according to the corresponding relationship between pixels and units to obtain the particle shape. However, it can only process certain irregular particles, so the prediction results do not necessarily have practical application. In addition, the influence of particle shape on the mechanical properties of granular materials in high bench dump remains unclear. Moreover, the classification standards for coarse particle shapes in the dump is not completely conclusive. Consequently, this leads to imperfect disaster prevention and control in existing high bench dump, which seriously restricts the overall defense level of major mine disasters. Therefore, through the construction of arbitrarily shaped irregular complex particles, changing of the parameter interval of random particles to obtain characteristic particles, and conduction of characteristic particle simulation experiments based on discrete element numerical analysis, the influence of coarse particle shape on the mechanical characteristics of the dump can be analyzed, This can facilitate a new idea for studying the particle shape of the dump.

Considering the existing research, this study proposed a three-dimensional random particle construction method based on the high bench dump of a mine in Jiangxi Province. The characteristics of the random particles of coarse-grained materials in the dump were evaluated, and the influence of three types of characteristic particles of “block, strip, and flake” on the mechanical characteristics of the high bench dump was simulated. The study provided a new perspective for understanding the macroscopic and microscopic mechanical behavior of coarse-grained soil, with significant practical implications for engineering applications such as earth-rock dam design, slope stability analysis, and granular material design. They helped prevent and mitigate geological disasters caused by coarse-grained soil shear and enhanced the safety and economy of engineering design.

Study area

Study area overview

The study area is located in Shangrao City, Jiangxi Province, and is characterized by a subtropical humid climate. The average annual temperature in the mining area is 17.2 °C. Precipitation is predominantly during the plum rain season, with the majority of the annual rainfall occurring from March to June, which accounts for 50–60% of the total annual rainfall. The average annual rainfall ranges from 1900 mm to 2000 mm, with the highest recorded annual rainfall being 2870.4 mm, and the maximum daily rainfall reaching 311.7 mm. Currently, four steps have been formed in a high bench dump in Jiangxi: 200, 223, 239, and 250 m steps (Fig. 1a). According to the on-site investigation, the grading above 200 m steps is yet to be started because the height of each step in the dump is less than 40 m. The height of the 200 m step is 110 m, and the dump is discharged by the “one slope to the end” method, see (Fig. 1b,c).

To study whether the dumping method results in the particle size classification phenomenon of the accumulated particles, the particle size distribution of the 200 m step dump must be studied via the direct measurement and sieving methods. The combination of these two methods can render the obtained particle size distribution closer to the reality, and play a complementary and verification role. Particle size surveys of high bench dumps greater than or equal to 100 mm can be conducted via slope sampling (see Fig. 1d), from which the variation of the particle size of large stones with the accumulation height of the dump can be determined. Small particles of the dump were primarily concentrated in the middle and upper parts of the steps, whereas the large stones were primarily accumulated in the middle and lower parts of the steps. In case of the particles of the dump being greater than 0.1 mm and less than 100 mm, the particle size was determined by sieving method. Seven measurement points were considered from the 200 m step dump to perform a screening test (Table 1). The results indicated that the high bench dump had a relatively higher content of fine particles in the middle and upper parts. Below the middle part, the uniform distribution showed no significant variation, which suggests that the vertical grading of the 200 m high bench dump was not very pronounced (Fig. 1e).

Fundamentals and theory

Discrete element particles flow fundamentals

The discrete element method was developed by Cundall and Strack, which can effectively analyze the internal meso-structural characteristics of geotechnical materials. In addition, it can facilitate an understanding of the basic properties of coarse-grained from a macroscopic perspective. The fundamental idea of the discrete element method4 is to discretize the research object into a collection of rigid elements such that each rigid element satisfies its own equation of motion. Further, the overall motion form of the discontinuum element can be solved using the time-step scheme method. The contact form of the bonded-particle model can be divided into contact among particles themselves, and the contact between particles and walls, as shown in Fig. 4, where, RA and RB are the radii of particles A and B, respectively, d is the spherical distance between the contact particles or the vertical distance from the particles to the contact point of the wall, xiA and xiB are the position vectors of the centers of particles A and B, respectively, and the normal displacement Un is generated by the contact between particles-particles or particles-walls. Further, ni is a unit normal vector, when the particles are in contact with the particles and ni is connected in the direction of the center of the two particles (Fig. 4a). When the particles are in contact with the walls, ni follows the direction of the straight line from the center of the particles to the shortest distance from the walls, as shown in (Fig. 4b).

Methods for building three-dimensional irregular particle models

Based on ellipsoid primitives, by employing the probability method of generating random numbers with uniform distribution, irregular particles having arbitrary shapes can be constructed via changes to the angle and spindle length of random points (Fig. 5). The specific generation method is as follows:

Determination of random point angles of complex particles

The position of any point on the surface of the ellipsoid can be determined by the five elements (R1, R2, R3, θ, φ). To reflect the randomness of the particles it was assumed that the complex particles constructed with ellipsoid primitives had N vertices and the number of vertices in the upper and lower halves were N1 and N2, respectively. In the Cartesian coordinate system, the angle of a random point in the upper half is:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left. \begin{gathered} \theta _{i} = \frac{{2\pi }}{{N_{1} }} + \delta \frac{{2\pi }}{{N_{1} }} \cdot (2\eta _{1} - 1) \hfill \\ \phi _{i} = \eta _{2} \cdot \frac{\pi }{2} \hfill \\ \end{gathered} \right\}$$\end{document}

where η1 and η2 are two values that are independent and evenly distributed between [0, 1] and δ is a variable (typical value of 0.3). The angle of the accumulation point in the lower part can also be determined in this manner.

Description of particle size

Particle size is an important indicator for measuring the particle size distribution of granular materials in the dump. Based on the ellipsoid primitive, the particle size can be considered approximately equal to the first spindle length R1 of the ellipsoid. Further, through an investigation of the on-site survey, the particle size was found to conform to the lognormal distribution, that is, the length of the first spindle of the ellipsoid primitive followed the logarithmic distribution.

Spatial distribution of particles

The spatial distribution of particles has a certain influence on the shear strength of soil mass. Considering the randomness of the spatial distribution of particles, when constructing the random structure of the accumulation, it was assumed that the center of the ellipsoid obeyed a uniform random distribution within a given sample area. Consequently, its spatial azimuth formula is expressed as:2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left. \begin{gathered} x_{0} = x_{{\min }} + \eta _{x} (x_{{\max }} - x_{{\min }} ) \hfill \\ y_{0} = y_{{\min }} + \eta _{y} (y_{{\max }} - y_{{\min }} ) \hfill \\ z_{0} = z_{{\min }} + \eta _{z} (z_{{\max }} - z_{{\min }} ) \hfill \\ \end{gathered} \right\}$$\end{document}

where xmin and xmax, ymin and ymax, and zmin and zmax are the minimum and maximum values in each side of the test area, respectively, and ηx, ηy, and ηz are three independent and evenly distributed random numbers.

Characteristic particles consrtuction

To study the effects of flakes, strips, and blocks on the mechanical properties of bulk materials in the dump, the single shape characteristics of the particles were enlarged to render the single characteristics more obvious to facilitate more accurate analysis of the relevant characteristics. The irregular particle construction method, combined with key parameters such as sphericity, elongation, and flatness of the characteristic particles, accurately simulated the geometric characteristics of the particles. Assuming a uniform random distribution, the value interval for these parameters was set within [0.5, 1]. For specific feature studies, this interval was adjusted to [0.3, 0.5], which allowed for a mathematical modeling and discretization of the particle surfaces. This approach ensured the uniqueness and complexity of each particle in three-dimensional space. With the help of 3D model processing software Rhinoceros, the calculated vertex coordinates were transformed into detailed 3D models. These models were then further processed and optimized, including smoothing, size adjustment, and material attribute assignment, among other processes. These steps were crucial to ensure the engineering applicability of the model and the accuracy of the analysis, as shown in (Fig. 6).

Test method

Test grading scheme

In this study, through the combination of the classification and statistical method of coarse material content with the results of sample particle size screening and the needs of the research situation, respectively, three groups with different coarse particle contents P> 5 mm (15, 40, and 70%) were selected as a representative gradation for large-scale direct shear tests at the top, middle, and bottom of the high bench dump. The test adopted an electro-hydraulic servo of 1000 kN large-scale direct shear and its geometric size was 500 × 500 × 410 mm. Additionally, with a requirement for the maximum particle size to be under 80 mm, a size of 60 mm was chosen as the threshold, and any components surpassing this limit were reduced accordingly. From the on-site measurement results, it is evident that the ultra-particle size content of the dump was in the range of 20–55%. Therefore, the coarse particle size was reduced by the equivalent substitution method, and the gradation curve of the dump is shown in (Fig. 7).

In order to verify the accuracy of the numerical results, the representative particle size contents of 15, 40 and 70% were selected to carry out the large-scale direct shear test, as shown in (Fig. 8), in which the vertical loads are set at 100, 200, 300 and 400 kPa, and the shear rate is 0.03 mm/s. Numerical analysis of large-scale direct shear tests

In this study, PFC discrete element (DEM) simulation analysis software was used to conduct numerical analysis of direct shear tests. The three-dimensional shear simulation test device of the high bench dump was composed of 10 walls, as shown in (Fig. 9a). In addition, the size of the wall (500 mm × 500 mm × 410 mm) and particle size range (0.075–60 mm) were consistent with the actual direct shear test. In the servo process of the test, there were 4 quadrilateral walls on both sides. The lower left, lower right, and left baffles moved simultaneously during the shear simulation test to prevent the particles from spilling out of the pores during the shear process.

Currently, the methods for generating random particles include explosive repulsion, stepwise expansion, and gradation generation methods. This study used the gradation generation method to divide the particle system into multiple groups of particle systems whose random generation and actual distribution were relatively consistent, as shown in (Fig. 9b).

Shear simulation test: (a) Fabrication of shear box; (b) Spherical particle model samples.

In the process of building simulated samples, it was considered that the simulated samples were equivalent to the actual samples. However, the replacement of all spherical particles with irregular particles is beyond the scope of computer operation. Therefore, the fine particles with a particle size of 0.075–10 mm were replaced by randomly distributed spherical units, the medium particles of 10–20 mm are replaced by spherical particles with the same gradation, and the large particles of 20–60 mm are replaced by the in-situ replacement method9,20–25. Thus, the command flow is used to replace the original spherical particles with the constructed irregular particles to ensure that the particle size distribution and position were consistent with that of the original model. The specific modeling steps are as follows:

(1) The ball particles with a particle size larger than 20 mm in the sample were traversed by FISH language programming, a random particle model was introduced to establish clump particles, and the ball particles were deleted to achieve the replacement of particles with the same location and particle size. The pseudo-code is as follows:

Loop for each ID = ball_point

gravel(x, y,z, r)=(x(ID), y(ID), z(ID), r(ID))

If r > 0.02 then

Create clump gravel(x, y,z, r)

Delete ball(ID)

End if

End loop

(2) The clump particles were randomly rotated to achieve a specific distribution angle, and a random seed was set to ensure consistent random rotation angles for the same particle in different models.

(3) The velocity of the clump was fixed at zero to ensure that the clump remained stationary. The cycle calm was used to adjust the positions of the soil particles to avoid large local stresses and large local voids. The velocity of the clump was released, and the overall stress adjustment of the sample was carried out to ensure uniform internal stress within the sample. Servo control was applied to the specified confining pressure and bonding was imposed.

(4) During loading, information such as the stress-strain curve, coordination number, and particle displacement during the deformation and failure process of the sample was recorded.

The simulation using the constructed irregular particles ensured that the particle composition of the simulated samples were consistent with that of the practice test, thereby guaranteeing the reliability of the numerical simulation. Moreover, through the determination of the characteristic particles of coarse grain material in the dump, three types of characteristic particle model samples were obtained. Consider the block particles as an example. The results of replacing the original spherical particles measuring 20–60 mm are shown in (Fig. 10a), and the irregular block particle model samples are shown in (Fig. 10b). The same method was used for the flake and strip particle model samples.

From Fig. 12a, it was observed that the shear stress of discharge materials with different coarse-grain contents increased linearly with the increase in normal stress. As the coarse particle content increased, the number of coarse particles also increased, leading to longer distances between particles, a weakened filling effect of fine particles, a reduction in contact points, and a decrease in soil cohesion. In a saturated state, small particles attached to the surfaces of larger particles were successively detached, which resulted in a decrease in the cementation between existing particles and a significant reduction in cohesion. Meanwhile, an increase in the content of coarse particles led to a decrease in the cohesion of saturated coarse particles.

The internal friction of coarse particles was divided into sliding friction and biting friction. Friction generated by sliding between particles was classified as sliding friction, while friction generated by the bite action between particles was classified as biting friction. The larger the coarse particle content, the more irregular the particle shape and the rougher the contact surface, which resulted in a larger sliding friction angle. Furthermore, an increase in coarse particle content reduced the fine particles, thereby weakening the roundness between soils and highlighting the irregularity of coarse particles. This resulted in a strengthening of the bite between coarse particles and an increased friction angle of the bite2,3,9,10.

In the saturated state, the action of water reduced the roughness of the contact surface between particles, causing the sliding friction angle to become smaller13,27. To cause shear failure along a shear plane within the soil body, the interlocking particles had to be lifted from their original positions. The saturation of the soil body reduced the interlocking action and the internal friction angle. However, even with the ‘lubrication’ effect of soil saturation, an increase in coarse particle content still required a significant amount of energy for specimen dilatancy. Therefore, in a saturated state, the greater the coarse particle content, the smaller the decline in the internal friction angle.

Additionally, Fig. 12b,c showed that with the increase in coarse particle content under natural and saturated conditions, the friction strength of coarse particles in the dump under low normal stress changed little, and the cohesion of dump played a dominant role, resulting in no significant change in shear strength. As normal stress increased, the friction strength of the sample increased, and the shear strength also increased gradually. However, with the increase in coarse particle content, the increasing trend of shear strength in the natural sample slowed down, while the trend in the saturated sample was more pronounced. This was related to the decrease in the internal friction angle with the increase of coarse particle content under the saturated state of dump.

Therefore, the greater the content of coarse particles, the less influence of fine particles, the more irregular the shape of coarse particles, and the more significant the impact on the strength and deformation of coarse particles. However, large laboratory tests have large workloads and limitations, and it is often difficult to study particle shape in actual tests. Thus, it is necessary to conduct research on the characteristic particles in the dump.

Comparative analysis of direct shear simulation of random particle model and spherical particle model

Through the conduction of the direct shear simulation test with a normal force of 100–400 kPa on the numerical sample of random and spherical particles in the dump, the stress-strain relationship between the random and spherical particle models under different normal stresses was obtained. Further, the irregular particle and standard sphere models with coarse grain content of 70% were compared and analyzed with the actual test results, and the authenticity and rationality of the constructed irregular particles were discussed.

Figures 13 and 14 show that from 100 to 400 kPa, both the actual test and simulation results indicated that the shear stress increased with the increase in vertical pressure. This suggested that the irregular particle model could capture the basic characteristics of soil shear strength, and the simulation results of irregular particles were close to the actual data. The deviation ranged from 2.08 to 4.98%, whereas the standard spherical particle model deviated significantly, with variations from 5.29 to 19.39%. Upon comprehensive comparison, the simulation results of irregular particles had a good degree of fitting with the actual data, exhibiting good consistency and essentially the same change law. When simulating soil shear behavior, the spherical particle model performed well under low normal stress, but its prediction accuracy decreased with the increase in stress. Particularly at 300 kPa, the model’s prediction deviation was large. Therefore, under high normal stress, the rearrangement of particles in soil, the increase in the contact area between particles, and the change in friction between particles became more complex, rendering the spherical particle model inapplicable.

An excessive content of coarse particles diminished the influence of fine particles on the strength characteristics of the soil mass. Consequently, the more irregular the shape of the coarse particles, the more likely they were to produce mutual bite, embedding, and friction, leading to rotation dislocation and slippage between particles. As a result, the bearing capacity of the sample decreased after reaching its peak, and its shear stress-strain curve showed a slight softening. In the irregular particle model, the fine particles of the sample were replaced by spherical particles. The spherical particles rotated in a staggered manner when the specimen was loaded, and the continuous increase in stress caused the position between particles to change continuously. Thus, the stress-strain curve of the irregular particle model was micro-hardened, and the peak value was not obvious. The standard sphere model differed greatly from the actual test, and the severe movement between the particles resulted in the hardening of the stress-strain curve. The simulation results of the comprehensive comparison of irregular particles exhibited a preferable degree of fitting with the actual fit. Further they exhibited good consistency and the change rule was fundamentally the same. Thus, the relevant rules obtained by the numerical simulation test results were in line with the actual rules on site.

Influencing mechanism analysis of characteristic particles on the material mechanical properties of dump

By changing the interval values of elongation, sphericity, and flattening rate, the flake, strip, and block characteristic particles were obtained. A direct shear simulation test with normal force of 100–400 kPa was conducted on the single characteristic sample with 70% coarse particle content, and the stress-strain relationship of different characteristic samples under different normal stresses was obtained.

Figure 15 shows that under the same confining pressure, the peak strength of the block sample was the largest, followed by the strip sample and then the flake sample. Under low confining pressure, the stress-strain curves of the strip and flake samples coincided, and the stress-strain curves of the three shape characteristics changed more significantly with the increase in confining pressure. Figure 16 shows that the actual test results were closer to the block characteristic stress-strain curve. This can be attributed the actual dumping material as a mixture causing these three characteristic samples to exist simultaneously, which resulted in the stress-strain curve of the sample exhibiting characteristics in between the flake, strip, and block samples. The actual results were closer to the block characteristic curve, which showed that the coarse grains with block characteristics were greater in number in the granular material of the dump, and the strip and flake coarse particles were relatively less. Therefore, through the numerical simulation of the single characteristics of the dump material, the direct shear test was performed to roughly analyze the shape characteristics and content of the coarse particles that played a leading role in the dump field. This can also better explain the variation of the stress-strain curve under different confining pressures.

Theoretical analysis indicated that replacing spherical particles with characteristic particles of the same size resulted in changes to the initial density due to the variability of the irregular particle shapes, as shown in (Fig. 17). Compared with many scholars, this paper studied the shear characteristics of high bench dump under different natural and saturated conditions through actual large-scale direct shear tests, which proved the importance of coarse grain, and revealed the influence of coarse grain content change on shear strength and interaction between particles. Additionally, by combining particle screening results from the dump, a method for constructing random complex particles was proposed, and three types of shape-characteristic particles were created. Numerical analysis was used to discuss the impact of particle shape on the mechanical properties of the dump, which can more accurately reflect the actual distribution and interaction of soil particles and reveal the influence mechanism of particle shape on shear strength. For coarse particles, the larger the size and the more irregular the shape, the stronger the occlusal and interlocking between particles, thereby enhancing the soil’s shear strength. Consequently, the shear strength differs significantly from that of spherical particles, and the self-locking effect of occlusal between particles should be considered, especially in the shear zone where particle movement is intense and dilatation amplitude is large. Unlike spherical particles, which derive their shear strength mainly from sliding friction due to their smooth surfaces, irregular particles with angular edges produce greater occlusal and interlock due to their rough surfaces and irregular shapes, leading to higher shear strength. These findings offer a new perspective for understanding the macroscopic and microscopic mechanical behavior of coarse-grained soil and have significant practical implications for engineering applications such as earth-rock dam design, slope stability analysis, and granular material design. They aid in preventing and mitigating geological disasters caused by coarse-grained soil shear and enhance the safety and economy of engineering design. However, due to computational limitations, future research should replace all coarse particle shapes with a variety of shape characteristics to make simulation test results more authentic.

Conclusion

This study applies the mechanical properties of high-bench dump, focusing on the impact of particle shape and coarse grain content. The following specific conclusions were drawn:

On-site and large-scale direct shear tests revealed a grading fraction in high-bench dumps, with coarse grain content rising from top to bottom. Higher coarse grain content, associated with more irregular particle shapes, reduced cohesion and increased the internal friction angle, significantly affecting shear strength under low normal stress. While shear strength increased with normal stress, the trend’s magnitude decreased.Comparative analysis of direct shear simulations using random and spherical particle models revealed significant discrepancies between the standard spherical model and actual test results. This divergence was attributed to the spherical model’s underestimation of inter-particle bite, nesting, and friction. Conversely, the random particle model, generated by varying the angle of random points and the length of ellipsoidal spindles, accurately simulated the particles from the actual high-bench dump. The simulation results closely matched the site’s observed shear behavior.The shape and content of coarse particles directly affected the mechanical properties of the dump, as indicated by the stress-strain curves. Under low normal stress, the curves for flake, strip, and block samples were consistent, but the block sample showed the highest peak intensity at higher stresses. The bottom of the high-bench dump contained more block-like coarse particles and fewer flake or strip particles.

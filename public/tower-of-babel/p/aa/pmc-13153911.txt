Sex Differences in Ventricular Antiarrhythmic Effect of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitor
Authors: Takashi Kurita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

Drug therapy for heart failure, which began with angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, is seeing new developments with the advent of angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2is).1, 2, 3 Meanwhile, gender has been recognized as a critically important factor determining the pathophysiology and prognosis of heart failure, and the existence of gender differences in the efficacy of heart failure drugs also is suggested.4 Particularly, the newest heart failure drug, SGLT2i, has demonstrated efficacy in multiple meta-analyses of patients with heart failure, regardless of diabetes status or presence of reduced left ventricular function, making them highly attractive agents.5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 This editorial paper first summarizes meta-analyses focusing on gender differences in SGLT2i efficacy (using randomized controlled trials with cardiovascular events as endpoints), followed by a discussion of the corresponding study by Nakano et al (EMPA-ICD [Empagliflozin in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Treated with an Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator]) published in this issue of JACC: Asia.12,13

Sex Differences in Effect of SGLT2i in the Meta-Analyses and EMPA-ICD

To the best of the author's knowledge, there are a total of 7 meta-analyses examining gender differences in SGLT2is. Table 1 summarizes these by publication date (study 8 refers to data from Nakano et al12 in this issue of JACC: Asia). All analyses consistently confirm a reduction in cardiovascular events with SGLT2i, regardless of gender. However, regarding comparisons of efficacy between men and women, results vary: some show no gender difference (papers 1, 3, 6, and 7),5,7,10,11 some indicate a benefit for men (papers 2 and 5),6,9 and others show the opposite (paper 4).8 A consistent conclusion has not been reached. This variability in results is likely because of significant differences in patient populations (presence or absence of type 2 diabetes mellitus [DM], heart failure, or reduced left ventricular ejection function) and in the combinations of composite endpoints used. Furthermore, the higher statistical power observed in men across many studies can be attributed to differences in patient numbers (with men constituting approximately 65% of participants), as evidenced by the lack of substantial differences in HR. As discussed in the corresponding study by Nakano et al,12 several factors (including pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, underlying heart disease, circulating plasma volume, and the amount of systemic or epicardial adipose tissue) have been proposed to explain why SGLT2is may be more beneficial in men. However, these differences do not necessarily favor men, and the mechanism by which SGLT2is induce their favorable effects remains unclear.

Meanwhile, the unique aspects of the present EMPA-ICD study lie in utilizing the implantable cardioverter-defibrillator’s (ICD's) function as a long-term and accurate monitoring tool for ventricular arrhythmias (VAs) and defining the endpoint as more specific VAs (nonsustained ventricular tachycardia [NSVT], sustained ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation).12,13 Although observational studies using similar methods have reported reductions in VAs (primarily in NSVT) in patients with a cardiac implantable electrical device,14 this issue is noteworthy for demonstrating efficacy in a randomized controlled trial. Subanalyses of DAPA-HF (Study to Evaluate the Effect of Dapagliflozin on the Incidence of Worsening Heart Failure or Cardiovascular Death in Patients With Chronic Heart Failure)15 and meta-analysis of multiple randomized controlled trials16 involving SGLT2is have shown that these drugs reduce sudden death. However, a contradiction has been pointed out in the latter analysis:16 no significant efficacy was confirmed for the frequency of fatal VAs compared with the control group. Sudden death is the ultimate hardest outcome of arrhythmia and is almost invariably captured in data collection. In contrast, the presence of VAs may have been easily overlooked because of the insufficient accuracy or density of the monitoring methods used to detect them. On the other hand, the EMPA-ICD study used a highly reliable monitoring method based on ICD data,12,13 which may contribute to elucidating the mechanism by which SGLT2is prevent sudden death. However, because this study does not demonstrate an effect sufficient to suppress appropriate ICD therapy, it remains questionable whether SGLT2is exhibit efficacy sufficient to improve the prognosis of male patients for whom ICD is indicated. Furthermore, compared with other meta-analyses, this issue clearly has a lower proportion of women (see Table 1), and it must be noted that significant differences in underlying cardiac disease between men and women may have influenced the results. Larger prospective clinical trials are needed to investigate sex differences in the antiarrhythmic effects of SGLT2is.

Significance of Reducing VA Events

The reduction in NSVT episodes likely contributed significantly to this study's confirmation of SGLT2i-induced reduction in VA events and its gender differences (the types of VA events that were primarily suppressed are not specified in this paper).12,16 Makimoto et al17 presented data showing that NSVT occurrence in patients with primary prevention ICD implantation correlates with subsequent appropriate ICD therapies. With larger registries and longer follow-up periods, it may be possible to demonstrate that SGLT2is reduce incidence of ICD therapies. However, in recent years, the trend in tachycardia detection criteria for ICDs in primary prevention patients has shifted significantly toward “longer duration” and/or “higher rate cutoffs” settings.18 In particular, the recently published APPRAISE ATP (Assessment of Primary Prevention Patients Receiving An ICD - Systematic Evaluation of ATP) study19 suggested that avoiding unnecessary electrical interventions for relatively low-rate ventricular tachycardias and waiting for spontaneous termination whenever possible may suppress subsequent electrical storms, further promoting this trend. However, on the other hand, it must be acknowledged that suppression of NSVT itself contributes to reducing ICD therapy rates and, consequently, improving patient prognosis has become quite challenging.

Regarding the Antiarrhythmic Effects of SGLT2i

SGLT2is directly prevent the progression of atherosclerosis in diabetic patients through their blood glucose–lowering effects,20 contributing to the prevention of heart failure development and fatal VAs. Meanwhile, numerous clinical trials have demonstrated that SGLT2is also exhibit myocardial protective effects in nondiabetic patients.2,3,5, 6, 7,9,10,15,16,21,22 This is thought to result from the combined action of multiple pathways, including the interrelated hypoglycemic effect, which collectively reduce the risk of heart failure and arrhythmias. Particularly in patients with heart failure, a moderate reduction in body fluid volume without affecting renal function and electrolytes abnormalities decreases cardiac chamber pressure and excessive myocardial stress. This reverses myocardial remodeling (fibrosis and hypertrophy), which is a substrate for arrhythmia development. Reverse remodeling also prevents excessive sympathetic activation and mitigates oxidative stress and myocardial inflammation. Furthermore, as demonstrated in several meta-analyses, SGLT2is reduce the risk of atrial fibrillation by providing an indirect favorable effect on VAs.16,21,22 Virtuous cycle effects on the cardio-renal linkage via renal protection are also anticipated. Furthermore, SGLT2is are thought to exert direct beneficial effects on cardiomyocytes. These drugs properly influence the activity of L-homolamban, inducing phosphorylation of calcium/calmodulin-independent protein kinase II. This effectively reduces excessive calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, thereby lowering the incidence of arrhythmias. Furthermore, SGLT2is possess potent delayed inward sodium current (INa) inhibitory effects and are thought to exert a direct antiarrhythmic effect.

Conclusions

Although the corresponding study by Nakano et al12 has limitations, such as a small patient population and variability in underlying cardiac disease, it revealed a novel aspect: the antiarrhythmic effect of SGLTis (reduction in VAs) is more frequently observed in male ICD patients with type 2 DM. This finding is intriguing and important as it suggests directions for future trial designs. These include elucidating the mechanism of action of SGLTis (why they are more effective in men, and why they provide antiarrhythmic effects), assessing efficacy beyond the presence of type 2 DM, and evaluating the effect in secondary prevention ICD patients with established arrhythmogenic substrate.

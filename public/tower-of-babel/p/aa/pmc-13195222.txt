Efficacy and Safety of Fibroblast Activation Protein Targeted Radioligand Therapy for Advanced Solid Tumour: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Xiaoli Zhang, Jianpeng Cao, Xue Jiang, Yuxiao Xia, Ying Huang, Qian Liu, Lina Liu, Dongkun Xu, Hongjie Wu, Wenling Tu, Yuhong Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveFibroblast activation protein (FAP) is a promising cancer theranostic target. While FAP-based diagnostics have proven highly accurate, the efficacy and safety of FAP-targeted radioligand therapy (FAP-RLT) for advanced, treatment-refractory solid tumours are poorly characterized.MethodsWe performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of clinical trials reporting therapeutic outcomes and adverse events of FAP-RLT up to 25 January 2025 (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, Cochrane Library, Web of Science/MEDLINE). Primary endpoints were objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and incidence of grade ≥ 3 adverse events.ResultsThe objective response rate of FAP-RLT treatment was 11.7% (95%CI: 3.2%-23.2%; p < 0.001), and the disease control rate reached 44.4% (95%CI: 25.2%-64.5%; p = 0.004). Specifically, peptide ligands demonstrated superior therapeutic efficacy compared to small molecule ligands. Among radionuclides, 177Lu exhibited better outcomes than 90Y. In specific tumour types, patients with iodine-refractory thyroid cancer derived the greatest benefit from FAP-RLT, followed by those with lung cancer, sarcomas, and breast cancer. Conversely, patients with colorectal cancer and pancreatic cancer exhibited limited therapeutic responses. The pooled median overall survival was 7.26 months, while the median progression-free survival was 4.77 months. Grade ≥ 3 adverse reactions were observed in 2.2% (95%CI: 0.1%-6.0%; p = 0.018) of the cases, 11 out of 222 patients, mostly caused by hematotoxicity.ConclusionFAP-RLT has demonstrated significant disease control in multiple advanced solid tumours while maintaining a favourable safety profile. These findings can contribute to clinical treatment expectations and support informed policy decision-making in oncology.Keywords: fibroblast activation protein (FAP), radioligand therapy (RLT), radiotheranostics, efficacy, adverse events

1. Introduction

Cancer remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with an estimated 19.98 million new cases and 9.74 million related deaths recorded in 2022. 1 While radical resection is typically curative for early-stage tumours, many patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, necessitating a shift to comprehensive therapeutic strategies including chemotherapy, external irradiation, targeted therapies, and immunotherapy. 2 Nonetheless, each treatment protocol has inherent limitations, such as suboptimal efficacy, restricted applicability, drug resistance, and severe adverse effects. 3 Radioligand therapy (RLT) utilises the emitted radiation from the radionuclides to achieve both diagnostic and therapeutic effects. 4 This precise, personalised approach can overcome tumour heterogeneity and has the advantages of specificity, low toxicity, and low resistance compared to conventional therapies. 5

Targeted fibroblast activation protein (FAP) radioligand therapy (FAP-RLT) offers a novel theranostic approach by combining radionuclides with ligands to selectively target tumour sites expressing FAP. FAP is expressed in over 90% of epithelial-derived tumour tissues and is minimally expressed or absent in normal adult tissues, making it an attractive target within the tumour microenvironment. 6 Furthermore, FAP influences tumour development by promoting cell proliferation, invasion, and immune suppression, which positions it as a promising biomarker.7-9 Meta-analyses have demonstrated that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT imaging surpasses 18F-FDG in diagnostic performance for detecting various types of solid cancers, encompassing primary tumours as well as lymph nodes and distant metastases.10,11

Intriguingly, increasing evidence suggests that FAP also holds therapeutic potential. Therapeutic radionuclides such as 90Y, 225Ac, and 177Lu, when targeted to FAP through ligands like FAPI-04, FAPI-46, FAP-2886, SA.FAPI and their dimers have shown promising therapeutic effects against a range of epithelial malignancies, including colorectal, breast, sarcoma, lung, and pancreatic cancers.12-16 However, existing studies, limited by small sample sizes, heterogeneous tumour types, and substantial result variability, have hampered a comprehensive evaluation of FAP-RLT efficacy. This study aimed to systematically identify and synthesize primary research to investigate the efficacy and safety of FAP-RLT in patients with advanced, FAP-positive solid tumours refractory to conventional therapies. Furthermore, we sought to compare efficacy estimates across subgroups stratified by tumour type, radionuclide, and ligand to better elucidate its clinical potential.

2. Materials and Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis strictly followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 Statement and was registered with PROSPERO (https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/) under registration number CRD42024499451.

2.1 Search Strategy and Selection Criteria

The PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science/MEDLINE, and clinicaltrials.gov databases were searched for the relevant articles published till January 25, 2025, with no restrictions on article language. The PIO questions guiding the review were: (1) Participants: patients with inoperable, metastatic, advanced solid tumours refractory to standard therapies, and confirmed to be FAP-positive via PET or SPECT; (2) Intervention: FAP-RLT; (3) Outcome: efficacy and safety parameters.

The following MeSH and free words were included: “radiopharmaceutical or radionuclide or radioligand” and “treat or therapy or manage' and “FAP or FAPI or fibroblast activating protein or FAPα”.

Data were independently evaluated by two researchers with disagreement adjudicated by a third researcher. When patient data originated from the same clinical trial or institution, only the most comprehensive, recent, and relevant studies were included. Inclusion criteria were original research studies that included prospective and retrospective studies. Tumours or metastases were confirmed by histopathology or combined clinical follow-up. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) reviews, editorials, letters, and meeting abstracts; (2) case reports; (3) absence of clinical efficacy metrics or safety data; and (4) non-clinical trials.

2.2 Outcomes

Primary endpoints comprised the objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and the occurrence of grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs). Secondary outcomes included median overall survival (OS) and median progression-free survival (PFS). These indices were evaluated using 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours. 17 The ORR was defined as the proportion of patients exhibiting significant tumour reduction (complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) sustained over a predefined duration, while the DCR extended this measure to include patients demonstrating stable disease (SD) (CR + PR + SD). Adverse events were categorized by the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), with grade ≥ 3 AEs typically indicating severe incidents that necessitate medical intervention. Their high incidence can adversely affect patients’ quality of life or result in the discontinuation of treatment. PFS was defined as the time from the first dose of FAP-RLT to the first evidence of progression or death or the end of the study period; OS was defined as the time from the first dose of FAP-RLT to death from any cause.

2.3 Quality Assessment

The studies included in this review were all single-arm trials and did not provide specific data on the geographic distribution or socioeconomic factors of the disease. To ensure the rigor and reliability of our research, we employed the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) quality assessment tool, which evaluates studies based on the inclusion criteria for participants, clinical data, and the appropriateness of the statistical methods among others.

2.4 Statistical Analysis

Random-effects meta-analyses were conducted to quantify pooled effectiveness and hazard estimates. The Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation and the exact binomial method were utilized to compute the weighted pooled effect estimates and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs).18,19 Furthermore, z-scores (weighted mean divided by the standard error of the weighted mean) and associated p-values are provided for each metric to judge the precision of the pooled estimate across studies. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The Egger’s test was employed to assess funnel plot asymmetry to assess publication bias. 20 The trim-and-fill method was applied to adjust the effect size in the presence of significant publication bias. Heterogeneity was evaluated using the I2 statistic and the Chi-squared test. Substantial heterogeneity was deemed present when p < 0.10 or I2 > 50%, thereby necessitating comprehensive subgroup analyses. 21 In studies including multiple tumour types and their respective subtypes, aggregate treatment effect should be estimated according to tumour type, while subtype-specific analyses may be appropriately conducted within subsequent stratified subgroups. Sensitivity analyses were conducted using the leave-one-out method, whereby each study was omitted in turn to determine whether the effect remained within the overall 95% CI. 22 All analyses were performed using Stata 17 software.

3. Results

3.1 Study Selection

We identified 65 articles, of which 29 were excluded due to their focus on animal studies, leaving 36 articles for further consideration (eFigure 1). After additional screening to exclude studies lacking relevant endpoints or with sample sizes fewer than five, a total of 15 studies met the inclusion criteria. Of these, 11 patients did not recevie with FAP-RLT. Data were ultimately extracted from 222 patients.13,23-36 Study characteristics were described in Table 1 and Table 2. Among the included literature, 5 were prospectively designed, while 10 were retrospective. Colorectal tumours (n = 66), breast cancer (n = 32), thyroid cancer (n = 33), sarcoma (n = 52), lung cancer (n = 16), pancreatic cancer (n = 13) and several other tumour types were included in this study and the diagnosis was confirmed using histopathological findings. All patients included in the study had received at least two conventional treatments, which could have been surgery, external irradiation, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy. Additionally, their tumours were confirmed to be positive for FAP through PET scans. The quality of the studies reviewed was generally high, with 11 classified as good quality and 4 as fair quality (eTable 1).

3.2 Efficacy Evaluation

In the above-mentioned selected studies, 13 studies reported estimates of post-treatment effects,13,23,24,26,28-36 with 2 studies failing to achieve the anticipated disease control.24,27 Among 195 patients who received FAP-RLT as salvage therapy and were evaluated for response, 27 achieved tumour remission, resulting in ORR of 11.7% (95% CI 3.2%-23.2%, Z = 3.57, p < 0.01) These studies exhibited significant heterogeneity (Chi2 = 46.07, p < 0.001, tau2 = 0.19, I2 = 73.95%) (Figure 1A). Egger’s linear regression asymmetry test indicated pontential pulication bias (p = 0.01, eFigure 2A). Due to publication bias, the trim-and-fill method was used, resulting in an 8.0% overall proportion with 4 added studies (95% CI: 2.0%-22.6%, p < 0.01). Sensitivity analysis confirmed that omitting any single study did not affect the overall effect of the analysis, indicating stable results(eFigure 2B).

Forest plot of objective response rate (A) and disease control rate (B)

The objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) were reported in patients with advanced solid cancers receiving FAP-RLT, according to a pooling of the 13 studies

Notably, 81 out of 195 patients achieved successful tumour control, resulting in DCR of 44.4% (95% CI: 25.2%-64.5%, Z = 6.23, p < 0.001). These studies also showed significant heterogeneity (Chi2 = 86.64, p < 0.001, tau2 = 0.41, I2 = 86.15%) (Figure 1B). Publication bias tests revealed significant publication bias (p = 0.03) (eFigure 2C). Following correction with the trim-and-fill method and adding 4 studies, the adjusted overall proportion was 32.1% (95% CI: 13.3%-50.8%, p < 0.001). Sensitivity analysis revealed no substantial change in DCR estimates upon excluding any single study (eFigure 2D).

3.3 Subgroup Analyses

Given the differences in ligand types, the physicochemical properties of radionuclides, and the histological types of tumours, the influences brought by these factors were also explored in this study. Therefore, we conducted subgroup analyses to assess the potential impact of these factors on treatment outcomes (eTable 2 and Figure 2). Monoclonal antibodies did not achieve the expected therapeutic effects, whereas small molecules such as FAPI-04, FAPI-46, and peptide ligands (FAPI-2286) demonstrated higher efficacy. Specifically, the ORR of small-molecule and peptide ligands was 13.2% and 22.8%, respectively, and the DCR was 54.6% and 57.3%, respectively. Regarding radionuclides, 131I was only used to label monoclonal antibodies and did not show therapeutic effects. The ORR of 177Lu and 90Y were 20.2% and 7.5%, respectively, with a significant difference between them; while the DCR was similar, at 56.1% and 54.4%, respectively. Regarding tumour typing, the ORR and DCR were as follow: colorectal cancer (n = 48, ORR: 0.6%, DCR: 20.7%), breast cancer (n = 28, ORR: 10.2%, DCR: 50.0%), radioiodine-resistant thyroid cancer (n = 17, ORR: 25.9%, DCR: 63.9%), sarcoma (n = 41, ORR: 16.8%, DCR: 57.1%), lung cancer (n = 15, ORR: 21.0%, DCR: 53.5%), and pancreatic cancer (n = 11, ORR: 0%, DCR: 13.3%). For advanced solid tumours with high expression of FAP, FAP-RLT utilizing 177Lu- and 90Y-labelled small molecules and peptides has demonstrated efficacy, albeit with significant variability across different histological types. Specifically, 177Lu therapies and peptide ligands exhibit superior therapeutic outcomes compared to 90Y therapies and small-molecule ligands, respectively.

3.4 Survival Outcomes

Among the 15 studies, 9 studies, including 143 patients, provided survival data,13,26,28-30,33-36 while the remaining studies lacked data due to inadequate follow-up or insufficient clinical outcome assessment. The pooled median OS was 7.26 months, with a range of 5.25 to 9.28 months, while the pooled median PFS was 4.77 months, ranging from 2.63 to 6.91 months (Figure 3). Egger's linear regression for publication bias was not significant (p > 0.05). No outlier was detected based on the sensitivity analysis (eFigure 2E-F). For patients with advanced solid tumours treated with pembrolizumab, the median PFS was 4.1 months (95% CI: 2.4 - 4.9), and the median OS was 23.5 months (95% CI: 13.5 months to not reached). 37 Various factors, including tumour type, disease stage, and treatment approach, were known to influence the estimates, leading to considerable variability. Consequently, the clinical relevance of these survival data is compromised by the limited sample size and the inherent constraints of the studies, which called for a more detailed analysis and contextual interpretation of the data within specific clinical scenarios.

3.5 Adverse Events

The most reported grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) during FAP-RLT treatment were related to hematotoxicity (Table 2). Among 222 patients with solid tumours across 15 studies, grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 2.2% of cases (95%CI: 0.1%-6.0%; Z = 2.372, p = 0.018) (Figure 4). Specifically, one patient experienced exacerbated pain, another developed anaemia, and 9 patients exhibited leukopenia or thrombocytopenia. Notably, hematotoxicity was generally reversible after treatment discontinuation . 29 The anaemia in one patient might also have been influenced by concurrent chemotherapy during the same period. 26 No instances of hepatic or renal impairment were identified across all studies, nor were any severe, life-threatening treatment-related adverse events or hypersensitivity reactions observed. Despite some associated risks, the overall safety profile of FAP-RLT was deemed acceptable.

4. Discussion

Despite advances in anti-tumour treatments, the majority of cancers remain incurable.2,38,39 The increase in tumour heterogeneity during treatment regimens lowers therapeutic efficacy, and induces drug resistance and adverse effects.38,40 This warrants novel theranostic approaches that integrate diagnosis and treatment. 41 This term was first used by John Funkhouser in 1998, and has since become a key tool of precision cancer treatment. Radionuclides are ideal theranostic agents that have been integrated into oncology treatments through diverse strategies. 42 The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved various radio-theranostics, including 90Y-ibritumomab tiuxetan, 43 177Lu-DOTATATE, 44 131I-iobenguane, 45 and 177Lu-PSMA-617. 46

The tumour microenvironment (TME) is a complex system that includes tumour cells, immune cells, fibroblasts, blood vessels, and the extracellular matrix (ECM), and their associated signalling pathways offer numerous potential therapeutic targets.8,9 FAP is a type II transmembrane serine protease that hydrolyzes the ECM, which in turn fosters tumour proliferation, migration, invasion, and immune evasion, thereby culminating in adverse prognoses. While FAP exhibits minimal expression in normal adult tissues, it is markedly upregulated in over 90% of epithelial-derived tumour tissues. 6 Thus, targeting FAP can not only increase the sensitivity of tumour detection but also lessen the risk of drug resistance due to the stability and abundance of stromal components. 7

FAP-RLT is an emerging theranostic approach that relies on ligand affinity, stability, and physicochemical properties of the radionuclides. Notably, differences in radionuclide physical properties may contribute to variability in therapeutic response. For example, β-emitters such as 177Lu and 90Y differ in tissue penetration range and energy deposition, with 90Y having a longer path length and higher mean energy, which may favour the treatment of larger lesions, whereas 177Lu may be more suitable for smaller lesions. These differences may partially explain between‑study heterogeneity in therapeutic response. In addition, the pharmacokinetic properties of FAP-targeting agents may further influence therapeutic efficacy. Early antibody-based FAP-targeted therapies have shown prolonged tumour retention and high specificity but are limited by slow extravasation, prolonged circulation time, and increased systemic toxicity.47,48 In contrast, small-molecule FAPI radioligands demonstrate rapid tumour uptake and fast clearance from non-targeted tissues, resulting in improved tumour-to-background ratios but potentially reduced tumour retention time. This fundamental difference may impact the therapeutic index and optimal radionuclide selection. Strategies such as albumin-binding modifications or peptide conjugation have been explored to prolong tumour retention and improve therapeutic efficacy.49-51

FAP-2286 exhibits prolonged retention and enhanced uptake in tumours compared to FAPI-46, 52 despite reports of increased renal toxicity. 53 Bispecific heterodimers that target FAP and other biomarkers, such as 68Ga-FAP-RGD, 177Lu-OncoFAP and 177Lu-BiOncoFAP, may further improve targeting specificity and retention.54,55 Alpha particles are characterized by their shorter path length and elevated linear energy transfer (LET), and are capable of directly inducing double-strand DNA break and overcoming the hypoxic TME. 56 However, α-emitters also has off-target effects that limit their clinical utility. Notably, Liu et al. recently showed that β-emitters has a higher energy transfer efficiency and persists for a longer period. 16 The integration of FAP-RLT with other therapeutic modalities can substantially augment its anti-tumour efficacy. FAP-RLT is more likely to function as a complementary strategy rather than a replacement for conventional therapies, particularly in heavily pretreated patients. For instance, chemo- and photodynamic therapies have been combined with photosensitizers, 51 and the combination of 177Lu-FAPI and immunotherapy has also been tested.57-59 FAP-RLT has also been explored in non-neoplastic diseases such as fibrosis and inflammation,60-69 further supporting its broad theranostic potential.

Although FAP-RLT has shown promise, the available clinical evidence is fragmented and heterogeneous, largely derived from small, early-phase or retrospective studies with variable tumour types, radionuclides, and treatment protocols. Dosimetry, follow-up duration, and outcome reporting are inconsistent, and most patients receiving FAP-RLT have advanced, treatment-refractory disease after multiple lines of therapy. Consequently, the overall efficacy, safety, and optimal treatment parameters of FAP-RLT remain unclear. These limitations underscore the need for a systematic and quantitative synthesis of available data, which can integrate existing evidence, improve statistical power, and provide more robust insights into the therapeutic potential and limitations of FAP-RLT in advanced solid tumours.

The results of our meta-analysis indicated that FAP-RLT demonstrated an acceptable short-term safety profile within the reported dose ranges. However, safety conclusions should be interpreted with caution and limited to the available follow-up period, as delayed toxicities such as renal impairment or bone marrow suppression cannot be excluded. In addition, distinguishing treatment-related adverse events from disease-related complications remains challenging in heavily pretreated populations.

There are several limitations to consider. The heterogeneous study populations and complex grouping strategies may have introduced unmeasured confounding and collinearity, contributing to overall heterogeneity. The relatively small sample size further limited the ability to explore its sources, which may relate to differences in patient age, disease severity, and study design. Therapeutic efficacy cannot be adequately evaluated based solely on administered activity. Absorbed dose to tumours and organs at risk is a more relevant determinant of response and toxicity. However, dosimetric data were inconsistently reported, limiting cross-study comparisons. This underscores the need for standardized dosimetry reporting in future FAP-RLT research.

5. Conclusion

FAP-RLT is an effective and safe treatment option for advanced solid tumours, and is associated with a lower incidence of adverse reactions compared to external irradiation and chemotherapy. Given its broad clinical potential and substantial market opportunities, FAP-RLT is likely to advance significantly in future.

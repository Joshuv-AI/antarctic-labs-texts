Determination of the relationship between raftlin, oxidative stress levels, and vitamin D receptor gene polymorphism in diabetic patients
Authors: Figen Guzelgul, Nihan Bozkurt, Merve Catak, Kubra Sahin, Marwa Abdelmageed, Shahab Ahmed Salih Gezh, Sadegul Savkin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The pathogenesis of diabetes is affected by a complex interplay between numerous environmental and genetic factors. While single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are not directly used as biomarkers of the disease, they can be considered an important guide for determining predisposition.Objective:To investigate the roles of raftlin, malondialdehyde (MDA), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and the vitamin D receptor (VDR) BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) polymorphism, in the clinical progression of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).Design:A retrospective observational case–control study.Methods:A total of 300 subjects were included in the study: 104 with T2DM, 85 with prediabetes, and 111 healthy controls. The VDR BsmI polymorphism was genotyped using the polymerase chain reaction-restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR-RFLP) method, while serum MDA, SOD, and raftlin were analyzed via the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) method.Results:No statistically significant differences were observed in the allelic and genotype frequencies of the VDR gene BsmI polymorphism between T2DM prediabetes patients and controls (p = 0.97, p = 0.58, respectively). MDA levels were higher, while raftlin levels were lower in patients with T2DM and prediabetes compared to controls (p < 0.05). Within the T2DM cohort, HbA1c (p = 0.014), MDA (p = 0.001), and raftlin (p < 0.001) differed significantly between the BB and bb genotypes, with a positive correlation between the bb genotype and HbA1c (p = 0.008, r = 0.287). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis revealed raftlin’s diagnostic potential among T2DM patients with the BB and bb genotypes, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.839, a specificity of 69.2%, and a sensitivity of 81.5%.Conclusion:To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate that raftlin exhibits significant diagnostic efficacy in individuals with the bb genotype within a clinical T2DM cohort.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM), which can affect all age groups, genders, and races worldwide from childhood to adulthood, is a significant public health problem. According to recent studies, DM ranks among the leading causes of death in adulthood. In addition, DM not only contributes to multi-organ damage, but also imposes a substantial economic burden on healthcare systems. A study conducted in 2019 estimated that the prevalence of diabetes could reach 578 million in 2030 and 700 million in 2045. 1 However, the rise in diabetes incidence, especially after the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, suggests the possibility of exceeding these estimated numbers. 2 DM is an endocrine disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia due to impaired insulin secretion, impaired insulin action, or both. 3 DM is not a discrete clinical entity but a heterogeneous group of metabolic disorders characterized by diverse etiologies, pathogeneses, and genetic backgrounds. 4 DM is categorized into four primary classifications: Type 1 (T1DM), Type 2 (T2DM), gestational diabetes (GDM), and other specific types resulting from causes such as monogenic syndromes, exocrine pancreatic diseases, or drug-induced hyperglycemia. Notably, T2DM accounts for over 90% of the global diabetes prevalence. 5 T2DM may develop at older ages, when the already reduced beta-cell reserve is further impaired as a result of an infection, stress, surgery, pregnancy, or weight gain. 6 In addition to these factors, T2DM is also believed to arise from a combination of environmental, behavioral, and genetic factors. Based on family and twin studies, T2DM is estimated to be 25% to 72% heritable. 7 In addition to the above factors, inflammation is also considered one of the primary triggers of T2DM. 8 During the development of DM, pro-inflammatory cytokines may cause insulin resistance in various tissues by inhibiting the insulin signaling pathway. 9 Identifying potential pathways linking inflammation to diabetes has sparked increasing interest in targeting inflammation to help prevent and manage diabetes and related conditions. 10 Cross-sectional and prospective studies investigating the relationship between inflammation and DM have demonstrated that inflammation has a three-pronged effect. First, it has been reported that inflammation suppresses the insulin signaling pathway via Serine/Threonine (Ser/Thr) phosphorylation of the insulin receptor substrate (IRS) by JUN N-terminal kinase (JNK) and IκB kinase-β (IKKβ). Second, secreted inflammatory mediators induce insulin resistance by activating leukocytes, leading to an inflammatory phenotype. Finally, the release of various inflammatory mediators, such as chemokines, increases the inflammatory signaling and tissue remodeling capacity of resident cells by recruiting circulating leukocytes to the stressed tissue. 11 In addition to inflammation, the effects of oxidative stress on cellular signaling play a critical role in the pathogenesis of DM. Increased production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) may contribute to impaired insulin signal transduction by altering the structural organization of lipid raft microdomains that host insulin receptors. 12 In the present study, the levels of raftlin were investigated in DM cases, a protein that has been recently recognized as a novel and reliable inflammation indicator. As a primary structural protein of lipid rafts, raftlin plays a multifaceted role in regulating inflammatory processes and has emerged as a potential diagnostic marker. 13 Disruption of lipid raft architecture is known to alter metabolic responses via oxidative stress pathways, contributing to endothelial dysfunction and impaired glucose homeostasis. 14 Therefore, raftlin expression levels may provide critical insights into the intersection of inflammatory signaling and oxidative stress–related metabolic deterioration in the context of T2DM progression.15,16 Considering the relationship between inflammation and diabetes, we hypothesize in our study that the level of raftlin, the major protein of the lipid raft in the insulin signaling pathway, may vary under different glycemic conditions. The factors triggering the development of DM include an increase in pro-inflammatory mediators and a decrease in anti-inflammatory cytokine levels due to low vitamin D concentrations. 17 Recent studies have demonstrated that vitamin D, a fat-soluble hormone-like vitamin whose primary function is to regulate calcium-phosphate metabolism, exerts significant effects over a wide range of diseases, from infectious to neurodegenerative diseases. 18 Vitamin D is also notable for its antioxidant properties. Studies have shown that in hyperglycemic individuals, oxidative stress increases while antioxidant levels decrease. The increase in ROS and reactive nitrogen species (RNS) in response to decreased antioxidant systems can cause damage pancreatic beta cells and consequently impair the effective functioning of the intracellular insulin signaling pathway. 19 This suggests that low vitamin D concentrations may play a role in the pathogenesis of diabetes. 20 While insufficient sunlight exposure and poor diet are implicated as causes of vitamin D deficiency, gene polymorphisms in vitamin D receptors (VDRs) are also considered important contributing factors. 21 In addition, genetic polymorphisms in vitamin D-related genes are thought to predispose to impaired glycemic control and T2DM.

In addition to vitamin D, there are numerous important antioxidant systems in our body that form a defense line against free radicals. Among the best-known antioxidant systems is the superoxide dismutase (SOD) enzyme. SOD catalyzes the dismutation of superoxide radicals (O2•–) into hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and molecular oxygen (O2), thereby forming a key component of the antioxidant defense system. 22 ROS exert their most harmful effects by inducing lipid peroxidation. The peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids leads to an increase in malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, which is considered a good marker for lipid peroxidation. Studies have shown that MDA levels increase significantly in various diseases as a result of tissue damage. 23 Understanding the biological interaction between environmental factors and genetic predispositions in diseases may be possible through various study results and in-depth analysis of biological pathways. In the present work, we aimed to demonstrate the effect of a single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) in VDR, which may also possess antioxidant properties, on the clinical course of T2DM and on oxidative stress and inflammation. In this context, the VDR BsmI (rs1544410) gene polymorphism was investigated, while MDA, SOD, and raftlin levels were examined to assess oxidative stress and inflammation. Unlike other studies, our study evaluated the level of raftlin protein both according to different T2DM clinical courses and in relation to the VDR BsmI (rs1544410) gene polymorphism, investigating their relationship. To our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the relationship between raftlin levels and VDR BsmI (rs1544410) polymorphism in the context of T2DM-related inflammation and oxidative stress.

Materials and methods

Study design

A case–control study was conducted. This study included cases diagnosed with T2DM and pre-diabetes who presented to the Department of Endocrinology, Faculty of Medicine, Tokat Gaziosmanpasa University between October 2023 and April 2024, based on routine analyses along with healthy control subjects. The study was conducted and reported following the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 24

An a priori power analysis was conducted using G*Power (version 3.1.9.7; Heinrich-Heine University Düsseldorf, Germany) for a one-way ANOVA (fixed effects, omnibus test). Assuming a medium effect size (f = 0.25), an alpha level of 0.05, and 90% power with three groups, a minimum total sample size of 207 participants was determined.

Our study consists of two phases. The first phase involves determining routine biochemical parameters for the cases and the VDR BsmI (rs1544410) gene polymorphism, and evaluating its relationship with DM. The second phase of the study involves separating the groups previously categorized by clinical course based on allele frequencies. These groups were then statistically evaluated in relation to raftlin, MDA, and SOD parameters.

Participants

The cases participating in the study were divided into three groups based on their clinical presentations: T2DM (n = 104), prediabetes (n = 85), and control (n = 111), with a total of 300 cases (Table 1).

Demographic and clinical data, including routine biochemistry and hemogram parameters, were obtained from patients’ medical records.

The control group comprised normoglycemia subjects (FPG < 100 mg/dL and HbA1c < 5.7%) with no documented history of diabetes or prediabetes who attended routine health evaluations during the same study period.

Participants in the prediabetes and control groups were selected to achieve comparable distributions of age, sex, and baseline metabolic characteristics to those of the T2DM group. All groups were recruited from the same outpatient clinic and within the same time frame to minimize selection bias. Demographic characteristics (age and sex) and baseline metabolic parameters were recorded for all participants and are summarized in Table 1 to assess comparability between groups.

Exclusion criteria: Individuals were excluded if they had T1DM, gestational diabetes, acute infection, or inflammatory disease at the time of sampling. In addition, individuals with chronic inflammatory or autoimmune disorders, chronic kidney or liver disease, and malignancy were excluded. Other exclusions included pregnancy, lactation, current use of systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy, and incomplete key clinical or laboratory data.

Biochemical analysis

Routine biochemical parameters were assessed to characterize the metabolic status of the participants and to support accurate clinical classification of the study groups. Considering that T2DM is associated with alterations in glucose homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and systemic metabolic regulation, these measurements were included to provide a comprehensive metabolic background and to control for potential confounding factors.

Blood samples were collected from patients between 08:00 and 10:00 in the morning after a minimum of 8 h of fasting. Venous blood samples were collected in tubes without anticoagulant and then centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 15 min at +4°C using Hettich Zentrifugen Rotanta 460R, Germany. Routine biochemical analysis, including Hb A1c %, glucose mg/dL, insulin uIU/mL, C-peptide ng/mL, triglycerides (TG) mg/dL, high-density lipoprotein (HDL) mg/dL, low-density lipoprotein (LDL) mg/dL, BUN mg/dL, aspartate transaminase (AST) U/L, alanine transaminase (ALT) U/L, folate ng/mL, B12 pg/mL, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) ng/dL, free T4 (FT4) ng/dL, folate ng/mL, vitamin B12 pg/mL, and vitamin D ng/mL, were performed in the clinical biochemistry laboratory of Tokat Gaziosmanpasa University Hospital using a ROCHE Cobas 6000/c501/e601 chemistry analyzer (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany). Plasma levels of raftlin pg/ml, MDA ng/ml, and SOD ng/ml were determined via quantitative sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) using a commercial kit (Human raftlin-1 kit Fine Test USA cat no: EH11785, Human MDA Fine Test USA cat no: EH4174, Human SOD Fine Test USA cat no: EH4706-1). All assays were performed following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Genotyping

Blood samples were collected from all participants into EDTA tubes to obtain genomic DNA, and the isolation process was performed using a DNA isolation kit (RTA Lot: 2403176, Kocaeli, TURKIYE) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. All isolated DNA samples were stored at −20°C until analysis. VDR gene BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) polymorphism genotyping was determined by polymerase chain reaction-based restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR-RFLP) assay. PCR amplification was performed using the following primer sequences: forward 5′-CAA CCA AGA CTA CAA GTA CCG CGT CAG TGA-3′; reverse 5′-AAC CAG CGG GAA GAG GTC AAG GG-3′. Appropriate PCR conditions for the primers were employed as follows: initial denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, denaturation at 94°C for 45 seconds, annealing at 60°C for 45 s, elongation at 72°C for 1 min, and final extension at 72°C for 5 min. The number of cycles was 40. PCR products (825bp) obtained after amplification were digested with the BsmI (Mva1269I) restriction enzyme. The restriction process was carried out by incubating the mixture containing 1.2 μl restriction buffer (10×), 1.5 μl BsmI (Mva1269I) enzyme (10 u/μl) (MBI Fermentas, Vilnius, Lithuania), 0.3 μl dH2O, and 5 μl PCR product at 37°C for 2 h. Following the restriction process, 2% agarose gel electrophoresis was applied to evaluate the VDR BsmI polymorphism. The digestion products obtained were as follows: BB 825 bp; Bb 825 bp, 650 bp and 175 bp; bb 650 bp and 175 bp (Figure 1). The genotypes obtained using Bsm I restriction endonuclease were accepted as B (A) (in the absence of the restriction site) or b (G) (in the presence of the restriction site). 20 RFLP was repeated three times to confirm the results.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20. The one-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of continuous variables. Data conforming to a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normally distributed data were expressed as median and interquartile ranges. Two-group comparisons were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test as the data were non-normally distributed. When comparing continuous variables between multiple groups, the one-way ANOVA test was applied when data followed a normal distribution, and the Kruskal–Wallis test was used for skewed data. The results were considered significant at p < 0.05.

SPSS 16.0, WinPepi (Version 11.65), and OpenEpi (Version 3.01) statistical software were used to analyze allele and genotype frequencies. Genotype and allele frequencies in the patient and control groups were determined using the chi-square (χ2) test. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Values of p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Spearman correlation was used to analyze the correlations between the study parameters, including VDR mutations, where a correlation coefficient of r = 0.70–0.89 was considered strong, r = 0.40–0.69 was moderate, and r = 0.10–0.39 was weak. 25 Given the non-normal distribution of the biochemical parameters, generalized linear models (GLMs) were employed to evaluate the predictive effects of the VDR BsmI polymorphism and HbA1c on the study parameters. To assess the diagnostic performance of MDA and raftlin in patients with T2DM, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was applied. The results were considered significant at p < 0.05.

Results

Biochemical analysis results

The characteristics of all cases are summarized in Table 1. The study included a total of 300 cases aged between 29 and 45 years: 104 cases diagnosed with T2DM (59 women and 45 men), 85 cases diagnosed with prediabetes (46 women and 39 men), and 111 healthy controls (61 women and 50 men) based on routine analysis results. According to the results obtained, there were significant differences between the T2DM, prediabetes, and control groups in terms of Hb A1c %, glucose mg/dL, insulin uIU/mL, C-peptide ng/mL, TG mg/dL, LDL mg/dL, BUN mg/dL, AST U/L, TSH ng/dL, and FT4 ng/dL parameters (p < 0.05).

According to our results, while MDA levels were significantly higher in T2DM and prediabetes cases compared to the control group (p = 0.019), raftlin levels were significantly lower in these groups compared to healthy controls (p = 0.002). No statistically significant difference was observed in SOD levels among the study groups (p = 0.163; Table 2).

Gene polymorphism results

The genotyping of the VDR gene BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) polymorphism among the T2DM, prediabetes, and control groups using the PCR-RFLP method (Figure 1) is presented in Table 3. Examining the allele and genotype distributions of the VDR gene BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) polymorphism revealed no significant differences between the combined group of T2DM and prediabetes patients and the control group (p = 0.97 and p = 0.58, respectively). Similarly, the distribution of BB + Bb versus bb genotypes did not show any significant difference in prediabetes patients (OR: 1.4; 95% CI: 0.67–2.85; p = 0.3) and T2DM (OR: 1.01; 95% CI: 0.49–2.07; p = 0.8). In addition, the distribution of BB versus Bb + bb revealed no significant difference in either prediabetes (OR: 1; 95% CI: 0.54–1.85; p = 0.9) or T2DM patients (OR: 0.9; 95% CI: 0.51–1.61; p = 0.7). In the T2DM group, the frequency of B genotypes was 58.2%, while the frequency of b genotypes was 41.8% (p = 0.84). In the prediabetes group, the frequency of B genotypes was 54.7%, while the frequency of b genotypes was 45.3% (p = 0.64; Table 3).

Association of VDR BsmI (rs1544410) gene polymorphism with raftlin, MDA, and SOD results

Following genotyping, serum raftlin, MDA, and SOD levels were measured using ELISA in 104 patients with T2DM (BB n = 35, Bb n = 57, bb n = 19), 85 individuals with prediabetes (BB n = 27, Bb n = 39, bb n = 19), and 111 controls (BB n = 35, Bb n = 51, bb n = 18). Within the T2DM cohort, pairwise comparisons were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test. Significant genotype-specific differences were observed for MDA, HbA1c, and raftlin between individuals carrying the BB and bb genotypes. Specifically, the bb genotype carriers exhibited a significant elevation in MDA and HbA1c levels compared to the BB group (p = 0.001 and p = 0.014, respectively). The increase in the concentrations of MDA and HbA1c in the bb carriers was accompanied by a significant downregulation of raftlin relative to the carriers of the BB genotype (p < 0.001). Regarding SOD, serum levels were lower in the bb cohort compared to the BB group; however, this did not achieve a statistically significant difference between the two cohorts (p = 0.220; Table 4; Figure 2). Further comparison between the Bb and bb genotypes within the T2DM cohort revealed a significant increase in MDA levels among the individuals carrying the bb genotype compared to the Bb group (p = 0.041), whereas raftlin was significantly lower in the bb group relative to the Bb group (p = 0.001; Table 5; Figure 2). By contrast, SOD levels did not differ statistically between these two groups.

For further investigation of the association between VDR polymorphism and the main study parameters (MDA, SOD, and raftlin), GLMs were employed for MDA, SOD, and raftlin. To control the models for potential confounding factors, the models were adjusted for age, gender, BMI, and HbA1c. The MDA model demonstrated statistical significance (χ2 = 20.966, p = 0.001). Moreover, VDR polymorphism was significantly associated with MDA (χ2 = 22.921, p < 0.001), where patients carrying the BB genotype demonstrated lower MDA concentrations compared to the bb group (B = −1.386, p < 0.001). Similarly, the raftlin’s model was statistically significant (χ2 = 14.747, p = 0.011). After adjusting for age, HbA1c, gender, and BMI, the VDR polymorphism was the sole significant independent predictor of raftlin levels. The BB group showed significantly higher raftlin levels compared to the bb group (B = 1.270, p = 0.004). On the contrary, the SOD model did not reach statistical significance (χ2 = 4.695, p = 0.454; Figure 3).

HbA1c, glycosylated hemoglobin; MDA, malondialdehyde; SOD, superoxide dismutase; VDR, vitamin D receptor.

Diagnostic value of raftlin and MDA

Finally, ROC curve analysis was performed to determine the diagnostic values of raftlin and MDA based on genotype in T2DM patients. Accordingly, MDA demonstrated an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.804 (95% CI: 0.655–0.953, p = 0.002). At a cut-off value of 28 ng/ml, the specificity and sensitivity were 86.4% and 68.8%, respectively, among individuals with the BB and bb genotypes in T2DM patients. On the other hand, raftlin showed an AUC of 0.839 (95% CI: 0.711–0.967, p = 0.001). At a cut-off value of 130 ng/ml, raftlin achieved a specificity of 69.2%, and sensitivity of 81.5% (Figure 4).

Discussion

DM, whose incidence is increasing worldwide, represents a significant public health problem. Identifying all agents that may play a role in its pathogenesis will be an important step in combating the disease. In this study, the correlation between the VDR gene BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) polymorphism and raftlin, a marker of inflammation, and MDA and SOD, markers of oxidative stress, was evaluated for the first time among T2DM, prediabetes individuals, and healthy controls.

Consistent measurements are critical for assessing risk factors for T2DM. DM is generally diagnosed by assessing HbA1c levels >6.5% (>47 mmol/mol), which is generally used to assess three-month plasma glucose levels, random plasma glucose levels (>200 mg/dL) in individuals with DM symptoms, OGTT 2-h post-test >200 mg/dL or FPG > 126 mg/dL on at least two separate days after an overnight fast of at least 8 h. 26 Numerous studies have shown a relationship between blood glucose levels and other biochemical parameters. Marchesiri et al. reported elevated liver function tests (AST and ALT) in individuals with insulin resistance. 27 Atherogenic dyslipidemia, resulting from elevated TG and LDL cholesterol levels and low HDL cholesterol levels, is present at the time of diagnosis or even in the prediabetes period. This explains the high risk of coronary artery disease in DM patients. 28 In addition, there are studies demonstrating a close link between thyroid disease and diabetes. These studies have shown that elevated glucose levels may trigger thyroid hormone dysfunction, and conversely, thyroid dysfunction can affect blood glucose levels. 29 While the relationship between BUN levels and diabetes remains unclear, according to various studies, BUN levels are often elevated in DM patients with diabetic nephropathy (DN), suggesting that BUN may be an important indicator of impaired renal function and glucose homeostasis. 30 Consistent with the literature, in the current investigation, a statistically significant difference between the T2DM, prediabetes and control groups was observed in terms of Hb A1c%, glucose mg/dL, insulin uIU/mL, C-peptide ng/mL, TG mg/dl, LDL mg/dl, BUN mg/dL, AST U/L, TSH ng/dl, and FT4ng/dl parameters (p < 0.05; Table 1).

T2DM is closely associated with pancreatic β-cell dysfunction, insulin resistance, and inflammation. 31 The immune system, stimulated by chronic inflammation caused by various diseases such as diabetes, activates macrophages that can produce pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and ROS. Therefore, an increase in oxidative stress is inevitable in inflammatory conditions. 32 Lipid rafts located on the cell surface contain many proteins, such as caveolin-1, that regulate the inflammatory response. 33 A study has shown that hyperglycemia affects the expression or phosphorylation of caveolin-1. 34 Among the parameters investigated in our study, raftlin, which is considered a promising novel biomarker for inflammation, is a major protein of lipid rafts. It regulates B-cell antigen receptor (BCR) signaling and functions in autoimmune and vascular inflammatory responses. 35 In a study by Mesen et al., the serum raftlin levels in patients with diabetic retinopathy were found to be negatively correlated with glucose levels. 36 In our study, the relationship between the raftlin protein, MDA, and SOD was determined according to the clinical course of diabetes (Table 2). Thus, beyond the usability of the raftlin protein as a biomarker for DM, an attempt was made to contribute a new perspective to the literature, for the first time, in terms of understanding its effects on the pathogenesis of DM by evaluating its association with the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism. In line with the literature, our results indicated that MDA levels were significantly elevated in T2DM cases (p = 0.019), whereas raftlin protein levels were significantly reduced among individuals of the group (p = 0.002; Table 2). The observed decrease in raftlin levels in T2DM may be associated with oxidative stress-induced disruption of lipid raft microdomains secondary to chronic hyperglycemia. As lipid rafts serve as essential platforms for insulin receptor clustering and downstream signaling, reduced raftlin expression may reflect structural alterations contributing to impaired insulin signaling efficiency. Increased lipid peroxidation and membrane reorganization may compromise raftlin stabilization, thereby decreasing protein levels. Furthermore, chronic low-grade inflammation and immune regulatory alterations potentially related to vitamin D deficiency may also contribute to suppressed raftlin expression. Collectively, these findings suggest that raftlin may not merely represent an inflammatory marker but could also reflect membrane-associated mechanisms involved in insulin signaling dysregulation in T2DM.

Studies have reported that the global prevalence of DM is approximately 6.1%, 37 while in Turkiye, its prevalence among individuals over the age of 20 has been reported to be 13.7%. 38 Factors affecting the incidence of T2DM include both environmental and genetic factors. 39 Vitamin D has been widely studied for its effects on T2DM, a chronic condition characterized by insulin resistance, inflammation, and metabolic dysfunction. The active form of vitamin D, 1,25-OH (2D) D3, which interacts with VDR, plays a role in multiple metabolic processes, such as affecting pro-inflammatory cytokines, activating the AMPK signaling pathway, inhibiting mTOR signaling, and promoting adipocyte differentiation. These actions enhance insulin sensitivity and reduce chronic inflammation, which plays a key role in metabolic dysfunction. 40 Vitamin D deficiency, a condition frequently observed today, may result from environmental factors or SNPs in the VDR gene. 41 Several studies have evaluated the relationship between mutations in the VDR gene (FokI, ApaI, BsmI, and TaqI) and T2DM. According to a meta-analysis conducted by Li et al., no significant relationship was observed between the BsmI, ApaI, and TaqI variants and the risk of T2DM; however, findings suggest that the FokI polymorphism in the VDR gene may be a risk factor for T2DM, particularly in Asian populations. 42 In a study evaluating the relationship between the VDR FokI, ApaI, BsmI, and TaqI variants and T2DM in the Polish population, it was stated that these four polymorphisms had no association with T2DM. 43 In the present study, no statistically significant difference was found between the BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism and T2DM, prediabetes, and healthy individuals (p > 0.05; Figure 1; Table 3). These findings, consistent with the literature, suggest that the susceptibility to SNPs may vary among individuals of different ethnic groups. Emerging bioinformatics and systems biology evidence suggests that VDR genetic variability may influence oxidative stress–related signaling networks and inflammatory responses. Omics-based analyses have demonstrated that redox imbalance contributes to diabetic complication pathways through complex gene regulatory interactions, 44 while network-level studies indicate that vitamin D–VDR signaling modulates immune and metabolic stress responses. 45 In addition, in-silico functional analyses of deleterious VDR variants support the concept that genotype-dependent alterations in receptor activity may influence downstream inflammatory signaling. 46 These mechanisms may partly explain the genotype-specific differences observed in raftlin expression and oxidative stress markers in the present study.

In our study, we also assessed HbA1c, a key biomarker of blood glucose levels, as well as raftlin, MDA, and SOD levels in T2DM individuals with the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism. According to our findings, comparison between bb and BB genotype groups showed that HbA1c and MDA levels were significantly higher in cases with the bb genotype (p = 0.014 and p = 0.001, respectively), while raftlin levels were significantly lower in the same cohort (p < 0.001; Table 4). Furthermore, we observed a weak positive correlation between the bb genotype and HbA1c (p = 0.039, r = 0.228; Table 6). In addition, GLM analysis revealed an independent association between the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism and MDA, SOD, and raftlin. Even after adjusting the models for age, gender, BMI, and HbA1c, the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism remained the only significant driver of serum MDA and raftlin levels. However, no significant association was observed between SOD and the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism among the T2DM patients. A robust association was observed between MDA and VDR polymorphism, where bb genotype carriers demonstrated significantly elevated MDA levels compared to BB and Bb genotype carriers. The absence of significant correlation between HbA1c and MDA suggests that the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism may overshadow glycemic control in determining the extent of lipid peroxidation among T2DM patients. By contrast, the SOD model did not show any significant association with the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism, and none of the covariates could significantly predict SOD levels in the adjusted model. The conflict among oxidative stress markers, MDA and SOD, may be attributed to their different roles, as MDA represents cumulative membrane damage while SOD is an antioxidant enzymatic defense. Also, a limited sample size may contribute to this dissociation. Regarding raftlin, the adjusted GLM provides a novel prospective as it revealed a significant association with the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism within the T2DM cohort, regardless the demographic properties and the glycemic fluctuations of the patients. The bb genotype exhibited significant lower raftlin levels relative to the BB and Bb cohorts. Demographic and glycemic covariates did not significantly affect the results of the raftlin’s model suggesting that in T2DM, raftlin expression may be regulated by VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) gene polymorphism rather than the transient glycemic shifts. Zhang et al.’s study reported an association between DN and the VDR BsmI (rs1544410 A>G) polymorphism. 47 However, a study conducted in 2015 found that this polymorphism was not statistically significant between cases with and without DN. 48 These conflicting results suggest that the clinical manifestations of diabetes may be influenced by more than one gene, different ethnic backgrounds, and the heterogeneity in vitamin D deficiency cases.

Finally, we performed a ROC curve analysis to evaluate the biomarker properties of raftlin and MDA in T2DM patients with the bb genotype. The ROC curve analysis for MDA showed that at a cut-off value of 28.5 ng/ml, the sensitivity was 68.8%, the specificity was 86.4%, the AUC was 0.804, and the confidence interval was between 0.655 and 0.953. In the ROC curve analysis for raftlin, at a cut-off value of 130 ng/ml, the sensitivity was 69.2%, the specificity was 81.5%, the AUC was 0.839, and the confidence interval was between 0.711 and 0.967 (Figure 4). The biomarker potential of raftlin has been demonstrated in studies of certain inflammation-related diseases. In their study, Hursitoglu et al. demonstrated that raftlin is a good diagnostic marker in individuals with major depressive disorder (MDD) associated with oxidative stress and inflammation. 13 Furthermore, Bilgen et al. reported that raftlin, MDA, and SOD have excellent diagnostic performance in wound healing. 49 Evidently, as supported by our findings, raftlin emerges as a potential diagnostic or prognostic marker in diseases associated with inflammation and oxidative stress. Although raftlin is not currently included among established biomarkers used in T2DM management, our findings suggest that genotype-specific alterations in raftlin levels may reflect underlying inflammatory and oxidative stress-related metabolic disturbances. Particularly in individuals carrying the bb genotype, reduced raftlin levels were associated with poorer glycemic control and increased oxidative stress markers. From a clinical perspective, incorporating biomarkers that integrate genetic background with inflammatory and metabolic status may enhance early risk stratification, especially during the transition from prediabetes to overt T2DM. Nevertheless, further longitudinal studies are required to determine whether raftlin provides incremental predictive value beyond conventional markers such as HbA1c and fasting glucose.

The study has certain limitations

VDR gene polymorphisms may differ in their clinical manifestations across different ethnic groups. The fact that the study was conducted only in the province of Tokat, Turkiye, constitutes a limitation.

Conclusion

The pathogenesis of diabetes is affected by a complex interplay between numerous environmental and genetic factors. While SNPs are not directly employed as biomarkers of the disease, they can be considered an important guide in determining predisposition. The results of our study show that oxidative stress and inflammation are higher in T2DM patients, and that HbA1c, oxidative stress, and inflammation are further elevated in T2DM patients with the bb genotype. Notably, this study is the first to demonstrate that raftlin shows good diagnostic performance in T2DM patients with the bb genotype. We believe that our results provide support for elucidating the pathogenesis of DM, a multifactorial disease. Nonetheless, further large-scale studies involving diverse populations and multiple genetic and biochemical markers are essential to validate these findings and to fully elucidate the underlying mechanisms of the disease.

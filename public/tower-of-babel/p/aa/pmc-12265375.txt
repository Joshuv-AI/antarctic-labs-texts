The role of Hippo/YAP1 in cancer-associated fibroblasts: Literature review and future perspectives
Authors: Dipti Athavale, Curt Balch, Yanting Zhang, Xiaodan Yao, Shumei Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are activated fibroblasts that play a role in numerous malignant phenotypes, including hyperproliferation, invasion, and metastasis. These phenotypes correlate with activity of the Hippo pathway oncoprotein, Yes-associated protein-1 (YAP1), and its paralog, transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ). YAP1/TAZ are normally involved in organ growth, under the regulation of various kinases and upon phosphorylation, are retained in the cytoplasm by chaperone proteins, leading to their proteasomal degradation. In CAFs and tumor cells, however, a lack of YAP1 phosphorylation results in its translocation to the nucleus, binding to TEAD transcription factors, and activation of mitogenic pathways. In this review we summarize the literature discussing the central role of YAP1 in CAF activation, the upstream cues that promote YAP1-mediated CAF activation and extracellular matrix remodeling, and how CAFs mediate tumor-stroma crosstalk to support progression, invasion and metastasis in various cancer models. We further highlight YAP1+CAFs functions in modulating an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and propose evaluation of several YAP1 targets regarding their role in regulating intra-tumoral immune landscapes. Finally, we propose that co-administration of YAP1- targeted therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors can improve therapeutic outcomes in patients with advanced tumors.

Introduction

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), prominent components of the tumor stroma, participate in tumor growth, metabolism, metastasis, and drug resistance [1–3]. CAFs are activated, hyperproliferative fibroblasts that express alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and secrete high levels of fibrillar collagen, fibronectin, laminin, tenascin C, periostin, and matrix metalloproteases, resulting in increased deposition (“desmoplasia”) of extracellular matrix (ECM) [4,5]. CAFs possess more actin stress fibers, α-SMA, and focal adhesions than non-activated fibroblasts. Increased collagen deposition and crosslinking contribute to ECM stiffness, a physical trait of cancer [6]. Tissue-residing quiescent fibroblasts, tissue-specific stellate cells, differentiated mesenchymal stem cells, or transdifferentiating endothelial cells/adipocytes, can all give rise to CAF populations [7]. Based on single-cell RNA transcriptional profiling, several CAF subpopulations have been identified, including myofibroblastic CAFs (myCAFs), inflammatory CAFs (iCAFs), metabolic CAFs (meCAFs) and antigen-presenting CAFs (apCAFs); these subsets, however, vary with cancer type and stage [7–9]. Regulatory molecules from signaling pathways such as transforming growth factor-β (TGFβ), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), epidermal/fibroblast growth factor (EGF/FGF), and Hippo-YAP can all facilitate CAF activation, as can other factors, such as reactive oxygen species and cancer cell derived-extracellular vesicles (EVs) [1,7,10]. Moreover, a CAF-associated gene signature is correlated with worse prognosis and poor treatment outcomes, in several cancer types [11–15].

Hippo signaling is an evolutionary conserved and constitutively active cascade that regulates biological processes such as cell fate, proliferation, survival, organ size, and tissue homeostasis [16,17]. The Hippo upstream regulator, neurofibromin 2 (NF2, also known as Merlin) serves as a scaffold for serine/threonine kinases mammalian sterile 20-like kinases 1 and 2 (MST1 and MST2), which then phosphorylate the large tumor suppressor 1/2 kinase (LATS1/2) [18]. Adapter proteins, including Salvador homolog 1 (SAV1) and MOB kinase activator 1A/B (MOB1A/B), aid in a phosphorylation cascade of Hippo kinases, ultimately leading to phosphorylation and deactivation of the downstream effector proteins Yes-associated protein-1 (YAP1) and its paralog, transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ) [16,19]. Phosphorylation of YAP1/TAZ increases its binding to the scaffolding protein 14-3-3 and cytosolic sequestration, followed by degradation via ubiq-uitination. In contrast, unphosphorylated YAP1/TAZ translocates to the nucleus, bind TEA domain family transcription factors 1–4 (TEADs 1–4), and regulate downstream target genes involved in cell/tissue growth [20]. YAP1 and TAZ along with their transcriptional binding partners, TEAD 1-4, are promising anticancer targets and various novel inhibitors abrogating the protein-protein interaction between YAP/TAZ-TEAD were reported [21,22]. These inhibitors successfully induced cancer cell death, reducing the tumor size therefore hold potential for clinical trials [23,24]. Moreover, blockade of YAP-TEAD up regulates major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC class I)-driven tumor antigen processing and presentation (APP) enhancing cytotoxic T-cell mediated tumor cell killing [25]. In contrast, YAP expression, nuclear localization, and activity is essential for activation of wound healing response in normal fibroblasts [26]. In that regard, pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases 4 promotes diabetic wound healing in senescent fibroblasts, via inducing nuclear YAP1 and c-Jun N-terminal kinase dephosphorylation [26].

The functional role of Hippo/YAP1 in epithelial tumor cells is well studied in a variety of cancers [27–30]; however, the role and mechanisms of YAP1 mediated CAF activation in tumor metastasis and immune modulations are less explored. In this review, we highlight relevant recent literature insights regarding the role of the Hippo-YAP1 pathway in CAFs. We first reaffirm the central functions of YAP1 in driving CAF activation, thereby modulating various downstream effects such as ECM remodeling and metabolic reprogramming. Secondly, we describe upstream regulators/pathways involved in YAP-driven CAF activation. Finally, we discuss literature illustrating YAP1’s role in regulating tumor and stroma crosstalk along with recent studies implying the role of YAP+CAFs in immunosuppression in the tumor milieu.

YAP1 is central to CAF activation

Biochemically, YAP1 nuclear entry is regulated by phosphorylation while at the mechanical level, extracellular matrix (ECM) rigidity regulates YAP1 nuclear import in mouse embryonic fibroblasts, increasingYAP1 nuclear-to-cytosolic ratio [31]. Stiffness of the ECM is believed to be transmitted to the nucleus, via focal adhesions and the cytoskeleton, flattening the nucleus and “stretching” nuclear pores to an open position thus increasing YAP1 nuclear import [31]. Another key player, the RhoA GTPase senses the ECM stiffness via focal adhesions leading to actin polymerization and stress fiber formation. This activates FAK-Src signals inhibiting LATS1/2 mediated YAP1 phosphorylation thus facilitating its nuclear transport [32,33].

Biophysical forces such as tissue stiffness or shear stress are sensed at the cell surface and converted to intracellular biochemical signals leading to activation of a transcriptional program, a process termed as mechanotransduction [32]. YAP and TAZ are involved in mechno-transmission as they relay cytoskeletal tension to the nucleus activating genes involved in cell growth and differentiation [32]. CAFs in neoplastic stroma are also involved in mechano-transduction bysynthesizing ECM proteins and inducing stromal remodeling, thus contributing to an aberrant tumor microenvironment (TME) [34]. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), performed on fibroblasts isolated from different breast cancer stages, revealed that CAF gene expression significantly associated with the YAP1/TAZ pathway [35]. While YAP1 was predominantly nuclear in CAFs, it remained cytosolic in normal fibroblasts (NFs). Interestingly, overexpression of constitutively active YAP1, or ablation of its upstream MST1/2 kinases in NFs, increased gel contraction and promoted cancer cell invasion [19]. YAP1-driven expression of Anillin (ANLN), Diaphanous-Related Formin 3 (DIAPH3), and filamin A (FLNA), and regulation of MYosin Light chain 9 (MYL9), are critical for ECM remodeling by CAFs. Thus, YAP1-activated CAFs enhance cell tension by increasing the activity of MYL9 to stiffen the matrix. Conversely, matrix stiffening, via actin cytoskeleton and Src kinase activate YAP1 in fibroblasts, creating a feed forward loop to further activate fibroblasts mechanically [35]. In contrast, YAP1 ablation in fibroblasts decreases their ability to form focal adhesions and thick collagen fibers within matrices [35].

YAP1 is not only involved in CAF activation but also regulates metabolic reprogramming, tumor cell proliferation and invasion. ECM stiffness promotes YAP1 translocation to the nucleus, where it activates genes involved in amino acid synthesis (Glutaminase; GLS1) and transport (Solute Carrier Family 1 Member 3; SLC1A3), in carcinoma cells and in CAFs. These YAP1 target genes then support aspartate/glutamate exchange in the TME, to regulate tumor growth and aggressiveness [36].

Upstream molecular regulators of YAP1 in CAFs

Curiously, regarding YAP1 upstream regulation in fibroblasts isolated from different stages of breast cancer, activity of canonical negative YAP1 regulators (e.g., MST1/2 and LATS1/2) did not differ between NFs and CAFs [35]. Moreover, elevated LATS kinase activity and YAP serine 127 phosphorylation in CAFs indicated that reduced activity of negative YAP regulators might not necessarily increase YAP activation. While YAP1 interacted more with 14-3-3 in NFs, in CAFs, despite increased YAP1 phosphorylation, its binding to TEAD1 and TEAD4 increased, promoting transcription of YAP1 target genes (CTGF; connective tissue growth factor, AMOTL2; Angiomotin Like 2, ANKRD1; Ankyrin Repeat Domain 1) [35]. Of these, CTGF is intimately involved in angiogenesis and propagation of the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), in addition to activation of CAFs [37]. Also notably, the acidic pH of extracellular fluid promoted differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells to CAFs, via GPR68 (a proton-sensing G-protein-coupled receptor; GPCR), and the YAP1 axis [38].

In the pancreatic cancer (PC) tumor microenvironment (TME), tumor cell YAP1 activated quiescent pancreatic stellate cells (PSCs) to a myofibroblast-like phenotype, in a paracrine manner, promoting tumor growth, cell invasion, and metastasis [39]. Conversely, mouse xenografts, developed by co-injecting YAP1-null PCs and YAP1-wild type PSCs showed reduced desmoplasia, as measured by decreased α-SMA expression and tumor growth [39]. Interestingly, treatment of PSCs with supernatant derived from YAP1-depleted PCs reduced intra-and extracellular TGF-β1 expression [39], suggesting regulation by soluble paracrine factor(s), while ezrin-containing pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)-derived exosomes drove fibroblast activation, via YAP1 exacerbating metastasis [40].

Myeloid cell leukemia 1 (MCL1), a regulator of mitochondrial integrity highly expressed in human-breast CAFs, governs transcriptional expression of genes involved in fibroblast contractility and actomyosin organization (MYH10; myosin heavy chain 10, MYH2; myosin heavy chain 2, ACTA2; Actin Alpha 2, Smooth Muscle and ITGA11; Integrin Subunit Alpha 11), most likely via YAP1 nuclear translocation [41]. By contrast, overexpression of EMI domain-containing protein 2 (EMID2) reduced α-SMA expression in primary skin fibroblasts and shifted enrichment of the ECM from collagen/fibronectin to laminin and abrogating stress fibers. These ECM changes including cytoskeletal actin organization and reduced CAF activation were associated with nuclear exclusion of YAP1 and inhibition of TGFβ maturation [42]. In another study, co-culturing of ovarian squamous carcinoma cells (OSCCs) with immortalized NFs led to the development of a myofibroblast-like phenotype, an activated RhoA-YAP1 signaling axis, and increased gel contractility and matrix stiffness, modulating OSCC invasiveness [43,44]. Contrarily, ovarian cancer cell derived-microRNA-141 targets YAP1 and reduced its nuclear translocation in stromal cells, leading to elevated expression of a proinflammatory chemokine GROα/CXCL1 increasing proliferation and aggressiveness of ovarian cancer cells. Further, stromal cell-specific knockdown of YAP1 led to GROα-enriched TME facilitating tumor growth and metastasis [44]. Against current consensus, this would suggest that YAP1 depletion (versus enrichment) in CAFs is associated with tumor proliferation and dissemination [44].

YAP1/TAZ is known to interact with numerous other signaling pathways. For example, in crosstalk with Wnt signaling, ablation of β-catenin in human stromal fibroblasts reduced expression of various ECM proteins (F-actin, fibronectin, paxillin and vimentin), activation markers (α-SMA), and ECM remodeling ability. It is known that β-catenin physically interacts with YAP1, controlling its nuclear translocation and fibroblast activation in a melanoma model [45]. During hyperglycemia-induced expression of YAP1/TAZ, CAF activation markers (FAP; Fibroblast Activation Protein Alpha, fibronectin, COL1A1; Collagen Type I Alpha 1 Chain, and COL11A1; Collagen Type XI Alpha 1 Chain) in CAF15 cells (a human PDAC CAF cell line) promoted collagen deposition in vivo, while a YAP1/TAZ inhibitor (K975) abrogated these effects [46]. Paradoxically, knockdown of the Wnt inhibitor Dickkopf-3 (DKK3) in CAFs, reduced YAP1/TAZ nuclear localization and transcriptional activity, leading to myosin light chain 2 (MYL2) and Src inactivation, and decreased actomyosin contractibility [47]. Role of Hippo-YAP1 pathway in mediating crosstalk between CAFs and cancer cells

To delineate YAP1 mediated CAF-to-tumor cell crosstalk, YAP1 was knocked down in an immortalized murine CAF cell line. This reduced expression of matrix-remodeling proteins such as MYL9, paxillin, F-actin, and α-SMA, via downregulation of Src kinase activity [21]. Conversely, xenografts generated by co-injecting YAP-overexpressing NFs and prostate cancer cells showed increased Src expression and accelerated tumor growth [48]. Interestingly, the LncRNA, WEE2-AS1, is highly expressed in CAF-secreted small extracellular vesicles (sEV). When such sEVs are internalized by colorectal cancer cells, WEE2-AS1 acts as a scaffold to promote degradation of the Hippo pathway adapter protein MOB1A, further decreasing phosphorylation of LATS1 and YAP1, increasing nuclear YAP1 levels to induce cell proliferation [49]. Likewise, CAF-derived annexin 6-containing EVs conferred drug resistance to gastric cancer cells, via activating a β1 integrin-focal adhesion kinase (FAK)-YAP1 axis [50]. Similarly, CAF-derived insulin-like growth factor-2 (IGF2) interacted with its IGF1 receptor (IGF1R), expressed on cancer cells, to activate YAP1 nuclear translocation and transcription of target genes, in a colorectal cancer (CRC) model [51].

The tumor stroma can also restrict cancer growth. For example, cancer cells sense mechanical compression exerted by excessive ECM deposition around tumor tissue [52]. Compression induced by a solid barrier caused export of nuclear YAP1 and reduced cancer cell proliferation [52,53]. Several studies have elucidated paracrine regulation of tumor cell YAP1 via CAF secretory factors. CAFs produce sEVs containing lysyl oxidase (LOX), which converts collagen fibrils to stable collagen fibers that crosslink the ECM. The cross-linked matrix then phosphorylates FAK and paxillin, which either directly or indirectly (via Rho/ROCK) regulate actomyosin contraction, leading to YAP1 nuclear translocation and the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), in oral squamous cell carcinoma cells [54]. Thus, the cumulative effect is two-way interactions between CAFs, the ECM, and Hippo/YAP1 in driving tumorigenesis. Stromal fibroblast derived-ECM protein, periostin, induced YAP1 nuclear translocation in a murine colorectal cancer model, via activating integrin-FAK-Src pathway [55]. Moreover, in human colon cancer tissues CAF-derived periostin was increased and positively correlated with YAP1 levels, translational evidence for tumor-stroma crosstalk. Similarly, podoplanin (PDPN)-positive CAFs induced periostin secretion via PI3K/AK signaling, activating FAK/YAP1 in gastric cancer cells [56]. Finally, fibroblasts expressing prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2) produced prostaglandin E2 (PGE2). PGE2 supported tumor initiation by promoting YAP1 dephosphorylation, its nuclear translocation, and activation of a YAP1-TEAD transcriptional program, via prostaglandin E receptor 4 (PTGER 4) [57]. Another study illustrated the role of YAP1/TAZ-activated fibroblasts in wound healing and cancer initiation, where YAP1/TAZ activation in intestinal smooth muscle cells (ISMCs) is essential for their transformation to the fibroblasts participating in wound healing and promoting azoxymethane/Dextran Sodium Sulphate (AOM/DSS)-induced colitis-associated cancer in young mice [58]. Modulation of an immunosuppressive TME

Very few experimental studies have assessed the specific role of YAP1 in CAFs and TME immune regulation. In one study, exosomes purified from Epstein-Barr virus-infected nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) cells activated YAP1 and increased production of the immunosuppressive cytokines interleukin-8 (IL8), C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2), and interleukin-6 (IL6) in CAFs [59]. Additionally, YAP1 showed significant negative correlation with the density of CD8a+ T lymphocytes having a granzyme B (GZMB) signature in NPC tissues, and inhibition of YAP1 activation decreased immunosuppressive cytokine release [59].

COL11A1, specifically expressed by CAFs, has emerged as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in lung adenocarcinoma, positively correlating with immune suppression and T-cell exhaustion markers such as programmed-death ligand 1 (PD-L1), Cytotoxic T-Lymphocyte Associated Protein 4 (CTLA4), and T cell immunoglobulin mucin domain-containing protein 3 (TIM3) [60]. YAP1 overexpression in hepatocytes induces transcription of another collagen family member, COL1A1 along with fibrosis [61,62]. Conversely, ablation of type I collagen in α-SMA+ myCAFs facilitated recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and decreased T/B cell content, accelerating pancreatic cancer progression. This would indicate that α-SMA+ myCAF-derived COL1A1 regulates tumor immunity and restricts tumor growth [63].Therefore, comprehensive analysis is required to understand expression patterns of various collagen family members in YAP1+CAFs, and their downstream role in modulating immune cell recruitment and functions. CAFs contribute to a complex immune landscape within tumors, affecting PD-L1 expression and influencing therapeutic outcomes [64]. Interestingly, YAP1 has been reported to regulate PD-L1 expression at the transcriptional level, in human lung cancer cell lines [65]. These observations encourage additional functional studies to discover YAP1-driven expression of PD-L1 in CAFs, and its effect on response to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), which block PD-1 and PD-L1 interaction. Additionally, YAP+-CAFs-derived secretory factors can modulate clinical response of tumors to ICIs. For example TGF-β signaling associated with CD8+T cell exclusion from the tumor parenchyma and enrichment in peritumoral stroma. However, the combination of TGF-β blockade with an ICI aided CD8+ T cell penetration into the center of the tumor parenchyma, eliciting strong anti-tumor immune response and tumor regression in a mouse model of metastatic urothelial cancer [66]. Likewise, it has been proposed that YAP1 inhibition can transform immunologically “cold” into “hot” tumors, sensitizing these to immune checkpoint inhibitors, as indicated in liver [67] and oral squamous cell cancer models [68]. Taken together, this evidence suggests that YAP1 activation in CAFs can be a major determinant to clinical outcome of ICI treatment, and co-targeting YAP1 signaling can aid in anti-PD-L1 treatment. Future directions

To date, a number of CAF biomarkers have been reported, including α-SMA, FAP, fibroblast-specific protein 1 (FSP-1 or S100A4), CTGF, and platelet-derived growth factor receptor α/β [7]. However, although recent single cell RNA sequencing studies have identified a variety of CAF subpopulations expressing different biomarkers [7,69], a YAP1 activation gene signature remains elusive across heterogenous CAF subsets and needs to be explored. Further, detailed experiments are warranted to evaluate CAF-secreted cytokines induced by YAP1 activation, and their impact on driving an immunosuppressive TME. Moreover, the mechanical and biological mechanisms of YAP1 activation in TME fibroblasts remain to be dissected. For instance, can ECM stiffening propagate YAP1 activation to nearby normal fibroblasts? What are the biological signaling cues (in terms of coactivators or scaffolding proteins) that mediate YAP1-TEAD transcriptional gene regulation in CAFs? What are the downstream molecular effectors of YAP1 activation in CAFs? Most reports described in this review highlight the role of CAF-derived factors affectingYAP1 nuclear localization and its activity in tumor cells. Nonetheless, precise function of YAP1-driven tumors in regulating CAF activation and CAF secretory profiles remains unknown. Additionally, single-cell RNA sequencing studies of CAF subsets, present in YAP1-active tumors, will not only facilitate development of rationally designed therapeutic approaches, but also indicate disease prognosis. YAP1/TAZ expression holds potential for informing clinical status. For example, nuclear YAP1 expression was higher in patients’ stromal cells from prostate tumors of a malignant tumor grade with lymph node and seminal vesicle metastasis [48]; such correlation needs to be explored in the future. Finally, combining YAP-targeted therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors holds promise towards improving patient outcomes in a number of cancers.

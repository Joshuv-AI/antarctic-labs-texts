Joint probabilistic modeling approach for harmonic and three-phase unbalanced disturbance sources
Authors: Zheng Ren, Xiao-Lu Chen, Chun Wang, Yue Lan, Xu Feng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHarmonics and three-phase imbalance are two common disturbances in power systems that interact with and affect each other, leading to more complex impacts on power system performance. To comprehensively and accurately analyze the distribution and interaction mechanisms of harmonics and three-phase imbalance, this study investigated the joint emission level of harmonic and three-phase unbalanced disturbance sources (H-TU-DS). This paper proposes a joint probabilistic emission level modeling approach. The power, harmonic, and three-phase unbalanced data of H-TU-DS are multidimensional, and the feature dataset is filtered by calculating multiple correlation coefficients to achieve dimensionality reduction without losing data information. Considering the different operating characteristics of the H-TU-DS, an improved fuzzy C-means (FCM) algorithm is applied to classify operating conditions, enabling a comprehensive and accurate analysis of the joint emission level. Based on the nonparametric kernel density function, joint probability modeling of H-TU-DS is performed using an adaptive bandwidth improvement method, effectively enhancing the accuracy of the modeling process. Finally, the effectiveness and accuracy of the proposed methods for feature dataset filtering, operating condition classification, and joint probability modeling are demonstrated through comparisons with both simulated and actual data. The proposed model facilitates the prediction and assessment of the impact of H-TU-DS on power quality after grid connection, enabling the effective prevention of power-quality problems through advanced treatment.Keywords: Harmonic, Three-phase unbalance, Nonparametric kernel density, Joint probability modeling

Introduction

With advancements in economics and technology, user requirements for power quality have been rising. In addition to a stable and continuous power supply, users now demand a high level of security and reliability [1]. A key prerequisite for delivering high-quality power is understanding the emission characteristics of power quality disturbance sources. Harmonics and three-phase imbalance are two common disturbances in power systems. The commissioning of new energy units, smelting loads, rolling mills, and similar equipment contributes to harmonic and three-phase unbalance disturbances in the power grid. Such equipment acts as both a harmonic and three-phase unbalance source. The interaction between harmonics and three-phase imbalance results in a joint emission characteristic of these disturbances. To evaluate the disturbance characteristics of these sources and their impact on system stability, a joint emission level model of harmonic and three-phase unbalanced disturbance sources (H-TU-DS) was developed.

To date, existing studies on grid disturbance source modeling have primarily focused on individual power quality factors. Numerous studies have examined either harmonic disturbance sources or three-phase unbalanced disturbance sources in isolation, while few have addressed the joint modeling of H-TU-DS. Existing modeling methods for harmonic sources can be categorized into two approaches: "modeling based on mechanism analysis" and "modeling based on data analysis." The mechanism-based approach typically involves analyzing the volt–ampere characteristics of voltage and current to construct a physical model that describes the coupling relationship between harmonic voltage and harmonic current [2]. Researchers have proposed various physical models for harmonic sources including the constant-current source model [3], the harmonic coupling conductance matrix model [4], and the Norton equivalent model [5,6]. On the contrary, harmonic source modeling based on monitoring or simulation data is commonly used to develop machine learning models [7] and probabilistic statistical models [8,9]. In terms of three-phase unbalanced modeling, Reference [10] introduced a mathematical model for unbalanced analysis by representing the negative sequence unbalance factor with a Thevenin equivalent circuit. Similarly, Reference [11] developed an unbalanced source as a three-phase unbalanced impedance and created a simulation model by adjusting the impedance magnitude. Additionally, Reference [12] introduced a stochastic assessment method to analyze voltage unbalance sensitivity for different maximum sizes of single-phase photovoltaic systems (SPPVS) with varying levels of PV penetration in a typical secondary radial distribution network (SRDN) in Spain. In practical engineering, the calculation of three-phase imbalance is often based on user-monitoring data [13]. Researchers have primarily employed the iterative harmonic penetration (IHP) method to quantitatively assess the impact of disturbance sources on power systems. For example, References [14,15] improved the IHP method to evaluate the effect of nonlinear devices, such as PV systems, on power systems, with Reference [14] specifically considering the impact of background harmonics. However, for disturbance sources exhibiting both harmonic and three-phase imbalanced characteristics, no explicit joint modeling approach has been proposed by researchers to date.

In the field of joint modeling, methods are mainly divided into parameter estimation and non-parametric estimation methods. Parameter estimation methods include copula analysis [16], Gaussian mixture models [17], joint distribution mixture models [18], etc. These methods require the assumption that the observed data follow a certain distribution before parameter estimation can be performed. However, parameter estimation approaches have limitations when dealing with complex data that do not conform to known distributions, as they rely on a priori assumptions about the input data. For the H-TU-DS, the distribution characteristics of the data during actual operation are unknown, making it impossible to assume a specific probability distribution. Therefore, non-parametric estimation methods were employed for joint modeling in this study. Among non-parametric methods, the kernel density function is widely used due to its ability to adapt to data with multiple probability distributions without requiring parameter estimation [19]. Therefore, a non-parametric bivariate kernel density estimation function was selected for this study.

This paper proposes a joint modeling method for H-TU-DS. By collecting power, harmonic, and three-phase unbalanced data at the point of common coupling (PCC) of the H-TU-DS, we analyzed and calculated multiple correlation coefficients among these three types of data. Based on the magnitude of these correlation coefficients, the feature dataset that best represented the power-quality emission level of H-TU-DS was identified. Given the differences in power quality emission levels under different operating conditions, a sub-operating condition analysis was performed. A bivariate non-parametric kernel density function with an improved adaptive bandwidth was then used to construct the joint probability distribution model for H-TU-DS under different operating conditions. Finally, the validity and accuracy of the proposed method were confirmed through simulations.

H-TU-DS operating condition classification based on FCM algorithm

H-TU-DS in this study exhibited both harmonic and three-phase unbalanced emission characteristics. In a previous study, the data indicators at the PCC of H-TU-DS were filtered to obtain the feature dataset X. The data indicators in X reflect the operating and power quality emission characteristics of the H-TU-DS. Given that the power quality problems generated by H-TU-DS may vary under different operating conditions, this study employed a sub-operating condition analysis method, where operating condition classification is realized by dividing the data in X. In other words, X is divided into two or more categories to facilitate operating condition classification. Theoretically, a clustering algorithm can be applied to classify the operating conditions of the H-TU-DS.

The feature data indicators in X exhibit a non-uniform distribution overall, but with local clustering, and the FCM clustering algorithm offers high flexibility and accuracy for handling such data. Therefore, this paper proposes a method for dividing the operating conditions using the FCM algorithm, which partitions X into different categories. Each category corresponds to a distinct operating condition. Under the same operating condition, the power quality emission levels of H-TU-DS can be considered consistent.

FCM clustering algorithm

The sampling points in X were discrete, and the FCM algorithm classified categories based on the affiliation of different sampling points to the clustering center [20]. The degree of fuzziness of the sampling points in the clusters is controlled by the fuzzy parameter m. The affiliation matrix of the sampling points is calculated, and the larger the value of m, the fuzzier the affiliation of each sampling point to the clustering center. Empirically, the value of m is often set to 2 [21,22]. The FCM algorithm calculates the affiliation matrix element value in the range [0,1], providing flexibility and better reflecting the advantages of the original data structure [23]. 4. Joint Probabilistic Modeling for Disturbance Sources.

The FCM algorithm was employed to classify H-TU-DS into different operating conditions, with the total number of operating conditions denoted as K. Harmonic and three-phase unbalanced feature data exhibit distinct distribution characteristics under different operating conditions. To provide a clearer analysis of the feature data distribution characteristics of H-TU-DS across different operating conditions, this paper proposes a joint probabilistic modeling method for the H-TU-DS.

Example analysis

To verify the validity and accuracy of the joint H-TU-DS probabilistic modeling method proposed in this paper, actual power quality monitoring data from the PCC of an AC arc furnace in a large city in central China were analyzed using an arithmetic example. The voltage level of the PCC was 35 kV, the minimum short-circuit capacity was 367.6 MVA, and the capacity of the power supply equipment was 90 MVA.

To ensure that the PCC data accurately reflected the operating conditions of the load over a certain period, the sampling period was set to one month, with a sampling interval of 1 h, resulting in a total of 720 sampling points. The data formats collected by the power quality monitoring device included the maximum, minimum, average, and 95 % probabilities of large values. The data types collected were the three-phase total active power, three-phase total reactive power, three-phase total apparent power, zero-sequence voltage unbalance, negative-sequence voltage unbalance, zero-sequence current unbalance, negative-sequence current unbalance, current THD for each phase (A, B, and C), and voltage THD for each phase(A, B, and C). AC Arc furnace working condition classification results.

The classification of operating conditions was initially subjective. To reduce the influence of subjectivity, the CWB index was used to objectively select the optimal number of operating conditions for the AC arc furnace, determined to be 2. Based on the operating characteristics of an AC arc furnace, its actual operation can be divided into two periods: melting and refining. During the refining period, voltage fluctuations decrease while the harmonic problem becomes more serious, corresponding to operating condition 1. Conversely, during the melting period, the large current required leads to significant voltage fluctuations and a more severe three-phase imbalance, corresponding to operating condition 2. The actual operating characteristics of the AC arc furnace align with the classification of operating conditions in this study, verifying the accuracy of the classification.

Conclusions

In this paper, a joint probabilistic modeling method for H-TU-DS was proposed. In the example analysis, the H-TU-DS feature dataset was selected using multiple correlation coefficients and it was verified that the selected feature dataset contributed to the modeling accuracy. H-TU-DS was then divided into different operating conditions and the accuracy of the operating condition classification was confirmed by matching the actual operating characteristics of H-TU-DS with the identified operating conditions. Finally, a kernel density model with adaptive bandwidth optimization was applied to model H-TU-DS under sub-operating conditions. By comparing the actual joint probability values with the simulated joint probability values, the error indices were found to be minimal, verifying the high accuracy of the proposed method.

The joint probabilistic modeling method proposed in this paper addresses the problem of modeling the emission characteristics of H-TU-DS by combining harmonic emission characteristics and three-phase unbalanced emission characteristics into a single model. This approach not only provides a basis for analyzing the joint emission characteristics of H-TU-DS but also simplifies the number of models required. The proposed model facilitates the prediction and assessment of the impact of H-TU-DS on power quality after grid connection, enabling the effective prevention of power-quality problems through advanced treatment.

However, the method presented in this paper is only applicable to simple H-TU-DS. Future studies will focus on the scalability of the proposed approach and improve its accuracy for adaptation to larger power networks with different topologies.

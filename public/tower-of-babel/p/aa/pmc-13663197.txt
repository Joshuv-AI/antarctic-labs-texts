Critical ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome leading to severe malnutrition: shedding some lights on an extremely rare case report from Vietnam and narrative review
Authors: Anh Dinh Bao Vuong, Ngoc Hai Tran, Tuan Kiet Tao, Phuc Nhon Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOvarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a self-limited iatrogenic complication of controlled-ovarian stimulation. The condition occurs spontaneously or secondary to exogenous gonadotropins which may cause a severe condition. Herein, we outline an extremely rare complication of a such entity leading to severe malnutrition. Additionally, the patient underwent resected surgery of ovarian hyperstimulated tissue in pregnancy.Presentation caseA 34-year-old nulliparous pregnant with a history of polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) received an ovarian stimulation protocol at a private clinic setting. The patient was scheduled for in vitro fertilization (IVF) due to infertility during 15 months. However, on the day 4–5 of trigger, the patient was detected with co-existence of OHSS and spontaneous pregnancy. The pregnant woman was hospitalized at 25–26 weeks of gestational age (GA) for critical OHSS complicating in asthenia and malnutrition following the expectant management of OHSS at 16 weeks of GA. Ultrasound scan revealed multiple anechogenic enlarged cysts extending to the bilateral diaphragm and a great amount of intraabdominal fluid accumulation. During hospitalization, the woman was unable to feed herself, thus leading to severe loss of weight, amyotrophy, and anemia rapidly. Her condition was completely worsened. Seriously, a significant disturbance of bowel motility was noted. The electrocytes disturbance was remarkable. Serum albumin decreased gradually. Therefore, the patient underwent laparotomy to reduce the abdominal fluid and cytoreductive surgery owing to critical OHSS. After surgical intervention, the serum laboratory returned progressively to normal value and the clinical symptoms were steadily subsided. The patient was discharged and continued her pregnancy. Ultrasound showed a complete regression of the ovarian cysts; however, the fetal growth restriction (FGR) was recorded. The patient underwent a planned cesarean delivery at 36 weeks 3 days of GA due to severe FGR and fetal-pelvic disproportion. Both mother and neonate were discharged uneventfully.ConclusionIn the patient with PMOS, co-existence of OHSS and spontaneous pregnancy at the beginning of ovarian stimulation should be recognized. In general, a rapid progression of OHSS associated with adverse outcomes relating to gastro-intestinal complications are extremely scarce. FGR ought to be taken into account in a pregnancy with OHSS. Surgical intervention with a multidisciplinary management could improve materno-neonatal outcomes. Introduction

Ovarian stimulation (OS) is defined as pharmacological treatment with the intention of inducing the development of ovarian follicles. It can be used for timed intercourse or insemination and in assisted reproductive technology (ART), to obtain multiple oocytes at follicular aspiration [1]. Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a common complication of ovarian stimulation in ART. After gonadotropin superovulation for in vitro fertilization (IVF), the mild form occurs in about 20% to 33% of IVF cycles. Meanwhile, the reported incidence of moderate OHSS is 3% to 6% and for severe forms without self-limited progression is 0.1% to 2% [2, 3].

Regardless of etiopathogenesis, the underlying mechanism behind the development of ovarian cysts, especially during the gestational period is diverse [4]. In addition, OHSS secondary to treatment with exogenous gonadotropins, clomiphene citrate, and gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) has been described extensively in the literature [5, 6]. Spontaneous OHSS can occur following a hyperstimulation of the follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) receptor by FSH or other glycoprotein hormones such as chorionic gonadotrophin hormone (hCG), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), and luteinizing hormone (LH); mutations of the FSH-receptor gene can make these receptors abnormally sensitive to hCG, TSH, or both [7, 8]. However, spontaneous OHSS without exogenous stimulated agents such as multiple gestations, hypothyroidism, polycystic ovary syndrome, or molar pregnancies has also been mentioned [7, 9, 10]. Additionally, OHSS without any precipitating diseases or risk factors, even in late pregnancy have been documented [11, 12].

Clinically, OHSS could be presented in variable manifestations from mild to critical [13, 14]. OHSS can usually be managed by out-patient care. If severe-to-critical cases are diagnosed, indication of hospitalization is pivotally necessary for appropriate rehydration, monitoring of fluid balance, and eventual drainage of ascitic fluid [15]. Commonly, complications include abdominal pain, follicular rupture, cyst hemorrhage, and ovarian torsion [16]. Severe cases of OHSS involving adverse events such as renal failure and thrombosis has been reported [17].

In addition, the proper management should be adequately considered in the context of related pregnancy including modification of stimulation strategies in non-pregnant women and arrest of the exogenous gonadotropins [18]. The standard of care for treatment includes monitoring of appropriate clinical parameters, intravenous hydration, fluid balance management, thrombosis prophylaxis, mitigation of symptoms, and ascites treatment should prevent severe morbidity in most cases [19, 20]. Herein, we present a critical case of OHSS treating with a surgical approach following controlled ovarian stimulation on the PMOS patient. Particularly, the female patient was detected with spontaneous conception on the day 4–5 of ovarian stimulation. We also conducted a literature search in English on the PubMed database using the keywords “case” AND “ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome” of papers published from January 2019 to September 2024. To our knowledge, this is the first report concerning the greatest amount of exudated fluid, the largest size of the bilateral ovaries, and the decision of cystectomy in pregnancy on the coexistence of severe OHSS and pregnancy. We believe that this report is instructive for physicians conducting infertility treatment.

Presentation case

A 34-year-old Vietnamese primigravida woman was transferred to our tertiary referral hospital for a presumptive diagnosis of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) on pregnancy of 16 weeks of gestational age (GA) from a private clinic setting. The patient had no medical diseases. The results of serologic testing for hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and syphilis were negative. Her coagulation profile was normal.

Her history of gynecologic disease did not reveal the ovarian tumors. Bilateral tubal patency test (salpingography) was unremarkable. However, the patient had a history of polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) before marriage. She had no regular follow-up with endocrinologist or gynecologist; thus, she had no previous treatment of metformin. In addition, the patient underwent unprotected intercourse for 15 months without successfully natural conception. The sperm analysis of her partner was normal. Previously, she had no treatment of intra-uterine insemination (IUI) or timed-intercourse method. Therefore, she was indicated to receive assisted reproductive technology (ART). After counseling, the couple desired for in vitro fertilization (IVF).

In addition, the patient stated that her menstrual cycle occurred 28 days prior to the beginning of ovarian stimulation. She had last sexual intercourse 16 days before the beginning of ovarian stimulation. Before starting protocol, the blood pregnancy test was negative. On the day of trigger, eight antral follicle counts (AFC) were observed in each ovary. Total 16 AFCs were observed. Endometrial thickness was measured at 11 mm. Anti-mullerian hormone (AMH) level was measured at 5.59 ng/mL (normal range for PMOS patient: 2.41–17.1 ng/mL). Due to limitation of retrospective data, information concerning estradiol (E2) level at external clinic setting could not be assessed. The patient received a progestin-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS) protocol. The patient was treated with Progynova (Estradiol) 2 mg by oral administration twice a day during 4 days before trigger. Later, the patient received PUREGON (Follitropin-β, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)) 300IU (international units) /0.36ML by subcutaneous injection 75 UI per day during 5 days. In addition, she received IVF-M 75/150 UI (Menotropin, human menopausal gonadotropin-hMG, combination of FSH and luteinizing hormone (LH)) by intramuscular injection 225 UI per day during 5 days and Duphaston (Dydrogesteron) 10 mg twice a day. The patient was scheduled to be monitored following this treatment and continued with ovarian stimulation protocol if appropriate. However, on the day 4 and 5, she was detected with a natural conception.

At the first admittance to our hospital, her vital signs were unremarkable including normal body temperature (36.9 °C), pulse rate (84 beats per minute), blood pressure (104/68 mmHg), respiratory frequencies of 20 times/min, and oxygen saturation (100%) in the normal limits. No infectious signs were detected. The 153-cm-tall patient weighed 46 kg before pregnancy. She was excessively slim. Her weight was 59 kg at admission. Her current body mass index (BMI) was 19.65 kg/m2. Physical examination revealed paleness of the mucous membranes, edematous lower limbs, vulva swelling, and abdominal percussion for shifting dullness. She complained of abdominal bloating and abdominal pain located at the right iliac region with tension. The cervix was closed and no vaginal bleeding was markable. The fundal height of the uterus was approximately at 16 cm. The abdominal girth measurement increased to 95 cm. The serum laboratory tests revealed the electrolyte imbalance with sodium of 132.2 (normal range: 136–146) mmol/L and potassium of 3.10 (normal range: 3.4–4.5) mmol/L. Serum albumin level decreased at 31.34 (normal range: 35–52) g/L. Her hemoglobin level was noted at 98 (normal range: 108–164) g/L. Other serum laboratory tests such as liver enzymes and creatinine levels were normally detected.

Seriously, ultrasound evaluation revealed an excessively ovarian enlargement secondary to multiple contiguous cysts with anechoic content, and a small amount of abdominal fluid collection. The right ovarian size was 150 × 286 mm, the biggest cyst was measured at 122 mm. The left ovarian size was 169 × 268 mm, and the biggest cyst was measured at 210 mm. A vital fetus corresponding to 18 weeks of gestation was confirmed. The patient underwent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the pelvis, which revealed expansive cystic lesions with multiple thin septa in both ovaries, without any solid component, and with a small element of probable hemorrhagic content. MRI excluded the appendicitis and ovarian torsion. The patient was treated with electrolyte balance and oral-intake water. She was sent to home for out-patient follow-up after exclusion of other etiologies relating to acute abdominal pain.

Two months later, the patient was rehospitalized for severe OHSS that she could not eat well and leading to loss of weight rapidly. The patient experienced abdominal distention, discomfort, and nausea. Over the course of hospitalization, vital parameters, laboratory data, fluids intake-output, and abdominal girth were strictly monitored. Although the hemodynamic condition was stable, the vital signs were remarkable. The woman presented with shortness of breath progressive to breathlessness. Her respiratory frequency was exceedingly rapid at 31 times/min. The auscultation revealed no abnormal murmurs, but decreased pulmonary sound at the bottom of the lungs was detected. However, her peripheral oxygen saturation was measured in a normal range of 98–100%. The patient was underweight, malnourished, and drowsiness. She was measured with an abdominal circumference of 110 cm and a weight of 62 kg. Her weight did not increase substantially compared with 2 previous months. Whereas, her abdominal girth was markedly increased compared to the 26 weeks of GA (Fig. 1A). By using diuretic drugs, her uterine output was noted from 1400 ml to 7350 ml during 24 h. She was immobile due to a critical condition. The laboratory tests showed low hematocrit and severe disorders of electrolytes (Table 1). Bilan of triglycerides, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C) were slightly variable at 1.75 (normal value < 1.7) mmol/l, 5.2 (normal value < 5.1) mmol/l, 1.16 (normal value > 1.3) mmol/l, and 3.4 (normal value < 4.1) mmol/l, respectively. The liver function tests were normal. Notably, her serum albumin level was lowest at 24.32 (normal range: 35–52) g/L. An ultrasound scan revealed the vital fetus corresponding to 25–26 weeks of gestation and the abdominal cavity was significantly full-filled with increased-fluid-volume cysts (Fig. 1B and Table 2). The chest X-ray showed pleural effusion and transthoracic echocardiography revealed a circumferential pericardial effusion without any evidence of hemodynamic compromise.

Severely, the patient’s nutritional condition was supported with a stomach catheter (nasogastric tube), but the digestive system did not work effectively due to increased abdominal pressure. The patient was given intravenous (IV) fluid supplement to maintain her blood volume. Moreover, crystalloid solution was administered to prevent hemoconcentration and an intravenous albumin therapy to suppress the symptoms of OHSS. Iron supplementation and supplemental vitamin B1 were also given. Additionally, acetaminophen was used for pain relief. Moreover, the patient required a physical therapy owing to being motionless for a long duration time.

The patient was strictly monitored for emergency condition at the intensive care unit for one week. After a multidisciplinary team consisting of obstetrician, gynecologist, endocrinologist, anesthetist, neonatologist, sonographer, and nutritionist, the patient underwent the laparotomy with sub-umbilical skin incision under general anesthesia to reduce the stimulated ovarian tissue and to withdraw the fluid collection. Regarding prophylactic management of extreme preterm birth, magnesium sulfate was given for fetal neuroprotection and corticosteroid therapy for fetal lung maturity before surgery. Thromboprophylaxis using enoxaparin was administered. The patient’s consent was obtained for surgery. Risks of hemorrhage, postoperative infection, preterm birth, and cardiac-pulmonary arrest were sufficiently communicated to the patient. Upon laparotomy, bilateral bulky ovaries containing multiple fluid cysts measuring 10 × 15 cm in size were found, extending to the subphrenic and subhepatic borderline, compressing the gravid uterus corresponding to 26 weeks of GA. The right ovary measuring 20 cm in size, adherent to the anterior abdominal wall presented two points of active hemorrhage. During surgery, hyperstimulated ovarian tissue (cystectomy) was removed partially (Fig. 1C). A drainage of tense ascites was performed to alleviate her discomfort. The hemodynamic status was stably maintained during surgery. Total blood loss was 20 ml. The duration time of surgery was approximately 60 min. On macroscopic examination, nearly 11 L of clearly-straw-colored ascitic fluid was collected (Fig. 1D).

After surgery, the patient was recovered gradually. Importantly, no postoperative intraabdominal hemorrhage was detected. Intestinal gas was detected. However, the uterine contraction was noted, thus intravenous tocolytic agent (Atosiban) was used. The uterine contraction disappeared steadily in the next days. On the 4th -5th − 6th -7th postoperative day, after withdrawing intraabdominal fluid, her abdominal girth and her weight were markedly decreased at 89 cm, 43 kg; 83 cm, 42 kg; 80 cm, 41 kg; and 80 cm, 40 kg, respectively (Fig. 1E). She was transferred to postoperative ward for monitoring. Later, she gained 2 kg during the second week of postoperative course. On the following days, an ultrasound scan revealed a complete regression of the cysts and the disappearance of the collection of intraperitoneal free-fluid. However, the fetal growth restriction (FGR) classified as stage 1 was noted. Total symptomatic relief was deemed to be observed. A broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy was administered. Total blood count test revealed normal range of leucocytes. The C-reactive protein (CRP) increased up to 188.8 mg/l on the day 3. However, CRP decreased rapidly to 41.35 mg/l on the day 5. The D-dimer was measured at 4.35 ug/ml. Despite, ultrasound scan revealed no thrombus in the lower limbs, prophylactic therapy was maintained. Histopathological examination showed benign ovarian cysts (Supplemental Fig. 1A).

On the postoperative day 22, the patient was discharged at 29 weeks of GA and underwent an expectant management. The patient continued to be monitored routinely for fetal growth. At 34 weeks of gestation, the fetal weight was 1,700 g and the fetal heart tracing was normal. Until the gestational age of weeks 36–37, the fundal height of the uterus was measured at 24 cm. Cardiotocography (CTG) was classified as group I according to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) classification (Supplemental Fig. 1B). Ultrasound scan revealed the remained ovarian tissue without abnormal mass (Supplemental Fig. 1C).

The pregnant woman was hospitalized for a scheduled cesarean delivery owing to severe FGR. The cesarean delivery was successfully performed, resulting in the birth of a healthy-female infant weighted at 2,100 g with Apgar scores of 7 points at 1 min and 8 points at 5 min. During cesarean section, the bilateral ovaries were observed normally (Supplemental Fig. 1D-E). The total estimated blood loss was 200 ml. On the day 3th postoperative day, the mother was recorded with mild-digestive disorder. However, this condition was well-responsive with the medical treatment. Both mother and newborn were sent to home on day 9 without sequelae. Four weeks after delivery, both ovaries were still normal aspect on the ultrasound. The risk of OHSS recurrence in subsequent pregnancy was also informed to the patient. The patient and her infant were uneventfully monitored during 1 year after treatment. The woman and her family were grateful for saving her life and her child’s life. The timeline management was summarized in a flowchart diagram (Fig. 2).

Discussion

Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is an iatrogenic adverse event that occurs as a result of ovarian stimulation (OS), particularly, in high-risk women. It is caused by an unusually high ovarian response to stimulation and results in enlarged ovaries and fluid shift from intravascular compartment to the third space [21]. Nowadays, OHSS could be avoided by several prevention methods, ranging from identification of high-risk patients, choice of a correct protocol stimulation, trigger with gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonists, or the freeze-all strategies [15]. A proper endocrinological assessment of patients is recommended as it may avoid unfavorable outcomes. Biomarkers could be used for pre-treatment stratification of risk, with the use of GnRH antagonist protocols, a GnRH agonist trigger, and elective cryopreservation of all embryos [13]. Other strategies including cabergoline and calcium gluconate are useful if proceeding to a fresh embryo transfer [18].

In our case, the patient was diagnosed with polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) before treatment of ovarian stimulation. On the day 4–5 of a progestin-primed ovarian stimulation protocol, the patient was detected with co-existing naturally- conceived pregnancy and ovarian hyperstimulation. Endogenous hCG following the start of controlled ovarian stimulation may have caused severe-onset OHSS. This underscores the importance of an early and appropriate multidisciplinary clinical investigation to avoid unfavorable patient outcomes.

Nevertheless, OHSS could occur among low-risk women or non-underlying diseases. Most cases of OHSS are early-onset OHSS, which occurs shortly after injection of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG). However, late-onset OHSS, which occurs during pregnancy, also requires caution. Late-onset OHSS occurs 12–17 days after injection of hCG and develops in pregnancy in 96.7% of cases [17]. Rarely, the late onset of OHSS in third trimester relating to naturally conceived pregnancy has also been documented [12].

Regardless of the pathogenesis of the disease, OHSS is based on massive transudation of protein-rich fluid from the vascular compartment into the peritoneal, pleural, and pericardial spaces, with a variable picture of clinical manifestations depending on its severity. Due to malnutrition, the woman did not achieve a gained weight according to the nutrient recommendation in pregnancy [22]. In the present case, the patient was satisfactory to critical presentation of OHSS including severe abdominal pain, rapid weight gain, pleural effusion, tachypnea, adult respiratory distress syndrome, arrhythmia, pericardial effusion, oliguria, and worsening of laboratory tests as well as ultrasound findings [20].

Specially, one of the most dangerous complications of OHSS is venous thromboembolism (VTE). Risk of pulmonary embolism among high-risk pregnancies should be also warranted during treatment [23]. Laboratory tests such as D-dimer, Fibrin monomer should be added to detect early coagulation disorder among these patients [24]. Thromboprophylaxis has shown to be cost-effective and widely used, meanwhile, there are controversies regarding the usage of low-dose aspirin (LDA) as a preventive measure. Prophylactic treatment for thromboembolism was adopted by administering low molecular weight heparin, the pregnancy was complicated by thromboses of the left subclavian, axillary, humeral and internal jugular veins during the second trimester of gestation. In the present case, VTE was absent. A case of OHSS occurring in a pregnant woman carrying the factor-V -Leiden mutation [25].

Importantly, in accordance with the findings of Sangtani et al., OHSS may lead to preterm birth (odds ratio (OR): 2.14; 95% confidence interval (CI), 1.26–3.65; P < 0.01) and small-for-gestational-age neonates (OR: 4.78; 95%CI, 1.61–14.19; P < 0.01). Thereafter, a strict obstetrical care should be raised for a pregnant women with OHSS [26]. In the present case, the fetus was diagnosed with fetal growth restriction (FGR) at 27 weeks of gestation. This sequela is a caudal relationship with critical OHSS causing a malnourished condition during pregnancy [27]. The risk of preeclampsia should also be informed to the OHSS pregnant women in the late-term pregnancy [4].

Accordingly, conventional management is primarily focused on supportive care until the spontaneous resolution of the condition. Intravenous hydration should be initiated with a crystalloid solution to prevent hemoconcentration and provide adequate end-organ perfusion. Urine output should be maintained at a minimum of 30 mL/hour [2]. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs with antiplatelet properties should not be used [2, 3].

If OHSS is considered to be a compartment syndrome, early intervention with culdocentesis and paracentesis would seem the most logical plan of care [2]. Ovarian puncture by laparoscopic assessment or blinded method should be prudent since it is related to a high risk of intraperitoneal hemorrhage. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) has emerged as one of the mediators intrinsic to the development of OHSS. The inhibition of vascular permeability appears to be an attractive and novel therapeutic approach to preventing and treating OHSS. Antiangiogenetic agents such as VEGF antagonists or doxycycline could offer theoretical advantages, but are still uncommon from clinical applications [19]. Accordingly, fluid resuscitation, supportive care, paracentesis, and prophylactic anticoagulation are recommended [18].

In line with Palomba et al., only a few interventions currently can be considered effective in reducing the incidence of OHSS and its severity [28]. Critical ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome should be managed by a multidisciplinary team according to the affected organs [2, 29–31]. In our critical case, besides the conventional management, the patient underwent laparotomy for a resected procedure of hyperstimulated ovarian tissue. This surgical approach has not been previously documented in the recent literature (Supplemental Table 1). However, further the evidence-based research approach is required for this novel approach.

Most recently, Leathersich et al. suggested that women with PMOS are at a substantially increased risk of developing OHSS compared to those without. A strategic approach is essential to minimize the risk of OHSS. Therefore, clinicians should carefully stratify the patients at high risk prior to treatment and provide a sufficient counselling regarding pros and cons of IVF treatment, as well as alternative treatment options [32].

Conclusion

In summary, among the patients diagnosed with PMOS receiving ovarian stimulation process, the coexistence of OHSS and spontaneous pregnancy should be warranted. Particularly, OHSS associated with adverse outcomes relating to gastro-intestinal complications is extremely rare for obstetric healthcare professionals. Regarding fetal outcome, a fetal growth restriction ought to be taken into account in pregnancy with OHSS. Surgical intervention aiming to withdraw the tense ascites and removal of stimulated ovarian tissue is rarely required. However, this assessment and a multidisciplinary team could be considered in severe cases which causes potentially life-threatening complications for both mother and fetus. Concisely, enhanced multidisciplinary collaboration with anesthesiology and obstetricians to develop unified clinical standards and standardized workflows could help junior and less-experienced healthcare personnel understand and apply the management more consistently.

RFWD3: A pivotal E3 ubiquitin ligase in DNA damage response, non-neoplastic disorders and multi-system tumorigenesis
Authors: Yue Ding, Mingyue Zhu, Jiaping Shu, Guxin Zhou, Rong Jin, Min Chen, Ru Zhang, Fangyuan Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
RFWD3 is a multifunctional RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase and a central hub in the DNA damage response (DDR) network. It regulates replication fork stability, homologous recombination repair, and cell cycle checkpoints through K63-linked ubiquitination of critical DDR effectors, thereby safeguarding genomic integrity. Germline RFWD3 dysfunction disrupts genomic homeostasis, leading to hereditary non-neoplastic disorders, including Fanconi anemia and premature ovarian insufficiency, while somatic aberrations drive tumorigenesis across multiple organ systems. This narrative review summarizes the molecular mechanisms underlying RFWD3 function in the DDR network, then examines its roles in non-neoplastic disorders and tumorigenesis, and finally integrates recent advances from molecular genetics to translational medicine. Additionally, this review highlights the context-dependent oncogenic and tumor-suppressive functions of RFWD3 in different disease settings and provides a theoretical framework for the precision diagnosis, prognostic stratification, and targeted intervention of RFWD3-associated disorders.

1. Introduction

The ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) governs protein fate, protecting genomic integrity and cellular homeostasis. 1 Among 600 E3 ubiquitin ligases, RING finger and WD repeat domain 3 (RFWD3) is a critical mediator of DDR and replication stress tolerance, linked to genomic instability and disease.2,3 Initially regulating p53 stability and replication checkpoints, RFWD3 is now recognized to modulate DDR pathways such as homologous recombination (HR) repair, replication fork protection, and cell cycle checkpoints. 3 Its unique modular domain architecture enables high-affinity interaction with replication protein A (RPA) and RAD51 recombinase, thereby spatiotemporally modulating ubiquitination dynamics at damaged chromatin.4,5

RFWD3 primarily ubiquitinates RPA and RAD51 to regulate stalled fork turnover, mediates interstrand crosslink (ICL) repair and translesion DNA synthesis via PCNA, and exhibits unique substrate specificity that distinguishes it from other E3 ligases, 2 highlighting its pathological relevance. Intriguingly, biallelic RFWD3 mutations cause Fanconi anemia (FA) complementation group W (FANCW), a rare disorder with bone marrow failure, congenital malformations, and cancer risk. 6 Genetic studies in Indian FA cohorts have identified biallelic RFWD3 mutations as a cause of FANCW. 7 Additionally, RFWD3 dysregulation contributes to telomere disorders and tumorigenesis by disrupting DNA repair and genomic stability. 3 RFWD3 research has progressed in three phases: initial characterization as a p53 and replication checkpoint regulator 3 ; 2017 breakthroughs linking its inactivation to FANCW and resolving its dual ICL repair mechanisms5,6; and current oncology-focused work linking aberrant expression to progression and therapy resistance.

Despite data on RFWD3’s structure and functions in DNA repair and diseases, three gaps remain: the molecular link between non-neoplastic disorders and malignancies, its stage-specific roles, and an integrated DDR network with metabolic and immune modulation. Existing reviews on RFWD3 have focused on its E3 ligase functions, FA pathogenesis, and germline variant-linked tumor susceptibility.2,8 Chauhan et al. detailed RFWD3-mediated ubiquitination and FA pathogenesis but lacked a pan-cancer expression landscape or tumor immune regulation data. 2 Elzinga-Tinke et al. reviewed genetic susceptibility loci for testicular germ cell tumors, including RFWD3, in the context of genome-wide association studies but did not address RFWD3’s role in sporadic tumors or its clinical prognostic value. 8

This review unifies evidence across benign and malignant disorders to build a translational framework. Specifically, it addresses several unmet needs in the field: (1) elucidating the mechanistic link RFWD3-associated non-neoplastic disorders to malignant transformation via the unifying mechanism of genomic instability; (2) defining the context-dependent roles of RFWD3 across the disease spectrum; (3) synthesizing its pan-cancer expression and signaling profiles; and (4) integrating synthetic lethality, protein-protein interaction (PPI) inhibitors, and predictive biomarkers into a unified precision medicine framework. This narrative review adheres to the SANRA guidelines. 9 Literature for this review was retrieved from PubMed, Web of Science, and Embase databases, with keywords including “RFWD3”, “DNA damage response”, “tumorigenesis”, “Fanconi anemia”, and related terms.

2. Structural characteristics and biochemical functions

2.1. Domain composition

RFWD3’s modular structure includes three conserved domains: an N-terminal RING finger, a central coiled-coil, and a C-terminal WD40 repeat domain. 5 The RING finger contains a conserved CxxC motif crucial for E3 ligase activity, enabling E2 binding and ubiquitin transfer. 3 The central coiled-coil domain acts as a PPI module, allowing RFWD3 to form homodimers or interact with DDR factors. 6 Homodimerization via this domain enhances RFWD3’s avidity for chromatin substrates, making it critical for recruitment to DNA damage sites. 5 It binds high-affinity substrates such as RPA2. 3 Both the coiled-coil and WD40 domains are required for high-affinity RPA interaction, which is essential for RPA-dependent recruitment of RFWD3 to DNA lesions. 10 WD40 mutations disrupt RPA binding, impairing recruitment to DNA lesions and mimicking FA phenotypes. 11

2.2. Substrate recognition and ubiquitination modification

RFWD3 recognizes a selective set of substrates involved in DDR, DNA replication, and cell cycle control, with binding mediated by its WD40 domain. 4 Key substrates include RPA, RAD51, p53, and SMARCAL1, which maintain genomic stability.3,11

The heterotrimeric single-stranded DNA (ssDNA)-binding protein RPA is a substrate of RFWD3. 5 RFWD3 binds RPA2 via its WD40 domain, promoting K63-linked polyubiquitination at DNA damage sites. 4 This ubiquitination triggers RPA dissociation from ssDNA, facilitating recruitment of downstream repair factors such as RAD51. 3 Three distinct mechanisms underlie this dissociation process: steric hindrance induced by ubiquitination, electrostatic repulsion from the negative charge of ubiquitin, and VCP/p97-mediated extraction.6,12,13 Mutation of the RFWD3 binding site impairs RPA ubiquitination, causing persistent RPA accumulation. 4 RFWD3 and RPA also regulate each other’s phosphorylation, indicating a coordinated DDR circuit. 10

RAD51, the core recombinase in HR repair, is a primary substrate of RFWD3. 5 RFWD3-mediated ubiquitination of RAD51 enhances its filament formation on ssDNA, crucial for homologous strand invasion and DNA repair. 3 RFWD3 also facilitates timely RAD51 removal from damage sites after repair, preventing aberrant recombination and genomic instability. 5 Loss of RFWD3 results in delayed and persistent RAD51 foci during replication block recovery, indicating defective HR progression. 10

p53, a crucial tumor suppressor regulating cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, is also regulated by RFWD3. 1 RFWD3 interacts with p53 and promotes its K63-linked polyubiquitination, thereby enhancing p53’s transcriptional activity and protein stability in response to DNA damage. 5 This modification contrasts with MDM2’s K48-linked polyubiquitination, which targets p53 for degradation, highlighting RFWD3’s unique role in fine-tuning p53 activity. 1

SMARCAL1, a replication fork remodeler, is ubiquitinated by RFWD3 after DNA damage, releasing it from RPA-coated ssDNA without degradation. 11 This prevents excessive fork remodeling and MUS81-dependent cleavage, limiting genomic instability. 11 SMARCAL1 ubiquitination occurs specifically within its RPA-binding domain, and disruption of either RFWD3’s catalytic activity or its RPA-binding capacity completely abrogates this modification, causing persistent SMARCAL1 accumulation on RPA-coated ssDNA chromatin. 11

RFWD3 primarily catalyzes K63-linked polyubiquitination, associated with non-degradative roles such as signal transduction and DNA repair. 4 However, it may also mediate K48-linked ubiquitination of select substrates, leading to proteasomal degradation. 1 For instance, RFWD3 promotes degradation of 53BP1 via K48-linked ubiquitination, balancing HR and non-homologous end joining repair pathways. 1 Its ability to catalyze both types underscores its versatile role in DDR. RFWD3’s ubiquitination activity is regulated by modifications and PPIs. 3 ATM/ATR phosphorylation enhances its activity and chromatin recruitment. 5 RPA enhances this phosphorylation, forming a feedback loop that amplifies DDR signaling. 10 Additionally, K48-linked auto-ubiquitination at multiple lysine residues controls RFWD3 protein turnover and steady-state abundance. 3 Interaction with RPA and DDR factors stabilizes RFWD3’s active conformation. 4

3. RFWD3 in DNA replication fork stability and processing

3.1. Mechanisms in responding to replication stress

Replication stress triggered by nucleotide pool depletion or DNA lesions induces replication fork stalling, wherein RFWD3 mediates critical, non-redundant functions in fork protection and efficient restart. RFWD3 binds RPA via its domains, enabling rapid recruitment to stalled forks. 12 It mediates K63-linked RPA ubiquitination, aiding RPA dissociation and repair factor recruitment for fork restart. 14 This event is critical for replication fork restart, as depletion of RFWD3 reduces the efficiency of fork restart by impairing the displacement of RPA and the subsequent loading of recombination factors. 14 RFWD3 also interacts with PCNA, stabilizing itself at forks to ensure efficient RPA ubiquitination. 15 Uniquely, RFWD3 collaborates with SHPRH and HLTF to catalyze K63-linked polyubiquitination of PCNA at K164 and performs unique functions in replication stress regulation. Specifically, RFWD3 functions as an upstream recruiter that binds to both PCNA and RPA, and subsequently modulates polyubiquitin chain elongation. RFWD3 recruits the ASCC2-ASCC3 complex for fork remodeling, while SHPRH/HLTF promotes template switching. In RFWD3-deficient cells, ASCC recruitment and fork unwinding are impaired, reinforcing RPA accumulation and stress signaling. 16 To date, no published studies have further dissected the distinct molecular mechanisms and non-overlapping biochemical functions that distinguish RFWD3 from SHPRH and HLTF during PCNA polyubiquitination.

In the absence of Schlafen family member 11 (SLFN11), RFWD3 cooperates with PRIMPOL to form a DNA damage tolerance axis that promotes gapped DNA synthesis and replication fork restart. SLFN11 blocks this axis through its ATPase domain and stable fork retention, preventing RFWD3 and PRIMPOL recruitment to stalled forks, impairing restart and worsening replication-related DNA damage. 17 Loss of RFWD3 impairs replication fork progression, prolongs S phase duration, and increases the accumulation of RPA and other replisome components on chromatin, indicating elevated levels of replication fork stalling. 15 PCNA-mediated RFWD3 stabilization is RPA-independent, as RPA depletion does not affect RFWD3 chromatin binding under normal conditions. 15

3.2. Regulation of replication fork reversal and protection

Replication fork reversal is regulated by RFWD3 through its interaction with fork remodeling factors and RPA. In BRCA2-deficient cells, RFWD3-dependent RPA ubiquitination produces hyperubiquitinated RPA (ubq-pRPA) at reversed forks. 18 Depleting RFWD3 rescues fork degradation by reducing ubq-pRPA and MRE11-mediated resection. 18 These cells show persistent pRPA-coated ssDNA from RFWD3-mediated RPA ubiquitination. 19 Blocking MRE11-dependent resection suppresses fork degradation but not pRPA accumulation or fork collapse, indicating that RFWD3-mediated RPA ubiquitination is a critical, MRE11-independent fork fate determinant. 19

Furthermore, RFWD3 competes with chromobox protein homolog 3 (CBX3) for binding to RPA2’s C-terminal domain. CBX3 inhibits RFWD3-mediated RPA ubiquitination and traps RPA at stalled forks, while RFWD3 counteracts this to promote RPA turnover and fork protection. 20 RFWD3 also recruits ZRANB3 to stalled forks, enhancing fork stability. 21 RFWD3-dependent PCNA K164 polyubiquitination recruits ASCC2, which brings ASCC3 to unwind DNA and promote fork reversal. 16 Additionally, RFWD3 prevents spontaneous fork stalling by ensuring RPA ubiquitination. 15 These findings establish RFWD3 as a bifunctional regulator promoting fork reversal while preventing degradation, positioning it as a critical barrier to tumorigenesis.

4. RFWD3 in HR repair

4.1. Promotion of RAD51 filament formation and stabilization

HR repair of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) and stalled replication forks is dependent on RFWD3-mediated regulation of RAD51 nucleofilament assembly and disassembly dynamics. RFWD3-mediated RPA ubiquitination facilitates RPA displacement from ssDNA, a prerequisite for RAD51 loading and filament formation. 13 Meanwhile, RFWD3 directly ubiquitinates RAD51, promoting its timely removal from DNA damage sites upon repair completion. 13 UHRF1 negatively regulates RFWD3 activity to control the timing of RAD51 removal, ensuring orderly HR progression. 22 In BRCA2-deficient cells, RFWD3 depletion increases RAD51 accumulation at stalled forks, indicating that RFWD3 precisely regulates RAD51 levels to balance filament stabilization and disassembly. 18 RFWD3 further interacts with BRCA2, required for efficient RAD51 filament formation at reversed forks. 18 Intriguingly, RFWD3 shows contradictory roles in RAD51 dynamics, promoting filament assembly early and disassembly later via ubiquitination, controlled by structure, phosphorylation, and VCP/p97 levels. 13

4.2. Coordination of HR repair and DNA end resection

RFWD3 coordinates DNA end resection by interacting with the resection machinery and ubiquitin signaling molecules, and promotes the recruitment of crucial factors to DSB sites. 23 The RFWD3-PRP19 phosphorylation-ubiquitination circuit enhances resection via RPA ubiquitination. 24 Furthermore, RFWD3 collaborates with ASCC3 to unwind DNA at stalled forks, enhancing resection and HR. 16 In ICL repair, RFWD3-dependent ubiquitination of RPA and RAD51 is essential for coupling resection with HR, with its loss impairing repair. 23 In HR-deficient genetic backgrounds, RFWD3 depletion significantly compromises DNA end resection. 18 Overall, RFWD3 governs RAD51 filament dynamics, resection, and HR fidelity, preserving genomic stability and suppressing malignant transformation.

5. RFWD3 in cell cycle checkpoint activation

5.1. Upstream regulator of p53

RFWD3 acts as an upstream regulator of p53, enhancing its stability and transcriptional activity after DNA damage by forming a complex with MDM2 to catalyze K63-linked polyubiquitination. 25 This differs from MDM2’s K48-linked ubiquitination that degrades p53, underscoring RFWD3’s distinct role. 25 Beyond checkpoint regulation, RFWD3 also ensures replication fidelity by stabilizing the origin recognition complex in a p53-dependent manner, preventing aberrant origin firing. 26 Under replication stress, RFWD3-driven p53 activation prevents aberrant origin firing and replication catastrophe. 12 Its loss impairs p53-mediated cell cycle arrest, causing uncontrolled growth and genomic instability. 5 Following DNA damage, ATM/ATR phosphorylates RFWD3, enhancing its interaction with MDM2 and p53 to potentiate p53 signaling. 25

5.2. Regulation of the ATR-Chk1 pathway

RFWD3 is a component of the ATR-Chk1 pathway, crucial for sensing replication stress and maintaining genome integrity. It is recruited to RPA-coated ssDNA at stalled forks, where it works with PRP19 to ubiquitinate RPA, aiding ATR-ATRIP recruitment and activation. 24 Unlike the PRP19-driven ubiquitin cascade, RFWD3-mediated RPA polyubiquitination independently drives ATR activation. RFWD3 modifies multiple RPA residues through non-degradative ubiquitination, remodeling RPA-ssDNA assemblies to enhance ATR-ATRIP docking and stabilize the complex at damaged chromatin.12,27 This RPA-ssDNA remodeling occurs independently of PRP19, allowing RFWD3 to initiate ATR signaling even in the absence of PRP19, although both E3 ligases function cooperatively to amplify signaling under persistent replication stress. 24 Functionally, this ubiquitination enhances ATR-driven phosphorylation of targets, essential for checkpoint activation. 24 Nevertheless, the detailed mechanistic link connecting RFWD3-dependent RPA ubiquitination to direct ATR-ATRIP activation separate from the PRP19 cascade, as well as the full regulatory crosstalk between RFWD3 and TOPBP1/ETAA1 axes, has not been elucidated by existing literature and represents key avenues for follow-up investigation.

RFWD3 coordinates ATR-Chk1 signaling through differential regulation of the two canonical ATR activators, ETAA1 and TOPBP1, with distinct mechanisms for each. ETAA1-driven ATR activation depends on the interaction between ETAA1 and RPA-ssDNA, and RFWD3-mediated RPA ubiquitination promotes ETAA1 recruitment to stalled forks. In contrast, TOPBP1-mediated ATR activation exhibits delayed kinetics and is not directly regulated by RFWD3, although RFWD3-dependent RPA ubiquitination remains essential for maximal Chk1 phosphorylation.12,27 Furthermore, cell-type-specific differences in ATR signaling output between U2OS and HeLa cells are partially attributed to the differential expression of ETAA1 and TOPBP1.

From a mechanistic perspective, RFWD3 E3 ligase activity is essential for Chk1 activation, given that RING domain mutations completely abrogate checkpoint signaling. 12 RFWD3 depletion reduces Chk1 phosphorylation at Ser345 under replication stress, impairing checkpoint activation and increasing fork collapse. 12 It also controls ATR-mediated RPA2 phosphorylation at Ser4/8 and Ser33, essential for checkpoint signaling and fork restart. 14

6. Types of genomic instability caused by RFWD3 deficiency

6.1. Chromosomal structural variations and gene amplification

RFWD3 deficiency impairs DNA repair and replication fork stability, causing chromosomal structural variations and gene amplification. In BRCA2-deficient cells, loss of RFWD3 increases chromosomal breaks due to defective fork protection and impaired HR repair. 18 This instability is exacerbated by disrupted RFWD3-ASCC3 interaction, which impairs RFWD3-dependent PCNA polyubiquitination and leads to excessive chromosomal breaks and mis-segregation. 16 FA patients with biallelic RFWD3 mutations exhibit significantly increased chromosomal fragility. 5 RFWD3 deficiency also promotes gene amplification by inducing elevated levels of replication stress and fork stalling. 20 In prostate cancer, CBX3 overexpression sequesters RPA away from RFWD3, preventing RPA ubiquitination and thereby increasing replication stress-induced copy number variations and oncogene amplification. 20 RFWD3-deficient, BRCA2-mutant cells show increased loss of heterozygosity and telomeric allelic imbalance, accelerating tumor progression. 18 These findings identify RFWD3 as a suppressor of chromosomal instability.

6.2. Mutation spectrum characteristics

RFWD3 deficiency alters the mutation spectrum, increasing replication errors and defective repair. Loss of RFWD3 increases mutation frequency, favoring G:C-to-A:T transitions and indels. 14 This pattern suggests impaired replication fidelity and mismatch repair (MMR) defects, as RFWD3 loss causes persistent RPA accumulation at stalled forks, hindering MMR recruitment. 15 It also disrupts TLS and the RFWD3-PRIMPOL axis, increasing mutagenesis at replication-blocking lesions and unrepaired ssDNA gaps. 17 FA patients with RFWD3 mutations exhibit transversion-enriched spectra, indicating unrepaired ICLs and replication stress. 5 RFWD3 deficiency reduces TLS efficiency, further promoting mutagenesis, 28 underscoring its role in resolving replication lesions and maintaining repair fidelity.

7. RFWD3 mutations and association with diseases

7.1. Non-neoplastic diseases

Germline RFWD3 mutations, predominantly biallelic pathogenic variants or loss-of-function (LoF) alterations, are linked to FA and premature ovarian insufficiency (POI), with phenotypes from impaired DNA repair or meiotic processes.

7.1.1. Fanconi anemia

FANCW is a defined non-neoplastic disorder caused by biallelic RFWD3 mutations, and affected patients typically present with syndromic short stature and are born small for gestational age. 29 A novel biallelic missense variant in RFWD3 was recently identified in a patient presenting with microcephaly, craniofacial dysmorphism, severe visceral malformations, and delayed tooth eruption, matching the FANCW phenotype. 29 This ultra-rare variant follows autosomal recessive inheritance, with both parents being asymptomatic heterozygous carriers, and its pathogenicity is further supported by strict phenotypic concordance with previously reported cases. 29 The WD40-domain variant I639K is the most common pathogenic mutation in FANCW patients. Mechanistic dissection of this variant has yielded two non-mutually exclusive models of RFWD3 function in ICL repair. Feeney et al. showed that I639K disrupts RFWD3-RPA interaction, preventing recruitment to stalled replication forks and blocking early ICL repair. 23 Knies et al. demonstrated that the mutant retains partial E3 ligase activity but fails to ubiquitinate RPA and RAD51, causing persistent retention at damage sites and defective late-stage HR. These studies established that RPA-dependent recruitment and substrate ubiquitination are essential for RFWD3 in ICL repair. 5 Loss of RFWD3 disrupts ICL repair and HR, leading to FA chromosomal instability. 5

7.1.2. POI

POI, defined as ovarian failure before the age of 40, is associated with germline RFWD3 mutations. 30 LoF variants impair critical meiotic processes such as meiotic recombination and chromosome segregation, resulting in massive oocyte attrition, premature ovarian reserve exhaustion, and infertility. 30 This expands RFWD3’s phenotypic spectrum beyond FA. 30 Overall, RFWD3 mutations cause non-neoplastic disorders involving genomic instability or reproductive defects, reflecting its roles in DNA repair and meiosis.

7.1.3. Telomere biology disorders

Emerging evidence shows RFWD3 is critical for telomere homeostasis, with dysfunction linked to telomere biology disorders. RFWD3 deficiency exacerbates telomere impairment caused by RPA2 mutations. 3 At the mechanistic level, RFWD3 regulates RPA turnover at telomeres, with its depletion disrupting replication. Genetic variants affect telomere length. 31 Under stress, RFWD3 dysfunction compromises replication fidelity, causing shortening and instability. 17 Thus, RFWD3 is a critical upstream regulator of telomere integrity.

7.2. Neoplasm

Biallelic germline LoF mutations in RFWD3 mainly cause congenital defects and reproductive disorders, while somatic dysregulation drives tumorigenesis. RFWD3 dysregulation drives initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance across diverse human cancers via tissue-specific oncogenic programs.

7.2.1. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)

RFWD3 is upregulated in NSCLC tissues and correlates with poor overall survival (OS) and smoking history. 32 At the mechanistic level, RFWD3 promotes NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion while inhibiting apoptosis via the ERK/p38 pathway, modulating BAX, BCL-2, cleaved-caspase-3, MMP-2, and MMP-9. 33 Tobacco smoke, PM2.5, and BaP upregulate RFWD3 through AhR binding to its promoter. 34 RFWD3 also drives immune escape by stabilizing TREX1 via competition with TRIM24, suppressing the cGAS-STING-IFN pathway, and reducing CD4+, CD8+ T cells and NK cells while increasing myeloid-derived suppressor cells. 34 Inhibition of RFWD3 sensitizes NSCLC cells to PD-L1 blockade by restoring STING pathway activation and reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment. 34 Additionally, RFWD3 overexpression is more pronounced in smoker-derived NSCLC tissues, linking environmental carcinogens to its oncogenic role. 32

7.2.2. Bladder cancer

RFWD3 is highly expressed in bladder cancer compared to normal tissue, with its overexpression linked to advanced N stage and poor prognosis. 35 Functional assays confirm that RFWD3 knockdown inhibits cell proliferation, colony formation, and migration while inducing apoptosis. 35 Conversely, RFWD3 overexpression enhances malignant phenotypes, highlighting its tumor-promoting role. 35 Mechanistic studies show RFWD3 regulates cell phenotypes indirectly, without direct ubiquitination of core apoptotic proteins. 35

7.2.3. Multiple myeloma (MM)

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified RFWD3 as a susceptibility locus for MM, with a non-synonymoussingle-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) showing genome-wide significant association with MM risk. 36 This variant maps to 16q23.1 and is linked to impaired DNA repair fidelity. 36 RFWD3’s role in MM is supported by its involvement in the dysregulated FA signaling pathway, contributing to genomic instability. 37 RFWD3 deficiency in MM cells impairs TLS, increasing mutagenesis and accelerating disease progression. 28

7.2.4. Osteosarcoma

RFWD3 is overexpressed in post-chemotherapy osteosarcoma tissues and is associated with cisplatin (DDP) resistance, although a causal relationship has not been definitively established. 38 At the mechanistic level, RFWD3 binds and ubiquitinates PHGDH at Lys137, leading to its degradation and NAD+ accumulation. 38 This NAD+ elevation enhances the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, increasing aspartic and glutamic acid for nucleotide synthesis, aiding DNA repair and DDP resistance. 38 Lomitapide inhibits the RFWD3-PHGDH interaction, synergizing with DDP to suppress osteosarcoma growth. 38 RFWD3 expression inversely correlates with DDP sensitivity in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models, confirming its role as a chemoresistance marker. 38

7.2.5. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Gain- and LoF experiments have confirmed RFWD3 drives HCC proliferation and metastasis via the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, with its knockdown reducing β-catenin nuclear translocation and subsequent target gene expression. 39 High RFWD3 expression in HCC correlates with microvascular invasion, advanced TNM stage, and poor prognosis. 40 Additionally, RFWD3 modulates immune infiltration, with high expression linked to increased MDSC recruitment and reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration. 40

7.2.6. Glioblastoma (GBM)

Functional studies demonstrate that RFWD3 knockdown suppresses GBM cell growth and migration by blocking G2/M cell cycle progression, with G2 phase accumulation and reduced cyclin B1 and CDK1 expression. 41 In GBM patient samples, RFWD3 is overexpressed in tumor core regions compared to peritumoral normal tissue, and patient-derived expression data show a correlative association with tumor grade. 41 Additionally, RFWD3 interacts with RAD51 to enhance HR repair in GBM cells, contributing to temozolomide resistance. 42

7.2.7. Endometrial cancer

Estrogen drives endometrial cancer by upregulating RFWD3 via ERα binding to its promoter. 27 RFWD3 overexpression enhances proliferation and reduces apoptosis through PI3K/AKT activation, while promoting immune-inflammatory changes via increased IL-6 and TNF-α secretion. 27

7.2.8. Pancreatic cancer

Expression quantitative trait loci identified a regulatory variant in RFWD3 linked to pancreatic cancer prognosis, influencing expression via transcription factor binding. 43 High RFWD3 expression is significantly associated with lymph node metastasis and poor OS, while its knockdown reduces cell migration and invasion by inhibiting epithelial-mesenchymal transition. 43

7.2.9. Testicular germ cell tumor (TGCT)

GWAS and meta-analyses identified RFWD3 as a TGCT susceptibility gene, with SNP rs4888262 linked to risk. As an E3 ubiquitin ligase regulating p53 via the RFWD3-MDM2-p53 complex, RFWD3 is involved in gonadal development and germ cell survival.10,25 SNPs in RFWD3 map to genes controlling germ cell migration and differentiation, supporting biological plausibility. 8 TGCT originates from carcinoma in situ due to blocked germ cell maturation, and RFWD3 dysfunction may drive genomic instability in tumor progression. 8

7.2.10. Acute myeloid leukemia (AML)

RFWD3 is a hub gene in the FA signaling pathway, linked to genomic stability and AML. In pediatric AML, it was identified as one of five prognostic signatures via Lasso regression using multi-cohort data. 44 A risk score based on RFWD3 expression stratifies patients into high- and low-risk groups, with higher expression correlating with poorer OS in training and internal validation cohorts. 44 External validation in the BEAT-AML cohort confirmed the model’s validity. 44 Functional analyses show RFWD3-related signatures are involved in DNA repair, cell cycle regulation, and peroxide metabolism. 44 Biallelic RFWD3 mutations cause FA, a disorder predisposing to AML, underscoring its dual role in FA pathway regulation and AML tumorigenesis. 5

7.2.11. Other tumors

RFWD3 promotes tumor growth in various cancers. In colorectal cancer, it enhances E2F1-mediated BIRC5 transcription, aiding proliferation. 45 In gastric carcinoma, RFWD3 is upregulated by 125I seed irradiation, linked to apoptosis and cell cycle arrest via DNA demethylation. 46 Its downregulation reduces proliferation and migration. 47 In low-grade glioma, RFWD3 is a critical prognostic factor, with high expression predicting poor outcomes. 48 In adrenocortical carcinoma, recurrent RFWD3 mutations drive genomic instability. 49 GWAS identify RFWD3 as a locus for leukocyte telomere length regulation. 31

7.2.12. Common features and clinical implications

RFWD3 has four critical biological roles in human diseases: it serves as a prognostic biomarker in cancers, drives oncogenesis via overexpression, functions as a context-dependent disease modifier based on genetic background, and acts as a therapeutic target validated by preclinical studies. 34 By combining context-dependent functional evidence from various experimental and clinical settings, a paradigm is proposed for RFWD3’s dual role as a genome guardian or oncogenic driver, shaped by three critical determinants: gene dosage/mutation pattern, endogenous DDR context, and cell-intrinsic molecular background.

This pan-cancer evidence identifies RFWD3 as a multifaceted oncogenic catalyst affecting various malignancies (Table 1). 7.3. Cross-linkage between RFWD3-related non-neoplastic disorders and malignancies

RFWD3-related non-neoplastic disorders and malignant tumors share a unifying pathogenic mechanism, which is genome instability caused by LoF mutations or aberrant hyperactivation of the RFWD3-governed DDR network. Biallelic germline LoF variants in RFWD3 cause autosomal recessive FANCW-type FA and POI, conferring a significantly elevated risk of tumor development in FA carriers with increased lifelong risks of hematological and solid tumors. Such phenotypes stem from defects in DNA ICL repair and chromosomal aberrations. Mechanistically, germline RFWD3 mutations disrupt meiotic HR during fetal oogenesis, causing massive oocyte apoptosis and POI. In postnatal somatic ovarian cells, the same HR-deficient background predisposes to replication-associated DNA damage and genomic instability, driving ovarian malignancies later in life. Sporadic RFWD3 overexpression from carcinogen exposure or epigenetic changes rarely causes congenital disorders but drives tumorigenesis through DDR reprogramming, metabolic remodeling, and immune evasion (Figure 1). It modulates HR repair and replication fork stability via RPA/RAD51/PCNA ubiquitination and BRCA2 binding, enabling chemotherapy resistance and synthetic lethality in BRCA2-mutant tumors. RFWD3 degrades PHGDH to maintain NAD+ homeostasis, activating the TCA cycle for nucleotide biosynthesis, proliferation, and invasion. It stabilizes TREX1 to degrade cytosolic dsDNA, inhibiting cGAS-STING/IFN production and impairing anti-tumor immunity. RFWD3 activates ERK/p38 MAPK, Wnt/β-catenin, and PI3K/AKT pathways while downregulating p53/ATR-Chk1, enhancing migration, invasion, and immunosuppression. Pathways synergize: metabolism fuels DNA repair; genomic instability activates oncogenic signaling upregulating RFWD3; TCA cycle enhances MDSC immunosuppression; oncogenic signaling reshapes the tumor immune microenvironment. 8. Potential of RFWD3 as a cancer therapeutic target

8.1. Application of synthetic lethality strategy

The synthetic lethality strategy, exploiting two genes where combined disruption induces cell death but individual disruption is tolerable, shows promise for RFWD3-targeted cancer therapy, especially in tumors with DDR or genomic stability defects. In BRCA2-deficient cells, RFWD3 loss exacerbates replication fork instability and impairs HR repair, leading to hypersensitivity to RFWD3 inhibition, a synthetic lethal interaction useful for treating BRCA2-mutant tumors. 18 RFWD3 also interacts with the ATR-Chk1 pathway, with combined inhibition of RFWD3 and ATR synergistically exacerbating DNA damage and apoptosis in DDR-proficient tumors by disrupting replication stress tolerance. 24 PARP inhibitors, approved for HR-deficient cancers, have been shown to synergize with RFWD3 inhibition, as RFWD3-stabilized RAD51 filaments are critical for HR repair of PARP inhibitor-induced lesions. 13

8.2. Small molecules targeting RFWD3

Targeting RFWD3’s PPIs is a viable drug development approach, with preclinical studies confirming essential therapeutic interfaces (Table 2). Lomitapide is an FDA-approved lipid-lowering agent. 38 In vitro, Lomitapide at concentrations of 1μM and 10μM disrupts the binding between RFWD3 and PHGDH and exhibits a significant synergistic anti-tumor effect when combined with cisplatin. 38 However, this study did not measure Lomitapide plasma concentrations, evaluate pharmacological tractability at human doses, assess its MTP inhibitory role in anti-tumor effects, or establish a therapeutic window as an RFWD3-PHGDH inhibitor. 38 A major challenge include higher required concentrations than clinical doses and constraints from lipid-lowering effects. 9. Future directions and challenges

9.1. In-depth deciphering of RFWD3’s substrate network and regulatory kinetics

Despite significant progress, critical gaps remain in understanding RFWD3 biology. The complete substrate repertoire and its tissue-specific regulation have not been systematically mapped. While RPA, RAD51, PHGDH, and TREX1 are validated substrates, proteomic studies are needed to identify cell-type-specific targets and indirect transcriptional effectors 45 and additional uncharacterized substrates. 35 Elucidation of these substrates using quantitative proteomics will clarify the tumor-specific oncogenic mechanisms of RFWD3. Furthermore, the kinetic regulation of RFWD3 remains poorly understood. Investigating how these post-translational modifications dynamically modulate RFWD3 subcellular localization, substrate specificity, and E3 ligase activity will yield critical insights for therapeutic targeting.

9.2. Establishment of more precise disease models

Current preclinical models fail to capture RFWD3-related cancer heterogeneity, limiting translational progress. Conditional RFWD3 GEMMs with driver mutations are urgently required to model tumor initiation and progression. 5 PDX models with defined RFWD3 expression and DDR/FA status 38 will support evaluation of RFWD3-targeted therapies. Organoid models with tumor microenvironment components can clarify RFWD3’s role in immune escape and guide combination immunotherapies. 34 Addressing the discrepancy between preclinical effective doses and established clinical doses should be a priority, with future studies rigorously evaluating lomitapide’s anticancer efficacy and safety at clinically achievable concentrations.

9.3. Advancing clinical translation and precision medicine

Clinical translation of RFWD3-targeted therapies necessitates addressing key challenges, starting with identifying predictive biomarkers (Table 3), including RFWD3 expression levels,35,38 mutations in DDR/FA pathway genes,5,19 PHGDH expression, 38 and TREX1/cytosolic dsDNA levels. 34 These biomarkers will help stratify patients likely to benefit. Lomitapide faces challenges due to supraphysiological dosing and narrow therapeutic windows associated with toxicity risks, although structural optimization or tumor-targeted delivery could improve its druggability. Trial design should prioritize combination strategies to overcome resistance, 34 and safety profiling is critical given RFWD3’s role in normal tissues, as synthetic lethality and tissue-specific targeting can reduce toxicity. 10. Discussion and conclusion

This narrative review establishes RFWD3 as a central DDR hub and delineates its dual roles in non-neoplastic diseases and tumorigenesis. Unlike prior reviews that have focused on FA pathogenesis or germline variant-associated cancer susceptibility,5–7 this work makes four distinct contributions to the field: 1) mechanistically linking genomic instability-driven benign disorders to systemic malignancies; 2) defining four context-dependent functional paradigms of RFWD3 in disease; 3) presenting a comprehensive pan-cancer expression and signaling profile across over a dozen tumor subtypes; 4) integrating synthetic lethality, PPI inhibitors, and predictive biomarkers into a unified precision medicine framework. These findings address critical knowledge gaps in RFWD3 biology.

This review also has several limitations. First, as a narrative review, it may omit gray literature and regional studies not indexed in PubMed, Web of Science, or Embase. Second, most functional evidence derives from preclinical cell and animal models, lacking large-scale clinical validation. Third, tissue-specific substrate networks and post-translational regulatory kinetics of RFWD3 remain incompletely characterized. Fourth, no RFWD3-targeted agents have entered clinical trials, with unknown long-term safety and resistance profiles.

In conclusion, RFWD3 is a non-redundant genomic stability guardian whose dysregulation underlies diverse non-neoplastic disorders and multi-system tumorigenesis. It represents a robust prognostic biomarker and promising preclinical therapeutic target for genome instability-related diseases.

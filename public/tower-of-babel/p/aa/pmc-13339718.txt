Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance cymoxanil
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Mariano Lopez Romano, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Oriol Magrans, Iris Mangas, Silvia Mazzega, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea, Juan Manuel Parra Morte, Simone Rizzuto, Miguel Santos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authorities of the rapporteur Member State, Lithuania, and the co‐rapporteur Member State, Finland, for the pesticide active substance cymoxanil are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative uses of cymoxanil as a fungicide on potatoes, tomatoes and grapes. Maximum residue levels (MRLs) were assessed in grapes. The reliable end points, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment, are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed. Concerns are reported where identified.

SUMMARY

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659, lays down the procedure for the renewal of the approval of active substances submitted under Article 14 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The list of those substances is established in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 686/2012 as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2016/183. Cymoxanil is one of the active substances listed in that Regulation.

In accordance with Article 1 of Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, the rapporteur Member State (RMS), Lithuania, and co‐rapporteur Member State (co‐RMS), Finland, received an application from Cymoxanil AIR4 Task Force (CTF), Agria SA and Société Financière de Pontarlier (SFP) for the renewal of approval of the active substance cymoxanil. The CTF is composed of the following companies: Belchim, Corteva (formerly DuPont), Indofil, Oxon and United Phosphorus Limited (UPL). On 10 December 2021, the applicant SFP informed the RMS to formally withdraw its application from the European approval process. As a result, the assessments in relation to the representative uses of the plant protection product as a fungicide supported by the applicant SFP have been considered obsolete. In addition, CTF submitted an application for maximum residue levels (MRLs), as referred to in Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on cymoxanil was provided by the RMS in the renewal assessment report (RAR), and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 13 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659. The following conclusions are derived.

The uses of cymoxanil according to the representative uses as a fungicide, with foliar spray application on potatoes, grapes and tomatoes, as proposed at EU level result in sufficient fungicidal efficacy against the target pests.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that needed to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to identity, physical–chemical and technical properties of the active substance and the formulation for representative uses, and analytical methods.

In the area of mammalian toxicology and non‐dietary exposure, an issue not finalised was identified for the compliance of batches with the reference specifications. The phototoxicity and photomutagenicity potential of cymoxanil could not be finalised since the phototoxicity studies provided were not considered as appropriate for UVB absorbers. In view of the inconclusive ED assessment, the discussed health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) cannot be confirmed as valid. Therefore, the dietary and non‐dietary risk assessment cannot be finalised. Finally, the genotoxicity potential and HBGVs setting for some metabolites cannot be concluded.

In the area of residues, the consumer risk assessment could not be finalised due to outstanding data gaps on identity and toxicity of several metabolites and the fact that HBGVs for the active substance could not be considered as valid.

The data available on environmental fate and behaviour are sufficient to carry out the required environmental exposure assessments at EU level for the representative uses.

In the area of ecotoxicology, low risk was concluded for all representative uses regarding aquatic organisms, soil organisms, non‐target terrestrial plants and organisms involved in sewage treatment processes. In contrast, a high long‐term risk to birds and mammals was identified for several uses, and the risk posed by the metabolite IN‐U3204 to birds and mammals could not be finalised. For bees, a low acute risk was concluded for all uses, whereas for chronic risk, a high Tier 1 risk was identified for uses with ‘Cymbal 45’ and the assessment for the representative use on potatoes with ‘Rival Duo’ could not be finalised. For non‐target arthropods other than bees, low risk was concluded for uses with ‘Cymbal 45’, whereas for uses with ‘Rival Duo’ on potato, a high off‐field risk was indicated.

With regard to the assessment of the endocrine disruption (ED) properties, based on the available data and assessment on humans and non‐target organisms, the endocrine disrupting potential of cymoxanil cannot be concluded according to points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605.

BACKGROUND

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, 1 as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659 2 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’), lays down the provisions for the procedure of the renewal of the approval of active substances, submitted under Article 14 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. 3 This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States, the applicant(s) and the public on the initial evaluation provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS) and/or co‐rapporteur Member State (co‐RMS) in the renewal assessment report (RAR), and the organisation of an expert consultation where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 13 of the Regulation, unless formally informed by the European Commission that a conclusion is not necessary, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether the active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria in Article 4 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 within 5 months from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of an additional 3 months where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 13(3). Furthermore, in accordance with Article 13(3a), where the information available in the dossier is not sufficient to conclude the assessment on whether the approval criteria for endocrine disruption are met, additional information can be requested to be submitted in a period of minimum 3 months, but not exceeding 30 months, depending on the type of information requested.

In accordance with Article 1 of the Regulation, the RMS, Lithuania, and co‐RMS, Finland, received an application from Cymoxanil (AIR4) Task Force (CTF), Agria SA and Société Financière de Pontarlier (SFP) for the renewal of approval of the active substance cymoxanil. The CTF is composed of the following companies: Belchim, Corteva (formerly DuPont), Indofil, Oxon and United Phosphorus Limited (UPL). In addition, CTF submitted applications for maximum residue levels (MRLs) as referred to in Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 4 Complying with Article 8 of the Regulation, the RMS checked the completeness of the dossier and informed the applicants, the co‐RMS (Finland), the European Commission and EFSA about the admissibility.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on cymoxanil in the RAR, which was received by EFSA on 28 February 2020 (Lithuania, 2020). The RAR included a proposal to set MRLs, submitted under Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA distributed the RAR to the Member States and the applicants, Cymoxanil (AIR4) Task Force (CTF), Agria SA and Société Financière de Pontarlier (SFP), for consultation and comments on 24 August 2020. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the RAR. EFSA collated and forwarded all comments received to the European Commission on 24 October 2020. At the same time, the collated comments were forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of reporting table. In addition, the applicants were invited to respond to the comments received. The comments and the applicants' response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicants in accordance with Article 13(3) of the Regulation were considered in a telephone conference between EFSA, the RMS and the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) on 2 March 2021. On the basis of the comments received, the applicants' response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicants and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of mammalian toxicology, residues, environmental fate and behaviour, and ecotoxicology.

The outcome of the telephone conference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments, is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

In addition, in accordance with the provisions of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659, following a consultation with Member States in the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconferences 64 on mammalian toxicology (November 2021) and 67 on ecotoxicology (November 2021), the applicants were given the opportunity to submit, within a period of 30 months, additional information to address the approval criteria set out in point 3.6.5 and/or point 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605, 5 and/or documentary evidence demonstrating that cymoxanil may be used such that exposure is negligible, or the conditions for the application of the derogation under Article 4(7) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 are met.

On 10 December 2021, the applicant SFP informed the RMS to formally withdraw its application from the European approval process. As a result, the assessments in relation to the representative uses of the plant protection product as a fungicide supported by the applicant SFP have been considered obsolete.

On 12 June 2024, the applicant CTF submitted further information on the endocrine disruption properties of cymoxanil, which was subsequently evaluated by the RMS. EFSA received the updated RAR with the revised endocrine disruption assessment from the RMS on 26 September 2024. A public consultation on the revised RAR on the endocrine disruption made available after the 30‐month clock stop was conducted between 20 December 2024 and 18 February 2025. All comments received, including from the applicants and Member States, were collated in the format of a reporting table (on the revised RAR on the assessment of the endocrine disrupting properties).

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment and on the proposed MRLs took place with Member States via a written procedure in May 2026.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment of the active substance and the formulations for representative uses, evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of cymoxanil as a fungicide, with foliar spray application on potatoes, tomatoes and grapes, as proposed by the applicants. In accordance with Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, risk mitigation options identified in the RAR and considered during the peer review, if any, are presented in the conclusion. MRLs were assessed in grapes.

A list of the relevant end points for the active substance and the formulations and the proposed MRLs is provided in Appendix B. In addition, the considerations as regards the cut‐off criteria for cymoxanil according to Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 are summarised in Appendix A.

THE ACTIVE SUBSTANCE AND THE FORMULATIONS FOR REPRESENTATIVE USES

Cymoxanil is the ISO common name for (2EZ)‐2‐cyano‐N‐(ethylcarbamoyl)‐2‐(methoxyimino)acetamide. Both E and Z isomers are part of the active substance.

The formulations for representative uses for the evaluation were ‘Cymbal 45’ 7 (code no. BCP 308 F), a wettable dispersible granule formulation (WG) containing 450 g/kg of pure cymoxanil, and ‘Rival Duo’, a suspension concentrate (SC) formulation containing 50 g/L of pure cymoxanil and 400 g/L of pure propamocarb.

The information on the active substances and the formulations for representative uses, including the co‐formulants in these formulations, was considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. One of the components of a co‐formulant in the formulation ‘Rival Duo’ is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (below the level set in Regulation (EU) 2021/383 8 paragraph (14) for acceptable unintentional impurity). 9 One of the co‐formulants in the formulation ‘Cymbal 45’ is currently an approved active substance under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009. 10 The dose at which the substance is present as co‐formulant is not expected to exert any pesticidal activity. 11

Propamocarb was considered as a second active substance in ‘Rival Duo’ in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. 12

Details on the composition of the formulations cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009; however, this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review. A proposal for classification of the formulations according to Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 13 was provided by the applicant and assessed by the RMS (please see Volumes 3 CP of the RAR).

The representative uses evaluated for the formulation ‘Cymbal 45’ were foliar spray applications on grapes, potatoes and tomatoes (field, greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent protected structures) as fungicide against Phytophthora infestans and Plasmopara viticola. The representative uses evaluated for the formulation ‘Rival Duo’ were foliar spray applications on potatoes as fungicide against Phytophthora infestans. Full details of the GAPs can be found in the list of end points in Appendix B.

Data were submitted to conclude that the representative uses of cymoxanil proposed at EU level result in a sufficient fungicidal effect following the guidance document SANCO/2012/11251‐rev. 4 (European Commission, 2014).

IDENTITY, PHYSICAL/CHEMICAL/TECHNICAL PROPERTIES AND METHODS OF ANALYSIS

The following guidance documents were followed in the production of this conclusion: European Commission (2000a, 2000b, 2010a).

The proposed reference specifications for cymoxanil are based on batch data from industrial scale production. There was not an agreement between the CTF and Agria on a common reference specification of the technical materials. Currently, there are two EU reference specifications for cymoxanil. For the renewal, the RMS and the applicants proposed two separate updated EU reference specifications, one for the CTF with minimum purity of the active substance as manufactured of 970 g/kg (E:Z isomer ratio 99:1) and one for Agria with minimum purity of the active substance as manufactured of 985 g/kg (E:Z isomer ratio 99.99:0.01). It is noted that the toxicological relevance of some impurities was not finalised (see Section 2); hence, additional data consisting of spectral data, content of the impurity(ies) before and after storage of the formulation(s) and method for its analysis in the formulation(s) might be required. Nitrosamines, specified at 1 mg/kg, are considered new relevant impurities for both proposed reference specifications (see Section 2). A data gap was set for 5‐batch data for the content of nitrosamines (relevant for Corteva, member of the CTF) (data gap, see Section 10). It is proposed to update the current EU reference specifications, as the specification proposed by one of the applicants contains additional significant impurities, and nitrosamines is a new relevant impurity. The current and the proposed reference specifications cannot be concluded as sufficiently supported by the test material used in (eco)toxicity studies (see Sections 2 and 5).

The proposed minimum purities of 970 and 985 g/kg met the requirements of the FAO specification, developed under the new procedure, for cymoxanil 419/TC (March 2006) of not less than 970 g/kg.

The main data regarding the identity of cymoxanil and its physical and chemical properties are given in Appendix B. Data gaps for the n‐octanol/water partition coefficient of all the metabolites that are components of the residue definition for risk assessment, and for viscosity data for the neat formulation ‘Rival Duo’ were set (data gaps, see Section 10). A data gap was also set for the content of nitrosamines before and after storage of the formulations, and for methods for nitrosamine analysis in the formulations (data gap, see Section 10).

Sufficient methods are available for the generation of data required for the risk assessment. Appropriate methods of analysis are available for the determination of the active substance and impurities in the technical material and for the determination of the active substance in the formulations for representative uses. A data gap relevant to the analysis of Z‐isomer in the Agria batches was set (data gap, see Section 10). Appropriate liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) method is available for monitoring cymoxanil in food and feed of plant origin, with limits of quantification (LOQs) of 0.01 mg/kg in high‐water content, high‐acid content, high‐oil content and dry commodities. A data gap was set for Agria for a primary monitoring method in high‐oil content, high‐acid content and dry commodities, and for an independent laboratory validation (ILV) for all commodity groups (data gap, see Section 10). Extraction procedure used in the methods was not verified; however, not required as for high‐water commodities, residues ≥ 0.01 mg/kg were not observed as a result of representative uses, and high‐oil content and dry commodities are not covered by intended uses. A data gap was set for CTF for data to demonstrate the extraction efficiency of the procedure for high‐acid content commodities (data gap, see Section 10). A residue definition for monitoring in food of animal origin was not proposed; therefore, relevant monitoring methods are not required. The residues of cymoxanil in soil can be monitored with LC‐MS/MS methods with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (CTF) and 0.05 mg/kg (Agria). Appropriate LC‐MS/MS methods are available for monitoring residues of cymoxanil and IN‐KQ960 in groundwater, drinking water and surface water with LOQs of 0.1 μg/L; however, a data gap was set for an ILV for cymoxanil in drinking water for Agria (data gap, see Section 10). Residues of cymoxanil in air can be monitored by HPLC/UV (LOQ of 0.17 μg/m3).

Residues of cymoxanil in body tissues can be monitored by LC‐MS/MS with LOQs of 0.01 mg/kg. Residues of cymoxanil in body fluids (plasma) can be determined by the LC‐MS/MS method with LOQ of 0.05 mg/L. A data gap was set for CTF and Agria for a method to monitor IN‐W3595 in body fluids and tissues and for Agria for a method to monitor cymoxanil in body fluids (data gap, see Section 10).

MAMMALIAN TOXICITY

The toxicological profile of the active substance cymoxanil and its metabolites was discussed at the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 64 and Teleconference 190 in November 2021 and November 2025, respectively. The assessment is based on the following guidance documents: European Commission (2003 and 2012), EFSA (2014), EFSA PPR Panel (2012).

Regarding the proposed reference specification, insufficient data are provided to conclude on the toxicological relevance of the impurities leading to an issue not finalised for the assessment of the compliance of the toxicological batches with the reference specification for all applicants (data gap, see Section 9.1). The test material used in toxicity studies is not fully representative of the original and newly proposed reference specifications for the active substance and associated impurities. Nitrosamines are relevant impurities and their level has to remain below 1 mg/kg; this level was not exceeded where analysed, but not all applicants provided a validated analysis.

The analytical methods used in feed, body fluids and tissues, air and any additional matrices used in support of the toxicity studies are overall considered fit‐for‐purpose.

The oral absorption of cymoxanil is estimated to account for 73% 19 of the administered single low dose of 2.5 mg/kg body weight (bw), based on urinary and biliary excretion, cage wash and liver and kidney residues within 48 hours. In the rat, cymoxanil is widely distributed throughout the body, with higher residue levels being reached in kidneys and liver. There was no evidence of accumulation.

Cymoxanil is extensively metabolised mainly to polar fractions containing bound glycine conjugated with endogenous substances. A major metabolite IN‐W3595 was identified in urine, faeces and bile.

IN‐W3595 is the major metabolite in all species (mouse, rat, rabbit, dog) in a comparative in vitro metabolism study, making up ≥ 30% of total radioactivity, with no major inter‐species differences observed and no unique human metabolites identified. Based on these results, the residue definition for body fluids and tissues should include, in addition to cymoxanil, the metabolite IN‐W3595.

Based on acute toxicity studies, cymoxanil has a harmonised classification as Acute Tox 4 by the oral route (H302). It is neither a skin nor eye irritant and it is classified as a skin sensitiser (Skin Sens 1, H317). 20

Potential phototoxicity and photomutagenicity of cymoxanil cannot be concluded since in vitro phototoxicity tests were performed above 320 nm, while the test material absorbs mainly at wavelengths below 325 nm. A data gap was identified for testing cymoxanil at adequate wavelengths leading to an issue that could not be finalised (see Section 9.1). 21

Short‐term oral toxicity studies were provided for rats, mice and dogs; rats and dogs were the most sensitive species. In rats, the target organs were the testes resulting in a no‐observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of 6.54 mg/kg bw per day 22 (90‐day rat study). In dogs, adverse effects included clinical signs, reduced body weight, body weight gain and food consumption, haematological changes, histological changes in testes, epididymis, thymus and eyes that resulted in an overall NOAEL of 2.8 mg/kg bw per day (1‐year dog study). 23 No local or systemic adverse effects were observed in a 28‐day dermal toxicity study in rats up to the limit dose of 1000 mg/kg bw per day. Cymoxanil is classified as STOT RE 2, H373 24 based on effects on blood system, thymus and eyes.

Based on the available genotoxicity studies, the substance is unlikely to be genotoxic in vitro. 25

After long‐term exposure in rats, target organs for toxicity included testes, eyes, lungs, liver and sciatic nerve degeneration resulting in a NOAEL of 4.08 mg/kg bw per day. 26 In mice, target organs were the liver, epididymis, stomach and duodenum resulting in a NOAEL of 4.19 mg/kg bw per day. Cymoxanil showed no treatment‐related tumours in rats or mice and was concluded unlikely to be carcinogenic for humans.

With regard to reproductive toxicity studies, reduction in live‐born pups, in the mean number of corpora lutea and implantations and increase in post‐implantation losses resulted in a reproductive toxicity NOAEL of 31.6 mg/kg bw per day. The same parental toxicity NOAEL of 31.6 mg/kg bw per day is based on reduced body weight and reduced food consumption during premating, gestation and lactation. The offspring NOAEL is 10.5 mg/kg bw per day based on reduced body weight (in the absence of parental toxicity). 27

With respect to foetal development in rats, increased incidences of skeletal variations, delayed ossification and increased incidences of skeletal malformations were observed together with reduced maternal body weight gain and food consumption, resulting in a maternal and developmental toxicity NOAEL of 10 mg/kg bw per day. In rabbits, clinical observations (anorexia) and decreased body weight gain were observed in dams with a NOAEL of 8 mg/kg bw per day; at this dose level, increased incidences of skeletal malformations were observed in foetuses resulting in a developmental lowest observable adverse effect level (LOAEL) of 8 mg/kg bw per day. 28

Accordingly, the harmonised classification Repr. 2, H361fd was established under the CLP Regulation. 29

In a combined neurotoxicity study with a 90‐day toxicity study in rats, a NOAEL for general toxicity was set at 6.54 mg/kg bw per day based on spermatid degeneration and increased testes weight; no signs of neurotoxicity were observed up to the highest dose tested of 224 mg/kg bw per day. No evidence of developmental neurotoxicity was seen in rats up to 100 mg/kg bw per day.

Regarding immunotoxicity, cymoxanil did not show potential for immunotoxic effects in two 28‐day immunotoxicity studies in rats and mice. Thymus findings (atrophy and lymphoid depletion, associated with reduced weight) were observed at ≥ LOAEL in the 90‐day study in dogs (9.7 mg/kg bw per day). These were concluded to be secondary to the systemic toxicity (i.e. exacerbation of the physiological involution by stress) and unlikely a direct substance specific effect. 30

The health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) were discussed pending the conclusion on the ED assessment. 31 However, in view of the inconclusive ED assessment (see Sections 6 and 9.1 ), the HBGVs cannot be confirmed as valid. Hereafter, the HBGVs discussed by the experts (indicated as tentative) are reported for information: the tentative acceptable daily intake (ADI) is 0.027 mg/kg bw per day, based on the developmental LOAEL of 8 mg/kg bw per day for increased incidences of visceral/external/skeletal malformations and variations in a developmental toxicity study in rabbits, and applying an uncertainty factor (UF) of 300 (including a default UF of 100 and an extra UF of 3 for the LOAEL to NOAEL extrapolation). This is supported by the NOAEL of 2.8 mg/kg bw per day from a 1‐year toxicity study in dogs. This value differs from the previous peer review (EFSA, 2008a; European Commission, 2010b) where an ADI of 0.013 mg/kg bw per day was based on a 1‐year toxicity study in dogs since the respective NOAEL was revised at a higher level in the current assessment. The tentative acute reference dose (ARfD) is 0.027 mg/kg bw, based on the same point of departure and UF as the tentative ADI. This value differs from the previous peer review (EFSA, 2008a) where an ARfD of 0.08 mg/kg bw was based on a developmental toxicity study in rabbits since the NOAEL of this study was revised as a LOAEL in the current assessment. The tentative acceptable operator exposure level (AOEL) and acute AOEL (AAOEL) are 0.02 mg/kg bw per day, based on the same Reference Point and UF as the tentative ADI and ARfD, respectively, and applying a correction factor for limited oral absorption of 73%. The proposed tentative AOEL differs from the previous peer review (EFSA, 2008a) where an AOEL of 0.01 mg/kg bw per day was based on a 1‐year toxicity study in dogs, applying an UF of 100 and correction factor of 75% (since the NOAEL of this study and oral absorption values were revised in the current assessment). Due to the lack of valid HBGVs, the dietary and non‐dietary risk assessment cannot be concluded (see Section 9.1).

Dermal absorption of cymoxanil 32 in the ‘Cymbal 45’ formulation has been assessed in an in vitro study with human skin. The dermal absorption values to be used for risk assessment are 0.056% for the concentrate, 13% for the 1:3000 spray dilution, 16% for the 1:3750 spray dilution and 19% for the 1:4500 spray dilution. For the ‘Rival Duo’ formulation, default values of 50% for both concentrate and dilution are applicable.

Considering that the no valid HBGVs could be established, EFSA is presenting below the hypothetical non‐dietary risk assessment for risk managers' information only. It is noted that the RMS included the non‐dietary exposure estimates in the RAR. For the ‘Cymbal 45’ formulation, the non‐dietary exposure estimates 33 for the operators are below the tentative (A)AOEL with the use of gloves during mixing/loading and application (MLA) (in addition to standard workwear), for the application on potatoes and tomatoes (indoor and outdoor) while wearing hood and visor (MLA) is also required for the use on grapevines. For the workers, the use of workwear is sufficiently protective during the inspection and harvesting activities in potatoes and tomatoes, respectively, according to the EFSA calculator and applying refined DFR and DT50 values, while a re‐entry of 3 days is needed for workers re‐entering grapes. For residents and bystanders, the exposure estimates are below the tentative (A)AOEL for tomatoes and potatoes, while the use on grapes triggers an exceedance of the tentative AAOEL for bystander children, even applying drift reduction and 10 m buffer zone.

For the ‘Rival Duo’ formulation, the non‐dietary exposure estimates for the operators are below the tentative (A)AOEL with the use of gloves during MLA, plus hood and visor during mixing/loading (ML). The worker exposure estimates are below the tentative AOEL with refined DT50 and DFR values. The resident and bystander exposure estimates are below the tentative (A)AOEL with drift reduction, buffer zone of 10 m, refined DT50 and DFR values. The combined exposure with propamocarb is exceeding the tentative (A)AOEL for all categories (operators, workers, bystanders and residents) (see Table 6).

Toxicological information is provided for metabolites IN‐KP533, M‐5 (AS999), IN‐T4226, IN‐KQ960, IN‐U3204, IN‐JX915, IN‐R3273 and IN‐W3595.

The genotoxic potential could not be concluded for IN‐JX915, IN‐R3273 and IN‐T4226 (data gap, see Section 9.1). For IN‐U3204 (claimed to correspond to M10, as described in Section 3), data gaps were identified both for genotoxicity and general toxicity (data gap, see Section 9.1). RESIDUES

The assessment in the residue section is based on the following guidance documents: European Commission (2019).

Cymoxanil was discussed at the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 66 in November 2021.

Representative use residues

The metabolism of cymoxanil was investigated upon foliar application of radiolabelled [2‐14C]cymoxanil in valid studies with grapes, tomatoes (fruit crops), potatoes (root crops) and lettuce (leafy crops) which cover largely the critical GAPs for the representative uses. Cymoxanil is recovered only in mature grapes and lettuce. In tomato and potato, it is completely metabolised and the major part of the radioactivity is incorporated predominantly into natural constituents such as glycine and starch/sugars. In grape and lettuce, the two major metabolites, in relative and absolute amounts, were IN‐KP533 and IN‐W3595. The metabolite M10 occurred in significant absolute and relative levels in mature grape bunches. It is claimed to be metabolite IN‐U3204 which was detected in low levels in another supplementary grape study. No convincing evidence was provided to support this claim and a data gap is set for a full elucidation of the structure of M10, after which its genotoxicity and general toxicity should be addressed (see Sections 2 and 9.1). The photolytic metabolites IN‐R3273, IN‐JX915 and IN‐T4226 were only reported in the recent greenhouse study with grapes but not in any of the outdoor studies. Given their high absolute amounts found at 1N rate, their formation under outdoor conditions cannot be fully excluded. EFSA is of the opinion that GAP compliant residue field trials analysing for these photolysis metabolites would be necessary and their genotoxicity potential should be addressed (see Sections 2 and 9.1). The tentatively identified metabolite ‘AS999/glycine‐related’ is reported at high absolute and relative amounts in lettuce. Although not relevant for the proposed representative uses, a full elucidation of its structure is needed as it might impact the residue definition for risk assessment for leafy crops which are noted under Article 12 MRL (EFSA, 2015, 2019b) (data gap, see Section 10). The metabolite IN‐KQ960 was found in minor amounts in lettuce. Based on these findings, it is proposed to change the current residue definition which is cymoxanil into a general plant residue definition for risk assessment as ‘Sum of cymoxanil, IN‐W3595, IN‐KP533, expressed as cymoxanil’ following foliar application. This proposal is provisional and should be reconsidered especially for a potential inclusion of the compounds for which data gaps were identified (see above and Section 2). As cymoxanil was recovered in all crops except in potato the plant residue definition for monitoring remains unchanged ‘cymoxanil’ for all crops following foliar treatment. Although not required, a rotational crop metabolism study was presented. A sufficient number of residue field trials for all representative uses analysing only for cymoxanil is available. Therefore, a complete set of valid residue field trials for all the representative uses analysing for all compounds included in the residue definition for risk assessment is requested (data gap, see Section 9.1). Quantifiable residues of cymoxanil in grapes and tomato trigger nature of residue study in processed commodities. The required study should also address the stability/fate of the compounds currently included in the residue definition for risk assessment (data gap, see Section 9.1).

The dietary burden calculation does not trigger animal studies for the use in potato, the only of the representative uses resulting as feed item. It should be noted that the calculation considers only cymoxanil and not the two metabolites included in the residue definition. The calculation should be updated once the results from the residue field trials become available. The results from the potato metabolism study do not suggest that the results of the current calculation will significantly change. A valid metabolism study with ruminates revealed as major residue in milk: lactose and fatty acid, and in liver and kidney: formic acid, hence cymoxanil was not present in any of the tissues and milk. An additional study demonstrated that cymoxanil is rapidly and extensively degraded in rumen fluid, suggesting that residues of the parent are not expected in animal tissues. This study is applicable only to ruminants and cannot be extrapolated to pigs due to the use of rumen‐fluid–specific experiments. It is also noted that the fate of the two metabolites included in the plant residue definition for risk assessment could not be addressed by this metabolism study. Currently, residue definitions for animals are not needed (see also EFSA, 2015, 2019b).

Results from ecotoxicological studies indicated that residues of cymoxanil can reach concentrations up to 0.95 mg/kg in aerial parts (nectar). Therefore, a data gap is set for a study determining the magnitude of pesticide residues in honey after application to grapes 35 (data gap, see Section 10).

The consumer risk assessment for the representative uses cannot be finalised because the HBGVs for cymoxanil are only tentatively set (see Section 9.1). In addition, the plant residue definition for risk assessment is provisional, residue data for IN‐W3595 and IN‐KP533 are missing and further data gaps prevent finalisation of the consumer risk assessment (see Section 9.1).

It is noted that the formulation ‘Rival Duo’ proposed for use in potatoes contains propamocarb hydrochloride as a second active substance. Data on residues were not presented in the RAR of cymoxanil. In the previous EFSA evaluation of propamocarb (EFSA, 2006), residues were reported to be below the LOQ of the method of analysis (0.1 mg/kg) when employing a similar GAP (6 applications resulting in a maximum total dose of 6.498 kg per hectare) to potatoes.

Maximum residue levels

The currently applicable MRL for table and wine grapes is 0.05 mg/kg. 36

This level was recommended by EFSA following the evaluation of confirmatory data under the Article 12 MRL review for cymoxanil, based on four additional SEU wine‐grape residue trials (EFSA, 2019b). The SEU residue trials suggest an MRL of 0.02 mg/kg as the results from all trials were below the LOQ except one trial in SEU resulted in a residue level of 0.015 mg/kg. Considering that the MRL proposal from the NEU residue trials for grapes evaluated in the MRL review was higher (at LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg) than the one from the SEU (0.02 mg/kg), a revision of the existing MRL in table and wine grapes from 0.3 to 0.05 mg/kg was appropriate.

The applicant CTF submitted an application supported by SEU and NEU grape residue field trials with an identical PHI of 28 days to substantiate an MRL request of 0.3 mg/kg. In one of the trials in the SEU, a quantifiable residue of 0.19 mg/kg is reported. Therefore, the request by the applicant for an MRL at 0.3 mg/kg is supported by data. Furthermore, it is noted that SFP withdrew its application which included also residue data for grapes with all residues for cymoxanil in the NEU zone of < 0.02 mg/kg (LOQ).

A dietary risk assessment for an MRL of 0.3 mg/kg in grapes, as well as an Article 12 MRL screening assessment covering all authorised uses, cannot be performed because the HBGVs for cymoxanil are only tentatively set. In addition, the plant residue definition for risk assessment is provisional and residue data for metabolites IN‐W3595 and IN‐KP533 are missing (see Sections 2 and 3.1).

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

Cymoxanil was discussed at the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 65 in November 2021.

Cymoxanil consists of the E and Z‐isomer forms; however, the Z‐isomer was never detected in the environmental fate and behaviour studies.

The rates of dissipation and degradation in the environmental matrices investigated were estimated using FOCUS (2006) kinetics guidance. In soil laboratory incubations under aerobic conditions in the dark, cymoxanil exhibited very low to low persistence, forming the major (> 10% applied radioactivity (AR)) metabolites IN‐KQ960 (max. 12% AR) and IN‐W3595 (max. 18.1% AR) exhibiting very low to low persistence, IN‐U3204 (max. 24.7% AR) and IN‐T4226 (max. 11.7% AR) exhibiting very low persistence. Mineralisation of cyanoacetamide‐2‐14C radiolabel to carbon dioxide accounted for 66% AR after 120 days. The formation of unextractable residues of this radiolabel (not extracted by acetonitrile/water) accounted for 47% AR after 120 days. In laboratory soil photolysis studies, cymoxanil degraded more rapidly than in the dark control forming the major metabolite IN‐JX915 (max. 11% AR) which exhibited very low persistence. Cymoxanil exhibited high to very high mobility in soil. Metabolites IN‐KQ960, IN‐W3595, IN‐U3204, IN‐T4226 and IN‐JX915 exhibited very high mobility. Member State experts concluded that the adsorption of cymoxanil and all metabolites formed in soil was not pH dependent. 37

In a lysimeter study of 2‐year duration, the mean annual concentration of cymoxanil was < 0.1 μg/L. Metabolites IN‐U3204, IN‐T4226, IN‐R3273 and oxalic acid were found to reach a maximum annual average concentration of < 0.01 μg/L, 0.02 μg/L, 0.01 μg/L and 0.03 μg/L, respectively. No other known metabolites were detected in any leachate sample.

In laboratory incubations in dark aerobic natural sediment water systems, cymoxanil exhibited very low to low persistence, forming the major metabolites IN‐U3204 (max. 25% AR in water, exhibiting very low to low persistence), IN‐KQ960 (max. 13% AR in water, exhibiting moderate to very high persistence), IN‐W3595 (max. 23% AR in water, exhibiting low to high persistence), IN‐JX915 (max. 10% AR in water, exhibiting very low to low persistence), IN‐T4226 (max. 11% AR in water, exhibiting low persistence), IN‐KP533 (max. 21% AR in water, exhibiting low to moderate persistence), IN‐R3273 (max. 6% AR in water, exhibiting low to moderate persistence), IN‐R3274 (max. 29% AR in water, exhibiting low to moderate persistence), AS999 (max. 29% AR in water, exhibiting low to medium persistence). The unextractable sediment fraction (not extracted by acetonitrile/water) the cyanoacetamide‐2‐14C radiolabel accounted for 16%–26% AR at study end (64–101 days). Mineralisation of this radiolabel accounted for 29%–76% AR at the end of the study. The rate of decline of cymoxanil in a laboratory sterile aqueous photolysis experiment was slower than that which occurred in the aerobic sediment water incubations. Chromatographically resolved components accounting for > 10% AR were metabolites IN‐JX915 (max. 53% AR) and IN‐R3273 (max. 36% AR).

The necessary surface water and sediment exposure assessments (Predicted environmental concentrations (PEC) calculations) were carried out for the metabolites IN‐U3204, IN‐KQ960, IN‐W3595, IN‐JX915, IN‐T4226, IN‐R3273, IN‐R3274, IN‐KP533 and AS999 using the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2001) step 1 and step 2 approach (version 3.2 of the Steps 1–2 in FOCUS calculator). For the active substance cymoxanil, appropriate step 3 (FOCUS, 2001) and step 4 calculations were available. 38 The step 4 calculations appropriately followed the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2007) guidance, with no‐spray drift buffer zones of up to 10 m being implemented for the drainage scenarios (representing a 78%–85% spray drift reduction), and combined no‐spray buffer zones with vegetative buffer strips of up to 10 m (reducing solute flux in run‐off by 80% and erosion run‐off of mass adsorbed to soil by 95%) being implemented for the run‐off scenarios. The SWAN tool (version 5.0.1) was appropriately used to implement these mitigation measures in the simulations. However, risk managers and others may wish to note that while run‐off mitigation is included in the step 4 calculations available, the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2007) report acknowledges that, for substances with KFoc < 2000 mL/g (i.e. cymoxanil), the general applicability and effectiveness of run‐off mitigation measures had been less clearly demonstrated in the available scientific literature, than for more strongly adsorbed compounds.

The necessary groundwater exposure assessments were appropriately carried out using FOCUS (European Commission, 2014) scenarios and the models PEARL 4.4.4, PELMO 5.5.3 and MACRO 5.5.4 39 for cymoxanil and its metabolites. The potential for groundwater exposure from the representative uses by cymoxanil above the parametric drinking water limit of 0.1 μg/L was concluded to be low in geoclimatic situations that are represented by all nine FOCUS groundwater scenarios.

The applicant (CTF) provided appropriate information to address the effect of water treatment processes on the nature of the residues that might be present in surface water and groundwater, when surface water or groundwater are abstracted for drinking water.

The PEC in soil, surface water, sediment and groundwater covering the representative uses assessed can be found in Appendix B of this conclusion. A key to the wording used to describe the persistence and mobility of the compounds assessed can be found in Appendix D of this conclusion.

The risk assessment was based on the following documents: European Commission (2002a and 2002b), SETAC (2001), EFSA (2009, 2013) and EFSA PPR Panel (2013).

Several aspects pertaining to the risk assessment of cymoxanil were discussed at the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 67 in November 2021 and Teleconference 193 in December 2025.

The formulation for representative uses ‘Rival Duo’ contains a second active substance. A data gap was identified for an additive risk assessment for all taxa for which a formulation risk assessment is not available (data gap, see Section 10). 40

The batches used in the ecotoxicity studies could not be considered fully representative of the original and the newly proposed reference specification for the active substance and associated impurities (relevant for CTF). This is because impurity levels were not provided in most of the batches used (data gap, see Section 10).

The representative greenhouse uses on tomatoes with ‘Cymbal 45’ cover both permanent greenhouses and non‐permanent protected structures. Suitable acute, short‐term and reproductive data with cymoxanil were available for birds. In addition, an acute study was available with the formulation for representative uses ‘Rival Duo’. The geomean of the available short‐term toxicity studies was used for the acute risk assessment. 41 Low acute risk at the screening step was concluded for all representative uses with ‘Cymbal 45’ and ‘Rival Duo’, whereas a high long‐term risk was indicated at that step for all uses with both formulations. At Tier 1, low long‐term risk could be concluded for the uses on grapes and potatoes. Based on the available data and risk assessment, low risk was concluded for all intended uses with ‘Cymbal 45’ and ‘Rival Duo’ formulations at Tier 1.

A screening risk assessment for the metabolites was performed for non‐target soil macro‐ and meso‐fauna, assuming 10‐fold increased toxicity of the metabolites compared to the parent substance. For all representative uses, low risk from metabolites was concluded.

Suitable ecotoxicity tests on the active substance were available to assess the risk for soil micro‐organisms. Since no toxicity data were available to assess the risk for the metabolites of cymoxanil, a screening risk assessment was carried out by assuming a 10‐fold increased toxicity of the metabolites compared to the parent substance. On the basis of the available toxicity data, low risk was concluded for all the representative uses under assessment.

Toxicity data on the active substance and the formulations for representative uses were available to evaluate the risk for terrestrial non‐target plants. Based on the available data and risk assessment, low risk was concluded for all the representative uses under assessment.

The risk to organisms involved in sewage treatment processes was also concluded to be low for all the representative uses under assessment.

ENDOCRINE DISRUPTION PROPERTIES

The endocrine disrupting properties (ED) of cymoxanil for human and non‐target organisms were discussed in the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 64 (November 2021) and Teleconference 189 (November 2025).

The assessment of the endocrine disruption (ED) potential of cymoxanil for humans was carried out according to the ECHA/EFSA guidance (EFSA, 2018). In determining whether cymoxanil interacts with the oestrogen, androgen and steroidogenesis (EAS) or thyroid (T) mediated pathways, consideration was given to the number and type of effects induced, as well as the magnitude and pattern of responses observed across studies. Particularly, the conditions under which effects occur were considered, notably whether endocrine‐related responses occurred at dose(s) that also resulted in overt toxicity. Overall, the assessment provides a weight‐of‐evidence analysis using the available evidence in the data set to determine potential interaction of cymoxanil with the EAS and T signalling pathways.

For the T‐modality, a pattern of T‐mediated adversity was not identified in a sufficiently investigated data set. Therefore, the ED criteria for humans for the T‐modality are not met (Scenario 1a of the ECHA/EFSA ED Guidance (2018) is applicable).

For the EAS‐modalities, no EAS‐mediated adversity was observed. However, EAS‐mediated parameters were not sufficiently investigated (i.e. lack of OECD TG 416, version from 2001, or OECD TG 443) ruling out the possibility to exclude other potential EAS‐mediated adverse effects.

The endocrine mediated activity was sufficiently investigated and negative for the E modality (i.e. ER ToxCast model) and for the A modality (i.e. OECD TG 441 Hershberger Assay and supportive negative AR ToxCast model). For the S‐modality, in the absence of valid level 2 studies, 56 the ED potential of cymoxanil cannot be concluded. In addition to clarifications received within the proper timeline, the applicant CTF submitted updated documents after the regulatory timelines; consequently, this information could not be considered within the current peer review process. Nevertheless, in the interest of completeness, EFSA reviewed all the submitted information and concluded that the issues identified initially in the available study were not addressed or dismissed.

The outcome of the assessment for humans applies also to wild mammals as non‐target organisms.

For non‐target organisms other than mammals, an Amphibian Metamorphosis Assay (AMA, OECD TG 231) and a Fish Short Term Reproduction Assay (FSTRA, OECD TG 229) were available. Both were discussed at the Pesticide Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 67 (November 2021) and Teleconference 189 (November 2025).

Regarding the T‐modality, the AMA was considered reliable and negative. 57 Therefore, cymoxanil does not met the ED criteria for non‐target organisms for the T‐modality.

Regarding the EAS‐modalities, the FSTRA was considered reliable and some of the effects observed (i.e. histopathological findings in male gonads) were considered likely treatment related. 58 However, the available information did not allow to differentiate whether those effects are ED‐mediated or direct effects on target organs. Therefore, when also considering the lack of reliable in vitro data for the A‐ and S‐modalities, it was not possible to draw a conclusion on the ED properties through the EAS‐modalities.

Based on the available information on humans and non‐target organisms, the assessment of the endocrine disruption potential of cymoxanil according to points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605, could not be finalised (see Section 9.1).

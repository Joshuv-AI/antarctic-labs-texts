Estimation of median effective doses and 95% effective doses of oliceridine combined with propofol for day-case hysteroscopy under total intravenous anesthesia with preserved spontaneous breathing: an up-and-down sequential allocation trial
Authors: Jiliang He, Yijun Ma, Keyi Wang, Hao Zhou, Ce Chen, Mengjun Chen, Zhongen Shen, Weifeng Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOliceridine is a novel developed G protein-biased µ-opioid receptor agonist, offering a significant reduction in the risk of opioid-related respiratory depression and a lower incidence of gastrointestinal adverse effects. This sequential dose-finding study was conducted to determine the median effective doses (ED₅₀) and 95% effective doses (ED₉₅) of oliceridine coadministered with propofol during day-case hysteroscopy under total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) with preserved spontaneous breathing (PSB), thereby supporting the optimization of clinical anesthesia protocols.MethodsIn the study, 31 women undergoing day-case hysteroscopy were included. All patients were given a specific dose of oliceridine combined with propofol (2.5 mg/kg). A modified Dixon up-and-down sequential approach was adopted, with the starting dose of oliceridine set at 1.5 mg. The dosage of oliceridine was adjusted according to the response of the previous patient: a positive response led to an increased dose, while a negative response prompted a decrease, with a fixed dose ratio of 1:1.2. A positive response was defined as the occurrence of involuntary movement, frowning, or an MOAA/S score > 1 during cervical dilatation. The formal trial commenced from the first crossover and continued until seven crossovers were observed. Using the probit model, the ED₅₀ and ED₉₅ with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were calculated. Secondary outcome measures included the time to anesthesia emergence, the VAS scores at PACU entry and discharge, the incidence of PONV, and the number of patients requiring analgesics in the PACU.ResultsFor day-case hysteroscopy, the estimated ED₅₀ and ED₉₅ of oliceridine in combination with 2.5 mg/kg propofol were 1.309 mg (95% CI: 1.165–1.477 mg) and 1.675 mg (95% CI: 1.497–2.508 mg), respectively. All patients maintained spontaneous respiration throughout the procedure, and no episodes of respiratory depression were observed. The mean time to anesthesia emergence ranged from 3 to 4 min. The VAS scores at PACU entry and discharge remained below 3 in all patients, and no supplemental analgesics were required. No gastrointestinal adverse events were observed in the PACU.ConclusionsGiven its lower incidence of opioid-related adverse effects, oliceridine combined with propofol may offer a promising and well-tolerated approach for day-case hysteroscopy under TIVA with PSB. Background

Hysteroscopy is a fundamental minimally invasive technique in gynecology for both diagnosis and treatment, and is currently considered the gold standard for evaluating uterine pathology [1]. The majority of hysteroscopic procedures are performed in day-case settings, characterized by short operative durations and efficient turnover [2]. Consequently, the optimal anesthetic strategy for day-case hysteroscopy should facilitate rapid recovery from anesthesia while minimizing postoperative complications, thereby supporting same-day discharge in alignment with the principles of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS). Total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) with preserved spontaneous breathing (PSB) provides the benefits of fast anesthetic emergence and prompt postoperative recovery [3]. Within the framework of ERAS, this approach has therefore gained increasing acceptance and popularity for day-case hysteroscopy.

Propofol remains the cornerstone of TIVA and is typically administered in combination with opioids [4]. However, when coadministered with propofol, conventional opioids can induce significant respiratory depression, which restricts their applicability for TIVA with PSB. In addition, opioid-related adverse effects, particularly postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), may substantially compromise rapid recovery and prolong hospital discharge [5].

Oliceridine, a novel developed G protein-biased µ-opioid receptor agonist, provides potent analgesia with attenuated opioid-related respiratory depression and lower incidence of gastrointestinal side effects through selective G protein signaling [6]. Therefore, oliceridine may theoretically offer a more suitable alternative to conventional opioids for TIVA with PSB in day-case surgery. However, the dose-response profile of the oliceridine-propofol combination in day-case hysteroscopy remains undefined, and robust clinical evidence is currently lacking. Determining the optimal dose of oliceridine coadministered with propofol for day-case hysteroscopy performed under TIVA with PSB is beneficial for promoting postoperative recovery and improving clinical efficiency, thereby aligning with the principles of ERAS.

Therefore, this study aimed to estimate the ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine coadministered with propofol for day-case hysteroscopy under TIVA with PSB, thereby providing evidence to support the optimization of anesthetic regimens.

Methods

Approval for the study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Affiliated Cixi Hospital, Wenzhou Medical University, on November 8, 2024. The trial was subsequently registered in the China Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400094657) on December 25, 2024. The study was conducted at this hospital between March and April 2025, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Female patients (18 to 65 years), classified as American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–II and scheduled for operative hysteroscopy, were estimated for eligibility. The study excluded participants who met any of the following conditions: a body mass index (BMI) ≥ 25 kg/m², a history of severe cardiopulmonary disease, renal or hepatic insufficiency, or any known allergy or contraindication to opioids.

Prior to hysteroscopy, all patients maintained preoperative fasting and drinking according to standard guidelines. Upon admission to the operating theatre, an intravenous line was placed, and routine monitoring was initiated, including non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP), heart rate (HR), continuous electrocardiogram (ECG), and pulse oxygen saturation (SpO₂). The patients’ level of consciousness was evaluated using the Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness and Sedation (MOAA/S) score [7, 8] (Table 1).

Before induction of anesthesia, patients were preoxygenated with 5 L/min of oxygen via a face mask for two minutes. Subsequently, the induction of anesthesia was commenced with a bolus of intravenous oliceridine at the assigned dose, followed three minutes later by intravenous propofol at 2.5 mg/kg. Once the patient’s MOAA/S score reached 0, a vaginal speculum was inserted, and cervical dilation was performed.

The modified Dixon’s up-and-down sequential method was used to calculate the ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine in the study. During the induction phase of anesthesia, the initial oliceridine dose of 1.5 mg was determined based on the prescribing information, our prior clinical experience, and pre-experimental results. Dosage adjustments for subsequent patients were determined based on the response observed in the previous one. A positive response was defined as the patient exhibiting involuntary movements, frown, or an MOAA/S score >1 point during the performance of cervical dilation, which resulted in an increased dose for the next patient [9, 10]. Conversely, a negative response resulted in a dose reduction. Dose adjustments were made according to a fixed ratio of 1:1.2. The primary testing phase commenced after the first crossover in patient responses and ended after seven crossovers. If a negative response occurred, an additional intravenous bolus of propofol (1 mg/kg) was administered to deepen anesthesia, which could be repeated until the hysteroscopy was completed. Based on their results, patients were categorised as either the “positive response” or “negative response” group.

After completion of cervical dilation, a laryngeal mask airway (LMA) was inserted. If patients exhibited involuntary movements during LMA insertion, an additional bolus of propofol (1 mg/kg) was administered. General anesthesia was maintained with a continuous propofol infusion at 2–3 mg/kg/h throughout the surgical procedure. All anesthetic procedures were performed by an anesthesiologist with the rank of attending or higher, and hysteroscopy was conducted by an experienced gynecologist of equivalent rank. A second anesthesiologist, who was blinded to the oliceridine dose assignments, was responsible for monitoring the patients’ response and systematically collecting data throughout the entire course of the study.

Throughout the surgical procedure, essential vital signs, including BP, HR, and SpO₂, as well as the end-tidal carbon dioxide tension (ETCO2) and respiratory rate (RR), were continuously monitored and recorded. In cases of bradycardia (HR < 50 bpm), intravenous atropine (0.5 mg) was administered for treatment. Mean arterial pressure (MAP) < 65 mmHg was treated with a bolus of intravenous norepinephrine (4 µg). In cases of respiratory depression (defined as any of the following: SpO2 < 90%, RR ≤ 5 breaths/min, or ETCO2 > 50 mmHg), oxygen flow was increased, and manual ventilation was provided as needed. When a patient’s VAS score in the PACU exceeded 3, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) were administered, and the number of patients requiring analgesics was recorded.

The primary outcome measure was ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine combined with propofol for day-case hysteroscopy. The secondary outcome measures included the time to anesthesia emergence (defined as the interval from the cessation of propofol administration to spontaneous eye opening), the VAS scores (0–10; 0 representing no pain and 10 representing the worst imaginable pain) at the time of PACU entry and discharge, the incidence of PONV and the number of patients requiring analgesics in the PACU.

Statistical analysis

According to the modified Dixon up-down method [11–13], obtaining a minimum of six independent crossover points (i.e., reversals in response between adjacent subjects) is considered sufficient to provide a reliable estimation of ED₅₀. To achieve this, and to account for potential dropouts or non-evaluable cases, we planned to enroll approximately 20 to 40 patients, depending on how quickly the required number of crossover pairs was reached during the study.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics 27.0. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with intergroup comparisons analyzed using independent t-tests.Variables with non-normal distributions were presented as medians (interquartile ranges) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as numbers (percentages) and were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test. The dose-response relationship was evaluated through probit analysis to determine oliceridine’s ED₅₀ and ED₉₅ values (with 95% CI). Data visualization, including sequential plots and dose-response curves, was created using GraphPad Prism 9.0. A p-value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Hysteroscopy has become the gold standard for the diagnosis and treatment of intrauterine pathology for its high diagnostic accuracy, minimal invasiveness, and rapid recovery. Advances in hysteroscopic instrumentation have expanded its application to a wide range of gynecologic interventions, including polypectomy, myomectomy, and adhesiolysis, which can often be performed safely without hospital admission [14, 15]. With the widespread adoption of hysteroscopic procedures in outpatient settings, there is an increased demand for pain management. Studies have demonstrated that the combination of pharmacological and non-pharmacological strategies, as well as the implementation of operator-dependent measures, can enhance patient tolerance and ensure procedural success [16–19]. Nevertheless, substantial pain and discomfort may still occur on some occasions. Consequently, deeper sedation-analgesia regimens are often preferred in clinical practice.

However, reliance on deeper sedation-analgesia introduces a new set of unique anesthetic challenges in day-case hysteroscopic surgery. Adequate intraoperative sedation and analgesia are essential to ensure surgical success, while rapid postoperative recovery is equally critical to enable same-day discharge. This dual requirement places anesthesiologists in the challenging position of balancing effective sedation–analgesia with minimal anesthesia-related adverse effects. Therefore, optimization of anesthetic protocols, through appropriate technique selection, drug combinations, and dosage adjustments, is essential to ensure both surgical success and rapid recovery, in line with the principles of ERAS [20]. In this context, determining the ED₅₀ and ED₉₅ of anesthetic agents is critical for establishing safe and effective dosing regimens.

To address this need, our study employed the Dixon up-and-down sequential method, which is widely recognized as an efficient approach for estimating effective doses in clinical trials due to its efficiency, reduced sample size, and concentrated dose intervals [21, 22]. While most previous studies have focused on the use of oliceridine for postoperative pain, our study specifically evaluated its intraoperative application. We found that the ED₅₀ of oliceridine with propofol was 1.309 mg (95% CI: 1.165–1.477 mg), and the ED₉₅ was 1.675 mg (95% CI: 1.497–2.508 mg), which we recommend as reference values for day-case hysteroscopic surgery under TIVA with PSB.

The principles of ERAS emphasize shortening hospital stay, ensuring adequate postoperative analgesia, promoting early mobilization, and minimizing perioperative stress and inflammation [23, 24]. In this context, intravenous anesthesia with an LMA while preserving spontaneous breathing has been recognized as an optimal strategy for day-case hysteroscopy, as it prevents positive pressure-related lung injury, maintains physiological respiratory mechanics, and sustains diaphragmatic function. Collectively, these benefits enhance ventilatory efficiency, reduce postoperative atelectasis and hypoxemia, and facilitate faster recovery [25]. Thus, careful selection of anesthetic agents is essential to implement this regimen successfully, ensuring adequate analgesia with minimal respiratory depression.

Propofol, with its favorable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties, is widely used for TIVA in day-case hysteroscopy, typically in combination with opioids such as fentanyl, sufentanil, or remifentanil [26, 27]. However, when coadministered with propofol, these conventional opioids markedly increase the risk of respiratory depression, thereby limiting their suitability for TIVA with PSB. In addition, opioid-related adverse effects, particularly postoperative nausea and vomiting, may compromise rapid recovery and delay discharge. Among conventional opioids, remifentanil is often preferred because of its rapid onset and predictable offset, even after prolonged infusion without accumulation [28]. These pharmacological advantages have made propofol-remifentanil-based TIVA a common regimen in day-case procedures [29]. Nonetheless, remifentanil carries a high risk of intraoperative dose-dependent respiratory depression and muscle rigidity, as well as a significant incidence of postoperative hyperalgesia. Taken together, these limitations suggest that a propofol-remifentanil-based regimen may not be optimal for day-case hysteroscopy performed under TIVA with PSB.

The clinical limitations of conventional opioids are primarily attributable to their pharmacological mechanisms. At the cellular level, they act on µ-opioid receptors and activate two predominant intracellular signaling pathways [30]: the G-protein pathway, which is responsible for mediating analgesic effects, and the β-arrestin pathway, which not only contributes to opioid-related adverse effects but also exerts a feedback inhibition on G-protein-mediated analgesia. This intertwined receptor activation inherently connects the analgesic benefits of conventional opioids to their side effect profiles, underscoring the need for safer, more selective opioid agents.

In this context, oliceridine has emerged as a novel G protein-biased µ-opioid receptor agonist that mediates analgesia primarily through selective activation of G protein-mediated signaling, while markedly minimizing engagement of the β-arrestin pathway [31]. Compared with conventional opioids such as morphine, oliceridine demonstrates substantially greater G protein coupling efficiency while recruiting only about 14% of the β-arrestin activity of morphine [32]. This selective signaling profile is associated with a substantially lower incidence of opioid-related adverse effects, particularly respiratory depression, which represents a major advantage of oliceridine over conventional opioids. Therefore, a propofol-oliceridine-based regimen may theoretically represent an optimal anesthetic strategy for day-case hysteroscopy performed under TIVA with PSB.

Consistent with this rationale, our study demonstrated that all patients maintained spontaneous respiration throughout the procedure, and none required manual ventilation. Although brief episodes of apnea occasionally occurred following propofol administration, these events were transient and resolved spontaneously without additional intervention. The mean time to anesthesia emergence was 4 min in the Positive group and 3 min in the Negative group, a difference that may be explained by the need for additional propofol dosing in the Positive group in response to intraoperative positive reactions, which potentially prolonged recovery. Moreover, the consistently low VAS scores (< 3) at PACU entry and discharge, without the need for supplemental analgesics, suggest that oliceridine may provide effective postoperative analgesia following hysteroscopy.

In accordance with the prescribing information approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) [33], the present study adopted a fixed-dose regimen of oliceridine rather than weight-based dosing. This strategy is supported by several pharmacological and clinical considerations. First, oliceridine’s selective receptor signaling confers a wide therapeutic window and favorable safety profile, thereby justifying fixed dosing [34]. Second, its relatively small volume of distribution (primarily confined to the central compartment) and hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 enzymes minimize the impact of body weight, with genetic variability playing a more prominent role in plasma levels [35, 36]. Additionally, clinical trials have demonstrated consistent efficacy and safety with fixed intravenous doses across diverse patient populations, with no significant correlation between body weight and clinical outcomes [37].

Although a fixed-dose approach may not fully capture interpatient variability, its application in the present study is supported by both pharmacological rationale and existing clinical evidence. Descriptive subgroup analyses stratified by age and BMI (Supplementary Table S1) revealed no significant differences in ED₅₀, ED₉₅, or perioperative outcomes across these subgroups. While some numerical variations were noted (e.g., a slightly higher ED₉₅ in patients with BMI > 23 kg/m²), no consistent trend was identified. Due to the limited sample size and insufficient dose–response distribution in the subgroups, the SPSS regression model failed to converge stably, resulting in the absence of confidence intervals for ED₅₀ and ED₉₅, or yielding excessively wide intervals. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted cautiously, and larger-scale studies are warranted to validate whether age and BMI exert clinically meaningful effects on anesthetic response.

This study has several limitations. First, its scope was restricted to the female patients with ASA physical status I-II, limiting generalizability to broader populations, including the elderly, individuals with obesity, and those with significant comorbidities. Therefore, further investigations are warranted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of this anesthetic combination in more heterogeneous cohorts. Second, the small sample size may have introduced selection bias; larger, multicenter trials are required to corroborate these findings. Third, postoperative pain and gastrointestinal adverse events were assessed only in the PACU, and longer-term outcomes were not captured. Finally, the depth of anesthesia was not quantified using BIS or entropy monitoring, precluding a more granular assessment of sedation.

Conclusions

Given its lower incidence of opioid-related adverse effects, oliceridine combined with propofol may offer a promising and well-tolerated approach for day-case hysteroscopy performed under TIVA with PSB. The estimated ED₅₀ and ED₉₅ of oliceridine in combination with 2.5 mg/kg propofol were 1.309 mg (95% CI: 1.165–1.477 mg) and 1.675 mg (95% CI: 1.497–2.508 mg), respectively, providing a reference for optimized dosing in clinical practice.

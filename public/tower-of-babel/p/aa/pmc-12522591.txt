Effect of goal-directed fluid therapy on the postoperative outcome in head and neck cancer surgery: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xuan Liang, Chunhua Xi, Hongling Zhou, Shanshan Wang, Dongjing Shi, Yue Wang, Wenjia Shen, Guyan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative complications have become the main cause of prolonged hospitalization and reduced postoperative survival rate among surgical patients. Goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) has been reported to reduce the incidence of postoperative complications and mortality, shorten the hospital stay, and improve the outcome in major abdominal surgery patients. Introduction

Postoperative complications have become the main cause of prolonged hospitalization and reduced postoperative survival rate among surgical patients, leading to significant declines in both their short-term and long-term quality of life [1, 2]. Traditionally, patients undergoing major abdominal or vascular surgery are considered to be a high-risk group for developing postoperative complications.

In China, the incidence of head and neck cancer ranks ninth among all malignancies, with over one hundred thousand new cases expected to be diagnosed each year [3, 4]. Because of the special location of cancer, patients often suffer from significant changes or loss in essential functions such as breathing, diet, or language [5]. The swallowing and eating difficulties caused by tumors often result in nutritional issue; preoperative dehydration and low blood volume are prevalent in these patients [6]. The facial deformities, speech impairments, and altered anatomical structures in the respiratory and digestive systems resulting from surgical interventions not only result in specific pathological and physiological changes in breathing patterns and eating habits but also inflict severe psychological distress and social isolation upon patients. Therefore, the occurrence of perioperative systemic complications in this population is not uncommon compared with other types of cancers [7–9].

As a central link of perioperative anesthesia management, fluid therapy is a crucial measure to ensure the patient’s life safety during surgery. Both excessive or insufficient perioperative fluid input can result in organ dysfunction, delay the recovery of organ function, and increase the incidence of postoperative complications. In patients undergoing head and neck microvascular reconstruction surgery, excessive intraoperative fluid administration may increase the risk of postoperative pulmonary complications, including pneumonia, atelectasis, prolonged mechanical ventilation, or unplanned reintubation [10]. This kind of fluid overload is also an independent risk factor for surgical complications such as free flap necrosis, anastomotic edema, or wound infection after head and neck cancer surgery, which may affect the surgical effect and even lead to surgical failure [11]. The vascular endothelial damage, increased capillary permeability, and local inflammation, which are caused by excessive fluid overload, may provide a reasonable explanation for this phenomenon. When the excessive fluid accumulated in the pulmonary interstitium or local tissues, it impaired diffusion and oxygenation function, compressed the anastomosis site or pedicle of the flap, and finally led to the occurrence of systemic or surgical complications. However, restrictive fluid administration may result in hypovolemia, leading to hypotension, low cardiac output, and subsequent insufficient perfusion to vital organs. This is also a significant factor affecting the outcome of such patients. Previous studies by our team found that the cumulative duration of intraoperative hypotension and oliguria was associated with postoperative thrombotic complications in head and neck cancer patients [12].

Fluid therapy guided by traditional pressure indicators (such as arterial blood pressure, central venous pressure, or their combination) may indicate the occurrence of low blood volume, but they cannot provide sufficient information on the overall filling status of the circulatory system. Therefore, it cannot tell us when to stop the fluid infusion. It is difficult for anesthetists to accurately guide clinical fluid therapy by using this method. Goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) can individualize perioperative fluid infusion by monitoring dynamic hemodynamic indicators, which can increase oxygen delivery and ensure optimal organ perfusion [13]. GDFT has been reported to reduce the incidence of postoperative complications and mortality, shorten the hospital stay, and improve the outcome in non-cardiac surgical patients [14–16]. Most clinical studies mainly focused on patients who have undergone major abdominal surgery; the benefit of this strategy of GDFT in patients undergoing other types of surgery remains controversial. In particular, except for a few retrospective studies and clinical trials with limited samples, there is still a lack of high-quality randomized controlled trials to determine whether GDFT has an impact on the occurrence of postoperative complications in patients after head and neck cancer surgery.

The purpose of this study is to evaluate whether GDFT can reduce the occurrence of serious postoperative complications and shorten the postoperative hospital stay, compared with a standard conventional fluid therapy in patients undergoing head and neck cancer surgery. We hypothesized that the GDFT strategy would have a positive effect on the prognosis for this kind of patients, ultimately improving their quality of life and reducing medical costs.

Methods

Study design

This trial is a prospective, single-center, partly blinded, randomized controlled trial to investigate whether GDFT can reduce the occurrence of serious postoperative complications and shorten the postoperative hospital stay, compared with a standard conventional fluid therapy in patients undergoing head and neck cancer surgery. The framework for the trial is a difference trial. This study will be conducted at Beijing Tongren Hospital, Capital Medical University, a tertiary hospital in China. The study protocol follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) reporting guidelines [17], and the reporting of this trial adheres to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines [18].

Ethics approval

The study will be performed at Beijing Tongren Hospital, Capital Medical University. And we will adhere to the Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki throughout the entire study. All experimental protocols were approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee of Beijing Tongren Hospital (approval No. TREC2024-KY059) and were registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06468852, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06468852) on June 21, 2024. Written informed consent forms will be obtained from all included subjects. Any revisions to the protocol or the informed consent form will be sent for approval by the ethics committee. Revised protocols are distributed to investigators with mandatory training and updated in trial registries. If necessary, additional consent from the participants will be obtained.

Participants

We will recruit 340 patients scheduled for head and neck cancer surgery at Beijing Tongren Hospital. On the day preceding surgery, a researcher authorized by the principal investigator will check the surgery schedules to identify potential participants. For eligible patients meeting inclusion/exclusion criteria, this trained researcher will visit the patient to comprehensively explain the purpose of this study, intervention, and the postoperative follow-up arrangements, thus ensuring patients’ full comprehension of research details prior to obtaining written informed consent. During the patient’s hospitalization and the postoperative follow-up period, the investigator will follow up with the participants and provide diagnostic and treatment recommendations to enhance protocol adherence. Randomization and blinding

Due to the significant impact of free flap reconstruction on intraoperative fluid requirements and hemodynamic management, flap status serves as a mandatory stratification factor for randomization. Randomization sequence will be created by an independent researcher not involved in the anesthesia using Stata 15.1 statistical software, with separate sequences created for patients requiring free flap reconstruction and those not requiring flap reconstruction. For each stratum, a 1:1 allocation is maintained using random block sizes of 4. All participants conforming to the inclusion criteria will be randomly allocated to the standard conventional fluid therapy group (group C) or the GDFT group (group G). An investigator who is not involved in the study will prepare sequentially numbered, sealed opaque envelopes containing randomization information, with distinct envelope colors (red for free flap, blue for non-free flap) to ensure allocation concealment.

Patients were unaware of their group allocation. Due to the presence of the cardiac index trend monitoring device, care providers who supported the fluid strategy administration in the operating room could not be blinded. The postoperative investigators were blinded to the allocation. Unblinding is only allowed when serious adverse events occur.

Intervention

Intraoperative management

All patients followed a standard fasting protocol. Solid food was allowed up to 6 h before surgery, and clear fluids up to 2 h before surgery. All patients received basic anesthetic monitoring by pulse oximetry, electrocardiogram, non-invasive blood pressure, bispectral index (BIS), and temperature monitoring. Invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring will be established before induction under local anesthesia and sedation, the arterial line was connected to the Vigileo monitor via the FloTrac pressure transducer, and the intravascular pressure measurement is referenced to mid-axillary line level. At least one peripheral vein will be opened, and the use of a central venous catheter depends on the requirement of the surgical procedure.

Both groups will receive standard general anesthesia. All patients will be induced with propofol 1.5–2.0 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.5 μg/kg, and cisatracurium 0.2 mg/kg. After endotracheal intubation, the lungs are ventilated with a tidal volume of 8 mL/kg of ideal body weight; the respiratory rate is set to maintain normal end-tidal carbon dioxide level using a constant fresh gas flow of 2 L/min. Remifentanil and propofol are used for the maintenance of anesthesia with a target BIS value of 40–60, and cisatracurium boluses are given as needed.

In group G, fluid optimization follows the algorithm depicted in Fig. 2 until the end of the surgery. Stroke volume variation (SVV) ≤ 12% and cardiac index (CI) ≥ 2.5 L/min/m2 are the goals of fluid therapy, while the mean arterial pressure (MAP) is kept between 65 and 90 mmHg. Five milliliters per kilogram of crystalloids will be infused during the induction period, followed by an infusion rate of 2 mL/kg/h to supplement the physiological requirements. If SVV > 12% for more than 5 min, a 250 mL bolus of colloid will be given. Another 250 mL bolus of colloid will be administrated if SVV was still more than 12% or SV decreased over 10%. If the CI < 2.5 L/min/m2, inotropes are used to achieve this minimum CI, serving as a safety parameter to prevent the patient from being exposed to a low cardiac output state. If SVV and CI reach the target range but MAP could not maintain the expected level, vasopressors were started. Assess the patients every 5 min intraoperatively to ensure that all parameters adhere to the target range according to the study algorithm. Fluid challenges should not be initiated in case of SVV changes caused by external factors such as alterations in the operating table position, adjustments in ventilator settings, or the use of vasopressor or inotropic drugs.

GDFT algorithm

In group C, fluid management will be carried out according to the clinical practice routine, maintaining MAP ≥ 65 mmHg and urine output ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/h, intraoperatively. The fluid supplementation for the induction period and physiological requirements uses the same strategy as in group G. When the anesthesiologist empirically determines that the fluid infusion is sufficient but MAP does not maintain the expected level, vasopressor or inotropic drugs are given to maintain the blood pressure (Fig. 3).

Convention fluid therapy algorithm

In both groups, the heating blankets will be used to achieve normothermia; a hemoglobin value below 7 mg/dL will be considered to be a transfusion trigger. At the end of the surgery, 4 mg intravenous ondansetron will be routinely administered to prevent postoperative nausea and vomiting. No concomitant interventions are prohibited, as all critical procedures are protocol-standardized.

Postoperative management

After the operation, the patients will be transferred to the anesthesia care unit (PACU) or intensive care unit (ICU). All patients will receive patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) treatment, which involves non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and/or opioids with a standard analgesic regimen.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is serious postoperative complications occurred within 30 days after surgery. The serious postoperative complications were defined as CDc ≥ 3.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcome measures include the occurrence of all complications within 30 days after surgery, the length of hospital stay after surgery, the cost of hospitalization, and the quality of recovery.

Oversight and monitoring

An anesthesiologist, an otolaryngologist, and a nurse in the anesthesia recovery room will be employed at the coordination center for this study. Two statisticians will guide the statistical analysis of the data obtained for this study. The data monitoring committee (DMC) consists of an anesthesiologist, an ethics expert, a statistician, and a clinical trial management expert. All team members are independent of the sponsor, and there are no conflicts of interest. The DMC will hold a monthly meeting to review research progress, check the integrity of the data, and monitor the occurrence of adverse events.

When a serious adverse event occurs (such as anaphylactic shock, persistent arrhythmia, bronchospasm, and other events seriously affecting hemodynamic stability), the trial should be terminated immediately, and the patient should be given timely and effective treatment. The investigators will record the symptoms, severity, duration, outcome, and correlation with intervention measures of these serious adverse effects and report all the details to the Clinical Research Ethics Committee. The sponsor and its institution will provide full medical care coverage and proportionate financial compensation for all study-related injuries without financial burden to participants.

Sample size calculation

PASS 15.0 was used to calculate the sample size. It is reported that the incidence of serious postoperative complications in patients with head and neck tumor surgery is 19.6% [11]. However, this proportion may be underestimated due to the potential biases inherent in the retrospective analysis and the limited sample size of the study population. We estimate that at least 30% of patients may experience serious complications, including death. A prior study that implemented a similar intervention in elective abdominal surgery demonstrated a 50% decrease in postoperative complications [19]. Thus, we hypothesize that GDFT would reduce the incidence of serious postoperative complications in patients undergoing head and neck tumor surgery to 15%. The difference test of the rate between the two groups was selected, α: 0.05, 1 − β: 0.8. The estimated sample size for the bilateral test was 153 cases in each group. The sample size was increased to 170, considering the 10% probability of patient dropout or loss to follow-up.

Statistical methods and analysis

All analyses will be performed in a modified intention-to-treat population, which included all patients who had received both randomization and GDFT intervention during surgery. The per-protocol analysis will be used as a sensitivity analysis as well. If needed, multiple imputation will be used to handle missing data.

Statistical analysis will be performed using SPSS 25.0. Continuous data will be presented as mean with standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile ranges (IQR), while binomial data will be presented as absolute numbers (percentage). All data will be tested for normality and normal variances by using parametric or non-parametric tests as appropriate. Independent sample t-test or Mann-Whitney U test will be used to analyze continuous data, while Fisher’s exact test will be used to analyze binomial data. All tests are two-tailed, and a value of P < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up

The critical importance of completing the follow-up will be emphasized during participant enrollment. Participants may withdraw from the study at any time without providing reasons for discontinuation. Designated research staff will conduct all follow-up assessments according to the predetermined schedule. For patients discharged before study completion, telephone follow-ups will be implemented. For participants who deviate from fluid therapy protocol, we will systematically collect baseline characteristics (demographics, clinical history, randomization group), intraoperative hemodynamic parameters (cardiac index, stroke volume variation), intervention metrics (total crystalloid/colloid volumes, cumulative vasopressor dosage), 30-day major complications, and serious adverse events.

Data collection and management

During the research period, information of all patients will be continuously monitored, including medical records, hospitalization records, and follow-up records during hospitalization and after discharge. All data of the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative will be documented in the case report forms promptly, comprehensively, and accurately by two independent personnel. Dedicated personnel will periodically audit the data to ensure data accuracy and productivity. Throughout the entire research period, all data will be kept confidential and stored in a secure database, with no recording of the names and other personal information of the subjects. Only individuals authorized by the corresponding author have access to this database. Additionally, researchers may email the corresponding author to obtain access privileges for conducting international prospective meta-analyses. No interim analysis and subgroup analysis have been designed for this study.

Discussion

Over the past 10 years, the effect of GDFT optimization has been validated in some specific surgical patients. In patients undergoing intracranial aneurysm clipping, transesophageal echocardiography guided (TEE)-GDFT can maintain sufficient MAP under a low fluid load state, with no adverse effects on postoperative complications [20]. GDFT has also achieved success in reducing postoperative complications, especially postoperative bleeding in hip revision arthroplasty patients [21, 22]. In both adults and children who underwent abdominal surgery, GDFT contributes to a faster recovery of gastrointestinal function [23, 24].

However, only a few studies have focused on head and neck cancer patients. Head and neck cancer surgery, particularly those involving free flap reconstruction, has specific requirements for meticulous fluid management. The absence of lymphatic drainage in the free flap renders them vulnerable to edema. Overly aggressive fluid administration can lead to flap or recipient site edema, thereby increasing mechanical stress on the vascular pedicle, compromising the integrity of the donor-site sutures, and ultimately resulting in surgical complications. On the other hand, excessively restricted fluid infusion can reduce the blood supply to the flap [25]. A retrospective study with a small sample size showed that SVV-guided GDFT reduced intraoperative fluid load and shortened hospital length with no effect on postoperative complications in free flap surgery for head and neck cancer. But several factors undermined the reliability of this study; the participants who received different interventions came from different time periods, potentially resulting in disparities in anesthesia management or surgical techniques. Additionally, the research did not employ standard ventilation strategies, which could have affected the accuracy of SVV measurements [26].

Therefore, we designed this single-center, randomized, controlled clinical trial to test the hypothesis that, compared with the standard conventional fluid therapy strategy, GDFT reduces the occurrence of serious postoperative complications and shortens the postoperative hospital stay in patients undergoing head and neck cancer surgery. Our study is designed based on a solid scientific framework and has multiple advantages. First, Beijing Tongren Hospital is one of the largest otolaryngology and head and neck surgery centers in China, performing more than 2500 head and neck cancer surgeries annually. There are sufficient cases to ensure the completion of the study. Second, all study personnel are trained to follow standard procedures for preoperative visits, postoperative follow-up, and assessments. Computer-generated block randomization minimizes the chance of allocation bias. Although intervention providers cannot be blinded due to the presence of monitoring equipment, we require all the investigators responsible for the assessment of postoperative complications do not participate in the intraoperative anesthesia management to eliminate their conflict of interest with the study findings. Third, the primary outcome is clearly defined and will without exception be documented in the patient’s medical records. There will be no data missing related to the primary outcome measures.

Due to the complexity of the clinical trial set-up, there are still some critical considerations that need special attention during the study process. There are many techniques for managing GDFT, including assessment of SVV, TEE, and pulmonary artery catheterization (TAC). Each has its advantages and disadvantages. TAC is an invasive technique that carries potential life-threatening complications, which is considered unreasonable in most patients. TEE is a reliable and relatively non-invasive tool for volume assessment, but most head and neck cancer surgery involves the oral-pharyngeal or esophageal regions, which makes the use of TEE very challenging. Therefore, we chose SVV-guided GDFT using the FloTrac/Vigileo monitor. This is a minimally invasive technique based on pulse contour analysis of the radial artery pressure signal. It has similar reliability to TEE or TAC, and only requires radial artery puncture which is necessary for most head and neck cancer surgery [27, 28]. Thyroid cancer is also a prevalent type of head and neck cancer, typically affecting younger individuals. Most cases have a favorable prognosis, with fewer postoperative complications, and the majority of thyroid surgeries can be completed within 2 h. Including too many thyroid cancer patients in the study may underestimate the incidence of postoperative complications or cause a higher dropout rate due to inadequate operation duration. Therefore, we excluded such patients as study subjects. The choice of intervention fluids is another consideration that needs to be addressed. Fluid resuscitation with colloids may increase the risk of renal replacement and mortality, while GDFT optimization using crystalloids may lead to increased total intravenous fluid volume and hyperchloremia. Recent studies have shown that there is no significant difference in improving tissue oxygen supply and reducing postoperative complications between crystalloids and colloids [22, 29]. The total volume of fluid administered and the timing of fluid administration may be more crucial than the specific type of fluid itself. In this study, we chose to use colloids for fluid optimization to control the total volume of fluid infusion.

In conclusion, for this study, we want to demonstrate whether GDFT has a positive effect on postoperative outcomes in head and neck cancer patients. Results of the study will provide evidence for selecting appropriate intraoperative fluid management methods in such patients.

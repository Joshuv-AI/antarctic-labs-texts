Systemic Treatments in Moderate-to-Severe Atopic Dermatitis in Pediatric Patients up to 12 Years of Age: Real-World Treatment Outcomes from the PEDISTAD Registry
Authors: Amy S. Paller, Danielle Marcoux, Michele Ramien, Eulalia Baselga, Vania Oliveira Carvalho, Ledit R. F. Ardusso, Marlies de Graaf, Suzanne Pasmans, Mirna Toledo-Bahena, Cory Rubin, Joel C. Joyce, Lara Wine Lee, Rajan Gupta, Bryan Adams, Marius Ardeleanu, Annie Zhang

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtopic dermatitis (AD), a chronic systemic disease, can cause intense skin itching and negatively impact sleep, mood, and quality of life (QoL) for patients and families.MethodsPEDISTAD is an ongoing, 10-year, observational registry describing disease characteristics, atopic comorbidities, and treatment patterns in pediatric patients (aged <12 years at enrollment) with moderate-to-severe AD. This 3-year interim analysis evaluates clinician-reported and caregiver-reported/patient-reported outcomes (Eczema Area and Severity Index [EASI], percent body surface area affected, worst itching/scratching, Children’s Dermatology Life Quality Index, and Dermatitis Family Impact) in children treated with dupilumab, methotrexate, and/or cyclosporine. Outcomes were assessed as change from therapy start to last observation (either data cutoff date or treatment discontinuation).ResultsMean (±SE) EASI scores at the time of the last 3-year interim observation were consistent with mild disease in the dupilumab cohort and moderate disease in the methotrexate and cyclosporine cohorts. Improvements in pruritus were numerically greater in the dupilumab cohort relative to the methotrexate and cyclosporine cohorts, while improvements in QoL were similar in the dupilumab and methotrexate cohorts, with no significant change in the cyclosporine cohort. Rates of AD exacerbation were numerically lower with dupilumab treatment relative to methotrexate treatment which were numerically lower than cyclosporine treatment. Dupilumab discontinuation rates were numerically lower relative to methotrexate which were numerically lower than cyclosporine.ConclusionsThis PEDISTAD 3-year interim analysis of dupilumab, methotrexate, and cyclosporine treatment in children with AD demonstrates numerically greater improvements in AD signs, symptoms and QoL with dupilumab treatment relative to methotrexate and cyclosporine [Video abstract and graphical abstract available].Clinical Trial RegistrationNCT03687359.Graphical abstract Video abstract Supplementary file1 (MP4 58163 KB)Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40257-025-00962-8.

Key Points

In PEDISTAD, a real-world, observational study of children <12 years of age with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD), a subgroup of patients receiving systemic treatments (dupilumab, methotrexate, and/or cyclosporine) had a substantial baseline disease burden based on AD severity and most had one or more type 2 inflammatory comorbidities at baseline.Patients receiving dupilumab showed consistent improvements across all clinician-reported or caregiver-reported/patient-reported outcomes and had numerically greater improvements relative to methotrexate or cyclosporine.Rates of AD exacerbation were lower with dupilumab relative to methotrexate and cyclosporine, and lower with methotrexate relative to cyclosporine.

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, relapsing, inflammatory systemic disease that can cause intense skin itching and negatively impact sleep, mood, and quality of life for patients and their families [1]. Childhood AD is often diagnosed before age 5 and has a global prevalence of around 3–20% [1–3]. Children with AD often have other atopic comorbidities, including asthma, food allergies, and/or allergic rhinitis, and are at an increased risk of developing atopic comorbidities throughout their lifetime [4, 5]. Given these factors, AD can have a long-term impact on patients and their caregivers.

AD treatment in pediatric patients is often challenging and typically involves the use of moisturizers and topical treatments (such as topical corticosteroids [TCS] and/or topical calcineurin inhibitors [TCI]) [6]. However, topical treatments alone in patients with moderate-to-severe AD are often insufficient or fail to achieve optimal disease control. In these cases, systemic treatments may be used, including cyclosporine (although this is typically limited to short-term use [up to 1–2 years] and is associated with a range of adverse events) and dupilumab [6].

Dupilumab is a fully human VelocImmune®-derived [7, 8] monoclonal antibody that blocks the shared receptor component for interleukin (IL)-4 and IL-13, thus inhibiting signaling of both IL-4 and IL-13 [9, 10]. Randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trials demonstrate improved clinician-reported and caregiver-reported/patient-reported outcomes in children with moderate-to-severe AD aged 6 months to 5 years and 6 to 11 years treated with dupilumab [11, 12]. However, dupilumab has not been compared with other systemic treatments in randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trials or real-world-evidence studies in children.

PEDISTAD is a real-world, observational study that enrolled children <12 years of age with moderate-to-severe AD who received a range of AD treatments [13]. Here, we report clinician-reported and caregiver-reported/patient-reported outcomes among pediatric patients who received dupilumab, methotrexate, or cyclosporine.

Methods

Study Design, Patients, and Treatment

The study design and inclusion/exclusion criteria for PEDISTAD have been reported previously [13, 14]. Briefly, PEDISTAD is an ongoing, global, 10-year, observational registry describing the disease characteristics, atopic comorbidities, and treatment patterns in pediatric patients with moderate-to-severe AD (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03687359). Eligible children include those <12 years of age at enrollment with moderate-to-severe AD receiving systemic treatment (including biologics, cyclosporine, methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, and corticosteroids), phototherapy, or topical treatment, who would be candidates for systemic therapy.

This interim analysis provides outcomes at study entry (baseline) and for up to 3 years of follow-up among patients enrolled in PEDISTAD. Treatment outcomes were assessed for three treatment cohorts: dupilumab, methotrexate, and cyclosporine. Patients receiving multiple therapies could be included in multiple treatment cohorts and we did not exclude any patients who bridged treatments. Patients were assigned to treatment cohorts based on the intention-to-treat principle and were included in a treatment cohort upon treatment initiation for the first time during the study until the study data cutoff date. Treatment was not dictated by a study protocol but rather prescribed at investigator/clinician and caregiver discretion. All analyses are descriptive. Because this is an interim analysis, only observed data are summarized, with no missing data imputation.

Outcomes Assessed in this Analysis

All outcomes were assessed in the follow-up population as the change from therapy start to the last treatment observation, defined as either the last value prior to treatment discontinuation or the last value prior to the data cutoff date (June 2022). Clinical outcomes assessed in this analysis include the Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI; range: 0–72) and body surface area (BSA) involvement (range: 0–100%) [15–17]. Patient-reported outcomes include worst itching during the previous night and worst itching during the current day in children 6 to <12 years old (range: 0–10). Caregiver-reported/assisted outcomes include Worst Scratch/Itch–Numeric Rating Scale (WSI-NRS) [18, 19] during the previous 24 hours in children <6 years old (range: 0–10), Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM; range: 0–28) [20–22], Children’s Dermatology Life Quality Index (CDLQI; age 4 to <12 years; range 0–30) [21, 22], and Dermatitis Family Impact (DFI; range: 0–30) [23]. Data for the Infants’ Dermatitis Quality of Life Index (IDQOL; age <4 years) at the time of last observation are not presented due to insufficient sample sizes (n < 20) for analysis (n = 8, 5, and 6 patients at time of last observation for the dupilumab, methotrexate, and cyclosporine cohorts, respectively). Statistical significance was determined by analysis of variance (ANOVA). The cumulative discontinuation rate of treatment was estimated in the follow-up population using Kaplan-Meier analysis. Time to discontinuation of the first therapy taken by the patient was analyzed. Censored data refers to “no discontinuation event from therapy initiation to data cut-off date.”

Ethics

This study is conducted in accordance with the principles established by the 18th World Medical Assembly and all subsequent amendments and the guidelines for good epidemiology practice. Each country assures that ethics approval has been received and local regulatory requirements are met. Ethics approval has been obtained in all countries currently participating in PEDISTAD. Study data will be disseminated in manuscripts submitted to peer-reviewed medical journals as well as in abstracts submitted to congresses, and in the resulting posters and presentations.

Results

Patients

In total, 1329 patients under the age of 12 years were enrolled in PEDISTAD, and 178 patients withdrew from the study at the time of the 3-year data cutoff. In this interim analysis and within the subset of patients receiving systemic treatment, 372 received any of the three treatments of interest, of which 211 received dupilumab, 130 received methotrexate, and 142 received cyclosporine. Most patients received only one of the three treatments (276/372 [74.19%]), while fewer patients received two (82/372 [22.04%]) or three (14/372 [3.76%]) treatments. Patients who did receive more than one treatment did so consecutively and not in combination. The median (quartile 1, quartile 3) treatment observation period was 16.1 (11.6, 21.9) months for dupilumab, 17.3 (7.5, 31.6) months for methotrexate, and 10.4 (4.7, 23.1) months for cyclosporine (Table S1, see electronic supplementary material [ESM]).

Around half of patients in all three treatment cohorts used one or more concomitant topical medications for AD during the observation period (dupilumab: 44.6%; methotrexate: 53.6%; cyclosporine: 66.1%). The most common concomitant medications included TCS (dupilumab: 51.1%; methotrexate: 53.6%; cyclosporine: 67.8%), emollients (dupilumab: 28.8%; methotrexate: 40.5%; cyclosporine: 46.6%), and TCI (dupilumab: 25.5%; methotrexate: 32.1%; cyclosporine: 39.0%) (Table S2, see ESM).

The cumulative discontinuation rates at 3 years based on Kaplan-Meier analysis were 13.27% for the dupilumab cohort, 36.92% for the methotrexate cohort, and 56.34% for the cyclosporine cohort (Fig. 1). The most common reasons for discontinuation (reported in ≥3 patients) in the dupilumab cohort were other (6 [2.8%]), adverse event (4 [1.9%]), lack of efficacy (4 [1.9%]), and disease well controlled (3 [1.4%]); the most common in the methotrexate cohort were lack of efficacy (12 [9.2%]), caregiver/patient decision (9 [6.9%]), disease well controlled (8 [6.2%]), other (7 [5.4%]), and adverse event (3 [2.3%]); the most common in the cyclosporine cohort were lack of efficacy (19 [13.4%]), disease well controlled (16 [11.3%]), other (11 [7.7%]), caregiver/patient decision (10 [7%]), adverse event (6 [4.2%]), concomitant medication (6 [4.2%]), and low compliance (3 [2.1%]) (Table S3, see ESM).Fig. 1Time to treatment discontinuation among patients treated with dupilumab, methotrexate, and/or cyclosporine. Clinician‑Reported and Caregiver-Reported/Patient‑Reported Outcomes

Mean EASI scores and percent BSA affected by AD improved significantly from the start of treatment to the last observation for dupilumab, methotrexate, and cyclosporine treatment cohorts (EASI: p < 0.0001, p < 0.0001, p = 0.0005; BSA: p < 0.0001, p < 0.0001, p = 0.0001; Fig. 2a, b). Mean EASI scores at the last observation reflected mild disease severity in the dupilumab cohort and moderate disease severity in the methotrexate and cyclosporine cohorts. Safety Assessment

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were reported in 51/210 (24.3%), 40/130 (30.8%), and 47/142 (33.1%) patients treated with dupilumab, methotrexate, and cyclosporine, respectively; serious TEAEs were reported in 5/210 (2.4%), 3/130 (2.3%), and 4/142 (2.8%) patients, respectively (Table 2). Discussion

In this study, baseline disease characteristics reflected a substantial and multidimensional AD disease burden despite standard treatment. The high burden and inadequate response to topical therapies suggest a continuing unmet need for effective therapies with demonstrated safety for moderate-to-severe AD. Throughout the observation period for this 3-year interim analysis, all three systemic treatments significantly improved at least one of the clinician-reported and caregiver-reported/patient-reported outcomes assessed, with the numerically greatest improvements observed for the dupilumab cohort. Specifically, at the time of the last 3-year interim observation, the mean EASI score for patients treated with dupilumab was consistent with mild disease [24], and the mean EASI scores for methotrexate and cyclosporine were consistent with moderate disease. Improvements in itching/scratching were also numerically greater for dupilumab relative to methotrexate and cyclosporine, with significant improvements observed for nighttime and daytime itching among children 6–12 years of age in the dupilumab cohort, but not the methotrexate or cyclosporine cohorts. For quality-of-life (QoL) measures (DFI and CDLQI), improvements were similar for dupilumab and methotrexate, both of which showed numerically greater improvements relative to cyclosporine. Additionally, patients on dupilumab discontinued treatment at a numerically lower rate than those on methotrexate, followed by cyclosporine.

The findings from this 3-year interim analysis of PEDISTAD (n = 372) are aligned with the findings from the 2-year analysis that included 306 patients [25]. Comparing the current with the former analysis, median treatment durations almost doubled for dupilumab (16.1 vs 8.1 months), increased moderately for methotrexate (17.3 vs 13.0 months), but remained similar for cyclosporine (10.4 vs 10.7 months) [25]. The latter is likely because cyclosporine is generally only for short-term use [6]. The greater effectiveness observed in PEDISTAD for dupilumab versus cyclosporine is consistent with a previous report demonstrating better effectiveness for dupilumab versus cyclosporine in adults with moderate-to-severe AD in an indirect comparison using regression models [26]. However, the current results differ from those of a recent prospective observational cohort study from the United Kingdom (UK) and Ireland that included patients of all ages with AD [27]. In line with the current study, improvements in EASI, POEM, peak pruritus NRS, and Dermatology Life Quality Index were greater with dupilumab than with methotrexate, regardless of baseline severity [27]. However, outcomes with dupilumab compared with cyclosporine varied by baseline disease severity, with cyclosporine tending to be more effective in patients with the most severe AD and dupilumab generally being more effective in those with less severe AD [27]. Several factors could help to explain these differences. In the current study, patients in the dupilumab and cyclosporine cohorts had similar disease severity, but patients in the UK/Ireland study [27] who received cyclosporine tended to have more severe disease than those who received dupilumab. This could indicate the use of cyclosporine as a rescue treatment to control flares, giving more scope for disease severity improvement. Also, the current study only included children aged <12 years while the UK/Ireland study [27] included patients of all ages. It is important to note that the UK/Ireland study only included nine patients <11 years old in the cyclosporine group and overall patient numbers were small.

A systematic review and network meta-analysis of 20 different systemic immunomodulatory treatments among patients with AD concluded that dupilumab and cyclosporine probably had similar efficacy, but methotrexate was likely less effective [28]. However, this was based on placebo-controlled rather than head-to-head trials, and it is not clear how many children were included in the comparison of the randomized controlled trials for dupilumab and cyclosporine. Of note, recent American guidelines for the treatment of patients with moderate or severe AD strongly recommend dupilumab, conditionally recommend cyclosporine, but now recommend against methotrexate [6]. They also say that patients should not have to try cyclosporine before switching to more effective and safer treatments such as dupilumab [6]. It therefore seems likely that future analyses of PEDISTAD will have increasing numbers of patients taking dupilumab (and for longer treatment durations) but limited additional numbers of patients taking methotrexate and no increase in cyclosporine treatment durations.

The overall incidence of TEAEs was numerically lower in dupilumab-treated patients relative to methotrexate- and cyclosporine-treated patients, while the rates of serious TEAEs, TEAEs related to treatment, and TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation were similar across the three treatment cohorts. One notable difference across treatment cohorts was a lower incidence of AD flares (defined as dermatitis, atopic) in the dupilumab cohort (5/210 [2.4%]) relative to the methotrexate and cyclosporine cohorts (10/130 [7.7%] and 22/142 [15.5%], respectively) and a lower incidence with methotrexate compared with cyclosporine. These findings suggest that dupilumab provided the best disease control in this group of pediatric patients, followed by methotrexate and then cyclosporine.

Limitations of this study include the fact that this is an interim analysis of 3/10 years of data, with analysis of two timepoints and a lack of a placebo group due to the observational nature of the study. Additionally, methotrexate and cyclosporine were dosed differently than dupilumab and some patients received more than one treatment during the study. Missing data were also not taken into account. Furthermore, since the analysis groups are not randomized, no formal comparisons can be made.

Conclusions

This 3-year interim analysis of dupilumab, methotrexate, and cyclosporine treatment in children under 12 years of age with moderate-to-severe AD in real-world settings revealed a substantial disease burden in all treatment cohorts at baseline and numerically greater improvements in most clinician-reported and caregiver-reported/patient-reported outcomes with dupilumab treatment relative to methotrexate or cyclosporine. Improvements in quality-of-life measures were similar for dupilumab and methotrexate, though improvement in itch was only significant for dupilumab.

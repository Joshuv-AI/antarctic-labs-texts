In vitro evidence of HER2-selective cytotoxicity by scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA immunotoxin in gastric cancer cells
Authors: Hamid Sedighian, Arash Abdi, Zoleikha Goleij, Mahdi Fasihi-Ramandi, Abbas Ali Imani Fooladi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The delivery of therapeutic agents in a targeted manner shows great potential for improving the degree of efficacy in cancer therapies by minimizing harm for normal tissues and reducing treatment-related complications. Among patients with gastric cancer, the HER2 receptor is expressed in approximately 10–30 % of cases. The scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA was cloned into the pET28a vector, followed by protein expression and purification. HEK-293 cells served as the control (HER2-negative), while NCI–N87 cells were utilized as the gastric cancer cells (HER2-positive). The cells were subjected to exposure different concentrations (35 and 50 μg/mL) of immunotoxin, after which growth was assessed using the MTT test. The capability of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA to induce apoptotic pathway in NCI–N87 and HEK-293 cells was estimated through flow cytometry. Furthermore, the affinity for binding the IT, lactate dehydrogenase release, and the measurement of apoptosis pathway enzymes (caspase-9 and caspase-3 activities) were also conducted. The outcomes demonstrated that cytotoxic effects occurred in NCI–N87 cells with a dose-proportional manner approach, whereas no such impact was seen in HEK-293(P < 0.001). Flow cytometry analysis of NCI–N87 cells treated with scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA revealed a significant increase in apoptotic rate at dose of 35 and 50 μg/mL (55.6 % and 74.2 %). Additional analysis showed that exposing HER2-expressing NCI–N87 cells to the scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA caused a notable to enhance in apoptotic caspases activity (3 and 9) (P < 0.001). Results of our study indicated that the scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA induces apoptosis in the NCI–N87 cell line derived from gastric cancer. This study emphasizes the potential of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA to specifically target HER2-expressing cancer cells.

Introduction

In the last 50 years, the incidence of gastric cancer has gradually declined worldwide, yet it continues to be the fifth most prevalent type of cancer. In 2020, more than one million individuals were diagnosed with gastric cancer, accounting for 5.6 % of all cancer cases [1]. The incidence rates of malignancy of the stomach reveal considerable economic as well as regional differences, with elevated rates observed in Europe, East Asia, as well as in developed nations. Although gastric malignancy was the primary factor accountable for cancer-related fatalities. Globally, more than 760,000 people die each year from gastric cancer, representing about 7.7 % of all cancer-related deaths [2].

The human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a transmembrane receptor present on the cell surface in monomeric form [3]. In about 15–30 % of breast malignancy and 10–30 % of gastric malignancy, there is an increased expression of HER2, making it a significant biomarker for prognosis and prediction [4]. So far, targeting HER2 has proven to be a significant approach for treating HER2-positive cancers, leading to the development of various anti-HER2 medications [5]. These include HER2 antibodies and their derivatives like trastuzumab and pertuzumab drugs [6]. The crucial function of HER2 in cancerous progression and its biological characteristics render it a desirable approach for malignancy treatment. There is a demand for innovative and improved treatment-related approaches that focus on targeting HER2 [7]. Immunotoxins (ITs) engineered conjugates that combine monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) with bacterial or plant-derived toxins [8]. Once inside the cells, the toxin triggers signaling pathways that result in cell death [9]. Several ligand-based ITs have been developed based on these ligands functions and are presently undergoing different phases of clinical trials [10]. Pseudomonas exotoxin A (PE) consists of a single polypeptide chain made up of 613 amino acid residues, which are arranged into three unique structural and functional domains [11]. The truncated variant, PE38, was developed by deleting a large portion of domain Ib, yet it retained its cytotoxicity and ability to inhibit protein synthesis via ADP-ribosylation [12]. This research utilized the active part of Shiga toxin (STX) to develop the ITs [13]. STXA is an enzymatically active fragment widely applied in IT design [14]; when combined with PE, it exerts potent cytotoxic effects following internalization of the chimeric protein [15].

Cell culture is a fundamental technique in biotechnology and is extensively utilized in biology, medicine, and drug research and development. Today, with the availability of human cancer cells and the challenges associated with animal studies, it is advisable to use human cancer cell lines prior to conducting clinical trials. Because clinical studies are highly sensitive, preclinical cell culture experiments are essential for testing novel drug candidates [16]. In this investigation, the scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA chimeric protein was created by excising domain III of PE and substituting it with STXA using recombinant DNA technology. In this study, our hypothesis was that the chimeric protein could exert anti-tumor effects by binding to the HER2 receptor in NCI–N87 cells. Accordingly, we assessed its apoptotic properties, binding affinity to HER2, and overall antitumor activity using both HER2-positive NCI–N87 gastric cancer cells and HER2-negative HEK-293 cells. Domain II of PE played an essential role in facilitating the transport of STXA across the cell membrane [13].

Material and method

Cell lines and culture condition

The HER2-positive gastric cancer cell line NCI–N87 (PRID: CVCL_1650) and the HER2-negative HEK-293 cell line (PRID: CVCL_0045), were sourced from the Pasteur Institute (Tehran, Iran). NCI–N87 cells exhibited a great extent of HER2 expression [17], while HEK-293 cells showed no detectable HER2 expression [18]. Therefore, the HEK-293 cell line was employed as a negative cell line in the research. Both cell lines were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium high glucose (from Bioidea, Iran). The culture media were enriched with FBS (Fetal bovine serum) (Gibco, USA), penicillin (Bioidea, Iran), and streptomycin (Bioidea, Iran) and cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5 % CO2.

Transformation and expression of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA

The recombinant pET28a-scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA vectors were cleaved with restriction enzymes, following the producer's instructions [13]. Subsequently, the structures were introduced into E. coli BL21(DE3) from the Pasteur Institute of Iran (Tehran, Iran). The transformed bacteria were cultured in 5 mL of LB (Luria-Bertani) medium supplemented with 20 μg/mL kanamycin and incubated overnight at 37 °C. The expression levels of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA were subsequently assessed utilizing sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS/PAGE) (DNA Biotech Co., Iran). To optimize protein expression, various concentrations of Isopropyl β-d-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) (0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, and 1 mM) and different incubation times (2, 5, 7, and 16 h) were tested. Recombinant proteins were isolated by harvesting bacterial cells 16 h post-IPTG induction. The cells were lysed via sonication, and the lysate was centrifuged to collect inclusion bodies. These pellets were washed with urea-containing buffers, solubilized in 8 M urea, and incubated for 16 h. After centrifugation, the supernatant was analyzed by SDS-PAGE. Recombinant proteins were then purified using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography, with unbound proteins removed via an imidazole wash and target proteins eluted using a higher imidazole concentration. Western blot analysis further verified the presence of the recombinant protein [13].

Evaluation of the cytotoxic effects of recombinant proteins

The cytotoxicity of the recombinant protein was assessed using the methylthiazolyldiphenyl-tetrazolium bromide (MTT) test (MO, USA). The NCI–N87 human gastric cancer cells, which overexpresses the HER2 antigen, and the HEK-293 cell line, which lacks HER2 expression, were cultured in 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well. The cells were incubated at 37 °C and after 24 h, the cells were treated with varying doses of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA (1, 10, 25, 35, 50, and 75 μg/mL) for 48 h. Each chimeric protein treatment was done in sets of three. After the exposure period, the medium was replaced with 5 mg/mL MTT. The absorbance was subsequently analyzed at optical density (OD) 570 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, USA). Doses of 1–75 μg/mL were chosen based on our previous studies, while 35 and 50 μg/mL were determined by calculating the I50 in the MTT assay.

Assessment of receptor attaching was conducted utilizing the cell-ELISA technique

The capacity of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA chimeric proteins to attach to antigens was assessed using a binding assay employing ELISA. NCI–N87 and HEK-293 cells were individually plated in 96-well culture plates at a count of 15 × 103 cells. After one day, the supernatant from the wells was eliminated, and the wells were washed with PBS. Subsequently, they were fixed with 10 % formaldehyde. Following fixation, the cells were washed again with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), blocked with 3 % bovine serum albumin, and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. Following this, the cells were exposed to various doses of recombinant protein (35 and 50 μg/mL) for 1 h. The cells were rinsed five times with PBS and subsequently incubated at room temperature for 1.5 h with an anti-hexa histidine horseradish peroxidase (HRP) conjugated antibody diluted to a ratio of 1:2000. Following this, a tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) solution containing 1.5 % H2O2 was introduced to each well, and color development occurred following the addition of a stop solution (H2SO4). The OD was subsequently measured at 450 nm using an ELISA plate reader.

Apoptosis assessment using flow cytometry analysis

For flow cytometric assessing, the Annexin V-FITC cellular apoptosis identification kit was utilized. NCI–N87 and HEK-293 cells were plated at a density of 5 × 105 cells per well in a 6-well plate. Subsequent to one day, the cells were subjected to various concentrations of chimeric protein (35 and 50 μg/mL) for 48 h. Following this treatment period, the culture media was discarded, and the treated cells were trypsinized at 37 °C. The harvested cells were suspended again in 1 mL of annexin V-binding buffer. Subsequently, 5 μL of annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide were added to 1 × 105 cells. After a 15-min incubation in the dark, the cells were assessed using a FACScan flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson, San Jose) and FLOWJO software version 10.

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release test

The cytotoxic effects of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA was assessed by measuring the release of intracellular lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) into the peripheral culture media, which indicates death of cells. In accordance with the Biorexfars kit (Cat number: BXC0242A, Fars, Iran), recombinant proteins at concentrations of 35 and 50 μg/mL were administered to NCI–N87 and HEK-293 cells, each at a count of 2 × 105 cells per well, respectively. For preparing positive and negative control cells, 20 μl of Permi Solution (Triton X-100) and sterile water were added, respectively. The suspension was then centrifuged (400×g, 5 min), and 50 μl of the supernatant was combined with 50 μl of a solution containing LDH assay buffer (Resazurin) and co-substrate (NADH). This mixture was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark before measuring absorbance at 570 nm using an ELISA microplate reader. The background absorbance from uncultured wells was deducted from that of treated and control cells. The relative cytotoxicity percentage was then determined using the following formula:Relativecytotoxicity=ODoftreatedsample−ODofnegativecontrolODofpositivecontrol−ODofnegativecontrol×100

Assessment activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3

NCI–N87 and HEK-293 cells, seeded at a count of 5 × 105 cells per well, were subjected to 35 and 50 μg/mL of chimeric protein (scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA) and incubated for 24 h. Following this incubation, caspase-3 and caspase-9 activity were analyzed using the colorimetric assay from the Biorexfax kit (Cat number: BXC0654C, Fars, Iran). All steps of the ELISA test were performed according to the kit protocol.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were executed three times, and the results are reported as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Data analysis employed an unpaired t-test for independent samples and one-way ANOVA with GraphPad Prism.

Toxicity effects in vitro from chimeric protein

The cell-killing effects of chimeric proteins (scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA) were assessed using the MTT assay on NCI–N87 and HEK-293 cells. After 48 h of incubation with the chimeric proteins, the survival of NCI–N87 cells diminished proportionally to the dose, reaching approximately 88.6 %, 76.3 %, 66.5 %, 59.3 %, 52.3 % and 33.3 % at dose of 1 (∗P < 0.05), 10 (∗P < 0.05), 25 (∗∗P < 0.01), 35 (∗∗∗P < 0.001), 50 (∗∗∗P < 0.001), and 75 μg/mL (∗∗∗P < 0.001), respectively. In contrast, no significant cytotoxic effect was observed in HEK-293 cells compared to NCI–N87 cells, with the exception of the highest concentration of 75 μg/mL (∗∗P < 0.01). Considering the existence of the HER2 receptor in the NCI–N87 cell line, we anticipated that our recombinant protein would have a more pronounced effect in eliminating this cell line, and our observations confirmed this expectation. Discussion

Gastric cancer treatment today primarily involves chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and surgical tumor excision. Although these approaches have proven effective for some patients in managing the disease, the overall mortality rate remains high in most cases [19].

In light of the adverse side effects experienced by gastric cancer patients, researchers have introduced a novel treatment strategy known as IT therapy. This approach involves linking a toxin molecule to an antibody that specifically recognizes and binds to proteins on the surface of malignant cells [20].

Our earlier research showed that scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA induces apoptosis and suppresses tumor growth in HER2-positive cells such as SKBR-3, but not in HER2-negative cells such as MCF-7 [13]. In this study, we chose NCI–N87 cells to represent HER2-positive cells and HEK-293 cells to represent HER2-negative cells. Research has indicated that the NCI–N87 cell line exhibits high expression levels of HER2 receptors and possesses significant characteristics associated with gastric cancer. According to studies by Colagar et al., HEK-293 cells are not entirely devoid of the HER2 receptor, as they occasionally express it [21]. Consequently, at high concentrations, the scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA can attach to the HEK-293 cell line, gain entry into the cells, and induce cellular death [22].

We evaluated the cytotoxic activity of the recombinant IT using the MTT assay. The results clearly demonstrated its targeted cell-killing capability. Following the MTT analysis and determination of the inhibitory concentration (IC50), we selected two concentrations, 35 and 50 μg/mL, to examine the effects of the recombinant protein on the cells. Our findings revealed that NCI–N87 cells are highly sensitive to the recombinant IT, while HEK-293 cells showed no reactivity at low concentrations and only minimal cytotoxicity at higher doses. In the HEK-293 cell line, the recombinant protein showed no significant effects at concentrations of 35 and 50 μg/mL, likely because of the low HER2 receptor expression on the cell surface. The lack of receptor binding prevents IT recognition, leading to the HER-antibody complex being degraded via the lysosomal pathway [23].

In the current research, we confirmed the attachment of the IT to NCI–N87 gastric cancer cells by examining its binding using the ELISA method. The attachment of the chimeric protein to the receptor is crucial for the internalization process and its impact on tumor cells. In a prior study involving IT applied to SKBR-3 cell lines, beneficial effects were primarily noted at a dose of 75 μg/mL for cells expressing the HER2 receptor. In contrast, our present research indicates that IT demonstrates beneficial effects at lower concentrations in gastric malignant cells with the HER2 receptor. When comparing the results of the previous study to our findings, it is evident that scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA affects gastric cancer cell lines at lower concentrations. It may be attributed to the high density and increased sensitivity of receptors on the surface, which has resulted in greater effectiveness at lower dose of IT in NCI–N87 gastric cancer cell lines. Given that HER2 activation and overexpression are closely linked to gastric cancer aggressiveness and metastasis, the IT may improve these metastatic effects through its action on HER2 receptors. [24]. We conducted an ELISA to assess the attachment rate of the chimeric protein to the HER2 receptor, ensuring effective attachment. Our data further demonstrated a positive correlation between the binding of the IT to its receptor and the reduction in proliferation of the NCI–N87 cell line. This conclusion is drawn from the binding of the anti-His antibody to the chimeric protein that is attached to the receptor, suggesting that this interaction may lead to growth inhibition and cell cycle inhibition through the stimulation of apoptosis process.

The cytotoxicity of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA was assessed with the MTT assay and LDH release, reflecting mitochondrial activity and membrane damage. Our results demonstrated an elevation in cytotoxicity of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA in NCI–N87 cell line, evidenced by the leakage of intacellular LDH into the adjacent culture medium, which takes place following cellular death. The harmful impact of the IT is probably linked to its toxic component (STXA), which disrupts ribosome function, triggers heightened cytokine activity due to ribotoxic stress, and induces cell death through apoptosis [25]. The findings from Maak et al.'s study indicated that Shiga toxin exposure induced cytotoxicity in human pancreatic adenocarcinoma tumors, likely due to receptor-mediated uptake of the toxin [26].

In the present research, the results from the MTT and LDH analyses demonstrated the antiproliferative activity of chimeric protein on the NCI–N87 cell line at dose of 35 and 50 μg/mL. Notably, we found that NCI–N87 cells are highly responsive to scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA, while HEK-293 cells do not react at low concentrations and only demonstrate minimal cytotoxicity at higher concentrations (75 μg/mL). The observed effects of recombinant protein at elevated concentrations in HEK-293 cells likely result from either off-target toxicity or nonspecific protein overload. Research indicates a significant association between HER2 receptor expression and LDH activity in gastric cancer [27]. A 2018 study demonstrated that pharmacological modulation of LDH levels can enhance therapeutic outcomes by promoting cancer cell apoptosis and suppressing invasive potential in gastric malignancies [28].

However, the reduced effects and antitumor activity of the scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA on the HEK-293 cells line may be attributed to less effective targeting of the receptor through its ligand portion. The overall amount of HER2 that is internalized by these cell lines is associated with the density of the antigen present on the cell membrane. Consequently, an increased presence of HER2 on the cell surface results in greater internalization of IT [29]. Cao et al. demonstrated in their in vivo treatment experiments that PE-based IT could effectively inhibit tumor growth and expand the therapeutic window in HER2-positive cell lines [30]. Jang et al. showed that IT increased both specificity and toxicity in breast cancer cells that express HER2. They propose that the IT could broaden the range of applications for IT in cancer therapy [31]. Khoshbakht et al. demonstrated that the PE-based IT can bind to the MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cell line (which is GRP78-positive) and subsequently eliminate cancer cells through internalization and apoptosis. They concluded that the engineered recombinant PE-based IT could have therapeutic potential against GRP78-positive tumor cells [32]. Other studies have also indicated that IT can effectively activate apoptosis in cancerous cells with the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR). Their findings support the idea that IT could function as a novel therapeutic agent for targeting EGFR-positive cancers [33,34].

Different types of cellular stress can result in one of three kinds of cellular demise: necrosis, apoptosis or autophagy. In contrast to apoptosis and necrosis, autophagy-induced cellular death is reversible. Additionally, necrotic cells experience disruption of their cell membranes, which triggers an inflammatory response, whereas apoptotic cells do not induce inflammation and are rapidly cleared by macrophages through phagocytosis [35]. Conversely, certain anti-cancer therapies may induce apoptosis alongside inflammatory responses, which occur as a result of danger-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) being released from cancer cells [36].

The effectiveness of chemotherapy in medical oncology is determined by activation of apoptotic pathways in tumoral cells. Caspase-3, a protease belonging to the caspase family, serves as a key regulator of chemotherapy-induced apoptosis in tumors. However, reports indicate that caspase-3 may not be the sole protease responsible for initiating apoptosis [37].

Caspase-9 is also a crucial enzyme in the process of apoptosis. Stimulation of caspase-9 in the process of apoptosis causes the cell cycle to stop (G0/G1) and induce apoptosis and cell death [38]. Cytochrome c is released by activation of caspase-9 and plays a role in activating effector caspases to facilitate mitochondrial remodeling. This mechanism ensures that cells with an active apoptosome can still undergo death even without effector caspases [39].

Flow cytometry showed that scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA (35 and 50 μg/mL) increased both stages of apoptosis (early and late) in NCI–N87 more than in HEK-293 cells. Our findings demonstrated that the scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA prompted caspase-dependent apoptosis, which was marked by a significant stimulation of caspases enzymes [[3], [9]] in NCI–N87 cells when compared to HEK-293 cells at dose of 35 and 50 μg/mL. Consistent with our findings, GhadakSaz et al. demonstrated that recombinant proteins enhance gene expression and up-regulate caspase-3/9 production via the intrinsic apoptotic pathway, thereby exerting antitumor effects [40]. In present investigation, the expression levels of main apoptosis proteins (Bax and Bcl-2), as well as the rate of recombinant protein internalization, were not investigated. To acquire a deeper insight into the apoptosis process and the rate of recombinant protein uptake, it is advisable to assess these parameters in future research. Also, given the recombinant protein positive effects on the HER2-expressing gastric cancer cell line, authors recommend further investigation using gastric cancer animal models to validate its therapeutic potential.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a fusion protein combining monomeric Herceptin to target HER2 receptors with PE and STXA toxins to induce cancer cell death. The chimeric protein exhibited stronger toxicity toward HER2-positive NCI–N87 gastric cancer cells compared with HER2-negative HEK-293 cells. Binding and internalization of scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA were significantly greater in NCI–N87 cells, highlighting its specificity for HER2-overexpressing cancers. The inability to induce apoptosis in HER2-negative HEK-293 cells further supports this specificity. Overall, our findings suggest that scFv(Herceptin)-PE-STXA is a promising candidate for targeted therapy in HER2-positive gastric cancer, and further research in preclinical and clinical settings is warranted.

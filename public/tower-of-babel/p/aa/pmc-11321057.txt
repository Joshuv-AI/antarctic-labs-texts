Spindle epithelial tumor with thymus-like elements (SETTLE): a diagnostic challenge with distinct therapeutic implication; case report
Authors: Prerna Chadha, Meenakshi Kamboj, Sunil Pasricha, Vikas Arora, Vishal Yadav, Manoj Gupta, Anurag Mehta
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Spindle epithelial tumor with thymus-like elements (SETTLE) is a rare malignant neoplasm of the thyroid gland which is believed to arise from intrathyroidal thymic tissue. It predominantly affects young adults and children presenting with a thyroid mass of variable duration and rarely occurs in adults. It has a high overall survival with a tendency for delayed metastasis. SETTLE is a biphasic lobulated tumor composed of spindle shaped cells along with glandular formations seen on histopathological examination. Despite its typical morphology it is commonly misdiagnosed on histopathology due to its rarity and overlapping morphology with other close mimics such as a carcinoma, synovial sarcoma and thymoma. Herein we report such a case occurring in a middle aged female presenting with a neck mass. She had an initial diagnosis of metastatic poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma possibly with an orophayngeal primary in view of co expression of CK, p40 and p16 on immunohistochemistry. The patient underwent surgical resection with modified neck dissection. On review at our hospital it was diagnosed as SETTLE and she remains disease free after a follow-up period of 1 year. Diligent histopathological examination espoused with a judicious panel of IHC markers in conjunction with clinicoradiological findings forms the mainstay of diagnosis. Diffuse and strong p16 immunoexpression has not been documented or evaluated in literature so far, and needs to be explored for its diagnostic utility in this rare entity.

Introduction

Spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like elements (SETTLE) is a rare malignant neoplasm of the thyroid. SETTLE is believed to arise from intrathyroidal thymic tissue [1] or branchial pouch remnants [2]. It is a slow growing indolent neoplasm presenting with a neck/thyroid mass of variable duration. It usually affects young adults and children and has a good overall survival. Despite its typical morphology on histopathology, it may be misdiagnosed due to its rarity and expression of epithelial markers such as Pancytokeratin (CK), p40 and high molecular weight keratins on immunohistochemistry (IHC). Surgery forms the mainstay of treatment in resectable cases and nodal dissection is unwarranted in node negative cases [3]. Only 47 cases of SETTLE have been documented in indexed literature so far of which only 5 have occurred in older age group patients of more than 50 years [2, 4–10]. Herein, we report such a case occurring in a 56 year old lady who was treated with surgical resection and remains disease free after a period of 1 year post operatively.

Case report

A 56 year old lady presented with complaints of a neck swelling since 3–4 months. The swelling was insidious in onset, painless and gradually progressed to its present size. The patient did not give any history suggestive of hyper or hypothyroidism. There was no history of any lack of appetite or weight loss. No other relevant medical history was present. On local examination, a thyroid mass (moving with deglutition) was noted towards the left side of the neck with no palpable cervical lymph nodes. She underwent fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) which was reported as suspicious of papillary neoplasm. Subsequently, a contrast enhanced computed tomography (CECT) scan of the neck revealed a well defined lobulated heterogeneously hypoenhancing soft tissue space occupying lesion along the lower pole of left lobe of thyroid gland measuring approximately 4.5 × 2.6 × 3.2 cm with a ring shaped calcification within (Fig. 1). There was presence of retrosternal extension inferiorly into the superior mediastinum with no evidence of any compression or encasement of the surrounding vessels or extension into the trachea. Isthmus and right lobe of the thyroid appeared normal. Few subcentimetric non necrotic bilateral level II cervical lymph nodes were seen. The laryngeal cartilages and hyoid bone were unremarkable. The patient was clinically diagnosed as Papillary carcinoma thyroid.

The patient underwent surgical resection comprising of (a) Left hemithyroidectomy with attached retrosternal portion, (b) Left hemithyroid and (c) Left modified neck dissection (MND). The “left hemithyroidectomy with retrosternal portion” appeared as an encapsulated mass with smooth external surface on gross examination. The cut section showed a solid firm to hard grey white tumor. No normal thyroid gland was identified grossly. It was reported as a nodal metastatic deposit of a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (CK, p40 and p16 positive on IHC) with two more adjacent metastatic nodes. A primary from orpharynx was suggested in view of p16 positivity.

The “left hemithyroid” was signed out as follicular adenoma with lymphocytic thyroiditis while the MND yielded 34 reactive lymph nodes, all free of tumor.

The patient was referred to our tertiary cancer care centre. The submitted slides and paraffin embedded blocks from the “Left hemithyroidectomy with retrosternal portion” showed a partially encapsulated, lobulated tumor with focal mushrooming into the surrounding fat. The tumor had a biphasic appearance with epithelial like tubular elements intricately admixed with long fascicles of spindle shaped cells. Cleft like spaces and tubules with intraluminal grey-blue mucinous material were seen. The spindle shaped cells had scant cytoplasm with elongated nuclei, fine chromatin and inconspicuous nucleoli. The epithelial component comprised of cuboidal to columnar cells with nuclei having similar bland chromatin. Interspersed thick collagen was noted with focal calcification. Mitosis was scarce (1–2/mm2) and no necrosis was seen. Periphery of the tumor showed regressed thymic tissue. No normal thyroid parenchyma was identified (Fig. 2). Four lymph nodes included in the specimen were all free of tumor.

On histopathology, the differentials ranged from primary epithelial malignancy of thyroid such as medullary carcinoma (spindle cell type) to mesenchymal neoplasms like synovial sarcoma (SS) and solitary fibrous tumor (SFT) as well as thymic neoplasms including thymoma. In view of a circumscribed tumor with low mitotic activity in a resectable specimen, anaplastic carcinoma thyroid was not amongst the top differentials. On IHC the tumor cells expressed CK, CK5/6, p40, p16, TLE1, Vimentin and CD99 diffusely while EMA and SMA both showed focal heterogenous expression. Immunostains for PAX8, TTF1, CD20, CD5, CD117, STAT6 and CK7 were negative. Tdt was negative in the sparse intratumoral reactive lymphocytes. Ki67 labelling index was approximately 05% and p53 showed wild type expression (Fig. 3). A final diagnosis of SETTLE was rendered.

Separately sent “left hemithyroid” showed thyroid follicular nodular disease with lymphocytic thyroiditis, devoid of any atypia or malignancy. The MND included 34 lymph nodes all of which were free of tumor.

A follow up Positron emission tomography (PET) scan was performed which did not show any residual disease. A direct laryngoscopic examination was also done which did not show any mucosal abnormality. The patient was kept under follow up and remains disease free after a post-operative period of one year.

Discussion

SETTLE is a singular malignant neoplasm of the thyroid originally described by Chan and Rosai [11]. It is an indolent tumor with a predeliction for young adults [1, 5]. Our patient was a postmenopausal lady and only five cases have been reported in indexed literature so far, presenting in older age group patients > 50 years of age [4, 6, 12–14]. Table 1 enumerates all these cases presenting in older age group patients. Out of the five cases published in literature so far, three of the patients presented with a neck mass of variable duration, one patient presented with symptoms of Superior vena cava syndrome and Disseminated intravascular coagulation due a large neck mass extending into the mediastinum with distant metastasis at presentation while one case was incidentally detected during workup for a parathyroid adenoma. The tumor size ranged from 1 cm to about 10 cm in maximum dimension.

SETTLE can be infiltrative and typically shows a biphasic morphology on histology with presence of epithelial glandular like and spindled cells with scant cytoplasm. Ippolito et al. demonstrated that many cases show a tubopapillary to glomeroid architecture as part of the epithelial elements [3]. The tumor cells have a bland chromatin and are devoid of any significant atypia or mitosis. Our case too showed a similar morphology. Additionally, regressed thymic tissue was also identified peripherally correlating with the thymic origin. Despite its characteristic histomorphology, it may be misdiagnosed due to its rarity and existence of morphologic mimics. The differentials considered are spindle cell neoplasms of the head and neck such as sarcomatoid squamous cell carcinoma, spindle cell variant of medullary thyroid carcinoma, anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, thymoma and SS [6]. Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma is a grossly infiltrative tumor with locoregional metastasis at presentation exhibiting high grade nuclear atypia and pleomorphism on microscopy as opposed to the bland nature of tumor cells seen in this case. It may show PAX8 positivity and expression of p40 & CK5/6 in the squamous cell carcinoma phenotype [15]. Medullary carcinoma of thyroid can show a spindle cell morphology but usually also shows presence of amyloid and diffuse TTF1 positivity unlike our case. The presented case was misdiagnosed as a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma on initial workup possibly due to the co expression of CK, CK5/6 and p40 in the absence of expression of markers of primary thyroid malignancies such as PAX8 and TTF1. Additionally it also showed p16 positivity which further acted as a trap for the histopathologist due to its common association with pharyngeal squamous cell carcinomas [16] and led to the erroneous diagnosis along with a suggestion of an oropharyngeal primary. p16 expression in SETTLE has not been documented so far and needs evaluation in the documented cases to determine its incidence and significance in this rare tumor.

A judicious panel of IHC in conjunction with the clinicoradiological findings with a high index of suspicion can aid in differentiating SETTLE from its other close mimics such as thymoma and SS. Subtle morphological clues like presence of mast cells, increased nuclear grade and mitosis, presence of apoptotic debris and non lobulated pattern of growth point towards a SS which also shows TLE1 positivity while a thymoma (type A or B) generally shows a more jigsaw pattern of architecture with coexisting immature T cells. On IHC, SS is negative for p40 which helped in ruling out this differential. Thymoma especially type A with spindle and glandular pattern also shows both HMWCK and p40 expression and may also show CD20 expression, but is negative for TLE1. Usually both the spindled and epithelial elements in SETTLE are immunopositive for CK, HMWCK, CK7, p40, p63, Vimentin, EMA, CD99, bcl2, CD117 and TLE1 while being negative for CK20, thyroglobulin, PAX8, TTF1, TdT, S100, CD34 and Calretinin [1]. Our case was immunonegative for CK7 and CD117 which may be negative in 10% and 25% of cases respectively [5]. STAT6 was done to rule out an SFT. Recondo et al. performed a comprehensive review and included 42 cases of SETTLE with a mean age of 19 years. The study showed that though it was an indolent neoplasm with very less disease related mortality, around 40% of the cases developed metastasis in the long term [2].

SETTLE lacks the genetic translocations associated with SS and no hallmark genetic mutation has been described. However, single cases may be associated with KRAS, NRAS, KMT2D or KMT2C mutations [17]. Non metastatic tumors are treated with surgical resection and nodal dissection may be avoided [3]. In our case, surgery with nodal dissection was performed due to an erroneous diagnosis. It has a good overall survival but may show delayed metastasis with lung being a common site [2]. Hence, the importance of a close and long term follow up cannot be overemphasized in this indolent malignant neoplasm.

Conclusion

To conclude, SETTLE is a rare malignant tumor of the thyroid gland with a favourable clinical outcome. The key to recognizing this tumor lies in the knowledge of its existence, correlation with clinicoradiological findings and diligent histopathological examination espoused with a judicious panel of IHC markers. Diffuse and strong p16 immunoexpression has not been documented or evaluated in literature so far, and needs to be explored for its diagnostic utility in this rare entity.

Identification and validation of lactylation-related diagnostic biomarkers for type 2 diabetes by WGCNA
Authors: Najun Zhu, Suping Gu, Ying Shen, Lvxia Zhou, Weiping Tu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lactylation, a novel post-translational histone modification, has emerged as a critical regulatory mechanism in various metabolic disorders. However, its role in the pathogenesis of type 2 diabetes (T2D) remains poorly understood. This study aims to investigate the potential of lactylation-related genes as diagnostic biomarkers for T2D. Differential analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) were performed on the GSE164416 dataset. Genes obtained from these analyses were intersected with the lactylation-related genes to screen candidate genes. The LASSO, SVM-RFE and random forest algorithms were applied to screen the characteristic genes, and their diagnostic efficacy was verified in the independent cohort. The functions and immune associations were analyzed by GSVA, ssGSEA, and TF-miRNA regulatory network analysis, and qRT-PCR, Western blot and CCK-8 experiments were conducted in the T2D cell model for verification. Lactylation-related IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM were identified as potential diagnostic markers for T2D. These three genes were significantly upregulated in T2D samples and exhibited excellent diagnostic performance (AUC >0.80) in both the training set and validation set. The GSVA analysis revealed that these three genes were involved in key biological processes such as immune regulation, transcriptional modification, metabolic homeostasis and cytoskeleton remodeling. Cell experiments demonstrated that the three genes were upregulated in T2D cell models and knockdown of their expression could promote cell viability. This study identified and validated three potential diagnostic markers related to lactylation for T2D, providing new molecular evidence for the early diagnosis and mechanism research of this disease.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a prevalent and complex metabolic disorder that poses a significant global public health challenge due to its high prevalence, disability rate, and mortality.(1) According to the 2021 GBD study, DM ranks among the leading causes of death worldwide, affecting individuals across all age groups, including children, adolescents, and the elderly.(2) Type 2 diabetes (T2D), the most common subtype of DM, accounts for over 90% of all cases and is primarily characterized by mechanisms such as insulin resistance and pancreatic β-cell dysfunction (reduced insulin secretion and increased apoptosis), which distinctly differ from the immune-mediated pathogenesis of type 1 diabetes (T1D).(3,4) Current clinical diagnosis of DM primarily relies on glucose-related indices, including fasting plasma glucose, oral glucose tolerance test, and glycated hemoglobin.(5) However, these metrics exhibit notable limitations in early risk identification, dynamic monitoring, and complication risk prediction.(6–8) While several biomarkers—such as MicroRNAs,(9) BCAAs,(10) 7-methylxanthine,(11) and NogoR(12)—have been reported to show predictive potential for T2D, their clinical utility remains constrained. The heterogeneous pathophysiology of T2D, involving chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and epigenetic regulation,(13,14) contributes to the suboptimal sensitivity and generalizability of existing biomarkers. Thus, there is an urgent need to identify more specific and functionally relevant diagnostic biomarkers to advance precision diagnosis and personalized therapeutic strategies for T2D.

With the advancement of metabolomics and epigenetics, lactate and its mediated post-translational modification—lactylation—have been recognized as critical regulators in DM and its multisystem complications. For instance, in diabetic nephropathy, lactylation promotes renal tubular or podocytes epithelial-mesenchymal transition, mitochondrial dysfunction, and fibrosis by upregulating pathways such as KLF5 and ACSF2.(15–17) In diabetic cardiomyopathy, lactate abnormally transports and elevates H4K12la in macrophages via MCT4, exacerbating inflammatory responses and myocardial injury.(18) In diabetic retinopathy, lactylation drives FTO expression, modulates m6A methylation levels, accelerating angiogenesis and neuronal damage.(19) Additionally, lactylation participates in other DM-related pathological processes, including vascular calcification(20) and cognitive impairment.(21) Collectively, lactate not only functions as a metabolic byproduct in glucose homeostasis but also, through lactylation, may profoundly influence transcriptional activity, chromatin states, and inflammatory factor expression, thereby contributing to the molecular pathogenesis of T2D. However, systematic investigations into lactylation-related genes and their regulatory mechanisms in T2D remain scarce, and the potential of lactylation as a target for early diagnosis or subtype stratification of T2D remains to be elucidated.

This study aims to identify lactylation-related diagnostic biomarkers for T2D by integrating publicly available transcriptomic data with a lactylation gene set and constructing a weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to pinpoint key modules significantly associated with T2D. Through multi-algorithm machine learning, GSVA and ssGSEA approaches, we refined characteristic genes and investigated their functional enrichment pathways and immune infiltration profiles. Furthermore, transcriptional regulator (TF) and miRNA network analyses elucidated upstream regulatory mechanisms of these candidate genes. Our findings are expected to provide novel theoretical insights and methodological frameworks for molecular subtyping, early diagnosis, and potential therapeutic targeting in T2D. Materials and Methods

Date acquisition

Public T2D transcriptomic datasets were obtained from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), including GSE164416 as the training set and GSE25724 as the validation set. Detailed sample characteristics for each dataset are presented in Table 1. The lactylation gene set used in this study was based on the list compiled by Cheng et al.(22) In that paper, the authors integrated the previous proteomic research data based on mass spectrometry(23) and lactylation-related information reported by other literature,(24) ultimately reporting a gene set containing 327 lactylation-related genes. This study adopted the integrated gene list as the basic dataset for analysis (Supplemental Table 1*).

Identification of DEGs and functional enrichment analysis

DEGs were identified from the GSE164416 cohort comprising normal and T2D samples using the “DESeq2” package(25) with thresholds of |log2FC| >1 and FDR <0.05. Subsequently, upregulated DEGs (up-DEGs) were selected as the input gene set for GO and KEGG pathway analyses using the “clusterProfiler” package(26) with significance thresholds of p. adjust <0.05. Wikipathways enrichment analysis was performed by downloading the human pathway annotation files from Wikipathways web (https://www.wikipathways.org) and applying the “enricher” function in “clusterProfiler”.

Identification of T2D-associated key gene modules using WGCNA

We conducted WGCNA using the “WGCNA” R package(27) on the GSE164416 dataset to identify gene modules significantly associated with T2D, with sample’s T2D grouping information serving as the phenotypic trait for network construction. The analysis began with network topology analysis to determine the appropriate soft-thresholding power, ensuring the constructed network satisfied scale-free topology criteria. Subsequently, we calculated gene adjacency relationships and transformed them into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to evaluate gene-gene similarity. Based on the TOM dissimilarity measure, we performed hierarchical clustering to generate a gene dendrogram. For module identification, we implemented dynamic tree cutting with a minimum module size of 30 genes to initially partitioned the dendrogram. These modules were then refined through merging using a threshold of 0.2 to consolidate modules with similar expression patterns, resulting in biologically meaningful and stable gene modules. Finally, we employed the “pheatmap” package to visualize the correlation between gene modules and T2D phenotypic traits, identifying the key modules significantly associated with T2D.

Identification of lactylation-related signature genes in T2D and evaluation of their diagnostic value

To identify lactylation-related DEGs in T2D, we performed a Venn analysis on DEGs obtained from differential expression analysis, T2D-associated module genes identified by WGCNA, and the lactylation-related gene set. Subsequently, multiple machine learning algorithms were employed to screen for signature genes. For LASSO regression analysis, we utilized the “glmnet” package(28) to eliminate genes with multicollinearity from the intersected gene set while identifying feature genes with higher coefficients. In SVM-RFE analysis, we performed 10-fold cross-validation using the “e1071” R package(29) and “svmRFE” algorithm to determine the optimal feature subset by minimizing classification error. For RF analysis, we employed the “randomForest” package(30) to construct multiple decision trees and identify feature genes based on their importance scores. The consensus genes identified by all three machine learning algorithms were then collected through Venn analysis. We further examined the differential expression patterns of these consensus genes in T2D samples within the training set. To evaluate the diagnostic potential of the signature genes, we generated ROC curves in both training and validation sets using the “pROC” R package.(31)

GSVA analysis of diagnostic genes

Based on the median expression levels of the diagnostic genes, we stratified the training set samples into high-expression and low-expression groups. To investigate the underlying biological differences between these groups, we performed GSVA with KEGG and GO gene sets as predefined reference collections to calculate enrichment scores, thereby evaluating the activity changes of functional pathways across different expression groups.

Analysis of immune features in T2D and their correlation with diagnostic genes

To examine differences in the immune microenvironment between the T2D group and healthy controls, we performed ssGSEA to quantify immune cell infiltration levels and immune-related functional activity. Boxplots were generated to display differences in immune features between the two groups. Additionally, to assess potential associations between diagnostic genes and immune cells, we calculated Spearman correlation coefficients between them. The results were visualized as lollipop plots using the “ggplot2” R package.(32)

Construction of TF-miRNA regulatory network for hub genes

To further elucidate the upstream regulatory mechanisms of hub genes, we utilized the Network Analyst platform (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) to construct regulatory networks involving TFs and miRNAs targeting these hub genes. The regulatory relationships were derived from the RegNetwork database to reveal potential transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms.

Cell culture and model construction

The mouse pancreatic β cells (MIN6, QS-M031) were purchased from Keycell Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China) and cultured in DMEM medium (Gibco, Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Thermo Fisher) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin double antibody (P/S) (Thermo Fisher). The cells were cultured at 37°C and 5% CO2. When the cell confluence reached 60–80%, the cells were continuously treated with 200 ‍μM palmitic acid (MCE, Monmouth Junction, NJ) and 25 ‍mM glucose (MCE) to construct an in vitro model of T2D.

Cell transfection

The small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM (si-IKZF1, si-S100A4, and si-VIM) and their negative control (si-NC) were all synthesized by GenePharma (Suzhou, China). Cell transfection was carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions using LipofectamineTM 3000 (Thermo Fisher). MIN6 cells were seeded at a density of 2 × 104 cells/well in a 6-well plate. Subsequently, the transfection reagent LipofectamineTM 3000 was used to transfect si-IKZF1, si-S100A4, and si-VIM, or the corresponding negative control (si-NC) at 37°C for 48 ‍h. The transfection efficiency detection and related experiments were conducted 48 ‍h after transfection.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol reagent (Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), and its concentration and purity were determined by a spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher). Subsequently, RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the FastKing cDNA First-Chain Synthesis Kit (without genomic DNA) (TIANGEN, Beijing, China). The qPCR reaction system was constructed according to the Talent Fluorescence Quantitative Detection Kit (SYBR Green) (TIANGEN), with a total reaction volume of 20 ‍μl. The reaction was performed on the ABI 7500 Real-Time Fluorescence Quantitative PCR Instrument (Thermo Fisher). β-Actin was used as the reference gene, and the relative expression level of the target gene was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Primer information is shown in Table 2.

Western blot (WB)

Total proteins were extracted from cells using RIPA lysis buffer containing protease inhibitor (PMSF) (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The total protein concentration in the lysate was determined using BCA protein concentration assay kit (Beyotime). Then, 20 ‍μg of protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel (SDS-PAGE) electrophoresis. After separation, the proteins in the gel were transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (Sigma Aldrich, St. Louis, MO), and incubated with the primary antibody overnight at 4°C. Subsequently, the membrane was incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature for 2 ‍h. After washing with PBST, the protein bands were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence detection reagent (ECL) (Yeasen Biotechnology) and iBright imaging system (Thermo Fisher). Antibody information: IKZF1 (ab191394; Abcam, Cambridge, UK), S100A4 (ab197896; Abcam), VIM (ab92547; Abca), Pan-Kla (STJ11101522; St John’s Laboratory, London, UK), β-actin (ab213262; Abcam), Goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (ab6721; Abcam).

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8)

The cells that have undergone different treatments were inoculated into 96-well plates at a density of 2,000 cells per well, and 10 ‍μl of CCK-8 reagent (Yeasen Biotechnology) was added to each well at 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 ‍h respectively. Then, the cells were further incubated in a 37°C constant temperature incubator for 4 ‍h, and the absorbance (OD) values of each well were measured at 450 ‍nm wavelength using a multi-functional microplate reader (Thermo Fisher).

Statistical analysis

All experiments were repeated three times, and the data were presented as the mean ± SD. The comparison between the two groups of data was conducted using the Student t test. All data analysis and chart visualization were performed using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad, Boston, MA). A p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Differential expression and enrichment analysis

Differential expression analysis between normal and T2D groups in the GSE164416 dataset identified 585 DEGs, including 554 upregulated and 31 downregulated genes (Fig. 1A). To investigate potential biological functions and regulatory pathways associated with T2D, we performed Wikipathways, GO, and KEGG enrichment analyses on the upregulated genes (Fig. 1B–D). The Wikipathways analysis showed that upregulated genes were significantly enriched in multiple immune-related and cell signal transduction pathways, including the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, miRNA targets in extracellular matrix and membrane receptors, inflammatory response pathway, focal adhesion-PI3K-Akt-mTOR signaling pathway, and cytokine and receptor interactions (Fig. 1B). GO enrichment analysis revealed these genes were primarily involved in immune activation and inflammatory response processes such as regulation of T cell activation, regulation of leukocyte and intercellular adhesion, regulation of cell-cell adhesion, positive regulation of lymphocyte activation, and leukocyte mediated immunity (Fig. 1C). Further KEGG pathway analysis demonstrated significant enrichment of upregulated genes in protein digestion and absorption, ECM-receptor interactions, complement and coagulation cascades, cell adhesion molecules, and allograft rejection (Fig. 1D). These analyses suggest that in T2D, multiple key pathways involving immune regulation, inflammatory response and cell adhesion may be persistently activated, indicating their potential important roles in T2D pathogenesis.

Identification of lactylation-related genes in T2D based on WGCNA

To establish a robust gene co-expression network, we performed stringent preprocessing of the original expression matrix (containing 18,455 genes and 57 samples). Variance-based filtering was applied to retain the top 25% most variable genes, resulting in 4,614 high-variability genes for downstream analysis. Using this dataset, we constructed the co-expression network via WGCNA algorithm. The soft thresholding power was set to 11, which resulted in a scale-free R2 of 0.85, confirming the network’s scale-free property (Fig. 2A). Hierarchical clustering analysis identified 9 distinct gene modules within the co-expression network (Fig. 2B). Subsequent module-trait relationship analysis revealed the MEblue module as the most significantly correlated with T2D phenotype (Fig. 2C). This module contained 1,178 T2D-associated genes. To identify potential lactylation-regulated key genes in T2D pathogenesis, we intersected these module genes with 585 T2D-related DEGs and 325 lactylation-related genes, ultimately obtaining 7 T2D-lactylation-associated candidate genes (Fig. 2D).

Identification of lactylation-related signature genes and evaluation of their diagnostic value in T2D

To further evaluate the diagnostic potential of the 7 identified lactylation-related genes, we applied three machine learning algorithms for feature selection. In LASSO regression analysis with cross-validation (optimal penalty parameter λ = 4), four genes with non-zero coefficients were selected: VIM, MNDA, IKZF1, and S100A4 (Fig. 3A). SVM-RFE algorithm with 10-fold cross-validation (based on minimum error criterion) identified four most discriminative genes: VIM, S100A4, IKZF1, and LGALS1 (Fig. 3B). RF analysis ranked the top five important genes as: VIM, S100A4, IKZF1, LGALS1, and MNDA (Fig. 3C). Intersection of these results yielded three consensus signature genes: VIM, S100A4, and IKZF1 (Fig. 3D). Expression analysis in the training set revealed significant upregulation of all three signature genes in T2D samples (p<0.01) (Fig. 3E). ROC curve analysis demonstrated their diagnostic potential, with AUC values in the training set being 0.722 (IKZF1), 0.798 (S100A4), and 0.778 (VIM), while in the validation set they were 0.881, 0.833, and 0.810, respectively (Fig. 3F and G). To investigate the significance of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM in T2D, we treated MIN6 cells with or without 200 ‍μM palmitic acid (MCE) and 25 ‍mM glucose (MCE) to establish an in vitro diabetic model, and set up groups: Con and Model. qRT-PCR and WB analysis showed that compared with the Con group, the mRNA and protein levels of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM were significantly upregulated in the Model group (Fig. 3H and I). Collectively, IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM consistently showed strong diagnostic value across both cohorts, suggesting their potential as T2D biomarkers.

Functional pathway enrichment characteristics of diagnostic genes based on GSVA

To further elucidate the potential biological functions of the diagnostic genes IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM, we stratified samples into high- and low-expression groups based on each gene expression levels and performed GSVA enrichment analysis for GO biological processes and KEGG pathways. The low-expression group of IKZF showed significant enrichment in immune-related processes including regulation of T cell-mediated cytotoxicity, Weibel–Palade body formation, and negative regulation of antigen receptor-mediated signaling, with corresponding enrichment in the intestinal immune network and antigen processing/presentation pathways. In contrast, the high-expression group demonstrated activation of fundamental nuclear processes such as ribosome biogenesis, rRNA processing, and nuclear replication, along with enrichment in post-transcriptional modification and DNA repair pathways including nucleotide excision repair, RNA degradation, and basal transcription machinery (Fig. 4A and B). S100A4 low-expression samples were enriched in vascular function and cell motility processes (angiogenesis and endothelial cell proliferation/migration) and immune-regulatory pathways (TGF-β signaling, hematopoietic cell lineage differentiation, and cytokine-receptor interactions). The S100A4 high-expression group exhibited metabolic and secretory regulation features (pyrimidine metabolism, glutamate metabolism, and potassium channel activity) and significant involvement in amino acid/polysaccharide metabolic pathways (lysine degradation and glycosaminoglycan degradation) (Fig. 4C and D). Analysis of VIM expression revealed that low-expression samples were enriched in inflammation and cellular plasticity processes (immune cell migration, epithelial-mesenchymal transition regulation, and interferon-mediated immune response) and immune/inflammatory pathways (TGF-β signaling, NOD-like receptor signaling, and leukocyte transendothelial migration). The high-expression group predominantly showed enrichment in cellular structure/stress-response processes (mitochondrion transport, hypoxia-induced apoptotic signaling, and cytoskeletal microtubule function) and metabolic/genome stability pathways (glycan metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, and base excision repair) (Fig. 4E and F). Collectively, these three diagnostic genes exhibited distinct functional enrichment patterns under different expression states, suggesting their potential involvement in T2D pathogenesis through diverse mechanisms including immune regulation, cell cycle control, transcriptional modification, and metabolic pathways.

Analysis of immune infiltration characteristics and correlation with diagnostic genes

To further investigate the differences in the immune microenvironment between the T2D group and the control group, we evaluated the infiltration levels of immune cells and immune functions using the ssGSEA algorithm. The results revealed that the T2D group exhibited significantly higher enrichment levels in multiple immune functions and immune cell types compared to the control group, including APC co-inhibition, immune checkpoints, cytolytic activity, HLA expression, inflammation-promoting responses, macrophages, mast cells, MHC class I, neutrophils, parainflammation, pDCs, T helper cells, and TILs. In contrast, the control group showed higher infiltration levels of CD8+ T cells (p<0.05) (Fig. 5A). Furthermore, we explored the correlation between the three diagnostic genes (IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM) and immune cells/functions. The results demonstrated that IKZF1 was significantly positively correlated with HLA, TILs, parainflammation, immune checkpoints, neutrophils, macrophages, APC co-inhibition, inflammation-promoting responses, cytolytic activity, T helper cells, and pDCs, while showing a significant negative correlation with CD8+ T cells (p<0.05) (Fig. 5B). S100A4 exhibited significant positive correlations with macrophages, TILs, immune checkpoints, APC co-inhibition, parainflammation, HLA, neutrophils, pDCs, T helper cells, inflammation-promoting responses, mast cells, and cytolytic activity (p<0.05) (Fig. 5C). VIM was significantly positively correlated with TILs, T helper cells, HLA, mast cells, neutrophils, macrophages, immune checkpoints, parainflammation, APC co-inhibition, inflammation-promoting responses, cytolytic activity, APC co-stimulation, and pDCs, while also displaying a significant negative correlation with CD8+ T cells (p<0.05) (Fig. 5D). Collectively, these analyses suggest that individuals with T2D exhibit a more complex immune infiltration microenvironment, and the identified diagnostic genes may participate in the immunopathological mechanisms of the disease by regulating key immune cells and functional pathways.

Prediction analysis of miRNA and TF regulatory networks

To investigate the upstream regulatory mechanisms of the diagnostic genes (IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM), we constructed miRNA-gene and TF-gene regulatory networks based on database predictions. By applying a connectivity threshold (Degree >2), we identified 16 potential regulatory miRNAs, including: hsa-miR-26b-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p, hsa-miR-27b-3p, hsa-miR-424-5p, hsa-miR-126-3p, hsa-miR-18b-5p, hsa-miR-15a-5p, hsa-miR-34a-5p, hsa-miR-16-5p, hsa-let-7b-5p, hsa-miR-92b-3p, hsa-miR-18a-5p, hsa-miR-92a-3p, hsa-miR-27a-3p, hsa-miR-106a-5p, and hsa-miR-30b-3p (Fig. 6A). Additionally, using the RegNetwork database and a threshold of Degree >1, we further identified 7 TFs that may regulate these diagnostic genes: ZBTB11, MAZ, GATA2, SIN3A, ZNF175, ZFX, and ADNP (Fig. 6B). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis revealed potential regulatory relationships among miRNAs, TFs, and target genes, providing a theoretical foundation for understanding their roles in the pathogenesis of T2D.

Reducing expression of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM can decrease their lactate levels and enhance the viability of pancreatic β cells

To clarify the functions of these genes, we knocked down the expression of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM in MIN6 cells and treated them with palmitic acid and glucose to induce a T2D cell model. qRT-PCR analysis showed that IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM were successfully knocked down (Fig. 7A). Since IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM are all genes related to lactylation modification, we further detected the overall lactate level in the cells by WB. The results showed that, compared with the control group, the total lactylation in the cells was significantly inhibited after knocking down IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM (Fig. 7B). Additionally, CCK-8 assay revealed that, compared with the control group, the expression of IKZF1, S100A4, or VIM being downregulated significantly increased the proliferation of MIN6 cells (Fig. 7C). These results further confirmed that IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM, as T2D biomarkers, could affect the viability of pancreatic β cells.

Discussions

T2D is a chronic metabolic disorder driven by multiple factors. Its pathogenesis involves not only insulin resistance and pancreatic β-cell dysfunction but is also closely associated with chronic inflammation, immune dysregulation, and metabolic reprogramming.(33–36) Serum lactate levels in patients with DM positively correlate with cardiac diastolic dysfunction, while salivary lactate dehydrogenase levels are significantly elevated in patients with DM and periodontitis, suggesting that lactylation-related molecules may serve as non-invasive biomarkers for microvascular complications.(18,37) The currently reported biomarkers for T2D, such as branched-chain amino acids, inflammatory factors, and miRNAs, mainly reflect systemic metabolic imbalance or chronic inflammatory states, and have certain value in disease risk assessment and monitoring.(38–40) However, these biomarkers mostly remain at the phenotypic or correlational level, and their explanatory power at the molecular mechanism level is still relatively limited, especially difficult to reveal the intrinsic connection between metabolic reprogramming and immune imbalance. In contrast, lactylation, as an emerging post-translational modification related to metabolism, can directly respond to the cellular metabolic state and participate in transcriptional regulation and immune regulation. Its diagnostic and mechanistic significance in diabetes still needs to be systematically clarified. This study, for the first time, adapt a lactylation-centric approach, systematically integrating differential expression analysis, WGCNA, machine learning-based biomarker screening, immune infiltration assessment, and functional annotation to identify novel diagnostic biomarkers strongly associated with T2D. Furthermore, we comprehensively investigated their biological mechanisms and regulatory networks in disease progression.

Our differential expression analysis identified a substantial number of upregulated genes in the T2D group, which were significantly enriched in immune- and inflammation-related pathways, including inflammatory response pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling, and focal adhesion. The activation of these pathways suggests a central role of inflammatory responses in T2D pathogenesis. Consistent with previous research, inflammatory response is not only a key contributing factor to T2D development, but further intensifies in the persistent hyperglycemic environment, thereby promoting the onset of various diabetic complications (e.g., cardiovascular, renal, and neurological damage).(41,42) The PI3K-Akt pathway, a critical regulator of insulin signal transduction, shows abnormal enrichment in T2D and is believed to impair glucose uptake and cause systemic metabolic disturbances, which is a fundamental mechanism underlying insulin resistance.(43) Notably, dysregulation of the focal adhesion pathway also holds particular significance in diabetic bladder pathology. Emerging evidence indicates a strong association between this pathway’s disruption and functional impairment of the bladder detrusor muscle.(44) In summary, the aberrant activation of immune-inflammatory pathways likely plays a pivotal regulatory role in both T2D pathogenesis and complication development.

Through integrated analysis employing WGCNA, intersectional screening, and machine learning algorithms, we identified three lactylation-related diagnostic genes strongly associated with T2D: IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM. All three genes demonstrated significant overexpression in T2D patients and exhibited robust diagnostic performance across both training and validation cohorts, suggesting their potential biological significance in T2D pathogenesis. IKZF1, a member of the IKAROS TF family, plays critical roles in lymphocyte development, differentiation, and immune responses.(45) Dysregulation or mutations of IKZF1 have been closely linked to various immune-related disorders, where it modulates immune reactions, maintains immune tolerance, and suppresses excessive immune cell activation.(46) Notably, while direct evidence connecting IKZF1 to T2D remains limited, computational analyses have revealed associations between IKAROS TF binding sites and increased T2D risk in Mexican populations.(47) Furthermore, Swafford et al. demonstrated that IKZF1, encoding IKAROS, not only relates to childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia but also exhibits protective effects against T1D.(48) Our study is the first to identify significant IKZF1 overexpression in T2D from a lactylation perspective. GSVA enrichment analysis revealed that its high-expression group predominantly enriches post-translational modification and nucleotide excision repair pathways, while the low-expression group shows bias toward antigen presentation and immune regulation pathways. These findings suggested IKZF1 may regulate T2D’s chronic inflammatory progression through dual mechanisms involving epigenetic regulation and immune modulation. S100A4, a member of the S100 calcium-binding protein family, serves both as a fibroblast marker and participates extensively in inflammatory responses and tumor metastasis.(49,50) While previous studies have not fully elucidated its post-translational modification mechanisms, emerging evidence indicates that elevated serum S100A4 levels are not only associated with T2D but also demonstrate potential as a diagnostic biomarker for diabetic nephropathy.(51,52) Our study revealed that S100A4 exhibits lactylation-related overexpression in T2D, suggesting its potential involvement in chronic inflammation and metabolic dysregulation through novel lactylation-mediated regulatory mechanisms. Furthermore, GSVA functional annotation demonstrates that aberrant S100A4 expression correlates with the regulation of metabolic-immune balance, indicating its possible role in disease progression by linking immune-inflammatory responses with metabolic remodeling processes. VIM, an intermediate filament protein expressed in various cell types,(53) has been reported to show elevated expression in T2D. This elevation is thought to be associated with the dedifferentiation, plasticity, and potential loss of cellular identity in both α-cells (glucagon-secreting) and β-cells (insulin-secreting), which may consequently lead to islet cell dysfunction.(54) Our findings demonstrated that VIM was not only significantly upregulated in T2D but also displayed lactylation modification tendencies. Functional enrichment analysis revealed its involvement in inflammation, immunity, substance metabolism, and DNA stability maintenance pathways. These results suggested that VIM served as an important lactylation marker in T2D and participated in tissue pathological damage and functional abnormalities by coordinating mechanisms such as cellular structural remodeling, immune responses, and metabolic stress. It is worth noting that although IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM have all been identified as lactylation-related diagnostic markers, their potential clinical application forms may vary. S100A4, as a secreted protein, has been studied and confirmed to have significantly elevated serum levels in T2D and related complications. Therefore, it has more potential as a non-invasive body fluid biomarker. In contrast, VIM, as a cytoskeletal protein, and IKZF1, as a nuclear transcription factor, although not suitable for direct body fluid detection, their abnormal expressions can still serve as molecular diagnostic markers at the tissue or cell level to reflect the functional status of pancreatic islet cells, the degree of immune imbalance, and the risk of disease progression. Moreover, these molecular biomarkers also have significant value in mechanism research, disease stratification, and the screening of potential therapeutic targets.

We further verified the functional significance of the aforementioned genes in T2D through in vitro experiments. After constructing a T2D model by treating MIN6 cells with palmitic acid and high glucose, IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM were significantly upregulated at both mRNA and protein levels, confirming their stable abnormal expression under diabetic-like conditions. After further knocking down these genes, the overall lactate level in the cells significantly decreased, and the proliferation ability of MIN6 cells significantly increased, suggesting that IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM may inhibit pancreatic β-cell activity and aggravate cell functional damage by regulating the lactate level. In summary, this study not only expanded the functional understanding of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM in T2D from the perspective of lactate modification, but also confirmed their key role in regulating the lactate level and pancreatic β-cell activity through in vitro experiments. These findings provide new experimental evidence for elucidating the pathological mechanism of the lactate-immune-metabolism axis in T2D and offer potential candidate targets for future intervention strategies targeting lactylation-related molecules.

Further immune infiltration analysis revealed significantly enhanced activity of multiple immune cells and functions in T2D samples, particularly demonstrating marked enrichment of macrophages and neutrophils, suggesting their potentially pivotal roles in shaping the T2D immune microenvironment. An existing study has established T2D as a classic inflammatory metabolic disorder, where chronic low-grade inflammation is primarily driven by macrophage activation across various tissues and organs.(55) Neutrophils, as critical mediators of early inflammatory responses, not only facilitate interactions between macrophages and antigen-presenting cells but also exacerbate localized chronic inflammation through pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion.(56) In the inflammatory context of T2D, neutrophils release elastase and myeloperoxidase, collectively contributing to neutrophil extracellular trap formation and thereby sustaining inflammatory cascades.(57) Our results demonstrated that the expression levels of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM exhibited significant positive correlations with multiple immune cell populations, while showing a negative association with CD8+ T cells. This pattern implies that these biomarkers may be closely linked to T2D-specific immune cell composition and functional dysregulation.

Finally, through predictive analysis of miRNA and TF regulatory networks, we constructed potential upstream regulatory mechanisms. Our findings identified multiple immune-related miRNAs (e.g., hsa-miR-155-5p, hsa-miR-26b-5p) and TFs (e.g., GATA2, SIN3A) that may regulate the expression of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM, suggesting their potential roles in modulating the T2D immune microenvironment and associated lactylation modification processes. Specifically, several transcription factors identified in this regulatory network, such as GATA2 and SIN3A, have been reported to play important roles in immune regulation and cellular stress responses.(58–61) GATA2 is a zinc finger transcription factor that participates in hematopoietic cell differentiation and immune homeostasis, and its dysregulation is associated with inflammation and metabolic disorders.(62–64) The predicted regulatory relationship between GATA2 and IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM indicates that these lactylation-related diagnostic genes may be controlled by immune-related transcriptional programs. It is notable that both IKZF1 and GATA2 are key regulators of immune cell differentiation,(63,65) suggesting a potential synergistic regulatory axis that links immune imbalance with gene expression changes in T2D. At the same time, the transcriptional regulation of these transcription factors on S100A4 and VIM may help with the cell cytoskeleton remodeling and cellular stress response associated with inflammation under diabetic conditions. Although these regulatory relationships are based on database predictions, the constructed TF-gene and PPI networks provide a biologically reasonable framework that links transcriptional regulation, immune dysregulation, and lactylation-related gene expression in T2D. However, these interactions still need to be verified through experiments, and more research is required to confirm the direct binding and regulatory effects of these transcription factors with target genes.

In summary, this study systematically identified and validated three lactylation-related diagnostic biomarkers for T2D from a novel lactylation perspective, revealing their potential involvement in T2D progression through the regulation of immune responses, metabolic processes, and transcriptional modifications. These findings expanded the understanding of lactylation’s role in T2D and provided new therapeutic targets and theoretical foundations for interventions targeting the immune-metabolic axis. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. (1) Although we have conducted preliminary functional validation of the abnormal expressions of IKZF1, S100A4, and VIM and their effects on the overall lactate level and the viability of pancreatic β cells through the MIN6 cell diabetic in vitro model, there is still a lack of systematic validation using in vivo animal models and independent clinical samples. Future studies can combine T2D animal models and multi-center clinical cohorts to further clarify the causal roles of these lactylation-related genes in overall metabolic homeostasis, immune regulation, and disease progression, thereby enhancing the biological depth and clinical translational value of the research conclusions. (2) Although we established miRNA and TF regulatory networks, the dynamic regulatory processes and interactions with other epigenetic modifications remain unexplored. Subsequent studies could integrate multi-omics data, temporal analysis, and single-cell multi-omics technologies to systematically characterize the spatiotemporal specificity of lactylation-mediated regulatory networks in the T2D immune microenvironment. (3) This study was conducted based on a public database for analysis. The sample size, disease stage coverage, and the range of comorbidities were limited. Particularly, the external validation dataset GSE25724 only contained 13 samples, which might reduce the statistical stability of model performance evaluation and increase the risk of sampling bias, thereby limiting the generalization ability of the model across different populations. Although this dataset provided independent validation, the relevant results still need to be interpreted with caution. In the future, it is necessary to conduct systematic validation of the model and related markers in larger-scale, multi-center, multi-ethnic, and prospective clinical cohorts, combining clinical phenotypes and disease stages, to systematically verify the robustness and clinical translational potential of the model. In summary, the results of this study should mainly be understood as exploratory findings, aiming to depict the potential role of lactylation-related molecules and their regulatory axes in T2D, rather than providing a model with clinical diagnostic efficacy. These findings provide a reference framework for subsequent more systematic clinical and in vivo validation studies.

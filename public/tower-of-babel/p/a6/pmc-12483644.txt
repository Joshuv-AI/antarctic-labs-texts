Percutaneous closure of patent foramen ovale with complex anatomy using the Memopart™ occluder device
Authors: S. Gili, F. Cannata, M. Muratori, G. Santagostino Baldi, G. Teruzzi, G. Calligaris, D. Trabattoni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We conducted a single center observational study aimed to assess safety and efficacy of Balmed Memopart PFO occluder in treatment of complex PFO anatomies including at least one of the following: tunnel length ≥ 8 mm, atrial septal aneurysm, lipomatous hypertrophy of fossa ovalis rims (≥10 mm), cribriform or additional multiple small defects on the fossa ovalis, residual large Eustachian ridge or Chiari network. This analysis demonstrated Memopart PFO occluder device to be a safe and effective option for PFO closure, with good procedural success rate (100 %) and no significant safety issues (1 mild residual shunt at 24 h).

Introduction

Percutaneous closure of patent foramen ovale (PFO) is recommended in selected patients with cryptogenic stroke to prevent recurrent events, being superior to medical therapy [1].

Complex PFO anatomies have been reported in about 50 % of cases and associated with a higher risk of residual shunt and negative prognostic implication [2,3]. This report presents the results of the Balmed Memopart PFO occluder (Shanghai Shape Memory Alloy Co. Ltd) in patients with complex PFOs.

Methods

This observational, single-center cohort study, aimed to assess the performance of the Memopart PFO occlude in patients with challenging PFO anatomies, was performed between February and July 2024.

Inclusion criteria were clinical indication to PFO closure according to current guidelines and the presence of a complex PFO anatomy, including at least one of the following: tunnel length ≥ 8 mm, giant atrial septal aneurysm, lipomatous hypertrophy of fossa ovalis rims (≥10 mm), cribriform or additional multiple small defects, residual Eustachian ridge or Chiari network [3].

Exclusion criteria were inability to provide informed consent and age < 18 years.

All patients underwent a complete pre-procedural diagnostic workup with contrast echocardiography, EKG monitoring, carotid Doppler US, thrombophilic screening, cerebral magnetic resonance imaging.

PFO occluder device

The Memopart™ PFO is a double-disc, self-expandable device designed for closure of PFO. The two discs are made of nitinol filaments welded together and connected by a central waist, with an internal thin layer of polyester fabric to increase the sealing capability. This is a self-centering, retrievable and repositionable device, adapting to any anatomic PFO subset. A special oxidative treatment ensures a reduced release of nickel ions (release close to 0 %), whereas a superficial thermical treatment increases flexibility, trackability and easiness of device release. All device sizes are available also in a “hubless” fashion, associated with faster endothelization and lower thrombogenicity (Fig. 1). Disc diameters range from 18 to 34 mm. The release system is based on a screw mechanism while the delivery system is 10–12 F sheath compatible. Procedure

All procedures were performed with bilateral venous femoral approach, under ultrasound-guided puncture. Intraprocedural anticoagulation was obtained with unfractioned heparin at the dose of 80 UI/Kg and periprocedural antibiotic prophylaxis administered. Pre-procedural anatomic assessment, device sizing and intra-procedural guidance were performed with intracardiac-echo (ICE). After assessing by both ICE and fluoroscopy the correct and stable positioning of the device across the atrial septum with tug test, the occluder was released and implanted. Procedural success was defined as stable device positioning confirmed by fluoroscopy and ICE. Contrast-enhanced transthoracic echocardiography with bubble test was performed 24 h after the procedure to confirm the proper positioning and to exclude residual right-to-left shunt.

Dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin and clopidogrel) was prescribed for 2 months, followed by single antiplatelet therapy for at least 10 additional months.

Results

The Memopart device was implanted in 10 patients with median age of 59 years (interquartile range [IQR] 47–67 years). Atrial septal aneurysm was present in 6 pts., a long tunnel with lipomatous septum secundum in 3, and residual Chiari network in 1. Procedural and Anatomical Characteristics

All procedures were completed successfully with mean procedural time of 23.5 min (IQR 19.3–25.0 min), fluoroscopy time of 6.5 min (IQR 3.0–9.0 min) and DAP 502.5 cGy·cm2 (IQR 304.8–1236.3 cGy·cm2) and no complications.

We used the “hubless design” devices and different right-left disc sizes in two cases only.

Contrast transthoracic echocardiography (cTTE) performed before discharge revealed mild residual shunt at contrast-enhanced transthoracic echocardiography in one patient 24 h after the procedure. One patient (patient#7) presented a residual moderate-severe shunt, which was found to be determined by the concomitant presence of a large pulmonary fistula (but no residual shunt at PFO level).

Discussion

The present case series shows that the Memopart PFO Occluder can be safely and effectively adopted for PFO closure in the presence of complex anatomies. This is the first case series of consecutive patients with complex PFO anatomies treated with Memopart PFO occluder.

In our study, post-procedural cTTE showed mild residual right-left shunt in one out of ten (10 %) patients, only. Residual shunt is a known issue with percutaneous PFO closure. In a recent prospective study by Deng et al., using various closure devices, residual shunt was detected in 22.5 % by cTTE 24 h after the procedure, being moderate or severe in 8.6 % of the cases [4]. Marchese et al. documented a significant residual shunt in 21.6 % of the patients at 3 months, by contrast-enhanced transcranial colour-Doppler, with a large fossa ovalis longitudinal dimension and ASA being independently associated with a higher probability of this occurrence [5]. In the large randomized clinical trials prevalence of residual shunt varied due to different definitions and timing of assessment. Considering the complex PFO anatomies included in our case series, it is noteworthy that only one patient reported a mild residual shunt. This finding is of utmost importance given that residual shunt has been shown to be associated with a higher risk of stroke or TIA recurrence following PFO closure [4].

At the same time no significant procedural complications occurred, in particular no device embolization and no perforation. Hubless devices were implanted in all cases, with a consequent lower risk of atrial wall damage due to the blunt profile of the left disc.

Further follow-up and larger scale experiences are needed to confirm the promising results showed in our small case series.

Conclusions

The Memopart PFO occluder device is a safe and effective option for PFO closure in patients with complex anatomy with good procedural success rate and no significant safety issues in this small cohort of consecutive patients treated in a high-level center.

Cadmium-induced apoptosis via ROS/JNK pathway in chicken primary kidney cells and antagonism of taxifolin
Authors: Yiming Zhang, Zhe Li, Xinyu Liu, Bing Zhao, Tong Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cadmium (Cd) is a toxic environmental pollutant that can induce reactive oxygen species (ROS)-mediated apoptosis in kidney cells. Taxifolin (Tax), a dihydroflavonol with notable antioxidant activity, has recently attracted attention, but its effect on Cd-induced kidney cell apoptosis remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the protective mechanism of Tax against Cd-induced apoptosis in chicken primary kidney cells. We combined network pharmacology, network toxicology, and molecular docking to identify potential key targets, and then conducted in vitro verification. We hypothesized that Tax alleviates Cd-induced apoptosis by suppressing oxidative stress and inhibiting the JNK pathway, thereby modulating mitochondrial pathway-related apoptotic genes. AO/EB and Hoechst 33258 staining showed that Tax significantly reduced Cd-induced apoptosis. Detection of antioxidant enzyme mRNA levels and ROS confirmed that Tax alleviated Cd-induced oxidative stress. qRT-PCR and western blotting revealed that Cd activated the JNK pathway and increased expression of pro-apoptotic genes (Bad, Bid, Bax, Bak, DIABLO, Caspase-3, Caspase-9) while decreasing anti-apoptotic genes (Bcl-2, XIAP). Notably, Tax specifically inhibited Cd-induced JNK activation and reversed these apoptotic effects. Molecular docking confirmed interactions between Tax and JNK. The use of the JNK pathway inhibitor SP600125 also further confirmed this view. We conclude that Tax antagonizes Cd-induced apoptosis in chicken primary kidney cells by inhibiting the ROS/JNK pathway and modulating mitochondrial apoptosis-related gene expression.

Introduction

Cadmium (Cd) is a common environmental heavy metal pollutant and has a close association in the adverse effects of various organs in many species (Kawsar et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2024). It is estimated that approximately 16.1 % of agricultural land in China has been polluted by Cd in recent years, with a total polluted area of about 6.3 million square kilometers (Li et al., 2021). Cd accumulates in livestock that consume contaminated feed and is transferred to humans through the food chain, causing serious harm to both animals and humans (Kar and Patra, 2021). Cd can accumulate in multiple organs and tissues, leading to a high incidence of related diseases, including lung fibrosis, intestinal injury, reproductive toxicity and neurotoxicity (Chen et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2019a; Xue et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2019). A large number of studies have shown that Cd is an accumulated nephrotoxic substance. Due to the filtering function of the kidney, Cd tends to accumulate and persist in the kidney (Saleem et al., 2025). Cd exposure can cause metabolic disorders in chickens, thereby resulting in kidney damage (Wang et al., 2024). In numerous studies, oxidative stress has been identified as one of the main factors contributing to kidney dysfunction in chickens exposed to Cd (Wang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025). Understanding the underlying mechanism of Cd-induced kidney toxicity is crucial for developing effective strategies to prevent kidney disease by Cd exposure.

Apoptosis, also known as programmed cell death, serves as a molecular control point in biological processes (Chen et al., 2026). A variety of external stimuli can trigger apoptosis. When the generated ROS exceeds the scavenging capacity of the antioxidant defense system, it can cause oxidative stress in various tissues and induce apoptosis by inhibiting mitochondrial membrane potential and inducing cell cycle arrest (Sies 2015; Xu et al., 2025). Studies have demonstrated that Cd exposure can induce apoptosis in tissues such as the kidneys, liver, and testes of chickens by exacerbating oxidative stress (Bao et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025). C-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), also referred to as stress-activated protein kinase (SAPK), participates in various stress responses triggered by multiple factors, particularly oxidative damage (Solinas and Becattini 2017; Wang et al., 2021). A study found that Cd-induced apoptosis of mouse Leydig cells involves the activation of the ROS/JNK pathway (Ren et al., 2020). Conversely, blocking the ROS-dependent JNK signaling pathway may alleviate Cd-induced neuronal apoptosis (Chen et al., 2020).

In recent years, the benefits of flavonoids for human health have received widespread attention. Tax is an important natural flavonoid compound in nature. Compared with other flavonoids, Tax has lower toxicity (Makena et al., 2009). It exhibits a variety of biological activities, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antiviral and anti-apoptotic properties (Kostyuk and Potapovich, 1998). Tax can prevent diabetic cardiomyopathy by enhancing the activity of endogenous antioxidant enzymes, inhibiting oxidative stress and reducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis (Sun et al., 2014). A few studies have found that Tax can counteract the damage caused by Cd toxicity (Manigandan et al., 2015). Additionally, Tax can significantly alleviate acrylamide-induced oxidative damage and inflammatory damage (such as kidney tubular atrophy and necrosis) in rats (Bedir et al., 2021). Studies have shown that flavonoids can regulate antioxidant levels and inhibit the JNK signaling pathway to protect cells from damage. Tax can reverse hexavalent chromium-induced phosphorylation of p38 and JNK caused by oxidative stress, regulate the expression of Bax and Bcl-2 at the mitochondrial level, and inhibit apoptosis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells and THP-1 cells (Cao et al., 2020). Furthermore, Tax induces the phosphorylation of the JAK2/STAT3 cascade, thereby regulating the expression of anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 family proteins (Zai et al., 2018).

Based on the accumulating evidence, we hypothesized that the ROS-dependent JNK signaling pathway is potentially involved in Cd-induced kidney toxicity. Moreover, we were interested in the versatile functions of Tax, especially its role as a prominent antioxidant. Although Tax has been proven to antagonize Cd-induced kidney toxicity, its protective effect on chicken kidney cells has not been reported. Furthermore, the role of the ROS/JNK pathway in Cd-induced apoptosis of chicken kidney cells and the regulatory mechanism of Tax remain unclear. This study determined the causal relationship between Cd and kidney toxicity and, more importantly, reported Tax intake prevents Cd-induced kidney toxicity. The aim of this study is to fill this research gap and provide targeted candidate substances and a theoretical basis for the prevention and control of Cd poisoning in poultry.

Materials and methods

Reagents and antibodies

The antibodies used in this study were purchased from Wanleibio (Shenyang) Co., Ltd. The nucleotides were synthesized by Tsingke Biotech Company. Other chemical reagents were provided by Harbin Baijiesi Technology Co., Ltd.

Extraction and treatment of chicken primary kidney cells

All procedures used in this study were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (NEAUEC20230308) of Northeast Agricultural University. Kidneys were aseptically collected from chicken embryos (16 to 19 day‐old). The kidney capsules were removed, and the kidneys were washed twice with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS; 0.1 mol/L phosphate buffer containing 0.85 % NaCl, pH 7.2). They were then cut into small fragments using a surgical blade and washed twice with PBS pre-warmed to 37 °C. The fragments were stirred in 10 mL of 0.25 % (w/v) trypsin solution at 37 °C for 15-20 min. After allowing the fragments to settle, the supernatant was decanted, and a small amount of 1640 medium was added to terminate the digestion process. Single-cell suspensions were prepared by gently pushing the mixture of medium and fragments through a sterile stainless-steel mesh with a pore size of 200 μm. The cells were suspended in 1640 medium that contained 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Grand Island, NY), and 1 % antibiotic–antimycotic solution (Sigma). The density of primary chicken kidney cells was adjusted to 1 × 106 cells/mL(Wan et al., 2019).

The chicken primary kidney cells were randomly divided into 4 groups and inoculated into a 12-well plate: the C group (without any treatment), the DMSO group (treated with 0.1 % DMSO solvent, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.), CdCl2 + Tax group (treated with 67 μL of 7.5 μg/mL CdCl2 + 14 μL of 0.15 μg/mL Tax), the CdCl2 group (treated with 67 μL of 7.5 μg/mL CdCl2). (The cells were pretreated with Tax for 12 h before adding CdCl2 and culturing for 24 h). The cells were cultured in a humidified atmosphere containing 5 % CO2 at 37°C.

CCK-8 toxicity test

Chicken kidney cells were inoculated into a 96 well cell culture plate, and each well was assigned to one of the following groups: cell-free medium (blank control well), control cell well without Cd chloride (CdCl2) (sample control well), CdCl2 treated cell well (treated sample well). Each group had 3 parallel samples. The cells were inoculated with CdCl₂ solutions at concentrations of 3.5 μg/mL, 4.5 μg/mL, 5.5 μg/mL, 6.5 μg/mL, and 7.5 μg/mL. After treatment with CdCl₂ solution for 24 h, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well, and the cell culture plate was further cultured at 37 °C for 2 h. Finally, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm. (The method for determining the maximum antagonistic concentration of Tax against CdCl2 toxicity was the same as above, with Tax (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.) concentration gradients of 0.05 μg/mL, 0.10 μg/mL, 0.15 μg/mL, 0.20 μg/mL, and 0.25 μg/mL). In this study, CdCl2 was purchased from Seedior Experimental Company (C805430, China). The powdered reagent was dissolved in ultra-pure water, and after complete dissolution, it was filtered through a 0.22 μm sterile filter membrane, which was then used for subsequent experiments.

Inhibitor treatments

To verify the function of the ROS/JNK pathway, the chicken primary kidney cells were pre-treated with the specific JNK inhibitor SP600125 (20 μM; Abmole, Shanghai, China) or the ROS scavenger N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC, 2 mM; Abmole, Shanghai, China) for 12 hours, and then exposed to CdCl₂. For the combined treatment, the cells were first pre-treated simultaneously with SP600125 and Tax for 12 hours, and then CdCl₂ was added.

AO/EB staining

Chicken primary kidney cells were seeded in 12-well plates and 5 μL each of AO and EB (Leagene, China) was added for staining. The cells were then observed and photographed under a fluorescence microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan).

Hoechst 33258 staining

After culturing chicken kidney cells in a 12-well plate for 36 h, the residual liquid in each well was removed, and each well was washed twice with PBS. 300 μL of Hoechst 33258 dye solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) was added to each well to cover the cells, and the cells were stained in an incubator for 20 min (37 °C, 5 % CO2). Finally, each well was rinsed with PBS. The apoptosis of cells was analyzed under a fluorescence microscope, and the fluorescence of apoptotic cells was quantitatively analyzed using Image J software.

Flow cytometry assay

An Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Dalian Meilun Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China) was used to detect apoptosis in primary kidney cells by flow cytometry. First, adherent cells in a 6-well plate were digested and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min. The cells were counted, and 1 × 106 cells/mL were collected. Then, the cells were incubated with 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC and 5 μL of propidium iodide (PI) in 500 μL of buffer solution for 15 min. Finally, a FACSCAnto II flow cytometer (Becton and Dickinson, USA) was used to detect apoptosis signals.

Intracellular ROS production detection

According to the manufacturer's instructions, an ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) was used to measure the ROS level in kidney cells. The treated kidney cells from the four groups were stained with the ROS probe (DCFH-DA) for 30 min, washed three times with PBS, and anti-fluorescence quenching agent was added. Finally, the cells were observed under a fluorescence microscope (EVOS FLAuto, Life Technologies, USA).

Total RNA isolation and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was isolated from chicken kidneys using the TRIzol extraction method, and first-strand cDNA was synthesized using a qRT-PCR synthesis kit (A5001, Promega, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions (Lu et al., 2022). Gene expression levels were detected by qRT-PCR using a Light Cycler® 480 System (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) with fast Universal SYBR Green Master (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). All reactions were performed in a 10 μL reaction volume. All primers (Table 1) were designed using Primer Premier 7.0 software (PREMIER Bio-soft International, USA). The comparative 2-ΔΔCt method with GAPDH as an endogenous control was used to calculate gene expression(Livak and Schmittgen, 2001). The gene sequences used in this study were all retrieved from NCBI. The analysis of network pharmacology

Tax targets were searched through the Swiss database (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) and Super-PRED database (http://prediction.charite.de/index.php). Kidney injury targets were identified using the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). In addition, Venn analysis was conducted to obtain the intersection genes, and then the STRING database (https://www.string-db.org/) was used to construct a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. CytoScape software was used for visualization, and GO and KEGG enrichment analysis were performed using the DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) (Xia, et al., 2025).

The analysis of network Toxicology

Cd targets were searched through the Swiss database (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) and Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (http://prediction.charite.de/index.php). Kidney injury targets were identified using the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). Additionally, Venn analysis was conducted to obtain intersecting genes, and then the STRING database (https://www.string-db.org/) was used to construct a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. CytoScape software was used for visualization, and GO and KEGG enrichment analysis were performed using the DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/).

Molecular docking

Molecular docking was used to assess potential binding sites between Tax and JNK. The three-dimensional structures of Tax and JNK were retrieved from the NCBI database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) and Swiss-Model database (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/), respectively. AutoDock Vina software was used for docking, and PyMol 2.4.0 was used to display the most promising binding sites. In addition, the CB-dock2 database (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2) and SWISSDOCK database (https: //www.swissdock.ch/) were consulted to predict the binding potential between Tax and JNK.

Cellular thermal shift assay

Cells were divided into the DMSO (control) and the Tax groups. Cells were collected when the cell density reached 80 %-90 %. After washing with PBS, the cells were resuspended. The cell suspensions of the two groups were placed at different temperatures (37, 41, 45, 49, 53, 57, and 61 °C), respectively. Subsequently, cell lysis was performed using liquid nitrogen and two repeated freeze - thaw cycles. The cells were centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected. 5 × SDS-PAGE protein loading buffer was added to the supernatant, and the mixture was boiled at 100 °C for 10 min to denature the proteins. The denatured protein samples were used for WB analysis.

Treatment of experimental waste

After the experiment, all the animal carcasses, dissected waste tissues and organs were collected together and placed in special yellow medical waste bags, sealed. Labels were attached, indicating the experiment name, animal species, processing date, and contaminants. They were then handed over to the school experimental animal center for high-temperature and high-pressure sterilization and incineration. The waste liquid from cell culture medium is collected in a dedicated waste container and mixed with 75 % alcohol for a thorough action for more than 30 min. After being subjected to high-pressure steam sterilization (121°C, 20 min), it is then discharged into the sewer. Solid wastes such as culture bottles, culture plates, pipettes, pipette tips, chambers, etc. are placed in yellow medical waste bags and sealed. After being uniformly subjected to high-pressure sterilization, they are handed over to a professional hazardous waste disposal company for incineration. Cultured media, buffers, tissue homogenate solutions, etc. containing Cd are separately collected in dedicated heavy metal waste containers. Centrifuge tubes, pipette tips, slides, culture dishes that have come into contact with Cd are classified and collected as toxic and hazardous laboratory waste. They are uniformly handed over to the hazardous waste storage room of school for disposal, and centralized and harmless treatment by qualified units is carried out.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate. The data analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 statistical software for Windows. Differences between different groups were assessed using one-way ANOVA or unpaired Student’s t-test. The data are expressed as means ± SD. P < 0.05 was determined as a statistically significant difference (Dawson and Trapp, 2004).

When the Tax concentration was 0.15 μg/mL (0.5 μM), the cell viability was the highest, exceeding 90%. Tax attenuates Cd-induced apoptosis in chicken primary kidney cells

AO/EB staining was used to detect the number of apoptotic cells in the control group, DMSO treatment group, Cd + Tax treatment group, and Cd treatment group. As shown in Fig. 2A, a small number of apoptotic cells were observed in the DMSO treatment group. The number of apoptotic cells in the Cd treatment group increased significantly, while the number of apoptotic cells in the Tax treatment group decreased significantly, indicating that Tax inhibits Cd-induced apoptosis of chicken kidney cells. Then, Hoechst 33258 staining was used to detect the morphological changes and apoptosis of primary chicken kidney cells treated with Cd and Tax. Apoptotic cells showed blue fluorescence with nuclear chromatin condensation. The number of apoptotic cells increased significantly in the Cd treatment group and decreased significantly in the Cd + Tax treatment group (Fig. 2B-C). In addition, flow cytometry results showed that the apoptosis rate in the DMSO group increased slightly (not significant), the apoptosis rate in the Cd group was 15.7 % higher than that in the DMSO group, and the apoptosis rate in the Cd + Tax group was 12.7 % lower than that in the Cd group (Fig. 2D-E) (P < 0.05). Discussion

A large body of evidence indicates that industrial fuels, municipal waste combustion, metal refining, and smoking release the harmful heavy metal Cd into the ecological environment (Huang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). After poultry ingest contaminated feed, Cd can accumulate in organs such as the kidneys for a long time, causing growth retardation and reduced egg production in chickens and laying hens, and threatening human health through the food chain (Kar and Patra, 2021). Although previous studies have confirmed the toxic mechanism of Cd on mammalian kidneys, the physiological structure of poultry kidneys differs significantly from that of mammals, and the molecular pathways of Cd-induced apoptosis in chicken kidney cells have not been fully elucidated. As a natural low-toxicity flavonoid, Tax has shown antagonistic effects against Cd toxicity in models such as mice and zebrafish (Manigandan et al., 2015), but its protective effect and mechanism of action on primary chicken kidney cells have not been reported. Focusing on the actual needs of poultry farming, this study systematically explores for the first time the mediating role of the ROS/JNK pathway in Cd-induced apoptosis of chicken kidney cells and the regulatory mechanism of Tax, providing theoretical support for the development of Cd poisoning prevention and control technologies in poultry (as shown in Fig. 8).Fig. 8Schematic diagram of the mechanism of Cd-induced apoptosis in primary chicken kidney cells and the potential antagonistic effect of Tax. The results showed that chicken primary kidney cells treated with Cd exhibited typical apoptosis-related changes, which then activated the JNK signal cascade (a downstream effect of oxidative stress), while Tax exerted an antagonistic effect on Cd toxicity via the JNK signaling pathway.Fig 8 dummy alt text

Schematic diagram of the mechanism of Cd-induced apoptosis in primary chicken kidney cells and the potential antagonistic effect of Tax. The results showed that chicken primary kidney cells treated with Cd exhibited typical apoptosis-related changes, which then activated the JNK signal cascade (a downstream effect of oxidative stress), while Tax exerted an antagonistic effect on Cd toxicity via the JNK signaling pathway.

Oxidative stress is a state of imbalance between oxidation and antioxidant in the body. It is considered an important factor leading to tissue damage and apoptosis and plays a crucial role in toxicological mechanisms. It has been found that antioxidant defense capacity is related to ROS produced by harmful metals (Debnath et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2019b). The most commonly used biomarkers of oxidative stress are antioxidants and antioxidant enzymes (Gong et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2020). Previous studies have shown that Cd exposure leads to a decrease in the activity of antioxidant enzymes such as CAT, SOD, GSH-px, and GPX in tissues and an increase in MDA content (Rahman et al., 2017; Zhao et al., 2018). In addition, Cd exposure leads to a decrease in the levels of antioxidant factors such as GPX1, SOD1, SOD2, HO-1, and NQO1, and induces oxidative stress (Yiming et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2022). Consistent with these findings, our results show that Cd exposure causes a decrease in antioxidant enzyme activity and ROS accumulation in chicken primary kidney cells, leading to oxidative stress. Recent studies have shown that oxidative stress can promote apoptosis by disrupting mitochondrial dynamics (Gilkerson 2018). A large number of studies have shown that when cells undergo oxidative stress, a large amount of ROS can activate the expression of the JNK pathway (Chen et al., 2018). ROS has been proven to be an inducer or mediator of the activation of MAPK family members, which plays an important role in autophagy and apoptosis (Luo et al., 2019). Exposure of osteosarcoma cells to polyphyllin-VI leads to the accumulation of ROS and promotes JNK phosphorylation, which then down-regulates the expression of Bcl-2 and promotes apoptosis (Yuan et al., 2019). Metformin induces cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and autophagy in human osteosarcoma cells through a ROS-dependent JNK cascade reaction (Li et al., 2020). Similar to the above studies, we detected the protein and mRNA levels of JNK and mitochondrial apoptosis pathway-related genes, and the results showed that JNK expression increased with the increase of ROS, suggesting the activation of this pathway. In addition, we found that JNK phosphorylation can regulate apoptosis by regulating related genes in the mitochondrial pathway. Our results demonstrate that Cd induces apoptosis in chicken primary kidney cells by triggering the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway via the ROS/JNK pathway.

Tax is a natural flavonoid compound with a good antioxidant effect, which can effectively scavenge oxygen free radicals and improve the body's antioxidant level (Tanaka et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2021). Studies have shown that Tax exerts a protective effect on human keratinocytes by significantly reducing Cd-induced ROS accumulation (Moon, et al., 2019). Tax protects kidney tissue from Cd-induced nephrotoxicity by improving redox status, regulating inflammation, and reducing apoptosis (Algefare 2022). In addition, Tax can protect kidney cells from apoptosis caused by exogenous toxins (Bedir et al., 2021). Tax treatment reverses ROS accumulation and mitochondrial dysfunction. Further studies have found that Tax reverses the phosphorylation of the JNK cascade pathway, thereby regulating the expression of Bcl-2 family proteins related to the mitochondrial pathway and alleviating apoptosis and hepatotoxicity (Zai et al., 2018). The results of network pharmacology and molecular docking indicated that JNK is the core intersection target of Cd toxicity and the protective effect of Tax, and Tax can directly inhibit JNK activity by binding to key amino acid residues such as ASP-151 and LEU-172 of JNK (binding energy -8.1 kcal/mol). CETSA experiments further confirmed that Tax can enhance the thermal stability of JNK, verifying their direct interaction. This study found that the ratio of p-JNK/JNK in chicken kidney cells increased significantly after Cd treatment, while taxifolin could reverse this trend and simultaneously down-regulate the expression of pro-apoptotic genes Caspase-3 and Caspase-9 and up-regulate the level of the anti-apoptotic gene Bcl-2.

To verify the involvement of the ROS/JNK pathway and further elucidate the mechanism by which Tax counteracts Cd-induced apoptosis, we conducted experiments using the JNK inhibitor SP600125 and the ROS scavenger NAC. Both SP600125 and NAC significantly reduced Cd-induced apoptosis, confirming that JNK activation and ROS generation are the key mediators of Cd-induced apoptosis in chicken primary kidney cells. These findings are consistent with previous reports, which indicate that Cd-induced cell apoptosis is mediated through oxidative stress and JNK activation in various cell types (Tong et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2017). From inhibitor experiments, we observed the additive protective effect when Tax was used in combination with SP600125. Although using SP600125 alone could partially reverse the apoptosis induced by Cd, compared to using SP600125 alone, the combination with Tax could produce a greater protective effect (Fig. 7). This cumulative effect indicates that the anti-apoptotic mechanism of Tax is not limited to the direct inhibition of JNK. Our results in this study support this view. Tax effectively reduced the generation of ROS induced by Cd (Fig. 3), its effect being similar to that of NAC, functioning by weakening the upstream activation signals of JNK. This scavenging of ROS may have increased the direct inhibitory effect of SP600125 on JNK, thereby enhancing the protective effect. Molecular docking and CETSA confirmed the direct interaction between Tax and JNK (Fig. 5), indicating that Tax may directly regulate the activity of JNK in a manner different from that of SP600125. Overall, these research results suggest that Tax has a multi-target protective effect against Cd-induced apoptosis, including the elimination of ROS and the inhibition of the JNK pathway. This multi-target mechanism is a characteristic shared by many natural flavonoids, which are well-known for their ability to interact with multiple signaling pathways rather than a single molecular target (Abukhalil et al., 2025). The cumulative effect observed with the combined treatment of Tax and SP600125 highlights the comprehensive protective effect of Tax and provides new insights into its mechanism of action.

This study still has certain limitations. The experiment was conducted only at the cellular level, and the protective effect of Tax in vivo has not been verified. Further animal experiments are needed to clarify its optimal dosage, administration route, and long-term safety. In subsequent research, we can also explore the synergistic effect of Tax with other antioxidants (such as selenium and vitamin E) and the broader application of compound additives in actual breeding. Moreover, we can conduct an in-depth analysis of the cross-interaction between the JNK pathway and other signaling pathways (such as the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway). The mechanism network can be further improved through experiments involving combined treatment with pathway inhibitors. Furthermore, in this experimental design, a treatment group using only Tax was not included. However, previous studies have shown that at concentrations lower than 0.5 μM, Tax does not cause significant cytotoxicity or apoptosis in normal cells. Our data also indicate that the DMSO group shows no significant differences in cell viability, apoptosis rate, or oxidative stress markers compared to the C group, which indirectly suggests that the low concentration of Tax used in this study is unlikely to have an intrinsic effect on the measured results. Nevertheless, future research that can include a group using only Tax will be able to further strengthen the conclusion about the protective effect of Tax against Cd-induced damage.

Conclusion

This study clearly demonstrates that the ROS/JNK/mitochondrial apoptosis pathway is the core mechanism of Cd-induced kidney damage in chicken primary kidney cells. Tax effectively antagonizes Cd-induced cell apoptosis through the dual effects of antioxidation and inhibition of JNK pathway activation. This research not only fills the research gap in the molecular mechanism of Cd-induced kidney cell damage in poultry but also provides a solid theoretical basis and experimental support for the application of Tax as a green feed additive in poultry farming in Cd-contaminated areas and for the prevention and control of Cd-induced kidney damage. It has important practical significance for ensuring the health of poultry and the quality and safety of poultry products.

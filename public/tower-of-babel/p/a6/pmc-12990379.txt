Metabolomics reveals multi-target bactericidal mechanisms of calcium-enhanced slightly acidic electrolyzed water (CSAEW) against Pseudomonas fluorescens and its synergistic preservation of fresh-cut lettuce
Authors: Xinyu Li, Mengting Zhou, Wenman Wang, Jiating Huang, Mengli Kang, Hongfen Zhu, Xingquan Liu, Shanggui Deng, Jiangang Ling
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study demonstrated that slightly acidic electrolyzed water (CSAEW) effectively inactivates Pseudomonas fluorescens and preserves fresh-cut lettuce. CSAEW treatment induced severe membrane damage, causing potassium leakage (0.53 mmol/L), protein release (93.31 μg/mL), and elevated conductivity. SEM images showed collapsed membranes with pores. Metabolomics identified sn-Glycerol 3-phosphate and Ethenodeoxyadenosine as key reduced metabolites. KEGG analysis indicated disruptions in purine and glycerophospholipid metabolism, leading to ATP depletion and membrane dysfunction. In preservation trials, CSAEW reduced weight loss by 37.36%, retained 94.3% chlorophyll, maintained higher firmness, reduced color change, increased vitamin C content, and decreased bacterial count by 1.43 log CFU/g. Calcium ions further enhanced antimicrobial efficacy. CSAEW serves as a dual-functional technology for pathogen inactivation and quality preservation in fresh-cut vegetables.Keywords: Calcium-supplemented slightly acidic electrolyzed water (CSAEW), Pseudomonas fluorescens, Metabolomics, Fresh-cut lettuce, Multi-target bactericidal mechanism

Introduction

As a fast-expanding category in ready-to-eat foods, fresh-cut lettuce encounters substantial food safety risks, with documented rises in lettuce-related foodborne outbreaks. (Qu et al., 2021). Pseudomonas fluorescens within the Pseudomonas genus plays a significant role by forming biofilms on food processing equipment surfaces (stable biofilms can develop after 7 days of cultivation in a simulated dairy processing environment) and secreting heat-stable proteases (which can persist after milk heat treatment and lead to spoilage of the final product) (Sivri et al., 2023). Ozonated water treatment has caused a decrease in the sensory scores of lettuce samples in terms of appearance, texture, and overall impression, creating an urgent need for dual-functional alternatives. Calcium chloride-enhanced slightly acidic electrolyzed water (CSAEW) combines hypochlorous acid bactericide with calcium pectin stabilization. However, the multi-target mechanisms of this strategy against P. fluorescens remain uncharacterized.

Strong oxidants can exert bactericidal effects by oxidizing the phospholipid bilayers of microorganisms and denaturing membrane proteins, and this mechanism is consistent with the principle of ozone for removing Pseudomonas fluorescens biofilms (Rahman et al., 2010). However, the transient activity of HOCl limits its ability to penetrate biofilms, as the active chlorine components (including HOCl) in electrolyzed water tend to decompose or volatilize over storage, leading to a decline in effective concentration (Rahman et al., 2016). Calcium chloride (CaCl₂) supplementation addresses this limitation: Ca2+ ions crosslink pectin carboxyl groups, increasing cell wall rigidity and reducing electrolyte leakage in fresh-cut produce (Liu et al., 2024). While preliminary studies show Ca2+-enhanced SAEW (CSAEW) reduces Bacillus cereus on sprouts by 2.1 log CFU/g (Zhang et al., 2022), its efficacy against cold-adapted P. fluorescens biofilms and the metabolic impact remain unknown. This gap impedes optimized application for simultaneous pathogen inactivation and quality preservation.

We hypothesize that CSAEW concurrently disrupts P. fluorescens viability and preserves lettuce quality through dual mechano-metabolic actions: HOCl induces membrane poration (evidenced by K+/ATP leakage and nanoscale SEM imaging), while Ca2+ crosslinks pectin networks to retard senescence. To validate this, we employ the integration of cellular leakage kinetics (conductivity, protein efflux), high-resolution SEM, and LC-MS metabolomics (Pang et al., 2022) to quantify membrane damage and metabolic perturbations (e.g., purine/glycerophospholipid dysregulation). Parallel preservation trials dynamically monitor microbial inactivation (log CFU/g reduction), browning suppression (PPO/POD kinetics), and nutrient retention (vitamin C, chlorophyll). This the first mechanistic elucidation of CSAEW's bactericidal-preservative synergy against psychrotrophic biofilms establishes a theoretical and practical framework for electrolyzed water optimization in fresh-cut vegetables.

Materials and methods

The lettuce was purchased from Freshippo supermarket, located in the Yinzhou District of Ningbo City, Zhejiang Province. Following the purchase, the lettuce was stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C. Leaves of similar maturity, free of pests and mechanical damage, were selected and processed immediately for subsequent experiments.

Preparation of electrolyzed water

CSAEW was prepared by electrolyzing (AnyWhere-320 W, Beijing Ruiande Technology Co., Ltd.) a mixture of 2% (w/v) CaCl₂ and acetic acid in a slightly acidic electrolyzed water generator (Hao et al., 2017). The resulting solution was diluted with sterile deionized water to achieve target physicochemical parameters (pH: 5.0–6.5; ORP: >900 mV; ACC: 20–50 mg/L), adjusted using acetic acid. NSAEW was synthesized similarly by substituting CaCl₂ with 2% NaCl. Key parameters (pH, ORP, ACC) were measured using a benchtop pH meter and high-range chlorine detector. Strain activation and preservation

P. fluorescens lyophilized powder was rehydrated according to the Kwik-Stik™ protocol, suspended in sterile hydration fluid, and streaked onto Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA) plates for overnight incubation at 28 °C. Single colonies were inoculated into Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB) and cultured at 28 °C with shaking (180 rpm, 18 h). Bacterial suspensions (1 mL) were cryopreserved in bead tubes at −80 °C under aseptic conditions (Sun et al., 2022).

Bactericidal mechanism of calcium chloride electrolyzed water against P. fluorescens

Preparation and collection of bacterial samples

A cryopreserved P. fluorescens bead was inoculated into 150 mL Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB) and incubated at 28 °C for 18 h to activate bacterial growth. The culture was centrifuged (6000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), washed twice with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and resuspended to achieve a standardized inoculum (107 CFU/mL) (López-Gálvez et al., 2010). For bactericidal assays, 0.5 mL of the suspension was mixed with 4.5 mL CSAEW for 60 s, followed by reaction termination with 0.85% sodium thiosulfate (Hao et al., 2017). Bacterial cells were pelleted (1000 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen (30 s), thawed on ice, and washed with PBS to preserve cellular integrity. Final pellets were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen (15 min) and stored at −80 °C. Control samples replaced CSAEW with PBS. All procedures adhered to sterile protocols to prevent cross-contamination.

Determination of potassium ion(K+) leakage

The bacterial suspension containing P. fluorescens from Section 2.3.1 was centrifuged at 8000 ×g for 10 min at 4 °C (Centrifuge Model HC-3018R, Zhongke Zhongjia, Anhui, China). The supernatant was collected, and potassium ion (K+) concentration was quantified using a commercial potassium assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China) following the manufacturer's protocol. Results were expressed as mmol/L, calibrated against a standard curve. Triplicate measurements were performed for each sample.

Determination of protein leakage

The supernatant obtained under identical centrifugation conditions (8000 ×g, 10 min, 4 °C) was analyzed for protein content via a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). Absorbance was measured at 562 nm using a microplate reader (Model: ReadMax1900, Shanghai Shanpu Biotechnology Co., China), with bovine serum albumin (BSA) as the standard (0–2000 μg/mL, R2 > 0.98). Protein leakage was expressed as μg/mL. All assays were conducted in triplicate.

Conductivity measurement

Using a DDS-11 A conductivity meter, five leaf discs (150–200 mg fresh weight, no midrib) were washed and immersed in 20 mL deionized water. The initial conductivity (R0) was measured. After gentle shaking for 3 h, conductivity was measured again (R1). Samples were then boiled for 5 min, cooled, and the maximum conductivity measured (R2). Scanning Electron microscopy (SEM)

The bacterial pellet (centrifuged at 8000 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C) was fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde overnight, sequentially dehydrated in ethanol gradients (50–100%, 10 min per step), treated with isoamyl acetate, freeze-dried, and sputter-coated with gold prior to SEM observation (Yang et al., 2022).

Metabolite extraction and analysis

To the bacterial pellet, 1000 μL of extraction solvent (methanol:acetonitrile:water = 2:2:1, v/v) spiked with isotope-labeled internal standards was added. The mixture was vortex-mixed for 30 s, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen for 1 min, thawed, and vortex-mixed again. This freeze–thaw cycle was repeated three times. The sample was then sonicated in an ice-water bath for 10 min, incubated at −40 °C for 1 h, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was transferred to an injection vial for LC–MS analysis. A quality control (QC) sample was prepared by combining equal volumes of supernatant from each sample.

Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide column (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) maintained at 4 °C using a Vanquish UPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The injection volume was 2 μL. Mobile phase A consisted of 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonia in water; mobile phase B was acetonitrile.

Mass spectrometry was conducted on an Orbitrap Exploris 120 instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific) controlled by Thermo Xcalibur 4.4 software. Full MS and MS/MS data were acquired with the following parameters: sheath gas flow rate, 50 arbitrary units; auxiliary gas flow rate, 15 arbitrary units; capillary temperature, 320 °C; full MS resolution, 60,000; MS/MS resolution, 15,000; stepped collision energy (SNCE), 20, 30, and 40 eV; spray voltage, +3800 V (positive) and − 3400 V (negative).

Inoculation of fresh-cut lettuce with Bacteria and electrolyzed water treatment

Bacterial inoculation

A cryogenic bead from the preservation tube (Section 2.3.2) was transferred to 150 mL of TSB medium and incubated at 28 °C for 18 h. The bacterial broth was centrifuged (6000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C), and the supernatant was discarded. The pellet was washed twice with pre-cooled 1 × PBS and resuspended to achieve a bacterial suspension of ∼104 CFU/mL. Fresh-cut lettuce was inoculated with P. fluorescens following the method described by (Sun et al., 2022). Lettuce was rinsed with tap water to remove soil and debris, exposed to UV light for 20 min, and cut into 3 cm × 3 cm squares using a sterilized stainless-steel knife on a clean bench. The cut lettuce pieces were washed three times with sterile distilled water, air-dried, and sprayed evenly with the bacterial suspension using a sterile atomizer.

Electrolyzed water treatment

The inoculated fresh-cut lettuce samples were treated following the method described by Koseki et al. with slight modifications (Koseki et al., 2003). Briefly, samples were immersed in NSAEW, CSAEW, or sterile water (control) for 1 min and drained. Each 50 g sample was then packaged in a 0.02 mm polyethylene bag and stored at 4 °C. At each sampling interval, three replicates per group were randomly selected for quality analysis.

The L*, a*, and b* three-color systematic determination methods were employed to quantify the color and luster-related indices of freshly harvested lettuce. In this context, L* indicates brightness, with a larger L* value corresponding to a whiter color. The a* indicates the range from green to red, while b* indicates the range from blue to yellow. The ΔE value was calculated by the specified formula (3), and the results demonstrated uniformity across all test positions.

Chlorophyll content

Chlorophyll content was determined using a portable SPAD chlorophyll meter. Measurements were taken at the midrib of lettuce leaves and expressed as SPAD values.

Total soluble solids (TSS)

Lettuce juice was extracted by grinding fresh-cut samples. TSS (%) was measured using a refractometer calibrated with distilled water.

Vitamin C content(Vc)

The determination of vitamin C content was conducted using a vitamin C (ascorbic acid) assay kit, with the results expressed in mg/100 g.

Total phenolic content

Total phenolic content was determined using a modified Folin-Ciocalteu assay (Ma et al., 2021). Briefly, 0.5 g fresh-cut lettuce was homogenized in 2 mL 60% ethanol and centrifuged (12,000 rpm, 4 °C, 10 min). The supernatant (0.5 mL) was reacted with 0.5 mL Folin-Ciocalteu reagent and 3 mL distilled water. After 5 min dark incubation, 1.5 mL Na₂CO₃ (0.5 mol/L) was added. Following 1 h light-protected incubation at 25 °C, absorbance was measured at 765 nm. Results were expressed as mg gallic acid equivalents per 100 g fresh weight (mg GAE/100 g FW).

Browning-related enzyme activities

Polyphenol oxidase (PPO) and peroxidase (POD) activities were determined using assay kits, with results expressed as U·g−1·min−1 and U/g, respectively.

Enumeration of P. fluorescens

A quantity of 5 g of the sample should be placed in a sterile homogenizing bag, to which 45 mL of sterile saline should be added. The contents should be tapped to homogenize and filter. The filtrate was diluted in a graded manner, coated on Pseudomonas CFC selective medium, and incubated at 28 °C for 24 h. The number of colonies on the plate was counted and the result was expressed as log CFU/g. The aforementioned experiments were conducted per aseptic procedures.

Results and discussion

Effect of CSAEW treatment on cellular content leakage in P. fluorescens

The measurement of potassium ion (K+) efflux in cellular electrolyte leakage assays is predicated on the fact that potassium is the predominant intracellular electrolyte, exhibiting a steep transmembrane concentration gradient. Its leakage is primarily mediated by stress-activated specific channels, including K+-selective channels and non-selective cation channels (NSCCs). The magnitude of K+ efflux serves as a highly sensitive indicator of alterations in membrane permeability and is directly implicated in reactive oxygen species (ROS)-regulated programmed cell death and metabolic reprogramming. Furthermore, it provides a critical basis for assessing cellular stress tolerance (Demidchik et al., 2014). As shown in Fig. 1, CSAEW treatment significantly disrupted the membrane integrity of P. fluorescens, evidenced by a 284.7% rise in K+ leakage (0.137 ± 0.002 to 0.527 ± 0.022 mmol/L), and a 118.6% surge in protein efflux (42.70 ± 0.44 to 93.31 ± 1.40 μg/mL) (p < 0.001). This leakage is attributed to dual mechanisms: 1) Oxidative damage from hypochlorous acid oxidizing membrane phospholipids and sulfhydryl groups (Wang et al., 2023), compromising selective permeability; 2) Structural destabilization via Ca2+ displacing Mg2+ in lipopolysaccharides, weakening outer membrane integrity and disrupting ion homeostasis (Rahman et al., 2016). Concurrently, HOCl-induced protein denaturation (Xiang et al., 2018) led to cytoplasmic enzyme release, while membrane potential collapse (Ding et al., 2025) inhibited ATP synthesis. These results clearly indicate that membrane barrier function was compromised, allowing intracellular components to leak out. This evidence of membrane damage provides an initial mechanistic explanation for the morphological damage and metabolic dysfunction observed in later sections. These findings align with the dual-action model of electrolyzed water (Du et al., 2024), underscoring CSAEW's efficacy through oxidation-structure synergy for microbial inactivation.

Effect of CSAEW treatment on conductivity of P. fluorescens suspension

The changes in conductivity, which serve as a critical indicator of cell membrane permeability, were analyzed to elucidate the impact of CSAEW on P. fluorescens. Electrical conductivity is a key parameter for the ion transport mechanisms, structural dynamic changes, and physiological and pathological states of cell membranes (Biasio & Cametti, 2011). As shown in Fig. 2, CSAEW treatment significantly increased the conductivity of the bacterial suspension to 18.98 mS/cm, approximately 1.6-fold higher than the control. This elevation in conductivity corresponds to the leakage of intracellular electrolytes, as directly evidenced by the K+ efflux data presented in Section 3.1. This elevation aligns with previous findings demonstrating that electrolyzed water disrupts microbial membrane integrity, leading to electrolyte leakage and increased conductivity (Hao et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2023). Specifically, the hypochlorous acid (HOCl) and reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated in CSAEW likely compromise membrane phospholipid bilayers and proteins, as observed in SAEW-treated Shewanella putrefaciens (Liu et al., 2022). Such damage facilitates the efflux of intracellular ions (e.g., K+, Na+) and cytoplasmic components, thereby elevating extracellular conductivity. These mechanistic insights are consistent with studies on P. fluorescens inactivation, where membrane degradation correlated with metabolic dysfunction and cell death (Kumar et al., 2019; Sivri et al., 2023). Together with the observed leakage of potassium ions and proteins, the increased conductivity provides multi-faceted evidence for the loss of membrane barrier function, establishing a logical link to subsequent observations of structural damage and metabolic disruption. The results underscore CSAEW's efficacy in destabilizing bacterial membranes, a key factor in its antimicrobial action within food preservation contexts.

Scanning Electron microscopy (SEM) observations

SEM is applied to characterize the surface structure of tissues and the morphological integrity of cells (Allen & Goldberg, 1993).SEM analysis revealed morphological alterations in P. fluorescens after CSAEW treatment (Fig. 3). Treated cells exhibited slight surface damage, shrinkage, and localized pores, whereas control cells maintained intact and smooth surfaces. These physical deformations are a direct morphological manifestation of the membrane integrity loss driving the earlier leakage and conductivity phenomena. These findings confirm CSAEW-induced cellular injury. Consistent with our results, (Hyun-Ji et al., 2019) reported similar morphological changes in Salmonella Typhimurium, Staphylococcus aureus, and Bacillus cereus following slightly acidic electrolyzed water treatment.

Quality assurance

Quality control (QC) is essential to ensure the reproducibility and accuracy of metabolomic data, particularly for microbial metabolomics, where sample variability can arise from external environmental influences. Reduced inter-sample variability reflects enhanced method stability and higher data quality. To evaluate system performance, QC samples were analyzed for correlation consistency and internal standard (IS) stability. A high Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) among QC samples (ideally >0.85, approaching 1) indicates robust method repeatability, while minimal variability in isotopically labeled IS metabolites, as evidenced by a median relative standard deviation (RSD) ≤10%, confirms analytical system stability. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the QC samples exhibited a PCC of 0.962, with an RSD of 9.83% for IS responses, demonstrating exceptional data integrity. These metrics align with established metabolomic QC criteria, such as those implemented in multi-omics platforms (Pang et al., 2022) and confirm the suitability of the dataset for subsequent multivariate analyses. The stringent QC protocols employed here mitigate technical noise, ensuring reliable detection of biologically relevant metabolic shifts in P. fluorescens under CSAEW treatment.

At the mechanistic level, the extreme upregulation of phosphatidylserine (PS) suggests the initiation of apoptotic pathways, likely through the externalization of PS on the outer membrane, a hallmark of programmed cell death (Lin et al., 2023). Concurrent severe fluctuations in other membrane phospholipids such as PC and PG reflect widespread disruption of membrane integrity and function (Vásquez et al., 2025), with PG alterations particularly indicating mitochondrial membrane stress and possible dysfunction in energy metabolism (Fizíková et al., 2023; Mejia & Hatch, 2015).

Furthermore, a pronounced depletion of key signaling molecules including cGMP points to the breakdown of central second messenger systems, impairing critical cellular communication and regulatory functions (Friebe et al., 2019). Together, these changes illustrate a coherent mechanistic trajectory: CSAEW exposure disrupts membrane systems, incapacitates energy and signaling networks, and halts synthesis of essential metabolites, collectively driving the cell toward apoptotic collapse.

Metabolic pathway enrichment analysis of differential metabolites

Metabolites interact within cells through diverse metabolic pathways, and their expression varies across biological systems (Ogata et al., 1999). To elucidate the metabolic alterations in P. fluorescens induced by CSAEW, pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the KEGG database. A metabolic pathway network was constructed, and results were visualized with a pathway impact threshold of 0.01, where pathways exceeding this value were considered biologically relevant (DH et al., 2018). As shown in the KEGG bubble plot (Fig. 5D), six pathways were significantly enriched in the CK vs. CSAEW comparison: purine metabolism, phenylalanine metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, pyruvate metabolism, taurine and hypotaurine metabolism, and glycerolipid metabolism. These pathways collectively highlight critical disruptions in energy metabolism, membrane integrity, and nucleotide homeostasis caused by CSAEW treatment.

Effects of calcium chloride slightly acidic electrolyzed water (CSAEW) on storage quality of fresh-cut lettuce

Impact of CSAEW on weight loss

As shown in Fig. 6A, compared to the control group and the NSAEW group which exceeded this threshold, the CSAEW treatment significantly reduced weight loss in fresh-cut lettuce. Importantly, the weight loss of CSAEW-treated lettuce was maintained at 4.20% on day 10, which was below the commercially acceptable level of 5% and represented a 37.36% reduction compared to the control group. This efficacy stems from dual mechanisms: (1) Calcium-mediated structural stabilization: Ca2+ ions crosslinked pectin in cell walls, reducing membrane permeability and transpiration-driven water loss post-mechanical injury (Liu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022); (2) HOCl-driven microbial control: Hypochlorous acid suppressed spoilage microorganisms (e.g., P. fluorescens), minimizing enzymatic degradation of cell matrices that exacerbate moisture leakage (Rahman et al., 2016). By day 10, CSAEW-treated lettuce exhibited 1.60-fold lower weight loss than controls (p < 0.05), demonstrating its dual role in preserving cellular integrity and delaying wilting, critical for extending shelf life in high-moisture produce.

Effect of CSAEW on hardness

CSAEW-treated lettuce retained superior hardness (1.86 N at day 10, 1.12- and 1.24-fold higher than NSAEW and controls, p < 0.05) (Fig. 6B), attributed to dual mechanisms: (1) Calcium-mediated pectin crosslinking stabilized the middle lamella, enhancing cell wall rigidity and reducing water loss-induced softening (Liu et al., 2024); (2) HOCl-driven microbial inhibition suppressed P. fluorescens and its polygalacturonase secretion, mitigating enzymatic cell wall degradation (Rahman et al., 2016). This synergy between structural reinforcement and microbial control aligns with calcium's role in maintaining oxidative stability (Yang et al., 2022), underscoring CSAEW's efficacy in preserving texture integrity.

Influence of CSAEW on color parameters

Color difference parameters (ΔE, L, a) serve as critical indicators of enzymatic browning and visual quality degradation in fresh-cut produce, directly reflecting biochemical alterations during storage(Yang et al., 2022). CSAEW treatment significantly enhanced the color stability of fresh-cut lettuce (Fig. 6C, Fig. 6D and Fig. 6E). Although the L* value (lightness) decreased in all groups during storage, the control group exhibited an accelerated browning trend from day 4 onward (p < 0.05), whereas CSAEW treatment effectively delayed this process and maintained higher lightness until day 8. The a* value (red-green chromaticity) showed an increasing trend throughout storage, indicating a general shift toward redness and loss of greenness; however, CSAEW treatment significantly suppressed this change. On day 10, the a* value of the CSAEW group was significantly lower than that of both the control and NSAEW groups (p < 0.05), indicating 61.59% and 39.98% better greenness retention compared to the control and NSAEW groups, respectively. The total color difference (ΔE) increased over storage time, but by day 10, CSAEW had reduced ΔE by 42.97% compared to the control and by 27.92% compared to the NSAEW group. These results are consistent with the inhibitory effect of CSAEW on PPO activity and its role in calcium-mediated structural stabilization, collectively contributing to the improved visual quality of the product.

Effect of CSAEW on chlorophyll content

Chlorophyll degradation in fresh-cut lettuce, driven by enzymatic activity and oxidative stress postharvest, was markedly attenuated by CSAEW treatment. SPAD values revealed that CSAEW-treated lettuce retained 26.93 (5.74% decline) by day 10, outperforming NSAEW (24.32, 13.91% decline) and controls (19.15, 33.09% decline) (p < 0.05) (Fig. 6F). This preservation is mechanistically linked to calcium ions in CSAEW stabilizing chlorophyll-protein complexes via pectin crosslinking, thereby reducing membrane permeability and limiting chlorophyllase access to substrates (Liu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022). Additionally, hypochlorous acid (HOCl) suppressed microbial proliferation, indirectly mitigating chlorophyll breakdown by reducing ROS accumulation (Rahman et al., 2016). The synergistic action of calcium-mediated structural stabilization and HOCl-driven microbial control underscores CSAEW's efficacy in delaying senescence and maintaining visual quality, aligning with studies demonstrating calcium's role in nutrient retention (Du et al., 2024).

Effect of CSAEW on Total soluble solids (TSS) content

Total soluble solids (TSS) content, reflecting cellular metabolic activity, declined in all groups during storage, yet CSAEW treatment maintained 1.63-fold higher TSS levels than controls by day 10 (p < 0.05), a finding consistent with previous reports by (Zhang et al., 2024) (Fig. 6G). This preservation stems from dual mechanisms: (1) Calcium ions stabilized cell wall pectin via crosslinking, reducing sugar leakage and inhibiting hydrolytic enzymes (e.g., amylases) that degrade polysaccharides (Liu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022); (2) Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) in CSAEW suppressed microbial proliferation, minimizing enzymatic consumption of soluble substrates (Rahman et al., 2016). In contrast, NSAEW's lack of calcium led to accelerated TSS loss post-day 6, aligning with studies showing calcium-deficient treatments fail to sustain nutrient integrity. These findings highlight CSAEW's synergistic role in balancing structural stabilization and microbial control to prolong shelf life.

Impact of CSAEW on vitamin C content

Vitamin C degradation in fresh-cut lettuce, driven by enzymatic oxidation (e.g., polyphenol oxidase) and ROS accumulation post-mechanical injury, was significantly mitigated by calcium-supplemented slightly acidic electrolyzed water (CSAEW) (Fig. 6H). As evidenced, CSAEW-treated lettuce retained 8.51 mg/100 g vitamin C at day 10, 3.89-fold higher than controls, attributed to dual mechanisms: (1) Calcium ions stabilized cell wall pectin networks, reducing membrane permeability and limiting oxidative substrate leakage (Liu et al., 2024); (2) Ca2+-mediated suppression of cytosolic calmodulin signaling downregulated NADPH oxidase activity, thereby lowering H₂O₂ generation. Concurrently, hypochlorous acid (HOCl) in CSAEW directly inactivated ascorbate oxidase and PPO (Rahman et al., 2016). This synergistic integration of calcium-enhanced membrane protection and electrolyzed water-driven enzyme inhibition underscores CSAEW's potential as a multi-target preservative for nutrient retention in fresh-cut produce.

Total phenolic content

CSAEW treatment significantly preserved the total phenolic (TP) content of fresh-cut lettuce over 10 days, showing only a 9.4% reduction (from 15.59 ± 0.75 to 14.13 ± 0.45 mg/100 g FW), which was superior to both NSAEW (30.1% reduction) and the control group (53.2% reduction) (p < 0.001) (Fig. 6I). The initial TP content of CSAEW-treated lettuce was already 19.4% higher than that of the control (p < 0.05), attributable to reduced phenolic leakage resulting from calcium-stabilized pectin networks (Hou et al., 2022). The active components inhibited Pseudomonas fluorescens by disrupting cell membranes, reducing bacterial load by 1.43 log CFU/g (p < 0.05), while Ca2+ significantly reduced permeability loss through upregulation of related genes and enhanced phenylpropanoid synthesis (Liu et al., 2024). This multi-factor synergistic mechanism aligns with the electroactivated water‑calcium interaction reported by (Du et al., 2024), establishing CSAEW as a potent strategy for TP preservation.

Effect of CSAEW on polyphenol oxidase (PPO) and peroxidase (POD) activities

Polyphenol oxidase (PPO) and peroxidase (POD) are pivotal enzymes in enzymatic browning, catalyzing the oxidative polymerization of phenolic compounds into melanin, particularly under mechanical stress (Sui et al., 2023). As shown in Fig. 7, PPO and POD activities in fresh-cut lettuce initially increased (days 0–6), likely due to cellular disruption releasing vacuolar phenolics and H₂O₂—key substrates for enzymatic activation (Yang et al., 2022). Subsequently, activity declines (days 6–10) correlated with progressive nutrient loss and substrate depletion. Notably, CSAEW-treated lettuce consistently exhibited the lowest enzyme activities, attributable to dual mechanisms: (1) Calcium-mediated structural stabilization: Ca2+ ions crosslinked pectin in cell walls, reducing membrane permeability and limiting phenolic leakage, thereby restricting substrate availability for PPO/POD (Liu et al., 2024); (2) Electrolyzed water-driven enzyme inactivation: Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) in CSAEW directly oxidized sulfhydryl groups and disrupted tertiary structures of PPO/POD, irreversibly suppressing their catalytic activity (Rahman et al., 2016). Ca2+-enhanced treatments stabilized cellular compartments and delayed enzymatic browning (Hou et al., 2022). The synergy between calcium's physical barrier and HOCl's oxidative action underscores CSAEW's efficacy in preserving visual and nutritional quality of fresh-cut produce.

Effect of CSAEW on Pseudomonas fluorescens in fresh-cut lettuce

The effect of slightly acidic electrolyzed water (SAEW) with calcium chloride on the growth of Pseudomonas fluorescens on fresh-cut lettuce is shown in Fig. 8. Both NSAEW and CSAEW treatments inhibited the growth of P. fluorescens on the surface of fresh-cut lettuce. During days 0–4, there was no significant difference in the inhibitory effect between NSAEW and CSAEW (p > 0.05), indicating that both types of SAEW had bactericidal effects against P. fluorescens on lettuce. From day 6 until the end of storage, the inhibitory effect of CSAEW was significantly stronger than that of the other two groups (p < 0.05). Throughout the storage period, the bacterial count in the CSAEW-treated group remained the lowest. This may be attributed to the antibacterial activity of Ca2+ in a slightly acidic pH environment, which delayed the soft rot of fresh-cut lettuce and thereby suppressed the growth of P. fluorescens. This may be attributed to the combined effect of Ca2+ and HOCl, where Ca2+ destabilizes the outer membrane of bacteria, thereby facilitating the penetration and action of HOCl, leading to enhanced generation of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) and more severe damage (Rahman et al., 2016).

CSAEW-treated samples exhibited significantly higher appearance scores (8.60 ± 0.17) compared to controls (8.00 ± 0.26, P < 0.05) and NSAEW-treated samples (8.43 ± 0.21), with superior texture retention (8.30 ± 0.17 vs. 7.60 ± 0.17 in controls). By Day 8, CSAEW maintained commercially viable scores for appearance (6.17 ± 0.15) and texture (6.00 ± 0.20), outperforming NSAEW by 11.6% and 11.1%, respectively (p < 0.05).

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicate that Calcium-Supplemented Acidic Electrolyzed Water (CSAEW) contributes to maintaining the safety and quality of fresh-cut lettuce. This effect may stem from the synergistic action of two mechanisms: metabolomic and physiological data suggest that hypochlorous acid (HOCl) compromises bacterial survival by disrupting cell membrane integrity and interfering with purine metabolism, while calcium ions (Ca2+) likely strengthen the cell wall structure of lettuce tissue through pectin cross-linking. This combined action resulted in a reduction of Pseudomonas fluorescens by 1.43 log CFU/g and limited chlorophyll degradation to 5.7% over the 10-day storage period. Future research should focus on the functional validation of the key metabolic pathways identified herein and the optimization of CSAEW production parameters for industrial-scale application.

CT-based intratumoral, peritumoral radiomics and clinical features: a combined model for perineural invasion prediction in PDAC
Authors: Qi Lan, Yuying Wang, Fan Xia, Yalin Sun, Yingying Cao, Zhongqiu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo develop and validate a general radiomics nomogram capable of identifying perineural invasion (PNI) status in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) patients.MethodsA total of 175 pancreatic cancer patients were retrospectively enrolled in this study. Patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 123) and a test cohort (n = 52) at a ratio of 7:3. Senior physicians manually delineated the intratumoral region of interest (ROI), and the peritumoral ROI was obtained by expanding 3 mm outward from the intratumoral ROI. After extracting and selecting radiomics features, the ExtraTrees algorithm was used to construct intratumoral, peritumoral, and intratumoral + peritumoral radiomics models, respectively. The optimal radiomics model was selected to construct a combined model with clinical characteristics. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to evaluate the predictive performance of the models.ResultsMultivariate analysis showed that carbohydrate antigen 199 (CA199) (p < 0.05) and vascular invasion (p < 0.05) were associated with an increased risk of PNI. In the training cohort, the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity of the combined model were 0.855, 76.8%, and 80.5%, respectively; in the test cohort, they were 0.844, 76.5%, and 77.8%, respectively. The performance of the combined model was superior to that of the clinical model or radiomics model alone.ConclusionsThe combined predictive model integrating intratumoral + peritumoral radiomics features based on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CE-CT) with clinical characteristics can effectively predict PNI in pancreatic cancer.Keywords: Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, Perineural invasion, Radiomics, Computed tomography

Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, with a 5-year overall survival rate of less than 10% (Siegel et al. 2025). A major contributor to its poor prognosis is perineural invasion (PNI), a pathological process characterized by tumor cell infiltration into the perineurium, endoneurium, or perineural space (Tan et al. 2021). As a hallmark of PDAC, PNI occurs in 80–100% of cases and is closely associated with aggressive tumor behavior, including local recurrence, distant metastasis, and intractable neuralgia, which significantly impairs patients’ quality of life and survival outcomes (Göhrig et al. 2024; Schorn et al. 2017). Preoperative identification of PNI is therefore of paramount importance for clinicians to make informed decisions regarding surgical resection extent, adjuvant therapy administration, and prognostic stratification (Lin et al. 2024; Crippa et al. 2022).

Currently, the gold standard for PNI diagnosis is postoperative histopathological examination. However, this approach is invasive and retrospective, and it lacks the ability to provide real-time information for preoperative treatment planning to determine whether a nerve plexus dissection should be performed. Conventional imaging modalities, such as contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CE-CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), are widely used for preoperative staging of PDAC. While previous studies have indicated that traditional imaging evaluations based on ultrasound, CT, and other examinations offer certain indicative value for diagnosing extra-pancreatic nerve invasion, conventional imaging lacks specific manifestations for intratumoral or intrapancreatic neural infiltration (Levy et al. 2006; Mochizuki et al. 2010). This makes it difficult to precisely capture microscopic signs of nerve involvement, resulting in insufficient specificity and sensitivity. Thus, there is an urgent need for a non-invasive, accurate, and reproducible method to predict PNI preoperatively in PDAC patients.

Radiomics, a burgeoning field that converts medical images into high-dimensional quantitative features using computational algorithms, has shown great potential in overcoming the limitations of conventional imaging (Lambin et al. 2012). By capturing the underlying tumor heterogeneity and microenvironmental characteristics that are invisible to the human eye, radiomics has been successfully applied in various oncology scenarios, including tumor diagnosis, staging, treatment response evaluation, and prognosis prediction(Aerts et al. 2014). In the context of PDAC, previous radiomics studies have focused primarily on intratumoral regions to predict PNI (Deng et al. 2025; Lu et al. 2025). However, PNI is not only a feature of the primary tumor but also involves interactions between the tumor and its surrounding microenvironment, including the perineural space, stroma, and blood vessels (Thiel et al. 2025). The peritumoral region may contain early pathological changes related to PNI, such as inflammatory infiltration, stromal remodeling, and neovascularization. Ignoring peritumoral radiomics features may lead to an incomplete characterization of the tumor microenvironment and limit the predictive performance of radiomics models for PNI.

The purpose of this study is to develop a method that integrates CT imaging features from both the interior of the tumor and its surrounding 3 mm area, combined with clinical factors, to construct a prediction model, so that it cannot only improve the accuracy of preoperative prediction of PNI in PDAC, but also provide a reliable basis for precise treatment planning.

Patients and methods

Patients’ selection

From the records of Jiangsu Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine affiliated with Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine, a total of 175 patients with pancreatic cancer pathologically confirmed postoperatively and complete preoperative CE-CT data were enrolled between 2017 and 2025, including 116 with positive PNI(PNI+) and 59 with negative PNI(PNI-).

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathological confirmation of pancreatic cancer by surgical resection; (2) CE-CT performed within one month before surgery; (3) clearly visible lesion on CT images; and (4) availability of complete clinical and laboratory examination data within two weeks prior to surgery. Exclusion criteria included: (1) history of other malignant tumors or previous pancreatic surgery; and (2) prior interventional or therapeutic procedures such as endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography, biliary/pancreatic stent placement, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy before CT examination. The enrolled patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 123) and a test cohort (n = 52) at a ratio of 7:3 (Fig. 1).

Process of the patients enrolling

CT scan protocol

The images were acquired using a 128-slice multidetector CT scanner (Philips Brilliance). All examinations were performed using a specified standard scanning protocol with the following parameters: tube voltage, 120 kV; tube current, 240–300 mA; pitch, 0.8–1.0; collimation, 128 mm × 0.6 mm; and rotation time, 0.5 s. The scanning procedure consisted of an initial non-contrast scan, followed by an enhanced scan. For enhancement, a non-ionic contrast agent (Bayer Schering Pharma Ultravist 370) was administered intravenously via an elbow vein using an automatic injector (Bayer Medrad Mark V Plus) at a dose of 1.5 mL/kg body weight and an injection rate of 3 mL/s. The line was then flushed with 20–30 mL of saline. CT scanning was performed at the arterial early phase (25–30 s), arterial late phase (pancreatic parenchymal phase, 40–45 s), venous phase (60–80 s), and delayed phase (120 s).

CT evaluation

The collected clinical data included age, gender, carbohydrate antigen 199 (CA199), and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA). All CT images were retrieved from the Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS). A radiologist with 5 years of experience in abdominal radiology evaluated and recorded the CT findings without prior knowledge of the pathological results. These assessments were subsequently reviewed and confirmed by a senior radiologist with over 10 years of experience in abdominal imaging. The conventional radiological features were assessed and documented on the CT images as follows: maximum tumor diameter, tumor location, margin, necrosis, pancreatic duct dilatation, bile duct dilatation, parenchymal atrophy, calcification, vascular invasion, lymph node invasion status and organ invasion.

Pathology evaluation

All patients underwent radical tumor resection. The resected specimens were routinely fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, processed, and stained with hematoxylin-eosin (H&E). Pathologists then evaluated the tumor for PNI status. While Nozzoli et al. (2024) proposed a PNI scoring system based on nerve diameter, number, location, perineurium involvement, and mitotic figures within the perineural invasion, this method lacks large-scale validation and sees limited clinical application. Therefore, this study adopted the widely accepted PNI diagnostic criteria revised by Liebig et al. (2009), which define PNI as tumor cells encircling more than one-third of the nerve circumference or invading any layer of the nerve sheath, including the epineurium, perineurium, or endoneurium (Fig. 2).

The red line outlines the nerve, and the red arrow indicates the tumor. The tumor encircles more than one-third of the nerve circumference, which is indicative of PNI(HE 200×)

Image segmentation

The CE-CT images of pancreatic cancer patients were acquired in the pancreatic parenchymal phase. In this phase, the hypovascular tumor with poor enhancement contrasts sharply with the peak-enhanced normal pancreatic parenchyma, facilitating optimal tumor visualization (Lu et al. 1996). All images were resampled to adjust the voxel size to 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm, and the window width and window level were uniformly set to 350 HU and 50 HU, respectively. Subsequently, the preprocessed CT images were imported into the ITK-SNAP software. Two radiologists with more than 5 years of experience in abdominal radiological diagnosis manually delineated the tumor boundaries layer by layer, with exclusion of calcified, cystic and necrotic areas, to obtain the intratumoral region of interest (ROI). The delineated ROIs were further reviewed and calibrated by another radiologist with 15 years of clinical experience. Afterwards, the OpenCV algorithm package in the Python library was used to expand the ROI outward by 3 mm, and manual modification was performed to exclude the areas extending beyond the pancreas or involving calcification, thus finally obtaining the 3 mm peritumoral ROI. This margin width was selected based on previous radiomic studies demonstrating that a 3 mm peritumoral band optimally captures the microenvironmental changes associated with tumor invasion and early recurrence while minimizing the inclusion of unrelated normal tissue (Wu et al. 2025; Xie et al. 2023). A total of 50 cases of patient images delineated by the two radiologists were randomly selected, and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was applied to analyze the consistency of the measured traditional radiological parameters and the extracted radiomic features between the two radiologists (Fig. 3).

Radiomics feature extraction, selection, and signature construction

Radiomics features of intratumoral, 3 mm peritumoral, and combined intratumoral-peritumoral ROIs were extracted separately using the Pyradiomics software package, including first-order statistical features, gray level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features, gray level dependence matrix (GLDM) features, gray level run-length matrix (GLRLM) features, gray level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features, neighborhood gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features, and shape features. All features were normalized using the Z-score method. For features with an ICC > 0.75, Pearson correlation analysis was performed; among features with a correlation coefficient > 0.9, only one was retained. Through 10-fold cross-validation, the minimum redundancy maximum relevance (mRMR) algorithm and the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithm were applied to screen out non-zero features.

Univariate logistic regression analysis was performed to screen clinical features and conventional radiological features, with feature variables showing p < 0.05 retained. These screened features were then incorporated into multivariate logistic regression analysis, and backward stepwise regression analysis was further applied to identify the most valuable feature variables for constructing the clinical model.

Radiomics models for intratumoral (RMintra), 3 mm peritumoral(RMperi), and combined intratumoral-peritumoral model (RMintra+peri) were established based on the selected radiomics features. Using the ExtraTrees algorithm, an optimal radiomics model was constructed on the basis of the optimal radiomics feature set.

A clinical-radiomics combined model was built by integrating the clinical indicators selected via multivariate analysis and the radiomics model with the highest predictive efficacy. A nomogram was plotted to enable the visualized prediction of PNI risk.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 27.0.1). p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Continuous variables conforming to a normal distribution were compared using the t-test, while non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using non-parametric tests. For categorical variables, the χ² test was used when all expected frequencies were ≥ 5. For categorical variables with expected frequencies < 5, the Fisher’s exact test was applied instead to ensure the validity of the statistical inference.

First, univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted on related variables. Variables with p < 0.05 were then included in a multivariate logistic regression analysis. Significant variables (p < 0.05) from the multivariate analysis were used to construct a clinical prediction model based on multivariate logistic regression. The predictive performance of the model was evaluated by calculating the area under the curve (AUC) and plotting the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was applied to assess the clinical net benefit of the model.

Radiomics feature selection and model construction

In this study, only features significantly associated with PNI in pancreatic cancer (p < 0.05) were selected. A total of 48, 49, and 48 features were retained based on the ROI of intratumoral, peritumoral 3 mm, and intratumoral + peritumoral 3 mm, respectively. After feature dimensionality reduction using the LASSO algorithm, radiomics features were retained. Finally, 4, 9, and 12 radiomics features with the highest predictive value were selected from the intratumoral, peritumoral 3 mm, and intratumoral + peritumoral ROIs, respectively (Fig. 4).

Nomogram construction

The RMintra+peri was independently associated with pancreatic cancer PNI. In the training cohort, the AUC of the RMintra+peri model was 0.833 (95% CI: 0.7589, 0.9062), and this performance remained stable in the testing cohort, with an AUC of 0.803 (95% CI: 0.6526, 0.9536). For the clinical model, its AUC was 0.692 (95% CI: 0.5961, 0.7885) in the training cohort and 0.673 (95% CI: 0.5193, 0.8271) in the testing cohort.

By integrating the RMintra+peri with the clinical model, a combined nomogram model (Fig. 5) was constructed for PNI prediction. This combined model exhibited favorable performance: in the training cohort, its AUC reached 0.855 (95% CI: 0.7820, 0.9274), while in the testing cohort, the AUC was 0.844 (95% CI: 0.7324, 0.9555). Through comparative analysis (Table 3), the combined nomogram model achieved a higher AUC than both the standalone radiomics models and the clinical model in both cohorts.

Discussion

PDAC is characterized by high malignancy, poor prognosis, strong invasiveness, and early metastasis. Most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, resulting in an extremely low 5-year survival rate (Siegel et al. 2025). Previous Cox multivariate regression analyses have confirmed that perineural invasion is an independent risk factor influencing the prognostic survival of PDAC patients. Among 216 PDAC patients who underwent surgery, the median survival time of PNI-positive patients (n = 170) was significantly shorter than that of PNI-negative patients (n = 46, 18 months vs. 24 months, p = 0.02) (Crippa et al. 2022). Thus, early assessment of PNI is of great significance for improving patient survival.

Traditional intratumoral radiomics mainly focuses on the heterogeneity of the tumor core region. Deng et al. (2025) constructed a nomogram by extracting features from CT-defined tumor regions to predict PNI in pancreatic cancer, achieving AUCs of 0.846 and 0.778 in the training and test cohorts, respectively. However, relying solely on intratumoral features is insufficient to capture the subtle early manifestations of PNI. In recent years, the peritumoral region has been recognized as a rich source of biological information. Our selection of a 3 mm margin is consistent with emerging evidence across different imaging modalities. Recent studies have shown that a 3 mm peritumoral band on CT effectively predicts postoperative recurrence (Wu et al. 2025), while similar margins on multiparametric MRI have demonstrated high accuracy in evaluating lymph node metastasis and histological grade (Xie et al. 2023). These findings collectively suggest that the immediate 3 mm peritumoral microenvironment harbors critical features reflective of tumor invasive potential and pathological aggressiveness.

PNI represents the invasion of surrounding nerve bundles by pancreatic cancer cells, and the peritumoral region is crucial for PNI research. However, there are currently few studies on PNI prediction based on the peritumoral 3 mm region. In this study, based on pancreatic parenchymal phase CT images, we constructed a predictive model integrating intratumoral + peritumoral radiomics features with clinical characteristics. The model achieved AUCs of 0.855 and 0.844 in the training and test cohorts, respectively, demonstrating improved predictive performance compared to the standalone clinical model or independent radiomics model. This provides a novel non-invasive strategy for preoperative assessment of PNI.

Univariate and multivariate analyses in this study revealed that elevated serum CA199 level was an independently associated factor for PNI in pancreatic cancer (p < 0.05), which is consistent with the conclusions of previous clinical studies (Lu et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2013). As a core tumor marker for pancreatic cancer, the high expression of CA199 reflects the increased malignancy and invasiveness of tumor cells, suggesting that it can serve as a convenient clinical indicator for assessing the occurrence of PNI in pancreatic cancer.

Large peripancreatic blood vessels, such as the portal vein and superior mesenteric artery and vein, have a close anatomical accompanying relationship with neural structures including the celiac plexus and superior mesenteric plexus. The abundant interstitial space around the vascular sheath provides a natural pathway for tumor cell invasion. The neatly arranged collagen fibers in the interstitium not only offer structural support for the rapid migration of tumor cells within the tumor nest, guiding them to move toward and invade blood vessels, but also provide a channel for cancer cells to directly spread along the vascular-neural interstitium and break through the perineurial barrier when vascular invasion occurs. These factors collectively promote tumor invasion and distant metastasis. (Sharma et al. 2021) Meanwhile, pseudosynaptic connections exist between sensory nerve terminals and cancer cells, which further facilitates the occurrence of PNI (Ren et al. 2025) .

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, its single-center retrospective design and modest sample size introduce potential selection bias and risk of model overfitting. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings requires external validation in larger, multicenter cohorts. In addition, the ROIs were delineated only based on pancreatic parenchymal phase CT images, and multimodal imaging data (such as ultrasound and magnetic resonance imaging) were not integrated, which limits the comprehensive assessment of tumor heterogeneity. And future multimodal imaging studies could enhance predictive performance. Third, manual segmentation of regions of interest is subject to inter-observer variability, and the optimal peritumoral margin for capturing PNI-related features remains unstandardized. Finally, while our model shows promise, its clinical impact on treatment decisions and patient outcomes requires confirmation through prospective trials.

Conclusion

Based on pancreatic parenchymal phase CT images, this study extracted radiomics features from the intratumoral and peritumoral 3 mm regions, and constructed a combined model integrating these features with clinical factors to generate a nomogram. Among the constructed models, this nomogram demonstrated superior predictive performance for preoperative non-invasive prediction of PNI in pancreatic cancer. This tool enables non-invasive preoperative assessment of PNI, and by potentially aiding in the identification of high-risk patients, it may inform more personalized treatment strategies. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate its clinical utility and impact on patient outcomes.

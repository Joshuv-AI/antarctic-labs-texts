Fibroblast Growth Factor 21: Bridging Nutrient Stress Signaling and Exercise-Induced Adaptive Responses
Authors: Dae Ho Lee
Published: 2026

The metabolic effects of exercise extend well beyond the period of physical activity, persisting for up to 24 hours and contributing to sustained improvements in glycemic control and insulin sensitivity, particularly in individuals with diabetes [1,2]. Consistent with this, data from the Molecular Transducers of Physical Activity Consortium highlight the systemic nature of exercise adaptation, demonstrating extensive multi-omic remodeling across a comprehensive analysis of 19 distinct tissues following endurance training in rats [3]. Notably, transcriptional changes were more pronounced in white and brown adipose tissues than in skeletal muscle, while the liver exhibited the greatest degree of proteomic and metabolomic alterations. These findings demonstrate that exercise-induced benefits are mediated through coordinated, multi-organ responses, with distinct roles played by metabolic tissues, especially the liver and adipose tissue [3].

Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) has emerged as a key endocrine regulator integrating nutritional and physiological stress signals [4]. Predominantly secreted by the liver, FGF21 is induced by various metabolic cues, including high carbohydrate intake, protein restriction, and fasting, through transcriptional regulators such as carbohydrate response element-binding protein, activating transcription factor 4, and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha [5].

Accumulating evidence supports a distinct role for FGF21 in mediating exercise-induced metabolic benefits [6]. Exercise, characterized by increased glucagon and reduced insulin levels, also stimulates circulating FGF21, further linking it to systemic energy homeostasis [7]. Acute exercise increases circulating FGF21, and its signaling is required for improvements in insulin sensitivity, particularly in diet-induced obesity models [8]. Both human and animal studies consistently show that exercise enhances hepatic FGF21 expression without significantly altering skeletal muscle levels, confirming the liver as the primary source of circulating FGF21 [6,9].

Importantly, obesity is characterized by elevated circulating FGF21 levels but reduced tissue sensitivity, particularly in adipose tissue, due to the downregulation of FGF receptor 1 and β-klotho [5]. Notably, these impairments can be reversed by exercise, suggesting restoration of FGF21 responsiveness is a key mechanism underlying its metabolic benefits [4,8].

In a paper now published in the Journal, Zhang et al. [10] provide a compelling perspective on the heterogeneous metabolic responses to acute exercise in type 2 diabetes mellitus. In their study, 60 minutes of aerobic exercise significantly reduced blood glucose, insulin, and C-peptide levels compared with a resting control, yet no overall difference in next-day 24-hour glycemic control was observed compared to the resting control group [10]. Thus, an intriguing analysis was conducted by the authors from this point onward. Approximately half of the participants demonstrated improved glucose variability and were classified as ‘responders,’ whereas the remainder were ‘non-responders.’ Only responders exhibited favorable next-day glycemic profiles on continuous glucose monitoring, with reductions in 24-hour coefficient of variation and standard deviation, along with improvements in mean sensor glucose, time in range, and time in tight range. Notably, baseline clinical characteristics did not differ between groups. Instead, baseline metabolomic differences appeared to distinguish responders from non-responders. Among 20 discriminant metabolites, several gut microbiota-derived compounds, such as phenol sulfate, p-cresol sulfate, and indoxyl sulfate, were particularly notable, as they originate from aromatic amino acid metabolism and are known to increase following bariatric surgery [11]. These findings suggest that host–microbiome metabolic interactions may influence delayed glycemic responses to exercise, which warrants further study. Mechanistically, responders exhibited greater post-exercise increases in FGF21 and free fatty acids. Their FGF21 levels also correlated with specific baseline metabolites (p-cresol sulfate, dimethylglycine, and 6-bromotryptophan) [10]. Additionally, longitudinal untargeted metabolomic profiling across multiple pre- and post-exercise time points revealed distinct trajectory patterns dominated by lipid-related metabolites, indicating a shift toward enhanced lipid mobilization and oxidation after exercise. Advanced analyses, including time-lag correlation and mediation modeling, identified 79 correlated metabolites and 19 mediators, largely associated with lipolysis, fatty acid oxidation, and tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediates. Importantly, temporal dynamics suggested that glucose metabolism is rapidly activated but transient, whereas lipid metabolism is delayed, sustained, and closely aligned with the FGF21 response [10]. Supporting causality, FGF21 knockout mice exhibited attenuated metabolic benefits of exercise, which were restored by recombinant FGF21 administration [10].

Collectively, these findings delineate a mechanistic axis linking baseline metabolomic profiles, exercise-induced FGF21 signaling, lipid metabolic reprogramming, and delayed glycemic improvement, thereby providing a conceptual framework to explain the heterogeneity in exercise responsiveness [12]. In this context, the present study offers important insights into the role of FGF21 as a central mediator of adaptive responses to exercise in individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus. A notable strength of the work lies in its demonstration of marked inter-individual variability in exercise responsiveness, which appears to be closely associated with differences in FGF21 dynamics.

Before interpreting these compelling data, an important consideration is the translational gap between preclinical and human studies. Prolonged FGF21 administration has been shown to reduce body weight, adiposity, hepatic lipid accumulation, and hyperglycemia in diet-induced obese mice, while improving insulin sensitivity and extending lifespan. However, the glucose-lowering effects of FGF21 appear to be less pronounced in humans. This may reflect species-specific differences, including those related to brown adipose tissue biology. Variability across FGF21 analogs complicates interpretation, leaving the extent of true FGF21-specific effects uncertain [4].

In this context, recent advances in long-acting FGF21 analogs further underscore the therapeutic relevance of this pathway. For example, pegozafermin has demonstrated significant efficacy in patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). In a phase 2 study, treatment with pegozafermin led to improvements in MASH histology and fibrosis, along with favorable changes in circulating lipid profiles and glycemic control without significant changes in body weight [13]. Thus, it will be important for future studies to determine whether the weight-reducing and longevity-associated benefits observed in rodents can be recapitulated in humans, and to elucidate the mechanisms or receptor subtype-specific pathways through which these effects are mediated. Such efforts may ultimately enable the development of strategies that harness the pleiotropic therapeutic potential of FGF21 for broader metabolic and aging-related indications.

Additionally, several unresolved questions merit consideration. It remains unclear whether post-exercise FGF21 responses contribute to the preservation of skeletal muscle mass and attenuation of sarcopenia, particularly given its limited direct action in muscle. It is also unclear whether responders derive greater metabolic benefit or whether non-responders may be more amenable to exogenous FGF21-based interventions. Stratification by glycemic status, combined with multi-omics integration, may provide further mechanistic resolution and enable the identification of predictive biomarkers. Although catecholamines are unlikely to mediate FGF21 secretion directly, their measurement could offer insight into coordinated neuroendocrine and humoral responses, facilitating a more integrated understanding of post-exercise glycemic regulation. Finally, although associations among diet, gut microbiota, and metabolic stress are well-established, defining their causal relevance to FGF21 response to exercise remains a critical priority for future studies.

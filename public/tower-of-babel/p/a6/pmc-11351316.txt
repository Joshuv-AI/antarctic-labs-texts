Retinal ganglion cell complex thickness in subjects with diabetes mellitus and uncontrolled hypertension in China
Authors: Mengyang Li, Qing Zhang, Ye Zhang, Kai Cao, Xiaohua Pan, Hui Feng, Fei Xiang, Zhi Li, Shuning Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo assess the interactive relationship between blood pressure status and diabetic mellitus (DM) with ganglion cell complex (GCC) thickness in elderly individuals in rural China.MethodsParticipants aged 50 years and older in a rural area of Daxing District, Beijing, were recruited in this study from October 2018 to November 2018. All subjects underwent a comprehensive systemic and ocular examination. Blood pressure status was graded as normotension, controlled hypertension and uncontrolled hypertension according to blood pressure measurements and the use of any medication for hypertension treatment. GCC parameters were measured by spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT). Generalized linear models (GLM) adjusted for related potential confounders were used to assess the interaction between DM and blood pressure status.ResultsAmong 1415 screened subjects (2830 eyes), a total of 1117 eyes were enrolled in the final analysis. GLM analysis showed a significant interactive relationship between DM with uncontrolled hypertension status (β = 3.868, p = 0.011). GCC thickness would decrease 0.255 μm per year as the age increased (β=-0.255, p < 0.001). In a subgroup of 574 subjects with uncontrolled hypertension, DM was associated with an increased average of GCC thickness (β = 1.929, p = 0.022).ConclusionsThe present results revealed a significant interactive relationship between blood pressure status and DM. The average GCC thickness increased in individuals with DM combined with uncontrolled hypertension, which should be considered in the measurement of GCC. Further studies are warranted to explore ganglion cells changes as a non-invasive method to detect neuron alterations in individuals with DM and uncontrolled hypertension.Trail registrationThe registration number of the present trial in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry is ChiCTR2000037944.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-024-03644-x.

Background

Glaucoma preferentially affects the three innermost layers of the retina: the nerve fiber, ganglion cell, and inner plexiform layers, where axons, cell bodies, and dendrites of the ganglion cells are the main components, respectively [1]. Consequently, the combination of these three layers, regarded as the ganglion cell complex (GCC), has been applied to diagnose glaucoma and monitor the disease progression with high sensitivity and specificity [2]. Moreover, since neurosensory retina is an extension of the central nervous system (CNS) [3], GCC measurements have also been recognized as a biomarker for CNS diseases, such as Multiple sclerosis [4] and Parkinson’s disease [5].

Several studies have characterized the GCC thickness in healthy eyes and investigated the effect of systemic and ocular factors on these parameters. Thereinto, age has been identified as an important determinant factor of GCC thickness [6–8]. Likewise, past literature has also reported sex and axial length as independent factors associated with thinner GCC or ganglion cell–inner plexiform layer (GCIPL) thickness [7, 8]. However, most of these studies were limited by small sample sizes, in the meantime did not look into the relationship between GCC thickness and systemic parameters such as blood pressure, blood glucose level or body mass index (BMI).

Rural residents account for more than half of Chinese population, yet our knowledge of this particular population in terms of epidemiological characteristics is still limited. Hence, with over 1400 subjects recruited in a rural area of Beijing, systemic and enhanced ophthalmic examinations were performed in Daxing Eye Study to explore potential ocular anomalies in this rural population. Of note, there is a large population of elder subjects suffering from hypertension and/or DM in China [9, 10], while higher fasting plasma glucose (FPG) has been confirmed to be independently associated with a higher prevalence of hypertension [11]. Although coexistence of glaucoma and these aforementioned systemic diseases are common in these individuals [12], their GCC thickness profile and associated factors have not been well documented. Therefore, we performed data analysis with information derived from Daxing Eye Study to assessed GCC thickness with DM and different status of blood pressure. Effects of DM and uncontrolled hypertension as well as their interactive relationship were futher explored with generalized linear model.

Methods

The Daxing Eye Study is a population-based cohort study recruiting subjects aged 50 years and older in Zhangziying Town, a rural area of Daxing district, Beijing. Individuals aged 50 years or more whose residential location had been in this town for more than 6 months were considered eligible. After being identified from the Resident Register record kept by the local government health service center, the subjects were then notified to participant in the study. As a result, a total of 1415 eligible subjects were recruited. All subjects underwent a comprehensive systemic and ocular examination. A detailed interviewer questionnaire was conducted to collect demographic variables including previous ocular and systemic diseases information and a medical history. Information collected included age, gender, height, body weight, heart rate, diastolic blood pressure (DBP), systolic blood pressure (SBP) and blood glucose level. BMI was calculated as the ratio of body weight (in kilograms) divided by body height (in metres) squared. The diabetes status was defined according to the American Diabetes Association Classification and Diagnosis of Diabetes [13]. Subjects with a random blood glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L were considered as diabetic. Besides, participants with self-reported diabetes diagnosis or the use of diabetic medications were as well categorized as DM. The definition of hypertension was made with reference to the 2018 ESC/ESH Guidelines for the management of arterial hypertension [14]. Normotension was defined as an SBP of < 140 mmHg and DBP of < 90 mmHg without any anti-hypertensive treatment. “Controlled hypertension” was defined as the presence of SBP < 140 mmHg and DBP < 90 mmHg with the use of medication to treat hypertension, and “uncontrolled hypertension” was defined as a SBP ≥ 140 mmHg and/or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg with or without the use of medication [15].

Slit-lamp biomicroscope was performed by experienced ophthalmologists. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and spherical refractive error (SER) of both eyes were tested and recorded. Intraocular pressure (IOP) was measured using a noncontact tonometer (Topcon CT-80; Topcon Medical Systems, Inc., Oakland, NJ, USA). Ocular biometry parameters including axial length (AL), anterior chamber depth (ACD), lens thickness (LT) and central corneal thickness (CCT) were measured by Lenstar (Lenstar LS900; Haag Streit, Bern, Switzerland). The IOP and ocular biometry parameters were measured 3 times and the average value was taken for statistical analysis.

GCC parameters were measured by the RTVue-XR Avanti OCT (Optovue, Fremont, CA, US). The scanning was performed by a trained operator and read by an experienced ophthalmologist masked to other clinical examinations. The GCC scan covered a 7 × 7 mm rectangular area of the macula centered 1 mm temporal to the fixation point. There were 15 vertical scans in total, where each scan had 0.5 mm spacing. Macular GCC thickness or inner retinal thickness, defined as the distance from the internal limiting membrane (ILM) to the IPL, was calculated automatically by the device. The quality of OCT scans was described with signal strength index (SSI). Images with algorithm segmentation failure, obvious decentration misalignment or SSI less than 45 were excluded [16].

Statistics

The mean and SD were used for the basic statistical description of normally distributed continuous variables, while the median value and range were used to describe the nonnormally distributed continuous variables. The categorical variables were described with counts and percentage. Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was used for continuous variables, and the Chi-square test was used for categorical variables. A general linear model adjusted for related potential confounders were generated to examine the interaction effects of blood pressure status and glucose level on GCC thickness. Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Packages for the Social Sciences (SPSS, version 22, Chicago, IL, USA) and an open-source R program (https://www.r-project. org/, Version 4.2.2). p-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Study population

A total of 1415 subjects (2830 eyes) were examined. Only the right eye of each study participant was included. 97 eyes were excluded for IOP over 22mmHg, glaucoma or retinal diseases. Glaucoma was diagnosed on the basis of optic disc evaluation, IOP, previous perimetry results, and self-reported glaucoma history. The final diagnosis was made according to the International Society of Geographical and Epidemiological Ophthalmology criteria [17]. Qualified OCT images for GCC assessment with SSI ≥ 45 were available for 1208 eyes. 91 eyes were further excluded due to the missing values of systemic or ocular parameters. A total of 1117 eyes were eventually included for the analysis. The study profile is shown in Fig. 1. In this study, 341 participants (30.5%) were normotension, 202 participants (18.1%) had controlled hypertension, and 574 participants (51.4%) had uncontrolled hypertension. Diabetes was found in 259 (23.2%) of the subjects, whereas the rest 858 subjects (76.8%) were absent of such conditiion.

Interactive effects of DM and hypertension status on GCC thickness

Firstly, we explored the interactive effects of DM and blood pressure status on GCC thickness (Fig. 2). In participants without diabetes, the mean GCC thickness were 97.05 μm, 95.85 μm and 95.02 μm in subjects with normotension, controlled hypertension, and uncontrolled hypertension, respectively. There was no significant difference between groups with different status of blood pressure. In subjects with diabetes, the mean GCC thickness were 95.11 μm, 95.27 μm and 96.82 μm for subjects with normotension, controlled hypertension, and uncontrolled hypertension, respectively, and the average GCC thickness was significantly thicker in those combined with uncontrolled hypertension. Generalized linear models adjusted for related potential confounders (Sex, age, BMI, heart rate, IOP, CCT, ACD, LT, AL and average SSI) were used to assess the interaction between DM and blood pressure status (Table 2). In the initial model with interaction terms, a significant interactive relationship between DM with uncontrolled hypertension status was detected (β = 3.868, p = 0.011), indicating an increase in average GCC thickness of 3.868 μm in subjects with DM combined with uncontrolled hypertension. Age was another key influencing factor (β=-0.255, p < 0.001) and GCC thickness would decrease 0.255 μm per year as age increased.

To confirm the effect of diabetes on GCC in individuals with uncontrolled hypertension, model 2 including 574 subjects with uncontrolled hypertension was established. The results of GLM demonstrated an increase in average GCC thickness of 1.919 μm in participants with diabetes in comparison with those without diabetes (β = 1.929, p = 0.022). Age remained as a strong factor negatively correlated with GCC (β=-0.326, p < 0.001), indicating that GCC thickness would decrease 0.255 μm per year as age increased.

Distribution of GCC thickness by age groups was described in Table S1. The effect of outliers was evaluated by sensitivity analysis. Outliers were defined as median ± 1.5 IQR then removed from the generalized linear model, and the interaction between DM and blood pressure status was still significant (Table S2).

Discussion

Retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) parameters including GCC and GCIPL measured by OCT are especially useful for diagnosis and follow-up of glaucoma, neuro-ophthalmological and retinal diseases, as it provides high-resolution, objective, and quantitative assessments of RGCs and the outer retina. Previous reports on GCC or GCIPL measurements of healthy subjects with SD-OCT were exhibited in Table 3. In the present study, after assessing systemic and ocular correlated factors for GCC thickness, our results revealed a significant interactive relationship between DM and hypertension status.

Previous studies have reported inconsistent results regarding the effect of DM or hyperglycemia on ganglion cell layer (GCL). The increase of GCIPL thickness has been observed in patients with DM and no or mild nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy (DR) [18, 19]. On the contrary, Gundogan’s study reported GCC thickness were thinner in patients with Type 1 DM without clinically diagnosed DR [20]. In Bloch’s study, GCC thickness was not associated with blood glucose level or diabetes [21]. In addition, Zeng et al. reported that GCC thickness was comparable between healthy subjects with type 2 DM patients without diabetic retinopathy (DR) [22]. However, this study didn’t include blood pressure status in the analysis.

It was noted that subjects with DM and uncontrolled hypertension presented a significantly increased average GCC thickness. Previous studies have indicated hyperglycemia changes of GCL are multifactorial, putting forward two pathways in terms of GCL in DR -the neurodegenerative pathway and the vascular pathway. In the neurodegenerative pathway, a slow thinning of the GCL might be present due to atrophy or reduction of neurons. By contrast, in the vascular pathway, the microvascular abnormalities under hyperglycemia lead to increased vascular permeability and capillary rarefaction, resulting in intracellular and extracellular edema in the GCL [23, 24]. Therefore, a thickening of the GCL might be an early sign for the development of macular edema with vasculature leakage [18, 25]. Since our study excluded individuals with macular edema on OCT, the thickening of GCC might be resulted from the diffuse swelling of ganglion cell layer. Several studies with healthy individuals as controls have suggested that systemic hypertension is associated with GCC or GC-IPL thinning [26–28]. Nevertheless, population studies have failed to report this association between blood pressure and GCC or GCIPL thickness [29, 30], possibly because of the implementation of linear models, the differences in genetic background, and confounding of other factors. Anderson et al. found that GCC was thinner in individuals with hypertension (94.3 μm) vs. those with normal blood pressure (96.3 μm; P < 0.001), and an increase in any of the blood pressure parameters was associated with GCC thickness in univariable analyses. Yet this association was not independent from the effect of age [21]. In our study, we eliminated the confounding effects by establishing blood pressure status and DM as interaction terms and revealed the association and interaction between the two factors. The effects of other factors such as age and BMI were also evaluated in the generalized linear model as confounders.

Pathophysiological alterations in hypertension might be an explaination for the mechanism of this interactive relationship. Microcirculatory abnormalities and vessel wall remodeling in arterioles are major pathological hallmarks of hypertension [31]. The retinal microcirculation normally demonstrates the ability of autoregulation, that is, active modification of vascular caliber in response to local signals, keeping retinal blood flow essentially constant [32]. This property protects the tissue from ischemia in hypoperfusion, whereas hyperperfusion caused by hypertension may cause endothelial damage and flow dysregulation [33]. In case of altered vascular permeability induced by DM and increased osmotic pressure caused by elevated blood glucose, the ganglion cells under hyperperfusion might present as diffuse edema. Thus, for individuals with combined uncontrolled hypertension and diabetes, it should be noted that there might be ganglion cells edema and early damage without visible vascular retinopathy. In those participants with uncontrolled hypertension but not diabetic, there was no significant increase of GCC thickness, possibly for the absence of the microvascular alterations caused hyperglycemia. Since patients with uncontrolled hypertension and hyperglycemia are very common in clinical practice, the effect of glycemic fluctuations on measurements should be considered when assessing GCC thickness in these patients.

There are also limitations in our study. Firstly, the diagnosis of diabetes status was based on the measurement of random blood glucose. Therefore, both type 1 DM and type 2 DM were included since we could not differentiate them according to current laboratory examinations. HbA1c level and hyperlipidemia were not included in the analysis. Secondly, the present study only involved individuals aged 50 years and above, so establishment of GCC thickness profile in all age groups were not feasible. Finally, subjects excluded due to poor OCT scans or failure to obtain ocular biometry parameters were older and with higher levels of blood pressure and blood glucose. This could introduce bias and limit the generalizability of our study.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we assessed systemic and ocular determinants of GCC thickness and revealed a significant interactive relationship between DM and blood pressure status. GCC thickness is increased in individuals with DM and uncontrolled hypertension. This might be an early sign for macular edema and should be considered in the measurement of GCC. Further study is warranted to explore ganglion cells changes in patients without clinically diagnosed retinopathy but with DM and uncontrolled hypertension, which might provide non-invasive methods to detect neuron alterations in these individuals.

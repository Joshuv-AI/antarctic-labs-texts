Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease mortality rate trends in Taiwan, 2002–2022: a joinpoint regression analysis
Authors: Ching-Hsiung Lin, Yi-Rong Li, Shu-O Chiang, Hao-Chien Wang, Meng-Chih Lin, Shih-Lung Cheng, Chong-Jen Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic respiratory diseases, particularly chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), are the eighth leading cause of death in Taiwan. Although COPD management has advanced in the previous two decades, mortality trends remain unclear. The present study analysed COPD mortality rates in Taiwan from 2002 to 2022.MethodsCOPD mortality and population data were obtained from Taiwan’s National Health Statistics and the Ministry of the Interior’s Demographic Yearbook. For comparison, global COPD mortality data were sourced from the WHO mortality database. A joinpoint analysis was conducted to assess trends in age-specific and age-standardised mortality rates across sex, region or country.ResultsFrom 2002 to 2022, Taiwan had 100 147 deaths attributed to COPD. The age-standardised mortality rate decreased with an annual average percentage change (AAPC) of −3.79%, which was more pronounced in women (AAPC: −4.28%) than in men (AAPC: −3.79%). The largest decline occurred from 2016 to 2022, with an AAPC of −7.70%. Most COPD-related deaths occurred among older individuals, with significant reductions in mortality rates observed among men aged ≥60 years and among women aged ≥50 years. A downward trend in COPD mortality rates was noted in most counties from 2016 to 2022, although patterns varied. The overall COPD mortality rate has declined in most countries since 2002, including Taiwan, which ranks third in Asia in terms of reductions in COPD mortality rates during this period.ConclusionFrom 2002 to 2022, COPD mortality rates in Taiwan declined considerably across sexes and regions, although patterns varied. In Asia, Taiwan’s reduction in the rate of COPD mortality ranks third behind those of the Republic of Korea and Singapore. The reductions observed in COPD mortality rates in Taiwan may be attributable to tobacco control initiatives and nationwide COPD care programmes.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is the most prevalent chronic respiratory disease globally.1 2 Although various interventions have been developed to prevent and treat COPD, the disease remains a global health challenge that significantly influences morbidity, socioeconomic burden and mortality. In 2019, the estimated global prevalence of COPD was 10.3% among individuals aged 30–79 years.1 Because the global population is ageing and increasing, the global burden of COPD is expected to increase by an estimated 23% from 2020 to 2050.3 Results from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2019 reveal that COPD is also a major contributor to global disability-adjusted life years, ranking fourth for individuals aged 50–74 and third for those aged ≥75 years.4 Notably, COPD was the third leading cause of death globally in 2019, responsible for 3.3 million deaths.5 Additionally, the global death toll from COPD increased by 14.1% from 2009 to 2019.5 6 In most countries, the age-adjusted mortality rate for men steadily declined. However, a distinct trend was observed for women in several countries between 1994 and 2019,7 highlighting the variance in COPD mortality rates across sexes and regions.

In Taiwan, a national epidemiology survey conducted in 2016 reported a prevalence of COPD of 6.1%.8 By 2019, the prevalence among individuals aged 30–79 years using the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease definition was 11.9%.1 Additionally, the age-standardised point disability-adjusted life year rate was 365 per 100 000 individuals.2 Moreover, the estimated economic burden of COPD for Taiwan was projected to be NT$20.3 billion for the period from 2020 to 2050.6 Studies examining the epidemiology and burden of COPD have highlighted its substantial influence on healthcare resources and the economy in Taiwan. Chronic lower respiratory diseases, particularly COPD, ranked as the seventh leading cause of death in Taiwan prior to 2016.9 Between 1981 and 1993, age-adjusted COPD mortality rates decreased from 8.26 to 4.91 per 100 000 individuals.10 However, these rates steadily increased thereafter, reaching 17.88 per 100 000 individuals by 2002.10 Other studies examining mortality trends from 1999 to 2007 across sex and location in Taiwan revealed that age-adjusted mortality rates declined among both sexes during this period.11 Overall, the COPD mortality rate among men was approximately three times higher than that among women.11 Moreover, the variability of COPD mortality rates across townships can largely be attributed to factors such as smoking, socioeconomic status and air pollution.11 On the basis of these findings, the present study concluded that mortality from COPD varied substantially across the previous two decades. Additionally, COPD mortality rates varied considerably by sex and location, consistent with global trends.

COPD mortality declined across age and sex between 1994 and 2019 in several populations, such as those in Hong Kong, Israel, Japan and the Republic of Korea.7 Nevertheless, no study has assessed changes in COPD mortality rates in Taiwan over the previous 20 years, nor has any study yet compared the overall long-term trends of these mortality rates with similar rates from other countries. In the past two decades, numerous advances in the management of COPD have been developed, such as new medications, updated national COPD guidelines and a nationwide integrated care model implemented in Taiwan. To enhance and assess the effectiveness of national health policies and promote the equitable allocation of healthcare resources in COPD prevention and management efforts, current data on COPD mortality rates and variations in these rates across sexes and locations are urgently required.

The present study analysed COPD mortality rates in Taiwan from 2002 to 2022. This study reported the age-adjusted mortality rates and evaluated the changes in mortality rates across sex and location from 2002 to 2022 using joinpoint analysis. This study also compared the long-term trends in COPD mortality rates between Taiwan and other countries.

Methods

Data source and data extraction

Data were sourced from publicly available government statistics such as the Ministry of Health and Welfare’s Leading Causes of Death and the Ministry of the Interior’s mid-year population data. Mortality records spanning 2002–2022 were sourced from the Cause of Death Statistics provided by the National Health Statistics of Taiwan.9 International Classification of Diseases, 9th revision (ICD-9) codes were used for records created before 2008, and International Classification of Diseases, 10th revision (ICD-10) codes were used for records created after 2008 to reflect updates in classification codes. Specifically, COPD mortality data were identified using ICD-9 codes 490, 491 and 496 for the period 2002–2007 and ICD-10 codes J40–J44 for the period 2008–2022. Mid-year population data were obtained from the Demographic Yearbook published by the Ministry of the Interior.12

This study obtained COPD mortality data for countries in Asia, Central and South America, Europe and Africa and North America and the Caribbean between 2002 and 2022 from the WHO mortality database as described in an earlier study.13 Countries with missing mortality data during this period were excluded. Data from 7 countries in Asia, 10 countries in Central and South America, 27 countries in Europe and Africa and 5 countries in North America and the Caribbean were included in the analysis.

Statistical analysis

Crude mortality rates were calculated using mid-year population figures in the denominator. Age adjustment was directly calculated using 5-year age intervals, employing the WHO 2000 age structure as the standard. The annual age-adjusted COPD mortality rate per 100 000 individuals was stratified by sex and region. Age-specific mortality rates were calculated for five age groups (40–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–79 and ≥80 years) by dividing the number of deaths by the corresponding mid-year population. To conduct a trend analysis of age-adjusted COPD mortality rates, joinpoint regression was employed using V.4.8.0.1 of the Joinpoint Regression Programme developed by the National Cancer Institute. Joinpoint regression analysis was employed because it provides objective findings on significant changes in mortality trends over time, rendering it particularly suitable for detecting the influence of public health policies and epidemiological shifts over time. The Joinpoint Regression Programme identifies the optimal trend line across several years of data, concurrently evaluating whether a multisegmented line offers a superior fit compared with a single trend line on a logarithmic scale.14 Best-fitting points, termed ‘joinpoints’, are selected when the rate significantly changes. Trend changes deemed statistically significant at an alpha level of 0.05 were determined through Monte Carlo permutation tests involving 4499 randomly permuted datasets. CIs for the joinpoints were calculated using empirical quantiles of the average annual percentage change (AAPC) and annual percentage change (APC). The analysis covered death rates from 2002 to 2022 and employed a maximum of three joinpoints. A maximum of three joinpoints was employed for the 20-year study period to ensure adequate statistical power and mitigate overfitting.15 Once the line segments had been defined, the estimated AAPC and APC with 95% CIs were calculated to assess the statistical significance of trends within the model.

Results

Trends in the crude and age-standardised COPD mortality rates

A total of 100 147 deaths from COPD occurred in Taiwan from 2002 to 2022. Figure 1A shows crude COPD mortality rates remained <20 per 100 000 individuals between 2002 and 2010. However, a notable upward trend in this rate was observed from 2011 to 2016. After 2016, the crude mortality rate of COPD declined through 2022. The age-standardised COPD mortality rate consistently decreased from 2002 to 2022 (figure 1B), with an AAPC of −3.79% (95% CI: −4.93% to −2.62%), of which the first and last were characterised by the greatest changes. The initial (2002–2007; APC: −4.92% (95% CI: −8.16% to −1.57%)) and final periods (2016–2022; APC: −7.70% (95% CI: −9.90% to −5.45%)) were both characterised by significant declines in the COPD mortality rates. Throughout the study period, men had higher crude and age-standardised COPD mortality rates than women, but women showed greater decline (AAPC: −4.28% (95% CI: −5.76% to −2.78%)) compared with men (AAPC: −3.79% (95% CI: −4.63% to −2.95%)). The age-standardised COPD mortality rate among men significantly declined throughout the study period (2002–2016; APC: −2.03% (95% CI: −2.75% to −1.30%); 2016–2022; APC, −7.78% (95% CI: −10.19% to −5.31%)). Similarly, women also experienced three significant change periods, with the greatest changes in the initial period (2002–2007; APC: −8.32% (95% CI: −12.23% to −4.24%)) and final period (2017–2022; APC: −8.41% (95% CI: −12.12% to −4.53%)) (online supplemental table 1). The male-to-female ratio increased from 2.90 (2002) to 3.90 (2015), then narrowed to 2.99 (2022).

Trend in age-specific COPD mortality rates

The trends in age-specific COPD mortality rates from 2002 to 2022 are shown in figure 2A. Across the Taiwanese population, notable declines occurred in groups aged >60 years: AAPCs of −3.53% (95% CI: −5.18% to −1.86%) for ages 60–69, −4.96% (95% CI: −5.94% to −3.96%) for ages 70–79 and −2.87% (95% CI: −4.18% to −1.54%) for ages ≥80. The years corresponding to the joinpoints varied across age groups. The 70–79 age group showed the most significant decline across all ages, with three change periods where initial and final periods had the most substantial changes. In this age group, mortality decline was more pronounced in the final period (2016–2022; APC: −8.35% (95% CI: −10.43% to −6.22%)) than in the initial period (2002–2009; APC: −5.39% (95% CI: −6.84% to −3.93%)) (online supplemental table 2). Among men, downward trends occurred throughout the study period in ages >60 years, with men aged 70–79 showing the largest decrease (AAPC: −5.75% (95% CI: −6.82% to −4.66%)), consistent with overall population trends. In this age group, decline was more pronounced finally (2018–2022; APC: −9.2% (95% CI: −13.22% to −5.00%)) than initially (2002–2018; APC: −7.08% (95% CI: −8.59% to −5.55%)) (figure 2B and online supplemental table 2). The age-specific mortality trend in women differed from the overall population and men, showing significant decreases among ages >50 years. Women aged 60–69 had the greatest decrease, with significant declines in both initial (2002–2014; APC: −13.23% (95% CI: −17.16% to −9.11%)) and final periods (2014–2022; APC: −6.93% (95% CI: −9.14% to −4.66%)). These results demonstrate that age-specific COPD mortality changes have varied by age and sex in Taiwan since 2002 (figure 2C and online supplemental table 2).

(A) Age-specific COPD mortality rates. (B) Age-specific COPD mortality rates among men. (C) Age-specific COPD mortality rates among women. COPD, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Trends in age-standardised COPD mortality rates by county

An earlier study reported COPD mortality disparities across Taiwan regions from 1999 to 2007.11 Similarly, this study revealed significant age-standardised COPD mortality reductions from 2002–2007 to 2016–2022, with greater changes in 2016–2022. Hence, this study analysed geographical distribution changes in COPD mortality rates to elucidate variations contributing to observed declines. Overall, most regions showed downward trends from 2016 to 2022. In 2016, 7 counties and cities had a mortality rate greater than 15 per 100 000 individuals, specifically Pingtung and Taitung counties in southern Taiwan, Changhua and Yunlin counties in midwestern Taiwan, Nantou City in central Taiwan and Miaoli and Yilan counties in northern Taiwan. Between 2017 and 2019, fewer than seven counties reported mortality rates exceeding this threshold, and the distribution of counties and cities with mortality rates exceeding 15 per 100 000 varied annually. Finally, from 2020 to 2022, the trend in age-standardised COPD mortality significantly declined in most regions (figure 3A).

(A) Age-adjusted COPD mortality rates by region. (B) Age-adjusted COPD mortality rates among men by region. (C) Age-adjusted COPD mortality among women by region. COPD, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Although four regions (Keelung City, Miaoli County, Nantou City, Pingtung County) did not exhibit a significant downward trend from 2020 to 2022, COPD mortality rates stabilised. Joinpoint analysis revealed Taoyuan had the largest mortality reduction (AAPC: −14.83% (95% CI: −20.92% to −8.28%)), followed by Kaohsiung County (AAPC, −9.83% (95% CI: −12.42% to −7.16%)), New Taipei City (AAPC, −9.48% (95% CI: −13.27% to −5.53%)), Taipei City (AAPC, −9.45% (95% CI: −13.73% to −4.95%)) and Changhua County (AAPC, −9.04% (95% CI: −11.99% to −5.98%)). Regions with AAPC below median (−7.18%) included Taitung (AAPC, −6.44% (95% CI: −12.41% to −0.07%)), Nantou (AAPC, −5.4% (95% CI: −7.98% to −2.75%)), Chiayi (AAPC, −5.11% (95% CI: −18.41% to 10.36%)), Yunlin (AAPC, −4.66% (95% CI: −7.44% to −1.79%)), Hualien (−4.67% (95% CI: −13.15% to 4.64%)), Tainan (−4.42% (95% CI: −9.69% to 1.16%)), Hsinchu (−2.13% (95% CI: −15.74% to 13.68%)), Miaoli (−1.22% (95% CI: −4.88% to 2.58%)) and Keelung 7.94 (7.94% (95% CI: 1.54% to 14.74%)). Most were located in central, southern and eastern Taiwan (online supplemental table 3). Among both sexes, a downward trend in age-standardised COPD mortality occurred in most regions from 2016 to 2022, consistent with overall findings (figure 3B,C). Additionally, the magnitude of the changes in age-standardised mortality rates varied by sex. Among men, Taoyuan showed the largest decrease (AAPC: −13.56% (95% CI: −17.51% to −9.43%)), followed by Changhua County (AAPC, −8.47% (95% CI: −10.47% to −6.43%)), Kaohsiung County (AAPC, −8.25% (95% CI: −10.72% to −5.71%)), Yilan County (AAPC, −7.53% (95% CI: −11.79% to −3.06%)) and New Taipei City (AAPC, −7.42% (95% CI: −11.82% to −2.81%)). Among women, Penghu County had the largest decrease (AAPC: −21.07% (95% CI: −41.39% to 6.30%)), followed by Taipei City (AAPC, −14.77% (95% CI: −20.11% to −9.07%)), New Taipei City (AAPC, −13.94% (95% CI: −17.86% to −9.83%)), Pingtung County (AAPC, −13.11% (95% CI: −18.56% to −7.29%)) and Taitung County (AAPC, −12.81% (95% CI: −28.22% to 5.9%)) (online supplemental table 3). These findings demonstrate that changes in age-standardised COPD mortality rates varied by county and city and sex from 2016 to 2022.

Comparison between global and Taiwanese trends in age-standardised COPD mortality rates

Since 2002, the age-standardised COPD mortality rate has declined in Taiwan. This study compared Taiwan’s reductions in COPD-related mortality with global trends in COPD mortality rates. As indicated in the heatmap in figure 4A, the age-standardised COPD mortality rate declined in most countries since 2002. Globally, the country reporting the largest decline was Mauritius (AAPC: −8.29, 95% CI: −10.19 to −5.96), followed by Singapore (AAPC: −7.71, 95% CI: −8.65 to −7.20) and the Republic of Korea (AAPC: −7.22, 95% CI: −7.74 to −6.66). Taiwan ranked eighth globally in COPD mortality rate reductions. In Asia, the country with the largest decline was Singapore, followed by the Republic of Korea and Taiwan. By contrast, the three countries with the highest growth rates in COPD mortality were Paraguay (AAPC: 6.66, 95% CI: 5.21 to 8.32), Grenada (AAPC: 2.49, 95% CI: −2.15 to 6.45) and Greece (AAPC: 2.23, 95% CI: −0.40 to 4.09; figure 4B).

Discussion

Our findings reveal an overall decline in COPD mortality rates from 2002 to 2022 in Taiwan. First, age-standardised COPD mortality rates decreased among both men and women over the previous two decades, consistent with global trends, with the largest decline occurring from 2016 to 2022. Although the decline was slightly more pronounced for women than for men, the gap in mortality rates between the sexes narrowed over time. Second, most COPD deaths occurred among older individuals, although significant declines were noted among men aged ≥60 and women aged ≥50. Third, age-standardised COPD mortality rates across Taiwanese regions decreased at varying rates among both sexes. Fourth, trend change points were identified by sex, age group and region. These findings could be combined with national and local data for each time point to generate hypotheses regarding underlying factors, assisting health policymakers in formulating appropriate interventions. Furthermore, Taiwan ranked eighth globally and third in Asia for COPD mortality reduction magnitude.

This study demonstrates that crude COPD mortality rates in Taiwan increased between 2002 and 2016, then steadily declined until 2022. Conversely, age-standardised mortality rates declined over time. This discrepancy reflects Taiwan’s changing population age structure, as Taiwan’s ageing rate exceeds two times that of European countries and the USA. Taiwan progressed from an ageing to aged society in only 24 years (2003–2018). This demographic shift contributed to divergent trends between crude and age-standardised COPD mortality rates. Standardised rates highlight mortality changes independent of population age structure alterations16 17 . Overall age-standardised mortality rates substantially declined in two phases: 2002–2007 and 2016–2022. From 2002 to 2007, age-standardised mortality rates trended downward, consistent with other studies. However, an earlier study documented increased age-standardised COPD mortality rates from 1993 to 2002.10 The shift after 2002 may be attributable to several factors (figure 5). First, new COPD treatments were developed, including inhaler therapies approved for national health insurance coverage to increase treatment access. Second, national tobacco control policy has been enforced since 2002. Taiwan allocated considerable resources supporting outpatient smoking cessation clinics and creating a national smoking cessation hotline. Furthermore, schools, workplaces and restaurants were mandated smoke-free between 2003 and 2004.18 This population-level tobacco policy reduced smoking rates, potentially preventing COPD or arresting progression.

From 2016 to 2022, age-standardised COPD mortality decline was more pronounced than 2002–2007. Contributing factors may include (figure 5): antismoking legislation and national COPD mitigation programmes, including Taiwan’s COPD guidelines,19 national COPD integrated care programmes,20 the ‘Disease Specific Care–Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease’ certification programme and COPD early detection programmes.21 Taiwan’s COPD guidelines used rigorous evidence-based methodologies, including Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation standards and systematic integration of international evidence with local data.19 Earlier studies showed Taiwan’s COPD pay-for-performance programme was associated with reduced exacerbated COPD symptoms and improved quality of life.20 22 The programme enhances comprehensive COPD care quality, contributes to slowing disease progression and may partially explain observed mortality trend reductions. Additionally, in 2017, Taiwan implemented a COPD early detection programme incorporating guideline-recommended screening strategies for at-risk individuals. This programme identified mild or moderate COPD in nearly 70% of screened individuals.21 Taiwan’s comprehensive adoption of evidence-based COPD management practices involved systematic transition from empirical to evidence-driven healthcare delivery, potentially contributing to improvements in mortality trends observed in the present study.

Regarding sex-based differences, this study revealed COPD mortality rates declined among both sexes from 2002 to 2022, with more pronounced declines in women than men. This finding is not unique to Taiwan, with similar patterns documented in Hong Kong, Israel and Japan.7 By contrast, studies from the European Union, USA and Canada reported more pronounced mortality reductions among men.14 23 24 The findings of the present study thus facilitate a useful comparison with comprehensive analyses conducted in other populations. For example, one study analysed COPD mortality from 1979 to 2021 in the USA, revealing increasing trends among women and white individuals and stable trends among men and African American individuals.25 Although women in the USA experienced increased COPD mortality rates over four decades, women in Taiwan experienced more pronounced decline (AAPC: −4.28%) than men (AAPC: −3.79%) from 2002 to 2022. This regional variation may be attributable to sex-based mortality differences or disparities in medical management approaches. Phenotypic differences exist between Eastern and Western populations, and within Asian populations,26 although no evidence suggests COPD mortality rates are higher among Asian women than elsewhere. One study reported that women with COPD may experience greater dyspnoea and anxiety than male counterparts, but these factors do not translate into different mortality rates.27 No definitive evidence of sex-based differences in standard COPD treatment efficacy has been reported.28 Several mechanisms may explain reduced COPD mortality among Taiwanese women since 2002. First, historically higher smoking prevalence among men may have resulted in more severe disease progression, potentially limiting treatment responsiveness. Second, women typically demonstrate more proactive healthcare-seeking behaviours, facilitating earlier diagnosis and intervention.29 Third, inherent biological differences in COPD susceptibility and progression may exist, as suggested by one phenotypic study.30 The substantial mortality reduction, particularly from 2016 to 2022 (AAPC: −7.70%), likely reflects cumulative effects of comprehensive tobacco control policies since 2002 and nationwide COPD care programmes introduced in 2016. These findings have crucial public health implications, demonstrating that integrated approaches combining population-level prevention with targeted clinical interventions can achieve substantial mortality reductions. Notably, the present study observed that mortality trends varied by age group, with sex-based differences reversed when comparing individuals >70 versus <70 years. Additionally, inconsistent trends were observed between sexes across Taiwan regions. These complex patterns suggest individual risk factors and local variables may crucially alter sex-based mortality trends, although comprehensive analysis was beyond this study’s scope. Our findings demonstrate that COPD mortality gaps between sexes have narrowed over time in Taiwan, consistent with observations in the European Union, USA and Asia.7 14 24 Future research should elucidate biological and social mechanisms underlying sex-based mortality differences, quantify policy intervention contributions and develop sex-sensitive COPD management strategies. Additionally, the observed geographical heterogeneity across Taiwan warrants further investigation into the socioeconomic and environmental factors influencing mortality trends.

Notably, the joinpoint analysis revealed unexpected shifts in age-specific trends between sexes. COPD mortality rates for women and men aged ≥70 years declined since the early 2010s. However, among men aged ≤50 years, COPD mortality slightly increased throughout the study period. Among men aged >60 years, mortality rates reduced. Only men aged ≥80 years experienced modest increases from 2009 to 2012. Among women, COPD mortality rates considerably declined in all age groups from 2002 to 2022. For women aged ≥60 years, mortality rates declined since before the early 2000s, though increased from early 2000s to 2015s, then consistently declined since the early 2010s. These findings demonstrate heterogeneous mortality trends across age groups in both sexes. The observed decline in COPD mortality rates likely reflects public health interventions in tobacco control and disease care that improved outcomes, although sex and age disparities warrant further study. These observations suggest that COPD awareness and management strategies have benefitted individuals of all sexes and ages, potentially indicating a shift towards more personalised and sex-sensitive approaches in medical care and public health policies.

This study revealed overall COPD mortality reduction from 2016 to 2022 across regions, differentially affecting the overall population and both sexes in Taiwan. This suggests COPD awareness and management strategies are effective across most regions. However, geographical disparities in COPD mortality persist, consistent with 1999–2007 observations.11 These disparities were more pronounced among men than women. Age-standardised mortality rates were lower in predominantly northern, urbanised regions such as Taipei, New Taipei City, Taoyuan and Hsinchu than other regions. Nevertheless, not all these regions exhibited marked downward trends. Counties with slight downward trends were primarily located in Taiwan’s central, southern and eastern regions, where individuals tend to be older, rural-dwelling and working in agriculture.16 Variations may be attributable to COPD risk factors prevalent in these regions, including smoking habits, biomass smoke exposure, air pollution levels, ageing populations, socioeconomic disparities and altitude.3138 These differences highlight the need for region-specific interventions and enhanced healthcare access to achieve equitable health outcomes across Taiwan.

This study has several limitations. First, the study period coincided with ICD version transitions that may have caused abrupt mortality rate changes in 2008. However, no evidence suggests substantial changes were associated with ICD version differences, and studies documented strong concordance between ICD-9 and ICD-10 for assessments of the primary causes of death.39 40 Second, the exclusive reliance on death certificate data posed challenges because COPD accuracy as a listed death cause is debatable. Previous studies have documented the considerable under-reporting of COPD on death certificates, with COPD not mentioned in 42% of patient deaths and frequently omitted as the primary cause in exacerbated COPD cases.41 Such omissions may have led to underestimated COPD mortality. Third, ecological studies such as the present study cannot account for individual risk factors such as socioeconomic status, medical history or prognostic factors, limiting patient-level hypothesis validation. Furthermore, ecological designs cannot establish causal relationships between policy interventions and mortality trends; future individual-level studies are required to evaluate evidence-based practices’ direct influence on COPD outcomes.

Conclusion

COPD mortality rates in Taiwan considerably declined across sexes and regions from 2002 to 2022, although the patterns of this decline varied. These findings offer valuable insights for health policymakers seeking to tailor interventions that address diverse health patterns and local needs. Furthermore, a downward trend in COPD mortality rates was observed in most countries for which data were available, including Taiwan. In Asia, Taiwan’s reduction in COPD mortality rates ranked third, behind those of the Republic of Korea and Singapore. This high ranking may be attributable to Taiwan’s tobacco control initiatives and nationwide COPD care programmes.

The Mediating Role of Patient Satisfaction With Nursing Care Quality in the Association Between Provision of Compassionate Care and Patient Loyalty: A Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Zahra Hoseinabadi, Amir Sadeghi, Leili Tapak, Zahra Purfarzad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimTo examine the association between the provision of compassionate care and patient loyalty and to assess the mediating role of patient satisfaction with nursing care quality in this association between the provision of compassionate care and patient loyalty.DesignA correlational cross‐sectional study.MethodsA total of 508 hospitalized patients were recruited using stratified proportional random sampling from five hospitals affiliated with Hamadan University of Medical Sciences, Iran, between November 2023 and July 2024. Participants completed the Compassionate Care Assessment Tool, the Patient Satisfaction with Nursing Care Questionnaire, and a patient loyalty questionnaire. Data were analysed using SPSS version 24 for descriptive and inferential statistics, including Pearson's correlation, independent‐samples t‐tests, one‐way analysis of variance and linear regression. Mediation analysis was conducted using AMOS version 24.ResultsStructural equation modelling showed that provision of compassionate care was positively associated with patient satisfaction (β = 0.751, p < 0.001) and patient loyalty (β = 0.230, p < 0.001). Patient satisfaction with nursing care quality was positively associated with patient loyalty (β = 0.493, p < 0.001) and partially mediated the association between provision of compassionate care and patient loyalty (indirect β = 0.370, p < 0.001). The model explained 49% of the variance in patient loyalty (R 2 = 0.49).ConclusionHigher levels of perceived compassionate care were positively associated with greater patient loyalty, both directly and indirectly through higher patient satisfaction with nursing care quality. These findings highlight the pivotal role of compassionate care in improving patient satisfaction and fostering patient loyalty.Implications for Nursing PracticeHealthcare organisations should implement evidence‐informed strategies to strengthen compassionate nursing care by addressing barriers and enhancing facilitators at the individual, patient and family and organisational levels. Nurses should promote compassionate communication and patient‐centred, expectation‐sensitive care to improve patient experience, satisfaction and loyalty.

Introduction

Compassionate care is widely recognized as a fundamental ethical principle in clinical guidelines, nursing codes and health policies worldwide (Su et al. 2024). For patients and their families, illness and hospitalization are often accompanied by considerable emotional distress and uncertainty. As the largest group of healthcare professionals and those who spend the most time in direct contact with patients, nurses play a pivotal role in alleviating this distress and improving patients' healthcare experiences through compassionate and supportive care (Babaei et al. 2022). In healthcare settings, compassion extends beyond the provision of technical and routine clinical care. It encompasses empathy, respect for human dignity, recognition of patients' unique characteristics, active listening, positive interpersonal communication, appropriate use of humour and a genuine commitment to understanding individual needs (Babaei and Taleghani 2019; Naseri et al. 2022). Through compassionate interactions, nurses communicate respect, value and recognition of patients as individuals whose worth extends beyond their illness or diagnosis (Naseri et al. 2022).

Evidence suggests that compassionate care is associated with a range of favourable patient outcomes, including pain relief, improved quality of life, fulfilment of care needs and enhanced psychological resilience (Pehlivan and Güner 2020). Moreover, by fostering effective communication, treatment adherence and psychological safety, compassionate care may be positively linked to broader healthcare outcomes such as patient satisfaction and loyalty (Chen et al. 2022). Compassionate care has also been correlated with greater patient autonomy, improved quality and safety of care, reduced healthcare costs, increased nurse satisfaction and enhanced moral empowerment among healthcare professionals (Naseri et al. 2022). Despite these well‐documented benefits, the mechanisms through which compassionate care is related to organizational outcomes, particularly patient loyalty, remain insufficiently understood.

Patient loyalty has emerged as a critical indicator of organizational performance and a source of sustainable competitive advantage in contemporary healthcare systems. It refers to patients' intention to revisit the same healthcare institution for future healthcare needs and their willingness to recommend that institution to others (Chen et al. 2022). Higher levels of patient loyalty have been associated with improved continuity of care, reduced complaints, lower marketing costs, increased market share and enhanced organizational performance (Sedighi et al. 2025). Previous studies have identified several determinants of patient loyalty, including perceived service quality (Shie et al. 2022), patient trust (Sedighi et al. 2025; Shie et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2021), patient satisfaction (Liu et al. 2021; Rachmatillah and Munawar 2025), nurses' caring behaviours (Sedighi et al. 2025) and patients' experiences of nursing care (Chen et al. 2022). Nevertheless, the contribution of compassionate nursing care to the development of patient loyalty has received limited empirical attention, particularly in the context of developing healthcare systems.

One potential mechanism underlying the association between compassionate care and patient loyalty is patient satisfaction with nursing care quality. Patient satisfaction is widely regarded as a key indicator of healthcare quality and reflects the extent to which healthcare services meet or exceed patients' expectations, values and preferences (Donmez et al. 2022; Alhussin et al. 2024). Higher satisfaction levels have been associated with better adherence to treatment recommendations, improved health outcomes and reduced burdens on caregivers and healthcare systems (Hosseini et al. 2023). Furthermore, substantial evidence demonstrates positive associations between nursing care quality and patient satisfaction (Safa et al. 2021; Aghaei et al. 2022; Kim‐Soon et al. 2022; Bentum‐Micah et al. 2020), as well as between patient satisfaction and loyalty (Liu et al. 2021; Rachmatillah and Munawar 2025; Bentum‐Micah et al. 2020). These findings suggest that patient satisfaction may serve as an important explanatory pathway through which compassionate nursing care is linked to patient loyalty.

Despite the growing body of research on compassionate care, patient satisfaction and patient loyalty, the potential mediating role of patient satisfaction with nursing care quality in the association between compassionate care and patient loyalty has not been directly examined. Existing studies have largely investigated these variables in separate research streams, providing only indirect evidence of their interrelationships. In Iran, Sedighi et al. (2025) reported that nurses' caring behaviours were associated with patient loyalty through patient trust rather than satisfaction (Sedighi et al. 2025), whereas Beheshtaeen et al. (2025) identified an association between compassionate care and reduced missed nursing care without examining loyalty outcomes (Beheshtaeen et al. 2025). Internationally, Chen et al. (2022) reported that the association between patient experience with nursing care and patient loyalty was partially mediated by patient satisfaction (Chen et al. 2022), while studies conducted in Egypt and Indonesia demonstrated that the association between service quality and loyalty was fully mediated by patient satisfaction (El Garem et al. 2024; Arman et al. 2023). Other studies have highlighted patient trust as a mediator between service quality and loyalty (Shie et al. 2022; Kristinawati and Gunardi 2023). However, most previous investigations have focused on general service quality rather than compassionate nursing care specifically, and the potential mediating role of patient satisfaction in this context remains largely unexplored.

Addressing this gap is particularly important in healthcare systems where nurses constitute the primary point of patient contact and are uniquely positioned to shape patients' perceptions of care. Therefore, this cross‐sectional study aimed to examine the mediating role of patient satisfaction with nursing care quality in the association between perceived compassionate care and patient loyalty.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

The research employed a correlational cross‐sectional design between November 2023 and July 2024. The study population consisted of all patients hospitalized in the medical and surgical departments from five hospitals affiliated with Hamadan University of Medical Sciences during the study period in Iran. Inclusion criteria were providing written informed consent, willingness to participate in the study, absence of diagnosed mental health disorders, age over 18 years and hospitalization for more than 2 days in the medical or surgical departments. Patients who did not return the questionnaire or returned an incomplete questionnaire were excluded from the study.

The sample size was calculated using the standard formula for detecting a correlation coefficient. Assuming a two‐sided type I error of 0.05, a statistical power of 0.80 and a minimum expected correlation coefficient of r = 0.13, Fisher's z transformation was used as follows: C = 0.5 × ln[(1 + r)/(1 − r)] = 0.1307. Therefore, the required sample size was calculated as N = [(Z α + Z β )/C]2 + 3 = 462, where Z α = 1.9600 and Z β = 0.8416. After accounting for a possible 10% non‐response rate, the final required sample size was set at 509. Since no directly comparable previous study was available for determining the expected correlation coefficient, r = 0.13 was selected as a conservative small but meaningful effect size to ensure adequate power for detecting weak associations among provision of compassionate care, patient satisfaction with nursing care quality and patient loyalty.

A stratified proportional random sampling method was used to determine the number of participants from each hospital based on the number of beds in the medical and surgical departments. First, the sample size for each hospital was calculated proportionally according to the number of eligible inpatient beds in that hospital relative to the total number of eligible beds across all participating hospitals. Then, within each hospital, the required sample size for each medical and surgical department was determined proportionally based on the number of beds in that ward. Within each department, a list of eligible patients was prepared, and participants were selected using simple random sampling until the required sample size for that ward was reached.

Data Collection Instruments

The study used several data collection instruments, including a demographic information questionnaire that collected data on gender, marital status, education level, place of residence, age, history of hospitalization and length of hospital stay. In addition, the Compassionate Care Assessment Tool (CCAT), the Patient Satisfaction with Nursing Care Quality Questionnaire (PSNCQQ) and a patient loyalty questionnaire were administered.

Compassionate Care Assessment Tool (CCAT)

The Compassionate Care Assessment Tool (CCAT) was originally developed by Burnell in 2011 as a 28‐item measure to evaluate compassionate nursing care in cancer patients. Subsequently, Burnell and Agan (2013) examined the validity of the tool among patients in medical and surgical wards in the United States. This led to a revised version consisting of 20 items grouped into four factors: meaningful connection (eight items; Cronbach's α = 0.867), patients' expectations (five items; α = 0.801), caring attributes (four items; α = 0.774) and capable practitioner (three items; α = 0.781). The items are rated on a four‐point Likert scale, ranging from ‘not important’ to ‘extremely important’ (for the importance dimension) and from ‘never’ to ‘all of the time’ (for the provision of care dimension). Thus, the total score ranges from 20 to 80 (Burnell and Agan 2013; Vaisi‐Raygani et al. 2021; Dalvandi et al. 2019). Vaisi‐Raygani et al. (2021) translated and validated the Persian version of the CCAT. Confirmatory factor analysis supported the validity and reliability of the Persian version, retaining the original four‐factor structure. For the importance and provision domains, the Cronbach's alpha coefficients were 0.918 and 0.933, respectively, and the intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) were 0.848 and 0.907, respectively (Vaisi‐Raygani et al. 2021). In the present study, the Cronbach's alpha values for the importance and provision domains were 0.94 and 0.93, respectively.

Patient Satisfaction With Nursing Care Quality Questionnaire (PSNCQQ)

Developed by Laschinger et al. (2005), this questionnaire consists of two sections. The first section includes 19 items that assess patients' satisfaction with nursing care, whereas the second section contains four questions that evaluate the overall quality of service and nursing care received during hospitalization, the patient's health condition upon admission, as well as their willingness to recommend the nursing care facility to friends and family. For the purpose of this study, only the first section was used. Patients rate the items on a 5‐point scale, where 1 represents poor satisfaction and 5 represents excellent satisfaction. The questionnaire's total score ranges from a minimum of 19 to a maximum of 95, with higher scores indicating greater patient satisfaction with nursing care (Laschinger et al. 2005). The reliability of the questionnaire was confirmed by the developers, who reported a Cronbach's alpha of 0.97. Exploratory factor analysis conducted by Laschinger et al. revealed a one‐factor structure with factor loadings above 0.70 and satisfactory fit indices (Karaca and Durna 2019). Subsequent studies have also supported the single‐factor structure of the questionnaire (Milutinović et al. 2012; Mamić et al. 2024). The validity and reliability of this questionnaire were confirmed in a study conducted in Iran by Amiri et al. (Amiri et al. 2020). The internal consistency of the questionnaire, as measured by Cronbach's alpha, was reported to be 0.95 in that same study (Amiri et al. 2020).

Questionnaire of Patient Loyalty to the Hospital

To assess patient loyalty, the loyalty dimension of the questionnaire developed by Parasuraman, Zeithaml and Berry was used. This dimension consists of five items, each rated on a 5‐point Likert scale ranging from ‘strongly agree’ to ‘strongly disagree’ (scored as follows: strongly agree = 5, agree = 4, neutral = 3, disagree = 2 and strongly disagree = 1). Thus, the total score ranges from 5 to 25, with higher scores indicating greater loyalty (Zeithaml et al. 1996). The validity and reliability of this questionnaire have been confirmed in several studies conducted in Iran (Arab et al. 2012; Aghily et al. 2017). Sedighi et al. (2025) reported the Cronbach's alpha of the questionnaire to be 0.92 (Sedighi et al. 2025). In the present study, the Cronbach's alpha coefficient was 0.92.

Procedures

After obtaining approval from the Ethics Committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences and coordinating with hospital officials, eligible patients were identified in each department according to the predefined inclusion criteria. The questionnaires were coded and distributed anonymously, in person and in sealed envelopes. Data were collected during hospitalization after patients had stayed in the department for more than 2 days, to ensure that they had sufficient experience of nursing care to evaluate compassionate care, satisfaction with nursing care quality, and loyalty‐related perceptions. Questionnaires were administered at a convenient time that did not interfere with patients' treatment, rest or clinical care. The patients completed the questionnaires using a self‐administered method, and the completed questionnaires were collected by the investigator.

A total of 520 questionnaires were distributed across five hospitals. Of these, 518 questionnaires were returned and 10 were excluded because of incomplete responses. Finally, 508 valid questionnaires were included in the analysis, yielding a valid response rate of 97.7% based on the number of distributed questionnaires.

Ethical Considerations

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences (Approval No. IR.UMSHA.REC.1402.533). Before participation, all participants received comprehensive information about the study objectives, the voluntary nature of participation, the confidentiality of their data, their right to withdraw from the study at any time without penalty, and the assurance that declining or discontinuing participation would not affect the care they received. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data collection.

Data Analysis

Data were entered into SPSS version 24 by the first author; double data entry was not performed. To verify data entry accuracy, the corresponding author randomly checked 10% of the records by comparing the SPSS entries with the numbered paper questionnaires, and no discrepancies were found. Questionnaires with incomplete responses on the main study variables were excluded from the analysis; therefore, complete‐case analysis was performed.

Descriptive statistics, including frequency, percentage, mean and standard deviation, were used to summarize participants' characteristics and study variables. Independent‐samples t‐tests and one‐way ANOVA were used to compare mean scores across demographic groups, and Pearson's correlation coefficient was used to examine bivariate associations among the main study variables. Multiple linear regression analyses using the enter method were conducted to assess the associations of compassionate care dimensions and patient satisfaction with nursing care quality with patient loyalty while controlling for potential confounders. Control variables were selected based on their theoretical and empirical relevance and included gender, marital status, education level, place of residence, age, history of hospitalization and length of hospital stay.

The assumptions of regression analysis were assessed before model interpretation. Normality was evaluated using skewness and kurtosis values within the ±2 range. Multicollinearity was assessed using tolerance and variance inflation factor (VIF), with tolerance values greater than 0.10 and VIF values below 10 considered acceptable. Independence of errors was evaluated using the Durbin‐Watson statistic, with values between 1.50 and 2.50 considered acceptable (Aminzadeh et al. 2025; Sathyanarayana and Mohanasundaram 2024; Hashemi et al. 2025). Because all study variables were measured using self‐reported questionnaires collected at a single point in time, common method variance (CMV) was assessed using Harman's single‐factor test. All measurement items were entered into an unrotated principal component analysis. The first unrotated factor explained 47.03% of the total variance, which was below the recommended threshold of 50%, suggesting that CMV was unlikely to pose a serious threat to the study findings (Podsakoff et al. 2003).

AMOS version 24 was used to conduct path analysis within a structural equation modelling framework to examine the statistical mediating role of patient satisfaction with nursing care quality in the association between provision of compassionate care and patient loyalty. The model was estimated using maximum likelihood estimation. The indirect effect was tested using the bootstrap method with 5000 resamples and 95% bias‐corrected confidence intervals. Model fit was assessed using the chi‐square to degrees of freedom ratio (χ 2/df) ≤ 3, root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) ≤ 0.08, comparative fit index (CFI) ≥ 0.90, goodness of fit index (GFI) ≥ 0.90, incremental fit index (IFI) ≥ 0.90 and normed fit index (NFI) ≥ 0.90 (Aminzadeh et al. 2025; Sathyanarayana and Mohanasundaram 2024; Hashemi et al. 2025). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Most participants in this study were female (54.10%), married (75.80%), had education below a diploma level (34.60%) and lived in urban areas (70.50%). The mean age was 40.81 years, with an average hospitalization history of 1.45 times and a mean hospital stay of 5.51 days. A statistically significant difference was found in the mean score of provision of compassionate care between men and women, with men scoring higher than women (p = 0.039). Age showed a positive and significant relationship with both the perceived importance of compassionate care (r = 0.211; p < 0.001) and the provision of compassionate care (r = 0.161; p < 0.001). Additionally, length of hospital stay was inversely and significantly associated with patient loyalty to the hospital (r = −0.190; p < 0.001). No other relationships were statistically significant (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

Pearson's correlation coefficients revealed significant positive relationships between provision of compassionate care and patient satisfaction with nursing care quality (r = 0.722, p < 0.001), as well as between provision of compassionate care and patient loyalty to the hospital (r = 0.564, p < 0.001). A significant positive correlation was also found between patient satisfaction with nursing care quality and patient loyalty to the hospital (r = 0.632, p < 0.001) (Table 3).

Regression analysis showed that patient satisfaction with nursing care quality was significantly associated with three dimensions of compassionate care provision: patients' expectations (β = 0.286, p < 0.001), meaningful connection (β = 0.221, p < 0.001), and caring attributes (β = 0.221, p < 0.001), whereas the capable practitioner dimension was not significant. None of the control variables were significantly associated with patient satisfaction with nursing care quality, and the model explained 53.0% of its variance. For patient loyalty, the strongest predictor was satisfaction with nursing care quality (β = 0.451, p < 0.001). Positive associations with patient loyalty were observed for meaningful connection (β = 0.150, p = 0.037) and caring attributes (β = 0.104, p = 0.041), whereas patients' expectations and the capable practitioner dimension were not significantly associated with patient loyalty. Among the control variables, higher education (associate degree vs. below diploma: β = −0.092, p = 0.016; bachelor or above vs. below diploma: β = −0.104, p = 0.016), older age (β = −0.123, p = 0.003) and longer hospital stays (β = −0.156, p < 0.001) were associated with lower patient loyalty. The loyalty model accounted for 46.2% of the variance (Table 4).

As presented in Table 5, Figure 1, the structural equation model examined the mediating role of patient satisfaction in the relationship between compassionate care and patient loyalty while adjusting for the demographic covariates that were significantly associated with patient loyalty (i.e., education level, age and length of hospital stay). The model demonstrated an excellent fit to the data (χ 2/df = 1.663, GFI = 0.922, CFI = 0.980, IFI = 0.980, NFI = 0.952, RMSEA = 0.036). Compassionate care showed a significant standardized direct association with patient satisfaction (β = 0.751, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.712–0.783]). Compassionate care also showed a significant standardized total association with patient loyalty (β = 0.600, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.545–0.651]), while patient satisfaction was significantly associated with patient loyalty (β = 0.493, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.400–0.583]). After adjustment for the demographic covariates, the standardized direct coefficient of compassionate care on patient loyalty remained significant (β = 0.230, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.134–0.328]). Among the covariates, having an associate degree (vs. below diploma) (β = −0.074, p = 0.036), older age (β = −0.084, p = 0.022) and longer hospital stays (β = −0.140, p < 0.001) were significantly associated with lower patient loyalty, whereas having a bachelor's degree or above (vs. below diploma) was not significantly associated with patient loyalty (β = −0.056, p = 0.121). The standardized coefficient of compassionate care on patient loyalty through patient satisfaction was significant (β = 0.370, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.299–0.441]), indicating statistical partial mediation. Overall, compassionate care was positively associated with patient loyalty both directly and indirectly, with patient satisfaction statistically partially mediating this association, even after controlling for demographic covariates.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that compassionate nursing care is a significant correlate of both patient satisfaction with nursing care quality and patient loyalty. Furthermore, patient satisfaction statistically partially mediated the association between compassionate care and patient loyalty, highlighting the multifaceted mechanisms through which compassionate care may be linked to patient outcomes.

Consistent with previous research, our findings revealed a discrepancy between patients' expectations of compassionate care and the level of compassionate care actually received (Dalvandi et al. 2019). The patients rated the ‘capable practitioner’ dimension highest and the ‘meaningful connection’ dimension lowest. Similar patterns have been reported in previous studies, where professional knowledge and technical competence received higher ratings than interpersonal and relational aspects of care (Safa et al. 2021; Zare et al. 2022). The higher score for ‘capable practitioner’ may be explained by the fact that this dimension is readily observable and directly associated with immediate clinical outcomes, as patients can easily recognize nurses' competence, confidence, technical skills and effective use of medical equipment. In contrast, ‘meaningful connection’ encompasses more complex and less tangible attributes, including respect, unconditional acceptance, emotional presence, humour, support for patients' spiritual beliefs, facilitation of spiritual resources, forgiveness, inner beauty and assistance with difficult personal concerns (Burnell and Agan 2013; Vaisi‐Raygani et al. 2021). The lower rating of ‘meaningful connection’ may therefore reflect the challenges nurses face in addressing the emotional, relational and spiritual dimensions of care within busy healthcare environments. Previous studies have identified several organisational and contextual barriers to compassionate care, including heavy workloads, staffing shortages, inadequate institutional support, time constraints, limited opportunities for therapeutic interactions and a healthcare culture that often prioritises routine tasks and technical efficiency over person‐centred care (Babaei and Taleghani 2019; Pehlivan and Güner 2020).

Patient satisfaction with nursing care quality was above the questionnaire midpoint, although findings from previous studies have been inconsistent. While several studies reported moderate satisfaction levels (Aghaei et al. 2022; Heravi‐Karimooi et al. 2023; Parizad et al. 2021), others documented higher levels of satisfaction (Safa et al. 2021; Abbasi Farajzadeh et al. 2022). One possible explanation for these discrepancies relates to the influence of the COVID‐19 pandemic on patients' perceptions of healthcare professionals. During the pandemic, nurses were widely recognized as frontline heroes, which may have led patients to adjust their expectations and express greater appreciation for the care they received. In contrast, patients in post‐pandemic settings may evaluate nursing care against more normative or ideal standards, resulting in comparatively lower satisfaction scores. Therefore, the lower satisfaction observed in the present study compared with the findings reported by Safa et al. (2021) may reflect a return to pre‐pandemic expectations rather than a decline in the quality of nursing care itself (Safa et al. 2021). Furthermore, a systematic review and meta‐analysis conducted in Iran reported higher levels of patient satisfaction in studies undertaken before the COVID‐19 pandemic, particularly in public hospitals, emergency departments and studies with larger sample sizes (Hosseini et al. 2023). Differences in healthcare resources, staffing levels, organizational support, patient expectations and healthcare system characteristics across settings may further explain the variability in satisfaction outcomes reported in the literature.

The findings of this study indicate that the dimensions of compassionate care, including meaningful connection, patients' expectations and caring attributes, are positively associated with patient satisfaction with nursing care quality. This finding is consistent with previous evidence demonstrating a significant relationship between nursing care and patient satisfaction across diverse healthcare settings (Kim‐Soon et al. 2022). Likewise, studies examining healthcare service quality have shown that patients who perceive higher‐quality care report greater levels of satisfaction (Farrokhi et al. 2022; Rauf et al. 2024). A plausible explanation for these associations lies in the inherently holistic nature of compassionate care. Unlike purely task‐oriented care, compassionate care extends beyond the management of physical symptoms to encompass emotional, social and spiritual dimensions of patients' experiences. Through respectful communication, empathy, emotional presence, support for individual values and beliefs, patient involvement in care decisions and the demonstration of professional competence, compassionate care may promote a sense of being valued, understood and supported (Burnell and Agan 2013; Zamanzadeh et al. 2017). Such experiences are likely to strengthen patients' perceptions of care quality and contribute to more favourable evaluations of nursing services. Notably, patients' expectations emerged as the strongest predictor of satisfaction with nursing care quality. This finding underscores the central role of expectation management in shaping patients' healthcare experiences and is consistent with service quality theory, which posits that satisfaction is largely determined by the extent to which perceived performance meets or exceeds prior expectations (Zeithaml et al. 1996). From this perspective, compassionate care may be particularly effective because it enables nurses to better understand, anticipate and respond to individual patient needs and expectations. Consequently, healthcare organizations seeking to improve patient satisfaction should not only focus on technical aspects of care delivery but also invest in strategies that facilitate individualized, expectation‐sensitive and relationship‐centered nursing practice.

An important finding of the present study was the statistical mediating role of patient satisfaction with nursing care quality in the association between compassionate care and patient loyalty. This finding suggests that compassionate care is associated with loyalty both indirectly, through its association with patients' satisfaction with nursing care, and directly, through mechanisms that extend beyond satisfaction alone. When nurses provide compassionate care by recognizing patients' distress, responding empathetically, and actively addressing sources of suffering, patients are more likely to perceive that their needs and concerns are acknowledged and valued. Such experiences are associated with more positive evaluations of nursing care quality, which in turn are associated with patients' attachment to the healthcare organization and are associated with a greater willingness to return to or recommend the hospital to others (Chen et al. 2022). The observed mediating effect is consistent with emerging evidence highlighting the central role of patient satisfaction in the pathway linking care quality and patient loyalty. For example, studies conducted in Vietnam and Indonesia have demonstrated that compassionate and high‐quality care is positively associated with patient satisfaction, which subsequently is associated with greater patient loyalty (Rachmatillah and Munawar 2025; Le and Aliswag 2025). Furthermore, a recent systematic review concluded that compassionate interactions, particularly those characterized by effective verbal and non‐verbal communication and the development of trusting relationships, consistently are associated with patient satisfaction and treatment adherence (Varghese and Khakha 2025). Taken together, these findings reinforce the proposition that compassionate care may function as a key mechanism through which healthcare organizations can improve patients' experiences and strengthen long‐term relationships with service users. Notably, the mediating effect identified in the present study was statistical and partial rather than complete, indicating that additional pathways may explain the association between compassionate care and patient loyalty. Previous research has shown that trust in nurses mediates the association between caring behaviours and patient loyalty (Sedighi et al. 2025), while empathy has been found to exert a direct association with loyalty independent of patient satisfaction (Amir et al. 2025). These findings suggest that compassionate care not only is associated with patients' cognitive evaluations of care quality but also may foster emotional bonds and trust‐based relationships that directly are associated with loyalty. From a theoretical perspective, the present findings support a multidimensional model in which compassionate care is associated with loyalty through interconnected cognitive, emotional and relational processes, with patient satisfaction representing one important, but not exclusive, mechanism.

Higher educational attainment, older age and longer hospital stays were associated with lower patient loyalty. Although age was not significantly associated with patient loyalty in the bivariate analysis, it emerged as a significant negative predictor after adjustment for other variables in the regression and structural models. This suggests that the independent association between age and patient loyalty became evident only after accounting for potential confounding factors. Older patients may also have greater healthcare experience and higher expectations, leading to more critical evaluations of healthcare services (Rohita and Nurkholik 2025; Diwan et al. 2020). These findings highlight the importance of tailoring patient engagement strategies to patients' demographic characteristics and healthcare experiences.

Strengths and Limitations

This study contributes to the growing body of evidence on compassionate nursing care by simultaneously examining its direct association on patient loyalty and its indirect association through patient satisfaction. The use of validated measurement instruments and mediation analysis strengthens the robustness of the findings. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the cross‐sectional design precludes causal inference. Because of the cross‐sectional design, the proposed mediation pathway should be interpreted as statistical mediation rather than evidence of a causal mechanism or temporal sequence. Second, data were collected from a limited number of hospitals, potentially affecting the generalisability of the findings. Third, self‐reported measures may be subject to social desirability and recall biases. Fourth, although Harman's single‐factor test suggested that common method variance was unlikely to be a serious concern, this approach has limited sensitivity for detecting method bias. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted with caution, and future studies are encouraged to use more robust approaches, such as the Common Latent Factor (CLF) or marker‐variable techniques, to further evaluate common method bias. Future longitudinal and multicentre studies are recommended to establish causal pathways and evaluate interventions designed to enhance compassionate care.

Conclusions

Compassionate nursing care represents a critical component of healthcare quality and is associated with patient satisfaction and loyalty. The findings indicate that satisfaction with nursing care quality statistically partially mediates the association between compassionate care and patient loyalty, suggesting that both direct and indirect mechanisms may explain patient loyalty. Given the relatively low scores observed in the meaningful connection dimension and the strong influence of patients' expectations on satisfaction, healthcare organisations should prioritise interventions that foster therapeutic nurse–patient relationships, improve communication and support individualised care delivery. Organizational strategies such as optimizing nurse staffing levels, allocating protected time for patient interaction, incorporating compassionate care indicators into performance evaluations and integrating compassionate care standards into accreditation frameworks may help bridge the gap between patient expectations and actual care experiences. Ultimately, strengthening compassionate care may not only be associated with better patient experiences but may also contribute to sustainable patient loyalty and long‐term service effectiveness.

Implications for Nursing Practice

Given the significant positive effects of compassionate care on patient satisfaction and patient loyalty identified in the present study, healthcare organizations should adopt evidence‐informed, multilevel strategies to strengthen compassionate nursing care in clinical settings. In line with current evidence, these strategies should address barriers related to nurses' psychological well‐being, professional development, patient and family interactions and workplace conditions while reinforcing facilitators such as self‐compassion, continuing professional education, therapeutic communication, positive role modelling, supportive leadership, collaborative teamwork and active patient and family engagement (van den Berg et al. 2025). Nursing practice should also foster meaningful nurse–patient relationships and expectation‐sensitive care through compassionate communication and the routine use of patient feedback to improve care. In addition, incorporating compassionate care indicators into quality improvement initiatives may help sustain high standards of compassionate nursing care. Embedding these strategies into routine nursing practice may strengthen compassionate care, leading to greater patient satisfaction, stronger patient loyalty and improved quality of care.

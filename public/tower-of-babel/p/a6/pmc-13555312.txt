Comorbidities in clinical cohorts of patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Marcus C.H. Leong, Seema Green, Mingkun Guan, Tyson M. Walters, Emma Marshall, Chunbouy Sok, Nicole S.L. Goh, Christopher J. Ryerson, Yet H. Khor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundComorbidities can influence treatment decisions and health outcomes in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the types and prevalence of comorbidities, as well as their impact on health outcomes, in clinical cohorts of patients with IPF.MethodsOvid MEDLINE and Embase were searched up to August 2024 for eligible observational studies with ≥100 adults with IPF that reported at least one comorbidity. Meta-analyses were performed for comorbidity prevalence, with associations between comorbidities and health outcomes narratively synthesised.ResultsA total of 251 studies (n=606 398 participants) were included. Common comorbidities were systemic hypertension (pooled prevalence 39%, 95% confidence interval (CI) 34–44%), dyslipidaemia (30%, 95% CI 20–38%), gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (27%, 95% CI 23–31%), hiatus hernia (20%, 95% CI 9–33%), overweight/obesity (22%, 95% CI 18–27%), diabetes mellitus (21%, 95% CI 19–23%), ischaemic heart disease (20%, 95% CI 17–23%) and heart failure (15%, 95% CI 13–18%). Few studies evaluated the relationships between comorbidities and health-related quality of life or IPF disease progression. Lung cancer was the only comorbidity that was consistently associated with increased short-term and long-term mortality in patients with IPF.ConclusionsA wide variety of comorbidities affect clinically encountered patients with IPF, with many having higher prevalence rates than those reported in clinical trial cohorts. This highlights the need for personalised care in the management of IPF. The relationships between different comorbidities and health outcomes warrant future studies to better inform targeted screening and management.

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is one of the most common subtypes of chronic progressive fibrosing lung disease, with an estimated global incidence between 0.9 and 13 per 100 000 persons [1]. Whilst patients with IPF typically have declining lung function with poor prognosis following diagnosis, their disease trajectories vary [2]. Current gold standard therapy for IPF includes antifibrotic drugs, which significantly slow disease progression and confer survival benefits [3]. However, patients on these therapies continue to experience a considerable disease burden, with 16–50% being treated owing to adverse drug reactions and other factors [4, 5]. There has been a shift towards personalised care in IPF, accounting for different disease characteristics, patient factors and comorbidities that affect individuals’ disease course [6, 7].

Comorbidities can be overlooked amongst the complex care required for patients with IPF. Understanding the prevalence and impact of comorbidities affecting this population can help prioritise and deliver targeted management. Cardiovascular risk factors and diseases, as well as gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD), are common among patients participating in pharmaceutical randomised controlled trials (RCTs) for IPF [8]. However, it is unclear whether this is representative of real-world settings owing to restrictive RCT eligibility criteria, with different comorbidities being used as exclusion criteria [9]. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to determine the prevalence of comorbidities and their association with health outcomes in patients with IPF from observational studies. This knowledge is fundamental to advancing the development of comprehensive management and facilitating the translation of RCT results for clinical care in IPF.

Methods

This systematic review was performed according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis guidelines [10]. The protocol was prospectively published on the PROSPERO register of systematic reviews (CRD42024576578).

Search strategy

Relevant studies were identified using Ovid MEDLINE and Embase from inception until August 2024. Full search terms are described in table S1.

Eligibility criteria

We included observational studies involving ≥100 adults aged ≥18 years with IPF using any diagnostic criteria that reported at least one comorbidity. Non-English publications and conference abstracts were excluded.

Study selection

Two authors (M.C.H. Leong and S. Green) independently screened the search results, with sequential review starting with titles and abstracts followed by full-text publications. Disagreements at each stage were resolved through consensus or a third author (Y.H. Khor). For studies with overlapping populations that reported the same comorbidities, the study with the largest cohort was prioritised, followed by the most recent publication. For overlapping studies that reported different comorbidities, the earliest publication was considered the primary study for extracting study and patient characteristics, as well as for the risk of bias assessment, with the remaining publications considered secondary studies for data on different comorbidities and/or outcomes.

Data extraction and risk of bias

Data extraction was performed by one author using a standardised spreadsheet, with independent verification by a second author (M.C.H. Leong, S. Green, M. Guan or T.M. Walters). Data regarding pulmonary hypertension were not included, because pulmonary hypertension was evaluated in a recent systematic review [11] and is often a consequence of IPF rather than a comorbidity. For the evaluation of associations with comorbidities, health outcomes of interest included health-related quality of life (HRQoL), disease progression, acute exacerbation (AE) and mortality. The risk of bias assessment was performed independently by two authors (M.C.H. Leong, S. Green, M. Guan or T.M. Walters) using a validated 10-item checklist for prevalence studies described by Hoy et al. [12], focusing on assessment of internal and external validity (table S2). Disagreements were resolved through consensus or a third author (Y.H. Khor).

Data synthesis

Reporting of comorbidities was categorised by organ system and presented using descriptive statistics: median (range) for continuous data, and count and percentages for categorical data. For the prevalence of comorbidities reported in at least five independent studies, meta-analyses with a random-effects model were performed using Stata (v16.1 StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA). Heterogeneity of data was assessed using I2 statistics and rated as low (0–29%), moderate (30–50%) or high (>50–90%) [13]. Prespecified subgroup analyses by the World Bank income classification (high-income countries (HICs) versus low-to-middle income countries (LMICs)) were performed [14] when at least two studies per subgroup were available. Meta-analyses of associations with health outcomes were not possible due to study and reporting heterogeneity. Therefore, the data were synthesised narratively, with associations between commonly evaluated comorbidities and health outcomes for individual studies summarised using harvest plots for overviews of the directions of associations.

Results

Characteristics of included studies

Of the 14 172 studies identified from the initial search, 2378 underwent full-text assessment and 253 were included, with 189 primary studies and 64 secondary studies (figure 1). Included primary studies were predominantly of retrospective design (n=114, 60%) and were conducted between 2006 and 2015 (n=100, 53%), with Europe (n=77, 41%) and Asia (n=60, 32%) as the major contributing regions (table 1). Details of included studies are presented in table S3. The IPF diagnostic criteria used were primarily based on the contemporary clinical practice guidelines during study conduct (table S4). Reported participant characteristics varied in demographics, with lung function impairment ranging from mild to severe (table 1).

Risk of bias assessment

The risk of bias assessment for included primary studies is summarised in figure S1, with details provided in table S5. Most included studies had high overall risk of bias (n=141, 75%), with very few having low risk (n=14, 7%). Of the 10 domains evaluated, the comorbidity definition was most judged as high risk due to unclear diagnostic methods/criteria (n=172, 91%), followed by indirect data collection from healthcare databases or proxies (n=147, 78%), and potential selection bias during recruitment/case identification (n=93, 49%).

Comorbidity reporting

Included studies reported on a median of four comorbidities (range 1–25). The most frequently reported comorbidities by organ systems were cardiac disorders (n=153, 60%), respiratory disorders (n=139, 55%), endocrine disorders (n=135, 53%), gastrointestinal disorders (n=128, 51%) and cardiovascular risk factors (n=118, 47%) (table S6 and figure S2). A total of 77 different comorbidities were evaluated, with the most commonly reported being diabetes mellitus (n=124, 49%), COPD (n=115, 45%), ischaemic heart disease (n=97, 38%), systemic hypertension (n=95, 38%) and GORD (n=90, 36%) (figure S3).

Comorbidity prevalence

Cardiovascular risk factors had the highest pooled prevalence of 37% (95% confidence interval (CI) 32–41%), with systemic hypertension (39%, 95% CI 34–44%), dyslipidaemia (29%, 95% CI 20–38%) and overweight/obesity (22%, 95% CI 18–27%) commonly reported (table 2 and table S7). Cardiovascular disorders had a pooled prevalence of 21% (95% CI 19–23%), with ischaemic heart disease the most prevalent reported disease at 20% (95% CI 17–23%). Gastrointestinal disorders had a pooled prevalence of 21% (95% CI 18–25%), with a pooled prevalence of 27% (95% CI 23–31%) for GORD and 20% (95% CI 9–33%) for hiatus hernia. Endocrine disorders also had a pooled prevalence of 21% (95% CI 19–23%), as did diabetes mellitus. Respiratory disorders had a pooled prevalence of 18% (95% CI 16–21%), with COPD (20%, 95% CI 17–24%) being the most common disease, followed by asthma (8%, 95% CI 6–10%). The I2 statistic of the pooled prevalence for the analysed comorbidities ranged between 89% and 99%, with all classified as having high heterogeneity. A wide range of other diseases, such as ophthalmological disorders and inflammatory bowel disease, were reported in small numbers of studies, precluding meta-analysis (table S8).

Subgroup analysis comparing HICs and LMICs demonstrated largely similar prevalence for organ system disorders, with the I2 statistic remaining high (table S9). Compared to the HIC subgroup, the LMIC subgroup had a significantly lower prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors, as well as renal and rheumatological disorders. The LMIC subgroup had reduced prevalence of arrhythmias, heart failure, thyroid disease, chronic liver disease, malignancy and asthma.

Associations between comorbidities and health outcomes

One study evaluated six comorbidities for their association with HRQoL [15], five studies evaluated 14 different comorbidities for their associations with AE-IPF [16–20] and 41 studies evaluated 38 comorbidities for their associations with mortality [16, 17, 20–57]. No studies evaluated the associations between comorbidities and IPF disease progression.

A single study evaluated the association between comorbidities and HRQoL using the Assessment of Quality of Life-8-Dimensions instrument for health state utility [15], which combines the physical and psychosocial dimensions (table S10). Cardiac disease and arthritis were associated with reduced HRQoL, with arthritis primarily driven by the physical dimension and cardiac disease driven by both dimensions.

The most evaluated comorbidities for association with AE-IPF were COPD (n=4) [16–18, 20] and diabetes mellitus (n=3) [17–19]. Only one study reported a significant positive association with AE for COPD [20] and for diabetes mellitus [19], with the remaining reporting no associations. Other comorbidities were evaluated in one or two studies for an association with AE-IPF, and were not statistically significant with the exception of systemic hypertension (table S11).

Different mortality outcomes were evaluated: 1) short-term mortality, including in-hospital mortality (n=7), with two including lung transplantation as an event; and 2) long-term mortality, including overall mortality (n=17), overall survival (n=14), 2-year mortality (n=2) and 1-year mortality (n=1) (table S12). All studies that evaluated short-term mortality for lung cancer (n=4) [23, 24, 45, 51], chronic kidney disease (n=3) [22–24] and pulmonary embolism (n=3) [21, 24, 50] reported a significant positive association with short-term mortality, except one study that found a nonsignificant association with lung cancer [45] (figure 2). Conversely, all studies that evaluated diabetes mellitus (n=3) reported a significant negative association with short-term mortality [21, 22, 50]. Other comorbidities were only evaluated for associations with short-term mortality in one or two studies, showing largely nonsignificant relationships. The most frequently evaluated comorbidity with long-term mortality in patients with IPF was COPD (n=14), with a positive association in two studies [28, 44] and no association in 12 studies [16, 17, 20, 27, 32, 34, 37, 39, 52, 54, 57, 58] (figure 2). Lung cancer was the only comorbidity that had consistent associations with increased long-term mortality (six of eight studies) [34, 39–41, 54, 58].

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis comprehensively evaluated the prevalence and impact of comorbidities affecting patients with IPF in clinical cohorts. Acknowledging the varying reporting across different studies, common comorbidities affecting clinically encountered patients with IPF were systemic hypertension, dyslipidaemia, GORD, hiatus hernia, obesity and diabetes mellitus. Notably, few studies evaluated the associations between comorbidities and health outcomes in patients with IPF, without any statistically significant relationships observed for AE-IPF. Lung cancer was the only comorbidity with a consistent positive association with both short-term and long-term mortality.

We found that cardiovascular risk factors related to metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease and GORD were common in IPF. Given the wide variety of common comorbidities among patients with IPF, involvement of other specialists in their management is crucial, particularly primary care physicians. Of note, selected conditions, such as psoriasis and inflammatory bowel disease, may be overestimated in our analysis, because they may represent a secondary cause to interstitial lung disease (ILD). On the other hand, obstructive lung disease, such as COPD, may be underdiagnosed in the presence of ILD, owing to the opposing physiological changes that are challenging to detect on spirometry. It is noteworthy that differences in comorbidity patterns were observed between LMICs and HICs, with cardiovascular, renal and rheumatological disorders being more prevalent in HICs. This likely reflects geographical influence related to socioeconomic factors and healthcare access on the development and/or detection of various comorbidities. Further research is required to better understand the regional differences in relevant comorbidities, allowing local clinicians to tailor the care delivery for patients with IPF.

Several comorbidities of high prevalence in clinically encountered patients with IPF have been underrepresented in RCT populations, including COPD, cardiac arrhythmias and hiatus hernia. This likely reflects restrictive RCT eligibility criteria that exclude patients with selected health conditions that may confound study outcomes or increase the risk of adverse drug reactions [59, 60]. This may limit the generalisability of therapeutic benefits and safety profiles of pharmaceutical agents studied in clinical trials. Given that the discovery of both efficacious and well-tolerated novel drugs is essential to improve health outcomes in patients with IPF [3], it is crucial to balance the risk of including patients of different health statuses with the benefits of improved validity in future trials and post-market research. Moreover, as antifibrotic drugs continue to extend the life expectancy of patients with IPF, the impacts of comorbidities and polypharmacy will likely become more pronounced, and therefore more important to understand.

Although many studies evaluated the prevalence of various comorbidities in patients with IPF, only a few studies investigated the impact of very few comorbidities on health outcomes, with a focus on mortality. Associations of a wider range of comorbidities with HRQoL and IPF disease progression remain largely unexplored. With a shift towards personalised care for IPF, developing strategies to improve symptoms and HRQoL has been identified as a research priority [61, 62]. Many comorbidities can contribute to symptom burden in patients with IPF, including GORD for chronic cough [63, 64] and cardiovascular disease for dyspnoea [65]. Furthermore, some comorbidities may contribute to the progression of IPF, such as micro-aspiration in GORD [66] and tractional injuries in obstructive sleep apnoea [67]. There is a need to explore whether effective management of these comorbidities can result in improved outcomes in patients with IPF. Given that lung cancer was consistently associated with mortality outcomes in IPF cohorts, future studies of lung cancer screening in this population are warranted to establish the risk–benefit ratios for early detection and potential complications arising from the treatments.

This systematic review has several limitations. Many included studies had a high risk of bias in comorbidity definitions and data collection methods that could affect result accuracy, causing possible over- or underestimation. However, this largely reflects variation in the diagnostic approaches for different comorbidities in real-world settings. The high heterogeneity observed in meta-analyses did not resolve with subgroup analyses according to the country income level. This indicates the presence of other influencing factors, which may be related to patient characteristics and IPF diagnostic criteria or disease stages. Our findings highlight a gap in assessing associations between comorbidities and health outcomes in IPF, which is a crucial step in identifying clinically relevant conditions for treatment. Given the differences in study design, participant eligibility criteria and statistical analyses undertaken for evaluating the relationships between comorbidities and health outcomes, meta-analyses could not be performed. Furthermore, assessing AE as an outcome in IPF is challenging and often poorly characterised in observational studies, causing potential inaccuracy, in addition to its low occurrence with concerns of being underpowered [68]. Notably, the importance of multimorbidity or the combination of different comorbidities could not be evaluated in this study, and these are likely to have a significant influence on the disease course of IPF. Future studies may also assess the effects of comorbidities on cause-specific hospitalisation in patients with IPF. Despite these limitations, this systematic review provides a comprehensive synthesis of current knowledge, serving as the groundwork for further research.

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provides multiple important insights into the prevalence and significance of comorbidities in patients with IPF. Multimorbidity is common in IPF, with different patterns between HICs and LMICs. Given the identified deficiencies in the evaluation of associations between comorbidities and health outcomes in patients with IPF, further studies are warranted to better inform targeted screening and management, whilst avoiding unnecessary investigations and treatments. Advances in our understanding of the clinical relevance of comorbidities in IPF will support clinicians in optimising patient care.

Gravity-sensing device for measuring bending angles in digestive endoscopes: Design and clinical evaluation
Authors: Ming-yan Luo, Li-qiu Liu, Zhao-xia Lun, Chao-yuan Huang, Xu-chao Chen, Yuan Huang, Zhao-li Fu, Chang-kun Zhou, Hua Yang, Wang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We developed a novel, gravity-sensing device to measure bending angles of digestive endoscopes, addressing inefficiency and inaccuracy of traditional planar measurement cards. This single-operator tool utilizes a freely rolling ball bearing within a calibrated ring to quantify angles. A multicenter instrument comparison demonstrated that the device significantly improved measurement efficiency and consistency while reducing associated maintenance costs, offering a practical solution for endoscopy unit quality control.

Introduction

Precise control of the bending section is crucial in complex endoscopic procedures such as endoscopic mucosal resection and endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) 1 . However, mechanical wear from prolonged use can lead to angular deviations, compromising procedural success 2 . Studies indicate that a significant proportion of endoscope malfunctions originate from such inaccuracies 3 . The widely used angle measurement card requires dual-operator collaboration and is prone to visual interpretation errors and inefficient. To overcome these limitations, we designed a novel angle measurement device based on a gravity-sensing principle, enabling fast, accurate, and single-operator angle assessment.

Device description and technical innovation

The core of the device is a ring-shaped cavity formed by high-transparency polycarbonate housings. A calibrated dial fixed inside the front housing features precision markings from 0 to 360 degrees, color-coded into zones indicating endoscope status (e.g., green for normal, red for maintenance required). A stainless-steel gravity ball rolls freely within this sealed cavity. An integrated bubble level in the rear housing ensures horizontal baseline calibration. A silicone-padded clamping fixture with a positioning ruler allows for secure and rapid attachment to various endoscope tips ( Fig. 1 ). Measurement principle

When the endoscope bends, the attached device rotates. The gravity ball rolls under its own weight to the lowest point, its position directly corresponding to the bending angle on the calibrated dial, with an estimated error range of ± 1.5 degrees.

Key innovations

The single-operator workflow eliminates the need for a second nurse to hold and read a card, streamlining the process.

With dual-system calibration, the bubble level ensures baseline accuracy, while the gravity ball automatically compensates for minor deviations.

The color-coded zones in the status alert system provide an immediate, intuitive visual assessment of endoscope angular performance.

The enclosed system and free-rolling mechanism reduce human reading errors associated with traditional cards, providing enhanced accuracy.

Clinical application and evaluation

This was a pragmatic, multicenter, randomized controlled instrument comparison study. The sample size of 60 endoscopes encompassed all available eligible instruments across three high-volume centers during the study period, aiming for a real-world preliminary evaluation. Co-primary endpoints were measurement time and inter-operator concordance rate (defined as a difference of ≤ 5 degrees between independent measurements by two nurses, a clinically acceptable threshold). Maintenance costs were analyzed as an exploratory economic outcome.

A multicenter randomized controlled instrument comparison was conducted over 6 months, involving 60 gastroscopes and colonoscopes. These were randomized to measurement with either the novel device (experimental group) or the traditional angle card (control group, Fig. 3 ). All participating nurses received standardized training. Endoscopes were balanced regarding models and standard angulation ranges ( Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Table 2 ) and were rotated among centers to mitigate differential wear.

The traditional angle measurement card.

The experimental group utilized a single-operator procedure: the endoscope tip was secured in the clamp, the bubble level was centered, the angle knob was adjusted, and the stabilized gravity ball value was recorded. The control group followed the standard dual-operator procedure with the measurement card.

The novel device significantly reduced measurement time and improved inter-operator consistency compared to the traditional card ( Table 1 ). Cost-effectiveness

Use of the novel device was associated with a substantial reduction in maintenance costs. During the 6-month study period, total costs were significantly lower in the experimental group ( Table 2 ; P < 0.001, Mann-Whitney U test). This cost advantage was maintained during the 6-month follow-up period, with costs remaining substantially lower in the experimental group ( Table 2 ).

Discussion

This study validates clinical utility of a novel gravity-sensing angle measurement device. It demonstrated superior operational efficiency, higher measurement consistency, and significant cost savings compared with the conventional method. The device design effectively addresses key shortcomings of the traditional card by simplifying workflow and improving accuracy. Although the absolute time saving per measurement (~34 seconds) may seem modest, the transition to a single-operator workflow eliminates coordinator dependency, reduces labor costs, and streamlines the quality control process in a busy endoscopy unit. The observed association between device use and lower maintenance costs suggests that more accurate and consistent measurements may lead to more precise maintenance decisions (e.g., reducing unnecessary repairs). However, a causal relationship cannot be definitively established from this study design.

Conclusions

Although future iterations could benefit from automation, such as integrating image recognition for automated reading, the current device provides a reliable, practical, and cost-effective tool for proactive endoscope performance monitoring and quality management in digestive endoscopy units.

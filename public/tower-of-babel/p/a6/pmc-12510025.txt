Genotype-matched recombiant inactivated Newcastle disease virus vaccine confer protection against genotype Ⅻ challenge in geese with maternal antibodies
Authors: Jinlian Ren, Yichun Chen, Rimei Yao, Lijin Lai, Xiaotong Yan, Hongtao Xiao, Qiuyan Lin, Tao Ren, Libin Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe majority of Newcastle disease virus (NDV) strains circulating in China, which exhibit a tendency toward increased virulence, are from genotype Ⅻ infections obtained from waterfowl. Based on our previous findings, the genotype Ⅻ strain E115 was non-pathogenic in geese but induced notable clinical symptoms in chickens without mortality. However, in recent years, we isolated the genotype Ⅻ strain E117 from dead geese and confirmed its lethality in geese through animal regression tests. Genotype-matched vaccination has been proposed as a potential solution. In this study, two NDV vaccination strains, mE115 and mE117, were rescued and evaluated for their resistance to wild-type virus attacks. In vivo analysis revealed that compared with that of the commercial vaccine group, the mE117, and mE115 immune groups exhibited higher antibody levels. The results of the immune protection test showed that the mE117 and mE115 vaccines prevented geese from dying when exposed to the E117 challenge. In contrast, the commercial vaccine group exhibited a survival rate of 62.5%. Furthermore, viral shedding drastically decreased to 0% in the mE117 and mE115 immunised groups, whereas virus shedding was observed in the visceral organs, oropharynx, or cloaca in the commercial vaccine group. Compared with that of the commercial vaccine, the two vaccine candidates developed in this study provided complete protection for geese against genotype Ⅻ challenges. These findings provide a foundation for the development of more effective vaccines to control Newcastle disease.Keywords: Newcastle disease, Genotype Ⅻ, Reverse genetics, Immunization, Vaccine efficacy

Introduction

Newcastle disease (ND) is a highly virulent infectious disease caused by the Newcastle disease virus (NDV) that affects avian species and leads to substantial financial losses in the global poultry industry. NDV belongs to the family Paramyxoviridae and the genus Orthoavulavirus (Dimitrov et al., 2019). Its genome comprises six transcriptional units (3′-NP-P-M-F-HN-L-5′), which include instructions for making six proteins: nucleocapsid (NP), matrix protein (M), fusion (F), haemagglutinin-neuraminidase (HN), large polymerase (L), and phosphoprotein (P). The NDV genome is directly correlated with certain sequence patterns in the F-glycoprotein cleavage domain associated with virulence attenuation (Peeters et al., 1999; de Leeuw et al., 2003; Panda et al., 2004). The cleavage site of the attenuated strain is 112G/E-K/R-Q-G/E-R↓F117, whereas the virulent strain is 112R/K-R-Q-R/K-R↓F117.

The earliest reports of genotype Ⅻ NDVs originated from Peru and South America. Since 2004, NDV has caused numerous epidemics in chicken flocks (Diel et al., 2012; Chumbe et al., 2017). At least three genotypes of Newcastle disease virus have been identified within genotype Ⅻ: Ⅻa strains, which have been detected exclusively in Peru and Colombia (Diel et al., 2012; Chumbe et al., 2015; Chumbe et al., 2017), ⅫⅫb strains, which have been found only in Guangdong Province, China (Liu et al., 2013; Xiang et al., 2020), and Ⅻd strains, which was first described in Vietnam (Le et al., 2018) . Both the intracerebral pathogenicity index and the F protein cleavage site show that these strains are extremely virulent. To date, genotype Ⅻa remains the only strain of NDV identified in Peru, posing a significant threat to chicken populations (Chumbe et al., 2017). The frequent outbreaks reported each year, despite extensive immunisation campaigns, may be attributed to antigenic mismatch between vaccine strains and circulating field strains. In Guangdong Province, China, genotype Ⅻ NDV was successfully isolated from geese between 2010 and 2011. A phylogenetic evolutionary study classified isolates from China as subtype Ⅻb and those from South America as subtype Ⅻa. Our previous study showed that the isolated genotype Ⅻ NDV was non-lethal to geese (Xiang et al., 2020). During our investigation, genotype Ⅻ NDV isolated from dead geese in Guangdong Province was discovered, suggesting that certain strains could infect geese and cause illness or death. The emergence of these NDV genotypes indicates that the pathogenicity of the virus may increase alongside its prevalence in guinea fowl flocks in Guangdong. Recently, goose flocks in Guangdong Province have shown a higher prevalence of these new NDV genotype strains, potentially indicating an increase in virulence. China has effectively controlled the spread of Newcastle disease in poultry; however, disease prevention and management in waterfowl populations remain insufficient and require more attention. Genotype Ⅻ NDVs are exhibiting an expanding transmission range among goose populations. This epidemiological trend requires intensive efforts to contain the avian-origin NDVs specific to geese. Surveillance data showing the expanding spread of these viruses in geese underscores the need to strengthen biosecurity protocols in China, particularly through targeted waterfowl vaccination programs. A wealth of existing research strongly indicates that vaccines genotypically matched to the prevailing NDV genotypes are more effective at preventing viral shedding and controlling ND (Miller et al., 2007; Dimitrov et al., 2017; Qiao et al., 2021). The inadequate coverage of genotype Ⅻ NDV vaccines has prompted the development of reverse genetic technology. Using this technology, we rationally attenuated the E115 and E117 chimeric variants by targeting their virulence-associated F protein cleavage sites. In this study, we evaluated the biological characteristics and immune efficacy of recombinant E115 and E117 strains, comparing them with the currently used genotype VII ND vaccine in geese. The attenuated recombinant viruses, mE115 and mE117, exhibited high proliferation efficiency and provided complete immune protection in geese, indicating their potential as vaccine candidates.

Materials and methods

Ethics statements

All protocols related to animal use were performed under the guidelines of the South China Agricultural University Experimental Animal Welfare Ethics Committee (permit number:2024A021). All animal experiments were performed in the authorized animal biosafety level 3 (ABSL 3) facilities at South China Agricultural University.

Virus, vaccines, cells and animals

Genotype Ⅻ NDV strain E115 used in this experiment were isolated and stored by the Department of Animal Infectious Diseases, College of Veterinary Medicine SCAU (Guangzhou, China). The cloning vector TVT 7R (0.0) was a gift from Hongzhuan Wu (Alabama State University). The modified vaccinia virus Ankara expressing T7 polrmerase (MVA/T7) and baby hamster kidney fibroblast cells constitutively expressing T7 RNA polymerase (BSR T7/5 cells) were generously given by Xusheng Qiu (Shanghai Veterinary Research Institute, CAAS).

Specific pathogen-free (SPF) chicken embryos and SPF chickens used in the experiment were provided by Dahuanong Biotechnology Co., LTD (Guangdong). The one-day-old non-immunized geese were provided by Guiliu Poultry Co., LTD (Foshan).

Virus isolation and sequence analysis

A cloacal swab from a deceased goose in a breeding farm of Guangdong, China was collected, preserved in Phosphate buffered saline (PBS) with antibiotics (penicillin and amphotericin B, 4,000 U/ml; streptomycin, 2 mg/ml), and inoculated into 9-day-old SPF embryonated chicken eggs. After 72 hours of incubation at 37°C, the allantoic fluid was harvested and tested via haemagglutination assay (HA) and haemagglutination-inhibition assay (HI). RNA was extracted from the allantoic fluid utilizing a Takara RNA extraction kit, and cDNA was synthesized using Invitrogen Reverse Transcriptase III. The full genome was amplified by PCR and sequenced using Sanger sequencing for result validation, as previously described (Xiang et al., 2017).

Pathogenicity in geese

One-day-old goslings (n=10) were intramuscularly infected with 106 50% egg infectious dose (EID50) to verify the pathogenicity of E117 in geese, whereas a control group was given PBS. Every day, clinical signs were observed and noted. At 3 days post-inoculation (dpi), goslings were euthanized and necropsied. Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was used to examine viral replication in the brain, proventriculus, lungs, kidneys, spleen, duodenum, and other organs (Fan et al., 2024).

Constructions of plasmids and rescue

The recombinant viruses were generated by co-transfecting BSR-T7/5 cells with the reconstructed genomic plasmid and helper plasmids encoding NP, P, and L.

The complete NDV genome was cloned into the TVT vector to create a full-length plasmid. Total RNA was extracted by using total RNA extraction kit(Feijie, Shanghai, China). Complementary cDNA was synthesized using the PrimerScriptTM Reverse Transcriptase (Takara, Beijing, China). To obtain the full genome of strains, cDNA was used as a templete for PCR amplification with primers (Supplemental Table S1). To reduce the pathogenicity, the full-length plasmids mE115 and mE117, which contain the mutated F gene, were constructed by incorporating these modifications during plasmid assembly. During amplification, modifications were incorporated into the genomes of strains E117 and E115 through homologous recombination (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The F protein cleavage site was altered to match the amino acids (112GRQGRL117) from the LaSota strain, resulting in the generation of recombinant viruses named mE117 and mE115. Molecular markers were created using synonymous mutations: (i) a C-to-G substitution at position 5,725 (c.5725C>G) in mE115; and (ii) an A-to-T transversion at nucleotide 5,246 (c.5246A>T) in the L gene of mE117, which introduced a unique NruI site (TCGCGA). Viral-specific primers (E117-1F/E117-1R, E117-2F/E117-2R, E117-3F/E117-3R) were utilized to amplify three overlapping PCR fragments. Each fragment was then individually cloned into the TVT vector (T-7F/T-7R) to obtain the full-length plasmid T-E117. Subsequently, site-directed mutagenesis was performed using primers mE117-f/mE117-r to generate the modified plasmid T-mE117. Similarly, plasmid T-mE115 was constructed. As shown in Supplemental Figure 1, homologous recombination was employed to link genome to the vector. Illumina MiSeq confirmed all genomic modifications.

The recombinant viruses were generated by co-transfecting BSR-T7/5 cells with the reconstructed genomic plasmid and helper plasmids encoding NP, P, and L. The recovered viruses, named mE117 and mE115, were propagated in SPF embryonated chicken eggs through three successive passages.

The helper plasmids (pCI-NP, pCI-P, and pCI-L) were constructed and stored by our laboratory (Sun et al., 2017). For transfection, helper plasmids along with the viral genome plasmids were introduced into BSR-T7 cells cultured in 6-well plates at approximately 80% confluency. Following transfection, the cells were incubated for an additional three days after the addition of TPCK-trypsin to a final concentration of 1 μg/mL the next day. The clarified supernatant was then used to inoculate 9-day-old SPF embryonated chicken eggs, which were incubated for four days. The harvested allantoic fluid was aliquoted and stored at -80°C. Initial confirmation of viral recovery was achieved through the HA assay.

Viral growth kinetics

Chicken embryo fibroblast (CEF) cells were infected with the virus at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01 and incubated at 37°C for one hour to assess viral growth kinetics. Following incubation, the cells were washed twice with PBS, and the viral inoculum was aspirated. Fresh DMEM, supplemented with 1 μg/mL TPCK-trypsin and 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS), was added to the medium. Supernatants were collected at 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 and 72 hours post-infection (hpi). The quantity of virus in the supernatants capable of infecting 50% of tissue cultures was determined using the Reed-Muench method.

Vaccine preparation

The virus was propagated in 9-day-old SPF chicken eggs. Viral particles were completely inactivated by treatment with 0.1% formaldehyde at 37°C for 24 hours, with continuous shaking on a rotary shaker at 200 rpm. Three consecutive blind passages in embryonated eggs confirmed the complete loss of infectivity. The inactivated vaccine was then formulated by emulsifying the inactivated virus with Marcol 52, a white oil adjuvant.

Viral loads and virus shedding

According to a previous study, three geese from each group were randomly selected, euthanized, and necropsied at 3 days post-challenge (dpc). Tissue samples were collected from the trachea, spleen, lungs, duodenum, brain, glandular stomach, and kidneys for nucleic acid extraction. qPCR was subsequently employed to quantify the viral load in the tissues (Kong et al., 2021).

Data analysis and processing

GraphPad Prism 9.0 software was used for data analysis. ANOVA followed by Tukey's post-hoc test was employed to assess variations in viral excretion among vaccination cohorts. Significant differences are denoted by *(P<0.05), ** (P<0.01), ***(P<0.001), ****(P<0.0001).

Immunization and challenge experiments

Groups of ten 2-week-old geese were intramuscularly injected with either a commercial oil-adjuvanted genotype VII vaccine, mE115, mE117, or 200 μL of PBS (control). Weekly blood samples were collected post-vaccination to measure hemagglutination inhibition (HI) antibody levels against the E117 strain.

At 21 days post-immunization, the geese were challenged intramuscularly with 10⁶ EID₅₀ of the E117 strain. ND clinical symptoms were monitored daily following the challenge. Oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs were collected at 3, 5, 7, and 9 dpc for virus isolation. To assess viral distribution in tissues including the trachea, liver, lung, glandular stomach, brain, and duodenum, geese exhibiting severe symptoms (depression, twisted necks, ataxia, or tremors) or mortality were euthanised.

Results

E117 Strain was assigned to subgenotype Ⅻb

The NDV strain E117 was isolated from deceased goslings in 2022 and designated as Goose/CH/GD/E117/2022. Genomic analysis revealed that E117 carries the fusion protein cleavage motif 112RRQKRF117, a molecular marker indicative of highly pathogenic NDV strains in avian hosts. Phylogenetic reconstruction based on the complete F gene (1662 bp) further classified E117 as genotype Ⅻb. Notably, South American and Vietnamese strains were categorised as genotype Ⅻa. E117, along with other Chinese isolates, clustered exclusively within the Ⅻb subclade (Fig. 1), suggesting possible regional evolution of this genotype.

Phylogenetic analysis. All Newcastle disease virus fusion gene sequences were downloaded from the Genbank database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank). Phylogenetic analysis was performed using Phylosuite. Sequence comparison and trimming were performed with MAFFT v7.505 and trimAl v1.2rev57, respectively. The best-fitting model (GTR+F+I+G4) was selected using ModelFinder v2.2.0 based on the Bayesian Information Criterion. Maximum likelihood phylogenies were inferred using IQ-TREE v2.2.0 under the GTR+F+I+G4 model with 1000 standard bootstraps. Phylogenetic trees were visualised using iTOL (https://itol.embl.de).

Pathogenicity in goslings

To assess the pathogenicity of the E117 strain, 10 immunologically naïve goslings (2-weeks old) were intramuscularly infected with 10⁶ EID₅₀ of the E117 virus. Clinical signs and mortality were monitored daily throughout the trial. Following inoculation, the infected goslings exhibited progressive lethargy, recumbency, and anorexia, along with neurological symptoms such as torticollis, generalised tremors, and hindlimb splaying, resulting in 100% mortality by 7 days post-inoculation. Histopathological examination revealed systemic haemorrhage as the major contributor to acute mortality (Fig. 2). Viral loads in various organs were quantified using qPCR. As shown in Fig. 3, the virus was detected in the trachea, liver, lungs, glandular stomach, brain, and duodenum, with notably higher viral loads in the brain, duodenum, and lungs. No virus was detected in the kidneys or spleen.

Microscopic examination revealed congestion and haemorrhage in the lungs and kidneys (100×, objective magnification). Serological responses post-immunisation

A cohort of 32 geese with maternally derived antibody titres of ≤4 log2 was randomly divided into four equal groups (n=8 per group). Three experimental groups received 0.2 mL subcutaneous injections of the mE117, mE115, or a commercial ND vaccine, while the fourth group served as an unvaccinated control. Serum samples were collected weekly from the vaccinated geese. As shown in Fig. 6 A, the mE117 and mE115 vaccinated groups achieved protective haemagglutination inhibition titres (≥7 log2) by 21 days post-initial immunisation. Fourteen days post-infection (dpi), the mE117 group demonstrated superior immunogenicity, with a mean antibody titre of 5.4 log2, significantly higher than the 3.0 log2 observed in the mE115 group. This indicated that the inactivated mE117 vaccine may induce antibody production in geese more rapidly than that of the inactivated mE115 vaccine. Over time, mE117 vaccination elicited significantly higher neutralising antibody levels (8.6 ± 1.07 log2) than vaccination using mE115 (6.38 ± 1.41 log2), highlighting its immunogenicity advantage (mE117 > mE115 > commercial vaccine). The commercial vaccine group showed no substantial increase in titres during the monitoring period, while maternal antibodies in the control group declined to undetectable levels (≤2 log2) by day 21.

Virus shedding and viral loads

At 21 days post-immunisation, all groups were challenged intramuscularly with 10⁶ EID₅₀/0.2 mL of the virulent E117 strain. Clinical signs, including activity levels, feeding behaviour, neurological symptoms, and mortality, were monitored daily for 10 days. As shown in Fig. 6 B&C, the control geese began exhibiting severe symptoms at 3 days post-challenge, characterised by escalating lethargy, anorexia, and neurological signs such as torticollis, tremors, and hindlimb paralysis. These symptoms ultimately led to 100% mortality by 7 days post-challenge, along with significant weight loss. The commercially vaccinated group exhibited a delayed onset of disease, with one goose showing symptoms at 3 days post-challenge and a mortality rate of 37.5% (3/8) at the conclusion of the study (Fig. 6 B). Conversely, the mE115- and mE117-vaccinated groups remained clinically normal throughout the observation period, indicating complete protection against the lethal challenge, with 0% morbidity and mortality.

To comprehensively assess vaccine-induced protection against viral shedding, oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs were collected from challenged geese at 3, 5, 7, and 9 dpc. Swab specimens were suspended in PBS containing penicillin-streptomycin (10,000 U/mL each) and subsequently inoculated into 9-day-old SPF embryonated chicken eggs (0.2 mL per egg). The presence of virus in the allantoic fluid, collected between 24 and 96 h post-inoculation, was assessed using a haemagglutination assay with 1% chicken erythrocytes. No live virus was detected in the mE115 or mE117 vaccinated group throughout the observation period (Table 2). Conversely, the commercially vaccinated group exhibited temporary oropharyngeal shedding at 3 dpc, with a positivity rate of 62.5% (5/8). The control group showed continuous viral excretion, with 100% (7/7) oropharyngeal positivity at 3 dpc. No viral shedding was detected in the mE115 and mE117 groups. These results highlight the superior protective efficacy of the mE115 and mE117 vaccinations in completely preventing viral transmission compared with that of the commercial vaccine.

Discussion

Genotype Ⅻ NDV is the predominant epidemic strain affecting poultry animals in South America and Southeast Asia and is characterised by a high pathogenic potential. To date, all Chinese genotype Ⅻ NDV isolates belong to genotype Ⅻb, exhibiting significant genetic and antigenic differences compared with those of Ⅻa and Ⅻd (Chumbe et al., 2017; Le et al., 2018; Xiang et al., 2020).Genotype VII vaccines are vital for preventing NDV infections in Asian ducks; however, concerns have been raised about their cross-protective efficacy owing to the recent isolation of antigenically distinct Ⅻb sub-genotype viruses in Guangdong Province. Rare ongoing outbreaks caused by genotype Ⅻ NDVs highlight their capacity to evade immunity conferred by traditional vaccinations, particularly those based on genotype VII strains. In a previous study, we identified the genotype Ⅻb strain E115 in geese from live poultry markets. The MDT, ICPI, and F protein cleavage site patterns collectively indicated that E115 is a virulent NDV strain, consistent with the World Organisation for Animal Health criteria. Epidemiological tracking has shown effective transmission of the Ⅻb strain among goose flocks without causing clustered mortality, suggesting a unique equilibrium between viral fitness and host adaptation in waterfowl populations (Xiang et al., 2020).

Although the LaSota vaccine is also widely used in China at present, multiple studies have shown that its protection rate against prevalent strains is relatively low (Izquierdo-Lara et al., 2019; Hossain et al., 2023). After the promotion of the VII-based vaccine in China, the transmission of NDV among chicken farms has been basically eliminated, indicating that this vaccine is currently more suitable for China's poultry industry. However, in clinical practice, highly pathogenic ND can still break out in pigeons and geese after immunization with the VII-based vaccine. Therefore, the use of VII-based as the control group instead of LaSota in this study better reflects the insufficiency of current commercial vaccines in providing protection against ND in geese. In the animal challenge assays of this study, the newly identified E117 strain exhibited increased virulence, causing 100% mortality in the infected poultry groups. Phylogenetic analyses revealed a significant genetic divergence between genotype Ⅻ NDV and commonly used genotype VII-based vaccine strains, resulting in a substantial antigenic mismatch. These dual challenges of immune evasion and rising pathogenicity present significant obstacles to current poultry health management systems. Consequently, updating vaccination and disease control measures is urgently needed.

Some genotype-matched vaccine strains have been attenuated using reverse genetics (Bello et al., 2020; Ruan et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020; Xiao et al., 2022). To develop genotype-matched vaccines targeting prevalent NDV variants, we used a reverse genetics approach to genetically modify the F protein cleavage sites of E115 and mE117 by replacing their polybasic motifs with attenuated equivalents. The recombinant viruses mE115 and mE117 showed markedly reduced virulence profiles via reverse genetics, with ICPI scores of 0.288 and 0.03, and MDTs of 118 and 117 h, respectively. These findings are consistent with the biological criteria for lentogenic NDV classification. The biological evaluation showed that the mE117 recombinant strain achieved a haemagglutinin titre of 10 log2 in chicken embryo allantoic fluid, which is tenfold higher than that of mE115. Therefore, mE117 showed superior viral output, which is expected to lower production costs.

Considering that the attenuated NDV vaccine may cause immune interference with other vaccines and the possible risk of virulence reversal, inactivated vaccines were used in this study. Immune protection experiments revealed that all vaccinated groups exhibited increased antibody titres following immunisation, with the mE117 group exhibiting the most significant increase. At 21 days post-vaccination, the mE117 group had a mean HI titre of 8.6±1.07 log2, surpassing the mE115 group's titre of 6.3±1.41 log2. This difference likely reflects the strain-specific antigenic characteristics of mE117. When tested against the E117 antigen, the commercial vaccine group showed minimal antibody increase, indicating limited cross-reactivity between the vaccine-induced antibodies and genotype Ⅻ viral epitopes. In subsequent virulent challenge trials, geese vaccinated with mE117/mE115 showed complete clinical protection with a 100% survival rate and no viral shedding. In contrast, the commercial vaccine group experienced a 37.5% mortality rate and persistent oropharyngeal shedding. Geese immunised for 21 days until they reached 5 weeks of age showed significantly enhanced innate immune resistance. Consistent with previous studies, higher HI antibody titres were associated with protective efficacy (Yang et al., 2017; Ferreira et al., 2021).

Under the same EID50 conditions, the mE115 and mE117 vaccines provided complete protection to geese, highlighting their advantages in production and immunisation efficacy. When SPF geese are absent, the maternal antibody elimination period ranges from 2–4 weeks. Maternal-derived antibodies are crucial for protecting poultry chicks from clinical diseases. NDV-specific maternal antibodies can affect vaccine effectiveness (Hu et al., 2020). Immunisation studies commenced only after blood samples confirmed that maternal antibody levels had declined to the minimum criteria. Geese with maternal antibody titres of ≤4 log2 at 2 weeks of age were selected for the study; however, a few goslings with titres approaching 5 log2 were randomly assigned to each group. Notably, despite elevated maternal antibody levels, the mE115 and mE117 vaccines provided complete protection in some geese, demonstrating their ability to overcome the interference of maternal antibodies. We propose that this result can be attributed to the high antigenic match between the vaccine and the target strain. Our previous study indicated that the nucleotide similarity between genotype VII strains and genotype Ⅻb strains was only 87.6%, and the antigenic correlation (R-value) is 0.813, which is insufficient to induce cross-neutralizing antibodies against Ⅻb strains (Xiang et al., 2020). Therefore, the antibodies produced by commercial vaccines target specific epitopes of genotype VII strains that are different from genotype Ⅻb strains, making them ineffective in blocking viral fusion or entering host cells. Antibodies from the genotype Ⅻ NDV vaccine effectively bind to the sites of the genotype Ⅻ NDV, preventing it from entering and replicating, which is the foundation for achieving 100% protection.

In addition to the cleavage site, mutations in other regions of the NDV-F protein have also been shown to affect viral replication and pathogenicity (Samal et al., 2012; Samal et al., 2013; Ji et al., 2017). In our previous study, we observed that unlike other genotype Ⅻ NDV strains from Peru and Vietnam, the F protein of E115 harbours N240S and T496I substitutions. Unlike the other strains that caused chicken mortality, E115 did not (Xiang et al., 2020). The recently isolated E117 virus also harbours these substitutions; however, despite sharing these residues, E117 and E115 differ in pathogenicity. Previous studies show that mutations in other NDV proteins (e.g., NP, HN, and L) can enhance viral pathogenicity (Yu et al., 2017; Yan et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021; Lu et al., 2022; Peng et al., 2022). Additionally, hens infected with E115 exhibit higher viral replication in visceral organs than geese do, with minimal viral loads detected in the brain (Xiang et al., 2020). In this study, we observed high viral titres in the brains of infected geese, with the brain showing the highest replication levels among all visceral organs. Further research using reverse genetics techniques is necessary to explore the specific residues and mechanisms driving the increased virulence from the E115 to the E117 genotype Ⅻ NDV.

Live-bird markets and backyard poultry production throughout Asia, Southeast Asia, and Africa have created major opportunities for NDV transmission. Migratory birds like geese and herons can carry virulent strains during seasonal migration, contaminating water sources and feeds through faecal excretion, thus introducing the virus into domestic poultry environments (Rahman et al., 2018). Wild animals infected with NDV often exhibit asymptomatic or subclinical symptoms. Backyard farms, often situated near wetlands or forests, share drinking and foraging resources with wild birds, increasing direct interspecific interactions (Pauly et al., 2019). Some regions have implemented mandatory vaccination programs; however, effective ND management is significantly hindered by limited grassroots veterinary services coverage and farmers' lack of awareness about the risks posed by asymptomatic infections. Genotype Ⅻ NDV is transmitted efficiently through direct contact among chicken populations and between geese and chickens, including bidirectional transmission from geese to chickens. These transmission mechanisms promote viral persistence, enhance geographic spread, and create opportunities for rapid viral evolution (Xiang et al., 2020). Therefore, mE117 and mE115 may serve as superior options for developing a subunit vaccine against genotype Ⅻ NDV.

Conclusion

In summary, we developed two potential genotype Ⅻ NDV vaccines (mE115 and mE117) using reverse genetics. Both vaccines elicited elevated antibody titres, provided complete protection in geese, and markedly reduced shedding of genotype Ⅻ NDV. Furthermore, comparative analyses with commonly used commercial vaccines showed that mE115 and mE117 exhibited superior replication efficiency and effectively overcame maternal antibody interference, eliciting robust immune responses. These findings suggest that mE115 and mE117 are promising candidate strains for NDV vaccination of waterfowl.

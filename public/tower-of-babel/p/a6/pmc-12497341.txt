LncPRESS1 promotes the progression and cisplatin resistance of breast cancer through upregulation of ENO1 and activation of glycolysis
Authors: Longzhu Ke, Wei Gao, Yuanxiu Leng, Jie Chen, Hong Chen, Shangshang Li, Yingwen Zhang, Li Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOverexpression or downregulation of long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) plays an essential role in the progression and chemotherapy-resistant of breast cancer (BRCA). LncPRESS1 is a lncRNA regulated by p53, suggesting its potential in participating in carcinogenesis. However, few evidences reported the role of LncPRESS1 in tumor development.MethodsThe clinical significance of LncPRESS1 was explored in BRCA and normal samples derived from the patients. Overexpression and knockdown of LncPRESS1 were performed by lentivirus. CCK-8 and colony formation were conducted to examined the proliferation ability of BRCA cells. Apoptosis was measured by PI/Annexin V staining and the activity of caspase 3/7. Xenografted tumorigenesis in nude mice was applied to explore the role of LncPRESS1 on BRCA progression and cisplatin response. RT-qPCR was used to detect the mRNA level, while immunoblotting was applied to check the protein level.ResultsIn this study, we identified that LncPRESS1 was significantly upregulated in BRCA tissues as compared with normal tissues, and in the cancer tissues with late stage or node metastasis. LncPRESS1 abundance was not changed among the patients based on the expression of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, or epidermal growth factor 2. In vitro, LncPRESS1 suppressed the apoptosis, while enhanced the proliferation and cisplatin resistance in BRCA cells. Upregulation of LncPRESS1 was also observed in the patients who did not response to cisplatin treatment. In vivo, knockdown of LncPRESS1 suppressed the tumorigenesis of BRCA cells and enhanced the effectiveness of cisplatin. Mechanistically, LncPRESS1 activated glycolysis via upregulation of ENO1. Overexpression of LncPRESS1 dictated the sensitivity of BRCA cells to ENO1 inhibitor, ENOblock. Importantly, treatment of ENOblock reduced the resistance of cisplatin in BRCA cells with overexpression of LncPRESS1.ConclusionIn summary, we demonstrated that LncPRESS1 facilitated BRCA progression and cisplatin resistance via activation of ENO1-mediated glycolysis. Combination of ENO1 inhibitor and cisplatin was a promising therapeutic stragtegy for BRCA patients with overexpression of LncPRESS1.Keywords: LncPRESS1, Breast cancer, Cisplatin, Glycolysis, ENO1

Introduction

Breast cancer (BRCA) has surpassed lung cancer to become the most common cancer globally [1]. In China, there were approximately 416,000 new BRCA cases each year [2]. BRCA is classified into three types based on the expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), or epidermal growth factor 2 (HER2): ER+/HER2−, HER2+, and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). ER+/HER2 − breast cancer is the most common, accounting for 70%, while HER2 + and TNBC each account for 15%. TNBC is characterized by the absence of ER expression, PR expression, and overexpression or gene amplification of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) [3]. During the past decades, numbers of therapies have developed for the treatment of cancer patients. The overall survival of the patients was improved due to the great progress on the development and management of surgery, chemotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immune therapy [4]. Unfortunately, there is still a number of patients, especially the advanced ones, who have limited treatment options. Therefore, exploring the mechanisms contributing to the development of BRCA will be helpful to the patients.

Long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) refers to a class of RNA molecules that are over 200 nucleotides in length and lack protein-coding function [5]. Their biological functions are involved in regulating the expression of coding genes, RNA stability, and the assembly of protein complexes at multiple levels and stages of life activities [6]. In recent years, the role of lncRNA in tumorigenesis has received increasing attention [7]. Abnormal expression of lncRNA can disrupt the balance between cell proliferation and apoptosis, causing continuous cell division, and leading to tumor occurrence [8]. Additionally, increasing studies reported that lncRNA regulated the sensitivity of cisplatin in triple-negative breast cancer [8–10]. Exploring the essential lncRNAs triggering the progression and chemotherapy resistance is important for us to understand the mechanisms of breast cancer development.

In this study, we investigated the role of LncPRESS1, which is regulated by P53 [11], in the development and cisplatin response of BRCA. The clinical relevance of LncPRESS1 was evaluated in based on the samples from the BRCA patients. The function and mechanisms were examined by constructing LncPRESS1 overexpression and knockdown cell lines. We demonstrated that LncPRESS1 upregulation contributed to the accelerated progression and reduced cisplatin effectiveness in BRCA by activation of glycolysis.

Results

LncPRESS1 is upregulated in BRCA patients

Firstly, we evaluated the clinical relevance of LncPRESS1 in BRCA. The normal and cancer tissues were collected from the BRCA patients and subjected to RT-qPCR analysis of LncPRESS1. The results showed that LncPRESS1 was significantly upregulated in the cancer tissues as compared with their adjacent and the normal tissues (Fig. 1A and B). Then we explored whether LncPRESS1 levels were correlated with the clinicopathological characteristics of the patients. The levels of LncPRESS1 were increased in the patients at stage III/IV as compared with those at stage I/II (Fig. 1C). The patients with lymphatic metastasis had higher LncPRESS1 levels than those without lymphatic metastasis (Fig. 1D). These results suggested that LncPRESS1 upregulation was correlated with the progression of BRCA. In addition, we found that LncPRESS1 abundance was not significantly downregulated or upregulated among the patients of luminal, HER2 positive and triple negative (Fig. 1E). To analyze whether LncPRESS1 affected the sensitivity of cisplatin, we divided the BRCA patients into non-responder and responder group. LncPRESS1 level was reduced in the responder group as compared with non-responder group (Fig. 1F). Lastly, we showed that LncPRESS1 was upregulated in BRCA cells, including MCF-7, MDA-MB-468, HCC1937 and MDA-MB-231, as compared with MCF 10 A cells (Fig. 1G). Taken together, LncPRESS1 overexpression is associated with the progression of BRCA.

LncPRESS1 is critical for the proliferation ability of BRCA cells

Next, we investigated the function of LncPRESS1 in BRCA cells. As MDA-MB-231 cells had higher LncPRESS1 level comparing with other cell lines, we knocked down LncPRESS1 in MDA-MB-231 cells. RT-qPCR results demonstrated the knockdown efficacy of LncPRESS1 (Fig. 2A). CCK8 and colony formation results demonstrated that depletion of LncPRESS1 suppressed the growth of BRCA cells (Fig. 2B and D). By contrast, we overexpressed LncPRESS1 in MCF-7 cells since the cells had relatively lower expression of LncPRESS1. RT-qPCR results confirmed that LncPRESS1 was successfully overexpressed in MCF-7 cells (Fig. 2E). In addition, overexpression of LncPRESS1 enhanced the proliferation ability of MCF-7 cells (Fig. 2F and H). These results validate that LncPRESS1 plays an oncogenic role in BRCA.

LncPRESS1 suppresses the apoptosis of BRCA cells

To explore the influence of LncPRESS1 on apoptosis of BRCA cells, we subjected the cells to Annexin V and PI staining. Flow cytometry analysis showed that knockdown of LncPRESS1 increased the apoptotic cells in MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 3A). Oppositely, overexpression of LncPRESS1 inhibited the apoptosis of MCF-7 cells (Fig. 3B). The quantification results were presented in Fig. 3C. Moreover, knockdown and overexpression of LncPRESS1 increased and reduced the activity of caspase 3 to caspase 7 in BRCA cells (Fig. 3D). Collectively, LncPRESS1 suppresses the apoptosis in BRCA cells.

Overexpression of LncPRESS1 confers the BRCA cells to cisplatin treatment in vitro and in vivo

As the expression of LncPRESS1 was correlated with the response of cisplatin in BRCA patients, we investigated whether knockdown and overexpression of LncPRESS1 influenced the sensitivity of cisplatin. The results showed that downregulation of LncPRESS1 increased the sensitivity of cisplatin in MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 4A). By contrast, overexpression of LncPRESS1 reduced the cytotoxicity of cisplatin in MCF-7 cells (Fig. 4B). To study the in vivo function of LncPRESS1, we implanted shCtrl and shLncPRESS1 MDA-MB-231 cells into the nude mice. We found that knockdown of LncPRESS1 suppressed the tumorigenesis of MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 4C and D). Furthermore, downregulation of LncPRESS1 dramatically enhanced the effectiveness of cisplatin in vivo (Fig. 4C and E). These results indicate that depletion of LncPRESS1 not only blocks the tumorigenic ability of BRCA cells, but also promotes cisplatin effectiveness in vivo.

LncPRESS1 upregulates ENO1 and enhances Glycolysis in BRCA cells

Next, we studied the mechanisms underlying how LncPRESS1 contributed to BRCA progression. Since color of culture medium turned yellow faster in BRCA cells when overexpressing LncPRESS1, we predicted that LncPRESS1 might regulate glycolysis. As expected, the glucose consumption and lactate production were reduced after knocking down LncPRESS1 in MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 5A). On the contrary, overexpression of LncPRESS1 enhanced the glucose consumption and lactate production in MCF-7 cells (Fig. 5B). The mRNA and protein levels of ENO1 were positively regulated by LncPRESS1 (Fig. 5C and D). Moreover, ENO1 was upregulated in BRCA tissues (Fig. 5E). There was a positive correlation between LncPRESS1 and ENO1 in BRCA patients (Fig. 5F). Furthermore, the expression of ENO1 was reduced in non-responder group as compared with responder group (Fig. 5G). Collectively, LncPRESS1 upregulates ENO1 and activates glycolysis in BRCA.

ENOblock and cisplatin synergistically suppresses the growth of BRCA cells with overexpression of LncPRESS1

Lastly, we explored the effect of ENO1 inhibitor, ENOblock, on the sensitivity of cisplatin in BRCA cells. Initially, we found that overexpression of LncPRESS1 promoted the cytotoxicity of ENOblock, while knockdown of LncPRESS1 suppressed the sensitivity of ENOblock in BRCA cells (Fig. 6A and B). Since overexpression of LncPRESS1 resulted in lower response of cisplatin, we treated LncPRESS1 overexpressing cells with ENOblock and cisplatin. We found that combination of ENOblock and cisplatin dramatically inhibited the growth of MCF-7 cells (Fig. 6C), indicating the synergistical effect of ENOblock and cisplatin. The apoptosis and caspase 3/7 activity were significantly enhanced after the treatment of ENOblock and cisplatin (Fig. 6D and F). Thus, the resistance of cisplatin in LncPRESS1 overexpressing BRCA cells can be overcome by the treatment of ENOblock.

Discussion

Long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) are a class of RNA molecules that are larger than 200 nucleotides in length [5]. LncRNAs can not only affect the expression or stability of coding RNA, acting as a precursor or adsorption molecule of miRNA, but also influence translation and stability of proteins [12, 13]. They can specifically regulate the expression of individual genes [14] or broadly control chromosomal regions. Furthermore, lncRNA can interact with enzymes that modify epigenetic marks, recruit these modification factors to specific DNA sites, and thereby affect DNA methylation or chromatin state, regulating gene silencing or activation [15].

Numerous studies have shown that dysregulation of lncRNAs contributes to the development of cancers by controlling the expression of oncogenes, tumor suppressor, regulation of cancer stem cell, immune escape and metabolism [7, 16–18]. For instance, overexpression of LncRNA FOXD2-AS1 facilitates the progression of oral squamous-cell carcinoma through activation of PLOD1/Akt/mTOR pathway via repression of MiR-185-5p [19]. Targeting and engineering lncRNAs have become the promising strategies for the treatment of cancers [20], such as gastric cancer [21] and lung cancer [22]. In addition, overexpression or downregulation of lncRNAs also play a pivotal role in the development of BRCA. For example, GAS5 lncRNA is a biomarker in BRCA [23]. Upregulation of DNA damage inducible transcript 4 antisense RNA1 (DDIT4-AS1) facilitates the progression of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) via autophagy [24]. LINC00115 overexpression enhanced the stemness ability of BRCA by potentiation of SETDB1/PLK3/HIF1α signaling [25]. LncRNA HAGLROS stimulated the evolution of BRCA via modulating miR-135b-3p/COL10A1 and polarization of M2 macrophage [26].

Tumor protein p53 (TP53 or P53) is the most common mutated tumor suppressor gene in human cancers. Dysregulation of P53 triggers the development and progression of cancers by modulating cancer cell survival, proliferation, stemness, apoptosis, ferroptosis, metabolism and immune escape [27–30]. There were numbers of proteins or lncRNAs participating in P53-driven tumor progression. For instance, downregulation of sodium voltage-gated channel beta subunit 3 (SCN3B) conferred the poor prognosis in glioma patients when P53 was wide-type but not mutated [31, 32], suggesting that the function of SCN3B was depending on the status of P53. Based on RNA-seq and ChIP-seq, Abhinav et al. identified that LncPRESS1 was a P53 regulated lncRNA and played an essential role in the pluripotency human embryonic stem cells [11]. However, the significance of LncPRESS1 in BRCA remains unclear. In this study, we found that LncPRESS1 was increased in BRCA tissues. The patients at late stage or with lymphatic metastasis had upregulated LncPRESS1. Based on the in vitro assays which were widely applied to examine the proliferation or death of cancer cells [33], we demonstrated that overexpression of LncPRESS1 inhibited the apoptosis and facilitated the proliferation of BRCA cells. By contrast, knockdown of LncPRESS1 increased the apoptosis and suppressed the growth of BRCA cells. In vivo, knockdown of LncPRESS1 inhibited the tumor development of BRCA cells in nude mice. These results demonstrated the tumor promoting function of LncPRESS1 in BRCA.

Abnormal expression of lncRNAs regulates the sensitivity of chemotherapies in breast cancer [34]. For instance, knockdown of DDIT4-AS1 enhanced the response of TNBC to the treatment of paclitaxel [24]. Upregulation of LINC00115 conferred the resistance of BRCA cells to paclitaxel [25]. Silencing of lncRNA SNHG15 significantly enhanced the sensitivity of cisplatin in BRCA [35]. LncRNA HULC modulation of IGF1R-PI3K-AKT axis conferred the resistance of cisplatin in BRCA [36]. These studies demonstrated the important function of lncRNAs in regulating chemotherapy response. Here, we showed that LncPRESS1 expression was reduced in BRCA patients who showed response to cisplatin. In vitro and in vivo results confirmed that overexpression of LncPRESS1 reduced, while knockdown of LncPRESS1 enhanced the cytotoxicity of cisplatin in BRCA cells. Previous studies reported that patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) model were beneficial to drug screening for BRCA patients [37–39]. In our study, we applied cell-derived xenografts (CDXs) model to reveal the effect of LncPRESS1 on the response of cisplatin in BRCA. We will construct PDXs model to further validate whether dysregulation of LncPRESS1 dictates the sensitivity of cisplatin in BRCA in the follow up study.

In normal cells, glucose is catabolized into pyruvate, which then enters the tricarboxylic acid cycle for oxidative phosphorylation or is converted into lactic acid under hypoxic conditions. In tumor cells, aerobic glycolysis is preferentially selected regardless of oxygen concentration, suggesting that aerobic glycolysis is an important condition for the malignant growth of tumors [40]. Aerobic glycolysis also participates in regulating the sensitivity and resistance to radiotherapy or chemotherapy in cancer patients [41, 42]. In this study, we found that glucose consumption and lactate production, the readout of glycolysis, were positively regulated by LncPRESS1. The glycolytic enzyme, ENO1 [43], was downregulated and upregulated after knocking down and overexpressing LncPRESS1, respectively. Ectopic expression of LncPRESS1 dictated the sensitivity of BRCA cells to ENO1 inhibitor, ENOblock. Importantly, ENOblock treatment overcame the resistance of LncPRESS1 overexpressing BRCA cells to cisplatin, suggesting that potentiation of glycolysis contributes to the malignant growth and cisplatin resistance of BRCA cells with overexpression of LncPRESS1.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrated that LncPRESS1 was an important tumor-promoting lncRNA in BRCA. LncPRESS1 facilitated BRCA progression and cisplatin resistance via activating ENO1-mediated glycolysis. Combination of ENO1 inhibitor and cisplatin synergistically blunts the growth of BRCA cells with overexpression of LncPRESS1. Our study highlights the important function of LncPRESS1 in BRCA progression. ENOblock is a promising drug for the treatment of BRCA patients who had high expression of LncPRESS1 and were resistant to cisplatin.

Materials and methods

BRCA patients

The normal and cancer tissues were collected from the BRCA patients who underwent surgery before any other interventions at GuiHang Guiyang Hospital. The clinicopathologic analysis of the BRCA patients were conducted by three independent pathologists. An informed consent was signed by each patient. The study was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of GuiHang Guiyang Hospital (GHGYYY-KXXM-2024-20).

Cell culture

MCF 10 A (human normal mammary epithelial cells), MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468 and HCC1937 (human BRCA cells) were purchased from Procell (Wuhan, China). These cells were cultured according to the protocols recommended by the manufacturers. MCF 10 A: DMEM/F12 + 5% HS + 20 ng/mL EGF + 0.5 µg/mL Hydrocortisone + 10 µg/mL Insulin + 1% NEAA + 1% P/S; MCF-7: MEM (NEAA) + 10 µg/mL Insulin + 10% FBS + 1% P/S; MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468: Leibovitz’s L-15 + 10% FBS + 1% P/S, HCC1937: RPMI-1640 + 10% FBS + 1% P/S. The cells were grown in a 37 °C cell incubator.

Overexpression or knockdown of LncPRESS1

The lentivirus of LncPRESS1 overexpression and knockdown was packaged in 293T cells. BRCA cells were infected with the lentivirus and stable cell lines were selected by puromycin. The targeting sequences of knockdown were shLncPRESS1#1: 5’-AACAGAGAATTGAGCAGAGAA-3’, shLncPRESS1#2: 5’-AAAGCAAGGAAATGTGACGCC-3’ and shCtrl: 5’-CCTAAGGTTAAGTCGCCCTCG-3’.

Immunoblotting

The proteins were extracted from BRCA cells by using RIPA (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Protein concentrations were measured by BCA kit and equal amount of the proteins were loaded on SDS-PAGE gels. Subsequent procedures were performed according to the protocols as described previously [44]. Primary antibodies of ENO1 (11204-1-AP) and GAPDH (10494-1-AP) were purchased from Proteintech. HRP-conjugated Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG(H + L) (SA00001-2) was from Proteintech.

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

RNAs were extracted from the tissues and cells by using Trizol (Invitrogen). Reverse transcription was conducted by M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Promega). SYBR green qPCR super mix (TransGen Biotech) was used for qRT-PCR reactions: 37˚C (30 sec), 1 cycle; 94˚C (5 sec) and 60˚C (30 sec), 40 cycles; 94˚C (15 sec), 60˚C (30 sec) and 94˚C (15 sec), 1 cycle. The primer sequences used for qPCR were: LncPRESS1, forward 5’-CAGTAATTCTCCAGCAACAG-3’, reverse 5’-TGGCAGGTAATCATCTCATAT-3’; ENO1, forward 5’-TGGTGTCTATCGAAGATCCCTT-3’, reverse 5’-CCTTGGCGATCCTCTTTGG-3’; GAPDH, forward, 5’-TGACTTCAACAGCGACACCCA-3’, reverse, 5’-CACCCTGTTGCTGTAGCCAAA-3. The expression of LncPRESS1 and ENO1 was normalized to GAPDH.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK8) assay

The viability of BRCA cells were detected by CCK8 assay, according to the manufactures’ instructions. In brief, a total of 3000–5000 BRCA cells were seeded in triplicate in 96-well plates, treated with or without cisplatin (Selleck) and ENOblock (Selleck). Cell viability was detected by incubating the cells with CCK8 for 3 h. Then OD450 was detected on microplate reader. Cell proliferation was calculated with the following formula: OD450 of indicated time/mean OD450 of day 1 × 100%.

Colony formation

A total of 1000 shCtrl, shLncPRESS1#1 and shLncPRESS1#2 MDA-MB-231 cells, a total of 500 Ctrl and LncPRESS1 overexpressing MCF-7 cells were seeded in triplicate in 6-well plates. Seven days later, the colonies were formed and were washed by PBS for three times. Then, they were fixed with methanol for 30 min and stained by crystal violet for 20 min.

Cell apoptosis and detection of caspase 3/7

The apoptosis of BRCA cells were detected by PI/Annexin V-FITC assay kit, according to the protocols as described previously [44]. The apoptosis was analyzed on Beckman flow cytometer. The activity of caspase 3 to 7 was measured by using the assay kit from Promega. The procedures were briefly described as follow: (1) Seeding 10,000 cells in 96-well plates; (2) Adding 100 µL Caspase-Glo in each well; (3) Slowly shaking and incubating at room temperature for 2 h; (4) Measuring the activity on microplate reader.

Glucose consumption and lactate production

A total of 2 × 105 cells were seeded in triplicate in 12-well plates. Twenty-four hours later, the medium was harvested for the detection of glucose and lactate by using the kit from Abcam, following the manufacturers’ instructions. Relative glucose consumption and lactate production were normalized to cell number, which was analyzed when examining the abundance of glucose and lactate in culture medium.

Animal experiment

Animal experiments were conducted on BALB/c nude mice (female, aged 4–6 weeks), which was obtained from Charles River. Six mice were included in each group. A total of 5 × 106 shCtrl and shLncPRESS1 MDA-MB-231 cells were subcutaneously implanted into the right arm of the BALB/c nude mice. Two weeks after the implantation, the mice were administrated with cisplatin (2 mg/kg) once a week. Another 4 weeks later, the mice were subjected to anesthetization by inhalation of 3% isoflurane. When the mice were under deep anesthesia, they were humanely sacrificed by cervical dislocation. Immediately, the tumors were collected and weighted. The experiments were approved by the Ethical Committee for Animal Experimentation of GuiHang Guiyang Hospital (GHGYYY-KXXM-2024-29) and following the provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Statistical analysis

The statistical data (mean ± standard error of mean (SEM)) were analyzed on GrpahPad Prism 8 software. Student’s t test was applied to analyze the difference between two groups. One-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test, was applied to compare the difference when more than two groups. Statistical difference was significant when p < 0.05.

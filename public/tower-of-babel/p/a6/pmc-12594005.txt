Enhanced Water Splitting Using a Densely Structured Dendritic Bismuthinite Photoanode with FeOOH Cocatalyst
Authors: Magzhan Amze, Vladislav Kudryashov, Yerbolat Magazov, Asset Aliyev, Nurxat Nuraje
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Solar water splitting is a promising method for sustainable hydrogen production, but current systems suffer from efficiency limitations. Bismuth sulfide (Bi2S3), a semiconductor with desirable optical properties and a suitable band gap for water oxidation, has drawn considerable interest for its potential. Despite various approaches, including organic- and inorganic-based heterostructures and plasmonic enhancement, the efficiency of Bi2S3-based photoelectrodes remains below the theoretical maximum, primarily due to severe charge recombination and sluggish surface reactions. This study addresses these issues through morphology engineering and surface functionalization. A densely packed dendritic Bi2S3 photoanode, obtained from an electrochemically deposited bismuth metal sacrificial structure, exhibited enhanced light absorption, while decoration with an ultrathin layer of an oxyhydroxide cocatalyst further contributed to efficiency by improving surface reaction kinetics. After these modifications, the photocurrent of the Bi2S3/FeOOH photoanode increased 1.7 times from 2.2 mA·cm–2 (1.23 V vs RHE) to 3.7 mA·cm–2 compared to the pristine Bi2S3. The solar energy conversion efficiency showed a 3-fold increase at low bias potentials, and its photon-to-current conversion efficiency improved 1.5-fold (32–48% @ 600 nm). This work underscores the importance of the morphology and surface modifications in advancing the efficiency of photoanodes for solar water splitting.

Introduction

Solar water splitting utilizes abundant and available sunlight energy to produce green and high-energy-density hydrogen fuel. Semiconductors were first demonstrated to split water under light irradiation in an electrochemical cell by Honda and Fujishima in 1972. Since then, many photoanode and photocathode materials have been developed. However, even after half a century of efforts, photoelectrochemical water splitting systems lack the performance to turn them into a commercially viable option for energy production. In light of this, developing novel materials and morphologies with outstanding solar-to-chemical energy conversion characteristics has become one of the holy grails of materials science.

Photoelectrochemical (PEC) water splitting consists of three main processes determining the overall performance of photoelectrodes. First, light is absorbed to generate electron–hole pairs, followed by further charge separation and transfer in the semiconductor. Finally, redox reactions occur on the surface of the photoelectrode–electrolyte interface. Researchers have investigated many strategies to overcome inefficiencies in all steps to improve both the hydrogen and oxygen evolution reactions. For instance, a widely studied BiVO4 photoanode suffers from limited charge separation and oxygen evolution kinetics, which results in a recombination of photogenerated carriers and corrosion of the photoelectrode. To address this, cocatalyst integration is a promising solution to enhance the electron–hole separation and transport in this photoanode. In addition to the enhanced charge transfer kinetics, the cocatalyst may further passivate the surface of BiVO4 by lowering the oxygen evolution reaction overpotential and decreasing the photocorrosion. Another effective approach to improve the performance of photoelectrodes for PEC water splitting is the construction of heterojunction electrodes, where two complementary semiconductors are coupled to broaden the absorption spectrum and enhance the photogenerated carriers’ separation. These methods to enhance the charge separation and transfer through structural control and cocatalyst integration are also crucial for narrow band gap semiconductors, like Bi2S3.

Bismuth sulfide (Bi2S3) is a semiconductor material with high optical absorption (104–105 cm–1), low toxicity, and a narrow band gap (1.3–1.7 eV) suitable for solar water oxidation. , Recently, several research groups have reported the water photooxidation performance of different Bi2S3-based systems: among them are inorganic semiconductor heterojunctions (Bi2S3/Co2O3, Bi2S3/In2S3), organic–inorganic heterostructures (Bi2S3/PANI, r-GO/Bi2S3), and plasmonic particle decorated ones (Bi2S3/Au, Bi2S3/Ag). − Despite these attempts, the efficiency of the obtained systems is far below the theoretical maximum of the Bi2S3 material (about 30 mA·cm–2). In the literature, the highest photocurrent density for the system without a sacrificial agent in a neutral medium (Bi2S3/Co2O3) is only 7.0 mA·cm–2. The reported best stand-alone Bi2S3 has a photocurrent density of 2.4 mA·cm–2 without any material modification. The low efficiency is explained by the short minority carrier diffusion length and severe surface recombination. , This paper addresses these two issues in light of morphology engineering and surface functionalization.

The light absorption of the thin film photoelectrode is dependent on its thickness. The thicker the electrode, the higher the absorption. However, with the increase in the thickness, the charge recombination increases because the excitons generated further from the surface at a distance greater than the diffusion length tend to recombine rather than reach the surface and participate in the reaction. Different morphologies of photoelectrodes for water splitting, like nanorod, nanotube, nanosheet arrays, nanonets, and inverse opal, were previously prepared to enhance the light absorption and active area without compromising the charge separation. − The dendritic structure is another morphology that provides the same advantage. − In this work, dendritic Bi2S3 photoanodes were synthesized by using electrochemically deposited dendritic Bi metal as a sacrificial structure.

Another effective strategy to enhance the performance of photoelectrode materials is the decoration with cocatalysts (electrocatalysts), as mentioned above. The main idea is to integrate electrocatalysts tested for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) or oxygen evolution reaction (OER) with the photoelectrodes, with the band alignment suitable to energize them. Cocatalysts improve the efficiency of key reactions like the OER in solar water splitting by exhibiting better surface kinetics and lower overpotential than the photoelectrode material itself, resulting in faster charge transfer and also reducing the surface recombination by passivation. Notable examples of successful integrations include OER cocatalysts like cobalt phosphate (Co-Pi), nickel oxyhydroxide (NiOOH), and FeOOH. − ,− In the case of the Bi2S3 photoanode, the cocatalyst decoration has not been extensively studied. In this work, an ultrathin FeOOH layer was deposited on the surface of the Bi2S3 photoanode, which increased the water oxidation performance of the photoelectrode. After coverage improvement and surface modification, the photocurrent of the photoanode increased from 2.2 mA·cm–2 (1.23 V vs RHE) to 3.7 mA·cm–2 compared to the pristine Bi2S3, and its photon-to-current conversion efficiency improved from 32 to 48% (at 600 nm). The combination of morphology selection and surface functionalization approaches reveals the potential of Bi2S3 and presents it as a good candidate for a water oxidation photoelectrode.

Experimental Section

Synthesis of Bi2S3 Dendritic Structure

FTO substrate was cleaned in 2% Hellmanex solution, water, acetone, methanol, and isopropanol by ultrasonication for 15 min each. 20 mM of bismuth­(III) nitrate pentahydrate (Bi­(NO3)3·5H2O) was dissolved in ethylene glycol. Reverse pulse electrodeposition was used to synthesize the Bi metal sacrificial structure. The three-electrode cell was constructed with FTO, Ag/AgCl (3 M KCl) electrode, Pt wire as working, reference, and counter electrodes, respectively, and bismuth­(III) nitrate solution as an electrolyte. A varied number (100, 300, 600, 900, 1400, and 1800) of 2 s of −1.8 V and 4 s of 0.1 V reverse pulse cycles were applied to deposit Bi metal on FTO. Next, the obtained thin films were annealed in a muffle furnace at 550 °C (5 °C/min) for 1 h in air. The black Bi films turned to yellowish Bi2O3 films after annealing. Finally, the obtained oxide films were sulfurized in a tube furnace in an Ar atmosphere. In one section of the tube, an elemental S was placed as a sulfur source and heated up to 150 °C, while in the neighboring section, the samples were heated up to 400 °C (5 °C/min) for 1 h. This gives the dendritic structure of Bi2S3. The coverage of FTO was poor; there were large areas of uncovered FTO. To address this issue, a second Bi metal electrodeposition was performed on the as-prepared FTO/Bi2O3 film. All of the parameters of electrodeposition, annealing, and sulfurization were the same.

FeOOH Cocatalyst Decoration

The as-prepared Bi2S3 electrodes were immersed in ethanol for 1 min to remove the entrapped gases and to make the substrate more hydrophilic. Subsequently, the sample was immersed in FeCl3·6H2O (10 mM, 40 mL) solution for 5 min. 2 M NaOH solution was then added dropwise to adjust the pH of the iron chloride solution to alkalescence (∼8) by gently stirring (200 rpm) for 7 min. After that, the solution remained there for 50 min. Finally, the electrodes were washed with deionized water and blow-dried to obtain the FeOOH cocatalyst-decorated Bi2S3 samples.

Structural Characterization

The thin films were examined using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, ZEISS Crossbeam 540) and a transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEOL JEM 1400 Plus). An X-ray diffractometer (XRD, SmartLab (Rigaku)) and an X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS, NEXSA (Thermo Scientific)) were used for the crystal structure study and elemental analysis, respectively. UV–vis was taken with PerkinElmer Lambda 1050 equipment.

Photoelectrochemical Tests

The photoelectrochemical measurements were conducted using a PalmSens4 Potentiostat in an ordinary three-electrode cell configuration using the samples as the photoanode, a Pt coil as a counter electrode, and a Ag/AgCl electrode as the reference electrode. The water splitting current was measured with 1 M Na2SO4 (pH ≈ 7) aqueous solutions at the scan rate of 2 mV·s–1. The cell was illuminated with simulated AM 1.5 light by a Newport TLS260–300X xenon lamp solar simulator, and the light power density was adjusted by a silicon solar cell to 100 mW·cm–2. The potentials in the collected data were converted to the RHE by the equation ERHE=EAg/AgCl+0.197V+0.059×pH To calculate the solar energy conversion efficiency (η), the following equation was used η=J×(1.23−V)/P where V is the applied potential (vs RHE), J is the current (mA·cm2) under AM 1.5G irradiation, and P is the irradiance of the AM 1.5G (100 mW·cm2).

The incident photon-to-current efficiency (IPCE) was measured under monochromatic light by using the wavelength filter of the solar simulator. The power of the incident light was measured by the photometer. IPCE was calculated using the following equation IPCE=(1240×i)/Pλ×λ

where 1240 (V nm) is the multiplication of the Planck constant (h) and the speed of light (c), i (mA·cm–2) is the photocurrent density, P λ (mW·cm–2) is the power of the monochromatic light, and λ (nm) is the wavelength of the monochromatic light. GC test was conducted with GAS CompactGC4.0. ηsep and ηinj were calculated by the following equations ηsep=JNa2SO3/Jmax ηinj=JNa2SO4/JNa2SO3 where J Na2SO3 is the photocurrent density in 0.5 M Na2SO3 electrolyte, J Na2SO4 is the photocurrent density in 0.5 M Na2SO4 electrolyte, and J max is the maximum water oxidation photocurrent corresponding to the absorption property of the photoanode (∼32 mA·cm–2)

Results and Discussion

Electrochemically deposited dendritic Bi metal was used as a sacrificial structure (Figure a). The deposition was conducted by successively applying −1.8 and +0.1 V pulses for 2 and 4 s, respectively. This technique is known as pulse reverse electrochemical deposition and is directed to improve the morphology of the deposited thin film. −1.8 V potential is applied to deposit Bi metal on the surface, whereas +0.1 V potential is applied to remove the loosely bound high-energy deposits. Bimetal thin film electrodeposition was performed in an ethylene glycol solution. Ethylene glycol can readily dissolve the salts of bismuth, which otherwise require a reduction of pH to make aqueous solutions. With the increase in the number of cycles of such pulses, the height of the dendritic structures increases, which in turn increases the thickness of the film (Figure S1). Electrodeposition of Bi metal generally results in dendritic structures. − However, the structure of the Bi film deposited by pulse reverse electroplating technique differs from the commonly obtained dendritic structures in the literature and results in much thicker, fern-like morphology when converted to the final product (Figure b), in contrast to thin and net-like structures deposited by applying the same potential directly.

(b) SEM image of dendritic Bi2S3 on FTO prepared by two electrodepositions. (c) TEM image indicating the ultrathin FeOOH cocatalyst layer on Bi2S3.

The obtained dendritic Bi thin film can be annealed in air to prepare dendritic Bi2O3 and, later, sulfurized to Bi2S3 (Figure S2). However, the final product will have areas with an exposed conductive substrate surface. To address this issue, it was decided to perform the second electrodeposition of Bi metal on the Bi2O3/FTO substrate, applying the same procedure. This resulted in an improvement in the coverage (Figure S3). Finally, FeOOH cocatalyst was deposited on the surface of the Bi2S3 photoanode by a facile chemical bath method. This resulted in an ultrathin 1–2 nm amorphous FeOOH cocatalyst (Figure c).

The crystal structure of Bi2S3 was confirmed by XRD analysis. As shown in Figure a, all major peaks of the sample are consistent with the PDF No.: 01-079-9789 reference. The other peaks were attributed to the FTO substrate. One of the major crystal structures corresponding to the (202) orientation (22.36°) is identified in the TEM image (Figure b). The Raman spectroscopy result also indicates the formation of Bi2S3 on FTO (Figure S4).

XPS characterization was carried out to investigate the surface states of Bi2S3. The Bi 4f and S 2p peaks of pristine Bi2S3 are shown in Figure b. The two strong peaks located at 158.8 and 164.1 eV are assigned to Bi 4f7/2 and Bi 4f5/2, respectively, which match with the standard Bi3+ peaks in Bi2S3. The small peaks at 161.5 and 162.9 eV correspond to S2– (S 2p3/2 and S 2p1/2, respectively). Additionally, the Fe 2p and O 1s spectra for Bi2S3/FeOOH are collected. As illustrated in Figure c, the peaks associated with Fe 2p1/2 and Fe 2p3/2 are located at 725.3 and 711.4 eV, respectively. The other additional peaks are attributed to Fe3+ satellites. O 1s spectra in Figure d show that the major peaks are located at 530.1, 531.5, and 532.5 eV and correspond to Fe–O–Fe, Fe–O–H, and H–O–H bonds, respectively (Figure d). Therefore, the XPS results further confirm the formation of an ultrathin layer of FeOOH on the surface of Bi2S3.

The photoelectrochemical (PEC) performances of the samples were characterized by using a three-electrode cell illuminated with an AM 1.5G solar simulator. First, the optimal number of cycles of pulses was determined to be 900 cycles (Figure S5). It was found that the electrodes with improved coverage with the second electrodeposition have higher photocurrent, especially at lower applied voltages, compared with the ones with poor coverage (Figure S6a). The effect of the improved coverage may lead to two advantageous aspects. First, it increases the mass of active material per area, which leads to an increase in absorption and photocurrent per area. UV–vis spectroscopy analysis of the samples with poor coverage and good coverage shows that the light absorption has increased after coverage improvement (Figure S6b). Second, it prevents the back reaction on the surface of the photoanode. The exposed conductive substrate is found to negatively affect the efficiency due to the back reduction of photo-oxidized products on its surface. Others hypothesize that it may lead to a shunt in the charge transport, which bypasses the photoactive layer. The Tauc plot of obtained Bi2S3 shows that its band gap is about 1.24 eV (Figure S6b inset).

The photocurrent-applied potential curves for Bi2S3 and FeOOH-modified Bi2S3 are shown in Figure a. At 1.23 vs RHE under the simulated solar AM 1.5G illumination, the photocurrent of Bi2S3/FeOOH (3.7 mA·cm–2) is approximately 1.7 times higher than that of pristine Bi2S3 (2.2 mA·cm–2). In addition, the onset potential of Bi2S3/FeOOH is negatively shifted compared to the pristine one, which indicates that the introduction of the FeOOH ultrathin layer cocatalyst on the photoelectrode surface decreases the energy barrier for charge transfer across the electrode–electrolyte interface and ensures a more efficient transfer of holes in the PEC water oxidation.

The stability of the Bi2S3 and Bi2S3/FeOOH photoanode was tested via a chopped chronoamperometry test under simulated solar irradiation. The results show that similar to other chalcogenide electrodes, Bi2S3 suffers from photocorrosion (Figure b) in both pristine and surface-modified cases. XRD and XPS analyses of the samples after the stability test were conducted to identify the corrosion path and product. XRD results show that a strong peak at about 44° increases, while the peaks related to Bi2S3 decrease. The XRD peak of the corroded sample was attributed to NaBiS2 as a possible corrosion product (Figure S7a). XPS analysis at Bi 4f and S 2p demonstrates the shift of the main peaks and rise of new, smaller peaks in place of peaks for Bi 4f7/2, Bi 4f5/2, S 2p3/2, and S 2p1/2 (Figure S7b). In addition, a peak attributed to Na 1s was identified at 1071.6 eV (Figure S7c). Therefore, it may be assumed that during the photocorrosion of Bi2S3, Bi is reduced from +3 to +1 in NaBiS2. GC experiment with Bi2S3/FeOOH conducted at 1.1 V vs RHE with a 0.5 M Na2SO3 sacrificial agent further indicates that the photoanode suffers severe photodegradation (Figure S8). Even with the sacrificial agent, the amount of hydrogen produced is two times lower than the amount of hydrogen calculated by Faraday’s law from the charge passed through the photoelectrode surface. It means that Bi2S3, similar to other chalcogenides, undergoes corrosion in aqueous solution under oxygen-generation conditions. In this work, the FeOOH layer is not a perfectly continuous, impermeable film compared to the films synthesized by other methods. It is related to large dendritic structures of pristine Bi2S3, so that Bi2S3 is exposed to the electrolyte, where degradation is still occurring. To stabilize the FeOOH-modified Bi2S3 photoanode, additional strategies to protect the electrode should be considered.

The incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurements further confirm the favorable effect of the surface modification: the IPCE of the Bi2S3/FeOOH at 1.23 V vs RHE at 420 nm is about 50% (Figure c), which is about 1.6 times higher than that of Bi2S3 (∼30%). The solar energy conversion efficiencies of Bi2S3 and FeOOH-modified Bi2S3 were calculated from the corresponding current–potential curves. The advantage of decoration with FeOOH cocatalyst becomes evident at lower potentials like 0.6 V, where the solar energy conversion efficiency is three times higher than that of the bare Bi2S3. At higher applied bias potentials, the difference becomes less prominent, though the cocatalyst-decorated sample shows higher efficiency throughout the range of applied bias potential (Figure d).

Further photoelectrochemical tests were conducted in 0.5 M Na2SO4 and 0.5 M Na2SO3 electrolytes to study the charge behavior in the bulk and on the surface (Figure S9). Na2SO3 acts as a hole scavenger and allows for studying the performance without surface kinetics limitations. The charge separation efficiency increased significantly after decorating the surface with FeOOH (Figure a). It suggests that FeOOH affects the surface charge recombination either by increasing the charge consumption on the surface (and not allowing it to accumulate and recombine on the surface) or creating a favorable band alignment, or both. On the other hand, the charge injection efficiency of pristine Bi2S3 is massively higher than that of Bi2S3/FeOOH. The possible explanation for this observation might be that FeOOH decoration of the surface can suppress the corrosion reaction and promote the water oxidation reaction, whereas on pristine Bi2S3, the corrosion and water oxidation reactions may compete and contribute to the higher charge injection efficiency. These observations indicate that the addition of FeOOH cocatalyst increases the photocurrent of the electrode via better charge utilization. It means that after photogeneration of charge carriers, each electron–hole pair reaching the photoanode–electrolyte interface is more likely to drive the OER rather than recombine. Considering the use of FeOOH cocatalyst with a widely studied BiVO4 photoanode, it significantly enhances surface hole kinetics and suppresses recombination. Several studies showed that BiVO4 photoanode suffers from poor hole utilization and stability, but after coupling it with a FeOOH OER cocatalyst, the IPCE value increases 5 times, reaching 96% conversion of charge carriers to O2. , Analogically, the integration of FeOOH cocatalyst on synthesized Bi2S3 has a similar purpose of lowering the surface reaction barriers and scavenging holes for water oxidation, which explains the improvement of photocurrent density and IPCE values in this work.

To contrast the performance of the Bi2S3/FeOOH photoanode with that of other Bi2S3-based photoelectrodes, Table was constructed by compiling recent studies. The main emphasis of comparison was drawn on the main performance metrics: photocurrent density, IPCE, and the amount of hydrogen produced in 1 h. Regarding the photocurrent density, in this work, the synthesized Bi2S3/FeOOH photoanode delivers 3.7 mA·cm–2 at 1.23 V vs RHE, which is higher than or comparable to the performance of other Bi2S3-based photoelectrodes under similar conditions. For example, Subramanyam et al. reported a Bi2S3/rGO electrode showing 2.91 mA·cm–2, while their pristine Bi2S3 delivered 1.49 mA·cm–2 at 1.23 V vs RHE, which is 1.5 times less than the photocurrent achieved by the bare Bi2S3 photoanode developed in this work. There are other studies reporting higher photocurrent density values for Bi2S3-based photoanodes, but these studies either showed low IPCE and H2 yield values or did not show these data at all. For example, the photoanode based on nanostructured Bi2S3–In­(OH) x S y showed the highest photocurrent density of 7.77 mA·cm–2 at 1.23 V vs RHE, but the amount of H2 produced was not reported, whereas the IPCE reached only 4% in the visible light range. In contrast, the reported Bi2S3/FeOOH photoanode achieves 48% IPCE at 600 nm, which is 3 times higher than the IPCE value reported for the Bi2S3/rGO photoelectrode. Although having a lower IPCE value, Subramanyam et al. observed 30 μmol H2 production in 1 h, while most other works on Bi2S3-based photoanodes did not report any H2 generation. Therefore, 2.4 μmol·cm–2 of H2 produced in 1 h by the FeOOH-modified Bi2S3 electrode presents a promising validation of solar water splitting Bi2S3-based photoanodes, which will be further investigated.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study addresses the efficiency challenges of the Bi2S3 photoanode caused by severe recombination and sluggish surface reactions through morphological engineering and surface functionalization. By improving the coverage of the conductive substrate, the system showed enhanced light absorption and reduced recombination, preventing the back reduction of photo-oxidized products. The introduction of an ultrathin FeOOH cocatalyst further boosted photoelectrochemical performance, leading to a 1.6-fold increase in photon-to-current conversion efficiency compared to pristine Bi2S3 and a notable improvement in charge transfer efficiency, as evidenced by the negative shift in onset potential. However, despite these advancements, the stability of the Bi2S3-based photoanode remains a challenge, necessitating future research on protective layers with the appropriate band alignment and interface quality. Overall, this work demonstrates that facile approaches like morphology selection, conductive substrate coverage improvement, and surface functionalization are promising strategies to enhance the efficiency of photoanodes for solar water splitting applications.

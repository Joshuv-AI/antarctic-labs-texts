Catechinic compounds suppress free radicals in camellia oil by donating hydrogen atoms: quantum chemistry calculation combined with EPR detection
Authors: Yue Chen, Yong Zhu, Likang Qin, Xiaoling Yang, Qizhi Long, Bo Zhou, Haiyan Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPhenolic compounds have been serving as primary antioxidants in retarding lipid oxidation, however, the conventional method for screening phenolic candidates is time-consuming and costly. In this study, phenolics were selected in accordance with the bond dissociation energy (BDE) of O−H and the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) of the scavenging reaction via quantum chemistry calculation, the efficiency of selected phenolics was subsequently assessed by free energy barrier (ΔG‡), eventually, the suppression efficiency of the candidates was verified by using electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) determination. Among six catechinic compounds, (−)-gallocatechin gallate (GCG) and (−)-epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) were identified as the most promising candidates, both of which showed potential efficiency for scavenging alkyl peroxyl (•OOR), alkoxyl (•OR), oleic acid (•OA-C11) and linoleic acid (•LA-C11) radicals. In the thermally-induced camellia oil model (180 °C, 20 min), the inhibition rates of EGCG against alkyl (•R), alkoxyl (•OR) and DMPO oxidative free radicals (•X) were 60.06 ± 0.38 %, 59.74 ± 2.57 % and 92.05 ± 1.70 %, respectively, analogously, the efficiencies of GCG scavenging these radicals ranged from 68.40 ± 0.82 % to 94.27 ± 0.33 %. This indicates that the proposed methodology can effectively screen lipid antioxidants, furthermore, both GCG and EGCG are capable of retarding oxidation in camellia oil.Keywords: Free radicals, Catechinic compounds, Gibbs free energy change, Free energy barrier, Inhibitory efficiencies

Introduction

Camellia oil contained abundant unsaturated fatty acids, which were prone to transforming alkyl (•R), alkoxyl (•OR) and alkyl peroxyl (•OOR) radicals under heating conditions, the radicals catalytic oxidation of camellia oil not only led to significant economic losses, but also posed serious health risks (Cao et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2022). Free radicals were characterized as the chemical substance containing at least one unpaired electron, and it is prone to capturing electrons from other molecules (Ratera et al., 2021), substantial evidences had indicated that radicals dysregulation resulted in a variety of pathologies, including cardiovascular disorders and neurodegeneration (Pena-Bautista et al., 2019; Zhong et al., 2019; Gupta et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2022), thus, the inhibition of radicals was of paramount importance in lipid.

Catechinic compounds were primarily derived from tea, fruits and cocoa, and they possessed potent free radicals scavenging properties attributable to their flavonoid skeleton and galloyl moiety (Panche et al., 2016). Extensive studies had confirmed that phenolic compounds could efficiently scavenge radicals, and the number of phenolic hydroxyl groups played a crucial role in their scavenging activity (Jiang et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2024). Recent research had indicated that natural phenolic compounds and their complexes were promising candidates for improving the oxidative stability (Ai et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026). Consequently, the bond dissociation energy (BDE) of phenolic compounds was negatively correlated with their efficiency for scavenging activity, which indicated the required energy for cleaving the phenolic hydroxyl bond (Amić et al., 2020; Erickson et al., 2023; Rohman et al., 2023). The hydrogen atom from phenolic compounds (Ar-OH) neutralized the free radical (R•), which was counteracted to a stable compound (RH), while the phenolics were in reverse transformed into phenoxyl free radical (Ar-O•), which were stable and did not further react or promote oxidation. (Erickson et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024). In the scavenging process, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) could predict the spontaneity of the reaction, in consequence, the ΔG of the reaction was also served as an evaluation indicator for the activity of phenolic compounds (Lin and Zhao, 2021). Moreover, the scavenging reaction did not occur until the energy reached the minimum threshold, and the free energy barrier (ΔG‡) indicated the possibility of a reaction occurring. Innovative strategies that combined theoretical calculations with experimental verification were being increasingly adopted (Zheng et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024). Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) was the spectroscopic technique for direct detecting unpaired electrons (free radicals), moreover, it could simultaneously capture multiple radicals. In the calculation of free radicals, EPR was an advanced calculation method with merits of high sensitivity, high resolution, high stability and the capability for quantitative and qualitative analysis.

To sum up, it was established that a low cost-efficient strategy for selecting antioxidants, which was composed by quantum chemistry calculation coupled with EPR determination. Catechinic compounds were selected by means of quantum chemistry calculation, these were carried out by computing and comparing the BDE values of these catechinic compounds and the ΔG of the according scavenging reaction, and the efficiencies of selected catechinic compounds were subsequently assessed by ΔG‡ of scavenging reaction, eventually, the suppression efficiencies of the candidates in camellia oil were verified by using EPR determination.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and samples

Camellia seeds were donated from Ceheng county, Guizhou, China, then the camellia oil was obtained by appropriate pressing.

(−)-Gallocatechin gallate (GCG) (95%) and (−)-epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) (98%) were procured from J&K Scientific (Beijing, China). 5,5-Dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) was obtained from Aladdin Ltd (Shanghai, China). Other solvents, namely methanol and ethyl acetate, came from Shanghai Yi En Chemical Technology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China), and toluene was provided by Chongqing Chuandong Chemical Co., Ltd (Chongqing, China).

Quantum chemistry calculation

Structure optimization and frequency calculation

The spatial structures of phenolic compounds were constructed by using Gauss View 6.1 (Gaussian, America), subsequently, Gaussian 16 W software (Gaussian, America) was employed to perform structural optimization and frequency calculations for the phenolic compounds to obtain the thermal correction to enthalpy for each compound (Li et al., 2023; Wazzan and Safi, 2023; Zhu et al., 2024).

The B3LYP functional combined with the 6-31+G(d,p) basis set was a well-established and widely accepted protocol in computational studies of phenolics, and the level of theory performed well in balancing computational efficiency and accurately describing the electronic properties of phenolics, and it was particularly outstanding in identifying free radicals scavenging pathways (Alov et al., 2015; Spiegel et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024). Furthermore, the pentyl ethanoate solvent model was selected to simulate the lipid system, and this approach accounted for the dielectric effects of the lipid matrix(Spiegel et al., 2023; Louanas et al., 2025).

Energy calculations at single geometries

Energy calculations of optimized phenolic compounds were implemented by using Gaussian 16W software with the M062X/def2-TZVP basis set, that obtained higher-accuracy electronic energy values (Cao et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024).

Assessment of free radicals scavenging activity: phenolic compounds in thermally-induced camellia oil

Sample preparation methodology and instrumental configuration

GCG and EGCG standards were dissolved in a minimal volume of methanol, diluted to 5 mL with ethyl acetate, and 1 mL of the standard solution was pipetted into 10 g of camellia oil to yield a final concentration of 1.38 mmol/kg referring the National Food Safety Standards of China (NHFPC, 2024), and 10 g camellia oil was served as the blank control group (Zhu et al., 2024).

Electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy (Bruker EMXplus-10/12, Germany) was employed to assess free radicals scavenging activity, with the following instrument parameters: center field at 3355 G, sweep width of 100 G, sweep time of 30 s, number of scans was 3, microwave power was adjusted to 20 mW, and modulation amplitude maintained at 2.0 G (Cui et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2024).

Verification inhibitory efficiencies of GCG and EGCG towards lipid radicals by EPR

The verification of free radical inhibitory efficiency was based on established methods with adjustments (Liu et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2024). Briefly, 400 μL of the lipid sample was mixed with 100 μL of DMPO spin trap (500 mmol/L, dissolved in toluene) in a 2.5 mL centrifuge tube. Subsequently, 100 μL of the mixture was transferred into a 4 mm nuclear magnetic tube, which was inserted into a resonator preheated to 180 °C, and free radical signals were monitored at 10 min, 20 min, 30 min, 40 min and 50 min to obtain the DMPO radical adduct spectra.

The DMPO radical adduct spectra were analyzed by spin fitting using Bruker Xenon software (Bruker, Germany), and free radicals were identified in accordance with their hyperfine splitting constants (HFS), g-factor and quantified by fitting the parameters consisting of line width, line shape and peak area, the inhibitory rate (IR) was calculated using Formula 5.

Statistical analysis

Experiments were conducted in triplicate, and data was reported as mean ± standard deviation. The calculation was carried out using Gaussian 16W, and the results were subjected to Duncan's multiple range test for statistical significance.

Results and discussion

The BDE of phenolic hydroxyl group of phenolic compounds

In bulk oil, phenolic compounds scavenge radicals through donating hydrogen atoms from their hydroxyl group (Zhu et al., 2024), nevertheless, the hydroxyl group at the optimal active site exhibits a greater propensity for H-atom donation compared to other hydroxyl groups. In this process, the BDE, indicating the lowest required energy for donating hydrogen atoms was negatively associated with the efficiency of phenolics (Amić et al., 2020; Rohman et al., 2023). (+)-catechin and (−)-epicatechin had the characteristic flavonoids ring structure, which was composed of two aromatic rings (A and B) joined by an oxygenated heterocyclic ring (C) (Wang et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2024), both of which were identified by disparities in 3-OH site, they shared same phenolic hydroxyl groups which presented in the site of 3-OH, 5-OH, 7-OH, 3′-OH and 4′-OH. The BDE of hydroxyl groups in (+)-catechin ranged from 77.35 to 102.37 kcal/mol, while, it ranged from 78.25 to 104.66 kcal/mol in (−)-epicatechin, and the 4′-OH was the optimal active site for (+)-catechin and (−)-epicatechin. CG is generated as the 3-OH of (+)-catechin linked with gallic acid by esterification reaction, and ECG is synthesized from (−)-epicatechin and gallic acid by esterifying process, both of them possess potential scavenging activity in sites of 5-OH, 7-OH, 3′-OH, 4′-OH, 3″-OH, 4″-OH and 5″-OH, the BDE of hydroxyl groups in CG, ECG were 75.96 to 85.99 kcal/mol and 75.52 to 85.78 kcal/mol. Both CG and ECG, the 4″-OH site was going to present optimal activity, which was higher than that in corresponding parent compounds, this suggested that the galloyl significantly elevated the compounds' activity, this originated from the formation of intramolecular hydrogen bonds between each site of 3″-OH, 4″-OH and 5″-OH, stabilizing the phenoxyl radicals (Olszowy, 2019; Zhu et al., 2024). CG and ECG were going to transform into GCG and EGCG respectively as their 5′-H were substituted by -OH, additionally, both of them shared 8 active sites in 5-OH, 7-OH, 3′-OH, 4′-OH, 5′-OH, 3″-OH, 4″-OH and 5″-OH, they showed the optimal active site in 4′-OH site, of which the BDE presented the bottom-ranked within the group of among the congener ((+)-catechin, (−)-epicatechin, CG, ECG, GCG and EGCG), this indicated that the emerging of 5′-OH reduced the BDE of 4′-OH by generating intramolecular hydrogen bonds between each site of 3′-OH, 4′-OH and 5′-OH (Cuvelier et al., 1992; Olszowy, 2019), meanwhile, this also indicated that the hydroxyl groups on the B-ring exhibited higher reactivity than the substituent group on the C-ring (Zhu et al., 2024). Phenolic compounds scavenge radicals primarily through donating the optimal active site hydrogen atoms from their hydroxyl group, nevertheless, chelation may occur by other phenolic hydroxyl sites. The potential activity of phenolic compounds was negatively associated with BDE, and it was positively associated with the stability of phenoxyl radicals (Zhu et al., 2024). The BDE was associated with the number of phenolic hydroxyl groups, the position of phenolic hydroxyl groups and steric hindrance effects (Bonache et al., 2018; Zuo et al., 2018; Santos et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2023).

The Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) about catechinic compounds scavenging oleic acid radical

Phenolic compound scavenges free radical by donating hydrogen atom (H•), in which the free radical (R•) will be converted into a stable compound (RH), while the phenolic compound is going to deteriorate into a phenoxyl free radical (Ar-O•) (Erickson et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024), as shown in Formula 2, the ΔG was served as the indicator for determining the spontaneity of the reaction (Lin and Zhao, 2021); when ΔG < 0, the forward reaction was spontaneous without external driving force; when ΔG > 0, the reverse reaction was spontaneous with external energy; when ΔG = 0, the system was in equilibrium (Ho and Ofomaja, 2005; Roy et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024), consequently, the ΔG can predict the possibility of reaction occurring. In the present study, the oleic acid radical was regarded as the target on account of its dominant contribution to total fatty acid in camellia oil, and the ΔG was calculated in accordance with Formula 3, apparently, the difference between G (Ar-O•) and G (Ar-OH) governed the ΔG of reaction.

It was presented in Table 2 that the ΔG originated from the individual H atom, releasing from active site of catechinic compounds, scavenging oleic acid radical. Both in (+)-catechin and (−)-epicatechin, the ΔG was no more than zero as the H atom was donated by site of 3′-OH and 4′-OH. The reaction was spontaneous as the H atom was released from site of 3′-OH, 4′-OH and 4″-OH of CG and ECG, the ΔG were minimum in the 4″-OH, which were −4.15 kcal/mol and −5.08 kcal/mol, respectively. GCG and EGCG shared identical spontaneity in the site of 3′-OH, 4′-OH, 5′-OH and 4″-OH, 4′-OH was the optimal active sites of spontaneous reaction, and the ΔG were −7.21 kcal/mol and −6.24 kcal/mol, respectively, of which the ΔG presented the bottom-ranked within the group of among the congener. The ΔG presented the lowest values as the H atom was released by the optimal active site, indicating the most likely possibility to scavenge free radicals. According to thermodynamic theory, ΔG = ΔH – TΔS (Zheng et al., 2018), Given that the absolute entropy contribution (TΔS) was only a few kJ/mol, (Rimarčík et al., 2010; Vagánek et al., 2014), the ΔG was predominantly determined by ΔH (Zhu et al., 2024). For the scavenging reaction, the spontaneity of the reaction was negatively correlated with ΔG, ΔH, BDE, G(Ar-O•) and H(Ar-O•) (Zhu et al., 2024), while it was positively associated with the potential activity of catechinic compounds.

The free energy barrier (ΔG‡) of catechinic compounds scavenging lipid radicals

The ΔG merely indicates the possibility of the reaction occurring, moreover, the reaction is not going to occur until the reactants convert into the transition state (TS), and the energy difference between the reactants and the TS is identified as free energy barrier (ΔG‡) (Cheng et al., 2022; Stocker et al., 2023). In the present study, it was calculated that the ΔG‡ of GCG and EGCG (H from the optimal active site) separately neutralized •OOR, •OR, •OA-C11 and •LA-C11. GCG exhibited a lower ΔG‡ for scavenging •OR and •OOR, with the values at 9.88 kcal/mol and 18.64 kcal/mol, respectively, both of which were lower than that of GCG scavenging •OA-C11 and •LA-C11, contrastively, the ΔG‡ was at 17.96 kcal/mol as EGCG scavenged •OOR, the EGCG showed a lower efficiency than GCG as scavenging •OA-C11 and •LA-C11. Verification inhibitory efficiencies of GCG and EGCG towards lipid radicals

In oil system, the efficiency of phenolics is not only determined by their endogenous properties but also influenced by the external environment (Brewer, 2011), hence, a verification trial is indispensable for selecting lipid antioxidants. EPR determination was commonly adopted for evaluating the scavenging efficiency of phenolic compounds towards lipid radicals (Jiang et al., 2020; Rahmani-Manglano et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024), in which free radicals were identified in accordance with their HFS, g-factor and quantified by fitting the parameters consisting of line width, line shape and peak area (double integral area).

Free radicals including alkyl (•R) and alkoxyl (•OR) were present in the heated camellia oil, and their EPR spectrums, spin-fitting parameters were shown in Fig. 1 and Table 4, respectively. GCG and EGCG effectively suppressed the generation of free radicals in camellia oil (Fig. 2), this may be attributed to the phenolic compounds scavenging lipid radicals by donating hydrogen atoms, on the other hand, the decrease in radicals may also be due to the termination reactions of auto-oxidation when two substances with unpaired electrons bind together, such as an antioxidant and a lipid radical (Juncos et al., 2024). The inhibitory activities of GCG and EGCG towards •R and •OR showed a time dependence, the highest efficiencies of GCG and EGCG towards •R as well as GCG towards •OR were occurring at 20 min (180 °C), with the rate of 69.64 ± 0.27 %, 60.06 ± 0.38 %, 68.40 ± 0.82 %, respectively, with respect to the inhibition rate of GCG and EGCG towards •R and •OR, it showed a decreasing trend with the extension of heating time (Fig. 3), this was attributable to the thermal acceleration of lipid oxidation chain reactions, where the rate of radical generation surpassed the antioxidant capacity for consumption and regeneration. Our previous study showed that EGCG, myricetin and piceatannol showed a remarkable activity to inhibit the generating of total radicals in camellia oil at 120 °C for 50 min, with the rate between 69.4 ± 11.0 % and 75.7 ± 4.1% (Zhu et al., 2024), this difference may originate from the phenolic varieties, heating condition as well as radical species, in addition, this also suggested that phenolic compounds give play to a preferable efficiency in an endurable condition. Under a consistent condition, the inhibitory rate of GCG towards lipid radicals were higher than that of EGCG, this was in accordance with the activity evaluation results from aspects of ΔG‡ and ΔG. GCG and EGCG are isomers of each other, they are synthesized when gallocatechin linked with gallic acid by means of esterification reaction and present a discriminative configuration (Khan and Mukhtar, 2019) (2S,3R-GCG and 2R,3R-EGCG, as shown in Fig. 4), this may account for their efficiency differences. Moreover, the DMPO oxidative radical (•X), originating from DMPO oxidation(Yang et al., 2022), showed an increasing trend with the extension of heating time, both GCG and EGCG showed golden efficiency to inhibit the generating of •X with the rates between 86.15 ± 2.96 % and 99.17 ± 0.02 %. Furthermore, the antioxidant activity of phenolic compounds could be comprehensively validated through chemical indices (peroxide, anisidine and conjugated dienes values), volatile oxidation markers and chemical composition analysis (Juncos et al., 2024).

Conclusions

This study established a low cost-efficient strategy for screening antioxidants by integrating quantum chemistry calculations with EPR spectroscopy. Our findings identified GCG and EGCG as superior scavengers for lipid radicals, primarily due to their favorable thermodynamic properties (low BDE and ΔG), and it demonstrated exceptional capacity in suppressing •OOR, •OR, •OA-C11 and •LA-C11 radical. The GCG and EGCG efficiently postponed the thermal oxidation of camellia oil, in which •R and •OR are dominant targets. This research provided a reliable theoretical and experimental basis for screening potent natural antioxidants to inhibit lipid radicals, however, the current evaluation was performed only under simulated thermal processing conditions, therefore, the oxidative stability of these compounds during actual storage requires further investigation.

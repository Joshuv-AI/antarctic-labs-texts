Customizing the WHO Checklist for Pediatric Precision Care
Authors: Vijay Kumar Kundal, Raksha Kundal
Published: 2025

Dear Sir,

It is generally established that using the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist (SSC) improves operating room team coordination while decreasing perioperative complications and mortality.[1] Although it is useful for adult surgeries, its awareness and use must be examined in setups where pediatric surgery is frequently performed. The WHO suggests that end users review and customize the checklist to promote safe operations. The SSC is divided into three parts: sign-in, which is done before anesthesia is administered; time-out, which is done before skin incision; and sign-out, which is done right after skin closure or before the patient departs the operating room. At our newly developed center, we encountered issues during the WHO checklist sign-in. Each time the nursing officer asked whether the patient had confirmed their identity, site, procedure, and consent, they assumed this step was not applicable in the case of nonverbal children. We almost had a near-miss event. Following the event, we considered customizing our institute’s SSC.

Just after the query “Has the patient confirmed his/her identity, site, procedure, and consent?” we added a new query: “In the case of nonverbal patients, has the identity been confirmed?” In addition, we included columns for hospital number, name, age, gender, procedure name, and the signatures of the surgeon, anesthetist, and circulatory nurse for extra safety to avoid any miscommunication or mistakes.[2]

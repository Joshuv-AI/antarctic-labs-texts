The role of viruses and molecular markers in the diagnosis and treatment of cancers and rare diseases: challenges and perspectives
Authors: Amirhassan Rabbani, Nejat Mahdieh
Published: 2025

Introduction

Common and rare diseases continue to impose a significant burden on healthcare systems worldwide. Viruses and molecular markers are increasingly recognized as pivotal in pathogenesis, early diagnosis, and the development of targeted therapies. Recent studies from Iran and other parts of the world have underscored the critical role of these factors in disease identification and management.

Viruses in cancer: from diagnosis to targeted treatment

Oncogenic viruses, such as Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), are significantly associated with various types of cancers. One well-known cancer linked to this virus is gastric cancer. A recent study conducted in Iran reported that the prevalence of EBV-associated gastric cancer (EBVaGC) in the Iranian population is approximately 5.8%, which is lower than the global average (1). This study also identified important molecular markers, such as miR-BART7-3p, which were recognized as potential diagnostic and therapeutic factors in gastric tumor tissues. These molecular markers can be utilized for more accurate and targeted identification of EBV-associated cancers. However, a major challenge in this area is the inability to identify specific miRNAs in plasma, emphasizing the need for improved non-invasive methods for early diagnosis and monitoring of EBV-associated cancers. This limitation underscores the necessity for advancements in diagnostic and therapeutic techniques, particularly those based on the use of interfering RNAs to inhibit viral signaling pathways.

Recent research suggests that identifying epidemiological and genetic differences in viruses across various populations can lead to the development of more targeted and precise treatments. Genetic variations in response to viral infections and their interactions with host cells can influence oncogenic processes, highlighting the need for personalized therapies. Additionally, the development of diagnostic tools specific for the early detection of virus-induced cancers, as well as RNA-based therapies capable of inhibiting viral signaling pathways, presents a significant opportunity to improve treatment outcomes and prognosis for these types of cancers. The use of interfering RNAs can effectively inhibit the oncogenic activities of viruses and enhance therapeutic outcomes (2).

Overall, further research into identifying genetic and epidemiological differences in viruses, as well as clinical applications of interfering RNAs, could play a crucial role in the development of new therapeutic approaches and improving outcomes for patients with virus-related cancers. Given the biological complexities and genetic diversity in different populations, advancements in this field will not only aid in more accurate diagnoses but also pave the way for targeted and personalized treatments in the future.

Biomarkers for non-invasive diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), particularly liver cancer, is one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality due to its late-stage diagnosis. The disease is often identified at advanced stages when treatment options are limited. In a recent study, a panel consisting of three key genes (RANSE2, TNF-α, and MAP3K7) was designed and investigated. The results showed that this panel could distinguish liver cancer from chronic hepatitis and healthy individuals with 98.4% accuracy. These findings were confirmed using machine learning techniques and demonstrated high performance in independent evaluations. The use of machine learning in this study not only enhanced diagnostic accuracy but also paved the way for the development of non-invasive diagnostic tools for liver cancer (3).

Optimizing machine learning algorithms and bioinformatics analyses can aid in the identification of novel biomarkers and improve diagnostic methods. These tools can integrate complex molecular and clinical data, leading to more accurate and faster disease diagnoses (4). Utilizing such approaches alongside non-invasive methods, such as blood tests based on cfDNA or miRNA, can enhance patient monitoring across various stages of the disease. Additionally, the development of artificial intelligence and machine learning-based tools for analyzing data derived from omics techniques (such as transcriptomics and proteomics) could facilitate the identification of key molecular pathways and novel therapeutic targets.

Moreover, integrating molecular data with clinical information, including medical images and laboratory results, can assist in designing predictive models that are effective in both early diagnosis and disease prognosis. These approaches not only lead to personalized treatment but can also contribute to reducing costs and improving patients' quality of life.

Diagnosis and treatment of rare cases: retroperitoneal lymphangioma and MEN1-related disorders

Differential diagnoses in rare and complex diseases, such as retroperitoneal lymphangioma and disorders associated with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 1 (MEN1), are critical. These diseases present significant challenges in diagnosis and treatment due to their clinical variability and similarities to other disorders. Two case studies conducted in Iran have demonstrated that the use of advanced molecular techniques can aid in the accurate diagnosis and successful treatment of these conditions. For example, the correct diagnosis of retroperitoneal lymphangioma was achieved through precise imaging and genetic testing, ultimately leading to successful surgery. Additionally, for MEN1-related disorders, the analysis of mutations in the MEN1 gene facilitated the design of an appropriate therapeutic approach (5, 6).

The development of molecular diagnostic tools, such as next-generation sequencing (NGS) and advanced genetic analyses, enables faster and more accurate identification of rare diseases. While NGS is widely used for detecting mutations in rare genetic disorders, countries with resource constraints often rely on simpler methods. For instance, in congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH), eight common mutations in the CYP21A2 gene have been identified (7). Similarly, mutations in a small gene like GJB2 have been shown to contribute to a high percentage of hearing loss cases (8). Optimized methods can be employed for detecting these specific mutations. Moreover, emerging technologies, such as genomic editing tools like CRISPR-Cas9, along with the development of new biomarkers, hold the potential to design targeted therapies for these rare disorders.

The treatment of rare diseases requires extensive research and the application of advanced technologies due to the complexity of their pathophysiology and the scarcity of epidemiological data. The combination of molecular technologies with interdisciplinary approaches, including immunotherapy and genetically mapped surgical techniques, could bring about significant advances in managing these diseases. Furthermore, genetic and biological databases, which collect information on rare disease patients, can serve as valuable resources for future research, aiding in the development of more effective and personalized treatments.

Hematological consequences in transplants and the role of vitamin D in liver transplantation

Organ transplantation, including liver transplants, is one of the most challenging yet effective treatment options for patients with organ failure. However, post-transplant outcomes can be influenced by various factors, such as Passenger Lymphocyte Syndrome (PLS) and vitamin D deficiency. A study demonstrated that timely diagnosis and treatment of PLS using a combination of immunosuppressive therapies and plasmapheresis had successful outcomes in reducing complications and improving graft function (9). On the other hand, vitamin D deficiency before liver transplantation, especially in patients with cirrhosis, was associated with an increased risk of acute rejection. This finding highlights the importance of monitoring and correcting vitamin D levels before transplantation (10). Vitamin D, as a key regulator in the immune system, can reduce the risk of transplant rejection by inhibiting inflammatory pathways and promoting immune tolerance.

A greater focus on the role of vitamins and nutritional supplements in managing and improving post-transplant outcomes can help reduce complications and increase the success of treatments. Recent studies suggest that targeted supplementation with vitamin D or other similar nutritional factors can not only reduce the risk of transplant rejection but also positively impact the quality of life for patients. By enhancing immune regulation and mitigating inflammatory responses, these supplements offer potential therapeutic benefits in the post-transplant care of patients.

Additionally, the use of molecular and genetic techniques, such as genomic sequencing and RNA profiling, can enable more accurate prediction of post-transplant complications (11). These tools can assist in personalizing treatments and preventing the onset of serious complications, ensuring better patient outcomes and reducing the need for invasive interventions. By identifying genetic predispositions and molecular markers, clinicians can tailor therapies to each patient's unique genetic makeup, ultimately improving the success rates of organ transplants.

Conclusion

The integration of molecular and viral findings with advanced diagnostic and therapeutic techniques has opened new opportunities for tackling both common and rare diseases. Further research in virology, genetics, and bioinformatics can play a pivotal role in improving disease diagnosis and treatment methods. Therefore, interdisciplinary collaboration and the application of cutting-edge technologies, especially in the precise fields of genetics and bioinformatics, can be a significant step toward advancing global health. Progress in these areas will lead to the development of targeted, personalized, and non-invasive treatments, which can significantly improve patients' quality of life.

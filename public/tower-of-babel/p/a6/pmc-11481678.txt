A novel hybrid algorithm based on improved marine predators algorithm and equilibrium optimizer for parameter extraction of solar photovoltaic models
Authors: Ziyuan Liang, Zhenlei Wang, Ali Wagdy Mohamed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the field of solar photovoltaic (PV) systems, the accurate and reliable extraction of parameters from PV models is crucial for effective simulation, evaluation, and control. Although various optimization algorithms have been widely used for parameter extraction in solar PV systems, the accuracy and reliability of the parameters extracted by these methods usually fall short of the expected standards. To address these shortcomings, a novel hybrid algorithm that combines the improved marine predators algorithm (MPA) with the equilibrium optimizer (EO), named IMPAEO, is proposed. In the IMPAEO, an elite opposition-based learning strategy is introduced to expand the exploration range of the algorithm to enhance population diversity; an adaptive weight coefficient is employed to improve the updating rule of the MPA, facilitating a balance between global exploration and local exploitation; the EO operator is integrated to enhance the performance of the MPA in the local exploitation phase to improve the solution quality and convergence speed; and the linear population size reduction (LPSR) technique is introduced to dynamically reduce the population size and improve the search performance. The effectiveness of the proposed IMPAEO is validated using the CEC2017 benchmark test suite, which is also applied to extract parameters of the single diode model, the double diode model, and three different PV modules. Comparative analysis with other algorithms demonstrates that the IMPAEO exhibits significant advantages in terms of solution quality, convergence speed, and algorithm stability. Consequently, the proposed IMPAEO not only proves to be efficient and reliable in parameter extraction for solar PV models but also offers a promising alternative in this field.Keywords: Photovoltaic models, Solar cell, Parameter extraction, Marine predators algorithm, Equilibrium optimizer

Introduction

The swift expansion of the global economy, coupled with significant advances in human civilization, has precipitated a trio of critical issues that confront humanity today: the energy crisis, escalating environmental pollution, and the dwindling reserves of non-renewable energy resources [1], [2], [3]. Given these global challenges, the pursuit of sustainable and environmentally friendly energy sources has become increasingly urgent. Under these circumstances, there has been a marked increase in the interest in renewable energy sources such as wind, solar, hydro, and tidal power in recent years. Among these, solar energy is especially notable for its high potential, sustainability, and environmental benefits [4].

Solar photovoltaic (PV) systems play a pivotal role in transforming sunlight into usable electrical energy. At the heart of these systems lie solar modules, which are crucial for the effective operation of PV power generation. However, ensuring the optimal performance of these systems requires accurate and reliable mathematical models that can emulate the intricate current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of solar modules. Developing such models is essential for the precise monitoring and management of the modules' operational dynamics, in addition to forecasting the power output of PV systems. This endeavor not only augments the efficiency of energy conversion but also significantly contributes to the optimization of solar energy utilization [5], [6].

In spite of the growing interest in PV systems, accurately modeling their behavior remains a complex and challenging task. The progress in PV technology has introduced a variety of models to elucidate the current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of PV cells. Among these, the single diode model (SDM), double diode model (DDM), and PV module model (MM) stand out, each depicted using an equivalent circuit framework [7], [8], [9]. The fidelity of these models in capturing the unique characteristics of PV systems is paramount for comprehensive analysis. Consequently, the extraction of accurate model parameters emerges as a critical challenge, necessitating enhancements in precision to better inform the construction and optimization of PV systems. Thus, the demand for methodologies that are both effective and precise in deducing the various parameters of these models is of utmost importance.

Extensive academic research has proposed numerous techniques for extracting parameters from solar PV cell models, categorizing these methods into analytical, deterministic, and meta-heuristic algorithms (MAs). Analytical methods leverage mathematical equations to ascertain the values of elusive parameters, offering a swift and straightforward means of achieving results [10]. Although these methods require specific initial assumptions, which can cause discrepancies between simulated results and actual performance metrics of the PV models, deterministic approaches also have their limitations. Methods like the Newton-Raphson approach [11], [12], the Lambert W function [13], [14], and iterative curve fitting exhibit high sensitivity to initial parameter settings, often leading to suboptimal local solutions. These approaches require the objective function's equations to exhibit properties such as convexity and differentiability. However, these conditions are rarely satisfied given the nonlinear and multimodal characteristics inherent in extracting parameters from PV models, thereby obstructing the achievement of optimal outcomes.

As the complexity of PV systems continues to evolve, traditional methods are increasingly unable to meet the demands of precision and efficiency in parameter extraction. MAs offer the distinct advantage of flexibility in problem formulation, both conceptually and computationally, making them inherently simple yet powerful tools for addressing multimodal challenges. Consequently, a significant number of researchers have favored the application of MAs for extracting parameters in PV systems. The utilization of MAs spans a broad spectrum of approaches for parameter extraction across various PV models, categorically divided into three main types. The first classification involves the direct use of established algorithms, such as the harmony search (HS) [15], bacteria foraging algorithm (BFA) [16], and gaining–sharing knowledge based algorithm (GSK) [17]. Additionally, methods like the fireworks algorithm (FWA) [18], flower pollination algorithm (FPA) [19], and transient search optimization (TSO) [20] also fall into this category.

The second category involves refining existing algorithms. Ru et al. [21] introduced a chaos learning butterfly optimization algorithm (CLBOA) which has proven more accurate in parameter extraction for PV models. Belabbes et al. [22] introduced an enhanced snake optimization algorithm (ISOA), tailored for efficient parameter extraction in three distinct PV cell types. The results from comparative studies indicate that the ISOA surpasses the conventional SOA in efficiency and achieves quicker parameter extraction with a reduced population size. Shaheen et al. [23] developed the Enhanced Artificial Gorilla Troops (EAGT) optimizer, which introduces a fitness-driven crossover mechanism and a periodic Tangent Flight operator. These modifications allow for precise parameter extraction from the PV Triple-Diode Model, optimizing both exploration and exploitation phases. The efficacy of EAGT was confirmed through its outstanding performance on the STM6-40/36 and KC200GT. Long et al. [24] introduced the hybrid seagull optimization algorithm (HSOA), which incorporates advanced methods to balance exploration and exploitation alongside a differential mutation strategy. This advancement greatly improves the precision, convergence speed, and dependability of PV model parameter extraction. Gu et al. [25] introduced the elite learning adaptive differential evolution (ELADE), which enhances the precision of PV cell model parameter extraction. It integrates adaptive mechanisms, elite learning, and chaotic elimination strategies, and its effectiveness is validated through comparative and statistical analysis. Qaraad et al. [26] developed a variant of the moth flame optimization technique, which integrates local escape operators (IMFOL). This innovation significantly enhances both the accuracy and efficiency in determining parameters for photovoltaic systems, thereby greatly improving the modeling of data for solar cells and PV modules.

The final category involves the combination of two or more algorithms. For instance, Eslami et al. [27] proposed a robust hybrid technique integrating adaptive rat swarm optimization (ARSO) and pattern search (PS) aimed at reliably and efficiently extracting PV parameters. Ram et al. [28] introduced the hybrid algorithm BPFPA, which mitigates poor convergence and ensures higher solution quality through a unique crossover between the original FPA and the bee search algorithm. Sayeda et al. [29] introduced an innovative hybrid optimization approach that merges the high exploration capability of TLBO with the high exploitation efficiency of the equilibrium optimizer (EO). This technique significantly enhances the controller gains of PV systems under both stable and dynamic irradiance conditions, as evidenced by its comparative superiority to existing algorithms. Furthermore, Devarapalli et al. [30] presented an innovative EHGWOSCA for precise parameter extraction of solar PV modules. Their method was evaluated across different PV module types, such as monocrystalline, polycrystalline, and thin-film, demonstrating notable accuracy enhancements over conventional methods. In a related study, Abd El-Mageed et al. [31] presented a hybrid algorithm, IQSODE, which merges queuing search optimization with differential evolution. This approach demonstrated enhanced accuracy, speed, and reliability in extracting PV model parameters, as confirmed by manufacturer datasheets.

Despite the noteworthy advances in parameter extraction techniques, there remain considerable opportunities for improvement, particularly in balancing exploration and exploitation, accelerating convergence, and avoiding premature convergence. While the three main categories of algorithms show promise in extracting PV model parameters, they still face limitations in terms of convergence speed, reliability, and overall effectiveness. For instance, DE is highly sensitive to control settings [32], TLBO tends to converge at a sluggish pace [33], the whale optimization algorithm (WOA) often suffers from premature convergence, and the marine predators algorithm (MPA) exhibits imbalances between exploration and exploitation [34], [35]. Furthermore, the no free lunch theorem [36] underscores that no single algorithm can be universally optimal for all optimization tasks, which further motivates the development of enhanced methods. This insight drives the need for hybrid metaheuristic algorithms that can effectively combine the strengths of individual approaches to achieve a better balance between exploration and exploitation. For instance, Khajehzadeh et al. [37] proposed a GSA-based method for the nonlinear design of retaining structures, achieving a balanced optimization between geotechnical and structural requirements with better performance than PSO and GA. Eslami et al. [38] applied PSO with chaotic behavior for tuning SVCs and PSSs, proving its effectiveness in damping interarea oscillations through eigenvalue and time-domain analysis. Moreover, Eslami et al. [39] introduced a chaotic PSO (CPSO) for coordinated PSS and SVC design, showing significant improvements in oscillation damping and stability over traditional manual methods. Akbari et al. [40] improved SSA (ISSA) for multimachine systems by enhancing exploration and preventing premature convergence, achieving better accuracy and stability than conventional methods. Eslami et al. [41] developed a hybrid GA-SQP algorithm for damping controllers, combining GA's global search with SQP's precision for robust oscillation damping across various conditions. Additionally, Eslami et al. [42] compared GA and PSO for PSS design, highlighting their distinct strengths in stability, computational efficiency, and parameter accuracy across different configurations. Our investigation reveals that hybrid algorithms outperform standalone methods in addressing the complex parameter extraction problem in PV models. By integrating the complementary features of different sub-algorithms, hybrid approaches can significantly enhance convergence performance and reliability. As a result, hybrid metaheuristics have gained increasing interest among researchers seeking robust solutions for solar PV parameter extraction.

Faramarzi et al. introduced the MPA [43], a distinctive metaheuristic approach, noted for its minimal parameter requirements and straightforward reproducibility. Compared to other techniques like GSK and COA, MPA offers significant advantages. It has demonstrated effective applications across numerous practical scenarios [44], [45], [46]. However, MPA also has some shortcomings, such as low exploitation capability and lack of population diversity, leading to slower convergence. Furthermore, Faramarzi et al. [47] proposed a novel MA based on physical principles, called EO. It has been implemented in many areas like image segmentation [48] and optimal power flow (OPF) [49]. Despite its remarkable local exploitation capabilities, it falls short in exploring the solution space, leading to an inadequate balance between exploration and exploitation.

In this study, we present IMPAEO, a novel hybrid optimization algorithm designed for parameter extraction in solar PV models. This approach incorporates an EOBL strategy to broaden the exploration capabilities and utilizes adaptive weight coefficient for refining the update mechanism within the MPA. Additionally, the inclusion of the EO operator significantly augments MPA's efficacy during local exploitation phases and integrates a linear population size reduction (LPSR) technique to avoid premature convergence. The proposed IMPAEO

Motivation

The MPA as an emerging optimization algorithm, has demonstrated commendable performance in various optimization problems. However, when applied to parameter extraction in solar PV systems, MPA encounters certain challenges, particularly in the exploitation phase. Because the iteration numbers are divided between exploration and exploitation phases in MPA results in relatively limited exploitation capabilities. Consequently, the algorithm may need numerous iterations to achieve convergence, especially when addressing nonlinear and multimodal optimization challenges. Furthermore, MPA exhibits deficiencies in balancing global exploration and local exploitation, sometimes overly focusing on exploration at the expense of exploiting discovered solutions, resulting in slower convergence rates. Moreover, reduced population diversity heightens the risk of converging to a local optimum.

The IMPAEO aims to enhance the exploration range and population diversity by introducing an EOBL strategy, thereby avoiding premature convergence and ensuring an efficient search over the entire solution space. Furthermore, an adaptive weight coefficient is used to improve MPA's update rule, facilitating a dynamic equilibrium between exploration and exploitation, ensuring efficient traversal of the search space by the algorithm. Additionally, the incorporation of the EO operator specifically enhances local exploitation, while the application of the LPSR technique improves search performance and reduces computation. These enhancements enable the IMPAEO algorithm to achieve greater accuracy and faster convergence in the parameter extraction process.

In conclusion, IMPAEO was developed to overcome the shortcomings of MPA in solar PV parameter extraction. It enhances exploration diversity, better balances exploration and exploitation, and improves local search efficiency. These improvements make IMPAEO a promising option for precise and dependable parameter extraction in solar PV systems.

EOBL strategy

Tizhoosh et al. [57] proposed the OBL strategy, based on the idea that a potentially better solution could exist on the opposite side of a suboptimal one during problem-solving. The primary principle is to identify the opposite solution to a feasible one and to compare both. The superior solution, whether the original or its opposite, is selected for the next iteration. The values of f(X) and f(X′) are assessed. If f(X) has a superior fitness compared to f(X′), then X is preserved; otherwise, X′ is adopted.

Adaptive weight coefficient

The application of weight coefficient has been recognized as a effective strategy for achieving an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation in MAs [61], [62]. Ayyarao et al. [63] have further refined this method by developing a novel weight update mechanism in their war strategy optimization algorithm (WSO), achieving notable success in balancing exploration and exploitation. Similarly, Yang et al. [64] have improved the WOA by introducing adaptive weights and dynamic convergence factors, thereby enhancing its search efficiency and convergence behavior. The empirical findings indicate that the WOA, with its newly incorporated adaptive weights, exhibits significant advantages and effectiveness in addressing global optimization challenges.

In this research, we propose a novel adaptive weight coefficient that dynamically adjusts the position of the prey within the search space. This coefficient is smartly calibrated to increase perturbations for prey that are further from the optimal position, and decrease them as they approach it. By utilizing an adaptive weight coefficient, the algorithm can dynamically modulate the coefficient size based on the prey's fitness value and the iteration number. When the fitness value of the prey is below a predefined threshold m1, it suggests that the prey occupies a superior position within the population. Consequently, a smaller adaptive weight coefficient is assigned, which reduces its activity range and bolsters its local search capability. For prey with fitness values between m1 and m2, the adaptive weight coefficient is not only influenced by the fitness value but also varies dynamically with the iteration count. This method enables more varied position updates for the prey, thereby improving the algorithm's accuracy, speed, and global search capability. In instances where the fitness value exceeds the threshold m2, it indicates a relatively inferior position of the prey within the population. Under such circumstances, a larger adaptive weight coefficient is allocated, intensifying the disturbance to the prey and improving its global search capability. Hybridization with EO operator

The hybridization of multiple optimization algorithms into a unified framework has proven to be a highly effective strategy for addressing a diverse array of problems [65], [66]. By combining the strengths of different algorithms and mitigating their weaknesses, this approach can achieve improved performance and efficiency in solving complex optimization challenges. Additionally, this collaborative method can enhance the robustness and adaptability of the optimization framework, enabling it to effectively tackle a broader spectrum of problem domains and achieve better results. Through the synergistic combination of various optimization techniques, the resulting framework can leverage the unique advantages of each individual algorithm, thereby creating a more versatile and effective optimization tool that can provide more comprehensive solutions to complex problems.

Typically, striking the right balance between exploration and exploitation is essential for the effectiveness of MAs. Achieving a proper balance between these opposing dynamics is key to effectively addressing complex optimization challenges. A review of the MPA shows that its capability for local exploitation might not be maximized. This limitation can be traced to its evolutionary process being segmented into three distinct phases, which does not allow sufficient iterations for both exploration and exploitation. Moreover, the leading predator in MPA serves as a navigator for the group's evolution, and if it settles into a local optimum, other members are prone to follow, often resulting in convergence to suboptimal solutions. These issues cumulatively impair MPA's performance in terms of local exploitation and convergence, thus diminishing the algorithm's overall effectiveness and efficiency. Consequently, the exploitation process within MPA warrants additional exploration and enhancement to boost its capability and deliver superior results in solving complex optimization scenarios.

Unlike MPA, EO utilizes a unique updating rule that guides population updates through an equilibrium pool, encompassing the top four optimal solutions and their averages. This method demonstrates enhanced exploitation efficiency and a reduced tendency to get caught in local optima. Given these benefits, we advocate for a hybrid approach that incorporates the EO operator strategy to leverage EO's superior exploitation strengths and apply them as a local exploitation technique within MPA. The proposed integration aims to enhance MPA's local exploitation capabilities while overcoming its issues with gradual convergence and getting trapped in local optimum.

Consequently, we anticipate that integrating LPSR will similarly enhance the effectiveness of IMPAEO.

Computational complexity analysis

In this subsection, we employ the Big-O notation to analyze the computational complexity of IMPAEO. Firstly, the EOBL strategy is only applied to elite individual within the population, contributing a complexity of (1×D×Max_Iter). The adaptive weight coefficient does not introduce additional overhead, as it is only utilized during position updates. The EO operator retains a complexity of O(N×D×Max_Iter), as described in the original literature. Additionally, the complexity of the LPSR technique is O(N×Max_Iter). Therefore, the overall computational complexity of IMPAEO remains O(N×D×Max_Iter), which is comparable to that of the original MPA algorithm.

Experimental results and analysis

In this section, the performance of IMPAEO is validated by first evaluating its effectiveness with the CEC2017 test functions across three dimensions (D=10, 30, and 50), which examined the impact of different components on the algorithm. Following this, IMPAEO was utilized to extract parameters from several PV models, including SDM, DDM, and three distinct PV MM, thereby further demonstrating its effectiveness.

The CEC2017 benchmark test suite was selected for this study due to its comprehensive coverage of diverse optimization problem types, including unimodal, multimodal, and composite functions. This diversity enables a thorough evaluation of the algorithm's capability in both global exploration and local exploitation, which are crucial for addressing complex, multimodal optimization challenges, such as those encountered in PV parameter extraction. The CEC2017 benchmark test suite comprises 29 functions, each with unique characteristics, excluding the unstable function F2 [69]. Each algorithm was tested through 30 independent runs, with Max_FEs set at D⁎10000.

Statistical analysis on five PV models

Delving deeper into the stability and robustness of IMPAEO, this subsection presents a comprehensive statistical analysis. The average RMSE values are utilized to perform the Friedman test, with the results depicted in Fig. 15. This figure compares IMPAEO with other algorithms across five PV models. Additionally, Fig. 16 provides a comparison of the average CPU time for all algorithms across the five PV models, offering insights into the computational efficiency of each method. The solution distributions of nine algorithms over 30 trials are illustrated in Fig. 17 using boxplots for each PV model, highlighting the variability and consistency of the solutions produced by each algorithm.

As illustrated in Fig. 15, IMPAEO achieves the highest rank in the Friedman test, surpassing ELADE, KOA, GSK, ITLBO, AGDE, IJAYA, MPA, APGSK-IMODE, AGSK, TLABC, CMAES, WOA, and SCA in descending order. The Friedman test results indicate that IMPAEO demonstrates consistent performance across all tested PV models, confirming its robustness and reliability in handling complex optimization problems. In addition to stability and robustness, computational efficiency is a crucial factor in evaluating algorithm performance, particularly in real-world applications. Fig. 16 provides a detailed comparison of the average CPU time for all algorithms across five PV models. Although IMPAEO does not have the shortest CPU times in all cases, it remains competitive overall. Specifically, for models like SDM and Photowatt-PWP201, IMPAEO's CPU time is moderate compared to other algorithms. While some algorithms, such as IJAYA and ITLBO, achieve shorter CPU times, they do so at the cost of accuracy and robustness, as demonstrated in the performance comparisons in Figure 15, Figure 17. These results indicate that while IMPAEO may not be the most computationally efficient algorithm in all scenarios, it strikes a favorable balance between computational cost and performance. The trade-off between CPU time and solution quality is evident, as IMPAEO consistently provides reliable results with a reasonable computational time, ensuring its applicability in scenarios where both accuracy and efficiency are crucial. Furthermore, the comparison in Fig. 17 unequivocally demonstrates that IMPAEO excels in both accuracy and robustness among the 14 algorithms analyzed. The boxplot analysis confirms that IMPAEO consistently delivers more reliable and tightly clustered solutions across the 30 trials, further supporting its robustness in parameter extraction tasks.

In summary, the combination of strong performance in the Friedman test, balanced CPU time, and superior solution distribution underlines the effectiveness of IMPAEO as a comprehensive optimization tool for PV model parameter extraction. The algorithm not only outperforms competitors in accuracy and robustness but also maintains a reasonable computational cost, making it a reliable choice for practical applications.

Conclusions and future works

In this study, we proposed an innovative hybrid algorithm, IMPAEO, specifically designed to enhance the precision and reliability of parameter extraction in PV models. IMPAEO integrates several advanced strategies, including the introduction of EOBL mechanism to boost population diversity, an adaptive weight coefficient to better balance exploration and exploitation, the incorporation of the EO operator for improved local exploitation, and the application of LPSR technique to mitigate premature convergence. Our comprehensive evaluations, utilizing the CEC2017 benchmark test suite along with multiple PV model parameter extraction tasks, demonstrate that IMPAEO offers superior performance. When compared with other competitive algorithms, this novel method shows marked improvements in solution accuracy, convergence speed, and overall operational stability. The experimental analysis leads to the following specific conclusions:

•The CEC2017 benchmark results illustrate that IMPAEO substantially outperforms its single algorithm, highlighting the enhanced optimization capabilities achieved through hybridization.

•Statistical analysis shows that IMPAEO consistently delivers significant or at least comparable performance against established algorithms, highlighting its effectiveness in PV parameter extraction.

•A thorough examination of I-V and P-V characteristic curves across five distinct solar PV cell models shows that IMPAEO consistently produces either superior or equivalent results, underscoring its robustness and precision in parameter extraction.

The findings of this research affirm that IMPAEO emerges as a strong candidate for optimizing PV models, offering significant advancements in both accuracy and robustness during parameter extraction. Moreover, the algorithm shows promising potential for application in broader energy optimization challenges, such as optimizing solar panel layouts, addressing optimal power flow problems, and fine-tuning parameters for energy storage systems. These promising application areas suggest fruitful directions for future work, where the IMPAEO could be further extended and refined to tackle more complex optimization problems in the energy sector.

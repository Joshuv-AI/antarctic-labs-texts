Maternal influenza vaccination intention and associated factors among pregnant women in Guizhou Province, China
Authors: Feng Jiang, Bin Qu, Yuanxue Gao, Jun Feng, Ning Tang, Xingui Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDespite global recommendations, maternal influenza vaccination coverage in China remains critically low. This hospital-based cross-sectional study (January–February 2022) applied the Health Belief Model (HBM) to examine determinants among 564 pregnant women in Guizhou Province. Mann-Whitney U tests and modified Poisson regression were employed to compare inter-group distributions and estimate prevalence ratios (PRs). Analysis revealed only 11.9% (67 of 564) of respondents received pre-pregnancy vaccination, and 36.7% (207 of 564) intended to be vaccinated during pregnancy. Key HBM-based predictors of vaccination intention included: (i) perceived barriers, primarily fetal/maternal safety concerns and familial opposition; (ii) cues to action and perceived benefits; and (iii) perceived susceptibility and severity. PRs and 95% CIs across quantiles indicated statistical significance in influenza vaccination intention by levels of perceived barriers (contrast = –0.34; 95% CI: −0.51, −0.17; Ptrend < 0.001), cues to action, and perceived benefits (contrast = 0.34; 95% CI: 0.13, 0.55; Ptrend = 0.002). These context-specific determinants provide the basis for our intervention strategies and measures. Integrated interventions, such as prenatal counseling, mobile health education, financial incentives, peer networks, and reminders, may enhance maternal influenza vaccination coverage, warranting broader implementation.

Introduction

Influenza is an acute respiratory illness caused by seasonal influenza A and B viruses, which circulate globally.1 Severity ranges from mild to life-threatening across all ages. Pregnant individuals and their infants are particularly susceptible,2 facing elevated risks of severe complications compared to the general population.3 Pregnant women are at increased risk for morbidity and death from influenza during seasonal epidemics and pandemics.4–7 The 2009 A(H1N1)pdm09 pandemic further demonstrated these vulnerabilities.8 Physiological changes in the cardiovascular, respiratory, and immune systems during pregnancy increase susceptibility to severe complications, including pneumonia, respiratory failure, and death.4 Moreover, a prospective cohort study in three middle-income countries found that prenatal influenza infection was linked to a 10-fold increase in stillbirth risk and lower birth weight.9,10 Therefore, protecting pregnant women and newborns from influenza is a critical public health priority.

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends seasonal influenza vaccination for pregnant women to protect them and their newborns from infection and reduce the risk of influenza-related complications and hospitalization.11 Vaccination prevents infection in pregnant women and protects infants through maternal antibody transfer.12 Inactivated influenza vaccines (IIVs) are widely used among pregnant individuals globally.13 Numerous large-scale studies and meta-analyses confirm the safety of IIVs for both mother and fetus, with no increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes.14–18 Since 2009, China has sought to include pregnant women in its influenza vaccination policy. The Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) issued technical guidelines (2010–2011) designating pregnancy and planned pregnancy during influenza season as priority indications for vaccination.19,20 Subsequent iterations of China’s influenza vaccination guidelines have consistently maintained this priority status.21 More recently, China has advanced its influenza vaccination initiatives. Strategic documents such as the “Healthy China Action (2019–2030)” and the “14th Five-Year Plan for National Health” emphasize vaccine supply and coverage among key populations.

Despite robust evidence and promotion, seasonal influenza vaccine coverage remains low in China. During the 2020–2021 and 2021–2022 epidemic seasons, coverage was 3.16% and 2.47%,22 respectively. A significant gap persists between national influenza vaccination recommendations for pregnant women and the low uptake in practice; coverage among pregnant women is no more than 1%.2,23 This disparity highlights the need to understand cognitive and social factors influencing vaccination intentions, which is essential for developing effective interventions. Influenza vaccines are not included in China’s national Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI), and policies vary across geographic regions and administrative tiers. Reimbursement options include free vaccination, partial or full coverage through medical insurance, and out-of-pocket payment.24 Although several areas offer free vaccination, pregnant women are not included among the eligible populations. In Guizhou, influenza vaccination is primarily self-funded, with limited insurance coverage in select regions, and pregnant women are excluded from free vaccination programs.

Influenza vaccine hesitancy is common, with few countries reporting immunization rates above 50% in pregnant women.9 Uptake depends on maternal acceptance and intention, which is a strong predictor of health-seeking behavior such as vaccination.25 Understanding intention is therefore essential to improving actual uptake. Vaccination intention is influenced by regional demographics, socioeconomic status, sociocultural context, and perceived influenza risk.26 Existing studies on influenza vaccine attitudes and uptake among pregnant individuals in China remain limited to developed regions (e.g., Beijing, Shanghai, Suzhou).20,27,28 Guizhou Province, an inland western region, differs economically, culturally, and demographically from developed eastern coastal areas. These factors strongly determine health literacy and vaccine acceptance. Guizhou has a rich diversity of ethnic minority groups (e.g., Miao, Bouyei, Dong), who represent a significant share of the population. Cultural beliefs, traditions, and trust in the healthcare system within these communities can shape attitudes toward vaccination. The factors influencing intentions in Guizhou may differ substantially from those in more affluent eastern coastal provinces. Data on maternal vaccination rates and intentions among pregnant women in less-developed inland provinces are scarce. Without region-specific evidence, national policies may be ineffective, as they cannot address the unique barriers and facilitators in these populations. Influenza vaccination is not routinely included in prenatal care in Guizhou; consequently, the determinants of vaccine acceptance in this population remain uncharacterized.

In conclusion, this study addresses a critical evidence gap by focusing on pregnant women in Guizhou Province. We conducted a cross-sectional study to identify structural and cognitive determinants of influenza vaccine acceptance. Applying the Health Belief Model (HBM)29 – a framework positing that perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, and cues to action collectively shape health behaviors – we modeled vaccination intention. These evidence-based insights will inform the design of culturally congruent interventions to improve regional influenza maternal immunization coverage.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

A cross-sectional survey on maternal influenza vaccination intentions was conducted in Guizhou Province, China, from January to February 2022. The survey was integrated with an existing study on vaccination knowledge and willingness.29,30 From 484 registered healthcare facilities, 15 were randomly selected. Of these, 11 provide obstetric services, and four were excluded for not providing such services. The facilities (five state-owned and six private) spanned nine counties and three hospital tiers: one tertiary, five secondary, and five primary/community institutions.

Participants were consenting pregnant women attending obstetrics clinics at the selected hospitals. Questionnaires were administered in person and via hospital-managed digital groups. Inclusion criteria were current pregnancy, first-time enrollment, and informed consent. Records with implausible responses were excluded.

Ethical approval

This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guizhou Provincial CDC (IRB No. S2020-06; October 25, 2021). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Study tools

The study questionnaire (Supplementary File 1) was refined through pilot testing to assess determinants of vaccination intentions. Maternal influenza vaccination intention was measured on a 3-point scale (Disagree/Unsure/Agree), with all other items rated on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree). Demographic and health variables included age, gestational age, parity, education, occupation, residence (county/city), household registration type, ethnicity, comorbidities (preexisting/gestational), pre-pregnancy influenza vaccination history, and monthly household income per capita.

Data analysis

Questionnaire items were analyzed using exploratory factor analysis (EFA) with concurrent validity and reliability testing in SPSS Statistics 31.0 Professional software. All HBM constructs used a 5-point Likert scale (1–5), with dimension scores derived from item summation. EFA with principal component analysis and varimax rotation was used to established scale dimensionality. Retention criteria (eigenvalue >1, scree plot inflection, and interpretability) yielded a 14-item instrument across three dimensions. Based on item content, Factor 1 was labeled Cues to Action and Perceived Benefits (8 items); Factor 2, Perceived Barriers (3 items); and Factor 3, Perceived Susceptibility and Severity (3 items). Bartlett’s Test of Sphericity (value = 7,775.292, df = 91, P < .001) and the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin test (KMO = 0.903) were calculated. Cronbach’s alpha coefficients for the three dimensions – Factors 1, 2, and 3 – were 0.940, 0.930, and 0.864, respectively. The cumulative variance in Rotation Sums of Squared Loadings was 78.598% (Supplementary File 2).

Categorical and ordinal variables were presented as frequencies (percentages), while continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range). Univariate analyses included chi-square tests for sociodemographic associations and Mann-Whitney U tests to compare item/dimension distributions across vaccination intention groups, where intention was dichotomized (Disagree/Unsure = 0; Agree = 1). Given binary and clustered data, a modified Poisson regression approach with a robust cluster variance estimator was used to estimate prevalence ratios (PRs).30 Standard errors were adjusted for clustering at the hospital level (total number of clusters = six). Five clusters represented individual healthcare facilities and the sixth cluster combined facilities with fewer than 50 survey participants. Multivariable analysis employed hierarchical modeling: variables with P ≤.1 in univariate screening were entered into four progressively adjusted models calculating PRs. Model 1 incorporated core HBM dimensions (cues/benefits, barriers, and susceptibility/severity). Model 2 added pre-pregnancy influenza vaccination. Model 3 was further adjusted for demographic/clinical covariates (age, parity, education, occupation, income, residence, and comorbidities). Model 4 included all preceding variables plus pre-pregnancy vaccination. In addition to per-point PRs, the PRs and 95% CI contrasted across quantiles (Quintile 2 vs. Quintile 1, Quintile 3 vs. Quintile 1, Quintile 4 vs. Quintile 1, Quintile 5 vs. Quintile 1) were calculated in Model 4 using linear contrast estimation. Sensitivity analysis stratified participants by medical/nonmedical occupational status. All analyses were conducted at α = 0.05 (two-tailed).

Results

None of the 564 participants received an influenza vaccination during their current pregnancy. Within one year before pregnancy, 67 women (11.9%) were vaccinated, while 497 (88.1%) were not. Regarding vaccination intentions, 36.7% (n = 207) expressed willingness, while 63.3% (n = 357) reported hesitancy or uncertainty.

Sociodemographic characteristics

Participants had a median age of 27 years (IQR 24–31), median parity of 1 (IQR 0–1), and median gestation of 29 weeks (IQR 18–36). Education levels were middle school or below (36.3%, n = 205), high school/technical school (36.3%, n = 205), and bachelor’s degree or above (27.3%, n = 154). Residential distribution included urban (44.5%, n = 251), township (24.1%, n = 136), and rural (31.4%, n = 177) areas. Occupationally, 83.5% (n = 471) worked in nonmedical settings and 16.5% (n = 93) worked in medical settings. Multivariable analysis

Eleven factors (P ≤ .1) were identified in the univariate analyses: cues to action and perceived benefits, perceived barriers, perceived susceptibility and severity, pre-pregnancy influenza vaccination, age, having children other than the current pregnancy, preexisting disease, educational level, occupation, registered residence, and monthly per capita income. Correlations between these factors were assessed. The highest absolute correlation coefficient was 0.493, indicating low to moderate correlation and no evidence of multicollinearity.

Using modified Poisson regression analysis with a robust cluster variance estimator, four models were developed to identify factors associated with maternal influenza vaccination intentions. Significant associations were found for perceived barriers (PR = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.87–0.96, P = .001), cues to action and perceived benefits (PR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.01–1.08, P < .01), and parity (PR = 1.31, 95% CI: 1.01–1.71, P = .041). In contrast, perceived susceptibility and severity (PR = 0.98, 95% CI: 0.96–1.00, P = .010), along with other sociodemographic factors, showed no significant association with vaccination intentions (Table 4). For every one-point increase in perceived barriers, pregnant women’s intention to vaccinate decreased by 8%; for every one-point increase in cues to action and perceived benefits, intention increased by 4%. Discussion

Our study revealed strikingly low influenza vaccination coverage among women in Guizhou Province, China, with only 11.9% (67/564) vaccinated in the year preceding pregnancy. Maternal influenza vaccine acceptance during pregnancy was also suboptimal at 36.7% (207/564). Notably, vaccination intention varied significantly across key sociodemographic factors, including occupation, educational attainment, parity status, preexisting conditions, and pre-pregnancy influenza vaccination history.

Influenza vaccination during pregnancy is established as safe and effective.31,32 Given the significant risks of influenza to maternal and fetal health, global and national guidelines designate pregnant women as a priority group.12,21 Nevertheless, coverage among pregnant women in mainland China remains persistently low, with no more than 1% coverage.2,23 This demonstrates a critical gap in protection against maternal influenza. Although our study observed higher rates – 11.9% pre-pregnancy vaccination and 36.7% acceptance during pregnancy – these remain suboptimal compared with international standards. Closing the gap in maternal influenza vaccination is a global public health priority.9 Notably, our observed rates exceed those reported in Beijing (15.3% intention, April 2013–March 2014),20 Zhengzhou (32.6% intention, 2020),33 and Suzhou (1.91% pre-pregnancy coverage, 3.57% intention, September – November 2023).27 Such persistently low uptake and high hesitancy warrant urgent public health intervention.

Within the HBM framework applied in this study, perceived barriers (PR = 0.92; contrast = −0.34, Ptrend < 0.001) and combined cues to action/perceived benefits (PR = 1.04; contrast = 0.34, Ptrend = 0.002) demonstrated significant negative and positive associations, respectively, with maternal influenza vaccination intentions. The robust link between perceived barriers and vaccination intention highlights the critical need to address obstacles to implementation. Consistent with studies in other countries, the most commonly reported barrier in this study was concern regarding the safety of maternal vaccines for mothers and fetuses.34 To our knowledge, this is the study first to identify such safety concerns as a primary barrier to influenza vaccination among pregnant women in Guizhou Province, a finding consistent with local barriers to maternal pertussis vaccination.35 In addition to safety concerns, familial opposition for constitutes a key social barrier in Guizhou. This aligns with the “Social Processes” component of the WHO BeSD framework (2022),36 highlighting that vaccination decisions are often not made in isolation. In many contexts, the family unit acts as the primary decision-making body, meaning that even if a pregnant person is willing to be vaccinated, opposition from family members can create a formidable obstacle.

A systematic review37 demonstrated that key facilitators of vaccination during pregnancy include recommendations from healthcare professionals, previous vaccination, knowledge about vaccination, and communication with and support from social groups. Consistent with global empirical evidence, our findings confirm that doctors’ strong recommendations and transparent communication regarding vaccine safety and efficacy profiles are pivotal determinants of maternal immunization uptake. Beyond provider recommendations and transparent communication, our study identified two additional facilitators of maternal vaccination uptake: (1) cost-free immunization services and (2) observable social proof through peer vaccination behaviors during pregnancy. These initiatives require policy and economic support. Influenza vaccination before pregnancy was statistically significant in univariate analysis but not in the modified Poisson regression using a robust cluster variance estimator. This suggests that the observed association may result from residual confounders or unmeasured variables. Women who engaged in specific preconception health behaviors were more likely to receive seasonal influenza vaccinations. Failure to engage in these behaviors may indicate to healthcare providers that patients are at higher risk of remaining unvaccinated during pregnancy.38 Further studies are required to clarify the association between pre-pregnancy vaccination practices and influenza vaccination during pregnancy.

According to the research, we confirmed that the HBM framework is effective in elucidating both the intention to accept and the actual uptake of influenza vaccination during pregnancy. In the context of disease knowledge and perception, factors of perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, and cues to action can be grouped into three dimensions: (1) cues to action and perceived benefits, (2) perceived barriers, and (3) perceived susceptibility and severity. This grouping is similar to that of the BeSD framework. The HBM constructs correspond to BeSD components as follows: perceived susceptibility, severity, and benefits align with “Thinking and feeling”; barriers (e.g., family opposition to vaccination) align with “Social processes”; and cues to action (e.g., doctor recommendations and observing others receive maternal vaccination) align with “Motivation.” Although the BeSD 2022 edition was designed for childhood and COVID-19 vaccination, the framework may also be applicable to maternal influenza vaccination.

Studies on the efficacy of influenza vaccination in pregnant women in China are relatively rare. To provide compelling evidence for vaccination during pregnancy, research efforts in this area should be intensified. To effectively increase vaccination intention and translate it into actual uptake, evidence-based strategies should be rigorously developed and evaluated. These strategies should be embedded within antenatal care and may include dedicated family-inclusive vaccine counseling sessions during prenatal visits, interactive mobile health education addressing real-time safety concerns, fee waivers, peer education or experience-sharing activities, and structured appointment reminders with facilitated scheduling. In addition to individual vaccines, the attitudes and decisions of other stakeholders – such as policymakers, vaccine service providers, manufacturers, and cold chain operators – are closely related to the implementation of vaccination for pregnant women. No single intervention has consistently and effectively increased influenza vaccine uptake during pregnancy.9 To improve uptake, practical factors must be integrated into immunization strategies. The effectiveness of such interventions can be assessed through randomized controlled trials or quasi-experimental studies to determine their impact on vaccination rates in this population.

In this study, parity was identified as a positive predictor of maternal influenza vaccination intention. However, this effect was not statistically significant when HBM scale scores were divided into quintiles for analysis (PR = 1.29, 95% CI: 0.97–1.71, P = .081). Previous studies have reported inconsistent findings regarding the impact of parity on vaccination, which aligns with our results. A 2021 study in Cameroon found that having children (aOR = 1.49; P = .04) was independently associated with higher COVID-19 vaccine acceptance among women.39 Conversely, earlier Canadian data (2010–2012) on seasonal influenza vaccination suggested that multiparous women were less likely to be vaccinated during pregnancy.40 These discrepancies may reflect contextual differences in health systems, cultural factors, and vaccine types. Inconsistent evidence regarding parity and childbearing status suggests that their influence is complex and context-dependent, warranting cautious interpretation and further investigation.

This study confirmed persistently low influenza vaccination rates among pregnant women in Southwest China, with safety concerns and familial opposition identified as critical barriers. The HBM effectively predicted vaccination intention, whereas parity status emerged as a novel determinant requiring further investigation. Multidimensional interventions addressing perceptual, social, and structural barriers and facilitators are urgently required to improve influenza immunization.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. The small convenience sample from team surveys in 11 healthcare facilities providing obstetric services may have introduced selection bias. Self-reported data are subject to response bias, and the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. This study was conducted in a specific temporal and geographical context. The contemporaneous COVID-19 situation might have influenced respondents’ perceptions of influenza and vaccination intentions. Consequently, the generalizability of these findings is limited.

Conclusion

Seasonal influenza vaccination among pregnant women in Guizhou Province remains low, with pre-pregnancy coverage (11.9%) and marginal acceptance during pregnancy (36.7%). Key determinants of vaccination intention include barriers – primarily fetal/maternal safety concerns and familial opposition – and facilitators such as doctor recommendations, accessible vaccine information, cost-free provision, parity status, and peer vaccination observation. To enhance maternal influenza vaccination rates, a comprehensive strategy including prenatal vaccine counseling, mobile health education, cost reduction, peer-led support, and systematic appointment reminders may be effective and warrant broader adoption.

Expandable versus inflatable mediastinoscopic combined with laparoscopic for radical esophagectomy
Authors: Weidong Zhang, Wentao Wu, Dong Cui, Kefeng Shi, Yuzhu Wu, Wei Li, Rulin Qian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundA few medical centers have used the “inflatable mediastinoscope” technology in radical esophagectomy for esophageal cancer, and its safety and short-term efficacy have been limitedly verified. However, this technology has some defects. Therefore, we developed a new method for radical resection of esophageal cancer: the expanded mediastinoscopic combined with laparoscopic radical esophagectomy (EMLE).MethodsFrom June 2019 to June 2022, we successfully performed 176 cases of radical resection of esophageal cancer using the new EMLE technique and recorded the baseline and perioperative patient data. We also retrieved four published clinical studies on the inflatable mediastinoscopic combined with laparoscopic radical esophagectomy (IMLE) and compared the perioperative safety differences between EMLE and IMLE.ResultsThe EMLE group comprised 176 patients, whereas the IMLE group had 173 patients. Compared to IMLE, patients undergoing EMLE showed almost no differences in baseline characteristics but demonstrated significant advantages in terms of operative time (329.71 vs. 168.84 min) and blood loss (167.84 vs. 94.87 mL) (P < 0.001). The incidence of postoperative complications was significantly lower in the EMLE group compared to the IMLE group (P < 0.001). Specifically, the incidence of postoperative recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy was significantly lower in the EMLE group than in the IMLE group (P < 0.001). No significant differences existed in the days of the postoperative hospital stay and number of resected lymph nodes.ConclusionEMLE has no disadvantages compared with IMLE. It is a radical resection of esophageal cancer with more advantages in surgical operation and blood loss control and is also worth promoting.Keywords: Expandable mediastinoscopy, Inflatable mediastinoscopy, Radical esophagectomy, Esophageal cancer, Comparative study

Introduction

The global incidence and mortality of esophageal cancer rank 7th and 6th, respectively, and it is a common tumor of the digestive system [1]. For esophageal cancer, radical surgical resection is the first choice and the main treatment method [1]. Compared with open surgery, video-assisted thoracoscopic and laparoscopic radical esophagectomy (VATLE) has become a major minimally invasive treatment for esophageal cancer because it avoids greater trauma caused by thoracotomy and laparotomy. The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend this surgical method because it reduces surgical trauma in patients and effectively promotes rapid recovery [2]. However, this operation must be conducted through the right thoracic cavity, and the body position must be changed. The operation time is relatively long, and the patient must have good cardiopulmonary function reserve. Moreover, if patients have chest adhesions, a previous history of lung disease, chest malformations, and other factors affecting the thoracoscopic surgical approach, thoracoscopic radical resection of esophageal cancer will be quite difficult, and some patients may even abandon the surgical treatment.

Scholars have conducted exploratory studies on surgical methods for esophageal cancer to address the limitations of its radical resection. Since 2017, several teams—Mori [3], Fujiwara [4], Wang [5], and Daiko [6]—have successively reported the application of inflatable mediastinoscopic combined with laparoscopic radical esophagectomy (IMLE) in treating esophageal cancer. These studies have demonstrated that an inflatable mediastinoscope is more minimally invasive than transthoracic minimally invasive surgery and thoracotomy in the radical resection of esophageal cancer and can avoid defects in the transthoracic approach. These studies also verified the feasibility and safety of radical resection of esophageal cancer under an inflatable mediastinoscope in the perioperative period.

Based on our rich experience in mediastinoscopy, minimally invasive esophagectomy, and the development of modern television mediastinoscope, our team developed an expanded mediastinoscopic combined with laparoscopic radical esophagectomy (EMLE)—a new surgical method, in 2019. From June 2019 to June 2022, we successfully performed this surgical technique on 176 patients at our medical center and recorded their perioperative data. To preliminarily evaluate the perioperative safety of EMLE and the difference between EMLE and IMLE, we compared the perioperative data between EMLE patients and previously reported IMLE patients.

Methods

Patients

EMLE: The present study included 176 patients with esophageal cancer undergoing EMLE for radical esophagectomy from June 2019 to June 2022 in our hospital. The inclusion criteria for this surgery type were as follows: gastroscopic and pathological diagnosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, clinical stage I–IIIA; age between 18 and 80 years; cardiopulmonary function that could tolerate surgical treatment; patients without other cancers; patients voluntarily participated in the clinical trials. The exclusion criteria were as follows: cervical esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; patients refused surgical treatment. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Henan Provincial Chest Hospital (2019-03-008), and written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

IMLE: We searched the PubMed database for four [3–6] eligible clinical studies on inflatable mediastinoscopes, which included comprehensive perioperative data. This data encompassed patient baseline information, operation time, operative blood loss, intraoperative lymph node dissection, and postoperative complications such as pneumonia, recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) palsy, anastomotic leak, pleural effusion, chylothorax, and the need for reoperation. We used a meta-analysis to explore the incidence of perioperative outcome indicators for IMLE.

Surgical techniques

The EMLE and IMLE groups were the same in terms of patient position, anesthesia method, and doctor arrangement. The procedure was initiated with single-lumen intubation under general anesthesia. The patient was placed in a supine position, the neck was properly elevated, and both lungs were ventilated. During the operation, there were two groups of doctors: the mediastinoscopy group and the laparoscopy group.

EMLE group

We used the expandable television mediastinoscope system and the “four-channel lymph node dissection” concept to complete the EMLE. The surgical procedure was performed as follows.

Cervical mobilization of the esophagus.

Cervical mobilization of the esophagus.

A 5 cm “arc” incision was taken along the front edge of the left sternocleidomastoid muscle to the anterior midline of the neck. The gap between the muscle and the left side of the trachea esophagus was separated. The left paraesophageal neck lymph nodes and left supraclavicular lymph nodes (left paraesophageal lymph node and 1 L) were dissected, and the left RLN was protected, which was the first channel lymphadenectomy. The cervical esophagus was pulled to the left, and the right cervical lymph node and the right supraclavicular lymph node (right paraesophageal lymph node and 1R) were dissected; this is the second channel lymphadenectomy (Figs. 1 and 2).

Differences in the instruments used during the procedure. The EMLE group used an expandable television mediastinoscope system, free forceps under the mediastinoscope, and an ultrasonic scalpel. The IMLE group used a 5 mm 30-degree lens, Maryland forceps, Ligasure™ Maryland Jaw sealer, and some assisted exposure devices. Differences in neck surgical incisions. The EMLE group required one left neck incision, whereas the IMLE group required two incisions, two-incision protective covers, and two dedicated sealing covers. 2.Mediastinoscopic mobilization of the upper and middle thoracic esophagus.

Mediastinoscopic mobilization of the upper and middle thoracic esophagus.

The expandable mediastinoscope was placed in front of the trachea, reaching the right edge of the trachea and dissecting the lymph nodes of the right RLN (2R, 4R). Moving forward to the subcarinal space of the trachea, the subcarinal lymph nodes were dissected [7]. Reaching the left edge of the trachea, the left RLN lymph nodes were dissected (2 L, 4 L). This process was the third channel lymphadenectomy. The mediastinoscope was placed around the esophagus, expanded and slowly advanced forward, and freed around the thoracic esophagus to the level below the carina. The laparoscopic procedure was performed, and the paraesophageal lymph nodes (8U, 8 M) were cleaned during this process, which was the fourth channel lymphadenectomy, as displayed in Fig. 3.

Differences in procedures and visual field during the surgical operation. The two differed primarily in mobilizing the upper and middle esophagus in the mediastinum. The EMLE group mobilized the esophagus by expanding the space of the expandable mediastinoscope, and surgery was performed around the esophagus and trachea. Whereas the IMLE group mobilized the esophagus by expanding the space of the mediastinum via CO2 gas inflation, the surgical procedure was performed around the esophagus. EMLE, Expandable mediastinoscopic and laparoscopic radical esophagectomy; IMLE, Inflatable mediastinoscopic and laparoscopic radical esophagectomy

During the operation, the assistant pulled the esophagus in different directions according to the operator’s needs and used the mediastinoscope to expand and slowly forward. The thoracic surgeon used two instruments—a suction device and an ultrasonic scalpel—to operate in the expanded space of the mediastinum.

3. Laparoscopic gastric mobilization, formation of gastric conduit and gastroesophageal anastomosis.

Abdominal stage operations were performed under pneumoperitoneum, including formatting a gastric conduit and dissociating the middle and lower thoracic esophagus through the esophageal hiatus. The fabricated tubular stomach was pulled up to the cervical incision via the mediastinal cavity, and an end-to-side gastroesophageal anastomosis was performed using a circular stapler. This process involves simultaneous dissection of the 8 L, 9, and 15–20 lymph nodes, and the procedure was described in previous literature [5]. Surgery was performed after placing a neck drainage strip around the anastomotic site, a mediastinal drainage tube through the abdominal incision, and a nasojejunal feeding tube for two weeks of nutritional support, followed by closure of the neck and abdominal wounds.

For the specific operation process of the EMLE group, refer to our previous article [7].

IMLE group

Preparing a neck incision and mobilization of the upper mediastinal esophagus.

Preparing a neck incision and mobilization of the upper mediastinal esophagus.

Incisions of 3–5 cm on the outer edge of the sternocleidomastoid muscle on both sides of the clavicle were marked, the gaps on the left and right sides of the neck esophagus were entered, the bilateral RLN was marked, and the bilateral paraesophageal lymph nodes and supraclavicular lymph nodes were separately dissected. Two 3.5 cm diameter incision protective covers were placed and covered with a dedicated sealing cover. CO2 gas was injected into the mediastinum at a pressure of 8–10 mmHg to create an artificial pneumomediastinum (Fig. 2). A 5 mm 30-degree lens was inserted, Maryland forceps, Ligasure™ Maryland Jaw sealer, and assisted exposure devices were placed into two sealing covers (Fig. 1).

Along the left side of the esophagus, the paraesophageal tissue was freed until the aortic arch level. It was then separated downwards along the right side of the esophagus to the lower edge of the left main bronchus. During this process, the left and right RLN and paraesophageal lymph nodes were explored simultaneously. The chief surgeon performed esophageal free lymphadenectomy in the inflatable space of the mediastinum, as illustrated in Fig. 3.

The surgical process was similar to that of the EMLE group, with reference to previous literature [5].

For the specific operation process of the IMLE group, refer to the articles [3–6]. All complications, including pneumonia, RLN paralysis, anastomotic leakage, pleural effusion, chylothorax, 30-day reoperation and in-hospital mortality, were monitored according to the Esophageal Complications Consensus Group (ECCG) recommendations [8].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.1). A meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the incidence of perioperative outcome indicators for the IMLE surgical approach in the treatment of esophageal cancer, with continuous variables represented by weighted mean differences (WMD). Statistical comparisons between the IMLE and EMLE groups were conducted using Chi-square test for categorical variables and Z-test for continuous variables. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 176 patients were enrolled in the EMLE group, and 173 were included in the IMLE group. Table 1 presents the demographic and clinical characteristics of all patients. There were 122 males and 54 females in the EMLE group, and their mean age was 65.74 ± 8.20 years. The tumor location included 26 cases of upper thoracic (14.77%), 98 of middle thoracic (55.68%), and 52 of lower thoracic areas (29.55%). The staging of these tumors was 58 cases of stage I (32.95%), 70 of stage II (39.77%), 47 of stage III (26.70%), and 1 of stage IV (0.57%). 53 cases of patients (30.11%) received preoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy. As shown in Table 1, apart from gender, there were no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between the EMLE group and the IMLE group.

Discussion

VATLE is a classic minimally invasive surgery for esophageal cancer [9–11] that has the advantages of less trauma and faster recovery. However, severe thoracic adhesions, abnormal chest structures, or poor cardiopulmonary function limit VATLE application, potentially forcing surgery cancellation. As transcervical mediastinoscope radical resection of esophageal cancer combines the benefits of minimally invasive surgery with reduced chest pain, fewer cardiopulmonary complications, and avoidance of adverse chest conditions (e.g., thoracic adhesion, chest wall deformity, abnormal chest structure), thereby expanding the surgical indications to create conditions for more patients to cure esophageal cancer [3–6]. Two cervical mediastinoscope-assisted surgical methods exist: IMLE and our developed EMLE. IMLE was introduced earlier by Mori [3] and Fujiwara [4] and then gradually improved by Wang [5] and Daiko [6]. Currently, IMLE has become a safe and reliable minimally invasive surgery for esophageal cancer, which is similar to VATLE.

Tissue exposure in the mediastinal cavity is essential in transcervical mediastinal surgery. To optimize tissue exposure in the narrow mediastinal cavity, we operated our newly developed EMLE and IMLE differently in the mediastinal cavity. IMLE was used to inject 8–10 mmHg of carbon dioxide into the mediastinum through a closed neck incision to better expose the mediastinum cavity. We employed a new expandable video-assisted mediastinoscope, inserting it through two expandable retractors to establish a pneumomediastinum and optimize surgical exposure. Our operation method avoids complex procedures, respiratory/circulatory instability, and instrument interference from inflatable mediastinoscopy, making it more suitable for widespread use. Another advantage of our operation is that bilateral cervical regional lymph nodes can be removed through a single arc incision on the left neck, compared with bilateral cervical incisions in the IMLE group, resulting in a smaller cervical incision [3–6].

The EMLE group has advantages in terms of operation time and intraoperative blood loss in both groups due to the operating characteristics of the expandable mediastinoscope itself. For example, unlike IMLE, which requires confined space preparation and multiple instruments in the narrow mediastinum (causing instrument interference and slower progress), the EMLE technique directly uses the mediastinoscope’s expandability for field exposure, eliminating the need for additional equipment. Daiko [6] inserted four instruments through incisions on both sides of the neck during the operation, which increased the interference of the instruments and impeded the operation’s progress. Furthermore, the IMLE group required bilateral cervical incisions for neck lymph node dissection, which are more complex and time-consuming than our arc single-incision operation. When unexpected bleeding occurs in the mediastinum during surgery, the EMLE group is more convenient, fast, and effective in dealing with it because the group has the characteristics of no inflation, only two surgical instruments, and a clear field of vision during the whole operation. In IMLE procedures, mediastinal pleura injury compromises effective inflation, disrupting surgical flow and potentially leading to procedure failure. However, in the EMLE group, there was no such concern.

Regarding the incidence of postoperative complications, we looked at pneumonia, RLN paralysis, anastomotic leakage, pleural effusion, chylothorax, 30-day reoperation, and in-hospital mortality. We found that the incidence of RLN palsy was significantly lower in the EMLE group than in the IMLE group (P < 0.001). The reduced RLN injury rate in EMLE versus IMLE stems primarily from the advancements in surgical techniques and instrumentation. The EMLE procedure utilizes an expandable mediastinoscope to create a stable surgical field, eliminating the need for CO2 insufflation. This avoids the tissue displacement and pressure fluctuations associated with gas inflation, thereby better protecting the RLN. Moreover, EMLE provides enhanced visualization stability with minimal instrument obstruction, enabling precise anatomical identification and preservation of the RLN. The single-incision design in the neck further reduces surgical complexity and the potential risk of nerve injury. Overall, EMLE simplifies the surgical process, enhances safety, and highlights the advantages of its innovative approach.

The two groups had no significant difference in the number of resected lymph nodes. We innovatively put forward the concept of “four-channel lymphadenectomy” under an expandable mediastinoscope [7], which can improve the scope, quantity, and efficiency of lymph node dissection. Compared to right thoracic esophagectomy, EMLE is safer, more feasible, and more thorough in the dissection of the upper mediastinal lymph nodes, especially the left RLN lymph nodes. This finding allays scholarly concerns about the adequacy of lymph node dissection in mediastinoscopic radical esophagectomy. To dissect the right cervical paraesophageal lymph nodes and right RLN lymph nodes, we made an incision in the right neck of patients in the IMLE group. However, in the EMLE group, the lymph nodes in the right cervical paraesophageal region were dissected by pulling the trachea esophagus and the right edge of the cervical incision to both sides and reducing one cervical incision. For this operation mode, Wang [5] also found the feasibility of this method after much practice of radical esophagectomy under an inflatable mediastinoscope. Our study data were compared with previously reported lymph node metrics from IMLE technique research, aiming to demonstrate that the number of lymph nodes dissected in our approach is comparable to, or not inferior to, that of the IMLE procedure. Furthermore, current guidelines for esophageal cancer management do not specify definitive requirements regarding the extent of lymph node dissection. Regarding follow-up, our ongoing prospective cohort has not yet reached the predetermined endpoint, which explains the absence of published survival data at this stage. However, we commit to progressively disclosing long-term follow-up results in future updates.

There are some limitations to our study. Our comparative control group was derived from previously published data from relevant clinical studies, with possible heterogeneity, affecting the comparative results. This was a retrospective study, and the evidence level is not as high as that of a prospective randomized controlled study. In addition, we only compared EMLE with IMLE but did not make a comprehensive comparison with thoracotomy and minimally invasive thoracoscopic surgery for radical esophagectomy. However, because EMLE is a new surgical method, only a few cases were available for research. In the future, we will conduct a prospective randomized controlled study to compare the differences between several surgical methods to comprehensively evaluate EMLE and obtain more clinical evidence.

Conclusion

Cervical mediastinal resection of esophageal cancer is feasible and safe, providing good operating space in the narrow mediastinum with either an inflatable mediastinoscope or a dilatable mediastinoscope. Compared with inflatable mediastinoscope, dilatable mediastinoscope has more prominent advantages for radical resection of esophageal cancer and can avoid some disadvantages of inflatable mediastinoscope, such as complicated operations, bilateral neck incisions, and the risk of unstable respiratory circulation. Therefore, EMLE is a feasible radical resection plan for esophageal cancer, has great application potential in the future, and is expected to become one of the standard surgical methods for radical resection of esophageal cancer.

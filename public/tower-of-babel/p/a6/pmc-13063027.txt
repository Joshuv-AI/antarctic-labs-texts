Identification and Geochemical Significance of Novel des-A-Triterpenoids and Mononuclear-Aromatic des-A-Triterpenoids in Tertiary Terrestrial Oils from the Northern of the South China Sea
Authors: Zichao Ran, Meijun Li, Hong Xiao, Zhiwei Gao, Mingzhu Yuan, Surui Dong, Jianfeng Zhang, Qing Zhao, Haochen Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A series of mononuclear-aromatic des-A-triterpenoids (MADATTS) and des-A-triterpenoids (DATTS) were simultaneously detected in terrestrial oils from the Baiyun Sag, Pearl River Mouth Basin, South China Sea. Besides five previously reported MADATTS, i.e., C23 mononuclear-aromatic des-oleanane (C23MADAO), C23 mononuclear-aromatic des-ursane (C23MADAU), C24MADAO, C24MADAU, and C26MADAU, this study identified a novel molecular marker in these oil samples, namely C26 mononuclear-aromatic des-oleanane (C26MADAO). C26MADAO elutes before C26MADAU in m/z 187 and 352 → 187 chromatograms, with a molecular ion at M+·352, base peak ion at m/z 187, and diagnostic ion fragment at m/z 199, 213, and 337. MADATTS with the same carbon number and identical substitutions on the B-ring exhibit strong positive correlations in their chromatographic peak abundance but show no evident correlation with the DATTS counterparts that share corresponding similar molecular skeletons, indicating that the formation mechanisms of the two series of compounds are different. In addition, the chromatographic peak abundance of both MADATTS and DATTS shows strong correlations with the C29 diasteranes/C29 regular steranes (C29-DiaSt/C29-St) and C30 rearranged oleananes/C30 hopane ((I + II + III)/C30H), which possibly reflects that their formation process is jointly controlled by terrestrial organic inputs and the catalysis of acidic clay minerals. Based on the sedimentary facies characteristics, conventional biomarker parameters, and distribution patterns of MADATTS and DATTS, this study finely reclassifies the Baiyun Sag oils into five groups and provides a comprehensive interpretation of their origins across different subregions.

Introduction

The degradation of nonhopanoid type pentacyclic triterpenes derived from higher plants is widespread in nature and occurs primarily during the early stage of diagenesis. , These reactions are triggered by the photochemical, microbial-mediated, and thermal transformation of polar pentacyclic triterpenoid compounds with oxygenated functional groups at the C-3 position (the ring bearing the oxygenated functional groups is designated as the A-ring, followed sequentially by the B-, C-, D-, and E-rings). − This process yields a series of ring cleavage/opening and aromatic triterpenoid compounds that share a common biological source. , Terpenoids with nonhopanoid skeletons and A-ring degradation represent ubiquitous molecular markers in both ancient strata and modern sediments. ,,,− Specifically, these derivatives originate from α/β-amyrin in higher plant tissues, which undergo oxidation to yield the corresponding 3-ketones. Photochemically induced removal of the keto functional group at C-3 initiates A-ring fission, generating 3,4-seco-derivatives. , Mediated by microorganisms, the A-ring fragment undergoes further cleavage reactions, producing the corresponding aliphatic compounds. Under specific conditions, microbial-mediated aromatization proceeds sequentially from the B-ring through the C- and D-rings to the E-ring, ultimately forming fully aromatized structures without a complete A-ring. ,,

Des-A-triterpenoids (DATTS) and mononuclear-aromatic des-A-triterpenoids (MADATTS) are intermediate products formed during the degradation of pentacyclic triterpenoids from higher plants. , Structurally, DATTS retains four aliphatic six-membered rings (B, C, D, E) and methyl groups at specific carbon positions following the A-ring cleavage. In contrast, MADATTS features an aromatic B-ring, three aliphatic six-membered rings (C, D, E), and methyl groups. Both compound classes preserve the diagnostic carbon skeletons of their precursors (oleanane, ursane, lupane, and arborane types) and are more stable than their parent triterpenoids. ,, However, variations in the degree of ring cleavage, unsaturation, aromatization, and methyl group position reflect differences in biological sources, depositional redox conditions, and microbial mediation, resulting in distinct geochemical implications. ,,,

Carbon isotope evidence further supports a higher plant origin for MADATTS and DATTS. ,, Their δ13C values are comparable to those of aromatic and aliphatic higher-plant triterpenoids but are significantly heavier than those of microbial-derived hopanoids, clearly distinguishing their biological sources. However, certain compounds, such as des-A-arbor-9­(11)-ene identified in Lake Chaka sediments, show unusually light δ13C values, suggesting that minor contributions from microorganisms or algae cannot be excluded. ,, Previous studies have demonstrated that DATTS preferentially form under dysoxic to suboxic conditions, such as mangrove sediments, wetlands, and environments with elevated groundwater levels. − ,− These settings provide limited oxygen but sufficient microbial activity to promote partial oxidation and the A-ring cleavage of higher-plant triterpenoids. In contrast, under highly reducing and sulfur-rich evaporitic conditions, A-ring cleavage is largely inhibited. , Sulfurization processes protect the molecular structure of triterpenoids, preventing the formation of A-ring-cleaved or A/B-aromatized derivatives. Nevertheless, compounds showing aromatization at later rings (D or E) or containing incorporated sulfur atoms are occasionally detected, indicating selective preservation under extreme anoxic conditions. MADATTS, on the other hand, tend to occur in more oxic or periodically aerated environments. Their formation is mainly driven by aerobic microbial oxidation and dehydrogenation, processes that enhance B-ring aromatization. High MADATTS abundance has been reported in seasonally dry, aerated peatlands, where aromatic triterpenoid derivatives dominate over aliphatic forms. − , Collectively, these findings indicate that the formation of MADATTS and DATTS is strongly governed by spatial and temporal variations in redox conditions, providing important insights into depositional environment reconstruction. Additionally, the catalytic effect of acidic clay minerals (hydride transfer) can reduce the activation energy of dehydrogenation reactions, significantly enhancing the efficiency of aromatization. ,− Molecular dynamics simulations have shown that the transformation from A-ring-degraded triterpenoids to B-ring-aromatized products is an endothermic process that requires either enzymatic or inorganic catalysis to proceed efficiently.

Previous research has detected indicators of terrestrial higher plant input, such as C15 sesquiterpene, oleanane, rearranged oleananes, taraxastane, des-A/B-ring triterpenoids, − and bicadinanes in crude oils and source rocks from the Baiyun Sag. These biomarkers collectively suggest that the source materials in this region received a substantial contribution of specialized terrestrial organic matter during deposition. Furthermore, spatial variations in the distribution and relative abundance of these molecular markers, which reflect differences in depositional and biochemical conditions across the Baiyun Sag and reveal the key mechanisms controlling the formation and enrichment of individual biomarkers, provide important insights into diagenetic processes, organic geochemical evolution, and sedimentary environmental dynamics. In this study, MADATTS were identified for the first time in crude oil samples from the Baiyun Sag of the Pearl River Mouth Basin, South China Sea. Through systematic molecular geochemical analysis, the factors influencing the enrichment of MADATTS and DATTS, as well as their formation and evolutionary pathways, were elucidated. In combination with conventional terrestrial higher plant-derived biomarkers, this work further refines the oil group classification in the Baiyun Sag and provides a more detailed interpretation of crude oil sources in the area.

Geological Background

The Pearl River Mouth Basin is the largest Cenozoic sedimentary basin along the northern margin of the South China Sea. It lies at the convergence of the Pacific, Eurasian, and Indo-Australian plates (Figure a). Tectonically, the basin has undergone three major evolutionary stages, which include the syn-rift stage during the Late Cretaceous to Late Paleocene, the transitional stage from the Early Eocene to Early Miocene, and the postrift stage after the Early Miocene. − This study focuses on the Baiyun Sag, which is located in the deep-water area of the Zhu II Depression within the Pearl River Mouth Basin (Figure b), covering an area of approximately 25,500 km2. It can be divided into four subsags (the Main Subsag, the West Subsag, the East Subsag, and the South Subsag). Based on the distribution of hydrocarbon-bearing structures, the study area can be further categorized into five regions including the western part of the Baiyun Sag (WBS), the Panyu Uplift (PLU), the northeastern part of the Baiyun Sag (NEBS), the eastern part of the Baiyun Sag (EBS), and the southern part of the Baiyun Sag (SBS) (Figure c).

Schematic map showing the location (a), structural units (b), sampled well (c), and comprehensive stratigraphic column (d) of the Baiyun Sag, Pearl River Mouth Basin. Reprinted in part with permission from Lu et al. Copyright 2024 Elsevier.

The hydrocarbon resources in the Baiyun Sag are primarily condensate and natural gas accumulations, mainly distributed in the PLU and EBS, whereas limited occurrences of black oil reservoirs are confined to the NEBS and WBS. Previous research has demonstrated that two sets of Eocene source rock sequences, namely the Wenchang and Enping formations, are developed within the study area. Overlying these are two reservoir systems, the Oligocene Zhuhai Formation and the Miocene Zhujiang Formation. The Miocene Hanjiang and Yuehai Formations, together with the Pliocene Wanshan Formation, consist of thick marine mudstone sequences that serve as effective regional seals (Figure d). , This well-developed assemblage provides favorable conditions for hydrocarbon generation, migration, and preservation in this region. The Wenchang Formation source rocks were deposited in shallow to semideep lacustrine environments and constitute the dominant hydrocarbon-generating interval in the study area (Type II1–II2 kerogen). The Enping Formation primarily consists of deltaic coal-bearing source rocks (Type II2–III), with a relatively small contribution to oil and gas. ,

Detailed Experiments

The crude oil samples were first deasphaltened by filtration with petroleum ether. The residual solutions were fractionated by column chromatography on a silica gel/alumina (3:1, w/w; the specified particle sizes ranged from 0.149 to 0.075 mm) column. Saturated hydrocarbons were eluted with petroleum ether, aromatic hydrocarbons were eluted with a mixture of dichloromethane and petroleum ether (2:1, v/v), and NSO fractions were eluted with a mixture of dichloromethane and methanol (9:1, v/v). Gas chromatography (GC) analysis of the saturated hydrocarbons was performed on a GC-2010 chromatograph with a flame ionization detector and a 30 × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm HP-5MS capillary column. Helium was used as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1.2 mL/min, with the temperature programmed from 100 to 300 °C at 4 °C/min, followed by a 10 min hold at 300 °C. The mass spectrometer operated in electron ionization mode at 70 eV. For the analysis of both saturated and aromatic hydrocarbons, gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) was performed using an Agilent 6890 GC/5975 iMSD system with an HP-5 MS capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). The oven temperature was initially set at 50 °C (1 min), ramped to 120 °C at 20 °C/min, then to 310 °C at 3 °C/min, and held for 25 min. Mass spectra were acquired using electron ionization at 70 eV, with an emission current of 300 μA and a scanning range of m/z 50–570. Further analysis of saturated hydrocarbons was carried out using a 7010B triple quadrupole mass spectrometer coupled with an Agilent 7890B gas chromatograph (GC-MS-MS) in multiple-reaction monitoring (MRM) mode with electron ionization at 70 eV; for MRM transitions, a collision energy of 6 eV was applied. Chromatographic separation was performed on a 60 × 0.32 mm DB-1 MS fused silica capillary column with a 0.25 μm film. The oven temperature was programmed from 60 °C (2 min), increased to 220 °C at 3 °C/min, and then to 300 °C at 2 °C/min (30 min). Helium was used as the carrier gas at 1.2 mL/min, with transfer line, source, and quadrupole temperatures set at 300 °C, 250 °C, and 150 °C, respectively. Nitrogen was used as the collision gas at a rate of 0.5 mL/min.

Although previous researchers have conducted work on the detection and identification of C21DAO, C25DAO, C24DAU, C24DAL, C24DAO, and C27DAO, ,, accurate identification of these six unusual compounds required further analysis in transitions, including m/z 290 → 191, m/z 346 → 191, m/z 330 → 191, m/z 330 → 287, and m/z 372 → 191 (Figure b–f). According to published research, C21DAO elutes after C20TT in the m/z 191 mass chromatogram. It is characterized by the molecular ion at M+·290 with the base peak ion at m/z 191 (Figure g), and is clearly observed in the chromatogram of m/z 290 → 191 transition (Figure b), which has been identified as C21 tricyclic terpane. C25DAO is the second to elute in these compounds, which can be further identified by the mass spectra characteristics of the molecular ion at M+·346, the base peak ion at m/z 191, and identical diagnostic ion fragment at m/z 163, 177, and 331. The mass chromatogram of m/z 346 → 191 transition confirmed the occurrence of C25DAO (Figure c). C24DAO, C24DAL, and C24DAU are typically eluted in sequence between C24TT and C24TeT, with C24DAL eluting adjacent to C24DAO in the mass chromatogram of m/z 191. The mass spectra of C24DAO, C24DAL, and C24DAU exhibit molecular ions at the same nominal M+·330, with C24DAO and C24DAU sharing the same base peak at m/z 191, and diagnostic ion fragments at m/z 163, 177, 206, and 315, which correspond to C24 des-A-oleanane and C24 des-A-ursane. Whereas C24DAL, as C24 des-A-lupane, has a base peak at m/z 163 with diagnostic ion fragments at m/z 123, 191, 177, and 287. C24DAO and C24DAU can be accurately identified in the m/z 330 → 191 transition (Figure d), whereas C24DAL is the only compound observed in the m/z 330 → 287 transition (Figure e). The mass spectra of C27DAO, identified as C27 tetracyclic terpane, show a base peak at m/z 191, a molecular ion peak at M+·372, and diagnostic ion fragments at m/z 123, 177, 329, and 357, and it is the last to elute in this series of compounds, with a significant detection in m/z 372 → 191 transition (Figure f).

MADATTS were previously detected in Eocene shale from Germany and France, Permian Kupferschiefer from Germany, , modern lake sediments of Lake Chala from Eastern Africa, Miocene sediments from Hokkaido, , and Permian to Triassic sediments from southeastern China by GC and GC-MS. The presence of base peak at m/z 145, 159, and 187 is typically diagnostic of mononuclear-aromatic terpenoid, which includes C23, C24, and C26 mononuclear-aromatic A-ring-degraded pentacyclic triterpenoids derived from either oleanane or ursane precursors. ,,, Owing to their predominantly aliphatic tricyclic skeleton and overall low polarity, these compounds exhibit chromatographic behavior similar to that of saturates and therefore appear in the saturated hydrocarbon fraction following group component separation. Referring to the identification results of Wang and Visscher (2021), this study identified five MADATTS at m/z 145, 159, and 187 (marked by Peak 1–4 and 6 in Figure a) in saturated hydrocarbon components, based on a comparison of their relative elution order and mass spectra. Meanwhile, analysis of parent-to-daughter transitions of 310 → 145, 324 → 159, and 352 →1 87 is diagnostic for MADATTS, further confirming their presence in representative crude oils (Figure b). The compounds represented by Peaks 1 and 2, which are detected in the chromatogram at m/z 145, are the only compounds observed in the m/z 310 → 145 transition (Figure b) and exhibit similar mass spectral characteristics, showing the same base peak ion at m/z 145. Both have molecular ions at M+·310, and identical diagnostic ion fragment at m/z 157, 171, and 295 (Figure e and f). Peaks 2 and 3 contain molecular ions at M+·324 with the same base peak ion at 159 and diagnostic ion fragment at m/z 171, 185, and 309, which present significantly high abundance in m/z 159 and m/z 324 → 159 transitions. The mass spectra of Peaks 5 and 6 are composed of predominantly ion fragments at m/z 187, 199, 213, and 337 with molecular ions at M+·352. Additionally, before the elution of Peak 6, this study first detected a compound with the same mass spectral characteristics and marked Peak 5 in the mass chromatograms of m/z 187 and m/z 352 → 187 (Figure a and d).

Representative mass chromatograms of GC-MS showing the presence of MADATTS (a), GC-MS-MS showing the presence of MADATTS (b–d), and mass spectra and structure of corresponding compounds (e, fragment assignments follow Wang and Visscher, 2021), for crude oils from the Baiyun Sag, Pearl River Mouth Basin.

These three pairs of compounds, observed in the mass chromatograms of m/z 145, 159, and 187, exhibit very similar fragmentation patterns, as evidenced by four series of homologous fragment ions differing by successive 14-Da and 28-Da increments (m/z 145–159–187, m/z 157–171–199, m/z 171–185–213, and m/z 295–309–337, Figure e), elucidating a des-A-oleanane/ursane tetracyclic skeleton with varying alkyl substitutions at the B-ring. Ursane-type compounds are known to elute significantly later than their oleanane-type isomers. ,, Consequently, early-eluting compounds correspond to oleanane-type, while late-eluting counterparts represent ursane-type in the mass chromatograms of each selected ion. Thus, by the analysis of mass spectra, Peaks 1, 3, and 5 are identified as C23, C24, and C26 mononuclear-aromatic des-A-oleananes (MADAO), whereas interpreting compounds 2, 4, and 6 as C23, C24 and C26 mononuclear-aromatic des-A-ursanes (MADAU), their corresponding molecular structures are detailed in Figure e (Table ).

Distribution and Relationship in MADATTS and DATTS

Several MADATTS and DATTS were detected in the crude oil samples (Figures – and Table ), and since all of these compounds possess a triterpenoid skeleton with varying degrees of ring fission or aromatization, their structural diversity likely reflects differences in both formation pathways and geochemical significance.

Distribution of MADATTS and DATTS

DATTS display relatively low abundances in WBS, PLU, and SBS, but significantly higher concentrations in NEBS and EBS (Table ). In contrast, MADATTS display more localized variations, with relatively low abundances in the WBS, PLU, and SBS, but variable levels, with both high and low values within the NEBS and EBS. Overall, the spatial variation patterns of MADATTS and DATTS are broadly consistent. However, the inconsistencies observed among sample data within the NEBS and EBS reflect that differences in crude oil sources and formation processes may exist within these subregions.

Relationship in DATTS

C24DAL (C24 des-A-lupane), C24DAO (C24 des-A-oleanane), C24DAU (C24 des-A-ursane), and C27DAO (C27 des-A-oleanane) exhibit high relative abundances at m/z 191 and possess similar molecular structures and skeletons. Their concentrations decrease progressively from east to west and south across the study area (EBS ≥ NEBS > PLU > WBS > SBS; Figure and Table ). The coefficients of determination (R 2) for their relative abundance parameters (C24DAO/C24TT, C27DAO/C24TT, C24DAU/C24TT, and C24DAL/C24TT) range from 0.87 to 0.93, showing strongly linear correlations. Such relationships indicate common biological precursors and similar diagenetic conditions that governed A-ring cleavage.

Relationship in MADATTS

Three pairs of MADATTS were identified, showing high abundances in selected ion chromatograms of m/z = 145, 159, and 187, respectively. All of them have the structure characteristics of A-ring cleavage and B-ring aromatization. As shown in Figure a–c, strong linear correlations (R 2 ranging from 0.77 to 0.90) are observed among MADATTS with the characteristics of the same base peak ion and molecular ion. Similarly, MADATTS with different molecular skeleton types (oleanane-type or ursane-type) also exhibit strong positive correlations (R 2 ranging from 0.77 to 0.91) (Figure d–f), comparable to those of DATTS, suggesting that they may also have followed a similar evolutionary pathway.

Relationship between MADATTS and DATTS

Among the MADATTS and DATTS identified in this study, three pairs of structurally corresponding compounds were recognized. These compounds differ only in whether the B-ring is aromatic, while sharing the same molecular skeleton type and identical substituent positions and types on the B-ring. Such structural relationships provide a useful basis for examining the link between A-ring cleavage and B-ring aromatization in higher-plant-derived triterpenoids. Specifically, C24DAO and C23MADAO both possess an oleanane-type skeleton with a methyl group at C-10, while Z and C23MADAU, as well as C27DAO and C26MADAO, share the same molecular structure and identical substituents at the corresponding carbon positions on the B-ring (Figures g and e). Despite these structural similarities, the abundance correlations between the corresponding MADATTS and DATTS are weak (R 2 = 0.12–0.41; Figure ), suggesting that A-ring cleavage and B-ring aromatization occurred under partially decoupled yet related geochemical conditions during diagenesis.

Affecting Factors for MADATTS and DATTS

Crude oils from the Baiyun Sag, characterized as terrestrial oil, exhibit a high Pr/Ph ratio, a cheilanthanes distribution dominated by C19 tricyclic terpane and C20 tricyclic terpane, and regular steranes primarily composed of C27 regular steranes and C29 regular steranes. , Notably, even though oils from different regions within the sag exhibit high relative abundances of terrestrial higher-plant biomarkers, ,, certain geochemical parameters still show variations among different units, suggesting that they may have distinct formation processes and material source inputs (Table ).

Thermal Evolution

The relative abundance of odd- and even-carbon-numbered n-alkanes is commonly used to assess the thermal maturity of crude oils, primarily through indices such as the Carbon Preference Index (CPI) and the Odd-to-Even Predominance Index (OEP). , Additionally, the methyl-phenan-threne index (MPI1) and methyl-dibenzo-thiophene index (MDBI), based on the differential thermal stability of aromatic hydrocarbon isomers, can also be utilized to assess thermal maturity. − The results from these parameters indicate that CPI values for the crude oils range from 0.83 to 1.20, while the values of the OEP range from 0.91 to 1.17 (Table ). The EqVR 0 values, calculated using the MPI1 and MDBI indices, range from 0.71% to 0.89% (average 0.80%) and 0.55% to 1.00% (average 0.89%), respectively (Table ). The thermal maturity differences across regions show minimal variation, suggesting that crude oils from Baiyun Sag are all derived from source rocks within the oil window. Therefore, thermal maturity does not seem to be a significant factor influencing the formation mechanisms of MADATTS and DATTS. This inference is consistent with their widespread distribution in modern lacustrine sediments, supporting the interpretation that they are products in the early stages of diagenesis. ,,,,,

Biological Sources

The oleanane distribution is widely observed in both source rocks and crude oils from Baiyun Sag, whereas ursane has not yet been reported. Only a few studies have mentioned compounds derived from A-ring cleavage of ursane-type triterpenoids, , and the present samples likewise show no detectable occurrence of ursane. Given the strong internal correlations in relative abundances within the MADATTS and DATTS groups (Figures –), it is suggested that the processes of A-ring cleavage and subsequent B-ring aromatization are the key reactions controlling the formation of compounds. To elucidate the main factors controlling the generation and enrichment of MADATTS and DATTS in sedimentary organic matter, two representative oleanane-type compounds (C24DAO and C23MADAO) were selected for further analysis. By comparison of their distributions with a range of geochemical parameters indicative of different organic matter sources and depositional conditions, this study aims to clarify the factors controlling the formation and enrichment of MADATTS and DATTS.

Oleanane, derived from betulin, taraxerene, and other pentacyclic triterpenoids produced by angiosperms, , serves as a key biomarker of terrestrial angiosperm-derived organic matter input. It has been detected at significant relative abundances in the crude oil from the Baiyun Sag, with notably lower relative abundance levels in the WBS and PLU compared with other regions (Figure ), indicating regional variations in the contribution of terrestrial angiosperm-derived material. The formation of oleanane involves a complex reaction process in which α/β-amyrin undergoes dehydration and reduction, driven by the combined microbial and thermal stresses during diagenesis. The molecular skeleton similarity between oleanane and compounds such as C24DAO and C23MADAO suggests that they may share common biological precursors. However, the conditions governing the formation and evolution of C24DAO and C23MADAO might differ from those of oleanane, as reflected by their relatively moderate correlations (R 2 = 0.48 and 0.41, respectively).

Bicadinanes are considered to primarily originate from the dammar resins secreted by plants of the Dipterocarpaceae family. − In practical geochemical applications, the (W + T)/C30 hopane ratio serves as a reliable parameter for crude oil classification in the Baiyun Sag. , In this study, the relative abundance shows a strong positive correlation with C24DAO (R 2 = 0.80), but a relatively weak relationship with C23MADAO (R 2 = 0.51) (Figure ). Bicadinanes are mainly formed through the thermal degradation of dammar resin during diagenesis, and are minimally affected by secondary alteration, , making them a reliable indicator of terrestrial higher-plant input. The strong correlation between bicadinanes and C24DAO suggests a common terrestrial source control, whereas the weaker correlation with C23MADAO reflects that B-ring aromatization requires more complex and specific geochemical conditions than those in resin pyrolysis and in A-ring cleavage of triterpenoids. This implies that MADATTS enrichment is influenced by multiple factors beyond source input.

Depositional Conditions and Diagenesis

The Pr/Ph ratio of crude oils across the study area shows no significant overall correlation with the abundances of C24DAO and C23MADAO (Figure ). In WBS and PLU, relatively high Pr/Ph ratios coincide with low relative concentrations of these compounds. This pattern likely reflects the limited input of specific terrestrial higher-plant material and the resulting scarcity of triterpenoid precursors, which decouples Pr/Ph variations from the formation of C24DAO and C23MADAO. Consequently, the Pr/Ph ratio becomes unreliable for evaluating environmental effects on these compounds, although it may still indicate general redox conditions. In contrast, in the NEBS, EBS, and SBS, where sedimentary facies correspond to delta-front and shallow-water settings, higher Pr/Ph ratios are associated with increased C24DAO and C23MADAO abundances. The formation of these compounds involves redox-sensitive steps, such as A-ring cleavage and B-ring aromatization. Therefore, their enrichment in these mixed oxic–anoxic environments likely reflects enhanced photochemical reactions and microbial transformations in the early stage of diagenesis, leading to the observed positive correlations. The variations in the Pr/Ph ratios of crude oils in these areas are closely coupled with changes in terrestrial organic matter input (Figures and ). Therefore, it cannot be simply inferred that an oxidizing environment promotes the formation and enrichment of these compounds.

Analogously, acidic clay minerals may also catalyze the A-ring cleavage and B-ring aromatization of higher-plant triterpenoids, paralleling their known role in the rearrangement of sterols. The C29-diasteranes/C29-steranes ratio (C29-DiaSt/C29-St) is commonly used to characterize the catalytic rearrangement intensity of organic matter during early diagenesis and indirectly reflects the acidity of the sedimentary environment. − A relatively strong positive correlation is observed between the C29-DiaSt/C29-St ratio and the relative abundances of C24DAO and C23MADAO (R 2 = 0.52 and 0.58, respectively), especially in regions characterized by higher terrestrial organic matter input (NEBS, EBS, and SBS), where the R 2 values reach 0.82 and 0.65 (Figure and Table ). Such relationships imply that acidic sedimentary conditions enriched in clay minerals facilitate A-ring cleavage and subsequent B-ring aromatization of higher-plant-derived triterpenoid precursors, thereby promoting the formation of MADATTS and DATTS.

Lu et al. (2022) identified rearranged oleanane in crude oils from the Baiyun Sag, indicating complex diagenetic transformations of higher-plant triterpenoids. These rearranged oleananes differ from conventional oleanane structures by an A-ring contraction from a six- to five-membered ring. Their formation and enrichment appear to be governed by the combined influence of terrestrial higher-plant precursors and acid-catalyzed rearrangement mediated by clay minerals. , Consistently, in this study, the relative abundances of C24DAO and C23MADAO show strong positive correlations with those of rearranged oleananes (R 2 = 0.63 and 0.82, respectively), particularly within C23MADAO (Figure ). This relationship indicates that, compared with A-ring cleavage reactions, A-ring rearrangement and B-ring aromatization likely share more similar initiation mechanisms, involving acid-catalyzed dehydration and molecular rearrangement under early diagenetic conditions. This finding highlights the coupled roles of acidic clay minerals and the input of terrestrial higher-plant organic matter from specific species sources in controlling the formation of MADATTS.

Formation Pathway of MADATTS and DATTS

The formation and enrichment of MADATTS and DATTS appeared to be influenced by both the input of organic matter derived from specific angiosperm species and the abundance of acidic clay minerals. Previous studies have proposed possible formation pathways and conditions for C24MADATTS and C23MADATTS (Figure , Pathway 11–2). Building upon these earlier hypotheses, the present study suggests a plausible formation mechanism for C26MADAO, which is reported here for the first time. It is inferred that C26MADAO could be generated through one of the degradation and ring-opening pathways of β-amyrin. In this process, β-amyrin undergoes dehydrogenation to form β-amyrone, followed by the photochemical cleavage of the C3–C4 bond on the A-ring, yielding the corresponding organic acid. Subsequent microbial mediation may lead to the formation of a degraded A-ring triterpenoid (C27DAO). The newly formed intermediate is then likely to undergo B-ring aromatization, catalyzed either by acidic clay minerals or facilitated by microbial activity, ultimately resulting in the formation of C26MADAO. Furthermore, molecular markers corresponding to A-ring aromatization were not identified in the analyzed samples, implying that these compounds may be restricted to certain coal-forming settings, where aromatization likely precedes A-ring cleavage during early diagenetic transformation (Figure , Pathway 2).

Application in Oil Classification

On the basis of the indexes of oleanane, bicadinanes, C24DAO, and C23MADAO in crude oils from the Baiyun Sag, this study refines the three groups previously proposed by Fu et al. (2019) and Ran et al. (2022) into five distinct groups (Figure ). , By integration of the formation and evolutionary mechanisms of molecular markers derived from specific higher plants with the spatial distribution characteristics of sedimentary facies, the genetic relationships among oils from different regions are further elucidated.

Map showing the distribution of sedimentary facies, molecular marker parameters, and crude oil groups in Baiyun Sag.

Previous studies classified the crude oils from the Baiyun Sag into three genetic groups, with those from the WBS and PLU belonging to one group, those from the NEBS and EBS constituting another, and the SBS oils in the southern part of the sag forming a third group. In the refined classification, oils from WBS and PLU remain within the same group. Sedimentary facies characteristics of the Wenchang Formation indicate that both areas were located within a large braided-river delta depositional system. Oils from these regions exhibit relatively low abundances of oleanane and bicadinanes, reflecting the limited input of terrestrial higher plant organic matter derived from specific angiosperm sources. Consequently, the conditions necessary for significant enrichment of MADATTS and DATTS were not present, resulting in relatively low abundances of C24DAO and MADATTS. The NEBS-A constitutes a distinct group characterized by moderate oleanane and relatively high bicadinanes contents, indicating a substantial contribution of terrestrial angiosperm-derived organic matter. The high relative abundance of C24DAO combined with comparatively low levels of C23MADAO suggests that the depositional environment in this area was unfavorable for the aromatization reactions required for the formation and enrichment of MADATTS, but more conducive to A-ring cleavage reactions of higher-plant-derived triterpenoids. Therefore, the oils from this region are characterized by high C24DAO contents and moderate relative abundances of C23MADAO. Oils from NEBS-B and EBS-A represent another group showing moderate relative abundances of oleanane and bicadinanes. These oils exhibit high C24DAO contents but low concentrations of C23MADAO, suggesting that their depositional environments were even less favorable for the formation of MADATTS. This group represents oils derived from sources with moderate input of terrestrial angiosperm organic matter, marked by elevated C24DAO and yet depleted C23MADAO abundances. The oils from the SBS form a distinct group of marine-influenced oils. They are derived from the Liwan Sag in the south of Baiyun Sag, which was marine during the Wenchang Formation deposition. In this marine setting, the catalytic effect of acidic clay minerals on A-ring cleavage and aromatization was largely suppressed, yielding oils with high oleanane contents but relatively low concentrations of C24DAO and C23MADAO.

Conclusions

Six mononuclear aromatic des-A-triterpenoids (MADATTS) and six des-A-triterpenoids (DATTS) were simultaneously detected in crude oils from the Baiyun Sag in the Pearl River Mouth Basin. The structures of these compounds were determined based on their retention times and diagnostic fragment ions. C26 mononuclear-aromatic des-oleanane (C26MADAO) was detected for the first time in geological samples. It is characterized by a molecular ion at M+·352 with diagnostic fragment ions at m/z 187, 199, 213, and 337, eluting before C26MADAU in the selected ion chromatogram at m/z 187 and the MRM chromatogram of the m/z 352 → 187 transition.

MADATTS or DATTS containing similar molecular structures have undergone similar formation and evolutionary processes. However, MADATTS and DATTS with the same molecular skeleton type may represent different products derived from the same precursor, which are controlled by distinct geological conditions at different stages of the diagenetic processes. The enrichment mechanism of MADATTS and DATTS is primarily controlled by the higher plant input of specific species as well as the synergistic effect of clay mineral catalysis.

Combined the relevant parameters of MADATTS and DATTS with conventional biomarker parameters and sedimentary facies characteristics, the crude oils were further classified from three into five groups in the Baiyun Sag, providing new insights into the petroleum genesis of different regions in the study area.

A Transcriptional Switch for Recombinant Protein Expression in Seed Plastids
Authors: Malihe Mirzaee, Evelyn V Wachowski, Injy H Elgamal, Pal Maliga
Published: 2026

The RNA polymerase of bacteriophage T7 (T7 RNAP) is highly selective for its promoters and is widely used for the expression of recombinant proteins in Escherichia coli (Studier et al. 1990). In plants, the plastid genes are transcribed by two RNA polymerases: the plastid‐encoded multisubunit plastid RNA polymerase (PEP) and the phage‐type, nuclear‐encoded single subunit RNA polymerase (NEP). NEP does not recognize the T7 RNAP gene 10 promoter (PT7) and leader containing a ribosome binding site in vivo (McBride et al. 1994) or in chloroplast extracts (Liere and Maliga 1999), and T7 RNAP does not recognize plastid NEP promoters (Liere and Maliga 1999), making the T7 RNAP suitable for application as an orthogonal transcription system in plastids. Here, we employ the T7 RNAP and the cognate PT7 promoters for seed‐specific expression of plastid transgenes activated by a seed‐specific promoter.

Expression of recombinant proteins in plastids has the advantages of high‐level protein accumulation, expression of multiple genes in operons, and maternal inheritance for biocontainment (Maliga 2021). However, plastid transgenes are usually expressed in leaf chloroplasts, which are perishable and require lyophilization of leaf or purification of recombinant proteins (Twyman et al. 2003). In contrast, seeds are natural storage organs that accumulate and protect the proteins during extended storage. Recently, we showed that seed plastids can serve as a platform for recombinant protein expression using a post‐transcriptional activation system based on seed‐specific expression of a plastid‐targeted PPR10 RNA‐binding protein, PPR10GG, which binds its cognate site, BSGG, located upstream of plastid transgenes (Mirzaee et al. 2024). Because this system is regulated post‐transcriptionally, the mRNA is present even in the absence of PPR10GG, for example, in leaves, resulting in recombinant protein accumulation in non‐target tissues. Background levels of recombinant proteins in a posttranscriptionally regulated system are high, ~2% of total soluble cellular protein (TSP) in leaves. Transcriptional control restricts plastid mRNA production to the target tissue, avoiding unintended recombinant protein accumulation in non‐target tissues. T7 RNAP has previously been used to selectively transcribe plastid transgenes driven by the cognate PT7 promoter, by expressing it from the nucleus and importing it into leaf chloroplasts (McBride et al. 1994; Lossl et al. 2005; Latif et al. 2022). Here, we extend the utilization of the orthogonal T7 RNAP/PT7 regulatory system for seed‐specific expression of plastid transgenes.

For seed‐specific expression of transgenes in plastids, we designed a two‐component system. The first component is a nuclear transgene encoding the T7 RNAP translationally fused to a Rubisco small subunit plastid‐targeting transit peptide (TP) and expressed under control of the soybean glycinin G1 seed‐specific promoter (pMM64). When expressed in the nucleus, the TP‐T7RNAP is translated in the cytoplasm, and the enzyme is imported into the plastids. The second component is a plastid gfp reporter gene driven by the PT7 promoter. The gfp transgene has leader sequences with efficient ribosome binding sites that are known to facilitate high‐level protein expression: the T7 phage gene 10 leader (pMM7) or the Bacillus thuringiensis Cry9Aa2 leader (pMM33) (Figure 1a). The gfp reporter genes in plasmids pMM7 and pMM33 were cloned into the TVV1 plastid transformation vector and introduced into the tobacco plastid genome by biolistic transformation (Figure 1a). Stable integration of the plastid reporter constructs and homoplastomic status of independently transformed Nt‐pMM7 and Nt‐pMM33 lines were confirmed by DNA gel blot analysis (Figure 1b).

For blotting, DNA was digested with BamHI and probed with the wild‐type BamHI fragment. For RNA blotting, total RNA from transplastomic lines was hybridized with a gfp probe. Hybridization with an AAD probe served as a transcription control, as AAD is expressed from the plastid rbcL promoter (PrbcL) and confers spectinomycin resistance for selection of transplastomic events. (c) Immunoblot detection of T7RNAP and GFP. T7RNAP is detected specifically in seeds, consistent with expression from the PG1 promoter. GFP accumulates only in seeds containing both the plastid PT7:Gfp and nuclear TP‐T7RNAP transgene. (d) GFP accumulation in seeds in the transcriptional and post‐transcriptional system. GFP levels are shown as Mean ± SE (n = 3). (e) Wild‐type tobacco (Nt‐WT) and Nt‐pMM7/pMM64 and Nt‐pMM33/pMM64 plants in the greenhouse.

In the transplastomic plants, the gfp transgene is inserted in the trnV and 3′rps12/7 intergenic region, where no significant transcription was reported from the direction of the ribosomal RNA (rrn) operon (Zoubenko et al. 1994). Indeed, probing gfp mRNA accumulation on RNA gel blots did not reveal any signals, consistent with the absence of transcription from the rRNA operon and the inability of NEP to recognize the T7 promoter (Figure 1b). It should be noted that both strands of the radioactive probe were labelled; therefore, it would have recognized RNA transcribed from either strand. The probe detected ample mRNA from the selectable marker spectinomycin resistance (aadA) gene as expected.

When we obtained the uniformly transformed Nt‐pMM7 and Nt‐pMM33 plants, we transformed them with Agrobacterium carrying the TP‐T7RNAP (pMM64) binary vector (Figure 1a) and selected for kanamycin resistance. Altogether 20 independently transformed pMM7 and pMM33 plants were obtained. We then tested GFP accumulation in the seeds and leaves of plants carrying the plastid and nuclear transgenes (Figure 1c). We found that GFP, on average, accumulated to a higher level from the T7g10 leader sequence than the Cry9Aa2 leader (Figure 1d). The T7g10 leader yielded 125.6 ± 13.6 mg kg−1 GFP accumulation in seeds, whereas the Cry9Aa2 leader yielded only 14.8 ± 1.2 mg kg−1 GFP. Compared to the post‐transcriptional PPR10GG/BSGG seed plastid expression system, which produced ~0.78 mg kg−1 GFP in seed plastids (Mirzaee et al. 2024), the transcriptionally regulated T7RNAP system increased GFP yield ~19‐fold (Cry9Aa2 leader) to ~161‐fold (T7g10 leader). GFP was absent in the seeds of a few lines, as expected in the case of position effect of the TP‐T7RNAP gene. GFP did not accumulate in the leaves of the plants, as expected. There were a few exceptions though. These can be explained by the rearrangement of the plastid DNA or insertion of the T7RNAP transgene downstream of a leaf‐specific enhancer, and have not been further investigated.

We established a transcriptional switch enabling seed‐specific expression of recombinant proteins in seed plastids. This strategy offers a conceptual advantage over the post‐transcriptional activation PPR10GG/BSGG system (Mirzaee et al. 2024). In the absence of T7RNAP, the PT7 promoter remains silent and no GFP transcript is produced, preventing unintended expression in non‐target tissues. The T7RNAP/PT7 module functions as a regulatory switch that restricts plastid transgene expression to the seed plastid. Expression of T7 RNAP in chloroplasts was shown to induce male sterility and developmental abnormalities (Lossl et al. 2005). The transplastomic plants carrying the reporter genes expressed from a T7 promoter driven by a nuclear‐encoded TP‐RNAP did not exhibit any obvious phenotype (Figure 1e). The T7RNAP/PT7 transcriptional switch expands the plastid engineering toolbox and establishes a framework for tissue‐specific expression of recombinant proteins or RNAs in diverse plant tissues, such as roots and seed pods.

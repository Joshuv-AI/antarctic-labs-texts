Control of a fixed wing unmanned aerial vehicle using a higher-order sliding mode controller and non-linear PID controller
Authors: Yibeltal Antehunegn Admas, Hunachew Moges Mitiku, Ayodeji Olalekan Salau, Crescent Onyebuchi Omeje, Sepiribo Lucky Braide
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have seen a rise in use during the last few years. Such aircrafts are now a convenient way to complete dangerous, dirty, and tedious tasks. Given that their operation involves a control problem which is non-linear and coupled, it is difficult to analyse. This paper presents the modeling and control of a fixed-wing unmanned aircraft as a contribution to this field. The system’s flight dynamics is derived using Newton’s second law of motion. The system is designed to have a non-linear Proportional Integral Derivative (NPID) controller and a higher-order sliding mode controller (HOSMC). When simulating the system using MATLAB Simulink software, an external disturbance was added to test the robustness of the controllers. Five performance indices which include mean square error (MSE), integral time square error (ITSE), integral absolute error (IAE), integral time absolute error (ITAE), and integral square error (ISE), were used to compare the controllers performance. These indices are used to provide a numerical assessment of the two controllers’ performance. The outcomes demonstrate that the roll, pitch, and yaw states performed better than the super-twisting sliding mode controller. On the airspeed control, the non-linear PID performed better than the super-twisting sliding mode controller.

Introduction

In the past 20 years, research in the area of vehicle dynamics and control is an active area of study. The goal of research in this area of engineering has been to gain a complete understanding of how vehicles behave dynamically under various driving conditions, including both the everyday moderate driving and severe emergency driving conditions. This information is used to develop creative designs that enhance vehicle operations and address problems such as ride quality and vehicle handling stability. New and creative ideas have been tested and integrated into vehicles with the help of computers to carry out complex design simulations and electronics that can be used as controllers1–3.

There are four fundamental kinds of vehicle vibration models: quarter vehicle model, bicycle vehicle model, half vehicle model and full vehicle model. Quarter vehicle model has two degree of freedom, fewer complexes to model, studies only heave motions and an overall simpler structure, but occurring subsystem interactions are neglected. Bicycle and half vehicle model has four degree of freedom and it is moderate complex to model compare to quarter vehicle model. Three degree of freedom of which are heave motion for both models, one degree of freedom of which is pitch and roll motion for bicycle and half vehicle model respectively. Full car model has seven degree of freedom and five degree of freedom of which are heave motion and others are pitch and roll motion. In full vehicle model, with expenses of complexity to model and the interactions of sub system coupling effect, it can be achieved best performance results in real application. It can also be added driver set suspension system to all vehicle vibration models and their degree of freedom is raise by one. This paper examines the effects of a vehicle vibration model using a full vehicle model that has eight degrees of freedom. There are currently three types of vehicle suspensions that are frequently used: semi-active, active, and passive. With many benefits, passive suspension is the least complicated system. However, the limitation of passive suspension in mitigating undesired vibration resulting from irregularities in the road is a drawback4–8. Actuators that are hydraulic, pneumatic, or electromagnetic that are typically positioned in parallel with a spring and a damper can be used to apply active control forces9. The biggest benefit of pneumatic actuators is that they are simple. The effectiveness of hydraulic actuators in high-power applications is widely recognized. Electromagnetic accuracy is consistently significantly higher than hydraulic and pneumatic accuracy10–13. It is commonly known that improving the performance of the suspension with an active damping system is an effective approach. Based on control techniques, a variety of active control strategies are offered to enhance ride performance. Performance will improve with a more successful control strategy14–17. Therefore, a key factor influencing the system’s efficiency is the choice of suspension control strategy.

Drones are utilized for infrastructure inspection, agricultural management, and environmental monitoring. When compared to manned aircraft or ground-based techniques, unmanned aerial vehicles frequently result in lower operating costs. Overall, an unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) combines precision and accuracy with cost-effectiveness and operational efficiency to boost safety which makes them an invaluable tool in a variety of sectors. The limitation of this paper are battery life and flight duration, complexity in operation, limited hovering capability, and higher initial cost and maintenance.

Related works

Various literature’s have been reviewed on vehicle suspension system modeling and control design of which some of them are the discussed as follows.

Authors in Ref.18 presented different control algorithms (Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR), Linear Quadratic Gaussian (LQG) control, Adaptive sliding control, H ∞ control, sliding mode control, fuzzy logic, previewed control, optimal control and neural network in terms for ride comfort. According to this article, the neural network control method outperformed the other control algorithms previously mentioned. The study expands on several control algorithms, culminating in the neural network control technique for vehicle models.

The suspension system improves noise and vibration reduction, ride quality, passenger comfort, and steering stability. The authors in Ref.19 presented a half-car active suspension model with four degrees of freedom (4 DOF). MATLAB-Simulink is used to create controllers such as the proportional integral derivative, linear quadratic regulator, fuzzy, and adaptive neuro fuzzy inference system (ANFIS). The responsiveness of these controllers was investigated by comparing the random road profile (ISO 8608) to the typical passive suspension system. The results reveal that the ANFIS-based controller outperforms existing controllers on the parameters “Settling Time” and “Amplitude” of road disturbances.

The authors in Ref.20 used an improved mathematical model to develop more effective robust control strategies for an active suspension system. In order to achieve that, a mathematical model for the vehicle’s active suspension system is obtained using the Euler Lagrange equation. To obtain a more suitable model, the dynamics of the driver’s seat are incorporated. Sturdy H 1 controllers are built into the system to reduce the impact of traffic disruptions on the car and its occupants. The driver’s seat heave acceleration and the vehicle’s rotational acceleration around its center of gravity are measured to assess the effectiveness of the active suspension system. Simulation results validate the proposed controllers’ effectiveness.

A half-car test bed was built in Ref.21, and in order to protect the driver from uneven road disturbances, backstepping, PID, and ASB controllers are used. In comparison to the passive system, the experimental results demonstrate that the back stepping, ASB, and PID controllers reduce up to 76.8%, 71.3%, and 60.9%, respectively. Matlab, Simulink, and Dspace are the software tools that are employed.

Based on variable structure control theory, the paper offers a strong approach to designing an actuator for an active suspension system that can meet all of the required design specifications while staying within the actuator’s limitations. A state space form of the quarter car mathematical model is shown. A thorough analysis of the proportional-integral sliding mode control algorithm is provided, which resolves the mathematical model’s mismatched condition issue. Two types of controllers assess the robustness and performance characteristics of the active suspension system, which is then contrasted with the passive suspension system.

The results show that, in comparison to the linear quadratic regulator approach and the passive suspension system, the proposed proportional-integral sliding mode control strategy is more resilient and effective in controlling the vehicle22. This study presents the modeling, control, and optimization of a nonlinear quarter-car suspension system. The Matlab/Simulink ® environment is used to develop and simulate a nonlinear quarter vehicle mathematical model with a seat and a driver. The input road is assumed to be a class C road with a vehicle traveling at 80 mph. PID and LQR control actions are used to actively control the suspension system. A GA based optimization algorithm is used to determine the PID and LQR control parameters rather than relying solely on intuition or a trial-and-error approach.

The optimization function in Ref.23 is modeled as a multi-objective problem with the following components: suspension space, tire deflection, controller force, frequency weighted RMS acceleration, and VDV. When compared to passive suspension systems and traditional parameters, improved parameters give better control. Additional simulations are run on the suspension system using a model of the driver and seat. The PID controller lowers VDV and RMS head acceleration to improve ride comfort. Time and frequency domain results are displayed.

In order to accomplish these objectives and outperform the current systems in suspension performance, this paper investigates the best control strategies. A newly constructed actuator is modeled, linearized, and combined with a quarter-car model to create and evaluate H-infinity (H ∞) control structures for cascaded and direct control approaches. Several simulations and input data from a real test track are utilized to evaluate controller performance. A cutting-edge controller used in a previous project is contrasted with the outcomes. The analysis in Ref.24 demonstrates how the new controllers are superior to the current ones. With no increase in energy consumption, the suspension’s performance is enhanced, particularly at low frequencies. Using road-level measurements can also result in an additional increase in control bandwidth.

The authors in Ref.25 proposed a model-free fractional-order sliding mode control (MFFOSMC) method for a nonlinear quarter car active suspension system in order to maintain acceptable limits for both dynamic wheel load and suspension deflection while also improving ride comfort. Model-free and fractional-order sliding mode control based on an extended state observer (ESO) were combined by the MFFOSMC. While the ESO is used to estimate the unknown uncertain dynamics, the model-free control is used to lower the high-order derivative output, insert necessary performances, and simplify the existing controller. To guarantee quick convergence within a limited amount of time, fractional-order sliding mode control is utilized. In the Co-Simulation Platform, a comparison with iPID, TDEC, and traditional PID controllers is carried out. According to the findings, the model free fractional-order sliding mode control (MFFOSMC) meets the system requirements by achieving a respectable trajectory tracking performance.

The inverse optimal control problem of fuzzy adaptive output feedback was examined in this study for the quarter-car ASS model using an electromagnetic actuator and a nonlinear spring. The quarter-car model’s immeasurable states were obtained by designing a fuzzy state observer. In Ref.26, a novel fuzzy adaptive output feedback inverse optimal control scheme was developed by combining the inverse optimal control principle with the backstepping design technique. The suggested control strategy can ensure that the vehicle’s attitude is stabilized and accomplish the goal of inverse optimization control. The presented control strategy’s validity has been confirmed by the simulation results.

The paper in Ref.27 deals with the displacement control of active suspension systems in vehicles using a human body model and an active seat suspension. For active suspension systems, a neural network-based dynamic surface control approach is created. To ensure that the displacements of vehicle active suspensions do not exceed their time-varying constraint limits, asymmetric time-varying barrier Lyapunov functions are used. In active suspension systems, neural networks are employed to approximate unknown functions, while non-strict form functions are handled using their basis function features. The controller’s complexity is decreased by using the dynamic surface control technique. Two simulations validate the control strategy’s benefits.

For active suspensions of cars based on a full-car model with seven degrees of freedom, in Ref.28, a H8 controller was used. The tt-framework ensures the system’s stability robustness against unmodeled system dynamics and variations in system parameters like tire stiffness and sprung mass. Its performance is compared to that of a typical LQG controller in computer simulations where a car is driven through a laterally asymmetric bump and on a regular road. It is discovered that the suspension using the Hoo controller uses less power and is more stable, with less acceleration of the sprung mass in the rolling, pitching, and heaving directions.

In order to enhance ride comfort, active damping control techniques like FOSMC, ISMC, and HOSMC are developed for the quarter-car model in Ref.29. The LQR, PID, and conventional passive suspension system are contrasted with the designed sliding mode strategies. MATLAB/Simulink simulations are used to analyze the performance of each active control on the active vehicle suspension system in terms of wheel deflection, suspension travel, and vertical displacement. Additionally, controller performances based on performance indices are computed. The ability of the STA-based HOSMC to reduce external road disturbances that affect vehicle stability and improve control performance for the FOSMC, ISMC, PID, LQR, and passive suspension system was confirmed by the quantitative and qualitative analysis.

The study in Ref.30 designed a high order sliding mode controller for the quarter car active suspension system. For comparison, a first order sliding mode controller has also been created. The goal was to increase passenger comfort during rides without compromising the vehicle’s ability to maintain a safe distance from the road, and in particular, to lessen any potential chattering in the control signal when compared to a first-order sliding mode controller. Searches with multiple objective genetic algorithms were used to determine the parameters of the controllers that were designed. Simulations have been used to obtain long time responses. An analysis of those findings led to the conclusion that the high order sliding mode controller had produced the greatest improvement in ride comfort. Additionally, compared to the first order sliding mode controller, the high order sliding mode controller required less control effort and chattered less, which is better since excessive chattering could damage the actuator and other mechanical parts of the system.

From the literature we infer that, the vehicle vibration modeling and the selection of controller are high significant on the performance of vehicle suspension system31–33. In this paper, modeling and control of a vehicle suspension system was addressed and the performance characteristics of the system i.e., percentage overshoot, rise time and steady state error was significantly improved as compared with previous literatures. Vertical vehicle dynamic model for linear 8 DOF vehicle model suspensions systems using Lagrange equation has been formulated and derived. Robust controller, HOSMC with STA is designed and its performance is evaluated under two different road disturbance inputs. MATLAB/Simulink software is used to simulate the work.

Methodology

First, a literature review was done to determine what had already been done regarding vehicle suspension modeling and controller design for ride quality and road handling in order to meet the goals of this paper. Second, use the Lagrange equation to drive the mathematical model of the suspension system of the vehicle. Third, use static timing analysis to design the Higher Order Sliding Mode Control for the model. Then, use MATLAB/Simulink to simulate and implement the controllers. Among the various advantages of the proposed system such as improved robustness reduced chattering, high precision and adaptive capability, we have identified some challenges such as computational load and implementation challenges, tuning complexity.

In summary, the proposed HOSMC offers robustness and precision at the cost of increased complexity and computational load, while nonlinear PID controllers provide a simpler and more flexible approach but may lack the robustness and precision of HOSMC in certain senarios.

Step 2: controller design

To develop the control law based on the sliding surface, the control law was designed to drive the system states to the sliding surface and maintain there tuning parameters and adjust parameters such as the switching gain and the equivalent control.

Conclusion and recommendations

Conclusion

This paper presented the modeling and control of a vertical vehicle dynamic model for a linear eight degree of freedom vehicle model suspension system using derived Lagrange equations. A robust controller, HOSMC with STA, was developed and its performance was tested under two distinct road disturbance inputs. MATLAB/Simulink program was used to simulate the work. The results show that, the amplitude and settling time of; the driver set displacement, vehicle body bounce, roll and pitch displacement, and all wheel deflections are extremely decreased in the controlled active suspension than in the uncontrolled passive suspension systems for both road disturbances. This shows that the designed HOSMC significantly improves the ride quality and road handling under different road disturbances. In order to achieve the specified objectives, firstly, literature survey was conducted to identify the gap in knowledge. Secondly, the mathematical model of the vehicle suspension system was derived using Lagrange equation. Thirdly, we designed the HOSMC with STA for the model, and implemented and simulated the controllers using MATLAB/Simulink. Finally, we compared the simulation results of passive and active suspension system.

Recommendations

Subsequent work can improve this system’s dynamic modeling by lowering the number of assumptions made, such as the mass being constant. Hardware in real-time flight and loop simulation can further analyze the suggested controllers to verify their robustness. The non-linear PID’s tuning mechanism can be expanded to include other tuning mechanisms, such as particle swarm optimization (PSO) and genetic algorithms (GA).

Development of biodegradable hydrogels with adjustable moisture retention capacity for agricultural applications
Authors: Abdugani Azimov, Aidarbek Bolysbek, Gani Iztleuov, Shyngiskan Duissebayev, Zhandos Ashirbayev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study developed and tested biodegradable hydrogels designed to improve soil water retention capacity and enhance the water stress resilience of crops. The primary research objective was to experimentally assess the effects of different hydrogels on the agroecological conditions of East Kazakhstan Region (Kazakhstan), which is characterized by an arid and sharply continental climate. The study involved three categories of agricultural land: irrigated (6 sites, 180 ha in total), rainfed (5 sites, 150 ha), and arid lands (4 sites, 120 ha). Agrochemical and biological soil analyses included assessments of climate factors, agrochemical indicators, and desertification levels. Biodegradable hydrogels were developed using microcrystalline cellulose, carboxymethyl cellulose, and hydroxyethyl cellulose, with the addition of zeolite and bentonite minerals. Physicochemical and mechanical properties of the hydrogels were measured employing various techniques, including infrared spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, and scanning electron microscopy. The study confirmed the positive impact of biodegradable hydrogels on soil moisture retention capacity, plant growth, and biomass accumulation. The developed biodegradable hydrogels demonstrate considerable potential for improving soil properties and enhancing water use efficiency in agriculture, especially in moisture-deficient regions. The results can inform the development and implementation of sustainable agricultural technologies that facilitate climate change adaptation and contribute to food security.

Introduction

Climate change, soil degradation, and water scarcity pose serious challenges to modern agriculture. These factors are especially pronounced in arid and semi-arid regions, where declining land productivity exerts a direct impact on food security1. According to the latest report by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, degradation affects approximately 33% of the world’s agricultural land, and this proportion continues to increase2. The consequences of erosion, desertification, and the loss of soil organic matter affect more than 1.5 billion people worldwide3. As documented by the World Bank, annual crop losses from drought and erosion amount to up to 8% of global agricultural output. At the same time, more than 70% of available freshwater is used in agriculture, underscoring the sector’s high dependence on water resources4. Water scarcity leads to the deterioration of agroecological conditions and the need to reconsider traditional agricultural practices. Some regions—such as Central Asia, the southern provinces of India, and California— have already recorded critical levels of water deficit5.

The use of hydrogels represents one of the most promising strategies for reducing water use in agriculture and improving the resilience of agroecosystems. These polymeric materials exhibit significant water absorption and retention capacities. In agronomy, hydrogels are used to reduce irrigation frequency, minimize evaporation and nutrient leaching, and enhance plant resistance to temporary drought. In countries experiencing severe water shortages—such as India, Egypt, China, and Australia—hydrogels have already become an integral part of integrated irrigation and resource-saving systems3,6,7.

However, the synthetic hydrogels commonly available on the market are typically produced from acrylates and acrylamides, which degrade poorly in natural environments. This raises concerns about microplastic accumulation in soils and potential toxic effects on agroecosystems. Additionally, such materials are often developed without considering local soil and climatic conditions, which limits their effectiveness. Research shows that in soils with high pH, salinity, or low microbial activity, the moisture absorption efficiency of these gels can decrease by 40–50%6,8.

In this context, research efforts are increasingly directed toward synthesizing biodegradable hydrogels based on natural raw materials, such as cellulose, starch, chitosan, or agricultural waste derivatives. These materials are not only environmentally safe but also offer modification possibilities—for example, through the incorporation of natural zeolites, which can enhance water retention and serve as slow-release fertilizers. The use of organic acids (such as citric or succinic acid) as crosslinking agents further reduces the chemical load on the environment and allows the hydrogel structure to be tailored for specific applications9.

This study focuses on developing a biodegradable hydrogel adapted to the agroclimatic conditions of East Kazakhstan Region (Kazakhstan)—one of the key agricultural areas characterized by severe deficits of productive moisture. Its sharply continental climate, wind erosion, annual precipitation of only 160–180 mm, and high evaporation rates make traditional crop-growing methods ineffective without moisture-saving technologies. Agrochemical analyses conducted as part of this project revealed that degradation, pesticide residue contamination, and desertification have already affected a large portion of the region’s arable land.

The primary research objective was to experimentally evaluate the effectiveness of various hydrogels under the arid, sharply continental climate of the East Kazakhstan Region. Additionally, the study aimed to determine their optimal application parameters for enhancing crop resilience to water stress. The present investigation examined the physicochemical properties of the developed product, its interaction with different soil types, and its potential use as a carrier for agrochemicals. The proposed approach has scalability potential not only for East Kazakhstan Region but also for areas with similar climates—such as Mongolia, Iran, Uzbekistan, and North China.

The findings may contribute to sustainable agriculture by informing strategies to enhance crop yields and reduce the chemical burden on ecosystems. Thus, this research focuses on integrating environmentally friendly materials into sustainable agrotechnologies that combine efficiency, adaptability, and reproducibility under water stress conditions. The data obtained in the study could serve as a foundation for further advancements in “green” agriculture and climate-adaptive agrochemistry.

Literature review

Over the past decades, interest in employing hydrogels for agricultural applications has risen steadily, especially in regions confronted with acute water shortages. Hydrogels are considered a promising solution for increasing the efficiency of water use in agriculture under arid and sharply continental climatic conditions. Their practical application has been actively advancing in regions such as Asia, the Middle East, North Africa, and Australia10.

Superabsorbent polymers based on acrylates and acrylamides are the most widely used hydrogels that can absorb water 300–500 times their own weight. These materials have been successfully integrated into drip irrigation and substrate-based cultivation systems. In arid regions of China, hydrogel application has increased cotton yields by 18–22% while reducing irrigation requirements by one-third11. Similar technologies are employed in controlled-environment greenhouses, where water-use efficiency improves by 30–40%12.

In addition to their high water-retention capacity, hydrogels help maintain stable moisture levels in the root zone, mitigating plant stress during critical growth stages. This is particularly important during seed germination and early root development. For example, in Central Asia and the Middle East, the use of hydrogels for cereal crops (such as barley and sorghum) has increased yields by 15–25% and extended water availability in the topsoil layer by 10–14 days compared to control plots13,14.

Nevertheless, synthetic polymers have several drawbacks, including slow degradation, potential microplastic accumulation, and toxic degradation byproducts. As a result, recent research has shifted toward biodegradable alternatives based on natural polysaccharides—such as cellulose, alginates, starch, and chitosan15. Developments in this field are advancing in countries with well-established biotech industries, where agricultural waste is used as the raw material. These biodegradable hydrogels exhibit good compatibility with agroecosystems, particularly in alkaline soils with low buffering capacity16.

Yet, comparisons between hydrogels and other innovative water-saving technologies show mixed results. For instance, biochar—produced through organic waste pyrolysis—can increase the water retention capacity of sandy soils by up to 30% while fostering soil microbial activity17. On the other hand, its effectiveness in clay soils is often limited. Other methods, such as mulching, nanofertilizers, and semipermeable membranes, provide localized benefits but require precise application and site-specific adaptation18.

Hydrogels offer significant advantages due to their versatility and structural adaptability for different agroecological zones. While some technologies, such as AI-based precision irrigation systems, demonstrate high potential efficiency, they require substantial investments and technical training for human workers. In comparison, hydrogels represent a less capital-intensive yet more flexible water-saving solution19,20.

Despite accumulating positive results, methodological inconsistencies persist in the literature. Some studies are limited to short growing cycles, fail to consider long-term effects, or employ restricted monitoring parameters. These limitations hinder the reproducibility and generalization of existing findings for different climatic and soil conditions. Moreover, there is a lack of comprehensive data regarding the long-term effects of hydrogel use on soil microbiota, soil structure, and physicochemical characteristics21,22.

Therefore, standardized, multifactorial, and long-term field studies are necessary to assess seasonal and climatic variability. Another crucial research direction involves developing multifunctional hydrogels that combine water retention with nutrient and micronutrient delivery capabilities while ensuring complete biodegradation without soil contamination. Such a comprehensive approach is essential to establish optimal conditions for using hydrogels as sustainable farming tools in the context of global climate change.

Problem statement

Contemporary climate change, water resource depletion, and soil degradation necessitate agriculture’s transition to sustainable water-saving technologies. Hydrogels have been actively implemented in agricultural practices across several countries. However, their long-term effects on soil agrophysical properties, moisture dynamics, and crop yields under various climatic conditions remain insufficiently studied. Another pressing issue is the lack of standardized strategies for selecting hydrogel types depending on specific agricultural landscapes.

The novelty of this research lies in the new data on the relationship between agrochemical indicators and land use types, as well as the correlation between soil bulk density and porosity. These factors have previously been understudied in the context of this region. Furthermore, significant variations in soil pollution levels depending on irrigation practices and precipitation emphasise the necessity of water-saving technologies. With regard to hydrogels, the study introduces new formulations incorporating components such as methyl cellulose, hydroxyethyl cellulose, and organic acids (succinic and citric). These hydrogels are promising for agricultural applications, particularly in arid conditions, due to their high moisture sorption capacity and stability. Furthermore, the study showed that agrominerals improved the performance of biodegradable hydrogels and reduced the need for fertiliser.

The aim of the study was to experimentally evaluate the effectiveness of various hydrogel types under the arid and sharply continental climate of the East Kazakhstan region. The study focused on identifying optimal application parameters to enhance crop resilience to water stress. The experiment involved monitoring agrochemical and biological characteristics of agricultural soils—levels of pesticide and synthetic fertilizer contamination—and assessing soil physicochemical properties. Additionally, the study examined the influence of hydrogels on soil moisture regimes and crop yield parameters.

Research hypothesis

The application of adapted hydrogels, including biodegradable compositions, will significantly improve soil water retention capacity and crop productivity without causing adverse environmental impacts.

Research questions

1) Which hydrogel types (synthetic vs. biodegradable) demonstrate optimal effectiveness under moisture-deficient agricultural conditions?

2) What long-term effects do hydrogels exert on soil structure and physicochemical properties?

Materials and methods

Agroecological assessment of agricultural lands in East Kazakhstan region: monitoring and analysis of degradation processes

The study was conducted in East Kazakhstan Region (Kazakhstan) from October to December 2024. The monitoring covered territories representing different land use types, which were categorized into three conditional groups:

Irrigated agricultural fields: 6 sites totaling 180 hectares. These lands are actively used for crop cultivation with irrigation systems.

Rainfed arable lands (non-irrigated): 5 sites totaling 150 hectares. These lands are cultivated without irrigation systems, making them more vulnerable to droughts.

Arid lands: 4 sites totaling 120 hectares, maintained primarily in their natural state for livestock grazing or conservation purposes. Hydrogels were not applied to these sites, as they served as controls for agroecological studies.

Each category included fields with different agroecosystems, providing a clearer basis for identifying the relationships between land use types, agrochemical indicators, and soil degradation levels. The first research phase aimed to determine agrochemical and biological characteristics of agricultural lands and assess desertification levels in the East Kazakhstan Region. A comprehensive monitoring program was implemented, including analysis of climatic, agrochemical, and biological parameters. Climatic monitoring involved regular measurements of precipitation (including snow and rainfall), soil temperature regimes, irrigation frequency and volumes, and agricultural land area. These data provided insights into trends in water availability and soil aridity under the region’s environmental conditions.

The agrochemical survey of agricultural lands included the sampling and laboratory analysis of soil from various horizons (0–20 cm, 20–40 cm, and 40–60 cm). In laboratory conditions, the following indicators were measured: humus, nitrogen, available phosphorus, potassium, sulfur, and soil acidity (pH). The analyses were conducted in accordance with current regulatory documents (GN No. 425 dated 25.06.2015) and GOST standards: GOST 26213-91 (humus), GOST 26488-85 (nitrogen), GOST 26205-91 (phosphorus and potassium), GOST 26490-85 (sulfur), and GOST 26483-85 (pH). The study covered three groups of fields, with soil samples differing by sampling depth and agroecosystem type. All measurements were performed under temperature-controlled conditions. The temperature ranged from 21 to 25 °C, and the relative humidity ranged from 54% to 66%, depending on the indicator measured.

The analysis results showed both the general fertility status of the soils and the extent of their contamination with chemical substances. Special attention was given to identifying potential accumulation of pesticide residues, synthetic fertilizers, and other chemical compounds introduced into the soil through agricultural activities. Given the increasing climate-related risks and changing land use patterns, the data served as the basis for further evaluation of soil degradation and desertification levels.

Additionally, soil samples were tested to determine bulk density and porosity. These tests were carried out using methods specified in GN No. 425 to assess soil structural characteristics and water-holding capacity. A comparative analysis was performed for soil samples collected from arid lands.

Synthesis and selection of effective biodegradable hydrogel compositions

The second part of the study focused on synthesizing and selecting an effective biodegradable hydrogel composition. The key parameters included its ability to absorb and retain moisture, as well as regulate its release depending on soil temperature conditions. The synthesis was based on microcrystalline cellulose (MCC), carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC), and hydroxyethyl cellulose (HEC), derived from agricultural residues and annual plants. A NaOH/thiourea/water solution was used to dissolve MCC and CMC. The ability of these compounds to form a hydrogel structure was then studied in combination with HEC and crosslinking agents, namely succinic and citric acids. The particle size of the hydrogel ranged from 50 to 200 μm, facilitating uniform distribution in the soil and effective interaction with soil particles. This particle size also enables the hydrogel to retain water efficiently and respond quickly to changes in soil moisture and temperature.

During the formulation stage, both the composition of the polysaccharide components and the types of acids used as crosslinking agents were varied. The mass ratio of the components ranged from 1:1:1 to 2:1:1, allowing for the optimization of the hydrogel’s structure and properties. In total, five different formulations were synthesized, including combinations of MCC, CMC, and HEC, with the addition of either succinic or citric acid. A control sample without acid-based modifiers was also prepared. Each sample was analyzed in terms of particle size (50–200 μm), pH level, synthesis temperature, water absorption capacity, structural stability, and moisture release rate.

At this stage, the goal was to determine the optimal compositions and component ratios that provided the best hydrogel-forming capacity. An effective crosslinking agent and synthesis conditions—such as pH, temperature, and reaction duration—were carefully selected. Special emphasis was placed on developing formulations specifically adapted to the East Kazakhstan Region. The design incorporated critical environmental factors identified in previous research phases: precipitation patterns (including snow and rainfall), soil temperature regimes, and agricultural water availability. The synthesized hydrogels were tested for water absorption properties, structural stability, and their potential to release moisture slowly and in a controlled manner. These characteristics enable biodegradable hydrogels to function effectively under the climate stress conditions typical of the study region.

Microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) was derived from cotton lint waste, carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) from cotton cellulose pulp, and hydroxyethyl cellulose (HEC) from the wood pulp of local coniferous trees. All of these materials were sourced as agricultural and forestry by-products in Kazakhstan, ensuring cost-effectiveness and sustainability. The polymers were dissolved in an aqueous solution of 7% NaOH and 12% urea at − 12 °C. Cross-linking was achieved by the addition of 5–10 wt% of either succinic acid or citric acid relative to the dry polymer. Zeolite (clinoptilolite) and bentonite from local Kazakh deposits were incorporated at 10–20 wt% during gelation to form composite hydrogels. The mixtures were frozen at − 20 °C for 12 h, thawed, and washed with distilled water until a neutral pH was reached. The hydrogels were then freeze-dried. The final particle size (50–200 μm) was obtained by milling and sieving.

SEM analysis (Hitachi SU3500, 15 kV) revealed a porous morphology with pore sizes of 50–300 μm. XRD (Rigaku MiniFlex 600, Cu Kα radiation) confirmed the partial amorphisation of the cellulose following cross-linking and mineral incorporation. TGA (Netzsch TG 209 F1, 10 °C/min under N₂) showed that mineral-enriched formulations exhibited improved thermal stability up to 350 °C.

Development of biodegradable hydrogels containing agrominerals

The third research phase focused on producing biodegradable hydrogel samples by immobilizing a hydrogel matrix with various types of agrominerals to provide fertilizing and biostimulating properties. The study examined the physicochemical characteristics of zeolite-containing minerals and identified the most effective modifying agents, including mineral acids that enhance the bioactive qualities of agrominerals.

Five variants of composite hydrogels were synthesized, incorporating zeolite, bentonite, and their combinations. Modification was performed using citric acid, succinic acid, hydrogen sulfide acid, and a mixture of mineral acids. Key testing parameters included moisture sorption capacity, nutrient release percentage, and effects on plant biomass growth under laboratory conditions. The obtained data were used to assess the effectiveness of the agromineral hydrogel combinations in order to select the most promising formulations for subsequent applications. This phase aimed to develop a hydrogel combining water retention properties with slow nutrient release and plant growth stimulation functions.

Evaluation of physicochemical and mechanical properties of biodegradable hydrogels

The fourth research component involved a comprehensive analysis of the developed material’s structure, stability, and functionality. Mechanical properties were investigated along with the chemical structure analysis using infrared (IR) spectroscopy. X-ray diffraction analysis examined the crystalline lattice, while scanning electron microscopy (SEM) characterized surface morphology. Thermal stability was assessed through thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). Additional studies included moisture absorption and release kinetics, temperature sensitivity, and swelling/shrinkage in various environments (saline solutions, organic solvents, different pH levels). Significant analytical focus was directed toward hydrogel biodegradation in soil, including degradation rate and duration.

Laboratory and field testing

The fifth and final phase of the study focused on the practical evaluation of the developed material’s effectiveness. Laboratory experiments simulated the processes of moisture retention and release under varying temperature conditions. The hydrogel’s fertilizing and growth-stimulating properties were assessed when incorporated into soil. To evaluate plant growth in laboratory conditions, the study used plants of the Brassicaceae family, specifically cabbage (Brassica oleracea). The experiment was conducted under controlled conditions, including regulated temperature, humidity, and light exposure. Plants were grown in pots filled with prepared soil to ensure stable access to nutrients. The temperature was maintained within the range of 20–25 °C. Light exposure was set to 12–16 h per day. Soil and air humidity were also controlled to evaluate the effects of the hydrogels on the water retention and nutrient properties of the growth medium. Field trials examined the impact of the hydrogel on agrochemical and biological soil parameters, including soil moisture capacity, bulk density, biological activity, and plant germination rates. The obtained data confirmed the potential applicability of the biodegradable hydrogel as an environmentally safe and functional tool for sustainable agriculture in East Kazakhstan Region.

Control and experimental plots were established across three categories of agricultural land: irrigated, rainfed, and arid lands showing signs of degradation. Within each category, plots with hydrogel application (experimental) and without (control) were designated. Growing conditions, soil treatment, and irrigation regimes were identical. This approach enabled an objective assessment of the hydrogel’s influence on soil physicochemical properties and crop agrobiological parameters under different agroecological conditions.

Each plot, both control and experimental, covered an area of 100 m². For each of the three land categories—irrigated, rainfed, and arid—there were three control and three experimental plots. This setup provided sufficient representativeness for statistical analysis and comparative testing between plots with and without hydrogel addition. Wheat, one of the most widely cultivated and significant crops in Kazakhstan, was grown on all plots. Wheat was chosen due to its broad distribution across various climatic zones of the country and its importance for the agricultural sector.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the research data was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics software, version 25.0. The study employed descriptive and comparative statistical methods, including one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), the Mann-Whitney U-test, and Pearson or Spearman correlation analyses, depending on variable distributions. The significance threshold was set at p < 0.05. For the first data block, ANOVA and Mann-Whitney U-tests were used to compare soil agrochemical parameters, climatic factors, and degradation levels among different plot groups. The second block applied ANOVA and multiple comparison tests to evaluate how hydrogel composition and synthesis conditions affected hydrogel formation. The third block incorporated ANOVA and correlation methods to assess differences in properties among samples containing various agrominerals and modifiers. The fourth block utilized ANOVA and Mann-Whitney U-tests to analyze variations in moisture absorption, swelling capacity, stability, and biodegradation. In the fifth block, differences between control and experimental plots were evaluated using ANOVA, the Mann-Whitney U-test, and correlation analysis. The key parameters included the soil water retention capacity, its physicochemical and biological characteristics. The assessment also focused on agrobiological indicators, such as seed germination (the proportion of seedlings relative to the total number of seeds sown), growth rates (dynamics of increase in plant height and mass over time), and plant biomass (total mass of above-ground parts). The results of the analysis were presented as mean values with standard deviations, confidence intervals, and graphical representations, ensuring reliable data interpretation.

Additionally, a moderate negative correlation was found between soil bulk density and porosity (r = -0.53, p = 0.052). This suggests a potential relationship between these parameters, particularly in the context of soil structure changes across different land use types. These results highlight the importance of considering soil types and their agrochemical characteristics when monitoring degradation processes and desertification.

The average annual precipitation varied considerably: 390 ± 15 mm at irrigated sites, 310 ± 18 mm at rainfed sites, and 220 ± 20 mm at arid sites. The agricultural land area was distributed as follows: 12.4 ha for irrigated lands, 18.7 ha for rainfed lands, and 21.2 ha for arid lands. Irrigation frequency averaged 6 times per month for irrigated lands, 3 times for rainfed lands, and once monthly for arid lands.

Soil temperatures during summer showed marked variation. Irrigated sites maintained 21.5 ± 0.9 °C, rainfed sites exhibited 23.2 ± 1.1 °C, and arid sites reached 25.8 ± 1.3 °C. Statistical analysis confirmed substantial temperature differences between sites (ANOVA: F = 11.2; p = 0.003). A strong positive correlation emerged between irrigation frequency and soil moisture content (r = 0.89; p = 0.001). A moderate negative correlation was observed between precipitation levels and the integrated pollution index (IPI) for soil (r = -0.61; p = 0.021), indicating rainfall’s diluting effect on soil contaminants.

Regarding agrochemical pollution, the IPI ranged from 0.67 ± 0.05 (irrigated) to 0.91 ± 0.07 (arid). The indicator of rainfed sites constituted 0.74 ± 0.06. These differences were found to be statistically significant according to the Mann-Whitney U-test (4.2; p = 0.008), confirming higher pollution levels under moisture-deficient conditions. Nitrate concentrations varied from 38.5 ± 2.4 mg/kg (irrigated) to 46.7 ± 3.1 mg/kg (arid). Phosphate levels measured 27.1 ± 1.9 mg/kg (irrigated) versus 33.8 ± 2.3 mg/kg (arid). Pesticide residues ranged from 52 ± 4 µg/kg (irrigated) to 66 ± 6 µg/kg (arid), with a t-test confirming significant differences (t = 3.7; p = 0.004). The monitoring results demonstrate that arid sites exhibit not only moisture deficits and elevated soil temperatures but also considerably higher agrochemical pollution levels. These findings underscore the urgent need for water-saving and environmentally sustainable solutions, particularly biodegradable hydrogel applications, to stabilize agroecosystems and reduce the chemical burden.

Synthesis and selection of optimal compositions for biodegradable hydrogels

The highest moisture retention was observed in the composition containing MCC, HEC, and succinic acid (220%), while the lowest value was recorded for the formulation without crosslinking agents (170%). This difference was statistically significant (p = 0.042). Discussion

The findings from the agroecological assessment of agricultural lands in East Kazakhstan Region provide novel insights into regional degradation processes. Analysis of agrochemical and physicochemical soil properties revealed distinct variations in humus content, phosphorus, potassium, and pH levels across different land use types. These differences highlight the importance of considering land classification when evaluating soil degradation and desertification.

Comparative analysis with existing literature

Agbna and Zaidi23 highlight hydrogels as a promising agricultural technology for enhancing crop resilience, improving soil water retention, and optimizing nutrient delivery. The authors underscore the hydrogels’ economic and environmental advantages, such as reduced irrigation demands and mitigation of soil erosion. Patra et al.24 demonstrated that agricultural hydrogels effectively manage water resources under moisture-deficient conditions by minimizing evaporation, filtration, and water loss. These improvements lead to better crop yield and quality. A study by Piccoli et al.12 provides a systematic review of hydrogel applications in agroecosystems. The results confirm their effectiveness in water-scarce conditions and for agrochemical delivery, while emphasizing the need for further research on biodegradability and impacts on soil properties. A review by Ali et al.25 discusses hydrogels as sustainable agriculture tools due to their water retention capacity, controlled nutrient release, and positive influence on agribusiness, particularly in degraded soils and water-deficient regions. Abdullahi et al.26 examined water-retaining polymers, including hydrogels, for sustainable viticulture under climate change. The authors found that these polymers reduced water consumption, improved soil structure, and facilitated erosion protection within their experiment. A study by Amboka et al.27 presents experimental data from Kenya, where synthetic hydrogels substantially improved soil moisture retention and plant survival rates. These findings suggest their potential for degraded land restoration and climate adaptation. Abdelhak28 identifies land degradation and desertification as critical global challenges. The researcher supports the use of polymer materials—particularly hydrogels—to enhance soil stabilization and prevent erosion, especially in drought-vulnerable developing regions that experience excessive anthropogenic pressure. Boutagayout et al.29 investigated hydrogel applications as an adaptive strategy for agriculture facing climate change, particularly in arid regions. Their findings indicate that hydrogels enhance crop yields while reducing water requirements. However, the authors emphasize the need for additional studies to evaluate long-term effects on soil ecosystems and environmental safety. Demo and Asefa Bogale30 demonstrated the efficacy of natural biopolymers (alginate and chitosan) as hydrogel materials for sustainable agriculture. These hydrogels enhance soil structure, moisture retention, and nutrient absorption while offering environmental advantages through their biodegradable and eco-friendly properties. A study conducted by Verma et al.31 discusses the use of “smart” hydrogels that respond to environmental changes for controlled water and fertilizer release. While these technologies show considerable promise for precision agriculture, the authors note critical requirements for standardized protocols and safety evaluations.

A common thread across all reviewed studies is their emphasis on enhancing agroecosystem resilience amid land degradation and climate change. Some studies particularly highlight the need to maintain soil water balance and optimize plant growth conditions23–25,29. These objectives align with our investigation of precipitation patterns, soil temperature regimes, and their impacts on soil agrochemical properties and structure. The importance of long-term soil monitoring, as documented by previous authors, forms a fundamental aspect of our analysis12,25,29. Our study specifically examines degradation processes through assessments of soil agrochemical and physical parameters.

At the same time, notable distinctions exist between our research and the reviewed literature. Previous studies primarily investigated hydrogel and water-retaining polymer applications as agricultural technologies that improve soil water regimes, enable controlled nutrient release, and facilitate climate adaptation5,12,23–26,28–31. For instance, Abdelhak28, Abdullahi et al.26, and Amboka et al.27 demonstrate experimental effects of hydrogels on specific crops and regions (e.g., viticulture and land restoration in Kenya). In contrast, our study does not focus on the use of polymeric materials or agrochemicals. Instead, it provides a comprehensive assessment of the current state of soils across different types of agricultural land (virgin lands, pastures, arable lands) and their degradation dynamics under local conditions.

Furthermore, Demo and Asefa Bogale30 and Verma et al.31, who examine innovative solutions such as biopolymers and “smart” hydrogels, our investigation analyzes natural agroecological conditions and conventional parameters (humus, phosphorus, potassium, pH, bulk density, porosity). We also evaluated climatic and irrigation parameters, including irrigation frequency, precipitation, and soil temperature. Particular attention was given to establishing statistically significant differences between land use types, with measurable variations identified in key agrochemical characteristics—an aspect rarely treated as a separate analytical focus in the mentioned studies.

Our study conducted a comprehensive regional assessment of agricultural lands from an agroecological perspective based on rigorous statistical analysis of multiple parameters ranging from soil chemical properties to climatic factors. Additionally, we developed and tested a new hydrogel demonstrating both high water retention capacity and rapid soil biodegradation. Unlike most studies that focus on specific technological solutions (e.g., hydrogels, polymers), we provided baseline data on natural soil conditions without the use of innovative additives. This approach enables objective assessments of current degradation levels and establishes a foundation for subsequent applications of adaptive agricultural technologies.

Several limitations should be noted. Firstly, the study was conducted within a confined geographical area, which may limit the generalisability of the findings to other regions. Secondly, the study did not capture long-term changes in agrochemical parameters, which may only become apparent after several years. Potential future climatic changes in the region that could affect soil agrochemical characteristics were also not considered. While our findings align with existing knowledge about agricultural land degradation, the value of this study lies in its detailed analysis of the factors influencing soil properties. This analysis has practical implications for developing sustainable farming recommendations in arid regions.

Conclusions

This study developed a biodegradable hydrogel containing agrominerals that demonstrated enhanced moisture sorption, nutrient release, and plant growth stimulation properties. Statistical analysis revealed measurable differences between the various hydrogel formulations across all key parameters. The highest moisture sorption capacity (210%) was achieved by samples combining zeolite and bentonite with succinic acid. Nutrient release efficiency reached 65% when using succinic acid with zeolite-bentonite hydrogel. Plant biomass growth under laboratory conditions ranged from 5% (using unmodified zeolite) to 25% (zeolite-bentonite with succinic acid). Agroecological assessment of the studied soils indicated moderate degradation levels and residual pesticide contamination from previous applications. These findings highlight the importance of developing environmentally safe and sustainable soil restoration technologies. Biodegradable hydrogels show particular promise in such conditions by improving soil fertility and phytomass production while minimizing environmental impacts.

The research demonstrated that the moisture retention and nutrient release capabilities of biodegradable hydrogels exerted positive effects on plant biomass. Correlation analysis established clear relationships between moisture sorption and plant biomass growth, as well as between nutrient release efficiency and plant growth stimulation. These results confirm the strong potential of the developed hydrogels for agricultural applications aimed at enhancing plant growth and stress resilience.

Practical implications and further research

The practical value of this study lies in the development of an effective biodegradable hydrogel designed to improve soil properties. This hydrogel enhances water retention and nutrient availability under moisture-limited conditions. Such materials could benefit sustainable agriculture, particularly in water-scarce regions where improved plant growth technologies are essential.

Future research should focus on optimizing hydrogel composition to achieve better moisture sorption and nutrient release performance. Additional studies should examine the effects of these hydrogels on different plant species under various field conditions and soil types. Another important research direction involves investigating the long-term impacts of hydrogel application—biodegradation and ecological sustainability within agricultural systems.

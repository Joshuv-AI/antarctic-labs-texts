Direct Correlation of Levels of Meibum Cholesteryl Esters, Hydrocarbon Chain Saturation, and Protein With Lipid Structure, in Relation to Age and Dry Eye
Authors: Muhammed Shah Shaji, Khanom Mahbuba, Douglas Borchman, Vance Jaeger, Djihane Machane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeMeibum compositional, structural, and functional relationships have been inferred indirectly. The current study measured the relationships among cholesteryl esters (CEs), lipid hydrocarbon chain unsaturation (HCunsat), meibum protein, and meibum structure, directly on the same samples as correlation does not necessitate cause.MethodsExactly 367 meibum samples were collected from a diverse group of donors, categorized by race, sex, and age including cohorts with individuals with (with CDED) and without (Cn) dry eye disease (DED) where “C” refers to cohort and “n” refers to normal. Meibum composition was measured using 1H-NMR. Meibum phase transition characteristics were measure using infrared spectroscopy.ResultsA decrease in HCunsat and CEs with DED results in stiffer, more tightly packed, and less mobile lipids. Meibum proteins have an opposite effect on CDED lipids than CE and HCunsat, moderating structural changes due to compositional variation. Lipid hydrocarbon chain order and phase transition temperature were higher in DED than in healthy eyes, regardless of age, but both decreased with age in DED, indicating reduced order in older donors. In meibum without protein, DED samples showed greater stiffness and higher phase transition temperatures, even at similar unsaturation levels, whereas protein presence minimized these differences.ConclusionsA decrease in HCunsat and CEs with DED would likely inhibit the flow of meibum from the meibomian glands and cause a discontinuous patchy tear film lipid layer, resulting in deteriorated spreading, decreased surface elasticity, and an unstable tear film. Therapies to increase HCunsat and CEs could be successful in treating DED.

The tear film is a 2 to 5.5-µm thick layer1–5 on the ocular surface that protects the eye from microorganisms and dust,6–8 A 40 to 74 nm layer of lipid,9–12 called the tear film lipid layer (TFLL) sits atop the tear aqueous layer. It helps tears spread evenly and keeps the tear film stable by lowering surface tension.13–16 Aproximately 80% of the TFLL comes from the meibomian glands in the eyelids,17 and the other 20% is likely to come from sebaceous glands.18 The TFLL is composed of >90% non-polar lipids primarily wax esters (WEs), cholesterol/sterol esters (CEs), and some triacylglycerols and <10% polar, amphiphilic lipids, such as (O-acyl)-ω-hydroxy fatty acid, and a small fraction of phospholipids.19 Molar ratios of CE/WE using mass spectrometric techniques vary greatly, from 0.30 to 0.85 (Table 1).

Dry eye disease (DED) is a common eye condition where the tear film becomes unstable, leading to discomfort and vision problems.20 Meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) is a key factor related to DED,21,22 1H-NMR spectroscopic studies show that people with MGD tend to have a lower ratio of CE to WE in their meibum compared with healthy individuals.23–26 They also have more protein in their meibum27 and the lipid hydrocarbon chains have fewer double bonds (more saturated)28 compared with individuals without dry eye. There are only a few published spectrometric studies of meibum from donors with MGD (see Table 1).29–31 Problems with one of the spectrometric studies31 have been addressed.32

Lipid saturation (lack of double bonds) is closely linked with lipid order and phase transition temperature, which in turn influences tear film stability and tear breakup.16,33,34 Meibum phase transitions (i.e. melting) have been measured to be near eye's surface temperature. Thus, even small changes in lipid saturation have the potential to greatly affect TFLL rheology as they can induce solidification. An increase in saturation can raise the phase transition temperature by as much as 20°C.35 The level of saturation is related to elasticity on an aqueous surface.36

Tears and meibum contains proteins such as keratin, lipocalin, lysozyme, and other surfactant proteins.37 These proteins play a critical role in maintaining the tear film's stability, viscosity, and protective functions. In healthy conditions, specific proteins within the meibum contribute to regulating surface tension, facilitating lipid transport, and providing antimicrobial defense. Meibum from donors with MGD have elevated levels of protein.27 An abnormal protein composition can increase meibum viscosity, disrupt tear film stability, and promote ocular surface inflammation.38 The compositional changes listed above may destabilize the tear film.39,40 These differences in TFLL composition have been associated with faster tear break-up times.33,41

Mass spectrometry was indispensable in measuring over 236 lipid species in tears,17 and 196 species of (O-acyl)-ω-hydroxy fatty acids,42 and the combinations of “over 30,000 molecular species” possible in human meibum.43 The 1H-NMR or infrared (IR) spectroscopic techniques cannot offer this level of specificity. However, a 1H-NMR or IR spectroscopic approach is less expensive and quicker compared with mass spectrometry. Furthermore, with 1H-NMR or IR spectroscopy the sample is not destroyed so the same sample could be used for mass spectrometric measurements, other spectroscopic techniques, or rheological studies. Complicating the quantification of each of the thousands of lipid moieties in the meibum by mass spectrometry is the need for standards for each lipid class because each class has a different ionization efficiency or response factor. The 1H-NMR or IR spectroscopic techniques do not require standards for each lipid class.

Currently, it is unclear whether changes in the CE/WE ratio, hydrocarbon chain saturation, or lipid order are related to tear film stability or are markers for and/or contributes to DED. Nor is it known how native CE influences the structure of meibum, however, there are clues.44 To bridge this gap in knowledge, the current study was conducted and is the largest structural study of meibum encompassing 164 samples without dry eye and 193 samples with MGD. The retrospective analysis of published 1H-NMR spectral data as well as the analysis of 62 new spectra of human meibum lipids, using multiple normalization algorithms and cross-validation between equations enhances the robustness of the lipid compositional measurements, an advantage over the previous studies.18,23,24,45 Unique to the current study is the analysis of human meibum lipid composition and structure on the same sample with and without meibum protein.

Methods

Materials

Deuterated chloroform and glass NMR tubes were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA. The 9-mm microvials with Teflon caps were purchased from Microliter Analytical Supplies Ind., Suwanee, GA, USA.

Clinical Analysis

Participants were assigned to the normal cohort (Cn) when visual observation of the meibomian gland orifices showed no evidence of keratinization or plugging with turbid or thickened secretions and no dilated blood vessels were observed on the eyelid margin. Cn was used in previous publications where “n” refers to normal and “C” refers to cohort.25,28 The participants did not recall having dry eye symptoms.

Participants with MGD were assigned to the dry eye disease cohort (CDED). The diagnosis of MGD was made according to the criteria of Foulks and Bron.46 Plugging of the meibomian glands in at least 5 of 10 orifices in the central portion of the upper eyelid was a requirement for diagnosis. Visual observation of the character of meibomian gland-expressed secretion had to be turbid, turbid with clumps, or paste like. Inflammation of the eyelid margin, as evidenced by swelling of the eyelid margin and 2+ vascular injections of the posterior lid margin, was necessary for diagnosis. The presence of telangiectasia of the posterior eyelid margin was confirmatory of chronic disease but was not required for study enrollment. Inclusion criteria were the presence of symptomatic meibomian gland dysfunction in subjects between 18 and 80 years of age who were not taking systemic or topical antibiotics and were not using topical anti-inflammatory medications. Exclusion criteria were a history of allergy to azithromycin, altered lid anatomy (with the exception of MGD), wearing eye makeup, inability to comprehend the informed consent, or inability to complete the prescribed therapy and follow-up. Dietary history was not assessed, but none of the subjects were taking omega-3 essential fatty acid supplements. All subjects underwent a complete examination of the eyelids and anterior segment of the eye, including measurement of intraocular pressure before entry into the study. Symptoms were measured on a four-point categorical scale of none, mild, moderate, or severe, according to the subject's response to questions regarding itching, burning, foreign body sensation, eyelid redness, and eyelid swelling. Signs evaluated were slit lamp-observed conjunctival injection, fluorescein tear breakup time, ocular surface staining with fluorescein, appearance of the eyelid margin, and character of meibomian gland orifices. All signs were graded on a four-point categorical scale, with the exception of tear breakup time, which was measured in seconds after a complete blink that followed instillation of 5 µL topical 1% fluorescein solution.

Meibum samples were collected from both eyes in most donors. Informed consent was obtained from all donors, and all study procedures were approved by the University of Louisville Institutional Review Board (IRB #11.0319, August 2016). The research followed the ethical guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Participants were recruited through the Kentucky Lions Eye Center, the Robley Rex VA Medical Center, and the James Graham Brown Cancer Center, all located in Louisville, Kentucky.

Collection of Meibum and NMR Analysis

Meibomian glands were expressed and meibum lipid was obtained from living subjects, as described by Kilp et al.,47 for the cohorts with meibum protein: Cn and CDED.24 The expressed meibum lipid was collected with a platinum spatula under a slit lamp. After the IR spectroscopic study, the sample was dissolved into 0.5 mL of deuterated chloroform in a 9-mm microvial with a Teflon cap (Microliter Analytical Supplies Ind., Suwanee, GA, USA).

For Cn and CDED measured with a 700 megahertz (MHz) nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectrometer, meibum was expressed from 4 eye lids using an ILUX instrument (Alcon, Fort Worth, TX, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The ILUX instrument warms the eye lid for approximately 90 seconds and applies gentle pressure on the eye lid to express the visible meibum magnified by ILUX.48 Approximately 0.5 mg of meibum lipid was collected per individual. The sample was dissolved into 0.5 mL of deuterated chloroform in a 9-mm microvial with a Teflon cap (Microliter Analytical Supplies Ind., Suwanee, GA, USA). These samples were immediately stored on dry ice under argon gas and transferred to a facility where they were kept at −20°C under argon gas for subsequent 1H-NMR analysis followed by IR spectroscopic analysis. Analyses were performed within 3 weeks of collection of the sample.

1H-NMR Analysis

Vials containing samples were sonicated in an ultrasonic bath (Branson Ultrasonics, Sterling Heights, MI, USA) for 10 minutes. The resulting solution was transferred into a glass NMR tube (Sigma-Aldrich) for immediate analysis. NMR spectra were obtained at 25°C using a Varian VNMRS 700 MHz NMR spectrometer (Varian, Lexington, MA, USA) that was equipped with a 5 mm 1H(13C/15N) 13C-enhanced pulsed field gradient cold probe, Varion, Inc. (Palo Alto, CA, USA). A solution of deuterated chloroform the resonance of CDCl3 at 7.24 parts per million (ppm) was used to verify the chemical shift values of the samples and standards. The same resonance was used to verify the chemical shift values of the samples and standard for both the 500 and 700 MHz NMR data.

Most of the meibum from the Cn were analyzed using a 700 MHz spectrometer. The 1H-NMR spectra were measured with a minimum of 1250 scans with a pulse width of 45 degrees and a relaxation delay of 1.000 seconds at 25°C. This process requires approximately 1.5 hours per sample to ensure a high signal-to-noise ratio. Spectral data acquired using a 500 MHz (Inova-500 spectrometer; Varian, Lexington, MA, USA) used the following parameters: 800 scans were acquired with a pulse width of 60 degrees, and a relaxation delay of 1.0-second at 25°C.

Phasing, curve fitting, and integration of the spectral data were performed using GRAMS 386 software (Galactic Industries Corp., Salem, NH, USA). After analysis, the samples were returned to their original 9-mm micro vials for storage.

Measurement of the Levels of Cholesteryl Ester and Wax Ester

The intensities of the CE resonances at 1.0 and 1.3 allowed us to calculate the molar ratio of CE/CH2 for 117 of Cn and 86 samples of CDED for the first time using these resonances. The 117 samples measured in the current study are 116% more than the 54 samples measured in the previous study.25 Thirty-two additional samples from donors with dry eye were collected and the molar ratios of CE/ (WE + CE) and CE/CH2 were measured for the first time in the current study. The intensities of the CE resonances at 1 and 1.3 allowed us to calculate the molar ratio of CE/ (WE + CE) and CE/CH2 for 86 CDED samples for the first time using these resonances. The 86 samples measured in the current study are 79% more than the 48 samples measured in the previous study25 using other resonances.

To quantify the level of CE, the following intensities of the resonances were measured: the resonance at 5.36 ppm, associated with the proton =CH- on carbon 6 of cholesterol, the resonance at 4.6 ppm associated with the proton O-CH- on cholesterol carbon 3, the cholesterol resonance at 1 ppm associated with –CH3 on carbon 19, and the cholesterol resonance at 0.63 ppm associated with –CH3 on carbon 18 (Fig. 1). The level of CE was normalized to the level of WE and CE, RCE/ (WE + CE), using Equations 1 and 2, and the intensity of the WE proton–CH2-COO-CH2- near 4.1 ppm. For the normalization of CE to the hydrocarbon chain CH2 resonances, RCE/CH2, Equations 3 and 4 were used. The intensity of the methylene resonances at 2.29, 2.0, and 1.25 ppm are assigned to protons associated with fatty acids (-CH2-COO-), =CH-CH2, and -CH2- moieties, respectively.

As a gage of experimental error, and deviation of RCE/ (WE + CE) and Runsaturation/ (WE + CE) from person to person, samples were collected from a Caucasian 53-year-old man without DED once (age at first collection) then again after 13 and 15 years. Then at the age of 68-years-old, samples were collected 5 times, a week apart. The idea is, that if the variation in the data from one person measured over a 15-year period was small, the experimental deviation must also be small.

Hydrocarbon Phase Transitions Measured Using Infrared Spectroscopy

Lipid phase transition parameters were determined following the methodology described.49 In brief, the conformational state of lipid hydrocarbon chains was evaluated based on the relative proportions of trans and gauche rotamers, where trans configurations correspond to more ordered chains and gauche configurations indicate disorder. The frequency of the CH2 symmetric stretch, ν˜ sym , is dependent on the amount of trans or gauche rotamers50 and has been widely used to characterize lipid phase transitions,49–60 providing a measure of trans rotamer content as a function of temperature.49–54,59,60 Because each rotamer exists in either the trans or gauche state, plots of ν˜ sym versus temperature can be modeled by a two-state sigmoidal equation shown below, as described.49(7)ν˜sym=P1+P2/1+P3/TP4

The phase transition data were fitted to Equation 7 using the four-parameter logistic model implemented in SigmaPlot software, version 10 (Richmond, CA, USA). The lipid order at 33.4°C was obtained by extrapolating the ν˜ sym value from the fitted phase transition curve and converting it to the percentage of trans rotamers, a quantitative measure of lipid conformational order,50 using Equation 8. (8)%transrotamers=100X(2855.36-ν˜sym)/(2855.36-2848.00)

To estimate the Van't Hoff enthalpy (ΔH) of the lipid phase transition, Arrhenius plots of ln (% trans rotamers) versus 1/T (T in kelvin) were generated following the method described.60 The slope of the linear region near the transition temperature was used to calculate ΔH. The corresponding change in entropy (ΔS) was determined from the relationship ΔS = ΔH/T, assuming equilibrium conditions (ΔG = 0), where T is the phase transition temperature. The Arrhenius plots obtained for tear lipid phase transitions exhibited excellent linearity, with correlation coefficients exceeding 0.998.

Spectra were collected using a Nicolet 5000 Magna Series FTIR spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Anhydrous lipid samples were deposited on an AgCl window and placed in a temperature-controlled IR cell jacketed with an insulated water coil connected to a Neslab R-134A circulating water bath (NESLAB Instruments, Newton, NH, USA). Temperature was monitored by a thermistor in direct contact with the sample cell window, and the water bath was programmed to maintain the set temperature within ±0.1°C. Heating and cooling rates were controlled at 1°C every 15 minutes.

At each temperature point, 300 interferograms were recorded and averaged at a spectral resolution of 1.0 cm⁻¹. IR data were analyzed using GRAMS/386 software (Galactic Industries, Salem, NH, USA). The OH–CH stretching region (3500–2700 cm⁻¹) was baseline-corrected, and the center of mass of the CH₂ symmetric stretching band was calculated by integrating the top 10% of the band intensity using a baseline parallel to the corrected OH–CH region. For validation, CH₂ stretching frequencies were also determined via curve fitting of the full CH stretching region.

Statistical Analysis

The Student's t-test was used to measure P values for averages. Critical values of the Pearson Product-Moment Correlation Coefficient were used to measure P values to determine linearity. Data are presented as averages with the standard error of the mean.

Factors Related to Lower Lipid Order of CDED Compared With Cn

Three compositional factors are likely to influence meibum structural order: meibum protein, CE, and saturation. A lower level of saturation with DED has been published (see the opening paragraph).

Experimental Error Associated With Measuring Cholesteryl Esters

As a gage of experimental error, and deviation of RCE/ (WE + CE) from person to person, samples were collected from a Caucasian 53-year-old man without DED once (age at first collection), then 5 times, a week apart, after 13 and 15 years. For the average RCE/ (WE + CE), using Equations 1 and 2 were: 0.43, 0.43, 0.39, 0.43, 0.43, 0.41, and 42 for sample collected once, then 5 times, a week apart, and after 13 and 15 years, respectively. The relative deviation of these values was 3%. As the relative deviations of RCE/ (WE + CE) from participant to participant were much greater than 3%, they are not likely due to experimental error. Relative to the level of hydrocarbons, RCE/ CH2, the level of CE did not change significantly with age (P = 0.42, r = 0.00951, n = 70).

RCE/ (WE + CE) was significantly higher for Hispanics compared with Asians in Cn, P = 0.039 (see Table 5). All other differences between races and sex were not significant, P > 0.05.

Discussion

Previous studies related to meibum lipid structure (stiffness), composition, and function were done on separate cohorts. As correlation does not necessitate cause, the current study measured meibum lipid composition and structure on the same samples. For instance, we found that in different cohorts, meibum protein levels increased with MGD27 (see Fig. 3) as did lipid order60 leading one to suspect that protein caused the increase in lipid order. However, in the current study, we found the opposite was true. Concomitant with higher levels of protein, CE and HCunsat also increased and that protein was found to decrease lipid order in the same sample illustrating the need to measure composition structure and function on the same samples.

The major findings of the current study are (1) lipid order was higher with CDED compared with Cn; (2) hydrocarbon chain order and lipid phase transition temperatures are higher in CDED compared with Cn even for younger participants (3 to 39 years old); (3) CE was lower in CDED compared with Cn; (4) HCunsat was lower in CDED compared with Cn; and (5) compositional variation was due to individual variations and not due to experimental variation.

Advantages of the Current Study Over Our Previous Spectroscopic Studies

Unique to the current study is the large number of samples studied, the use of multiple normalization algorithms and cross-validation between equations that enhanced the robustness of the lipid ratio measurements, the higher sensitivity of the 700 MHz spectrometer, and the larger amount of meibum collected 0.5 mg compared with ng amounts per individual, all of which contributed to a more accurate measurement of meibum lipid composition.

A major contribution of the current study is that meibum lipid composition was measured in 239 samples, the largest meibum lipidomic study yet published. Another advantage of the current study is that the samples measured using the 700 MHz spectrometer allowed the use of the 1 and 0.65 ppm resonances as well as the 5.36 resonance were used to measure CE in contrast to just using the 4.6 ppm resonance resolved in the 500 MHz spectrometer.25 The 1 and 0.65 ppm resonances are 6 times more intense than the 4.6 ppm resonance resulting in lower variability and a better signal to noise ratio compared to the previous study.25

A unique aspect of the current study is that the higher resolution of the 700 MHz spectrometer allowed the quantification of the methylene resonances at 2.29, 1.25, and 2.0 ppm that were used to normalize the level of CE, an advantage over the previous study.25

An advantage of the current study over our previous studies23,60 is that all of the meibomian glands were expressed in the current study compared with only five glands expressed in the previous study. The amount of meibum collected in the current study was more than 8 times greater, 0.5 mg than that of the previous study23,60 providing better signal-to-noise ratio.

Meibum CE Content Changes With AG

For Cn, meibum CE content decreased significantly with age, but only by 0.2%, so the magnitude of the change with age is likely not to contribute to meibum structure or function. Age was also not a factor for CDED. For Cn, 164 meibum samples from donors aged 1 to 83 years old were analyzed, the largest age-related study of CE to date. It is likely that the large variability with age, r = 0.2023, for the linear regression line of CE versus age, could explain why a smaller study of 7 infants, 7 children, and 27 adults indicated CE was higher in the adult cohort compared with the child cohort.24 A later study showed that there was no significant change, P = 0.23, in the level of CE between a cohort of 39 donors 1 to 12 years of age and a cohort of 38 donors older than 20 years old.61 It is noteworthy that teenagers have a higher amount of CE compared with children and adults.61 Age and the prevalence of MGD62,63 and the prevalence of DED in general are related.39,40,62–64 The current study supports the idea that CE differences with age are unlikely to contribute to DED.

Sex and race did not significantly affect RCE/ (WE + CE), except for a higher RCE/ (WE + CE) in Hispanics versus Asians in Cn. These findings support the interpretation that although demographic factors may have subtle effects, systemic disease and age are more influential in altering meibum lipid profiles.

Spectral reproducibility was evaluated through repeated sampling of one individual over time. The relative deviation of RCE/ (WE + CE) values across 5 weekly samples and 2 additional time points (after 13 and 15 years) was only 3%. For RCE/CH2, the relative deviation was 6.5%. The low intra-individual variation compared with the inter-group variation indicates inter-group variation is unlikely due to experimental variability.

Measurements from both the 500 MHz and 700 MHz spectrometers were consistent, although data from the 500 MHz instrument were more variable.

The current study is the largest age-related structural study of meibum, encompassing 162 samples without dry eye and 192 samples with MGD. Measurement of meibum lipid phase transitions took 4248 technician-active-hours to complete. Unique to this study is the finding that hydrocarbon chain order and lipid phase transition temperatures are higher in CDED compared with Cn even for younger participants (3 to 39 years old). Higher hydrocarbon chain order and phase transition temperatures result in “stiffer,” more tightly packed and less mobile lipids. One may expect that more ordered lipid could inhibit the flow of meibum from the Meibomian glands. On the ocular surface, more ordered lipids are likely to form a discontinuous patchy TFLL, resulting in deteriorated spreading and decreased surface elasticity.65 One of the earliest meibum surface film studies confirm this idea.66 Indeed, meibum has a high surface tension resulting in poor spreading.67–79 A more ordered lipid would be expected to attenuate the capability to restore TFLL structure between blinks.

Although a major change in meibum lipid order occurs between birth and 1 year of age,16 in the current study, lipid order did not change with age above 2 years old. Surface rheology studies on eight human meibum samples indicate that the reciprocal compressibility of meibum lipid increases with age, indicating a stiffer, more elastic lipid layer forms on an aqueous surface with age.33 The apparent contradictory results, lack of change in lipid order with age and change in the reciprocal compressibility with age, may be because elasticity and meibum hydrocarbon chain order are not necessarily related when meibum structural order is high.66

Meibum Lipid Compositional Structural Relationships in the Same Sample

The current study examines which compositional changes with DED cause a change in lipid order. Three major compositional changes between CDED compared with Cn have been reported in the current study and others (1) an increase in lipid saturation28; (2) a decrease in the CE/WE ratio70; and (3) an increase in meibum protein levels.27

Meibum Lipid Hydrocarbon Chain Unsaturation, Compositional, Structural, and Functional Relationships

A major finding of the current study is that, for meibum lipid extract devoid of protein, HCunsat was found to decrease the order and phase transition temperature of meibum lipids. This result agrees with a study in which human meibum was catalytically saturated.71

A 1H-NMR study of human meibum indicated that meibum HCunsat decreases with DED, Sjögren's syndrome, and Parkinson's disease compared with Cn.28 Rheological studies show that a decrease in HCunsat results in the formation of stiffer, thicker, and more elastic films with the effects being proportional to the saturation level and altered spreading and heterogeneous lipid layer structure.71 Thus, a reduction in HCunsat with DED is expected to contribute to lower breakup times and higher blink rates.

Meibum CE Compositional, Structural, and Functional Relationships

The rigor of recent 1H-NMR and IR spectroscopic studies of human meibum indicate that the level of CE decreases with MGD.23–26,60 The current study confirms the 1H-NMR and IR spectroscopic studies. The current study found that a decrease in CE increased meibum hydrocarbon chain order and phase transition temperature and decreased cooperativity between lipids. This is a novel finding as there are limited studies on the relationship between CE and meibum lipid structure. Although model synthetic CE studies have been made,26 because the hydrocarbon chains of CE and WE are a complex mixture of chain lengths, saturation, and branching,19,43,46,72,73 results from model systems of simple synthetic lipids are questionable. In a previous study, CE and WE were separated from a 66-year-old Caucasian man and sequentially mixed together.44,71 Based on this limited study, a lower CE/WE ratio, as observed in MGD, is likely to increase cohesion between lipid molecules, leading to the formation of abnormally rigid lipid aggregates that exhibit restricted or uneven spreading at the air–tear interface. Such structural rigidity is a characteristic feature of the lipid layer in MGD and may contribute to a heterogeneous tear film with impaired reorganization during blinking and reduced dilatational elasticity.71 Another finding of the rheology study,71 was that differences in the hydrocarbon chain composition of CE did not influence the surface rheology results. The rheological study involved only one donor44,71 but supports the more extensive findings of the current study.

The values obtained for CE/WE molar ratios using 1H-NMR, 0.52 ± 0.05 mol:mol, were within the range of values obtained using mass spectrometry, 0.30 to 0.94 mol:mol (see Table 2). The current study was one of the first to document that the large variation of CE/WE molar rations is due to variations from person to person and not due to spectroscopic experimental deviation. Future studies related to experimental deviation using spectrometry are warranted.

Meibum Protein Structural Relationships

The current study measured the influence of meibum protein on meibum phase transitional characteristics for the first time. Meibum protein levels increase with MGD and decrease with age.27 Meibum protein decreased the magnitude of the phase transition indicating that lipids in the annulus surrounding the proteins were not involved with the meibum lipid phase transition. The maximum frequency decreased with protein for both Cn and CDED. The maximum frequency is a gauge of the maximum amount of fluid lipids at higher temperatures. A decease in the maximum frequency suggests that the meibum proteins preferentially segregate with and bind to more fluid lipids. The proteins, β-carotene and keratin at 50% did not affect the phase transition of human meibum so other proteins must be involved.74 A novel role of meibum proteins is to moderate meibum structural changes due to lipid compositional changes with age and DED.

For Cn, protein has little effect on meibum lipid order and phase transition temperature, but for CDED, meibum protein tempered the contribution of HCunsat to increase lipid order with DED. This may be because CDED has more meibum protein compared with Cn.27 The influence of HCunsat to decrease the order, phase transition temperature of meibum lipids is countered by meibum protein which has an opposite effect. Similarly, meibum protein also tempered the contribution of CE to decrease the order, phase transition temperature and increase the cooperativity of meibum lipid. Protein lowered the meibum hydrocarbon chain order and phase transition temperature of CDED to near that of Cn.

A decrease in HCunsat and CE with DED result in “stiffer,” more tightly packed and less mobile lipids which could inhibit the flow of meibum from the meibomian glands and are likely to form a discontinuous patchy TFLL,65,69 resulting in deteriorated spreading and decreased surface elasticity. Meibum proteins moderate structural changes due to meibum compositional changes within the CDED cohort alone.

The results also suggest that meibum proteins may play a role in disrupting the stability of meibum lipids; however, the mechanisms by which these proteins exert their effects remain unclear. Future studies characterizing the structure and function of well-known meibum proteins and exploring how lipids organize around them would be prudent. Probing protein function in non-aqueous environments presents unique challenges, as most biophysical characterization techniques are designed for aqueous systems. Molecular modeling may be essential for gaining insight into protein–lipid interactions and identifying the fundamental chemical mechanisms underlying the proteins’ moderating effects.

The major findings of the current study are:i.Lipid order was higher with CDED compared with Cn;ii.hydrocarbon chain order and lipid phase transition temperatures are higher in CDED compared with Cn even for younger participants (3 to 39 years old);iii.CE was lower in CDED compared with Cn;iv.HCunsat was lower in CDED compared with Cn;v.compositional variation was due to individual variations and not due to experimental variation.

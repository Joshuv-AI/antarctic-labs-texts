Site-Specific Labeling of PD-L1 Fab through Disulfide Rebridging for ImmunoPET Imaging
Authors: Jiyao Yu, Charles Yen, Qunfeng Fu, Sheng-Yao Dai, Melwin Cheng, Jianghong Rao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Immuno-positron emission tomography is a powerful tool for noninvasive in vivo visualization of antibody distribution and binding in tissues or organs. However, the current chemical toolbox for generating immunoPET probes compromises key attributes such as homogeneity, structural integrity, and batch-to-batch reproducibility. Quinoxaline bis-nitriles have been reported to site-selectively label antibodies via disulfide bond rebridging with robust efficiency and stability. In this study, we applied this approach to establish this chemistry for rapidly generating structurally defined Fab-based probes for in vivo imaging. Initially, we employed a NODAGA-functionalized bis-nitrile derivative to generate a PD-L1 PET probe for in vivo PET imaging with 64Cu. Next, a functional handle was installed on the quinoxaline bis-nitrile scaffold to enable site-specific chemical PEGylation of Fab. This modification resulted in an immunoPET probe with reduced renal uptake and an improved signal-to-noise ratio. Overall, the results demonstrate that this labeling chemistry is robust and versatile for generating functional Fab conjugates for PET imaging in cancer research.

Introduction

Immuno-positron emission tomography (immunoPET) combines the target specificity of monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) with the sensitivity of positron emission tomography (PET) imaging, enabling quantitative measurement of antigen expression at the whole-body level. , With Abs radiolabeled with positron-emitting isotopes, researchers can quantify their biodistribution and binding to antigen targets in real time. , Thus, immunoPET facilitates disease monitoring and improves therapy outcome predictions, making it a powerful tool for both research and clinical applications. Traditionally, radiolabeled Abs are generated through stochastic conjugation between the primary amine side chain of lysine (Lys) residues and electrophilic reagents, such as N-hydroxysuccinimides (NHS) esters, Traut’s reagent, or isothiocyanates. − Due to the abundance of solvent-accessible Lys residues, such stochastic labeling produces highly heterogeneous products, resulting in suboptimal immunoreactivity and poorly defined affinities for target antigens. − Stochastic labeling of Abs through Lys or reduced Cys residues yields heterogeneous antibody-drug conjugates that have been associated with aggregation, increased toxicity, and inconsistent pharmacokinetics (PK). ,− To address this problem, new chemistries have been developed to achieve well-defined homogeneous radioimmunoconjugates including (i) modifying the heavy chain glycan of mAbs with azide-bearing unnatural sugars for strain-promoted cycloaddition, ,− (ii) conjugating the C-terminus of target protein with metal chelators via sortase-mediated transpeptidation with an engineered recognition motif (LPXTG), − and (iii) site-specific mutagenesis ensued by side chain specific chemical transformation. ,− These methods rely on chemoenzymatic and genetic engineering to install specific sites for labeling. , However, most commercial antibodies and Fabs are distributed without disclosing protein sequences. Consequently, rebridging solvent-accessible interchain disulfide bonds has emerged as an attractive alternative for site-specific labeling, as most IgG mAbs contain four accessible disulfides and antigen-binding fragments (Fabs) contain only one. Hence, the repertoire of rebridging reagents has been expanded tremendously over the past decade, including next generation maleimides (NGMs), pyridazinediones (PDs), − bis-sulfones/allyl sulfone derivatives, − divinylpyrimidines (DVPs), , divinyltriazines (DVTs), , arylene-dipropiolonitriles (ADPN), diethynyl phosphinates, and oxSTEF. While most of these linkers lack inherent handles for further functionalization, seminal work spearheaded the chemical generation of multivalent bispecific synthetic antibodies (SynAbs) and checkpoint inhibitory T cell engagers (CiTEs) via inverse electron-demand Diels–Alder (iEDDA) reactions between tetrazine and strained alkyne (BCN) containing dibromopyridazinedione (Br2PD) rebridged Fabs. , Akin to other reagents, Br2PDs impose a degree of structural deviation as the S–S distance is inevitably elongated from rebridging. Although antigen-binding affinities of full-length antibodies are typically preserved after rebridging, − peptide-based PET probes are more susceptible to such structural perturbation. Furthermore, Br2PD-mediated interchain disulfide bond rebridging is reversible at physiological pH, − which can affect the long-term stability and immunoreactivity of the protein conjugates. Pyrazine bis-nitriles, as recently described by the Baker lab, are intrinsically selective for adjacent bis-thiols. Unlike the aforementioned reagents that react sequentially with monothiols via addition–elimination pathways, bis-nitrile conjugation undergoes a proximity-dependent mechanism. The initial thiol addition to the nitrile group is reversible and forms a transient thioimidate intermediate. Productive conjugation is only favored upon engagement of a second proximal thiol, resulting in an amino dithioacetal trapped by cascade cyclization (Scheme ). The cyclic amino dithioacetal linkage is stable in the presence of a large excess of competing free thiols, a key advantage for postrebridging functionalization (e.g., thiol-Michael or thiol-click reactions), or iterative rounds of disulfide rebridging. Consistent with this mechanism, quinoxaline bis-nitriles can readily cross-link reduced IgG Fab to form stable one-carbon bridged dithioacetal adducts with stoichiometric control. The resulting stoichiometrically labeled Fab conjugates remained stable in the presence of excess glutathione and largely retained their binding property.

Full-length IgG Abs (∼150 kDa) typically exhibit long circulation time (1 day to 1 week) and poor tumor penetration. On the other hand, Fab fragments (∼50 kDa) can render better tumor penetration and rapid blood clearance, while retaining the binding specificity of IgG Abs. In this study, we selected a mouse αPD-L1 Fab to construct an NODAGA-αPD-L1 Fab probe and evaluated its performance for in vivo 64Cu-PET imaging, without requiring the amino acid sequence. We then further improved the chemical utility by incorporating an additional functional handle for chemical PEGylation of the Fab conjugate. The resulting immunoPET probe produced consistent PET images of tumors in vivo with high sensitivity and specificity. This method will help address long-standing challenges in batch-to-batch reproducibility and mitigate the pharmacokinetic constraints inherent to chemically labeled Fab-based probes.

Previous murine immunoPET studies have frequently employed stochastically labeled antibodies, in full-length or fragment formats, to evaluate PD-L1 expression in vivo. Despite adequate binding affinity and, in the case of full-length IgG, suitable systemic circulation, these randomly labeled PET probes often exhibited modest to low tumor uptake accompanied by substantial activity in clearance organs. − Fab-based probes supported imaging at early time points (1–6 h) but generally achieved lower maximal target retention with elevated kidney and bladder activity.

Importantly, tumor uptake depends strongly on PD-L1 expression levels, with parental CT26 tumors showing substantially lower accumulation than the PD-L1 overexpressing cell line. , In a related study, site-specific radiolabeling was applied to a reduced IgG to monitor therapy-induced modulation of tumor PD-L1 expression in vivo. While this strategy generated a chemically defined PET probe, tumor uptake of the 89Zr-labeled tracer remained low in mice bearing wild-type CT26 tumors. Notably, persistent radioactivity was observed across multiple vital organs, including heart, liver, kidney, and lung, even at extended imaging time points (up to day 5). , Together, these findings indicate the dominant roles of antigen density, probe format, and clearance mechanisms in monitoring PD-L1 expression by immunoPET.

Results and Discussion

Design and Synthesis of Site-Specific Adjacent Bis-Thiol Rebridging (SABRE) Chelators

To establish rapid production of immunoPET probes with a defined 1:1 stoichiometric ratio to radiotracers, we devised a chimeric chemical modality featuring a bis-thiol-reactive warhead for site-specific labeling of Fabs and a metal chelator for radiolabeling. An additional azide handle may be introduced to support chemical PEGylation as a strategy to modulate the PK properties of the immunoPET probe. 64Cu (t 1/2 = 12.7 h; Eβ+ = 653 keV, 17.4%) garnered interest due to its suitable half-life for Fab imaging. Among available macrocyclic chelators for 64Cu, NODAGA enables efficient complexation under mild conditions with good radiochemical yields. , To evaluate this design concept, we synthesized a site-specific adjacent bis-thiol rebridging chelator, SABRE-1, which incorporates a NODAGA chelator for radiometal coordination and a quinoxaline bis-nitrile scaffold for 1:1 labeling via disulfide rebridging (Scheme ). The design of SABRE-2 features an additional azide functional handle to facilitate orthogonal bioconjugation, such as strain-promoted azide–alkyne cycloaddition (SPAAC) or copper-catalyzed azide–alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC). This SABRE-2 design permits facile attachment of poly­(ethylene glycol) (PEG) for PK optimization, or a near-infrared (NIR) fluorophore for in vivo optical imaging.

Site-Specific Labeling of αPD-L1 Fab Immunoconjugates

PD-L1 is a broadly expressed antigen across a range of tumor types and plays a key role in regulating tumor immune evasion. The expression of PD-L1 is dynamically influenced by the tumor microenvironment, making it an important target for PET imaging. We first generated antimouse PD-L1 Fab by digesting the commercially sourced full-length IgG αPD-L1 with immobilized papain, followed by purification using protein A/G spin columns. The digested Fab was analyzed by SDS-PAGE and liquid-chromatography mass spectrometry (LC-MS) to confirm its purity and expected protein mass (Figures D and S1). Incubation of digested Fab (35–80 μM) with 10 equiv of TCEP for 90 min at 37 °C generated reduced Fab (Fabred) with two free cysteines. Subsequent treatment of Fabred with excess SABRE-1 readily produced NODAGA-Fab, as confirmed by both SDS-PAGE and LC-MS (Figure B,E). In light of previous studies demonstrating that site-specific installation of a PEG moiety on VHHs can improve signal-to-noise ratio and reduce kidney uptake, − and given that Fab-based immunoPET probes have been reported to exhibit substantial renal absorption in vivo, ,, we turned our attention to chemically PEGylating the αPD-L1 Fab following successful disulfide rebridging with the SABRE-1 chelator. Several groups have reported murine immunoPET studies aimed at noninvasive longitudinal monitoring of immunodynamics, in which 20 kDa PEG was installed on various antibody fragments to extend circulation and improve target-to-background contrast. ,,, This PEG size is generally compatible with efficient radiometal-labeling, with reported radiochemical yields often exceeding 80%. Across Fab or Fab’-like formats, 20 kDa PEG has been employed as a practical compromise that can extend blood half-life while largely preserving binding. , Accordingly, we selected a 20 kDa PEG as a pragmatic approach to prolong circulation, while avoiding the risk of off-target radiotoxicity induced by an excessively large PEG polymer. The initial strategy, relying on the SPAAC reaction between SABRE-2 rebridged Fab and a large excess of DBCO-mPEG (20 kDa) did not successfully produce PEGylated Fab conjugates, possibly hindered by limited solvent accessibility at the protein site. Likewise, installation of DBCO-AF 488 to SABRE-2 rebridged Fab showed low conversion efficiency (Figure S5). In the optimized protocol, SABRE-2 was functionalized with commercially sourced DBCO-mPEG (20 kDa) via SPAAC (Scheme S1). Excess small molecules were then removed using a centrifugal filter (3 kDa MWCO). The resulting polymer (SABRE-2 PEG) was lyophilized and dissolved in DMSO to a final concentration of 10 mM. Nearly quantitative production of NODAGA-PEG-Fab was achieved by incubating Fabred with 10 equiv of SABRE-2 PEG at ambient temperature, as confirmed by SDS-PAGE (Figure K).

Evaluation of Rebridged αPD-L1 Fab Immunoconjugates

Prior to PET imaging, we evaluated the impact of disulfide rebridging and PEGylation on the binding properties of Fab constructs. Using biolayer interferometry (BLI), we measured and compared the association rate constant (k on), dissociation rate constant (k off), and equilibrium dissociation constant (K d) values of the Fab constructs (Figure S4 and Table ). The full-length αPD-L1 Ab exhibited the strongest binding affinity, primarily driven by its slow off-rate (k off < 1.00 × 10–7 s–1). Among the Fab constructs, the parental Fab and NODAGA-Fab displayed comparable kinetic profiles, with moderate association rates (k on = 2.22 × 105 M–1s–1 and 1.98 × 105 M–1s–1, respectively) and similar dissociation rates (k off = 3.39 × 10–4 s–1 and 3.41 × 10–4 s–1, respectively), indicating bis-nitrile rebridging exerts a minimal effect on binding affinity. NODAGA-Fab-PEG maintained a similar k on (2.76 × 105 M–1s–1), albeit with a nearly 5-fold increase in k off (1.74 × 10–3 s–1). Despite the distal position of the PEG chain relative to the paratope (Figure S13), the hydrated PEG chain may impose an entropic incentive during ligand dissociation, which could overestimate the apparent k off. The serum stability of rebridged Fab conjugates was assessed with a fluorescent surrogate in a biological milieu. To this end, SABRE-2 fluorophore was prepared via SPAAC between SABRE-2 and DBCO-BDP (Scheme S2), resulting in a pair of isomers (SABRE-2 BDPs). Freshly prepared Fabred was readily rebridged with an excess of SABRE-2 BDPs (10 equiv) at ambient temperature. Upon completion, excess reagents were removed via ultracentrifugation, yielding a fluorescent NODAGA-Fab-BDP conjugate (Figure S6). Successful production and purity of NODAGA-Fab-BDP were confirmed by LC-MS and SDS-PAGE (Figure F,G). NODAGA-Fab-BDP was incubated in human serum at 37 °C with aliquots collected at 4, 8, and 24 h. Each aliquot was diluted, and serum albumin was removed using filter columns (50 kDa MWCO). LC-MS analysis revealed that NODAGA-Fab-BDP retained its original retention time after 24 h, with no detectable peak corresponding to free SABRE-2 BDP isomers (Figure H,I). These findings suggest that NODAGA-Fab-BDP remained largely stable in human serum. However, slight broadening of the UV-trace was observed, which may be attributed to either (i) partial proteolysis of NODAGA-Fab-BDP by serum proteases or (ii) potential chemical interactions between the BODIPY moiety and serum components. This LC-MS method did not allow for the identification of the minor degradation product(s). In HeLa cell lysate, quinoxaline bis-nitrile 2 labeled lysate proteins in a dose-dependent manner, whereas preincubation of 2 with equimolar EDT effectively blocked bis-nitrile labeling (Figure S7).

In Vivo and Ex Vivo Biodistribution for PD-L1 Expression Evaluation

To evaluate the PD-L1 expression level in vivo, we radiolabeled NODAGA-Fab and NODAGA-PEG-Fab with [64Cu]­CuCl2 in 0.1 M NaOAc buffer (pH = 5.5) at 40 °C. The isolated radiochemical yields (RCY) following PD-10 column purification were 96% for [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab and 97% for [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab. Radioactive instant thin layer chromatography (iTLC) confirmed high radiochemical purities (RCP) of 91 and 96%, respectively (Figure A). The biodistribution of [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab and [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab was studied in female BALB/cJ mice bearing CT26 tumors, using PET/CT imaging. [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab or [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab (5.5 MBq) were each administered intravenously (via tail vein) to each group (n = 3 per bioconjugate). The PET/CT images were acquired over the course of 24 or 40 h postinjection. Longitudinal PET/CT images revealed that [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab rapidly accumulated in kidneys and bladder across all time points, indicating predominantly renal absorption and low blood pooling (up to 5.57%ID/g at 8 h postinjection) for sufficient tumor-targeting delivery (Figure S9). As a result, the overall tumor signal intensity remained relatively low over the 24 h time course. While the background signal decreased over time, tumor accumulation of [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab was limited, peaking at 3.82%ID/g observed at 8 h postinjection. While the tumor-to-blood (TB) ratio remained relatively constant, the tumor-to-muscle (TM) ratio continued to decrease over time (Figure D). The results suggested modest [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab uptake at the tumor site, accompanied by rapid systemic clearance, highlighting the benefits of further probe engineering for improving PK properties in vivo. In stark contrast, [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab exhibited enhanced tumor-targeting ability and sustained signal retention over time (Figure S9). At 24 h postinjection, a strong and well-localized PET signal was most discernible at the tumor site (8.97% ID/g), with minimal background noise (Figure B), which indicates that [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab accumulates at the target site efficiently. As shown by the red arrows, the tumor signal remained clearly visible even at 40 h (3.10% ID/g), suggesting prolonged in vivo stability (Figure C). A gradual increase in the TB ratio led to improved PET contrast, which is indicative of prolonged tumor retention and effective systemic clearance. Moreover, the TM ratio also increased over time, reaching up to 13-fold signal enhancement at 40 h postinjection (Figure E). The reduction in off-target signals, particularly in the kidneys and bladder, suggests that site-specific PEGylation efficiently mitigated renal clearance in vivo. Overall, [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab exhibited an extended circulation half-life, more efficient tumor accumulation, enhanced PET imaging contrast, and a more favorable PK profile.

The ex vivo tumor and normal tissue uptake of [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab and [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab in BALB/cJ mice bearing subcutaneous CT26 tumors were evaluated at 24 and 40 h, respectively (n = 3 per group). At respective time points, mice were sacrificed; the organs were dissected, weighed, and counted for radioactivity. Aligning with PET/CT imaging, [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab exhibited modest tumor uptake (1.8 ± 0.6%ID/g) and high kidney accumulation (30.0 ± 4.3%ID/g), indicative of fast renal clearance and limited tumor retention (Tables S2 and S3). At this time point, modest radioactivity was observed in liver and spleen, consistent with reported fast clearance of Fab. − Radioactivity in liver and spleen may also reflect (i) mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS) sequestration of Fab and/or (ii) partial transchelation of 64Cu to endogenous metal-binding ligands, given the tight regulation of copper homeostasis in biological systems. − The corresponding TM and TB ratios were 2.0 and 0.89, respectively (Table S3). The results suggest that while [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab showed moderate tumor targeting ability, its relatively smaller molecular weight likely contributed to suboptimal tumor retention. To assess the effect of site-specific chemical PEGylation on the PET probe, the PEGylated variant was evaluated at 40 h postinjection. Ex vivo analysis of mice administered with [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab revealed higher tumor uptake (8.0 ± 3.5%ID/g) and reduced kidney accumulation (2.6 ± 0.4%ID/g), representing a 12-fold decrease in renal absorption compared to [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab (Figure H and Table S3). Consistent with prolonged in vivo circulation, blood radioactivity levels were also elevated (3.0 ± 1.1%ID/g). The TM and TB ratios increased to 12 and 2.7, representing 6- and 3-fold enhancement, respectively (Table S3). Cardiac blood-pool uptake analysis, using heart region of interest (ROI) measurement as an imaging-based surrogate for circulating blood-pool activity, revealed that [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab was more persistent than [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab at later time points. [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab showed higher heart ROI uptake at 8 h (9.92 ± 2.89 vs 6.57 ± 1.97%ID/g) and 24 h (5.48 ± 1.10 vs 0.70 ± 0.39%ID/g) than [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab (Table S4), suggesting prolonged circulation of the PEGylated probe.

In Vivo Near-Infrared Fluorescence Visualization and Ex Vivo Immunofluorescence Staining of PD-L1

With SABRE-2 in hand, the PEGylated NIR fluorescent probe was synthesized for in vivo optical imaging (Scheme S3). The synthesis involved the coupling of compound 8 with commercially available IRDyeⓇ800CW NHS ester. The dye-labeled compound 10 was purified with reversed-phase HPLC and dissolved in MeOH; its concentration was approximated based on the absorption of IRDyeⓇ800CW in methanolic solutions (λmax = 778 nm and ε = 0.3 μM–1 cm–1). Following this, intermediate 10 was reacted with 0.95 equiv of DBCO-mPEG (20 kDa) via SPAAC (see SI for synthesis details). The isolated polymer product 11 was reconstituted in anhydrous DMSO (15 mM). The PEGylated Fab conjugate (Fab-NIR-PEG) was prepared following the established protocol for the NODAGA-PEG-Fab probe (Figure E).

Postinjection, Fab-NIR-PEG rapidly accumulated at the tumor site, followed by the kidney and liver. No NIR signal was detected from other major organs (i.e., heart, spleen, lungs, and brain). Longitudinal NIR imaging revealed a strong, sustained NIR fluorescence signal at the tumor site (Figure A). Fluorescence intensity at the tumor site continued to increase up to 24 h (Figure F), indicating effective tumor targeting and retention of the Fab-NIR-PEG probe. Ex vivo biodistribution (Figure B) confirms target specificity, with a strong fluorescence intensity in the tumor and kidneys, alongside a moderate signal in the liver. Kidney signal may reflect accumulation of fluorescent catabolites generated upon probe metabolism. Subsequent control experiment with the unconjugated free dye showed no selective tumor localization and higher signal accumulation in both liver and kidneys (Figure C,G), in line with nonspecific renal and partial hepatobiliary clearance. These results highlight both the favorable PK profile and tumor specificity of Fab-NIR-PEG, consistent with PET/CT images for targeted tumor imaging with minimal long-term off-target retention.

In the follow-up immunofluorescence staining experiment, we utilized a PEGylated fluorescent surrogate, Fab-BDP-PEG, to visualize target engagement (see SI for synthesis details). Colorectal CT26 tumors were excised from BALB/cJ mice, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin wax, and sectioned into thin slices (∼5 μm) for analysis. The tissue slices were subsequently processed via deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval as needed. For imaging, the slices were incubated with Fab-BDP-PEG either in the presence or absence of excess unlabeled full-length αPD-L1 Ab. In the absence of blocking Ab, Fab-BDP-PEG produced a strong green fluorescence with predominant membrane localization and partial intracellular diffusion (Figure S12). Preincubation with excess unlabeled αPD-L1 Ab led to a drastic reduction in green fluorescence signal, suggesting specific binding. Nuclei staining with DAPI remained comparable between the two conditions.

Conclusion

Functional Fab conjugates hold significant potential for advancing PET imaging techniques in cancer research. The application of thiol bis-nitrile chemistry serves as an efficient method for site-specific labeling of Fab fragments through disulfide bond rebridging. Building on this approach, we have successfully established a robust and modular chemical platform that facilitates the rapid generation of functional Fab constructs for immunoPET imaging. The introduction of an azide functionality into the bis-nitrile scaffold provides a biorthogonal handle for downstream diversification, which we utilized to perform chemical PEGylation. This modification helped improve the PK properties of the resulting PET probe.

With this chemical tool in hand, we quickly accessed NODAGA-Fab and NODAGA-PEG-Fab probes. We labeled both probes with 64Cu for in vivo PET imaging. The [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-Fab probe exhibited moderate tumor targeting ability and rapid renal clearance. In contrast, [64Cu]­Cu-NODAGA-PEG-Fab showed prolonged circulation, enhanced tumor accumulation, and superior tumor-to-background contrast. This improvement enabled clear visualization of PD-L1 positive tumor up to 40 h postinjection. BLI evaluations revealed that neither rebridging nor PEGylation adversely affected the binding affinity of the immunoPET probes. Complementary fluorescence imaging studies corroborated the PET/CT imaging results, further validating prolonged circulation, minimal off-target retention, and reduced renal uptake of the PEGylated Fab probe.

Collectively, we established a rapid and reliable method to generate Fab immunoconjugates for in vivo imaging without requiring knowledge of the protein sequence by rebridging the conserved interchain disulfide bond. We highlighted the importance of chemical engineering strategies for customizing Fab fragments in this work. The SABRE chemistry may serve as a platform enabling precise stoichiometric and site control over Fab radiolabeling, and a well-suited launching pad for a wide array of imaging modalities. These findings lay a strong foundation for the future development of targeted theragnostics in cancer research.

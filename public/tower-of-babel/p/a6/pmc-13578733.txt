Characterization of accumulated space charge and charge-caused AC gas discharge characteristics in enclosed air spaces with novel electrostatic induction method
Authors: Disheng Wang, Kecheng Tao, Lin Du, Tao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Space charges affect the discharge process in an enclosed space of an electrical device. Due to the limitations of the measurement technique, it is difficult to study charge accumulation and its effect on discharges. In this paper, a characterization of the space charges and the charge-caused discharge characteristics via electrostatic induction is proposed. For a 10 mm needle-plate model, numerical relations between the induced voltage and the space charge is derived, enabling the calculation of real-time space charge quantities. Then the characteristic space charge parameters are extracted and verified to be able to represent the accumulation of space charge and discharge characteristics. Variations of the parameters at different applied voltages are also investigated. Over a single AC voltage cycle, the equivalent charge quantity Q and the charge change rate dQ can represent pulse characteristics such as amplitude and repetition rate. With the phase φi and φt where the dQ curve undergoes significantly, the charge effect during the initial and final discharge can be analyzed. At different DC and AC-DC superimposed voltages, the discharge stages are distinguished based on the characteristic charge parameter versus applied voltage curve, and charge effect at different applied voltages are concluded according to φi and φt.

Introduction

Inside electrical equipment such as switchgear, partial discharges may occur due to factors such as burrs on the enclosure1. Because the space is enclosed and the outer shell is coated with insulating paint, the space charge generated by the discharge is difficult to dissipate. The accumulated charge creates a charge field2,3, which significantly affects discharge characteristics4–6. Under different conditions, such as different applied voltage waveforms, voltage polarity, and electrode spacing, the effect of space charge on the discharge can be either inhibitory or facilitative, leading to changes in the discharge strength and breakdown voltage4–7. For example, at DC voltages, the breakdown voltage varies with the gap distance, exhibiting either polar or non-polar effects4. Studying the space charge variation and the charge-caused discharge characteristics under different conditions can help determine whether the intensity of the discharge is likely to cause insulating damage at the electrode surface and whether the breakdown voltage has been reached, providing a reference for insulating protection.

In the interiors of common electrical devices, the voltages between the electrodes are either AC or AC-DC superimposed voltages, at which it is difficult to analyze the space charge and the charge-caused discharge characteristics due to limitations in methods for measuring space charge under discharge conditions. Most existing studies rely on the pulse current method, which analyzes discharge characteristics and space charge effects based on the variation patterns of pulse parameters8–13. This approach only indirectly reflects the effect of space charges, and high precision is required for discharge pulse acquisition. The pre-applied voltage method establishes the accumulated space charge condition by applying a high voltage DC bias and determines the amount of space charge by integrating over the corona current. A test voltage is then applied to analyze the characteristics of the discharge under the influence of the charge. This method is suitable only for experimental testing, and does not account for charge dissipation or changes in space charge induced by the test voltage14–17. Numerical simulations can intuitively illustrate changes in space charge and their impact on discharge18–20. However, due to computational constraints, they can only simulate processes on microsecond scales and do not reflect long-term charge accumulation.

To address the issues described, a method based on electrostatic induction is proposed to measure the space charge. The salient features of the detection method are that it uses passive device sensing and does not require high-precision device sampling, and that it can real-time obtain the discharge space charge quantity inside the enclosing device, which overcomes the shortcomings of the pulse current method, which does not directly reflect the space charge situation, and the pre-voltage method, which can only obtain the amount of space charge generated by the pre-applied voltage. With the obtained space charge quantity, not only the space charge variation but also the partial discharge characteristics can be analyzed. First, for a sensor with a metal plate as its core, a numerical relation between the space charge and the induced voltage on the plate is derived based on electrostatic theory. The extraction of the induced voltage is then investigated to calculate the real-time spatial charge, whose characteristic parameters are then determined and the charge-caused partial discharge characteristics are shown to be characterized by the parameters. Then variations of the characteristic parameters at different AC voltages or AC-DC superimposed voltages are investigated, by which the variation law for the space charge and the partial discharge at different voltages are obtained.

Fundamental theory

Take a typical enclosed electrical switch cabinet. As shown in Fig. 1, partial discharges may occur on the ingoing bus bar due to factors such as burrs on the metal surface. In order to measure the space charge due to these discharges, it is proposed to place a metal plate as a sensor on the cabinet wall opposite the discharge location, with the voltage signal from the plate routed outside the cabinet. The metal plates are insulated from the cabinet walls with rubber.

Fig. 1Discharge scenario and sensor.

Discharge scenario and sensor.

The actual discharge scenario is simulated using the discharge model shown in Fig. 2. A high voltage is applied to the upper electrode plate to simulate a bus bar, and a needle electrode is set up to simulate the discharge from a metal burr. A sealed chamber is constructed using fiberglass-reinforced plastic. Under needle-plate discharge conditions, the voltage on the metal plate consists of several components. The stray capacitance C1 exists between the upper and lower plates and forms a voltage divider circuit with the ground circuits C2 and R1. Consequently, the voltage U1 applied to the upper plate results in a voltage U2’ on the lower plate.

Fig. 2Discharge module.

Discharge module.

Since the magnitude and position of the space charge change little as it moves away from the strong electric field near the tip of the needle, it can be considered as a static charge. According to the principle of electrostatic induction21, a positive induced charge q is generated on the metal induction plate, creating an induced voltage U2” across the grounded circuit R1 and C2. By establishing the relationship between U2” and Q, the discharge charge can be calculated, thereby enabling an analysis of the discharge behavior.

Since the high-frequency discharge pulse signal is primarily applied to the resistor R1, the discharge pulse can be extracted from the U3 signal.

Partial discharge characteristics on single power-frequency voltage cycle

The experiments applied a waveform generator Tektronix AFG3052C and a high voltage power amplifier Matsusada AMP-20B20 to generate AC or AC-DC superimposed voltages. The gap distance between the high-voltage needle and the ground plate is 10 mm. During the experiment, the temperature range was 293 K to 295 K and the humidity range is 70% to 75%. Multiple experiments are performed with different applied voltages. U1, U2 and U3 are measured. U2, Q and dQ calculations are performed. The Q curves are calculated as an average over four periods to reduce the effects of transient noise and random discharges. Also the amplitude V and phase φ of the pulse in U3 are recorded. The charge characteristic parameters are then applied to analyze the discharge characteristics and charge effects, which are compared with the characteristic parameters of regular discharge pulses.

Initial discharge stage

Figure 5 shows the experiment results at a 10.5 kV AC voltage. At this point, no significant discharge pulse is observed in the U3 waveform. U2’ shows no significant distortion, but the Q curve exhibits a distinct change during the negative half-cycle between 202° and 330°, corresponding to a negative-positive double peak with a minimum value of dQmin = -192.94 pC and a maximum value of dQmax = 188.63 pC. This indicates that either no distinct firing pulse was generated during the negative half-cycle, or that such a pulse could not be extracted due to interference from background noise. That is, for initial discharge detection, the charge parameter is more sensitive than the discharge pulse parameter. Mostly because the charge parameter signals are obtained from the accumulated space charges after multiple discharges, they are relatively strong. Moreover, they are signals that vary with the AC cycle, making it easy to eliminate background noise and interference from self-oscillations. The Qav = -44.54 pC is relatively small and the net space charge is negative overall.

The discharge pulse was detected during the negative half-cycle of U3 at 11.2 kV AC voltage. The discharge pulse was detected during a positive half-cycle of U3 at 11.7 kV AC voltage. Uq curve and discharge pulse φ-V points distribution under AC 12 kV.

First, the space charge variation can be directly observed. During the positive half-cycle, Q increases over the discharge phase interval primarily because electrons rapidly enter the needle electrode, while the corona region continuously generates positive ions that slowly migrate toward the migration zone. Some of these positive ions recombine with residual negative ions from the previous negative half-cycle. Consequently, the absolute value of the net space charge decreases. During the negative half-cycle discharge interval, Q decreases. This is primarily because the produced positive ions lose their charge upon reaching the needle, while the negative charges migrate from the corona zone as electrons and are mainly converted into negative ions within the migration zone. Consequently, the absolute value of the net space charge increases. The changes in Q during the discharge intervals are denoted as ΔQ+ and ΔQ-, which are calculated as the difference between the maximum and minimum points during the ascending or descending phase respectively, as shown in Fig. 6. During positive and negative half-cycle discharges, the absolute value of dQ changes significantly, forming peaks and troughs on the dQ curve, such as between 82° and 101°, and 247° and 278° in Fig. 6. Outside the discharge phase interval, the undissipated positive or negative ions in the migration zone slowly diffuse or enter the lower electrode plate, so that the change in charge Q is small and the dQ value is low and relatively stable.

Then, the characteristics of the partial discharge can be reflected in the charge parameters. The initiation phase φi and termination phase φt phase of the discharge can be extracted according to the undergone significant changing of dQ curve, as well as according to the discharge pulse. The termination phase φt of both the positive and negative half-cycle extracted by dQ is slightly lag which extracted by the final φ-V point. Analysis using Eq. (4) and Eq. (5) indicated that on this time, d0 is actually decreasing with disc charge migration, so that deduced Q changes even without Q variation and dQ does not equal to 0. The initiation phase φi extracted by dQ is slightly advance which extracted by the first φ-V point in the negative half-cycle, primarily because the initial discharge lacks a distinct pulse. Since dQ is obtained by averaging over a 4-cycle signal like Q, it is insensitive to transient noise and random pulses. Moreover, it is a low-frequency signal and, therefore, can be further processed by high-frequency filtering. So compared to relying on discharge pulses, extracting φi and φt with dQ reduces errors caused by the absence of a distinct pulse or interference. φi and φt can reflect the space charge effect. For example, before the last discharge in the positive or negative half-cycle, accumulated ions with the same polarity as the needle electrode weaken the electric field, shifting the φt closer to 90° or 270°. Specially, before the first discharge in the negative half-cycle, the tip electric field is enhanced by the residual positive ions from the previous positive half-cycle, which reduces the required external electric field strength compared to the no-space-charge case, resulting in a φi farther from 270°.

The dQ characteristics can also comprehensively reflect the V and n characteristics to a certain extent. For example, in the positive half-cycle, the dQ peak dQmax corresponds to the phase φmax = 88.5°, where the φ-V point is most numerous and n is maximum. With relatively small V variations, Q changes more significantly than at other phases, resulting in a secondary peak on the dQ curve. Similarly, in the negative half-cycle, the dQ minimum dQmin corresponds to phase φmin = 265.8°, where n is relatively large, causing a minor trough on the dQ curve. Thus, based on dQmax and dQmin, the phase with larger discharge n can be determined.

Meanwhile, Q and dQ reflect the overall strength of both the positive and negative half-cycles of the discharge, which cannot be captured by discharge pulse parameters alone. For instance, |ΔQ+| = 0.62nC < |ΔQ-| = 1.04nC. Qa = -3.39nC, and throughout the entire cycle, Q < 0, indicating a net negative space charge. These parameters collectively indicate that the negative half-cycle discharge is relatively stronger under this voltage.

Analysis of the φ-V points of the discharge pulses reveals that the pulse amplitude V near the start phase of the positive half-cycle exceeds that of subsequent phases. The initial discharge induces a larger variation in the space charge such that the corresponding dQ curve exhibits a secondary peak at this phase, reflecting the V₃ character of the initial discharge. Additionally, dQmax corresponds to the phase φmax = 82.8°, at which the φ-V points exhibit relatively larger amplitude V. During the negative half-cycle 260°~280° phase angle, the maximum voltage V ceases to increase, and the φ-V curve exhibits a plateau. Due to the minimal variation of V, dQ is dominated by n. As a result, the dQ curve in this region is relatively flat with small numerical variation. The minimum dQ corresponds to φmin = 267.4°, where n is relatively large at this phase.

Under 14 kV AC voltage, |ΔQ+| = 14.64nC > |ΔQ-| = 13.35nC, Qa = 4.54nC, and the net space charge exhibits a positive overall charge. That is, the positive half-cycle discharge is relatively stronger.

In addition to the characteristics of the discharge development stage, during the streamer discharge stage around 75° in the positive half-cycle, the voltage V at some φ-V points is significantly higher, which reflects the streamer discharge. The secondary peak of the dQ curve essentially corresponds to the φ-V points with higher V, where dQmax aligns with φmax = 82.8°. This indicates that in the presence of significantly larger V, the dQ curve more clearly reflects the discharge voltage characteristics.

Additionally, near the negative half-cycle at 270°, the amplitude V at the φ-V point decreases as the applied voltage increases, while the repetition rate n increases, resulting in a “concave” distribution. The dQ curve in this region is relatively flat with small numerical variations, reflecting the V-n character of the discharge in general in this phase interval. dQmin corresponds to φmin = 82.8°. It can be deduced that there is a relatively high n in this phase interval, but it is difficult to deduce the character of the decreasing V.

At this voltage, |ΔQ+| = 29.11nC > |ΔQ-| = 25.96nC, with Qa = 10.58nC, indicating that the net space charge exhibits a positive overall charge. This means that the positive half-cycle discharge is relatively stronger during this phase.

In summary, phase characteristics such as φi and φt can be extracted from the dQ curve, which to some extent reflect the combined characteristics of the single-cycle discharge pulse parameters V and n. dQmax and dQmin can characterize discharge phases with larger values of V or n. Based on the Q characteristic, the overall strength of the discharge can be assessed, and the formation of space charge can be inferred. Combining the Q and dQ features can explain the role of the accumulated charge in affecting the characteristics of the discharge. Furthermore, since Q acquisition does not require high frequencies, it reduces the requirement on the performance of the acquisition device.

For positive half-periodic discharge, the initial voltage gradually increases with the applied voltage amplitude. This is primarily due to the enhanced weakening effect on the needle electrode electric field due to the increased background charge in the residual positive coronal zone prior to discharge. At 12 kV, the termination voltage of a positive half-cycle discharge is almost equal to the applied voltage amplitude. The termination voltage gradually rises as the applied voltage amplitude increases. This is primarily due to the accumulation of positive charge from multiple discharges at the end of the discharge, which enhances the weakening effect of the electric field on the needle electrode. The increase in the termination voltage is significantly larger than the increase in the initial voltage. This is mainly because the charge accumulated from multiple discharges near the end of the half-cycle discharges has not yet dissipated substantially, leading to a stronger residual charge effect compared to the negative half-cycle case.

For negative half-cycle discharges below 13kV, Vi initially exhibits minimal variability. Subsequently, Vi decreases significantly as the applied voltage amplitude increases. The primary reason is that as the discharge intensifies, the residual positive charge from the preceding positive half-cycle increases, enhancing the electric field strength at the needle electrode. At 11.5 kV, the negative half-cycle breakdown voltage Vt is almost equal to the applied voltage amplitude. As the applied voltage amplitude increases, so does the Vt, but the magnitude of the variation is relatively small. This occurs because the accumulated positive charge in the corona zone strengthens the needle electrode electric field, partially counteracting the weakening effect of the accumulated negative charge from multiple discharges on the needle electrode electric field.

As discussed above, the effect of space charges on the initiation and termination of discharges can be analyzed in terms of the variation of Vi and Vt with the applied voltage. Specifically, the positive half-cycle Vi starts to increase significantly from the 16 kV discharge initiation phase, while the negative half-cycle Vi starts to decrease in absolute value from the 13 kV discharge development phase. These laws can be used to identify the discharge stage.

Analysis of discharge characteristics under AC-DC superimposed voltage

Electrodes inside converter valves and other converter devices may be subjected to an AC-DC superposition voltage, where the DC component enhances the accumulation of monopole charges. To investigate the discharge characteristics under such voltages in order to protect these devices, using the same experimental method. U1, U2, and U3 are measured, and U2”, Q and dQ are calculated and the amplitude and phase of the pulses in the U3 signal are recorded.

Single power-frequency voltage cycle

To maintain a consistently positive or negative polarity environment, a DC voltage of ± 8 kV is superimposed with a power frequency AC voltage. Under the superimposed voltage of DC + 8 kV and AC 6 kV, the Q and dQ curves along with the φ-V spectrum are shown in Fig. 12. At AC and positive polarity DC superimposed voltages, the initial discharge voltage is smaller than in the ac case. Because the positive charge generated by the discharge is further away from the tip of the needle, moreover, the positive charges will migrate away from the tip of the needle within phases of 180°~360°. Consequently, the weakening effect of the charge field before discharge is reduced, compared to the case of a negative polarity discharge at the previous half-cycle in the ac case. The initial rise of the termination voltage in the superimposed voltage condition is similar to that in the AC case, but the subsequent increase is slower. Mainly because the development of the discharge is suppressed by the persistent positive space charge, the degree of single discharge development is relatively lower than in the AC case, so that there is less residual space charge after the discharge. At the same time, when the applied voltage drops to near the discharge termination voltage, the reduction rate of the applied voltage during the discharge recovery time after one discharge is relatively low compared to the AC case. The field strength loss is smaller and the termination of the discharge is relatively less likely.

At AC and negative polarity DC superimposed voltages, the initial voltage rises as the AC voltage component increases, which is different from the AC case, because the residual positive space charge near the tip electrode from the previous cycle is largely dissipated due to the negative space charge background, the persistent negative charge weakens the electric field at the electrode tip. Compared to the AC case, the termination voltage increases more significantly with applied voltage. Because, in contrast to the ac case, the residual positive space charge near the electrode tip is relatively small before the termination of the discharge against the negative charge background. Simultaneously, for negative discharges, the recovery time of the field after a discharge is extremely short, during which a reduction of the applied voltage has a relatively small effect on the field.

Conclusion

In this paper, the equivalent charge is calculated based on the source-field relation between the space charge and the induced voltage, and subsequently investigate the characteristics of the gas discharge in an enclosed space at both AC and AC-DC superimposed voltages, as well as the cumulative charge effect based on the space charge characteristics. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) Based on electrostatic theory, a numerical relationship has been established between the discharges space charge and the induced voltage on the sensing plate. The induced voltage can be extracted by a sensing device with the sensing plate to calculate the space charge quantity, by which not only the space charge variation but also the partial discharge characteristics can be analyzed. Since the charge quantity signal corresponds to the accumulated space charge after multiple discharges, it is relatively strong and low-frequency, avoiding the need for high-precision sampling and insensitive to background noise and interference from self-oscillations.

(2) Within a power frequency cycle, dQ reflects the combined characteristics of the discharge pulses V and n, while dQmax and dQmin characterize the discharge phases with the largest V or n values in the positive and negative half-cycles, respectively. The overall background charge situation can be assessed by Qa. The φi and φt reflect when and how the accumulated charge varies and affects the starting and end of the half-cycle discharge process.

(3) At both AC and AC-DC superimposed voltages, discharge stages can be identified using the characteristics of the ΔQ+, ΔQ- and Qa correlation curves. According to the Vi and Vt correlation curves, only in the negative half-cycle at the AC voltage, the needle electric field is enhanced before the initial discharge by the residual positive charge from the preceding positive half-cycle. In other cases, the residual charge has the effect of weakening the original electric field and suppressing the discharge for both initial and final discharges.

Discussion

In this paper, only the cases of needle-plate electrodes and air medium are studied. From the discharge plasma simulation results at 11 kV as shown in Fig. 17, it can be seen that the net space charge after discharge is concentrated in a very small region near the tip of the needle during the initial phase of needle-plate partial discharge. Thus the charges near the needle electrode are assumed to be point charges, as shown in Fig. 3. This assumption reduces the range of the spatial charge distribution and does not take into account the broadening of the charge distribution due to the development of the discharge.

According to Eq. (3), since the induced charge depends only on the dielectric constant of the gaseous medium, this approach is expected to be applicable to various gases. However, the space charge quantity and distribution under different gas properties and different electrode conditions needs to be further investigated through discharge plasma simulations and other formalisms. Otherwise, the calculated Q and dQ are inaccurated, and can only be used for qualitative analysis.

Furthermore, beyond the test range, excessively high temperatures are expected to enhance thermal motion and increase the space charge diffusion, while at the same time the internal hot air flow of the device may alter the space charge distribution. Excessive humidity accelerates the reaction of partial discharges and promotes the diffusion of space charges. Once the above environmental conditions induce a change in the net space charge distribution, it affects the accuracy of Q and dQ.

Since only metal plates are installed inside the equipment, and the sensor circuits are all passive devices, the sensors are expected to have good long-term stability.

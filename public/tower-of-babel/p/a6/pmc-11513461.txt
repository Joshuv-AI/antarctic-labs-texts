Selenium promotes broiler myoblast proliferation through the ROS/PTEN/PI3K/AKT signaling axis
Authors: Shengchen Wang, Bing Tian, Xinyu Feng, Yangyang Hu, Liyang Zhang, Ziwei Zhang, Shiwen Xu, Yun Hu, Xiaoyan Cui, Tingting Li, Xugang Luo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSelenium (Se), an indispensable trace element in broiler chickens, is closely associated with the growth and development of skeletal muscles. However, the role of Se in the proliferation of broiler myoblasts and its specific biological mechanisms have not been elucidated. In the present study, an in vitro growth model of broiler pectoral myoblasts cultured with Se (Na2SeO3) for 24 h was established. Using light microscopy, Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay, and flow cytometry, we found that compared to the control (Con) group, Se supplemental level obviously promoted myoblast proliferation and prevented cell cycle arrest from the G1 phase to the S + G2 phase. Through intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation detection, western blotting, and quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), the study showed that the reduced ROS production caused by Se supplementation significantly decreased PTEN expression and activated the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in myoblasts, thereby promoting the P53/P21/CyclinD1-regulated cell cycle progression, as well as the expression of proliferation-related myogenic regulatory factors (MRF). Our findings support the potential of Se to maintain the proliferative capacity of chicken myoblasts and emphasize the importance of Se intake in regulating skeletal muscle growth and development in poultry.Key words: selenium, broiler myoblast, proliferation, ROS, PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway

INTRODUCTION

Broiler chickens are important edible animals that account for a large proportion of the global meat industry (Wang et al., 2024). Skeletal muscle contributes 40 to 50% of the body weight of broilers. The generation of skeletal muscle affects the growth performance and meat quality of broilers, which mainly depends on a series of orderly regulated myogenic events initiated by muscle satellite cells (also known as myoblasts) (Sancho-Munoz et al., 2021; Yin et al., 2013). Myoblasts must undergo rapid complete activation and proliferation through the precise spatiotemporal expression of multiple myogenic regulatory factors (MRF), including paired box 7 (Pax7), myogenic factor 5 (Myf5), and myogenic differentiation factor D (Myod), to provide an adequate stem cell pool for subsequent differentiation and myofiber formation (Buckingham and Rigby, 2014). The proliferation homeostasis of myoblasts directly determines the performance of muscle shaping, whereas disruption can impair the normal mass of skeletal muscles (Chakkalakal et al., 2012).

Selenium (Se), an indispensable trace element in vertebrates, plays a pivotal role in a variety of biological processes, exhibiting strong antioxidant, immune-enhancing, and tissue metabolism-regulating abilities (Rayman, 2012). Broiler chickens are highly susceptible to Se intake and benefit greatly from adequate Se supplementation, which enhances the Se content in skeletal muscles, sustains healthy muscle growth and development, protects against degenerative muscle diseases, and ultimately improves meat quality (Van Vleet and Ferrans, 1976; Wang, et al., 2024). Recently, increasing evidences have revealed that Se controls the fate of cells in chicken skeletal muscle, thereby determining the number and proportion of different types of cells within the muscle. For example, Fan et al. (2018) found that Se deficiency induces apoptotic cell death in broiler myoblasts by reducing the expression of selenoprotein K, and Hassan et al. (2014) revealed that Se can stimulate the adipogenic differentiation of cultured broiler embryonic fibroblasts isolated from 6-day-old embryos in a dose-dependent manner. Wu et al. (2012) demonstrated that the addition of appropriate levels of Se could can promote the transformation of broiler myoblasts into multinucleated cells and myotubes. However, the role of Se in broiler myoblast proliferation has not been thoroughly studied.

The phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/Akt kinase (PI3K/AKT) signaling pathway, which plays crucial roles in controlling cell proliferation, migration, metabolism, and other basic functions through protein phosphorylation expression, is regarded as a key node for maintaining cell growth and stability (Aoki and Fujishita, 2017; Pompura and Dominguez-Villar, 2018). It has been reported that the activation of PI3K/AKT can promote cell proliferation by regulating the progression of the cell cycle (Song et al., 2019). For instance, in the osteoblasts under hypoxic conditions, suppression of the PI3K/AKT pathway can effectively increase the cellular levels of P53 and its target P21 to cause cell cycle arrest and proliferative viability decrease (Zou et al., 2015). Conversely, Yu et al. (2015) found that after insulin-like growth factor-1 treatment, the myoblasts in broiler chickens entered a new cell cycle and increased the mRNA expression levels of the cell cycle-dependent gene CyclinD1 via the PI3K/AKT pathway. In addition, recent studies have demonstrated that the PI3K/AKT pathway can be negatively regulated by an important intracellular signaling molecule named phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN), and the reactive oxygen species (ROS), as chemical messengers of cell signaling, which overproduction can inhibit cell proliferation by targeting the PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway (Gao et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020; Worby and Dixon, 2014).

Notably, the crucial biological role of Se lies in the inhibition of intracellular ROS overproduction by modulating the cellular antioxidant system. Meanwhile, it was proved that Se deficiency disrupted the activities of antioxidant enzymes (CAT, GPX, and SOD) in broiler myoblasts, which could be alleviated by the ROS inhibitor N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC) (Yao et al., 2016). Consequently, it is plausible to postulate that Se may facilitate the proliferation of broiler myoblasts via the ROS/PTEN/PI3K/AKT signaling axis. To test our hypothesis, we cultured broiler embryo primary pectoral myoblasts in vitro to investigate the effect of Se supplementation on cell proliferation viability, cell cycle progression, intracellular ROS levels, and expression levels of the PTEN/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway-related genes. These findings have important implications for revealing the intrinsic mechanisms of Se regulation of skeletal muscle growth in broiler chickens.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

All procedures conducted in this study were approved by the Animal Experimentation Ethics Committee of Yangzhou University (SYXK (Su) 2021-0027), and adhered to the Experimental Animal Affairs Management Regulations established by the Ministry of Science and Technology in China.

Isolation and Cultivation of Primary Pectoral Myoblasts of Broiler Embryos

Primary cultured pectoral myoblasts of Arbor Acres broiler embryos were prepared using a previously described method (Zhang et al., 2022a). In brief, after cleaning and disinfecting the surface of the eggshell, the breast muscles of 14-d-old broiler chick embryos were collected, washed with D-Hanks solution (H1045, Solebao, China), and then minced, and digested with type I collagenase (2 mg/mL, C8140-100mg, Solebao, China) at 37°C for 20 min. The suspensions containing the digested cells were sequentially filtered using the 100 μm and 40 μm meshes to remove large debris, and centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 5 min to collect the cell precipitates. Then, the cell precipitates were resuspended in the complete culture medium, which consisted of 79% Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (DMEM)/F12 basis medium (10565018, Gibco, USA), 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS; 10091148, Gibco, USA), and 1% antibiotics (100 IU/mL penicillin and 100 mg/mL streptomycin, 15070-063, Gibco, USA), and purified with 3 times of differential attachments in a humidified incubator (Model 3110 series, Thermo Electron, USA) containing 5% CO2 at 37°C. Finally, the purified myoblasts were plated into 6-well or 96-well plates containing complete culture medium, cultured, and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2.

Treatments of Primary Pectoral Myoblasts, Sample Collections and Preparations

To estimate the effect of Se supplementation on proliferation viability, cell cycle, intracellular ROS production, and related gene expression levels in myoblasts, a 1-factor factorial experiment with a completely randomized design was used. When the cell density reached 60 to 70%, the complete culture medium in each well was discarded, and the primary pectoral myoblasts were incubated in the Se-deficient basal culture medium (control [Con], 98% DMEM/F12 basis medium + 1% antibiotics + 5 μg/mL insulin + 10 μg/mL transferrin) and the basal medium supplemented with 0.5, 1.0, or 1.5 μmol Se/L as Na2SeO3 for 24 h, respectively. Therefore, there were a total of 4 treatment groups: Con, 0.5 Se, 1.0 Se, and 1.5 Se. The cells cultured in 96-well plates were used to evaluate proliferation viability (8 replicates per treatment and 1 culture well/replicate). The growth status of cells cultured in 6-well plates at 8, 16, and 24 h was observed through a light microscope, and the cells were collected at 24 h to evaluate the cell cycle (3 replicates per treatment and 1 culture well/replicate). Finally, according to the results of proliferation viability and cell cycle detections, the Con group and 1.0 Se group cells cultured in a 6-well plates were stained with dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) to analyze the intracellular ROS production (3 replicates/each treatment and 1 culture well/replicate), scraped in TRIzol reagent to analyze gene expressions related to cell proliferation (Pax7, Myod, Myf5, proliferating cell nuclear antigen [PCNA], and Ki67), key genes regulating cell cycle (P53, P21, and CyclinD1), key signal pathways genes (PTEN, PI3K, and AKT), as well as antioxidant genes (superoxide dismutase 1 [SOD1], superoxide dismutase 2 [SOD2], glutathione peroxidase 1 [GPX1], glutathione peroxidase 2 [GPX2], and catalase [CAT]) (6 replicates/each treatment and 2 culture wells/replicate), or lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (R0010, Solarbio, China) for analyses of the protein expression levels of the above mentioned biological process genes, including Pax7, Myod, PCNA, P53, P21, PTEN, phosphorylated-PI3K, (P-PI3K) phosphorylated-AKT (P-AKT) (6 replicates/each treatment and 3 culture wells/replicate).

Determination of Cell Proliferation Viability

The effect of Se supplementation on the proliferation viability of primary pectoral myoblasts was determined using a commercial Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; C0037, Beyotime, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, after 24 h of Se treatment, the medium used to culture primary pectoral myoblasts in 96-well plates was discarded. The cells in each well were washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and then incubated with CCK-8 working solution (100 μL DMEM/F12 basis medium + 10 μL CCK-8 regent) for 1 h at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. The absorbance of each well was measured at 492 nm using a SPARK microplate reader (Spark, Tecan, Switzerland).

Determination of Cell Cycle

The effect of Se supplementation on the cell cycle of primary pectoral myoblasts was determined using a commercial Cell Cycle and Apoptosis Analysis Kit (C1052, Beyotime, China), according to the manufacturer's instructions. In brief, after 24 h of Se treatment, the medium used to culture primary pectoral myoblasts in 6-ell plates was discarded. The cells in each well were collected and then fixed in 70% alcohol for 24 h at 4°C. Subsequently, the fixed cells from each well were treated with 535 μL propidium iodide working solution (500 μL staining buffer + 25 μL propidium iodide + 10 μL RNase A) at room temperature for 1 h in the dark, and the red fluorescence at an excitation wavelength of 488 nm was recorded using flow cytometry (FACSCalibur, Becton Dickinson, USA). The cell cycle was analyzed using the FlowJo Software (V10.0.7, TreeStar, USA).

Determination of Intracellular ROS Production

The effect of Se supplementation on intracellular ROS production in primary pectoral myoblasts was determined using a commercial ROS detection kit (E004, Nanjing Jiancheng, China), according to the manufacturer's instructions. In brief, at 24 h of Se treatment, the old medium used to culture primary pectoral myoblasts in 6-well plates was discarded, and the cells in each well were washed twice with PBS and then incubated with DCFH-DA working solution (1 mL DMEM/F12 basis medium + 1 μL DCFH-DA) for 30 min at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. The DCFH-DA is a sensitive probe that can be oxidized by intracellular ROS to the fluorescent 2,7-dichlorofuorescin (DCF). Images of fluorescent ROS labeled with DCF were obtained using a fluorescence microscope, and ROS production was quantified by Image J (V1.8.0.112, National Institutes of Health, USA).

Total RNA Isolation and Real-Time Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction

Total RNA from primary pectoral myoblasts was isolated using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and the concentration and purity of the RNA were measured using an ultramicro ultraviolet spectrophotometer (Q5000, Quawell, USA) at 260/280 nm. First-strand cDNA for RT-qPCR was obtained using the SuperScript III First-Strand Synthesis for RT-PCR Kit (R312, Vazyme, China). After formulating the premix system with diluted cDNA (1 μL), 2 × SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (5 μL), ROX reference (0.2 μL), PCR-grade water (3.4 μL), and each primer (0.2 μL, 10 μM), the quantification of mRNA expression was performed using the ABI Sequence Detection System (PRISM 7500, Applied Biosystems, USA). All primers used in this study are listed in Table 1, and the internal reference genes β-actin and GAPDH genes were used to complete data normalization using the 2-ΔΔCt method.

Total Protein Extraction and Western Blotting

Total protein from primary pectoral myoblasts was extracted according to a previously described method (Wu et al., 2024), and the concentrations of the obtained proteins were determined using a BCA kit (23225, Thermo Fisher, USA). Total protein (20 μg/each sample) was loaded and separated by 12% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, transferred onto a nitrocellulose membrane (IPVH00001, Merck Millipore, USA), blocked with 5% nonfat milk-TBST for 2 h at 37°C, and incubated with primary antibodies Pax7 (1: 500, WL04954, Wanleibio, China), Myod (1: 500, WL04662, Wanleibio, China), PCNA (1: 500, WL03213, Wanleibio, China), P53 (1: 1000, WL01919, Wanleibio, China), P21 (1: 1000, WL0362, Wanleibio, China), PTEN (1: 500, WL01901, Wanleibio, China), P-PI3K (1: 1000, WL0362, Wanleibio, China), P-AKT (1: 1000, WL0362, Wanleibio, China). On the second day, the membranes were washed with TBST and incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat Anti-Rabbit IgG secondary antibody (1:5000, HX2031, Huaxingbio, China). Finally, the bands were visualized using a chemiluminescence image scanner (5200, Tanon, China). GAPDH was used as an internal reference to determine the target protein expression levels.

Statistical Analysis

The data obtained from cell proliferation viability and cell cycle determination were subjected to 1-way analysis of variance (1-way ANOVA) using the general linear model procedure in the SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute Inc, USA). After confirming that 1 μM Se supplemental level was the most suitable concentration for exploring the promotion of Se on the proliferation of broiler myoblasts, the data obtained from ROS production, RT-qPCR and western blotting between the Con group and the 1.0 Se group were subjected to Student's t test in SAS 9.4 software. Principal component analysis (PCA) analysis was performed using SPSS and correlative analysis was performed using the R programming language. Differences between the means were analyzed using the least significant difference (LSD). Each replicate well was the experimental unit, and P < 0.05 was regarded to be statistically significant.

RESULTS

Effect of Se Supplementation on the Proliferation Viability and Cell Cycle of Primary Pectoral Myoblasts

As shown in Figure 1, we analyzed the effect of Se supplemental levels on the proliferative potential of primary pectoral myoblasts. Microscopic observations revealed a gradual increase in cell density in the Se-treated groups with the increasing culture time, especially at 24 h, and both the 1.0 Se and 1.5 Se groups exhibited significantly higher cell densities than the Con group (Figure 1A). The CCK-8 assay results showed that Se supplementation increased (P < 0.05) the proliferative viability of myoblasts, and the cells in both the 1.0 Se and 1.5 Se groups had the highest (P < 0.05) proliferation viability (Figure 1B). Flow cytometry analysis showed that compared with the Con group, Se supplementation significantly decreased (P < 0.05) the cell type percentage in the G1 phase, and increased (P < 0.05) the cell type percentages in the S and S + G2 phases, but did not affect (P > 0.05) the G2 phase in myoblasts (Figures 1C–G). Notably, the 1.0 Se group displayed the lowest (P < 0.05) G1 and highest (P < 0.05) S+G2 percentages, but there was no difference (P > 0.05) in the S phase percentage among the 3 Se groups. Altogether, these results demonstrate that Se supplementation prevents cell cycle arrest by promoting the transition of myoblasts from the G1 phase to the S + G2 phase, thus promoting the proliferation viability of primary pectoral myoblasts.

Effect of Se Supplementation on the Expressions of the Genes Related to Primary Pectoral Myoblasts Proliferation

As shown in Figure 2, based on the results of CCK-8 and cell cycle analyses, the Con and 1.0 Se groups were selected to investigate the Se supplementation on the expression levels of genes related to myoblast proliferation. The RT-qPCR results showed that compared with the Con group, the 1.0 Se group significantly increased (P < 0.05) mRNA expression levels of myoblast proliferation-related MRFs (Pax7, Myod, and Myf5) and the proliferative marker gene Ki67 (Figures 2A–D), and there was an upregulation trend (P > 0.05) in the mRNA level of PCNA (Figure 2E). Western blotting results revealed that compared with the Con group, the 1.0 Se group exhibited increased (P < 0.05) protein expression levels of Pax7, Myod, and PCNA (Figures 2F–I). In addition, compared with the Con group, the 1.0 Se group exhibited decreased (P < 0.05) mRNA and protein expression levels of cell cycle negative regulatory genes (P53 and p21) and increased (P < 0.05) mRNA expression level of the downstream target gene CyclinD1 (Figures 2J–O).

Effect of Se Supplementation on the Expression Levels of PTEN/PI3K/AKT Pathway-Related Genes in Primary Pectoral Myoblasts

As shown in Figure 3, the effect of Se supplementation on the expression levels of PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway-related genes in myoblasts was investigated. RT-qPCR results showed that compared with the Con group, the 1.0 Se group remarkably upregulated (P < 0.05) the mRNA levels of PTEN, PI3K, and AKT in myoblasts (Figures 3A–C). Consistently, in comparison with the Con group, the 1.0 Se group increased (P < 0.05) the PTEN protein expression level and promoted (P < 0.05) the phosphorylation levels of PI3K and AKT (Figures 3E–G). These data demonstrate that Se activates the PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway in primary pectoral myoblasts.

Se Supplementation Inhibits the ROS Overproduction in Primary Pectoral Myoblasts

As shown in Figure 4, the effect of Se supplementation on intracellular ROS production in myoblasts was determined. Fluorescence staining and quantitative analysis revealed that the intracellular ROS levels in the 1.0 Se group were obviously lower (P < 0.05) than those in the Con group (Figures 4A and B). Meanwhile, compared with the Con group, the 1.0 Se group remarkably upregulated (P < 0.05) the mRNA levels of GPX1 and GPX2 in myoblasts (Figures 4E and F), but had no effect (P > 0.05) on the mRNA levels of SOD1, SOD2, and CAT (Figures 4C, D, and G). These data demonstrate that Se can inhibit ROS overproduction in primary pectoral myoblasts via the GPX system.

DISCUSSION

The proliferative potential of skeletal myoblasts directly determines the muscle health and meat quality. As per the findings of the current study, we discovered that Se supplementation increased the proliferation viability in primary pectoral myoblasts in broilers. Under Se incubation, intracellular ROS production and PTEN expression were suppressed and the expression of PI3K/AKT was enhanced, thus alleviating the cell cycle arrest-mediated myoblast proliferation inhibition which supports our hypothesis. These data provide an important theoretical basis for the practical application of Se element in poultry nutrition regulation strategies.

An in vitro myoblast culture model is essential for exploring skeletal muscle physiology and pathology (Fan et al., 2023). During myoblasts proliferation, cells labeled with Pax7 initially express Myf5, which can induce the expression of MyoD. Subsequently, myoblasts that acquire MyoD expression (Pax7+MyoD+) initiate the proliferation process and are defined as proliferative muscle progenitor cells (Buckingham and Rigby, 2014; Hu et al., 2021). Although previous studies have shown that Se supplementation can promote the proliferation of various types cells in chickens, such as peripheral lymphocytes, thymocytes, and spermatogonial stem cells, the effect of Se on the proliferation process of broiler myoblasts has not been reported (Chang et al., 1994; Peng et al., 2011; Shi et al., 2014). In the present study, through direct observation and biochemical analysis, we found that, in comparison with the Con group, incubation with Se supplementation increased myoblasts growth density and enhanced cell proliferation viability levels, which was accompanied by high expressions of the proliferation related MRFs (Pax7, Myf5, and MyoD) and proliferation markers (PCNA and Ki67), emphasizing the role of Se in promoting the proliferation of broiler myoblasts. However, it is noteworthy that our results were based on an appropriate level of Se supplementation and treatment time, as it is well known that excessive Se intake has a strong toxic effect on cell viability (Tinggi, 2003).

When the program of cell proliferation is initiated, cells begin to enter the cell cycle, which serves as the foundation for cell proliferation. The P53/P21/CyclinD1 pathway directly controls cell cycle progression. As a transcription factor renowned for its involvement in cell apoptosis and the cell cycle, P53 modulates the transcriptional expression of P21, which can form a complex with CyclinD1 and inhibit its expression, thus resulting in cell cycle arrest (Engeland, 2022; Karimian et al., 2016). For instance, it has been reported that the expression of P53 and P21 in myoblasts from older animals is significantly higher than that in myoblasts from young growing animals (Machida and Booth, 2004). Here, our data demonstrated that compared with the Con group, the expression of P53 and P21 in the Se-treated group was significantly reduced, while the expression of CyclinD1 was significantly increased, accompanied by a decrease in the proportion of myoblasts in the G1 phase and an increase in the proportion of myoblasts in the S+G2 phase, suggesting that Se promote the myoblasts proliferation by preventing cell cycle arrest. However, cell proliferation is a complex process involving the interactions between multiple signaling pathways and regulatory factors. Although the changes in genes expression of the P53/P21/CyclinD1 pathway have been identified, we could not determine that P53 is the only upstream signaling molecule controlling P21 and CyclinD1 in response to Se addition. For instance, in proliferating myoblasts, evidence implies that some MRFs can specifically regulate cell cycle-related proteins. Myf5 enhances CyclinD1 transcription and translation to promote cell cycle process in the early stage of proliferation, whereas MyoD can upregulate P21 expression to induce permanent cell cycle arrest in the late stage of proliferation (Kitzmann and Fernandez, 2001; Panda et al., 2016). In the present study, these 2 MRFs, with consistent expression trends under Se action, had completely opposite regulatory directions in cell cycle progression, emphasizing the importance of dynamic changes in the cell cycle in the proliferation and differentiation of myoblasts.

Under physiological conditions, the production and elimination of ROS are typically maintained a relatively balanced state. However, when the content of trace elements in the body is abnormal, ROS levels increase sharply, leading to oxidative stress (Apel and Hirt, 2004). Although existing studies have illustrated that Se supplementation can restrain the excessive accumulation of ROS and the occurrence of oxidative stress, but the specific manifestation of this mechanism in broiler myoblasts still requires further validation (Wang et al., 2022b; Wu, et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2023). Myoblasts are a type of cells that are highly sensitive to intracellular ROS generation, and even subtle changes in intracellular ROS levels can affect signal transduction within myoblasts (Baker et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022a). Similarly, our current data showed that Se treatment significantly increased the mRNA expression levels of the Se-containing antioxidant genes GPX1 and GPX2, and inhibited the overproduction of intracellular ROS in broiler myoblasts. However, this finding represents only 1 aspect of Se antioxidant function, and further exploration is needed to investigate the changes in the downstream signaling pathways of ROS involved in regulating cell proliferation. The PTEN/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway controls multiple biological processes and plays a crucial role in the cell proliferation behavior. Lu et al. (2016) reported that the mutant of PTEN in A549 cells induced activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and promoted cell cycle-regulated proliferation. Zhang et al. (2022b) showed that dietary Se deficiency suppressed PI3K/AKT pathway expression and induced cell cycle arrest in the chicken liver via upregulating PTEN expression. Meanwhile, increasing evidences have demonstrated that ROS, as an upstream signaling chemical substance, can activate the expression of PTEN, thereby inhibiting the proliferation-promoting process regulated by PI3K/AKT (Feng et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2023). In the current study, we found that Se supplementation decreased the expression of PTEN and induced the mRNA expression and protein phosphorylation of PI3K and AKT in the myoblasts of broiler chickens, indicating that Se may positively regulate the proliferation of broiler myoblasts by modulating the ROS/PTEN/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.

CONCLUSION

This study established an in vitro broiler breast muscle myoblasts culture model under Se incubation to investigate the effects of Se on the proliferation of broiler myoblasts. It was found that an appropriate Se supplemental level could promote the proliferation and the cell cycle type transition from the G1 phase to the S + G2 phase in broiler myoblasts, accompanied by changes in the expression of related proliferation regulatory genes. Mechanistically, Se supplementation may restrain ROS excessive generation and PTEN expression to activate the phosphorylation of the downstream signaling genes PI3K and AKT, which is the main reason for promoting myoblast proliferation potential (Fig. 6). Given the crucial role of Se in myoblasts proliferation and differentiation, our study contributes to the accumulation of a theoretical basis for skeletal muscle development and growth of broilers.

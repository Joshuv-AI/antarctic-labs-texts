Ventral hippocampal autophagy and mitophagy dynamics shape behavioral responses to chronic mild stress in adult male rats
Authors: Paola Brivio, Maria Teresa Gallo, Elena Volpari, Piotr Gruca, Magdalena Lason, Ewa Litwa, Fabio Fumagalli, Mariusz Papp, Francesca Calabrese
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a highly prevalent psychiatric condition characterized by a range of symptoms that often lead to reduced quality of life. Although chronic stress is a major risk factor for the development of MDD, only a subset of individuals exposed to stress develop depressive symptoms, while others remain resilient. Emerging evidence suggests that autophagy and mitophagy, key cellular processes involved in maintaining homeostasis and energy balance, may play a critical role in the response to stress. In this study, we investigated the impact of 6 weeks of chronic mild stress (CMS) on autophagy and mitophagy pathways in adult male rats, aiming to explore their potential association with vulnerability or resilience to stress-induced anhedonic-like behavior. By analyzing key autophagy and mitophagy markers in the dorsal (dHip) and ventral hippocampus (vHip), we describe region- and phenotype-specific alterations that may reflect distinct neurobiological adaptations to stress. In particular, we observed enhanced mitophagy alongside an overall impairment of autophagy in the vHip of vulnerable rats, while resilient animals showed preserved activity. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms associated with stress susceptibility and may inform future studies aimed at identifying novel therapeutic targets for MDD.

Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a chronic and recurrent psychiatric condition, whose core symptoms include depressed mood, feelings of worthlessness, social withdrawal, anhedonia, and suicidal ideation. This symptomatology is associated with a marked reduction in overall quality of life (Herrman et al., 2022). Despite MDD is one of the leading causes of disability worldwide, affecting nearly 16 % of the global population, the underlying mechanisms remain only partially understood.

Among the principal risk factors for the onset of depression, stress has emerged as a critical contributor, playing a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of the disorder. Prolonged exposure to stress has been shown to increase vulnerability to depression (McEwen et al., 2015). However, only a small proportion of individuals exposed to stress develop depressive disorders, while the majority display resilience to the adverse effects of chronic stress. Resilient individuals activate a range of adaptive stress-response mechanisms that enable them to cope effectively with stressful life events without developing persistent psychopathology (Cathomas et al., 2019; Nestler and Russo, 2024; Russo et al., 2012).

Recent evidence suggests that the dysregulation of autophagic processes is implicated in both the pathogenesis of depression and the efficacy of antidepressant treatments (Griffey and Yamamoto, 2022). Autophagy is a fundamental mechanism for maintaining cellular homeostasis under stress conditions. It is a tightly regulated process by which misfolded proteins and damaged or aged organelles are sequestered into double-membraned vesicles called autophagosomes, which subsequently fuse with lysosomes to degrade their contents (Behrends et al., 2010). Mitochondrial autophagy, or mitophagy, is specifically essential for maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis. To preserve the integrity of the mitochondrial network and the intracellular environment, cells employ mitophagy to selectively degrade damaged or dysfunctional mitochondria. Autophagy and mitophagy are mutually functional, they interact and cooperate for the maintenance of a proper cell homeostasis (Audano et al., 2018). Moreover, both mechanisms are closely involved in the regulation of energy metabolism, thus contributing to the production of ATP and nutrient resources for cells under starvation conditions (Feng et al., 2024).

Within this scenario, we recently observed that the different susceptibility to develop a depressive-like behavior was linked to alterations in bioenergetics following a short CMS protocol (Brivio et al., 2022a), whereas a longer period of chronic stress exposure induced a more stable behavioral phenotype and long-lasting neurobiological adaptations characterized by an alteration of the substrate utilization for the production of energy in vulnerable and resilient animals (Brivio et al., 2023).

In this study we investigated the effect of chronic stress on autophagy and mitophagy, aiming to evaluate their potential involvement in differential susceptibility to depression in an animal model based on chronic mild stress (CMS) exposure during adulthood. We then exposed adult male rats to a six-week CMS protocol, subdividing the rat populations in vulnerable and resilient rats, on the base of the anhedonic response to chronic stress. We focused on investigating alterations of key targets involved in these cellular pathways. The analyses were conducted in the hippocampus, a medial temporal lobe structure critically involved in the stress response (McEwen et al., 2016), which may also exhibit differential responses in subjects with distinct stress susceptibility (Larosa and Wong, 2022). In particular, we focused on the dorsal (dHip) and ventral (vHip) subregions of the hippocampus, as they differ in their connectivity with other brain areas and in receptor distribution, as well as in neurotransmitters composition, leading to functional distinction and distinct responses to the same stimulus, such as stress. The dorsal hippocampus is primarily associated with cognitive functions, while the ventral counterpart is more closely linked to stress and emotional regulation (Fanselow and Dong, 2010).

In summary, our findings show that chronic mild stress induces region- and phenotype-specific changes in autophagy and mitophagy pathways, with vulnerable animals exhibiting alterations in autophagy and mitophagy in the ventral hippocampus, whereas resilient animals maintain more stable autophagy/mitophagy dynamics. These molecular differences suggest that the balance between autophagy impairment and mitophagy activation specifically in this hippocampal subregion may underlie susceptibility or resilience to stress. Identifying and targeting these pathways may therefore represent promising avenues for novel therapeutic interventions in MDD.

Methods

Animals

Adult male Wistar rats (50 days-old) were brought into the animal facility for one month of adaptation before the start of the experiment. The first 10 days after arrival the animals were housed in groups of 10, then they were singly housed in standard laboratory conditions: food and water freely available on a 12-h light/dark, constant temperature (22 ± 2 °C) and humidity (50 ± 5 %). All procedures used in this study have conformed to the rules and principles of the 86/609/EEC Directive and have been approved by the Local Bioethical Committee (approval code: 4/2019; approval date 03/01/2019) at the Maj Institute of Pharmacology, Polish Academy of Sciences, Krakow, Poland.

Chronic mild stress and sucrose consumption test

Rats were trained to consume 1 % sucrose solution for 6 weeks before the starting of the CMS procedure and sucrose consumption was monitored at weekly intervals throughout the duration of the study. On the basis of their sucrose intakes in the final baseline test (week 6 of adaptation), the animals were randomly divided into two matched groups: one group was exposed to the CMS procedure for a period of 6 consecutive weeks, and the other one was not subjected to the CMS (No stress group). CMS consisted in of a random combination of 10–14h periods of stress, including food or water deprivation, 45° cage tilt, intermittent illumination every 2 h, soiled cage with 250 ml water in sawdust bedding, paired housing, low intensity stroboscopic illumination-150 flashes/min, and periods of rest (no stressors). The weekly monitoring of sucrose intake allowed us to separate animals showing an anhedonic-like phenotype (vulnerable:sucrose intake minor of the mean of the control group at the 6th week of CMS—2 standard deviation (sd) (∼70 %), by the animals that did not develop anhedonic-like behaviour despite CMS procedure (resilient:sucrose intake major than the mean of the control group at the 6th week of CMS—2sd) (∼30 %).

RNA preparation and gene expression analysis by quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was isolated by a single step of guanidinium isothiocyanate/phenol extraction using PureZol RNA isolation reagent (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Italy) and quantified by spectrophotometric analysis as previously described (Brivio et al., 2019b). An aliquot of each sample was treated with DNase (ThermoFisher scientific, Italy) to avoid DNA contamination. Real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was performed to assess Bcl2, Becn1, Ctsb, Lamp1, Map1lc3/LC3, Ulk1 (See details in Table 1a–b) mRNA levels. RNA was analyzed by TaqMan qRT-PCR instrument (CFX384 real time system, Bio-Rad Laboratories, Italy) using the iScriptTM one-step RT-PCR kit for probes (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Italy) (see (Brivio et al., 2020) for details). Samples were run in 384 well formats in triplicate as multiplexed reactions with the normalizing internal control 36B4. Protein extraction and Western blot analysis

Western blot analysis was used to investigate ATG5, ATG12, BECLIN1, BNIP3L/NIX full length (fl) and truncated (tr) form, CATALASE, LC3 II, mTOR, PARKIN, PINK, P62, SOD2, TFEB and ULK1 (phospho Ser757 and total) in the subcellular fractions. Tissues were manually homogenized using a glass-glass potter in a pH 7.4 cold buffer containing 0.32 M sucrose, 0.1 mM EGTA, 1 mM HEPES solution in the presence of a complete set of proteases (Roche, Monza, Italy) and phosphatase (Merck Life Science S.r.l, Milano, Italy) inhibitors. The total homogenate was centrifuged at 1000g for 10 min at 4 °C to obtain a pellet enriched in nuclear components, which was suspended in a buffer (20 mM HEPES, 0.1 mM dithiothreitol, 0.1 mM EGTA) with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. The supernatant obtained was further centrifuged at 10000 g for 15 min at 4 °C to obtain the pellet corresponding to the membrane fraction which was re-suspended in the same buffer prepared for the nuclear fraction. The purity of the fraction obtained was detailed in (Brivio et al., 2019a). Total protein content was measured according to the Bradford Protein Assay procedure (Bio-Rad Laboratories S.r.l, Segrate, Italy), using bovine serum albumin as a calibration standard. 10ug of protein were run under reducing conditions on 10 % SDS-polyacrylamide gels and then electrophoretically transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes (Bio-Rad Laboratories S.r.l, Segrate, Italy). The blots were blocked with 5 % nonfat dry milk (Euroclone, Milano, Italy) and then were incubated with the primary and secondary antibodies summarized in Table 2. Immunocomplexes were visualized by chemiluminescence using Chemidoc MP imaging system (Bio-Rad Laboratories S.r.l, Segrate, Italy). Statistical analysis

The results were analyzed with the one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey multiple comparison test. Each experimental group consists of 6 rats. Significance for all tests was assumed for p < 0.05.

Considering the body weight gain through the whole CMS procedure (Fig. 1C), one-way ANOVA revealed a significant stress effect (F2-16 = 14, p < 0.001). Accordingly, after 6 weeks of CMS both vulnerable and resilient rats had a reduction in body weight gain with respect to no stress rats (respectively vulnerable: 18.33, p < 0.05 vs no stress, resilient: 16.66 p < 0.01 vs no stress).

These results indicated that 6 weeks of CMS allow to discriminate vulnerable and resilient subpopulations of rats based on their hedonic phenotype while both groups, regardless of behavioral phenotype, showed a reduction in body weight gain.

Six weeks of CMS modulated mitophagy in the vHip of vulnerable rats

Given that we previously demonstrated that a short CMS protocol influences mitophagy mechanisms in the vHip (Brivio et al., 2022a), here we first investigated whether prolonged stress exposure induced stabilization of the stress-induced changes on mitophagy players in both vHip and dHip.

In the vHip, one-way ANOVA showed a significant effect of stress on PINK1 (F2-16 = 4.52, p < 0.05), PARKIN (F2-16 = 9.95, p < 0.01), CAT (F2-16 = 7.39, p < 0.01) and SOD2 (F2-16 = 4.04, p < 0.01) protein levels. Indeed, PINK1 was specifically upregulated in vulnerable vs no stress (+92 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) (Fig. 2A), PARKIN was enhanced in vulnerable vs both no stress (+69 %, p < 0.001 vs no stress) and resilient animals (+40 %, p < 0.01 vs resilient) (Fig. 2B), whereas the protein levels of the antioxidant CAT were increased in both vulnerable (+29 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) and resilient (+40 %, p < 0.01 vs no stress) rats in comparison to no stress (Fig. 2E) and SOD2 was upregulated in vulnerable rats vs both no stress (+43 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) and resilient animals (+34 %, p < 0.05 vs resilient) (Fig. 2F).

By contrast, BNIP3L/NIX protein levels were unaffected by 6 weeks of chronic stress either in the full length (Fig. 2C) form or in the truncated one (Fig. 2D).

On the opposite, in dHip we did not find any significant effect of stress on the mitophagy markers (Fig. 2G-H-I-L) and on SOD2 (Fig. 2K), while one-way ANOVA revealed a significant stress effect on CAT (F2-16 = 6.8, p < 0.01), with its protein levels being enhanced specifically in resilient rats (+31 %, p < 0.01 vs vulnerable).

These findings indicate a region-specific activation of mitophagy pathways uniquely associated with the vulnerable phenotype.

Autophagosome formation was altered in the vHip of vulnerable rats

Next, we assessed the protein levels of key components involved in autophagosome formation, as well as of ATG proteins, key players of the autophagic machinery (Nishimura and Tooze, 2020) and P62, an autophagy adaptor (Liu et al., 2016). ULK1 and BECLIN are pivotal initiators of this process, driving the formation of the autophagosome (Zachari and Ganley, 2017), while LC3 II localizes to both pre-autophagosomal and autophagosomal membranes and it is degraded by lysosomal hydrolases following autophagosome-lysosome fusion (Tanida et al., 2004). Moreover, through its interaction with Bcl2, BECLIN is crucial in regulating the balance between autophagy and apoptosis (Marquez and Xu, 2012).

In the vHip, we observed a significant effect of stress in the one-way ANOVA on BECLIN (F2-16 = 6.34, p < 0.01) and LC3 II (F2-16 = 3.59, p < 0.05) protein levels as well as on Bcl2 (F2-16 = 9.41, p < 0.01) mRNA levels, while we did not find a statistical effect on ULK1 protein expression (Fig. 2A). Indeed, upregulation of BECLIN (+42 %, p < 0.01 vs no stress) (Fig. 3B) and LC3 II (+34 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) (Fig. 3C) protein levels was observed in vulnerable vs no stress, whereas Bcl2 was enhanced in vulnerable vs both no stress (+24 %, p < 0.01 vs no stress) and resilient (+19 %, p < 0.01 vs resilient) groups (Fig. 3D).

On the opposite we did not observe any modulation in the markers of autophagy due to chronic stress exposure in the dHip (Fig. 3E-F-G-H).

In vHip, one-way ANOVA revealed a significant effect of stress on mTOR protein expression (F2-16 = 8.24, p < 0.01) with its levels being increased in vulnerable rats in comparison to both no stress (+77 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) and resilient animals (+56 %, p < 0.01 vs resilient) (Fig. 4B). Indeed, one-way ANOVA showed a significant stress effect on both cytosolic (F2-16 = 3.55, p < 0.05) and nuclear (F2-16 = 7.51, p < 0.01) TFEB protein levels, with their expression being respectively upregulated (+93 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) and downregulated (−47 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) in vulnerable in comparison to no stress group.

Conversely, in dHip, despite we found no changes in mTOR protein levels (Fig. 4D), while comparing the protein expression of TFEB in the two intracellular compartment, we observed a significant effect of stress in the one-way ANOVA (nucleus: F2-16 = 3.68, p < 0.05; cytosol: F2-16 = 6.74, p < 0.01), with TFEB protein levels downregulated specifically in resilient rats vs no stress (nucleus: 13 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress; cytosol: 20 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress).

These findings seem to point to region-specific and phenotype-dependent regulation of TFEB, suggesting that while in the vHip of vulnerable animals the reduced nuclear translocation of TFEB appears consistent with mTOR's inhibitory effect on TFEB, in the dHip the TFEB downregulation observed specifically in resilient rats likely occurs via mTOR-independent mechanisms.

CMS affected the transcription of TFEB responsive genes in the vHip of vulnerable rats

We then analyzed the expression of key genes whose transcription is regulated by the TFEB protein. Indeed, by translocating into the nucleus, TFEB modulates the expression of target genes containing the CLEAR sequence in their promoter regions, thereby influencing autophagy and lysosomal biogenesis.

As shown in Fig. 5A, in the vHip one-way ANOVA showed a significant effect of stress on Ulk1 (F2-16 = 3.47, p < 0.05), Becn1 (F2-16 = 4.05, p < 0.05), Map1lc3/LC3 (F2-16 = 6.54, p < 0.01) and Ctsb (F2-16 = 3.81, p < 0.05) gene expression levels. In particular, we observed that Ulk1 and Becn1 mRNA levels were decreased in vulnerable rats (Ulk1: 24 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress; Becn1: 13 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress) while Map1lc3/LC3 and Ctsb were increased in this subgroup of stressed rats (Map1lc3/LC3: +41 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress; Ctsb: +18 %, p < 0.05 vs no stress). Discussion

The results herein presented show that exposure to six weeks of chronic mild stress induced an anhedonic-like phenotype in a subset of stressed animals and resulted in a dysregulation of the cellular processes of mitophagy and autophagy mainly in the vHip.

Consistent with previous findings, we observed that starting from the first week of CMS, vulnerable rats exhibited a statistically significant reduction in sucrose intake compared to non-stressed animals, indicating the onset of a pathological-like phenotype. This trend persisted throughout the entire CMS duration. Conversely, the remaining CMS-exposed animals, termed “resilient,” showed resistance to stress and maintained sucrose intake levels comparable to those of non-stressed controls (Brivio et al., 2022a, 2022b, 2023, 2024; Calabrese et al., 2020).

Furthermore, CMS exposure was found to influence body weight gain both in vulnerable and resilient rats indicating an overall stress effect on body weight. This result is consistent with our previous data reporting that the development of an anhedonic-like phenotype following chronic stress is associated with altered weight gain dynamics (Brivio et al., 2021b; Guidotti et al., 2013). Moreover these data indicated that a longer period of stress exposure impacted body weight, regardless of the behavioral phenotype. Even if weight loss is a well-known symptom (hallmark) of depression and stress related disorders at large, the underlying mechanisms are not fully elucidated to date. Interestingly, stress-induced body weight loss has been linked with dysregulated autophagy pathways, which are also implicated with eating behaviors (Bajaj et al., 2022) and metabolic dysregulations. (Coupé et al., 2012).

In this context, we demonstrated that exposure to a short CMS protocol affected brain metabolism and key players of mitophagy within the vHip (Brivio et al., 2022a).

Given that regulatory mechanisms dynamically evolve over time, we investigated whether the stable behavioral phenotype observed following prolonged stress was paralleled by persistent stress-induced changes in mitophagy and autophagy pathways, with the aim of elucidating their potential involvement in the differential susceptibility to stress in both the vHip and dHip.

Our results revealed that both pathways were selectively altered in vHip, indicating a subregion-specific regulation of autophagy and mitophagy in response to 6 weeks of CMS, in line with our recent finding demonstrating that this brain region is particularly affected by this same experimental paradigm (Brivio et al., 2021a, 2022a, 2022b, 2023, 2024; Herrman et al., 2022).

Regarding mitophagy, we observed that both PINK and PARKIN, two proteins involved in the ubiquitin-dependent PARKIN-mediated mitophagy pathway (Harper et al., 2018) were significantly increased in the vHip of vulnerable, but not resilient, rats while we did not find changes in the expression of BNIP3L/NIX, a mediator that recruits autophagosomes to damaged mitochondria (Novak et al., 2010), in either the full-length or truncated forms of this protein.

Actually, these results are different to what we found after two weeks of CMS, with PINK being significantly upregulated in resilient rats and both forms of BNIP3L/NIX upregulated in vulnerable rats (Brivio et al., 2022a), suggesting that PARKIN-dependent mitophagy becomes predominant during prolonged stress while BNIP3L/NIX-mediated mitophagy is activated during the early phase of chronic stress exposure, thus reflecting a broader induction of the mitophagy response.

Furthermore, considering the protein levels of CAT and SOD2, enzymes involved in protecting mitochondria from oxidative damage, in the vHip we observed a significant increase in CAT expression in both vulnerable and resilient animals. Notably, after two weeks of CMS, we found this increase specifically in the same hippocampal subregion of vulnerable animals (Brivio et al., 2022a), suggesting that after prolonged stress exposure resilience depend upon a strong activation of antioxidant mechanisms.

Subsequently, we analyzed the expression levels of key proteins involved in the autophagy pathway, a multi-step process by which cellular content is sequestered into autophagosomes and delivered to lysosomes for degradation (Griffey and Yamamoto, 2022). We observed that the levels of ULK1, a serine/threonine kinase that acts as a node to convert stress signals into autophagosome formation (Lee and Tournier, 2011), did not undergo significant changes in either hippocampal subregion following six weeks of CMS. However, in vHip of vulnerable rats, we detected a trend toward an increase of the ratio of phosphorylated (Ser757) and total ULK1. A similar effect was also observed in the hippocampus following foot shock-induced stress (Chen et al., 2024). Regarding BECLIN, a protein whose activation initiates the formation of the phagophore that will enclose organelles and proteins for degradation (Prerna and Dubey, 2022), we observed a significant increase in the vHip of CMS-vulnerable rats. A similar effect was also found for LC3 II, a protein located on both the inner and outer surfaces of the autophagosome, where it plays a role in membrane hemifusion and cargo selection for degradation. These results are consistent with those reported by Zhang and colleagues, who observed increased levels of BECLIN and LC3 II in the hippocampus of rats exposed to 21 days of chronic stress (Zhang et al., 2020).

These findings indicate that while phospho ULK1 Ser757 and total ULK1 protein levels remain unchanged, with a similar effect on ATGs and P62, vulnerable animals show increased expression of BECLIN-1 and LC3 II, which may reflect an accumulation of autophagosomes, even if these markers can also accumulate when autophagy is stalled at later stages, thus not necessarily indicating enhanced autophagic flux.

An important feature of BECLIN is, indeed, its interaction with Bcl2, an anti-apoptotic mediator that prevents BECLIN from assembling the autophagosome structure (Griffey and Yamamoto, 2022). In line with the increase in BECLIN protein level within vHip, we observed a significant increase in Bcl2 gene expression in vulnerable rats. Therefore, considering the interaction between Bcl2 and BECLIN, these findings suggest that the increased BECLIN levels may be inhibited by the concurrent upregulation of Bcl2 expression, potentially interfering with the autophagosome formation in vulnerable rats.

Another key regulator of the autophagy processes is mTOR, which is activated on the cytosolic side of lysosomes by a protein complex that senses amino acids (Sancak et al., 2010). mTOR mediates different autophagy-related mechanisms: once it is triggered, it inhibits autophagy by modulating ULK1 (Ganley et al., 2009), and modulates TFEB cellular localization, thus directly influencing its transcriptional activity (Martina et al., 2012). We observed that mTOR was significantly increased in the vHip of CMS-vulnerable, indicating that chronic stress activated mTOR thus affecting autophagy mechanisms. Similarly, it has been demonstrated that chronic stress elevated mTOR levels, and that rapamycin, an mTOR inhibitor, injection alleviates not only depression-like behavior but also affected autophagy in the lateral habenula (Yang et al., 2025). The modulation observed in the vHip of vulnerable rats aligned with the alteration of TFEB localization. Indeed, its increase favored the retention of TFEB in the cytosol thus inhibiting its nuclear translocation, and in turn resulting in a reduced activation of the autophagic process. In line, TFEB is a transcription factor responsible for the expression of genes involved in autophagy. Its activity depends on cellular localization: it promotes gene transcription when located in the nucleus and is inactive when retained in the cytosol (Sha et al., 2017). A reduction in nuclear TFEB has also been reported in the literature, associated with the onset of neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and ALS (Wang et al., 2016).

These findings suggest that elevated mTOR levels lead to reduced nuclear translocation of TFEB and consequently inhibited the gene transcription. Accordingly, we found that Ulk1 and Becn1 were significantly downregulated in CMS-vulnerable rats. These results suggested an impairment in the transcriptional activity of TFEB within the vHip, likely contributing to the pathological phenotype of vulnerable animals, as highlighted by the significant correlation among TFEB responsive genes and sucrose intake, supporting the notion that this hippocampal subregion is primarily involved in the management of the anhedonic behaviour.

Overall, these findings suggest that, in vHip, mitophagy compensates for an overall compromised autophagic system, as indicated by the overall reduction of TFEB driven transcription of core autophagy-related genes in vulnerable rats, to degrade damaged mitochondria in face to chronic stress.

To summarize, our data indicated that six weeks of CMS exposure led to dysregulation of cellular mitophagy and autophagy processes in a region-specific manner within the hippocampus. Specifically, we observed a general induction of PARKIN-dependent mitophagy in the vHip of vulnerable subjects, as well as increased oxidative damage in all stressed animals. These results suggested that such processes may underlie the development of a depression-like phenotype, consistent with evidence linking mitochondrial dysfunction in various brain regions to the pathogenesis of the disorder (Bansal and Kuhad, 2016). Regarding autophagy, we observed a general inhibition of the process specifically in vulnerable animals exposed to six weeks of CMS. Such inhibition may contribute to the development of the anhedonic phenotype, as previously reported (Yang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2017). In line, we recently demonstrated that the anhedonic-like behaviour in adult male rats induced by prenatal manipulation of the serotonergic system was associated to the inhibition of autophagy processes within vHip (Gallo et al., 2023).

Furthermore, the increase in LC3 II protein observed in the ventral hippocampus suggested that chronic stress promotes the initial formation of autophagosomes, which, however, fails to be completed due to mTOR-mediated inhibition. It is important to note that LC3 II also participates in mitophagy by interacting with mitophagy receptors located in the outer mitochondrial membranes. Therefore, its increase also aligns with the mitophagy induction we observed.

Finally, the reduced body weight gain observed in vulnerable rats is consistent with autophagy inhibition, in agreement with findings by Tao and colleagues, who reported that inhibition or deletion of key autophagy-related genes can impair adipocyte differentiation and lipid accumulation (Tao and Xu, 2020).

In conclusion, these data indicate a potential role of autophagy and mitophagy in the differential susceptibility to developing a depression-like phenotype following chronic stress exposure and suggest that resilience may be associated with a balanced regulation of autophagic mechanisms to counteract excessive stress exposure.

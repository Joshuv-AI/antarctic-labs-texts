BLNK as an important prognostic indicator for survival in pediatric classic hodgkin lymphoma patients: a single-center retrospective study
Authors: Yiwei Yan, Yuanyuan Hou, Xiuli Zhu, Jian Chen, Wenting Zhang, Yuqiao Diao, Xiaofei Lu, Lian Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractClassic Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) is a malignant tumor in children. At present, some patients cannot be cured with first-line treatment, and many cured patients die prematurely because of the late toxic effects of treatment. In addition, effective early survival prediction biomarkers are lacking. The aim of this study was to explore the predictive value of B-cell linker protein (BLNK) in cHL. Sixty-three patients from the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University were included in the study from January 2008 to March 2024 (training set). Immunohistochemistry was used to measure the expression of BLNK. Eleven patients with < 10% BLNK-positive cells (BLNK-negative, BLNK-) and 52 patients with 10 ~ 49% BLNK-positive cells (BLNK-positive, BLNK+) were included. The correlations between BLNK expression and clinicopathological features were analyzed. Univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were used to determine the independent prognostic variables of overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Construction and validation of nomogram to predict PFS of cHL patients. In addition, we included 29 patients with cHL who were admitted between January 2000 and December 2007 as a validation set to explore the robustness of the above prediction model. The OS and PFS of cHL patients in the BLNK+ group were significantly longer than those of patients in the BLNK- group. BLNK expression, the presence of bulky disease, the B lymphocyte count, LDH levels and the neutrophil count were determined to be independent prognostic factors by multivariable Cox regression analysis of PFS. A nomogram model was constructed based on these factors. The discriminative ability of the nomogram was good; the C-index was 0.844 (0.761, 0.926) in the training set and 0.824 (0.717, 0.931) in the validation set. The results of the calibration curve suggested strong agreement between the forecast and actual observations in both the training set and the validation set. Subgroup analysis successfully identified a treatment-sensitive population of cHL patients to predict prognosis according to the BLNK level. In the subgroups without giant tumor lesions, an age≥9 years, St. Jude Stages III–IV, chemotherapy regimens (1/2), a CRP concentration >7.5 mg/L, a B lymphocyte count >5.7 × 10^6/L, a lymphocyte count ≤ 3.68 × 10^9/L, and a platelet count ≤ 387 × 10^9/L had a greater effect on PFS in the BLNK-negative group. An age ≥9 years, St. Jude Stages III–IV, the absence of a giant tumor lesion, a chemotherapy regimen (2), a CRP concentration >10.2 mg/L, a B lymphocyte count >18.5 × 10^6/L, an IgG concentration >6.91 g/L, a neutrophil count ≤ 5.6 × 10^9/L, a lymphocyte count >0.49 × 10^9/L, a platelet count >298.5 × 10^9/L, a PLR >199.31, and an NLR >3.17 had a greater effect on OS in the BLNK-negative group. Decreased BLNK expression predicts prolonged PFS among patients with cHL. The validated nomogram integrating BLNK and clinical parameters provides potential predictive value for PFS of cHL. Introduction

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) is a subtype of malignant lymphoma and has a heterogeneous tumor microenvironment and a relative lack of malignant Hodgkin cells and Reed–Sternberg cells with characteristic phenotypes [1, 2]. The global incidence of cHL is low, with bimodal age peaks among young adults (15–34 years) and individuals >50 years in high-income countries, including the United States; notably, young adults constitute the majority of the cohort [3]. The estimated age-stratified annual incidence rates per 1,000,000 are 29 (15–19 years), ~ 10 (10–14 years), 3.5 (5–9 years), and 1 (0–4 years) [3]. Currently, multidrug chemotherapy with or without radiotherapy is the first-line treatment for cHL. The 5-year survival rates for early- and late-stage patients are approximately 90% and 80%, respectively [4, 5]. However, late treatment-related toxicities, such as second primary malignancies and cardiac events, are common problems in cHL treatment [6]. Approximately 10–25% of patients will develop refractory disease or relapse to refractory cHL (R/R cHL) after remission [7, 8]. Currently, therapeutic strategies based on cHL gene expression profiles have not been thoroughly investigated.

B-Cell linker protein (BLNK) encodes a cytoplasmic linker or adaptor protein that plays a critical role in B-cell development. Studies have shown that this protein links B-cell receptor-associated kinase activation with downstream signaling pathways, thereby affecting various biological functions [9, 10]. BLNK expression is downregulated in primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma and Hodgkin’s lymphoma, and the downregulation of BLNK expression is an important contributing factor to malignant lymphocyte transformation [11]. Marafioti et al. [12] reported that BLNK expression is decreased in mediastinal B-cell lymphomas and that the expression pattern is similar to that of classical Hodgkin’s disease. BLNK expression is also reduced in tumor cells in classical Hodgkin’s disease and is involved in intracellular signaling [13]. Moreover, Kuang et al. [14] reported that BLNK was differentially expressed in cHL and enriched in the B-cell receptor signaling pathway. Thus, BLNK has strong potential as a target for the diagnosis or treatment of cHL [14].

Recent studies have indicated that B-cell-specific signaling molecules such as BLNK may participate in the prognostic regulation of childhood Classic Hodgkin lymphoma. BLNK modulates the tumor microenvironment through the PI3K-Akt and NF-κB pathways, and its deficiency is associated with immune evasion by Hodgkin–Reed–Sternberg (HRS) cells. In pediatric cHL prognostic studies, commonly used factors include demographic data (e.g., age and sex), clinicopathological features (e.g., disease stage: histological subtypes, Ann Arbor staging system I–IV, bulky disease, presence of B symptoms, and LDH levels), and treatment-related factors (e.g., therapeutic regimens and responses). A study published in 2023 by Jiang et al. [15] revealed that EBV infection is the only independent prognostic factor for progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients with nodular sclerosis cHL, whereas a German multicenter adult study [16] revealed age (>50 years) and frontline treatment regimen as independent risk factors. A 2025 Chinese multicenter pediatric study [17] suggested that bulky disease may be an independent risk factor for event-free survival (EFS). Currently, consensus on independent risk factors for pediatric cHL are lacking, and the clinical significance of BLNK in childhood cHL remains unclear, particularly its synergistic effects with conventional indicators (e.g., LDH and B symptoms), which warrant further exploration.

Currently, long-term survival data and predictive markers for pediatric patients are lacking. The aim of this study was to explore the impact of BLNK on long-term survival through retrospective cohort analysis and the early predictors of disease prognosis to provide new perspectives for the discovery of potential therapeutic strategies to improve prognosis.

Materials and methods

Patients and ethics

This work was a single-center retrospective cohort study conducted from January 2008 to March 2024 (total: 63 patients). This study included all pediatric patients who were diagnosed with and treated for classical Hodgkin lymphoma at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University. To verify the accuracy of the data model, we included 29 patients with cHL who were admitted to the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University from January 2000 to December 2007 as a validation set. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) through tissue biopsy, pathological morphology, immunohistochemistry, genetic testing, immunophenotyping, cytogenetics, and molecular biology examinations; (2) patients who were newly diagnosed and had not received any prior treatment; (3) patients whose clinical staging was performed using the St. Jude staging system based on the revised Cotswold Conference criteria for staging and risk stratification; and (4) patients whose complete clinical records were available for analysis. The following patients were excluded from this study: (1) Patients whose follow-up information was incomplete and whose prognosis was unknown. (2) Patients who had received radiotherapy, chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy. (3) Patients with other primary malignant tumors. (4) Patients with acute infection.

The collection and publication of patient information were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Hebei, Shijiazhuang, China; Ethical approval number: 2024KS081). To protect their personal information and respect their privacy, this study does not disclose the data of the research participants. The content/data of this study may be obtained from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Expression of BLNK by immunohistochemistry

S-P immunohistochemistry was used to determine the relative expression of BLNK in tissue samples from children with cHL. The results were observed and assessed by two senior pathologists in a double-blind manner. Five random fields (20× magnification) were selected, and 1000 cells were counted to identify positively stained cells. The cell positivity rate was calculated as (number of positive tumor cells/1000) × 100%. All the IHC results were rigorously validated by experienced pathologists at our institution who were blinded to the study groups. In this study, the percentage of BLNK immunohistochemically positive cells ranged from 2% to 23%. The intensity grading criteria [18–22] were as follows: <10% (n = 11) positive cells, 10% to 49% (n = 52) positive cells, 50% to 95% (n = 0) positive cells, and >95% (n = 0) positive cells. In this study, < 10% positive cell was defined as BLNK-negative (BLNK-), and 10% to 49% was defined as BLNK-positive (BLNK+).

Clinical and laboratory variables

The following data were collected: general clinical information (age and sex), relative expression levels of BLNK [with BLNK-positive cell number < 10% (BLNK-negative, BLNK-), BLNK-positive cell number 10 ~ 49% (BLNK-positive, BLNK+)], and initial examination indicators: the presence of EB virus infection, the presence of B symptoms (fever with a temperature exceeding 38 ℃, night sweats soaking through clothing, and unexplained weight loss of more than 10% within the past 6 months), disease staging, giant tumor lesion (a single lymph node with a diameter greater than 6 cm or a cluster of lymph nodes with a diameter greater than 10 cm), histological types (nodular sclerosing/mixed cellularity), laboratory inspection indicators (CRP: C reactive protein; LDH: lactate dehydrogenase; IgG: immunoglobulin G; N: neutrophil count; L: lymphocyte count; PLT: platelet count; PLR: platelet to lymphocyte count ratio; NLR: neutrophil to lymphocyte count ratio), and chemotherapy regimens (Scheme 1: HD-2004 protocol COMP/ABV→EA→CHOP alternating; Scheme 2: HL-2013 protocol COMP/ABV→IFOS/EMVP alternating; Scheme 3: CNCL-2017-HL protocol Cycle A→COPP/ABV→Cycle C), as well as any additional treatment methods such as immunotherapy and evaluation results at different stages.

Follow-up

All the enrolled pediatric cHL patients were followed up through telephone calls, letters, and outpatient visits. The objectives of the follow-up were to assess disease progression, specifically progression-free survival (PFS), and survival outcomes, including overall survival (OS). The follow-up started from the date of diagnosis of cHL and ended on March 1, 2024, or when the patient reached the endpoint (death). The follow-up duration ranged from 1.1 to 256.0 months.

Nomogram generation

In this study, the independent risk or protective factors selected by multivariate Cox proportional hazards analyses were included in the nomogram, and the 12-, 24-, 36-, and 60-month survival rates of patients with cHL were predicted. The R software package “rms” was used to generate the nomogram.

Validation of the nomogram

First, the nomogram was validated by the concordance index (C-index). The functions in R software were used to calculate the C-index, which ranged from 0.5 to 1.0; a value closer to 1 indicated better discriminative ability of the model. The C-indices in the training set and validation set were calculated with the Cox regression model method, and the discrimination of the nomogram was estimated based on the C-index. Moreover, the differences between the actual prognosis and that predicted by the nomogram were compared with a calibration curve. The calibration curve was used to visualize the difference between the actual prognosis and that predicted by the nomogram. With respect to the calibration curve, the 45-degree line in the chart represents the perfect model for comparison with the actual prognosis. Decision curve analysis (DCA) is a method for evaluating prediction models and can be used to determine whether the use of a nomogram to provide information for decision-making has more advantages than disadvantages [14]. In this study, the net benefit and threshold probability in comparison with those of the nomogram were determined by DCA. Moreover, the sensitivity and specificity of the nomogram were determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.

Statistical analysis

In this study, statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 20.0 (Chicago, IL, USA) and R software (version 3.5.3). Conduct both univariate and multivariate analyses on all variables simultaneously to identify factors with independent prognostic value. The Kaplan–Meier method with a two-sided log-rank test was used to explore the OS/PFS of cHL patients. Decision curve analysis was performed with the R software package “rmda”. Subgroup analysis was conducted using Cox regression analysis. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Comparison of general clinical characteristics in pediatric patients with cHL

According to the immunohistochemical staining levels of BLNK (Fig. 1), 63 pediatric patients were divided into two groups: the BLNK-positive group (n = 52; Fig. 1Cand D) and BLNK-negative group (n = 11; Fig. 1a and b). A comparison of the general data between the two groups revealed that, compared with the BLNK-positive group, the BLNK-negative group had a greater disease progression rate (P < 0.001), significantly shorter progression-free survival (PFS) (P = 0.002), and a greater mortality rate (P < 0.001). No statistically significant differences were found between the two groups in terms of sex, age, pathological type, presence of large tumor masses, presence of B symptoms, disease stage, EB virus infection status, chemotherapy regimen, CRP level, B lymphocyte count, LDH level, IgG level, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, platelet count, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, or platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (P > 0.05) (Table 1).

Cox multivariate analysis of PFS and OS in children with cHL

BLNK was significantly associated with the PFS of cHL patients. Univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were used to determine the independent prognostic variables of PFS. Univariate analysis revealed that the expression of BLNK in pathological tissue (HR = 0.10; 95% CI = 0.04–0.25; P < 0.001), the presence or absence of B symptoms (HR = 2.58; 95% CI = 1.13–5.9; P = 0.024), and the neutrophil count (HR = 1.10; 95% CI = 1.00–1.20; P = 0.039) were candidate risk factors for disease progression (Table 2). Multivariate analysis revealed that the expression of BLNK in pathological tissue (HR = 0.03; 95% CI = 0–0.16; P < 0.001), the presence of Giant tumor lesion (HR = 21.78; 95% CI = 1.11–429; P = 0.043), the B lymphocyte count (HR = 0.96; 95% CI = 0.93–1.00; P = 0.030), LDH levels (HR = 1.01; 95% CI = 1.00–1.02; P = 0.030), IgG levels (HR = 0.24; 95% CI = 0.07–0.82; P = 0.030), and the neutrophil count (HR = 6; 95% CI = 1.49–24.18; P = 0.012) were risk factors for disease progression (Table 2).

To investigate the risk factors associated with overall survival (OS) in pediatric patients with cHL, we conducted a Cox multivariate analysis. The results of the univariate analysis indicated that the expression of BLNK (HR = 0.06; 95% CI = 0.01–0.29; P < 0.001), platelet count (HR = 1.01; 95% CI = 1.00–1.01; P = 0.019), and the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) (HR = 1; 95% CI = 1.00–1.01; P = 0.005) were candidate risk factors for disease progression (see Table 3). However, the multivariate analysis revealed that the differences in OS were not statistically significant (Table 3).

Construction and validation of a nomogram to predict the PFS of cHL patients

Cox multivariate analysis revealed that the expression of BLNK in pathological tissue, the presence of griant tumor lesion, B lymphocyte count, LDH level, IgG level, and neutrophil count were significant risk factors for disease progression. Based on these significant risk factors, a nomogram for predicting disease progression in children was developed (Fig. 3).

Discussion

BLNK plays a key role in many types of cancer. BLNK is a positive regulator of Met signaling and regulates the localization of Met, including ligand-dependent transport, in non-small cell lung cancer cell lines [9]. Liu et al. [23] reported that the upregulation of BLNK expression in human Erbb2-positive breast cancer cells blocks the ability of these cells to form tumors in mice. Increased BLNK mRNA levels were associated with increased relapse-free survival in approximately 400 patients with ERBB2-positive breast cancer [23]. In addition, BLNK is a key gene that potentially affects the progression of autophagy-associated thyroid cancer, and BLNK promotes the proliferation of thyroid cancer cells [24].

Recent epidemiological studies indicate that the incidence of cHL has been increasing worldwide, with variations based on geographical location and demographic factors [25]. The prognosis for pediatric patients with cHL has significantly improved over the past few decades because of advancements in treatment protocols, including chemotherapy and radiation therapy. The five-year survival rate for children with cHL now exceeds 90% [26]. However, this statistic does not capture the nuances of survival outcomes among different patient subgroups. Factors such as age at diagnosis, disease stage, and histological subtype can influence prognosis, underscoring the need for personalized treatment approaches [27]. Recent research emphasizes the importance of survival prognostic markers in effectively stratifying risk and tailoring therapy. Currently, established prognostic factors include the presence of bulky disease and the patient’s response to initial therapy [28]. However, reliable biological markers that may further refine prognostic predictions are scarce. Biomarkers have the potential to provide insights into disease biology, predict treatment responses, and assess the likelihood of relapse. Despite considerable advances in understanding the pathophysiology of cHL, the identification of specific biomarkers remains a significant challenge. Preliminary studies have suggested potential candidates, including cytokines and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, but these candidates are yet to be validated in large-scale, multicenter trials [23]. The absence of standardized biomarkers hinders the development of effective monitoring strategies and could impact long-term outcomes for pediatric patients.

In this study, a retrospective cohort design was used to explore the clinical characteristics and survival data of 63 pediatric patients diagnosed with cHL over the past 20 years, with the aim of investigating whether factors such as BLNK affect the prognosis of cHL. Previous studies have indicated an association between the expression levels of BLNK and the clinical features of patients with Hodgkin lymphoma. Some clinical studies have shown that patients with high BLNK expression typically have better outcomes, which is potentially related to the key role of BLNK in regulating immune responses and promoting apoptosis in tumor cells [23]. Specifically, BLNK is involved in several signaling pathways, including the PI3K/Akt pathway, which is crucial for the survival, proliferation, and differentiation of B cells. Additionally, studies have shown that low BLNK expression is associated with metastasis, progression, and poor prognosis in Hodgkin lymphoma.

In this study, BLNK was the independent prognostic variable for PFS. Some studies have shown that BLNK is associated with patient prognosis in many types of cancer. Tian et al. [29] reported that abnormal BLNK expression has been reported to affect the prognosis of breast cancer patients. Lee et al. [22] reported that in colorectal cancer patients, the frequency of lymph node metastasis (p = 0.031), right colon cancer (p = 0.026), mucinous adenocarcinoma (p < 0.001), and perineural infiltration (p = 0.049) was greater in the high-BLNK-expression group than in the low-BLNK-expression group. BLNK protein overexpression is an independent risk factor for colorectal cancer recurrence [22]. In addition, Zhao et al. [30] demonstrated that the inflammatory response-associated gene BLNK is associated with prognosis in patients with multiple myeloma and validated a prognostic risk model of six inflammatory response-associated genes (VCAM1, RGS1, KIT, CD81, BLNK, and BIRC3). Risk models are independent predictors of overall survival [4, 30]. In this study, BLNK was not a prognostic indicator of OS, which may be due to the longer overall survival of cHL patients. Nevertheless, this study is the first to suggest that BLNK is an independent risk factor for PFS in patients with cHL.

In our study, there was discrepancy between PFS and OS risk factors. BLNK was not an independent prognostic indicator of OS, but it was associated with PFS. In addition, giant tumor lesion was an independent adverse prognostic factor in PFS of cHL, closely associated with tumor microenvironment remodeling and immune evasion. Studies have demonstrated that patients with bulky tumors often exhibit higher metabolic activity (PET-CT SUVmax ≥ 10) and shorter 5-year PFS [31]. In studies by Jurisić, V and Beltran, B et al., LDH levels were identified as prognostic factors affecting survival, with statistical significance confirmed in both univariate and multivariate analyses [32, 33]. A Peruvian study involving 60 patients with classical Hodgkin lymphoma revealed that relatively higher B lymphocyte counts correlate with improved clinical outcomes [33]. The tumor microenvironment of classical Hodgkin lymphoma is characterized by abundant immune cell infiltration, where neutrophils directly kill tumor cells by releasing reactive oxygen species (ROS) and extracellular traps (NETs). However, their pro-tumor effects cannot be overlooked: histones in NETs can activate the TLR9 pathway, promoting tumor cell proliferation [34]. The interaction between neutrophils and CD4 + T cells further complicates this scenario: by secreting IL-17, neutrophils induce Treg cell differentiation, suppressing anti-tumor immune responses [34].The crosstalk between neutrophils and B cells/macrophages plays a particularly critical role in HL progression. Neutrophils secrete IL-6 and TNF-α to promote B cell differentiation into plasma cells while inhibiting apoptosis [35]. The dynamic balance between neutrophils and NK/DC cells significantly impacts HL immunotherapy efficacy. Neutrophils suppress NK cell cytotoxicity by secreting PGE2 and TGF-β [36, 37]. Tan et al. found that poorer progression-free survival (PFS) was associated with high peripheral absolute neutrophil count (pANC) (HR 2.99, p = 0.0392), low peripheral absolute lymphocyte count (pALC) (HR 3.95, p = 0.0038). The study concluded that high pANC and low pALC portend worse prognosis for HL, suggesting future investigations should explore dose intensity adjustments based on post-treatment blood counts [38].Higher macrophage infiltration was observed in stage III-IV cases (p = 0.02), correlating with elevated ESR and neutrophil counts [39].Collectively, these studies demonstrate that neutrophil counts influence pediatric prognosis, consistent with our finding that neutrophil count serves as an independent risk factor for PFS. Recent research reveals neutrophils’ dual role in cancer immunotherapy: depending on surface molecular markers, they can either promote tumor growth or suppress progression. The abundance of anti-tumor neutrophils in tumor tissues correlates with improved immunotherapy efficacy [40]. Neutrophil accumulation may occur across tumor types and is a common feature of therapies capable of controlling tumor growth [41].Furthermore, clinical data analysis of 59 cHL patients by Valsami, S et al. indicated that patients with elevated IgG levels (P = 0.01) exhibited relatively better prognosis [42]. In this study, BLNK was either negative or weakly expressed in pediatric cHL patients, as well as giant tumor lesion, LDH, IgG, B lymphocyte counts and neutrophil counts, which may lead to an imbalanced tumor microenvironment and disease progression. but the high cure rate (long-term survival rate of 88% in this study) and advancements in treatment intensity may offset its negative impact on OS. This is consistent with Rossi et al. [9] (2024), who suggested that in the era of immunotherapy, the predictive power of traditional prognostic markers for OS may be overshadowed by treatment response.This may also be a critical reason for the discrepancy between PFS and OS risk factors.

In recent studies analyzing prognostic factors for Hodgkin lymphoma, the Ann Arbor staging system (I–IV) has frequently been included. However, the 2023 study by Jiang et al. [15], a German multicenter adult study [16], and a 2025 Chinese multicenter pediatric study [17] did not identify staging as an independent risk factor for overall survival (OS). Similarly, Hsi et al. [43] reported that OS in patients with HL was independent of interim treatment response, stage, fever, and albumin level.‌ Possible explanations include the following: (1) High chemosensitivity and biological variability‌: Modern intensive chemotherapy regimens (e.g., CNCL-2017-HL CycleA→COPP/ABV→ Cycle C) achieve comparable complete remission rates across all stages in pediatric patients [26, 44, 45]. Even advanced-stage patients achieve >80% 5-year OS after dose adjustment [3], minimizing statistical disparities with early-stage patients. (2)‌Superior treatment tolerance in children‌: The presence of fewer comorbidities (e.g., cardiopulmonary diseases) increases tolerance to intensive therapy, mitigating the prognostic impact of tumor burden. (3) Unique pediatric cHL biology, such as a high EBV positivity rate (~ 80%) and a distinct tumor microenvironment, may compensate for stage-dependent molecular features through immune regulatory mechanisms [26]. Recent research suggests that ‌risk stratification‌ (e.g., high- vs. low-risk groups) may be more critical than Ann Arbor staging for personalized therapy [1]. Hsi et al. [31] reported significant differences in weighted 5-year event-free survival between risk groups (75.2% vs. 90.3%, p = 0.0138), independent of stage. However, standardized risk stratification for pediatric cHL remains lacking globally, representing a potential future research direction.

Notably, multivariate analysis results for overall survival (OS)—such as odds ratio (OR) = 0 with confidence interval (CI) = 0–Inf—reflect statistical instability. This instability arises primarily from two factors: (1) limited death events‌ in the cohort (n = 8/63 deaths; see Table 1), and (2) complete separation phenomena‌ in specific subgroups (e.g., no deaths occurred among BLNK-negative patients with certain characteristics). Pediatric Hodgkin lymphoma cure rates have progressively increased. Recent European data report 5-, 10-, and 20-year survival rates that consistently exceed 80% [46]. Furthermore, a 2018 Italian study demonstrated the following: a 97.0% 10-year OS rate in a cohort of patients aged ≤ 7 years old [28] When both BLNK-positive and BLNK-negative groups surpass 90% survival, hazard ratio (HR) confidence intervals may widen substantially. Additional contributors to statistical imprecision include data bias that is inherent to retrospective cohort studies and statistical power limitations stemming from high cure rates and small subgroup samples. In our study (n = 63 cHL patients; 8 deaths; 88% survival rate), these constraints had similar effects.

Prognostic prediction models for classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) have received widespread attention in recent years. However, comprehensive prediction models tailored for different patient populations are lacking, which limits the application of personalized treatment. Existing models are primarily based on clinical and pathological features, and they do not adequately consider the impact of biomarkers. Therefore, further research into novel prediction models is essential to increase the accuracy of prognostic assessment. In this study, we constructed Kaplan‒Meier survival curves by analyzing the survival data of the enrolled patients and reported that compared with low expression, high BLNK expression is correlated with better PFS and OS, which is consistent with previous conclusions. Furthermore, the results of this study revealed that BLNK is a significant prognostic variable for PFS in cHL patients and that it is a risk factor for disease progression, as are large tumor masses, B lymphocyte count, LDH level, IgG level, and neutrophil count. Based on these identified risk factors, a nomogram was successfully constructed to predict disease progression in cHL patients. However, BLNK had a greater impact on PFS/OS among patients with other clinical factors, such as large tumor masses, St. Jude staging, chemotherapy regimens, and LDH level, which is consistent with the findings of existing studies [20]. This conclusion can assist us in to improving the identification of at-risk populations and monitoring of diseases to ultimately improve clinical outcomes.

Finally, we performed a subgroup analysis to examine the impact of BLNK on patients with cHL in different subgroups. In the groups categorized by sex, age, pathological type, the presence of B symptoms, the presence of EB virus infection, IgG levels, the neutrophil count, the PLR, and the NLR, the impact of BLNK on disease prognosis remained relatively stable, allowing for better early prediction of disease outcomes and providing clues for adjusting treatment plans. In this study, BLNK was not identified as a risk factor for OS; however, subgroup analysis revealed that relative to high BLNK expression, low BLNK expression predicted longer OS in subgroups with an age ≥ 9 years, the absence of large tumor masses, St. Jude Stages III–IV, chemotherapy regimen (2), a CRP >10.2 mg/L, a B lymphocyte count >18.5 × 10^6/L, an IgG level >6.91 g/L, a neutrophil count ≤ 5.6 × 10^9/L, a lymphocyte count >0.49 × 10^9/L, a platelet count >298.5 × 10^9/L, a PLR >199.31, and an NLR >3.17. These findings indicate that BLNK has a greater effect on OS in these populations, demonstrating better predictive value. However, the sample size was too small; therefore, the results should be interpreted with caution. Our subgroup analyses are constrained by the modest cohort size (N = 63). The SPIRIT 2025 statement says the following [47]: the plan must specify the subgroup analysis in advance, distinguishing between confirmatory analysis and exploratory analysis, while emphasizing that exploratory results need to be verified in subsequent studies. Small-sample exploratory subgroup analyses can generate only hypothetical positions (No. 20). In our study, the limited sample size positions the experimental findings as exploratory in nature. These results serve primarily to generate hypotheses for subsequent research rather than to establish definitive conclusions. Furthermore, the outcomes of the subgroup analyses did not alter the primary conclusions of the study. Moving forward, selected findings from subgroup analyses may be validated by expanding the number of research centers, increasing sample sizes, and extending study durations.

Limitations

First, it is important to note that this study is retrospective in nature, which inherently carries biases; thus, it is difficult to study all confounding factors, and it is difficult to determine whether a confounding factor has received outsized attention. Therefore, I did not include all possible variables as confounding factors in the study. Second, the relatively small sample size of our cohort may introduce confounding bias because of the limited positive data for certain variables. Our study sample size and small BLNK-positive subgroup (n = 11),the external validation of our nomogram model was based on a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which inherently carries limitations and may introduce statistical bias, and our subgroup analyses are constrained by the modest cohort size. To mitigate this limitation, our center is actively coordinating multicenter collaborations with partner institutions and extending the study period to accrue larger cohorts, aiming to increase the statistical power for validating BLNK expression thresholds in pediatric cHL. The follow-up duration for assessing overall survival in patients with classic Hodgkin lymphoma remains relatively short. Therefore, extending the follow-up period and increasing the sample size of the retrospective cohort will be important directions for future research.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study revealed that low expression of BLNK may be associated with prolonged OS and PFS in cHL pediatric patients and serves as an independent protective factor for PFS. In this study, a BLNK-based prognostic model capable of predicting PFS outcomes in cHL pediatric patients was developed.

Efficacy and safety of balcinrenone and dapagliflozin for CKD: design and baseline characteristics of the MIRO-CKD trial
Authors: Patrick B Mark, Erika De Sousa-Amorim, Anna L Eriksson, Maria Leonsson-Zachrisson, Nicolas J Guzman, Matthew T Miller, Yunyun Jiang, Hiddo J L Heerspink
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and hypothesisDespite benefits of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) being recommended for treatment of chronic kidney disease (CKD), there is still an unmet need for managing renal residual risk. Based on pre-clinical data, balcinrenone is a novel mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist that maintains cardio-renal benefits without increasing hyperkalaemia risk. Use of balcinrenone and dapagliflozin is expected to provide complementary and additive kidney and cardiovascular protection. The MIRO-CKD study tests the hypothesis that balcinrenone with dapagliflozin is superior to dapagliflozin alone in reducing albuminuria in patients with CKD.MethodsMIRO-CKD is a double-blind, active-controlled, phase 2b study evaluating the efficacy and safety of balcinrenone/dapagliflozin in 300 adults (planned) with CKD, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) 25–60 mL/min/1.73 m2 and serum potassium within normal range. Participants are being randomized 1:1:1 to balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 15 mg/10 mg, 40 mg/10 mg or placebo/dapagliflozin 10 mg for 12 weeks, followed by an 8-week wash-out period to assess off-drug effects. The primary endpoint is the change in log-transformed urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) from baseline to Week 12. Other outcomes include pharmacokinetics, safety and tolerability.ResultsA total of 614 patients were screened and 324 were randomized across 15 countries in Asia, Europe, and North and South America. There were 56.2% of participants taking SGLT2i and 87.1% renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system inhibitors at enrolment. The patient population are at increased risk of CKD progression by having CKD stage 3/4 and albuminuria (median UACR: 365.5 mg/g; mean eGFR: 42.2 mL/min/1.73 m2). 56.5% of participants had type 2 diabetes mellitus.ConclusionsMIRO-CKD is a fully recruited global trial enrolling a contemporary CKD population at increased risk of progression and with an increased risk of hyperkalaemia based on their reduced kidney function. The study will assess the efficacy, safety and tolerability of the combination of balcinrenone and dapagliflozin to identify an optimal dose for a future phase 3 study in patients with CKD.Clinical Trial Registration NumberNCT06350123.

INTRODUCTION

Significant progress has been made in the pharmacological management of chronic kidney disease (CKD) in the last years. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) slow the progression of CKD and reduce the risk of cardiovascular

complications and mortality in patients with CKD with and without type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [1]. The non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) finerenone and the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist semaglutide reduce the risk of kidney failure and cardiovascular disease in patients with CKD due to T2DM [2–6]. Clinical practice guidelines for patients with CKD and T2DM now recommend finerenone and semaglutide for kidney and cardiovascular protection [7]. In addition, SGLT2i are recommended for CKD without T2DM [8].

SGLT2i and finerenone reduce albuminuria by approximately 30% in the first months of treatment, while in the long term they reduce the risk of clinical kidney outcomes by approximately 20%–35% [2, 4, 9–12]. Despite this, albuminuria remains elevated in a substantial proportion of patients, and finerenone is associated with an increased risk of hyperkalaemia [2]. Post-hoc analyses from SGLT2i trials have demonstrated that the persistent ‘residual’ albuminuria is associated with a high risk of CKD-related outcomes [13, 14]. These findings suggest that therapies that effectively reduce albuminuria will likely exert clinical benefit in the long term, even on top of the guideline-recommended treatment.

The mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) is a nuclear receptor expressed in a broad range of tissues with two physiological ligands in humans—aldosterone and cortisol [15]. MR activation in the aldosterone-sensitive distal nephron regulates salt and fluid homeostasis and has a pivotal role in controlling blood pressure [16]. However, MR activation in non-classical MR target tissues may induce oxidative stress, vascular inflammation and fibrosis, thereby promoting pathophysiological changes in the heart, vasculature and kidney [17–19]. Inhibition of the MR is therefore a well-recognized target to reduce the risk of CKD-related outcomes. However, the steroidal MRAs spironolactone and eplerenone have never been investigated in large kidney trials.

Newer MRAs have been developed with different chemical structures and MR binding properties compared with the steroidal MRAs, which may increase tolerability while retaining efficacy. Based on preclinical data, balcinrenone is a novel, selective MRA which has a differentiated mechanism of action compared with approved MRAs, and provides cardio-renal protection without an acute effect on urinary electrolyte excretion, suggestive of a reduced risk of hyperkalaemia [20–23]. Since SGLT2is decrease the risk of serious hyperkalaemia possibly by increasing the sodium chloride concentration in the distal tubule, increasing the electronegative potential which stimulates potassium excretion, as well as by modestly increasing aldosterone levels which promotes kaliuresis, combining dapagliflozin with balcinrenone may further reduce hyperkalaemia-related adverse effects [24]. The efficacy and safety of balcinrenone in combination with the SGLT2i dapagliflozin has been investigated in patients with CKD and heart failure in the phase 2b MIRACLE trial [25]. The trial was started during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic and was stopped prematurely because of slow recruitment, limiting statistical power and interpretation of the results. Nevertheless, a trend towards albuminuria reduction was observed with the balcinrenone/dapagliflozin combination versus dapagliflozin alone, while hyperkalaemia was infrequent in particular at lower balcinrenone doses. These encouraging results supported the design and conduct of a larger phase 2 clinical trial in patients with CKD to more precisely characterize the efficacy, safety and dose–response of the balcinrenone/dapagliflozin combination. We here report the study design and baseline characteristics of the phase 2 balcinrenone/dapagliflozin the MIRO-CKD trial.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study participants

Eligible participants are males and females aged 18 years or older, with a diagnosis of CKD defined by an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) ≥25 and <60 mL/min/1.73 m2, urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) >100 and ≤5000 mg albumin/g creatinine and serum potassium ≥3.5 to ≤5.0 mmol/L. Participants with or without prior treatment with an SGLT2i are eligible for the study. For those receiving renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors—angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi) or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB)—dose must have been stable for at least 4 weeks before screening. However, participants with documented ACEi or ARB intolerance or currently not treated with a RAAS inhibitor are allowed to participate. Key exclusion criteria include autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, type 1 diabetes mellitus and uncontrolled arterial hypertension [systolic blood pressure (SBP) >160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) >100 mmHg)]. Patients with adrenal insufficiency are excluded to avoid potential confounding factors and taking into account contraindications for MRAs. Study design

MIRO-CKD is a phase 2b, multicentre, randomized, double-blind, active-controlled, parallel-group dose-finding study to assess the efficacy, safety and tolerability of a fixed dose combination of balcinrenone and dapagliflozin in participants with CKD stages 3 and 4 and albuminuria. The study is conducted across 106 sites in North America, South America, Asia/Pacific and Europe (Supplementary data, Table S1). Balcinrenone dose selection was informed by dose–response modelling of UACR reduction and the effects on serum potassium from the phase 2 dose-finding study of balcinrenone/dapagliflozin in patients with heart failure and CKD [25].

The study planned to randomize approximately 300 participants 1:1:1 to once-daily balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 15/10 mg, 40/10 mg or dapagliflozin/placebo 10 mg. All participants were centrally randomized to study interventions using a web-based Interactive Response Technology system, ensuring allocation concealment and preventing selection bias. A stratified block randomization method was employed, with a fixed block size of three to maintain balance across treatment arms within each stratum. Randomization was stratified based on three pre-specified screening variables: baseline eGFR (≥25 to <45 vs ≥45 to <60 mL/min/1.73 m²), SGLT2i use (yes/no) and T2DM status (yes/no). Participants receiving SGLT2i at baseline were switched to dapagliflozin at randomization as part of the study intervention. After randomization, participants are followed for 12 weeks of treatment and 8 weeks of follow-up assessments (Fig. 1). The total duration of the study is approximately 23 weeks.

The study planned to randomize 300 participants 1:1:1 to once-daily balcinrenone 15 mg/dapagliflozin 10 mg, balcinrenone 40 mg/dapagliflozin 10 mg or dapagliflozin 10 mg/placebo.

Use of MRAs is prohibited during the study and use of SGTL2is (other than study dapagliflozin) is prohibited during the treatment period. Because balcinrenone is a substrate of cytochrome P450 3A4, strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers are prohibited 1 week prior to randomization and during the treatment period.

Objectives, endpoints and assessments

The primary objective is to evaluate the effect of balcinrenone/dapagliflozin compared with dapagliflozin alone in reducing albuminuria. The primary endpoint is the relative change in UACR from baseline to Week 12. UACR is measured at baseline, and at Weeks 4, 8 and 12 from three first morning urine voids collected at home on 3 consecutive days (ideally day of visit and the preceding 2 days). Only in exceptional circumstances where a participant arrives at site without any morning void samples, a spot urine sample could be collected during the day of the visit.

Albumin and creatinine in urine are analysed centrally by LabCorp in five laboratories in a total of five countries: China, Japan, Switzerland, Singapore and the USA. Urinary albumin is measured using an immunoturbidimetric method and urinary creatinine is measured using a colorimetric (Jaffe) method.

Participants return for study assessments at Weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12, and for off-drug assessments at Weeks 16 and 20. Further details on visits and assessment schedule can be found in Supplementary data, Table S2.

Exploratory endpoints include dose–response relationship, proportion of patients with 30% and 50% albuminuria reduction and with a regression in albuminuria stage, pharmacokinetics of balcinrenone and dapagliflozin, and effects on potassium, eGFR and blood pressure over time. Plasma samples are stored for exploratory biomarker analyses with the Olink® Explore 3072 panel.

Safety analyses include assessment of adverse events, vital signs, clinical laboratory tests, 12-lead electrocardiogram and pre-specified events of special interest, including hyperkalaemia, hypotension and renal events/acute kidney injury. Clinical laboratory tests are assessed at screening, throughout the interventional period and at follow-up, and are summarized in Supplementary data, Table S3.

Statistical considerations

Sample size determination

Sample size calculations were based on an expected geometric mean ratio of 0.7 in relative UACR changes (standard deviation 1.0) on a log-transformed scale. A total of 300 evaluable participants (100 per treatment group) is estimated to provide 80% power at a one-sided alpha of 5% to detect a significant reduction in UACR for the high-dose (balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 40 mg/10 mg) group compared with dapagliflozin 10 mg.

Statistical analyses

The primary analysis will be conducted in the full analysis set, including all randomized participants who receive at least one dose of study treatment. The primary analysis will adopt the intention-to-treat strategy where all participants in a clinical trial are included in the analysis in the groups to which they were randomized, regardless of any deviation from randomized treatment. For treatment estimand, the treatment policy is adopted for handling intercurrent events such as initiation or change in concomitant treatment, premature discontinuation of investigational drug or use of prohibited medication.

UACR will be log-transformed to account for its log-normal distribution and analysed using a mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) approach. The model will include treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline log(UACR) and stratification factors. Results will be back-transformed to provide geometric mean relative change from baseline to Week 12. All available UACR values from all visits will be used regardless of any intercurrent events. Missing values will not be imputed. In this analysis it is thus assumed that any missing UACR values are missing at random. Exploratory analyses will assess proportion of patients with 30% and 50% UACR reduction and who regress to a lower albuminuria stage, effects on serum potassium levels, eGFR and blood pressure (SBP/DBP). Regression of albuminuria stage will be based on a shift from a higher to a lower category (e.g. A2 to A1 or A3 to A2/A1) and reduction of ≥30% or ≥50% from baseline. Changes in serum potassium, eGFR and blood pressure will be analysed using MMRM, adjusting for baseline and stratification factors. All safety analyses are based on descriptive statistics.

Ethics

MIRO-CKD is conducted in accordance with ethical principles derived from the Declaration of Helsinki, the Council for International Organisations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, the International Council for Harmonisation, Good Publication Practice Guidelines, and all applicable laws and regulations. Before the study was initiated, the protocol, protocol amendments, informed consent and other forms were reviewed and approved by a local independent review board/ethics committee. All study participants provided written informed consent before undergoing any study-specific procedures, and any who are re-screened were required to sign a new informed consent form. All participants are informed that their participation in the study is voluntary, and they may withdraw their consent at any time.

DISCUSSION

The MIRO-CKD trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy of balcinrenone in combination with dapagliflozin in patients with CKD with and without T2DM. The MRA finerenone has been shown to be effective in slowing CKD progression and reducing CKD-related clinical outcomes in patients with T2DM, while SGLT2i have been shown to have similar benefits in patients with or without T2DM. Because of their different mechanisms of action targeting key pathophysiological aspects of CKD progression, there is a sound rationale to combine the two drug classes to enhance kidney protection, particularly in patients with elevated albuminuria who are at high risk of adverse clinical outcomes. In addition, inhibition of aldosterone synthase is a new method targeting the aldosterone pathway and there are several phase 3 trials underway evaluating combinations of aldosterone synthase inhibitors, e.g. vicadrostat or baxdrostat, with SGLT2i in populations with CKD or heart failure [26–31].

Balcinrenone is a novel MRA demonstrating kidney benefits on a par with approved MRAs in pre-clinical disease models, and has a mechanism-based reduced risk for hyperkalaemia. In rat and mouse CKD and diabetic kidney disease (DKD) models, both balcinrenone and eplerenone reduced the high salt and aldosterone-induced renal injury measured as urinary albumin excretion. Both agents also improved kidney pathology scores, and these effects were additive to ACEi. However, in contrast to other MRAs [20–22], balcinrenone does not trigger the typical increases in the urinary sodium–potassium ratio in rat models with elevated aldosterone, thereby suggesting consequently a potentially reduced hyperkalaemia risk [23]. The differentiated binding pattern to the MR and cofactor recruitment is believed to explain the different receptor modulation and downstream effects compared with conventional MRAs.

In the phase 2b MIRACLE trial [25], 133 participants with heart failure and ejection fraction <60%, CKD stage 3 and UACR ≥30 to <3000 mg/g were randomized to receive daily balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 15/10 mg, 50/10 mg and 150/10 mg, or placebo/dapagliflozin 10 mg for 12 weeks. Median changes in UACR for the balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 15/10 mg, 50/10 mg and 150/10 mg arms were −24.5% (interquartile range −64.4, +3.5), −33.1% (−52.5, +1.7) and −55.1% (−69.8, −6.6), respectively, compared with −5.8% (−43.8, +55.0) in the dapagliflozin control group. However, differences between groups were not statistically significant. Mean serum potassium levels were comparable between the balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 15/10 mg and placebo/dapagliflozin 10 mg arms throughout the 12-week intervention, but tended to be higher in the balcinrenone/dapagliflozin 50/10 mg and 150/10 mg treatment arms compared with placebo.

Although the small sample size did not allow for establishment of a clear dose–response relationship, the pharmacokinetic model predicted that balcinrenone doses of 15 mg and 40 mg will have no or minor effect on serum potassium levels, therefore those doses were selected to be tested in the MIRO-CKD study.

The MIRO-CKD trial is designed to evaluate balcinrenone in combination with dapagliflozin, as SGLT2i have emerged as a cornerstone for the treatment of CKD-related complications. Moreover, clinical studies suggest that SGLT2i and MRAs can confer kidney protection through different yet complementary mechanisms. A crossover clinical study in patients with CKD showed that combined treatment with eplerenone and dapagliflozin reduced albuminuria, blood pressure and N-terminal B-type natriuretic peptide versus eplerenone or dapagliflozin alone [32]. In the MIRACLE study, treatment with balcinrenone/dapagliflozin led to a numerical reduction in albuminuria versus dapagliflozin alone [25]. Subgroup analyses of large trials in patients with heart failure or CKD indicate an additive effect on cardio-renal outcomes when patients added SGLT2i to ongoing MRA treatment [33, 34] and vice versa [4]. The CONFIDENCE study is an ongoing, randomized, controlled, double-blind phase 2 trial that investigates the efficacy of combined use of finerenone and empagliflozin in reducing albuminuria and blood pressure in patients with CKD and T2DM compared with either treatment alone [35, 36]. MIRO-CKD will add and extend the insight to a broader CKD cohort of patients with and without T2DM using the fixed dose combination of balcinrenone/dapagliflozin.

Balcinrenone/dapagliflozin is also an attractive combination from a safety perspective because of the possibly reduced hyperkalaemia risk of balcinrenone compared with other MRAs, and the potential effects of SGLT2i on RAAS inhibitor–mediated hyperkalaemia. SGLT2i modestly reduce potassium levels and reduce the risk of hyperkalaemia, potentially by increasing distal tubular sodium concentration thereby increasing the charge selective potential leading to enhanced potassium secretion. Meta-analyses from randomized controlled trials suggest that SGLT2i reduce the risk of serious hyperkalaemia defined as serum potassium ≥6.0 mmol/L [hazard ratio (HR) 0.84; 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.76, 0.93] and investigator-reported hyperkalaemia (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.68, 0.93) in patients with T2DM at high cardiovascular risk or with CKD [24]. In addition, the prespecified FIDELITY analysis of individual patient-level data from the phase 3 FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trials reported a reduced incidence of hyperkalaemia in patients receiving SGLT2i at baseline versus not, in both the finerenone and placebo arms [4]. Nevertheless, the risk of hyperkalaemia with approved MRAs still persists, and a new MRA with a differentiated mechanism of action like balcinrenone in combination with SGLT2i may reduce this risk. Hyperkalaemia is an adverse event of special interest in the MIRO-CKD trial and is monitored closely throughout follow-up to robustly characterize effects of balcinrenone/dapagliflozin on potassium.

Beyond its established effects on albuminuria, MR antagonism is an appealing intervention to mitigate the excess cardiovascular risk associated with CKD. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in people with CKD, with heart failure being a major contributor to this excess mortality [37]. Observational data suggest a causal pathway to cardiovascular death and heart failure that includes progressive increases in left ventricular mass as well as eGFR declines [38, 39]. In a randomized controlled trial in early CKD, spironolactone prevented this progressive increase in left ventricular mass compared with placebo, suggesting that MRA has a role in preventing progressive cardiac dysfunction in CKD. This is supported by the reduction in heart failure events observed with finerenone versus placebo in the FIGARO trial [40]. This is further supported by the FIDELITY and FINE-HEART pooled analyses, where finerenone was found to reduce the risk of hospitalization from heart failure (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.68, 0.95, and HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.75, 0.92, respectively) [4, 5].

The primary endpoint of the MIRO-CKD trial is the change in albuminuria from baseline. This endpoint has been chosen since it is a well-recognized surrogate endpoint for kidney failure in patients with CKD. Meta-analyses from clinical trials have demonstrated that treatments that reduce albuminuria by at least 25% over 6 months of treatment provide a high confidence that these treatments exert clinical benefit [41]. Moreover, post-hoc analyses from interventions that inhibit the RAAS system have suggested that 80%–90% of the clinical kidney benefit can be explained by the early reduction in albuminuria. Specifically, in the combined FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trials, the reduction in albuminuria—31% after 4 months of treatment with finerenone—accounted for 84% of the benefit of finerenone in reducing the risk of kidney failure [42]. Based on these studies, we designed the MIRO-CKD trial with 80% power to demonstrate at least 30% reduction in albuminuria.

The phase 2 MIRO-CKD trial enrolled participants with high risk of CKD progression as reflected by the low eGFR levels and albuminuria, with 56.5% of patients with T2DM. Compared with the phase 3 DAPA-CKD and FIDELIO-DKD trials [2, 9, 43], participants in the MIRO-CKD trial have similar eGFR but lower UACR levels (949 mg/g and 852 mg/g in DAPA-CKD and FIDELIO-DKD, respectively, vs 365 mg/g in MIRO-CKD) and lower RAAS inhibitor use (98%–99% vs 88%). Importantly, while the SGLT2i use was 4.6% and 8.4% in the FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trials with finerenone [2, 10], all participants in MIRO-CKD receive SGLT2i treatment during the randomized treatment period in line with current guidelines [8]. The proportion of participants with T2DM was 56.5% in MIRO-CKD, compared with 58.4% in ZENITH-CKD [44] and 71% in the phase 2 vicadrostat/empagliflozin trial [45].

Limitations of this study include firstly the lack of a balcinrenone alone arm, as this would require discontinuation or withholding of guideline-recommended SGLT2i, which would be considered unethical. Secondly, the results of this study are only applicable to patients who share the characteristics of the enrolled population. The final results of the MIRO-CKD trial cannot be generalized to patients with no or modest albuminuria. Other limitations include this being an exploratory phase 2 trial with relatively short study duration, which means that long-term efficacy and safety of balcinrenone/dapagliflozin will need to be confirmed in future trials.

In conclusion, the phase 2b MIRO-CKD trial will characterize the efficacy and safety of balcinrenone when administered in combination with dapagliflozin. The study will also provide information on optimal balcinrenone doses to progress to a phase 3 clinical outcome trial if successful.

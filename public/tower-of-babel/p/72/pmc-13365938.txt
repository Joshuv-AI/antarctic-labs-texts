IFPHOR0094 Integrating the Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB) Framework to Assess the Impact of Active Travel Reward Incentives on Risk Factors Related to Non-Communicable Diseases: A Rapid Review
Authors: Kethakee Andaraweera, Georgina Shadrick, James Wright, Arpana Verma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-communicable diseases (NCDs) are leading global causes of morbidity and mortality, while active travel (walking, cycling and wheeling) reduces risks of obesity, NCDs and related mortality. Incentive programmes targeting behaviour change play an important role in improving healthy behaviours. Using the Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB) as a guiding framework, this review synthesises evidence on whether incentives for active travel (AT) influence NCD related risk factors in adults, exploring their alignment with TPB constructs (TPBC).MethodsSystematic searches of CINAHL, Scopus and MEDLINE (2018–2025) were conducted and results were screened using predefined eligibility criteria. Study quality was appraised using Public Health and CASP checklists, and data on study design, incentive types, physical activity and health outcomes, and TPBC were extracted. Findings were synthesised thematically.Results45 studies met inclusion criteria, most with moderate to high quality; six had high risk of bias. 78% reported positive effects of incentives on AT‑related physical activity, with five showing sustained post intervention effects. Four reported no effect and one reported negative effect. No studies examined wheeling. Four studies measured change in TPBC, but only one study explicitly used TPB as a guiding framework. For these, both behaviour and TPBC moved in a positive direction, except in one study where behaviour did not change despite positive TPBC shifts.ConclusionIncentive interventions show potential to increase AT behaviour change particularly as part of multi-component programmes. Incentives may influence psychological constructs that support intentions and behaviour change, but direct evidence on TPBC remains limited.

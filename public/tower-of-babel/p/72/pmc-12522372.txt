Comparison of clinical outcomes between plastic braces versus sponge abduction packs in the early rehabilitation period after rotator cuff repair
Authors: Rui Du, Lei Wang, Dongmei Ai, Zhizhuo Li, Jing Zhang, Qing Jiang, Jianghui Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe study compares adjustable plastic braces fixed at 45° abduction versus sponge abduction packs for their effects on short-term pain and functional outcomes following arthroscopic rotator cuff repair, while analyzing the feasibility of replacing sponge abduction packs with plastic braces.MethodsA total of 48 patients were recruited between April and July 2024 and stratified by gender into 4 groups: plastic brace group and sponge abduction pack group (12 patients each). At 3 weeks post-operation, follow-up was conducted using the Western Ontario Rotator Cuff Index (WORC) as the primary measure for shoulder joint function, and Visual Analog Scale (VAS), Range of Motion (ROM), Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), and Short Form 12 (SF-12) as secondary measures for pain, mobility, sleep quality, and quality of life.ResultsA total of 44 patients were enrolled, 23 in the plastic brace group and 21 in the sponge abduction pack group. At 3 weeks postoperatively, significant between-group differences were observed in abduction angle (78.5 ± 7.9 vs. 72.9 ± 7.2, P = 0.02), whereas WORC scores showed no statistical significance (90.1 ± 24.0 vs. 71.8 ± 29.1, P = 0.22).In subgroup analysis by gender, the difference in WORC score was statistically significant between sponge abduction pack and plastic brace groups in females (70.0 ± 29.1 vs. 100.7 ± 19.6, P = 0.03).ConclusionThe use of a plastic brace instead of a sponge abduction pack after rotator cuff repair shows potential feasibility and results in a greater abduction angle in the early rehabilitation period. In addition, the plastic brace showed better clinical outcomes than the sponge abduction pack especially in females.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Center (ChiCTR2500095943),20,250,115.

Introduce

Rotator cuff tears are a common cause of shoulder pain and limited mobility, which can be caused by rotator cuff degeneration or trauma, and are often characterized by shoulder pain and weakness [1, 2].According to statistics, more than 25% of individuals over the age of 50 have rotator cuff tears, and more than 50% of individuals over the age of 80 are affected by rotator cuff tears [3]. Arthroscopic rotator cuff repair has become the primary treatment method for rotator cuff injuries and is effective in alleviating symptoms and restoring function [4, 5].

Patients may experience short-term pain [6, 7], poor sleep quality [2, 8], and other complications postoperatively.This may be related to the use of abduction braces for fixation postoperatively [2]. However, previous studies have primarily compared long-term follow-up of pain and functional recovery in patients who used internal rotation slings or sponge abduction packs after rotator cuff surgery [9–13]. No literature has been found comparing the use of plastic braces and sponge abduction packs for alleviating postoperative pain and discomfort in patients.Therefore, we hypothesize that the use of plastic braces for fixation, compared to sponge abduction packs, can improve pain, sleep, quality of life, and shoulder function at 3 weeks postoperatively, and we aim to analyze the feasibility of replacing sponge abduction packs with plastic braces.

Methods and materials

A randomized controlled trial with parallel design was used in this study. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital (Ethical approval number: 2024-592-02), and all patients signed informed consent forms.This study employed a randomized controlled design without blinding, including 48 patients from our center who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria between April and July 2024.Inclusion criteria: (1) Age 40–75 years [14]; (2) Preoperative MRI confirmed supraspinatus and/or infraspinatus tear, with tear size less than 2 cm [15]; (3) Full-thickness rotator cuff tear that can be repaired arthroscopically. Exclusion criteria: (1) Irreparable rotator cuff tears; (2) Tear size greater than 2 cm; (3) Patients with subscapularis or labral injuries; (4) Revision rotator cuff repair surgery; (5) Glenohumeral osteoarthritis; (6) Systemic inflammatory diseases;7.Patients lost to follow-up. Patient demographic data were collected from the medical record system, including age, affected side, acute/chronic condition, disease duration, diabetes, BMI, number of anchors, tear size and Goutallier. All patients had their MRI examinations performed within three months prior to surgery. Compared to current fixed-angle sponge abduction packs, the plastic brace enables adjustable abduction and rotation angles through a nut mechanism, allowing secure positioning at comfortable and precise angles to optimize biomechanical conditions. An installation abduction angle of 45° is appropriate. Representative device photographs are provided in Fig. 1.Fig. 1a The plastic brace and (b) the sponge abduction pack used in this study

Randomization

The study design is a randomized controlled trial without blinding. A random grouping was performed using an Excel-generated randomization list at the time of admission. Three columns were created: patient ID (1–24), randomization codes generated via RANDBETWEEN(0,1) function, and group allocation. Participants were subsequently assigned to either the plastic brace group or sponge group.The patients were divided into male (24 patients) and female (24 patients) groups, with each group further divided into subgroups of 12 patients for the plastic brace and sponge abduction pack treatments. A researcher generated the random allocation sequence, and another enrolled participants who assigned participants to interventions. Allocation concealment was not implemented. Due to the highly visible nature of the abduction brace, blinding of participants, personnel, and outcome assessors was not feasible.

Perioperative

The patients received a combination of general anesthesia and interscalene brachial plexus block, with manual release of the shoulder joint in the beach-chair position, followed by disinfection and draping.The arthroscope was inserted through the posterior portal, 2 cm below the posterior acromion, to access the glenohumeral joint space, performing both intra-articular and subacromial diagnostic arthroscopy.An observation portal (positioned 1 cm lateral to the midpoint of the acromion’s lateral border) and working portal (located 2 cm lateral to the anterolateral acromion corner) were established posterior-superior to the rotator cuff tear for tear assessment, edge débridement, and anchor fixation with suturing. Single-row or double-row suture techniques was chosen based on the condition.All patients underwent acromioplasty, and biceps tendon tenotomy was performed when necessary. Immediately post-operation, shoulder abduction braces/packs (Guan a.i., China) were applied at 30°−50° for immobilization.

Postoperative rehabilitation

Early postoperative mobilization and active rehabilitation exercises to reduce the risk of postoperative adhesions [16, 17]. The affected limb should be fixed in the scapular plane, with the abduction angle controlled between 30°and 50° [18, 19].The abduction brace should be used for 6 weeks postoperatively. Passive flexion, abduction, and pendulum exercises are performed within the first 3 weeks postoperatively. Internal rotation training is initiated 6 weeks postoperatively to prevent rotator cuff injury, as addressed in the text.​From 3 to 6 weeks, table wiping exercises are introduced, and wall climbing exercises are started between 6 weeks and 3 months.

Postoperative follow-up

The final follow-up at 3 weeks postoperatively uses the VAS for pain, ROM, PSQI, SF-12, and WORC to evaluate pain, range of motion, sleep quality, quality of life, and shoulder function.The WORC was designated as the primary clinical outcome, with VAS, ROM, PSQI, and SF-12 as secondary outcomes.

Statistics

Previous similar studies were reviewed [10], and the Constant Shoulder Score at 3 months postoperatively was reported as Mean ± SD (63.7 ± 20.7 vs. 48.6 ± 13.9). Sample size calculation was performed using the Gpower software(Düsseldorf, Germany, version: 3.1), with α set at 0.05, a power of 80% and effect size d 0.86. Each group included 23 patients, and considering potential dropout, the target was to include 24 patients per group.Since gender is a factor that affects postoperative pain and sleep [20, 21], stratification by gender was performed.

The WORC score at 3 weeks postoperatively was used as the primary endpoint, with a non-inferiority test applied. Based on the expected standard deviation of 22 points from previous studies [22], a difference of 13 points (out of 100) in the WORC index between the two groups was considered clinically significant.Therefore, if the lower limit of the one-sided 95% confidence interval (LCL) of the difference in total scores between the brace group and the abduction packs group is greater than -δ (−13), a non-inferiority conclusion can be drawn.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 22.0; IBM Corp, Armonk, NY), with a P value of < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Independent samples t-test was used to compare continuous variables at baseline and outcome variables between the plastic brace and sponge pack groups, while chi-square tests were used to compare categorical baseline variables. One-way ANOVA was used to compare continuous variables and outcome variables among the four subgroups, and multiple chi-square tests were applied to compare categorical variables at baseline. Postoperative outcomes comparison: plastic Brace vs. sponge abduction pack at 3 weeks

At 3 weeks postoperatively, the WORC scores (primary outcome) showed no significant statistically difference (90.1 ± 24.0 vs. 71.8 ± 29.1, P = 0.22, 95% CI 2.870 to 35.171). However, abduction angles (secondary outcome) differed significantly between groups (78.5 ± 7.9 vs. 72.9 ± 7.2, P = 0.02). Among the subgroups, there were statistically significant differences in VAS scores between the female and male plastic brace groups (3.7 ± 1.4 vs. 1.9 ± 1.0, P = 0.02, 95% CI −3.413 to −0.2689), and in WORC scores between the female plastic brace group and the female sponge pack group (70.0 ± 29.1 vs. 100.7 ± 19.6, P = 0.03, 95% CI 1.627 to 58.87).No statistically significant differences were observed across subgroups for PSQI, SF-12 (PCS and MCS), forward flexion, abduction, or external rotation angles. Additionally, neither VAS scores in the female brace/sponge pack group nor WORC scores in male/female brace groups showed significant differences.

Discussion

The study found that the plastic brace was not inferior to the sponge abduction pack in terms of WORC scores as the primary outcome. It was slightly superior to the sponge abduction pack in the secondary outcome of Abduction angle, while no statistical differences were found in PSQI, SF-12(PCS), SF-12(MCS), Forward Flexion, and External Rotation. Conti et al. also found that during the early follow-up period (1–3 months), the 15° external rotation brace group showed significantly better passive abduction, external rotation, and forward flexion range of motion compared to the 15° internal rotation sling group (p < 0.05) [9].It is evident that different types of braces have a certain influence on the abduction angle during 3-week postoperative recovery.

In the subgroup analysis, VAS scores were lower in the male plastic brace group compared to the female plastic brace group. WORC scores were higher in the female plastic brace group than in the female sponge abduction pack group.Pandey et al. enrolled 42 patients to compare the effects of an internal rotation sling versus an abduction brace after 6 weeks of postoperative immobilization.The results showed no significant differences between the two groups in pain levels, CMS scores, or rotator cuff healing rates within 1 year postoperatively [11].Similarly, Ghandour et al. found that although both groups showed significant improvements in VAS and CMS scores from surgery to 3 months postoperatively, no statistically significant differences were detected between groups [12].Notably, previous studies suggested that abduction braces had no significant effect on pain or functional scores, whereas subgroup analysis in this study further revealed that males experienced less pain with plastic braces than females, while females achieved better clinical outcomes with plastic braces than males.

Short-term fixation using an abduction brace after rotator cuff repair is one of the important rehabilitation methods. On one hand, it can maintain a low-level load on the tendon, stimulating tendon-bone healing [17, 18].On the other hand, it helps avoid excessive tension at the healing interface caused by gravity or other factors, thereby reducing the risk of tendon retear [19, 20].It also helps improve blood circulation [10, 11] at the healing interface and promotes the orderly arrangement of newly formed collagen postoperatively [21].It provides superior biomechanical effects.

Since early postoperative pain is associated with factors such as gender [20] age [23, 24], tear size, anchor use, and brace application, some studies have shown that the use of an abduction brace after rotator cuff repair can significantly reduce the supraspinatus tendon tension, thereby alleviating pain [25]. In this study, no significant differences in pain scores were observed between the two groups. However, subgroup analysis revealed that male patients using plastic braces had significantly lower pain scores than those using sponge abduction packs.

Limitations

This study also has some limitations.This study is a randomized controlled trial, but due to the fact that patients, researchers, and data collectors all know the group allocation, blinding was not possible.In terms of sample size calculation, due to the lack of studies on short-term follow-up of pain, ROM, sleep quality, quality of life, and functional status after rotator cuff repair, sample size was calculated based on the 3-month CMS score.Compared with prior research, each subgroup in this study comprised 12 patients, this is a limited sample size to yielding reliable results. Due to the extremely short preoperative waiting period in our clinical setting, functional assessment scores were not collected prior to surgery; only VAS scores were obtained.

Conclusion

The use of a plastic brace instead of a sponge abduction pack after rotator cuff repair shows potential feasibility and results in a greater abduction angle in the early rehabilitation period. In addition, the plastic brace showed better clinical outcomes than the sponge abduction pack especially in females.

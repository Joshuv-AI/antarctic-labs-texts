Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “The Context of Kidney Care in Pacific Island Countries”
Authors: Yogeshni Chandra, Anis Ta’eed, Shilpanjali Jesudason, Angus Ritchie
Published: 2025

The Author Replies:

We appreciate the comments in the letter to the editor1 regarding our recent editorial, “The Progress of Nephrology in Pacific Island Countries of Oceania”,2 which provides valuable additional context. We agree that there are substantial gaps in health service coverage in the Oceania region, especially in the middle-income Pacific Island countries, and these contribute to the growing burden of chronic kidney disease.

Beyond health system limitations, environmental factors, climate change, and other social and economic determinants of health exert a significant influence on kidney disease risk in the Pacific. Addressing these upstream drivers is essential and aligns with the World Health Organization Sustainable Development Goals and calls for integrated strategies that promote environmental sustainability, social equity, and kidney health.3

In this regard, the adoption of the World Health Organization kidney health resolution,4 earlier this year is a landmark step that specifically acknowledges the disproportionate impact of kidney health on small island developing states and emphasizes its heavy financial burden. We hope the resolution will prompt Pacific Island countries’ governments to invest in kidney health initiatives and integrate them into national health policies.

Alongside this, the Pacific Working Group of the Australia and the New Zealand Society of Nephrology has finalized a 5-year strategic plan for the middle-income countries of the Pacific. Its vision is to build capacity for adult and pediatric kidney care in the Pacific Island countries of Oceania, through the development of a robust and sustainable nephrology training system that is appropriate for the region. The goal is to achieve exchange of knowledge and expertise within Oceania, and facilitate equitable access to kidney health care for all.

An adaptive oppositional grey wolf optimizer for complex engineering problems
Authors: Othman Waleed Khalid, Nor Ashidi Mat Isa, Karrar Mohsin Alwan, Sew Sun Tiang, Ahbishek Sharma, Tarek Berghout, Jun-Jiat Tiang, Wei Hong Lim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMetaheuristic optimization algorithms are critical for solving complex, high-dimensional problems, however, their performance is frequently hindered by premature convergence, limited exploration capabilities, and an imbalance between search phases. While the grey wolf optimizer (GWO) has demonstrated considerable potential, its rigid linear control parameters and susceptibility to elite stagnation limit its scalability in highly deceptive landscapes. To overcome these critical drawbacks, this paper proposes the adaptive oppositional grey wolf optimizer (AOGWO), a robust and dynamically adaptive optimization framework. The proposed approach makes three primary contributions: (1) integrating a hybrid opposition-based learning (OBL) initialization framework to guarantee maximum initial spatial diversity, (2) implementing an adaptive cosine control strategy paired with a decaying Jumping Rate and Lévy flight perturbations to dynamically balance exploration and exploitation, and (3) proposing a highly targeted selective leading opposition (SLO) mechanism applied exclusively to the Alpha leader to prevent elite traps without incurring excessive computational overhead. The performance of AOGWO is evaluated using an extensive suite of 41 benchmark test functions (comprising the IEEE CEC2017 and CEC2022 suites) and seven challenging real-world engineering design problems. Comprehensive empirical results provide concrete data demonstrating AOGWO’s competitive performance. Quantitatively, AOGWO achieved the best mean performance on 24 out of 29 CEC2017 functions and all 12 CEC2022 functions. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test further showed statistically significant improvements over most competing algorithms at p < 0.05, while the Friedman test ranked AOGWO first overall across the evaluated benchmark suites. In constrained engineering design problems, AOGWO also produced competitive optimal solutions while satisfying the prescribed constraints. These findings suggest that AOGWO is a promising process innovation to advance practical optimization tasks, promote economic productivity, and improve resource efficiency.Keywords: Grey wolf optimizer, Selective leading opposite, Lévy flight, Opposition-based learning, Metaheuristic algorithms, Constrained engineering optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Optimization problems are becoming increasingly common in rapidly developing sectors such as engineering, computer science, aerospace, and automation, making optimization an essential field of study1. As these problems increase in complexity, there is a growing necessity for highly efficient and robust optimization techniques. Conventional mathematical optimization approaches, including linear programming2, nonlinear programming3, mixed-integer programming4, and dynamic programming5, are primarily deterministic and rely heavily on explicitly defined constraints and well-structured models. These traditional techniques, however, frequently struggle to properly address intricate, real-world scenarios. To overcome these mathematical limitations, Metaheuristic Algorithms (MHAs) have been introduced as an effective alternative6,7.

In recent years, MHAs have seen widespread adoption in real-world implementations because of their flexibility, inherent simplicity, and ability to quickly generate solutions8–10. Bypassing the limitations of traditional methods, MHAs do not demand strict mathematical formulations of the problem at hand. Instead, they rely on intelligent search tactics and heuristic guidelines, allowing them to adapt seamlessly to a wide variety of problem domains. Beyond theoretical benchmarks, the true value of advanced MHAs lies in their critical role in solving complex, highly constrained practical application problems across diverse industrial sectors. For instance, in mechanical11,12 and civil engineering13 applications, optimization algorithms are indispensable for minimizing material costs, reducing structural weight, and enhancing the operational efficiency of complex systems such as gear trains, tension springs, and cantilever beams. In these practical scenarios, the mathematical search space is often severely restricted by stringent physical and safety constraints, including maximum shear stress, deflection limits, and strict boundary conditions. Traditional deterministic methods frequently fail to navigate these disjointed, non-linear feasible regions. Consequently, developing robust MHAs capable of efficiently avoiding local optima and managing strict constraints directly translates to significant economic, environmental, and performance benefits in real-world manufacturing and structural design. In recent years, the literature has witnessed a significant surge in novel nature-inspired metaheuristics designed to tackle increasingly complex, high-dimensional optimization challenges. Notable recent advances include the Eurasian Lynx Optimizer (ELO)35, which mathematically models the solitary hunting and stalking behaviors of the Eurasian lynx, and the Cape Lynx Optimizer (CLO)36, which simulates the strategic survival and foraging tactics of the Cape lynx to balance exploration and exploitation. Furthermore, the relentless pursuit to push the boundaries of convergence speed and algorithmic robustness has led to the introduction of several other contemporary state-of-the-art frameworks. These include the Coati Optimization Algorithm (COA)37, the White Shark Optimizer (WSO)38, and the Walrus Optimizer (WO)39. The rapid proliferation of these modern algorithms highlights the ongoing evolution of MHAs and underscores the critical need for continuous algorithmic refinement to solve modern, heavily constrained engineering problems.

Despite these advancements, the widely adopted Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)34 stands out due to its unique biological foundation. GWO is modeled upon the social hierarchy and cooperative hunting tactics of grey wolves in their natural habitat. Operating as a population-based algorithm, GWO mimics this structured pack behavior where individual wolves act as candidate solutions navigating the search space. The central mechanism of GWO directs the movement of the pack based on the locations of the alpha, beta, and delta leaders, which function as core reference points. This effective strategy has been successfully utilized across numerous optimization fields, such as engineering design40, energy consumption41, medical diagnostics42, biomedical engineering43, and wireless sensor networks44.

Despite its demonstrated effectiveness, the standard GWO exhibits certain limitations when tasked with highly complex optimization problems. These constraints specifically include a vulnerability to stagnation in local optima45, constrained exploration capabilities41, and a lack of sufficient population diversity46. Even though various enhanced versions of GWO have been developed, continuously preserving an optimal balance between convergence speed and population diversity remains a significant challenge.

A common method for maintaining diversity while steering the search toward optimal zones is to adjust the algorithm’s control parameters or structural search mechanisms47. However, traditional structural modifications in MHAs frequently rely on static, fixed parameters throughout the optimization lifecycle. For instance, using a strictly linear decay for control variables enforces a rigid structural approach, regardless of the shifting dynamics of the optimization process48. This inflexibility limits an algorithm’s adaptability in dynamic search environments where the requirements for exploitation and exploration change iteratively. Specifically, employing rigid and highly exploitative parameters during the late stages of optimization can restrict computational resources, thereby severely degrading convergence efficiency. Conversely, inadequate exploratory parameters during the initial stages can confine the search, leading directly to premature stagnation. Moreover, identifying the optimal static parameter settings is intrinsically problematic due to varying landscape complexities and problem-specific traits. These issues underscore the pressing need to create an adaptive mechanism that can dynamically govern search behaviors based on real-time optimization performance.

Furthermore, conventional techniques for enhancing algorithmic exploration often rely on computationally heavy fitness-ranking or clustering procedures, adding considerable complexity and overhead49. Certain spatial distance calculations require expensive processing in high-dimensional domains, while fitness-based adjustments demand constant sorting and grouping operations. These convoluted procedures negatively affect reproducibility and scalability, which is especially critical in computationally sensitive or high-dimensional tasks. Therefore, developing a computationally efficient yet mathematically rigorous strategy that ensures a balanced distribution of solutions and enhances convergence power is vital.

While Opposition-Based Learning (OBL) has successfully boosted the exploration abilities of several MHAs, including GWO50, WO51, and HHO52. Existing OBL iterations display significant limitations. The majority of current OBL techniques depend on static parameters and strictly separate the exploitation and exploration phases53. Generalized OBL54, for instance, utilizes fixed opposition limits, reducing its adaptability within dynamic spaces. In contrast, Quasi-OBL55 focuses heavily on tuning local search regions but fails to maintain global diversity, ultimately struggling to simultaneously escape local optima and accelerate global convergence.

Additionally, although chaotic maps are widely recognized for their effectiveness in population initialization53, they are severely underutilized for guiding oppositional jumps during the iterative phases of the search. The lack of a unified oppositional framework that seamlessly merges dynamic exploration tactics (like chaotic perturbations and adaptive boundaries) with precise exploitation strategies (like threshold-guided refinement near elite solutions) greatly hinders existing MHAs from balancing the exploration–exploitation tradeoff.

For example, Dynamic Opposition (DO)56 learning features adaptive opposition bounds but lacks a targeted exploitation mechanism to prioritize optimal regions. On the other hand, Selective Leading Opposition (SLO)50 is excellent at refining solutions near elites but fails to sustain adequate global diversity. Consequently, it is imperative to develop a hybrid, integrated oppositional strategy, such as Joint Opposite Selection (JOS)57. JOS can effectively merge the global exploration power of DO’s chaotic boundary-expanding jumps with the targeted exploitation of SLO’s adaptive thresholding, solving current gaps and significantly elevating algorithmic performance in complex landscapes.

This research proposes an Adaptive Oppositional Grey Wolf Optimizer (AOGWO), which incorporates adaptive control mechanisms to improve the balance between exploration and exploitation in the original GWO. Instead of relying on fixed parameters, AOGWO adjusts its search behavior using cosine control and Lévy flight mechanisms based on feedback from the optimization process. These dynamic adjustments allow the algorithm to respond to varying landscape characteristics, supporting broader exploration in early stages and more focused refinement in later iterations.

Furthermore, AOGWO incorporates a hybrid OBL initialization mechanism designed to enhance the initial distribution of candidate solutions across the search space. This approach promotes diversity at the early stage of the optimization process, which helps reduce the likelihood of premature convergence. In addition, the structured initialization supports scalability and consistency when applied to higher-dimensional problems.

Unlike conventional GWO variants that apply multiple enhancement strategies uniformly across the population, the proposed AOGWO adopts a selective and decoupled design philosophy. Specifically, dynamic opposition-based learning is restricted to the initialization phase to enhance initial diversity, while the iterative search is guided by an Alpha-targeted SLO refinement mechanism. In addition, the activation of Lévy-flight perturbations is controlled by an adaptive cosine mechanism and a decaying jumping rate, allowing the algorithm to regulate exploration intensity over time. This design differs from existing multi-strategy metaheuristics by avoiding population-wide application of computationally intensive operators, thereby achieving a more efficient balance between search performance and computational cost.

Literature review

This section presents a comprehensive review of the original GWO and its various modified and hybrid variants, examining their search strategies, strengths, and limitations. This discussion is further extended to include recent MHAs that adopt JOS and OBL strategies to enhance performance. This analytical survey lays the groundwork for the proposed AOGWO, which integrates a hybrid initialization framework, and features adaptive control mechanisms for dynamically regulating the exploration–exploitation balance during the optimization process.

Original GWO

The grey wolf (Canis lupus) is an apex predator that exhibits a strict dominance hierarchy and advanced cooperative hunting behaviors. The standard GWO algorithm draws inspiration from this natural leadership hierarchy and hunting mechanism34. As a swarm intelligence optimization algorithm, GWO simulates these behaviors mathematically, where each wolf represents a candidate solution in the search space58. The social hierarchy of the wolf pack is divided into four distinct levels: alpha , beta , delta , and omega . The α wolf is considered the leader of the pack and represents the best candidate solution. The β and δ wolves are the second and third best solutions, respectively, they assist the α in making decisions and guiding the rest of the pack, which consists of the ω wolves59. Related works and GWO variants

In recent years, numerous enhanced versions of the GWO have been proposed to address its limitations, such as premature convergence and an imbalance between exploration and exploitation phases. These variants are broadly categorized into two groups: modified GWO, which involves structural or control parameter adjustments, and hybrid GWO, which combines GWO with other optimization techniques.

Modified GWO variants

Modifications to the basic GWO generally focus on enhancing the exploration and exploitation. Table 1 shows a summary of the modified GWO variants, demonstrating their used methodology, advantages, and disadvantages. Mittal60 proposed a modified GWO (mGWO) and enhances the standard algorithm by replacing the linear decay of the control parameter a with an exponential decay function. This methodological shift allows for a more prolonged and effective exploration phase before transitioning the pack into local exploitation. The primary advantage of this approach is a significant reduction in the probability of premature convergence when tackling complex objective functions. However, the extended exploration focus can act as a disadvantage by slowing down the final convergence rate in certain high-dimensional spaces. Furthermore, the exponential decay may lead to insufficient local fine-tuning iterations if the initial exploration phase is overextended.

Summary of Modified GWO Variants.

Long61 presented the Exploration-Enhanced Grey Wolf Optimizer (EEGWO) that utilizes an opposition-based learning strategy for population initialization alongside a non-linear adjustment strategy for the control parameter. This dual-methodology ensures that the initial search agents are broadly distributed and that the transition to exploitation is mathematically smoothed. The primary advantage is its exceptional capacity for broad search space exploration, making it highly effective for multi-modal landscapes. Despite this, a notable disadvantage is that the algorithm may struggle to finely tune solutions in highly constrained or tightly bounded problem domains, and it requires increased computational initialization time to evaluate the opposite population.

Kohli62 proposed Chaotic Grey Wolf Optimizer (CGWO) by employing various chaotic maps, such as logistic or tent maps, to replace the pseudo-random number generators in the standard algorithm. This approach dictates the variation of key parameters, ensuring a more uniform and mathematically bounded distribution of search steps. The standout advantage of CGWO is its remarkably fast initial convergence rate and improved global optimization capability. However, its significant disadvantage is that its performance is highly sensitive to the specific chaotic map chosen, requiring tedious problem-specific tuning. Additionally, the lack of an adaptive control mechanism means the chaotic sequence cannot dynamically adjust to the landscape during the later stages of optimization.

Vijay63 presented the Quantum Grey Wolf Optimizer (QGWO) translates the traditional mathematical position updates into quantum computing concepts using qubits and quantum rotation gates. This quantum-inspired methodology evaluates multiple superposed states simultaneously, drastically expanding the algorithm’s mathematical coverage. The primary advantage is an unparalleled global search capability that intrinsically maintains massive population diversity. The disadvantage is the highly complex mathematical implementation and steep learning curve required to adapt it to standard engineering problems, accompanied by a higher computational overhead per agent due to complex quantum gate updates.

Gupta64 proposed a Random Walk Grey Wolf Optimizer (RW-GWO) that integrates a random walk mechanism into the position updating phase to help search agents jump out of local optima. This methodology applies random step sizes and directions to the leader wolves, dynamically shifting their coordinates when search stagnation is detected. The key advantage of RW-GWO is its robust ability to effectively escape deceptive local optima during the later stages of optimization. However, the inherent randomness of this mechanism can be a disadvantage, as it may inadvertently degrade the precision of local exploitation near the true global optimum and unpredictably increase execution time if random steps frequently evaluate out-of-bounds solutions.

Gao59 introduced Variable Weights Grey Wolf Optimizer (VWGWO) which replaces the standard mathematical averaging of the α, β, and δ positions with a dynamic weighting mechanism. This method calculates adaptive weights based on the real-time fitness values of the three leaders, granting more influence to the most optimal wolf. Consequently, the main advantage is a targeted and highly accurate local search, significantly improving final solution quality. The disadvantage lies in its sensitivity; poorly tuned weight parameters can cause premature α dominance, dragging the entire pack into a local optimum. Furthermore, if the subordinate wolves find better local regions, the alpha-heavy weighting can prevent the pack from shifting focus efficiently.

Nadimi40 introduced Improved Grey Wolf Optimizer (IGWO) that incorporates a dimension-learning-based hunting (DLH) search strategy inspired by natural wolf behavior to maintain neighborhood diversity. By constructing a localized neighborhood for each wolf and sharing dimensional information, the algorithm effectively enhances its global search capabilities. This results in the distinct advantage of maintaining high population diversity throughout the optimization lifecycle, severely limiting stagnation. Conversely, the major disadvantage is the high computational cost and time complexity required to calculate neighborhood distances in every iteration, leading to severe scalability issues when dealing with extremely high-dimensional problems.

Meidani65 introduced the Adaptive Grey Wolf Optimizer (AGWO) that utilizes a dynamically adapting step-size mechanism that reacts specifically to the smoothness or ruggedness of the fitness landscape. By monitoring real-time fitness improvements, the algorithm intelligently scales the hunting steps of the search agents based on contextual feedback. The advantage of this adaptability is exceptionally high precision and efficiency during targeted local exploitation phases. The limiting disadvantage is its high dependency on the nature of the fitness landscape, often struggling if the terrain is extremely chaotic or deceptive, where noisy topological feedback can confuse the adaptive mechanism and lead to inappropriate step sizes.

The modified GWO variants primarily focus on manipulating internal mathematical operators, such as control parameters, initialization protocols, and weight updates, to overcome the base algorithm’s inherent premature convergence and imbalance issues. While strategies like chaotic maps, quantum principles, and adaptive weighting successfully enhance either global exploration or late-stage precision, they frequently introduce strict trade-offs. These enhancements consistently trade the simplicity of the original GWO for increased structural complexity, heightened parameter sensitivity, or a heavier computational overhead per iteration, highlighting the challenge of achieving algorithmic balance without sacrificing efficiency.

Hybrid GWO variants

Hybridization aims to overcome the specific weaknesses of GWO, often its exploitation limits, by merging it with the complementary strengths of other MHAs. Table 2 shows a summary of the hybrid GWO variants, presenting their used methodology, advantages, and disadvantages. Long66 proposed a hybrid algorithm (GWOCS) that structurally enhances the Grey Wolf Optimizer by introducing a novel transition mechanism and integrating the Cuckoo Search (CS) algorithm specifically for the decision-layer wolves α , β , and δ. The integration applies CS to the top-ranking leaders to dynamically improve their local search capabilities and refine the overall pack’s trajectory toward the optimum. The primary advantage of this hybridization is its high precision and robust performance in extracting parameters for complex, multimodal solar photovoltaic models under varying operating conditions. However, a notable disadvantage is that applying the CS mechanisms exclusively to the leader wolves increases the computational complexity per iteration and may risk over-exploiting the current best regions if the leaders are trapped in deceptive local optima.

Summary of Hybrid GWO Variants.

Zhang67 proposed the GWO-PSO hybrid algorithm that merges the leadership hierarchy of GWO with the personal and global memory retention mechanisms of Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). By integrating PSO’s velocity and historical best equations into the wolf pack updates, the search agents benefit from both swarm communication and individual memory. The prominent advantage is a massive enhancement in local exploitation capabilities and convergence speed. However, a significant disadvantage is that the combined exploitative nature of both base algorithms makes it highly prone to rapid loss of diversity and premature convergence.

Yadav68 introduced a GA-GWO hybrid algorithm designed to optimize maximum power point tracking in solar photovoltaic systems under partial shading conditions. It fuses the rapid, leader-driven convergence of the GWO with the diverse, evolutionary crossover and mutation operators of a Genetic Algorithm. The primary advantage of this hybridization is its high accuracy and faster tracking speed in locating the true global maximum power point, effectively preventing the system from stagnating at deceptive local power peaks. However, a notable disadvantage is the increased algorithmic complexity and computational burden, as executing both evolutionary and swarm-based mathematical operators simultaneously demands higher processing power for real-time hardware controllers.

Yu69 proposed a Hybrid Grey Wolf Optimizer and Differential Evolution (HGWODE) algorithm specifically designed to tackle complex 3D unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) path planning. It modifies the standard GWO position-update equations to force subordinate wolves to search more tightly around the leaders, while simultaneously integrating a rank-based DE mutation strategy to maintain broad search space coverage. The primary advantage of this hybridization is its exceptional capability to efficiently generate safe, optimal flight paths in obstacle-rich disaster scenarios by maintaining a strict balance between exploration and exploitation. However, a notable disadvantage is the increased structural complexity and computational cost per iteration, as executing both the modified hierarchical updates and the evolutionary DE mutation operators demands higher processing resources.

Zhao70 introduced a hybrid algorithm structurally enhances the standard GWO by integrating OBL for initial population diversity and the Sine Cosine Algorithm (SCA) for continuous boundary exploration. Xu76 enhanced the Fish Migration Optimization (FMO) algorithm by integrating an opposition learning strategy based on the elimination principle. This approach computes opposite candidate solutions and eliminates the worst-performing individuals in the population, replacing them with their superior opposite counterparts. This approach provides a significant boost in global exploration, which effectively increases overall population quality and prevents stagnation in local optima. However, the elimination principle utilized in the enhanced FMO algorithm carries the inherent risk of aggressively purging valuable genetic diversity from the population. By replacing the worst-performing individuals with their opposite counterparts, the algorithm may inadvertently discard agents that, while currently yielding poor fitness, possess unique positional coordinates critical for escaping local optima later in the search.

Jena77 developed the Opposition African Vulture Optimization Algorithm (OAVOA) by integrating standard OBL into the traditional AVOA structure. The concept of this enhancement involves computing and evaluating both the current positions of the search agents and their mathematically opposite counterparts within the defined boundaries of the search space. By analyzing these original and opposite candidate solutions during the initialization and iterative updating phases, the algorithm broadens its search scope. This OBL integration is a significant enhancement in global exploration capabilities, which actively prevents the search agents from stagnating in local optima and ensures a highly diverse population throughout the optimization process. However, a notable drawback of the OAVOA framework is that the continuous generation of opposite positions throughout the iterative process can inadvertently disrupt the algorithm’s later exploitation phases. This constant push for exploration makes it difficult for the search agents to effectively fine-tune solutions once they are near the true global optimum, potentially leading to suboptimal final results.

Sharma78 introduced an improved Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) algorithm that integrates a foundational OBL mechanism (OLVMFO). This approach applies OBL primarily during the population initialization phase by systematically generating and evaluating mathematically opposite counterparts to the original candidate solutions within the defined search boundaries. The embedding of this opposition strategy has enhanced the algorithm’s global search capabilities. By guaranteeing a more broadly dispersed and diverse initial population, the search agents are prevented from clustering in suboptimal regions early on, which establishes a much stronger foundation for broad exploration. However, this approach has its lack of dynamic adaptability during the later stages of the optimization process. Because the opposition strategy is primarily applied only during the population initialization phase, the algorithm lacks an active, ongoing mechanism to help search agents escape deep local optima encountered later in the search.

By simultaneously assessing both the original candidate solutions and their opposite counterparts, the algorithm ensures a broader and more rigorous exploration of the defined search boundaries. Zhang80 enhanced the standard Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) framework by integrating OBL. These limitations highlight the need for a more structured and computationally efficient framework that selectively applies diversity-enhancing and refinement-oriented mechanisms according to their most appropriate roles. To address this gap, the proposed AOGWO adopts a decoupled opposition-based design: Dynamic Opposition is used during Hybrid OBL initialization to improve initial population diversity, while Alpha-targeted SLO is applied during the iterative phase to reduce elite stagnation. This design distinguishes AOGWO from conventional population-wide opposition strategies by improving the balance between search effectiveness and computational cost.

Lévy flight-assisted position update

In the original GWO architecture, subordinate wolves update their positions by mathematically averaging their distances relative to the Alpha, Beta, and Delta leaders. While highly effective for unimodal problems, this averaging mechanism becomes a critical weakness in deceptively multimodal landscapes. If the top three leaders simultaneously fall into a deep local optimum, the mathematical averaging forces the entire pack to converge on that exact trap, resulting in permanent algorithmic stagnation.

To counteract this structural flaw, AOGWO integrates a Lévy flight-assisted position update, conditionally triggered by the adaptive jumping rate. Lévy flights are random walks characterized by step lengths drawn from a heavy-tailed probability distribution. In practice, a Lévy flight produces a sequence of many short, localized steps intermixed with occasional massive leaps. This unique fractal property perfectly mimics the foraging behavior of natural predators, allowing them to thoroughly search a local patch before suddenly vaulting to a completely new territory.

Oppositional mechanisms in AOGWO

Opposition-based learning is a mathematical strategy designed to enhance the exploration capability of metaheuristic algorithms by evaluating a candidate solution together with its spatial opposite. By considering both the original position and its opposite counterpart, the search process can increase the chance of sampling more promising regions compared with purely random search strategies. The original JOS framework57 applies both Dynamic Opposition (DO) and Selective Leading Opposition (SLO) during the iterative optimization process. In contrast, the proposed AOGWO decouples these two mechanisms according to their functional roles. Specifically, DO is incorporated into the Hybrid OBL initialization phase to enhance initial population diversity, whereas the iterative optimization phase relies on an Alpha-targeted SLO mechanism for leader refinement. This decoupled design avoids applying opposition-based operations uniformly throughout the population and reduces unnecessary objective-function evaluations during the main search process.

Alpha-targeted selective leading opposition

The SLO mechanism in AOGWO generates an oppositional candidate based only on the current best-performing solution, namely the Alpha wolf. Unlike conventional opposition-based approaches that apply refinement to all search agents, the proposed method restricts SLO to the Alpha leader only. This targeted design reduces redundant evaluations while maintaining focused exploitation around the most promising solution.

Computational complexity

From a theoretical perspective, the dominant computational cost of population-based MHAs arises from position updating and objective-function evaluation. Let N, D, and T denote the population size, problem dimension, and maximum number of iterations, respectively. The standard GWO requires O(ND) operations per iteration for updating the positions of all search agents, resulting in an overall complexity of O(TND), excluding the problem-dependent objective-function evaluation cost. In AOGWO, the Hybrid OBL initialization introduces an additional one-time cost of O(ND), since it is performed only before the main optimization loop. The Lévy-flight update is applied conditionally according to the adaptive jumping rate Jr, but it still scales linearly with N and D in the worst case. The Alpha-targeted SLO mechanism introduces only O(D) additional cost per iteration because it is applied to a single leader rather than the entire population. Therefore, the overall theoretical complexity of AOGWO remains O(TND), with only limited constant-factor overhead compared with the standard GWO.

To further assess the computational complexity of the proposed AOGWO algorithm, runtime experiments were conducted on a representative subset of CEC2017 benchmark functions, specifically F3 (unimodal), F9 (multimodal), F16 (hybrid), and F24 (composition). These functions were carefully selected to evaluate the algorithm’s efficiency across varying degrees of landscape complexity. The CPU runtime (in milliseconds) of AOGWO was recorded and compared against state-of-the-art algorithms, including OLIBCO, BMA-EOBL, FMO-OBL, PSO-OBL, HHO-JOS, mMGO, OAVOA, OBWOA, INFO, MPA, AO, and RUN. All algorithms were implemented in the same MATLAB environment and executed under identical hardware conditions to ensure a fair and rigorous comparison.

As depicted in Fig. 7, AOGWO demonstrates competitive runtime performance across the selected benchmark functions. The proposed algorithm consistently remains within the lower runtime range among the compared methods for F3, F9, F16, and F24, although it is not necessarily the fastest method in every case. This indicates that the additional mechanisms introduced in AOGWO do not impose excessive computational burden. Compared with more complex variants such as PSO-OBL and HHO-JOS, AOGWO achieves a more balanced trade-off between computational time and optimization performance. These results provide empirical support for the practical computational efficiency of the proposed framework.

The highly favorable runtime efficiency of the proposed AOGWO algorithm is directly attributed to its lean architectural design, which integrates three key methodological enhancements that maximize search quality while strictly minimizing computational overhead. The Hybrid OBL Initialization framework generates a highly diverse and well-distributed starting population exclusively at the onset of the optimization process. By completing this dynamic mathematical scattering before the iterative loop begins, the algorithm establishes a robust search foundation without incurring recurring computational penalties during the main execution phase.

The integration of the Adaptive Cosine Control and the decaying Jumping Rate (Jr) dynamically regulates the algorithm’s exploratory actions. The computationally intensive Lévy flight-assisted jumps are strictly bounded by Jr, which decays linearly over time. This design ensures that massive, resource-heavy spatial jumps are utilized primarily during the early exploration phases and are systematically phased out. Consequently, the algorithm smoothly transitions to the mathematically lighter GWO hierarchical averaging during the late-stage exploitation phase, avoiding wasteful calculations.

Unlike traditional opposition-based MHAs that apply OBL to the entire population, AOGWO implements a highly targeted SLO strategy. The SLO mechanism is applied exclusively to the single global best candidate (the Alpha leader) at the end of each iteration, and is further governed by a 50% probability threshold. This extreme selectivity prevents the algorithm from applying costly opposition operations indiscriminately, drastically reducing the number of extra objective function evaluations required per iteration.

Together, these mechanisms forge a computationally efficient optimization framework. By eliminating redundant population-wide calculations and leveraging intelligent, dynamically decaying control parameters, AOGWO maintains a highly favorable time complexity per iteration. Consequently, the algorithm demonstrates robust search performance while maintaining competitive runtime behavior, supporting its suitability for engineering optimization problems where both solution accuracy and computational efficiency are important.

Similarly, disabling the Alpha-targeted SLO mechanism weakens the final refinement process around the leading solution, which may affect stability and solution quality. Overall, the ablation results provide empirical evidence that the four components contribute to AOGWO’s performance, although their relative importance varies depending on the landscape characteristics.

Statistical analyses

To comprehensively evaluate the significance of the results obtained by the proposed AOGWO algorithm compared to the state-of-the-art MHAs, nonparametric statistical tests are conducted. These tests are essential for drawing statistically sound conclusions, as algorithmic performance data across multiple benchmark functions often do not adhere to a normal distribution. In this study, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test103 is utilized for pairwise comparisons, while the Friedman test104 is applied for multiple comparisons to determine the overall ranking of the algorithms. Furthermore, post hoc procedures including Bonferroni-Dunn, Holm, and Hochberg105 are employed to adjust the p-values and rigorously validate the significance of the observed performance differences. All statistical analyses in this study are conducted with a significance level of α = 0.05.

Wilcoxon signed-rank test

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is conducted to determine whether the performance differences between the proposed AOGWO algorithm and each of the competing MHAs are statistically significant. The test calculates the sum of positive ranks (R +) and negative ranks (R-). Here, R + represents the sum of ranks for the benchmark functions where AOGWO outperforms the compared algorithm, whereas R- denotes the sum of ranks where AOGWO is outperformed. A p-value less than 0.05 indicates a statistically significant difference. The decisions are denoted by " + ", "-", and " = ", representing that AOGWO is significantly better, significantly worse, or not significantly different compared to the competitor, respectively.

Tables 11 and 12 present the Wilcoxon signed-rank test results based on the CEC2017 and CEC2022 benchmark suites, respectively. For the CEC2017 suite, the proposed AOGWO achieves highly significant positive differences against all twelve competitors. Specifically, AOGWO completely dominates foundational and recently proposed algorithms alike, such as FMO-OBL, mMGO, OAVOA, OBWOA, AO, and RUN, recording perfect R + values of 435.0 and R- values of 0.0, with p-values far below the significance threshold. Furthermore, even against its most robust competitors, such as BMA-EOBL, HHO-JOS, and the newly integrated MPA, the proposed algorithm maintains decisive statistical competitive performance, demonstrating undeniable performance advantages. Similarly, for the complex CEC2022 suite, AOGWO demonstrates competitive performance. It achieves uniform R + values of 78.0 and R- values of 0.0 against every single competing algorithm, including the four recently added state-of-the-art benchmarks. These results conclusively affirm that the performance improvements delivered by AOGWO are mathematically rigorous, structurally persistent, and definitively not due to stochastic chance across both test suites.

Discussion

The evaluations conducted on the IEEE CEC2017 and CEC2022 benchmark suites, together with seven constrained engineering design problems, demonstrate that the proposed AOGWO can achieve competitive optimization performance across diverse problem types. Its performance is mainly supported by the Hybrid OBL initialization, the Adaptive Cosine Control with Lévy-flight perturbation, and the Alpha-targeted SLO mechanism. Nevertheless, although these mechanisms improve search diversity and reduce the risk of stagnation, they also introduce specific operational boundaries that should be acknowledged. Therefore, this section discusses the main limitations of AOGWO in terms of computational overhead, landscape dependency, search-space applicability, and parameter sensitivity.

First, although the Alpha-targeted SLO mechanism reduces unnecessary population-wide oppositional evaluations, AOGWO still introduces additional operations compared with the standard GWO. These include the Hybrid OBL initialization, conditional Lévy-flight perturbation, and leader-based SLO refinement. While the empirical runtime analysis in Section "Computational complexity" indicates that this additional cost remains manageable for the tested benchmark functions, the overhead may become more noticeable in ultra-high-dimensional problems or applications with strict real-time computational constraints. Therefore, the use of AOGWO in time-critical systems should consider the trade-off between improved search robustness and additional computational cost.

Second, there are specific topological scenarios where the Alpha-targeted SLO mechanism may experience reduced effectiveness. Since SLO refines the search based on the Alpha leader, its success depends strongly on the quality and location of the current best solution. If the Alpha leader is trapped in a deceptive local optimum, the opposite candidate generated around this leader may not necessarily guide the swarm toward a better region. This limitation may become more apparent in highly symmetric or deceptive landscapes, where the opposite region of a local optimum may still correspond to another poor or unpromising search area.

In addition, AOGWO may face challenges in severely fragmented composition landscapes where many local optima are closely distributed and the useful search region is extremely narrow. Under such conditions, the conditional Lévy-flight perturbation may not always provide sufficient directional guidance, while excessive exploratory movement may delay fine local exploitation. Therefore, although AOGWO improves robustness against premature convergence, it cannot completely eliminate stagnation risks in extremely deceptive fitness landscapes.

Finally, the current formulation of AOGWO is designed for continuous optimization problems. Its direct application to discrete or combinatorial optimization tasks, such as job-shop scheduling, feature selection, or vehicle routing, would require suitable binary, discrete, or permutation-based transformation mechanisms. In addition, although the adaptive cosine control and decaying jumping rate reduce the need for manual parameter adjustment, some predefined settings, such as the SLO activation probability, may still influence performance in unfamiliar problem domains. Future studies should therefore investigate adaptive or self-tuning parameter schemes to improve the generalizability of AOGWO across broader optimization scenarios.

Conclusion and future works

This study introduced the AOGWO, an advanced framework designed to systematically address the inherent structural limitations of the standard GWO, specifically its vulnerability to premature convergence and elite stagnation in complex, highly multimodal landscapes. By integrating four complementary methodological enhancements, the proposed framework improves the algorithm’s search dynamics and strengthens the balance between global exploration and local exploitation.

To achieve this superior performance, AOGWO incorporates four distinct core strategies. First, a Hybrid OBL initialization is employed to effectively scatter the initial population, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the search space and establishing a robust foundation that reduce the risk of early clustering in suboptimal regions. Second, the replacement of the standard linear decay parameter with an adaptive cosine control mechanism allows the algorithm to dynamically govern the optimization phases, gracefully prolonging the exploratory phase before triggering a swift, highly concentrated local search.

Third, a Lévy flight perturbation mechanism, operating in tandem with a decaying jumping rate, is introduced to execute sudden, stagnation-breaking leaps, sporting broader exploration of the space. Fourth, the incorporation of a highly targeted SLO mechanism exclusively interrogates the Alpha leader’s coordinates. This ensures continuous refinement of the global best solution, acting as an additional mechanism for reducing elite stagnation that commonly ensnare standard hierarchical algorithms, all while strictly minimizing redundant computational evaluations.

Beyond its foundational design, the internal structural integrity and efficiency of AOGWO were rigorously evaluated. Comprehensive ablation experiments were conducted, successfully isolating and validating the distinct mathematical and functional contributions of each of the four proposed enhancements. Furthermore, three distinct qualitative analyses were presented to provide profound insights into the algorithm’s internal mechanics. These analyses visually and empirically confirmed AOGWO’s capability to maintain high spatial diversity, seamlessly transition search phases, and establish highly stable convergence trajectories. Crucially, detailed computational complexity assessments demonstrated that the integration of these sophisticated mechanisms yields notable performance improvements without imposing excessive processing overhead, thereby verifying AOGWO’s practical viability for resource-intensive applications.

The optimization capabilities of AOGWO were extensively validated against a prominent twelve state-of-the-art MHAs across a diverse array of 41 benchmark functions from the challenging IEEE CEC2017 and CEC2022 suites. The empirical outcomes confirmed that AOGWO achieved the highest number of #BMF across both suites. It demonstrated exceptional proficiency in navigating highly deceptive hybrid and composition landscapes, outperforming competitors in both accuracy and convergence speed. These findings were conclusively substantiated by rigorous nonparametric statistical evaluations, where AOGWO secured the first rank in the Friedman test and exhibited statistically significant favorable results in the Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as well as multiple-comparison post hoc procedures (Bonferroni-Dunn, Holm, and Hochberg).

Furthermore, the practical robustness of the algorithm was affirmed through its application to seven complex real-world engineering design problems, including the Spring Design, Three-Bar Truss, Gear Train Design, Cantilever Beam Design, Pressure Vessel Design, Speed Reducer Design, and Rolling Element Bearing Design. Utilizing a death penalty constraint-handling method, AOGWO consistently generated competitive feasible solutions, demonstrating its capacity to efficiently traverse narrow constraint boundaries and aggressively exploit feasible optima in real-world scenarios.

While the proposed AOGWO demonstrates strong continuous optimization capability, several promising avenues for future research remain. First, the current framework operates strictly within continuous search spaces. Future studies should adapt the AOGWO architecture, specifically via binary or discrete transformation functions, to tackle discrete combinatorial problems, such as feature selection in machine learning, industrial scheduling, and routing optimization. Second, the algorithm could be expanded into a Multi-Objective AOGWO to concurrently optimize conflicting objectives, employing Pareto dominance principles and external archiving to address complex multi-objective engineering constraints. Additionally, exploring the algorithm’s potential in highly dynamic or uncertain environments, where the objective landscape changes over time, would further validate its adaptive resilience. Finally, integrating AOGWO as a robust weight-tuning mechanism for deep neural networks or as a hyperparameter optimization engine in complex predictive modelling presents a highly valuable and impactful direction for practical artificial intelligence applications.

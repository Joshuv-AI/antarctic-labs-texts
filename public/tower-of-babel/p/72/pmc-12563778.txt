Uncommon Presentation of Vitamin B12 Deficiency as Hemolytic Anemia
Authors: Santosh Govind Rathod, Raksha Thakur
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

A 20-year-old man presented with chief complaints of generalized weakness, loss of appetite, and difficulty in breathing for the last two months. On evaluation, the results of whole blood counts were as follows: white blood cell counts 4 ×103/μ, hemoglobin 4.6 g/dL, mean corpuscular volume 106 fL, red cell distribution 24.6%, hematocrit 24%, and platelet count 150 × 103/μL. The patient had pallor, no lymphadenopathy, or organomegaly on physical examination. Further evaluation revealed a total bilirubin of 2.4 mg/dL, indirect bilirubin of 2.1 mg/dL, lactate dehydrogenase 4257 U/L, and haptoglobin of <8 mg/dL. His Coombs test was negative, and the reticulocyte index on presentation was 12.1%. Peripheral blood smear showed hyper-segmented neutrophils with polychromatic cells. Iron studies showed ferritin 233 ng/mL (iron 182 μg/dL (regular 65–175), iron-binding capacity 215 μg/dL, and transferrin % saturation of 45% (regular 20–55). Folate was 11.33 ng/mL (normal >5.4), and his vitamin B12 was very low at 47 pg/mL (regular 211–911). To further evaluate the cause of hemolysis, we did a paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) profile and found a negative for PNH. In the present case, G6PD deficiency results and upper and lower gut endoscopy were within normal limits. Intrinsic factor and anti-parietal cell antibodies were negative. A diagnosis of megaloblastic anemia was made, and the patient was treated with methylcarbylamine 1000 ug for seven days and then weekly. After four weeks of treatment, the patient responded with normalization of the whole blood count.

Vitamin B12 acts as a cofactor for methionine synthase, which converts homocysteine to methionine, and at the same, it is also required for the conversion of 5-methyl tetrahydrofolate to tetrahydrofolate is crucial for DNA synthesis.[12] In Vitamin B12 deficiency, nuclear maturation lags behind cytoplasmic maturation.[34] The mechanism behind hemolysis in megaloblastic anemia is ineffective erythropoiesis due to defective DNA and cell maturation and intramedullary hemolysis. When a patient presents anemia with a hemolytic picture, one must keep a differential diagnosis of megaloblastic anemia.

Updated Viloxazine Pharmacology: Experiments Establish Norepinephrine Transporter Occupancy and Serotonin 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 Receptor Binding at Therapeutically Relevant Concentrations
Authors: Jennie Garcia-Olivares, Brittney Yegla, Jennifer Koch, Chungping Yu, Jonathan Rubin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesViloxazine, which has been used to treat depression and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), has been termed a moderate-affinity, selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor based on high selectivity for norepinephrine relative to serotonin and dopamine transporters. However, accumulated research suggests a more complex mechanism of action, based on studies showing activity at serotonin 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 receptors, as well as findings that viloxazine increases extracellular serotonin (along with norepinephrine and dopamine) in the rat prefrontal cortex. This in vitro pharmacology study aimed to replicate and expand prior experiments to better characterize viloxazine’s affinity for and activity at the norepinephrine transporter (NET) and individual serotonin receptors and to clarify how these effects contribute to the mechanism of action.MethodsUsing in vitro binding competition and functional assays and ex vivo receptor occupancy studies in rats, we assessed viloxazine activity at human NET isoforms and 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 receptors relative to clinically relevant unbound viloxazine plasma concentrations (0.4–3.6 μM).ResultsViloxazine showed moderate binding affinity for NET (inhibition constant [Ki] = 0.13 µM) and 5-HT2C (Ki = 0.66 µM), 5-HT2B (Ki = 0.83 µM), and 5-HT7 (Ki = 1.90 µM) receptors. In vitro functional studies showed viloxazine acted as a NET inhibitor, 5-HT2C partial agonist, and 5-HT2B and 5-HT7 antagonist. At clinically relevant concentrations, viloxazine could potentially occupy nearly 95% of NET, more than 80% of 5-HT2C and 5-HT2B, and 65% of 5-HT7 receptors. Subsequent ex vivo studies in rats confirmed high NET occupancy (67–94%) at clinically relevant concentrations.ConclusionsThese results validate previous experiments showing that viloxazine, in addition to displaying high NET occupancy, acts as a partial agonist at 5-HT2C receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2B and 5-HT7 receptors at clinically relevant concentrations for ADHD treatment. Therefore, both NET inhibition and serotonin receptor activity may contribute to viloxazine’s clinical efficacy. These findings are contributing to a renewed understanding of viloxazine’s pharmacodynamic profile and likely multimodal mechanism of action.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40268-026-00543-y.

Key Points

Viloxazine has multimodal effects, acting as a moderate affinity norepinephrine transporter inhibitor, a serotonin receptor 5-HT2C partial agonist, and a serotonin receptor 5-HT2B and 5-HT7 antagonist at clinically relevant concentrations.Our findings suggest that describing viloxazine’s mechanism of action as a “selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor” is incomplete; a more accurate description would be multimodal, with activity as a norepinephrine transporter inhibitor and a serotonin receptor modulator.

Introduction

Viloxazine was synthesized in the 1970s during the search for novel second-generation antidepressants, with the goal of creating a potent β-adrenergic receptor antagonist that would elicit antidepressant effects similar to those of tricyclic antidepressants and monoamine oxidase inhibitors [1, 2]. Viloxazine was verified to have antidepressant effects and approved in Europe to treat depression; however, early pharmacology studies showed poor affinity for β-adrenergic receptors in the central nervous system but increased norepinephrine (NE) in muscle tissue [2–4]. Despite multiple early pharmacological studies which showed that viloxazine affects serotonin (5-HT) neurotransmission (including its inhibition of the NE transporter [NET]) [3–7], the mechanism(s) underlying its modulation of serotonin, as well as therapeutic relevance of these effects, have not been well characterized or are omitted from mention. Viloxazine is often described as a selective NE reuptake inhibitor (NRI) only based on its ability to inhibit NET with little-to-no inhibition of serotonin and dopamine (DA) transporters (DAT).

An extended-release formulation of viloxazine (viloxazine ER capsules; Qelbree®; Supernus Inc.) is now approved in the United States for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) treatment in children and adults. However, viloxazine’s relatively low potency on NET inhibition when compared with atomoxetine (the only other selective NET inhibitor approved for ADHD [8]; inhibition constants (Ki) = 2300 nM vs 3.4 nM, respectively [9, 10]) left an unanswered question as to whether viloxazine’s pharmacological effects in the treatment of ADHD and other psychiatric symptoms are solely mediated by its activity as a NET inhibitor.

Modern studies confirmed that viloxazine has competitive binding and functional activity at NET and serotonin receptors (5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7) [9]. Specifically, viloxazine may act as a partial agonist at 5-HT2C and an antagonist at 5-HT2B and 5-HT7 receptors [9]. Additionally, viloxazine increased extracellular levels of NE, DA, and 5-HT in the rat prefrontal cortex during in vivo microdialysis studies [11]. The observed NE and DA elevations are consistent with prefrontal NET inhibition; however, 5-HT elevations cannot be attributed to serotonin transporter (SERT) inhibition, as viloxazine did not reduce 5-HIAA metabolite concentrations in this experiment nor has it shown clinically relevant functional activity at SERT (half-maximal inhibitory concentration [IC50] = 257 µM) [9]. Though uncertain, serotonin elevation may result from viloxazine’s action at serotonin receptors (i.e., 5-HT2B agonism and/or 5-HT7 antagonism) [9, 11, 12]. To achieve greater certainty of viloxazine’s effects in the brain at therapeutic concentrations, we conducted a series of experiments, using contemporary methodology, to validate and expand on prior findings.

We used in vitro binding competition assays to confirm targets of viloxazine binding and in vitro functional assays to further characterize viloxazine’s inhibition of NET and agonistic or antagonistic action at individual 5-HT receptors. We also performed an ex vivo target occupancy study in the rat brain to calculate NET occupancy at therapeutic concentrations. Together with results from existing studies, these experiments characterize the multimodal pharmacology of viloxazine on NET and 5-HT receptors and contribute to a re-evaluation of viloxazine effects.

Methods

Drugs

Viloxazine hydrochloride was supplied by Supernus Pharmaceuticals, Inc., Rockville, Maryland, United States; compound ID 100047426-1.

In Vitro Assays

All assays were performed in stably transfected cloned human complementary DNA sequences following standard operation procedures by Eurofins Cerep (Celle l'Evescault, France) and Eurofins DiscoverX (Freemont, California, United States). A heterologous expression system of human isoforms was used: NET, 5-HT2B, 5-HT7, and SERT were expressed in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, and 5-HT2C and 5-HT2A were expressed in human embryonic kidney (HEK)−293 cells. Data were analyzed using software developed by Eurofins and SigmaPlot v14.0. Additional details are provided in the Supplementary Methods, see electronic supplementary material (ESM).

In Vitro Binding Competition Assays

The binding of viloxazine to human isoforms of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs: 5-HT2A [evaluated due to high homology between 5-HT2 receptors], 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, and 5-HT7) and monoamine transporters (NET, SERT) was evaluated in competition binding assays using the specific radioligands listed in Table 1. When available, ligands for agonist and antagonist sites were used. Respective reference compounds were evaluated concurrently with test compounds when applicable. Viloxazine competition with specific ligands was expressed as percent inhibition of binding of the radiolabeled ligand specific for each target. The IC50 values and Hill coefficients (nH) were determined by nonlinear regression analysis, and inhibition constants (Ki) were calculated using the standard Cheng-Prusoff equation [13].

In Vitro Functional Assays

Several functional assays, conducted in different cellular systems and using multiple methods of detection, were performed to evaluate the effects of viloxazine on neurotransmitter uptake and GPCR signaling. These assays included radiolabeled-neurotransmitter cellular uptake, calcium (Ca2+) mobilization, cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP), β-arrestin, and internalization assays. The effect of viloxazine on inhibition of [3H]NE uptake in CHO cells expressing the human isoform of NET was measured using 0.2 μCi/mL [3H]NE incubated for 20 min at 37 °C. The amount of [3H]NE incorporated into the cells was measured by scintillation counting. The effect of viloxazine on [3H]NE uptake was expressed as percent of inhibition. IC50 values and nH were determined by nonlinear regression analysis. The Eurofins DiscoverX Calcium No WashPLUS fluorescence-based detection assay method (Calcium No WashPLUS) was used to determine whether viloxazine acted as an agonist or antagonist at 5-HT2B and nonedited 5-HT2C(INI) receptors by monitoring downstream Ca2+ release via Gαq activation. The DiscoverX HitHunter cAMP XS+ chemiluminescent assay was used to determine whether viloxazine acted as an agonist or antagonist at 5-HT7 receptors by monitoring downstream release of cAMP via Gαs activation. The Eurofins DiscoverX PathHunter β-Arrestin assay method was used to determine whether viloxazine 5-HT2C receptor agonism activates β-arrestin recruitment. The Eurofins DiscoverX PathHunter GPCR Activated Internalization assay method was used to determine whether viloxazine 5-HT2C receptor agonism activates receptor internalization. Additional assay details are included in the Supplementary Methods (see ESM).

Measurement of Ex Vivo NET Occupancy in the Rat Brain

An ex vivo method was used to evaluate viloxazine dose occupancy at NET in the healthy rat brain using the radioligand [11C]methyl reboxetine ([11C]MRB). The method was developed and performed by Invicro (now Perceptive) at the Imperial College London facility. Animal experimentation was conducted in compliance with institutional and national research guidelines and regulations, including the UK Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 and the transposed EU directive 2010/63/EU. All procedures were approved by the Animal Ethical Review Committee of Imperial College.

The ex vivo NET occupancy study required three rats in order to develop and optimize the high-performance liquid chromatography metabolite analysis method and determine the [11C]MRB volume of distribution using positron emission tomography (PET) and computed tomography (CT) scans. Details and results of these method development experiments are described in the Supplementary Methods, Tables S1–S4, and Figures S1 and S2 (see ESM). The methodology was confirmed to be suitable for the measurement of viloxazine NET occupancy, as [11C]MRB showed high overall occupancy at NET.

Based on methodology and relevant parameters established in the method development experiments, viloxazine dose occupancy was determined at NET using [11C]MRB with ex vivo tissue gamma counting methods. Twenty rats were split into five groups (4 rats/group) and each group was dosed with one of the following treatments: viloxazine 1, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg (dissolved in saline) or vehicle (saline alone) intraperitoneally 45 min before administration of [11C]MRB (15.9 ± 4.5 MBq), given intravenously via tail. Viloxazine dose selection was based on a previously published study that established dose dependency of NE elevation in the rat brain [11]. These doses were shown to provide unbound plasma concentrations in the rat that span the range of those in humans receiving recommended doses of viloxazine ER for ADHD. Blood was drawn for viloxazine plasma concentration analysis 5 min before [11C]MRB administration. Rats were euthanized 40 min after [11C]MRB administration, and blood and brain tissues from the cortex, thalamus, striatum, brain stem, and cerebellum were collected for determination of radioactivity.

Results

In Vitro Binding Competition Assays

Calculated Ki values confirmed that viloxazine had moderate affinity towards NET (Ki = 0.13 µM) and very weak binding to SERT (Ki = 14.00 µM; Table 1), aligning with previously reported affinity values (expressed as KD; Fig. 1a) [16]. With respect to the tested 5-HT receptors (Fig. 1b), viloxazine demonstrated moderate affinity for the 5-HT2C receptor (INI-human nonedited isoform) agonist site ([125I](±)DOI; Ki = 0.66 µM) that was 2-fold higher than toward the antagonist site ([3H]mesulergine; Ki = 1.50 µM). Viloxazine also showed moderate affinity for the 5-HT2B receptor antagonist site, competing with [3H]mesulergine (Ki = 0.83 µM) and for the 5-HT7 receptor agonist site (Ki of 1.90 µM with [3H]LSD). Conversely, viloxazine had low affinity for the 5-HT2A receptor agonist and antagonist sites, competing with the agonist [125I](±)DOI and the antagonist [3H]ketanserin with Ki of 6.70 and 16.00 µM, respectively.

Together, these results provide the following ranking of viloxazine binding affinity: NET > 5-HT2C ≈ 5-HT2B > 5-HT7 >> 5-HT2A >>> SERT. Affinity ratios relative to NET ranged from 5- to 14-fold for 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 receptors (Table 1). Viloxazine’s binding to 5-HT2A was > 50-fold lower than to NET, and its binding to SERT was 100-fold lower than to NET.

In Vitro Functional Assays

Viloxazine Uptake Inhibition at NET and SERT Monoamine Transporters

A [3H]NE uptake assay evaluating the inhibitory effect of viloxazine on the human isoform of NET expressed in CHO cells showed high inhibition of NET (IC50 = 0.370 µM; Table 2), which is consistent with a previously reported IC50 using a rat hypothalamic synaptosome preparation (0.26 µM) [9]. Because the binding experiments described above suggested negligible SERT binding and prior experiments showed no appreciable ability for viloxazine to inhibit SERT at concentrations seen following clinically used doses (IC50 of 257 µM for [3H]5-HT uptake in HEK-293 cells expressing the human isoform of SERT [9]), we considered further experiments to measure SERT inhibition to be unnecessary.

Functional Activity of Viloxazine at 5-HT Receptors

Activated 5-HT2 receptor subtypes are classically described as preferentially coupling to Gαq/Gα11 proteins, increasing hydrolysis of inositol phosphates (IP) and releasing Ca2+ from intracellular stores, leading to further downstream signaling. We previously reported that viloxazine showed submaximal agonistic activity at 5-HT2C receptors and antagonism to 5-HT2B receptors with relatively weak potencies using an IP1 assay [9]. These new experiments used a nonimaging Ca2+ mobilization method to determine the agonist or antagonist activity of viloxazine for the nonedited human 5-HT2C or 5-HT2B receptors, respectively. Viloxazine showed agonism at 5-HT2C (EC50 = 1.6 µM) but displayed submaximal efficacy (Emax = 89.2%; Fig. 2). Viloxazine displayed weak antagonism at 5-HT2B (IC50 = 36.4 µM). To evaluate potential for biased agonism of the activity on 5-HT2C receptors, other potential signaling pathways in addition to IP1 were tested. Viloxazine did not recruit β-arrestin or promote receptor internalization (EC50 > 150 µM), indicating that viloxazine preferentially activates IP1 canonical G-protein pathways at 5-HT2C. Canonical G-protein signaling was also tested at the 5-HT7 receptor, which is known to preferentially couple Gαs proteins upon activation, increasing cAMP production and leading to further downstream signaling. In the cAMP assay, viloxazine acted as an antagonist at 5-HT7 (IC50 = 27.2 µM).

Predicted Target Occupancy at Clinically Relevant Viloxazine Concentrations

As previously reported by Garcia-Olivares et al. [11], the estimated clinically relevant concentration of viloxazineunbound was obtained from the average plasma concentration (Cave) of viloxazine determined using population pharmacokinetic models [17]. The clinically relevant viloxazineunbound concentrations are estimated to be in a range from 0.4 µM (the lower CI of the Cave for the 100-mg/day dose) to 3.6 µM (the higher CI for the 400-mg/day dose in children); plasma concentrations at approved effective doses for ADHD in adolescents and adults also fall within this range.

Based on the experimentally estimated Ki values for NET, 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, 5-HT7 and SERT, we calculated the potential target occupancy at different viloxazineunbound plasma concentrations. The selected doses of viloxazine, based on clinically relevant concentrations of viloxazineunbound from prior studies [11], yielded a dose-dependent occupancy curve in which viloxazine plasma concentration increased with each dose and displaced [11C]MRB (viloxazineunbound concentration of 0.121 ± 0.015, 0.308 ± 0.053, 0.953 ± 0.1937, 3.059 ± 0.64 µM for viloxazine 1, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg, respectively; Fig. S3, see ESM).The calculated target occupancies predict that viloxazine could occupy > 80% of NET at clinically relevant concentrations of unbound viloxazine (0.4–3.6 µM), with no appreciable occupancy of SERT (Fig. 3). Occupancy of viloxazine could range from 40% to 85% at 5-HT2C and 5-HT2B receptors and 20% to 66% at 5-HT7 receptors; however, based on the weaker Ki value obtained for 5-HT2A (6.7 µM), its potential occupancy at 5-HT2A is < 40% for the higher end of the concentration range. Potential target occupancy of viloxazine generally increased over the clinically relevant plasma range (0.4–3.6 µM), with > 80% potential target occupancy at NET, 5-HT2C, and 5-HT2B at higher therapeutic plasma concentrations (Table S5, see ESM).

Discussion

The data presented confirm that viloxazine has multimodal pharmacologic effects as a moderate NET inhibitor and serotonin receptor modulator with activity at 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 receptors. Originally developed and used as an antidepressant, viloxazine has been compared with other antidepressants for its pharmacological properties associated with NE functions [3]. Like other antidepressants that inhibit NE reuptake, viloxazine protected mice against reserpine-induced hypothermia, transiently blocked [3H]NE uptake into the mouse heart in vivo, and potentiated NE-induced contractions of the cat nictitating membrane [3, 18]. However, in the initial pharmacological characterization, viloxazine presented significant in vivo pharmacological differences to other known monoamine modulators (such as d-amphetamine and monoaminoxidase inhibitors, which are motor stimulants and increase body temperature) by reducing locomotor activity and body temperature in mice, without gross impairment in coordination or hypnosis [3]. Viloxazine was also found to elicit a transitory increase in NE, DA, and 5-HT in the rat brain (different from effects of tricyclic antidepressants [3] or atomoxetine, which does not increase 5-HT in the prefrontal cortex [19]) with no evidence of blocking 5-HT reuptake in platelets or monoamine oxidase inhibition [2, 3]. Additionally, our recent in vivo microdialysis study in freely moving Sprague Dawley rats [11] confirmed these neurotransmitter increases in the prefrontal cortex, showing that viloxazine potentiates adrenergic and serotonergic functions via mechanisms other than SERT inhibition [3–5, 18, 20]. The data presented support the multimodal pharmacology of viloxazine as acting on 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 receptors, in addition to its previously described NET inhibition. The activity of viloxazine at these receptors expands our understanding of its potential mechanism of action in vivo, beyond known effects as a moderate NET inhibitor [4, 21].

Viloxazine is a Moderate NET Inhibitor Without Meaningful Affinity for SERT or the DAT

The in vitro binding competition and uptake assays were performed to confirm previously reported binding affinities of viloxazine at monoamine transporters [16]. In agreement with previously reported affinity values (KD), we found that viloxazine has submicromolar in vitro binding affinity for NET, but is remarkably selective towards NET (100-fold) when compared with SERT (Ki = 0.13 µM and Ki = 14.40 µM, respectively) [16]. Prior studies showed that viloxazine is practically devoid of binding to DAT [16] and does not affect DA uptake in striatal regions, even at 100 µM [16, 20, 22], so we did not determine affinity or potency for DAT in this study. Reuptake assays demonstrated that viloxazine was able to inhibit human NET with moderate potency (IC50 = 0.37 µM), consistent with our previous finding of an IC50 = 0.26 µM using rat synaptosomes [9]. These findings are also consistent with the aforementioned microdialysis study by our group [11], showing that clinically relevant doses of viloxazine (30 mg/kg, administered intraperitoneally) increased extracellular NE and DA in the prefrontal cortex, while significantly reducing the dihydroxyphenylglycol metabolite of NE; this pattern is consistent with NET inhibition, which is expected to also increase extracellular DA concentrations in the prefrontal cortex due to the role of NET in DA reuptake [23]. In a similar microdialysis study, a higher viloxazine dose (50 mg/kg, administered intraperitoneally) showed no significant increase in DA in the nucleus accumbens, a region with high levels of DAT expression but low levels of NET expression, also supporting its low risk for abuse or misuse [9]. The current study adds to the literature confirming that viloxazine is a moderate affinity NET inhibitor that is highly selective for NET relative to other monoamine transporters, such as SERT and DAT.

5-HT Receptor Subtypes 2 C, 2B, and 7 Are Viloxazine Targets

The in vitro binding competition assays found the following ranking of viloxazine binding affinity: 5-HT2C ≈ 5-HT2B > 5-HT7. Compared with these receptor sites, viloxazine showed a 10- to 24-fold weaker binding affinity for the agonist and antagonist sites of 5-HT2A (Ki = 6.70 µM and Ki = 16.00 µM, respectively). Based on these Ki values, our predictive target occupancy plots estimated viloxazine could occupy 38–85% of 5-HT2C and 5-HT2B receptors and 17–66% of 5-HT7 receptors at typical unbound plasma concentrations (Cave, 0.4–3.6 µM) seen with viloxazine doses used to treat ADHD.

Functional assays gave further insight into the agonist and antagonist actions of viloxazine at these 5-HT receptors. In a Ca2+ assay for 5-HT2C receptors, viloxazine agonist actions increased intracellular Ca2+ with an EC50 of 1.6 µM; however, its submaximal efficiency (Emax < 90%) in this assay (which was similar to the submaximal response [Emax = 78%] observed in a prior IP1 assay, where the EC50 was 32.0 µM [9]) suggests that viloxazine acts as a partial 5-HT2C agonist. No effects on β-arrestin recruitment or active internalization were observed, indicating that viloxazine exhibits functional selectivity towards canonical G protein pathways after activation of 5-HT2C. Antagonism at the 5-HT2B and 5-HT7 receptors was also identified in Ca2+ release and cAMP assays, respectively. For 5-HT2B, the weak potency as an antagonist (IC50 = 36.4 µM) was comparable with a previous report using an IP1 assay (IC50 = 27.0 µM) [9]. Similarly, antagonism to 5-HT7 resulted in weak potency in the cAMP assay (IC50 = 27.0 µM), which aligned with previous findings of cAMP accumulation at 100 µM (52% inhibition) [9]. Although not assessed in this study, viloxazine has shown no agonistic or antagonistic effect up to 300 µM in cells expressing 5-HT2A in an IP1 assay [9]. As determined in human studies, there are no viloxazine metabolites that meaningfully contribute to its pharmacological effects [24]; therefore, metabolites were not evaluated in this study.

The therapeutic relevance of viloxazine’s effects on serotonin receptors is not completely understood; however, 5-HT2C, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT7 receptors exert regulatory influence on DA and serotonin activity in the brain that may contribute to efficacy [25]. Specifically, research suggests that 5-HT2C partial agonism could contribute to observed reductions in impulsivity, and 5-HT2C agonists have shown promise in conditions associated with impaired impulse control that are common in ADHD, such as overeating, obesity, and substance use disorders [26–29]. Interestingly, a recent post hoc analysis of the adult phase III trial [30] suggested viloxazine may contribute to weight loss in adults with ADHD, with greater effects among participants with high baseline body mass index [31]. Modulation of 5-HT2C signaling may also play a role in treatment of depression [27, 28]. A potential therapeutic role for 5-HT7 antagonists has been suggested in the treatment of depression, anxiety, sleep, and cognitive disorders [32, 33], which are also commonly associated with ADHD, and 5-HT2B antagonists have suggested benefits in substance use disorders, schizophrenia, and depression [34, 35]. Antagonism of 5-HT2B receptors was also found to augment DA and 5-HT prefrontally in animal studies [34, 35], perhaps explaining the 5-HT elevation in our previously described microdialysis experiments.

Viloxazine Occupancy of NET at Clinically Relevant Concentrations

Given the affinity difference towards NET between atomoxetine and viloxazine (Ki = 3.4 nM [10] and Ki = 130 nM, respectively), another question addressed by our experiments was whether NET occupancy for viloxazine could reach the > 80% threshold thought to be required for therapeutic effects [15]. Our initial calculations (based on Ki = 0.13 µM) estimated that viloxazine could occupy close to 75% of NET at the lower end of the unbound plasma concentration range (100-mg dose) and 96% at the high end of the range (400-mg dose), which was confirmed by our ex vivo experiments presented here. The greater NET inhibitory effect with higher viloxazine doses could be consistent with the somewhat higher response rates seen with doses above 100 mg in short-term pediatric studies [36, 37].

These levels of occupancy are comparable to experimental estimates for atomoxetine and methylphenidate. Similar PET studies using [11C]MRB in rhesus monkeys have estimated that atomoxetine occupies > 90% of NET at clinical concentrations (1.0–1.8 mg/kg) [38, 39], and a study in human patients estimated that methylphenidate occupies 70–80% of NET at average recommended maintenance doses (0.7–1.1 mg/kg/day) [40]. The NET occupancy results are consistent with NET inhibition playing a central role in viloxazine mechanism of action in ADHD symptoms by increasing NE and DA levels in brain regions such as the prefrontal cortex [11]. Our results suggest that viloxazine sufficiently engages NET at recommended therapeutic doses, which, in combination with serotonergic activity, may orchestrate the therapeutic effects.

Limitations

The in vitro and ex vivo methodologies used in this study do not exactly replicate viloxazine action at NET and 5-HT receptors in the human brain, and extrapolation of plasma concentrations in rats to human brain concentrations may be inexact. Therefore, conclusions drawn from comparisons to clinically relevant human plasma concentration ranges are approximate. The contribution of the serotonergic component of viloxazine’s mechanism of action, specifically effects of 5-HT2C partial agonism on attention and impulsivity, is being evaluated in a parallel study and will be reported separately. Future in vivo investigations of viloxazine activity on 5-HT receptors, such as PET studies in humans, could further inform our understanding of target occupancy at clinically relevant doses.

Conclusions

Accurate description of drug pharmacology is important in assessing product efficacy, safety, and therapeutic choice. At plasma concentrations clinically relevant to the treatment of ADHD, viloxazine has the potential to act as a NET inhibitor and 5-HT receptor modulator, suggesting a multimodal mechanism of action that may be best described as a moderate NRI with direct 5-HT receptor activity. This pharmacological profile distinguishes viloxazine from other ADHD medications, such as stimulants that have activities towards DAT in combination with NET and/or SERT or nonstimulants (including atomoxetine, an NRI that is not known to affect 5-HT receptors in this way [16, 41, 42]). Other compounds in development for ADHD (e.g., centanafadine [43], solriamfetol [44]) also have primary activity towards monoamine transporters.

The data presented here raise several new questions about the role of 5-HT receptor engagement in the therapeutic effects of viloxazine in ADHD as well as the overall role of 5-HT modulation in ADHD. From a neurochemical and neurocircuitry perspective, additional studies are needed to explore the role of 5-HT (including 5-HT2C agonism or partial agonism and 5-HT2B and 5-HT7 antagonism) in the context of ADHD and other psychiatric conditions and understand how modulation of these 5-HT receptors may confer therapeutic benefits in ADHD.

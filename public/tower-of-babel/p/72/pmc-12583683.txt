Machine learning-enabled acoustic sensing for RPW infestation detection
Authors: S. Saranya, Betty Martin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Red Palm Weevil(RPW) infestation is a major challenge in palm production, often remaining undetected until severe internal damage has occurred. This proposed work presents a novel non-invasive auditory detection system that combines advanced signal processing with deep learning for the early identification of RPW. Acoustic signals from palm trees were pre-processed to suppress noise, and Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) was used to extract spectral features specific to RPW activity. To refine feature matching, cosine similarity was applied, followed by classification using a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) network. The proposed approach achieved an accuracy of 98.02%, outperforming traditional detection techniques. The novelty of this work lies in integrating LPC based features, cosine similarity, and Bi-LSTM for temporal pattern recognition, enabling highly reliable early detection. A Bi-LSTM RPW classifier combining LPC and cosine similarity, improving accuracy over conventional shallow models was developed. The robust RPW detection system provides accurate outputs despite noise, interference and signal distortion. A limitation of this work is that the evaluation was performed on a controlled dataset, and large scale field validation is required for wider applicability.

Introduction

The red palm weevil, Rhynchophorus ferrugineus, is a worldwide pest that generally occurs in tropical and subtropical climatic regions. Summarizing the existing work in detection of RPW the researchers have already studied a wide range of methods, which include pheromone traps, visual inspection, thermal imaging, and acoustic monitoring. Although these techniques have helped with early pest control, yet they have a lot of drawbacks. However promising acoustic approaches often rely on handcrafted features or shallow learning models, which are unable to capture temporal dynamics of RPW activity, and are frequently impacted by background noise. These difficulties bring attention to a critical area of research and the requirement for a reliable, non- invasive, and precise method that can identify RPW infestations early in the real world. In order to address this aspect, the current work presents a novel framework that enhances temporal pattern identification and detection reliability by combining linear predictive coding (LPC) with cosine similarity and Bi-LSTM.

Model architecture

The architecture of the proposed Red Palm Weevil (RPW) detection system has been designed to integrate the deep learning with signal processing in an end to end pipeline. The Fig. 1 shows the work flow of four major stages: Data Acquisition, Pre-processing, Feature extraction, Matching and Classification. Its designed to ensure accurate, non-invasive, and scalable pest detection in real agricultural environments. In the data acquisition stage, acoustic signals are recorded from palm trees using sensitive microphones capable of capturing of larval movement and chewing sounds. In pre-processing stage involves noise suppression and amplitude normalization to reduce the impact of background interference such as wind, insect sounds or human activity. Next in the feature extraction stage, Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) is employed to extract spectral features that represent the unique acoustic signals of RPW activity. The coefficients capture the spectral envelope of the audio signal and serve as compact representations of its distinguishing features. To improve the robustness, a feature matching step using cosine similarity is included, allowing the system to quantify the similarity between test signals and reference RPW or Non RPW signals. In classification stage utilizes a Bidirectional Long Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) networks. It processes the signal both forward and backward to capture temporal dependencies, leading to accurate discrimination between RPW and Non RPW activity.

Feature extraction using linear predictive coding (LPC)

In this stage, the Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) algorithm is used to extract key features from the audio data. LPC is a widely used technique in signal processing for representing the spectral envelope of a speech signal in a compact form(Abdel Farag El-Shafie H et al., 2020). Each sample is predicted by LPC as a linear mixture of the preceding samples. (Kostina, A.A et al., 2020) thereby modelling the spectral envelope of a signal. It is represented by the prediction filter’s coefficient. Establishing the required parameters, such as the LPC order, hop size, and frame length, was the first stage in extracting LPC features from an audio input. These factors dictated the ongoing segmentation and analysis of the signal.LPC order typically has values between 10 and 20, depending on the desired level of modelling accuracy and the properties of the signal under analysis4.

LPC captures the spectral envelope, which represent the unique acoustic patterns of RPW infestation. Results and discussion

The results were compared with the work of (Sayed, G.I. et al. 2019), who used a very limited dataset to develop a machine learning-based detection method for RPW in order to validate the performance of the suggested LPC-based Bi-LSTM model12. Although their model was promising, it struggled with over-fitting and poor generalization, which resulted in decreased accuracy and uneven performance on bigger or unknown datasets. The research work used a bigger and more varied dataset with 3000 RPW and Non-RPW samples, and it obtained a classification accuracy of 98.02%. Even in acoustically loud environments, robust identification was made possible by the combination of LPC feature extraction, cosine similarity-based feature matching, and a Bi-LSTM architecture.

Conculsion

The proposed work is a non-invasive acoustic detection system for Red Palm Weevil (RPW) infestation using Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) for feature extraction, cosine similarity for feature matching and a Bi-LSTM deep learning model for classification. The system achieved 98.02% accuracy, surpassing traditional RPW detection approaches that face challenges such as limited dataset, high computational cost and over-fitting. These results demonstrate that the model not only outperforms conventional detection techniques but also offers a reliable and scalable solution for monitoring agricultural pests in the real world. Notably, the results demonstrate that acoustic based machine learning techniques can help with sustainable pest control, lower financial losses, and facilitate prompt intervention. Beyond technical accuracy, this approach contributes to reducing crop losses, enabling timely interventions, and supporting sustainable pest management practices. While the results are promising, the proposed work is limited to controlled testing conditions. Therefore, large scale field validation across diverse environmental scenarios will be a critical future step to ensure robustness, adaptability, and real world applicability of the proposed system. In comparison with existing MobileNetV2 Bi LSTM offers reliable temporal modeling at greater computational cost Future research will optimize model efficiency and validate the frame work in real world field condition.Although the proposed RPW acoustic detection system achieved high accuracy, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The approach depends on the availability of high-quality recordings, and background noise in field conditions may affect signal clarity. While the dataset used was sufficient for model development, larger and more diverse data are needed to ensure broader generalizability. Future work will focus on noise robust feature extraction, extensive field scale validation, and integration with IoT based platforms to enhance real-time applicability and scalability.

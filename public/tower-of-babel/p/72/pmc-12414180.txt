LDL-C achievement in patients with coronary artery disease: a study protocol for the EDHIPO-MARCA retrospective registry
Authors: Brayan Daniel Cordoba-Melo, Juan Pablo Arango-Ibanez, Santiago Posso-Marín, Álvaro J Ruiz, Dora Inés Molina, Juan Esteban Gomez-Mesa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionElevated lipid profiles increase the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), a leading cause of mortality worldwide. Despite the availability of lipid-lowering therapy (LLT), adherence to therapy and achievement of Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C) target levels remain suboptimal. Coronary artery disease (CAD) presents substantial public health challenges, with LDL-C goal attainment rates reported to be between 30.0% and 54.0%. The EDHIPO MARCA (Evaluación De adherencia a la terapia HIPOlipemiante en pacientes de Muy Alto Riesgo CArdiovascular) study aims to evaluate LDL-C target achievement among Colombian patients with CAD.Methods and analysisThis is a retrospective and multicentre study aiming to evaluate LDL-C target achievement within 12 months of coronary angiography across multiple Colombian institutions. Data will be retrospectively extracted from medical records corresponding to the years 2011, 2012, 2016, 2017, 2021 and 2022, which were selected to correspond with the European Society of Cardiology/European Atherosclerosis Society guideline updates. Inclusion criteria included patients ≥18 years old with confirmed CAD and LDL-C reports recorded during outpatient follow-up. The study will evaluate a minimum sample size of 5000 patients, with data collected through medical records and managed using the REDCap platform. Statistical analyses will be conducted to assess LDL-C target achievement, associated factors and temporal trends using mixed-effects models. Uncertainty will also be explored through sensitivity analysis. The EDHIPO MARCA study will provide key insights into LDL-C target achievement in Colombia, contributing to both regional and global CAD management. Its findings will be used to help shape public health policies and serve as a foundation for future prospective research and interventions aimed at mitigating the burden of cardiovascular disease.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Comité de Ética en Investigación Biomédica of Fundación Valle del Lili, the coordinating institution and creator of the study protocol. Each participating centre will obtain approval from its local ethics committee prior to data collection. Data will be collected in a de-identified manner, ensuring confidentiality. In accordance with Colombian Resolution 8430, this study is classified as 'no-risk', and informed consent was not required. The findings will be disseminated through scientific events and published in international peer-reviewed journals to contribute to cardiovascular disease management and public health policies.Keywords: Lipid disorders, Protocols & guidelines, Coronary heart disease

Introduction

It is well-established that elevated lipid profiles significantly increase the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), making it one of the most important modifiable risk factors. Lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) remains a crucial component in reducing the incidence of ASCVD, such as coronary artery disease (CAD) and stroke in patients at high risk through the reduction of LDL-C and inflammation.1 2 These conditions hold global significance as they rank among the primary causes of mortality worldwide.3 ASCVD is the leading cause of death in Colombia, with a high prevalence of CAD, recorded at 96.57 cases per 100 000 individuals.4 CAD is caused by progressive buildup of atherosclerotic plaque in the coronary arteries and is the leading cause of myocardial infarction.5 Despite the high prevalence and associated mortality of CAD, adherence to therapy and achievement of LDL-C target levels remain suboptimal among affected patients. Large studies have found LDL-C target achievement rate (TAR) below 30.0% in patients requiring secondary prevention,6 and another large-scale study revealed that up to 50.0% of patients with atherosclerosis are not prescribed statin therapy, demonstrating the depth of the problem.7

Certain non-modifiable factors are associated with unfavourable outcomes in achieving LDL-C goals among patients with ASCVD; these include female sex, African ancestry, adverse effects of statins, higher baseline LDL-C levels on admission for percutaneous intervention and younger age. In contrast, modifiable factors influencing goal attainment include medication adherence, suboptimal dosing and dietary factors, such as fish intake, among other factors.8,10 Conducting detailed regional analyses is crucial for developing strategies to address these challenges. Such studies can help identify the magnitude of the problem, identify the distribution of such factors in the region and discover novel ones.

Given the global burden of cardiovascular disease, the low prevalence of optimal LDL-C TAR and the underuse of LLT in secondary prevention, further research is crucial to analyse prescription patterns, adherence and treatment target attainment in under-represented regions, such as Colombia. To address this issue, we designed the Evaluación De adherencia a la terapia HIPOlipemiante en pacientes de Muy Alto Riesgo CArdiovascular (EDHIPO MARCA) study to provide a comprehensive perspective on this problem in Colombian patients with CAD (figure 1). We aim to use our findings to assess the extent of the problem in Colombia and to inform the development of public health policies that address its root causes.

Methods and analysis

Study design and participants

EDHIPO MARCA is a planned retrospective to assess LDL-C TAR and adherence to LLT in patients with established CAD. We will analyse coronary angiogram reports and medical records from the years 2011–2012, 2016–2017 and 2021–2022 from different medical institutions over the country. Adult patients undergoing coronary angiography within the specified time frames will be evaluated. The study will include patients with CAD who are on LLT and have at least one LDL-C report documented during a follow-up visit within a 12-month time frame. The presence of atherosclerotic plaques, regardless of size or degree of occlusion, will be used to diagnose CAD based on angiographic findings. Chronic kidney disease and uncontrolled hypothyroidism were defined as exclusion criteria because these conditions may alter lipid metabolism. Thus, we aim to reduce potential confounding factors that could interfere with assessing LDL-C TAR with LLT (Box 1).

The time-frame periods were selected to represent three consecutive 2-year periods following the publication of each edition of the European Society of Cardiology/European Atherosclerosis Society (ESC/EAS) Guidelines for the management of dyslipidaemias in 2011,11 201612 and 2019.13 For the periods 2011–2012 and 2016–2017, the target LDL-C level was less than 70 mg/dL and for the period 2021–2022, the target LDL-C level was less than 55 mg/dL. To assess whether the LDL-C targets outlined in the 2019 guidelines were achieved, the period 2019–2020 was excluded, as 2020 was considered an atypical year in terms of patient volume reduction due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Therefore, the scientific committee decided to evaluate the years 2021–2022 instead. TAR will be evaluated through a retrospective review of clinical records up to 12 months following coronary angiographies.

The study was designed by the Cardiology Department and Centro de Investigaciones Clínicas (CIC) of Fundación Valle del Lili (FVL). A Scientific Committee was implemented to consolidate the project, review the protocol and coordinate the inclusion process of participant centres. The same Committee is going to be in charge of reviewing the progress of the research, the quality of information and the publication process. The regulatory and administrative processes required to include research institutions will be conducted by the CIC of the FVL.

Participating institutions

We plan to include at least 10 High-Complexity Institutions located in at least five of the main cities in the country (Bogotá, Medellín, Cali, Barranquilla and Bucaramanga) encompassing six Colombian territories (Antioquia, Atlántico, Bogotá, Cundinamarca, Norte de Santander and Valle del Cauca) (figure 2).

Data collection

Data collection will be carried out by the research team at each institution to ensure consistency and accuracy. A comprehensive data entry manual has been developed to guide the process, standardise the information and minimise errors. Weekly reports will be generated to monitor data entry progress and address any discrepancies. Collected data will include demographic characteristics, comorbidities, relevant clinical variables, imaging studies and follow-up outcomes, among other key information. All data will be securely captured and managed using the REDCap platform.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of our research. Since this is a retrospective study based on medical records, direct patient involvement was not feasible. However, the findings of the study will be shared through scientific publications and presentations to contribute to public health strategies related to LDL-C target achievement in CAD.

Sample Size

A target sample size of 5000 patients was set for convenience and feasibility. We calculated the feasibility of recruiting at least 500 patients with the inclusion criteria at FVL by analysing data on arteriographies: 918 in 2011, 820 in 2012, 598 in 2016, 546 in 2017, 1663 in 2021 and 1271 in 2022, totalling 5816 arteriographies. Of these, we estimated that at least 10.0% of the patients meet the inclusion criteria, representing 91 in 2011, 82 in 2012, 59 in 2016, 54 in 2017, 166 in 2021, and 127 in 2022, totalling 581 patients. The 10.0% estimate was considered a conservative value accounting for the prevalence of CAD in patients undergoing coronary angiography, which has been reported as 67.0% for non-obstructive CAD and 45.0% (median) for obstructive CAD at elective procedures.14 15 These calculations were performed ensuring ≥80% statistical power and 95% confidence intervals at the centre level.

Statistical analysis

Data quality will be assessed by randomly sampling 10.0% of the data and having a second researcher evaluate its validity. If the inter-rater agreement is less than 0.9, an additional 10.0% will be reviewed. If the inter-rater agreement remains below 0.9, the entire database will be examined to assess data quality.

An exploratory analysis will be performed to analyse missing data and ensure the statistics are at least viable. Exploratory analysis including descriptive statistics and data visualisation will be performed. The normality of the variables will be assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test or Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables will be presented using means and SD. On the other hand, variables that do not follow a normal distribution will be represented with medians and interquartile ranges. Qualitative variables will be presented with absolute frequency and percentages. The findings will be organised in frequency tables and graphs for visualisation.

The primary outcome, LDL-C TAR, will be defined according to the LDL-C goals specified in the clinical guidelines relevant to the evaluated year. Additional sensitivity analyses will compare this outcome against the latest ESC/EAS guideline (2019) and an alternative definition based on achieving a 50.0% reduction in LDL-C compared with baseline.

The primary analysis will evaluate LDL-C TAR using data from each patient’s last available follow-up. Central tendency measures (mean/median) will be calculated to describe the timing of the last follow-up. To provide a more in-depth assessment of the primary outcome, a mixed-effects model will be performed. This model will account for repeated measures and inter-patient variability. Sensitivity analyses will explore alternative outcome definitions (eg, ever achieved target at any follow-up).

Exploratory analyses will also evaluate LDL-C target achievement and clinical outcomes based on patients’ geographic origin (city or region) as a proxy for structural inequities in access to cardiovascular care. Regional categorisation will be based on socioeconomic indicators published by Departamento Administrativo Nacional de Estadística (DANE), Colombia’s national statistics authority.

For the concordance analysis between LDL-C enzymatic measure (gold standard) and LDL-C calculated by existing equations, we will analyse the following methods: De-Long, Sampson and extended Martin/Hopkins. These methods were chosen because they showed the best concordance in a recent study of a large group of Colombian patients.16 For this analysis, an intraclass correlation coefficient will be evaluated. The statistical analysis will be performed using R Studio V.4.1.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Analysis

The EDHIPO-MARCA study is a retrospective, multicentre, observational study with analytical intent evaluating LDL-C TAR within 1 year of coronary angiography in adults with CAD. We expect to uncover insights into the prevalence of LDL-C TAR among CAD patients, prescription practices for LLT and demographic variations in these practices, along with other related objectives. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the few multicentre registries with the aims mentioned for the CAD population. We hypothesise that LDL-C TAR in our study will be similar to that reported in the literature, which ranges between 31.1% and 54.0%.8,1017 18

An observational study conducted in Colombia, involving 746 patients with CAD, reported a statin prescription prevalence of 97.8%, with LDL-C TAR, defined as less than 70 mg/dL, observed in 56.0% of patients. It is important to note that hospitals recruiting patients for this study were considered tertiary care facilities.19 Another local study found a 34.7% LDL-C TAR on a cohort of 147 patients, from which 43.5% had very high risk, displaying similarities to our target population.20 Another local study published in 2013 reported an LDL-C TAR of 55.3% in patients requiring secondary prevention, using <70 mg/dL as the LDL-C-defined target.21 Finally, another local study published in 2013 found a prevalence of LDL-C TAR of 43.5% in patients at very high risk.22 Those studies report a wide range of TAR in Colombia comparable with what has been reported in the literature, supporting our hypothesis for future findings.

In high-income regions, LDL-C TAR and the prescription of high-intensity statins remain low, despite this drug class, whether used alone or in combination with ezetimibe and/or proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type nine inhibitors (PCSK9i), being the cornerstone of secondary prevention in patients with CAD.23 United States studies in CAD populations report LDL-C target attainment of ~54–58% (2016 threshold) and ~27–33% (2019 threshold),24 with only 70.8% receiving any statin and 48.9% high-intensity therapy within 21 days postacute coronary syndrome.25 In Europe, attainment can be even lower (18% and 7% for the 2016 and 2019 thresholds in Portugal),26 and real-world use of ezetimibe and PCSK9i is limited (9.3% and 1.2% in the DA VINCI study, respectively).27 28 However, in the Italian healthcare system, where care pathways are more structured, ezetimibe combination therapy can reach 74.5%, while PCSK9i use remains consistently low (21.6% for alirocumab and 19.5% for evolocumab).29 Simulation studies indicate that meeting 2019 targets broadly would likely require wider PCSK9i adoption.30

Although current guidelines define LDL-C targets for secondary prevention, a proportion of patients will achieve levels far below these thresholds, and values <30 mg/dL have been associated with both continued cardiovascular benefit and potential adverse effects, such as new-onset diabetes mellitus or haemorrhagic stroke.31 Additionally, a post-hoc ‘bottom-out’ subanalysis of the REAL-CAD study in Japanese patients with CAD suggested that an LDL-C level of about 70 mg/dL may be a threshold, beyond which further reduction offers no added long-term benefit.32 These findings highlight the importance and invite us to not only assess guideline-based target attainment but also explore cut-off values of achieved LDL-C associated with improved outcomes, particularly mortality.

Understanding the extent of LDL-C target non-achievement in patients with CAD is crucial for alerting local authorities and informing the development of public health policies, particularly as it involves a modifiable factor whose mitigation could significantly reduce the burden of cardiovascular disease. Identifying factors associated with decreased LDL-C TAR (eg, through regression modelling) could help highlight potential targets for public health policies. For example, in heart failure, suboptimal dosing and incomplete prescription of guideline-directed medical therapy have been associated with worse outcomes.33,35 Multiple studies have proposed strategies such as pharmacist-driven interventions, electronic medical record alerts and physician-focused educational programmes to address the issue.36,38

Similar to these approaches, our findings will guide strategies to address LDL-C non-achievement in patients with CAD. Additionally, this registry will provide valuable insights to guide and improve the planning of future prospective studies. The findings from this registry will be communicated through abstract presentations and peer-reviewed journal publications.

Ethics and dissemination

The protocol for this study was reviewed and approved by the Comité de Ética en Investigación Biomédica of FVL, the coordinating institution and creator of the protocol, under approval number 2023.2124. Each participating institution will be required to submit the protocol for review and approval by its respective ethics committee prior to data collection.

The information will be collected in a deidentified manner as the database will contain no variables that would allow the identification of subjects by any member of the research group. However, each subject will be assigned a unique identification number that only one designated key person can cross-reference with the original identification document, in case it becomes necessary to return to the original source. In accordance with Colombian Resolution 8430, this study is classified as 'no-risk' due to its retrospective documentary nature, with no direct patient contact and no collection of sensitive personal information. Consequently, informed consent was not required. This study complies with the Helsinki Declaration for medical research.39

The development of publications based on the study’s general and specific objectives will be determined by the Scientific Committee, composed of Juan Esteban Gómez-Mesa, Álvaro Ruiz and Dora Inés Molina. The sponsors will be notified each time a manuscript is submitted to an indexed journal. Authorship will follow the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) criteria, including substantial contribution, final review and approval, content responsibility and participation in final approval.40 All authors must meet these criteria to be recognised as contributors to any publication derived from the study.

The findings will be disseminated through presentations at scientific events and submitted for publication in international peer-reviewed journals to maximise the study’s impact on cardiovascular disease management and public health policies.

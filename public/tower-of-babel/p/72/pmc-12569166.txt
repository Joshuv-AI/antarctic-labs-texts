Adaptive sliding mode control for chaotic system synchronization using neural networks
Authors: Nidal Turab, N. Raghu, Satish Choudhury, Ahmed Zkear Abass, S. Emalda Roslin, Gaganjot Kaur, Ruby Pant, Gauri Chauhan, Abdolreza Khorasanikia Asl
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This research introduces an innovative control method designed for the synchronization and management of chaotic systems through the application of advanced neural network techniques. Specifically, a neural network-based sliding mode control framework is employed to enhance system stability and precision in synchronization tasks. Chaotic systems, particularly when arranged in master–slave configurations, exhibit behaviors that are highly sensitive to initial conditions and parameter variations, making them ideal candidates for the proposed approach. The core of this methodology leverages neural networks to estimate unknown nonlinear functions and dynamically adjust the control coefficients, ensuring high accuracy and adaptability in real-time. One of the key contributions of this study lies in addressing the complex issues of parametric uncertainty, external disturbances, and unmodeled dynamics that typically challenge conventional sliding mode control methods. By incorporating neural networks, the controller is equipped to effectively mitigate these uncertainties, ensuring robust performance even in the face of significant system variability. The adaptive nature of the control system allows for continuous adjustment, resulting in improved synchronization accuracy and faster convergence times. The stability and robustness of the proposed control system are rigorously proven using Lyapunov-based methods. Simulations show that synchronization of nonlinear chaotic systems occurs within 10 s, even under varying conditions. This efficiency demonstrates its practical applications in secure communications, biological systems, and power grids, marking a significant advancement in chaotic system control with broad industrial potential.

Introduction

The control of chaotic behavior has garnered significant interest because of its numerous applications in various fields such as neural networks, nonlinear systems, and several industrial systems. Remarkably, chaos is a basic concept in modern science. This concept refers to apparently random and complex phenomena that have a definite nature in the interior. In other words, very complex behaviors can be expected from a simple differential equation1–3. Due to the significance of chaotic behavior, numerous forms of chaotic systems have been established and studied recently. The differential equations related to these systems are apparently simple, but according to the analysis related to these systems in the relevant references, they have chaotic and complex behavior4–6. Chaotic systems have different characteristics; one of the most important characteristics of chaos is the high sensitivity to initial conditions. Controlling chaotic systems is crucial because even a minuscule difference in initial conditions can result in a significant difference in its state at later times. Many methods have been presented to control the phenomenon of chaos, which can be used according to the desired control goals. Approaches such as sliding mode control, neural and fuzzy control, etc. are among the different controllers that have many applications in this field7–10.

The sliding mode control technique is a simple methodology for robust control of nonlinear systems. It is considered a suitable approach for the problem of maintaining stability and uniform performance in the face of uncertainty in modeling. Further, it offers noteworthy features for controlling systems, including design simplicity, easy and low-cost implementation, and resistance to unstructured uncertainty11–14. Recently, this technique has been divided into many branches. The fundamental concept behind sliding mode control involves defining a sliding surface on which the system can converge to appropriate control, thereby ensuring system stability15,16. Several related techniques have emerged from this method to enhance this method by reducing chattering and improving limited-time stability. Amongst the sliding mode branch techniques, we can mention sliding mode, terminal integral sliding mode, or other such combinations, each of which, by defining its own sliding surface, enables the control of the system17–19.

The terminal sliding mode is a method to control the system based on the definition of its own sliding surface. Contrary to the sliding mode approach, this method defines the sliding surface in a non-linear and exponential manner. For this reason, the convergence of the system states is faster20. Also, this technique is divided into distinct branches, each of these branches is presented with a specific control objective21. With the expansion of the terminal sliding mode control method, various sub-branches emerged from it. Each of them pursued distinct aims. For instance, in22, the terminal sliding mode of the first order was employed to the nonlinear systems of the second order. Likewise, in23, the terminal sliding mode method is extended to linear and high-order single–input–single–output systems. Then, the general terminal sliding mode was established for linear multi-input-multiple-output systems. Then, the fast terminal sliding mode was proposed, which had the ability to quickly converge states on the sliding surface24. In25, the non-singular terminal sliding mode was also presented, which avoids the existence of singularities. Also, in the following years, the terminal sliding mode was introduced by combining the previously obtained concepts, which can be referred to as the non-unique fast-8 terminal sliding mode for second-order systems. In this controller, both features are encountered26. In terms of advantages and disadvantages, it can be noted that non-unique techniques lack the property of finite time convergence and exhibit slower convergence speeds compared to fast terminal sliding mode methods. While fast-unique terminal sliding mode control approaches have been proposed for second-order systems, no control methods within this branch have been introduced for high-order systems27–30. Also, in the method presented in31–33, despite the introduction of non-unique control method and limited time for high-order systems, the effect of uncertainty and unstructured collective disturbance is not considered.

Despite all the advantages of the terminal sliding mode control method, (like limited time convergence, reflecting the effect of uncertainty and unstructured disturbance, and the generality of the technique for high-order systems), one of the major disadvantages of this scheme is the existence of the chattering phenomenon in this control method34. This phenomenon can be seen in all sliding mode and terminal sliding mode methods and is caused by the discontinuous part in the control system and high frequency switching of the control signal. Fixing this phenomenon in systems is of particular importance35. Therefore, by using the terminal sliding mode methods and by using the positive features that exist in these methods, as well as by fixing its important defect, which is the chattering phenomenon, the system can be taken proper control. As a summary, non-unique terminal sliding mode methods lack the property of finite time convergence and exhibit slower convergence speeds in comparison to fast terminal sliding mode methods. Also, the fast terminal sliding mode method has a notable flaw, which is the possibility of a singularity in the system control. Additionally, fast-non-unique terminal sliding mode control methods have often been presented for second-order systems, and no control method has been introduced in this branch for high-order systems36.

In this paper, a novel control method is proposed for the synchronization of chaotic systems using advanced neural network techniques integrated into a sliding mode control framework. The contribution of the research lies in effectively addressing the challenges posed by chaotic systems, such as parametric uncertainty, external disturbances, and unmodeled dynamics. By employing neural networks, the controller dynamically estimates unknown nonlinear functions and adapts control parameters in real time, enhancing both the accuracy and robustness of the synchronization process. The adaptive nature of the control system enables faster convergence times and improved performance, even under varying conditions. Additionally, the stability of the proposed approach is rigorously proven using Lyapunov-based methods, while simulations demonstrate its practical effectiveness by achieving synchronization within 10 s. This work provides significant advancements in chaotic system control, with potential applications in secure communications, biological systems, and power grids.

Integral terminal sliding mode control

The terminal sliding mode control approach is a vigorous method that offers several advantages such as optimal settling time, appropriate response to uncertainties and external disturbances, and ease of design and implementation in the system. But the major challenge is chattering, which is a detrimental feature in control systems and a suitable method must be used to reduce its effects. In addition, this control method requires a relatively accurate model of the system, in fact and in cases where the system has unknown dynamics, a system should be used to estimate the unknown part.

Here, first, the terminal sliding mode method is briefly explained. Then, the sliding mode technique of the intelligent integral terminal is examined. In the smart integrated terminal sliding mode method, a new relationship for the sliding surface is proposed, which is a mixture of the sliding surface of the terminal sliding mode and the integral of a nonlinear function of the system states, and the factors of this relationship are computed with the bee algorithm. To maintain the mentioned control purposes, including the optimal settling time and chattering removal, along with improving the transient response of the system and the appropriate response in the existence of system uncertainties, the idea of merging the integral terminal sliding mode controller and the adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system is introduced. The adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system will be designed with the aim of approximating the unknown part of the system and removing some unknown terms of the system in the derivative of the Lyapunov function and overcoming uncertainty and disturbance. Also, the residual dynamics converge to the equilibrium point by the integral terminal sliding mode controller.

Together with the Hurwitz property of the observer dynamics, this guarantees that the synchronization error \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$e\left( t \right)$$\end{document} converges to zero. The detailed steps follow directly from conventional adaptive SMC proofs and are omitted here for brevity.

As seen in relation 34, type-2 fuzzy system plays an essential role as a complement to the control system by estimating the unknown functions of the system. Due to the large number of mathematical relationships in the article and to avoid confusion of the readers, the detailed analysis of the type-2 fuzzy neural network is not stated, but the readers can refer to7. Figure 2 shows a completely innovative and new structure of the sliding mode control system. The bee algorithm as a global search optimization calculates and adjusts SMC parameters. In the proposed design, the bee algorithm is used to optimize the sliding mode control parameters. The main parameters of the bee algorithm were set as follows: population size = 30, number of iterations = 100, and recruitment rate = 0.5. The objective function was the root-mean-square synchronization error (RMSE). The algorithm searches for the optimal parameter set that minimizes RMSE while ensuring the stability condition of the Lyapunov function is satisfied. The best solution was used in all simulations.

Conclusions

In this paper, a completely innovative technique based on sliding mode technique and computational intelligence was presented to control and synchronize chaotic systems. The sliding model method, which is one of the resistant control techniques, is very effective in controlling and synchronizing chaotic systems and has been widely used. But this method has two general defects, one is determining the coefficients of sliding surfaces and the other is the need to know the exact values of the nonlinear functions of the systems. In this work, for the first time, a type-2 fuzzy neural network was applied to solve both of the aforementioned defects. To assess the proposed control system, various scenarios were implemented. As seen, both the step response without parametric uncertainty and the step response with parametric uncertainty were investigated. The RMSE value obtained in the proposed method in two cases without uncertainty and with 20% uncertainty is equal to 0.093 and 0.125, respectively. In all the simulations, the proposed method is superior and can be used operationally.

Solubility and Crystal Shape of Vonoprazan Fumarate in Monosolvents and Cosolvent Mixtures
Authors: Hongcheng Li, Xinyue Qiu, Yan Wang, Jinting Yuan, Fumin Xue, Xiaohui Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vonoprazan fumarate (VPF) is a novel potassium-competitive acid blocker that exhibits superior acid suppression compared to proton pump inhibitors. The solubility of VPF in water, methanol, ethanol, dimethylformamide, and mixed solvents of water + acetonitrile, water + acetone, methanol + acetone, and ethanol + acetonitrile was determined by a gravimetric method within the temperature range of 283.15–333.15 K under atmospheric pressure. The solubility data of VPF are positively related to the temperature in all selected solvents. In pure solvents, the solubility of VPF follows the order of DMF > methanol > ethanol > water. In the four cosolvent mixtures, the maximum solubility effect occurred as the solvent composition changed. The maximum values can be observed at x 2 0 = 0.6 for water + acetonitrile and water + acetone mixtures, x 2 0 = 0.8 for ethanol + acetonitrile, and x 2 0 = 0.9 for methanol + acetone. The solubility data were fitted by the modified Apelblat model, van’t Hoff model, and modified Jouyban–Acree model. The modified Apelblat model had the best-fitting performance. The thermodynamic properties of the dissolution process were also calculated to interpret the solubility results. In addition, crystallization experiments were designed based on the solubility data. Single crystals of VPF were obtained, and the crystal structure was analyzed. Meanwhile, the crystal shape of VPF was also investigated, and the results show that the shape depends on both solvent type and solvent composition.

Introduction

Vonoprazan fumarate (VPF, C21H20FN3O6S; Figure ) is a potassium-competitive acid blocker, and it primarily inhibits gastric acid secretion through competitive and reversible blockade of potassium ion binding to H+/K+-ATPase. VPF is indicated for the treatment of gastric ulcer, duodenal ulcer, and erosive esophagitis and for Helicobacter pylori eradication. Martinucci et al. reported that VPF offers a faster, more potent, and sustained acid suppression compared to traditional proton pump inhibitors (PPIs). Yu et al. reported a four-step process of VPF starting from 5-(2-fluorophenyl)-1H-pyrrole-3-carboxylate, including ester hydrolyzation, methylamine substitution, sulfonyl chloride substitution, and amide reduction. The key to this strategy is a novel and practical protocol for amide reduction, with controllable impurities and a high overall yield. Additionally, Liu et al. developed a combined theoretical and experimental study on VPF, focusing on the crystallographic structure, atropisomerism, and molecular interactions with H+/K+–ATPase. The crystallographic data of VPF have also been reported by Gong et al. However, there are some differences in crystal systems and unit cell parameters. Crystallization is an important method for the separation and purification of drugs, which determines product properties and affects downstream processing. It is of great significance to control the crystal form and shape of VPF.

Chemical structure of the VPF.

In the field of drug design and development, solubility is a critical physicochemical property of pharmaceuticals, reflecting the solid–liquid equilibrium state. − Furthermore, the solubility profile of a drug not only significantly influences the selection of crystallization methods but also guides the screening of solvents and the design of formulations. − It plays a central role in optimizing crystallization processes and enhancing production efficiency. − It also affects the impact of impurities and ensures process robustness. Both crystal form and shape are first regulated based on the thermodynamic properties. , There are many drugs that have low solubility, which also brings about manufacturing problems in crystallization. Researchers have discovered that the solubility can be improved by cosolvency. For example, the solubility of sulfonamides and ethylparaben in acetonitrile + water cosolvent mixtures reached the maximum when the mass fraction of acetonitrile was 0.9. , Increasing the concentration of acetonitrile in the cosolvent mixture weakens the cohesive interactions of water, thereby reducing the “squeezing-out” effect that expels solute molecules from aqueous solution. The cosolvent effect also occurred for lenalidomide in mixtures of acetonitrile + methanol/isopropanol, arbidol hydrochloride in acetone + water, aripiprazole in mixtures of acetone + n-propanol/isopropanol, etc. There is literature indicating that the solubility of VPF is freely soluble in DMSO, but very slightly soluble in water and other organic solvents. These research findings motivated us to study the effect of solvent type on the dissolution behavior of VPF and the cosolvent effect of mixtures.

In this work, the solubility of VPF in four pure solvents (water, methanol, ethanol, and dimethylformamide (DMF)) and four binary solvent mixtures (water + acetonitrile, water + acetone, methanol + acetone, and ethanol + acetonitrile) was determined using a gravimetric method at different temperatures under atmospheric pressure. For the theoretical correlations, the modified Apelblat model, van’t Hoff model, and modified Jouyban–Acree model were used. The remaining solids were characterized by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and thermal analysis. We have also prepared VPF single crystals and performed single-crystal X-ray diffraction to identify the crystal structure. Crystallization was performed via slow solvent evaporation in various solvent types and compositions to investigate crystal shape using optical microscopy.

Solubility Measurement

In the solubility measurement experiment, the solvent selection at 293.15 K was carried out first by the gravimetric method. The solvents should have low toxicity and be easily removed by drying, including water, alcohols, ketones, esters, dimethylformamide, acetonitrile, etc. It was found that VPF was poorly soluble in most of them (Table S1). In order to ensure the accuracy of data measurement, solvents would be selected when the mole fraction solubility of VPF was larger than 1.0 × 10–4. Since methanol, ethanol, dimethylformamide, and water meet the requirements, they were selected to measure the solubility of VPF at different temperatures. In addition, to solve the problem of low solubility, we also explored the method of improving solubility by cosolvency in mixed solvents of water/alcohols + acetonitrile/acetone. −

In this work, the solubility of VPF in methanol, DMF, and three binary solvents (water + acetonitrile, water + acetone, methanol + acetone) was determined by the gravimetric method at 283.15–323.15 K. It was found that the mole fraction solubility of VPF in water, ethanol, and mixed solvents of ethanol + acetonitrile (in acetonitrile-rich mixtures) at low temperatures (283.15 and 288.15 K) was very small (<1.0 × 10–4) associated with relatively large uncertainty (Table S2). To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the measurements, the solubility in these systems was measured from 293.15 to 333.15 K. All solubility measurements were carried out at atmospheric pressure. An aneroid barometer (DYM3, Shanghai Yipin Instruments & Meters Co., Ltd., China) was used to measure the pressure, and the uncertainties were calculated using the method presented in the Supporting Material. The experimental procedures for determining the solubility data were as follows: Excess solid of VPF and about 80 mL of solvent were added into a glass tube, which was subsequently sealed with a rubber stopper and parafilm to prevent solvent volatilization. Then, the glass tube was placed in a double-layer jacket glass container. A thermostatic water-circulating bath (Ministat 230, Huber, Germany) with an uncertainty of ±0.05 K was used to control the temperature. Preliminary experiments were performed, and it was found that the suspensions can reach equilibrium after stirring for 10 h using a magnetic stirring device (MS–H-S type, DLAB Scientific Co., Ltd., China). In solubility measurements, all suspensions were stirred for more than 12 h to guarantee the solid–liquid equilibrium. After turning off the stirring, the solution was allowed to stand for about 2 h. The filtered supernatant was weighed immediately after being injected into a preweighed Petri dish using a preheated/precooled 10 mL syringe and a 0.45 μm filter membrane. All samples were dried in a vacuum drying oven (DZF-6000, Shanghai Yiheng Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd., China) at 323.15 K for at least 12 h to reach constant weight. The mass of the samples was analyzed by analytical balances (ME type, Mettler Toledo, Switzerland) with an accuracy of ±0.0001 g. All experiments were repeated at least three times, and the solubility was calculated by the average of the data to reduce the experimental error. In order to validate this gravimetric method, it was also applied to measure the solubility of other compounds (isoniazid, Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., mass fraction purity ≥ 0.990). As shown in Table S3, the experimental solubility of isoniazid in the ethanol + water mixture at 293.15 K is in good agreement with that in the literature, with the relative deviations lower than 3.50%. Therefore, the methodology used in this work is reliable.

Characterizations of the VPF Powder

In order to make sure that there was no polymorphic transformation or no accidental solvates had formed in the slurring and drying process, VPF samples collected from the solubility measurement were analyzed by recording PXRD patterns at room temperature. The measurement was carried out on a MiniFlex 600 instrument (Rigaku) with Cu Kα radiation. The tube voltage and current were set as 40 kV and 30 mA, respectively. The measurement angle was 2θ = 3–50°, the scanning speed was 8°·min–1, and the step size was 0.02°. Thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) were used to measure the thermostability of VPF. They were performed on an integrated thermal analyzer (TGA/DSC 3+, Mettler Toledo Instrument Co., Ltd., Switzerland) under a nitrogen purge atmosphere. The sample mass was 5–10 mg. The flow rate of nitrogen was 50 mL·min–1, and the temperature range was 298.15–623.15 K at a heating rate of 10 K·min–1.

Single-Crystal X-ray Diffraction (SCXRD)

SCXRD was carried out to analyze the crystal structure of VPF and find the equilibrium morphology. The single crystals of VPF were grown in methanol by slow solvent evaporation. VPF (0.3 g) was added to 30 g of methanol. The solution was suspended at 298.15 K for 12 h to obtain a saturated solution. Then, the solution was filtered through a 0.45 μm PTFE membrane and covered using parafilm with a few holes in it. Solvent evaporation occurred at room temperature, yielding crystals suitable for SCXRD analysis. A suitable crystal of VPF was selected and mounted on a D8 VENTURE dual wavelength Cu diffractometer with a Bruker PHOTON III detector. Data collection was performed at 193 K under Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5418 Å). The SCXRD data acquisition was monitored by the APEX6 program suite. Crystal structure was solved with the SHELXT structure solution program implemented in Olex 2 using intrinsic phasing and refined with the SHELXL refinement package using the full-matrix least-squares method. ,

Crystallization and Optical Microscopy

Crystallization experiments of VPF in different solvents were carried out. The mass of the VPF solid and solvents was calculated and added according to the measured solubility data at 323.15 K. The solid can dissolve in the solvents completely after stirring for a certain time at this temperature. When we added a small amount of VPF solid to the solution, no more solute could be dissolved. Therefore, a saturated solution of VPF at 323.15 K was prepared, and the reliability of the experimental solubility was also confirmed. The solution was filtered and evaporated slowly at room temperature to obtain VPF crystals. The shape of VPF crystals produced in different solvent types and compositions was characterized using an optical polarizing microscope (Olympus BX53, Japan).

Results and Discussion

Solid Characterization

The crystal form of VPF was detected by PXRD. The remaining solids were tested after being suspended in different solvents for 12 h. In solvent mixtures, residual solids collected at different solvent compositions were analyzed. In Figure , PXRD patterns of samples obtained when x 2 0 = 0.6 were taken as examples. The solids obtained from both pure and mixed solvents exhibit similar PXRD patterns, which is consistent with that of the raw material. We found that the PXRD patterns of the dried product of the filtered supernatant also show the same characteristic diffraction peaks. These results indicate that there is no polymorphic transition throughout the whole experiment.

PXRD patterns of VPF raw material and residual solids suspended in different solvents.

TGA and DSC curves of the VPF are shown in Figure . Within the temperature range of 303.15–463.15 K, no significant changes are observed in either the mass or heat flow of VPF, indicating its stability and the absence of phase transitions. When the temperature exceeds 473.15 K, TGA reveals a continuous mass loss. Concurrently, a distinct endothermic peak is detected between 473.15 and 523.15 K. This suggests that the melting process of the VPF was also accompanied by decomposition.

Crystal Structure of VPF

The crystal structure of VPF was analyzed, and detailed crystallographic data are presented in Table . VPF is crystallized in the monoclinic system with the P21/n space group. The asymmetric unit consists of a protonated vonoprazan molecule and a deprotonated fumaric acid molecule. It is noticed that the F atom exhibits disorder over two sites, with the refined occupancies being F1 = 0.605(4) and F1A = 0.395(4). The cell parameters of VPF are a = 7.7808 Å, b = 8.0231 Å, c = 34.712 Å, β = 92.348, and Z = 4. Compared to the crystallographic data reported by Liu et al. and Gong et al., the crystal structure resolved in this work is more similar to the latter (Table ).

Crystallographic Data of VPF

Mercury software was used for the analysis of the crystal packing. For a clearer presentation, only one position of the disordered fluorine atoms is displayed. As shown in Figure , there is an extensive network of intermolecular hydrogen bonds in the crystal structure of VPF. Fumaric acid molecules adopt a head-to-tail arrangement and are connected by hydrogen bonds O–H···O. The fumaric acid molecules align along the b-axis, leading to the formation of a chain-like structure. Along the c-axis, two vonoprazan molecules and two fumaric acid molecules interact via hydrogen bonds N–H···O, which generate a ring motif of four molecules. This supramolecular synthon extends along both the a-axis and c-axis, leading to the formation of a layered architecture. The interlayer stacking is primarily stabilized by van der Waals interactions.

Solubility Results

The mole fraction solubility data of VPF in four pure solvents (water, methanol, ethanol, and DMF) and four binary solvents (water + acetonitrile, water + acetone, methanol + acetone, and ethanol + acetonitrile) are shown in Tables – and plotted in Figures and . In addition, the raw data of the mass fraction solubility of VPF are listed in Tables S4–S8. It can be observed that the solubility of VPF in all solvents increased with temperature. In pure solvents, the solubility follows the order of DMF > methanol > ethanol > water. We found that the solubility of VPF in water is relatively lower, and it is almost insoluble in acetonitrile and acetone. This suggests that the ranking of solubility cannot be explained independently by solvent polarity. It might be affected by a combination of multiple factors, including solvent properties (e.g., polarity, dipole moment, hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor, and cohesive energy density), molecular structures of solute and solvent (e.g., functional groups, size, and shape), van der Waals forces, electrostatic interactions, etc. ,, Although water is a strongly polar solvent and possesses both hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor, it has a high cohesive energy density, indicating stronger solvent–solvent interactions. In the dissolution process of VPF in pure water, water molecules tend to contact closely with each other, and an extensive hydrogen-bond network will be formed, so that dissolving a solute requires breaking this network.

Significant cosolvency phenomena were observed in mixed solvents of water + acetonitrile and water + acetone (Figure a,b). The solubility data and cosolvency phenomenon will help to control the crystallization operation range.

Data Correlation

The modeling of experimental solubility data is performed by the modified Apelblat equation, van’t Hoff equation, and modified Jouyban–Acree model. The modified Apelblat equation is a semiempirical equation derived from an equation based on the Clausius–Clapeyron equation. , It is used to correlate the solubility of VPF at different temperatures. It is represented by eq . 4 ln⁡x1=A+BT+Cln⁡T where x 1 represents the mole fraction solubility of VPF and T represents the experimental temperature. A, B, and C are model parameters.

The van’t Hoff model, as represented by eq , indicates that the logarithm of mole fraction solubility is linearly related to the reciprocal of temperature. , 5 ln⁡x1=−ΔHdRT+ΔSdR where ΔH d and ΔS d indicate the standard enthalpy change and the standard entropy change for the dissolution process, respectively. R is the gas constant.

The Jouyban–Acree model provides comprehensive prediction concerning both solvent composition and temperature for solubility of VPF. It is expressed as , 6 ln⁡x1=x20(x1)2+x30(x1)3+x20x30∑i=0NJi(x20−x30)iT where J i denotes a model parameter. x 1 refers to the mole fraction solubility of VPF. N is the number of solvent components.

When combined with the modified Apelblat model, the modified Jouyban–Acree model can be obtained and expressed as follows 7 ln⁡x1=A1+A2T+A3ln⁡T+A4x20+A5x20T+A6(x20)2T+A7(x20)3T+A8(x20)4T+A9x20ln⁡T where A 1–A 9 are model parameters determined by nonlinear least-squares.

To evaluate the accuracy of the three models, the average relative deviation (ARD) and the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) were calculated by eqs and , respectively. 8 ARD=1n∑i=1n|(x1exp)i−(x1cal)i(x1exp)i| 9 RMSD=∑i=1n[(x1exp)i−(x1cal)i]2n where x 1 exp and x 1 cal represent the experimental and calculated mole fraction solubility, respectively. n is the number of data points.

The fitting accuracy of this model is highly dependent on the distribution range of the solvent composition ratio and may have less adaptability to polar nonprotonic solvents. The fitting results revealed that the modified Apelblat equation can correlate the solubility of VPF with the best-fitting performance, which provides the solid–liquid equilibrium data that are beneficial for further research and industrial applications.

The relative contributions of enthalpy (ζ H ) and entropy (ζTS) to the dissolution process were also calculated and compared. The thermodynamic properties for VPF in pure solvent and cosolvent mixtures are presented in Tables S12–S16. Among all of the solvents, the values of Gibbs energy are positive. In mixed solvents of water + acetonitrile and water + acetone, the Gibbs energy decreases, reaches the minimum at x 2 0 = 0.6, and then increases. The values of enthalpy and entropy in all solvents are all positive, indicating that the dissolution process is endothermic and spontaneous. The results also show that the enthalpy is the major contributor to the Gibbs energy of the solution.

The enthalpy–entropy compensation is a thermodynamic reflection of bond formation and rupture (hydrogen bonds), revealing the governing mechanism of cosolvent effects. , Specifically, the enthalpy of the solution quantifies energetic changes in drug–solvent interactions via hydrogen and van der Waals bonds, while entropy reflects molecular rearrangement during solvation. This relationship is conventionally analyzed through the construction of plots depicting Δsoln H° as a function of Δsoln G°, which characteristically yield nonlinear trends (Figure S3). In such plots, negative and positive slopes correspond to entropically driven and enthalpically controlled processes, respectively. Hence, according to the enthalpy–entropic compensation plot of VPF in (water + acetonitrile) cosolvent mixtures, the dissolution processes at x 2 0 = 0.2–0.3, x 2 0 = 0.4–0.6, and x 2 0 = 0.7–0.9 are driven by enthalpy conduction. The processes from x 2 0 = 0.3 to x 2 0 = 0.4 and from x 2 0 = 0.6 to x 2 0 = 0.7 are driven by entropy. In the water + acetone cosolvent mixtures, entropy conduction occurs only from x 2 0 = 0.6 to x 2 0 = 0.7. Besides, in the methanol + acetone mixtures, the dissolution processes from x 2 0 = 0.4 to x 2 0 = 0.6 and from x 2 0 = 0.7 to x 2 0 = 0.8 are driven by enthalpy conduction. The processes from x 2 0 = 0.6 to x 2 0 = 0.7 and from x 2 0 = 0.8 to x 2 0 = 0.9 are driven by entropy. In the mixture of ethanol + acetonitrile, entropy drives the dissolution process only from x 2 0 = 0.7 to x 2 0 = 0.9.

Effect of Solvents on the Crystal Shape of VPF

In the crystallization process, the solvent acts not merely as a dissolving medium but also as an active modulator that governs the structure and stability of liquid precursors, thereby steering the pathways of nucleation and ultimately determining the crystal form and shape. Crystallization experiments of VPF were performed in different solvent types and compositions. As shown in Figure , VPF crystals grown from four pure solvents exhibit a distinct shape. It is known that the solubility of VPF in DMF is significantly larger than that in the other three solvents. A concentrated solution of VPF in DMF led to much faster crystal growth along one direction, and elongated needle-like crystals were produced. Avoiding the formation of needle-like shape is crucial in pharmaceutical industry, which causes crystal breakage and difficulties in downstream processing. , It is necessary to control the crystal habit by decreasing the aspect ratio and increasing the crystal thickness. We discovered that polyhedral block-like crystals were obtained in water and methanol, while smaller plate-like crystals were formed in ethanol. Although the solubility of VPF in ethanol is similar to that in water at the crystallization temperature, the evaporation rate of ethanol is faster than that of water. As a result, higher supersaturation would be created in ethanol, leading to extensive nucleation and the formation of fine crystals. On the whole, water and methanol are the preferred solvents for improving the crystal shape of VPF.

Optical micrographs of VPF crystals prepared in pure solvent: (a) DMF, (b) water, (c) methanol, and (d) ethanol.

Microscopic images in Figure display the crystal shapes of VPF developed in four binary solvents. In water + acetonitrile mixtures, the overall shape of crystals obtained at different water content remains block-like, which has a low aspect ratio. VPF crystals prepared in water + acetone mixtures are rodlike when the initial mole fraction of water is 0.4, and the crystals grow into block-like forms in the water-rich region. As the mole fraction of methanol increases from 0.5 to 0.9 in mixed solvents of methanol + acetone, the rodlike crystals become wider and thicker. It seems that the acetone-rich region in both water + acetone and methanol + acetone mixtures tends to form elongated crystals. This is possibly because a higher content of acetone would accelerate the evaporation rate of the solution, which promotes fast crystal growth in one direction. It can be observed that a binary solvent of ethanol + acetonitrile results in platelets when the mole fraction of ethanol is 0.4. At a higher ethanol concentration, larger lath-shaped crystals appear, which is different from the crystal shape in ethanol. This may be related to the improved solubility in cosolvents. More solute molecules would be provided for crystal facet growth. Therefore, both solvent type and solvent composition play important roles in directing the crystal shape of VPF.

Optical micrographs of VPF crystals obtained at different solvent compositions in mixed solvents: (a) water + acetonitrile, (b) water + acetone, (c) methanol + acetone, and (d) ethanol + acetonitrile.

Differences in the crystal shape could be explained by the surface chemistry of crystal faces and the intermolecular interactions with solvents. As shown in Figure S4, the crystal habit of VPF simulated by the BFDH model is square-like, which is similar to the experimental crystal habit obtained in aqueous solutions. The main crystal faces were identified as (002), (011), and (101). The crystal thickness is controlled by facet growth of (002), and the growth of (011) and (101) would affect the aspect ratio of the crystal. Amino groups of protonated vonoprazan molecules are expressed on the (002) face. Meanwhile, the chain-like arranged molecules of fumaric acid are almost parallel to the (002) face, and they are all exposed on the surface. This topology is favorable for the proximity and adsorption of solvent molecules. The amino groups and carboxyl groups exposed on the surface would also be able to interact with solvents that contain either hydrogen-bond donors or hydrogen-bond acceptors. The (101) face expresses amino, sulfonyl, and methyl groups, as well as fumaric acid molecules. The active functional groups provide both hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, promoting intermolecular interactions with solvents. Since DMF has two methyl groups and a larger molecular size than other solvents, the adsorption of the solvent on the crystal face would bring about intermolecular van der Waals interactions as well as a steric hindrance effect, which hinders further integration of solute molecules. As a result, DMF exhibits the inhibition of the growth of the (002) and (101), resulting in the crystallization of thin and narrow crystals. Since acetone and ethanol also contain two methyl groups, similar steric hindrance and inhibitory effect on facet growth would be induced. On the (011) face, benzene rings, pyridine rings, and some carboxyl groups protrude through the surface, and sulfonyl groups appear at the depressed area. Water and methanol can easily adsorb to all three crystal surfaces and form hydrogen-bond interactions via hydroxy groups. However, the (011) face provides fewer hydrogen-bond donors, so that solvent molecules that only contain hydrogen-bond acceptors, like DMF, acetone, and acetonitrile, may generate weaker intermolecular interactions with the surface. Stronger inhibition of the growth of (101) than that of (001) would result in elongated crystals.

Conclusions

This study determines the solubility of VPF in methanol, DMF, and three binary solvents (water + acetonitrile, water + acetone, methanol + acetone) at 283.15–323.15 K and in water, ethanol, and mixed solvents of ethanol + acetonitrile at 293.15–333.15 K. The solubility of VPF in all selected solvents increases with the increase in temperature. In the above four monosolvents, the solubility at constant temperature can be ranked as DMF > methanol > ethanol > water. There is a cosolvency phenomenon in the mixed solvents. The solubility in water + acetonitrile and water + acetone has shown a maximum at a mole fraction of water x 2 0 = 0.6 at each temperature. In methanol + acetone and ethanol + acetonitrile, the maximum values appear at methanol-rich (x 2 0 = 0.9) and ethanol-rich (x 2 0 = 0.8) compositions, respectively. The experimental solubilities were correlated by the modified Apelblat model, van’t Hoff model, and modified Jouyban–Acree model, with the modified Apelblat model giving the most satisfactory results. The Gibbs energy, enthalpy, and entropy of the VPF solution were calculated, indicating that the dissolution process is endothermic and spontaneous. The enthalpy–entropy compensation was analyzed to reveal entropically driven and enthalpically controlled processes. The solid–liquid equilibrium data can provide guidance for the design and control of VPF crystallization. The single-crystal structure of VPF was solved, and crystal packing was analyzed. An intermolecular hydrogen-bond network was formed between VPF molecules via N–H···O and O–H···O, contributing to a chain-like arrangement along the b-axis and a 3D layered structure. We found that the shape of VPF crystals is largely influenced by both the solvent type and solvent composition. When VPF was crystallized in water, methanol, or water + acetonitrile, more favorable block-like crystals were obtained. In mixed solvents of water + acetone and methanol + acetone, the acetone-rich region led to the formation of rodlike crystals, while higher ethanol concentration in ethanol + acetonitrile brought about lath-shaped crystals. This work gives an important reference for the purification and crystallization process optimization of VPF.

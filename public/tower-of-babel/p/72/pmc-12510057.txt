DeepUS-ReconSeg: A multi-angle paired B-mode Ultrasound dataset for medical imaging reconstruction and segmentation
Authors: Imrus Salehin, Nazmul Huda Badhon, Md Tomal Ahmed Sajib, Nazmun Nessa Moon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article presents a curated dataset of 4,200 B-mode ultrasound images of human forearms, intended for use in deep learning-based image reconstruction and segmentation tasks. Data have been collected using the Verasonics Vantage 64LE system equipped with an L11-5v linear array transducer, known for its high spatial resolution. Each scanning session captures 100 frames per orientation across both arms of 14 healthy subjects, covering multiple anatomical views. The dataset provides grayscale B-mode images stored in MATLAB .mat format and is publicly available through Mendeley Data. This dataset is valuable for researchers in medical image analysis, especially those developing deep learning models for enhancing ultrasound imaging quality and anatomical structure segmentation.Keywords: Ultrasound imaging, B-mode data, Verasonics Vantage, Linear array transducer, Medical dataset, Forearm ultrasound

Background

This dataset has been generated to support the design and evaluation of deep learning models for ultrasound image enhancement and segmentation, particularly in cases where data are acquired under suboptimal imaging conditions. Using the programmable Verasonics Vantage 64LE system, B-mode images are captured at various aperture angles to simulate both high- and low-resolution scenarios common in clinical practice. The target anatomy—human forearm soft tissue—is selected due to its structural complexity and relevance to musculoskeletal and vascular imaging. The primary motivation is to generate a consistent, IRB-approved, and well-organized dataset suitable for training and benchmarking AI-based reconstruction and diagnostic tools that require paired or variable-resolution ultrasound data.

Data Description

The dataset comprises 4,200 grayscale B-mode ultrasound images, organized into 42 MATLAB .mat files [3], with each file containing 100 frames of size 256 × 256 pixels. These files represent two main categories:

•High-Resolution (HR) images: have been acquired using wide probe aperture angles (e.g., 75°) or high-frequency transducer settings (>15 MHz). These frames exhibit clearer boundaries and well-defined anatomical features such as muscle, fat, and skin interfaces.

High-Resolution (HR) images: have been acquired using wide probe aperture angles (e.g., 75°) or high-frequency transducer settings (>15 MHz). These frames exhibit clearer boundaries and well-defined anatomical features such as muscle, fat, and skin interfaces.

•Low-Resolution (LR) images: have been captured at narrow probe angles (1°–15°) or generated by synthetically downsampling HR frames to mimic common degradation in clinical ultrasound imaging.

Low-Resolution (LR) images: have been captured at narrow probe angles (1°–15°) or generated by synthetically downsampling HR frames to mimic common degradation in clinical ultrasound imaging.

All images are provided in grayscale format and stored in .mat containers compatible with standard medical image processing tools and deep learning frameworks. The complete dataset occupies approximately 1.97 GB and is fully anonymized, with files named according to acquisition parameters and subject IDs for clarity. Data preprocessing

Prior to organizing the dataset for public release, several preprocessing steps are carried out to ensure structural uniformity and enhance visual interpretability while preserving the raw diagnostic nature of the data. Each ultrasound scan, consisting of 100 consecutive grayscale frames, is stored in .mat format with a pixel resolution of 256×256. The data arrays are first processed to remove any singleton dimensions and confirmed to be in the correct 3D shape for downstream tasks.

To address the limited visibility of low-intensity echoes in raw B-mode images, logarithmic compression [4] is applied to the amplitude values using the log1p() function. This nonlinear transformation improves the dynamic range and makes subtle tissue structures more distinguishable. In selected samples, we have also applied the Hilbert transform [5] to simulate envelope detection, enhancing the contrast of tissue interfaces and mimicking real-time ultrasound system outputs.

No additional filters, noise removal techniques, or manual enhancements are applied. The file naming convention is standardized to encode subject ID, arm (left/right), and orientation (longitudinal/transverse), ensuring compatibility with batch processing pipelines and deep learning dataset loaders. The preprocessing preserves the integrity of raw clinical imaging signals while making the dataset suitable for both visual assessment and algorithm training.

The dataset is structured to support a wide range of deep learning applications in ultrasound image analysis, particularly in image reconstruction and segmentation tasks. Suitable algorithms include:

Limitations

This dataset is limited to ultrasound B-mode images of healthy adult forearms and does not include any pathological cases or other anatomical regions, which may restrict its use in broader diagnostic model training. Additionally, while the dataset is extensive in frame count and orientation diversity, it does not include manual annotations or segmentation masks, which may be required for supervised learning tasks involving region labeling or structure tracking. Finally, imaging is performed under controlled laboratory settings, which may differ from clinical environments in terms of noise and variability.

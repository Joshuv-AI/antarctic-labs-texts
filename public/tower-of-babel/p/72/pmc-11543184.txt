Outcomes of laser hemorrhoidoplasty for grade II–IV hemorrhoidal disease in Bangladesh
Authors: Md Saiful Islam, Abhigan B Shrestha, Faisal Chowdhury, Md RK Ziko
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Laser hemorrhoidoplasty is a minimally invasive procedure to treat hemorrhoids and has shown better outcomes. Less is known about its utility in a lower middle-income country; Bangladesh.Method:Between March 2023 and December 2023, 86 patients with grade II–IV hemorrhoids underwent laser hemorroidoplasty. Follow up was assessed on 1st week, 2nd week, and 6th week including pain, satisfaction, symptom improvement, incapacity for work, complications, reduction, and recurrence.Results:The study included 86 patients, with a higher proportion of male participants (73.3%) and a mean age of 55.43±13.6 years. The majority of subjects (68.60%) had grade 3 hemorrhoids. The average surgery duration was 14.70±4.70 minutes All individuals experienced substantial decreases in the size of their hemorrhoids (100%) and reported high levels of satisfaction after the operation (97.7%). The use of the Wilcoxon signed-rank test demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in both the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) and Patient Global Impression of Improvement (PGI-I) ratings during the three subsequent observation periods (1st week, 2nd week, and 6th week). The 6th week complication rate was 19.8%. Additional mucopexy was performed on all patients with grade IV and three patients with grade III hemorrhoids.Conclusion:Laser hemorroidoplasty is a day-care procedure, that is safe and efficacious and can be used in rural area settings with good patient satisfaction. Mucopexy is required in some cases of large hemorrhoidal mass to prevent recurrence.Keywords: diode laser, hemorrhoid, hemorroidoplasty, lower middle income country

Hemorrhoidal disease is a prevalent anorectal condition with a global prevalence of up to 27.9%. It has significantly impacted the quality of life of patients and has placed a significant burden on the medical and socioeconomic sectors1–3. It is anticipated that more than half of the population will experience hemorrhoid symptoms at some point in their lives4,5. The management substitutes for symptomatic hemorrhoids have undergone a transformation over time. These encompass a range of surgical options, nonsurgical approaches, and conservative approaches. After the conservative approach has been unsuccessful, surgical treatment is contemplated for symptomatic patients. So far, the gold standard has been considered to be traditional hemorrhoidectomy. However, its unavoidable complications, such as seromucous discharge, hemorrhage, anal stenosis, chronic fissure, and urinary retention, have impeded its extensive use1. In an effort to mitigate these complications, various minimally invasive nonexcisional procedures have been developed, including transanal hemorrhoidal dearterialization (THD), hemorrhoidal artery ligation (HAL), and laser hemorrhoidoplasty (LHP)6–8. Recent evidence has demonstrated that this daycare surgery for symptomatic hemorrhoids has improved outcomes in terms of both safety and efficacy with the implementation of laser diode technology. Despite its superiority in terms of improved outcomes, there is a scarcity of evidence9,10 to substantiate its short-term outcomes in low and middle-income countries such as Bangladesh, particularly in rural areas. Therefore, the objective of this prospective study that spans 6 months is to contribute evidence to the existing literature concerning its results.

Methods

Study design

This prospective cohort study, which was conducted at a single center from March 2023 to December 2023, assesses the results of laser hemorrhoidoplasty in terms of postoperative pain, hemorrhoidal prolapse reduction, symptom improvement, and recurrence. Postoperative follow-up periods included the first, second, and sixth weeks, during which a questionnaire was administered in a manner that was consistent with a previous study11. The protocol was registered on ResearchRegistry.com. The classification provided by Golighers was used to grade hemorrhoids. The prolapse in Grade II was spontaneously reduced, while Grade III necessitated digital reduction. Grade IV was characterized by a prolapse that was impossible to reduce12.

The Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score13, the Patient Global Impression of Improvement Scale (PGI-I)14 for symptom improvement, the Clavien–Dindo Scale, and the Vaizey score for incontinence were used to classify postoperative complications and the clinical evaluation of the surgeons.

After receiving ethical approval from the hospital’s Institutional Review Board (IRB), the research was conducted in accordance with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its subsequent amendments. We adhered to the strengthening the reporting of cohort studies in surgery (STROCSS) guideline to maintain the standard of the study procedure15. The M. Abdur Rahim Medical College ethical board committee granted ethical permission, with memo number MARMCD/2023/2059.

Inclusion criteria

Patients with age equal to or above 18 years. Symptomatic hemorrhoids are diagnosed as either II, III, or IV degrees by the surgeon. Patients have the ability to fill the questionnaire, which was originally in the English language and translated to the native language, that is Bangla by an expert. Absence of any contraindications to undergo laser hemorrhoidoplasty.

Exclusion criteria

Included associated anorectal sepsis. Accompanied inflammatory bowel disease affecting the anus or rectum. Prolapsed and inflamed thrombosed hemorrhoids.

Preoperative assessment

The main presenting complaints of the patients included fresh per rectal bleeding during defecation, feeling of swelling in the anus during defecation, chronic constipation, and pain during defecation (when anal fissures were associated with hemorrhoids). A routine physical examination includes proper history taking, general examination, digital rectal examination (DRE), and proctoscopy. Colonoscopy was done in patients above 50 years old to exclude colorectal carcinoma, and those suffering from chronic bloody diarrhea, along with routine laboratory investigations in all patients.

Operative technique

All the patients underwent laser hemorrhoidoplasty with lasotronix 1470 nm 15 W by the same surgeon who was experienced with the procedure. Spinal anesthesia was implemented during each surgical procedure.

Operative procedure

Step A: With the patient in a lithotomy position with spinal anesthesia, the laser fiber was introduced through the anal canal, and about 60–80 joules (J) energy was applied above the hemorrhoidal column to coagulate the feeding vessels cautiously without touching the mucosa to prevent mucosal injury.

Step B: A micropuncture was then made by the laser fiber ~0.5cm lateral to the internal sphincter. The laser fiber was then introduced upward to reach the submucosal plane at the apex of the pile mass. We then directly applied about 70–80J of energy to coagulate the hemorrhoidal vessels.

Step C: Afterward, the laser fiber is gradually withdrawn downward to reach the hemorrhoidal mass. About 80–150J energy was applied to ablate the vessels of hemorrhoidal mass.

So, about 200–250J of energy had to be applied for each hemorrhoidal column. The cold pack was placed as an intraoperative cooling step between laser applications. In the case of IV-degree hemorrhoids and large external components, mucopexy was performed using the figure 8 VICRYL suture for mucosa and submucosa to augment the shrinkage. Paracetamol (1g four times per day) was used to manage pain postoperatively. The operative procedure is shown in Figure 1.

Short-term follow-up

Outcomes were assessed with the questionnaire for three follow-ups, 1st week, 2nd week, and 6th week. The parameters were pain score, hemorrhoid reduction, symptom improvement, incapacity to work, satisfaction, and benefit from the operation. Improvement of symptoms using the patient global impression of improvement (PGI-I) scale for bleeding, moistening, soiling, itching, or pain.

Data analysis

Following data acquisition, statistical analysis was conducted utilizing Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) Version 25. Using descriptive analysis, the means of various scores and the patients’ demographic information were displayed. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was utilized to examine the normality of the data, and the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test was conducted to compare the median ranks of the scores. χ 2 and Fisher’s exact tests were used to examine the association between categorical variables. The criterion for statistical significance was set at P<0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 86 participants were recruited for this prospective study. The mean age was 55.49±12.68 years. The mean time of hospital stay was 1.01±0.24 days. The average time for surgery was 14.7±4.7 minutes All the patients were managed with paracetamol 1 gm postoperatively and did not require further escalation of analgesics. No intraoperative complications were observed.

In addition, resection of anal skin tag was done in 25 patients, 32 patients had fissurectomy simultaneously. A mean energy of 275 J per hemorrhoid was applied.

Out of 86 participants, 63 (73.3%) were male and 23 (26.7%) were female. The mean age of males and females was 55.43±13.6 years and 55.65±9.97 years, respectively. Grade 3 hemorrhoid was most common presentation in both males (40, 63.5%) and females (19, 82.6%). Table 1.

VAS and PGI score

Kolmogorov–Smirnov test of normality was performed for both VAS and PGI-I scores and they were found to be not normally distributed (P<0.05). To evaluate the changes in VAS score over the three follow-up periods, a Wilcoxon Signed Test was performed between the VAS score of 1st follow-up and 2nd follow-up, as well as 1st and final follow-up. The test revealed a statistically significant negative change in VAS when compared with 1st follow-up in both instances. Between 1st and 2nd follow-ups, z=−7.07, P<0.001 with a large effect size, r=0.54. Whereas when compared between the 1st and final follow-up 6 weeks days later, z=−7.64, P<0.001, also with a significant effect size, r=0.58.

To determine the change in PGI-I score over the follow-up period, another Wilcoxon Signed Test was performed between the PGI-I score of the 1st and 2nd follow-up and the 1st and final follow-up 6 weeks later. The test also found a statistically negative change in PGI-I score in both follow-ups, indicating increased satisfaction following the operation. For 1st and 2nd follow-up, z=−3.32, P=0.001 with a moderate effect size, r=0.25. For 1st and final follow-up, z=−4.0, P<0.001, with effect size, r=0.31.

The progression of the mean VAS score during the follow-up periods is illustrated in Figure 3. There were occurrences of two-point scores during the initial follow-up visit that were absent during subsequent follow-ups. Achieving a score of 1 during the final 6-week follow-up period was limited to two participants (2.40%), suggesting a substantial decrease in pain symptoms.

Hemorrhoid reduction, satisfaction, and incapacity to work

The hemorrhoid size of all participants (n=86, 100%) reduced after the operation, and of 86 patients, 84 (97.7%) were satisfied with the outcome of the procedure. All patients were monitored for several hours to determine whether they developed postoperative complications before being discharged. In the event of a postspinal headache, they were advised to adhere to a bed rest regimen and take paracetamol tablets. After 1.33±0.55 days of recuperation, the patients were able to resume their daily activities.

Postoperative complications

The 42-day complication rate was 19.8% (17/86). All the complications amounted to Clavien–Dindo I–IIIa (100%); iatrogenic laser perianal superficial skin burn: in two cases, postoperative bleeding within 14 days, mild bleeding in eight cases, and moderate bleeding: in two cases. Blood transfusions were not required for any cases of bleeding and were treated with tranexamic acid. A total of four residual pile masses were seen: one patient had residual pile mass with intermittent per-rectal bleeding, which was managed using a Diosmin+Hesperidin (450 mg+50 mg) tablet whereas the other three patients with pile mass did not have per-rectal bleeding and were kept for observation. Three elderly male patients with third-degree and all cases with fourth-degree hemorrhoids underwent LHP with mucopexy. One patient complains of incontinence of flatus with a Vaizey score of 5 out of 24. No systemic complications were seen.

Discussion

This is the first single arm-prospective study in Bangladesh to ascertain the short-term benefits of LHP in three consecutive follow-ups (1 week, 2 weeks, and 6 weeks), to the best of our knowledge. The results that have been replicated indicate that this minimally invasive surgery has a favorable safety and efficacy profile, as well as high levels of patient satisfaction. The VAS score and PGI score experienced substantial declines as the follow-up period progressed.

The mean age of patients in our study was 55.49 years, which is consistent with the prevalence in the real world (45–65 years)17. The majority of the patients had grade 3 hemorrhoids, which can be inferred from the use of traditional methods of treatment, self-medication, and concerns of painful surgery that impede early management, particularly in rural areas of Bangladesh18. Evidence from Bangladesh indicates that laser hemorrhoidoplasty is superior to other therapies9,10,19. Stapled hemorrhoidopexy, transanal hemorrhoidal dearteialization, rubber band ligation, endoscopic injection sclerotherapy, infrared coagulation, aluminum potassium sulfate, and tannic acid (ALTA) sclerotherapy are additional techniques20. In addition to laser treatment for hemorrhoids, various modalities were implemented, including CO2, helium/neon, and Nd-YAG (neodymium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet) lasers. In comparison to other lasers, the laser diode induces photocoagulation, which is characterized by its high precision and brief application duration, as well as a reduced risk of damage to deeper anatomical structures21,22. The laser is employed on the basis of the hypothesis that the hemorrhoidal plexus is congested due to superior hemorrhoidal artery overflow. Consequently, the plexus will be reduced in size, thereby treating the disease23. Laser diodes are available in a variety of wavelengths, including 810 nm, 980 nm, 1064 nm, 1470 nm, and 1940 nm. Nevertheless, our surgical procedure employs a 1470 nm laser diode, which is both safer and more effective than those used in previous investigations24–28.

Patient follow-up resulted in a substantial decrease in the VAS score and PGI score. This was also documented in other investigations11,29–31. Additionally, the pain was alleviated with a single dose of paracetamol 1 gm, in contrast to other operations that necessitated higher doses and a longer duration to achieve analgesic effects. The average hospital stay was 1.06±0.24 days for a daycare procedure, while the comparable hospital stay ranged from 1 to 3 days3,23,31,32,33. The return to normal work activities was restricted to no day-to-day activities, with an average of 1.33±0.55 days. One of the patients was treated with Diosmin+Hesperidin (450 mg+50 mg) daily for intermittent per rectal hemorrhage and residual pile mass. The medical management of hemorrhoidal symptoms, such as pain, heaviness, mucosal discharge, and pruritus, has made use of the combination34–36.

Postoperative hemorrhaging is also a prevalent issue among various complications. Numerous systematic reviews and meta-analyses have demonstrated that laser hemorrhoidoplasty has a lower incidence of postoperative hemorrhage than other medical procedures37–40. Within 14 days of the operation, four individuals experienced hemorrhage (mild-3 and moderate-1). These cases were managed conservatively and did not result in severe consequences.

Additional mucopexy was performed on certain third-degree and all fourth-degree prolapses. The incorporation of suture mucopexy in the correction of mucosal prolapse has been demonstrated to produce exceptional long-term outcomes with an exceptionally low recurrence rate. Grade III hemorrhoids are significantly associated with internal mucosal prolapse, which can be rectified through suture mucopexy in two layers40,41.

LHP has been demonstrated to enhance patient satisfaction by promoting a high quality of life27,39,42. Similarly, these outcomes were observed in our case. The majority of patients resumed their regular activities without any major restrictions after 1 day. The satisfaction rates were also high, and there was a decrease in prolapse. In rural areas, patient satisfaction should not be overlooked, as a negative impression and dread can always serve as a deterrent to pursuing appropriate surgical treatment. In light of this, we recommended that all patients undergo telemedicine or follow-up appointments at any time following their surgery to guarantee the most favorable results.

The patients were able to resume their daily activities with minimal interruption following the operation, as we observed throughout this study. The patients also reported a significant reduction in postoperative pain from the initial follow-up, as well as a high level of satisfaction that persisted in subsequent follow-ups. As previously indicated, patients from rural areas are less likely to seek treatment. Nevertheless, the minimal complications and rapid recovery of LHP can serve as an incentive for them to seek appropriate treatment and eliminate any further complications from hemorrhoids.

However, our investigation is subject to certain limitations. The results of the statistical analysis could have been influenced by the small sample size. Additionally, it was a study conducted at a solitary center with a brief follow-up period. Additional research is necessary to investigate the broad wavelengths of laser diodes and to compare them with other minimally invasive surgeries and emergent themes. Finally, research should be conducted to evaluate the efficacy profiles, which include the recurrence rate and other complications.

Conclusion

LHP is a safe and effective procedure with less pain, higher satisfaction, and shorter operative times. Certain patients require LHP with mucopexy to prevent future complications like recurrence.

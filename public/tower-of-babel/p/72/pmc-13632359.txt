Long-chain omega-3-enriched erythrocyte fatty acid pattern is associated with lower odds of obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease
Authors: Lianhui Chen, Yanni Xu, Jiani Zhang, Pengcheng Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundErythrocyte membrane fatty acids (FAs) reflect medium-term FA status and may capture integrated metabolic profiles beyond individual FAs. Their relationships with obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) remain insufficiently characterized.MethodsWe analyzed nationally representative U.S. adults with available erythrocyte FA measurements. Principal component analysis (PCA) was applied to 21 FAs, and the first five components were retained. Survey-weighted regression models evaluated associations of principal component (PC) scores with body weight status and MASLD defined by vibration-controlled transient elastography and cardiometabolic criteria. Pearson correlations assessed relationships between PC scores and energy-adjusted dietary FA intake. False discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied.ResultsAmong 4,979 adults, a long-chain omega-3-enriched pattern (PC2) was independently associated with lower odds of obesity (OR per 1-SD increase 0.74; 95% CI 0.68–0.81; q < 0.001) and was modestly correlated with higher dietary long-chain omega-3 intake (r = 0.22). Among 3,448 participants with valid elastography data, PC2 was similarly associated with lower odds of MASLD (OR per 1-SD increase 0.73; 95% CI 0.67–0.80; q < 0.001). PC3, characterized by higher eicosadienoic acid, gondoic acid, and linoleic acid, was also inversely associated with both obesity and MASLD.ConclusionIn this cross-sectional study of U.S. adults, a long-chain omega-3-enriched erythrocyte FA pattern was associated with 26% lower odds of obesity and 27% lower odds of MASLD. These findings suggest that erythrocyte FA patterns may reflect integrated dietary and metabolic FA status associated with obesity and MASLD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12937-026-01366-y.Keywords: Erythrocyte membrane fatty acids, Principal component analysis, Obesity, Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), Omega-3 fatty acids

Introduction

Obesity is a chronic multisystem disease that substantially increases the risk of cardiometabolic disorders, including type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD; formerly NAFLD) [1, 2]. MASLD represents the hepatic manifestation of metabolic dysfunction and is closely linked to obesity, insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and altered hepatic lipid handling [3, 4]. Because hepatic steatosis reflects an imbalance among fatty acid uptake, de novo lipogenesis, oxidation, and lipid export, obesity and MASLD together provide complementary phenotypes for examining how fatty acid status relates to adiposity and liver-related metabolic dysfunction [5].

Fatty acids (FAs) are important regulators of metabolic homeostasis, serving not only as energy substrates but also as key constituents of cellular membranes and mediators of lipid signaling [6]. As structural components of cell membranes, erythrocyte membrane FA composition is commonly used as a biomarker of longer-term FA status, showing slower diet-induced turnover than plasma lipids and reflecting the integrated contribution of habitual intake and endogenous metabolic processing [7, 8]. Existing studies have linked erythrocyte FA composition to obesity and obesity-related metabolic phenotypes, but the available evidence has largely relied on individual FAs, predefined FA classes, or selected FA ratios. For example, studies in children or adults have described differences in erythrocyte membrane FA profiles across overweight or obesity status and associations with insulin resistance-related traits, supporting the relevance of erythrocyte FA status to adiposity-related metabolic risk [9–11]. Evidence linking erythrocyte FAs to fatty liver-related markers and phenotypes is more limited. In overweight or obese children, individual erythrocyte membrane FAs have been associated with fatty liver index, a surrogate marker of hepatic steatosis risk, with divergent associations observed for arachidonic acid and palmitic acid [12]. In adults with NAFLD, dietary intervention-related improvement in fatty liver has been accompanied by changes in erythrocyte membrane FA composition, including reductions in selected saturated FAs (SFAs) and monounsaturated FAs (MUFAs), and increases in polyunsaturated FAs (PUFAs) levels [13]. Collectively, these studies support the relevance of erythrocyte FA composition to adiposity and adiposity-related metabolic risk, but the evidence directly linking erythrocyte FAs to fatty liver phenotypes remains limited. The pediatric evidence was based on selected overweight or obese children and used fatty liver index rather than an imaging-based diagnosis of hepatic steatosis, whereas the adult NAFLD evidence mainly came from dietary intervention settings and therefore reflects intervention-related changes rather than population-level associations. Moreover, prior studies have not evaluated erythrocyte FA status in relation to MASLD under the new metabolism-centered nomenclature, nor have they examined obesity and liver-related metabolic dysfunction within the same nationally representative sample. These limitations leave uncertainty about how erythrocyte FA status relates to both adiposity and liver-related metabolic dysfunction in the general adult population.

Beyond these outcome- and population-level limitations, an additional gap concerns how erythrocyte FAs have been modeled. Because erythrocyte membrane FAs reflect both dietary exposure and endogenous metabolic processing, and are interrelated through elongation, desaturation, de novo lipogenesis, and membrane remodeling pathways, they tend to vary as coordinated profiles rather than as isolated biomarkers [14, 15]. Analyses based on single FAs, predefined FA classes, or selected ratios may therefore miss integrated variation in erythrocyte FA composition that is relevant to metabolic phenotypes. Principal component analysis (PCA) and related factor-analytic approaches provide a data-driven method to summarize such correlated FA variation into interpretable pattern scores [16]. In circulating lipids, these approaches have identified FA patterns related to metabolic syndrome and incident type 2 diabetes [17, 18]. Erythrocyte-based pattern modeling has also been reported, but remains limited; a prior PCA study identified erythrocyte FA patterns associated with insulin resistance, including a de novo lipogenesis-related profile associated with higher homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) at baseline and follow-up [19]. However, whether coordinated erythrocyte FA patterns, and their dietary correlates, are associated with adiposity and objectively assessed liver-related metabolic dysfunction remains insufficiently characterized.

To address these gaps, we applied PCA to erythrocyte membrane FAs to derive biologically interpretable FA patterns and examined their associations with body mass index (BMI)-defined body weight status and MASLD in a nationally representative sample of U.S. adults from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). We further evaluated correlations between PCA-derived patterns and dietary FA intake to support biological interpretation. By integrating BMI-defined adiposity and hepatic steatosis outcomes within the same population, this study aimed to clarify how coordinated erythrocyte FA profiles relate to obesity and MASLD.

Methods

Study design and population

This cross-sectional study used data from NHANES (August 2021-August 2023), a nationally representative survey of the non-institutionalized U.S. civilian population conducted using a complex, multistage probability sampling design according to standardized survey procedures described in the NHANES 2021–2023 plan and operations report [20]. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The present analysis used publicly available, de-identified data in accordance with NHANES analytic guidelines.

Participant selection and analytic samples are summarized in Fig. 1. Adults aged ≥ 20 years with complete erythrocyte membrane FA measurements were eligible. After excluding participants who were pregnant, had missing BMI, or had missing key sociodemographic covariates, 5,045 participants comprised the primary analytic cohort.

Flowchart of study population selection and analytic samples. Abbreviations: BMI, body mass index; MASLD, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease

For analyses of body weight status, underweight individuals (BMI < 18.5 kg/m²) were excluded, because the primary objective was to examine associations across the adiposity spectrum from normal weight to overweight and obesity, and underweight may represent a biologically distinct group with potential heterogeneity related to undernutrition, chronic illness, or reverse causation, yielding a final sample of 4,979 participants. For analyses of hepatic steatosis and MASLD, participants were further restricted to those with valid vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) measurements and complete MASLD classification (n = 3,448).

Measurement of erythrocyte membrane FAs

Erythrocyte membrane FAs were measured in NHANES using gas chromatography coupled with electron capture negative-ion mass spectrometry. Esterified FAs were hydrolyzed from washed red blood cell membranes using sequential acid and base treatment under heat, extracted with hexane in the presence of stable isotopically labeled internal standards, and derivatized with pentafluorobenzyl bromide to form pentafluorobenzyl esters. Individual FAs were separated on a capillary gas chromatographic column and quantified by selected ion monitoring based on analyte-to-internal standard peak area ratios. FAs were expressed as the percentage of total identified erythrocyte membrane FAs (21 FAs in total). All laboratory procedures were conducted under standardized NHANES quality assurance and quality control protocols. Detailed laboratory procedures are available in the NHANES Laboratory Methods documentation [21].

Derived FA group metrics and enzymatic proxies

In addition to individual erythrocyte membrane FAs, several predefined FA group metrics and enzymatic proxy indices were calculated to facilitate the biological interpretation of PCs. Group-level metrics included total SFA, total MUFA, total PUFA, total n-6 PUFA, total n-3 PUFA, the omega-3 index [eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) + docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)], and total n-3 highly unsaturated FAs (n-3 HUFA; EPA + docosapentaenoic acid (DPA n-3) + DHA). The n-6/n-3 ratio, AA-to-EPA ratio, and DHA-to-EPA ratio were also computed to characterize relative PUFA balance and commonly used inflammation-related FA indices [22–24].

To characterize endogenous FA metabolism, established product-to-precursor ratios were calculated as widely used indices of estimated desaturase and elongase activities. These included stearoyl-CoA desaturase-1 (SCD1; 16:1n-7/16:0 and 18:1n-9/18:0), delta-5 desaturase (20:4n-6/20:3n-6), delta-6 desaturase (18:3n-6/18:2n-6 and 20:3n-6/18:2n-6), and elongase activity (18:0/16:0, 20:0/18:0, 22:0/20:0, and 24:0/22:0) [25–27].

Definitions of outcomes

BMI was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²). Participants were categorized according to World Health Organization criteria as normal weight (18.5–24.9 kg/m²), overweight (25.0–29.9 kg/m²), or obese (≥ 30.0 kg/m²). Underweight individuals (BMI < 18.5 kg/m²) were excluded from analyses of weight status.

MASLD was defined in accordance with the 2023 international consensus statement, which adopts a positive, metabolism-centered diagnostic framework [2]. Hepatic steatosis was identified using VCTE, with steatosis defined as a controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) ≥ 285 dB/m [28]. Consistent with the consensus definition, MASLD required the presence of hepatic steatosis together with at least one cardiometabolic risk factor, including overweight or obesity, impaired glucose metabolism, dyslipidemia, or elevated blood pressure. Participants with significant alcohol consumption (≥ 210 g/week for men or ≥ 140 g/week for women), viral hepatitis, or other known chronic liver diseases were excluded. The reference group (no steatosis) was restricted to participants with CAP < 285 dB/m and without significant alcohol consumption or other chronic liver diseases, thereby ensuring comparability with the analytic MASLD definition.

Covariates and dietary assessment

Sociodemographic covariates included age, sex, race/ethnicity, family income-to-poverty ratio (PIR), education level, and marital status. Lifestyle-related factors included smoking status, alcohol consumption, and physical activity. Alcohol intake was categorized as non-drinker, light, moderate, or excessive based on self-reported consumption. Sex-specific thresholds were applied using weekly alcohol intake (grams/week): light (< 140 g/week in women or < 210 g/week in men), moderate (140–350 g/week in women or 210–420 g/week in men), and excessive (> 350 g/week in women or > 420 g/week in men). Physical activity was expressed as metabolic equivalent minutes per week (MET-min/week), calculated as 4 × minutes of moderate-intensity activity plus 8 × minutes of vigorous-intensity activity per week. Dietary intake was assessed using up to two 24-hour dietary recalls conducted by trained interviewers using the USDA Automated Multiple-Pass Method according to standardized NHANES dietary interview procedures [29]. When two recalls were available, the mean intake across days was used to reduce within-person variability; otherwise, intake from the first recall was retained.

Statistical analysis

All analyses accounted for the complex, multistage probability sampling design of NHANES using the survey package in R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The phlebotomy subsample weight (WTPH2YR) was applied to obtain nationally representative estimates for adults with available erythrocyte FA measurements, and masked variance strata (SDMVSTRA) and primary sampling units (SDMVPSU) were incorporated to account for the survey design.

Descriptive statistics are presented as survey-weighted medians (interquartile range [IQR]) for continuous variables and unweighted counts with survey-weighted percentages for categorical variables. Group differences were evaluated using design-based Kruskal-Wallis tests for continuous variables and Rao-Scott adjusted chi-square tests for categorical variables.

PCA was applied to a prespecified set of 21 erythrocyte membrane FAs. FAs were standardized (mean-centered and scaled to unit variance) prior to analysis to ensure equal weighting across variables. Because erythrocyte FAs were expressed as percentages of total FAs and were expected to be intercorrelated due to shared dietary sources and endogenous metabolic pathways, PCA was used as an exploratory dimension-reduction approach to summarize coordinated variation across FAs into a smaller number of composite patterns. PC scores for the first 10 components were extracted. Based on variance explained and interpretability of loading patterns, the first five components were retained for subsequent association analyses. The retained PCs were interpreted as patterns of relative FA composition rather than as independent biological entities.

To aid interpretation of the derived patterns, Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated between PC scores and individual erythrocyte FAs. Additional correlations were computed between PC scores and predefined FA group metrics, FA ratios, and enzymatic proxy indices. Correlation matrices were visualized using hierarchical clustering based on Euclidean distance with complete linkage. Associations between PC scores and dietary FA intake were evaluated using Pearson correlation analyses. Dietary FAs were energy-adjusted using the residual method with total energy intake as the independent variable prior to correlation analysis.

For analyses of body weight status (normal weight, overweight, obesity), survey-weighted multinomial logistic regression models were fitted using normal weight as the reference category. Associations between PC scores and MASLD were assessed using survey-weighted logistic regression among participants with valid VCTE data, with no steatosis serving as the reference group. PC scores were modeled as continuous variables (per 1 standard deviation [SD] increase). Three sequential models were specified: Model 1 adjusted for age, sex, and race/ethnicity; Model 2 additionally adjusted for PIR, education, and marital status; and Model 3 further adjusted for smoking status, alcohol consumption, and physical activity.

To account for multiple testing across PC exposures within each outcome family, p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure. In multinomial models, FDR correction was applied across PC exposures and outcome contrasts within each model. In logistic models, FDR correction was applied across the five PC exposures within each outcome-specific model. All statistical tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was defined as an FDR-adjusted q-value < 0.05.

PC2 was characterized primarily by positive loadings for EPA (0.50), DHA (0.46), and DPA n-3 (0.33); consistent with this loading pattern, strong positive correlations were observed with total n-3 PUFA (r = 0.85), the omega-3 index (r = 0.84), and n-3 HUFA (r = 0.85), together with strong inverse correlations with the n-6/n-3 ratio (r = − 0.75) and the AA-to-EPA ratio (r = − 0.67).

PC1 primarily captured variation in overall membrane saturation, with broad positive loadings for PUFA species and negative loadings for saturated FAs, showing a strong positive correlation with total PUFA (r = 0.94) and a strong inverse correlation with total SFA (r = − 0.88).

PC3 was characterized mainly by positive loadings for eicosadienoic acid (0.50), gondoic acid (0.44), and linoleic acid (0.39), and showed inverse correlations with delta-6 desaturase proxies (r = − 0.59). PC4 showed its largest positive loadings for palmitoleic acid (0.48), myristic acid (0.37), and gamma-linolenic acid (0.31), and positive correlations with desaturation-related indices, most notably the SCD1 (16:1n-7/16:0) index (r = 0.72). PC5 showed positive coefficients for MUFA species and negative coefficients for PUFA species; consistent with this pattern, PC5 was positively correlated with total MUFA (r = 0.51).

Associations between PC scores and dietary FA intake

Following characterization of the retained PCs, correlations between erythrocyte membrane FA PC scores and energy-adjusted dietary FA intake were modest in magnitude overall (|r| ≤ 0.23) (Fig. 5). PC2 demonstrated the most coherent dietary pattern, showing positive correlations with dietary long-chain n-3 FAs, including EPA (r = 0.21), DPA (r = 0.17), and DHA (r = 0.23), as well as with total dietary n-3 PUFA (r = 0.22). In addition, PC3 showed modest positive correlations with dietary total PUFA, n-6 PUFA, and linoleic acid (all r = 0.14).

Characteristics of participants by body weight status

As shown in Table 1, among 4,979 adults included in the body weight status analyses, 1,266 (26%) had normal weight, 1,659 (33%) were overweight, and 2,054 (40%) had obesity. Participants with obesity generally showed less favorable lifestyle and cardiometabolic profiles, including lower physical activity, higher sedentary time, higher triglycerides, hsCRP, and HbA1c, and lower HDL cholesterol. Erythrocyte FA patterns also differed across BMI categories, with PC2 and PC3 scores decreasing from normal weight to obesity, whereas PC1 was comparable across groups; PC4 and PC5 differed statistically, but the absolute differences were small.

Associations between erythrocyte FA patterns and obesity status

Survey-weighted multinomial logistic regression models were fitted to examine associations between erythrocyte FA pattern scores and BMI categories, using normal weight as the reference group (Table 2). In fully adjusted models (Model 3), higher PC2 scores were strongly associated with lower odds of obesity. Each 1-SD increase in PC2 was associated with 26% lower odds of obesity (OR = 0.74, 95% CI: 0.68–0.81; q < 0.001). In contrast, the association with overweight was weaker and did not remain statistically significant after FDR correction (OR = 0.95, 95% CI: 0.90–1.00; q = 0.071).

Discussion

In this nationally representative study of U.S. adults, we identified distinct erythrocyte membrane FA patterns that were differentially associated with obesity and MASLD. Among the retained components, the long-chain omega-3-enriched pattern (PC2) showed the most consistent associations: in fully adjusted models, each 1-SD higher PC2 score was associated with 26% lower odds of obesity and 27% lower odds of MASLD. A second pattern (PC3) was also inversely associated with both outcomes, corresponding to 24% lower odds of obesity and 18% lower odds of MASLD per 1-SD increase, whereas PC4 was associated with 13% higher odds of MASLD. Overall, these findings suggest that coordinated erythrocyte FA profiles, particularly those enriched in omega-3 long-chain PUFAs, may reflect integrated FA status associated with obesity and MASLD phenotypes.

The inverse associations observed for the omega-3-enriched pattern (PC2) with both obesity and MASLD are biologically plausible. Long-chain omega-3 FAs such as EPA and DHA are incorporated into cell membranes, where they modulate membrane fluidity and inflammatory signaling [30]. In the liver, long-chain n-3 PUFA downregulate hepatic de novo lipogenesis partly via suppression of the SREBP-1c-regulated lipogenic program and promote FA oxidation through activation of PPARα, thereby limiting hepatic triglyceride accumulation [31]. These mechanisms may be relevant to the inverse association observed for MASLD, a phenotype closely linked to hepatic lipid handling. Importantly, PC2 showed modest but consistent positive correlations with dietary long-chain omega-3 FAs, suggesting that this membrane pattern partly reflects habitual omega-3 intake rather than solely endogenous metabolism. Together with its inverse correlations with the omega-6/omega-3 and AA/EPA ratios, PC2 appears to represent an integrated erythrocyte FA profile associated with long-chain omega-3 status. Collectively, these findings suggest that a long-chain omega-3-enriched erythrocyte FA pattern may reflect integrated dietary and metabolic FA status associated with lower odds of obesity and MASLD.

Beyond the omega-3-related pattern (PC2), PC3, characterized by positive loadings for eicosadienoic acid, gondoic acid, and linoleic acid and inverse correlations with delta-6-desaturase proxies, was also inversely associated with both obesity and MASLD. PC3 showed modest positive correlations with dietary linoleic acid, suggesting that this component may partly reflect essential unsaturated FA intake. Prior evidence has suggested that higher dietary linoleic acid intake is inversely associated with adverse hepatic metabolic outcomes, supporting the possibility that dietary linoleic acid may partly contribute to the inverse association pattern captured by PC3 [32]. The inverse associations of higher PC3 scores with both outcomes are also directionally consistent with prior evidence linking higher estimated delta-6-desaturase activity to greater adiposity, insulin resistance, and steatotic liver risk [25, 33, 34]. In contrast, PC4, which was strongly correlated with SCD1-related indices, was positively associated with MASLD but not with BMI-defined obesity. Taken together, these findings suggest that distinct erythrocyte FA patterns were differentially associated with obesity and MASLD, indicating that coordinated variation in erythrocyte FA composition may relate to obesity and MASLD in different ways.

These findings extend prior erythrocyte FA and pattern-based FA research by addressing several gaps that earlier studies could not fully resolve. Previous erythrocyte FA studies in relation to obesity or fatty liver-related phenotypes were mainly based on individual FAs, selected ratios, or intervention-related profile changes, often in pediatric, clinical, or otherwise selected populations [9–13]. Separately, factor-analytic FA studies have shown that coordinated FA patterns are informative for cardiometabolic outcomes, but circulating lipid studies largely focused on metabolic syndrome or type 2 diabetes [17, 18], whereas the available erythrocyte PCA evidence primarily addressed insulin resistance [19]. The present study connects these previously separate lines of evidence by applying a pattern-based erythrocyte FA framework to both adiposity and liver-related metabolic dysfunction in the same nationally representative adult sample. Its main contribution is therefore not simply the association of one FA with one outcome, but the demonstration that coordinated erythrocyte membrane FA variation can be linked to VCTE-defined MASLD while also being interpretable in relation to dietary FA intake and BMI-defined obesity. This approach provides a more integrated population-level assessment of erythrocyte FA status than prior single-analyte, ratio-based, or intervention-focused studies, and supports the use of erythrocyte FA patterns as a biologically interpretable exposure framework for studying obesity- and MASLD-related metabolic dysfunction.

This study has several strengths. It leveraged a nationally representative sample with standardized laboratory measurements, supporting the generalizability of the findings to U.S. adults. Erythrocyte membrane FAs were assessed as objective biomarkers of medium-term FA status, thereby reducing reliance on self-reported dietary data and short-term variability. In addition, the comprehensive and standardized dietary assessment in NHANES, including up to two 24-hour dietary recalls, allowed us to examine the dietary correlates of erythrocyte FA patterns and to interpret these patterns in the context of habitual FA intake. The application of PCA enabled the characterization of coordinated FA patterns rather than isolated FAs, aligning more closely with the integrated biology of membrane lipid composition. Analyses appropriately accounted for the complex survey design and incorporated sequential multivariable adjustment with FDR correction to limit spurious findings. Finally, MASLD was defined using VCTE with an established CAP threshold, enhancing phenotypic specificity.

Several limitations merit consideration. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and reverse causation cannot be excluded, as metabolic disorders may themselves alter FA metabolism and membrane remodeling. Accordingly, this design does not allow us to determine whether the observed erythrocyte FA patterns precede the development of obesity or MASLD, or instead arise as a consequence of these conditions. Although erythrocyte FAs reflect medium-term status, they integrate both dietary exposure and endogenous metabolic regulation, which limits direct mechanistic interpretation. Residual confounding may persist despite adjustment for major sociodemographic and lifestyle factors, and certain covariates (e.g., alcohol intake and physical activity) were self-reported. In addition, because erythrocyte FAs were analyzed as percentages of total FAs, they represent relative compositional measures; accordingly, the PCA-derived components should be interpreted as patterns of relative FA composition rather than absolute biological quantities. Also, PCA-derived patterns are data-driven and sample-specific, their structure may not be identical across populations. In addition, BMI-defined obesity is an imprecise proxy for adiposity because it does not directly measure body fat mass or fat distribution; measures such as waist circumference or more direct body-composition assessments may better capture central or ectopic adiposity [35]. Nevertheless, BMI remains a practical and widely used indicator in population-based studies. Furthermore, the present study was not designed to determine whether erythrocyte FA patterns improve identification or prediction of MASLD beyond conventional obesity-related measures, nor to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of such measurements; these questions warrant dedicated investigation in future studies. Finally, the modest correlations between PC scores and dietary FA intake indicate that membrane patterns capture influences beyond diet alone, but also suggest that direct dietary extrapolation should be made with caution.

Conclusion

In this nationally representative study of U.S. adults, an erythrocyte membrane FA pattern enriched in long-chain omega-3 PUFA was consistently associated with 26% lower odds of obesity and 27% lower odds of MASLD. These findings suggest that erythrocyte FA patterns, particularly omega-3-related patterns, may reflect integrated dietary and metabolic FA status associated with obesity and MASLD. Prospective studies with repeated measurements are warranted to determine whether these erythrocyte FA patterns precede or predict future obesity- and MASLD-related outcomes, and mechanistic studies are needed to clarify the biological pathways underlying these associations.

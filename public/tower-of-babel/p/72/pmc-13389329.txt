Network security situation prediction method based on KG and Parsimonious memory unit
Authors: Zhiyong Sun, Liheng Li, Yang Zong, Daoyu Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the continuous development of the Internet, the application scope of the Internet is constantly expanding, and network security issues are becoming increasingly prominent. To address long prediction time and low prediction efficiency in the current network security situation prediction model, this study first designs a KG of network security situation. Afterwards, the GCN and MLP are utilized to extract features from the data in the KG as data for the subsequent prediction model. It then uses the CBAM and PMU to predict the network security situation based on feature data. The test results demonstrated that during feature extraction, the feature extraction accuracy could reach 98.4%, and the prediction accuracy of the optimized PMU could reach 98.5%. Moreover, according to the designed model, its prediction accuracy could reach 96.7%, the prediction time only took 11.2 ms, and the MAE was only 0.081, which means the model’s prediction effect is good. When the model predicted under ± 30% network traffic disturbance, large wide area network conditions, 20% equipment failure, and network virus attacks, its prediction accuracy was higher than 90%. The research method can predict the network security situation, thereby providing early warning information for managers and reducing network risks.

Introduction

The continuous advancement of Internet technology has made the application scope of the network continue to expand1. Network attack methods are also becoming more and more complex. Attacks on the network will cause huge losses to individuals and enterprises2. The prediction model can judge the future development trend of the event based on existing data and take various measures based on the results3. Network Security Situation (NSS) prediction can detect security threats and abnormal trends in the network in advance, thereby providing early warning information to network security managers, thereby reminding managers to detect the network and effectively reduce network security risks4,5. Moreover, with the development of artificial intelligence, many intelligent algorithms have been used for NSS prediction.

Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) has been used to predict network security, but this method also has problems with long-term dependencies and poor dynamic change processing when predicting data, which limits its prediction accuracy6. Subsequently, some scholars introduced the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) to improve the prediction effect. However, the calculation of the LSTM algorithm takes a long time and is not conducive to real-time monitoring7. In recent years, some scholars have used KG (KG) to construct a graph topology structure about network situation elements to predict network security8. Although the constructed KG can predict NSS through its graph topology structure, the NSS prediction effect is poor due to the complex graph topology data and many redundant features9. Therefore, using KG alone to predict NSS is less feasible.

To solve the above problems, this study proposes to use Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) to process the Graph Structure Data (GSD) in KG, and use Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) to extract features from the GCN-processed data to remove redundant information in the graph topology structure. It then uses the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) to optimize the Parsimonious Memory Unit (PMU) to improve the PMU’s prediction efficiency of data trends. The main contributions and innovations of this research: (1) A KG graph topology structure about NSS elements is constructed to serve as the basic data for NSS prediction. (2) A GCN-MLP algorithm is designed using GCN and MLP to extract the features in the KG graph topology, remove redundant data, and reduce the time-consuming of subsequent predictions. (3) CBAM is used to optimize the PMU algorithm, so that PMU pays more attention to the important feature information of NSS prediction data when predicting data, thereby enhancing the prediction accuracy of PMU. (4) The constructed KG graph topology, GCN-MLP, and CBAM-PMU are organically combined to design an NSS prediction method to predict NSS.

The structure of this research: Sect. 1 introduces the current status and existing problems of NSS prediction, and introduces the methods that need to be used to solve the problems. Sections 2 and 3 discusses and details the work related to research methods. Section 4 introduces the environmental setup and result analysis, including ablation studies, comparisons of predictive models with advanced methods, and test results in real scenarios. Section 5 summarizes the main findings and proposes future directions.

Literature review

At present, many scholars have studied NSS prediction methods. A. Nazir and A. Binthiya et al. used the CNN algorithm to predict NSS. When using CNN alone to predict NSS, due to the poor analysis effect of CNN on data dependence, its prediction effect on NSS was poor, their prediction accuracies were 83.2% and 81.7%, respectively10,11. C. Sivanandam et al. used LSTM to predict NSS in 2024. When this algorithm predicted data, although the prediction accuracy improved by 12.2% compared to CNN, the LSTM algorithm’s prediction time is higher than that of CNN, resulting in lower overall efficiency12. S. Tripathi et al. also employed the LSTM algorithm for cybersecurity situational awareness prediction. Test results showed a 10.1% improvement in prediction accuracy but a 13.1% increase in prediction time13. M. Koca team has designed an anomaly detection attack model based on Apache Spark, but this model aims to reduce organizational costs and does not require high accuracy14. And the M. Koca team has also used CNN algorithm to detect gestures and perform security risk assessment. The test results show that the accuracy of gesture detection can reach 99.89%, but it cannot evaluate the security situation of gesture free command networks15. E. Gilliard et al. introduced KG for predicting network security situation in 2024. The test results showed that this method can improve the visualization level of network security situation elements by 17.2%. However, due to the large number of features and difficulty in understanding graph structured data, the prediction time of the prediction model was extended by 13.2%, and the accuracy was reduced by 8.9%16. L. Jin et al. also used KG to construct network security graph topology to predict intelligent transportation network security. Using KG alone can only improve prediction efficiency by 8.1%17.

GCN can process data in the graph topology structure and convert the GSD into vector data to facilitate subsequent predictions. L. Du et al. used GCN to process GSD in KG. After the introduction of GCN, the data in the graph topology could be converted into node representation vectors18. MLP can extract features through a multi-layer convolution structure. B. Xiao et al. used MLP to extract feature information from the original data in 2025. The feature extraction accuracy of MLP can reach 87.2%19. X. Yu et al. integrated the MLP and GCN algorithms to improve the feature extraction capabilities of MLP. After fusion, the feature extraction accuracy increased by 10.4%20. PMU is a simplified LSTM algorithm that removes redundant structures to improve its prediction speed. The Q. Liu team used the PMU algorithm to predict the normal distribution transformation in the network, but due to the large amount of network feature data, its prediction accuracy was only 90.2%21. O. Orang et al. also used the PMU algorithm to predict high-dimensional time series, but individual PMU did not perform feature extraction during the prediction process, resulting in a prediction accuracy of only 89.2%22. Y. Feng et al. used CBAM to assign weights to features in the data, improved the accuracy of the prediction model by 10.2%23.

In summary, the current NSS prediction model still has problems such as low prediction accuracy and long prediction time due to too many NSS elements and complex features. Given this, this study first uses KG to construct a graph topology structure about network security elements, and then uses GCN to process the GSD and convert the GSD into node vectors. Afterwards, it uses MLP to extract data features based on node vectors and remove redundant information to facilitate subsequent predictions. Moreover, CBAM is used to optimize the PMU so that the PMU focuses more on important features and improves its prediction accuracy. The designed model accurately analyzes NSS to ensure network security and resist network attacks.

Methodology

This study proposes an NSS prediction method based on KG, GCN-MLP, and CBAM-PMU. This method first constructs a KG about NSS elements to provide basic data for situation prediction. Afterwards, it uses GCN-MLP to extract features from the GSD in KG and uses it as intermediate data for NSS prediction. Finally, CBAM is used to optimize PMU, making PMU focus more on key features and improving the prediction accuracy of PMU. The constructed combination of KG, GCN-MLP, and CBAM-PMU results in a new NSS prediction model.

NSS elements and KG construction process

Nowadays, various network security issues have become more and more prominent, and accurate prediction of NSS is particularly important24. KG is a network that describes the objective world through a graph structure and can explain information such as concepts, entities and their relationships25. This study constructs a KG based on NSS elements to mine the connections between network data and provide data support for subsequent NSS predictions. The KG construction process based on NSS elements is shown in Fig. 1.

Fig. 1KG construction process.

KG construction process.

In Fig. 1, when building KG, the collected relevant data about NSS elements first need to be preprocessed. During preprocessing, the data was first cleaned, missing values are filled, and then the processed data are normalized to ensure the uniformity of the data. After data preprocessing, KG modeling is performed on network security elements to organize entities, attribute constraints, and relationships between attributes in the network field to obtain a standardized knowledge framework diagram. NSS elements are mainly composed of four categories: equipment, vulnerability, attack and incident. There will be a receiving and sending relationship between the device class and the event class, and there will also be a relationship between the device class and the vulnerable class. The attack class will use the vulnerable class to cause network attacks on the device class. The NSS element KG model can be constructed through the above relationships. It then constructs a KG based on the KG model, and first extracts information in the four categories of NSS elements from the preprocessed data as the basic data for KG construction. The category information in the information is used as the node in the KG, and the relationship in the data is used as the edge in the KG, so as to obtain the KG structure about the NSS elements. NSS feature learning based on GCN-MLP

After constructing the KG about NSS, in order to predict NSS, the GSD in the KG needs to be processed26. Moreover, the constructed KG contains a lot of data. When predicting NSS, it is also necessary to extract the feature information in KG first to reduce the time required for subsequent predictions. GCN is suitable for processing graph-structured data, and MLP can continue to extract features of the input data through its hierarchical nonlinear transformation structure and back-propagation optimization mechanism27. This study uses GCN to process the data in KG, and then uses MLP to extract feature information from the data. The basic structure of GCN and the KG data processing flow based on GCN are shown in Fig. 3.

Fig. 3GCN structure and GCN data processing flow.

GCN structure and GCN data processing flow.

In Fig. 3(a), GCN includes the layers of input, convolution, nonlinear activation, multi-layer stacking, and output layer. The input layer receives the entity’s own attribute matrix and the topological structure of the carrying graph. The convolutional layer is the core operation in GCN. It sums the neighbor features of each node according to the normalized weight to perceive the data structure. Nonlinear activation performs nonlinear transformation on the convolutional aggregation result to enhance the expressive ability. Multi-layer stacking performs multiple convolution and activation operations. With each additional convolution and activation operation, the receptive field of the node will expand by one order, and finally the structural characteristics of the data will be obtained. Finally, the output layer compresses the dimensions of the high-dimensional structural information through a Fully Connected Layer (FCL), and finally outputs the data structure information. NSS prediction method based on PMU and CBAM

After extracting the features of NSS through GCN-MLP, it is necessary to predict NSS based on the feature data. PMU is a kind of neural network that removes redundant parts such as input gates and forgetting gates in traditional gated units, and only retains memory vectors, hidden states and unit gates28. It can learn the timing of data through a more concise structure to predict future trends29,30. However, NSS features contain key features and secondary features, and it is difficult for PMU to distinguish feature weights, resulting in poor prediction results. CBAM can allocate attention weights to the data in two dimensions: channel and space, allowing the model to pay more attention to important information and ignore irrelevant information31,32. Therefore, this study uses CBAM to optimize PMU and proposes a CBAM-PMU to predict NSS. Figure 6 shows the basic structure of PMU and the prediction process of CBAM-PMU.

This stage first collects data related to NSS elements, including network device type, model, manufacturer, location, service port and other device data. The features extracted by GCN-MLP are converted into the temporal input structure of the subsequent CBAM-PMU through a sliding window mechanism. The conversion process is as follows: Firstly, the KG images within every 5 min are processed by GCN-MLP to generate a 64 dimensional fixed length vector; Afterwards, the feature vectors of the past 6 historical time periods are stacked into a 6 × 64 temporal matrix, and concatenated with 8-dimensional time encoding to form the final 6 × 72 input tensor, which is the temporal input structure of the subsequent CBAM-PMU. In the situation prediction stage, the optimized CBAM-PMU is used to first allocate the weight of the feature information, and then the simplified PMU gating unit is used to learn the time series characteristics of the data to obtain the future change trend of the data. Setup and analysis

Datasets and experimental parameter

To test the research method, UNSW-NB15 was selected as the test dataset. This dataset is a public dataset used for network intrusion detection research, providing a mixture of real network traffic and synthetic attacks to support the testing of security prediction methods. This study selects 10,000 pieces of data and divides them into the sets of training. Due to the fact that network security situation prediction is a temporal prediction task, it is necessary to simulate the scenario of “using historical data to predict the future” in real scenarios. Random partitioning can lead to future information being mixed into the training set, resulting in information leakage. So in this study, the dataset was divided into training set, testing set, and validation set in a ratio of 7:2:1 in chronological order. The data are first normalized to eliminate the impact of different dimensions on the model, and then the data are regularized to avoid simulation overfitting problems. When constructing a knowledge graph using the NUSW-NB15 dataset, the first step is to parse each individual traffic measurement in the dataset, extract the source IP or destination IP as an instantiated entity of a device, map the combination of attack type and port to a vulnerability entity, and map the entire record itself to a specific attack entity. When the attack is successful, a high-level event entity is generated. Subsequently, based on the semantic relationships defined by the ontology, directed edges are constructed, such as establishing a “communication” relationship between IP nodes that includes protocol and traffic attributes, establishing a “possesses vulnerability” relationship between devices and vulnerabilities, establishing a “initiate attack” relationship by pointing from the source device to the attack instance, and establishing a “exploit/target” relationship by pointing from the attack instance to the victim device or specific vulnerability. If a serious consequence is triggered, it is further connected to the “event” node to form a causal chain. Finally, by traversing all the datasets, duplicate entities are merged and attributes are updated at the nodes, and all the generated triples are stored in the graph database, thereby reorganizing the originally isolated traffic data of the NUSW-NB15 dataset into a cybersecurity situation knowledge graph with 9,634 nodes and 14,784 edges, rich in semantic associations. And, map different device types, models, etc. of equipment data in the UNSW-NB15 dataset to device nodes; Mapping different types of attacks in attack data to attack nodes; Different event types in event data are mapped to event nodes. Construct edges between corresponding nodes based on the receiving and sending relationships between devices and events, the existence relationships between devices and vulnerabilities, and the utilization relationships between attacks and vulnerabilities in the data. According to the statistical information of the KG dataset, the total number of points is 9634, the total number of edges is 14,784, the number of entity types is 4, the number of relationship types is 6, and the average node degree is 3.07. The processed data are fed into the model for training. The data preprocessing process strictly follows the principle of independent fitting of the training set to avoid data leakage: firstly, normalization is only performed on the training set, calculating the maximum and minimum values of each feature in the training set, generating normalization parameters, and applying them to the validation and testing sets. The Min Max normalization formula is used to scale the features to the [0,1] interval; Secondly, regularization processing (L2 regularization) is also only implemented during the training phase, constraining model complexity through weight decay coefficients, and the validation and test sets do not participate in any parameter learning or statistical calculation. Summary

To address poor prediction effect and long prediction time in the current NSS prediction model, this study first constructed the KG topology structure of NSS elements, and then used GCN-MLP to extract the NSS element features in the topology structure. Afterwards, CBAM-PMU was used to predict based on the extracted features, and a KGMCP model was designed to improve its prediction accuracy. In the test, compared with MLP, the feature extraction accuracy of GCN-MLP increased by 11.1%, and the prediction accuracy of CBAM-PMU increased by 16.2% compared with PMU. Introducing GCN into MLP and adding CBAM into PMU could improve the performance. The prediction accuracy of KGMCP, WOA-DBN, IGWO-XGBoost, and LASSO-RSF in the UNSW-NB15 dataset was 96.7%, 91.2%, 89.4%, and 88.2%, while their MSE values were 0.021, 0.036, 0.046, and 0.062. KGMCP had the best effect. Moreover, when predicting NSS under ± 30% network traffic disturbance, large wide area network conditions, 20% equipment failure, and network virus attacks in actual scenarios, the prediction accuracy was higher than 94% and the prediction time was less than 20 ms. Although KGMCP can accurately predict NSS and greatly improve its prediction accuracy, its prediction time and memory usage are not much different from other models. Future work can use methods such as lightweight shearing to lightweight the model to further shorten prediction time and reduce memory usage.

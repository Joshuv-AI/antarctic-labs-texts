FAM3B overexpression correlates with poor prognosis and immune dysregulation in colorectal cancer: insights from bioinformatics and Mendelian randomization analyses
Authors: Peng Huang, Pei Li, Chengshuo Zhang, Jialin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundColorectal cancer (CRC) ranks third in global cancer diagnoses and is the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths. Identifying novel therapeutic targets and effective prognostic markers is crucial to improve CRC outcomes. This study aimed to investigate the role of FAM3B in relation to clinicopathological features, prognosis, tumor microenvironment, and immune infiltration of CRC.MethodsWe obtained data from bioinformatics databases, immunohistochemical experiments, and two-sample Mendelian randomization analyses. Differential expression analysis, survival analysis, Cox regression, and tumor microenvironment/immune infiltration evaluation were performed. FAM3B expression patterns were validated by immunohistochemistry. The causal relationship between FAM3B and CRC risk was explored using SNP data obtained from IEU GWAS and FinnGen by calculating the MR-Egger regression, weighted median, and inverse-variance weighting estimations.ResultsFAM3B was significantly upregulated in CRC tissues compared to normal tissues (p < 0.001). High FAM3B expression was associated with poor prognosis (HR: 1.59, 95% CI 1.00–2.54, p < 0.05), advanced tumor stages, and metastasis. Patients with high FAM3B expression had lower overall survival rates compared to those with low expression. High FAM3B levels were correlated with increased tumor cell purity and specific immune infiltration, including higher levels of M1 macrophages and lower levels of CD8+ T cells, resting NK cells, and monocytes (p < 0.05). The multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed FAM3B as an independent prognostic factor. Mendelian randomization analysis indicated a causal relationship between FAM3B and CRC risk (IVW method OR = 1.07, 95% CI 1.00–1.14, p = 0.035).ConclusionHigh FAM3B expression levels are correlated with poor prognosis and advanced tumor stages in CRC patients, likely influencing CRC development by regulating the tumor microenvironment characteristics. These findings suggest that FAM3B could be a potential CRC therapeutic target, and the constructed clinical predictive model offers new insights for the diagnosis and treatment of CRC patients.Keywords: CRC, FAM3B, Bioinformatics analysis, Immune infiltration; mendelian randomization analysis

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) currently ranks third among global cancer diagnoses and is the second cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, posing a major public health challenge [1]. Metastases were identified in 20% of CRC patients at initial diagnosis, with those experiencing distant metastasis having a less than 14% 5-year survival rate [2]. Current therapeutic approaches for metastatic CRC include multi-agent chemotherapy regimens, targeted therapies, and immune checkpoint inhibitors [3]. However, despite the advancements, treatment resistance, tumor heterogeneity, and limited response rates in microsatellite stable tumors remain clinical challenges [4]. Recent developments in circulating tumor DNA monitoring and combination immunotherapies have been shown to improve the clinical outcomes of metastatic CRC patients [5], while novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets are still critically needed.

The family with sequence similarity 3 (FAM3) consists of four members encoding secreted cytokine-like proteins. FAM3 members have been shown to play critical roles in multiple physiological and pathological processes, such as glucose metabolism, lipogenesis, and insulin secretion. Additionally, FAM3 members have been recognized for their involvement in disease development in diabetes and cancer. FAM3B was originally identified as islet-specific modulating glucose homeostasis and insulin secretion [6, 7]. Moreover, it was found to participate in multiple molecular activities, such as apoptosis and inflammation [8, 9]. Furthermore, recent evidence has highlighted the pivotal contributions of the family with sequence similarity 3 (FAM3) members in driving the malignant behavior and development of various cancer types in humans [10]. Among them, FAM3B, initially found in the endocrine pancreas, has attracted our attention due to its participation in cancer development. Particularly, upregulated FAM3B has been demonstrated to facilitate the progression of cancers in the colon, esophagus, and prostate [8, 11, 12]. However, its potential clinical significance and implication for patients with CRC have not been interrogated thoroughly.

As an emerging cancer-associated molecule, the roles of FAM3B in tumor initiation and progression are still not fully understood and require further investigation [13, 14]. Comprehensively deciphering the expression patterns, functional impacts, and upstream/downstream regulatory networks of FAM3B in CRC will not only provide novel insights into the pathogenesis of CRC but also lay the foundation for identifying potential disease biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Furthermore, whether the roles of FAM3B are similar across different cancer types remains to be determined. Although previous studies have clearly demonstrated the functions of FAM3B in colorectal cancer, esophageal cancer, and prostate cancer, its specific roles and clinical implications in CRC are lacking in-depth investigation [9, 11]. Systematic research on the roles of FAM3B in CRC development and its clinical application prospects may provide new perspectives and approaches for the precise diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis evaluation of CRC.

Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis leverages genetic variations to act as instrumental variables, facilitating the investigation of causal links between exposures and outcomes. By doing so, MR effectively reduces the influence of confounding factors and the possibility of reverse causation, thereby providing more reliable evidence for causality [15]. In the context of unraveling the intricate interplay between FAM3B expression levels and CRC prognosis, MR presents a unique opportunity to disentangle this association. By exploiting genetic variants that are robustly linked to FAM3B expression as instrumental variables, MR can shed light on whether the observed relationship between FAM3B and CRC outcomes is indeed causal or merely correlative. This analytical framework holds the potential to provide invaluable insights into the mechanistic underpinnings of FAM3B’s role in CRC progression and prognosis, paving the way for novel therapeutic strategies targeting this molecular pathway. In this study, we adopted a multi-pronged strategy, integrating bioinformatics analyses and histological validation.

Method and materials

Data preprocessing

The gene expression profiles raw count data were sourced from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) databases, encompassing 650 CRC samples and 779 normal colon tissue samples. Clinical data for 630 CRC patients, including survival time, survival status, age, gender, tumor stage, and the primary tumor (T), regional lymph node (s) involvement (N) (TNM) stage classification, were also retrieved from the TCGA database. This comprehensive dataset provided the transcriptomic and clinicopathological information required for subsequent integrative bioinformatics analyses. The protein-coding gene expression data were merged, and the batch effects were removed using R (version 4.3.0) with the SVA R package (version 3.48). Protein-coding genes from CRC and normal colorectal tissues were subjected to differential expression analysis using the DESeq2 R package (|Log2FC|>1, Padj < 0.05).

To validate our findings from bioinformatics analysis, CRC tissues (n = 35) and the adjacent non-tumor tissues (n = 35) were collected from patients who underwent surgeries at the Department of General Surgery of Panjin Central Hospital. The inclusion criteria were: (1) Pathologically confirmed primary CRC without other malignancies; (2) Newly diagnosed cases without prior neoadjuvant therapy (chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy); (3) Complete clinicopathological records available. The exclusion criteria were: (1) Age < 18 years at diagnosis; (2) Received preoperative neoadjuvant therapy; (3) Severe perioperative complications or mortality; (4) Incomplete clinical records. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Panjin Central Hospital (approval number: 20241121LWLL-KS-01) and was performed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Bioinformatics analysis

Data obtained from the TCGA and GTEx databases, containing follow-up time, survival status, the clinicopathological records, were used to determine how FAM3B is associated with the CRC patients’ overall survival, clinicopathological features (age, gender, tumor stage, and TNM stages), tumor microenvironments (TME), and immune infiltration in CRC.

To assess the overall survival differences between high and low FAM3B expression groups, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed using the survival R package (version 3.5, surv_cutpoint function). Statistical significance was determined with the log-rank test, and survival curves were plotted with the survminer R package (version 0.4.9). Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis examined the effects of age, gender, tumor stage, TNM stage, and FAM3B expression levels (high vs. low) using the survival R package (version 3.5, coxph function). Significant variables from this analysis were then included in a multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify independent prognostic factors for CRC survival. These factors were utilized to develop a predictive model for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities using the rms R package (version 7.2), and the model’s performance was assessed via concordance index (C-index), receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, and calibration curve. The estimate R package (version 1.0.13) analyzed the TME of CRC samples from the TCGA database to obtain stromal, immune, and estimate scores. Comparisons between high and low FAM3B expression groups were made using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (p < 0.05 considered statistically significant). Immune infiltration profiles were assessed using CIBERSORT [16], and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests compared the infiltration levels of immune cells between the different FAM3B expression groups. Spearman correlation analysis evaluated the relationship between FAM3B expression and the abundance of specific immune cells.

Mendelian randomization analysis

The genetic instrumental variables (IVs) for FAM3B were sourced from the IEU OpenGWAS project (GWAS ID: prot-a-1050) and involved 3,301 samples and 10,534,735 SNPs. CRC outcome data were obtained from the FinnGen database (R10 version, finngen_R10_C3_COLORECTAL_EXALLC). Only publicly available summarized data were used, eliminating the need for ethical approval. MR analysis assessed the impact of FAM3B expression’s impact on CRC risk based on three assumptions: (a) IVs must correlate with FAM3B protein levels; (b) IVs should be independent of the outcome (CRC) given the exposure; and (c) the IVs must be independent of all confounders. IVs for FAM3B were selected based on the following criteria: (a) SNPs with a p-value < 0.000005, (b) clumping distance of 10,000 kb, (c) r2 < 0.001 to minimize linkage disequilibrium, (d) minor allele frequency > 0.01, and (e) non-palindromic SNPs. Confounding IVs were removed using a PhenoScanner. We employed three statistical methods in the MR analysis: MR-Egger regression, weighted median (WM) method, and inverse variance weighting (IVW) method. The IVW random-effects model was utilized when p < 0.05, while the IVW fixed-effects model was employed when p > 0.05. The results were visualized with corresponding p-values, odds ratios (ORs), and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). An OR > 1 indicated that the exposure factor was opposed to the outcome, whereas an OR < 1 suggested the exposure factor benefitted the outcome. Heterogeneity among SNPs was evaluated using Cochran’s Q test with the MR-R package in R. Forest plots visualized heterogeneity test results. The Egger-intercept method and MR-PRESSO test assessed pleiotropy. A p-value > 0.05 suggested no influence of the exposure variable on the outcome via other pathways, whereas a p-value < 0.05 indicated pleiotropy. Sensitivity analysis using the leave-one-out method was conducted to identify and assess outliers’ impact following the MR-PRESSO method.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

After de-paraffinization and rehydration, the tissue sections were treated with EvVision™ FLEX Target Retrieval Solution (pH 9.0, Dako, DM828). Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using EnVision™ FLEX Peroxidase-Blocking Reagent (Dako, SM801) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The sections were then incubated with TBST (Cell Signaling Technology, 99975) and 5% goat serum (Sigma, G9032) for 1 h, followed by incubation with an anti-FAMB3 antibody (1:200, 27131-1-AP, Proteintech, 1.5 h, room temperature). After washing, sections were incubated with EnVision™ FLEX/HRP detection reagent (Dako, SM802) as per protocol, then incubated with 3,3-diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate (Dako, DM827) for 5 min followed by washing and dehydration and covered with mounting medium.

Two pathologists blinded to this study evaluated and scored the images under a microscope. For each section, five random fields were selected and examined at 200× magnification. The immunoreactive score system was used to determine the expression levels of FAM3B [12]. In case of disagreement between the two pathologists, consensus was reached through re-evaluating the sections under a discussion microscope. Specifically, staining intensity was categorized as follows: 0 (no staining), 1 (light brown), 2 (moderate brown), and 3 (dark brown). The percentage of positively stained cells was scored as follows: 1 (0%–25%), 2 (26%–50%), 3 (51%–75%), and 4 (76%–100%). The product of these two scores resulted in a final staining score, which was classified into four levels: 0 (score 0–3), 1 (score 4–6), 2 (score 7–9), and 3 (score 10–12). Levels 0 and 1 indicated low FAM3B expression, while levels 2 and 3 indicated high FAM3B expression. To compare FAM3B expression between CRC and adjacent non-cancerous tissues, a chi-square (χ2) test was performed, with a significance level of p < 0.05. A power analysis confirmed that at least 62 (including CRC and adjacent tissues) were needed for this study. With a total sample size of 70, the study was adequately powered to ensure reliable immunohistochemistry results. SPSS (version 26.0, IBM Corp., USA) was used to perform the data analysis.

Results

FAM3B is upregulated in CRC tissues and is associated with the overall survival of CRC patients

To interrogate the role of FAM3B in CRC progression, sequencing data from TCGA and GTEX databases were merged and normalized, followed by differential expression analysis. The results demonstrated a significant upregulation of FAM3B in cancerous colorectal tissues compared to normal colorectal tissues (Fig. 1A). Further processing of TCGA data, removing cases with less than 30 days follow-up or missing survival status, yielded 581 CRC patients. These patients were divided into high and low FAM3B expression groups and compared their overall survival rates using the survival R package. Results indicated that high FAM3B expression levels were correlated with a significantly lower overall survival rate (Fig. 1B).

To validate the findings from bioinformatics analyses, FAM3B expression was examined in cancerous and normal colorectal tissues through IHC staining. The staining intensity of FAM3B was notably higher in CRC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 2). Specifically, the χ2 test revealed significant differences in FAM3B expression between cancerous and adjacent normal colorectal tissues. In CRC tissues, 16 out of 35 samples exhibited high FAM3B expression, while only 3 out of 35 adjacent normal colorectal tissues displayed high FAM3B expression (Table 1). Together, these findings suggested that FAM3B may contribute to the initiation/progression of the malignancy.

Association between FAM3B expression levels and clinicopathological features of CRC patients

We also determined the relationship between FAM3B expression and the age, gender, tumor stage, and TNM classifications of the CRC patients (n = 629, one patient was removed due to missing all clinicopathological records) (Table 2). A heatmap displayed the associated clinicopathological features for these 510 CRC patients (Fig. 3A). We identified that FAM3B expression is significantly higher in patients with distant metastasis (M1) compared to those without metastasis (M0) (Fig. 3B, p < 0.05). In addition, FAM3B expression was significantly upregulated in patients at stage IV compared to patients at stages I–III (Fig. 3C, p < 0.05). To reveal the potential prognostic factors, we removed patients whose clinicopathological records are not complete and obtained a cohort of 510 CRC patients. The median follow-up period was 1.86 years, during which 101 patients (19.8%) had died by the end of the study. There were 348 patients (68.2%) over 60 years old and 275 (53.9%) who were male. The number of patients with stage I, II, III, and IV disease was 94 (18.4%), 194 (38.0%), 140 (27.5%), and 82 (16.1%), respectively. T1, T2, T3, and T4 stage patients numbered 17 (3.2%), 91 (17.8%), 351 (68.8%), and 51 (10.0%), respectively. There were 298 (58.4%) N0, 123 (24.1%) N1, and 89 (17.5%) N2 patients. M0 and M1 stage patients were 428 (83.9%) and 82 (16.1%), respectively. FAM3B was highly expressed in 86 patients (16.9%) and lowly expressed in 424 patients (83.1%).

Construction and validation of a survival prediction model in CRC

Furthermore, using the identified independent prognostic factors, a nomogram was developed to estimate 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities for CRC patients with the rms R package (Fig. 5A). The model’s predictive performance was assessed using the C-index, ROC curve analysis, and calibration plots. A C-index value of 0.741 (95% CI 0.687–0.795) indicated strong discriminatory capability. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) values were 0.756 for 1-year, 0.777 for 3-year, and 0.764 for 5-year survival, indicating the model’s robust predictive accuracy (Fig. 5B). Calibration plots demonstrated excellent alignment between predicted and actual survival probabilities for all three time points (Fig. 5C). Collectively, these metrics validated this nomogram as a reliable and precise clinical prediction model for estimating CRC patient prognosis based on the integrated variables. The thorough evaluation process adhered to best practices for developing and testing prognostic models in oncology.

Impact of FAM3B expression on TME and immune infiltration in CRC

Apart from the clinical and macroscopic features of the CRC, we also investigated the cellular events, such as TME and immune infiltration, that are potentially affected by FAM3B. First, we interrogated the TME analysis on CRC samples from the TCGA database stratified by FAM3B expression levels. We noticed significant differences in stromal score, immune score, and ESTIMATE score between high and low FAM3B expression groups (Fig. 6A) (p < 0.05), indicating higher tumor cell purity in the high FAM3B expression group. Subsequently, utilizing CIBERSORT, we analyzed the immune infiltration status (Fig. 6B). The results revealed that tumors expressing high levels of FAM3B exhibited higher levels of M1 macrophages compared to low FAM3B expression tumors. Conversely, tumors expressing high levels of FAM3B displayed lower levels of CD8+ T cells, resting NK cells, and monocytes compared to the low FAM3B expressing group (Fig. 6C) (p < 0.05). Correlation analysis (Fig. 6D) revealed that higher FAM3B expression levels were positively associated with the infiltration of M0 macrophage, regulatory T cells, and memory B cells (p < 0.05). Conversely, FAM3B expression was negatively associated with the infiltration of CD8+ T cells, resting NK cells, and monocytes (p < 0.05).

Causal effect of FAM3B expression on CRC

MR analysis was conducted to assess potential causal relationships. By using genetic variants as instrumental variables, MR analysis uses the random allocation of genetic variants at conception, which mimics randomized controlled trials. Unlike observational correlations that may be confounded by environmental exposures or reverse causation, genetic variants are independent of these confounding factors [17]. This method allows us to determine whether FAM3B expressions causally affect CRC development. Upon completion of selecting valid SNPs, we employed the MR-Egger regression, WM method, and IVW method to evaluate the causal relationship between FAM3B expression level and CRC initiation and progression. The IVW method (OR = 1.07, 95% CI 1.00–1.14, p = 0.035) indicated that enhanced expression of the FAM3B gene increases the risk of CRC, with significant results. However, the results from other methods were not significant (Table 5). Cochran’s Q test to evaluate the heterogeneity degree among individual CRC IVs and the results indicated there was no heterogeneity among CRC IVs (p >0.05). Therefore, we applied the IVW fixed-effects model to assess the MR analysis size (Fig. 7A and B). The results revealed a positive beta value (0.069205527, p < 0.05), indicating a positive causal relationship between the expression of FAM3B and the risk of CRC initiation. Additionally, we validated the IVW methods using MR-Egger regression. The result showed that the intercept of MR-Egger regression was 0.018395654 with a p-value of 0.20639708, suggesting the absence of pleiotropy. We also utilized the leave-one-out method to validate the sensitivity of our results. As shown in Fig. 7C, the red line illustrates the outcome of the sensitivity, delineating the impact of each SNP on the relationship between FAM3B and CRC. We noticed that the red line was situated to the right of 0, indicating that the results were not influenced by individual SNPs. It also suggested a positive correlation between FAM3B expression and CRC risk.

Discussion

CRC is one of the three most common malignancies globally, and distant metastasis remains the leading cause of CRC-related mortality [18]. In this study, we found FAM3B was significantly upregulated in CRC tissues, and its high expression was associated with poor prognosis and advanced tumor stage. Echoing our findings, previous experimental evidence also revealed the upregulation of FAM3B in CRC and that upregulated FAM3B facilitated CRC metastasis [11]. The results of multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that FAM3B is an independent prognostic factor, along with age, tumor stage, and M stage. These findings are in line with studies showing that high FAM3B expression is correlated with worse survival outcomes in esophageal carcinoma [12], lower-grade glioma, and ocular melanoma [10]. Based on these prognostic factors, we developed a predictive model to estimate 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates in CRC patients, which demonstrated favorable predictive accuracy and discrimination. This model provides a valuable tool for clinicians to more accurately assess prognosis and guide treatment strategies.

In our survival analysis, FAM3B demonstrated significant prognostic value in both univariate and multivariate Cox regression models. The reduction in hazard ratio after adjustment for age, tumor stage, and M stage indicates that multiple prognostic factors share overlapping predictive information. Notably, FAM3B exhibited statistical significance as an independent prognostic factor, which confirmed its predictive value.

FAM3B may also contribute to tumor progression by modulating the TME. We found that high FAM3B expression was associated with enhanced tumor purity and altered immune cell composition, characterized by elevated M1 macrophages and decreased levels of CD8+ T cells, resting NK cells, and monocytes. These immune characteristics are consistent with a cancer-promoting environment and indicate that FAM3B may influence immune evasion mechanisms in CRC [10, 19]. However, these findings were derived from computational analysis using CIBERSORT and lack experimental validation. Future studies should incorporate experimental approaches to validate immune infiltration patterns at the cellular level.

The observed immune cell patterns related to high FAM3B expression require mechanistic investigations. Based on previous studies showing FAM3B modulates cell apoptosis and promotes epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) in CRC, we speculated that these cellular processes may indirectly affect TME. Enhanced tumor cell survival could reduce CD8+ T cell and NK cell effectiveness, while FAM3B-mediated EMT may change immune cell patterns. However, experimental data are needed to determine whether FAM3B directly influences immune cell function.

To determine whether FAM3B is causally involved in CRC development, we conducted an MR analysis. While the IVW method yielded a significant result, MR-Egger and weighted median methods did not. The reason behind the variance is that the IVW method provides higher statistical power but depends on stricter assumptions. On the other hand, MR-Egger and weighted median methods are more conservative but have reduced statistical power. Although tests for heterogeneity and pleiotropy did not demonstrate significant bias, the potential for weak instrument bias cannot be entirely ruled out. This indicates that FAM3B may contribute to CRC development in a more complex manner, involving a multifactorial network. Also, we cannot deny that while these findings imply a causal relationship between FAM3B and CRC progression, the weak effect size and method-related variance suggest that the causal evidence is limited. Future research with larger sample sizes and more robust instrumental variables is needed to validate causal inference.

The integration of immune-nutritional biomarkers in CRC prognosis has gained considerable attention in recent years, highlighting the escalating importance of systemic immune and nutritional status in cancer risk stratification. Recent studies have demonstrated that serum calcium ion levels serve as predictive biomarkers in CRC patients [20] and pan-immune-inflammatory values show significant associations with survival outcomes after surgery [21]. Prognostic models using neutrophil-to-platelet ratio and lymphocyte-to-hemoglobin ratio combined with absolute monocyte count have improved prediction accuracy [22]. Additionally, preoperative albumin-to-globulin ratio has been identified as an independent prognostic factor [23], while the Onodera prognostic nutritional score combined with inflammatory biomarkers provides better risk assessment [24]. Serum nutritional predictors have also been used to predict anastomotic leakage [25]. Our findings add FAM3B as a novel immune-associated biomarker that complements these existing serum-based indicators. While traditional immune-nutritional markers reflect systemic inflammation and nutritional status, FAM3B represents a tissue-specific marker that directly reflects the TME. The significant associations we observed between FAM3B expression and specific immune cell populations suggest that FAM3B provides molecular insights into local tumor immunity that may not be captured by serum markers. This tissue-based approach could potentially enhance current prognostic models by combining molecular tumor characteristics with established systemic immune-nutritional parameters.

Interestingly, FAM3B is upregulated and promotes tumor progression in colon [11], esophageal [12], and prostate [8] cancers, while it is downregulated in gastric [12, 14, 26], oral lichen planus [9], and certain prostate cancers [27]. The difference in FAM3B expression patterns across various cancers indicates the complex and context-dependent roles of FAM3B. The underlying reason for these opposing patterns lack investigations. We speculate that tissue-specific transcriptional control, epigenetic modification, and/or the presence of different isoforms may be involved in the different FAM3B expression patterns. Mechanistically, FAM3B has been reported to regulate tumor progression through various pathways. Previous studies revealed that FAM3B may promote CRC progression by facilitating epithelial–mesenchymal transition [4]. Although this finding has not been directly validated in CRC cells in our study, it indicates a potential role of FAM4B in enhancing tumor cell motility. Moreover, FAM3B has been shown to regulate cell apoptosis by regulating p53 or Bcl signaling pathways [8, 12]. The diverse transcriptomic signatures and molecular roles of FAM3B across various malignancies could potentially stem from alternative splicing events or post-translational modifications affecting the FAM3B protein. Further studies should focus on functional validation. For instance, engineered CRC cells with FAM3B depletion or overexpression could be used for proteomic analysis and functional assays to determine the signaling network involved in FAM3B-mediated CRC progression.

Employing MR analysis, we substantiated the causal relationship between FAM3B and CRC risk from a genetic perspective. Contingent upon the satisfaction of the three instrumental variable assumptions, the IVW method demonstrated a significant association between elevated FAM3B expression and increased CRC susceptibility. Visualization analysis of individual SNPs further corroborated their modulatory effects on CRC predisposition. Collectively, these findings furnish genetic evidence that substantiates the causal involvement of FAM3B in CRC pathogenesis. Although previous studies identified the relationship between FAM3B expression and CRC [11], those studies did not mitigate the confounding effects of reverse causality, whereby CRC potentially influences FAM3B expression levels, and horizontal pleiotropy, where the genetic instruments affect CRC risk through pathways independent of FAM3B. Our study, employing MR analysis, yielded more robust genetic evidence illuminating the etiological role of FAM3B in CRC, guiding subsequent mechanistic investigations and informing pertinent clinical applications.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in our study. First, our findings were primarily derived from bioinformatics analysis and require experimental validation. Second, the immune infiltration patterns were assessed using computational deconvolution approaches instead of experiment-based investigations. Third, our study did not assess the correlation between FAM3B expression and established serum immune-nutritional biomarkers, which limits our understanding of how FAM3B integrates with existing prognostic tools.

Conclusion

In summary, our study comprehensively investigated the differential expression, prognostic relevance, and immune infiltration role of FAM3B in CRC through bioinformatics approaches and clinical sample analysis. The significant overexpression of FAM3B in CRC tissues highlights its potential as a novel prognostic biomarker, providing insights for early diagnosis and more effective treatment strategies. These findings establish FAM3B as a valuable tissue-specific biomarker that complements existing serum-based immune-nutritional prognostic tools, potentially enabling more comprehensive patient assessment by integrating molecular tumor characteristics with systemic immune-metabolic parameters. Additional research is needed to clarify the specific molecular mechanisms through which FAM3B contributes to CRC development and progression, and the modulation of immune responses.

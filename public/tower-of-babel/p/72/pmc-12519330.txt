Study Protocol for a Delphi Process to Develop a Climate Impact Extension to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standard (CHEERS) 2022—the CHEERS ClimatE Checklist
Authors: Jule Oldenburg, Mattis Keil, Laura Maaß, Oliver Lange, Wolf Rogowski
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe healthcare sector has significant environmental impacts, particularly through greenhouse gas emissions. Reducing its climate footprint is therefore essential for achieving political goals such as net-zero and climate-friendly healthcare. While health economic evaluation (HEE) methods compare the costs and consequences of two or more interventions, these analyses rarely consider climate impacts. Some studies have begun to determine climate impacts parallel to or integrated into HEEs. Life cycle assessment (LCA) could be used to integrate climate impacts by considering these results as effects or monetised climate footprints. However, a reporting standard is needed for using these climate-extended economic evaluations in evidence-based decision-making. This protocol describes using an online Delphi process to incorporate climate impacts into the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standard (CHEERS), called CHEERS Climate Extension (CHEERS ClimatE).Methods and analysisThe development of CHEERS ClimatE will proceed through five key stages. First, the preliminary steering group develops in consultation with an advisory board a proposal for the CHEERS ClimatE reporting standard based on a transparency checklist that combines three standards for carbon footprint calculations into the CHEERS framework. The mapping was complemented by reviewing studies that incorporate climate impacts in HEE. Second, for the Delphi process, international experts in HEE and LCA with at least one year of academic experience will be invited to participate in an online pre-survey. We aim to recruit at least 40 participants. Expecting various drop-outs, we aim to reach a consensus with at least 20 participants per Delphi round. Third, an expected three-round Delphi process will be conducted to validate and refine the proposed elements. Participants will rate each item using a 9-point Likert scale and will have the opportunity to comment on each item and propose new items. Consensus is defined with the target of a 70% agreement. Unless consensus is reached, a moderated video conference may be held as a fourth round. Fourth, following other CHEERS extensions, the consented checklist will be piloted using thematically relevant case studies. While substantial changes are not anticipated, minor revisions to individual items may be considered and ratified by the steering group and advisory board. The fifth stage is the publication of the final checklist.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the ethics committee of the University of Bremen (2024–25). The findings of the Delphi study will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at conferences.

Introduction

Health economic evaluation (HEE) is a systematic approach used to compare the costs and consequences of different healthcare interventions. These evaluations are essential for addressing the challenge of limited healthcare resources, such as financial, personnel and time.1 2 In line with the goals of net-zero or climate-friendly healthcare,3 government guidelines for HEE or health technology assessments (HTAs) have started to include environmental impacts, predominantly focusing on greenhouse gases (GHG).47 Environmental impacts can be defined as the potential effect on the ecosystems, human health, or the overuse of finite resources from interactions between the technosphere and the ecosphere8 and include, for example, resource use, pollution, biodiversity loss or climate change. Climate change represents one environmental impact with particularly high policy relevance9 10 and international agreements.11 This shift towards incorporating climate impacts in HEE is embedded in recognising that climate change poses significant threats to global health.12 Consequently, healthcare systems are starting to assess and quantify their contributions to climate impacts.1318

A recently published systematic review19 categorised these methods that are used for integrating environmental impacts into HEE or HTA: among others, there are integrated evaluation methods which incorporate environmental impacts in various ways, such as the use of adjusted willingness-to-pay threshold,19 including environmental impacts as costs2026 or environmental impacts considered on the effect side.20 24 27 Further, parallel evaluation methods exist, which add incremental carbon footprint-effectiveness ratio (ICFER)28 or incremental carbon footprint cost ratio (ICFCR) besides overall measures of HEEs.28

The typically applied conceptual approach to measure environmental impacts is life cycle assessment (LCA) which is standardised by ISO 1404029 and 14044.30 Specific to the policy-relevant aspect of climate impacts, the climate footprint (CF) builds on LCA and is standardised by ISO 14067.31 Additional LCA-based guidelines for calculating CF include the GHG Protocol32 and PAS 2050.33 These standards and guidelines contain information that should be reported when calculating the climate footprint. The CF-checklist34 integrated the two most commonly used CF standards, ISO 1404029 and 14044,30 as well as the GHG protocol32 and PAS 2050,33 and can serve as a reference for elements to be reported in a CF analysis. For integrating the climate impacts in HEE, greenhouse gases can be monetised (eg, as the social cost of carbon (SC-CO2)) or considered as effects (eg, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) calculated with damage factors for every emitted kilogram of CO2).35

However, it has been highlighted that HTAs considering environmental impacts are still in early development, with limited data availability and analysis methods.36 37 There are also concerns about “greenwashing”, where the negative environmental impacts of technology are downplayed or overstated to achieve favourable HTA conclusions. Therefore, a critical need for guidelines to integrate environmental impacts into HTA has been identified, underscoring the necessity of a standardised reporting framework.36 38

For HEE, the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standard (CHEERS)39 assists readers and reviewers in identifying, interpreting and applying studies. CHEERS has demonstrated its adaptability in being extended to include value of information analyses (CHEERS-Value of Information (VOI))40 and health technologies using artificial intelligence (AI).41 42 Given the growing body of literature on HEE that incorporates climate impacts and the potential to extend CHEERS, we aim to develop a climate-extended reporting standard, called CHEERS Climate-extension (CHEERS ClimatE). The reporting standard aims to assess the transparency and quality of existing HEE incorporating climate impacts for informed sustainable decision-making. To ensure its applicability and validation for decision-makers, expert consensus is essential. One well-established method for expert consensus on multidisciplinary and complex topics is the Delphi process.4346 This technique is frequently used in social science research for various applications and topics with limited knowledge or uncertainty, as well as for assessing a situation or obtaining feedback.4749 Compared with focus group discussion, the Delphi technique allows anonymity for the free expression of thoughts.50 Additionally, participants receive feedback on the group’s overall voting behaviour after each round, enabling them to reconsider and potentially revise their assessments.5153

Objective

Accordingly, this study protocol outlines an online Delphi process that aims to develop a consensus-based list of elements to be reported in a HEE that integrates climate impacts.

Methods and analysis

The development of CHEERS ClimatE will proceed through five key stages (figure 1). This elaboration process is oriented around the guidance for developing health research reporting guidelines54 and other CHEERS Delphi processes.3942 Further details on how current development aligns with the health research reporting guideline54 are provided in box 1 in the online supplemental appendix. The Delphi study (active phase) is scheduled to begin with the publication of the study protocol.

Stage 1: Preliminary work

Steering group

A steering group will be constituted at the beginning of the Delphi process to lead and technically monitor its development. The initial steering group (consisting of the authors of this protocol) has expertise in health economics, LCA and environmental and climate impacts in the context of health systems. The composition of the steering group may evolve, and additional experts from the field of health economics and LCA may be invited to contribute additional expertise. In line with other CHEERS extensions,41 steering group meetings are only quorate if at least 50% of their members are participating.

Development of the CHEERS ClimatE proposal

As an initial starting point for the first round of the Delphi process, a proposal for CHEERS ClimatE has already been developed by four authors of this study protocol (WR, MK, OL and JO). This proposal was developed by mapping CF standards (for climate impacts) to CHEERS, as follows: first, as a reference standard for CF, we used the CF-Checklist.34 Second, we assessed which elements of the CF-checklist are already included in CHEERS, even if the wording may differ. Third, we identified overlaps with the CHEERS elements for the remaining elements in the CF-checklist to determine appropriate additions. Fourth, for the remaining items in the CF-checklist, we assessed which existing CHEERS item was most relevant or whether a new item should be added. This procedure was based on the thematic proximity of the elements to each other and on the existence of valid reasons for omitting certain elements in studies that do not conduct their own CF calculation but only add CF to an HEE. Fifth, ISO 1406731 was examined to identify potential elements for integration in addition to the existing information. Finally, a literature search was conducted for CHEERS elements that had not yet been completed by the CF analyses. The aim was to determine whether any remaining links between CF calculations and HEE should be integrated into CHEERS ClimatE. In addition to an extended CHEERS list, we added further explanations of integrating climate impacts into HEE. Overall, 18 items in the CHEERS ClimatE proposal differ from those in CHEERS; they both have the same number of items, although items 11 (selection of outcomes), 12 (measurement of outcomes) and 14 (measurement of valuation of resources and costs) may be split. Detailed information can be found in another publication about points to consider when reporting climate-extended evaluations. This article presents the reporting standard proposal for CHEERS ClimatE.55

Identification of relevant case studies

Several climate-extended HEEs21 28 56 were particularly identified through systematic reviews19 57 and serve as case examples to assess the applicability of the draft proposal.55 To validate the proposed reporting standard, within the recent reviews on climate-extended HEE,193638 57 JO and WR extracted full evaluations of both costs and effects, including climate impacts (see table 1 in online supplemental appendix). Information relevant to each point was extracted and qualitatively summarised. Further relevant case studies are sought throughout the Delphi process through rapid review. These case studies will also be used to further pilot CHEERS ClimatE after the Delphi process (see Stage 4).

Advisory board

An additional advisory board will be established to maintain an efficient steering group size and, at the same time, account for the variety of aspects related to methods or practice of evaluation. Additional experts in HEE, LCA and HTA, as well as representatives or stakeholders of professional societies, will be invited as members. The advisory board will critically review the draft proposal before the Delphi process starts and may offer further recommendations, which will be accounted for by the steering group. The advisory board members are also encouraged to participate in the Delphi process (see stage 2). During the Delphi process (see stage 3), the steering group may also consult advisory board members on specific questions. After the Delphi process, advisory board members will be invited to contribute as co-authors to the piloting of the CHEERS ClimatE checklist and the preparation of the manuscripts (see stages 4 and 5).

Stage 2: Recruiting

There is no clear recommendation for the ideal cohort size in Delphi studies. The widely used RAND/UCLA handbook argues that a survey round should consist of 7–15 experts.58 Finally, qualitative studies with several survey periods (such as Delphi studies) report an average of 50% high drop-out and non-responder rates.59 Therefore, we aim for a cohort size of at least 40 participants at the beginning. Considering the drop-out rates, we strive for a minimum of 20 participants in each Delphi round following the literature.49

For a comprehensive recruiting process, the steering group will initially contact individuals internationally who either (1) have knowledge or experience in HEE or (ideally extensions of) CHEERS, or (2) have knowledge or experience in environmental or climate impacts (for example, they have conducted LCAs, ideally in the healthcare sector). These people will receive an initial e-mail including further information on the research project and will be invited to forward the invitations to relevant potential participants in their network according to the snowball sampling principle.59 60 Finally, we plan to distribute the invitation via international professional societies such as the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research, the International Health Economics Association, the European Health Economics Association, Healthcare without Harm and the Forum for Sustainability through Life Cycle Innovation. The snowball sampling principle is intended to minimise selection bias by broadening the range of participants. All interested people who fulfil our inclusion criteria can participate in the study. The following inclusion and exclusion criteria have been defined for the Delphi study in box 1.

Before conducting the Delphi process, experts will be invited via e-mail to participate in a preliminary online survey through the European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)-compliant platform LimeSurvey (LimeSurvey GmbH, Hamburg, Germany).61 This preliminary survey marks the formal starting point of the Delphi process and serves multiple purposes: it provides participants with detailed information about data protection and requests their informed consent if they are interested in participating in the study. Additionally, during this preliminary survey, their socio-demographic data will be collected to enable subgroup analysis as part of the Delphi process. Participants will also be asked to enter their real names and email addresses and to create an alias. This alias will link their response across different Delphi rounds, enabling the tracking of decisions. Real names and e-mail addresses will be excluded from data analysis and used solely for contacting the experts.

Stage 3: Conducting an online Delphi process

We plan to conduct a Delphi process to validate and refine the relevance of the integrated elements in CHEERS ClimatE through expert opinion by rating each item on a Likert scale in line with previous CHEERS extensions.40 41 No discussion or interaction among participants will occur during the three Delphi rounds. Instead, all input will be submitted individually and pseudonymised. With the Delphi technique, the participants receive feedback on the group’s overall responses after each round.

Study procedure

After completing the preliminary survey and reaching a critical mass of participants (at least 40), the first round of the Delphi process will commence. LimeSurvey61 will be used for conducting the planned Delphi rounds (see figure 2), as well as for managing all survey activities during the Delphi process, including distribution, reminders and interim analyses.

Preliminary illustration of the first Delphi round question.

Through the Delphi process, experts will evaluate each proposed item using a 9-point Likert scale62 (based on the procedure developed by the original CHEERS.63 Table 1 presents the scale, which enables a nuanced rating from “completely unimportant” to “extremely important”. These ratings will later be used in the analysis to determine whether consensus has been reached for inclusion, representation or exclusion (see analysis). The items are arranged in the same order and categorisation as in CHEERS, with only explanatory text between the sections (see figure 2). If participants are not confident about the content of a vote, they can indicate this. Besides the Likert scale, the first two rounds will include an option for free text input, allowing participants to submit comments on each item, suggest revisions (eg, merging, splitting, or terminological adaptation of the items or including additional information) as well as propose new items. At the end of these rounds, all comments will be listed, and the expert can once again clarify which comments remain relevant. New or combined items from rounds one or two will continue into rounds two or three. The steering group will analyse the results from each round and prepare summaries including median and mean values, the 70th percentile, pseudonymised comments and potential new items, which will be provided to participants before rounds two and three (see chapter data collection and analysis). The final round is solely about agreeing or disagreeing with the modified items. Participants can complete the survey at their convenience during the survey period, with the option to save their answers and continue later, thus reducing the survey burden through the entire Delphi process. To retain participant numbers throughout the Delphi process, reminders will be issued via Limesurvey61 or e-mail through the steering group if participants have not completed a round. For each round, 2–3 weeks are planned for rating and another 2–3 weeks for analysis. These timeframes may be adjusted if necessary. Each survey round is expected to take a maximum of 1 hour (see the online supplemental appendix of CHEERS).63

During the Delphi study, (one member of) the steering group will serve as the contact person for participants; their e-mail address will be visible on all pages of the survey tool. The steering group will address any comments. If participants forget their alias, it can be requested from the contact person.

After each Delphi round, the steering group proposes revised items based on the ratings and comments. On completing the third Delphi round, a virtual consensus meeting with all available participants may occur if consent has not been reached on items. Similar to the procedures outlined in other CHEERS extensions,40 41 each item and the corresponding Delphi results without consent will be presented. The meeting will be moderated by the steering group and provide the opportunity for participants to discuss each item in detail. They can suggest revisions to the phrasing of items, and any proposed changes will be agreed on collectively before the final anonymous vote takes place. Therefore, the participating experts will vote anonymously on each element’s inclusion (“yes” or “no”); at least 50% of the votes from the attending participants will be required for inclusion.41 Elements with lower approval will be excluded.

Data collection and analysis

Descriptive statistics will be used to characterise the overall results of experts’ answers and conduct subgroup analyses.

First, a general evaluation of each Delphi round will be conducted to assess the level of agreement on each item. Two steering group members will extract and qualitatively analyse the comments and ratings from LimeSurvey61 after each Delphi round. The median and mean values and the 70th percentile for each element will be determined, and individual comments will be summarised. If applicable, newly proposed items or revisions will be reviewed and included in the next round for further evaluation. Consensus on an item is defined in line with previous CHEERS research.40 41 63 The consensus for including an item is reached if the mean rating for the respective element is higher than 7. If the mean scale score is 1–3, the item is excluded; if the mean score is 4–6 points, the element is presented again in the following round, as no consensus was reached. In line with other CHEERS Delphi processes, items with a mean score of less than or equal to four are excluded in the final round. Participants who state they are not confident in rating an item are not considered when calculating the mean rating to determine the consensus. However, to be sensitive to any improvements, their suggestions and ratings will be considered when revising the items for the next Delphi round. Additionally, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be conducted after each survey round to confirm the results and test for possible biases due to the overrepresentation of specific groups within the study cohort.

Second, subgroup analyses aim to identify patterns in assessment behaviour across different expert backgrounds. These analyses are intended to increase transparency, support a better understanding of whether specific perspectives might influence item ratings, and test the robustness and interpretation of the overall results. To define the subgroups, the following socio-demographic data will be collected from all participants who provide consent and participate in the pre-survey: year of birth, gender, country of current residence, academic discipline (education), years of experience in the discipline, assessment of expert status in LCA or health economics (5-point Likert scale from “No expertise” to “A lot of expertise”), how they found the questionnaire (newsletter, invitation, referral from others, etc.). Subgroup analyses will always be performed and reported at the group level. Individual responses will not be presented separately. No subgroup will consist of fewer than five participants, ensuring that individual identities remain protected.

Stage 4 and Stage 5: Piloting, ratification and publication of the final CHEERS ClimatE list

Based on other CHEERS extensions,41 the resulting checklist will be piloted by applying it to identified relevant case studies identified in the first stage (e.g. 19). This will allow the steering group, in consultation with the advisory board, to evaluate the checklist’s clarity, applicability and completeness before the final ratification of all items. If this process reveals the need for further adjustment, this will be made only based on full consensus of the steering group, in consultation with the advisory board and with documentation in the publication. The steering group will also compile an article featuring the final CHEERS ClimatE list, along with an explanation and elaboration document (E&E), in line with the original CHEERS publication and CHEERS extensions.40 41 63 The advisory board is invited to contribute to the publications. These documents are aimed to be published with open access. To assist with publicising the extension, we plan to share our published results on the ISPOR CHEERS website.64

Patient and public involvement

We aim to include patient representatives and other relevant stakeholders like HTA bodies in the advisory board.

Ethics and dissemination

Data protection and data management

The study is designed to protect participants’ privacy and handle personal information in compliance with EU regulations. All participants will receive comprehensive written information about the study and data protection before the study begins, and they will provide written consent to participate in accordance with §6 (1) (a) and §7 of the GDPR. Before the study starts, we will ensure that participants are giving informed consent. In the Delphi process, participant data will be pseudonymised through the generation of aliases, meaning that participants will not know who else is taking part in the survey. Participants will only be acknowledged by name in the subsequent publication if they explicitly agree.

Personal data will be stored and analysed separately from the survey responses during the Delphi process, linked by an alias to ensure pseudonymised data analysis. However, a final virtual meeting with all participants may be held at the end of the process to present, discuss and, if necessary, agree on the results. In this meeting, the results will already be summarised to prevent any conclusions about individual opinions. The study will not collect sensitive data besides the participants’ names and e-mail addresses for correspondence purposes. The survey will only ask for participants’ assessment of the relevance and practicability of elements in a climate-extended HEE. A data protection concept was developed as part of the ethics approval (2024–25) by the Ethics Committee of the University of Bremen.

Ethical consideration

The University of Bremen Ethics Committee has granted ethical approval for this project (2024–25). The ethics application is available on request, but it is in German. On request, a digital version can be provided, which can also be translated into other languages using online translation programmes. There will be no financial incentives to participate in the Delphi process. To proactively prevent drop-out, study participants will be offered the opportunity to be named in the acknowledgments of the published article.49 Additionally, we will issue participation certificates to all experts as proof of their participation in the study.

Dissemination

The study’s findings will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at conferences and meetings. The essence of CHEERS ClimatE is disseminating the idea of climate-smart healthcare, and the steering group welcomes anyone interested in joining or staying informed about our activities.

Expected results

The Delphi process will result in a list of items as a reporting standard, considering different perspectives on integrating climate impact in health economic evaluation. Further, various experts with experience in health economics or research in sustainability or environmental impacts consented to the reporting standard.

Discussion

Incorporating climate impacts into HEE is becoming increasingly important, as agreements to limit greenhouse gas emissions will inevitably impact the healthcare sector. In the case of decision problems, especially with similar costs and effectiveness, climate impacts can influence decisions on healthcare financing. Transparent research is essential to support evidence-based decision-making. Therefore, CHEERS-ClimatE might be of particular importance to healthcare decision-makers, as it will enable them to assess economic evaluations taking climate impacts into account.

This study only considers climate impact, which is relevant for decision-makers due to political agreements. However, further research is needed to include environmental impacts such as land use, water and toxicity. These are excluded to enable better feasibility for decision-makers.

Despite the recommendation of a Delphi process for developing reporting guidelines,54 clear guidance is lacking,45 although a framework has been proposed.65 The CHEERS update63 and extensions40 41 will guide this study’s approach. However, implementing the Delphi process poses challenges, such as high drop-out rates and scheduling difficulties. To mitigate this, we will send reminders, hand out participation certificates and offer flexible scheduling options. Additionally, recognising participants in the acknowledgments is expected to motivate involvement. Conducting the Delphi process virtually and in English aims to maximise experts’ participation. To promote geographic diversity and avoid a Global North-centric perspective, we will distribute invitations through relevant professional networks, such as the International Health Economics Association. To further broaden participation, we will complement this approach with snowball sampling by encouraging invited experts to share the call within their own networks. Through these strategies, we strive to facilitate widespread engagement and ensure the inclusivity and robustness of our findings. The methodology outlined in this study protocol may also serve as a roadmap for other CHEERS extensions, incorporating other concerns, for example, other environmental impacts or planetary boundaries.

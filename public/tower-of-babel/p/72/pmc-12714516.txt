Recurrent STEMI After Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement
Authors: Shanjot Brar, Suleman Aktaa, Krishnan Ramanathan, Philipp Blanke, Janarthanan Sathananthan, John G. Webb
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundA 63-year-old man underwent surgical aortic valve replacement with an On-X valve using COR-KNOT suturing to facilitate a mini-sternotomy approach.Case SummaryThe patient presented with recurrent inferior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarctions over a few-year period, despite optimal anticoagulation therapy. Repeated coronary angiography showed no obstructive coronary artery disease or thromboembolic occlusion. Intravascular ultrasound showed a filling defect in the ostium of the right coronary artery (RCA). A cardiac computed tomography confirmed the interference between a COR-KNOT and the RCA. Percutaneous coronary intervention of the RCA ostium was performed with good outcomes.DiscussionThis is the first case to report a ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction caused by an automated suturing. The combination of mini-sternotomy with COR-KNOT may have contributed to this rare but serious complication.Take-Home MessagesMultimodality imaging is crucial in establishing unusual diagnoses. COR-KNOT may interfere with coronary flow resulting in transient coronary occlusion when used in surgical aortic valve replacement.

Past Medical History

The patient has a history of bicuspid aortic valve with severe aortic stenosis for which he underwent surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) using a 27/29 mm On-X mechanical valve 4 years before the current presentation. The patient had normal coronaries on an invasive coronary angiogram before SAVR (Video 1). Transthoracic echocardiogram before surgery measured the aortic valve area at 1 cm2, and left ventricular outflow tract at 24 mm. The patient body surface area at the time of surgery was 2.15 m2. The surgery was performed with mini-sternotomy technique using COR-KNOT (LSI Solutions), which is an automated suturing device to fasten and trim surgical knots (Figure 2). After the surgery, the patient was maintained on warfarin with an international normalized ratio (INR) target between 1.5 and 2.5, in addition to aspirin.Figure 2COR-KNOT

One year after valve implantation, the patient presented with chest pain and transient inferior ST-segment elevation on his ECG. Due to the transient nature of the symptoms and the resolution of his ST-segments, urgent coronary angiography was deferred. A stress echocardiogram showed no evidence of myocardial ischemia. Over the following year, the patient developed symptomatic peripheral artery disease for which his INR target was increased to 2.5 to 3.5.

Deferential Diagnosis

The diagnosis of inferior STEMI was unequivocal in this patient given the clinical presentation and ECG findings. Before the angiogram, the team discussed that the underlying etiology of STEMI may be related to either a plaque rapture or a possible thromboembolic phenomena given the presence of a mechanical aortic valve and the history of peripheral artery disease.

Investigations

The ECG which was performed at the time of presentation is shown in Figure 1. The high-sensitivity cardiac troponin levels peaked at 177 ng/L (normal: <14 ng/L). The findings of the coronary angiogram are shown in Video 2.

Cardiac magnetic resonance showed normal biventricular size and systolic function with no evidence of late gadolinium myocardial enhancement. Thrombophilia work-up failed to detect a thrombotic pathology, and there was no evidence of a malignant etiology after computed tomography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis.

Management

The patient was discharged home a couple of days later after switching his aspirin to clopidogrel and his warfarin to therapeutic dose enoxaparin.

Second Presentation

The patient represented 2 months later with another inferior STEMI (Figure 3). Repeat coronary angiography again showed no obstructive coronary artery disease (Video 3). However, a filling defect was noted at the ostium of the right coronary artery (RCA), raising a suspicion of an interference with a COR-KNOT. A cardiac computed tomography scan was subsequently performed and confirmed this finding as shown in Figure 4. A transthoracic echocardiogram showed normal biventricular size and systolic function with a well-seated mechanical aortic prosthesis. Further Management

After involving the heart team, the decision was made to proceed with PCI to the RCA ostium, which was successfully performed using 2 layers of SYNERGY MEGATRON (Boston Scientific) drug-eluting stents (Video 5).

The patient was discharged on triple therapy with therapeutic anticoagulation dose of enoxaparin, clopidogrel, and aspirin. The aspirin was stopped after 1 month, and the enoxaparin was switched back to warfarin after 3 months.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient remained well with no further cardiac presentations or complaints. A repeat cardiac computed tomography 1 month after PCI showed patent RCA stents (Figure 5), and on clinical review, he reported no cardiac symptoms.Figure 5Cardiac Computed Tomography Scan After Percutaneous Coronary Intervention

Discussion

Our case describes a recurrent presentation with STEMI after SAVR with On-X valve which was implanted in a minimally invasive approach using a COR-KNOT fastener. The diagnosis of STEMI on the 2 occasions was definite, with convincing symptoms, ECG changes, and cardiac troponin elevation. However, there was no obvious coronary occlusion in the coronary artery tree nor angiographic evidence of an embolic culprit. Moreover, the patient was compliant with his oral anticoagulation before presentation, and INR was within therapeutic range on both occasions. In addition, the subsequent presentation was most likely caused by the same pathology.

Although coronary artery obstruction is a rare complication of SAVR, it is associated with high morbidity and mortality.1, 2, 3 Numerous factors may impact the likelihood of coronary obstruction after SAVR. Although some of these factors may be related to patient anatomy, such as coronary height and the size of the sinus of Valsalva, other factors may also be at play including the surgical technique, the selection of the valve type, and the tying method.

Mini-sternotomy has been shown to have similar long-term results compared with conventional technique in patients undergoing isolated SAVR for severe aortic stenosis.4 However, the outcomes of this technique across the various valve types (eg mechanical vs bioprosthetic) and tying methods (eg automated vs manual) have not been fully explored. In our case, the valve was implanted slightly higher than normal as shown on the cardiac computed tomography. This is likely due to the mini-sternotomy approach, but may also have contributed to the COR-KNOT interference with the coronary flow when the knot was positioned close to the RCA ostium.

The COR-KNOT fastener has emerged as a tool to facilitate heart valve surgical replacement by reducing intraoperative times and blood loss, and the need for prolonged mechanical ventilator support.5 Postmarketing surveillance of this device showed low rate of adverse events including ischemic heart disease.5

Previous cases reported paravalvular leak and aortic valve leaflet perforation with the COR-KNOT device.6, 7, 8, 9 Some of these complications have been linked to the orientation of the metallic fastener of the COR-KNOT device, particularly when deployed via a mini-sternotomy approach.10 To our knowledge, there has been no published case reports of proven coronary obstruction related to the use of COR-KNOT fastener after SAVR.

The patient we present here underwent his SAVR with a mini-sternotomy technique, and an automated tying method was used with a COR-KNOT device. Using computed tomography soronary reconstruction techniques, we have demonstrated that this technique can result in coronary obstruction. We further present a possible solution using PCI with appropriate short-term success. The long-term success of this will have to be carefully monitored.

Conclusions

Our patient underwent SAVR with a mini-sternotomy technique and the use of a COR-KNOT device. Hence, one may assume that the combination of these 2 strategies may require further investigations to avoid serious complications related to coronary artery obstruction.

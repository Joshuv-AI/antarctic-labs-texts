One Man’s Meat Is Another Man’s Poison: High Incidence of Severe Cutaneous Drug Reactions Induced by Enfortumab Vedotin
Authors: Yun Pan, Rentao Yu, Hong Huang, Xiaoli Chen, Xin Gou, Aijun Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Take Home MessageOur study investigates the incidence, onset timing, and risk factors of severe cutaneous adverse events (AEs) associated with enfortumab vedotin (EV) using data from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System. We found a high incidence of skin-related AEs, particularly Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN); with most skin reactions occurring within the first cycle of treatment, the most common time to onset of SJS/TEN is 14 d. Associate factors for skin-limited AEs included prior immunotherapy, male gender, and chemotherapy. We believe that our findings highlight the need for vigilant monitoring of these serious AEs in patients receiving EV, especially in high-risk populations. We hope that our study will contribute to improving patient safety and management.Keywords: Enfortumab vedotin, Cutaneous adverse event, U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s Adverse Event Reporting System, Urothelial cancer, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis

Introduction

Enfortumab vedotin (EV) is a novel antibody-drug conjugate targeting nectin-4, a cell adhesion molecule highly expressed in urothelial carcinoma (UC) [1]. It comprises a humanized monoclonal antibody linked to monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE), a potent microtubule-disrupting agent that induces cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis [2]. In 2019, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved EV for treating advanced or metastatic UC [3]. The EV-301 and EV-302 studies demonstrated that EV, either as monotherapy or in combination with pembrolizumab, improved overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) significantly [4,5].

However, the clinical application of EV has been accompanied by a range of adverse events (AEs), particularly those involving the skin. Cutaneous AEs (cAEs) associated with EV have gained increasing attention, with case reports highlighting potentially life-threatening skin reactions such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) [6]. Since the initial report on EV-associated dermatotoxicity in 2019, a growing number of case studies have emerged, further emphasizing the severity of these skin reactions and the importance of vigilant monitoring and timely decision-making regarding dose reduction or treatment interruption [7,8].

The purpose of our study is to provide a comprehensive analysis of the incidence, onset time, and associated factors for EV-induced skin complications, particularly SJS/TEN, among patients with UC receiving EV treatment. By addressing these critical areas, we hope to contribute to a better understanding of the safety profile of EV treatment.

Patients and methods

Data source

We obtained data from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), a publicly accessible database that compiles spontaneous AE reports submitted by health care professionals and patients. The seven different types of datasets were included in the FAERS data files: patient demographic and administrative information (DEMO), drug information (DRUG), AEs (REAC), patient outcomes (OUTC), report sources (RPSR), therapy start and end dates for reported drugs (THER), and indications for drug administration (INDI). Our analysis focused on FAERS data from January 1, 2019 to November 1, 2024. To extract and analyze medication-AE associations, we employed the FDA’s openFDA platform, which is renowned for its pharmacovigilance capabilities, including drug mapping and duplicate entry removal, thereby enhancing data analysis accuracy. The FAERS database contains spontaneous reports submitted for patients experiencing one or more suspected AEs or medication errors. Consequently, this analysis is based exclusively on patient reports where at least one AE was documented. Reports representing patients with no AEs are not present in FAERS and are therefore not included in this study.

Study parameters

We conducted a systematic search to identify relevant reports using only the following keywords: “EV,” “enfortumab vedotin,” “enfortumab,” and “PADCEV.” Ambiguous drug names or misspellings were excluded. AE reports were standardized using preferred terms (PTs) from the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) as regulated by the FAERS database, and cAEs were identified using PTs mapped to the system organ class (SOC) “skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders,” with additional manual review to ensure clinical relevance. Serious AEs are defined according to the ICH E2A criteria, including events resulting in death, life-threatening experiences, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly. Severe cAEs include SJS/TEN, acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP), and drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS) in our study. The openFDA platform includes information about sex, age, weight, reporter occupation, report year, AE types, and outcomes. The onset time were calculated using drug administration data and AE start data. Only adverse drug reaction reports categorized as “primary suspected medicine” were included. To address duplicate reports, we followed the FDA recommendations for data deduplication using primary identifiers (primaryid + caseid + event_date). Only the most complete (prioritizing drug administration data and AE start data) report was retained. In cases where multiple reports shared identical demographic or clinical information (eg, age, gender, AE type, and event onset date), the most recent or most complete entry was included in the dataset.

Disproportionality analysis

To assess the potential disproportionality in AE reporting, our study employed a comprehensive analysis using several established methodologies. We calculated the reporting odds ratio (ROR), which compares the odds of an AE occurring in treated patients versus untreated patients, thereby indicating the likelihood of an AE being associated with the drug. The proportional reporting ratio (PRR) was also used to quantify the reporting rate of a specific AE relative to all other AEs for a given drug, helping in identifying potential safety signals. Additionally, the Medicines and Healthcare Products Regulatory Agency method was employed to identify safety signals by comparing the observed and expected reporting frequencies.

To adjust for multiple reporting sources and reduce the impact of random variation in AE reporting, we used the multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker method. The Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) was applied as a probabilistic method to detect signals of disproportionate reporting by analyzing the strength of associations between drugs and AEs. The detailed calculation methods and definition of a positive signal are provided in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed by using R (version 4.4.2). Numeric data were presented as the median with an interquartile range (IQR). A descriptive analysis included the description of cAE types and onset time, and missing data were excluded from the analysis. A Sankey plot was used to categorize different types of cAEs by different MedDRA levels. A ridgeline plot was used to compare the onset time of different AEs. Given the structure of FAERS (containing only AE reports), we specifically examined the predictors associated with reporting multiple AEs compared with reporting only a single cAE, as well as SJS/TEN (vs other AEs) by using logistic regression. This analysis aims to identify the factors linked to a higher reported AE burden or more complex clinical presentation in patients experiencing a serious AE. In logistic regression, the missing data were imputed using the random forest method by MICE package. A total of ten complete imputed datasets were generated, and logistic regression was fitted separately on each imputed dataset. Parameter estimates and their standard errors from the ten analyses were then pooled using Rubin’s rules to obtain the final estimates and standard errors that account for the uncertainty associated with the missing data and the imputation process. All enrolled variables were first analyzed in univariable regression, and variables with p < 0.1 were enrolled in multivariable regression, which were listed in the final figure. A variance inflation factor was applied to evaluate the potential multicollinearity. As all variance inflation factors were <5 (see Supplementary Table 3 for details) in two logistic regression models, all variables were enrolled in the final analysis. A two-tailed p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and types of AEs

Our analysis of the FAERS database from January 1, 2019 to November 1, 2024 included 2736 patients who reported a total of 6520 AEs (Table 1, Table 2). Among all enrolled patients, 73.8% (n = 2018) were male. The median age was 73 yr (IQR, 66–79 yr), and the median weight was 59.0 kg (IQR, 52.1–65.0 kg).

The majority of AEs were reported by nature, with physicians reporting 44.2% (n = 1208) of cases, followed by consumers or non–health professionals (33.5%, n = 919), other health professionals (14.1%, n = 385), and pharmacists (7.7%, n = 210).

Among the 2736 patients, 84.1% (n = 2300) experienced serious AEs. Of these serious cases, 22.2% (n = 511) resulted in death due to any cause, while 6.0% (n = 138) were life threatening. Additionally, 1.5% (n = 35) experienced permanent disability and 42.3% (n = 972) required hospitalization. A small fraction (0.1%; n = 3) required medical or surgical intervention to prevent permanent impairment or damage. The average time to reported onset for all AEs induced by EV was 15 d (IQR, 7.0–43.0 d).

As shown in Table 2, cAEs were the most prevalent AEs, occurring in 37.54% of all cases, followed by general disorders and administration site conditions (29.9%), nervous system disorders (21.35%), gastrointestinal disorders (18.75%), and metabolism and nutrition disorders (17.25%). Notably, we used several disproportionality analyses, and the results showed that cAEs had the maximum positive signals among all AEs.

Types and onset time of cAEs by MedDRA classification

Based on MedDRA classification, we conducted a comprehensive classification of all cAEs (Fig. 1A), including SOC, high-level term (HLT), low-level term (LLT), and PT, as shown in Figure 1. At the HLT level, epidermal and dermal conditions were most frequent, accounting for 84.21% of all cAEs. At the LLT level, rash, eruption, and exanthems were most prevalent, comprising 28.07% of cases, followed by conditions of the dermis and epidermis (19.36%), bullous dermatoses (13.85%), and alopecia (11.13%). At the PT level, the most frequently reported AEs were rash (28.07%), alopecia (11.13%), SJS/TEN (8.59%), pruritus (7.99%), and exfoliative dermatitis (3.57%).

In addition, we analyzed the reported onset time of the top ten cAEs (Fig. 1B). An analysis of the temporal distribution of cAEs revealed that the majority occurred within 30 d after treatment initiation. Specifically, milder conditions such as rash and pruritus typically manifested earlier in the treatment course, whereas more severe conditions, including SJS/TEN, tended to appear later. Notably, the most common time to reported onset of SJS/TEN, as indicated by the mode of the data, was 14 d after treatment initiation.

Analysis of associated factors

In our analysis of the factors associated with the occurrence of cAEs, we compared patients who experienced AEs limited to the skin with those who had additional systemic AEs, as shown in Figure 3A. Several associated factors were identified. Patients who reported severe AEs at the time of reporting were found to be closely associated with systemic AEs (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 5.76, 95% CI 4.59–6.24; p < 0.001). Other significant associated factors included male sex (aOR 1.51, 95% CI 1.19–1.91; p < 0.001), prior immunotherapy (aOR 1.90, 95% CI 1.15–3.15; p = 0.013), and prior chemotherapy (aOR 1.51, 95% CI 1.19–1.91; p < 0.001).

In addition, we also analyzed the associated factors for SJS/TEN compared with other AEs (Fig. 3B). Patients who reported severe AEs at the time of reporting were at a significantly higher association for SJS/TEN (aOR 12.95, 95% CI 1.80–93.4; p = 0.011). Older age groups, particularly those aged 65–80 yr (aOR 2.06, 95% CI 1.15–3.68; p = 0.015) and over 80 yr (aOR 2.17, 95% CI 1.04–4.53; p = 0.038), were associated with SJS/TEN. Additionally, hypertension was identified as a significant factor associated with SJS/TEN (aOR 1.65, 95% CI 1.03–2.65; p = 0.038).

Discussion

In this study, we utilized the FAERS database to conduct a comprehensive pharmacovigilance analysis of cutaneous toxicities associated with EV. We collected and analyzed a total of 6520 AE reports related to EV from 2019 to the end of 2024, among which 1027 cases involved cAEs. Our analysis revealed that cAEs had the highest incidence rate among all reported events. To our knowledge, this is the most comprehensive pharmacovigilance analysis of cutaneous toxicities associated with EV. Our findings indicate that SJS/TEN is not only a possible but also a relatively common AE (with an incidence rate as high as 8.59% in our study) within the spectrum of skin reactions associated with EV.

Although EV has demonstrated significant improvements in OS and PFS for patients with UC, it is associated with a higher incidence of cAEs than conventional chemotherapy [4,5]. More importantly, our analysis revealed that the incidence of severe cAEs, such as SJS/TEN, is significantly higher with EV than with other commonly used drugs. Generally, it has been reported that the incidence rate of SJS/TEN is 1.58–2.26 cases per million people in the USA [9]. In contrast, our analysis revealed an incidence rate of 8.59%, which is significantly higher than the reported incidence. This stark contrast in incidence rates highlights the unique profile of EV-induced skin toxicity.

Conventional SJS/TEN is primarily caused by medications, with the pathogenesis involving drug-specific CD8+ cytotoxic lymphocytes and pathways such as Fas-Fas ligand-mediated apoptosis [10]. Genetic factors also play a significant role. It has been reported that specific HLA alleles (eg, HLA-B 15:02 in Han Chinese, and HLA-B 58:01 in European and Asian individuals) has been identified as associated factors [11]. Although EV-induced cAEs share clinical similarities with conventional SJS/TEN, their distinct differences in incidence, pathogenesis, and onset time, along with the high incidence of SJS/TEN associated with EV, suggest that its pathogenesis may differ from that of conventional drugs [12].

Potential mechanisms for EV-induced skin reactions include the dual expression of nectin-4, a protein highly expressed in both urothelial cancer cells and normal skin keratinocytes. This dual expression pattern may contribute to the high incidence of skin reactions [13,14]. Additionally, the MMAE payload released within cells can directly induce cytotoxicity, affecting both target and nontarget cells through a “bystander effect.” MMAE’s membrane permeability allows it to diffuse to nontarget cells, leading to unintended cytotoxicity in normal tissues [12,15,16]. These mechanisms collectively contribute to the elevated incidence of cAEs observed with EV treatment.

Given the high incidence of skin reactions associated with EV, it is essential to address why this drug remains a necessary treatment option. First, in the EV-301 trial, EV demonstrated median PFS of 5.55 mo and median OS of 12.91 mo, compared with 3.71 and 8.94 mo, respectively, in the chemotherapy group. In the EV-302 trial, the combination of EV and pembrolizumab achieved median OS of 31.5 mo and median PFS of 12.5 mo, compared with 16.1 and 6.3 mo, respectively, in the platinum-based chemotherapy group. These data indicate that, despite its close association with skin reactions, EV offers substantial benefits in prolonging patients’ lives and improving their quality of life. Additionally, the lack of alternative treatment options for patients with metastatic UC who have developed resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors is a critical factor [4,5].

Moreover, some studies suggest that skin reactions may serve as a predictive marker of treatment efficacy. Patients who experience skin reactions tend to have better cancer-specific survival and PFS, as well as a higher objective response rate [17]. In this situation, despite the high prevalence of cAEs, EV still provides a viable and effective alternative, making it a necessary choice for many patients, even when considering the close association with skin reactions.

However, given that SJS/TEN can be life threatening, it is crucial to monitor for these conditions in patients receiving EV, particularly for those with potential associations. Close observation of patients’ skin health is essential throughout EV treatment [18]. Understanding the onset time of these reactions and the associated factors that may predispose patients to these are equally important for early detection and intervention. The onset time for SJS/TEN induced by conventional drugs typically ranges from 4 d to 8 wk, with most cases occurring within 4 wk of drug exposure [19]. In contrast, our analysis revealed that the onset time for SJS/TEN induced by EV is significantly shorter, with the most common time being 14 d after treatment initiation. This shortened onset time suggests that SJS/TEN can occur as early as the first cycle of EV treatment. This finding underscores the need for earlier and more vigilant monitoring for SJS/TEN in patients receiving EV therapy. Immediate discontinuation of EV is recommended if SJS or TEN is suspected. The occurrence of erythema morbilliforme or erythema multiforme should raise suspicion for potential progression to severe drug-induced reactions. Prompt intervention is essential to prevent the development of severe conditions such as SJS or TEN [20].

The identification of high-association factors for EV-induced SJS/TEN should be prioritized in future research. Prior research has demonstrated that Black patients exhibit heightened susceptibility to EV-related cAEs, especially when treated with pembrolizumab within the 3 mo preceding EV therapy; additionally, a higher body mass index has been identified as a significant factor associated with cAEs of EV [21]. Our analysis also revealed that prior immunotherapy, male gender, age over 65 yr, and hypertension are associated with an elevated risk of developing SJS/TEN. These insights emphasize the critical role of considering demographic factors, prior treatments, and comorbidities as predisposing factors for severe cAEs. While not predictive of the initial occurrence of a cAE in the general exposed population, these findings may help characterize patients at risk of more complex AE profiles once an event occurs, potentially informing monitoring strategies or supportive care needs for specific subgroups within the affected patient population. These associations warrant further investigation in studies capable of assessing the overall cAE risk.

Our study is subject to limitations inherent in retrospective FAERS analyses. As a spontaneous reporting system, FAERS is subject to significant under-reporting and selective reporting biases, where severe or novel AEs are disproportionately reported compared with common or mild events, potentially distorting signal magnitudes and compromising cross-drug comparability. Duplicate reports may artificially inflate event frequencies despite our implementation of FDA-recommended deduplication protocols, as inconsistent data entry could permit residual duplicates. Critically, the absence of denominator data precludes incidence rate calculations and obscures the impact of confounding factors such as comorbidities, concomitant medications, and temporal drug utilization patterns—even when contextualized with external prescription data. Similarly, we cannot estimate the predictors of the occurrence of any serious AE or cAE among the entire population of drug-exposed patients, as data on patients without AEs are unavailable in this spontaneous reporting system. Furthermore, unverified causality, variable data quality, and stimulated reporting driven by media coverage introduce additional uncertainty. While disproportionality analyses partially mitigate these issues by focusing on relative reporting ratios, these constraints collectively emphasize that our results constitute hypothesis-generating signals requiring validation through controlled observational studies with structured exposure ascertainment.

Conclusions

In conclusion, despite the notable occurrence of skin reactions and the FDA’s black box warning, many patients choose EV therapy due to its substantial therapeutic benefits and the scarcity of alternative treatments. Understanding the unique onset time of EV-induced SJS/TEN and identifying patients with a higher risk of developing these are essential for effective management of these severe AEs. Future research should focus on elucidating the underlying mechanisms and developing strategies to mitigate skin toxicity while maximizing therapeutic benefits.

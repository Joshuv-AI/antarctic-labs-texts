Retrospective analysis of 104 cases of pediatric severe pertussis in Southern China

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe incidence of pertussis has increased significantly in the past year, with infants at high risk of severe disease and death. We aimed to retrospectively analyze pediatric severe pertussis in southern China.MethodsChildren diagnosed with severe pertussis who were hospitalized in the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU) of Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center from August 2023 to May 2024 were included. We compared clinical characteristics among different groups and identified risk factors for outcomes and indications for exchange blood transfusion (EBT) in severe pertussis.ResultsA total of 104 children with severe pertussis were included. The deceased patients had higher white blood cell counts (WBC) and higher rates of oliguria, pulmonary hypertension (PAH), and tricuspid regurgitation. Kaplan–Meier survival curves showed a lower survival rate in patients with oliguria, hypotension, WBC ≥ 36.0 × 109/L, neutrophil count ≥ 11.9 × 109/L, PAH, and tricuspid regurgitation. Univariate Cox analysis identified risk factors for prognosis: hypotension (HR = 9.36, 95% CI: 1.89–46.41, P = 0.006), WBC (HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.01–1.06, P = 0.002), neutrophil count (HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.01–1.08, P = 0.019), and PAH (HR = 12.50, 95% CI: 2.29–68.31, P = 0.004). Multivariate Cox analysis revealed that hypotension (HR = 6.44, 95% CI: 1.28–32.35, P = 0.024) and PAH (HR = 10.33, 95% CI: 1.87–57.18, P = 0.007) were independent risk factors. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis indicated that WBC (AUC = 0.836, cut-off = 30.2 × 109/L, P < 0.001) and lymphocyte count (AUC = 0.765, cut-off = 13.5 × 109/L, P < 0.001) could predict the need for EBT.ConclusionsIn pediatric severe pertussis with oliguria, hypotension or shock, WBC ≥ 36.0 × 109/L, neutrophil count ≥ 11.9 × 109/L, or PAH, the survival rate decreases significantly, necessitating more aggressive treatments. Both hypotension and PAH have been identified as independent prognostic risk factors. We recommend monitoring for EBT and defining its indications.Keywords: Pertussis, Pediatric, Pulmonary hypertension, Exchange blood transfusion

Introduction

Pertussis is an acute respiratory infectious disease caused by Bordetella pertussis (Bp), characterized by recurrent spasmodic coughing and severe complications, including apnea, hypoxemia, pulmonary hypertension, and pertussis encephalopathy. It remains a leading cause of childhood mortality globally [1]. Although widespread pertussis vaccine has reduced disease incidence, epidemiological surveillance has revealed recurring outbreaks globally [2, 3]. In the past year, pertussis outbreaks were reported worldwide [4–7]. Surveillance data from China’s National Notifiable Diseases Report System (NNDRS) revealed a rapid increase in the incidence of pertussis in China from August 2023 to May 2024, with particularly sharp rises since January 2024. Mortality also increased significantly, from 4 reported deaths in 2023 to 22 deaths during January-May 2024. Most fatalities occurred in infants under 3 months old, highlighting gaps in China’s immunization program that currently excludes both infants < 3 months and maternal vaccination [7].

In conclusion, pertussis recurrence has shown a marked surge since 2024, with infants facing a high risk of severe disease and mortality. Current management of pediatric severe pertussis, particularly regarding risk factors and optimal timing for exchange blood transfusion, still lacks substantial support from large-scale clinical data. To address this gap, our study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data, treatment, and prognosis of severe pertussis during this epidemic period at Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center, aiming to provide clinical recommendations for pediatricians for the management of severe pertussis.

Methods

All enrolled children were hospitalized in the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU) of Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, which provides care for critically ill children in Southern China, from August 1, 2023, to May 30, 2024. All patients were diagnosed with pertussis by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing of pharyngeal swabs and met the diagnostic criteria for severe pertussis. All patients were discharged from the PICU before May 30, 2024. The admission criteria for severe pertussis at our center are as follows: patients with clinical manifestations consistent with spasmodic cough, PCR-positive results, and at least one of the following: repeated apnea, shortness of breath, labile breathing, significantly altered heart rate, hypotension, listlessness, seizures, oliguria, significant edema, echocardiographic evidence of tricuspid regurgitation or pulmonary hypertension, or blood routine showing significantly elevated white blood cell count (≥ 30 × 109L) or lymphocyte count (≥ 12 × 109/L) [8].

We collected demographic data, clinical symptoms, examinations, and treatment information from the electronic medical record system. Demographic data included sex, age at onset (months), contact history, vaccination status, prematurity, comorbidities, and the course (time from onset to hospitalization in days). Clinical symptoms included fever, spasmodic cough, shortness of breath, cyanosis, vomiting, milk refusal, oliguria, convulsions, rapid heart rate, and hypotension. Examinations included full blood count, biochemistry, microbiology tests, X-rays, and echocardiography (ECHO).

Fever was defined as an axillary temperature recorded by the caregiver or upon admission exceeding 37.5 °C. Cyanosis was characterized by a purple complexion or percutaneous oxygen saturation (SpO2) < 94%. A rapid heart rate was defined as a heart rate > 170 bpm in patients ≤ 12 months, and > 140 bpm in patients > 12 months. Hypotension was defined as systolic pressure < 70 mmHg in patients ≤ 12 months, and systolic pressure < 70 + 2 × age (years) mmHg in patients > 12 months. Vaccination referred to the administration of the Diphtheria, Tetanus, and Pertussis (DTP) vaccine. A positive contact history was defined as close contact with an individual epidemiologically linked to the patient who had a preceding cough. When multiple full blood count tests were performed during hospitalization, the highest white blood cell (WBC) count was used for analysis.

Treatments included the duration of ICU stay, respiratory support, nitric oxide inhalation (NO), extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), exchange blood transfusion (EBT), continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and other medications (e.g., continuous muscle relaxation, vasoactive drugs). The endpoint event was death, and survival time was defined as the duration from hospitalization to the endpoint or until May 30, 2024.

Respiratory support included non-invasive mechanical ventilation and invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV), which comprised conventional mechanical ventilation (CMV) and high-frequency oscillatory ventilation (HFOV). Indications for intubation and IMV included repeated apnea, anoxemia uncorrectable by non-invasive mechanical ventilation, respiratory or circulatory failure, and pulmonary atelectasis. ECMO was indicated for pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, respiratory failure, and severe pulmonary hypertension unresponsive to ventilator support or drug treatment. The indications for EBT were based on relevant literature [8]. All enrolled patients received appropriate antibiotic treatment, including azithromycin, erythromycin, piperacillin, cefoperazone, and compound sulfamethoxazole.

Groups

Patients were divided into survivors and non-survivors, vaccinated and non-vaccinated groups based on outcomes and vaccination status. Clinical characteristics, treatment, and prognosis were compared between these groups. Additionally, based on the 66th percentiles of white blood cell (WBC) count and lymphocyte count (36 × 109/L and 20 × 109/L, respectively), patients were categorized into groups, and survival rates between these groups were compared.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 statistical software (IBM, Armonk, New York). Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation, and comparisons were made using the independent t-test. Variables with non-normal distributions, as determined by the Shapiro–Wilk test, were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Dichotomous variables were compared using the chi-squared test. Kaplan–Meier (K-M) survival curves and univariate/multivariate Cox regression were used to identify risk factors for severe pertussis prognosis. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was employed to analyze WBC and lymphocyte counts in determining the need for exchange blood transfusion and predicting disease prognosis. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Epidemic situation

We collected data on pertussis and severe pertussis cases in the PICU of our center from January 2023 to May 2024. As shown in Fig. 1, the incidence of pertussis and severe pertussis increased rapidly starting in August 2023, particularly in 2024. From January to May 2024, 2,481 pertussis cases, 108 severe cases, and 6 deaths were reported. Additionally, 5 death cases occurred in 2024, and 4 severe cases remained in the PICU until May 30, 2024. Therefore, this study included 104 severe cases.

Clinical features and death cases

A total of 104 children with severe pertussis were enrolled, including 57 males (54.8%), 71 (68.3%) aged ≤ 3 months, 54 (76.1%) aged ≤ 2 months, and 14 (13.5%) who had received the DTP vaccine. All vaccinated patients had received only the first dose of the DTP vaccine. Apart from spasmodic cough (101 cases, 97.1%), the most frequent symptoms included polypnea (98 cases, 94.2%) and cyanosis (84 cases, 80.8%). Fever was observed in 56 cases (53.8%). Seizures occurred in 8 cases (7.7%), and oliguria was observed in 14 cases (13.5%). Rapid heart rate was noted in 71 cases (68.3%), and hypotension in 11 cases (10.6%). A total of 64 cases (61.5%) were found to have co-infection with other pathogens. Among the cases with co-infection, viral co-infections were the most common (48 cases), particularly rhinovirus (19 cases) and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV, 16 cases). Co-infections with bacterial were observed in 30 cases, the most common pathogens were Staphylococcus aureus (7 cases), Streptococcus pneumoniae (6 cases), and Haemophilus influenzae (6 cases). What’s more, nearly half of the cases with co-infections (46.9%) were infected with two or more types of pathogens in addition to Bordetella pertussis. Among the five fatal cases, two were co-infected with rhinovirus and one with SARS-CoV-2. All patients received appropriate courses of macrolide antibiotics. Seventy-one patients (68.3%) required endotracheal intubation and invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV). Among these patients, 34 (32.7%) required high-frequency oscillatory ventilation (HFOV). Thirty-three patients (31.7%) received exchange blood transfusion (EBT), 6 patients (5.8%) received continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and 9 patients (8.7%) received nitric oxide inhalation (NO). One patient (1.0%) underwent extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO).

In this study, 6 patients (5.8%) died. Among the deceased, 2 were male, and 5 were aged ≤ 2 months. The remaining patient, aged 8 months, was a premature infant. The duration from onset to death ranged from 10 to 22 days. In 3 cases, antibiotic treatment began more than 7 days after onset. Hypotension was observed in 3 of the deceased. All 6 had oliguria, but none had seizures. The WBC count was > 70 × 109/L in 4 cases, and < 30 × 109/L in one case. Only one patient had no co-infection. Four cases exhibited pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), and 5 cases showed tricuspid regurgitation. All deceased patients received IMV, 4 received HFOV and CRRT, and 1 received ECMO. Four patients underwent exchange blood transfusion, with 2 of them requiring two transfusions. Differences between survivors and deaths

Compared with the survivors, the deceased patients exhibited lower rates of spasmodic cough (P < 0.001), higher rates of oliguria (P < 0.001), and hypertension (P = 0.001). Additionally, more rapid heart rates were observed in the deceased group, although this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.087). The deceased patients had significantly higher WBC counts (P < 0.001), CRP levels (P = 0.001), and APTT values (P = 0.001). Higher serum creatinine (Scr) levels were noted in the deceased, but this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.065). Echocardiographic findings revealed a higher incidence of PAH (P < 0.001) and tricuspid regurgitation (P < 0.001) in the deceased group (Table 1).

Differences between vaccination and non-vaccination

In comparing the vaccinated and non-vaccinated groups, we found that the non-vaccinated group had a more frequent history of positive contact (P = 0.003), more rapid heart rates (P = 0.029), a higher incidence of pneumonia (P = 0.039), and a higher rate of IMV therapy (P = 0.005). Similarly, there were trends toward more cyanosis (P = 0.094), longer ICU stay (P = 0.078), and greater use of vasoactive drugs (P = 0.098) in the non-vaccinated group, though these differences were not statistically significant (Table 3).

Risks for prognosis of severe pertussis

We analyzed the WBC and lymphocyte counts and selected the 66th percentile cut-off points to divide the patients into the following groups: WBC ≥ 36 × 109/L vs. WBC < 36 × 109/L, lymphocyte ≥ 20 × 109/L vs. lymphocyte < 20 × 109/L, and neutrophil ≥ 11.9 × 109/L vs. neutrophil < 11.9 × 109/L. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that patients with oliguria, hypertension, WBC ≥ 36 × 109/L, neutrophil ≥ 11.9 × 109/L, PAH, and tricuspid regurgitation had significantly lower survival rates. No statistical differences in survival rates were found in groups categorized by age ≤ 3 months, non-vaccination status, and lymphocyte ≥ 20 × 109/L (Fig. 2). Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that hypotension (HR = 9.36, 95% CI: 1.89–46.41, P = 0.006), WBC (HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.01–1.06, P = 0.002), neutrophil count (HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.01–1.08, P = 0.019), and pulmonary hypertension (HR = 12.50, 95% CI: 2.29–68.31, P = 0.004) were risk factors for the prognosis of pediatric severe pertussis. Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that hypotension (HR = 6.44, 95% CI: 1.28–32.35, P = 0.024) and pulmonary hypertension (HR = 10.33, 95% CI: 1.87–57.18, P = 0.007) were independent risk factors for poor prognosis in severe pediatric pertussis (Table 4).

Discussion

Our center had reported a recurrence of pertussis between 2018 and 2019, with no more than 10 severe cases each year [9]. However, pertussis epidemics have been reported globally, with most areas experiencing a surge in cases beginning in August 2023, continuing into 2024 [4–7]. According to the data from NNDRS, the number of pertussis cases and related deaths increased significantly compared to the previous epidemic in China [7]. Data from our study demonstrated a similar trend. From January to May 2024, the number of severe pertussis cases and deaths reached 108 and 5, respectively. This concentration of severe cases and deaths highlights the need for heightened clinical awareness. Factors associated with the resurgence of pertussis include enhanced surveillance measures, declining vaccination rates, insufficient vaccine efficacy, and macrolide-resistant Bp [7, 10–12].

Nordholm et al. reported that more than 50% of hospitalizations occurred among infants under 2 months of age [4]. Several retrospective studies have found that infants are at high risk for severe disease and death [13–17]. In our study, 76.1% of cases were in infants under 2 months, and only three cases occurred in patients over 6 years old. Furthermore, five of the six deaths were in infants under 2 months. These global data suggest that infants, particularly those under 2 months, are more likely to develop severe forms of pertussis. In China, the pertussis vaccination strategy includes four doses of the DPT vaccine at 3, 4, 5, and 18 months, but there is no vaccination program for infants under 3 months. In our comparison between the vaccination and non-vaccination groups, we observed more severe clinical symptoms, increased use of IMV, and longer ICU stays in the non-vaccination group. These findings suggest that vaccination can mitigate the severity of pertussis and reduce the need for advanced life support, such as IMV, NO, and ECMO. The previous study has demonstrated that administering the first dose of DTP vaccine at 2 months of age can significantly reduce disease burden [18]. Concurrently, some researches have confirmed the safety of maternal acellular pertussis vaccination during pregnancy, which provides effective protection against severe disease and mortality in young infants [19, 20]. In this study, deceased patients were aged under 2 months and thus ineligible for vaccination under the Chinese immunization schedule. Consequently, they could not receive vaccines. Furthermore, the high rate of contact with positive individuals underscores the critical importance of maternal immunization. China's National Disease Control and Prevention Administration announced an optimized DTP vaccination strategy on December 25, 2024. The updated protocol, implemented from January 1, 2025, introduces two key modifications including adding a fifth booster dose at 6 years of age and adjusting the primary immunization schedule to 2, 4, 6, and 18 months, establishing a complete 5-dose pertussis-containing vaccination program [21]. However, policy implementation faces multiple challenges such as public acceptance, operational execution, and regulatory oversight. Notably, the revised immunization schedule does not incorporate maternal pertussis vaccination, and its ultimate effectiveness requires further longitudinal evaluation through implementation research.

Over the past few decades, many retrospective studies have focused on the clinical manifestations and risk factors for severe pertussis. Factors such as pneumonia, WBC count, lymphocytes, and PAH have been frequently cited as significant [8, 9, 13–17, 22–26]. In this study, we compared survivors and deceased patients. Consistent with previous findings, the WBC count in deceased patients was more than double that of survivors. Additionally, deceased patients exhibited significantly less whooping cough, more oliguria, rapid heart rate, hypotension, and PAH, along with higher CRP, sCr, and APTT. These results suggest atypical symptoms and multi-organ dysfunction in the deceased patients. The presence of atypical symptoms in severe pertussis presents challenges for early identification and timely treatment. Recently, several studies have focused on developing predictive models for pertussis diagnosis and prognosis [27, 28]. However, the clinical feasibility of these models still requires further validation.

Previous studies have identified WBC count and PAH as risk factors for the prognosis of severe pertussis, though the cut-off value for WBC has varied from 30 × 109/L to 100 × 109/L [8, 9, 25, 26, 29–31]. In this study, the results of the K-M survival curve showed lower survival rates in patients with oliguria, hypotension, WBC ≥ 36.0 × 109/L, neutrophils ≥ 11.9 × 109/L, PAH, and tricuspid regurgitation. COX analysis further indicated that WBC count, neutrophil count, hypotension, and PAH were risk factors for prognosis, with hypotension and PAH serving as independent risk factors. These findings suggest that, in addition to WBC and PAH, shock and organ failure—especially renal dysfunction—are predictors of poor prognosis in severe pertussis. Moreover, our data indicate that neutrophil count may be a stronger predictor of outcomes than lymphocyte count in children with severe pertussis. This could be attributed to the fact that more than half of the cases in this study were co-infected with other pathogens. A recent study indicates that rhinovirus, human metapneumovirus, and parainfluenza virus are the most common co-infecting pathogens in pertussis cases, while human bocavirus co-infection has only been observed in patients with respiratory failure [32]. In our cohort, the most prevalent co-infections were RSV and rhinovirus. Among the five fatal cases, two were co-infected with rhinovirus and one with SARS-CoV-2. No cases of human bocavirus co-infection were detected. However, co-infected patients in this study exhibited diverse pathogen profiles, with some harboring multiple concurrent infections, introducing substantial confounding factors. Consequently, comparative analysis between co-infection groups revealed no significant differences in clinical manifestations, duration of ICU, treatments and outcomes. Hence, the potential impact of different co-infecting pathogens on clinical presentation, management, and prognosis of severe pertussis requires further investigation.

A study on the pathology of pertussis identified luminal aggregates of abundant WBCs in small pulmonary arteries, veins, and lymphatics, suggesting that leukocyte mass plays a crucial role in triggering the cascade leading to PAH [33]. In fact, leukocytosis, circulatory failure, and pulmonary hypertension create a vicious cycle that contributes to the poor prognosis of pediatric severe pertussis. EBT has been proposed as a strategy to reduce circulating WBCs and pertussis toxin, thereby alleviating PAH and breaking the vicious cycle [33–35]. However, most studies on EBT in severe pertussis are case reports. Based on data from Winter et al. [25], James D. Cherry et al. [8]. Summarized the indications for EBT in infants under 60 days with pertussis in 2018. These guidelines suggest that EBT is indicated for total WBC ≥ 48 × 109/L with lymphocytes ≥ 15 × 109/L, or WBC ≥ 30 × 109/L with lymphocytes ≥ 15 × 109/L, if there is a ≥ 50% increase in WBC count over 24 h, or WBC ≥ 25 × 109/L with lymphocytes ≥ 12 × 109/L in patients with cardiogenic shock, PAH, or organ failure.

In this study, 98 severe cases (94.2%) involved patients under 12 months of age, 33 of whom received EBT, with half of them being under 60 days. In some severe cases, both WBC and lymphocyte counts were observed to increase again within 6 h after EBT. The results of the K-M survival curve and COX analysis showed that oliguria, hypotension, WBC count, PAH, and tricuspid regurgitation were associated with a poor prognosis in severe pertussis. To assess the efficacy of EBT, ROC curve analysis was performed for WBC and lymphocyte counts. The results showed that WBC (AUC = 0.836, P < 0.001) and lymphocyte count (AUC = 0.765, P < 0.001) were effective predictors for whether exchange blood transfusion was needed. The cut-off values for WBC and lymphocyte counts were 30.2 × 109/L and 13.5 × 109/L, respectively. Based on these results, we propose new suggestions for EBT and monitoring of infants with severe pertussis who are ≤ 12 months of age (Table 5). Compared with existing guidelines, our recommendations specifically target infants under 12 months with severe pertussis. Importantly, more intensive monitoring is required during treatment, with prompt intervention when indicated. This study has several limitations: 1) as a single-center retrospective study, it may be subject to selection bias; 2) the indications for exchange blood transfusion (EBT) remain controversial in clinical practice, resulting in variations in treatment protocols and post-procedure monitoring. Future studies should focus on determining the optimal timing, standardized protocols, and monitoring parameters for EBT in severe pertussis cases.

Pertussis in infants is associated with significant morbidity and mortality. New public health strategies should prioritize pertussis vaccination in infants under 3 months of age and pregnant women. In pediatric severe pertussis cases presenting with oliguria, hypotension or shock, WBC ≥ 36.0 × 109/L, neutrophils ≥ 11.9 × 109/L, and PAH, the survival rate is significantly reduced, and more aggressive treatments are required. Hypotension and PAH are independent risk factors for the prognosis of pediatric severe pertussis. Based on these findings, we propose guidelines for the indication of EBT and monitoring for infants with severe pertussis who are ≤ 12 months of age.

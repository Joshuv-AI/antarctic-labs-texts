Therapeutic vaccination with a live M. bovis-BoAHV-1 combined vaccine accelerates pathogen clearance and restores mammary homeostasis in dairy cows with natural Mycoplasma bovis subclinical mastitis
Authors: Sen Zhang, Guoxing Liu, Haiqiang Cao, Aizhen Guo, Yingyu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Mycoplasma bovis (M. bovis) is the primary pathogen causing subclinical mastitis (SCM) in dairy cows. Although vaccination is a cornerstone of disease control, its efficacy as a therapeutic intervention in naturally infected SCM cases remains largely unexplored. This study is the first to evaluate the immunological intervention effectiveness of an M. bovis-BoAHV-1 combined vaccine in a field-based model of M. bovis natural infection. The vaccine was administered through the subcutaneous (SC) or intranasal (IN) route, with efficacy assessed using positive control and blank control groups as references. The results indicated that the combined vaccine effectively shortened the duration of SCM and alleviated the symptoms, with the SC route demonstrating superior intervention effects compared to the IN route. The pathogen excretion in the SC group decreased significantly and ceased by day 52. Meanwhile, somatic cell counts declined rapidly, with all cows achieving mastitis-negative status by day 49. The pathogen shedding duration and abnormal somatic cells status in the SC group were shortened by 7 and 14 days, respectively, compared to the IN group. The recovery process was further evidenced by the normalization of key mammary health biomarkers, coupled with a notable restoration of lactation performance, confirming the clinical resolution of the infection. Mechanistically, the combined vaccine markedly enhanced M. bovis-specific lymphocyte proliferation capacity, indicating effective stimulation of antigen-specific immunity. These findings provide the first field-practice confirmation of the therapeutic intervention efficacy of a combined vaccine against SCM, offering a promising and non-antibiotic solution to address antibiotic-resistant mastitis.

Introduction

Bovine mastitis is one of the most prevalent and economically damaging diseases in the global dairy industry, fundamentally limiting milk production, quality, and farm profitability [1]. Clinically, this disease is primarily categorized into clinical mastitis (CM) and subclinical mastitis (SCM). CM is typically characterized by observable inflammation and changes in milk properties, whereas SCM lacks visible pathological manifestations but can be diagnosed through laboratory methods such as an elevated somatic cell count (SCC) or pathogen detection [2, 3]. Therefore, SCM poses more insidious and far-reaching challenges to the dairy industry compared to CM. It not only leads to reduced milk production and degraded milk quality, causing substantial direct economic losses [4], but also incurs severe indirect costs due to repeated antibiotic treatments (often ineffective) and increased culling rates [5].

From the epidemiological and public health perspective, persistent subclinical infections serve as a major source of sustained pathogen transmission within cattle herds. The key issue lies in the fact that long-term or repeated antibiotic treatment for SCM greatly promotes the selection of multidrug-resistant pathogens [6]. In particular, M. bovis has garnered increased attention due to its unique pathogenicity and persistence in mastitis cases. M. bovis infection not only causes clinical outbreaks but also leads to the existence of persistent subclinical mastitis, substantially increasing transmission risks within cattle herds [7]. M. bovis also possesses complex immune evasion mechanisms, which enable it to resist phagocytosis by macrophages and neutrophils, inhibit the proliferation of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), and suppress variable surface antigen expression, thereby establishing long-term infection in mammary tissue [8]. Meanwhile, its inherent lack of cell wall structure confers natural resistance to antibiotics such as β-lactams, rendering conventional antibacterial therapies ineffective [9]. Furthermore, the isolation rates of antibiotic-resistant M. bovis strains in SCM cases are constantly rising [7, 10], posing a potential threat to public health [11].

Given the limitations of antibiotic treatment and the increasing pressure to reduce antibiotic residues in the dairy chain, vaccination has become a key component of comprehensive control strategies for bovine mastitis, aiming to reduce infections and block transmission routes. A previous study had confirmed that the M. bovis inactivated vaccine can control its infections under limited artificial infection conditions [12]. However, most current vaccine studies are confined to healthy individuals or experimental infection models, and there is a scarcity of relevant data regarding therapeutic or regulatory effects on dairy cows naturally infected with chronic mastitis in actual farming environments. Therefore, filling the “therapeutic immune intervention” gap is crucial for developing management protocols for SCM.

To address this need, this study investigated the immunological intervention effects of an M. bovis-BoAHV-1 combined vaccine in dairy cows naturally infected with M. bovis in an SCM status for the first time. Specifically, we systematically compared the impacts of subcutaneous and intranasal administration routes on pathogen shedding kinetics, SCC resolution, and the recovery of lactation performance under commercial production conditions. Our findings challenge conventional vaccine perspectives and explore sustainable immunological interventions. By shifting the focus from prophylaxis to therapeutic modulation, this study provides evidence-based insights into alternative strategies for managing M. bovis mastitis, reducing antibiotic dependency, and restoring the productivity of affected dairy herds.

Materials and methods

Cells, vaccine strains, and vaccine preparation

The Madin-Darby bovine kidney (MDBK) cells used for viral propagation were obtained from the China Institute of Veterinary Drug Control (Beijing, China) and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 condition.

The vaccine strains, M. bovis HB150 and BoAHV-1 gG-/tk-, were maintained at the National Key Laboratory of Agricultural Microbiology. M. bovis HB150 is a live attenuated vaccine strain derived from the wild-type HB0801 strain through continuous in vitro passage [13]. The gene-deleted marker vaccine strain, BoAHV-1 gG-/tk-, was constructed by double gene deletion using the BoAHV-1 HB06 wild-type strain as the parental template [14].

The combined vaccine was prepared by mixing M. bovis HB150 and BoAHV-1 gG-/tk- with a lyophilization stabilizer in equal volumes. The final formulation contained 2.0 × 108 CFU of M. bovis HB150 and 1.0 × 106.3 TCID50 of BoAHV-1 per dose. The mixture was subsequently lyophilized and stored at -20 °C until use [15].

Animal experimental design and group allocation

The animal experiments in two distinct phases were sourced and conducted at a large-scale commercial dairy farm (wuhan guangming dairy products co., ltd.) with a herd size of 2000 lactating cows in Hubei province, China. A total of 42 holstein cows in the mid-lactation stage were enrolled to evaluate the safety and therapeutic efficacy of the m. bovis-boahv-1 combined vaccine. During the entire two-phase experimental period, all cows were uniformly managed by the dairy farm.

Pre-experiment: safety and shedding evaluation

To assess the safety of the combined vaccine and monitor for potential vaccine-strain shedding, 18 healthy cows were enrolled in the pre-experiment. Inclusion criteria for these cows required somatic cell counts (scc) < 20 × 104 cells/ml and milk samples confirmed negative for m. bovis, staphylococcus aureus, streptococcus agalactiae, and streptococcus uberis via pcr and bacterial culture. The animals were randomly assigned to 3 groups (n = 6 per group):SC-safety group: healthy cows received a single dose of the combined vaccine (2 mL) via subcutaneous injection in the neck using a 5 mL syringe.IN-safety group: healthy cows received a single dose of the vaccine via intranasal inoculation using a 2 mL syringe (1 mL per nostril).BC-safety group - safety: healthy cows received no intervention.

Formal experiment: therapeutic efficacy trial

Following the confirmation of safety in the pre-experiment, a separate cohort of 24 cows was enrolled to evaluate the therapeutic impact on subclinical mastitis. The 18 cows meeting the subclinical mastitis criteria were confirmed positive only for M. bovis and negative for other major pathogens (Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus agalactiae, and Streptococcus uberis) using PCR detection and bacterial culture from collected milk samples, with SCC > 50 × 104 cells/mL and no visible abnormalities in milk or udder texture. Crucially, all selected cows had no history of prior antibiotic treatments before enrollment, with no antibiotics or any other therapeutic drugs added to the feed or drinking water during the experiment. Meanwhile, 6 healthy cows serving as negative controls were selected using the same criteria as in the aforementioned safety pre-experiment. Power analysis and sample size selection

The sample size of n = 6 per group was determined based on a prior power analysis using the Stata17 software [16]. According to preliminary data on M. bovis shedding reduction and SCC decrease, a large effect size was anticipated. At a significance level of 0.05 and a power of 95%, it was calculated that 6 cows per group would provide sufficient statistical power while adhering to the ethical principles of the 3Rs to minimize animal use [17].

Sample collection

For individuals in the pre-experiment groups, milk samples and anticoagulant and procoagulant blood samples were collected on days 0, 7, 14, 21, and 28 after immunization.

In the formal experiment, milk samples were collected from all cows at baseline (day 0) and at regular intervals (days 3, 7, 10, 14, 17, 21, 24, 28, 31, 35, 38, 42, 45, 49, 52, 56, 59, 63, 66, and 70) following vaccination. Besides, anticoagulated blood samples were collected on days 7, 14, 21, and 28.

Milk and blood samples were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. For serum preparation, the supernatants were harvested as serum samples. For whey preparation, the middle pale white aqueous phase between the upper fat layer and the lower cell sediment was collected as whey samples. All samples were stored at -20 °C.

Detection of M. bovis antibody, antimicrobial peptide, and haptoglobin

Commercial ELISA kits (Guosheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Harbin, China) were employed to quantify M. bovis-specific IgG antibodies in serum samples. Antimicrobial peptide (AMP) and haptoglobin levels were determined using commercial ELISA kits from Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China).

Somatic cell count

The somatic cell counts in fresh milk samples were determined using an automated cow somatic cell analyzer (hengmei electronic technology co., ltd., Weifang, China).

Milk production monitoring and total protein content analysis

Daily milk production data was sourced from the farm’s proprietary monitoring management system, which meticulously records the average daily performance for each cow at each milking session via electronic milk meters. For statistical analysis, the daily milk yield (kg/day) was recorded for each experimental animal at the specified time points.

Total protein content was analyzed using a rapid milk component analyzer (Yankai Instrument Equipment Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The analyzer was calibrated daily with fresh milk samples to ensure accuracy. All measurements were performed in triplicate to provide a representative mean value for each sample.

Monitoring of M. bovis and BoAHV-1 shedding

To monitor the shedding of M. bovis and BoAHV-1 in milk, genomic DNA was extracted from milk samples using an automated genomic extraction instrument (Vazyme Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China). The presence of M. bovis and BoAHV-1 was detected via PCR targeting the uvrC gene and the glycoprotein G (gG), respectively. The PCR conditions for the M. bovis uvrC gene were: 95 °C for 3 min; 95 °C for 15 s, 55 °C for 15 s, 72 °C for 30 s, 35 cycles; 72 °C for 5 min. For BoAHV-1 gG, the reaction conditions were: 95 °C for 3 min; 95 °C for 15 s, 60.5 °C for 15 s, 72 °C for 40 s, 35 cycles; 72 °C for 5 min.

To quantitatively analyze the shedding levels of M. bovis, RT-PCR was applied using the OPPD gene specific to M. bovis. The primer and probe sequences of the OPPD gene are as follows: OPPD-F: TCGCATAACATTAGTGTTGTCG, OPPD-R: TCTGGTGGGGTTCCTTGA, OPPD-P: FAM-CTCATCATCATTTTCAGGTATAGCCGAGAT-BHQ1. The RT-PCR program is as follows: 95 °C for 3 min, and 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 10 s, 60 °C for 20 s. It should be noted that the limit of detection for the RT-PCR assays in milk samples were determined to be 102 copies/µL.

Lymphocyte proliferation assay

PBMCs were isolated from sodium heparin anticoagulated blood samples. The blood was centrifuged at room temperature for 10 min to separate the components, and the middle cell layer was collected and treated with 4–6 times the volume of erythrocyte lysate at 4 °C for 2 min to lyse erythrocytes. This step was repeated until complete lysis of erythrocytes occurred. The supernatant was discarded, and the isolated PBMCs were washed twice with 10 mL of RPMI-1640 complete medium.

Isolated PMBCs (1.0 × 106 cells) were seeded into 96-well culture plates and stimulated with 10 µL of medium containing either concanavalin A (ConA, 25 µg/mL) or M. bovis whole-cell protein (25 µg/mL). The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 48 h. Following incubation, 10 µL of CCK8 reagent was added to each well, and the plates were incubated at 37 °C for an additional 4 h. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm. The stimulation index (SI) was calculated via the following formula: SI = mean OD value of stimulated wells/mean OD value of unstimulated wells.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software version 9.0. Data were presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). For baseline parameters (Table 1), differences across four groups were evaluated using ordinary one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. To assess the effects of vaccination over time, a two-way repeated-measures ANOVA was employed, with treatment group and time as fixed factors, followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for pairwise comparisons. Normality and homoscedasticity were verified using the Shapiro-Wilk and Brown-Forsythe tests prior to analyses, respectively. These tests were chosen to ensure the accurate and reliable interpretation of differences across the experimental groups. Statistical significance is denoted as follows: p < 0.05 (*), p < 0.01 (**), p < 0.001 (***), and p < 0.0001 (****).

Results

The combined vaccine is safe and effective for healthy lactating cows

In the first experiment, the combined vaccine was administered using two immunization patterns. Results demonstrated that high titers of M. bovis-specific IgG antibodies were detected in the serum samples from individuals in both vaccinated groups. Two-way RM ANOVA identified the vaccine route as the primary source of variation (65.30%, p < 0.0001). Notably, the SC-safety group exhibited a more rapid IgG antibody response, achieving complete seroconversion in all cows by day 14 post-vaccination, whereas the IN-safety group did not reach herd positivity until day 21. Despite these kinetic differences, Tukey’s multiple comparisons test revealed no statistically significant difference in antibody intensity between the SC and IN routes at any specific time point (p > 0.05) (Fig. 2A).

During the 28-day observation period following immunization, the somatic cells in milk from cows in both vaccinated groups remained stable below 20 × 104 cells/mL. Minor fluctuations observed in a few individuals were consistent with the physiological variations seen in the healthy BC-safety group, suggesting these variations were independent of vaccine administration (Fig. 2B).

In addition, no genomic DNA from the M. bovis and BoAHV-1 vaccine strain or wild-type strains were detected in the milk of cows from any of the 2 immunized groups at all sampling time points.

Taken together, the application of the combined vaccine effectively activates the M. bovis-specific IgG antibody response in lactating cows without compromising mammary health or inducing vaccine strain shedding. Consequently, this vaccine platform was deemed safe and suitable for the subsequent therapeutic intervention trials.

Baseline characteristics of cows in formal experiment

As detailed in Table 1, the baseline characteristics of the four groups in the formal therapeutic trial were evaluated at Day 0. All cows were in the mid-lactation stage, and no statistically significant differences were observed in physiological age, total days in milk, or initial daily milk production (p > 0.05).

Regarding initial SCC, significant differences were observed between the healthy NC group and the three M. bovis-infected groups (p < 0.0001). Crucially, no statistical variations were detected among the three infected groups (p = 0.2725), ensuring comparable disease severity prior to therapy.

Impact of the therapeutic vaccination on the amount and duration of M. bovis shedding in milk

The combined vaccine demonstrated a favorable therapeutic intervention effect regardless of whether it was administered through the subcutaneous or intranasal route, as evidenced by the generally falling trend in M. bovis shedding level in the milk of both the SC and IN groups. However, the subsequent clearance kinetics diverged between the two administration routes. Specifically, cows administrated with subcutaneous injection experienced a significant reduction in the M. bovis shedding by day 42, with this excretion ceasing entirely by day 52. While the persistence of M. bovis occurrence in the milk of the IN group was 7 days longer than that in the SC group, with this excretion extended to the 59th day. On the contrary, no obvious reduction in M. bovis shedding was observed in the PC group during the initial 42 days. Afterwards, this group exhibited a delayed pathogen shedding decline, yet 2 cows excreted the M. bovis until day 70 (Fig. 3A).

When quantitatively analyzing the difference in shedding levels between groups, the two-way RM ANOVA identified that the time factor (47.87%, p < 0.0001) and its interaction with vaccination time (p = 0.0079) jointly contributed to these disparities. Although the excretion amounts in the SC group were lower than those in the IN group at the majority of observation points, this modest gap did not result in a statistically significant difference between the two vaccinated groups (p > 0.05). However, a marked reduction in the M. bovis shedding amounts in the SC group was noted as early as day 28 compared to the PC group (p = 0.0191), and this substantial difference persisted consistently through day 42 (p < 0.01) (Fig. 3B).

Therapeutic vaccination enhances antigen-specific immunity

To evaluate the systemic immune response mobilized during therapeutic intervention, M. bovis-specific lymphocyte proliferative activity was longitudinally monitored. As a critical quality control, non-specific stimulation with ConA yielded comparable stimulation index levels across all groups (Fig. 4A), confirming the consistent cellular viability.

Fig. 4Enhancement of M. bovis-specific immune response after therapeutic vaccination. PBMCs were isolated and stimulated with (A) ConA (non-specific stimuli) or (B) M. bovis whole-cell protein to measure the lymphocyte proliferation capacity following vaccination

Enhancement of M. bovis-specific immune response after therapeutic vaccination. PBMCs were isolated and stimulated with (A) ConA (non-specific stimuli) or (B) M. bovis whole-cell protein to measure the lymphocyte proliferation capacity following vaccination

In contrast, stimulation with M. bovis whole-cell protein elicited distinct immunodynamic profiles, and the time factor (92.09%, p < 0.0001) was recognized as the dominant contributor for the total variation by the two-way RM ANOVA, with a significant interaction between vaccination route and time (p < 0.0001). Both vaccinated groups demonstrated a rapid enhancement of lymphocyte activity post-inoculation. Notably, the SC group exhibited a significant surge in SI between day 7 and day 14 (p = 0.0002), reaching a peak mean SI value of 2.163. The average SI values at specified observation points (days 7, 14, and 28) were significantly higher in this group compared to the untreated PC group (p = 0.0007, p < 0.0001, and p = 0.0107). While the IN group followed a similar upward trend, its magnitude was numerically lower. The NC group maintained a stable baseline, further confirming that the observed immune mobilization was antigen-specific. These results indicate that subcutaneous therapeutic vaccination provides the robust lymphocyte framework necessary for the superior pathogen clearance observed (Fig. 4B).

Resolution of mammary inflammation based on somatic cell counts

To evaluate the clinical therapeutic efficacy against SCM, milk somatic cell counts were monitored over a 70-day period. Following vaccination, the SC group demonstrated a rapid and consistent recovery profile. Individual trajectories revealed that while initial inflammation was severe, SCC levels in the SC group showed a highly significant reduction by day 21, and over half of the cows (4/6) achieved negative mastitis status by day 35. By day 49, somatic cells in all cows had dropped below 50 × 104 cells/mL. Cows in the IN group showed a dramatic reduction in somatic cells until day 35, and 1 cow still had somatic cells higher than 50 × 104 cells/mL at day 56. It was not until day 63 that all individuals tested negative for mastitis, with this pathological change lasting 14 days longer than in the SC group. In contrast, SCC levels in the PC group fluctuated and remained consistently above the pathological threshold throughout the study, with several individuals showing persistent chronic inflammation. SCC amounts in the NC group remained stable below 15 × 104 cells/mL throughout the entire observation period (Fig. 5A).

Comparative inter-group analysis further confirmed the superior therapeutic effect of the subcutaneous route. This differential characteristic was also displayed by the result of the two-way RM ANOVA analysis: the synergistic effect of vaccine route and post- immunization time profoundly influenced SCC levels among groups (p = 0.0001). The mean SCC level in the subcutaneous treatment group remained lower than that in the intranasal immunization group at most time points, with significant differences observed on days 21, 49, and 63 (p = 0.0207, p = 0.0007, and p = 0.0251, respectively). Meanwhile, the SC group also demonstrated preferable reductions in SCC amounts than the PC group throughout the 49–70 days during the observation period (p < 0.05) (Fig. 5B). These findings, coupled with the accelerated pathogen clearance data, illustrate that the combined vaccine effectively restores M. bovis-induced SCM and restores mammary health.

Modulation of inflammatory biomarkers associated with mastitis

Therapeutic vaccination significantly attenuated the local inflammatory response in the mammary gland, with the SC group demonstrating a more efficient and thorough resolution of inflammation. Two-way RM ANOVA showed that the vaccine route was the leading driver of these changes, accounting for 35.30% of the variation in AMP (p < 0.0001) and 41.25% of the variation in haptoglobin.

Regarding AMP levels, no significant differences were observed among the infected groups during the first 14 days post-vaccination. A critical divergence between the two vaccination routes emerged at day 21, where the SC group exhibited a markedly lower AMP concentration compared to the IN group (p = 0.0420). Although the SC group remained significantly higher than the healthy NC group at this time point (p = 0.0113), this marked the first instance where the subcutaneous route demonstrated superior recovery kinetics over the intranasal route. The first statistically significant therapeutic advantage of the SC group over the untreated PC group was established at day 35 (p = 0.0358). Following a period of non-significant fluctuation between days 42 and 63, the SC group reached a final, definitive reduction compared to the PC group at the study’s conclusion (day 70, p = 0.0474) (Fig. 6A).

The trend in haptoglobin changes revealed a crucial divergence in the quality of recovery: haptoglobin concentrations in the SC group had declined to levels that were no longer significantly different from the healthy NC group at day 49 (p = 0.8919), signaling a return to the physiological baseline. In contrast, the IN group did not reach a normal state comparable to the NC group until day 56 (p = 0.5725), indicating a delayed resolution of inflammation. However, by the end of the study (day 70), the PC group continued to exhibit pathologically high haptoglobin levels compared to the healthy NC baseline (p = 0.0045) (Fig. 6B). These molecular findings demonstrate that the subcutaneous route provides a more rapid and clinically complete resolution of mammary inflammation.

Recovery of milk production performance in cows with subclinical mastitis

To evaluate the economic benefits of the combined vaccine, daily milk production was monitored throughout the 70-day study period, and two-way RM ANOVA revealed that the vaccine route significantly influenced the trajectory of lactation recovery, explaining 43.76% of the total disparity (p < 0.0001).

The milk production of all cows in the SC group and IN group both showed a trend of initially declining and then steadily recovering throughout the observation period. Following the initial impact of SCM, both vaccinated groups showed a steady restoration of productivity. In contrast, milk production in the PC group remained severely depressed throughout the majority of the trial, failing to compensate for the lactation losses caused by the infection (Fig. 7A).

Intra-group comparisons relative to the pre-vaccination baseline (day 0) highlighted the robust recovery in the SC group. After a transient fluctuation in the early stage, the milk yield of SC cows showed a steady and significant increase, with daily production notably higher than its day 0 baseline value between days 35 and 70. In contrast, the milk yield of the IN group did not show a meaningful recovery relative to baseline until day 70 (p = 0.0433), suggesting that while the intranasal route supported recovery, it failed to achieve the same magnitude of production enhancement observed with subcutaneous inoculation. The performance of the PC group was most severely and persistently affected. It was not until day 56 that milk production reversed its initial decline, subsequently showing a considerable growth rate. However, this accelerated improvement failed to result in a daily production rate that differed from the original average (Fig. 7B).

Comparisons between groups and two-way RM ANOVA analysis further confirmed that vaccination status and vaccine route were the dominant contributors of milk production differences among groups (60.04%, p < 0.0001). The SC group achieved a successful return to healthy levels, maintaining significantly higher milk yields than the IN and PC groups at most time points, respectively (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01). Crucially, this elite recovery capability also enabled the SC group to eliminate statistical differences in lactation performance with the healthy NC group by day 28 (p = 0.2173). These results collectively illustrate that the subcutaneous therapeutic vaccination is uniquely effective in restoring and enhancing milk production performance to near-healthy levels in cows affected by SCM (Fig. 7C).

In the meantime, monitoring results for total protein content in milk samples showed that no remarkable variation in total protein content was observed in milk samples from any experimental group, including the blank control, except for a few extreme values (Fig. 7D).

Discussion

This study presented herein transcends conventional veterinary vaccine evaluations, redefining subcutaneous injection of an M. bovis-BoAHV-1 combined vaccine as a transformative immunotherapeutic intervention for bovine SCM. Our findings convincingly demonstrated that this method not only effectively suppresses pathogen shedding but also accelerates the functional recovery of mammary tissue. Consequently, it enables the clinical and functional resolution of SCM associated with M. bovis, which has long been regarded as a management dead end, to become a reality [18].

The prerequisite for applying attenuated live vaccines in lactating cow herds is to ensure that the induction of immune response is achieved while avoiding the occurrence of iatrogenic intramammary infections or systemic adverse reactions. Therefore, the delicate balance between attenuation level and immunogenicity is crucial [19]. Previous research indicated that a live vaccine targeting Staphylococcus aureus, although holding protective efficacy, caused a temporary increase in somatic cell counts [20]. The present findings in this study demonstrated that the combined vaccine had superior safety characteristics compared to traditional live vaccines, as evidenced by its ability to induce a rapid and robust immune response while maintaining stable somatic cell counts. Besides, no excretion of M. bovis or BoAHV-1 was detected in the milk, confirming that the antigen components of the vaccine did not breach the blood-milk barrier, which confirmed its excellent biosafety profile for field application.

The intractable nature of chronic M. bovis mastitis stems from its sophisticated evasion strategies, including intracellular sequestration, which renders it largely refractory to conventional antibiotics that fail to penetrate the protected microenvironment of the mammary gland, compelling a shift in treatment paradigms from direct antibacterial assault to host-directed immunotherapy [18, 21]. This study confirmed significantly accelerated M. bovis clearance in vaccinated cows, indicating that the memory immune responses might be effectively activated. Due to the facultative intracellular parasitic nature of M. bovis, its clearance process is highly dependent on cell-mediated immune (CMI) responses, particularly the activation of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells [22–24]. In this experiment background, the immune systems of the infected cows had already developed sensitivity to wild-type M. bovis strains, allowing the vaccine to act as a potent adjuvant to activate the host’s secondary immune response. Furthermore, the powerful proliferative capacity of PBMCs from inoculated individuals also directly corroborated this systemic T-cell expansion effect [25]. In the natural infection model established in this study, a persistent local inflammatory response in the mammary gland may lead to the continuous release of certain chemokines, thereby recruiting systemically expanded specific T cells. These antigen-specific T cells are likely to overcome the immunosuppression induced by M. bovis by activating resident macrophages, thereby promoting the targeted clearance of intracellular pathogens [26]. In contrast, while untreated positive individuals were also affected, their PBMCs showed delayed proliferative capacity, potentially attributed to lymphocyte apoptosis or immunosuppressive mechanisms induced by the M. bovis wild-type strain [8, 27]. Therefore, therapeutic vaccination not only reverses the local immune exhaustion or tolerance state induced by persistent wild-type infection but also strengthens CMI levels through secondary immunity, thereby accelerating the elimination of intracellular pathogens [28].

The decline in somatic cell counts indicates that mammary homeostasis is in the process of recovery. Vaccinated cows rapidly achieved a somatic cell count below 50 × 104 cells/mL, which is a practical standard meeting regulatory requirements for the dairy industry in many developing countries. However, this reduced value did not reach the baseline level observed in the healthy controls (less than 20 × 104 cells/mL) [29], which also represents the physiological threshold distinguishing health from SCM in the international dairy system [30]. This discrepancy may suggest that while the vaccine intervention achieved clinical resolution, complete repair of mammary parenchyma tissue may not have occurred simultaneously. Concurrently, the observed decline in AMP and haptoglobin levels within the immunized group revealed the transition from an active inflammatory status to the period of inflammation resolution [31, 32]. The two inflammatory biomarkers decreased rapidly and returned to healthy physiological baseline levels at day 35 (AMP) and day 49 (haptoglobin), respectively. This trend also closely correlates with the swift decline in milk M. bovis loads: the effective pathogen clearance induces cessation of pathogen-associated molecular pattern signals in the host immune system, leading to down-regulation of innate immune effectors, indicating that the host immune system terminates the unnecessary continuous activation, marking the restoration of mammary gland homeostasis [33]. Owing to the intervention effect of vaccination, excessive inflammatory responses have been effectively alleviated, potentially preventing progressive mammary gland tissue damage.

From an economic perspective, the primary goal of controlling mastitis lies in minimizing milk production losses and lowering culling rates [34]. Compared to untreated positive controls, vaccination significantly accelerated the recovery of lactation levels. This pronounced production restoration stemmed from the therapeutic intervention effect of the combined vaccine, which facilitated faster pathogen clearance and effectively limited the lasting inflammatory damage to mammary tissue, thereby creating conditions for prompt restoration of lactation function. The subcutaneous administration route is considered pivotal, facilitating efficient antigen drainage to regional lymph nodes and generating a potent systemic effector T-cell response [35, 36]. In contrast, the intranasal route may be hindered by innate barriers and mucosal tolerance mechanisms, resulting in insufficient systemic CMI response to eradicate established chronic infections.Also, the intranasal way also suffers from inherent physical losses and uneven dosage due to practical operational limitations [37, 38]. However, milk production in SC group did not fully recover to healthy baseline levels by day 70, suggesting that mammary alveolar epithelial cell apoptosis or parenchymal fibrosis that occurred during SCM might have caused partial permanent tissue damage [39]. In terms of milk quality, although total protein content appeared unchanged across experimental groups, this may mask the compensatory relationship between casein and serum proteins. Inflammatory responses typically inhibit casein synthesis while increasing the leakage of serum proteins such as serum albumin and immunoglobulin G into milk [40, 41], and this reduced casein proportion actually severely affects milk quality. The rapid recovery of productivity and the alleviation of chronic inflammatory damage further underscore the importance of early immunologic intervention, as it not only reduces the environmental burden of pathogen shedding and restores the production potential of infected cows, but also minimizes direct economic losses from involuntary culling due to M. bovis-targeted SCM cases [42, 43], which is essential for breaking the endemic transmission cycle of M. bovis within modern dairy systems.

This study still has several limitations that warrant further investigation. Firstly, the single-farm research design may limit the generalizability of findings to herds with different feeding management models or diversity of M. bovis strains. Secondly, the follow-up duration did not extend to the subsequent late lactation and dry-off periods, which are also critical windows for disease recurrence [44]. As a result, longitudinal studies are needed to validate the long-term sustainability of vaccine intervention effects and resistance to reinfection. Furthermore, in this study, “pathogen clearance” was defined as a reduction in the concentration of pathogens in milk samples to below the limit of detection, accompanied by resolution of inflammation. Therefore, further research is required to determine whether therapeutic vaccines induce true “pathogen clearance” or merely suppress active shedding.

Conclusions

This study provides the first field trial evidence confirming that subcutaneous interventional vaccination with an M. bovis-BoAHV−1 combined vaccine can serve as a promising alternative strategy for treating chronic bovine M. bovis mastitis. Compared to untreated positive cows, the vaccine intervention significantly accelerated pathogen clearance in mammary tissue, promoted inflammation resolution, and facilitated lactation recovery. This work challenges the conventional view that vaccines serve solely as preventive tools, achieving a true functional resolution. Against the background of escalating antimicrobial resistance, this immunological intervention offers a sustainable and antibiotic-alternative strategy that not only minimizes production losses but also aligns with “one health” requirements, providing a scientifically grounded and economically viable solution to address one of the most stubborn pathogens in the dairy industry.

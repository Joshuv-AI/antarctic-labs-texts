Comparative Evaluation of Subgingivally Delivered Gengigel and Nanobio Fusion Gel as an Adjunct to Scaling and Root Planing in the Management of Chronic Periodontitis: A Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial
Authors: Tabinda Khan, Swati Agarwal, Ellora Madan, Abhinav Abhinav, Pransi Gupta, Arham Siddiqui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To evaluate and compare the clinical effectiveness of subgingivally delivered Gengigel and nanobio fusion (NBF) gel as adjuncts to scaling and root planing (SRP) in patients with chronic periodontitis.Materials and Methods:This randomized controlled clinical trial included patients aged 18–65 years diagnosed with chronic periodontitis and presenting with periodontal pockets measuring ≥5 mm and ≤10 mm. Ninety-nine periodontal sites were randomly allocated into three groups: Group A (SRP alone), Group B (SRP with subgingival Gengigel), and Group C (SRP with subgingival NBF gel). Clinical parameters including plaque index, modified sulcular bleeding index, probing pocket depth (PPD), and clinical attachment level were recorded at baseline, 15 days, and 3 months. Statistical analysis was performed to assess intra- and intergroup differences.Results:All three groups demonstrated statistically significant improvement in clinical parameters from baseline to 3 months (P < 0.01). Intergroup analysis showed that both adjunctive therapy groups exhibited superior outcomes compared to SRP alone. Group B demonstrated greater and sustained reduction in gingival bleeding and PPD at 3 months, whereas Group C showed early clinical improvements.Conclusion:Subgingival delivery of Gengigel and NBF gel as adjuncts to SRP resulted in significant clinical benefits in chronic periodontitis management. Gengigel demonstrated a slight advantage in long-term reduction of inflammation and pocket depth.

INTRODUCTION

Chronic periodontitis is an inflammatory condition affecting the tooth-supporting tissues, characterized by periodontal pocket formation, attachment loss, and progressive alveolar bone destruction. The disease arises from complex interactions between microbial biofilm and host immune-inflammatory responses, leading to tissue breakdown and eventual tooth loss if left untreated.[1]

Mechanical debridement through scaling and root planing (SRP) remains the primary nonsurgical approach for periodontal therapy. Although SRP effectively reduces microbial load, complete elimination of pathogenic microorganisms from deep pockets, furcation areas, and anatomical irregularities remains challenging. These limitations have prompted the use of adjunctive therapeutic modalities to enhance clinical outcomes.[2]

Local drug delivery systems allow direct placement of therapeutic agents into periodontal pockets, achieving high local concentrations while minimizing systemic exposure[3] Gengigel®, a hyaluronic acid-based formulation, has been shown to exhibit anti-inflammatory, bacteriostatic, and wound-healing properties and supports tissue repair through modulation of inflammatory responses.[4] Nanobio fusion (NBF) gel is a nanoemulsion enriched with antioxidants and natural extracts designed to enhance penetration into periodontal pockets and provide antimicrobial and antioxidant effects.[5]

Although individual benefits of Gengigel and NBF gel have been reported, comparative clinical evidence evaluating their adjunctive efficacy remains limited. The present study was therefore undertaken to assess and compare the clinical effectiveness of these two agents when used alongside SRP in the treatment of chronic periodontitis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This single-center, full-mouth randomized controlled trial with a 1:1 allocation was conducted in the Department of Periodontology, Kothiwal Dental College and Research Centre, Moradabad, after obtaining ethical approval and written informed consent from all participants. Patients aged 18–65 years diagnosed with chronic periodontitis and presenting with at least three nonadjacent periodontal sites with probing pocket depths (PPDs) of ≥5 mm and ≤10 mm were included.

Exclusion criteria comprised patients who had received periodontal therapy or antibiotic treatment within the previous 3 months, smokers, pregnant or lactating women, individuals with systemic conditions influencing periodontal health, and those on medications affecting wound healing.[6]

A total of 99 periodontal sites were randomly allocated into three groups:

Group A: Scaling and root planing aloneGroup B: Scaling and root planing followed by subgingival application of GengigelGroup C: Scaling and root planing followed by subgingival application of NBF gel.

All patients underwent full-mouth SRP using ultrasonic and hand instruments. In Group B, Gengigel was delivered subgingivally using a blunt-tipped syringe, with a second application performed after 1 week. In Group C, NBF gel was applied subgingivally immediately after SRP. No additional medications were prescribed.[45]

Clinical parameters recorded at baseline, 15 days, and 3 months included plaque index (PI), modified sulcular bleeding index, PPD, and clinical attachment level (CAL). Measurements were obtained using a UNC-15 periodontal probe and customized acrylic stents to ensure reproducibility. Statistical analysis was performed using one-way ANOVA with post hoc Bonferroni test, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Intragroup analysis revealed statistically significant reductions in PI, modified sulcular bleeding index (mSBI), and PPD, along with significant gains in CAL in all three groups from baseline to 3 months (P < 0.01). Intergroup comparison at baseline showed no statistically significant differences, confirming comparability among the groups. At 15 days, Groups B and C demonstrated greater reductions in plaque accumulation and gingival bleeding compared to Group A. At the 3-month evaluation, Group B exhibited the greatest reduction in mSBI and PPD, followed by Group C. Improvements in CAL were also higher in the adjunctive therapy groups compared to SRP alone.

Overall, adjunctive use of both Gengigel and NBF gel resulted in enhanced clinical outcomes compared to mechanical therapy alone, with Gengigel demonstrating superior long-term effects [Tables 1–3].

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrated that adjunctive subgingival delivery of Gengigel and NBF gel significantly enhanced the clinical effectiveness of scaling and root planing in patients with chronic periodontitis. These findings support the role of local drug delivery systems in improving periodontal treatment outcomes.[3]

The sustained reduction in gingival inflammation and PPD observed in the Gengigel group may be attributed to the biological properties of hyaluronic acid, which plays a role in modulating inflammatory responses, promoting angiogenesis, and facilitating fibroblast proliferation.[4] These mechanisms may contribute to improved tissue healing and long-term stability.

NBF gel demonstrated notable early improvements, possibly due to its nanoemulsion formulation, which enhances penetration into periodontal pockets and delivers antimicrobial and antioxidant components directly to the diseased site.[5] However, the long-term clinical outcomes were comparatively more favorable with Gengigel.

The results of the present study are consistent with previous reports highlighting the benefits of adjunctive local drug delivery in periodontal therapy.[45] Nevertheless, the limited sample size and short follow-up period warrant further long-term and multicenter investigations.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of the present study, it can be concluded that both Gengigel and NBF gel, when used as adjuncts to scaling and root planing, result in significant clinical improvements in chronic periodontitis. Gengigel demonstrated a slight advantage in sustained reduction of gingival inflammation and PPD at 3 months. Adjunctive local drug delivery may be considered a valuable addition to nonsurgical periodontal therapy.

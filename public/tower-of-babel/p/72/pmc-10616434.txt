Welcome from the new CKJ Editor-in-Chief
Authors: Jürgen Floege
Published: 2023

A big thank you to the European Renal Association (ERA) council for electing me as the new Editor-in-Chief of Clinical Kidney Journal! I am very honored by this decision and hope to successfully continue the work of my esteemed immediate predecessors, Alberto Ortiz and María José Soler, as well as the ad interim Editor Mario Cozzolino, who managed the journal so well in particular during the hard times of the COVID-19 epidemic. Together they have continued to shape the journal to ensure that it maintains its place as a leading European medical publication addressing kidney disease.

A BRIEF LOOK BACK

Starting as Editor-in-Chief of CKJ on 1 September 2023 felt like a leap back in time. In 2005 I was Associate Editor of Nephrology Dialysis Transplantation (NDT) and under the great leadership of the NDT past-Editor Norbert Lameire, we felt there was a need for a second ERA journal to provide for enough room to publish the increasing number of articles that were being submitted. So, we founded ‘NDT Plus’ in 2008, subsequently renamed to Clinical Kidney Journal (CKJ) in 2011. While NDT offered publication of all original work in the field of kidney-related research, CKJ quickly established itself with a focus on clinical and translational work.

CKJ AT PRESENT

CKJ currently publishes only 19% of all work submitted. I thank and congratulate all authors whose work is accepted! At the same time, I realize that the majority of submitted papers will be rejected, causing frustration and disappointment, but publication ‘space’ restrictions do not allow a more liberal policy and I apologize to all authors whose work we could not and cannot accept for priority reasons.

High-quality publications rely on vigorous review. Thank you in advance to a stellar Editorial Board and in particular to the Associate Editors who shoulder a high burden of work! CKJ will continue using anonymized peer review as a vital component of our quality assessment of articles. This will inevitably delay publication and may be viewed as conservative, carrying the risk of rejecting innovative ideas and of course peer review often cannot detect errors or, in particular, misconduct. On the positive side, often reviews result in excellent reviewer feedback which enables clarifications to be made and studies to be improved; this, combined with an efficient Editorial Board and Office will increase the trust of authors and readers, and lastly the community. In addition, the methodological quality of clinical publications has improved dramatically over the last 10–15 years, influenced by reporting standards such as CONSORT (parallel-group randomized controlled trials), STROBE (observational epidemiology), SPIRIT (study protocols), STARD (diagnostic accuracy) and PRISMA (systematic reviews); authors are referred to www.equator-network.org and COPE: Committee on Publication Ethics | Promoting integrity in scholarly research and its publication. We will strongly encourage all authors to follow these standards. All this is of central importance in a clinical journal, where published data often contribute to guidelines and thus directly affect the management (and safety) of our patients.

The journal will continue to publish high-quality clinical and translational research related to any aspect of kidney disease. While CKJ mainly focusses on original work, a few editorials will accompany especially newsworthy or controversial articles, and we will continue publishing high-quality, practice-oriented reviews. There is also no question that CKJ will remain a fully open-access, online-only journal.

LOOKING INTO THE FUTURE

I approach my role as Editor-in-Chief with both excitement and at the same time some trepidation, as I am very aware of the many responsibilities that the Editor role entails. Given the secure footing of CKJ in the nephrology landscape there will be no immediate or radical changes, however CKJ will continuously adapt to a rapidly changing world of publication and information strategies.

CKJ will continue focusing on excellent clinical work, striving to be one of the leading global journals in nephrology. Although the journal clearly is European at its base, CKJ has become a showplace for the world (between 2020 and 2023, 43% of the new submissions were from Asia and 7% from the USA). A very important task for the future will be to make sure that CKJ and NDT work closely together and that they should be viewed as complimentary journals, serving particularly the over 20 000 ERA members but of course also the wider nephrology community. Thus, while CKJ originates from a European society we will make sure that we have adequate Asian-Pacific and American representation in the Editorial Board and we will also appoint a dedicated biostatistician among CKJ’s Associate Editors. We will also continue to occasionally transfer papers from NDT to CKJ should they fit more within the scope of CKJ.

Nephrology, in particular, is a field where there is often more opinion than evidence. Thus, pro–con debates will continue as an important feature of CKJ but we will now publish the debate including a moderator's conclusion in a single article instead of three separate publications. We will also continue the recently launched ‘In Context’ series in collaboration with the ERA Young Nephrologists’ Platform, ‘The forgotten sex—gender disparities in kidney disease’ series and the new methodological series in collaboration with the ERA Registry.

Electronic and social media have become a vital component of information dissemination. Links with the ERA website and newsletter, X (formerly known as Twitter) feeds (follow us @CKJsocial), joint journal club e-seminars with NDT, as well as theme-oriented article collections in collaboration with our publisher, Oxford University Press, will become more important in the future and will be used even more extensively. To meet this new way of information dissemination, we will also invest more in audio and video abstracts in addition to our current graphical abstracts.

Lastly, I very much look forward to working with Caroline Vinck in the Editorial Office. Without her longstanding expertise and guidance, the journal would hardly be possible.

It will be a pleasure working with such a dedicated team and serving the nephrology community, and of course patients worldwide.

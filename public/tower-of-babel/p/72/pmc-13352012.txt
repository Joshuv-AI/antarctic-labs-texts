Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial of Allogeneic Mesenchymal Stromal Cell Infusion in Diabetic Kidney Disease

Published: 2026

Introduction

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is the leading cause of kidney failure globally.1 It is initiated by hyperglycemia, which activates diverse pathways that drive progressive kidney injury.2 Despite recent pharmacological breakthroughs, the risk of DKD progression persists, because of poor adherence to multidrug regimens and/or pathophysiological complexity, underscoring the need for multitargeted treatment strategies.3 Mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) may address this need through the release or induction of paracrine mediators, which modulate multiple renal injury mechanisms.4,5 Recently, we reported safety and preliminary efficacy outcomes following a single i.v. infusion of 80 ×106 bone marrow–derived, anti-CD362-selected, allogeneic MSCs (ORBCEL-M) in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus and progressive DKD participating in cohort 1 of the multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, phase 1b/2a NEPHSTROM trial.6

Herein, we report on the results of the second dose cohort (ORBCEL-M 160 × 106 cells or placebo) of the NEPHSTROM trial and exploratory analyses of the combined data from the two cohorts.

Results

Participants

Sixteen patients were screened for cohort 2, of whom 13 were randomized to receive ORBCEL-M (n = 10) or placebo (n = 3) (Supplementary Figure S1); all completed the study. Baseline characteristics are shown in Supplementary Table S1.

Primary Outcome

All participants received the full infusion of ORBCEL-M or placebo. No infusion-related adverse events (AEs) occurred. Throughout the follow-up, 1 serious AE occurred in 1 ORBCEL-M recipient. This consisted of an acute kidney injury episode, which was deemed treatment-unrelated, and resolved without sequelae. There were 46 nonserious AEs, 32 in 9 ORBCEL-M recipients and 14 in 3 placebo recipients. All were considered treatment-unrelated. One new cancer, a malignant melanoma, was diagnosed in an ORBCEL-M recipient (Supplementary Table S2).

Secondary Outcomes

Kidney Function

The mean changes in measured glomerular filtration rate (GFR) and in estimated GFR (eGFR) (Chronic Kidney Disease–Epidemiology Collaboration or Modification of Diet in Renal Disease equations) at 6-, 12- and 18-months follow-up were not different in ORBCEL-M compared with placebo recipients (Table 1). Similarly, differences in the median annual rates of measured GFR and eGFR decline did not reach statistical significance (Table 1). Albuminuria did not differ between the groups throughout follow-up (Supplementary Table S3).

Metabolic Parameters and Blood Pressure

There were no clinically meaningful changes or between-group differences in glycemic parameters, lipid profile, C-reactive protein, or blood pressure throughout follow-up (Supplementary Table S3).

Immunological and Inflammatory Parameters

At 3 months postinfusion, 1 ORBCEL-M and 1 placebo recipient developed, respectively, anti- human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I and class II antibodies, which persisted until study end without associated AEs. Serum concentrations of proinflammatory biomarkers showed no significant changes or between-group differences throughout follow-up, except for increased serum sVCAM-1 over time in ORBCEL-M recipients (Supplementary Figure S2).

Combined Cohort 1 and 2 Analyses

The combined ORBCEL-M and placebo groups had similar baseline characteristics (Supplementary Table S4). The mean changes in measured GFR at 6, 12, and 18 months and the rate of decline of measured GFR during follow-up did not differ between ORBCEL-M and placebo groups (Supplementary Table S5). However, the mean changes in eGFR (Chronic Kidney Disease–Epidemiology Collaboration or Modification of Diet in Renal Disease equations) at 12 and 18 months were smaller and the rates of eGFR decline during follow-up were slower in the ORBCEL-M group (Supplementary Table S5, Figure 1).

Longitudinal peripheral blood mononuclear cell profiling showed no changes or between-group differences in the proportions of CD4+ T cells, regulatory T cells and memory regulatory T cells (Supplementary Figure S3). The proportions of classical and intermediate monocytes were similar between the groups throughout follow-up. However, proportions of nonclassical monocytes remained stable following ORBCEL-M but increasing following placebo. The between-group differences were significant at 1 and 12 months (Supplementary Figure S3).

Serum concentrations of proinflammatory biomarkers did not differ between groups throughout follow-up, except for serum neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin, which was significantly lower with ORBCEL-M than in placebo at 18 months (Supplementary Figure S4).

Discussion

The results for cohort 2 of the NEPHSTROM trial support the safety and tolerability of a single i.v delivery of 160 × 106 ORBCEL-M cells in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and progressive DKD through 18 months of follow-up. The only serious AE was an acute kidney injury episode in 1 ORBCEL-M recipient. This event occurred 9 months after cell infusion during a transient acute illness associated with fever, diarrhea, and vomiting and was followed by recovery of eGFR to baseline (Chronic Kidney Disease–Epidemiology Collaboration eGFRs before, during, and after the acute kidney injury were 21.7, 13.9, and 28.3 ml/min per 1.73 m2, respectively). In this context, and given the limited persistence of infused MSCs, the event was considered not to be caused by ORBCEL-M. In another ORBCEL-M recipient, a malignant melanoma was diagnosed 4 months postinfusion. Concerns exist that cultured MSCs could transform and induce tumors after administration; however, such an event has never been reported with human MSC-based therapies.7,8 In addition, MSC infusions have not, to date, been associated with increased risk of developing malignancy, even in immunosuppressed patients.8,9 Nonetheless, the fact that 1 ORBCEL-M recipient in cohort 1 died due to a malignancy (myeloma) and a second died of cardiovascular disease during follow-up highlights the need for continued caution and vigilance regarding the long-term safety of MSCs and other disease-modulating cell therapies.6

Only 1 ORBCEL-M recipient in cohort 2 developed anti-HLA class I (multiple HLA-B allotypes) antibodies, detectable at stable and intermediate titers from 3 months postinfusion. Notably, 1 placebo recipient developed de novo anti-HLA class II (DQ7,8,9) antibodies at intermediate to high titers from 3 months postinfusion. Medical records review for this previously nonsensitized participant did not identify sensitizing events such as blood transfusion or vaccination, which could explain anti-HLA antibody formation. This finding emphasizes the need for stricter monitoring of potentially sensitizing events in future trials of allogeneic MSCs as well as the challenge of definitively ascribing the development of de novo anti-HLA antibodies to allogeneic MSCs.

The renal functional measures during follow-up of cohort 2 did not differ between ORBCEL-M and placebo recipients. However, the analysis of combined eGFR results for both cohorts strengthens the preliminary evidence that a single infusion of ORBCEL-M at an approximate dose range of 1 to 2 × 106 cells/kg may favorably modulate the rate of progression of established DKD. Combined immune profiling data strengthen our observation that circulating numbers of proinflammatory monocytes may be stabilized following ORBCEL-M infusion. Nonetheless, no clear conclusion can be drawn regarding efficacy because the trial was statistically underpowered for this outcome and observed differences in renal functional trends may have been influenced by baseline differences in risk factors.

Other limitations of the trial must be acknowledged. First, premature trial termination because of the COVID-19 pandemic resulted in incomplete patient recruitment into cohort 2. Second, the generalizability of the results is limited. Specifically, despite robust screening of potentially eligible patients at 3 trial sites, all randomized cohort 1 and 2 participants were males with insulin-treated type 2 diabetes mellitus. Thus, it will be essential for future trials of ORBCEL-M to enroll patients that more broadly represent the prevalent DKD population.

In conclusion, no new safety signals emerged in cohort 2 following a single i.v. infusion of 160 × 106 ORBCEL-M cells, although definitive safety conclusions are precluded by the small sample size and the occurrence of malignancies in both cohorts. These findings extend to a higher dosing regimen our previous observation of low potential for ORBCEL-M to sensitize against allo-HLA.6 Combined longitudinal data from both cohorts strengthen the preliminary evidence that ORBCEL-M infusion in the range of 80 to 160 × 106 cells may slow the rate of eGFR decline and stabilize circulating proinflammatory monocytes. Ultimately, the outcome of the NEPHSTROM trial supports the pursuit of a larger, randomized controlled trial of ORBCEL-M infusions in adults experiencing progressive DKD despite maximally tolerated standard-of-care therapy.

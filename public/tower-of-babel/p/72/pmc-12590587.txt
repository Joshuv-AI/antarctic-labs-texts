SETD7 exacerbates diabetic nephropathy through activating A2B receptor-JAK2/STAT3 pathway
Authors: Qiuyuan Huang, Wen Zhong, Ruoxue Chen, Shenhan Xu, Huibin Wang, Jintao He, Yajie Hu, Honghong Chen, Chunxiang Fan, Xinhua Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveDiabetic nephropathy (DN) is a leading cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD), and there has been growing attention towards the role of epigenetics in its pathogenesis. This study aims to investigate the underlying mechanism by which SET domain-containing lysine methyltransferase 7 (SETD7) modulates the progression of DN.MethodsSETD7 knockout mice (Setd7−/−) and wild-type controls (Setd7+/+) were intraperitoneally injected with Streptozocin (STZ, 50 mg/kg) to induce DN. The mouse mesangial cell line SV40-MES-13 were stimulated by high glucose in vitro.ResultsSETD7 expression was predominantly upregulated in high glucose-induced glomerular mesangial cells (MCs), and in diabetic mice kidney. Notably, SETD7 promoted the transcription of adenosine A2B receptor (A2BR), which mediated the activation and phosphorylation of JAK2 (p-JAK2) via direct interaction. Subsequently, p-JAK2 facilitated the recruitment of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) to binding sites. This led to nuclear translocation of STAT3 and transcriptional regulation of target genes, ultimately promoting fibrosis and inflammation. Importantly, SETD7 deficiency reduced A2BR transcription, thereby inhibiting fibrosis and inflammation in high glucose-induced MCs. Consistently with these findings, SETD7 knockout in STZ-induced mice conferred significant protection against renal injury and reduced glomerular fibrosis.ConclusionsOur results demonstrate an important role and mechanism of SETD7 in DN by promoting fibrosis and inflammation through the A2BR-mediated JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway. Targeting SETD7 may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for halting the progression of DN.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13578-025-01488-6.

Introduction

Diabetes has emerged as a significant global public health challenge. With the prevalence of diabetes mellitus (DM) continuously rising worldwide, it imposes a substantial burden on both individual health and socioeconomic systems [1]. Among its complications, diabetic nephropathy (DN) is one of the most severe and is recognized as the leading cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) [2]. Early DN is characterized by distinct histopathological changes in the glomeruli, including glomerular hyperfiltration, glomerular enlargement with cellular hypertrophy, thickening of glomerular basement membrane (GBM), expansion of mesangial and capillary loops, and the development of nodular glomerulosclerosis. These alterations may progress to extensive glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial fibrosis [3, 4].

Glomerular mesangial expansion and GBM thickening, resulting from mesangial cells (MCs) enlargement and increased matrix secretion, are closely associated with albuminuria, a hallmark clinical feature of DN [5]. Current treatment strategies, such as glycemic and blood pressure control using renin-angiotensin system (RAS) blockers and sodium glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, have shown limited efficacy in slowing kidney damage and preserving renal function [6]. Despite these interventions, the prevalence of DN has continued to increase over the past three decades, underscoring the urgent need to identify novel therapeutic targets and elucidate underlying molecular mechanisms [7].

DN involves multiple pathological processes, including metabolic disturbances, hemodynamic changes, inflammation, and fibrosis [8]. Notably, the Janus kinase-signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK-STAT) signaling pathway plays a crucial role in regulating chemokines and proinflammatory factors in both glomerular and tubulointerstitial cells in DN [8, 9]. Hyperglycemia induces the expression of several JAK-STAT family members in renal cells, particularly in mesangial cells. In patients with early-stage and progressive DN, JAK1 and JAK2 are highly expressed in glomeruli, and STAT3 expression exceeds that of STAT1/2 [10]. Suppression of the JAK2/STAT3 pathway has been demonstrated to attenuate renal fibrosis and inflammatory responses [11–13].

Emerging evidence suggests that epigenetic mechanisms, such as histone post-translational modifications (PTMs) and DNA methylation, are critically involved in the regulating profibrotic and inflammatory genes expression in DN [14]. SET domain-containing lysine methyltransferase 7 (SETD7), best known for activating gene transcription through histone H3 lysine 4 methylation, also targets non-histone substrates [15]. Although SETD7-mediated methylation has been extensively studied in cancers including prostate, bladder, and lung cancer [16], emerging research suggests its role in macrophage-driven inflammation under diabetic conditions [17]. Nevertheless, the functional contribution and precise mechanisms of SETD7 in diabetic kidney diseases remain poorly understood [17, 18].

In this study, we unveil a novel mechanism through which SETD7 contributes to DN pathogenesis. We observed significant upregulation of SETD7 in mesangial cells of diabetic mice. Genetic knockout of SETD7 markedly ameliorated renal dysfunction, glomerular fibrosis, and inflammation. Further mechanistic investigations revealed that SETD7 enhances the transcription of the adenosine A2B receptor (A2BR), which in turn interacts with JAK2 to activate the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway. These findings position SETD7 as a potential therapeutic target for halting the progression of DN.

Materials and methods

Mice and animal models

The animal study protocol was conducted in accordance with the guiding principles of Institutional Ethics Committee of Fudan University, China. SETD7 knockout mice (Setd7−/−) were generated by using CRISPR/Cas9 to knockout SETD7 (Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc.). Control mice (Setd7+/+) for all experiments in this study were littermates. To induce DN, 8-week-old Setd7+/+ and Setd7−/− mice were intraperitoneally injected with a low-dose of Streptozocin (STZ, 50 mg/kg; Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) for five consecutive days [19, 20]. All mice were housed under conditions of unrestricted access to food and water, and their blood glucose levels were regularly measured over the next 14 weeks. For mice undergoing surgical or invasive procedures (such as general examination prior to kidney tissue collection), anesthesia was induced via intraperitoneal administration of phenobarbital (1%, 10 g/100 µL). Following the procedure, mice were humanely euthanized by cervical dislocation.

Cell culture and treatment

The mouse mesangial cell line SV40-MES-13 cells were obtained from Procell (Wuhan, China), and cultured in low-glucose DMEM/F12 (3: 1, v/v) containing 8% FBS (Gibco, NY, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S) (BasalMedia, Shanghai, China) at 37 °C in 5% CO2 incubator. For high glucose (HG) treatment, cells were exposed to D-glucose (33 mM) for 24 h. Cells exposed to 5.5 mM D-glucose and mannitol were used as controls.

To silence SETD7 and A2BR, SV40-MES-13 cells were infected with specific siRNA targeting Setd7 (siSetd7) or Adora2b (siAdora2b) or negative control siRNA (siScr). To inhibit SETD7 or STAT3, SV40-MES-13 cells were pretreated with 20 µM sinefungin or 5 µM stattic for 4 h before exposure to high glucose.

Kidney function measurement

Mouse urine samples were collected of 24 h using metabolic cages at 22 weeks of age, and the blood samples was collected to separate serum samples. Urine albumin was quantified using Albumin assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). Both urine and serum creatinine levels were measured using the Creatinine (Cr) Assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The urine albumin/creatinine ratio (ACR) was calculated as the ratio of albumin to creatinine (µg/mg). Creatinine clearance (Ccr) was calculated using the formula: urinary creatinine × urine volume / serum creatinine / 1440 (µL/min) [21].

Kidney histology analysis

To assess kidney function histologically, the paraffin-embedded kidney slides were subjected to Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) and Masson trichrome (Masson) staining. The stained slides were visualized under a light microscope (Carl Zeiss AG, Jena, Germany), and profile areas were traced using Image J software. The glomerular area was estimated based on the average area of at least 20 glomeruli per group. For mesangial expansion assessment, the mesangial expansion was defined as PAS-positive area exceeded two mesangial cell nuclei area in at least 10 glomeruli, sectioned at the vascular pole in each animal. PAS-positive and Masson-positive area were quantified relative to the total glomeruli area.

Isolation of glomeruli

Mouse glomeruli were isolated using the method previously described [22]. Briefly, mice were performed with a surgical procedure to ligate the renal arteries at the level of the adrenal gland and near the aorta below the renal arteries. Additionally, the vena cava and mesenteric artery were clamped at the height of the bifurcation. The mice were then perfused with 5 mL PBS to flush the residual blood, followed by a 5 mL perfusion of Dynabeads solution in the ligature area with a venous indwelling needle. The perfused kidneys were excised, decapsulated, and finely minced before being digested with collagenase A (Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) for 30 min at 37 °C on a rotator. The digested tissue was strained through a 100-mesh sterile sieve and the filtrate was collected by centrifugation. The pellet was resuspended in PBS, the glomeruli were washed three times, and collected using a magnet. The isolated glomeruli were further collected by centrifugation and homogenized in ice-cold RIPA buffer. The proteins extracted from glomeruli were subjected to western blot analysis.

GST-pull down and Co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP) assay

Fusion proteins GST-A2BR were synthesized by cloning the coding sequence of mice Adora2b gene into pGEX-4T-1 vector. The recombined plasmid was transformed into E. Coli DH5a and protein expression was induced by IPTG (Sangon, shanghai, China). The fusion proteins were purified using glutathione-sepharose beads. The total cell proteins lysates were prepared using solutions containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors.

For GST pull-down assay, GST-fusion proteins were initially bind to glutathione-sepharose beads and then incubated with the total protein lysates at 4 °C overnight on a rotator. For Co-immunoprecipitation assay, 1 mg of total protein was incubated with 1 µg of primary antibody or IgG overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, immune complexes were captured using protein A/G agarose beads (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, California, USA). After thorough washing with binding buffer, the beads were boiled in SDS loading buffer and the eluted proteins were analyzed by western blot.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP) -PCR

The experimental methodology was adapted from a previously established protocol [22]. Cells were collected in 10 mL of PBS and fixed with 1% formaldehyde to rotate for 15 min at room temperature. Subsequently, 0.125 M Glycine was added, and the mixture was rotated for an additional 5 min before being centrifuged to collect the sediment. The sediment was then sonicated to break it into pieces. For each immunoprecipitation experiment, 200 µg of the protein-chromatin complex was utilized. Antibody-protein complexes were captured using dynabeads protein G that had been pre-blocked (Invitrogen, MO, USA). The DNA fragments were reverse cross-linked at 65 °C and purified using a DNA Clean and Concentrator kit (ZYMO Research, California, USA). Samples were subjected to conventional PCR using mouse Adora2b 0-500 bp promoter-specific primers: Forward: 5’-GACCCAGCTTGGGACGC-3’; Reverse: 5’-TCTACTGGGGTAGTGTCGGG-3’.

Nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction

Nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins were extracted using NE-PER Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Extraction Reagents kit (ThermoFisher, MA, USA) according to manufacturer’s introduction. Initially, cells were collected and washed with PBS. Then, 200 µL of ice-cold Cytoplasmic Extraction Reagent I (CER I) was added to every 20 µL of cell pellet, followed by vortexing and a 10 min incubation in an ice bath. Cytoplasmic Extraction Reagent II (CER II) was added, vortexed, and placed in an ice bath for 1 min. The mixture was then centrifuged, and the clear supernatant, which contains the cytoplasmic protein, was transferred into a pre-cooled tube. Then the remaining pellet was dispersed with Nuclear Extraction Reagent (NER), centrifuged to clarify, and the resulting supernatant containing the nuclear protein was collected. The extracted proteins were then subjected to western blot.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analyses and graphs were conducted using GraphPad Prism software. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant, with significance levels denoted as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001. Student’s t-test was employed for comparisons between two groups, and one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for comparisons involving three or more groups.

Results

SETD7 expression is up-regulated in diabetic kidney

We initiated this study by evaluating the potential involvement of SETD7 in the pathogenesis of DN. Using datasets GSE200322 and GSE228727, we analyzed SETD7 expression in both STZ-induced type 1 diabetic (T1D) mice and db/db type 2 diabetic (T2D) mice. The results revealed a significant upregulation of SETD7 in both models (Fig. S1A). We then established DN models using STZ-induced mice and db/db mice, and observed significantly elevated SETD7 expression at both mRNA and protein levels in diabetic kidneys compared with the controls (Fig. 1A, B). Immunohistochemistry (IHC) further confirmed increased renal SETD7 in diabetic mice (Fig. 1C), which was supported by RNA-seq data from the datasets (Fig. S1B, C). After confirming enhanced fibrosis and inflammation in DN models (Fig. S1D, E), we screened several epigenetic regulators, including G9a, Ash2l, Cxxc1, Kmt2b, Smyd2, and Smyd3, and found that Setd7 mRNA exhibited the most pronounced upregulation in both STZ-induced and db/db mice (Fig. S1F, G).

To assess the impact of high glucose conditions on SETD7 expression in MCs, we treated mouse MCs with high glucose (HG) medium, using mannitol as an osmotic control. Consistent with findings in diabetic mice, MCs exposed to HG exhibited increased SETD7 expression at both mRNA and protein levels, accompanied by elevated inflammatory markers (iNOS and IL-6) (Fig. 1D, E). Immunofluorescence staining also revealed that enhanced SETD7 signal intensity under HG conditions (Fig. 1F). Collectively, these results suggest that SETD7 upregulation in diabetic kidneys and in high glucose treated MCs may play a role in the development of DN.

SETD7 deficiency alleviates renal injury in DN mice

To determine whether upregulation of SETD7 influences the progression of DN, we generated Setd7−/−mice. STZ-induced diabetic mice develop spontaneous kidney injury over time. We therefore administered STZ to both Setd7+/+ and Setd7−/− mice to evaluate the role of SETD7 in DN, with particular focus on glomerular damage. First, we confirmed that SETD7 knockout significantly reduced its expression in the glomeruli of DN mice, as demonstrated by western blot and IHC analysis (Figs. 2A, S2A). Furthermore, a similar reduction in SETD7 was observed in areas co-localized with Nephrin, a podocyte marker (Fig. 2B). Diabetic mice displayed increased Ccr, urine albumin extraction, and urine ACR, which are all established indicator of kidney damage assessed at 12 weeks after diabetic induction. Notably, compared with the STZ-induced Setd7+/+ mice, SETD7 deficiency significantly improved Ccr, urine albumin extraction, and urine ACR (Fig. 2C, D). Additionally, uric acid (UA) and serum urea nitrogen (BUN) levels were slightly decreased in STZ-induced Setd7−/− mice (Fig. S2B). These results support the conclusion that SETD7 deficiency attenuates glomerulus damage and improves renal function in DN.

To histologically evaluate the impact of SETD7 on DN, we assessed glomerular pathological features using H&E, PAS, and Masson’s staining. H&E staining revealed a significant reduction in glomerular hypertrophy following SETD7 loss in STZ-induced mice (Fig. 2E). Excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins within the glomerulus is a critical pathological event in DN progression [23]. Accordingly, we conducted a further assessment of the extent of glomerular fibrosis. Consistent with the improvement in renal function, ablation of SETD7 led to reduced collagen accumulation as indicated by decreased PAS and Masson-positive areas (Fig. 2E, F). Moreover, diabetic Setd7−/− mice exhibited less severe mesangial matrix expansion and glomerular hypertrophy, as measured by surface area (Figs. 2F, S2C). Meanwhile, the expression of several upregulated fibrosis and proinflammatory mediators (e.g. FN, Collagen 3, MMP9, CTGF, iNOS, COX-2, and IL-6) were significantly attenuated in kidney tissue of STZ-induced Setd7−/− mice (Fig. S2D). Consistent with these findings in whole kidney tissue, the protein expression levels of these fibrotic and proinflammatory mediators were also markedly decreased in isolated glomeruli after SETD7 knockdown (Fig. 2G). Collectively, these results suggest that SETD7 deficiency mitigates mesangial expansion and glomerular fibrosis, thereby attenuating the progression of DN.

SETD7 deficiency alleviates fibrosis and inflammation in mesangial cells

To further validate the role of SETD7 in high glucose-induced glomerular injury, we silenced SETD7 in MCs using siRNA. Compared with siScr, siSetd7 effectively reduced SETD7 expression and suppressed upregulation of inflammatory and fibrotic mediators (Collagen 3, Collagen 1, iNOS, IL-6, and IL-1β) in MCs under HG conditions (Fig. 3A). These results were further corroborated by immunofluorescence staining, which demonstrated reduced expression of SETD7, IL-6, and α-SMA in HG-treated MCs following SETD7 knockdown (Fig. 3B).

We further examined the effect of SETD7 inhibition on HG-induced fibrosis and inflammation in MCs using the SETD7 inhibitor sinefungin. Pretreatment with sinefungin prior to HG exposure significantly reduced the expression of key inflammatory and fibrotic mediators (FN, Collagen 3, iNOS, and COX-2), without affecting SETD7 expression itself (Fig. S3A). Sinefungin also decreased HG-induced mRNA levels of Il6 and Mmp2 (Fig. S3B). Collectively, these results demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of SETD7 reduces inflammatory and profibrotic responses in HG-induced MCs.

To determine whether SETD7 overexpression is sufficient to promote proinflammatory and profibrotic responses, we employed lentiviral-mediated SETD7 overexpression in MCs. Elevated SETD7 expression significantly increased protein levels of inflammation markers (iNOS, COX-2, IL-6, and IL-1β) and fibrosis-related factors (FN, Collagen 3, Collagen 1, MMP9, and α-SMA) (Fig. 3C). Consistent with this, immunofluorescence staining revealed enhanced fluorescence intensity of IL-6 and α-SMA even under basal conditions (Fig. 3D). Moreover, mRNA analysis confirmed upregulation of inflammatory genes (Nos2, Cox2, and Il6) and fibrosis-associated genes (Col3a1, Col1a1, and Mmp9) in SETD7-overexpressing MCs (Fig. 3E). These findings indicate that increased SETD7 expression alone is sufficient to elicit diabetes-like inflammatory and fibrotic responses in MCs.

SETD7 mediates A2BR transcription to promote fibrosis and inflammation in high glucose induced mesangial cells

Endogenous overexpression of A2BR has been implicated in renal fibrosis and inflammation [24–26]. Consistent with these reports, our observations indicate that MCs exposed to HG exhibited increased A2BR expression (Fig. S4A). Similarly, kidney tissues from both STZ-induced and db/db diabetic mice showed elevated protein and mRNA levels of A2BR (Fig. S4B, C). To investigate the functional role of A2BR in MCs under HG conditions, we performed in vitro loss-of-function experiments. Silencing A2BR led to a marked reduction in proinflammatory mediators (iNOS, COX-2, IL-6, and IL-1β) and fibrosis-related factors (FN, Collagen 3, Collagen 1, CTGF, and MMP9) (Fig. S5A). Immunofluorescence staining further confirmed decreased signals of A2BR, α-SMA, and IL-6 in A2BR-knockdown MCs under HG stimulation (Fig. S5B). Conversely, overexpression of A2BR in MCs increased the expression of fibrosis and inflammation markers (Fig. S5C), indicating that A2BR deficiency attenuates glomerular fibrosis and inflammation. Notably, we found that silencing SETD7 reduced A2BR protein expression (Fig. 4A, B), and pharmacological inhibition of SETD7 yielded similar suppressive effects (Fig. 4C). In contrast, SETD7 overexpression increased A2BR levels (Fig. 4D). These findings clearly indicate that SETD7 is a critical regulator of A2BR expression.

Immunofluorescence images revealed that HG stimulation significantly upregulated the expression of SETD7 in both cytoplasm and nucleus (Fig. 1F). Further subcellular fractionation studies revealed that SETD7 knockdown primarily reduced its nuclear localization (Fig. S6A), suggesting that SETD7 may promote A2BR gene transcription. To test this, we performed ChIP assays targeting the promoter region of Adora2b (which encodes A2BR). Under HG conditions, SETD7 was enriched in the proximal promoter region (0–500 bp upstream of the transcription start site) of Adora2b (Fig. S6B). Silencing SETD7 significantly reduced its occupancy at this promoter region (Figs. 4E, S6C). Accordingly, qPCR analysis showed that SETD7 knockdown decreased A2BR mRNA levels (Fig. 4F), indicating that SETD7 directly enhances Adora2b transcriptional activity.

Given SETD7 overexpression upregulates A2BR and A2BR deficiency attenuates fibro-inflammatory responses, we hypothesized that SETD7 promotes inflammation and fibrosis largely through A2BR. To test this, we silenced A2BR in MCs overexpressing SETD7. A2BR knockdown markedly attenuated the upregulation of fibrotic and inflammatory markers induced by SETD7 overexpression (Fig. 4G). Immunofluorescence results further showed reduced IL-6 expression in A2BR-knockout MCs even under SETD7 overexpression (Fig. 4H). These data indicate that SETD7 contributes to the pathogenesis of diabetic nephropathy partially by transcriptionally regulating A2BR expression, thereby promoting glomerular inflammation and fibrosis.

A2BR interacts with JAK2 to promote activation of JAK2/STAT3 pathway in high glucose induced mesangial cells

Previous studies have strongly suggested that the JAK2/STAT3 pathway is activated in DN by various stimuli, including hyperglycemia, reactive oxygen species, and advanced glycation end products [12]. Although A2BR has been shown to activate downstream pathways such as PI3K/AKT and p38/MAPK [27–29], its role in regulating the JAK2/STAT3 pathway under HG conditions in MCs remained unclear. Here, we investigated whether A2BR promotes fibrosis and inflammation through JAK2/STAT3 signaling in HG-induced MCs. Consistent with previous reports, HG stimulation significantly activated the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway, as indicated by increased phosphorylation levels of JAK2 and STAT3 (p-JAK2, p-STAT3). Notably, silencing A2BR markedly reduced these phosphorylation events (Fig. 5A). Conversely, A2BR overexpression enhanced JAK2/STAT3 activation (Fig. S7A). Since JAK2/STAT3 signaling depends on STAT3 phosphorylation and subsequent nuclear translocation to regulate target gene expression, we examined subcellular localization. Nuclear protein analysis revealed that A2BR knockdown significantly suppressed both STAT3 phosphorylation and its nuclear accumulation (Fig. 5B). Immunofluorescence staining further confirmed that A2BR silencing attenuated the HG-induced nuclear translocation of STAT3 and p-STAT3 (Figs. 5C, S7B). Moreover, knockdown of SETD7, the upstream regulator of A2BR, similarly inhibited JAK2/STAT3 signaling and reduced p-STAT3 nuclear translocation (Fig. S7C, D), while SETD7 overexpression activated the pathway (Fig. S7E). These results highlight the involvement of A2BR in regulating the activation of the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway in HG-induced MCs.

We next explored the mechanisms by which A2BR modulates JAK2/STAT3 signaling. Unlike other adenosine receptors, A2BR has low affinity for adenosine and may operate through non-canonical mechanisms [30]. For instance, A2BR-mediated p38/MAPK activation has been reported to occur independently of classical ligand binding [24]. We therefore hypothesized that A2BR might regulate JAK2/STAT3 signaling via direct protein interaction. Given the membrane localization of A2BR, we performed GST pull-down assays and found that HG promotes a specific interaction between A2BR and JAK2, but not between A2BR and STAT3. This interaction was abolished upon A2BR silencing (Fig. 5D). Co-IP assays further validated enhanced A2BR-JAK2 binding under HG conditions and its reduction following A2BR knockdown (Figs. S7F, 5E). Overexpression of A2BR strengthened this interaction (Fig. 5F), and similarly, SETD7 overexpression, which transcriptionally upregulates A2BR, also enhanced A2BR-JAK2 binding without affecting A2BR-STAT3 interaction (Fig. S7G).

To identify the domain within A2BR responsible for JAK2 binding, we constructed three His-tagged A2BR truncation mutants (Mut1, Mut2, Mut3) and a full-length plasmid (A2BR-WT), based on its four intracellular domains: ICL1, ICL2, ICL3, and the C-terminus [31] (Fig. 5G). His pull-down assays in transfected MCs revealed that A2BR-WT, Mut1, and Mut2 were able to bind JAK2, whereas Mut3 lacking the ICL3 domain failed to interact (Fig. 5H). These results demonstrate that the ICL3 domain of A2BR is essential for its interaction with JAK2 and likely facilitates JAK2/STAT3 pathway activation.

Loss of SETD7 suppresses A2BR and JAK2/STAT3 pathway in DN

Next, we further verified that SETD7 contributes to DN progression in vivo, at least partially, through A2BR-mediated JAK2/STAT3 activation. SETD7 deficiency downregulated A2BR expression and decreased JAK2/STAT3 activation in whole kidney tissues of DN mice (Fig. S8A). Similarly, loss of SETD7 significantly suppressed the elevated expression of A2BR and inhibited JAK2/STAT3 activation in isolated glomeruli from DN mice (Fig. 6A). Consistent with these findings, IHC analysis revealed that SETD7 deficiency attenuated A2BR-positive staining in the glomeruli of STZ-induced diabetic mice (Fig. 6B). Furthermore, SETD7 deficiency decreased the nuclear accumulation of STAT3, as indicated by decreased positive staining for nuclear STAT3 (Fig. 6C). These results demonstrate that SETD7 ablation attenuates A2BR expression and suppresses JAK2/STAT3 pathway activation in the diabetic glomerulus.

Previous studies have shown that adenosine binding to A2BR activates STAT3, leading to upregulation of IL-6 [32]. In line with this, we observed elevated IL-6 levels in both serum and kidney tissues of DN mice, which were significantly reduced upon SETD7 knockout (Fig. S8B). Given that STAT3 inhibitors have been reported to ameliorate renal fibrosis and diabetic nephropathy in diabetic animal models [19, 33], we treated MCs with the potent STAT3 inhibitor stattic. This treatment effectively suppressed HG-induced expression of fibrotic and inflammatory markers (Fig. 6D), supporting the functional involvement of STAT3 in diabetic renal injury. Collectively, these findings underscore the crucial role of SETD7 in regulating the A2BR-JAK2/STAT3 signaling axis, thereby promoting glomerular inflammation and fibrosis in diabetic nephropathy.

Discussion

This study establishes the crucial role of SETD7 in the progression of DN. We first observed a significant upregulation of SETD7 in the glomeruli of DN mice and cultured MCs exposed to high glucose. Genetic deletion of SETD7 in STZ-induced diabetic mice alleviated key pathological features of DN, while knockdown of SETD7 in MCs attenuated high glucose-induced fibrotic and inflammatory responses. Mechanistically, our findings indicate that SETD7 facilitates the transcription of A2BR and enhances the direct interaction between A2BR and JAK2, leading to activation of the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway. This cascade ultimately promotes pronounced fibrotic and inflammatory responses, thereby accelerating the progression of DN.

Emerging evidence suggests the importance of epigenetic mechanisms in the progression of diabetic kidney disease [34]. Prolonged hyperglycemia can induce chromatin remodeling, facilitating the recruitment of SETD7 and enhancing histone H3 lysine 4 methylation (H3K4me) at the proximal NF-κB promoter, which in turn promotes inflammatory responses [35]. Additionally, SETD7 has been implicated in elevating collagen and fibronectin expression, contributing to excessive ECM accumulation and fibrosis during DN pathogenesis [19]. Consistent with these reports, we observed elevated SETD7 levels in the kidneys of both STZ-induced and db/db diabetic mice, accompanied by typical DN pathological features. Among a panel of epigenetic regulators, including Setd7, G9a, Ash2l, Cxxc1, Kmt2b, Smyd2, and Smyd3, Setd7 expression was uniquely and significantly upregulated in both diabetic models. This distinct pattern of upregulation suggests that SETD7 may play a primary role in driving DN progression rather than merely reflecting a secondary effect of metabolic disturbance.

Notably, the function of SETD7 in DN diverges from that of other histone methyltransferases. For example, G9a (EHMT2), which mediates repressive H3K9 methylation, is upregulated in DN and contributes to silencing anti-fibrotic genes [36]. In contrast, SETD7 catalyzes the activating mark H3K4me1 and directly promotes A2BR transcription. Similarly, while Kmt2b, a regulator of H3K4me2, exacerbates renal injury by modulating TGF-β1 and ICAM-1 expression [37], SETD7 operates through the specific A2BR-JAK2/STAT3 signaling axis. These functional distinctions emphasize the unique position of SETD7 as a transcriptional activator within adenosine receptor signaling, highlighting its specific contribution to the epigenetic regulation of diabetic nephropathy.

In early stages of DN, glomerular hypertrophy is a hallmark feature that progresses to glomerulosclerosis [4, 38]. This study demonstrates that genetic knockout of SETD7 in mice significantly improves diabetic renal dysfunction, as evidenced by reduced creatinine levels and proteinuria, while also attenuating glomerular mesangial expansion and fibrosis. MCs, which provide structural support to glomerular capillaries and help regulate filtration through their contractile properties [39], play a central role in DN pathogenesis. Under hyperglycemic conditions, MCs undergo hypertrophy and enhance ECM production, leading to mesangial matrix expansion, albuminuria, and eventual kidney damage [38]. Furthermore, MCs secrete numerous cytokines and growth factors that promote inflammation and fibrosis [40]. Consistent with these findings, high glucose exposure induced fibrotic and inflammatory responses in cultured MCs, whereas inhibition of SETD7 using either pharmacological inhibitors or siRNA, effectively suppressed these pathogenic changes under diabetic conditions. Conversely, overexpression of SETD7 exacerbated fibrotic and inflammatory responses in MCs. These findings emphasize the critical role of SETD7 in mediating MCs dysfunction and the progression of DN.

Accumulating evidence underscores the importance of adenosine signaling in the pathophysiology of DM [41]. Unlike the other adenosine receptor subtypes (A1R, A2AR, A3R), A2BR is activated under high adenosine concentrations in specific pathophysiological contexts and is upregulated in renal glomeruli as kidney injury advances, a phenomenon consistently observed in both experimental models and human DN [30, 42]. In line with these reports, we detected elevated A2BR expression in both HG-induced MCs and kidneys of diabetic mice. Using genetic loss- and gain-of-function approaches, we further demonstrated that A2BR directly promotes MCs dysfunction: silencing A2BR alleviated HG-induced fibrosis and inflammation, while its overexpression exacerbated these effects. These results are consistent with prior studies showing that A2BR antagonism attenuates glomerular fibrosis in vivo [24]. Notably, we found that inhibition of SETD7 significantly downregulated A2BR expression and suppressed HG-induced pathological changes, mimicking the effects of A2BR knockdown. Importantly, blocking A2BR counteracted the pro-fibrotic and pro-inflammatory effects triggered by SETD7 overexpression. Together, these results not only establish A2BR as a downstream effector of SETD7 but also uncover a novel SETD7-A2BR regulatory axis in DN, bridging epigenetic regulation with adenosine receptor signaling, a connection seldom explored in renal diseases. Thus, our findings strongly suggest that SETD7 contributes to MCs injury, at least partially, through the upregulation of A2BR.

The sensitivity of SETD7 to hyperglycemia, which regulates chromatin expression status and activates the related gene expression [43, 44], was also demonstrated in our study. We demonstrated that high glucose facilitates the nuclear translocation of SETD7, leading us to hypothesize that renal SETD7 may regulate A2BR transcription. This hypothesis was supported by ChIP assays, which revealed enhanced binding of SETD7 to the Adora2b promoter under hyperglycemic conditions, directly stimulating its expression. Furthermore, A2BR blockade mitigated MCs injury induced by SETD7 overexpression, indicating that SETD7 promotes glomerular pathology in DN partly through transactivation of A2BR. These findings highlight the need for further investigation into the downstream signaling mechanisms mediated by A2BR in this context.

Inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-6, are known to contribute the progression of DN, including glomerular sclerosis and extracellular matrix deposition, as demonstrated in animal models [42, 45, 46]. Among the signaling pathways implicated, JAK2/STAT3 is considered central to IL-6 mediated signal transduction [46]. Notably, adenosine binding to A2BR has been shown to activates STAT3, leading to increased IL-6 levels [30], suggesting a potential link between A2BR and renal dysfunction through JAK2/STAT3 signaling. To further elucidate this relationship, we conducted a series of experiments. Our results demonstrate that the ICL3 domain of A2BR specifically interacts with JAK2, activating the JAK2/STAT3 pathway and facilitating the nuclear translocation of phosphorylated STAT3. This activation promotes the transcription of downstream genes involved in fibrosis and inflammation. Furthermore, we confirmed that SETD7 modulates IL-6 levels and regulates JAK2/STAT3 signaling in DN mice. By identifying the A2BR-ICL3-JAK2 interaction as a critical activation mechanism, our study enhances the understanding of A2BR signaling in MCs and highlights a potential target for interrupting pathological JAK2/STAT3 activation in DN.

Recent studies have emphasized the unique role of A2BR among adenosine receptors, showing that its antagonism confers protection against glomerular barrier dysfunction and proteinuria in diabetic kidney disease [26, 43, 47]. Our study identifies A2BR as a new transcriptional target of SETD7 in DN. However, we recognize that the protective effects of SETD7 deficiency may not be solely attributable to MCs. SETD7 is likely expressed in other renal cell types, such as podocytes, tubular cells, immune cells, and endothelial cells, all of which contribute to DN pathogenesis. Thus, the expression and functional relevance of SETD7 in non-MC populations warrant further investigation. Future studies using cell-type-specific SETD7 knockout models (e.g., in MCs, podocytes, or other relevant lineages) will be essential to dissect the cell-autonomous functions of SETD7 and determine whether the observed phenotypes originate primarily from MCs or involve intercellular crosstalk.

In conclusion, hyperglycemia-induced upregulation of SETD7 contributes to renal injury in DN. Our results demonstrate that SETD7 transcriptionally regulates A2BR expression, which in a complex with JAK2 activates the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway, driving fibrosis and inflammation. These findings establish the SETD7-A2BR-JAK2/STAT3 axis as a novel and mechanistically coherent pathway in DN, integrating epigenetic control, adenosine signaling, and inflammatory-fibrotic responses. From a clinical perspective, this pathway offers opportunities for dual therapeutic targeting, for instance, using SETD7 inhibitors in combination with A2BR antagonists, which may synergistically ameliorate MCs dysfunction, particularly in advanced DN where treatment options remain limited. These insights may facilitate the development of innovative therapies aimed at mitigating diabetes-associated kidney damage.

The Role of the TIMP1/LINC01615 Axis as a Regulator of the EMT Pathway in Gastric Cancer
Authors: Jiying Liu, Yeganeh Olyaee, Khatere Mokhtari, Chaoxiang Lv, Niloufar Salimian, Maliheh Entezari, Afshin Taheriazam, Mehrdad Hashemi, Mazaher Maghsoudloo, Ling Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveGastric cancer (GCa) is a common malignancy where the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathway and lncRNAs are key to proliferation and metastasis. This study aimed to identify lncRNAs regulating the EMT pathway as therapeutic and diagnostic targets in GCa.MethodsWe utilized the TCGA-STAD dataset and differential expression gene (DEG) analysis was performed to identify overlapping genes between DEGs and the EMT pathway. The LASSO feature reduction algorithm and ROC curve analysis were applied to prioritize candidate biomarkers. Additionally, immunological and immunotherapy analyses were conducted to evaluate the significance of TIMP1 in GCa. Pearson correlation analysis was performed between TIMP1 and all differentially expressed lncRNAs, and the most strongly correlated candidates were selected. Experimental validation of the results was carried out using RT-qPCR with 25 cancerous and 25 adjacent normal tissue samples in GCa.ResultsDEG analysis identified 2,926 DEGs, among which 87 were associated with the EMT pathway. The LASSO algorithm reduced this list to 11 genes, and ROC curve analysis identified TIMP1 as a candidate biomarker in GCa. Higher TIMP1 expression was associated with improved response rates. Correlation analysis revealed that LINC01615 was most strongly co-regulated with TIMP1 in GCa. RT-qPCR confirmed that both TIMP1 and LINC01615 were significantly upregulated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues, highlighting their potential as diagnostic biomarkers.ConclusionA strong correlation between TIMP1 and LINC01615 suggests their role in promoting GCa proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. TIMP1/LINC01615 axis regulates the EMT pathway and is a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in GCa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12575-025-00297-y.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GCa) is the fourth most prevalent cancer worldwide, with 989,600 new cases reported in 2008. It is also the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths, resulting in 738,000 fatalities annually [1, 2]. The disease typically presents symptoms only in its advanced stages. GCa incidence varies significantly across different regions. Over 50% of new cases are found in developing countries, with a 15–20-fold difference in risk between the highest and lowest risk populations [3]. Gastric cancer arises due to a combination of environmental influences [4, 5].

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a crucial and evolutionarily conserved process essential for embryogenesis and various pathophysiological conditions, particularly tumor development and advancement. In GCa, aberrant activation of EMT can transform epithelial cells into a mesenchymal phenotype, reducing epithelial characteristics while enhancing mesenchymal traits. This transition fosters cancer cell stemness, initiates tumor formation, facilitates invasion and metastasis, and promotes resistance to chemotherapy. Key cellular adhesion molecules, notably E-cadherin, are often downregulated during this process, facilitating the dissemination and systemic spread of tumor cells throughout the body [6].

Accumulating evidence suggests that noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs), including microRNAs (miRNAs) and long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), play a role in regulating the carcinogenesis process of GCa [7–9]. lncRNAs are RNA transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that do not translate into proteins and are less conserved than mRNAs in defining cell development [10]. These highly versatile transcripts have sparked new research avenues across nearly all life sciences fields [11–13]. Recent studies have identified lncRNAs as significant players in various cancer processes [14, 15]. Increasing evidence indicates that lncRNAs could serve as potential biomarkers for diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis in human cancers [16, 17].

Evidence indicates that abnormal expression of lncRNAs is closely associated with GCa, and specific lncRNAs have been implicated in both diagnosis and prognosis [18]. The molecular mechanisms through which lncRNAs contribute to GCa include genetic mutations, miRNA sequestration, interactions with transcription factors, and modifications of the epigenome, among others [19]. Recent research has explored novel avenues in the study of lncRNAs in GCa, such as investigating circulating lncRNAs in body fluids of GCa patients. Additionally, studies have highlighted the role of lncRNAs in drug resistance and identified several lncRNAs with prognostic value for predicting survival in GCa patients. The significance of lncRNAs in cancer is increasingly recognized, underscoring the need for further elucidation of the mechanisms underlying their involvement in GCa [19]. Much research has demonstrated that lncRNAs play crucial roles in regulating various aspects of cancer biology, including cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and induction of EMT [20, 21]. These lncRNAs are involved in regulating apoptosis, promoting immune evasion, and influencing the initiation and progression of cancer.

The advancement of cancer immunotherapy was catalyzed by the identification of immune checkpoints, which act as negative regulators of immune responses, along with the emergence of gene-editing technologies. These discoveries ultimately paved the way for the development of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and genetically engineered immune cells [22]. Combining ICIs with other medications has proven to be a highly effective strategy for enhancing their therapeutic efficacy [22].

This study aimed to investigate alterations in the expression levels of EMT-related genes and a candidate lncRNA in gastric cancer using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Furthermore, the study analyzed the correlation of these gene expressions with clinical parameters and evaluated their potential as diagnostic biomarkers for GCa. In addition to these objectives, we plan to extend our research to include discussions on the immunological landscape and the role of immunotherapy in GCa, aiming to identify novel immune-related targets and pathways that could complement EMT-based therapeutic strategies.

Materials and Methods

Dataset and Preprocessing

We utilized data from the TCGA, accessible at https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/, to analyze the TCGA-STAD cohort. The TCGAbiolinks R package was used for data extraction and access to 36 normal and 412 tumor samples from GCa cases. We calculated the mean raw read counts for each gene and manually filtered genes with an average count of less than one as these are likely to be of low biological relevance or represent technical noise, to ensure data quality and to improve downstream analyses. We then normalized for compositional biases and variations in sequencing depth between samples using the trimmed mean of M-values (TMM) normalization method available in the edgeR R package (version 4.4.2). This normalization step guaranteed the gene expression data from all samples were comparable. To improve interpretability and reduce data skewness, the normalized values were converted to a logarithmic scale (log2) [23]. It provided a stable, scale invariant basis for additional statistical analysis. Similar methods were employed for lncRNAs in STAD. Additionally, we extracted the gene list for the EMT pathway using the msigdbr R package [24].

Differential Expression Gene and Gene Set Enrichment Analyses

A differential expression gene (DEG) analysis was performed to find genes with notable difference in expression between normal and tumor samples in the STAD dataset. Analysis was done with the Limma R package, which is a common tool for statistical testing and linear modelling in gene expression research. With this package we had reliable statistical estimates and we could account for dataset variability. We used strict filtering criteria to find meaningful DEGs: We considered genes with an adjusted p-value (Adj.P.Value < 0.05) and an absolute log fold change (|logFC| >1) as statistically significant. These analyses were also applied precisely to lncRNAs. Moreover, we did pathway enrichment analysis and Gene Ontology (GO) term enrichment analysis on DEGs from STAD dataset to study their biological significance [25]. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using TCGAplot R package for these analyses [26].

Feature Reduction Analysis

By identifying the overlap between DEGs and the EMT pathway we pinpointed potential genes of interest. We also employed the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) feature reduction algorithm in R software to further select the relevant genes [27]. We further evaluated the predictive power of these genes by running receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis on all selected features, and identified the most significant genes related to STAD. We present this multi step approach which combines statistical rigor with pathway specific insights to identify the most important biomarkers for further investigation.

Diagnosis & Prognosis Analyses

For diagnostic and prognostic evaluations in STAD, TIMP1 gene expression data was taken from the normalized matrix. A ROC analysis was performed in GraphPad Prism (Ver. 8.0) to determine the diagnostic efficacy of TIMP1 expression levels in distinguishing tumor from normal tissues in the STAD cohort. The ROC curve and AUC was used to evaluate TIMP1s sensitivity and specificity as a possible diagnostic biomarker. To evaluate prognosis, we performed survival analyses using the TCGAplot R package, and identified association between TIMP1 expression and overall survival in patients with GCa. To confirm these results, we generated Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves using the survival (Ver 3.4-0) and survminer (Ver 0.4.9) R packages. This allowed us to separate patients into groups with high and low expression of TIMP1 based on median expression levels of TIMP1. To see if there was statistical significance between the survival curves, the log rank test was used. Taken together, these analyses indicate that TIMP1 may be a prognostic and diagnostic biomarker for GCa.

Tumor Microenvironment and TIMP1 Expression in Bulk RNA Samples

We utilized the TIMER2.0 database (http://timer.cistrome.org/) [28] to investigate the association between TIMP1 expression and immune infiltration levels in bulk RNA-seq cancer samples. TIMER2.0 integrates TCGA datasets across multiple cancers and multiple deconvolution algorithms, including CIBERSORT, TIMER and EPIC, to provide a comprehensive assessment of immune cell infiltration. This platform enables tumor microenvironment (TME) analysis of immune components in a variety of cancers to uncover the relationship between gene expression and immune responses.

TME and TIMP1 Expression in Single Cell Samples

We used the Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub 2 (TISCH2) (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/) [29], a single cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) database for studying the tumor microenvironment at higher resolution. An extensive collection of single cell datasets with detailed cell type annotations and metadata for a variety of cancer types is included in TISCH2. This resource supports exploration of TME heterogeneity through the discovery of specific cell populations, their functional states, and interactions with the tumor ecosystem. By combining bulk RNA-seq data from TIMER2.0 with single cell insights from TISCH2, we are able to provide a more nuanced view of how TIMP1 modulates immune infiltration for different cancers.

Immune Checkpoint Blockade Therapy

To test the correlation between TIMP1 expression and ICIs, we calculated correlation in GCa using the TISIDB database (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/) [30]. TISIDB is a comprehensive web based platform to integrate high throughput data from multiple sources such as TCGA, GEO and high quality published studies. It provides a user friendly interface for exploring the interactions between tumors and the immune system and provides a detailed investigation of immune related features, such as immune cell infiltration, chemokines and cytokine profiles. Using this resource, we explored the relationship between TIMP1 expression and ICIs, which are critical to cancer immunotherapy. We then tested immune evasion mechanisms and predicted tumor response to immune checkpoint blockade therapy using the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) algorithm (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/), a sophisticated computational framework. The TIDE algorithm integrates gene expression profiles in order to generate a predictive score that summarizes the interaction between immune dysfunction and exclusion pathways in the TME. The resulting TIDE score is a good predictor of immunotherapy effectiveness and an estimate of how likely cancer samples will escape immune surveillance. Increasing TIDE score corresponds to decreasing chance of a favorable response to ICI treatment and increasing chance of a positive outcome [23]. The goal of this analysis was to identify potential immunotherapeutic targets and understand the mechanistic underpinnings of tumor immune interactions in GCa to inform how TIMP1 may influence immune evasion and response to therapy in this malignancy.

Correlation Analysis and ceRNA Network Reconstruction

We performed pearson correlation analysis between TIMP1 and all the differentially expressed lncRNAs from the STAD dataset to identify lncRNAs associated with the EMT pathway in GCa. We then selected lncRNAs with a correlation coefficient > 0.5 and P < 0.05. One lncRNA that was co regulated with TIMP1 and had highest correlation was subsequently chosen for further analyses. Moreover, we downloaded all human miRNAs from the miRBase online database to reconstruct the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network. We then utilized RNAhybrid (v2.1.2) [31] to calculate the minimum free energy (MFE) between all human miRNAs and the candidate lncRNA.

Statistical Analysis

Graphs depicting gene expression differences between groups were generated using Prism (v8.0) - GraphPad software. Linear models were employed to calculate the differential expression of candidate genes in TCGA samples between groups, and significance levels were determined using False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction. For real-time data, gene expression levels in each sample were computed using the 2−ΔCt method, and statistical significance was assessed using t-tests. Survival analysis to evaluate significance levels was conducted using the log-rank test. Additionally, the potential of candidate lncRNAs as diagnostic biomarkers was evaluated using ROC curve analysis.

Results

Significant alterations inTIMP1 expression linked to the EMT pathway in GCa

We obtained and normalized the STAD dataset from TCGA and identified 204 genes associated with the EMT pathway using the MsigDB database (Supplementary Table S1). DEG analysis of STAD dataset revealed 2926 DEGs, including 2518 upregulated and 408 downregulated genes. Among these, 87 genes related to the EMT pathway exhibited significantly different expression levels between cancer and normal samples, including 7 downregulated and 80 upregulated genes, meeting the criteria of|Log Fold Change (logFC)| >1 and FDR < 0.05 (Supplementary Table S2). We then applied the LASSO feature reduction algorithm and identify 11 candidate genes (Table 3).Additionally, we performed ROC curve analysis for each of these genes and chose the top four with the highest ROC values. On further review, we found no published studies using our proposed approach for TIMP1 in GCa. Because of this, we concentrated on TIMP1 only for this study. To visualize TIMP1 expression across normal and tumor samples, clinical stages, and molecular subtypes in STAD (Figs. 1 A–C), we plotted boxplots. TIMP1 expression was significantly different in these box plots, suggesting that it may play a role in STAD progression and heterogeneity. We then generated a volcano plot (Fig. 1D) showing the DEGs with TIMP1 expression, and genes that showed the most drastic changes. In addition, we performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) based on median expression levels of TIMP1 and identified key biological processes and pathways that were enriched in high and low expression groups. Diagnosis & prognosis analyses

We separated the TIMP1 expression data into normal and cancer samples, and made the ROC curve figure and plotted the corresponding ROC plot (Fig. 2 A). The analysis revealed an AUC value of 0.938 for TIMP1, suggesting strong diagnostic capability and suggesting that TIMP1 could be an important candidate diagnostic gene for EMT in GCa. The high AUC value of TIMP1 suggests that it may be useful in separating normal from cancerous samples in GCa. We additionally stratified tumor samples into low and high expression groups based on median TIMP1 expression. We performed survival analysis to evaluate its prognostic significance by comparing overall survival (OS) between the low and high TIMP1 expression groups. Figure 2B shows the KM survival curve for this analysis. No significant difference was seen in survival between the two groups (p = 0.8), which implies that TIMP1 does not seem to have a prognostic value for OS in GCa. Tumor microenvironment andTIMP1 expression in bulk RNA samples

We then used the TIMER2.0 database to investigate the relationship between the TIMP1 gene expression and tumor purity in the STAD bulk RNA-seq dataset. We found that expression of TIMP1 was significantly correlated with tumor purity (Correlation = −0.176, p = 5.80E-04), indicating that high TIMP1 expression corresponds with low tumor purity in the GCa dataset. This finding suggests a potential role of TIMP1 in the shaping of TME by modulating the composition of non tumor components in tumor samples. In addition, TIMP1 showed the highest positive correlation with cancer associated fibroblasts (CAFs) (Correlation = 0.584, p = 4.22E-36), suggesting a potential role in promoting fibroblast activation and expansion in the TME. TIMP1 showed the weakest correlation with mast cells (Correlation = −0.173, p = 7.25E-4), indicating little involvement in the control of mast cell presence or activity. These results suggest that TIMP1 may be a critical regulator of TME composition in GCa, including its influence on CAFs and immune cell infiltration. However, the precise mechanisms whereby TIMP1 expression modulates the activities and behaviors of immune cells are unclear. These observations need further experimental validation to see if TIMP1 modulates immune cell function directly. TME and TIMP1 expression in single cell samples

To evaluate expression levels of TIMP1 across different cancer cell types in single cell datasets, we used the TISCH2 database. We analyzed two STAD datasets (GSE134520 and GSE167297) that provide annotations of the TME at a single cell resolution. We found that TIMP1 is broadly expressed in most cancer cell types in both datasets. In both datasets, notably, CD8 + T (CD8T) cells, plasma cells, dendritic cells (DCs), mast cells, and fibroblasts were found to overlap with TIMP1 expressing cells. Among these, fibroblasts showed the highest TIMP1 expression levels (GSE134520 = 5.94, GSE167297 = 7.39), which indicate potential role of fibroblasts as a major TIMP1 source in the TME. On the other hand, TIMP1 expression levels were the lowest in CD8T cells (GSE134520 = 0.96, GSE167297 = 1.12), which indicates that TIMP1 may not have a primary direct role in modulating CD8T activity in the TME. These results emphasize the cell type specific expression patterns of TIMP1 with a focus on high correlation to fibroblasts. This is consistent with its previously reported correlation with CAFs in bulk RNA analyses, adding to the evidence that may implicate it in the fibroblast driven remodeling of the TME. Immune checkpoint blockade therapy

With the TISIDB database, we looked into the relationship between TIMP1 and ICIs in GCa. Our analysis found significant correlation of TIMP1 with the majority of ICIs (Fig. 5 A). It is shown here that TIMP1 expression is substantially correlated with immune regulation in GCa. Our strong correlations between TIMP1 and expression of multiple immune inhibitory molecules suggest that TIMP1 may contribute to the tumor immune microenvironment. Results for the immune subtype (Fig. 5B) and molecular subtype (Fig. 5 C) were not statistically significant, however. This observation suggests that TIMP1 expression may be involved in or reflective of responsiveness to ICI and we speculate that this may be the case, but further experimental validation is needed to confirm this. Furthermore, we segregated the tumor samples into high and low TIMP1 groups based on TIMP1 expression levels. The TIDE score for each group was calculated with the TIDE algorithm. Our analysis showed that the TIDE score was higher in the low-TIMP1 group than in the high-TIMP1 group. It is inferred that samples with lower TIMP1 expression are less likely to respond favorably to immune checkpoint blockade therapy and samples with higher TIMP1 expression are more likely to respond favorably. Validation of TIMP1 and LINC01615 expression using RT-qPCR

To validate the RNA-Seq analysis data of TIMP1 and LINC01615, RT-qPCR technique was employed. Figure 9 A-B demonstrates a significant increase (p < 0.0001) in TIMP1 and LINC01615 gene expression in tumor tissue compared to healthy tissue based on Real Time PCR data, suggesting their potential as biomarkers for tumor tissue. Additionally, to assess the specificity and sensitivity of TIMP1 in this study based on RT-qPCR data, ROC curve analysis was performed. Discussion

GCa ranks as the fourth most commonly diagnosed malignancy globally [1, 2]. In GCa, aberrant activation of EMT can induce the transformation of epithelial cells into a mesenchymal phenotype, leading to a loss of epithelial features and an increase in mesenchymal properties [6].

One study showed that treatment options for advanced prostate and colon cancer are limited, emphasizing the need for new targeted therapies. This research identified elevated levels of TIMP1 in patient plasma, which were associated with poor clinical outcomes. While it was unclear whether TIMP1 acts merely as a biomarker or plays an active role in cancer progression, the study demonstrated that TIMP1 is overexpressed in tumor stroma and promotes tumor growth. It also showed that TIMP1 increases the accumulation and activity of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), contributing to a pro-tumor microenvironment. These findings suggest TIMP1 as a potential therapeutic target in cancer treatment [32].

Another study investigated the expression of TIMP1 RNA in primary non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and its association with clinicopathological features. The findings revealed that TIMP1 expression was significantly higher in adenocarcinoma compared to squamous cell carcinoma. Although no correlation was observed with sex, smoking status, or tumor stage, high TIMP1 RNA levels were linked to poorer overall survival. These results suggest that TIMP1 may serve as a prognostic marker in resected NSCLC and highlight its potential involvement in disease progression [33].

In breast cancer, improving prognostic stratification and identifying predictors of treatment response remain critical needs. Several studies have highlighted TIMP1 as a potential marker of both prognosis and chemotherapy response. Recent research suggests that TIMP1 can be measured not only in tumor tissues but also in blood or paraffin-embedded samples, enhancing its clinical applicability. Moreover, findings indicate that TIMP1 may contribute to treatment resistance by inhibiting tumor cell apoptosis. These insights support the potential role of TIMP1 in guiding future breast cancer management [34].

In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), elevated TIMP1 levels have been associated with poor prognosis. Recent findings suggest that beyond its classical inhibitory functions, TIMP1 promotes cancer progression through noncanonical signaling pathways. Notably, TIMP1 was shown to induce neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation via CD63 and ERK signaling. In PDAC models, TIMP1-driven NET formation contributed to tumor progression, and inhibition of either TIMP1 or NETs improved survival. Moreover, plasma TIMP1 levels correlated with NET biomarkers in patients, and their combination with CA19-9 enhanced prognostic stratification. These results highlight TIMP1’s functional role in shaping a pro-tumor immune microenvironment [35].

A recent bioinformatics-based study identified TIMP1 as a key diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in CRC. TIMP1 was found to be significantly overexpressed in CRC tissues and associated with enrichment of cancer-related signaling pathways. Notably, high TIMP1 expression correlated with increased infiltration of various macrophage subsets and neutrophils, as well as elevated expression of immune checkpoint molecules such as CTLA-4 and HAVCR2, indicating its potential role in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment. Furthermore, TIMP1 expression was linked to increased sensitivity to specific drugs and showed a positive association with ferroptosis-related genes, suggesting its involvement in therapy response and cell death regulation. These findings support TIMP1 as a promising therapeutic target and biomarker in CRC [36].

In colon cancer, TIMP1 has been identified as a critical factor associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis. This study demonstrated that TIMP1 is significantly overexpressed in colon tumor tissues, especially in cases with lymph node and distant metastasis. Elevated TIMP1 levels were independently associated with reduced disease-free and overall survival. Functional assays revealed that TIMP1 promotes proliferation and metastasis while inhibiting apoptosis through activation of FAK-PI3K/AKT and MAPK signaling pathways. These findings underline the oncogenic potential of TIMP1 and support its clinical utility as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in colon cancer [37].

Functional assays revealed that TIMP1 promotes RCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, primarily through activation of EMT signaling. These findings underscore the potential of TIMP1 as a valuable diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in RCC and suggest its involvement in driving tumor progression [38].

TIMP1 not only functions as an endogenous inhibitor of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) but also acts as a key signaling molecule driving EMT and enhancing invasive cellular behavior. EMT-promoting effect of TIMP1 occurred independently of its MMP-inhibitory domain. Moreover, TIMP1 enhanced the expression of MT1-MMP and MMP2 and facilitated pro-MMP2 activation via MT1-MMP, ultimately increasing cell migration and invasion [39].

At the mechanistic level, TIMP1 loss reprogrammed the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) of senescent tumor cells by activating MMPs. Clinically, concurrent PTEN and TIMP1 loss in prostate cancer is frequently observed and is associated with docetaxel resistance and poor outcomes in patients undergoing adjuvant therapy. Collectively, these findings shed light on the dual roles of tumor-associated senescence and underscore the potential therapeutic implications for prostate cancer treatment [40].

Liu et al. identified TIMP1 as a direct target of miR-6745, and its suppression replicated the tumor-suppressive effects of miR-6745 in GCa cells. In this study, functional analyses demonstrated that miR-6745 negatively regulates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by targeting TIMP1, thereby attenuating cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Additionally, a negative correlation between miR-6745 and TIMP1 expression was observed in a cohort of 42 GCa tissue samples. These findings suggest that the miR-6745/TIMP1 regulatory axis plays a critical role in modulating Wnt/β-catenin signaling and driving GCa tumorigenesis, offering a potential therapeutic avenue for mitigating GCa progression [41].

A study conducted in 2020 indicated that LINC01615 may contribute to the invasive potential of endometrial cancer (EC) cells during disease progression. Additionally, a robust risk model incorporating seven cancer-related lncRNAs (CRLs) exhibited high prognostic accuracy, highlighting LINC01615 as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for EC patients [42].

MMPs are implicated in the progression of pancreatic cancer (PCa). In a study by Bloomston et al. (2002), the effects of overexpressing natural TIMP1 on PCa cell growth, metastasis, and angiogenesis were investigated. The researchers found that TIMP1 overexpression inhibited PCa cell implantation, growth, metastasis, and angiogenesis, while promoting tumor apoptosis. Notably, this effect occurred without altering MMP2 production. These results suggest that TIMP1 could serve as a potential target for gene therapy in PCa [43].

A review of gastric cancer studies by Grunnet et al. (2013) found that higher levels of TIMP1 protein in tumor tissue extracts or plasma of GCa patients were correlated with worse patient prognosis [44].

MMPs are capable of degrading almost all ECM components. TIMPs, particularly TIMP1, are key regulators of MMP activity and, by extension, the extracellular environment. An imbalance in the MMP/TIMP ratio, which alters the ECM, can contribute to early tumorigenesis and distant metastasis. MMPs are also elevated in various inflammatory conditions. A study investigating the prognostic significance of MMP-8, MMP-9, and TIMP1 in CRC found that serum levels of MMP-8 and TIMP1 could serve as prognostic markers. In patients without a systemic inflammatory response, elevated MMP-8 and TIMP1 levels were associated with a poorer prognosis [45].

A 2020 study found that serum levels of MMP7, MMP9, TIMP1, and TIMP2 were significantly higher in CRC patients and individuals with adenomas compared to control subjects. These findings suggest that MMPs and their inhibitors, TIMP1 and TIMP2, are crucial in CRC invasion and also active in the early stages of premalignant adenomas. Moreover, MMP7, MMP9, TIMP1, and TIMP2 may hold potential as prognostic biomarkers in CRC [46].

Hermann et al. (2021) highlighted the insufficient attention given to sex disparities in cancer, with many underlying factors remaining poorly understood. The study identified TIMP1 as the most strongly male-biased secreted factor in patient-derived pancreatic tumors. Male-specific regulation of TIMP1 was observed in both PCa models and patients. TIMP1 expression was found to be a significant risk factor in men, revealing a subpopulation with elevated TIMP1 levels (T1HI) in both primary tumors and blood. T1HI men exhibited a higher risk of hepatic metastasis, not only in PCa but also in CRC and melanoma. This research underscores a sex-independent lifestyle influence on hepatic metastasis, offering potential new directions for precision medicine [47].

Recent studies have emphasized the significance of hematogenous dissemination in ovarian cancer, with a growing interest in monitoring circulating tumor cells (CTCs) as potential prognostic markers. A study aimed at identifying CTCs in ovarian cancer patients focused on cellular plasticity to gain deeper insights into their biology. Epithelial CTCs were detected in 21% of patients, with a higher frequency observed in those with extraperitoneal metastasis. This population expressed epithelial markers MUC1 and CK19, as well as mesenchymal and malignancy-associated genes, including TIMP1, CXCR4, and stem cell markers CD24 and CD44. The study also suggested that TIMP1 expression plays a role in promoting tumor proliferation. These findings highlight the potential of EpCAM + CTC molecular characteristics in identifying biomarkers for disease diagnosis and therapeutic targets, such as TIMP1 [48].

A 2022 study demonstrated that cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are a key component of the tumor microenvironment, influencing cancer cell behavior through various mechanisms. A significant aspect of their effect stems from their high secretory activity, which exposes cancer cells to bioactive factors such as IL-6. The study revealed that CAF-derived TIMP1 activates STAT3 in breast cancer cells, in collaboration with CD63 and integrin β1. In turn, STAT3 enhances TIMP1 secretion by breast cancer cells, establishing a positive feedback loop involving TIMP1/CD63/integrin β1/STAT3, which can be further amplified by IL-6. This feedback loop is crucial for CAF-induced migration and resistance to the anti-estrogen treatment fulvestrant, underscoring its role in cancer progression and therapy resistance [49].

MMP2 and TIMP1 are essential molecules involved in extracellular matrix remodeling and play significant roles in the metastatic progression of breast cancer. A study aimed at evaluating the expression levels of MMP2 and TIMP1 genes and their potential implications for breast cancer prognosis found that both MMP2 and TIMP1 expression was lower in tumors compared to controls. These results suggest that the expression profiles of MMP2 and TIMP1 could serve as independent prognostic and predictive biomarkers for breast cancer, potentially facilitating the identification of specific subgroups for personalized treatment strategies [50].

Immune checkpoint therapies (ICT) have revolutionized the treatment of advanced solid tumors. However, their efficacy is often hindered by insufficient immunogenicity, largely due to the inadequate activation and development of antitumor T cells by dendritic cells (DCs). Consequently, identifying biomarkers that enhance DC functionality in these tumors is of critical importance. While TIMP1 is known to influence immune cells, its specific interaction with DCs remains largely unexplored. Recent studies have indicated that elevated TIMP1 levels in metastatic melanoma correlate with increased CD8 + T-cell infiltration and improved patient survival, suggesting a potential role for TIMP1 in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment [51].

TIMPs, particularly TIMP1, exhibit a dual role in cancer by influencing both tumor progression and immune cell regulation. Emerging evidence suggests that TIMPs may counteract the immunosuppressive effects of tumor-associated cytokines such as TGF-β on Natural Killer (NK) cells. TGF-β promotes NK cell polarization toward a decidual-like phenotype, characterized by reduced cytolytic activity and increased expression of exhaustion markers, including TIM-3. However, recombinant TIMP1 has been shown to mitigate these effects by restoring activation markers such as NKG2D and partially recovering NK cell degranulation capacity. These findings highlight the potential of TIMP1 in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment and enhancing antitumor immunity [52].

A study demonstrated that combining dendritic cell-cytokine-induced killer (DC-CIK) immunotherapy with chemotherapy significantly enhanced immune function in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients. This combination therapy increased CD4 + T cell and Natural Killer (NK) cell levels while reducing serum TIMP1, a biomarker associated with tumor invasion and metastasis. These findings highlight the potential of DC-CIK immunotherapy in improving NSCLC treatment outcomes by modulating the tumor microenvironment and enhancing antitumor immune responses [53].

MDK and TIMP1 have been identified as key regulators of both prognosis and disease progression in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Moreover, their strong association with immune cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment underscores their potential as promising immunotherapeutic targets. By modulating tumor-immune interactions, these genes may provide novel avenues for therapeutic intervention, ultimately improving clinical outcomes in LUAD patients [54].

These findings highlight the potential of TIMP1 as a key regulator of immune responses within the tumor microenvironment, presenting a promising target for immunotherapeutic strategies. Further investigation is required to fully elucidate TIMP1’s immunomodulatory mechanisms and its role in enhancing NK cell function, which could contribute to more effective cancer treatments.

TIMP1’s strong diagnostic value likely reflects its elevated expression in early tumor development, making it a useful marker for tumor presence. However, its lack of prognostic significance might be due to tumor heterogeneity or compensatory pathways activated during later stages of cancer progression. Furthermore, TIMP1’s expression may not directly correlate with survival outcomes despite its involvement in tumor biology.

TIMP1 has been reported to modulate the tumor immune microenvironment by influencing macrophage polarization and T-cell infiltration across various cancer types. For instance, studies have shown that TIMP1 promotes M2 macrophage polarization, which is typically associated with immunosuppressive activity and tumor progression [55]. Moreover, TIMP1 has been implicated in the upregulation of PD-L1 expression, thereby contributing to immune evasion by suppressing cytotoxic T-cell responses [56]. These findings support our hypothesis that TIMP1 may play a role in immune escape mechanisms in GCa and highlight its potential relevance to immunotherapy.

Zhang et al. reported that LINC01615 is upregulated in gastric adenocarcinoma tissues and cell lines. Nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation assays demonstrated that LINC01615 is predominantly localized in the cytoplasm of gastric cancer cells, indicating its potential role in regulating gene expression by acting as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) to sequester miRNAs. This mechanism may prevent the degradation or translational repression of its target mRNAs. Bioinformatic analyses identified four candidate miRNAs—miR-4677-5p, miR-516b-5p, miR-632, and miR-1288-3p—as potential targets of LINC01615, and the downstream mRNAs associated with ferroptosis that are regulated by these miRNAs were also identified [57].

Xiang et al. (2022) demonstrated that ncRNAs play a crucial regulatory role in carcinogenesis and tumor progression. Their study revealed that LINC01615 is upregulated in breast cancer patients and is associated with reduced overall survival. Both in vivo and in vitro experiments indicated that LINC01615 overexpression enhances the metastatic potential of breast cancer cells. Furthermore, its expression is regulated by the transcriptional activator SIPA1, which contributes to carcinogenesis in breast cancer. These findings underscore the oncogenic role of LINC01615 in breast cancer progression [58]. In a recent study, researchers developed a novel prognostic signature termed EMTscore, based on EMT-related lncRNAs in LUAD. This signature, comprising eight lncRNAs with high expression in tumor tissues, demonstrated strong predictive power for overall survival and proved to be an independent prognostic factor. Among these, LINC01615 was identified as a key hub lncRNA. Functional assays revealed that LINC01615 plays an oncogenic role by promoting cell proliferation, migration, and EMT processes in lung cancer cells. These findings suggest that LINC01615 may not only serve as a prognostic biomarker but also represent a potential therapeutic target in LUAD [59].

The presence of LINC01615 may indicate an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, positioning it as a potential target for novel immunotherapeutic strategies aimed at overcoming resistance and enhancing clinical outcomes in clear-cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) patients [60].

To explore the potential mechanisms by which LINC01615 contributes to GCa progression, we performed an in silico prediction using the RNAhybrid tool. According to our findings, LINC01615 may function as a ceRNA by sponging miRNAs that are known to regulate the EMT pathway in GCa, including hsa-miR-940, hsa-miR-503, hsa-miR-23b, and hsa-miR-939 [61–65]. In addition to miRNA-mediated regulation, recent studies propose that certain lncRNAs can recruit chromatin-modifying enzymes or interact with EMT-inducing transcription factors such as ZEB1 or SNAIL [66]. Therefore, it is plausible that LINC01615 may facilitate EMT and immune evasion through both post-transcriptional and epigenetic mechanisms. These mechanistic hypotheses provide new directions for future functional studies.

Based on existing literature and bioinformatic predictions, we hypothesize that LINC01615 may act as a ceRNA, sponging miRNAs that otherwise target TIMP1 or EMT-related transcripts. Alternatively, LINC01615 may regulate EMT through chromatin modification or interaction with transcription factors involved in EMT. We also propose experimental approaches such as RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP), chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP), and dual-luciferase assays to test these hypotheses in future studies.

It appears that a logical next step for future research would involve both in vitro and in vivo validation. This may include knockdown and overexpression assays of TIMP1 and LINC01615 in GCa cell lines to examine their effects on EMT-related markers (e.g., E-cadherin, N-cadherin, Vimentin) through qPCR and western blot analyses. Additionally, wound healing and transwell assays could be utilized to assess changes in cell migration and invasion. To further investigate the underlying regulatory mechanisms, dual-luciferase reporter assays and RIP may be performed to explore the potential ceRNA relationship involving LINC01615. These experimental approaches would provide valuable insights into the functional role of the TIMP1/LINC01615 axis in GCa progression.

Conclusion

Based on the findings of this study, TIMP1 and lncRNA LINC01615 may play pivotal roles in the pathogenesis of gastric cancer and hold promise as novel biomarkers for diagnosis and therapeutic targeting. Given that TIMP1 significantly influences TME, particularly in modulating CAFs and immune cell composition, it could serve as a potential target for immunotherapy. The current evidence suggests that alterations in the TME and its interactions with immune cells may impact the efficacy of immune-based treatments. Therefore, targeting TIMP1 and LINC01615 could pave the way for the development of new immunotherapeutic strategies in GCa, potentially enhancing therapeutic responses by bolstering immune activity and reducing tumor invasiveness.

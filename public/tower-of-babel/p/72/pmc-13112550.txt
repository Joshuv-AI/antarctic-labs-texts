Biallelic KLHL24 Loss-of-Function Variants
Authors: Niharika Adak, Mathilde CSC Vermeer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding AuthorKey words: dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, intermediate filament degradation, KLHL24

KLHL24 (Kelch-like family member 24) serves as a substrate adaptor for the CUL3–RBX1 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, which regulates the degradation of intermediate filament proteins, including dermal keratins1 and vimentin2; cardiac vimentin3 and synemin3; and skeletal4 and cardiac desmin.5 Approximately a decade ago, pathogenic variants in KLHL24 were first identified as a novel cause of skin fragility.1 Since then, 91 patients from 48 families have been reported, falling into 2 distinct genotypic–phenotypic categories: monoallelic gain-of-function (GoF) variants associated with epidermolysis bullosa simplex and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM),5 and biallelic loss-of-function (LoF) variants associated with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM).4 In this issue of JACC: Case Reports, Kumar et al6 report the first case of nonconsanguineous biallelic KLHL24 LoF–associated HCM, in which the genotype appears to outpace the phenotype.

Case Presentation

A 16-year-old male underwent screening after a cardiac murmur was detected, despite having no exertional chest pain or palpitations and reporting no family history of HCM or sudden cardiac death. Cardiac imaging was largely unremarkable but revealed asymmetric hypertrophy with mid-myocardial fibrosis. Genetic testing identified 2 novel truncating LoF-KLHL24 variants: a likely de novo frameshift mutation, c.1156_1163del (p.I386Qfs7), in cis, and a paternally inherited stop-gain mutation, c.1411A>T (p.K471), in trans. Given the genotype-associated high arrhythmic risk, a prophylactic implantable cardioverter-defibrillator was implanted. At 2-month follow-up, the patient remained clinically stable with no documented arrhythmic events.

HCM due to Biallelic LoF Variants in KLHL24

The report by Kumar et al. represents the fifth published study describing HCM caused by biallelic-LoF variants in KLHL24. To date, 5 consanguineous families with heterozygous/homozygous-LoF variants have been reported (Figure 1), in which HET-LoF carriers were asymptomatic, whereas all HOM-LoF carriers (n = 15) were diagnosed with HCM between 11 and 41 years of age.4,7, 8, 9 The case reported by Kumar et al. is consistent with these observations but is notable because the phenotype was detected incidentally in an otherwise healthy male. The genotype-associated high arrhythmic risk was appropriately addressed, underscoring the importance of case reports in informing and potentially changing clinical practice. In addition, Kumar et al. present the first report of compound HET-LoF KLHL24 variants causing HCM.

With respect to the underlying pathology, ex vivo analyses from 2 previously published families with HOM-LoF variants demonstrated excessive desmin accumulation in skeletal muscle tissue, as detected by western blotting and immunohistochemistry.4 Despite this finding, none of the affected individuals reported signs of skeletal muscle deterioration. In contrast, analysis of one available myocardial biopsy revealed excessive desmin accumulation by western blotting.4 The pathogenic role of KLHL24-LoF was further supported by a zebrafish model, in which the effects of KLHL24 antisense morpholino treatment on cardiac development and function were examined.4 However, the precise mechanism underlying desmin accumulation could not be further elucidated, likely because these short-term temporal studies did not produce detectable changes in desmin expression.

Lessons From Monoallelic GoF Variants in KLHL24

More in-depth investigation has been conducted into the underlying pathology of KLHL24-GoF variants. Of the 91 published symptomatic KLHL24 carriers, 75 harbor monoallelic-GoF variants (Figure 1). These variants result in full penetrance of skin fragility, clinically manifesting as aplasia cutis congenita. Although this patient population is still relatively young, many individuals develop cardiac symptoms later in life, progressing to DCM, which often leads to heart transplantation, sudden cardiac death, or death due to heart failure.10

Ex vivo analyses of 3 explanted and 1 postmortem heart from different families with HET-GoF variants revealed reduced desmin levels by immunohistochemistry.3,5,11 One research group additionally demonstrated reduced levels of synemin and vimentin in explanted hearts.3 Both groups employed patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cell cardiomyocytes and engineered heart organoids to further investigate disease mechanisms.3,5 These studies provided direct evidence that cardiac desmin and indirect evidence that vimentin and synemin (and possibly nestin) are targets in KLHL24-GoF–induced DCM. Cardiomyopathy has also been observed in a mouse model of KLHL24-GoF, although these findings remain unpublished.12

Future Directions

As molecular information on KLHL24 increasingly informs clinical decision-making, there is a clear need for preclinical studies focused on the underlying pathology of KLHL24-LoF. Human stem cell–based models represent a valuable tool, and CRISPR–Cas9 approaches could be used to generate skeletal and cardiac organoids to assess the extent of desmin, vimentin, synemin, and nestin accumulation in both monoallelic and biallelic-LoF contexts. In addition, the development of small rodent or porcine models may help delineate clinical manifestations across multiple organ systems in HET/HOM-LoF states. Finally, although 16 biallelic LoF patients and their monoallelic LoF family members have been characterized to date, comprehensive longitudinal studies are urgently needed to define the clinical spectrum of KLHL24-associated HCM.

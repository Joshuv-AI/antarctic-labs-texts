IFPHOR0088 Methods for Evaluating Usability in Healthcare Technology Assessment: A Critical Literature Review
Authors: Tarteel Mohammedahmed, Alexander Thompson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHealth technology assessment (HTA) provides structured evidence on the effectiveness, safety, and cost-effectiveness of healthcare interventions. Yet usability - the extent to which technologies can be used by specified users with effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction - remains underdeveloped in HTA despite being crucial for adoption, workflow fit, and patient outcomes. Unlike economic endpoints, usability is context-sensitive and multi-component, making evidence less portable and harder to benchmark.ObjectivesThis review aimed to systematically map, synthesise, and critically appraise validated methods for usability assessment in healthcare technologies within an HTA context.MethodsA systematic search of MEDLINE, Embase, and CINAHL was conducted using a PCC (Population, Concept, Context) framework. Eligible studies were those reporting the development or psychometric validation of usability instruments for healthcare technologies. Data extraction covered study characteristics, psychometric properties, and thematic coverage. Quality appraisal drew selectively on CASP checklists adapted for mixed designs.ResultsFour instruments met the inclusion criteria: the Patient Satisfaction and Usability with Apps Questionnaire (pSUAPP), the Healthcare Systems Usability Scale (HSUS), the mHealth App Usability Questionnaire (MAUQ), and the National Usability-Focused Health Information System Scale (NuHISS). All demonstrated high internal consistency (α = 0.61–0.98). However, only pSUAPP and MAUQ established convergent validity, and only pSUAPP tested reproducibility. Critical gaps included lack of measurement invariance, responsiveness to change, and actionable thresholds, limiting decision utility for HTA. Thematic analysis showed divergence: patient-facing tools emphasised engagement and communication, while clinician/system tools prioritised workflow integration, information quality, and safety.ConclusionsValidated usability instruments exist but vary in methodological rigour and external validity. Their fragmented coverage limits comparability and weakens integration into HTA. Future work should focus on validating and harmonising existing tools, developing domain-specific families, and linking usability outcomes to adoption, safety, and cost-effectiveness to ensure decision-grade evidence for procurement and policy.

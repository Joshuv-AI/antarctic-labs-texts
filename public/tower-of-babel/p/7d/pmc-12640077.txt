A fusion strategy of multivariate flavor analysis techniques for the discrimination of aged sauce-flavor Baijiu
Authors: Dan Wang, Yan Chen, Xinyu Ma, Xiaobing Zhang, Ji Zhang, Siqian Guo, Jingming Li, Liping Xiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the present work, effective methods for determining the age of sauce-flavor Baijiu by multivariate data analysis and machine learning techniques were explored. Considering the complex and dynamic flavor changes during Baijiu storage, four analytical techniques, including gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS), gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (GC-IMS), electronic nose (E-nose) and electronic tongue (E-tongue) were integrated, to build a multilayered flavor profile of Baijiu. Four types of classification models were further constructed. The fusion data strategy combined with oversampling method of synthetic minority over-sampling technique (SMOTE) and neural network, significantly enhance the accuracy (0.96) and precision (0.97) of aged Baijiu determination (ranged from 1 year to 30 years). A total of 28 important features were screened out, including furfural, 2-hexanol (GC–MS), Area 65 (GC-IMS), and bitterness (E-tongue). Furthermore, potential correlations among different data sources were discussed. The astringency (E-tongue) showed a positive correlation with ethyl lactate (GC–MS) and Area 40 (GC-IMS).Keywords: Sauce-flavor Baijiu, Data fusion, Machine learning, Age discrimination

Introduction

Chinese Baijiu is one of the six major distilled spirits in the world and holds a significant place in traditional Chinese alcoholic culture (Y. Xu et al., 2022). According to different flavor characteristics, Chinese Baijiu is classified into 12 types, with its aroma and taste closely related to the geographical environment. Sauce-flavor Baijiu (SFB) is widely favored by consumers due to its unique flavor characteristics, including the sauce-like smell, rapid and strong taste experience, and post-consumption comfort. Generally, the age of the Baijiu is one of the key factors influencing the flavor profile. The flavor, especially the market value, varies significantly between different aged Baijiu (M. Li et al., 2024). Commonly, they were classified into the young-aged Baijiu group (≤5 years), the middle-aged Baijiu group (6–15 years) and the elder-aged Baijiu group (>15 years). Especially in the recognition and sales of the Chinese Baijiu market, the age specificity of the samples is usually emphasized with a boundary of 5 or 10 years. Consumers also normally believe longer storage years equate to higher quality.

Previous studies have shown volatile compounds are key indicators for identifying different raw materials, production processes, and the age of Baijiu (Song et al., 2020). Experienced tasting experts can even determine the age of Baijiu based on Sensory evaluation. Different analytical techniques have been used for discrimination the age of Baijiu, including gas chromatography-olfactometry-mass spectrometry (GC-O-MS), proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR), liquid chromatography coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Orbitrap HRMS), infrared spectroscopy, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (Zheng et al., 2021). Current reports focus on the use of GC–MS analysis, which can effectively separate volatile compounds and perform both qualitative and quantitative analysis (Liu et al., 2023). Over 2000 volatile compounds have been detected in Baijiu (Hong, Zhao, & Sun, 2023). The normally direct injection method of GC–MS can achieve rapid detection, while the flavor components in Baijiu are present in very small quantities, more complex sample pretreatment methods such as liquid-liquid extraction (LLE) and solid-phase microextraction (SPME) are often required (Hong et al., 2023). Additionally, not all changes in the GC–MS fingerprint were related to the age of Baijiu. It is necessary to select the features that are highly correlated with age to establish an age discrimination model (Liu et al., 2023).

GC-IMS is an emerging detection instrument known for its easy-operate, high sensitivity, low detection limit, and simple sample preparation (Parastar & Weller, 2024). Specifically, these advantages are reflected in its requirement for ambient pressure conditions, free of solvents and relying on nitrogen as the carrier gas. Furthermore, it typically performs headspace sampling for analysis. It enables the two-dimensional separation of volatile compounds in both gas chromatography columns and ion mobility spectrometers, showing impressive sensitivity. Currently, GC-IMS is widely applied in sample identification and differentiation, such as identification of brandy vintage and classification of botanical origin of honey (Li, Yang, Tian, Zou, & Li, 2020; Parastar & Weller, 2024). It can complement the results obtained from GC–MS and other techniques, with strong sensitivity to small molecular weight and volatile components, further improves differentiation by leveraging complementary information.

The electronic nose (E-nose) and electronic tongue (E-tongue) are machines that use an array of sensors to detect and differentiate complex odors and tastes, and they are widely applied in industries such as food and cosmetics (Zhang, Shao, Qiu, Wang, & Wei, 2020). Their advantages include high sensitivity, correlation with human sensory panel data, low cost, and short analysis time (Shen et al., 2024). In the field of Baijiu detection, the E-nose has been used for identifying Baijiu aroma types, age, brand, and origin (Peng, Tian, Chen, Li, & Gao, 2015).

The factors contribute to the flavor differences in the aging process of Baijiu mainly include both physical and chemical reactions. Physical reactions involve the volatilization of low-boiling components. The porous structure in ceramic materials facilitates the adsorption process, besides. The migration of abundant metal ions (such as Fe, Cu, Mg, and Al) enhance the flavor of young age Baijiu, and may catalyze chemical reactions, with iron acting as a pro-oxidant (L. Wang, Gao, Wu, Chen, & Xu, 2024). The chemical reactions mainly refer to redox reactions, esterification, and hydrolysis, which improve the quality and acceptability of Baijiu. In natural aging, ceramic jars with microporous structures are used to ensure gas exchange between Baijiu and the air. Oxygen is considered the initial oxidant, playing a key role in the formation of flavor compounds (Zheng et al., 2021).

However, the identification of the quality and age of Baijiu has always been a significant challenge. This is primarily due to the fact that, its main components are water and ethanol, which complicates and interferes with the comprehensive analysis and identification of other components in Baijiu. Besides, the utilization of a single source data for the discrimination of aged Baijiu often insufficient to fully profile the differences between various aged Baijiu. As mentioned above, despite the development of various instrumental analytical methods, their individual discrimination capabilities remain inadequate.

Data fusion strategy has received increasing attention, by combining the data advantages of multiple analytical techniques source. For example, the possibility of fusing GC-IMS data with other types of chromatographic and spectral data, each method offers unique advantages, and the combination of these data sets allows for enhanced differentiation by leveraging complementary information (Parastar et al., 2024). Previous works have presented the data fusion of GC–MS and proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) spectroscopy to identify the ages of Baijiu (Chen et al., 2024), and integrating tri-step infrared spectroscopy (IR) with electronic nose (E-nose) to evaluate red wines (Wang et al., 2022). Data fusion has been proven to be an innovative and promising approach. On the other hand, the challenges in predicting the age of Baijiu were not only stemmed from a single-data model, but also the number of real samples and the difficulty of analyzing samples from various sources (Li et al., 2025). They posed a challenge on the widely application of the discrimination of Baijiu age. In machine learning, imbalanced sample groups also lead to the challenge of model overfitting. Oversampling techniques have demonstrated effectiveness in real-world applications. The synthetic minority over-sampling technique (SMOTE) algorithm is used for oversampling of the model to balance the sample sizes of various types of data, and developed as the most widely adopted method (Douzas, Bacao, & Last, 2018). While its application in food is still relatively limited at present. But it is of great significance where insufficient food samples affect mathematical analysis.

Therefore, addressing the challenge of identification of aged Baijiu, this study aimed to propose a joint analysis of four flavor analysis techniques, including GC–MS, GC-IMS, and intelligent sensory technologies, to collectively construct multivariate flavor characteristics for baijiu across different aging groups. By combining of machine learning based on data fusion strategy, an optimal model was developed to achieve an objective method for distinguishing Baijiu age. Additionally, important flavor features from each single-technique were identified to further reveal their relevance in age difference. This work provides a novel perspective and a practical basis for the sauce-flavor Baijiu age discrimination.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

Seventy-seven chemical standards (purity ≥97 %) of alcohols, esters, acids, aldehydes, ketones and pyrazines were purchased. Detailed information about these compounds were summarized in Table S1. They were prepared as standard solution as followed. A certain amount (0.001 g) of each compound was accurately weighed and dissolved in methanol-water (70:30, v/v) to prepare stock solutions (1 mg/mL). Then, the stock solutions were diluted to a series of concentrations to construct standard solutions. Deionized water was prepared by ultrafiltration ultrapure water machine (WP-UP-WF-10, SiChuan Water Purifier Co., Ltd.).

Sample collection

A total of 60 samples of sauce-flavor Baijiu (SFB) from different ages (with an alcohol content of 53°) were collected. The information on the companies from which the samples were sourced was shown in Table S2. In this study, they represented the young-aged Baijiu group (≤5 years), the middle-aged Baijiu group (6–15 years) and the elder-aged Baijiu group (>15 years). They were specifically categorized into 6 groups with a 5-year interval (Year5, Year10, Year15, Year20, Year25, and Year30), respectively. After collection, all samples were sealed in their original bottles and stored at 18 °C in the dark.

GC- MS analysis

GC–MS analysis was based on the study by Wang et al. (Wang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024) with modifications, as followed: Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC–MS, Agilent 5977B GC/MSD, Agilent Technologies, America) with DB-FFAP capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent Technologies) was used. The carrier gas was high-purity helium (99.999 %) with a flow rate of 1 mL/min. Direct liquid injection was carried out with injection volume of 1 μL. The injection port temperature was set at 220 °C, and the split ratio was 10:1. The oven temperature settings was as follows: initially temperature was set at 40 °C for 2 min, raised to 60 °C at 3 °C/min, followed by a 2 °C/min increase to 70 °C, then increase to 180 °C at 3 °C/min ratio, a 6 °C/min increase to 200 °C, and a 12 °C/min increase to 220 °C, where it was held for 10 min. Mass Spectrometry was equipped with the electron ionization source (EI) at 70.0 eV ionization energy. The ion source temperature was 230 °C. Data acquisition was performed in SIM mode.

Qualitative analysis was carried out by comparing the NIST 20 library, mass spectrum information and retention time (RT) of the target compound with the standards. The calibration curves were used for quantification.

GC-IMS analysis

Based on previous study (S. Li et al., 2020), analysis conditions were as follows with a little modification. GC-IMS (FlavourSpec®, Gesellschaft für Analytische Sensorsysteme mbH, Germany) with MXT-WAX (15 m × 0.53 mm, 1 μm) was used. The column oven temperature was 60 °C, the drift tube temperature was 45 °C, the injection port temperature and transfer line temperature were both 80 °C. high-purity nitrogen (99.999 %) was used as carrier gas and drift gas. The sample was incubated at 80 °C for 20 min, with shaking at 500 rpm/min, then 500 μL of headspace gas was extracted for injection, and the analysis time was 30 min. Carrier gas program was followed, 0–2 min, carrier gas flow rate was 2 mL/min; 2–10 min, 2 to 10 mL/min; 10–20 min, 10 to 100 mL/min; 20–30 min, 100 to 150 mL/min. Drift gas flow was 150 mL/min.

Pretreatment method of sample was carried out based on previous work with adjustment (S. Li et al., 2020). The aged SFB samples were diluted 20 times with ultrapure water, shaken for 5 min, then stored in the dark for 6 h, a 100 μL of the diluted sample, with 10 μL of n-hexane, were transferred to a 20 mL headspace vial for analysis.

E-nose analysis

The analysis was performed using the PEN3 electronic nose (Airsense Analytics, Germany). The sampling time was 1 s/group, with a sensor self-cleaning time of 120 s, sample preparation time was 6 s, sample flow rate and air flow rate were 300 mL/min. The analysis time was 100 s. Before the instrument analysis, a 10 mL diluted sample in 50 mL headspace vial was incubated for 3 min, with three replicates for one sample.

The negative sensors W1C, W3C, and W5C were set as G0/G, the other positive sensors were set as G/G0. Here, G0 and G represent the resistance of the sensor in clean air and in the sample gas, respectively. The E-nose contains ten different metal oxide sensors, and the sensor array is shown in Table S3.

E-tongue analysis

Eight tastes analysis were carried out by the E-tongue (TS-5000Z, Insent Intelligent Sensor Technology, Inc., Japan), including sourness, saltiness, bitterness, astringency, umami, richness (aftertaste of umami), aftertaste-A (aftertaste of astringency), and aftertaste-B (aftertaste of bitterness). Measurement settings were as followed, Ag/AgCl reference electrode was used, sensors were washed in the positive and negative electrode solutions for 90 s, and washed in two reference solutions (120 s per solution) for stabilizing. Each sample was analysis for 30 s by the sensors, followed by two quick washing (3 s each), and then measured the aftertaste values in the reference solution. The sensors underwent automatic washing after each measurement, to ensure their cleanliness and preparedness for the next round of data collection.

The aged SFB samples were diluted 20 times with a 0.01 mol/L neutral phosphate buffer solution, shaken for 1 min and extracted 35 mL before analysis.

Model construction and evaluation

The data analysis and model construction were performed by python 3.10.16. Package of pandas 2.2.3 was used for data analysis. Package of scikit-learn 1.5.1 was applied for model construction, including linear kernel support vector machine (SVM) and a 3-layer neural network (MLP), The hidden layers were 50–100-50 neurons, a k-nearest neighbor (kNN) with k of 6, and decision tree (DT). Matplotlib 3.9.2 was used for visualization of results.

Data processing and statistical analysis

The raw GC–MS data was analyzed both qualitatively and quantitatively using Agilent Masshunter Qualitative Analysis 10.0 (Agilent Technologies, America) and MSD Chemstation Data Analysis Application 6.0. (Agilent Technologies, America). Corresponding linear equation was constructed by regression analysis of standards. Qualitative analysis of GC-IMS data was carried out by comparing the retention index (RI) with the database by GC-IMS Library Search V2.2.1 software. Etraction of peak volume was performed by LAV software (Version 2.2.1, G.A.S, Dortmund, Germany). Data from the E-nose were processed by WinMuster (Version 1.6.2) software.

MetaboAnalyst 6.0 (www. Metaboanalyst.ca) was applied to statistical analysis, including analysis of variance (ANOVA), heatmap analysis, principal component analysis (PCA), Bar charts and scatter plots were constructed by Origin2024, Cytoscape 3.10.1 was applied to correlation networks.

Results and discussion

The multivariate characteristics of SFB flavor

Quantitative profiling of SFB by GC–MS

A total of 69 volatile compounds were detected in the sample. According to the chemical structures, they were divided into 8 groups, including esters (24), alcohols (14), acids (9), pyrazines (8), phenols (5), furans (4), aldehydes (3), and ethers (2). These compounds were identified through the standards. Method validation of the GC–MS analysis was conducted to ensure the reliability of the results, including linearity, LOD, LOQ, precision and accuracy. As shown in Table S4, the results showed the spiked recovery ranged from 80.54 % to 121.31 %, with all RSD below 8.63 %. The linearity of the standard curves was validated by R2, most of them exceeded 0.999. By employing the verified GC–MS method, this work accurately quantified 69 volatile compounds in 6 groups with each category representing a 5-year interval. The results were presented in Table S5.

As shown in Fig. 1A, Y5 group had the highest total volatile compounds content, reaching 4193.69 mg/L, significantly higher than the other five groups (<3873.88 mg/L). Y25 and Y30 had the lowest total volatile compounds content, at 3349.70 and 3637.12 mg/L, respectively, with no significant difference. The total volatile compounds content decreased with increasing aging time, mainly due to significant changes in ester compounds.

GC–MS quantitative data characteristics: (A) Box plot of total volatile components of Baijiu in different years; (B) Content comparison of volatile components of different structural types; (C) Correlation scatter plot of volatility of different structural types; (D) PCA plot of Baijiu in different year groups.

Esters are the most abundant in Baijiu aromas, their quantity and content crucially impact the flavor of SFB. As the storage time increases, hydrolysis reactions of ester compounds lead to an increase in the content of acid and alcohol compounds (Xu et al., 2022). As shown in Fig. 1B, the total ester content in Y5 was significantly higher than the other groups, decreasing from 3341.55 to 2495.07 mg/L (a 25 % reduction). Among them, ethyl acetate had the highest content (>1333.10 mg/L), more than 1.5 times of other esters at least. It's most abundant (1917.59 mg/L) in the Y5. During the fermentation process of SFB by Daqu, microorganisms in the Daqu continuously generated ethyl acetate through enzymatic reactions (Niu et al., 2022). While it gradually volatilized or hydrolyzed during storage. As shown in Table S5, its content significantly decreased in the Y25 and Y30 group. Ethyl lactate exhibited similar characteristics. Both ethyl acetate and ethyl lactate were recognized as key aroma compounds as well, giving fruity smell in SFB (Wang, Hu, et al., 2022).

In contrast, the total contents of alcohols significantly increased with age, rising from 518.05 to 560.59 mg/L. For example, the content of 2-hexanol and 1,3-butanediol in Y30 increased 3 times and 7 times respectively, reaching 52.33 and 5.88 mg/L. 1,3-butanediol is a precursor to acid compound in SFB (Wu et al., 2022), Similarly, total contents of acids improved from 71.63 (Y5) to 231.37 mg/L (Y30). Butyric acid significantly increased from 21.38 to 41.78 mg/L, and hexanoic acid increased from 8.28 to 61.67 mg/L. Propionic acid and valeric acid showed the same trend of change in the aging process of SFB. Previous studies also confirmed the increasing contents of acids (Wu et al., 2022),. Furthermore, Niu et al. have proposed that valeric acid was significantly correlated with SFB aging (R2 = 0.931) (Niu, Zhu, & Xiao, 2020). In addition to esterification and hydrolysis reactions, oxidation reactions can also cause changes in the content of aroma compounds in SFB, such as the formation of acid compounds from alcohols reacting with oxygen.

For pyrazine compounds, they contributed to the roasted and nutty flavors in SFB, and the content of them in SFB is much higher than that in other flavor types of baijiu (M. Y. Wang, Yang, Zhao, Zhang, & Su, 2019). In this study, the content of 2,3-dimethylpyrazine was the highest, accounting for more than 80 % of the total amount of pyrazine compounds (ranged from 15.58 to 17.50 mg/L). Notably, the contents of 2-methylpyrazine, 2-ethylpyrazine, 2-ethyl-6-methylpyrazine, 2-ethyl-3,6-dimethylpyrazine, and 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine exhibited an initial decline followed by an increase with prolonged aging of baijiu. These compounds were considered as important indicators closely associated with the aging process in SFB (H. Xu et al., 2025).

As shown in Fig. 1B, the total content of furan compounds in SFB significantly decreased with the increase of age, dropped from 231.95 (Y5) to 114.59 mg/L (Y30). Among them, furfural was not only accounted for a high proportion of furan compounds in SFB itself, but also contributed to the difference in aroma between SFB and other types of Baijiu (Noviello et al., 2024).

For the other compounds, including phenolic and aldehyde derivatives, their contents displayed disparate trends. For example, the content of benzaldehyde significantly increased, while the content of phenylacetaldehyde significantly decreased. Nonanal was not detected in aged SFB, except for Y5 group. Diethoxy methane significantly increased inY15 group. This was due to the fact that as the aging time extended, it would continuously accumulated through the acetalization of ethanol and formaldehyde (Zhu, Fan, Xu, & Zhou, 2016). The scatter plots of the content were shown in Fig. 1C. Interestingly, phenols and furan compounds show a positive correlation between different aged SFB (R = 0.62), which were rarely reported before.

The accurate qualitative and quantitative results of compounds based on GC–MS provided the possibility to reveal the differences between SFB of different ages. On the other hand, the richness and complexity of the data made it difficult to derive the relationship between them and the aging time from a few compounds or a single analytical mode. For example, the PCA classification method used in Fig. 1D.

Fingerprint profiling of SFB by GC-IMS

Different polarity columns were compared first, including the MXT-WAX (15 m × 0.53 mm, 1.0 μm) and MXT-5 (15 m × 0.53 mm × 1.0 μm) columns, with all other conditions remaining the same. As shown in Fig. 2A, the topographic chromatograms of the same SFB sample by MXT-WAX and MXT-5 columns were obtained. After data processing, 67 signal peaks were extracted using MXT-WAX, and 53 signal peaks were extracted using MXT-5. MXT-WAX showed better performance in the extraction and separation of signal peaks from SFB samples; enabling optimal capture of aroma-related information from SFB. Therefore, MXT-WAX was selected as the chromatographic platform for the subsequent sample analysis. The sample pretreatment method of GC-IMS analysis was explored as well, including dilution times and auxiliary solvents (Fig. S1). According to the high ethanol content (53 %, v/v), direct analysis was easily subjected to interference. Thus, three dilution times of 5, 20 and 50 were tested. Ethanol signal intensity markedly decreased (gray dashed) after dilution, yet flavor compound signals decreased as well. At 5 times dilution, peaks of high concentration flavor compounds were partial overlapped (yellow/white dashed). At 20 times dilution, peak resolution significantly improved. However, 50 times dilution resulted in weak target signals. Thus, 20 times dilution emerged as optimal. Besides, incorporating n-hexane as an auxiliary solvent reduced ethanol interference, enabling more analytes to exhibit (white dashed, Fig.S2).

GC-IMS quantitative data characteristics (A) Comparison of signal peaks number in different chromatographic columns, (B) Comparison of fingerprint spectra of peak volumes in different years.

Among 67 peaks obtained from SFB of different ages group, 53 compounds were identified, including 17 ester compounds, 11 alcohol compounds, 1 acid compound, 1 pyrazine compound, 1 furan compound, 10 aldehyde compounds, 4 ketone compounds, 5 olefin compounds, and 3 other compounds. The specific identification information of these peaks was shown in Table S6, including retention index, retention time, drift time. By drawing the fingerprint spectra of volatile substances, the differences and relative content comparisons of SFB of different ages can be intuitively displayed. As shown in Fig. 2B, with the change of age, the color of the peaks deepens (A11) at first, then gradually decreases, representing the volatile compounds in the samples also increase or decrease accordingly with the age. Details of peak volumes of 66 volatile components in SFB based on GC-IMS were summarized in Table S7. It should be pointed out that although the database comparison was carried out through data of more than two dimensions, these qualitative results need to be further verified. It's also hard to deny GC-IMS improves the intuitiveness and visualization of data processing, as a rapid and convenient analytical method.

Intelligent sensory profiling of SFB by E-nose and E-tongue

The response values of the ten E-nose sensors were shown in Table S8. R(7), sensitive to sulfides, had the highest response value (>33.68). Sulfides in SFB have received attention in recent years, they produce high flavor intensities at low thresholds and are important for its flavor in SFB (Sun, Wang, & Sun, 2021). While the responses of R (1), R (3), and R (5) were relatively low (<1). Fig. 3A presented the differences among them by heatmap. There was a clear trend: in the analysis results of SFB using the E-nose, the first three groups (Y5, Y10 and Y15) and the last three groups (Y20, Y25 and Y30) showed a distinct contrast. According to the clustering results, R (1), R (3), and R (5) exhibited lower response values in the initial three sample groups, but increased in the last three groups, while the remaining sensors displayed opposing trends. This indicated the E-nose may have more potential to distinguish SFB samples aged over 15 years.

Electronic nose signal characteristics (A1) Heat map analysis, (A2) PCA analysis; Electronic tongue signal characteristics: (B1) Heat map analysis, (B2) PCA analysis.

Despite employing ten sensors for the analysis, unfortunately, the majority of sensor responses across disparate aging groups failed to exhibit significant differences. For example, there were no significant differences between the six aging groups for R (1) and R (9). For R (6), sensitive to short-chain alkanes, there were no significant differences among the first five groups. This is possibly related to the limitations of the E-nose, the weak anti-interference ability of the E-nose in complex environments may have had a certain impact on the analysis (H. Zhang et al., 2020).

The results of the nine E-tongue taste analyses of SFB were shown in Table S9. Response value of sourness was the highest, reaching above 26.65, followed by astringency (>13.01). They are typical sensory characteristics of SFB. Previous studies have associated them to acids, alcohols, aromatic compounds, and esters, respectively (J. Hong et al., 2023). These compounds were also associated with the pungency of SFB, such as the positive correlation between acids and esters and the pungent taste (He, Tang, Yu, Chen, & Xu, 2022). Heatmap was used to compare the taste values of different aging groups, as shown in Fig. 3B. Interestingly, the aging groups naturally clustered into three groups according to time, with one cluster every 10 years. This indicates that the E-tongue may distinguish SFB aged over 10-year intervals. There was a significantly trend of decreasing sourness and astringency with increasing SFB aging (from 34.83 to 26.65, and 14.93 to 13.01 respectively). All these results above showed the potential of intelligent sensory analysis technology in age discrimination.

Comparison of single-data and fusion-data on SFB classification

The dataset consists of 180 data of SFB flavor (3 repetitions). 155 variable dimensions were summarized, including the results obtained through four different methods, GC-IMS (67), GCMS (69), E-Tongue (9), and E-Nose (10).

Sixty samples were divided into 6 categories, with each category representing a 5-year interval. It is evident that there is a severe imbalance between the label types after the classification, which is highly likely to cause data overfitting. Therefore, the SMOTE algorithm is applied to oversample the data and balance the sample sizes of each label type. As shown in Fig. 4A, SMOTE expanded the sample size, without changes the overall distribution characteristics of the data.

The machine learning results of multimodal data fusion (A) Comparison of sample scatter plots by the SMOTE algorithm; (B) ROC curves of different models; (C) Confusion matrices of different models; (D) Top 28 features importance by MLP.

Four schemes were applied to model construction, including MLP, SVM, kNN and DT. Accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-Score were applied to evaluate the models., The results between single-data and fusion-data were shown in Table 1. SMOTE method significantly improves the accuracy and F1 value of most models, not only in single-data model, but also in fusion-data model. For example, in MLP model, the accuracy of the single-data based on GC-MS increased from 0.73 to 0.85, and the F1 value increased from 0.73 to 0.85. In SVM, the F1 value of the single-data based on the E-tongue increased from 0.27 to 0.45. These results indicate the potential of this strategy in enhancing machine learning capabilities and its application in SFB discrimination. It should also be pointed out this improvement is not absolute. For example, the accuracy of the DT model based on GC-IMS is 0.78 both before and after SMOTE.

Performance comparison of four models combined with single-data and fusion-data strategies.

In the single-data model results (after SMOTE), the GC–MS-based data model achieved the better performance, showing high F1-score across all models, including MLP, SVM, kNN, and DT. For example, in the MLP model, the F1-score was 0.90, with the highest accuracy (0.87) and precision (0.91). The GC-IMS data model followed. In the SVM model, GC-IMS (0.84) performed better than GC–MS (0.83). For E-tongue and E-nose. Despite the E-nose having more sensors (10), which allows it to distinguish compounds with different structural characteristics, its actual model performance was not satisfactory, with F1-score < 0.32 in four models. As a distilled liquor, Baijiu mainly consists of ethanol and water, and the limited proportion of effective compounds makes the analysis by E-nose more challenging.

The results based on the fusion-data outperformed the single- data model across different models (F1-score > 0.80). The accuracy, precision and F1-score improved significantly, such as in the DT model, where the precision increased from a maximum of 0.73 (kNN) to 0.82. Among the four models, the MLP performed better than the others, with accuracy, precision and F1-score F1 value reaching 0.96, 0.97 and 0.96 respectively. Moreover, based on the results above, this study further explored methods of partial data fusion, specifically E-nose and E-tongue were separately integrated with GC–MS and GC-IMS (Fusion-E-tongue and Fusion-E-nose respectively). These attempts were to eliminate redundant information and enhance model performance by selectively fusing subsets of data, as preliminary single data classification results suggested potential benefits. Results were presented in Table S10. While both Fusion-E- tongue and Fusion-E-nose consistently improved accuracy and F1 score (both maximum 0.93) compared to single data methods (< 0.87, Table 1), they did not perform better achieved by fusing data of four analytical techniques (0.96). Notably, the results indicated that E-tongue data contain more effective information than E-nose data in this study. These results may provide further insights for the selection of data and analytical techniques.

ROC curves were plotted to demonstrate the classification efficiency of each model and dataset, as shown in Fig. 4B. It validated the significant differences in classification results were not due to the classification threshold settings. A horizontal comparison showed the fusion-data model efficiency outperformed the single-data model, according to the correct judgment rate for positive samples was higher in model. In the vertical comparison showed the MLP performed better, showing a higher rate of correctly identifying positive samples from negative. The confusion matrix further validated the classification efficiency of the model for each group, as shown in Fig. 4C. The diagonal represents the number of correct classifications, while the off-diagonal cells represent misclassified samples.

The improvement of this performance was truly inspiring, which confirmed the effectiveness of this method. The data from diverse sources was effective complemented, enhancing accuracy significantly. Previous studies have also demonstrated the effectiveness of this strategy. For example, the combination of GC–MS and proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) spectroscopy to identify the of Baijiu aged from 3 to 9 years, the value of accuracy was 1.00 (B. Chen et al., 2024). Similarly, Wang et al. proposed an integration of tri-step infrared spectroscopy (IR) with electronic nose (E-nose) to rapid evaluate qualitative authentication and quantitative evaluation of red wines.(S. Wang et al., 2022). Their models with fusion data exhibited better predictive ability and accuracy than the others, which was the same in this study. Besides, hybrid analytical approaches for flavor profiling have gained growing attention in recent years. Zhou et al. (Zhou et al., 2025) systematically characterized the flavor evolution of Te-flavor Baijiu during different periods, using a combination of electronic sensory techniques and GC-IMS and GC–MS. Hao et al. (Hao et al., 2024) evaluated the flavor characteristics of Shanlan rice wines fermented for different time based on GC–MS, HS-GC-IMS, and electronic sensory analyses. It is also crucial to note that a sufficient number of authentic samples are required for training in this method. While SMOTE helps alleviate data imbalance issues, reliance on real samples remains essential to avoid compromised model performance.

Analysis of important features and explanatory analysis

Based on permutation feature importance, the most optimal classification features for the fusion-data in MLP were identified, resulting in the selection of the top 28 flavor feature variables (coefficient > 0.01), as shown in Fig. 4D. Among these flavor features, 12 compounds were screened out based on GC–MS, and furfural proved to be the most significant. It is typically described as a compound with roasted and nutty aroma characteristics, as reported in the SFB. In the work by Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2023), furfural was identified as a key aroma compound, with the highest OAV (Odor Activity Value) of 524.07 in SFB. Furfural is primarily derived from the bran, sorghum husks, wheat straw, and fermented grains, all of which are rich in lignocellulosic materials. Furfural and hydroxymethylfurfural are degradation products of pentoses and hexoses. They contribute to the formation of bitter, spicy, and complex flavor characteristics in SFB. In the study by Huang et al., it was noted that furfural gradually decreases with aging, which is consistent with the results in this study (Huang et al., 2024). Furfural not only plays a significant role in SFB but also in the quality analysis of oak-barrel aged wines, where it contributed to the woody aroma (Noviello et al., 2024). In the storage of certain fruit wines, furfural is considered as an indicator of oxidation, and it serves as a degradation product of ascorbic acid (Zhang, Li, & Fan, 2019).

Four ester compounds were screened out, including diethyl succinate, ethyl oleate, ethyl tetradecanoate, and ethyl lactate. Diethyl succinate gives a fruity smell, while ethyl oleate and ethyl tetradecanoate, as higher fatty acid esters, varied in concentration in SFB from 0.166 to 12.951 mg/L. Ethyl lactate is typically characterized by fruity, grassy, and sweet smell. Interestingly, these compounds are key indicators in the quality standards for Chinese national standard of Baijiu (Jia, Di, Zhang, & Shi, 2022). According to Jia et al., these ester compounds significantly contributed to sensory evaluation, decreasing in concentration during the aging process (Jia et al., 2022). In addition, they play a significant role in the flavor and taste of SFB. For instance, ethyl lactate enhanced the taste and significantly promoted the perceived aroma burst stage of SFB, prolonging the aftertaste (L. Chen et al., 2023) The storage also enhanced the variety of long-chain esters. This also included ethyl oleate and ethyl tetradecanoate as a type of age marker (Huang et al., 2024). These esters not only contributed to the complex and refined aroma profile of aged SFB, they also acted as distinctive indicators of the aging process, helping to characterize the particular age of the Baijiu. 2-hexanol, phenethyl alcohol, propionic acid, 3-methylbutanoic acid, and 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine were important compounds in the aging process of Baijiu. Alcohols are oxidized to aldehydes, and further oxidized to acids. As a result, the levels of aldehydes and acids increased, reaching a new equilibrium after storage.

In the IMS dataset, A65 played the most significant role in the classification results of the fusion-data model. However, due to the qualitative limitations of the IMS database, this compound was not successfully identified. Nevertheless, this signal peak has a significant impact on the discrimination of the age of SFB, as well as A18 and A66. Notably, the IMS dataset featured 13 important peaks. For some compounds that could be preliminarily identified, such as A12, it was identified as 2-hexanol through IMS database, their identification information was included in Table S6. Interestingly, this compound was also identified in the GC–MS, 2-hexanol (GC–MS) and A12 had the same importance coefficient, despite originating from different analytical methods (GC–MS, content, and GC-IMS, peak volume). They conveyed different meanings in their own dataset, but their roles in the quality evaluation of SFB was consistent. This may indicate that 2-hexanol might serve as a key marker. It is important to emphasize that, despite certain limitations in qualitative identification, the advantage of IMS lies in its efficiency and ease of operation as a rapid analytical method. It has played a prominent role in food quality identification (Parastar et al., 2024).

Surprisingly, the bitterness detected by the E-tongue had a significant impact on model classification. Although this is not an accurate qualitative method on specific compounds, it has played a broad role in flavor discrimination and quality identification, not only in SFB but also in other types of Baijiu (Aliya Liu et al., 2024) and wine. In most types of Baijiu, bitterness is considered an off-flavor due to its high intensity, but a moderate amount of bitterness can enrich the taste of Baijiu. Bitterness exists in all flavor types of Baijiu, including SFB, it's particularly given by non-volatile or high-boiling-point compounds at extremely low concentrations. There have been relevant reports tracking this typical taste characteristic (retronasal burnt flavor) in SFB (Zhao et al., 2019). The pyrazine compounds identified in this study are high-boiling-point compounds, including 2,5-dimethylpyrazine and 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine, which have also been reported as having burnt, nutty, and roasted smells (Yan, Chen, He, Nie, & Xu, 2020). Besides, the bitter peptides in SFB also more and gained attention, still needed to be explored (Luo, Kong, Xue, Wang, & Xia, 2020).

The sensor R(4) of E-nose was also screened out, which responds to hydrogen. However, this data was an overall reflection of the entire SFB sample. Relying solely on this information makes it difficult to obtain specific molecular discrimination information. This is limited by the working mode of E-nose analysis. Currently, there is also a method of combining E-nose with gas chromatography to further enhance the recognition ability of chemical signals (Y. Niu et al., 2018). Despite only one characteristic variable being screened out from the E-nose data, the correlation of the E-nose data can still be observed.

Correlation analysis of important features

The analysis of flavor characteristics and instrumental data suggested potential correlations between these datasets. Correlation analysis was further applied.

As shown in Fig. 5, a correlation network was constructed between intelligent sensory analysis technologies (E-nose and E-tongue) and GC–MS, GC-IMS. For taste and aroma characteristics, the astringency data of E-tongue showed a positive correlation with seven (12 total) GC–MS compounds, with ethyl lactate (B67) having a correlation coefficient of 0.65. Ten (13 total) IMS indicators also showed a positive correlation, with A40 having a correlation coefficient of 0.67. The remaining compounds showed weak negative correlations. In contrast, bitterness data were negatively correlated with the features mentioned above, including ethyl lactate and A40, with 12 (25 total) showing negative correlations.

Correlation network analysis of important features.

Interestingly, the E-nose R4 data showed a positive correlation with the E-tongue data and positive correlations with most of the GC–MS and GC-IMS data. R4 is considered a sensor that responds to signals from hydrogen, and the reason for this characteristic remains to be further explored.

Conclusions

In summary, this study established a flavor-integrated analytical techniques combined with machine learning models, achieving effective discrimination of different groups of aged sauce-flavor Baijiu (SFB). This method employed GC–MS, GC-IMS, E-nose and E-tongue successfully achieved the precise quantification of 69 volatile compounds, identification of 67 characteristic peaks, the capture of 10 signals and the determination of 9 flavor characteristics, respectively. Among them, total volatile compounds declined with longer aging. This drop was driven mainly by esters. Response value of sourness was the highest in E-tongue, followed by astringency. Integrated data fusion and neural network strategy were further applied, SFB according to 6 aged groups were successfully discriminated. Compared to single source data model, the data fusion model showed better performance in SFB analysis, and the incorporation of SMOTE method further enhanced model accuracy to 0.96 markedly. In addition, a total of 28 important features were screened out, such as furfural, 2-hexanol (GC–MS), Area 65 (GC-IMS), and bitterness (E-tongue). The taste characteristics are correlated with the volatile compounds. The astringency of E-tongue showed a positive correlation with ethyl lactate and Area 40. This work provides a new perspective and a practical basis for the sauce-flavor Baijiu age discrimination. Moving forward, future studies would further explore the flavor transformations and sensory dynamics during baijiu aging, particularly focusing on those important compound indicators identified.

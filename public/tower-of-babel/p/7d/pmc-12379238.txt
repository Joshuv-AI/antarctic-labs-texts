A national soil organic carbon density dataset (2010–2024) in China
Authors: Zhongxing Chen, Lingkun Chen, Rui Lu, Zihang Lou, Furong Zhou, Yecheng Jin, Jie Xue, Hancheng Guo, Zheng Wang, Yanyu Wang, Feng Liu, Xiaodong Song, Ganlin Zhang, Yang Su, Su Ye, Zhou Shi, Songchao Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSoil organic carbon (SOC) is a vital component of terrestrial ecosystems and a key indicator of soil quality, agricultural potential, and climate regulation. Despite growing interest in SOC distribution across China, high-resolution and standardized datasets remain scarce. Here, we present a national soil organic carbon density (SOCD) dataset containing 23,103 samples from 7,852 soil profiles collected between 2010 and 2024. The dataset covers soil depths down to 800 cm and includes bulk density (BD) values—both measured and imputed using an ensemble modelling approach. Records are georeferenced and annotated with land cover type, climate zone, and depth information, offering wide spatial and environmental coverage across diverse Chinese landscapes, including the previously underrepresented Qinghai–Tibet Plateau. Data sources include national soil surveys, open-access repositories, and peer-reviewed literature, with all variables standardized to ensure usability.Subject terms: Carbon cycle, Carbon cycle

Background & Summary

Soil organic carbon (SOC) is a critical component of terrestrial ecosystems, serving as a key indicator of soil health and ecosystem function. It contributes to multiple ecological and agricultural services, including nutrient cycling, erosion control, biodiversity maintenance, and carbon sequestration1,2. Globally, soils constitute the largest reservoir of terrestrial carbon, containing an estimated 2500 petagrams (Pg) of carbon to a depth of one meter, of which approximately 1500 Pg is in the form of SOC3,4. Even small changes in SOC stocks can significantly impact atmospheric CO₂ concentrations, with consequences for greenhouse gas emissions and climate dynamics5,6. Understanding SOC dynamics is therefore essential for sustainable land use and is closely aligned with China’s national goals of peaking carbon emissions by 2030 and achieving carbon neutrality by 20607.

Beyond its climate relevance, SOC sequestration is vital for agricultural systems as it enhances soil fertility and long-term productivity8–10. Soils are estimated to account for up to 25% of the total mitigation potential of natural climate solutions1. Efforts to increase SOC not only improve soil health but also support emerging frameworks for carbon accounting and trading9,11. Given the increasing policy and scientific interest in SOC, comprehensive data on SOC stocks and their spatial distribution is critical for both environmental monitoring and climate policy implementation12,13.

Despite growing research on SOC estimation at regional, national, and global scales14, accurately quantifying SOC in subsoils remains challenging. These difficulties are primarily due to limited observations, the absence of long-term monitoring, and pronounced depth-dependent variation in SOC content14. In China, such challenges are further exacerbated by the scarcity and uneven distribution of SOC measurements and the limited availability of robust estimation methods15–17. The country’s complex topography and pedo-climatic heterogeneity make uniform assessment difficult, and many existing datasets rely on broad-scale resource inventories that lack resolution18,19. Particularly in western regions such as the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, SOC observations are sparse7, complicating national assessments of soil carbon sinks.

A significant constraint in SOC stock estimation is the widespread lack of bulk density (BD) measurements, which are essential for converting SOC concentrations to density estimates15,20,21. In many soil types, traditional BD sampling is complicated by the presence of roots and gravel. Moreover, large-scale BD data collection is time-consuming and often infeasible22,23, resulting in incomplete BD records in most national soil databases and introducing uncertainty in SOC density estimates24.

To address these challenges, this study introduces an ensemble modelling (EM) framework to improve BD prediction accuracy and extend the coverage of SOC density (SOCD) across China. By integrating multiple machine learning models15, the EM approach increases predictive performance and enables the generation of a comprehensive SOCD dataset for the period 2010–2024. The dataset includes 23,103 samples from 7,852 soil profiles, with estimates extending to a depth of 800 cm.

Importantly, the dataset incorporates newly available observations from the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, significantly improving spatial completeness in a previously underrepresented region. The resulting SOCD dataset offers new insights into the vertical and spatial distribution of SOC across diverse soil and climate zones in China. It provides a valuable foundation for Earth system modelling and supports data-driven strategies in climate mitigation, carbon accounting, and sustainable agricultural management.

Methods

Study area

The study covers the entire territory of China, a country with highly diverse topographic features including mountains, plateaus, basins, plains, and deserts25,26. Geographically, China extends from 4°N to 53°N in latitude and from 73°E to 135°E in longitude, encompassing substantial climatic variation across regions. These climatic gradients result in diverse patterns of SOC accumulation, decomposition, and spatial distribution15. The country also contains a rich variety of soil types including red soil, brown soil, black soil, and chestnut calcium soil, each with distinct physical and chemical properties and spatial patterns7.

Soil data

A total of 7,852 soil profiles containing 23,103 samples collected across China between 2010 and 2024 were used to develop the national soil organic carbon density (SOCD) dataset (Fig. 1). The core dataset was obtained from the National Soil Series Survey and the Compilation of Soil Series of China project conducted during the 2010s7. Additional SOCD data measured between 2010 and 2015 were integrated from Xu et al.27, along with publicly available records from the Spatiotemporal Tripolar Environmental Big Data Platform (https://poles.tpdc.ac.cn/zh-hans) and the National Science and Technology Infrastructure database (https://www.nesdc.org.cn/). To ensure comprehensive coverage, we systematically compiled data from 82 previously published datasets identified through a literature review of the Web of Science Core Collection (https://www.webofscience.com/wos/)28–100. This review targeted studies published between 2015 and 2024 that contained relevant SOCD measurements for China. Only articles containing relevant keywords in the title and topic fields were considered. The keywords used were: TITLE: (China) AND TOPIC: (soil organic carbon OR soil organic matter OR soil organic carbon density). Notably, this study incorporates newly available SOC data from the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau (an area historically underrepresented in national datasets) significantly improving the spatial completeness and representativeness of the SOCD dataset. All data sources were publicly accessible and openly licensed.

Predictor variables

A comprehensive set of predictor variables was used to support the imputation of bulk density (BD) values, following established pedometric methodologies as described in previous studies101–103. These variables captured a wide range of soil-forming factors, including soil properties, topographic conditions, climatic influences, and vegetation characteristics, as summarized in Table 1. Soil-related predictors included pH, sand, silt, clay content, soil depth, and SOC concentration. All were extracted directly from the original literature. Where data gaps existed, missing values were supplemented using the National Soil Information Grids of China7. Elevation data were used to characterize topographic relief and were derived from the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) Digital Elevation Model with a spatial resolution of 90 meters104. Climatic variables included mean annual precipitation (MAP) and mean annual temperature (MAT), both obtained from the WorldClim Version 2 dataset at a spatial resolution of 1 kilometre. Potential evapotranspiration (PET) and the aridity index (AI) were sourced from the Global-PET database, which also provides gridded products at 1 kilometre resolution105. Vegetation and land surface characteristics were represented by the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), calculated as the annual mean from 2010 to 2023. This variable was generated using Landsat 5, 7, and 8 imageries on the Google Earth Engine platform, with standard pre-processing and temporal smoothing procedures applied. Land cover classification data were acquired from the China Land Cover Dataset (CLCD), which integrates observations from Landsat and the Chinese HJ-1 satellites at a spatial resolution of 30 meters106.

Model construction and performance evaluation

This study adopted an ensemble modelling (EM) strategy to enhance the accuracy of bulk density (BD) prediction across China by integrating multiple machine learning algorithms. The EM model was developed based on the Granger-Ramanathan framework107–109, which combines outputs from support vector machines (SVM), Cubist, random forest (RF), and gradient boosting machine (GBM) through a weighted linear regression approach. Each individual model was trained and evaluated using five-fold cross-validation to reduce the risk of overfitting and improve generalization performance15.

Previous research has shown that the EM approach consistently yields the highest predictive accuracy for BD estimation in China15. By leveraging the complementary strengths of different algorithms, the ensemble method reduces systematic biases and compensates for the limitations inherent in any single model. Based on its demonstrated performance, the EM model was selected as the primary tool for gap-filling missing BD values in this study.

Due to the large sample size, profile-based single random split is stable compared to k-fold cross-validation or repeated random split15. The randomly split on the profile level was used to divide the calibration (75%, 2,628 profiles with 10,609 samples) and validation (25%, 876 profiles with 3,561 samples) sets using the dataset from the National Soil Series Survey and the Compilation of Soil Series of China project (2009–2019) due to its high data quality and complete coverage of predictor variables. The root mean square error (RMSE), determination coefficient (R2) and Lin’s concordance correlation coefficient (LCCC) were used as the performance indicators on the validation set110.

The build-up of extended SOC stock datasets in China

The pedotransfer function (PTF) with the highest concordance correlation coefficient (LCCC), coefficient of determination (R²), and the lowest root mean square error (RMSE) was selected to impute missing bulk density (BD) values in the dataset. This gap-filling was applied to 1,896 soil profiles comprising 5,053 samples. SOCD values, expressed in kilograms per square meter (kg m−²), were computed for each soil layer using SOC content (g kg−1), BD (g cm−3), depth (cm), and the proportion of coarse fragments (CF, %) as inputs7.

Data Records

The dataset is available at Zenodo: China Soil Organic Carbon Density (SOCD) Dataset Version 9 (V9)111. This national-scale dataset was developed from 23,103 soil samples (7,852 profiles) across China using an ensemble modeling approach. It includes standardized information on soil properties (e.g., BD, SOC, pH, texture), geographic coordinates, layer depths, environmental covariates (elevation, climate, land cover), and metadata (e.g., methods, sampling years).

Version 8 (V8) of the dataset, which contains model training data and prediction results used to develop the ensemble pedotransfer functions, is also provided. While V8 is intended for reproducibility and method development, V9 represents the finalized, validated dataset ready for application and interpretation. Users should cite both the dataset and this article.

Technical Validation

Performance of ensemble modelling based PTFs

In this study, an EM approach was used to impute missing BD values due to its superior predictive performance. The model integrates outputs from four machine learning algorithms and demonstrated higher accuracy compared to traditional pedotransfer functions (PTFs) and standalone machine learning models15. The EM-based PTF achieved an R2 of 0.63, a RMSE of 0.13 g cm−3, and a LCCC of 0.78 for BD prediction (Fig. 2a). These results confirm the robustness of the EM model in estimating the spatial distribution of BD across diverse soil types in China.

The predictive accuracy of the EM model for SOCD was further evaluated. The results showed an R2 of 0.83, an LCCC of 0.93, and RMSE of 1.93 kg m−2, indicating strong agreement between observed and predicted values (Fig. 2b). These metrics support the reliability of the EM approach for nationwide SOCD estimation.

The extended SOCD dataset in China

The optimized EM-based PTF was applied to estimate missing BD values in a subset of 5,053 soil samples from 1,896 soil profiles. These were part of a larger dataset of 14,170 quality-controlled samples obtained from the National Soil Series Survey and the Compilation of Soil Series of China project. Using these predictions, SOCD values were computed for all available profiles, resulting in an expanded national dataset comprising 7,852 soil profiles and 23,103 samples.

In the extended dataset, the mean SOCD was 3.85 kg m−2, the median was 2.41 kg m−2, and the values ranged from 0.0018 to 434.37 kg m−2 (Fig. 3).

Usage Notes

This dataset provides one of the most spatially and vertically comprehensive assessments of soil organic carbon density in China to date. Its inclusion of deep soil layers up to 800 cm and improved representation of under-sampled regions such as the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau makes it a valuable resource for Earth system modelling, land degradation assessments, and climate-related carbon accounting. Although the present study focuses on plot-level data compilation and bulk density gap-filling, we acknowledge the importance of spatial uncertainty, particularly in regions with sparse sampling or extreme environmental conditions. While prediction uncertainty was not explicitly quantified in this work, future studies could build upon this dataset using geostatistical methods or ensemble-based uncertainty quantification to evaluate spatial variability in SOCD estimates.

Furthermore, the plot-level structure of our dataset enables its use as a training and validation resource for pixel-level SOCD prediction. By integrating this dataset with gridded environmental covariates or remote sensing products, researchers can generate high-resolution SOCD maps at the national scale. These pixel-based estimates would significantly enhance the spatial resolution and realism of soil carbon representations in Earth system models. We anticipate that the open-access dataset presented here will serve as a valuable foundation for such future applications.

Code availability

The complete source code for the ensemble modeling framework and bulk density prediction is publicly available at the following GitHub repository: https://github.com/miruo1203/Ensemble-modelling-China_SOCD/tree/new. The repository implements an advanced ensemble modeling framework that integrates four machine learning algorithms: Support Vector Machines (SVM), Cubist, Random Forest (RF), and Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), using the Granger-Ramanathan weighted regression approach. The ensemble model combines predictions from the individual algorithms through a linear weighted scheme, with the weights optimized by least squares regression on the validation dataset. This approach improves predictive performance and ensures model robustness. All models are trained using 5-fold cross-validation, with data split at the soil profile level to maintain spatial independence. The implementation includes: Modular scripts for each individual model (SVM.kt, Cubist.kt, RF.kt, GBM.kt); A script for ensemble integration (Ensemble modelling.kt); A comprehensive model evaluation function that calculates metrics including R2, Concordance, RMSE, RPD, and RPIQ; Optional feature selection via Forward Random Forest Selection (FRFS). The code requires the R packages kernlab, caret, dplyr, Cubist, and ranger. This framework provides a transparent, reproducible workflow for soil property modeling at national scales.

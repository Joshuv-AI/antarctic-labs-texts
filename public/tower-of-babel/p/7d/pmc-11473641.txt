Integrated machine learning and Mendelian randomization reveal PALMD as a prognostic biomarker for nonspecific orbital inflammation
Authors: Zixuan Wu, Xiaohua Liu, Kang Tan, Xiaolei Yao, Qinghua Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background: Nonspecific Orbital Inflammation (NSOI) remains a perplexing enigma among proliferative inflammatory disorders. Its etiology is idiopathic, characterized by distinctive and polymorphous lymphoid infiltration within the orbital region. Preliminary investigations suggest that PALMD localizes within the cytosol, potentially playing a crucial role in cellular processes, including plasma membrane dynamics and myogenic differentiation. The potential of PALMD as a biomarker for NSOI warrants meticulous exploration. Methods: PALMD was identified through the intersection analysis of common DEGs from datasets GSE58331 and GSE105149 from the GEO database, alongside immune-related gene lists from the ImmPort database, using Lasso regression and SVM-RFE analysis. GSEA and GSVA were conducted with gene sets co-expressed with PALMD. To further investigate the correlation between PALMD and immune-related biological processes, the CIBERSORT algorithm and ESTIMATE method were employed to evaluate immune microenvironment characteristics of each sample. The expression levels of PALMD were subsequently validated using GSE105149. Results: Among the 314 DEGs identified, several showed significant differences. Lasso and SVM-RFE algorithms pinpointed 15 hub genes. Functional analysis of PALMD emphasized its involvement in cell-cell adhesion, leukocyte migration, and leukocyte-mediated immunity. Enrichment analysis revealed that gene sets positively correlated with PALMD were enriched in immune-related pathways. Immune infiltration analysis indicated that resting dendritic cells, resting mast cells, activated NK cells, and plasma cells positively associate with PALMD expression. Conversely, naive B cells, activated dendritic cells, M0 and M1 macrophages, activated mast cells, activated CD4 memory T cells, and naive CD4 T cells showed a negative correlation with PALMD expression. PALMD demonstrated significant diagnostic potential in differentiating NSOI. Conclusions: This study identifies PALMD as a potential biomarker linked to NSOI, providing insights into its pathogenesis and offering new avenues for tracking disease progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74409-1.

Introduction

Nonspecific orbital inflammation (NSOI) emerges as a benign, non-infectious inflammatory entity, precisely localized within the orbital domain, and remains remarkably unbound by discernible systemic or localized etiological vectors. Encompassing between 6 and 16% of all orbital abnormalities and constituting 11% of neoplastic phenomena within the orbital ambit, NSOI exhibits a discernible predilection towards middle-aged demographics, with a conspicuous female predominance1,2. Despite its appreciable prevalence, the underlying pathophysiological mechanistic arcs propelling NSOI perpetuate as predominantly enigmatic. Scholarly narratives suggest potential interlinks with a spectrum of conditions, including but not limited to, Streptococcal pharyngitis, viral upper respiratory infections, and a myriad of autoimmune maladies—encompassing rheumatologic disorders, multifocal fibrosis, and Crohn’s disease2,3. The clinical tableau of NSOI unfurls heterogeneously, enshrouding a plethora of manifestations, such as dacryoadenitis and myositis, impacting singular or multiple extraocular muscles, amidst other atypical presentations4. Although systemic corticosteroids are ubiquitously hailed as the therapeutic zenith for presumptive NSOI diagnosis, their protracted utilization is frequently shadowed by a manifold of well-documented adverse repercussions5. Perturbingly, recurrence rates surpassing 50% have been documented, even amidst efficacious corticosteroid interventions6. Against this backdrop, elucidating the molecular pathways underlying NSOI is critical for advancing novel therapeutic strategies aimed at reducing recurrence and improving patient prognosis.

PALMD (palmdelphin) has emerged as a protein of significant interest, owing to its diverse roles in cellular function and disease progression. Recent research underscores its involvement in crucial cellular processes such as cytoskeletal organization, cell motility, and signal transduction. PALMD is also implicated in modulating the immune microenvironment, potentially affecting both innate and adaptive immune responses. In neoplastic diseases, PALMD expression and activity are linked to tumorigenic processes, including cancer cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. Its role in the immune microenvironment suggests that PALMD could be pivotal in determining the efficacy of immunotherapeutic strategies. The protein’s interactions with immune cells and cytokines offer potential avenues to enhance therapeutic responses in oncological settings. Recent genomic studies have identified a locus near the PALMD gene strongly associated with Calcific Aortic Valve Stenosis (CAVS)7. PALMD exhibits variable abundance across human tissues, with its precise biological functions remaining largely obscure. Evidence suggests that PALMD localizes in the cytosol, potentially influencing plasma membrane dynamics and myogenic differentiation8. Modulating PALMD expression or related pathways within the aortic valve may hold promise for CAVS treatment. In Uveal Melanoma (UM), low PALMD levels correlate with poor prognosis9. In vitro studies demonstrate that low PALMD expression promotes UM cell invasion and migration, whereas high PALMD expression inhibits these processes. These findings highlight the critical role of low PALMD expression in UM metastasis10. A comprehensive understanding of PALMD’s roles and regulatory networks, alongside other chemokines, is crucial for devising targeted therapies in retinal inflammatory diseases and beyond11. As research on PALMD advances, its dual functions in tumor biology and immune regulation present a compelling target for novel therapeutic interventions. Elucidating the precise mechanisms by which PALMD influences these pathways is essential for developing targeted treatments to improve outcomes for patients with various diseases.

The immune privilege of the eye, essential for preserving visual function, is primarily maintained by minimizing inflammatory responses that could jeopardize sensitive ocular tissues. However, disruption of this privilege—due to genetic, environmental, or infectious factors—can precipitate a variety of ocular pathologies, including uveitis, age-related macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy12. The ocular immune microenvironment is distinguished by a delicate equilibrium of immune cells and cytokines, collectively safeguarding the eye from pathogens while suppressing excessive inflammation. Central to this microenvironment are resident macrophages, microglia, and regulatory T cells, which orchestrate immune modulation and tissue homeostasis. Dysregulation of these components can lead to chronic inflammation and tissue damage, thereby accelerating disease progression. Moreover, the interplay between ocular and systemic immune responses is increasingly acknowledged. Systemic autoimmune diseases, such as multiple sclerosis and rheumatoid arthritis, often manifest in the eye, indicating a close connection between systemic immune dysregulation and ocular health. Similarly, systemic infections and inflammatory conditions can exacerbate ocular diseases, highlighting the immune system’s interconnectedness across different compartments. Understanding the mechanisms by which the ocular immune microenvironment influences both local and systemic diseases is critical for identifying novel therapeutic targets. Navigating the vast landscape of high-throughput data analysis with the sophisticated arsenal of bioinformatic tools has emerged as a pivotal approach to elucidate the functional networks interlinking genes across various disease models13. This strategy provides critical insights into molecular mechanisms. The comprehensive collection of high-throughput transcriptome sequencing data and detailed clinical annotations curated by the NSOI Initiative serves as a beacon, facilitating the exploration of transcriptional alterations and associated molecular pathways implicated in NSOI. These bioinformatics analyses yield profound insights, offering a multifaceted understanding of the pathophysiology and mechanisms underlying NSOI14,15. However, a significant gap remains in the research landscape—no prior study has employed bioinformatics to investigate the role of NSOI in its specific context. In addition, Non-coding RNAs, particularly miRNAs and lncRNAs, play crucial roles in gene expression regulation. miRNAs primarily function through post-transcriptional regulation by either promoting or inhibiting mRNA degradation and translation. In contrast, lncRNAs are involved in diverse regulatory functions, including chromatin remodeling, transcriptional activation, and interference mechanisms. Recent findings highlight the intricate interplay between miRNAs and lncRNAs, revealing competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks. This study aims to fill this gap by rigorously analyzing NSOI-related GEO datasets with a focused approach (Fig. 1).

Materials and methods

We used the approaches proposed by Zi-Xuan Wu, et al. 202316.

Transcriptional profiling data and determination of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

The mRNA expression datasets relevant to NSOI were obtained from the GEO. This study utilized datasets GSE58331 and GSE105149, both based on the GPL570-55999 platform (Table S1). In cases where multiple probes corresponded to a single gene, the arithmetic mean of these probe values was calculated to represent the gene’s final expression level. The Sva and Limma of R4.1.0 were then employed exclusively for multi-chips data rectification (batch normalize). Subsequent to the standardization of the datasets, batch effect normalization was executed employing the SVA package. The efficacy of batch effect rectification was gauged through PCA (Principal Component Analysis). Differential expression analyses between NSOI and control groups were conducted utilizing the Linear Models for Microarray Data (limma) package. Criteria for defining DEGs were set at an absolute log fold change (|log FC|) greater than 1 and an adjusted p-value less than 0.05, with the aim of isolating immune infiltration-associated genes in NSOI cases.

Predictive modeling and computational learning

In our endeavor to construct a predictive model with unparalleled precision and reliability, we adopted the glmnet package to implement Lasso regression, enhanced through rigorous cross-validation. This strategy effectively mitigated overfitting and heightened the model’s predictive accuracy across complex biological datasets. To extend our validation, we employed the advanced SVM-RFE algorithm via the e1071 package, meticulously engineering a robust machine learning model. Cross-validation was pivotal in scrutinizing the model’s error rates and precision, bolstering its robustness and dependability. Further enhancing our analysis, the Random Forest algorithm analysis was also used It generated numerous decision trees and integrated their outcomes, thereby reducing overfitting and augmenting the model’s generalization. A salient feature of this method—random selection of features and bootstrap sampling—enriched the diversity among decision trees, thereby elevating the overall model accuracy17,18. Then, the DEGs obtained before were calculated by different algorithms, and then intersected by VNN package to obtain the key genes, so as to construct the model. Utilizing the randomForest and ggplot2 packages, our focus on the analysis of DEGs, pinpointing pivotal genes for NSOI. In the final phase, we assessed the importance of these genes through an integrated approach that amalgamated insights from Lasso regression, RF, and SVM models. The genes identified through these comprehensive methodologies are now primed for further exploratory analysis. The AUC (area under the curve) value of 1.0 denotes an ideal diagnostic test, whereas an AUC close to 0.5 indicates a lack of discriminative power, equating to random chance. This metric is particularly valuable for assessing the diagnostic accuracy of medical tests and the predictive reliability of models at different thresholds. In our analysis, we utilized the R pROC package to integrate and evaluate the dataset combining NSOI outcomes with pivotal genes to assess their predictive accuracy. Additionally, the dataset GSE105149 was employed to validate these findings. Through the ROC curve, we established a robust methodological framework for evaluating the diagnostic performance of these biomarkers, thus enhancing our understanding of their potential utility in clinical settings.

Functional enrichment analysis

To elucidate the biological functions and signaling pathways involved in the differential expression landscape, we conducted GO and KEGG analyses. Using the R statistical environment, we explored how variations in PALMD expression influence BP, MF, and CC. Global gene-set enrichment analyses, including GSEA and GSVA, were employed to identify functionally coherent gene sets and signaling pathways differentially active in the studied samples. Enrichment scores and visual representations were generated to reveal dynamic activities and pathways across different risk stratifications. The R environment was used to investigate the impact of differential PALMD expression on BP, MF, CC, and related pathways.

Biomarker-immune infiltrate and miRNA-lncRNA network

Spearman’s rank correlation was employed to assess the relationship between diagnostic biomarkers and immune cell infiltration within the tissue microenvironment. Target gene information for the common miRNAs and lncRNAs was obtained from miRTarbase and PrognoScan databases. These databases include miRanda19, miRDB20, and TargetScan21. When the corresponding database matched the relevant miRNA, the score was marked as 1. It can be seen that when all three databases can be matched, it is 3 points. The miRNA was matched by spongeScan database22 to obtain the corresponding lncRNA data. An integrated regulatory network, highlighting the interplay between mRNAs, miRNAs, and lncRNAs along with their shared targets in NSOI, was constructed and visualized using Cytoscape software. In addition, to unravel the fundamental mechanisms underlying PALMD, we constructed a comprehensive Gene regulatory networks from GeneMANIA.

Establishing causality via Mendelian randomization

In our endeavor to ascertain the non-confounded relationship between genetic predispositions and NSOI incidence, a Mendelian randomization study was conducted, leveraging the TwoSampleMR package within R. This analysis aimed to explore the potential causal linkage between PALMD gene expression—designated as the exposure variable—and NSOI, identified as the outcome of interest. (1) IV Selection: We pinpointed STAT1 gene expressions closely linked to the exposure, employing a stringent significance cutoff of P < 5 × 10−8 to ensure the relevance of the chosen genetic instruments. (2) Ensuring Independence of IVs: To ascertain the independence of SNPs serving as risk factors, we utilized the PLINK clustering methodology to examine LD. SNPs were screened for LD, with those exhibiting an LD coefficient (r^2) greater than 0.001 or a physical proximity of under 10,000 kilobases being excluded. This step was crucial to uphold the independence of SNPs and mitigate the risk of pleiotropy that could confound causal inferences. (3) Statistical Robustness of IVs: The integrity of each instrumental variable was scrutinized through the calculation of the F-statistic (F = β^2/SE^2), where β represents the effect size of the allele, and SE is the standard error. Instrumental variables demonstrating an F-value less than 10 were disregarded to reduce the likelihood of bias introduced by latent confounders.

Results

DEG identification and construction of the model

Differential analyses were performed using the limma package of R4.31 software. With pheatmap package to build the heatmap23. We integrated datasets GSE58331 and GSE105149 and performed batch effect correction. PCA confirmed the successful segregation of patients into risk-specific cohorts (Fig. 2a-b). Among the 314 DEGs, several exhibited significant differences. Specifically, certain genes clustered within the treatment group (NLRC5, CXCR4, DENND1C, ARHGAP9, ADAM28, RHOH, IL7R, SPOCK2, LCK, CD3D, ITGAL, CD3G, etc.) while others were prevalent in the control group (MGST1, TGFBR3, ENPP6, NECAB1, TRHDE − AS1, NPR3, PGM1, TMEM100, IGFBP6, PLIN1, TIMP4, etc.) (Fig. 2c). Notably, some DEGs were significantly upregulated (CXCL9, TCIRG1, PIGR, IGHM, HLA-DQA1, PROM1, etc.), whereas others were markedly downregulated (LARP6, HLF, MGST1, ADH1B, C2orf40, PGM1, TGFBR3, etc.) (Fig. 2d, Table S2). We employed LASSO and Cox regression analyses to establish a gene signature (Fig. 2e-f). The SVM-RFE algorithm was utilized to build a machine learning model, demonstrating an accuracy of 0.894 and an error rate of 0.106 (Fig. 2g-h). Random forest analysis identified key genes such as SRPX, ITM2A, PGM1, and HLF (Fig. 2i-j). Attempts to integrate key genes from all three algorithms revealed that LASSO and SVM-RFE provided the most stable model constructions. Ultimately, we identified 15 hub genes (Fig. 2k, Table S3).

GSEA was employed to identify functional alterations among the DEGs of PALMD. In the high expression group of the GO analysis, functional enrichment was primarily observed in BP such as sensory perception of bitter taste, endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport, and cytoplasmic translation. Conversely, in the low expression group, functional enrichment was predominantly associated with adaptive immune response, leukocyte-mediated immunity, and lymphocyte-mediated immunity (Fig. 8a). In the KEGG analysis, the high expression group showed enrichment in pathways related to protein export, valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation, and ribosomes. In the GO analysis, the high expression group exhibited functional enrichment in biological processes such as regulation of CD40 signaling pathway and CD4 or CD8 positive alpha-beta T cell lineage commitment. In the KEGG analysis, the high expression group showed enrichment in pathways related to asthma, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, autoimmune thyroid disease, and hematopoietic cell lineage (Fig. 8d, Table S7).

Immune landscape characterization

The immunological environment plays a crucial role in the initiation and progression of NSOI. Notably, the risk-associated profiles revealed substantial differences in immune cell infiltration. Within the PALMD cohort, aDCs, APC co-inhibition, APC co-stimulation, B cells, CCR, and CD8 + T cells exhibited significant differences between the low and high-risk groups. However, Th2 cells and Type I IFN response did not show significant variance between the two groups (P > 0.05) (Fig. 9a). Within the immune cell landscape, naive B cells, resting CD4 memory cells, and resting dendritic cells were predominantly expressed in the treatment group. Conversely, monocytes, M0 macrophages, and activated mast cells were highly expressed in the control group (Fig. 9b). Additionally, we constructed an immune infiltration correlation matrix and heatmap to further elucidate these relationships (Fig. 9c-d). PCA successfully categorized patients based on immune profiles (Fig. 9e). A Lollipop plot was utilized to illustrate the expression patterns of correlation coefficients, highlighting resting mast cells, monocytes, M2 macrophages, activated NK cells, and regulatory T cells (Tregs) (Fig. 9f). Resting dendritic cells, resting mast cells, activated NK cells, and plasma cells were positively associated with PALMD, whereas naive B cells, activated dendritic cells, macrophages M0, macrophages M1, activated mast cells, activated CD4 memory T cells, and naive CD4 T cells were negatively associated with PALMD (Fig. 9g, Table S7).

Discussions

NSOI, a singular idiopathic ocular disorder, is delineated by unilateral, symptomatic orbital edema, notably devoid of precedent viral or systemic perturbations. Critical exacerbations in this condition may be driven by optic nerve dysfunction. Despite its clinical pertinence, the molecular orchestrations governing this condition substantially remain an enigma24. An accumulating corpus of evidence posits that modulations in gene expression are pivotal in the pathogenesis of NSOI. PALMD protein, a member of the paralemmin family, is implicated in regulating cell morphology and plasma membrane motility25. Furthermore, PALMD has been identified to play a specific role in morphological transformations during the myoblast differentiation process. Therefore, a nuanced exploration into the role of PALMD in NSOI not only promises to illuminate its molecular etiology but also pioneers innovative therapeutic pathways for this enigmatically understood condition. In the realm of NSOI, our comprehensive analytical approach unveiled 314 DEGs. Utilizing Lasso regression and SVM-RFE methodologies, we identified a specific set of DEGs intricately linked to NSOI. Through meticulous cross-validation, we pinpointed 15 hub genes, including RHOBTB3, GPR146, IGK, CCL4, PALMD, CORO2B, HLF, TNS1, IGSF10, PGM1, PLA2G16, ENPP6, MAP1B, IRF8, and AKR1C1. Among the hub genes, PALMD emerged as a particularly intriguing candidate due to its established roles in inflammation and immune responses. RHOBTB3 is involved in cellular trafficking and may influence retinal vascular diseases26. GPR146 and CCL4 are linked to immune modulation, potentially playing roles in inflammatory eye conditions such as uveitis. IGK is associated with immune responses, which are crucial in diseases like diabetic retinopathy27. PALMD and CORO2B have been suggested to impact tissue remodeling and structural integrity in ocular tissues, contributing to conditions like glaucoma. HLF and IRF8 are transcription factors implicated in immune regulation and may influence both inflammatory and degenerative eye diseases28. TNS1 and MAP1B are involved in cytoskeletal dynamics, relevant to retinal degeneration and glaucoma. IGSF10 and PGM1 have been linked to metabolic and developmental pathways that may affect retinal health. PLA2G16, ENPP6, and AKR1C1 are associated with lipid metabolism and may contribute to age-related macular degeneration and other lipid-related ocular pathologies29. These genes collectively represent a spectrum of molecular mechanisms relevant to ocular disease pathogenesis. The identification of these hub genes provides valuable insights for future research directions. This study lays the groundwork for further investigations into the pathogenesis of NSOI and the development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

In the early stages of Calcific Aortic Valve Disease (CAVD), inflammation is prominently marked by the activation of the leaflet endothelium, characterized by the upregulated expression of cell adhesion molecules, notably ICAM1 and VCAM130. These molecules facilitate the recruitment of inflammatory cells, such as monocytes and macrophages, into the valve tissue, subsequently catalyzing the secretion of proinflammatory mediators that drive the osteogenic differentiation and calcification of human valve interstitial cells (hVICs)31. The presence of leukocytes in pathological samples from CAVD patients has previously indicated an active inflammatory process in the initiation and progression of the disease. NF-κB, a critical transcription factor, plays a pivotal role across various cardiovascular pathologies11. Upon activation, NF-κB subunits, including p65, translocate into the nucleus, where they directly modulate gene transcription, governing cell proliferation, differentiation, and inflammation32. Recent studies have highlighted that silencing PALMD inhibits the NF-κB signaling pathway and reduces the expression of NF-κB target genes such as IL6, IL1β, ICAM1, and VCAM1 in hVICs. Inflammation has been shown to enhance the expression of cell adhesion molecules like ICAM1, facilitating the recruitment of inflammatory cells into valve tissue33. Wang demonstrated that suppression of PALMD expression in hVICs significantly reduced TNFα-induced monocyte adhesion to hVICs. Additionally, inhibition of NF-κB using the chemical inhibitor SC75741 nullified PALMD-induced osteogenic differentiation and inflammation in hVICs34. Understanding this regulatory architecture could provide crucial insights into modulating proinflammatory responses in both retinal and extra-retinal tissues. Our investigation supports the relevance of these DEGs, particularly PALMD, in the pathophysiology of NSOI. Data from the GSE105149 study suggest that a PALMD-associated trait could serve as a robust prognostic indicator. Despite these advancements, research elucidating the genomic alterations associated with PALMD remains conspicuously scarce, highlighting a gap that warrants further exploration.

In the multifaceted landscape of NSOI, emerging evidence challenges the traditional view that an amplified immune response is solely due to CD4 cell activity. Instead, a complex profile of preexisting T-regulatory cells, intertwined with both proinflammatory and regulatory elements such as cytokine imbalances, is now recognized as operative35. This intricate environment potentially predisposes individuals to a dysregulated immune reconstitution, creating a susceptible arena for opportunistic infections—whether extant, latent, or previously mitigated36. Afflictions such as tuberculosis, cytomegalovirus infections, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, Kaposi’s sarcoma, and various autoimmune disorders might either amplify or evade detection. Notably, cytomegalovirus retinitis is the predominant opportunistic infection associated with immunological recovery uveitis37,38. Emerging therapeutics focused on enhancing intracellular cAMP levels offer a promising avenue for mitigating chronic inflammation. Small-molecule PDE4 inhibitors, which prevent cAMP degradation, have demonstrated efficacy across a spectrum of inflammatory conditions, including inflammatory bowel disease, atopic dermatitis, and rheumatoid arthritis39,40. Building on our previous investigations, we meticulously examined the expression profile of PALMD within the immunological microenvironment. A lollipop plot was constructed to delineate the expression patterns of the correlation coefficients. Plasma cells, resting mast cells, activated NK cells, and resting dendritic cells emerged as the most correlated immune cells (Fig. 9f). Specifically, resting dendritic cells, resting mast cells, activated NK cells, and plasma cells were positively associated with PALMD expression. Conversely, naive B cells, activated dendritic cells, M0 macrophages, M1 macrophages, activated mast cells, activated CD4 memory T cells, and naive CD4 T cells exhibited a negative correlation with PALMD. This nuanced interplay between PALMD and various immunological cell types underscores the critical role of inflammation and immune responses in the pathophysiology of NSOI. These findings highlight potential avenues for targeted therapeutic interventions, emphasizing the importance of modulating the immune landscape in NSOI treatment strategies.

Navigating the scarcely charted interplay between biomarkers and NSOI, our exploration offers seminal insights into an embryonic yet burgeoning field. While extant literature deploys bioinformatics to elucidate relationships between immunization and ocular diseases, a discernible gap persists regarding the role of PALMD within the context of NSOI41,42. Emblematic studies, such as those by Liu et al., employed Weighted Gene Coexpression Network Analysis to identify hub genes in NSOI, while Hu et al. utilized advanced computational methodologies to uncover 11 principal genes in thyroid eye disease, including ATP6V1A, PTGES3, and PSMD12. Furthermore, Huang et al. combined exhaustive bioinformatics analyses with in vivo validations, identifying six pivotal genes in diabetic retinopathy, such as CD44 and CDC42. Our inquiry uniquely distinguishes itself by embracing a novel metabolic cellular framework to inform therapeutic strategies in NSOI, propelling the field forward through the application of innovative methodologies previously absent in research. This novel vantage point is substantiated by an extended dataset of PALMD sourced from GEO, endowing our investigation with a robust analytical substrate. Despite its theoretical and methodological contributions, our study is not without limitations. A substantive gap persists in understanding the underlying mechanistic pathways. While both in vivo and in vitro experimental paradigms offer tantalizing vistas for further research, these avenues remain to be thoroughly explored in the context of our investigation.

Conclusions

In navigating the complex matrix of oncological heterogeneity, this investigation incisively explores the pivotal role of PALMD within the context of Immune Inflammation, thus outlining its extensive prognostic implications. Employing advanced predictive modeling methodologies, the transcriptional architecture of PALMD is rigorously dissected, uncovering pronounced differential expression landscapes between NSOI and normal tissue specimens.

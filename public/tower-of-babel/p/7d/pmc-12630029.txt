Effects of Akkermansia muciniphila on growth, antioxidant status and jejunal transcriptome in LPS-stressed yellow-feathered broilers
Authors: Rongxia Zhu, Yanrong Jing, Jie Tang, Lijun Ou, Zecheng Zou, Caiyu Luo, Qien Qi, Xin Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study was to investigate the effect of Akkermansia muciniphila (AKK) on growth performance, serum antioxidant parameters and jejunal transcriptome in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stressed yellow-feathered broilers. A total of 80 male yellow-feathered broilers were randomly divided into four treatment groups: control group (Control); intraperitoneal injection of 0.5 mg LPS /kg BW (LPS); oral gavage of 1 mL (108 CFU/mL) AKK (AKK); and oral gavage of 1 mL (108 CFU/mL) AKK plus intraperitoneal injection 0.5 mg LPS /kg BW (AKKLPS). The study lasted for 21 days. The results showed that final body weight, average daily gain and feed intake were significantly lower in the LPS group than the other three groups (P<0.05). The weight and index of bursa of Fabricius of AKK group were significantly higher than the other three groups. The activity of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) was significantly lower in the AKKLPS group than in the LPS groups. Regarding gut morphology, jejunal villus height and crypt depth were not different within all groups, whereas the villus height to crypt depth ratio was significantly higher in the Control group. GO analysis showed that LPS upregulated functions related to cellular stress, while AKK upregulated functions associated with response to ketone. Compared to the LPS group, the AKKLPS group showed significant upregulated functions associated with oxidative stress defense and peroxisomal response. KEGG pathway analysis revealed that AKK group showed upregulated pathways related to immune signaling, intestinal mucosal immunity, and lipid metabolism in comparison with the control group. The AKKLPS group showed enhanced activation of pathways involved in innate immune recognition and intercellular communication compared to those challenged with LPS alone. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Akkermansia muciniphila can ameliorate the adverse effects of LPS stress in broilers, and this protective effect is likely mediated by its comprehensive regulation of the jejunal transcriptome, enhancing key immune and cellular defense pathways.Key words: Akkermansia muciniphila, Lipopolysaccharide, Broiler, Antioxidant, Jejunal transcriptome

Introduction

Oxidative stress occurs when external stress triggers excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the animal body. These ROS damage proteins and DNA, disrupt cellular structure integrity, and impair normal metabolic functions (Azad et al., 2010). When subjected to oxidative stress, poultry must redirect substantial energy from growth and production to fuel defense and repair mechanisms. This energy reallocation directly impairs performance and product quality (Sihvo et al., 2014), consequently increasing the overall cost of farming. In the case of oxidative stress, poultry activate the body's complex regulatory mechanisms to mobilize the large amount of energy used for its own growth or production needs to defend against oxidative damage, which will lead to an increase in the cost of farming, which may lead to a decrease in the performance of poultry production or product quality (Sihvo et al., 2014). In order to maintain the redox balance of the organism, the use of antioxidants is one of the important and effective ways to mitigate oxidative stress damage.

Akkermansia muciniphila (AKK) a next-generation probiotic, has been implicated in many studies showing that its presence is associated with human health (Cani et al., 2022). It is abundant in the gut microbiota of healthy individuals, and a reduction in its levels is believed to be linked to the development of certain diseases (Png et al., 2010). Compared to healthy populations, the abundance of AKK is decreased in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and the quantity of AKK in patients with acute appendicitis is inversely proportional to the severity of the condition (Png et al., 2010; Rajilic-Stojanovic et al., 2013). AKK supplementation has been shown to significantly improve food intake, body weight, organ indices and tissue damage in mice with colitis (Zhang et al., 2024). It may also improve serum biochemistry and inflammatory cytokines (Raftar et al., 2021).

While research on Akkermansia muciniphila (AKK) has predominantly focused on humans and mice, its effects on poultry remain largely unexplored. Therefore, this study utilized a lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced inflammation model in yellow-feathered broilers to investigate the effects of AKK on growth performance, immune organs, and serum antioxidant status. Furthermore, we conducted jejunal transcriptome sequencing to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying these effects, providing some preliminary results for its application in poultry industry.

Materials and methods

Cultivation of AKK

AKK (ATCC BAA-835) was anaerobically cultured in mucin-containing broth medium (1% porcine gastric mucin, 0.5% tryptone, 0.2% yeast extract, pH 6.8) at 37°C for 72 hours and harvested at late-log phase (OD600≈0.8-1.0). The concentration of cultured AKK was approximately 108 CFU/mL. The AKK culture was used directly for gavage to evaluate the overall effect of both bacterial cells and metabolites as a whole.

Animal experimental design

A total of 80 one-day-old, male yellow-feathered broilers, of similar initial body weight, were purchased from Guangzhou Muyuan Poultry Co., Ltd. and randomly divided into four groups: Control group (gavage of 1 mL PBS and intraperitoneal injection of an equal amount of saline); LPS group (gavage of 1 mL PBS and intraperitoneal injection of 0.5 mg LPS /kg BW); AKK (gavage of 1 mL 108 CFU/mL AKK and intraperitoneal injection of an equal amount of saline); AKKLPS group (gavage of 1 mL 108 CFU/mL AKK and intraperitoneal injection of 0.5 mg LPS /kg BW). Chicks were administered treatments via oral gavage every other day from day 7 to day 17. The LPS challenge was administered via intraperitoneal injection on days 17, 19, and 21. On day 17, the oral gavage was administered 2 hours prior to the intraperitoneal injection. Throughout the study, all chicks were housed under controlled environmental conditions with ad libitum access to feed and water. The temperature in the coop was maintained at 32-33°C during the first week, gradually decreased to 24°C by the end of the third week.

Sampling

The chicks were weighed on the first day and last day of the trial. The total feed provided and the feed remaining at the end of the trial were weighed to calculate the average daily feed intake (ADFI). On day 22, each group of chicks was weighed and slaughtered, and blood, liver, spleen, bursa of Fabricius, jejunal segments and jejunal mucosa were collected. Liver, spleen and bursa of Fabricius were weighed and organ indices were calculated. The blood samples were centrifuged at 3000r/min for 10min to collect serum. The serum and jejunal mucosa were stored at -80°C for subsequent analysis. Jejunal segments were harvested and immediately fixed in 10% formaldehyde solution at room temperature for further processing.

Determination of serum antioxidant indices and jejunal histomorphology

The serum was assayed for superoxide dismutase (SOD), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), and malondialdehyde (MDA) according to the kit from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute. The fixed jejunal specimens were processed for paraffin embedding following standard histological procedures. Briefly, the tissues were sequentially dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, cleared in xylene, infiltrated with and embedded in paraffin wax. The resulting paraffin blocks were then sectioned at 5 μm, mounted on glass slides, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for microscopic examination. Three full-thickness transverse sections were selected from each chicken. After that, each intestinal segment was observed under a microscope at 40 × magnification, and the ileal villus height and crypt depth were measured using the IMAGEEX image analysis software, which is integrated with the YLE-21DY microscopic imaging system. Three random fields of view were selected, and within each field, three complete, well-oriented villi and their adjacent crypts were measured. For each individual bird, the multiple measurements were then averaged to generate a single mean value for villus height and another for crypt depth. These final mean values were used for statistical analysis and for calculating the villus height/crypt depth (V/C) ratio.

RNA extraction and sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from the jejunal mucosa with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions, and then accurately examined for RNA integrity using an Agilent 2100 bioanalyzer. The mRNAs with polyA tails were enriched by Oligo magnetic beads, and then the obtained mRNAs were randomly interrupted with divalent cations in NEB Fragmentation buffer, and the libraries were constructed according to the NEB common library construction method (Parkhomchuk et al., 2009). The different libraries were subjected to Illumina sequencing to obtain raw data. The raw data were then filtered to obtain clean data, and the paired-end clean reads were compared to the reference genome using HISAT2 v2.0.5. The FPKM for each gene was then calculated based on the length of the gene and the number of reads mapped to that gene was calculated using feature Counts (1.5.0-p3). Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 software (version 1.20.0) on the two comparison combinations, and the resulting P-values were adjusted to control for false discovery rates using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. KEGG and GO enrichment analyses of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were performed using the cluster Profiler (3.8.1) software package.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan's multiple comparison. The following statistical model was used: Yij = μ + Ti + εij, where Yij is the measured response, μ = overall mean, Ti = fixed effect of treatment (i = 1 to 4) and εij = random experimental error. Significance was declared at P ≤ 0.05.

RESULTS

Growth performance and organ index

The results of mean weight and feed intake of broilers are shown in Table 1. The final weight, average daily gain and feed intake of the LPS group were significantly lower than the other three groups (P < 0.05). There was no significant difference in feed conversion ratio. In the measurements of immune organs, there was no significant difference in liver weights among the four groups, but the liver index was significantly higher in LPS. Spleen weight and index were significantly higher in AKKLPS and LPS groups than the other two groups. In addition, the weight and index of the bursa of Fabricius were significantly higher in AKK than the other three groups (P < 0.05) (Table 2).

Serum antioxidant indexes and jejunal histomorphology

To evaluate the effect of AKK on the antioxidant status of the broilers, the serum levels of SOD, T-AOC, GSH-Px, and MDA of broiler chickens were measured across all four treatment groups, and the results are shown in Table 3. There was no significant difference in GSH-Px, MDA among the four groups, while SOD was significantly higher in the AKKLPS group than in the Control and LPS groups. T-AOC was significantly lower in the AKKLPS group than in the LPS and AKK groups (P < 0.05). Although there were no significant differences in jejunal villus height or crypt depth among the four groups, the villus height to crypt depth ratio was significantly higher in the Control group compared to the other three treatment groups (P < 0.05) (Table 4).

Transcriptome profiling and analysis

A total of 751,367,632 raw reads were obtained from 16 samples. More than 99% of the bases had a mass value greater than 20 and more than 97% had a mass value greater than 30. The sequencing data was deemed high and met the criteria for subsequent sequencing (Table S1). Correlation analysis was conducted and the R2 values among the samples within each group were found to be above 0.8 (Fig. 1A). Principal component analysis (PCA) based on the full gene expression profiles revealed limited separation among the groups, with the first two principal components (PC1: 35.82%, PC2: 8.51%) collectively accounting for 44.33% of the total variance. No significant clustering of the four experimental groups was observed in the ordination plot (Figure 1B).

Discussion

AKK is associated with a healthy metabolic profile, improved body composition, and the inhibition of inflammation (Grajeda-Iglesias et al., 2021). LPS has a negative effect on broiler growth performance (Zhang et al., 2023). In current study, final body weight, feed intake, and average weight gain were significantly higher in AKKLPS group compared to LPS group. This is consistent with the findings of (Zhang et al., 2024). The development of immune organs affects the immunity of the body (Zhu et al., 2023). Bursa of Fabricius is the main lymphoid organ and an increase in its weight indicates improved immune function (Ifrah et al., 2017; Zong et al., 2017). It was reported that LPS significantly increased liver and spleen organ index, and decreased the organ index of the bursa of Fabricius (Bi et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022). While AKK supplementation successfully alleviated the growth depression induced by LPS, we did not observe a similar mitigating effect on the LPS-induced changes in relative organ weights, such as the increase in spleen and liver indices. This contrasts with some rodent studies (Zhang et al., 2024) and may be attributed to several factors. The immunological response to both LPS and probiotics is highly species-specific, and the dose of AKK used (10⁸ CFU/mL), while effective for gut-level benefits that support growth, may have been insufficient to systemically counteract the potent inflammatory response in avian organs.

Total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) reflects the cumulative effect of antioxidant substances in the body. The higher its activity within a certain range, the higher the antioxidant capacity of the body (Cecerska-Heryc et al., 2021). Superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) are regarded as the first line of the antioxidant enzyme defense system against oxidative stress (Cecerska-Heryc et al., 2021). Malondialdehyde (MDA) content reflects the degree of oxidative damage and overproduction leads to cytotoxicity (Celi, 2011). The SOD activity and T-AOC in the AKK group were relatively higher than those in the Control group, indicating that AKK could improve the antioxidant capacity of broilers to a certain extent. However, SOD activity was higher in the AKKLPS group than in the LPS group. An intriguing finding was that the total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) in the AKKLPS group was significantly lower than in the LPS group, despite having the highest SOD activity. This seemingly contradictory result likely reflects a complex reconfiguration of the antioxidant system rather than an exacerbation of overall oxidative stress. We speculate that while AKK induced key enzymatic defenses like SOD, the acute LPS challenge led to a significant depletion of the non-enzymatic antioxidant pool such as glutathione and vitamins. Therefore, the lower T-AOC may represent the immediate “cost” of neutralizing the oxidative burst via these non-enzymatic scavengers, even as the enzymatic system was being effectively upregulated. This represents a compensatory, temporarily unbalanced, antioxidant response.

It has been shown that AKK could increase villus height, maintain the morphological integrity of intestinal epithelial microvilli, and alleviate intestinal mucosal damage by enhancing the proliferation of intestinal stem cells (Wang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2020). Interestingly, neither the villus height nor the crypt depth of the jejunum was significantly different from the other groups, whereas the control group had the highest villus height to crypt depth ratio. One potential factor is the dosage and strain specificity. AKK (strain ATCC BAA-835) at a higher dose (10⁹ CFU/mL) showed limited therapeutic effects on colitis, suggesting that the benefits of this particular strain may be highly context-dependent (Liu et al., 2021). More importantly, the lower V/C ratio should not necessarily be interpreted as a pathological detriment, but rather as a potential indicator of enhanced epithelial dynamism stimulated by AKK’s unique metabolic function. Research indicates that AKK degrades mucin in intestinal mucus, and mucin deficiency leads to a compromised mucus barrier, resulting in crypt hyperplasia (Derrien et al., 2011; Van der Sluis et al., 2019). Consequently, a deeper crypt would mathematically result in a lower V/C ratio. This finding may not reflect mucosal damage, but rather a heightened state of epithelial readiness and renewal.

GO enrichment analyses showed that the LPS group was significantly upregulated compared to the Control group in the expression of negative regulation of communication, calcium channel activity, small GTP-binding enzymes and cell membrane as well as organelle membrane. The level of oxidative stress causes excessive synthesis of misfolded proteins, leading to disruption of vesicular transport, formation of endoplasmic reticulum stress, and abnormalities in the structure of the Golgi apparatus and organelle membranes (Tang et al., 2022). Under conditions of endoplasmic reticulum overproduction, the PERK gene is activated by homodimeric autophosphorylation, followed by translational expression of eIF2α, phosphorylation, and activation of ATF4 leading to transcriptional activation of the CHOP gene, which ultimately leads to the inhibition of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 and promotion of the expression of the pro-apoptotic protein Bax, which is the excessive apoptosis induced by endoplasmic reticulum stress, resulting in intestinal damage (Darling et al., 2017; Lei et al., 2017). These results indicate that LPS in this study caused disruption of vesicle synthesis and transport in broiler intestinal cells, and structural changes in some cell membranes and organelle membranes, which will probably lead to apoptosis. Ketone may act as immunomodulators in humans and animals, attenuating pathological inflammation through a variety of strategies (Qi et al., 2022). One of the key ketone, β-hydroxybutyrate, has been found to activate HCAR2 and inhibit the activation of the NF-κB signalling pathway, thereby reducing the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Fu et al., 2015). Some intestinal epithelial cells have been shown to express mHMGCS2 in large numbers, and intestinal epithelial cells also appear to contribute to the local ketone pool in animals (Ang et al., 2020; Suresh et al., 2025). Butyrate from polysaccharides fermented by some anaerobic bacteria is readily absorbed by colonic cells or transported to the liver for ketogenesis (Puchalska and Crawford, 2017). From the results of AKK vs Control, it is indicated that AKK may enhance immunity by promoting the expression of ketone in intestinal epithelial cells. The peroxisome produces catalase and synthesizes plasmalogen. Catalase breaks down hydroperoxide and plasmalogen have antioxidant properties that protect cells from oxidative damage (Lismont et al., 2019; Takahashi et al., 2019). And in this experiment, the gene that regulates the peroxisome is TMEM173, which encodes STING, which has antiviral ability (Rodríguez-García et al., 2018). During peroxisome division, the ring-shaped peroxisome-dividing (POD) machinery undergoes constriction via the dynamin-like GTPase DLP1, the process that requires substantial GTP energy expenditure (Imoto et al., 2017). Peroxisomes have a half-life of about 48 hours and are degraded mainly by autophagy (Huybrechts et al., 2009). In the GO enrichment results, autophagosome assembly, peroxisome, guanyl nucleotide binding, guanyl ribonucleotide binding, and transferase activity were upregulated in the AKKLPS group compared to LPS. The results suggest that AKK may relieved LPS-induced oxidative stress by promoting the peroxisome.

In the results of the KEGG analysis, Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, and intestinal immune network for IgA production were all upregulated in the AKK group compared to the Control group. Cytokines are secreted soluble peptides that precisely regulate a wide range of cellular functions (Lopez et al., 2010). Among them is the IL10 receptor system, which inhibits dendritic cell and macrophage function and blocks the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Broughton et al., 2012). Intestinal immune network for IgA production is primarily related to the adaptive immune response of the gut to the intestinal microbiota (Bischoff et al., 2014). In addition it has been shown that supplementation with exogenous ketone esters increased salivary sIgA levels (McKay et al., 2019; Shaw et al., 2021). Combined with the results of GO analysis, it suggests that AKK may indirectly regulate IgA-associated immune pathways by promoting the production of ketone in intestinal epithelial cells, thus achieving the ability to enhance intestinal immunity. Research indicates that AKK generates short-chain fatty acids by degrading mucin, and these short-chain fatty acids can increase ketone body production (Trapecar et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2019). Compared with the LPS group, the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway was upregulated in the AKKLPS group, and TMEM173 was also an upregulated gene in this pathway. Cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway is essential in multiple exogenous DNA-induced innate immune signals (Liu et al., 2015). Combined with the GO results, the analysis illustrated that AKK could upregulated peroxisomes and activate cytoplasmic DNA sensing pathway via TMEM173 gene. The synergistic enhancement of antioxidant defense was achieved, thus alleviating LPS-induced oxidative stress and inflammatory damage.

Nevertheless, several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged to contextualize our findings. Our study used only a single strain of Akkermansia muciniphila (ATCC BAA-835). Given that the functional properties of probiotics can be highly strain-specific, the beneficial effects reported here may not be generalizable to all AKK strains. The transcriptomic findings were not independently validated by qPCR, which is a recommended practice for confirming gene expression data. Therefore, these molecular results should be interpreted as strong, hypothesis-generating evidence rather than definitive conclusions. Subsequent studies should aim to compare multiple AKK strains and validate our findings in more stress or disease models.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that oral gavage of Akkermansia muciniphila (ATCC BAA-835) effectively mitigates the growth depression in LPS-challenged yellow-feathered broilers and enhances the specific parameters, such as bursa of Fabricius development. A consistent increase in total antioxidant capacity was not observed though. Jejunal transcriptome analysis revealed that AKK modulates intestinal immunity, potentially through a gut microbiota-SCFA-ketone body axis that influences IgA-related pathways. Furthermore, the upregulation of genes associated with peroxisomes and the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway indicates that AKK may bolster cellular antioxidant defenses and innate immune responses to alleviate LPS-induced stress. These findings highlight the potential of Akkermansia muciniphila as a probiotic intervention to enhance gut health and resilience in poultry.

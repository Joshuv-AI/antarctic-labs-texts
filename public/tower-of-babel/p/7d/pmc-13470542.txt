Tuberculosis Contact Investigations in 2025
Authors: Sunmi Han, Jiae Shim, Seongju Choi, Jieun Kim, Seungeun Lee, 선미 한, 지애 심, 성주 최, 지은 김, 승은 이
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTuberculosis (TB) is an airborne infectious disease that requires early detection and contact management to prevent community transmission. Timely diagnosis and treatment of latent tuberculosis infection (LTBI) can reduce the risk of progression to active TB. This study analyzed the results of TB contact investigations conducted in 2025 to evaluate the status of contact management and provide baseline evidence for future TB control policies.MethodsHousehold and congregate setting contacts of patients with pulmonary TB registered in the Korea Tuberculosis Network System in 2025 were analyzed. The analysis included contacts, additional TB cases, and individuals diagnosed with LTBI, with findings stratified by age group and congregate setting type.ResultsA total of 100,124 contacts from 15,280 households and 3,617 congregate settings were investigated, resulting in the early detection of 233 additional TB cases (232.7/105 contacts). The rate of additional TB cases was highest among contacts aged ≥80 years (496.0/105 contacts). LTBI was diagnosed in 13,797 individuals (24.7%), and 6,933 of 12,524 eligible individuals (55.4%) initiated treatment. LTBI rates increased with age, whereas treatment initiation rates decreased with age.ConclusionsContacts aged ≥80 years had the highest rates of additional TB cases and LTBI but the lowest treatment initiation rate, highlighting the need for active screening and improved treatment uptake in older contacts. Although the LTBI treatment initiation rate improved compared with the previous year, a sustained management system to support treatment completion is required. The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency plans to strengthen age-tailored management for this age group, support LTBI treatment among contacts of patients with multidrug-resistant TB, and enhance follow-up among untreated individuals.Keywords: Tuberculosis, Latent tuberculosis infection, Contact investigation, Household contacts, Congregate setting

Key messages

① What is known previously?

The incidence rate of tuberculosis (TB) among household and congregate setting contacts was approximately 16.2 and 4.6 times higher, respectively, than that in the general population.

② What new information is presented?

In 2025, 233 additional TB cases were identified among contacts (232.7 per 105 contacts), representing an incidence rate approximately 7 times higher than that in the general population. Latent tuberculosis infection (LTBI) was diagnosed in 13,797 (24.7%) patients, of whom 6,933 (55.4%) initiated treatment.

③ What are implications?

Completion of LTBI treatment can help reduce the risk of progression to active TB by 90%. Older contacts had higher TB and LTBI rates but lower treatment initiation rates, highlighting the need for strengthened treatment uptake and follow-up of untreated individuals. Additionally, supporting LTBI treatment for contacts of patients with multidrug-resistant TB can strengthen a prevention-focused management system.

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is a Class 2 infectious disease under the “Infectious Disease Control and Prevention Act” and is a respiratory infectious disease transmitted by the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex expelled into the air through droplets generated by coughing, sneezing, etc. Even if infected with the M. tuberculosis complex, most people remain in a state of latent tuberculosis infection (LTBI), with no clinical symptoms and no transmission to others, and only about 5–10% are known to progress to active TB [1].

According to the World Health Organization, approximately 10.7 million TB patients and 1.23 million deaths from TB were reported worldwide in 2024, and the Republic of Korea (ROK) had the second-highest TB incidence rate and the third-highest TB-related mortality rate among member countries of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development [2]. In 2025, the TB incidence rate in the ROK was 33.5 per 100,000 population [3], and the TB incidence rate among contacts of TB patients was 232.7 per 100,000 contacts, which was approximately seven times higher than that of the general population.

A TB contact investigation is conducted to interrupt community transmission through the early detection of additional TB cases and to prevent the development of active TB through LTBI treatment. It is classified into household contact investigations and congregate setting contact investigations. The scope of a contact investigation is determined for contacts at high risk of infection or developing active TB, based on a comprehensive consideration of the infectivity of the TB patient, the duration of contact, the characteristics of the contact setting, and the characteristics of the contacts [1,4].

Contact investigation is being implemented as one of the key initiatives of the Third National Strategic Plan for TB Control in the ROK (2023–2027). This report analyzes characteristics by age group and congregate setting based on the results of the 2025 TB contact investigation and aims to provide baseline data for the development of future TB control policies.

Methods

1. Subject of Analysis

The test results of contacts of TB patients who were notified and reported to the Korea Tuberculosis Network System (KTB net) from January 1 to December 31, 2025, as recorded in the Korea Tuberculosis Network System as of February 1, 2026, were analyzed.

The chest X-ray and LTBI test results of 17,464 household contacts of 15,280 respiratory TB patients and 82,660 congregate setting contacts in 3,617 congregate settings were analyzed.

2. Tests and Diagnostic Criteria

A household contact investigation is conducted in the following cases: first, for family members and cohabitants who lived in the same household or had regular contact with a respiratory TB patient (disease codes: A15.00–A16.91, A19.0–A19.9) from 3 months before the initiation of TB treatment until 2 weeks after treatment initiation, and second, when a case of extrapulmonary TB occurs in a child aged ≤8 years.

A congregate setting contact investigation is conducted under the following circumstances: first, when a TB patient’s respiratory specimen test result is confirmed to be positive; second, when the index patient’s respiratory specimen test result is negative but a cavity is identified on chest X-ray; third, when two or more active TB cases occur within 6 months in a congregate setting (within the epidemiologically transmissible range), regardless of infectivity; and fourth, when TB occurs in a child under 5 years of age, including extrapulmonary TB.

Contacts are selected based on a comprehensive consideration of the duration of contact with the index patient, the characteristics of the contact setting, and the contact’s risk of TB infection, with priority given to those who had contact for 8 hours or more per day or a cumulative total of 40 hours or more during the estimated infection period. Selected contacts undergo TB testing and LTBI testing (tuberculin skin test and interferon-gamma release assay), and all contacts undergo follow-up chest X-ray 3 months after the initial test. For contacts diagnosed with LTBI, treatment to prevent the development of active TB is provided at the treating physician’s discretion after considering the index patient’s drug susceptibility test results, the age of the person to be treated, risk factors for hepatotoxicity, treatment adherence, and chest X-ray findings [1,4].

3. Analytical Methods

The analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel 2021 (Microsoft) to analyze contacts by age group and congregate setting, additional TB cases identified through contact investigation, individuals with LTBI, and the status of LTBI treatment.

Results

1. Results of the 2025 Tuberculosis Contact Investigation

In 2025, an investigation of 100,124 household and congregate setting contacts of TB patients was conducted. By age group, contacts aged 19–64 years accounted for the largest proportion at 54.4% (54,489), followed by those aged ≥80, 65–79, 5–18, and 0–4 years at 19.3% (19,356), 19.2% (19,270), 6.5% (6,465), and 0.5% (544), respectively (Table 1). Among all contacts, 98,980 received TB testing, for a TB testing rate of 98.9%. Additional TB cases were identified among household contacts (100 cases; 572.6 per 100,000 contacts) and congregate setting contacts (133 cases; 160.9 per 100,000 contacts), for a total of 233 additional TB cases (232.7 per 100,000 contacts). The incidence rate of additional TB cases per 100,000 contacts was the highest among those aged ≥80 years, at 496.0.

Among individuals with LTBI, 12,524 were eligible for treatment after excluding contacts of patients with multidrug-resistant or extensively drug-resistant TB, persons with a history of TB or LTBI, and persons with abnormal pre-treatment test results (liver function, platelet count, and kidney function). Of these, 6,933 individuals (55.4%) initiated treatment. This number represented an 8.0%p increase compared with the previous year (7,718 persons, 47.4%) and tended to decrease with increasing age. As of the data extraction date (February 1, 2026), the LTBI treatment completion rate was 59.6%; 10.1% discontinued treatment due to developing TB, adverse effects, or death, and 30.3% were still on treatment (Table 1).

2. Results of the 2025 Household Contact Investigation

In 2025, the number of household contacts was 17,464, representing a 7.6% decrease from the previous year (18,893). This finding reflected the decline in TB patients in the ROK and the increasing proportion of single-person households. By age group, those aged 19–64 years accounted for the largest proportion at 57.1% (9,980), followed by those aged 65–79 and 5–18 years at 24.2% (4,224) and 8.9% (1,553), respectively. Through TB testing, 100 additional TB cases were identified, and the incidence rate of additional TB cases was 572.6 per 100,000 household contacts, 17.1 times higher than that in the general population (33.5 per 100,000). The incidence rate of additional TB cases was the highest among contacts aged ≥80 years, at 1,517.2 per 100,000 contacts. The LTBI rate was 27.3% (4,296). By age group, the LTBI rate was the highest and the lowest among those aged 65–79 (38.5%, 1,442) and 5–18 years (10.3%, 154), respectively. The LTBI treatment initiation rate was 69.9% (2,809); it was the lowest and highest among those aged ≥80 (29.6%, 123) and 0–4 years (100.0%, 68), respectively (Table 2).

3. Results of the 2025 Congregate Setting Contact Investigation

In 2025, a total of 3,617 congregate setting contact investigations were conducted in accordance with the criteria for conducting such investigations, representing a 4.2% increase from the previous year (3,470). By type of congregate setting, workplaces accounted for the largest proportion at 38.2% (1,381 investigations), followed by social welfare facilities at 34.2% (1,238 investigations) and healthcare institutions at 16.0% (579 investigations) (Figure 1). The incidence rate of additional TB cases per 100,000 contacts was 204.2, 193.4, and 118.5 in social welfare facilities, healthcare institutions, and other facilities, respectively, while no additional TB cases were identified in military or police facilities.

Number of tuberculosis contact investigations conducted in congregate settings (2021–2025)

The number of congregate setting contacts was 82,660, representing a 5.1% (4,436) decrease from the previous year (87,096). By age group, contacts aged 19–64 years accounted for the largest proportion at 44,509 (53.8%), followed by those aged ≥80 and 65–79 years at 17,906 (21.7%) and 15,046 (18.2%), respectively. There were 133 additional TB cases (160.9 per 100,000 contacts). The TB incidence rate per 100,000 contacts was the highest among contacts aged ≥80 years, at 413.3, and no additional TB cases were identified among contacts aged 0–4 years.

The LTBI rate among congregate setting contacts was 23.7% (9,501), and the treatment initiation rate was 48.5% (4,124). By age group, the LTBI rate was the highest among those aged 65–79 years at 36.4% (2,403), whereas the treatment initiation rate was the lowest among those aged ≥80 years at 10.0% (267) (Table 2).

By type of congregate setting, the LTBI rate among contacts in social welfare facilities was 29.7% (5,626); however, the treatment initiation rate was the lowest at 35.8% (1,844), and the treatment discontinuation rate was the highest at 12.7% (234). In addition, the LTBI treatment initiation rate was lower in healthcare institutions with a high proportion of older patients (48.2%, 498) and in social welfare facilities (35.8%, 1,844) than in other congregate settings (Table 3).

Discussion

According to the results of the 2025 TB contact investigation, 233 additional TB cases (232.7 per 100,000 contacts) were detected through early case finding, and the LTBI rate was 24.7% (Figure 2). In particular, the number of additional TB cases among contacts aged ≥80 years (96) increased by 54.8% compared with the previous year (62), and the TB incidence rate (496.0 per 100,000 contacts) was also the highest. In addition, as the age of contacts increased, the LTBI rate increased, whereas the treatment initiation rate showed a decreasing trend. This finding may be attributed to the combined effects of decreased immune function, concomitant chronic diseases, and possible reactivation of past infection in older adults [5], suggesting the need for more proactive screening and treatment for elderly contacts.

Although the number of household contacts has continued to decrease due to factors such as the decline in respiratory TB patients in the ROK and the increase in single-person households, the incidence of additional TB cases among household contacts remained approximately 3.6 times higher than that among congregate setting contacts and approximately 17.1 times higher than that in the general population. This finding was consistent with a previous study [6] and suggests that prolonged, close exposure within households plays an important role in increasing the risk of TB transmission. Among congregate setting contacts, the number of TB cases and contact investigations increased due to an increase in investigations at long-term care facilities associated with a higher proportion of older patients, whereas the overall number of contacts appears to have decreased due to a decline in TB cases in middle and high schools.

The LTBI rate did not differ substantially between household contacts (27.3%) and congregate setting contacts (23.7%); however, the treatment initiation rates differed, at 69.9% and 48.5%, respectively. This finding may be attributed to factors such as greater awareness of the risk of within-household TB transmission among household contacts, resulting in higher treatment initiation, whereas congregate setting contacts tend to perceive the risk of transmission as low because of lower contact intensity. Beginning in 2025, to encourage treatment among congregate setting contacts, the Ministry of the Interior and Safety expanded the assessment population for the local government joint evaluation indicator, “LTBI treatment management rate among contacts of TB patients,” from household contacts to include congregate setting contacts. Consequently, the LTBI treatment initiation rate among all contacts increased by 8.0%p compared with the previous year (47.4%).

LTBI treatment is one of the key strategies for preventing active TB, and completion of LTBI treatment is known to exert a preventive effect of up to 90% compared with no treatment [7,8]. In particular, because a previous study [9] confirmed the preventive effect of LTBI treatment even in older adults, it is important to comprehensively consider the risks and benefits of treatment rather than limiting treatment solely on the basis of advanced age. Accordingly, the Korean guidelines for tuberculosis (5th edition) strengthened its recommendation to actively consider LTBI treatment for contacts aged ≥65 years. Furthermore, given that treatment discontinuation is more often attributable to failure of follow-up management and treatment non-adherence than to adverse drug effects [10], a continuous management system is needed to support patients from treatment initiation through treatment completion.

The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) supports treatment management by linking LTBI treatment prescription records with the National Health Insurance Service and is working to strengthen contact management by expanding support for chest computed tomography (CT) screening costs (from July 1, 2025) for the early detection of TB among household contacts at high risk of developing TB. In addition, levofloxacin treatment for LTBI among contacts of patients with multidrug-resistant TB will be incorporated into the 2026 National Tuberculosis Control Guidelines, and National Health Insurance reimbursement will apply from January 1, 2026, establishing a foundation for preventing the development of multidrug-resistant TB, which is difficult to treat and imposes a substantial social burden.

However, this study is limited in that it was conducted using data registered in KTB net as of the data extraction date; therefore, it could not include cases that were not registered or cases for which information was entered after the data extraction date. In addition, the exposure settings and underlying conditions of individual contacts could not be taken into account. Because this study was based on an analysis of data from a single time point, it could not evaluate long-term treatment persistence or trends in the development of TB. Therefore, future follow-up studies based on the establishment of a cohort of contacts with LTBI are needed.

The KDCA will review expanding support for screening costs needed for the early detection of TB cases and will strengthen treatment management for individuals with LTBI as well as follow-up observation of individuals with untreated LTBI.

결핵환자 접촉자가 잠복결핵감염 치료를 완료하는 경우 최대 90%의 발병 예방효과가 있다. 고령층은 추가 결핵환자 발생률과 잠복결핵감염률이 높았으나 치료 시작률은 낮아 잠복결핵감염 치료 참여 제고와 미치료자에 대한 추적 관리 강화가 필요하다. 또한 다제내성 결핵환자 접촉자의 잠복결핵감염 치료 지원을 통해 발병 이전 예방 중심의 관리 체계를 강화해 나갈 예정이다.

서 론

결핵은 「감염병의 예방 및 관리에 관한 법률」에 따른 제2급 감염병으로 기침, 재채기 등 비말을 통해 공기 중으로 배출되는 결핵균(Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex)에 의해 전파되는 호흡기 감염병이다. 결핵균에 감염되더라도 대부분은 임상 증상이 없고 타인에게 전파되지 않는 잠복결핵감염(latent tuberculosis infection, LTBI) 상태로 존재하며 이 중 약 5–10%만이 활동성 결핵으로 진행하는 것으로 알려져 있다[1].

세계보건기구에 따르면 2024년 전 세계 결핵환자는 약 1,070만 명, 결핵 사망자는 123만 명으로 보고되었고, 우리나라는 경제협력개발기구 회원국 중 결핵 발생률 2위, 사망률 3위를 기록하였다[2]. 2025년 국내 결핵 발생률은 인구 10만 명당 33.5명이었고[3], 2025년 결핵환자 접촉자의 결핵 발생률은 접촉자 10만 명당 232.7명으로 일반인구 대비 약 7배 높은 수준이었다.

결핵 접촉자 조사는 추가 결핵환자를 조기에 발견하여 지역사회 전파를 차단하고, LTBI 치료를 통해 향후 결핵 발병을 예방하기 위한 목적으로 수행되며, 가족 접촉자 조사와 집단시설 접촉자 조사로 구분된다. 접촉자 조사 범위는 결핵환자의 전염력, 접촉시간 및 공간의 특성, 접촉자의 특성 등을 종합적으로 고려하여 감염 위험이 높거나 발병 위험이 높은 접촉자를 대상으로 결정된다[1,4].

접촉자 조사는 「제3차 결핵관리종합계획(2023–2027)」의 주요 과제 중 하나로 추진되고 있다. 본 보고서는 2025년 결핵 접촉자 조사 결과를 바탕으로 연령별 및 집단시설별 특성을 분석하고, 향후 결핵 관리 정책 수립을 위한 기초 자료를 제공하고자 한다.

방 법

1. 분석 대상

2025년 1월 1일부터 12월 31일까지 결핵통합관리시스템(Korea Tuberculosis Network System, KTB net)에 신고 및 보고된 결핵환자의 접촉자를 대상으로 2026년 2월 1일 기준 KTB net에 입력된 접촉자의 검사 결과를 분석하였다.

호흡기 결핵환자 15,280명의 가족 접촉자 17,464명과 3,617개의 집단시설 접촉자 82,660명의 흉부 X선 및 LTBI 검사 결과를 분석하였다.

2. 검사 및 진단 기준

가족 접촉자 조사는 다음의 경우 시행한다. 첫째, 호흡기 결핵환자(질병코드: A15.00–A16.91, A19.0–A19.9)가 결핵 치료 시작 시점을 기준으로 3개월 이전부터 치료 시작 후 2주까지 동일 공간에서 생활하거나 주기적으로 접촉한 가족 및 동거인인 경우, 둘째, 만 8세 이하 소아 폐외결핵 환자가 발생한 경우 시행한다.

집단시설 접촉자 조사 실시 기준은 첫째, 결핵환자의 호흡기 검체 검사 결과 양성이 확인된 경우, 둘째, 지표환자의 호흡기 검체 검사 결과 음성이나 흉부 X선상 공동이 확인된 경우, 셋째, 집단시설(역학적으로 전염 가능한 범위) 내 전염력의 유무와 관계없이 6개월 이내 활동성 결핵환자가 2명 이상 발생한 경우, 넷째, 폐외결핵을 포함한 만 5세 미만 결핵환자가 발생한 경우 시행한다.

접촉자는 지표환자와의 접촉시간, 공간 특성, 접촉자의 감염 위험도 등을 종합적으로 고려하여 선정하며, 전염성 추정기간 동안 하루 8시간 이상 또는 누적 기준 40시간 이상 접촉한 경우를 우선적으로 포함한다. 선정된 접촉자를 대상으로 결핵 검사와 LTBI 검사(투베르쿨린 피부반응검사[tuberculin skin test] 및 인터페론감마 분비검사[interferon-gamma release assay])를 시행하고, 초회 검사일 기준 3개월 후 전체 접촉자 대상 흉부 X선 추적검사를 실시한다. LTBI로 진단된 접촉자에 대해서는 지표환자의 약제감수성 검사 결과, 치료 대상자의 연령, 간독성 위험인자, 치료 순응도 및 흉부 X선 검사 소견 등을 종합적으로 고려하여 진료 의사의 판단에 따라 활동성 결핵 발병 예방을 위한 치료를 시행한다[1,4].

3. 분석 방법

본 연구의 분석은 Microsoft Excel 2021 (Microsoft) 프로그램을 이용하여 연령별, 집단시설별에 따른 접촉자와 접촉자 조사를 통해 발견된 추가 결핵환자 그리고 잠복결핵감염자와 LTBI 치료 현황을 분석하였다.

2. 2025년 가족 접촉자 조사 결과

2025년 가족 접촉자는 국내 결핵환자 감소 및 1인 가구 비중 증가에 따라 17,464명으로 전년(18,893명) 대비 7.6% 감소하였다. 연령별로 19–64세가 57.1% (9,980명)로 가장 많았고, 65–79세 24.2% (4,224명), 5–18세 8.9% (1,553명) 순이었다. 결핵 검사를 통해 발견된 추가 결핵환자는 100명이며 가족 접촉자 10만 명당 발생률은 572.6명으로 일반인구(33.5명/10만 명) 대비 17.1배 높았다. 추가 결핵환자 발생률은 80세 이상이 접촉자 10만 명당 1,517.2명으로 가장 높았다. 잠복결핵감염률은 27.3% (4,296명)이며, 연령별로는 65–79세가 38.5% (1,442명)로 가장 높았고, 5–18세(10.3%, 154명)에서 가장 낮았다. LTBI 치료 시작률은 69.9% (2,809명)였고 80세 이상(29.6%, 123명)에서 가장 낮게 나타났고 0–4세가 100.0% (68명)로 가장 높았다(표 2).

3. 2025년 집단시설 접촉자 조사 결과

2025년에는 집단시설 접촉자 조사 실시 기준에 따라 3,617건의 조사를 시행하였고, 전년(3,470건) 대비 4.2% 증가한 수치였다. 집단시설별 조사 건수는 사업장이 38.2% (1,381건)로 가장 많았고, 사회복지시설 34.2% (1,238건), 의료기관 16.0% (579건) 순이었다(그림 1). 접촉자 10만 명당 추가 결핵환자 발생률은 사회복지시설 204.2명, 의료기관 193.4명, 그 밖의 시설 118.5명 등으로 나타났고, 군부대/경찰의 추가 결핵환자 발생은 없었다.

집단시설 접촉자는 82,660명으로 전년(87,096명) 대비 5.1% (4,436명) 감소하였다. 접촉자 연령별로는 19–64세가 44,509명(53.8%)으로 가장 많았고, 80세 이상 17,906명(21.7%), 65–79세 15,046명(18.2%) 순이었다. 추가 결핵환자는 133명(접촉자 10만 명당 160.9명)으로 접촉자 10만 명당 결핵 발생률은 80세 이상에서 413.3명으로 가장 높았고, 0–4세 접촉자 중 추가 결핵환자는 발견되지 않았다.

집단시설 접촉자의 잠복결핵감염률은 23.7% (9,501명), 치료 시작률은 48.5% (4,124명)였다. 연령별 잠복결핵감염률은 65–79세가 36.4% (2,403명)로 가장 높았고, 치료 시작률은 80세 이상이 10.0% (267명)로 가장 낮게 나타났다(표 2).

결 론

2025년 결핵 접촉자 조사 결과, 추가 결핵환자 233명(접촉자 10만 명당 232.7명)을 조기 발견하였고, 잠복결핵감염률은 24.7%였다(그림 2). 특히 80세 이상 접촉자의 추가 결핵환자 수(96명)는 전년(62명) 대비 54.8% 증가하였으며, 결핵 발생률(접촉자 10만 명당 496.0명) 또한 가장 높게 나타났다. 또한 접촉자 연령이 증가할수록 잠복결핵감염률은 증가한 반면, 치료 시작률은 감소하는 경향을 보였다. 이는 고령층에서 면역기능 저하, 만성질환 동반, 과거 감염의 재활성화 가능성 등이 복합적으로 작용한 결과로 해석할 수 있으며[5], 고령층 접촉자에 대한 보다 적극적인 검진과 치료의 필요성을 시사한다.

연도별 전체 접촉자 조사 결과(2021–2025년)

국내 호흡기 결핵환자 감소 및 1인 가구 증가 등의 영향으로 가족 접촉자 수는 지속적으로 감소하고 있으나, 가족 접촉자의 추가 결핵환자 발생률은 여전히 집단시설 접촉자보다 약 3.6배, 일반인구보다 약 17.1배 높은 수준이었다. 이는 이전 연구와 동일한 결과로[6], 가족 내 장시간 밀접한 노출 특성이 결핵 전파 위험 증가에 중요한 영향을 미치는 것으로 볼 수 있다. 집단시설 접촉자는 노인 환자 비중의 증가에 따른 노인요양시설 조사 건수 증가로 전체 발생 및 조사 건수가 증가한 반면, 중‧고등학교에서의 결핵환자 발생 감소에 따른 영향으로 전체 접촉자 규모는 감소한 것으로 보인다.

잠복결핵감염률은 가족 접촉자(27.3%)와 집단시설 접촉자(23.7%) 간 큰 차이를 보이지 않았으나, 치료 시작률은 각각 69.9%, 48.5%로 차이를 보였다. 이는 가족 접촉자의 경우 가족 내 전파 위험에 대한 인식이 높아 치료로 이어지는 경우가 높은 반면 집단시설 접촉자는 접촉 강도가 낮아 전파 위험이 낮다고 인식하는 경향 등이 영향을 주었을 것으로 추정된다. 2025년부터 집단시설 접촉자의 치료 독려를 위해 행정안전부가 지방자치단체의 국가 주요 시책 추진 실적을 평가하는 지자체 합동평가지표 중 하나인 ‘결핵환자 접촉자의 LTBI 치료관리율’의 평가대상을 가족 접촉자에서 집단시설 접촉자까지 확대하면서 전체 접촉자의 LTBI 치료 시작률은 전년(47.4%) 대비 8.0%p 향상되었다.

LTBI 치료는 활동성 결핵 예방의 핵심 전략 중 하나로, LTBI 치료를 완료할 경우 치료를 시행하지 않은 경우에 비해 최대 90%의 예방효과가 있는 것으로 알려져 있다[7,8]. 특히 이전 연구에서는 고령층에서도 LTBI 치료의 예방효과가 확인되고 있어[9], 고연령을 이유로 치료를 제한하기보다 치료에 따른 위험과 이득을 종합적으로 고려한 접근이 중요하다. 이에 따라 「결핵진료지침(제5판)」에서는 65세 이상 접촉자에 대해서도 적극적인 LTBI 치료를 고려하도록 권고를 강화하였다. 또한 실제 치료 중단 원인이 약제 부작용보다는 추적관리 실패와 치료 이탈에 의한 경우가 많다는 점을 고려할 때[10], 치료 시작뿐 아니라 치료 완료까지 연계할 수 있는 지속적인 관리 체계 마련이 필요하다.

질병관리청은 국민건강보험공단과의 LTBI 치료약제 처방 내역 연계를 통한 치료 관리를 지원하고 있으며, 결핵 발병 고위험인 가족 접촉자의 결핵 조기 발견을 위해 흉부 CT (computed tomography) 검진비 지원(2025.7.1.~)을 확대하는 등 접촉자 관리 강화를 위해 노력하고 있다. 또한 다제내성 결핵환자 접촉자의 LTBI에 대한 레보플록사신 치료를 「2026 국가결핵관리지침」에 반영하고 2026년 1월 1일부터 요양급여가 적용되며, 치료가 어렵고 사회적 부담이 큰 다제내성 결핵의 발병을 사전 예방할 수 있는 기반이 마련되었다.

다만 본 연구는 KTB net 등록자료를 기반으로 자료 추출일을 기준으로 수행되어 미등록 되었거나, 자료 추출일 이후 정보가 입력된 경우 반영하지 못한 제한점이 있다. 또한 접촉자 각각의 노출 환경, 기저질환 등은 반영하지 못하였다. 본 연구는 단일 시점 자료를 이용한 분석으로 장기적인 치료 지속성 및 결핵 발병 추이를 평가하지 못하였으므로 향후 LTBI 접촉자 코호트 구축을 통한 추적연구가 필요하다.

질병관리청은 결핵환자 조기 발견을 위해 필요한 검진비 지원 확대를 검토하고, 잠복결핵감염자의 치료 관리뿐만 아니라 LTBI 미치료자에 대한 추적관찰을 강화해 나갈 예정이다.

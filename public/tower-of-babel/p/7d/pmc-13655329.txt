A phase II basket trial of Dual Anti-CTLA-4 and Anti-PD-1 Blockade in Rare Tumors (DART) SWOG S1609: vaginal cancer sub cohort results
Authors: Young Kwang Chae, Josie Czeskleba, Sandip Pravin Patel, Alex Mentor, William Robinson, Nathaniel L. Jones, Murtuza Rampurwala, Aung Naing, Joseph M. ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe SWOG S1609 Dual Anti-CTLA-4 & Anti-PD-1 blockade in Rare Tumors (DART) trial is the first basket study to include a sub-cohort assessing ipilimumab and nivolumab in patients with primary vaginal cancers with differing histology.MethodsDART is a prospective, open-label, multicenter, multi-cohort phase II clinical trial of ipilimumab (1 mg/kg intravenously) 6 weekly plus nivolumab (240 mg intravenously) 2 weekly across multiple rare tumor cohorts, with the vagina cohort (any vaginal histology) reported here. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) per RECISTv1.1; progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), clinical benefit rate (CBR; overall response plus stable disease [SD] ≥6 months), and toxicity are secondary endpoints.ResultsSeven evaluable patients (median age, 60 years; performance status 0–1; no prior exposure to immunotherapy) were analyzed, of whom 3 had adenocarcinoma, 2 had squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), one had small-cell carcinoma and one had undifferentiated histology. The ORR was 29%, with 1 patient (14%) with undifferentiated histology achieving complete response (lasting 14.8 months) and 1 patient with SCC histology (14%) attaining a partial response (lasting 45.2 months). The CBR was 43%. The 6-month PFS rate was 43% and the median OS was 11.7 months. Five patients (71.4%) experienced an adverse event (AE) with 4 (57.1%) having grade 3–4 AE’s.ConclusionIpilimumab plus nivolumab showed efficacy (ORR was 29% and CBR of 43%) and durability (one patient with prolonged SD >6 months) in a sub cohort of patients with vaginal cancer of differing histology without new safety signals.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT02834013

INTRODUCTION

Vaginal cancer is an uncommon gynecologic malignancy, accounting for approximately 1%–2% of gynecologic cancers in the United States [1]. Despite its rarity, an estimated 8,650 new cases and 1,870 deaths from vaginal cancer were reported in 2024 [2]. Primary vaginal cancer is defined as disease without evidence of cervical or vulvar cancer, or a history of either within the preceding 5 years [3]. Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is the most common histologic subtype, with a mean age at diagnosis of approximately 60 years, although cases also occur in younger women [4].

Most vaginal cancers (approximately 75%) are associated with human papillomavirus (HPV) infection [5]. Accordingly, established risk factors largely overlap with those for HPV-related disease, including cigarette smoking, early age at first intercourse, multiple sexual partners, and immunosuppression [6]. HPV-independent risk factors include postmenopausal status, chronic use of vaginal pessaries, and prior in utero exposure to diethylstilbestrol, historically linked to clear cell vaginal adenocarcinoma [78].

Vaginal cancer demonstrates substantial histologic and molecular heterogeneity with is of important prognostic and predictive significance. SCC comprises approximately 90% of cases [2] and can be broadly categorized as HPV-associated or HPV-independent. HPV-independent SCCs are more commonly keratinizing and characterized by p53 positivity and p16 negativity [910]. Adenocarcinomas account for 8%–10% of cases [2], with additional rare histologies including sarcoma, melanoma, small cell carcinoma, lymphoma, and carcinoid tumors [11]. At the molecular level, HPV-associated tumors frequently harbor PIK3CA mutations, whereas HPV-independent tumors more commonly exhibit alterations in TP53, TERT, and NOTCH1 [12]. This heterogeneity contributes to differences in tumor behavior, recurrence, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance, and extends to the tumor microenvironment, influencing response to treatment [13].

The primary treatment for the majority of curative intent vaginal cancers is radiation, chemoradiation and or surgery [14]. In the setting of advanced recurrent or metastatic vaginal cancer, treatment guidelines have been drawn based on retrospective and comparative studies and modelled after those for cervical cancer due to the rarity of the cancer and inability to carry out phase III trials [14]. Platinum-based chemotherapy (cisplatin or carboplatin/paclitaxel with bevacizumab) is listed in the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines as a preferred, first-line treatment option for patients with recurrent/metastatic vaginal cancer (based on the GOG 240 trial) [141516]. Immunotherapy has significantly transformed the management of cervical cancer. The NCCN guidelines for cervical cancer includes 2 immunotherapy-based regimens as preferred, first-line therapy options for the treatment of PD-L1-positive recurrent or metastatic cancer, and that recommendation is extrapolated to vaginal cancer management as well [1417]. Pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab regimens is a preferred treatment for vaginal cancer based on a study in cervical cancer patients (KEYNOTE-826) [1418].

There is a compelling need for more effective and less toxic treatment alternatives. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are currently under investigation in basket trials targeting gynecologic or HPV-associated neoplasms. This study presents the first results of ipilimumab and nivolumab in vaginal cancers in a sub cohort of the SWOG S1609 Dual Anti-CTLA-4 & Anti-PD-1 blockade in Rare Tumors (DART) trial.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This trial was conducted at >1,000 sites in the United States under the supervision of the Early Therapeutics and Rare Cancer Committee of the SWOG Cancer Research Network/National Cancer Institute (NCI). The original protocol and any modifications underwent thorough review and approval by SWOG, the NCI Central Institutional Review Board (CIRB), as well as the regulatory committees of participating institutions. Full informed consent for each patient was received. Nivolumab and ipilimumab agents used in the trial were provided from the Cancer Therapy Evaluation Program (CTEP) of the NCI through the NCI CRADA agreement with Bristol Myers Squibb (BMS). The ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier for this trial is NCT02834013. This study involves human participants and was approved by NCI IRB S1609. Participants gave informed consent to participate in the study before taking part.

1. Rationale for included study population

The basket trial focused on patients with cancers classed as rare and occurring less than 6 times per 100,000 people [19]. The study’s principal investigators reviewed each patient’s tumor pathology, imaging and grade. The types of tumors were categorized based on the WHO Classification of Female Genital Tumors, 5th Edition [20]. A centralized pathology review was not conducted in this study. This report presents the results from patients with primary vaginal cancers only.

2. Inclusion criteria and patient selection

Patients who were eligible for participation in the trial had a diagnosis of primary vaginal cancer of any histology. These patients had shown disease progression after receiving at least one line of conventional systemic therapy and had no available treatment options related to increased overall survival (OS). Each patient was initially categorized by the site primary investigator and then reviewed by the study authors at the end of the study, based on pathology and imaging reports and clinical history. To be eligible for enrollment, patients needed to be at least 18 years of age, have a Zubrod performance status of 0–2, adequate hematologic, hepatic, thyroid, adrenal axis, and renal function, with absolute neutrophil count ≥1,000/mcL, platelets ≥75,000/mcL, hemoglobin ≥8 g/dL, creatinine clearance ≥50 mL/min, total bilirubin ≤2.0 × institutional upper limit of normal (IULN), aspartate aminotransferase and alanine aminotransferase ≤3.0 × IULN, thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) or free T4 serum ≤ IULN, and normal adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) ≤ IULN. Women of childbearing potential were required to have a negative serum pregnancy test, and patients were required to practice adequate birth control during protocol participation.

3. Treatment and monitoring

Patients were administered 240 mg of nivolumab via intravenous (IV) route (2 weekly) and 1 mg/kg of ipilimumab IV (6 weekly). Dose adjustments and therapy interruptions were allowed per the protocol for managing toxicities. Stopping rules in this clinical trial for removing patients from the protocol included reasons such as disease progression, worsening symptoms, treatment delay exceeding 56 days or if warranted for severe toxicities or by patients’ request. Patients who experience immune-related toxicity with inability to decrease prednisone ≤10 mg per os daily were taken off protocol treatment entirely. Ipilimumab was discontinued for certain grade 3 or higher immune toxicities such as myocarditis as outlined in detail in the protocol. Dose delay which results in treatment interruption of >56 days required ipilimumab discontinuation however nivolumab monotherapy could be continued at investigator discretion if there was evidence of clinical benefit.

Patients were evaluated with a history and physical, laboratory analyses (complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, TSH, free thyroxine, ACTH, cortisol, lipase), and toxicity assessment at least every 6 weeks at the beginning of each cycle. Imaging studies which could include computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging for disease assessment were performed at a local level pre-study, week 8, week 16, week 24, and then every 12 weeks until progression and thereafter imaging was carried out at the discretion of the treating physician. Following disease progression, patients were followed for 10 years or until death, whichever occurs first.

4. Statistical methods and outcomes

The primary endpoint of this phase II trial was to assess the objective response rate (ORR) (confirmed complete and partial responses [CR and PR, respectively]) using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) v1.1 criteria per investigator. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS) per RECISTv1.1, OS, clinical benefit rate (CBR; stable disease [SD] ≥6 months plus ORR]), ORR per immune-related RECIST (iRECIST), PFS per iRECIST, and toxicity assessment per CTCAEv5. PFS was measured from the first day of the treatment protocol until the time of progression or death from any cause, with patients known to be alive without progression censored at their last contact date. OS was measured from the protocol registration date to the date of death from any cause, with surviving patients censored at their last contact date. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method; medians were calculated using the Brookmeyer and Crowley method. Confidence intervals (CIs) for point estimates (such as the 6-month PFS) were calculated using the log-log transformation. All analyses were performed using R version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

1. Patient characteristics

The S1609 study registered 7 patients with metastatic vaginal cancer between dates May 8th, 2017 and May 25th, 2018. In the group of 7 evaluable patients, 3 had adenocarcinoma, 2 had SCC, one had small cell carcinoma and one had undifferentiated histology. The median age of patients was 60 years, ranging from 41 to 73 years. Five patients identified as Caucasian and 2 patients identified as black. The median number of prior lines of therapy was 2. No patients had prior exposure to immunotherapy. Full patient demographics are outlined in Tables 1 and 2.

2. Outcomes

Among the 7 patients, the ORR was 29% (95% CI=4%–71%), with 1 patient with undifferentiated histology (14%) achieving CR (lasting 14.8 months) and 1 patient with SCC histology (14%) attaining a PR (lasting 45.2 months) (Fig. 1). An additional 2 (29%) patients with adenocarcinoma histology had SD with 1 having SD >6 months (response lasting 8.0 months). The overall 6-month PFS rate was 43% (95% CI=18%–100%, Figs. 2 and 3) and the median OS was 11.7 months (95% CI=5.8–inf). 3. Toxicities

A high percentage of the cohort experienced some grade of adverse event (AE) that may be at least possibly related to treatment (Table S1). Overall, 5 patients (71.4%) experienced an AE with 4 (57.1%) having a grade 3–4 AE. In one patient (14.3%), toxicity (immune-related hepatitis) led to drug discontinuation. The most common AEs (across all grades and at least possibly related to treatment) were cough (42.9%), fever (42.9%) and diarrhea (42.9%). There were no deaths due to AEs.

DISCUSSION

Our study prospectively investigated combination ICI therapy in a sub cohort of patients with vaginal cancers. This study included a small sample size of 7 patients with vaginal cancer; however, this is a rare disease and, to our knowledge, this represents one of the largest immunotherapy sub cohort studies of vaginal cancer patients carried out to date. Importantly, this study provides prospective evidence of clinical activity with dual immune checkpoint blockade in vaginal cancer, a population that has historically been underrepresented in disease-specific immunotherapy trials. In this vaginal cancer specific cohort, which consisted of patients with differing histologies, the combination of ipilimumab and nivolumab demonstrated durable clinical activity in undifferentiated carcinoma, SCC, and adenocarcinoma histologies. Notably, the observation of durable responses across multiple histologies suggests broader activity than previously reported and represents a key new insight from this study.

While prior evidence supporting immunotherapy in vaginal cancer has largely been limited to case reports or small numbers of patients grouped with vulval cancer embedded within heterogeneous basket trials, our findings add prospective, vaginal cancer–specific data. This observed grouping of vaginal and vulval cancer patients is despite these cancers representing distinct disease entities. The single-arm, phase II CA209-538 trial evaluated the combination of nivolumab and ipilimumab in a heterogeneous cohort of 43 patients with advanced rare gynecological cancers [21]. It is stated that 5 patients in that study had vaginal or vulval cancer, and one patient with primary vaginal SCC achieved a CR lasting almost 2 years. In comparison, our study demonstrates that durable responses with dual ICI therapy are reproducible in a vaginal cancer–specific cohort and are not limited to SCC histology alone, suggesting potential activity across multiple vaginal cancer subtypes.

An ongoing phase I trial (NCT03452332) is further investigating dual blockade of anti-PD-L1 and anti–CTLA-4 with durvalumab and tremelimumab in combination with stereotactic body radiation therapy for recurrent and metastatic gynecological cancers, including vaginal cancer [22]. Among 18 patients enrolled to date, an overall response rate of 16% has been reported, including 2 CRs and one PR, although the primary tumor sites and histologies associated with these responses have not yet been reported.

Evidence supporting ICI monotherapy in vaginal cancer remains limited. Nivolumab activity was assessed in the single-arm phase I/II CheckMate 358 trial, which included patients with cervical (n=19) and vulvar/vaginal (n=5) SCC [23]. No objective responses were observed among the vaginal cancer patients in that study. However, isolated responses to monotherapy have been reported, including a case report describing a patient with stage IVB vaginal SCC who achieved a CR with pembrolizumab despite rapid disease progression on prior platinum-based chemotherapy [24]. In the context of these findings, our study adds important insight by demonstrating that responses may be more consistently observed with dual ICI therapy than has been reported with monotherapy, although definitive comparisons cannot be made.

As described above, both responses and non-responses to dual ICI therapy were observed in our cohort, underscoring the biological heterogeneity of vaginal cancer. While this mirrors observations from prior reports, the prospective nature of this study and its exclusive focus on vaginal cancer provide new evidence supporting the feasibility and potential benefit of dual immune checkpoint blockade in this rare malignancy. Consequently, the choice between dual ICI therapy and monotherapy should be guided by careful individualized risk–benefit assessment. There remains little published work characterizing the tumor microenvironment in vaginal cancer, and limited data are available to inform biomarker-driven patient selection.

In related disease contexts such as cervical cancer and head and neck SCC, higher levels of CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes have been associated with improved responses to immunotherapy, and HPV positivity has been linked to increased PD-L1 expression and higher immune cell infiltration [2526]. Whether similar immunologic features predict response in vaginal cancer remains unknown. Our findings highlight the need for future studies incorporating correlative biomarker analyses to better identify patients most likely to benefit from immunotherapy.

Our study has both strengths and weaknesses. To our knowledge, this is the largest immunotherapy clinical trial in a sub cohort of a vaginal cancer only population. We have successfully addressed an unmet need in immunotherapy trials exclusively for patients with vaginal cancers by rapidly enrolling at both academic and community sites. The vaginal cancer cohort sample size is small due to the rarity of the disease and did not allow for randomization. Central pathology review, PD-L1 status, and tumor mutational burden were not available for enrolled patients. The lack of stratification by HPV or TP53 status prevented the comparison of outcomes between different subgroups.

In summary, the combination of ipilimumab and nivolumab in the treatment of a small cohort of 7 patients with vaginal cancers demonstrated 29% ORR and 43% CBR. The durable responses observed (14.8 months confirmed CR, 45.2 months confirmed PR, 8 months CB (SD >6 months) highlights and adds to the existing literature indicating the potential of this treatment approach. Future prospective studies are necessary to fully explore the potential of dual anti-PD1 plus anti-CTLA4, as a treatment option for vaginal cancers.

External validation of a simple risk stratification for portal hypertension-related clinical deterioration in patients with ascites
Authors: Stefania Gioia, Manuela Merli, Simone Di Cola, Silvia Nardelli
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the recent study by Balcar et al.1, which proposed a simple risk stratification model to identify patients with clinically significant ascites who are at high risk of portal hypertension-related clinical deterioration and may benefit from early transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) placement. Risk-based identification of patients who may benefit from early TIPS remains an important clinical challenge.

To externally validate this model, we analysed a cohort of patients with cirrhosis followed at our centre. Patients younger than 75 years with clinically significant ascites (grade 2–3) and decompensated cirrhosis were included. The same exclusion criteria as in the original study were applied. The composite endpoint of clinical deterioration within 12 months included ascites recurrence or progression, variceal bleeding, liver transplantation, or death, representing a pragmatic adaptation of the composite endpoint used in the original study.

The analysed cohort included 55 patients, predominantly male (81%), with a mean age of 58.9 ± 9.1 years, a mean MELD score of 11.1 ± 4.0, and a mean serum sodium level of 137.9 ± 3.3 mmol/L. The mean Child–Pugh score was 7.9 ± 1.4, with most patients classified as Child–Pugh B (71%), while 15% were Child–Pugh A and 15% Child–Pugh C. Grade 3 ascites was present in 42 patients (71%), whereas 17 (29%) had grade 2 ascites; esophageal varices were present in 47% of patients.

In our cohort, the incidence of clinical deterioration within 12 months was 66.7% among patients with grade 3 ascites and serum sodium ≤135 mmol/L, compared with 13.3% among patients with grade 2 ascites, supporting the concept that patients with grade 3 ascites and hyponatraemia represent the subgroup at highest risk (Fig. 1). These findings are consistent with the biological rationale and observations reported in the original multicentre cohort by Balcar et al.1

Hyponatraemia in patients with cirrhosis reflects advanced circulatory dysfunction and severe portal hypertension and has consistently been associated with worse outcomes in patients with ascites.2 In this context, the combination of ascites severity, serum sodium, and liver function provides a pragmatic approach to identify patients at particularly high risk of further decompensation.

An additional aspect that deserves consideration is the potential risk of hepatic encephalopathy (HE), which was not specifically addressed in the proposed stratification model. Hyponatraemia and advanced liver dysfunction are well-recognized risk factors for HE and have also been associated with an increased risk of post-TIPS encephalopathy.[2], [3], [4] Moreover, several studies have highlighted the importance of patient selection and pre-procedural assessment to reduce the risk of HE after TIPS placement.4 In fact, in our cohort, the incidence of HE within 12 months increased across risk strata: 6.7% (1/15) in patients with grade 2 ascites; 13.3% (2/15) in those with grade 3 ascites, sodium >135 mmol/L and MELD ≥12; 21.1% (4/19) in those with grade 3 ascites, sodium >135 mmol/L and MELD <12; and 33.3% (2/6) in those with grade 3 ascites and sodium ≤135 mmol/L. Therefore, while the identification of patients at high risk of portal hypertension-related complications is clinically valuable, careful evaluation of the competing risk of HE may be important when considering early TIPS strategies in this population.

In conclusion, this external validation supports the clinical relevance and transportability of this simple risk stratification model. Importantly, these findings further support the rationale for prospective trials evaluating whether earlier TIPS placement in patients with grade 3 ascites and hyponatraemia can reduce portal hypertension-related complications and improve outcomes. Notably, an ongoing randomized clinical trial investigating early TIPS in patients with ascites meeting similar high-risk criteria may provide important prospective evidence regarding this strategy.5

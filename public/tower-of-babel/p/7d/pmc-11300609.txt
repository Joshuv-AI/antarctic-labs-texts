Seismic response investigation of prestressed anchor cable supporting rock slope with weak interlayer in Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, China
Authors: Yaojiang Fan, Guoxiang Yang, Hailin Ye, Yun Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Earthquake-induced rock landslides in the eastern mountains of the Tibetan Plateau, especially landslides with weak interlayers pose a significant threat to major construction projects. Prestressed anchor cable is one of the main reinforcement methods of rock slopes. This paper combines shaking table model tests and numerical simulation to study the reinforcement effect and dynamic response characteristics of prestressed anchor cables applied to rock slopes with weak interlayers under strong earthquakes. The research results show that prestressed anchor cables can effectively reinforce slopes with weak interlayers. A small cable inclination, a small spacing and a high prestress are recommended in the seismic reinforcement design of prestressed anchor cable. In addition, the characteristics of slope progressive damage and prestress loss under the earthquake are found by the shaking table test. The results have been applied in hazard prevention and control of rock slopes on the Chengdu-Lanzhou Railway at the eastern Qinghai-Tibet Plateau.

Introduction

Chengdu-Lanzhou Railway is a major railway project in western China, which passes through the high-intensity areas of the eastern Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. The rock mass is strongly weathered, with joints and fissures developed, and there are several active faults in this area along the railroad, which result in the frequent occurrence of earthquakes1,2. So, earthquake-induced geohazards are typical and seriously restrict the construction of projects in this area. Especially, the stability of rock slopes with weak interlayers significantly influences the safety of the Chengdu-Lanzhou railway3–5. The weak interlayer as a weak part, controls the slope deformation and damage6–8. It is easy to deform and weaken under earthquakes, which leads to the deformation and failure of the whole slope. Failure of rock slopes with weak interlayers triggered by earthquakes is usually with large volume, high disaster-causing capacity, and fast startup and sliding of failure, for example, the Tangjiashan landslide and the Daguangbao landslide, which were induced by the Wenchuan Earthquake (Ms = 8.0) in 20089,10. It is of great engineering significance to study the dynamic response and seismic design of rock slopes with weak interlayers in the high-intensity areas of the eastern Qinghai-Tibet Plateau.

Presently, numerical simulation is a common and essential method to study the dynamic response of reinforcing structures of the slope. Lin, et al.11 compared the seismic effect of the anchored frame beam and the combined reinforcing structure-the anchored frame beam and gravity wall. The results proved the superiority of the composite reinforcing structure. Zheng, et al.12 compared the difference in the dynamic response of anti-dip bedding slope with and without reinforcing structure by discrete element program UDEC and studied the influence of inclination, spacing, and prestress of prestressed anchor cable on reinforcement effect. Li and Xiao13 analyzed the seismic stability of a two-stage soil slope reinforced by single-row piles based on a numerical method and found that the shear strength and critical sliding mass range of piles increased with the increase of the horizontal acceleration.

In addition to numerical simulation, model test also plays an important role in studying the seismic performance of the slope-reinforcing structure. Xu, et al.14 constructed a rock slope model anchored by self-adaptive anchor cables based on the Xiangjiapo landslide and proved the self-adaptive anchor cables' load transfer mechanism and dynamic response characteristics. Fan, et al.15 studied the dynamic response of slopes reinforced by double-row anti-slide piles and prestressed anchor cables by shaking table model tests. The results showed that prestressed anchor cables effectively weakened the amplification of slope acceleration, and anti-slide piles in the middle of the slope effectively suppressed the horizontal displacement of the slope. Long, et al.16 established a physical model of a rock slope with a weak interlayer supported by anchor cables. The shear effect and seismic response of the cable-grouting interface and grouting-rock mass anchorage interface under earthquake were discussed. It was found that there was a positive correlation between the shear effect on the anchorage interface and the acceleration response of the slope. Zhang, et al.17 studied the dynamic response of anti-slip piles in gravel landslides based on the shaking table test. The results show that the slope acceleration under anti-slip pile reinforcement exhibits an elevation amplification effect. The anti-slip piles show significant resonant coupling and unloading effects before and after reaching the bearing limit, respectively. Niu, et al.18 analyzed the horizontal acceleration response and response spectrum characteristics of the slope with a weak interlayer supported by a frame beam and anchor bolt and concluded that there was a significant difference in the acceleration response between the lower and upper weak interlayer.

Previous research has studied the seismic reinforcement methods of slopes, such as soil nails19, rock bolts20–22, prestressed anchor cables23, piles24–26, and combined support structures27–30. Prestressed anchor cable is a commonly used reinforcing method on high rock slopes, especially with weak interlayers in southwest China. However, in practical engineering, the seismic design of slope reinforced by prestressed anchor cable follows the pseudo-static method. The seismic inertial force is assumed as a force with constant direction and value, which is inconsistent with the actual situation and cannot reflect the natural seismic instantaneous dynamic response of slope and reinforcing structure31–33. In addition, fewer studies have been investigated on the seismic response of prestressed anchor cable-reinforced rock slopes with weak interlayers. The research on the dynamic response and seismic design of prestressed anchor cable still needs to be deepened34–36.

In this paper, the shaking table model tests show the dynamic response characteristics of prestressed anchor cable and reproduce the deformation and failure process of the unreinforced slope. A numerical model of a rock slope with a weak interlayer is constructed to study the seismic effect of prestressed anchor cable. The axial force response characteristics and seismic performance of prestressed anchor cable are analyzed, and the influence of prestressing, anchor cable inclination, and spacing on the prevention and control of prestressed anchor cable is studied. The results of this paper can provide insights into the prevention and control of rock slope disasters with weak interlayers and the seismic design of prestressed anchor cables.

Shaking table test

Engineering background

A rock slope between Maoxian Station and the Maoxian Tunnel of the Chengdu-Lanzhou Railway in Sichuan Province is selected as the prototype. The elevation of the slope is 1474 m to 1544 m, and the maximum height difference is about 70 m. The lithology of the slope is mainly phyllite. The weak interlayer is strongly weathered carbonaceous phyllite with quartz veins, the thickness of the interlayer is 1 m to 4 m. The prestressed anchor cables are distributed along the slope surface with a spacing of 4 m. The prestress of the prestressed anchor cable is 540 kN, and the diameter of the anchor hole is 110 mm. The inclination of the prestressed anchor cable is 20°. The anchorage section of the cables is 9 m long, and all of the anchor cables are anchored into sound rock. The length of the free section is from 9 to 22 m. The tensile strength of the anchor cable should be more than 1860 MPa.

Testing equipment

The testing equipment is the three-way electro-hydraulic servo-driven shaking table of the Open Laboratory of Earthquake Simulation, Institute of Engineering Mechanics, China Earthquake Administration, Harbin. In the shaking table test, the electro-hydraulic servo valve controls the movement of the actuator to achieve seismic waves with an input frequency range of 0.5 Hz and 50 Hz. The maximum excitation acceleration under full load can reach 1 g and 0.7 g in the horizontal and vertical directions respectively, and the maximum overturning moment is 75t·m. The ratio between the horizontal peak acceleration at any point and the peak input acceleration is defined as the horizontal acceleration amplification factor (AAF-X). Figure 4 shows the acceleration time history of the slope surface supported by prestressed anchor cables when the input peak acceleration is 0.2 g. Model slope deformation and failure

During the test, no obvious deformation and failure observed occurred in the reinforced slope, but progressive failure occurred in the unreinforced slope. Figures 9 and 10 show the unreinforced slope deformation and failure while the peak acceleration exceeded 0.6 g. When the input peak acceleration is 0.2 g and 0.4 g, there is no obvious deformation observed. When the input peak acceleration reached 0.6 g, cracks appeared near the upper end of the weak interlayer at the slope top. The cracks appeared and developed approximately vertically to the weak interlayer, which formed an X-shaped fracture (Fig. 9). When the input peak acceleration was 0.8 g, the cracks further extended, and the weak interlayer evolved into a sliding surface. The overlying rock mass of the weak interlayer moved downward along it. The rock mass near the upper end of the weak interlayer and the slope shoulder was cracked and seriously dislocated, and many tensile cracks developed near the upper part of the slope (Fig. 10).Figure 9Slope failure phenomenon when the peak acceleration is 0.6 g: (a) slope toe; (b) the top of the weak interlayer; (c) slope top.Figure 10Slope failure phenomenon when the peak acceleration is 0.8 g: (a) slope toe; (b) cracks at the slope shoulder; (c) outcrop in the weak intercalated layer at the slope crest.

Dynamic condition setting

Rayleigh damping and local damping are the two most used damping in FLAC3D dynamic analysis. Although Rayleigh damping can obtain good numerical results, it will significantly increase the computational burden. Local damping is more efficient. Although local damping probably introduces high-frequency noise, it is useful to filter the high-frequency component of the seismic wave to obtain good computational results. For the features of local damping, the frequency components of seismic waves higher than 10 Hz could be filtered44. Local damping coefficient with a value of 0.157 is selected in the numerical simulation45.

The numerical model always has boundaries. When seismic waves propagate to the model boundary, they will be reflected, resulting in wave distortion at the boundary surface. FLAC3D uses the free field boundary to establish a planar free field grid and a cylindrical free field grid around the model to simulate infinite rock mass conditions and reduce the reflection of waves at the boundary. The wave frequency and the wave velocity characteristics of the rock mass significantly influence the numerical accuracy of the wave transmission. Seismic sensitivity investigation of prestressed anchor cables

The prestress, inclination, and spacing of prestressed anchor cables are selected as the influencing factors to explore the dynamic response and seismic sensitivity of prestressed anchor cables in rock slopes with weak interlayers. D is defined as the displacement change rate, D = (DUR − DR)/DUR. And DUR is the displacement of the unreinforced slope, DR is the displacement of the reinforced slope. To clarify the axial force response of prestressed anchor cables under an earthquake, the axial force change rate is defined as F = (Fmax − F0)/F0. And Fmax is the peak axial force of the prestressed anchor cable, F0 is the initial prestress of the anchor cable. To obtain universal laws, A sine wave with a frequency of 10 Hz is inputted in X direction, and the peak acceleration is 0.6 g.

Prestress

According to the construction design of the prestressed anchor cables in 'code for the design of landslide stabilization' (GB/T 38509-2020), the initial prestress is 500 kN, and it increases from 500 to 2500 kN with an interval of 200 kN. Figure 18 shows the variation of D with prestress. Prestress significantly influences the displacement change rate. It is obvious that D increases with the increase of prestress, but the growth rate decreases gradually. The higher the elevation of the prestressed anchor cable is, the lower the D is.Figure 18Variation of slope surface displacement change rate D with prestress: (a) A1–A6; (b) A7–A12.

Discussions

Comparison of testing results and simulation results

Both numerical simulation and shaking table model tests were adopted to study the dynamic response of rock slope with weak interlayer and the seismic reinforcement effect of prestressed anchor cable under earthquake. FLAC3D can simulate the dynamic response of slope and analyze the seismic sensitivity of prestressed anchor cables, but there are still limitations in the simulation of slope deformation and failure process, and the interaction between rock mass and weak interlayer47,48. The physical model test is a scaled-down test following the similarity relation. However, the material strength of the prestressed anchor cable is difficult to control, so the similarity ratio of the model is not fully satisfied. Although they are hard to be eliminated, they can be reduced to a reasonable extent, and the testing results are relatively reasonable and reliable49–51. Nevertheless, the model test can reproduce the progress deformation and failure process of the model slope. Therefore, numerical simulation and shaking table test results can be mutually verified and supplemented.

However, a phenomenon different from the numerical result is observed, namely the prestress loss (Fig. 8). When the input peak acceleration is small, the slope is in a stable state. In the reciprocating motion of the seismic wave, the contact between the prestressed anchor cables and the rock mass may be loosened, or the anchor bar would be probably relaxed and cause prestress loss. With the increase of input peak acceleration, the prestressed anchor cables begin to work, and the loss of prestress decreases. Although the axial force time history of the numerical simulation is different from that of the shaking table test, the numerical simulation still captures some important aspects of the shaking table test: (1) The axial force time history shows two strong response periods, and the residual axial force of the anchor cable is mainly affected by the first strong response period; (2) The peak axial force of C10 and C12 in the numerical simulation meets the similarity ratio compared with the shaking table test.

Dynamic behavior of rock slope reinforced by prestressed anchor cables

Both numerical simulation and shaking table test have revealed that acceleration of rock slope amplified nonlinearly with elevation39,42, but it only makes sense when the large-scale damage does not occur in the slope. With the increase of input peak acceleration, obvious deformation and failure would occur, and the amplification of acceleration would be weakened (Figs. 5, 9, 10, and 14). After being reinforced by prestressed anchor cables, there is no significant change in the acceleration response (Fig. 14), but the slope displacement is significantly reduced (Fig. 15), which proves that the prestressed anchor cable is effective in reinforcing the rock slope with weak interlayer.

Dynamic acceleration response controls the dynamic deformation and failure of rock slope, it is complex and influenced by many factors. Previous and present studies have shown that elevation, topography, lithologic condition, slope structure as well as seismic wave characteristics, etc. can influence the amplification of acceleration52–55. The acceleration response is the result of the combination of various factors, and the study of this problem should combine multiple factors rather than a single factor. So, the AAF-X is not suitable for evaluating the performance of prestressed anchor cables.

Dynamic slope deformation and failure

In the previous study16,29,56, a single interlayer with a certain thickness or a plane without thickness was always considered in the seismic design of rock slope, it is regarded that the failure of the reinforcing structure as well as the weak surface undergoes shear failure under the reciprocating action of seismic force. In this paper, two intersecting weak interlayers with the same dip direction were designed. Under the combined action of the seismic force and weight of rock mass, the lower part of the weak interlayer was subjected to cyclic compression-shear failure under seismic force. Tensile shear failure occurred and developed in the upper part of the overlying rock mass. Finally, the weak interlayer gradually evolved into a sliding surface, and the overlying rock mass moved downward along it.

In general, slope deformation is a progressive process with four stages: the first stage is the appearance of tensile shear cracks around the slope shoulder under the tensile shear effects of seismic force (Fig. 9b, c), the second stage is the appearance of compressive shear cracks at the lower part of the slope (Fig. 9a), the third stage is the weakening of the sandwich and dislocation of local rock mass (Fig. 10a, c), and the fourth stage the sliding down of overlying rock mass along the weak interlayer (Fig. 10).

Seismic sensitivity of the prestressed anchor cables

The increase of prestress can effectively improve the reinforcing effect of prestressed anchor cable, but while the prestress exceeds 1300 kN, the growth rate of D and the reduction rate of F becomes slow (Figs. 18 and 20), indicating that the excessive prestress induced the prestressed anchor cable in a high axial force state at the initial moment, which is very likely to cause damage of the slope rock mass. The effect of prestress on different slope parts is distinct, caused by the different inclinations of the upper and lower parts of the weak interlayer. The maximum D appears while the inclination of the prestressed anchor cable is between 5° and 20° (Fig. 21), which is different from the inclination recommended by the existing specification. With the increase of the inclination, the reinforcing effect of the prestressed anchor cable is weakened. The prestressed anchor cables have the maximum F at 30° (Fig. 23), and the axial force response is the strongest, which is not conducive to the design and safety of anchor cables. So, it is necessary to adopt a small inclination in the seismic design of the prestressed anchor cable. The influence of the spacing of prestressed anchor cables is evident. Generally, the smaller the spacing, the better the reinforcement effect (Fig. 24).

Conclusion

In this paper, FLAC3D was used to study the dynamic response of prestressed anchor cables reinforced rock slopes with weak interlayers and the seismic sensitivity of prestressed anchor cables. The reliability of the numerical simulation results was verified by the shaking table test. The conclusions are as follows:

With the elevation increase, the constraint effect of the prestressed anchor cables on displacement is weakened. With the increase of input peak acceleration, the displacement constraint effect of the prestressed anchor cables is weakened. The prestressed anchor cables cannot always weaken the acceleration amplification, which is related to the input peak acceleration, slope structure, slope deformation, and other factors.

Shaking table test results show that weak interlayers control the formation of the slip surface. The lower part of the weak interlayer undergoes compressive shear failure and evolves into a shear slip surface. Tensile cracks are developed at the upper part of the weak interlayer and it mainly undergoes tensile shear failure. The whole slope undergoes progressive failure in four stages: the tensile cracks appear near the slope shoulder, the compressive shear cracks appear at the lower part of the slope, and the weakening of the sandwich and overall sliding.

The numerical results show that the prestress can enhance the seismic support effect of the prestressed anchor cables, but excessive prestress will cause damage to the rock mass. As the inclination of the prestressing anchor increases, the reinforcement effect is enhanced and then weakened. The smaller the spacing of the prestressing anchor cable, the more obvious the reinforcement effect. Prestress loss of anchor cable under earthquake was observed during the shaking table test. With the increase of input peak acceleration, the prestress loss of the anchor cable decreases gradually. When the input peak acceleration reached 0.8 g, the prestress loss was not recurrence.

Neuro-fuzzy system based proportional derivative gain optimized attitude control of CubeSat under LEO perturbations
Authors: Muhammad Faisal Shehzad, Aamer Bilal Asghar, Mujtaba Hussain Jaffery, Khazina Naveed, Zsolt Čonka
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrompt attitude stabilization is more challenging in Nano CubeSat due to its minimal capacity, weight, energy, and volume-constrained architecture. Fixed gain non-adaptive classical proportional integral derivative control methodology is ineffective to provide optimal attitude stability in low earth orbit under significant environmental disturbances. Therefore, an artificial neural network with fuzzy inference design is developed in a simulation environment to control the angular velocity and quaternions of a CubeSat by autonomous gain tuning of the proportional-derivative controller according to space perturbations. It elucidates the dynamics and kinematics of the CubeSat attitude model with reaction wheels and low earth orbit disruptions, i.e., gravity gradient torque, atmospheric torque, solar radiation torque, and residual magnetic torque. The effectiveness of the proposed ANFIS-PD control scheme shows that the CubeSat retained the three-axis attitude controllability based on initial quaternions, the moment of inertia, Euler angle error, attitude angular rate, angular velocity rate as compared to PID, ANN, and RNN methodologies. Outcomes from the simulation indicated that the proposed controller scheme achieved minimum root mean square errors that lead towards rapid stability in roll, pitch, and yaw axis respectively within 20 s of simulation time.Keywords: Adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system, Artificial neural network, Fuzzy logic, CubeSat, Low earth orbit disturbances, Satellite attitude control system

Introduction

Over the previous decade, spacecraft has played a vital role in the research and economic growth of the worldwide nation and has implementations in remote sensing, communication, networking, metrology, horticulture, etc. [1]. Knowing the attitude-behavior of a spacecraft before it is manufactured and placed in operation orbit enables the designer to select the optimal control system to ensure that the mission is accomplished as intended [2]. Small satellites came to the space research network as an alternative to high-cost satellites having long production periods when Jordi Puig-Suari and Bob Twiggs invented a modular design called the CubeSat in Nano (1 kg–10 kg) and micro (10 kg–100 kg) size models [3,4]. These CubeSats can be as beneficial as the larger satellites when an accurate and reliable 3-axis-stabilized CubeSat is established and verified [5]. Various feasible linear and nonlinear control approaches are becoming prominent for resolving attitude control issues by developing miniature reaction wheels/magnetic torques (MTs) as an actuator [6]. Though, CubeSat's inherent limitations i.e. size, density, and energy make it hard to accomplish high-performance missions. Moreover, the majority of the CubeSat's altitude is in low Earth orbit, where external disturbances are significant, affecting attitude stability significantly [7]. As, a result, many approaches have been implemented, with promising outcomes mentioned in the literature on the attitude control subsystem (see Table 4, Table 5).

An asymptotic three-axis attitude stabilization for the LEO satellite was developed by considering the synchronous utilization of three reaction wheels (RW) and three magnetic torques (MTs) for the concurrent operation of angular momentum regulation [15]. An optimal adaptive fuzzy proportional-integral-derivative type slide mode controller (AFPID-SMC) for the re-entry vehicle's attitude control subsystem was developed by online adjustment of the proportional-integral-derivative type sliding mode mechanism and estimation of disturbance by radial basis function neural network (RBFNN) subject to model uncertainty and turbulence [17]. The robust adaptive type-2 fuzzy neural network controller was introduced for attitude control of magnetic rigid satellites by using ellipse type-2 membership functions (MF) and slide mode control (SMC) adjustment rules [18]. Colagrossi et al. [21] addressed the 3-axis orientation control problem of an entirely magnetic actuated pico satellite by integration of a B-dot control algorithm and proportional-derivative controller.

In [22], adaptive second-order fast terminal slide mode control with neural network-based architecture was designed for the Lorentz-based satellite's attitude control system subject to model uncertainties and environmental turbulence in space. Such obscure disturbances and uncertainties are approximated by radial basis function neural network (RBFNN) and Lyapunov stability analysis ensured the overall stability of the system. Zhong et al. [23] examined the flexible satellite's three-axis orientation control issues and proposed an innovative Lyapunov stability analysis with a compensator to account for uncertainties in the robust and effective performance of system. An intelligent fuzzy PD + I controller was presented by Ref. [26] to control the Nano attitude control system of Innovative Satellite InnoSAT. In the controller section, a closed-loop hybrid ANFIS-PD control methodology is implemented to achieve the three-axis attitude control stability of CubeSat against continuous external disturbances.

Three RWs are modeled, and the angular speed of RWs is increasing due to the lack of its attitude handling capability shown in Fig. 13 in (a), thus the drive control torque from RWs attempted to tackle satellite attitude in (b) with oscillating behavior. This torque tries to control the dynamics of the satellite with non-convergent torque amplitude. The moment of inertia and angular speed of wheels determined the angular momentum of RWs in (c) and surges in angular momentum encountered due to the high angular speed of the wheels. Furthermore, after separation from the launcher, higher angular velocities of the satellite need to be controlled in minimum time but angular velocity keeps on getting fluctuations due to the non-adaptive nature of simple control in (d) and reduced to zero after 80 s. The angular momentum of the satellite depends on the angular velocity which is high even after its control, so momentum on each axis has unstable nonlinear behavior as shown in (e) till 80 s. As a result, the output attitude Euler angles and quaternions are diverted from desired attitude angles due to the high angular momentum of the satellite as shown in (f), (g), and attained target values at 100 s. Consequently, the PID controller is not able to handle this nonlinear behavior quickly. Hence, Euler angle error and quaternion error decrease by taking extra time which leads to poor attitude stability performance of CubeSat, which can be seen in (h) and (i).

Conclusions

In this study, the proposed ANFIS methodology is very effective to analyze its performance with respect to the stability of CubeSat under LEO disturbances to regulate the Euler angles. To accomplish this task, the adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system is designed along with the four-layered neural network. The appropriate PD attitude controller strategy based on ANFIS interacts with various aspects, like persistent variations in space environmental fluctuations, higher satellite angular velocity, distorted angular momentum, and nonlinear behavior of the reaction wheel. Eventually, the attitude control methodology achieved a minimum RMSE error and attained an Euler angle error near zero as related to fixed gain PID, adaptive ANN, and RNN controller. Performance evaluation and MATLAB graphs indicated that the ANFIS controller is more efficient for our proposed system. This work can be further extended in the future by the experimental testing of CubeSat attitude control with various techniques such as Granular extension of the Sugeno type fuzzy model (G-FIS) and Granular neural networks (GNN) and integral sliding mode control (ISMC).

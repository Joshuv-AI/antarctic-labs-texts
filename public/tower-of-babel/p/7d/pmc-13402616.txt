Alignment in yrast structure and negative-parity sideband in the proton-rich nucleus Sm
Authors: Sachin K Singh, S K Tandel, Saket Suman, M Hemalatha, S G Wahid, D Negi, S Mukhopadhyay, Md S R Laskar, R Palit
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe proton-rich nucleus Sm has been studied up to high spin and the level scheme has been extended beyond the six transitions previously reported in the ground-state band. Levels beyond E = 4 MeV and up to I = (16) have been identified. The first nucleon alignment in the yrast positive-parity structure has been established at a rotational frequency of 0.30 MeV, and is attributed to the breaking of a h proton pair. A negative-parity structure with its bandhead at E = 2061 keV and I = (7) has been identified and is attributed to the excitation of protons in the h and g orbitals. Cranked shell model calculations using the Ultimate Cranker code with standard Nilsson parameters indicate a prolate deformed minimum which persists to high frequencies. The calculated proton alignment frequency is in good agreement with the experimental value, and the neutron crossing is expected at a significantly higher frequency on account of the N = 72 deformed subshell gap.Keywords: Nuclear structure, Rotational bands, Nucleon alignments, Cranking calculationsSubject terms: Astronomy and planetary science, Physics

Introduction

The region of the nuclear chart with mass number A 130 for isotopes of Ba (Z = 56) to Gd (Z = 64) is found to manifest a diversity of phenomena. Owing to the significant number of valence protons and quite large neutron valence space, these nuclei are deformed and rotational bands have been observed in all the isotopes in this region studied thus far. Shape coexistence has been inferred from the level structures of Ba1, Nd2 and Sm3,4. At normal deformation, high-j proton and neutron orbitals contribute to the structure while for strongly deformed shapes, even higher-j intruder orbitals are involved. Superdeformed or highly deformed band structures have been identified in the Ce (Z = 58) isotopes Ce5, Ce6, and Ce7. Such bands with associated large deformation have also been established in Nd (Z = 60) isotopes Nd8, Nd9 and Nd10 in addition to the Sm (Z = 62) isotopes Sm11 and Sm12. K isomers based on the K = 8 neutron configuration have been located in the N = 74 isotones Ba13, Ce14, Nd2, and Sm15. Further, N = 72 isotones Ba16 and Ce17 exhibit evidence of K = 7 isomers. Magnetic rotation has also been observed in some cases, e.g., in Ba18. Nuclei in this mass region can accommodate among the highest angular momentum across the nuclear chart established for discrete states. For instance, levels with 68 have been assigned in the superdeformed band of Ce19.

Against this backdrop, it should be noted that the most proton-rich isotopes of these elements are not well studied owing to the absence of suitable stable projectile-target combinations which can be used for their production through compound nuclear fusion-evaporation reactions. For instance, in Sm, only the first six members of the ground-state rotational band have been identified20,21. In the case of its N = 72 isotone, Gd, there is no spectroscopic information on excited states available. For the even-A Sm isotopes, Sm and Sm, only a part of the ground-state rotational band had been identified21,22, while in Sm and Sm, detailed information on high-spin states was available3,15,23. The objective of this work was to address the paucity of data on proton-rich isotopes with a focus on rotational properties and the possibility of observing high-K states in Sm.

Experiment and results

Levels at high excitation in Sm were populated using the Cd(S,2p2n) reaction. The S beam with energy 160 MeV was delivered by the BARC-TIFR Pelletron-LINAC facility. The target was a self-supporting, 8 mg/cm Cd foil which was enriched from the natural abundance of 1.3 up to 97. The rays emitted by the evaporation residues were detected in two- or higher-fold coincidence using 11 Compton-suppressed clover high-purity germanium (HPGe) detectors24.

Search for a possible K isomer in Sm

Due to the availability of high- components of the neutron h and g orbitals near the Fermi level for N = 72 and 74 isotones of Ba (Z = 56), Ce (Z = 58), Nd (Z = 60), Sm (Z = 62), and Gd (Z = 64), relatively low-lying high-K states can be realized. In the N = 74 isotones, K = 8 isomers have been reported with half-lives ranging from 11 ms, 9.4 ms, 410 s, 15 s and 6 s in Ba, Ce, Nd, Sm and Gd, respectively2,13–15. In N = 72 isotones, K = 7 isomers with half-lives of 6.1 ns and 100 ns have been established in Ba and Ce, respectively16,17. Thus far, no data on excited levels in Gd are available, and prior to this work, information on Sm was also quite limited. While Nd had been studied earlier, no K isomer had been identified9. Based on the above available information, the present data were thoroughly examined for the presence of a possible K isomer in Sm. However, with the available statistics, no such isomer could be located. It cannot be precluded though that future higher-statistics data might reveal the existence of such an isomer.

Discussion

Nuclei in the A = 130 region are characterized by prolate deformation near their ground states. The magnitude of the deformation is found to be higher with an increase in the proton number farther away from the Z = 50 shell gap and towards the mid-shell region. On the contrary, with an increase in neutron number approaching the N = 82 shell closure, the deformation is found to be lower. These contrasting effects are reflected in the structure of nuclei along a given isotopic or isotonic chain. In both the proton and neutron sectors, for normal deformation, the highest value of j corresponds to the h subshell. In the case of normal deformed nuclei, this orbital plays a key role in the rotational behavior in terms of nucleon alignments and also in the realization of high-K states arising from intrinsic excitations. For highly deformed nuclei, orbitals from above the Z = N = 82 shell gap, like the i subshell are involved. On account of all of the above, a variety of nuclear structure phenomena are observed in this region.

Band crossings in positive-parity yrast band

The rotational behavior of N = 72 and N = 74 isotones of even-Z Xe (Z = 54) up to Sm (Z = 62) is discussed in this section. The lower-Z isotopes, viz., Xe and Ba, are characterized by smaller deformation, and the proton Fermi level is energetically more distant from the h subshell in comparison to the higher-Z elements, i.e., Ce, Nd and Sm. In all the cases, the neutron Fermi level is adjacent to the h subshell. As a result, the proton h orbital is expected to play a more significant role in the observed rotation alignments in Ce, Nd and Sm isotopes. The presence of a deformed subshell gap at neutron number N = 72 implies that the rotation alignment arising from h neutrons would lie at a considerably higher frequency. The first rotation alignment frequencies in Xe and Ba isotopes are found to be approximately 0.4 MeV, and band structures consistent with both proton and neutron h crossings have been reported. In the case of Ce, Nd and Sm isotopes wherever information is available, with the h proton orbital lying close to the Fermi level, the crossing frequency is found to be significantly lower, i.e., 0.3 MeV. To understand the rotational properties of Sm and those of neighboring nuclei, cranked shell model calculations were performed using the Ultimate Cranker code27 with standard Nilsson parameters. The evolution of collectivity for even-Z, N = 72 isotones with an increase in the proton number from Ba (Z = 56) to Gd (Z = 64) is illustrated by the change in magnitude of the quadrupole deformation () for the I = 0 ground state. The calculations indicate the following values: (a) Ce: = 0.21, (b) Nd: = 0.23, (c) Sm: = 0.25, and (d) Gd: = 0.26, as shown in Fig. 5. In all cases, the value of the triaxiality parameter, 0, indicating an axially symmetric prolate deformed shape of the nucleus. The increase in the quadrupole deformation is consistent with the higher degree of collectivity expected as the proton number moves farther from the Z = 50 shell closure towards the midshell region. In Sm, at higher spins, the calculations indicate a secondary oblate minimum which lies about 1 MeV higher in excitation than the prolate one. However, no excited levels corresponding to this oblate energy minimum are indicated from the present data.

Total energy surface plots for the ground states of N = 72 isotones Ce, Nd, Sm and Gd. The horizontal axis corresponds to a value of the triaxiality parameter = 0. In all cases, a prolate deformed minimum is found to be lowest in energy. An increase in the magnitude of quadrupole deformation for larger proton numbers is visible, with the energy minima found to be more well developed for higher atomic number.

The proton and neutron quasiparticle levels for Sm were calculated with the deformation parameters fixed at the value corresponding to the ground state ( = 0.25, = 0.02 and = 0). The h proton crossing is predicted at = 0.32 MeV (Fig. 6), which is in good agreement with the experimental value of 0.30 MeV. The h neutron crossing is expected at a much higher frequency of 0.52 MeV; this is attributed to the effect of the N = 72 deformed subshell gap. These calculations clearly favor a (h) assignment for the experimentally observed nucleon alignment.

A comparison of the experimental and calculated crossing frequencies for Sm (Z = 62) isotopes and N = 72 isotones of Sm is presented in Table 2. In Sm, the crossing frequency inferred from experiment is found to be 0.30 MeV as seen in Fig. 4(c). No rotation alignment was observed in Sm22 probably owing to the quite limited data. The calculated h proton crossing frequency for the lighter-mass Sm isotopes is consistent with the experimental value, and the corresponding neutron crossing is expected at a much higher frequency ( 0.5 MeV). The neutron crossing frequency is predicted to be lower for higher neutron numbers. For the N = 72 isotone Ba, owing to its smaller deformation, a somewhat higher h proton crossing frequency (0.36 MeV) is predicted. This is lower than the experimentally determined value of 0.41 MeV; the neutron h crossing is expected at the same frequency. The slight disagreement between the experimental and calculated values may possibly be attributable to the lower underlying deformation and shallower energy minimum, a situation in which the cranking prediction may not be as accurate. For this reason, Xe which is characterized by an even smaller deformation has not been included in this comparison. For Ce and Nd, the h proton crossing frequencies of 0.32 MeV from experiment are found to be identical with the calculated values (Table 2). The h neutron crossing is predicted at much higher frequencies. In the case of Gd, while there are no experimental data, the h proton crossing is calculated to be at a much lower frequency than the corresponding neutron alignment.

Negative-parity sidebands

Rotational bands built on two-quasiparticle configurations including the negative-parity h subshell and the positive-parity g or d orbitals have been observed in several even-even nuclei in this region. Specifically, in the Sm isotopic chain, the lowest-lying structure, excluding the ground-state rotational and the gamma-vibrational band, is based on the I = 7 state. The excitation energies of these states are E = 2275 keV and 2508 keV in Sm and Sm, respectively3,28. Rotational bands have been identified to be built on these states. In the present work, a new band built on the E = 2061-keV level in Sm has been established. This is quite similar in terms of the deexciting transitions to the ground-state band, and also in respect of its rotational sequence, to the 7 bands established in Sm and Sm. These bands have been assigned a two-quasiproton configuration involving the proton h and g orbitals3,28. A similar 7 assignment appears to be indicated in Sm. The increase in energy of the 7 bandhead with neutron number is consistent with the reduction in deformation from neutron number N = 72 corresponding to Sm to N = 76 associated with Sm. For higher deformations, the downsloping h and g orbitals lie at lower energies resulting in a smaller bandhead energy. It may be noted that negative rather than positive parity appears to be quite likely for the sideband since there are no low-lying, positive-parity 2-quasiparticle states with I = 7 expected in Sm. Therefore, the transitions deexciting this band are much more likely to have E1 rather than M1 character, consistent with the assignments outlined earlier.

Methods

The high-fold, multi-parameter data were sorted into multiple histograms including symmetric energy-energy matrices and three-fold energy cubes. A number of different matrices and cubes were generated with software coincidence time windows ranging from 100 ns to 1 s. The primary differences in the spectra for coincidence windows ranging from 100 ns to 1 s were in the following aspects: (a) better peak-to-background ratio for prompt rays in the 100 ns window, (b) longer-lived feeding was better visible in the 1 s window. Additionally, DCO (Directional Correlation from Oriented nuclei) matrices were created to ascertain the multipole order and electric or magnetic character of the rays, respectively25,26. The energy and efficiency calibrations were obtained with standard Eu and Ba -ray sources. For obtaining DCO ratios, an asymmetric energy-energy matrix was constructed with rays detected at with respect to the beam direction on one axis, and those detected at on the other. Summary

Positive- and negative-parity rotational band structures have been established in Sm. Eleven new rays have been placed in the level scheme. The ground-state rotational band has been extended and a new negative-parity structure has been established. One branch of the high-spin part of the positive-parity band is attributed to the rotation alignment of a pair of h protons while the negative-parity band likely arises from the occupation of the h and g proton orbitals. The observed rotation alignment in the yrast, positive-parity band at a rotational frequency of ≈ 0.30 MeV is consistent with the Ultimate Cranker value for the h proton crossing. The negative-parity structure is found to lie at lower excitation with a reduction in neutron number which is associated with an increase in the quadrupole deformation.

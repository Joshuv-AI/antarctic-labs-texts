First molecular phylogeny of Bartonella bovis in Iraqi cattle
Authors: Mithal K. A. Al-Hassani, Hadaf Mahdi Kadhim, Ahlam Ali Soghi Al-Galebi, Hasanain A. J. Gharban
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Bartonella bovis is one of the most neglected fastidious intracellular bacterial pathogens that infect a wide range of wild and domestic animals, including cattle.Aim:Detection of the prevalence of B. bovis in cattle using molecular assay, identification of the relationship of infection to animal risk factors (age and sex), and sequencing of B. bovis isolates to be analyzed phylogenetically and reported in the National Centre For Biotechnology Information (NCBI).Methods:A total of 290 cattle were selected randomly from different rural areas in Wasit province from September to December (2023), and subjected to collecting venous blood samples that were tested molecularly by the polymerase chain reaction (PCR). Phylogenetic analysis was conducted using the MEGA 7 Software.Results:Targeting the 16S rRNA gene, 7.24% of the study cattle tested positive for B. bovis. In terms of age, positivity rates, odds ratios (ORs), and relative risks (RRs) were significantly higher in cattle older than 7 years compared to other age groups: under 1 year, 1–3 years, and 3–7 years. Regarding sex, there was no significant variation in positive rates between females (7.48%) and males (5.56%); however, the OR and RR values indicated that females (1.376 and 1.345, respectively) were at a higher risk of B. bovis infection than males (0.727 and 0.743, respectively). Phylogenetic tree analysis of five local B. bovis isolates demonstrated their identity with the France strain/isolate at total genetic identity ranging from 99.64% to 99.98%.Conclusion:To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first molecular identification of the prevalence rate of B. bovis in cattle in Iraq using PCR assay and confirms phylogenetically the local B. bovis isolates in NCBI-BLAST. Therefore, additional information about the prevalence, genetic diversity in domestic and wild animals, and arthropod vectors is crucial for developing strategies for the prevention and control of B. bovis infections.

Introduction

Bartonella bovis is a pleomorphic Gram-negative, aerobic, and oxidase-negative bacterium that belongs to Alphaproteobacteria class, which infects cattle causing a disease known as bovine bartonellosis (Boularias et al., 2020; Tahir et al., 2023). To date, approximately 35 Bartonella species have been described worldwide, which are transmitted mainly by the hematophagous arthropodes including ticks, blood-sucking lice, biting flies, sand flies, and fleas (Ashtiani et al., 2024).

Various Bartonella species have been associated with persistent infections in domestic and wild ruminants that also can act substantially as a reservoir for the environment and a source of inadvertent human disease (Krügel et al., 2020; Tahir et al., 2023). In general, the prevalence rate of infection ranges from 50%–95% in rodents, cats, deer and cattle; in which, Bartonella infects endothelial cells/erythrocytes and escapes the host immunity to result in frequent bacteremia (Breitschwerdt and Kordick, 2000; Deng et al., 2018; Mozayeni et al., 2018).

Until recently, it seems that the application of traditional microbiologic diagnostics has a presence of considerable difficulties related to the isolation of Bartonella species from the blood of various animals (Gutiérrez et al., 2017; Vaca et al., 2022). In addition, specific laboratory diagnostics are needed to detect Bartonella-related diseases (Kamath and Luu, 2019). In recent years, molecular methods have emerged as an important clinical and rapid tool for the identification of various infections, that are difficult to be diagnosed using culture and serology (Dong et al., 2024; Rios et al., 2024). These have a broad range of polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based assays that either target the conserved binding sites flanking region of the rRNA gene and have species-specific variations (16-23S rRNA ITS), or target the organism-specific gene such as gltA and ribC (Paulauskas et al., 2022).

In Iraq as well as in neighboring (Iran, Jordan, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Syria, and Turkey) and other Arabic Gulf countries, no available studies have been recognized to demonstrate the Bartonella species in cattle. Therefore, this study aims to detect the prevalence of B. bovis in cattle using the molecular assay, identify the association of infection to animal risk factors (age and sex), and submission of some local B. bovis isolates to the National Centre For Biotechnology Information (NCBI).

Materials and Methods

Samples

Overall, 290 cattle of different ages [<1 (no. 69), 1–3 (no. 108), >3–7 (no. 82) and >7 (no. 31) years] and sex [females (no. 254) and males (no. 36)] were selected randomly from different rural areas in Wasit province during September to December (2023). Under aseptic conditions, jugular venous blood samples were collected using 5 ml disposable syringes into labeled EDTA-anticoagulant plastic tubes and kept frozen until molecular examination.

Total results for testing venous blood samples of 290 study cattle by PCR assay.

Molecular testing

After thawing of frozen blood with the water bath at 37°C, instructions of the Blood Protocol in the Geneaid Biotech Ltd. Kit (Taiwan) were followed to extract DNAs from blood. The samples of DNAs were tested by the Nanodrop System (Thermo-scientific, UK) to identify their concentration (ng/μl) and purity at an absorbance of A260/A280 nm. Targeting the 16S rRNA gene, one set of primers [HASF: (5´-CGC GTT GGA TTA GGC TAG TT-3´) and HASR: (5´-GTT TAC GGC GTG GAC TAC CA-3´)] was designed by the Primer3Plus Software based on the NCBI GenBank isolate (KU859920.1), and using the GoTaq® Green Master Mix Kit (Promega, Korea) to prepare the MasterMix tubes at a final volume of 20 μl. The PCR products were prepared in the Thermal Cycler System (Bio-Rad, USA) under the following conditions: 1 cycle for initial denaturation (95°C / 5 Minutes); 30 cycles for denaturation (95°C / 40 Seconds), annealing (40°C / 40 Seconds) and extension (72°C / 1 Minute); and 1 cycle for final extension (72°C / 7 Minutes). Electrophoresis of Agarose-gel stained with Ethidium Bromide was done at 100 Volt and 80 Am for 90 minutes, and the band size of positive PCR products was visualized using the UV transilluminator (Wised, Korea) at 560 bp.

Positive DNAs of five B. bovis samples were sent for sequencing (Macrogen, Korea), using the modified Sanger dideoxynucleotide sequencing method. The received sequence data were submitted in the NCBI, and recorded as the local B. bovis BB1, BB2, BB3, BB4, and BB5 strains. After the NCBI-BLAST analysis of local B. bovis strains and the NCBI-GenBank B. bovis as well as Bartonella, Brucella, and Ochrobactrum species, the MEGA 11 Software was served to build an alignment and to phylogenetic tree analysis.

Statistical analysis

The t-test and odds ratios (ORs) and relative risk (RR) in the GraphPad Prism Software version 9.0.1 (GraphPad Software Inc., USA) were served to identify significant differences between obtained values at p < 0.05 (Gharban and Ajaj, 2024).

Results

Targeting the 16S rRNA gene, the findings of the PCR assay revealed that 7.24% (21/290) of study cattle were positive for B. bovis; while 92.76% (269/290) were negatives (Figs. 1 and 2).

According to animal risk factors (age and sex), the findings of positive infections, OR and RR were varied significantly among their groups (Table 1). Concerning age, the current study showed that the positive B. bovis infections differed insignificantly in cattle aged <1 year (1.45%) and 1–3 years (2.78%); however, rate of positivity was increased significantly and gradually in cattle >3–7 years (10.98%) and >7 years (25.81%), respectively. Values of OR and RR demonstrated that the risk of B. bovis infection was elevated significantly with advancing age; as the lowest was seen in cattle of <1 year (0.148 and 0.16, respectively) and the highest in cattle aged> 7 years (6.587 and 5.139, respectively) when compared to other age groups.

Regarding the sex factor, the results of positive B. bovis infections reported the absence of significant variation (p < 0.914) between values of females (7.48%) and males (5.56%); however, values of OR and RR revealed significantly that females (1.376 and 1.345, respectively) at higher risk of B. bovis infection than males (0.727 and 0.743, respectively).

In the present study, phylogenetic analysis of five local B. bovis strains revealed its identity to the GenBank France B. bovis strains/isolate (NR_025121.1, KU859916.1, and KU859922.1) at a total genetic identity ranging 99.64%–99.98% and at total changes/mutations 0.002% (Figs. 3–5, Table 2).

Discussion

Worldwide, several authors suggested that Bartonella species are more different and prevalent in animals and humans due to the existence of different vectors and the wide range of hosts and reservoirs (Oteo et al., 2017; Cheslock and Embers, 2019; Portillo et al., 2020). Nonetheless, data for the vectors, reservoirs, and cases of infected humans in many countries with geographical distribution of Bartonella species (Ashtiani et al., 2024). In the present work, 7.24% of study cattle were positive to B. bovis. In comparison with the results of other studies, there were 6.8% in Poland (Welc-Falęciak and Grono, 2013), 6.84% in Egypt (Abdullah et al., 2021), 10% in Thailand (Bai et al., 2013), 15.3% in Algeria (Boularias et al., 2020), 17.65% in Spain (Antequera-Gómez et al., 2015), 20% in Ivory Coast (Raoult et al., 2005), 24% in Italy (Martini et al., 2008), 25% in Guatemala (Bai et al., 2013), 27.9% in Senegal (Dahmani et al., 2017), 36% in France (Maillard et al., 2004), 42.2% in Taiwan (Tsai et al., 2011), 59% in Italy (Maillard et al., 2006), 70% in French Guyana (Saisongkorh et al., 2009), 73% in Georgia (Bai et al., 2013), 75% in Israel (Rudoler et al., 2014), and 82.4% in USA (Cherry et al., 2009) but not in Japan and Kenya (0%), (Bai et al., 2013). This tremendous variation in the prevalence of Bartonella infections among the cattle population worldwide might be attributed to farming methods, diagnostic schemes, targeted gene, and pasture management. In this matter, the abundance and distribution of specific arthropods and environmental factors have been demonstrated to affect greatly on prevalence of B. bovis since heavy infestation and these factors could increase the risk of Bartonella transmission between cattle (Kho et al., 2015).

Close-related identity was shown between the local B. bovis strains to NCBI-BLAST B. bovis France isolate/strain (NR_025121.1 and KU859922.1).

In relation to age and sex factors, our findings were in agreement with those reported by a number of researchers (Antequera-Gómez et al., 2015; Boularias et al., 2020) and conversly to Maillard et al. (2006) who detected that heifers of >1 year old and <2 years old age and (92.5%) have the higher rates of B. bovis infection than those of >6 years old (29.2%). In comparison to young animals, large efforts are done for oldest animals to prevent and control the incidence and recurrence of different pathogens, which might be attributed to diminish immunity. Also, much more direct contacts between animals and vectors at older ages could lead to increasing the chance of acquiring diseases that may remain for long times. In females, the number of pregnancies and quantity of milk production could result in immunosuppressive effects on health status and higher level of Bartonella infection.

For several decades, molecular phylogeny of bacterial 16S rRNA gene had served widely to detect the environmental and clinical isolates and assign relationships at species level because highly conserved DNA sequences as seen in previous (Suau et al., 1999; Gorkiewicz et al., 2003; Pei et al., 2010) and recent (Johnson et al., 2019; Church et al., 2020; Church et al., 2020; Arredondo et al., 2023) studies. In this study, molecular phylogeny of the local B. bovis isolates based 16S rRNA gene confirmed its high identity to pathogenic B. bovis strains previously isolated from French cattle and European ruminants. Del Valle et al. (2014) showed that alignment matrix sequence of Bartonella genus based on the 16S rRNA gene has revealed 97%–99% of homology between species suggesting that this gene can be used as a rapid and easy technique for identification of Bartonella species.

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first Iraqi study that identified the prevalence rate of B. bovis in cattle using the conventional molecular PCR assay and confirmed the local B. bovis isolates in the NCBI-BLAST. We found that the prevalence rate of B. bovis in Wait province was relatively low; however, distribution of infection could be much wider than thought in other areas. Therefore, moreover information about the prevalence, genetic diversity in the domestic and wild animals, and arthropod vectors is important for formulation of strategies for prevention and control of B. bovis infections.

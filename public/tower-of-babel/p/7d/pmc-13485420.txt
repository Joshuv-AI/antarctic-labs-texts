Clinical Outcomes after Bilateral Mix-and-Match Implantation of Extended Depth of Focus and Trifocal Intraocular Lenses
Authors: Hye Rin An, Sanghyu Nam, Jung Yeob Han, Su Young Moon, Hun Lee, Hungwon Tchah, Jae Yong Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the clinical outcomes after bilateral mix-and-match implantation of extended depth of focus (EDOF) and trifocal intraocular lenses (IOLs).MethodsA total of 134 eyes of 67 patients who had met all inclusion and exclusion criteria and underwent bilateral cataract surgery were included. The Symfony or Symfony toric EDOF IOL (Johnson & Johnson Vision Care Inc.) was implanted in the dominant eye, and the PanOptix or PanOptix toric trifocal IOL (Alcon Laboratories Inc.) in the nondominant eye. The uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA), uncorrected intermediate visual acuity (UIVA), uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA), corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA), defocus curve, contrast sensitivity, and satisfaction questionnaire were obtained preoperatively and at 1, 6, and 12 months after surgery.ResultsAt 12 months after surgery, the mean UDVA (logMAR) was 0.03 ± 0.06 in the EDOF IOL-implanted eyes, 0.07 ± 0.11 in the trifocal IOL-implanted eyes, and 0.02 ± 0.06 in both eyes. The mean UIVA was 0.08 ± 0.08 in EDOF IOL-implanted eyes, 0.09 ± 0.11 in trifocal IOL-implanted eyes, and 0.03 ± 0.08 in both eyes. Mean UNVA was 0.51 ± 0.26 in EDOF IOL-implanted eyes, 0.22 ± 0.16 in trifocal IOL-implanted eyes, and 0.17 ± 0.12 in both eyes. Overall satisfaction rates were 43.3%, 41.1%, and 38.5% at 1, 6, and 12 months, respectively, while more than 70% of patients consistently reported that they would recommend the surgery to others. The most common causes of dissatisfaction were dry eye symptoms and reduced near vision.ConclusionsBilateral mix-and-match implantation of EDOF and trifocal IOL implantation showed good visual outcomes for distance, intermediate, and near vision until 12 months. Most patients satisfied with visual quality, but more dissatisfied with dry eye disease and poor near vision than photopic phenomena like glare and halos.

Trifocal intraocular lenses (IOLs) were developed to overcome the decreased intermediate vision of bifocal IOL and improve the patient’s independence from spectacles at intermediate distance with satisfied vision at far and near distances [1]. However, the newly added extra focus might lead to a loss of light energy and result in reduced optical quality [2]. Extended depth of focus (EDOF) IOLs were developed to improve intermediate vision by providing a continuous range of light from the far distance through the intermediate distance. Some EDOF IOLs provide a function to correct chromatic aberration which could increase contrast sensitivity and decrease the intensity and rate of glare and halo [3–5]. Nevertheless, the visual quality of EDOF IOL at near-distance vision is still inferior to multifocal or trifocal IOLs [6].

As one of the ways to resolve these limitations and to achieve good visual advantages for intermediate and near vision is a selection of various specific add-power of multifocal IOLs [7]. Also, there is another possibility to overcome these problems, a blended approach for a combination of differently designed multifocal IOLs could be the option [8,9]. This method is so-called mix-and-match implantation, claimed by Jacobi et al. [10]. With this method, careful selection of differently blended IOLs is needed considering their peaks of the focus curves to strengthen the visual outcomes [11,12]. The AcrySof IQ PanOptix TFNT00 IOL (Alcon Laboratories Inc.) was the first panfocal IOL developed under trifocal multifocal IOL with its unique optical design. However, in terms of function, these IOLs act as trifocal IOLs. This foldable trifocal IOL made it more independent of pupil size, and possible to produce good near visual quality in lower contrasts. Also, a negative spherical aberration is added on the anterior surface to compensate for the positive spherical aberration on the cornea. In the central portion, a diffractive zone is placed in the 4.5 mm of the optical zone and makes light dividing to an intermediate addition of + 2.17 diopters (D) and near to + 3.25 D [13].

The TECNIS Symfony ZXR00 IOL (Johnson & Johnson Vision Care Inc.) is a single-piece hydrophobic acrylic foldable IOL with a new design of its posterior diffractive surface that provides an extended focus range. The anterior surface of this IOL is aspheric to correct the positive spherical aberration. The mechanism of EDOF IOL is splitting the light energy into an extended focus which could decrease the overlapped images of near and far distances. These overlapped images usually occurred by conventional multifocal IOLs which could lead to halos. Symfony IOL is designed to reduce halos and also uses achromatic diffractive shapes which may correct the chromatic aberration to enhance contrast sensitivity [14].

In this study, we attempted to take advantage of previously-mentioned two IOLs by using the mix-and-match method and investigate visual outcomes, contrast sensitivity, and patient satisfaction after mix-and-match implantation of an EDOF IOL in the dominant eye and a trifocal IOL in the nondominant eye.

Materials and Methods

In this retrospective study, we analyzed 134 eyes of 67 patients who underwent uncomplicated bilateral phacoemulsification with mix-and-match implantation of an EDOF IOL in the dominant eye and a trifocal IOL in the nondominant eye performed by a single surgeon at a tertiary referral center between January 2018 and April 2022. This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Asan Medical Center (No. 2021-0497) and conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the use of deidentified data and the retrospective nature of the study.

All patients preoperatively underwent a dominant eye test. IOL power calculations were performed using the Barrett Universal II, SRK/T, Haigis, and Hoffer Q formulas, and emmetropia (plano) was targeted for both eyes. For mix-and-match bilateral implantation, the trifocal IOL (AcrySof IQ PanOptix TFNT00) was first implanted in the nondominant eye. With 3-to-7-day interval, the EDOF IOL (TECNIS Symfony ZXR00) was secondly implanted in the dominant eye. Toric IOL was inserted for each IOL when astigmatism correction was required by a toric calculator provided by the American Society of Cataract and Refractive Surgery (ASCRS) [15].

Patients were included with the following inclusion criteria: (1) older than 18 years and (2) transparent intraocular matters except for cataract. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) previous ocular trauma or intraocular surgery; (2) cornea opacities or pathology; (3) amblyopia; (4) strabismus; (5) abnormal pupils; (6) ocular infection; (7) complication during surgery; and (8) other autoimmune diseases.

All patients were scheduled for postoperative examinations at 1, 6, and 12 months. The preoperative examinations comprised slit-lamp examination, refractive status (Canon R-50, Canon USA Inc.), tonometry, corneal topography (Orbscan, Bausch & Lomb), wavefront aberrometry (OPD-Scan III, NIDEK Co. Ltd.), and biometry (IOLMaster 700, Carl Zeiss Meditec). Cataract surgery was performed under topical anesthesia using a 2.2-mm corneal incision, manual capsulorhexis with a 6.0-mm diameter, and IOL implantation in the bag. The postoperative examination measured monocular and binocular uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA), corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA), uncorrected intermediate visual acuity (UIVA) at 66 cm, uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA) at 33 cm. Visual defocus curves were monocularly and binocularly measured under the photopic condition by intervals of 0.50 spherical diopters from +0.50 to −4.00 D.

Contrast sensitivity was monocularly measured under uncorrected conditions using the Functional Acuity Contrast Test of the Ophtec 6500 test system (Stereo Optical Co Inc.) with spatial frequency at 1.5, 3, 6, 12, and 18 cycles/° (cpd) in the photopic (85 cd/m2) and mesopic conditions (3 cd/m2).

Patients filled out a questionnaire with overall satisfaction, the occurrence of photopic visual symptoms such as glare and halo, and dependence on spectacles for near vision for assessment. Overall satisfaction was asked using a 5-point Likert scale [16]: 1 = very dissatisfied, 2 = dissatisfied, 3 = neither satisfied nor dissatisfied, 4 = satisfied, and 5 = very satisfied. Photopic visual symptoms as glare and halo were scored on a 5-point scale from 1 = not at all, 2 = rarely, 3 = occasionally, 4 = often, and 5 = always. Patients were surveyed for the recommendation of their surgery to other people with allowed responses being yes or no.

The statistical analysis was done using the IBM SPSS ver. 21.0 (IBM Corp.). Preoperative data and postoperative outcomes were compared for each eye using the Wilcoxon signed rank test. The mean value for visual outcome, defocus curves, patients’ satisfaction, and spectacle independence were compared between groups using the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn post hoc correction. p-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

The study included 67 patients (17 male and 50 female patients), with a mean age of 62.79 ± 10.37 years. All patients completed the 1-month postoperative visit, while follow-up data at 6 and 12 months were available for the majority of patients. Preoperative manifest refraction was available in 129 of 134 eyes (96.3%), and the preoperative spherical equivalent values shown in Table 1 were calculated from these eyes. In the preoperative baseline subject and ocular characteristics (Table 1), uncorrected and best corrected visual acuities differed significantly between the eyes, whereas spherical equivalent, corneal astigmatism, and cataract grades showed no significant interocular differences.

In terms of mean monocular and binocular CDVA, UDVA, UIVA, and UNVA at 1, 6, and 12 months postoperatively (Table 2), significant differences among the three groups were observed in UDVA, UIVA, and UNVA at 1 and 6 months and in UIVA and UNVA at 12 months. At 12 months after surgery, the mean UDVA was 0.03 ± 0.06 logMAR in the EDOF IOL-implanted eyes, 0.07 ± 0.11 log-MAR in the trifocal IOL-implanted eyes, and 0.02 ± 0.06 logMAR under binocular conditions. The mean UIVA was 0.08 ± 0.08, 0.09 ± 0.11, and 0.03 ± 0.08 logMAR, respectively, and the mean UNVA was 0.51 ± 0.26, 0.22 ± 0.16, and 0.17 ± 0.12 logMAR, respectively. Post hoc analysis showed no significant differences between dominant (EDOF) and nondominant (trifocal) eyes in UDVA, UIVA, and CDVA (Table 3). At 1 month after surgery, UDVA (0.04 ± 0.07 logMAR vs. 0.06 ± 0.10 logMAR), UIVA (0.09 ± 0.10 logMAR vs. 0.12 ± 0.12 logMAR), and CDVA (0.04 ± 0.09 logMAR vs. 0.04 ± 0.06 logMAR) did not differ significantly between the eyes receiving the EDOF and trifocal IOLs (p = 0.254, p = 0.296, and p > 0.999, respectively), whereas monocular UNVA was significantly better in the trifocal IOL-implanted eyes (0.21 ± 0.17 logMAR vs. 0.44 ± 0.22 logMAR, p < 0.001). A similar pattern was observed at 6 and 12 months postoperatively, with no significant interocular differences in UDVA, UIVA, and CDVA, while UNVA remained significantly better in the trifocal IOL–implanted eyes at all time points (p < 0.001).

Regarding the binocular and monocular defocus curves at 1, 6, and 12 months after surgery (Fig. 1A–1C), the binocular defocus curves showed a better range of vision compared to each monocular defocus curve across all distances throughout the follow-up period. At 1 month after surgery, no significant differences in visual acuity were observed at vergences of +0.50 D and 0.00 D (distance) between the EDOF and trifocal IOL–implanted eyes (p > 0.999 and p = 0.254, respectively). The monocular defocus curve of the EDOF IOL-implanted eye showed significantly better visual acuity values at vergences of −0.50 and −1.00 D (distance 2 and 1 m) than the trifocal IOL-implanted eye (p = 0.003 and p < 0.001, respectively). At a vergence of −1.50 D (distance 66 cm), two eyes showed no significant difference (p = 0.296), but from −2.00 to −4.00 D (distance 50 to 25 cm), the trifocal IOL-implanted eye showed significantly better visual acuity values than EDOF IOL-implanted eye (p = 0.017, p < 0.001, p < 0.001, p < 0.001, and p < 0.001, respectively). The same pattern was consistently observed at 6 and 12 months postoperatively. Under binocular conditions, visual acuity remained better than Snellen 20/30 across a defocus range from +0.50 to −3.00 D throughout the entire follow-up period.

The contrast sensitivity was measured under both photopic and mesopic conditions in each eye implanted with the EDOF or trifocal IOL (Fig. 2A–2F). Under photopic conditions, the dominant (EDOF IOL–implanted) eye showed significantly higher contrast sensitivity at higher spatial frequencies (12 and 18 cycles/°) compared to the nondominant (trifocal IOL–implanted) eye at 1 and 6 months postoperatively. At 12 months, this difference persisted only at 18 cycles/°. Under mesopic conditions, no significant intergroup differences were observed at any spatial frequency throughout the follow-up period.

Postoperative satisfaction outcomes are summarized in Fig. 3A and 3B. At 1 month, 29 subjects (43.3%) reported being satisfied or very satisfied, with a mean satisfaction score of 3.2 ± 1.3. The proportion of satisfied patients slightly decreased to 41.1% at 6 months and 38.5% at 12 months, but remained relatively stable over time. Changes in satisfaction scores were observed in both directions, with 24 subjects showing improvement and 13 showing a decrease. Among the 13 subjects with decreased satisfaction, nine still reported overall satisfaction and indicated that they would recommend the surgery. Of the remaining four subjects, reasons for decreased satisfaction were identified as severe dryness (n = 2) and poor near vision (n = 2). The proportion of patients who would recommend the surgery was 80.6% at 1 month, 76.8% at 6 months, and 73.1% at 12 months.

In terms of the rate of visual disturbances (Fig. 4A, 4B), 25.4% of subjects reported that they experienced glare symptoms often or always, and 22.4% in halo symptoms 1 month after surgery. At 6 and 12 months after surgery, the proportions of patients reporting glare and halo symptoms showed a slight increase but remained relatively stable throughout the follow-up period. At 1 month after surgery, 49 subjects (73.1%) reported needing near glasses rarely or not at all, and similar proportions were maintained at 6 months (60.7%) and 12 months (73.1%) postoperatively (Fig. 5).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to figure out the clinical outcomes of bilateral mix-and-match implantation of TECNIS Symfony ZXR00 IOLs and AcrySof IQ Pan-Optix TFNT00 IOLs. In this study, mix-and-match implantation of an EDOF IOL and a trifocal IOL provided binocular visual acuity of 20/30 or better over a defocus range from +0.50 to −3.00 D, with a high level of spectacle independence.

We implanted the TECNIS Symfony ZXR00 IOL in the patient’s dominant eye and the AcrySof IQ PanOptix TFNT00 IOL in the nondominant eye by mix-and-match techniques. Previous studies have suggested that bilateral implantation of presbyopia-correcting IOLs may provide advantages in binocular visual function and stereopsis compared with unilateral implantation [17,18]. Gunenc and Celik [19] suggested that contralateral implantation of two different kinds of multifocal IOLs (refractive and diffractive multifocal IOLs) with cataract surgery might offer a better range of vision than using one kind of multifocal IOL. Eye dominance is also important in monovision [20]. The success of monovision depends on the suppression of intraocular blur, and this approach is based on the hypothesis that blur suppression may occur more readily in the nondominant eye [21]. This hypothesis may explain why correction of the dominant eye for distance vision and the nondominant eye for near vision has been associated with better patient acceptance, although the exact mechanism by which the blurred image from the fellow eye influences binocular near vision remains unclear [22]. Therefore, in this study, we decided to implant trifocal IOL which is known to have stronger near-vision than EDOF IOL into the nondominant eye.

Previous studies showed that TECNIS Symfony ZXR00 IOL has been shown to offer better outcomes for far and intermediate vision, while with a few disadvantages in the near vision [3]. However, ZXR00 IOL showed less postoperative photic phenomena compared with other multifocal IOLs [23]. AcrySof IQ PanOptix TFNT00 IOL showed significantly better near (at 40 cm) vision than Symfony IOL [8]. Especially, PanOptix IOL demonstrated a significantly 1-line better visual acuity at defocus curve level from vergence of −2.5 to −4.0 D than the Symfony IOL [24]. As shown in Fig. 1, our data show similar results. Throughout the postoperative follow-up period, Symfony IOL showed continuously good vision from a far distance of +0.5 D to −1.5 D (66 cm), and PanOptix IOL showed inferior visual acuity at a distance of −0.5 D (2 m) to −1.0 D (1 m) than Symfony IOL. But the visual acuity of PanOptix IOL increased from −1.5 D (66 cm) and peaked at −2.0 D (50 cm), and consequently showed better visual acuity than Symfony IOL from −2.0 D (50 cm) to closer −4.0 D (25 cm). The binocular visual outcome showed the advantages of each monocular vision. It showed good far-distance vision and good continuous vision leading up to the intermediate distance and showed over 20/30 visual acuity up to a vergence of −3.0 D (33 cm). At a distance closer than 33 cm, there was a sharp decline in visual acuity.

The contrast sensitivity of two IOL showed was within the normal range. Under photopic conditions, the dominant eye (implanted with the EDOF IOL) demonstrated significantly higher contrast sensitivity at higher spatial frequencies (12 and 18 cycles/°) (Fig. 2D, 2E) compared to the nondominant eye (implanted with the trifocal IOL) at 1 and 6 months postoperatively (p < 0.05). Although the difference was less pronounced at 12 months, the overall trend remained similar throughout the follow-up period. Plaza-Puche et al. [25] found that the contrast sensitivity of trifocal IOLs was not negatively inferior compared to bifocal IOLs even with the additional focus of trifocal IOLs in the intermediate range. Another study reported that EDOF IOLs performed better contrast sensitivity than trifocal IOLs [26]. Consistent with these findings, our results suggest that the Symfony IOL tends to provide better contrast sensitivity at higher spatial frequencies, although this difference was limited and not consistently observed across all conditions (Fig. 2).

Assessing patient satisfaction, a high proportion of subjects reported satisfaction after surgery, and although a slight decline was observed over time, overall satisfaction remained stable throughout the 12-month follow-up period. Furthermore, more than 70% of the patients consistently reported that they would recommend their surgery to others during the follow-up. Another study reported that about 80% of subjects were satisfied after mix-and-match implantation of EDOF IOL and bifocal IOL with a slight decrease over 3 months after surgery [27]. In this study, despite relatively moderate satisfaction rates, a higher proportion of patients expressed willingness to recommend the procedure, suggesting overall acceptance of the mix-and-match approach (Fig. 3).

Photic phenomena are usually the main reasons for multifocal IOL explantation [28]. In this study, the main cause of dissatisfaction was photic phenomena, as reported in the questionnaire. The proportion of subjects reporting glare increased slightly from 25.4% at 1 month to 30.8% at 12 months, and the proportion reporting halo increased from 22.4% to 26.9% during the same period (Fig. 4). Although the incidence of photic phenomena showed a mild increase over time, the overall satisfaction levels remained relatively stable throughout the 12-month follow-up period. It is interesting that the satisfaction scores increased in twenty-four subjects, whereas thirteen subjects showed a decrease. Among those whose satisfaction decreased, nine still reported overall satisfaction with the surgical outcomes and expressed willingness to recommend the procedure to others. Of the four subjects with a notable decline in satisfaction, two attributed this to severe dry eye symptoms and two to poor near vision. While early postoperative dissatisfaction is often related to photic phenomena, over time, ocular surface discomfort and near vision limitations may contribute more substantially to reduced satisfaction. Therefore, appropriate management of dry eye symptoms and sufficient preoperative counseling regarding near visual performance remain crucial for maintaining long-term patient satisfaction.

Independence from spectacles is one of the important factors in selecting multifocal IOL implantation [29]. In our study, 73.1% of subjects reported that they rarely or did not need spectacles at 1 month after surgery, 60.7% at 6 months, and 73.1% at 12 months (Fig. 5). Although there was a transient decrease at 6 months, the rate of spectacle independence recovered and remained stable by 12 months (Fig. 5). This result is inferior to about 80% of the independence result of bilateral trifocal IOL implantation [30,31]. Spectacle independence may vary depending on patients’ lifestyles, but our results still indicate a consistently high level of spectacle independence throughout the follow-up period. Additionally, binocular vision maintained visual acuity better than 20/30 at a distance of 33 cm (Fig. 1).

This study had several limitations, including its retrospective design, the absence of a control group, and the lack of preoperative contrast sensitivity data. In addition, the surgeries were performed between 2018 and 2022 using the TECNIS Symfony IOL, which was an established EDOF lens. Therefore, the findings of this study may not be directly generalizable to newer-generation presbyopia-correcting IOLs and contemporary surgical strategies.

However, compared with previous studies, this research included a relatively larger number of patients and provided a longer follow-up period of up to 12 months, allowing for a more comprehensive evaluation of postoperative visual and patient-reported outcomes. And it was thought that more interesting results would have been obtained if there were questionnaire data on preoperative near vision and photopic symptoms caused by cataracts.

The mix-and-match implantation of EDOF and trifocal IOL showed good visual outcomes through distal to the near distance over 12 months postoperation. This type of mix-and-match surgery compensates for the weakness of EDOF and trifocal IOL and has the advantage of being able to gain the strength of each IOL at the same time. More satisfied with near vision than EDOF IOL and less photopic complication than trifocal IOL implantation. Mix-and-match implantation can therefore be considered for reasonable choice in clinics.

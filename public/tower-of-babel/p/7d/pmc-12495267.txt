Bloomy Rind Sign in Leptomeningeal Metastasis of Primary Lung Adenocarcinoma
Authors: Wenyuan Tao, Xinhua Chen, Xin Zhang, Lijuan Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe bloomy rind sign, which appears as hyperintensities on FLAIR along the surface of the brainstem without gadolinium contrast enhancement, is a rare and early finding indicative of leptomeningeal metastasis in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, often associated with EGFR gene mutations.Keywords: bloomy rind sign, headache, leptomeningeal metastasis, lung adenocarcinoma

A woman in her 50s presented with vertigo, nausea, and vomiting for 2 months. She underwent lung surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy 6 years ago due to a primary lung adenocarcinoma. Investigation for cancer cell mutations was not performed since no metastasis had been detected in the pathology. On admission, her neurological examination revealed papilledema, neck stiffness, ataxia, and bilateral positive Babinski signs. While no abnormalities were detected by whole‐body fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography, brain magnetic resonance images (MRI) demonstrated symmetrical hyperintensity lesions along the ventral surface of the brainstem and the antero‐lateral surface of cerebellar peduncles on T2, fluid‐attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR), and diffusion weighted imaging (DWI) sequences (Figure 1A,B), with no gadolinium contrast enhancement (Figure 1C). The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) open pressure was higher than 400 mmH2O with a decreased level of glucose (2.13 mmol/L), chlorine (117.8 mmol/L), and an increased protein concentration (513.3 mg/L). CSF cytology revealed pleocytosis (12/uL) and the presence of cancer cells carrying an epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation (ex19del). Her symptoms, due to increased intracranial pressure, were relieved after the ventricular‐peritoneal shunt surgery, treatment with oral furmonertinib, and intrathecal injection with pemetrexed via Ommaya reservoir. At the 5‐month follow‐up, her MRI abnormalities along the brainstem and cerebellar peduncles slightly regressed, whereas a new lesion developed in the right cerebellum (Figure 1D–F).

(A–C) Axial brain MRI images on admission revealed symmetrical areas of increased signal intensity along the anterior and lateral surface of the brainstem on T2‐FLAIR sequences (A; white arrow), with restricted diffusion on DWI (B; white arrow) and no contrast enhancement on T1‐weighted images (C). (D–F) MRI findings at the 5‐month follow‐up showed decreased abnormalities along the brainstem and cerebellar peduncles (white arrow) but a newly developed lesion in the right cerebellum (white arrowhead).

The presence of non‐enhancing leptomeningeal metastasis, demonstrated as hyperintensities on FLAIR along the surface of the brainstem, has been described as the bloomy rind sign [1]. This is a rare and characteristic finding in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, primarily associated with EGFR gene mutations [2]. In this case, the bloomy rind sign reflects an early progression of cancer cells in CSF and on the brain surface, which was also prior to tumor metastasis in other organs. However, this sign is not exclusive to leptomeningeal metastasis, as it has also been shown in a case of varicella‐zoster virus brainstem meningoencephalitis [3]. Therefore, CSF cytological examination for tumor cells is crucial for the definitive diagnosis and treatment of early leptomeningeal metastasis presenting as the bloomy rind sign, especially in patients with primary lung adenocarcinoma.

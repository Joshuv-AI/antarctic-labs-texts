Prediction of postnatal treatment strategy in fetuses with isolated pulmonary stenosis
Authors: Ruan Peng, Xiao-Jing Zeng, Xia Yin, Li-Hong Wu, Ju Zheng, Hong-Ning Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFetuses with pulmonary stenosis (PS) may undergo surgical valvulotomy, pulmonary valvuloplasty via interventional catheterization or expectant management after birth, a predictive model for the possibility of surgery has not yet been established in previously published reports. This study aims to identify prenatal prognostic factors for a postnatal management approach in fetuses with isolated PS in a retrospective cohort of non-intervened fetuses between 24 and 34 weeks and to evaluate its performance prospectively.MethodsBaseline characteristics and outcomes of the study population were recorded. Prenatal cardiac and hemodynamic parameters were stratified by postnatal intervention treatment (surgical valvulotomy or percutaneous balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty) or expectant treatment at 1 year of age after birth. Classification and regression tree analysis was conducted to investigate the predictive capacity of prenatal echocardiographic parameters for postnatal treatment strategy at 1 year of age. We also prospectively validated it in the fetuses with isolated PS.ResultsA total of 71 live-born fetuses with isolated PS were enrolled from January 2014 to September 2022 in our institution for model construction. Among them, 37 received postnatal intervention treatment, including 17 who underwent surgical valvulotomy and 20 who underwent percutaneous balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty. The classification and regression tree model based on the right ventricular (RV) pressure, which was recorded as the maximum velocity of tricuspid valve regurgitation (TR-Vmax) combined with the RV/left ventricular (LV) length ratio and prenatal peak systolic velocity of the pulmonary valve (PV-PSV), showed a sensitivity of 100% and specificity of 100% for predicting postnatal outcomes. The highest probability of postnatal intervention treatment was attained for fetuses with TR-Vmax >135.5 cm/s (100%). The predictors were validated in another 10 fetuses from October 2022 to August 2023 in our institution prospectively. The sensitivity was 100% and specificity was 100% for predicting postnatal treatment strategy.ConclusionsPrenatal RV pressure estimates, which were demonstrated as TR-Vmax combined with the RV/LV length ratio and PV-PSV, could predict the postnatal management strategy for isolated PS with high sensitivity and specificity.Keywords: Pulmonary stenosis (PS), postnatal management, right ventricular/left ventricular length ratio (RV/LV length ratio), the maximum velocity of tricuspid valve regurgitation (TR-Vmax)

Introduction

The incidence of congenital pulmonary artery (PA) anomalies varies widely. Pulmonary stenosis (PS) and pulmonary atresia with intact ventricular septum (PAIVS) present heterogeneously (1). In fetuses with PS, their clinical presentation varies, depending on the type and severity of underlying lesions. Postnatal treatment for critical PS and PAIVS ranges from biventricular or univentricular circulation to one-and-a-half ventricular palliation (2).

PS is relatively common, accounting for 8–12% of all congenital heart defects in children (3). The most frequent cause of PS is excessive normal leaflet fusion, leading to bicuspid arterial valves. Fetuses with PS may undergo surgical valvulotomy or pulmonary valvuloplasty via interventional catheterization after birth, whereas others may receive expectant treatment. Identifying fetuses with isolated PS who require postnatal intervention treatment is crucial for prenatal counseling, optimizing perinatal care, and making decisions regarding fetal therapy.

Prenatal sonographic markers are used to predict the postnatal management approach, particularly the likelihood of univentricular or biventricular circulation after birth (4). The right ventricular (RV) size, tricuspid valve (TV) size, and coronary anomalies are regarded as the most important parameters for predicting postnatal outcomes (5,6). Nonetheless, models developed using small cohorts do not perform well in external validation, and their results are not as good as those reported in original publications (7) or in cohorts with relatively small sample sizes (8). Furthermore, a predictive model for the possibility of surgery has not yet been established in previously published reports in the literature, and some patients may focus more on the necessity of postnatal intervention treatment for newborn infants.

The current study aimed to establish a model of prenatal prognostic factors for postnatal management approach in a retrospective cohort of non-intervened fetuses between 24 and 34 weeks and to evaluate its performance. In this study, findings of isolated PS were distinguished from more severe findings of PAIVS. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD+AI reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2025-1-2669/rc).

Methods

Population selection

All cases of isolated PS diagnosed prenatally from January 2014 to September 2022 at The First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University for prenatal diagnosis and fetal echocardiography were retrospectively assessed as training dataset. Fetal echocardiographic findings at 24–34 weeks of gestation were reviewed. Isolated PS was diagnosed in fetuses with a high-velocity flow across the pulmonary valve (PV) (>100 cm/s), a color signal showing laminar flow up to the annular level and turbulent flow with a mosaic pattern from the annulus onwards, thickened and hyperechoic PV, absent ventricular septal defect, and normal offset of the tricuspid leaflets. (9) PS in recipient twins of a monochorionic twin pregnancy complicated by twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome was also included in this study. A prenatal diagnosis of PS was confirmed using postnatal echocardiography and/or surgery in all cases. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the ethics committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. For the postnatal follow-up, written consent for the children was provided by their legal guardians.

Measurements

A standardized fetal structural survey was conducted according to the guidelines proposed by the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology (10). Fetal echocardiography was performed using a segmental approach with standardized anatomical planes, incorporating pulsed waves and color Doppler imaging (11,12). A detailed anatomical evaluation was carried out after diagnosing PS to exclude intracardiac and extracardiac anomalies. The biparietal diameter, head circumference, abdominal circumference, femoral length, and humeral length were measured in fetuses, and their gestational age was determined. Fetal echocardiographic images and video clips were acquired during an episode of fetal apnea and were stored on an ultrasound system (GE Medical Systems, Kretztechnik, Zipf, Austria) for the final analysis. The operators were blinded to the clinical outcome data.

The tricuspid and mitral annulus diameters were measured at end-diastole in the four-chamber view. Raw measurements of the TV and mitral valve were transformed into Z-scores based on the gestational age derived from fetal biological measurements. The RV and left ventricular (LV) lengths were measured from the atrioventricular valve to the apex of the ventricular cavity at end-diastole in the four-chamber view; subsequently, the RV/LV length ratio was calculated (Figure 1). Z-scores were calculated using the data of Schneider et al. (13). The PA/aorta (AO) ratio was also recorded. The presence and degree of TV regurgitation (TR) and/or PV regurgitation (PR) were evaluated using color Doppler imaging. The maximum velocity of TR (TR-Vmax) was measured in the four-chamber view. Color Doppler imaging revealed reversed flow into the ductus arteriosus and the flow direction was also recorded. Ultrasound images and videos of the examination during 24–34 gestational weeks were collected for the examination. Two-dimensional measurements were performed three times on different cardiac cycles, and the average value was used for the final analysis. The operators are blinded to the postnatal treatment approach for the infants with isolated PS.

Clinical data

Karyotype analysis and genetic testing with chromosomal microarray analysis using amniotic fluid or umbilical cord blood were recommended for fetuses with a prenatally suspected diagnosis of PS. Clinical outcomes of the included infants, including neonatal complications and anatomical findings determined by neonatal echocardiography/computed tomography angiography or surgical procedures, were collected and reviewed. According to the guidelines for the management of congenital heart disease, PS can be classified as follow: mild stenosis: peak gradient ≤36 mmHg; moderate stenosis: peak gradient between 36 and 64 mmHg; severe stenosis: peak gradient ≥64 mmHg (14). Pediatricians obtained the clinical findings, postnatal examination findings, and treatment data. All included fetuses were divided into two groups according to the postnatal treatment approach at 1 year of age—namely, the postnatal intervention treatment group (surgical valvulotomy or percutaneous balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty) and the expectant management group. The determinants of surgical valvulotomy or balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty were as follows: percutaneous balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty is effective in reducing the PV gradient in patients with moderate or severe isolated PV stenosis; Surgical valvotomy is usually performed in severe PS when the pulmonary annulus is not hypoplastic and percutaneous balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty is not applicable (15).

Statistics

Analyses were performed using R version 4.0.5 (https://www.r-project.org/, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All continuous variables were assessed for normal distribution using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables were compared between the postnatal intervention treatment group and expectant management group at 1 year of age using the independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U-test. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages (%), and univariate comparisons of categorical variables were performed between the two subgroups using Fisher’s exact test. The study size was acquired according to the formula that n = Z21-α/2×p(1−p)/d2, α =0.05, p =0.1, d =0.05.

Decision-tree analysis based on the classification and regression tree analysis was conducted to investigate the predictive capacity of maternal age, gestational age, RV/LV length ratio, RV length Z-score, PA/AO ratio, TV Z-score, TR-Vmax, presence of TR, presence of PR, and presence of reversed flow into the ductus arteriosus for the postnatal treatment approach at 1 year of age. A predictive model of the postnatal management approach for fetuses with PS was established. The sensitivity, specificity, F1 score and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) were calculated. Five-fold cross-validation was used for internal validation. The model was prospectively validated in the fetuses with isolated PS from October 2022 to August 2023 in our institution prospectively. A two-sided P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 158 fetuses with PS without prenatal intervention were identified at our institution from January 2014 to September 2022. Among these fetuses, 85 were excluded owing to termination of pregnancy/intrauterine fetal death/selective reduction (n=25), lack of postnatal data (n=14), and gestational age <24 or >34 weeks (n=37). Additionally, one fetus who experienced neonatal death at 54 days after birth and ten fetuses without PS after birth were excluded. The false positive rate was 12.2%. Finally, a total of 71 participants were included in the final analysis to establish a predictive model.

Figure 2 presents the outcomes of all fetuses with PS at the final follow-up in the training dataset. The median follow-up duration was 36 months (range: 14–95 months) for the cohort. Thirty-nine (54.9%) patients had mild-to-moderate postnatal PS and were treated with either balloon valvuloplasty (n=5) or expectant management (n=34). Thirty-two (45.1%) patients had severe PS and were treated postnatally with surgical valvulotomy (n=17) or balloon valvuloplasty (n=15). Furthermore, 37 (52.1%) patients ultimately ended up with the postnatal intervention treatment (17 cases of surgical valvulotomy and 20 cases of pulmonary valvuloplasty via interventional catheterization), whereas 34 (47.9%) patients underwent postnatal expectant management. In the intervention treatment group, the initial treatment in two patients was balloon valvuloplasty, which failed; therefore, these two patients underwent surgical valvulotomy. Additionally, one neonate underwent a technically successful surgical valvulotomy but died at 12 days postoperatively.

Figure 3 shows the results of classification and regression tree analysis based on all diagnosed live-born fetuses with PS. The best prediction for postnatal intervention treatment at 1 year of age could be achieved using a combination of TR-Vmax, RV/LV length ratio and peak systolic velocity of the PV (PV-PSV) at different cut-off values. The classification and regression tree algorithm selects these predictors, including TR-Vmax, the RV/LV length ratio, and PSV-PV, because these parameters reduced the mean squared error compared to all other measured variables at the selected decision points. The classification and regression tree model for predicting the postnatal outcomes of isolated PS at 1 year after live birth had a sensitivity of 100% and specificity of 100%. The highest probability for a postnatal expectant treatment in this cohort was attained for fetuses with TR-Vmax ≤135.5 cm/s (81.0%) combined with RV/LV length ratio ≥0.766 (94.4%) and PV-PSV ≤254.5 cm/s (100%). Fetuses with TR-Vmax >135.5 cm/s had a low probability of 0% for a postnatal expectant treatment, whereas all fetuses with TR-Vmax >135.5 cm/s underwent postnatal intervention treatment at 1 year of age. The F1 score of the predictive model was 1.0. The AUC of the model in the internal validation using five-fold cross-validation was 1.0.

From October 2022 to August 2023, a total of 10 fetuses were included as a validation cohort (Table 1). In the validation cohort, the sensitivity and specificity were 100% and 100% for prediction of postnatal treatment strategy, respectively.

Discussion

In this study, we reviewed all fetuses with isolated PS and their live-born outcomes. Based on data from the last 9 years, we presented a model for predicting the postnatal treatment approach in fetuses with isolated PS. The model combined fetal cardiac morphological and hemodynamic parameters and demonstrated excellent performance in predicting whether a fetus would have to undergo intervention treatment or expectant management after birth, which is important for parental counseling.

Prenatal detection of PS, even critical PS, was significantly lower than that of PAIVS. The reported prenatal detection rate of PAIVS was varying from 50% to 68.2% (16-18) and the reported prenatal detection rate of PS was only 19.2% (19). PV anomalies might have been undetected for multiple reasons. First, the TV size in the four-chamber view is normal or almost normal during the second trimester (20). PS is a progressive congenital heart disease, and PV stenosis may occur during the third trimester and even after birth (21). Second, the outflow tract views of the RV are always obtained as grayscale still-frame images during echocardiographic screening (22). Mild forms of isolated PS without any consequences in the RV can only be diagnosed directly by visualization of thickening and dysplasia of the PV leaflets throughout the cardiac cycle or indirectly by visualization of post-stenotic dilatation at the origin of the main PA. If PS is suspected prenatally, irrespective of whether it is mild or critical, a thorough assessment should be performed using both pulsed and color Doppler imaging. A complete ultrasound evaluation is also valuable to exclude associated PV regurgitation and/or TR, reversed flow into the ductus arteriosus, and abnormal blood flow in the ductus venosus. The maximum velocity of TR was also measured and recorded, if available. If the fetus is suspected to have PS, a series of ultrasound examinations is recommended to exclude the diagnosis of PS.

The RV/LV length ratio and TR-Vmax are easy to use for the prediction of postnatal management strategy for fetuses with PS. Previous studies showed that TV Z-score ≤−3 at diagnosis and TV growth or different cutoff values of TV Z-score at different gestational weeks or TV Z-score ≤−4 at diagnosis are important parameters for predicting postnatal univentricular circulation (23-26). However, the TV Z-score did not predict whether the neonates had to undergo intervention treatment in this study. This may be because the participants in our study were different from those in previous studies, and almost half of the patients had mild-to-moderate PS, in whom the TV was almost normal. Previous studies also focused on the postnatal circulation approach for fetuses with critical PS and PAIVS, and the TV size was usually abnormal. These reasons led to different results. Furthermore, biomarkers were also associated with the postnatal balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty in the patients with PS. For example, troponin correlates with the severity of PS and associates with RV dysfunction (27).

Recording of maximal Doppler jet velocities of the PV appears to provide a reliable measure of the severity of valvular pulmonic stenosis (28). In the presence of moderate-to-severe stenosis, RV filling characteristics increase and end-diastolic pressures tend to rise, favoring an increase in the right-to-left shunting through the foramen ovale. The secondary fall in RV output may cause the variation of peak velocity measurements. However, the results of this study demonstrated that PV-PSV was valuable for predict the postnatal management strategy for isolated PS.

In this study, we present a classification and regression tree model for identifying fetuses with the possibility of undergoing intervention treatment at 1 year of age after birth. In this model, the best prediction for the postnatal treatment approach was achieved using a combination of TR-Vmax, RV/LV length ratio, and PV-PSV. TR-Vmax ≤135.5 cm/s alone predicted the expectant management outcome in 81.0% (34/42) of the cases regardless of RV pressure and velocity across the PV. For the prediction of postnatal expectant management, if the RV was relatively large (assessed by RV/LV length ratio ≥0.766), this increased the likelihood of postnatal expectant treatment to 94.4% (34/36), which was further increased to 100% (34/34) when combined with PV-PSV <254.5 cm/s. PV stenosis could result in increased RV pressure, leading to TR. If the flow across the outflow tract of the right side of the heart is still forward, TR-Vmax may accurately reflect the extent of PS.

The main advantage of our study is that we identified prenatal risk factors for postnatal intervention management, including one anatomical parameter (RV/LV length ratio) and two hemodynamic parameters (TR-Vmax and PV-PSV), are needed to predict the likelihood of a postnatal management strategy (expectant management or intervention treatment). RV/LV length ratio was used to evaluate the relative variation of RV size to the LV size. Thus, the marker of RV/LV length ratio could reflect the decrease of the RV size and we do not need to consider the heterogeneity of the gestational age. The parameters of TR Vmax and PSV-PV are two features of fetal cardiac hemodynamics not cardiac structures, which may be influenced the cardiovascular disease of PS and heterogeneity of variable gestational age may have relatively tiny association on these two parameters. Additionally, 5-fold cross-validation was used for internal validation, and the results indicated excellent performance. In addition, the potential risk factors have been prospectively validated in our institution, and the performance was also excellent. The findings of this study could provide useful information for individual risk stratification and could inform fetal intervention decision-making greatly.

The current study has limitations, particularly its retrospective design. In this study, we did not correlate the postnatal treatment approach with the functional measurements of RV, for example, evaluation the strain or strain rate of the myocardium with speckle tracking imaging. We did not use z-scores of cardiac structures as predictors for the postnatal management approach. Several pregnancies were terminated because of policies implemented in our country, and the excluded cases may represent the most severe disease spectrum of PS, potentially biasing the model toward better outcomes. We are uncertain that whether the relatively short follow-up period in some infants had a significant influence on the results. Additionally, the neonates were treated in different cardiac centers, and the non-standardized postnatal management strategies at different cardiac centers may have introduced a potential bias with regard to the rates of expectant management of clinical outcomes. Furthermore, we did not evaluate longitudinal changes in the PV. At last, because of the small sample, the classification and regression tree model performed excellently on the training data but may fail to generalize on new data. As a result, its predictive accuracy may drop significantly in real-world applications. The sample size was not large enough so we did not divide the cases with postnatal intervention into surgical valvulotomy and balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty subgroups. In the future, we intend to perform the analysis in the subgroups.

Conclusions

In conclusion, prenatal RV pressure estimates, which were demonstrated as TR-Vmax combined with the RV/LV length ratio and PV-PSV, are specific echo measurements in 3rd trimester, and these parameters could predict the postnatal management strategy for isolated PS with high sensitivity and specificity. These parameters can be easily determined using prenatal ultrasound and measured with high reliability. The findings of this study provide crucial information for clinical counseling.

Availability, Prices, and Affordability of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors: A Cross-Sectional Study in Anhui Province, China
Authors: Tongming Zhu, Kunkun Ge, Aizong Shen, Yingqi Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have revolutionized cancer therapy, yet their uptake in low- and middle-income areas is hindered by high prices and uneven distribution. Anhui Province, China—characterized by marked urban–rural economic contrasts—offers a critical setting to examine real-world access to these agents. The objective of this study was to assess the availability, price levels, and affordability of ICIs across Anhui Province and to identify policy levers that could narrow observed access gaps.Materials and MethodsWe applied the World Health Organization/Health Action International (WHO/HAI) standardized survey methodology to evaluate the availability, price differentials, and economic burden of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) marketed in Anhui Province, China. The survey was conducted in 2025 across 199 public hospitals. Affordability was further assessed using a four-tier framework comprising the WHO/HAI standard indicator (defined as the number of days’ wages required to afford 30 days of treatment), adjusted using local urban and rural per capita disposable income to account for China’s socioeconomic heterogeneity, and incorporating insurance reimbursement scenarios on the basis of Anhui provincial policy (85% for urban employee insurance and 70% for urban–rural resident insurance) to estimate out-of-pocket expenditure. In addition, catastrophic health expenditure (CHE), defined as household out-of-pocket health spending exceeding 40% of non-food expenditure, was used to capture household-level financial risk beyond individual income-based measures. Availability was defined as the proportion of facilities stocking a medicine on the survey day. Prices were collected at unit level and summarized as medians across facilities, with affordability assessed using the median price ratio (MPR), calculated as median local unit price relative to the international reference price (IRP) from the MSH International Drug Price Indicator Guide, in line with WHO/HAI methodology.ResultsSurveying 199 hospitals, we found that domestic PD-1 inhibitors—sintilimab, camrelizumab, and tislelizumab—were stocked in roughly 4 out of 5 facilities, whereas 11 of the 15 mainly imported ICIs appeared in fewer than 16% of hospitals; although some imported agents had acceptable median-price ratios (0–2), their absolute prices remained several-fold higher than domestic alternatives; thus even after insurance (85% urban, 70% rural), a year’s treatment with cadonilimab or durvalumab still exceeded the catastrophic-expenditure threshold by up to 40 times for rural households, while domestic sintilimab or camrelizumab stayed well below that line.ConclusionsDomestic ICIs are broadly available and relatively affordable, whereas imported brands remain scarce and financially out of reach; boosting reimbursement limits, expanding centralized procurement, and favoring cost-effective domestic options may help reduce Anhui’s access gap, although their implementation should take budget constraints and opportunity costs into account.

Key points for Decision Makers

Access to immune checkpoint inhibitors varies widely across Anhui Province, with lower availability in less developed areas and smaller hospitals.Lower-cost domestic medicines are more widely available and place less financial burden on patients, while higher-priced imported medicines remain difficult to access for many households.Improving insurance coverage and procurement strategies may help reduce access gaps, although such measures need to be balanced against overall healthcare budget constraints.

Introduction

Cancer cells evade immune detection through mechanisms such as immune checkpoints, which suppress T cell activity, allowing cancer progression. Immune checkpoints regulate immune responses by modulating ligand-receptor interactions [1, 2].Cancer cells alter immune ligands such as programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) to inhibit T cell function. Binding of PD-L1 to programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptors on T cells induces “exhaustion,” thereby impairing the immune response [3]. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) target immune checkpoints, such as the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway, restoring T cell function and enhancing immune-mediated tumor cell elimination. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved three ICIs for metastatic melanoma, a cancer with high mortality rates despite surgery or targeted therapy [4, 5]. Additionally, ICIs have shown excellent results in clinical trials for lung, prostate, and kidney cancers, demonstrating their significant therapeutic potential [6]. ICIs are categorized into PD-1 inhibitors (e.g., nivolumab, pembrolizumab), PD-L1 inhibitors (e.g., atezolizumab, durvalumab), and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) inhibitors (e.g., ipilimumab), all of which have proven effective in treating various malignant tumors, including lung cancer, melanoma, and liver cancer.

In 2022, there were nearly 20 million new cancer cases worldwide and approximately 9.7 million cancer-related deaths. Asia accounted for nearly half (49.2%) of new cases and more than half (56.1%) of cancer deaths [7]. However, in many low- and middle-income countries, the high cost of anticancer medications limits affordability and access for large populations [8].

To ensure fair and transparent pricing of ICIs, it is crucial to establish a system that generates reliable, high-quality data to guide appropriate pricing models. Understanding the factors that influence ICI availability, pricing, and affordability is essential before designing effective interventions to enhance access. Previous studies have assessed the accessibility of certain anticancer drugs. For instance, Chen [9] reported that in Hubei Province, China, among 32 anticancer drugs surveyed, 10 originator drugs were unavailable in both secondary and tertiary hospitals, while a total of 21 originator drugs were unavailable in at least one surveyed hospital across all hospital levels. In contrast, most generic drugs were readily available in secondary hospitals, with tertiary hospitals generally showing higher availability than secondary ones. Regarding pricing, generic drugs were generally affordable, while originator drugs had significantly higher retail prices. A survey in Thailand [10] found that out of 98 drugs, 55 were priced above international reference standards, including 28 anticancer medications. Similarly, Moye-Holz’s research in Mexico [11] showed that essential cancer drugs were less available than World Health Organization targets in both public and private healthcare settings, and affordability was also poor. Overall, the accessibility of anticancer drugs remains inadequate.

Anhui Province, one of China’s most populous regions, had a permanent population of 61.21 million by the end of 2023. The latest cancer registry report from Anhui indicated a malignant tumor incidence rate of 282.45 per 100,000 (313.42 for male and 250.12 for female individuals), significantly higher than the Chinese standard rate of 182.61 and the global rate of 178.85 per 100,000. The most prevalent malignant tumors in Anhui include lung, stomach, esophageal, colorectal, liver, and breast cancers.

Given the increasing cancer incidence and mortality rates in China, enhancing the accessibility of anticancer medications is vital for cancer treatment and prevention policies. While international and regional studies have explored ICI accessibility and pricing, systematic research on ICI supply and the economic burden on patients in eastern China, especially at the provincial level, is limited. This study is the first to encompass medical institutions across all 16 cities in Anhui Province, thoroughly examining the supply and pricing of ICIs in regions with varying economic statuses and hospital levels, thus addressing a significant research gap. Utilizing the World Health Organization/Health Action International (WHO/HAI) methodology for drug price and availability surveys, a cross-sectional study was conducted in all 16 cities of Anhui Province. Additionally, data on anticancer medications were gathered from 199 secondary and higher-level healthcare institutions in Anhui for the year 2023.

This research was carried out in the context of national medical reforms, including insurance negotiations and centralized drug procurement, aimed at enhancing the accessibility of new anticancer medications and alleviating patients’ financial burdens [12]. The study’s findings offer essential evidence for the government to develop scientifically sound and effective policies, promoting the equitable distribution and broad utilization of new anticancer drugs.

Materials and Methods

Study Period, Region, and Institutions

This study was conducted in Anhui Province from January to December 2023, following WHO/HAI standardized survey methods. A total of 199 public hospitals, including 75 secondary and 124 tertiary hospitals, were surveyed across 16 cities in the province. The cities were ranked A–P on the basis of per capita disposable income in 2023.

Drug Selection

We selected 15 immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) on the basis of the 2018 National Essential Medicines List and the 2023 National Medical Insurance Drug List, considering tumor incidence rates in Anhui. Data Collection

Data collection was standardized using WHO/HAI forms. Six trained investigators ensured accuracy and consistency, minimizing errors through spreadsheet verification.

Evaluation Criteria

This study focused on three main evaluation indicators: drug availability, price levels, and affordability.

Following the WHO/HAI methodology, drug availability is defined as the percentage of surveyed institutions that can provide a specific drug. Price Assessment

Price evaluation was conducted using the median price ratio (MPR), defined as the ratio of the median retail price of a drug in the local market to its international reference price. Local drug prices were sourced from Anhui’s procurement data, while international reference prices were obtained from MSH2015 guidelines and Pharmacy Checker [14].

Formula: MPR = Median unit price of the drug / International reference price.

Given that the drugs included are new anticancer medications with relatively high costs, MPR between 0 and 2 was considered acceptable, and MPR above 2 was deemed high [15]. Only ICIs with available international reference prices were included in the MPR calculation.

Affordability Assessment

Affordability of new anticancer drugs was evaluated using the WHO/HAI recommended method on the basis of median progression-free survival (mPFS) and catastrophic health expenditure indicators.

Affordability is defined as the total cost of a standard treatment regimen relative to the daily income of nontechnical government workers. Due to the absence of specific data for nontechnical government workers in China, the average of the four minimum wage categories in Anhui Province for 2023 was used. A drug is considered affordable if the cost for a 30-day treatment does not exceed 1 day’s disposable income.

Given the urban–rural disparities in China, an adjusted WHO/HAI method was applied using per capita disposable daily income from Anhui’s regional statistical bureaus [16].

For ICIs included in medical insurance, assuming patients are hospitalized, reimbursement rates were set at 85% for urban employees’ medical insurance and 70% for the New Rural Cooperative Medical Scheme. Affordability was also measured by the ratio of annual out-of-pocket drug costs to catastrophic health expenditure, with a ratio < 1 indicating affordability and > 1 indicating unaffordability, leading to poverty. Catastrophic health expenditure is defined as out-of-pocket medical costs exceeding 40% of nonsubsistence household expenditures, a key indicator of health equity [17].

Combined Analysis of Availability and Affordability

To assess the combined status of drug availability and affordability, a four-quadrant chart was created. Drug availability was set at a 50% threshold, and affordability at a ratio of 1. The Y-axis represents affordability values, while the X-axis represents availability.

Statistical Analysis

Data entry and preliminary organization were performed using Excel 2017, while statistical analysis was conducted using the testing methods available in GraphPad Prism. The study analyzed differences in the MPR values and affordability of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) across healthcare facilities of different levels and types. A significance level of P < 0.05 was used to indicate statistically significant differences, whereas P > 0.05 suggested no significant differences.

Tertiary hospitals had a clear advantage in ICI availability. As regional medical centers, they cater to patients with advanced or complex cancers, necessitating the use of novel, high-cost drugs (e.g., pembrolizumab), leading to higher procurement and usage. In contrast, secondary hospitals primarily supply lower-cost domestic ICIs (e.g., sintilimab and camrelizumab). These drugs are widely covered by insurance, suitable for treating common cancers, and affordable, reducing patients’ financial burdens.

From a drug-specific perspective, sintilimab and camrelizumab demonstrated high and consistent availability across both secondary and tertiary hospitals. Their broad insurance coverage and indications enable widespread use at all healthcare levels. Conversely, pembrolizumab and nivolumab were significantly more available in tertiary hospitals, reflecting their higher prices and the need for advanced medical services (e.g., dosage guidance and side effect management). Additionally, atezolizumab and nivolumab had nearly zero availability in both hospital levels, possibly due to their exclusion from insurance coverage and limited demand.

Health insurance policies significantly impacted the affordability of ICIs. Urban residents benefited from higher reimbursement rates (85%), resulting in a substantially lower financial burden compared with rural residents. The study also found that patients with out-of-pocket expenses exceeding 30%, particularly for high-cost drugs such as cadonilimab and durvalumab, often faced economic pressure that could lead to treatment discontinuation or abandonment. This study analyzed the relationship between drug affordability and availability using a quadrant chart (Fig. 5), uncovering significant inequalities in market distribution. The results showed that domestic PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (e.g., camrelizumab, sintilimab, tislelizumab, and toripalimab) exhibited the notable advantage of low affordability burden and high availability, clustering in the lower-right quadrant. These drugs, due to their low pricing strategies and extensive insurance coverage, have become the primary therapeutic options in the market, offering cost-effective immunotherapy solutions for patients in low- and middle-income regions. Discussion

This study evaluates the availability, pricing, and affordability of ICIs in Anhui Province, revealing significant disparities across regions, healthcare institutions, and income groups. It analyzes ICI supply at various levels of medical institutions, price variations, and the financial burden on households. These findings provide a detailed understanding of drug accessibility in clinical applications and explore critical factors influencing their widespread use, particularly the role of pricing and insurance policies in shaping patient affordability. These findings provide critical scientific evidence for policy optimization and resource allocation while offering specific recommendations for therapeutic choices in clinical practice. Domestic ICIs, supported by low-cost strategies and broad insurance coverage, have emerged as the primary therapeutic choices, while imported drugs remain concentrated in high-income regions due to high prices and limited insurance support.

ICIs have proven to be effective in treating late-stage cancers by improving survival rates. However, their high cost remains a major barrier to widespread use, especially in economically disadvantaged regions. This study highlights the availability and affordability challenges of ICIs in Anhui Province, showing significant regional disparities. Larger hospitals and cities have better access to these drugs, while rural areas and smaller healthcare facilities face shortages, compounded by limited procurement capacity and inadequate distribution networks. In addition, the uptake of some ICIs may be hindered by limited promotional efforts or distribution network constraints.

The price differences between large and small hospitals further contribute to the economic burden on patients. Price differences between large and small hospitals were observed, with lower prices generally seen in larger hospitals. This may be related to differences in procurement mechanisms and pricing arrangements across healthcare levels. For patients in low-income groups, these price disparities often result in treatment discontinuation. Although insurance coverage is widespread, reimbursement rates for expensive drugs such as ICIs are still insufficient, leaving many patients unable to afford full treatment. Research [30] indicates that although insurance policies partially alleviate these pressures, insufficient reimbursement for expensive drugs often leaves patients unable to afford full treatment costs. This issue is particularly pronounced among low-income groups. While some patients receive partial reimbursement through insurance, out-of-pocket expenses remain a significant barrier to treatment. Our study data show that for some low-income households, the cost of ICIs accounts for a substantial portion of their income, affecting their treatment decisions.

Globally, the availability and affordability of ICIs vary significantly between countries. In high-income nations such as the USA and parts of Europe, ICIs are often included in national healthcare systems, significantly reducing patients’ financial burdens [31]. However, even in these countries, patients without insurance or with insufficient coverage may still face substantial out-of-pocket costs [32]. In contrast, low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) encounter greater challenges. Studies in Thailand and Mexico show that ICI prices often exceed international reference standards, with limited availability in public healthcare facilities [7, 8]. Similarly, research in India reveals that most patients cannot afford ICI treatments without external financial support [33]. These findings align with our study in Anhui Province, where high costs and limited insurance coverage significantly restrict access to ICIs in economically underdeveloped areas. The pricing and affordability challenges of ICIs not only affect treatment outcomes for patients with cancer, but also impose substantial economic burdens on society.

To enhance drug availability and reduce patient burdens, this study recommends strengthening cancer drug price regulation and promoting pricing transparency at the policy level. Specifically, health insurance policies could be further optimized to improve reimbursement for anticancer drugs, although such adjustments should be considered in the context of budget constraints and opportunity costs. Additionally, local governments should be encouraged to provide subsidies to reduce the financial burden on low-income groups. Furthermore, establishing drug procurement platforms, promoting centralized procurement, and encouraging cross-regional resource sharing could help address uneven drug distribution. Our findings support these policy directions, particularly in improving insurance reimbursement and centralized drug purchasing to alleviate patients’ financial stress.

However, several limitations should be noted. The reference prices derived from the MSH 2015 International Drug Price Indicator Guide may not fully reflect current market conditions, particularly for innovative oncology biologics, whose prices have evolved substantially due to patent status changes, market competition, and national reimbursement negotiations. In addition, most immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) included in this study are patented monoclonal antibodies, and their pricing is influenced by heterogeneous health system structures, negotiation mechanisms, and market sizes across countries, resulting in substantial inter-country price variation; thus, international reference prices primarily indicate relative rather than absolute price differences. To mitigate these limitations, MPR in this study was interpreted strictly as a within-province comparative indicator across different ICIs in Anhui, rather than for cross-country price comparisons, thereby reducing bias associated with outdated or noncomparable reference values.

Future research could further explore regional and income-based disparities in drug availability and evaluate the impact of policy measures, such as insurance reform and centralized procurement, on drug accessibility and affordability. Moreover, as China continues to optimize cancer drug procurement and manufacturing policies, notable changes in the pricing and supply of domestic ICIs are expected, warranting further investigation into their market performance and consequent effects on affordability, while updated global pharmaceutical price databases may improve the precision of future MPR-based assessments.

Early warning of drug-induced cardiotoxicity: quantitative determination of Mfn2 biomarker via electrochemical immunosensing technology
Authors: Zixia Wang, Mian Chen, Diaoguo Li, Bolu Sun, Jiali Kang, Haiying He, Ying Lv, Xuanxiu Da, Miao Zhou, Yong Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDrug-induced cardiotoxicity (DIC) poses a significant challenge in both drug development and clinical practice, Making early and accurate assessment crucial. Existing studies have shown that mitochondrial fusion mediated by mitochondrial fusion protein 2 (Mfn2) is closely associated with DIC, and that upregulation of Mfn2 reduces drug-induced cardiomyocyte damage and apoptosis, suggesting that Mfn2 could be a potential biomarker for early warning assessment of DIC. Due to the defects of cumbersome operation, limited sensitivity, high cost, and difficulty in popularization of existing detection technologies, this study pioneers a novel electrochemical immunosensor for highly sensitive Mfn2 detection using a composite sensing substrate of MXene-derived sodium titanate nanorods and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (M-NTO-MWCNT). This technology enables early warning and assessment of drug-induced cardiotoxicity. The M-NTO component, with its unique nanorod structure, abundant active sites, and high surface area, significantly enhances sensitivity and provides abundant antibody immobilization sites. Meanwhile, MWCNTs improve electron transfer efficiency and selectivity due to their superior conductivity and interconnected network. Under optimized conditions, the sensor achieves a detection Limit as low as 1.85 ng mL−1 and a Linear range of 9.38 × 10−1–2.40 × 102 ng mL−1. Serum sample testing demonstrated excellent reproducibility (RSD < 5%), outperforming conventional ELISA methods. This study provides a new rapid and highly sensitive test solution for the early warning of drug-derived cardiotoxicity, offering technical support and scientific basis for safety assessment in new drug development. Introduction

Drug-induced cardiotoxicity (DIC) refers to pharmacological effects wherein drugs or their metabolites impair normal cardiac physiological function [1]. This phenomenon has become increasingly prominent in both drug development and clinical practice, emerging as a critical issue requiring urgent attention [2]. DIC is characterized by multifactorial origins, high incidence, potential lethality, and insidious onset. Its pathogenic mechanisms primarily involve mitochondrial dysfunction [3], oxidative stress [4], and dysregulation of cardiomyocyte ion channels [5]. Although the heart is not the most common target organ for adverse drug reactions, numerous pharmaceuticals can induce severe cardiac events, manifesting as cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, myocardial ischemia, heart failure, and structural/functional alterations [6]. Notably, DIC represents a major challenge during early-stage drug development [7].

Early diagnosis and real-time monitoring of drug-derived cardiotoxicity (DIC) are crucial for drug safety assessment and clinical management. Currently, methods such as electrocardiography [1], cardiac magnetic resonance imaging [1], endomyocardial tissue biopsy [1], echocardiography [8], and biomarkers [9] are mainly used to monitor the effects of drugs on the heart. Although these methods have improved the ability to predict DIC to some extent, they still have limitations. For example, there are problems such as radiation risk, demanding operational requirements, time-consuming and difficult to be portable and popularized, and they are not suitable for dynamic monitoring. In addition, some studies [10] mentioned other shortcomings of the existing monitoring tools for DIC, such as echocardiographic results with examiner variability and the insensitivity of LVEF for the detection of early subclinical cardiac diseases, and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging equipment with high operational requirements, high cost, and time-consuming. In summary, the current commonly used monitoring methods for DIC in clinical practice still have certain limitations. Therefore, searching for a detection method that best meets clinical requirements is of great significance for the early warning and diagnosis of DIC.

As a transmembrane dynamin located in the outer mitochondrial membrane, Mfn2 is mainly involved in the regulation of mitochondrial fusion, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and mitochondrial autophagy, as well as cellular energy metabolism and apoptosis [11]. Several studies have shown that Mfn2 is essential for the maintenance of mitochondrial function and has a protective effect against ischemia–reperfusion injury, heart failure, and other cardiovascular-related diseases [12, 13], and these results suggest that Mfn2 may be an important biomarker and therapeutic target for cardiovascular diseases [14]. In addition, Mfn2 has an important regulatory role in DIC. It has been shown that the antitumor drug adriamycin (Dox) induces cardiotoxicity in a dose-dependent manner, and Mfn2-mediated mitochondrial fusion is closely related to Dox-induced cardiotoxicity. Upregulation of Mfn2 reduces the damage and apoptosis of Dox-treated cardiomyocytes, and restoration of Mfn2-mediated mitochondrial fusion significantly attenuates adriamycin-induced cardiotoxicity and enhances its anticancer activity via metabolic conversion which enhanced its anticancer activity [14]. Therefore, Mfn2 could be a potential biomarker for early warning assessment of drug-derived cardiotoxicity.

Currently, methods for Mfn2 detection primarily include enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) [15], co-immunoprecipitation [16], and immunofluorescence [17]. However, these methods have various limitations, such as cumbersome operational steps, false positives, and restricted sensitivity. As an emerging analytical tool, electrochemical immunosensing technology demonstrates unique advantages, including high sensitivity, strong selectivity, a wide linear range, excellent specificity, and suitability for point-of-care testing (POCT) [18]. These merits have attracted significant research interest in clinical testing and biological monitoring [19]. For instance, Yang et al. [20]. developed a simple sandwich-type electrochemical immunosensor for the rapid and specific detection of tau protein, a typical biomarker of Alzheimer’s disease. Experimental results suggested that this detection technology could contribute to improved early diagnosis and prognosis of the disease. Additionally, a study [21] reported that an electrochemical immunosensor based on gold-labeled monoclonal anti-LipL32 antibodies could rapidly, simply, and sensitively detect pathogenic Leptospira whole-cell lysate samples. This immunosensor may serve as an alternative tool for diagnosing leptospirosis. However, the analytical performance of electrochemical immunosensors is limited by electron-transfer mediators and electrode substrate modification materials. Therefore, identifying high-quality materials to enhance sensor performance has become crucial for constructing high-performance sensors.

In recent years, with the groundbreaking advancements in materials science, novel conductive functional materials have been integrated into electrochemical immunosensors to enhance their performance. Among these, MXene has emerged as a key material for developing highly sensitive biosensors due to its high conductivity, low framework density, strong hydrophilicity, and excellent electrochemical properties [22]. For instance, Sweety et al. [23] developed an electrochemical immunosensor based on titanium dioxide-modified MXene for the detection of EpCAM antigen. The results demonstrated that this sensing platform exhibited a wide linear detection range, high sensitivity, and a low detection limit under optimized conditions, along with excellent reproducibility, long-term stability, and remarkable selectivity and specificity. Additionally, Zhang et al. [24] designed a highly sensitive electrochemical biosensor using a Ti₃C₂ MXene/Prussian blue (PB-MXene) hybrid nanoprobe for detecting exosomes and their surface proteins. MXene not only facilitated the in situ generation and efficient loading of PB but also amplified the electrical signal at low potential, minimizing interference from other substances. This sensor successfully detected exosomes from various cancer cells (HeLa, OVCAR, BT474) while maintaining high specificity in serum samples, offering a promising tool for early cancer diagnosis. However, MXene materials still have certain limitations. For example, MXene tends to undergo self-stacking, significantly reducing its dispersibility, conductivity, and specific surface area [25], [26] Moreover, MXene exhibits instability within the anodic potential window and shows an irreversible oxidation peak at approximately 0.43 V [27].

Multiwalled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), as one-dimensional carbon nanomaterials, exhibit excellent chemical stability, thermal conductivity, and electrical conductivity, making them widely applicable in electrochemical sensing. For example, Zhang et al. [28] developed a label-free immunosensor (BSA/Ab/AuNPs/MXene-MWCNTs-Nafion/ITO) for detecting carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) by compositing MWCNTs with MXene. The results demonstrated that MWCNTs effectively prevented MXene self-stacking while the composite material enhanced the antibody-loading capacity of AuNPs, thereby improving electron transfer efficiency and detection sensitivity. Additionally, researchers [29] designed a novel electrochemical amperometric immunosensor for the early diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma. By covalently immobilizing anti-Midkine antibodies on carboxylated MWCNT-modified carbon screen-printed electrodes, the sensor achieved highly efficient detection of the MDK biomarker, exhibiting outstanding selectivity, repeatability, reproducibility, and stability.

Based on the above findings, this study pioneers a novel composite sensing interface by synergistically integrating MXene-derived sodium titanate nanorods (M-NTO) with multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) for Mfn2 detection. The structural complementarity between materials (combining M-NTO’s high specific surface area with MWCNTs’ conductive network) significantly enhances sensor sensitivity, achieving an ultralow detection Limit of 1.85 ng mL−1. This approach establishes a new paradigm for highly sensitive Mfn2 detection. Specifically, this study first prepared MXene by etching Ti₃AlC₂ with HF, followed by oxidation/alkalization treatment to obtain sodium titanate nanorods (M-NTO) with excellent electrochemical stability and an open macroporous structure. Subsequently, M-NTO was composited with highly conductive MWCNTs to construct an M-NTO-MWCNT composite material featuring a large specific surface area, high stability, superior conductivity, and abundant surface functional groups. This composite material was then modified onto a glassy carbon electrode (GCE) surface to increase the effective interfacial area and enhance electron transfer efficiency. Meanwhile, Mfn2-specific antibodies (Mfn2-Ab) were immobilized on the composite as recognition elements. Ultimately, an electrochemical immunosensor based on M-NTO-MWCNT/GCE was successfully constructed, achieving highly sensitive and rapid detection of Mfn2 for early warning and monitoring purposes (as illustrated in Fig. 1).

Experimental part

Instruments and reagents

CHI 660E Electrochemical Workstation (Shanghai Chenhua Instrument Co., Ltd.), all electrochemical experiments were carried out at 25 °C using the traditional three-electrode system, which includes a glassy carbon electrode or a modified glassy carbon electrode (GCE, dia. 3 mm), a saturated calomel electrode (type 232), and a platinum wire electrode (≥ 99.99%) were used as the reference and auxiliary electrodes.

Multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNT, ≥ 95% deacetylated) (Sarn Chemical Technology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.); MXene (Ti3C2) (Suzhou Beike Nano Technology Co., Ltd.); Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) (Solarbio); ELISA kit (Mfn2) (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd.)

Preparation of M-NTO-MWCNT nanocomposites

The preparation process of the M-NTO-CNT composite material was obtained by referring to literature [30], as shown in Fig. 1. First, 100 mg of MXene powder (obtained via liquid-phase exfoliation of Ti3AlC2 using a LiF/HCl mixture) was dispersed in 5 mL of deionized water and ultrasonicated (300 W, 20 min) to form a homogeneous suspension. Then, 0.5 mL of glacial acetic acid was added to adjust the pH to 6–7. A 2 mol·mL−1 NaOH solution was added dropwise to raise the pH to 10, followed by Magnetic stirring for 15 min to ensure complete dissolution. Next, a 30% H₂O₂ solution was slowly introduced, and the mixture was transferred into a 10-mL polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)-lined autoclave for a hydrothermal reaction at 140 °C for 12 h. After cooling, the product was neutralized with dilute hydrochloric acid (1 mol mL−1), and the precipitate was collected via centrifugation (5000 rpm, 10 min), followed by washing three times each with deionized water and ethanol. The resulting solid was dried under vacuum at 60 °C for 8 h to obtain M-NTO composite powder.

Next, 50 mg of the M-NTO composite powder was dispersed in 50 mL of deionized water (concentration: 1 mg mL−1) and ultrasonicated in an ice bath for 30 min (150-W power). Separately, 50 mg of acidified MWCNT powder was dispersed in 50 mL of deionized water with 2 mg of sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) and ultrasonicated for 30 min. The two suspensions were combined, and the total mixed solution volume was adjusted to 100 mL, followed by Magnetic stirring at 500 rpm for 2 h. The mixture was then subjected to ice-bath ultrasonication for 10 min (100-W power) to prevent localized overheating. Finally, the product was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h to obtain the M-NTO-MWCNT composite powder.

Preparation of modified electrodes

The modification process of the Mfn2 electrochemical immunosensor is shown in Fig. 1(B). First, the M-NTO-MWCNT dispersion (20-mg M-NTO-MWCNT powder dispersed in 10 mL of deionized water) was ultrasonicated for 10 min to prepare a 2 mg mL−1 M-NTO-MWCNT dispersion. The M-NTO-MWCNT-modified GCE electrode (M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE) was prepared by pipetting 20 μL of M-NTO-MWCNT dispersion onto the surface of the GCE working electrode dropwise, and then allowed to dry naturally.

Preparation of electrochemical immunosensor

Add 40 μL of biotinylated Mfn2 antibody (Mfn2-Ab) 100 × dilution solution onto the surface of the M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE working electrode, and incubate at room temperature (20 °C) for 4 h. Then, rinse the electrode with pre-prepared 0.1 mol L⁻1 PBST (phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.05% Tween-80, pH 7.0) to remove physically adsorbed Mfn2-Ab, yielding Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE. Subsequently, incubate the electrode at 20 °C in blocking solution (0.1 mol L−1 PBST, pH 7.0, containing 0.05% Tween-80 and 2% BSA) for 50 min to block active sites, resulting in BSA@Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE. This constructs an electrochemical immunosensor for Mfn2 detection. Store the prepared sensor at 4 °C for future use.

Electrochemical measurements and immunoreactive processes

Electrochemical characterization of the base electrode

The electrochemical system used a three-electrode system with a platinum wire electrode as the counter electrode and a calomel electrode as the reference electrode, and the modified electrodes were electrochemically characterized by the CV and EIS methods in an electrolyte of 5 mM [Fe(CN)6]3−/4− with 0.01 M KCl added to the electrolyte. The scanning range of CV was − 0.4 ~ 0.8 V with a scanning rate of 60 mV s−1; EIS was set up with a frequency range of 1 Hz ~ 106 Hz, a sinusoidal signal amplitude of 5 mV, and a voltage of 0.22 V. The EIS method was used to characterize the modified electrode.

Electrochemical measurement

The prepared working electrode was washed with PBST buffer and incubated with the corresponding antigen solution at 4 °C overnight. Then, the electrode was washed again with PBST buffer. Finally, the differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) signal was utilized to detect whether the sensor had specifically bound to the antigen. The DPV was used to evaluate the performance of the electrochemical immunosensor, with the scan range set at − 0.3 ~ 0.5 V and the scan rate set at 100 mV s−1.

Collection of actual plasma samples

Normal adult blood was collected as the actual sample for Mfn2 assay and collected in EP tubes spiked with EDTA-Na2. The samples were centrifuged within 30 min of sample collection at 1000 × g for 15 min. The supernatant was taken and assayed by ELISA and constructed electrochemical immunoassay.

Characterization of MWCNT, MXene, and M-NTO-MWCNT by Raman spectroscopy

Raman spectroscopy is a non-destructive characterization technique based on the molecular vibrational properties of materials, and the changes in Raman spectra are very sensitive to the doping, intercalation, defects, and chemical modification of carbonaceous materials, which can provide information about the vibrational modes of the chemical bonds, structural defects, types of functional groups, and crystalline phases of the materials. Therefore, Raman spectroscopy (Raman spectrum) was used to characterize carbonaceous materials such as graphene (MXene), multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNT), and nanocomposites M-NTO-MWCNT. As shown in Fig. 2E, the characteristic peaks of MXene, MWCNT, and M-NTO-MWCNT electrode Materials appear at around 1350 cm−1 and 1580 cm−1, which correspond to the characteristic peaks of D and G of graphene-like and carbon nanotube materials, and the D band originates from the A1g vibration of sp3-hybridized carbon or defects, while the G band corresponds to the E2g in-plane stretching vibration of sp2-hybridized carbon, and the ratio of the intensity of D peak to that of G peak reflects the defect degree. The ratio of the D peak intensity to the G peak intensity reflects the degree of defects, and a high ratio (> 0.2) indicates structural disorder or oxidative damage. It can be seen that the MWCNT and MXene defects are very small, indicating excellent Material properties. The characteristic peaks at 285 cm−1 and 375 cm−1 are attributed to the characteristic spectral bands of metal–carbon/nitrogen stretching vibrations in the MXene-like materials. The responses on Raman spectra indicate that M-NTO-MWCNT possesses the special spectral bands of both MWCNT and MXene. The increased intensity of the RBM peaks of MWCNT suggests that it acts as a conductive backbone to effectively enhance the electron transport efficiency of the complex; and the broadening of the interlayer vibration peaks of MXene reflects that MWCNT mitigates the volume expansion of M-NTO. The mapping also proves the successful preparation of M-NTO-MWCNT composites, which corresponds to the results shown in the SEM image.

Characterization of MWCNT, MXene, and M-NTO-MWCNT by IR spectroscopy

Infrared spectroscopy is an important tool to analyze the surface functional groups, chemical bonding vibration modes, and interfacial interactions of materials. The structures of MWCNT, MXene, and M-NTO-MWCNT nanocomposites were characterized using infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), respectively. As shown in Fig. 2F, an O–H stretching vibration peak appeared near 3400 cm−1, which was due to the presence of adsorbed water molecules or surface hydroxyl groups. A C–H stretching vibration peak appeared near 2900 cm−1, indicating the presence of alkane functional groups. The appearance of C = C stretching vibration peak near 1600 cm−1 corresponds to the sp2 hybridized carbon skeleton of carbon nanotubes (MWCNT). In addition, a C–O stretching vibration peak appears in the region of 1000 cm−1, which is related to the oxygen-containing functional groups on the surface of carbon nanotubes. Since the MXene surface usually carries functional groups such as –OH, –O, and –F, absorption peaks associated with these functional groups are observed in the infrared spectra, such as O–H telescopic vibration peaks at 3400 cm−1, C = O telescopic vibration peaks near 1600 cm−1, and C–F telescopic vibration peaks near 1100 cm O–H at 3400 cm−1, C = O near 1600 cm−1, and C–F near 1100 cm−1. The infrared spectra of the M-NTO-MWCNT composites can be seen as a superposition of the characteristic peaks of M-NTO and MWCNT, which indicates that an effective interfacial bonding between M-NTO and MWCNT was carried out, and proves that the composites were successfully prepared.

Electrochemical characterization ofsensor preparation process

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) is a versatile electrochemical technique that analyzes the charge transfer, ion diffusion, and reaction kinetic properties of a material by measuring the current versus potential/time to reveal the nature of the material. The method has the advantages of fast and easy measurement and is suitable for trace or ultra-trace analysis [31]. In this study, CV was used to characterize the electrodes with different modifications in a 5 mM K4[Fe(CN)]6/K3[Fe(CN)]6 electrolyte containing 0.01 mM KCl, and the relevant parameters were set as follows: a potential range of − 0.4 ~ 0.8 V, and a scanning rate of 100 mV s−1. As shown in Fig. 3A, the current response of M-NTO@GCE (curve b) is not significant compared with that of bare GCE (curve a) because M-NTO is not electrically active; the current response signal of MWCNT@GCE (curve d) is significantly increased, and the peak current is elevated compared with that of bare GCE (curve a), which is attributed to the good electrochemical performance of multi-walled carbon nanotubes, but the van der Waals interactions between the walls of the tube van der Waals interactions Make MWCNT Massively aggregated and unevenly dispersed, which reduces its catalytic performance; 0.01 M chitosan (CS) was selected as the dispersant. M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE (CS) (curve d) exhibits significantly improved current response compared to bare GCE (curve a), M-NTO@GCE (curve b), and MWCNT@GCE (curve c), with a notable increase in peak current. This is because the combination of MWCNT with M-NTO, which has an open macroporous structure, results in better dispersion of MWCNT in chitosan (CS), and significantly reduces MWCNT aggregation. The synthesized M-NTO-MWCNT (CS) integrates the advantages of both, achieving a high specific surface area, excellent conductivity, and outstanding thermal stability. The dual optimization of its porous interface and conductivity significantly enhances charge migration efficiency, providing a structural foundation for efficient electron transport. When compared to M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE (CS) (curve d), the current response of Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE (CS) (curve e) is significantly reduced, with a notable decrease in peak current. The primary reason for this is that after Mfn2-Ab modification on the electrode surface, it impedes electron transfer and covers the active sites of the electrode, leading to a reduction in the electron transfer efficiency of the electrochemical reaction.

The EIS measurements of the different modified electrodes were carried out in a 5 mM K4[Fe(CN)]6/K3[Fe(CN)]6 electrolyte containing 0.01 mM KCl in the frequency range of 1 ~ 106 Hz. Optimization of experimental conditions

MWCNT concentration screening

The concentration of MWCNT affects the conductivity and electroactive surface area of the modified working electrode, which in turn affects the modification effect and analytical performance of the subsequent working electrode. As shown in Fig. 5A, GCE was modified with different concentrations (0.5 M, 1 M, 2 M, 3 M, and 4 M) of MWCNT, and the peak electrochemical response current reached its Maximum value when the concentration of MWCNT reached 2 M. When the amount of modification was further continued to increase, the peak response signal decreased because the high concentration caused the MWCNT Material to accumulate on the surface of the working electrode, and the electron transfer rate was hindered. Therefore, the concentration of MWCNT Material was selected as 2 M for the experiment.

Screening of M-NTO-MWCNT modification amount

The modification amount of M-NTO-MWCNT on the electrode surface affects the conductivity and electroactive surface area of the modified working electrode, which in turn affects the modification effect and analytical performance of the subsequent working electrode. As shown in Fig. 5C, the GCE was modified with different modification amounts (10 μL, 12 μL, 14 μL, 16 μL, 18 μL, and 20 μL) of M-NTO-MWCNT, and the peak electrochemical response current reached the Maximum value when the modification amount reached 14 μL. The peak response signal decreased when the modification amount was further increased because too much modification would cause the M-NTO-MWCNT composite to accumulate on the surface of the working electrode, and the electron transfer rate was hindered. Therefore, a modification amount of 14 μL of M-NTO-MWCNT composite was selected for the experiment.

Mfn2-Ab incubation time screening

In order to determine the effect of Mfn2-Ab incubation time on the response of the immunosensor system, 100 × concentration of Mfn2-Ab was taken and incubated on the M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE electrode for different times (10 min, 20 min, 30 min, 40 min, and 50 min) at room temperature of 20 °C. The incubation time of Mfn2-Ab was determined by the incubation time of the immunosensor system. As can be seen from Fig. 5D, the incubation time of Mfn2-Ab has a great influence on the electron transfer rate of the electrochemical immunosensor. Too short an incubation time resulted in a completely effective immobilization of the M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE surface by the Mfn2-Ab method, but too long an incubation time resulted in too high a protein content on the surface of the modified electrode, which insulated it. Therefore, when there was no significant change in the current response peak signal, Mfn2-Ab could already be stably immobilized on the electrode, and 40 min was chosen as the optimal incubation time for Mfn2-Ab.

Mfn2-Ag incubation time screening

To determine the effect of Mfn2-Ag incubation time on the response of the immunosensor system, Mfn2-Ag at a concentration of 240 ng mL−1 was incubated for different times (20 min, 30 min, 40 min, 50 min, and 60 min) on BSA@Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE electrodes at room temperature of 20 °C. The incubation time was determined by the incubation time of the Mfn2-Ag incubator. As can be seen from Fig. 5E, when the incubation time of Mfn2-Ag was less than 50 min, the antigen–antibody binding was incomplete, and when the incubation time of Mfn2-Ag was increased, the electrochemical peak currents would no longer change significantly. Therefore, 50 min was chosen as the optimal incubation time for Mfn2-Ag.

Performance analysis of electrochemical immunosensor

First, the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to detect the absorbance (OD) values corresponding to Mfn2 within the concentration range of 9.38 × 10−1 to 2.40 × 102 ng·mL⁻1. The OD values of the ELISA method showed a good linear relationship with the Mfn2 concentration (see Fig. 6A). The linear regression equation was OD = 0.01001CMfn2 + 0.12309 (R2 = 0.98421), and the detection Limit was 3.69 × 101 ng·mL−1 (S/N = 3). Under optimized experimental conditions, the performance of the constructed electrochemical immunosensor for Mfn2 detection was verified by testing Mfn2 standard samples at different concentrations. Figure 6 B shows the effective recognition response of the BSA@Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE electrochemical immunosensor to Mfn2 within the concentration range of 240, 120, 60, 30, 15, 7.5, 3.75, 1.875, and 0.9375 ng·mL⁻1. The DPV response values decreased with the increase in Mfn2 concentration (Fig. 6C) and exhibited a good linear relationship (Fig. 6D). The linear equation was Ip = 1.06382 − 1.72862 lgCMfn2 (R2 = 0.98767), and the detection Limit was 1.85 ng·mL−1 (S/N = 3). By comparison, the detection Limit of this sensor was reduced by approximately 20-fold, demonstrating significantly improved detection sensitivity. Therefore, the verified method demonstrates excellent analytical performance in detecting Mfn2 and can serve as an effective platform for analyzing Mfn2 in actual samples.

Specificity, reproducibility, and stability of the electrochemical immunosensor

Specificity is a crucial indicator for evaluating the analytical capability of a sensor. To assess the anti-interference ability of the fabricated immunosensor, 1.88 ng mL−1 of glucose (Glu), L-methionine (Met), glycine (Gly), ascorbic acid (ASA), and horseradish peroxidase (HRP) were sequentially added to a 1.88 ng mL−1 Mfn2 solution. As shown in Fig. 7A and B, the DPV response signals of the immunosensor incubated with Mfn2 and the interfering substances showed minimal differences, indicating that these substances had no effect on the detection of Mfn2. Therefore, the developed electrochemical immunosensor exhibits excellent specificity for Mfn2, which is of significant importance for clinical Mfn2 detection.

To investigate the reproducibility of the sensor, immunosensing tests were conducted using five electrodes prepared from the same batch. As shown in Fig. 7C and D, the electrochemical signals of the five sensors remained relatively stable, with a relative standard deviation (RSD) of only 1.33%, demonstrating that the designed electrochemical immunosensor in this study possesses good reproducibility. To evaluate the stability of the sensor, three BSA@Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE electrodes were sealed and stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C for 0–18 days. During this period, the electrodes were tested via DPV, with electrochemical detection performed each time for a fixed concentration (1.88 ng mL−1) of Mfn2, and the electrochemical response values were recorded. As shown in Fig. 7E and F, after 18 days of storage, the fabricated immunosensor retained 98% of its initial current response, indicating that the immunosensor exhibits acceptable stability for Mfn2 detection.

Analysis of actual samples

To further evaluate the usefulness of the electrochemical immunosensor prepared in this study, we used it for the detection of Mfn2 in human serum samples and compared it with the commercial ELISA method. The following tests were performed in actual serum samples using the standard addition method (n = 3). As shown in Table 1, after tenfold dilution of healthy human serum samples, standard concentrations (0 ng mL−1, 31.25 ng mL−1, 62.5 ng mL−1, and 125 ng mL−1) of Mfn2-Ag solution were added to human serum samples, and serum samples with different concentrations of Mfn2-Ag were drop-wise spiked on the immuno-electrode and tested for current response values. The results showed that the RSDs of the constructed electrochemical immunosensor and ELISA ranged from 1.18 to 3.76% and 0.54 to 3.30%, respectively, and the recoveries varied in the ranges of 98.30 ~ 99.40% and 99.50 ~ 101.30%, respectively, and there was no particularly significant difference between results obtained by the two assays. The experimental results showed that the constructed BSA@Mfn2-Ab@M-NTO-MWCNT@GCE electrochemical immunosensor was accurate and reliable for the detection of Mfn2, and was successfully applied to the detection of Mfn2 concentration in real serum samples.

Conclusion

This study pioneers the development of a novel label-free electrochemical immunosensor for highly sensitive Mfn2 detection, utilizing M-NTO-MWCNT composite material that combines large specific surface area, high stability, and superior conductivity through electrochemical immunosensing technology. Initially, a simultaneous oxidation and alkalization method was employed to prepare titanium sodium oxide nanorods (M-NTO), which exhibit strong electrochemical cycling stability and an open macroporous structure. Leveraging the properties of M-NTO and combining it with highly conductive multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), an M-NTO-MWCNT composite material was synthesized, integrating the advantages of both components—large specific surface area, high stability, superior conductivity, and abundant surface functional groups—thereby providing numerous active sites for biomolecule binding. After modifying the GCE with M-NTO-MWCNT, the effective surface area and electron transfer rate were significantly enhanced. Meanwhile, the immobilized Mfn2-Ab enabled highly selective recognition and detection of Mfn2, thus constructing an electrochemical immunosensor capable of early warning for Mfn2, a potential biomarker of drug-induced cardiotoxicity. Under optimized experimental conditions, the immunosensor demonstrated a Linear detection range of 9.38 × 10−1 to 2.40 × 102 ng mL⁻1 and a detection Limit of 1.85 ng mL−1 (3σ, n = 11). When applied to real serum samples, it exhibited excellent reproducibility and stability, with analytical performance surpassing that of the ELISA method, indicating its great potential for accurate and rapid detection of Mfn2 in clinical serum samples. Moreover, the developed electrochemical immunosensor demonstrates significant advantages over conventional ELISA in terms of detection sensitivity (~ 20-fold improvement), assay time (reduced from several hours to less than 50 min), and operational convenience. This research not only provides a novel approach for the screening, early warning, and prognostic risk assessment of drug-induced cardiotoxicity but also offers a scientific foundation for the development of efficient and convenient detection technologies.

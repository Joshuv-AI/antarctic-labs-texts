Sulphonated-graphene oxide nanocomposite membranes with PVA-ZnO nanostructures and mechanized agitation-enhanced pt coating for soft robotics bending actuator
Authors: Syed Khalid Mustafa, Ali Hamzah Alessa, Ibrahim Saleem S Alatawi, Jassim hosny al Dalaeen, Maidul Islam, Mohd Imran Ahamed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIonic Polymer-Metal Composite (IPMC) actuators have garnered significant scientific attention in robotics and artificial muscles for their ability to operate at low voltage, high strain capacity, and lightweight construction. The lack of uniform bending in IPMC actuators undermines their control precision and restricts their range of potential applications. This study utilized the unique properties of nanoscale materials and Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) to develop a membrane for soft robotic bending actuation. Subsequently, a platinum coating was applied to the membrane to mitigate the limitations of IPMCs in soft robotics applications. Herein, mechanized agitation was employed during the solution-casting process to enhance platinum metal (Pt-metal) coating on zinc oxide (ZnO) nanostructures within a PVA- sulphonated graphene oxide nanocomposite, achieving enhanced soft robotics bending actuation capabilities. The resultant membrane composed of sulphonated-graphene oxide and polyvinyl-zinc oxide coated with pt (PsGZ-Pt) was examined by exploring advanced analytical techniques such as Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and X-ray diffraction spectroscopy (XRD). Furthermore, the ionic exchange capacity (IEC), proton conductivity (PC), water uptake and water loss characteristics were evaluated to be 2.1 meq. g−1, 1.95 × 10−3 Scm−1, 59.62% at 45 °C and water loss at 9 min immersion found 38%, respectively. Electromechanical studies of the PsGZ-Pt membrane (size 30 mm length, 10 mm width, 0.07 mm thickness) showed an actuation force of 0.3253 mN and a displacement of roughly 22.8 mm at ± 3 VDC. These findings highlighted the PsGZ-Pt membrane’s potential as a low-cost alternative to expensive commercial IPMC actuators based on polymers. These results presented a straightforward, low-cost approach for synthesizing PsGZ-Pt utilizing conventional technologies. The PsGZ-Pt membrane shows promise for generating low-cost, high-performance actuation materials with a wide range of industrial applications.Keywords: Mechanized agitation, Nanocomposite membrane, Cost-effective membrane, Poly(vinyl alcohol), Sulphonated-graphene oxide, ZnO nanostructures, Pt-metal coating, Soft Robotics bending actuationSubject terms: Materials chemistry, Polymer chemistry

Introduction

Polymer-based actuators are a significant class of materials capable of producing a wide range of mechanical outcomes to varied outside stimuli, such as electrically generated energy. Several polymeric materials are electrically sensitive, such as dielectric elastomers (DEMs)1, ionic polymer-metal composites (IPMCs)2, conductive polymers (CPs)3, piezoelectric polymers (PEPs) and ionic polymer gels (IPGs)4. DEMS polymers change form when an electric field is applied, making them suitable for soft robotics and artificial muscles. IPMCs are made up of an ionic polymer membrane covered by metal electrodes. The movement of cations and water molecules causes the IPMC to bend when an electric voltage is applied. CPs are mechanically responsive to electrochemical stimuli and have been used as linear actuators. The most effective actuation methods are ionic entry and egress. However, conformational changes and molecular interactions have also been postulated. PEPs produce an electric charge when mechanically deformed, and vice versa. The direct piezoelectric action enables them to turn electrical energy into mechanical movement. IPGs are polymers that expand or contract in response to changes in the ionic environment, which an electric field can induce. Because of their numerous functional characteristics, such as the ability to generate significant stresses at low strains, these electrically sensitive polymeric materials are widely used in constructing micro/soft robotics and biomimetic artificial muscles.

Ionic Polymer-Metal Composite (IPMC) actuators have received a lot of interest because of their unique qualities, which include exceptional flexibility, lightweight design, ease of moulding into diverse shapes, low voltage needs, and outstanding bending actuation performance. These actuators are generally made of a polymeric membrane sandwiched between layers of conductive metal electrodes. When a solvated IPMC strip is exposed to a low positive voltage (1–5 V), it bends quickly toward the anode, driven by the flux of hydrated ions within the strip. The membrane’s potential to bend in the opposite direction when the voltage polarity is switched demonstrates its bidirectional deflection capability, which is intrinsically and practically beneficial5–8.

The bending behaviour and operational features of Ionic Polymer-Metal Composite (IPMC) actuators demonstrate that they are exceptionally versatile, operating effectively in both aqueous and non-aqueous conditions. The key premise behind IPMC bending behaviour is the existence of mobile cations within the ion-exchange membrane, as well as the movement of water or solvent molecules from the cathode to the anode area9,10. This mechanism, enhanced by the passage of water or solvent molecules across the ionomeric membrane, is essential for IPMC activation and is assisted by hydraulic, osmotic, and electrostatic forces11,12. To accomplish realistic usefulness and remarkable bending efficiency at exceptionally low applied voltages, IPMCs must have critical properties such as high-water uptake (WU) capacity, minimum water loss (WL), increased ion exchange capacity (IEC), and high proton conductivity (PC). In addition, the incorporation and adhesion of noble metal electrodes are critical for achieving electrical conductivity in IPMC actuators13.

Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop IPMC membranes that accomplish mechanical robustness, ion exchange capacity, actuation force, tip displacement or deflection levels, synthetic accessibility, environmental sustainability, flexibility, and proton conductivity. It is critical to tailor these attributes since various applications require particular material attributes. The sources emphasize the need for more cost-effective and customized IPMC membrane alternatives to Nafion, emphasizing improving various performance-related features to fulfil the individual needs of diverse applications14–18. Following this intent, the current work describes a novel technique for fabricating a cost-effective IPMC membrane. This innovative material blends Poly(vinyl alcohol)-Sulphonated Graphitic oxide particles (PsG) with ZnO nanostructures (ZnOns), which are then improved using a Pt-metal coating technique employing solution casting enhanced by mechanized agitation. This research represents a significant step toward offering a less expensive alternative to widely accessible PFSA-based membranes, especially for soft robotics usage19–21. The major goal of this research is to democratize access to superior actuation materials fabricated under mechanized agitation-enhanced Pt coating on PsGZ nanocomposite membrane for Soft Robotics Bending Actuation22,23. The unique properties of PsG, including a large surface-to-volume ratio, abundant active sites, and superior chemical/thermal stability, make it a versatile candidate for various applications24,25. Moreover, its outstanding electrical properties, along with excellent mechanical strength and flexibility, allow it to be used in sensors, actuators, and circuits within soft robots. In addition, PVA’s biocompatibility makes it appropriate for soft robotics applications in medicine and biology, and its capacity to improve overall flexibility and stretchability adds significant value to these materials26,27. Furthermore, research has shown that ZnOns display piezoelectric characteristics, enabling the generation of electrical charges in response to mechanical strain. This characteristic is pivotal for sensors and energy extraction in soft robotics contexts. Also, ZnOns has antibacterial properties, potentially useful for creating self-sterilizing surfaces or components in soft robotics, especially in medical frameworks28. Therefore, the synergistic combination of the PsG and ZnOns components and the Pt-metal covering will give the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane exceptional capabilities that its separate elements cannot achieve. The PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane shows great potential as a dependable contender for developing actuator technology and future industrial applications, owing to the unique features resulting from the synergy of its many components8,22,23,29.

Methodology

Instrumentation

The study included a variety of tools for extensive examination. FTIR spectroscopy was performed using a Nicolet™ iS50 (Thermo Scientific™) encompassing 4000 –400 cm−1. A Rigaku SmartLab diffractometer was used to conduct X-ray diffraction (XRD) investigation, while a JEOL JSM-6510LV was used for scanning electron microscopy. The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (TEM 2100, JEOL, Japan) operated at 200 kV on a carbon-coated copper grid was employed to capture the in-depth images of the synthesized materials. In addition, a PGSTAT 302 N potentiostat/galvanostat (Autolab) was utilized. A pH meter, magnetic stirrer, digital balance, and electric air oven were also required for drying and measuring.

Green Synthesis of ZnOns

Freshly plucked neem (Azadirachta indica) leaves were collected, properly cleaned with ordinary water to eliminate dirt and organic pollutants, and washed with double-distilled water (DDW). The freshly cleaned leaves had been air-dried at ambient temperature before being chopped into small pieces and poured into a beaker of DDW. After boiling for 30 min, the extract was cooled and filtered using Whatman No.1 filter paper. The resulting neem extract was kept in a closed beaker at 4 °C for future use.

To produce ZnOns, 10 mL extract was progressively incorporated into 90 mL of a 0.1 M zinc nitrate solution while stirring at the ambient temperature for approximately four hours. Following this, 50 mL of a 2.0 M NaOH solution was gently added to the original mixture while stirring continuously for another 2 h. The procedure produced a white precipitate, which was passed through filters and washed many times with distilled water as well as ethanol to remove contaminants. After that, the concentrated precipitate was oven-dried for an entire night at 60 °C. The dried ZnOns were obtained as a fine white powder and ready for examination30,31.

Characterization of ZnOns

Four major analytical techniques were used in the thorough characterization of the synthesized ZnOns i.e. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis, Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). For FTIR, a Nicolet iS50 FT-IR spectrometer was used to determine the details of functional groups present in the substance. The measurement was done between 4000 –400 cm−1. The membrane’s crystallinity was examined using a Rigaku Smart Lab X-ray diffractometer. The structural characteristics of a material can be better understood by XRD analysis. The surface morphology of the ZnOns was investigated using a JEOL JSM 6510 LV scanning electron microscope at 200 kV. Samples were made on copper grids covered with carbon paper. SEM evaluation offers extensive information on the material’s surface topography as well as morphology. These combined methodologies offered a full knowledge of the ZnO nanostructures’ structural, chemical, and morphological features, which is critical for determining their appropriateness for prospective applications.

Nanocomposite (IPMC) fabrication of PsGZ membrane

Initially, aqueous polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) solution was obtained by dissolving 4 g of PVA in 125 mL of DDW and agitating it for 8 h at 80 °C. The resultant solution has been filtered and preserved for further use. To achieve sulfonation, 4.2 mL of 4-sulfophthalic acid (50% in water) and 1.55 mL of graphene oxide (GO) were combined and continuously agitated at 80 °C for four hours. The combination was subsequently mixed for another 10 h at a comparable temperature before being ultrasonically treated for an hour. To put an end to the sulfonation reaction, the combination was progressively added to ice-cooled de-mineralized water while vigorously agitating. The sulfonated GO (sGO) was subsequently removed by centrifugation at 9000 rpm for 30 min and properly rinsed with water as well as ethanol.

In continuation, the filtered PVA solution and the homogenous sGO solution were mixed and stirred continuously for an entire day. To achieve full dissolution, the mixture was sonicated for 45 min. Next, ZnOns were introduced gradually by drops to the sGO and PVA mix (1.65 mL), then ultrasonically treated for 1.5 h. After that, the solvent was volatilized from the inorganic-organic nanocomposite by casting it onto petri dishes and heating it to 60 °C for 12 h in a controlled oven. After drying thoroughly, forceps delicately lifted the membrane substance from the petri dish. To achieve crosslinking, the material underwent one hour of heating at 90 °C in a thermostatic oven. Upon reaching room temperature, it was found that the ionomeric membrane obtained was mechanically stable, thus proving suitable for actuation investigations32 .Later, the dry polymer films were washed numerous times with acetone and de-mineralized water to eliminate any remaining solvents33. Finally, this process leads to the production of an almost homogenous PsGZ nanocomposite membrane.

Chemical plating of pt on PsGZ membrane

A set of solutions, including 0.1 M K2S2O8 and 10% v/v C6H5NH2 in 1 M HCl, as well as aqueous solutions of 0.04 M Pt(NH3)4Cl2.H2O, 5% NH4OH, and NaBH4 in de-mineralized water, were prepared to facilitate the Pt coating on the PsGZ membrane. Sandpaper was utilized to roughen the membrane’s surface before 45 min of ultrasonic cleaning. After that, the membrane was submerged in a solution of 2 M HCl and neutralized using de-mineralized water.

The Pt coating was made possible by concurrently introducing 5.5 mL of Pt(NH3)4Cl2.H2O and 1.7 mL of NH4OH onto the membrane, allowing complete absorption. This was promptly followed by a 6-hour digesting time to guarantee full interaction and elimination of surplus Pt ions and an extensive rinse with DDW. The transition from the form of Pt ions to metallic Pt was accomplished through the use of 1.8 mL of NaBH4 for 20-minute intervals, culminating in adding 8 mL of NaBH4 while stirring continuously. Mechanized agitation was successfully applied in the solution-casting process to enhance Pt-metal coating on the PsGZ membrane34.

The membrane was thoroughly cleaned with DDW to eliminate leftover residues after the reduction procedure. The process was subsequently stopped by soaking the membrane in 0.1 M HCl solution. The finished Pt-coated PsGZ membrane was dried and stored in a desiccator for future study and applications.

Deflection behavior of PsGZ-Pt Nanocomposite membrane.

After multiple iterations and more investigation with trials conducted at 2.0 VDC, a deflection of roughly 10.5 mm was discovered, as shown in Fig. 3. Interestingly, there was some hysteresis in the bidirectional deflection behaviour, which resulted in a deflection inaccuracy. Repeating the experiments repeatedly showed that the hysteresis effect grew stronger as the voltage increased. It became necessary to address the hysteresis-induced deflection error in order to improve the actuator’s performance. The method used included fine-tuning the voltage inside the DAC configuration in close coordination with the actuation control. By effectively reducing hysteresis, this tactic produced a repeatable behaviour that peaked at about 22.8 mm within the specified deflection range.

This work explores the PsGZ-Pt Nanocomposite membrane’s electromechanical details and explains how bidirectional deflection behaviour is controlled. The careful planning of the experiment and the insightful findings highlight the possible uses of this membrane in situations where precise and regulated bidirectional deflection responses are required.

Result and discussion

The crux of this work is the use of mechanical agitation in the solution-casting procedure, which has shown to be a critical component in obtaining a homogeneous and optimal Pt-metal coating on ZnO nanostructures embedded in a PVA sulphonated-graphene oxide matrix. This mechanical agitation, a significant parameter, has never been reported before in the context of producing Pt-coated PsGZ nanocomposites to improve their electromechanical characteristics for industrial use.

Traditionally, metal coating on nanocomposites results in random and uneven deposition, which causes membrane thickening and reduced flexibility. Such features impede the smooth bending necessary for successful actuation under applied voltage, especially in applications requiring accurate and dependable performance, such as soft robots. Consequently, this work investigates and verifies the concept that automated agitation may considerably improve the uniformity and quality of Pt coating, increasing the nanocomposite membrane’s overall functionality and performance.

As outlined in the introduction, the approach used in this study is based on an exhaustive analysis of the literature and precedents established by previous investigations. Mechanized agitation was carefully included in the solution-casting process to achieve complete mixing and deposition of Pt on ZnO nanostructures embedded in a PVA-sulphonated graphene oxide matrix. The resultant PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane was submitted to a battery of tests to determine its primary properties and operational performance. These assessments included structural investigation with FTIR spectroscopy, SEM, TEM and XRD methods to ensure component integration and Pt coating quality.

Moreover, the investigation analyzed the membrane’s ionic exchange capacity (IEC), proton conductivity (PC), and water uptake/loss characteristics, offering a comprehensive grasp of its physical and chemical features. Electromechanical tests were essential since they showed the membrane’s actuation force and displacement under electrical stimulation.

Properties and functionalities of the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane

In order to better analyze the primary characteristics and operations, the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane actuator underwent extensive evaluations. These assessments took a multipronged approach, illuminating several factors essential to comprehending its behaviour and possible applications. Some of them are presented below.

Proton Transfer/ Conductivity Assessment of PsGZ-Pt Nanocomposite membrane

Proton conductivity analysis was used to evaluate the proton transfer capacity of the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane. With a proton conductivity of 1.95 × 10−3 S·cm⁻¹, the IPMC membrane demonstrated its ability to promote the movement of hydrated H+ ions and, as a result, improve actuation capacity. The hydrated membrane (1 × 3 cm2) was subjected to conductivity testing at 25 °C using an impedance analyzer set to 10 mV. s⁻¹ AC perturbation and 100 kHz frequency. To calculate proton conductivity (σ), cross-sectional area (A), resistance (R), and membrane thickness (L) were taken into account38. When regulated voltage was applied using a digital power supply and a digital-analog card (DAC), the membrane behaved straight at 2.5 VDC, producing deflection. Following trials at 3 VDC resulted in a controlled and reversible deflection of about 22.8 mm. The measured increased conductivity of protons confirms efficient proton transfer, hence greatly increasing actuation capacity. These results provide helpful information for designing and optimizing PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane actuators, which satisfy the requirements to produce dependable and effective electromechanical devices.

Ion-exchange capacity (IEC) analysis of PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane

One important factor affecting proton transfer in IPMC membranes, which impacts actuator performance, is the Ion-Exchange Capacity (IEC). The IEC for the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane under study was found to be roughly 2.1 meq. g−1. This high IEC value is helpful since it increases the membrane’s capacity for Water Uptake (WU) capacity and makes it easier for Pt particles to adhere to its surfaces. IPMCs resistance is decreased by the uniform electrode and improved Pt particle distribution, leading to better-bending performance21,39.

Water Uptake (WU) capacity of PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane

Temperature dependence was seen in the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane’s water uptake capability. After an hour of immersion, the membrane showed maximal water absorption capabilities of roughly 48.5% and 59.62% at 25 °C and 45 °C, respectively Fig. 4. A longer immersion period of 25 h caused the water-holding capacity to decline gradually before stabilizing. Increased hydrophilicity, a desirable quality for improved performance under applied potential, is indicated by the high water uptake capacity40,41.

Water loss (WL) analysis of PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane

The performance of the IPMC actuator is negatively impacted by water loss, which is caused by damage to the electrode layer and water electrolysis. Testing was done on the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane’s ability to withstand water loss at various applied voltages (3–6 V), shown in Fig. 5. The findings showed that the water loss increased gradually as the applied voltage increased, peaking at 6 V for 9 min and accounting for about 38% of the total loss. Significantly, the water loss was modest and encouraging, indicating better membrane function. One important component in determining the longevity and effectiveness of the membrane is the formation of pores on the electrode surface as a result of electrolysis.

Precision Assessment of PsGZ-Pt Nanocomposite membrane

Extensive experimental repeatability was used to provide a precision assessment and guarantee the dependability of the PsGZ-Pt nanocomposite membrane. The capacity to absorb and lose water was examined at two distinct temperatures: room temperature (25 °C) and a raised 45 °C. Five careful replications of the tests using established procedures yielded consistent insights into the behavior of the membrane. The error bar calculations used the average values from these repetitions, providing a more realistic picture of the membrane’s performance. A particular focus was on temperature dependency, investigating changes in water intake and loss under various circumstances. This accurate evaluation confirms the membrane’s compatibility for real-world applications and increases trust in its dependability. The observed consistency between repetitions improves the predictability of the membrane and helps to create robust and reliable devices.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the work emphasizes readers with a thorough and captivating narrative by highlighting the innovative application of automated agitation in the synthesis process, the enhanced Pt coating on the membrane ensuing breakthroughs in soft robotics bending actuation. In support of this, the work presents a low-cost, high-efficiency Ionic Polymer Metal Nanocomposite (IPMC) membrane, designated as PsGZ-Pt, with outstanding actuation capabilities. The simple method for preparing PsGZ may streamline the synthesis process compared to previous complex procedures, reducing time and resource requirements. Besides the green protocol for ZnO ns synthesis aligns with principles of sustainability, safety, and efficiency, making it a promising choice for various technological and biomedical applications. Moreover, herein, sulfonation of graphene oxide improves membrane stability by enhancing dispersion, strengthening interfacial interactions, improving barrier properties, enhancing mechanical properties, and increasing chemical stability. These improvements collectively contribute to the membrane’s enhanced performance in soft robotic actuation. Various approaches were employed to evaluate the performance of the membrane. Specifically, at 3-volt direct current (VDC), the results showed a significant actuation force of approximately 0.3253 mN and a notable displacement of about 22.8 mm. These findings help produce IPMC membranes and address the pressing demand for cost-effective, high-performance actuation materials with significant industrial ramifications. Overall, the PsGZ-Pt membrane has remarkable electromechanical performance, representing a substantial improvement in the field of nanocomposite actuators.

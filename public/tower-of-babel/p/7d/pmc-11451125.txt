Efficacy and Safety of Suxiao Jiuxin Pills in the Treatment of Chronic Coronary Syndrome with Intolerance to Adverse Effects of Long-acting Nitrates: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Study
Authors: Yuanlong Hu, Yifei Wang, Shiqin Wang, Xirong Cui, Yong Feng, Zhengguang Li, Kegang Ji, Jianming Wang, Chenghua Sun, Yan Tang, Yunlun Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to investigate the short-term effects and safety of adjunct Suxiao Jiuxin Pills (SJPs) on conventional therapy in chronic coronary syndrome (CCS) patients who are intolerant to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates.MethodsThis was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A total of 174 CCS participants from eight clinical study centers in China were included in the modified intention-to-treat analyses. Participants with CCS and intolerance to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates were recruited and randomized to either the SJPs or the placebo group for a duration of 4 weeks.ResultsCompared to the placebo group, the SJPs group showed a significant improvement in the efficacy rate after 4 weeks (OR = 2.43, 95% CI = 1.32 to 4.47, P = 0.004). Besides, individuals without a history of alcohol consumption showed a greater improvement in the SAQ summary score compared to those with a history of alcohol consumption.ConclusionAdjunctive SJPs enhance the effectiveness of short-term conventional anti-angina treatment for patients with CCS who experience intolerance to long-acting nitrates, without significant adverse effects during application.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trials Registry Platform, ChiCTR2100050066. Registered 16 August 2021, https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=131470.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12906-024-04661-5.Keywords: Chronic coronary syndrome, Suxiao JiuXin Pills, Nitrates, Randomized controlled trial

Introduction

Angina is the main manifestation of myocardial ischemia, characterized by chest discomfort triggered by physical exertion or emotional distress [1, 2]. The international CLARIFY registry data indicates that angina symptoms affect 22.1% of patients with chronic coronary syndrome (CCS) [3]. Angina symptoms significantly reduce the quality of life, a fact that is frequently underestimated by clinicians [4]. Isosorbide mononitrate and other long-acting nitrates are commonly employed in clinical settings to alleviate angina symptoms [5, 6]. Headache is the most common adverse reaction related to nitrates, along with hypotension and flushing. Previous studies have indicated that 45% of patients with coronary heart disease suffer from nitrate-induced headache [7], which compromises patient compliance. Consequently, there is a need for alternative medications that relieve angina symptoms.

Enhancing myocardial perfusion is crucial for alleviating angina symptoms and improving quality of life, regardless of whether individuals have stable or unstable angina [1]. Long-acting nitrates are known to improve myocardial perfusion by promoting nitric oxide (NO) generation [8]. Suxiao Jiuxin Pill (SJPs) emerges as a prominent traditional Chinese patent medicine utilized for the treatment of acute ischemic heart disease, which was approved by China Food and Drug Administration (CFDA) in 1982 [9]. It is supported by extensive preclinical and clinical evidence [10, 11]. The pill is composed of Ligusticum striatum DC. (WFO accession number: wfo-0000362231, family Apiaceae, Chuanxiong Rhizoma) and Dryobalanops aromatica C.F.Gaertn. (WFO accession number: wfo-0000657511, family Dipterocarpaceae, Borneolum syntheticum). Pharmacokinetic studies have revealed that night main compounds (borneol, isoborneol, senkyunolide A, senkyunolide I, ligustilide, ferulic acid, neocnidilide, senkyunolide H and levistolide A) were identified in plasma after oral administration [12, 13]. Preclinical studies suggest that SJPs can activate the endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS)-NO pathway, inducing coronary artery relaxation [14, 15]. In addition to targeting the eNOS-NO pathway, ligustilide exerts calcium ion inhibitory activity by inhibiting Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), thereby improving vascular relaxation [13, 16]. Furthermore, senkyunolide A, ligustilide, ferulic acid, and borneol exert anti-inflammatory activity or antioxidant activity to protect aortic endothelium integrity [17–20]. Based on evidence from preclinical studies, SJPs exhibit therapeutic potential for CCS.

SJPs have gained attention for its potential applications in the antianginal treatment of CCS in recent years. A previous systematic review indicated that SJPs demonstrate therapeutic effects on CCS [21]. However, the inclusion of studies with inadequate methodological quality significantly limited the conclusions drawn from this study. In a recent randomized clinical trial, it was reported that SJPs demonstrated improvements in physical limitations, angina stability, and treatment satisfaction after 24 weeks of intervention [9]. Nevertheless, no studies have specifically addressed the short-term antianginal efficacy of SJPs in patients with CCS who are intolerant to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates. Rapid improvement of angina symptoms is an expectation for CCS patients who are unable to tolerate the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates. Rapid alleviation of angina symptoms is a critical expectation for CCS patients who cannot tolerate these adverse effects. Therefore, we conducted a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of short-term antianginal treatment with SJPs in CCS patients exhibiting intolerance to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates.

Material and methods

Study design

The primary aim of the study was to assess whether Suxiao Jiuxin Pills (SJPs) were superior to placebo in the treatment of chronic coronary syndrome pectoris in patients with intolerance to side effects of long-acting nitrates. The SJPs, containing Ligusticum striatum DC. (WFO accession number: wfo-0000362231, family Apiaceae, Ligusticum chuanxiong) and Dryobalanops aromatica C.F.Gaertn. (WFO accession number: wfo-0000657511, family Dipterocarpaceae, Borneolum syntheticum), were approved, marketed, and widely utilized in China. According to the Chinese Pharmacopoeia, each pill of SJPs is required to contain a minimum of 15.0 μg of Ligusticum chuanxiong, expressed in terms of ferulic acid (C10H10O4), and 2.9 to 4.4 mg of Borneolum syntheticum, expressed in terms of Borneol (C10H18O). This study was designed as a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study based on standard anti-anginal therapy and parallel groups. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The trial was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry Platform (http://www.chictr.org.cn/, Identifier: ChiCTR2100050066).

Participants

A total of 176 patients from 8 clinical study centers in China were included in this study. Patients aged 30 to 70 years with a diagnosis of chronic coronary syndrome were enrolled who were intolerant to side effects of long-acting nitrates. The patients were clinically stable, had a Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) Angina Severity Classification of I to III, and had received optimal medical treatment with a fixed dosage for at least 2 weeks. The diagnosis of chronic coronary syndrome was according to the Guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of chronic stable angina developed by the Chinese Society of Cardiovascular Diseases and the Editorial Board of the Chinese Journal of Cardiovascular Diseases in 2007 [22]. A patient is considered to experience common adverse effects to long-acting nitrates if they exhibit hypotension, headache, or flushing after taking long-acting nitrate formulations, such as isosorbide dinitrate or isosorbide mononitrate, at or below the recommended prescribed dose. If these symptoms lessen or disappear upon dose reduction or discontinuation, and the patient is unable to continue taking the medications due to intolerance to these symptoms—regardless of whether they are on the recommended prescribed dose, a reduced dose, or switching to another long-acting nitrate formulation—they are classified as intolerant to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates.

During the screening period, participants with a history or clinical evidence of poorly controlled or severe disease were excluded. These included poorly controlled hypertension, poorly controlled diabetes, severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency, severe arrhythmias, implanted pacemaker, cerebral vascular accident within one year, other severe cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, active or recurrent peptic ulcer disease, other diseases with high bleeding risk, other severe digestive diseases, malignancies, severe hematologic disorders, and uncontrolled psychiatric illness. Additionally, participants with aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and creatinine (Cr) levels exceeding 1.5 times the upper limit of normal were also excluded. All participants provided written informed consent, and the study was conducted in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Randomization and masking

Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to one of two groups of interventions: placebo group or SJPs group. An independent statistician used a stratified block randomization method to randomly assign participants at each study site. A random sequence was generated using SPSS software (version 22.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) with a random seed. To maintain blindness, placebo pills were the same size, shape, color, smell, taste, dosage forms, and outer packaging as SJPs. Personnel involved in this study were blinded to treatment assignment.

Procedures

This study consisted of a 2-week screening period followed by a 4-week treatment period. The placebo pills and SJPs used in the study were provided by the Six Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) Factory of Zhongxin Pharmaceuticals (Tianjin, China). During the 4-week treatment period, all participants were instructed to orally administer the study medication sublingually after meals, three times a day, taking 5 capsules each time (total of 15 capsules per day), continuously for 4 weeks. Throughout the treatment period, participants were prohibited from receiving any other anti-anginal therapy-related concomitant medications, Traditional Chinese Medicine therapies (acupuncture, moxibustion, Tuina, or cupping), as well as revascularization therapies (such as coronary artery bypass graft or percutaneous coronary intervention). Telephone or outpatient follow-up was conducted once a week, and participants were asked whether they had experienced any adverse events, and asked about the amount of pills taken to assess the treatment adherence.

Withdrawal criteria

The protocol treatment was discontinued if any of the following conditions occurred: continuous worsening of the subjects' conditions during the trial; the onset of specific comorbidities, complications, or physiological changes; self-administration of unauthorized medication or the concurrent use of prohibited medications specified in the protocol, which could impact the assessment of efficacy and safety; the occurrence of an adverse event or a serious adverse event; breaking the blind during the trial; and voluntary withdrawal of subjects from the trial.

Study endpoints and assessments

The primary outcome of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of the intervention after a 4-week period. The efficacy is determined by calculating the percentage change in the summary score of the Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ-SS) from baseline to post-intervention. Patients who demonstrate an improvement in the SAQ-SS of ≥ 20 are classified as "effective". Additionally, the severity of angina is assessed by evaluating its impact on five domains of the SAQ, namely: physical limitation, angina stability, angina frequency, treatment satisfaction, and quality of life [23]. The second endpoint of the study focused on the safety of the investigated SJPs, as well as changes in the Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) main syndrome score and lipid parameters, including total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and triglycerides (TG). Safety assessments were conducted by closely monitoring adverse events reported by the patients and analyzing laboratory test results. Based on the Guiding Principles for Clinical Research of New Chinese Medicines, the TCM main syndrome score is the sum of five patients' self-reported symptom scores (chest pain, chest tightness, shortness of breath, fatigue, and palpitation). More detailed scoring criteria for the TCM main syndrome score can be found in Supplementary Table 1.

Sample size estimation

According to the initially registered protocol, a total of 236 subjects were recruited. However, only 176 subjects who met the inclusion criteria were ultimately included in the randomization process. We noticed that the latest research report showed a lower efficacy rate of the SJPs intervention after 4 weeks than initially expected [9]. Therefore, we recalculated the sample size estimation. The sample size was calculated based on the anticipated curative efficacy rate in Power and Sample Size (HyLown Consulting LLC; http://powerandsamplesize.com/). It was assumed that the curative efficacy rate of the placebo group would be 16.30% and that of the treatment group would be 29.20% [9]. Accounting for a projected dropout rate of 20%, it was determined that each group should ideally consist of 52 participants, maintaining a 1:1 ratio. This sample size estimation would ensure 80% power with a significance level (α) of 0.05 for a two-sided test.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.2.0, R Core Team, https://www.r-project.org/) and RStudio (version 2023.06.2 Build 561, Posit, PBC, Boston, MA, https://posit.co/). All analyses of primary and secondary efficacy endpoints were conducted in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population at the endline. Intervention effects were estimated using univariable linear regression models to determine mean differences. Intervention effects for binary outcomes were reported as odds ratios (ORs) along with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) estimated from univariable logistic regression models. The mITT population included all randomized patients who received at least one dose of the study medication (SJPs or placebo).

Post hoc exploratory analyses were conducted to explore the factors influencing the anti-angina effectiveness of SJPs. Multivariate linear regression models were used to adjust for covariates and assess the correlation between potential factors and SAQ score. We examined the effects on the primary outcome through prespecified subgroup analyses based on age, gender, diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension, and alcohol consumption. Likelihood ratio tests were used to assess the multiplicative interaction and determine the variations in effect size among different subgroups of the population.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

The overall flow of trial is summarized in Fig. 1. A total of 236 patients diagnosed with chronic coronary syndrome, who intolerant to side effects of nitrates, were screened between August 1, 2021, and June 1, 2023, in 8 hospitals, and 176 eligible patients were enrolled. However, only 174 patients were included in the mITT analyses due to the fact that two subjects had taken SJPs or placebos less than once. Of them, the average age was 59.5 (SD 8.57), and 44.3% of participants were females. The detailed demographic and clinical characteristics of the participants are presented in Table 1. Antianginal therapies were commonly prescribed aspirin (73.6%), statin (71.3%), and β-Blockers (54.0%). At baseline, 96.0% of the subjects had SAQ summary scores below 80, indicating mild-to-moderate levels of angina physical limitation (mean score 59.64 ± 12.72) and angina frequency (mean score 67.59 ± 20.90).

Primary outcome

In Fig. 1, we present results of the primary as-treated analysis for angina status. Table 2 and Figs. 2– 4 demonstrate the primary outcome. A significantly higher number of patients achieved a response with SJPs compared to placebo after the 4-week treatment (Table 2 and Fig. 3). Specifically, 60.9% of patients treated with SJPs achieved a 20-point or greater improvement in SAQ-SS, while only 39.1% (OR = 2.43, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.32 to 4.47, P = 0.004) of patients in the placebo group achieved the same level of improvement (Table 2 and Fig. 3). Furthermore, SJPs demonstrated superior efficacy in improving SAQ-PL, SAQ-AS, and SAQ-AF, with 17.2% (OR = 2.81, 95% CI = 1.04 to 7.63, P = 0.042), 86.2% (OR = 2.25, 95% CI = 1.04 to 4.87, P = 0.040), and 56.3% (OR = 2.58, 95% CI = 1.39 to 4.77, P = 0.003) of SJPs-treated patients achieving a 20-point or greater improvement in these respective domains. In comparison, the placebo group showed lower percentages of improvement, with 6.9% for SAQ-PL, 73.6% for SAQ-AS, and 33.3% for SAQ-AF (Table 2 and Fig. 3). Further emphasizing angina frequency, SAQ-AF was categorized into ranges to improve the clinical interpretation. The transition from patients experiencing angina symptoms on a daily or weekly basis at baseline to patients experiencing symptoms on a monthly basis after intervention is considered an improvement in angina frequency. Figure 4 illustrates that 37.9% of patients treated with SJPs displayed improved angina frequency, while only 23.0% showed improvement following placebo intervention (P = 0.048; Chi-squared test).

The SAQ scores were reanalyzed as a continuous variable (Fig. 2). After 4 weeks, the SJPs intervention resulted in an improvement of 5.68 (95% CI = 2.37 to 8.99, P < 0.001, SJPs vs. placebo: 15.46 vs. 9.78) for the summary score in SAQ (SAQ-SS), compared to the placebo intervention. This was driven by improved physical limitation (SAQ-PL, β = 3.35, 95% CI = 0.77 to 5.93, P = 0.012) and reduced angina frequency (SAQ-AF, β = 10.34, 95% CI = 5.05 to 15.64, P < 0.001). Improved quality-of-life scores (SAQ-QL) and treatment satisfaction scores (SAQ-TS) were observed in patients randomized to SJPs compared with those randomized to placebo at 4 weeks, but those effect estimates did not attain statistical significance. Apart from these, subjects in the SJPs group had a 45.11 increase in angina stability scores (SAQ-AS), whereas subjects in the placebo group had only a 33.91 increase (β = 11.21, 95% CI = 2.22 to 20.19, P = 0.016).

Second outcome

After four weeks of intervention, the changes in the SJPs group for TC (β = -0.44, 95% CI = -1.00 to 0.12, P = 0.124), LDL-C (β = -0.06, 95% CI = -0.28 to 0.17, P = 0.629), and TG (β = -0.17, 95% CI = -0.44 to 0.10, P = 0.212) were not statistically significant when compared with the placebo group (Table 3).

Exploratory analyses

To explore the factors influencing the anti-angina effectiveness of SJPs, we conducted a post hoc exploratory analysis based on subjects in the SJPs group. The results of the univariate regression analysis showed that only alcohol consumption status was associated with the anti-angina efficacy of SJPs (Supplementary Table 2 and Supplementary Table 3). Upon adjusting for potential confounders, SJPs demonstrated significantly better anti-angina efficacy in subjects who had never consumed alcohol compared to those who were currently consuming alcohol (β = -11.58, 95% CI = -22.32 to -0.85, P = 0.038).

Adverse effects

Participants in this study were asked about any adverse events during the weekly telephone surveys. No participants reported experiencing adverse effects. Next, serum levels of AST, ALT, BUN, CR, and UA were measured to assess the presence of abnormalities associated with SJPs in liver and kidney function. Although the laboratory indicators in the SJPs group were higher than those in the placebo group, the differences did not reach statistical significance (Supplementary Table 4).

Subgroup and sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analyses were conducted to verify the robustness of the principal findings. The congruence between the results drawn from patients with an SAQ Summary score of less than 80 with the primary findings was established (Supplementary Table 5). A prespecified subgroup analysis was performed. The direction of effect estimates in all evaluated subgroups was consistent with the overall outcomes (Fig. 5). However, no significant improvements were observed in the population with current or former alcohol use (β = 1.82, 95% CI = -5.88 to 9.52, P = 0.646). Interaction tests indicated that the difference in changes in the SAQ-SS between the two groups was significantly larger among patients with no history of alcohol use (β = 10.58, 95% CI = 5.11 to 16.05, P < 0.001) than among patients with current or former alcohol use (P for interaction = 0.041). This suggests that patients with no history of alcohol use may be more responsive to SJPs.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the inaugural randomized controlled study that specifically addresses patients with CCS who experience intolerance to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates. The findings of this study demonstrate that SJPs have a significant positive impact on physical limitation, angina stability, and angina frequency. Moreover, our research has identified that patients without a history of alcohol consumption derive greater benefits from the addition of SJPs to conventional treatment.

A large body of clinical evidence has confirmed the benefits of SJP treatment for patients with unstable angina and acute myocardial infarction [10, 11, 21, 24]. Evidence from meta-analysis supports that SJPs are not inferior to nitroglycerin or isosorbide dinitrate in improving symptoms in patients with CCS [21, 25], even with a short-term intervention of just 4 weeks [21]. Furthermore, a recent randomized clinical trial demonstrated that SJPs also effectively improved stable angina on physical limitations, angina stability, and treatment satisfaction, after 24 weeks of intervention [9]. Interestingly, the aforementioned trial found that after 4 weeks of intervention, the SJPs did not demonstrate superior anti-angina efficacy compared to the placebo [9]. However, we obtain the opposite result, which provides new evidence that SJPs remain the effective in treating angina, even with short-term treatment (4 weeks) and in subjects intolerant to nitrate medication adverse reactions. The difference here may be attributed to the specificity of the population intolerant to the adverse effects of nitrates. We did not power our study to detect a significant difference in treatment satisfaction and quality of life, possibly because the 4-week short-term treatment with SJPs was insufficient to trigger significant clinical benefit in those two domains.

The preserved endothelial function may explain the observed anti-angina effect following short-term intervention with SJPs in individuals who experience adverse reactions to nitrate medication. Current evidence supports the notion that nitrate-induced headache signifies well-preserved vascular endothelial function. Nitrates undergo metabolism to produce nitric oxide, which causes dilation of the cephalic arteries. This represents the conventional theory explaining the occurrence of nitrate-induced headaches. The primary evidence supporting this theory is obtained from clinical studies that investigate the immediate effects of intravenous administration of nitric oxide donors [26, 27]. Recent research has demonstrated that isosorbide-5-mononitrate, an extended-release oral formulation, predominantly triggers vascular headaches by dilating extracerebral arteries [28]. Patients with normal or mild coronary artery disease are more prone to experiencing nitrate-induced headaches compared to those with obstructive coronary artery disease, indicating that nitrate-induced headache may serve as a valuable indicator of intact vascular reactivity and mild coronary artery damage [29–32]. Based on the aforementioned results, it can be inferred that the anti-angina effectiveness of SJPs relies on endothelial function.

Previous evidence has confirmed that high levels of alcohol intake can impair endothelial function [33–35]. Alcohol may reduce the bioavailability of nitric oxide by inducing oxidative stress [36–38], and promote endothelial cell senescence [39, 40]. Additionally, alcohol can cause excessive vasoconstriction [41]. Our research has found that patients who currently consume alcohol exhibit a diminished treatment response to SJPs. This finding further supports the premise that maintaining intact endothelial function is essential for the anti-anginal effect of SJPs.

The findings of this study provide support for the potential of short-term adjunct SJP therapy as an effective and safe option for managing CCS symptoms in patients who are intolerant to long-acting nitrates. Additionally, these findings suggest that abstaining from alcohol may enhance the therapeutic effectiveness of this treatment approach in patients with CCS who experience intolerance to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates. These results highlight the significance of our research in improving the management of CCS and emphasize the importance of investigating the underlying mechanisms and potential interactions between alcohol consumption and the effectiveness of adjunct SJP therapy in this specific patient population. Further studies are warranted to delve deeper into these aspects and expand our understanding in this area.

The current trial is subject to several limitations. Firstly, we did not conduct a longer follow-up for patients who discontinued the treatment after completing a 4-week course. Extending the follow-up period and assessing the angina status after the withdrawal of SJPs could have provided valuable insights into the long-term efficacy of the drug. Secondly, it is important to interpret the results of post-hoc exploratory analyses cautiously, as they may be susceptible to biases and should be viewed as preliminary findings. Thirdly, while the sample size was deemed appropriate given the longer duration of clinical trials and the challenges of recruiting special populations, future research may require a larger sample size to enhance reliability and further verify our findings.

Conclusions

The results of this multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial highlight the significant short-term therapeutic benefits of adding SJPs to conventional treatment for patients with CCS who experience intolerance to the adverse effects of long-acting nitrates, and this treatment approach demonstrates a favorable safety profile. Moreover, patients with no history of alcohol consumption, whether in the past or present, derived greater benefits from the addition of SJPs to conventional treatment.

Integrating spatial lymph node patterns and multimodal clinicopathological features to predict post-neoadjuvant recurrence in gastric adenocarcinoma: a machine learning nomogram
Authors: Zhao Fazhi, Hu Shangzhi, Ding Zhi, Liu Yang, Xiao Shuomeng, Zhou Yehan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveConventional TNM staging systems exhibit limited prognostic accuracy in gastric cancer patients following neoadjuvant therapy (NAT), creating an urgent need for novel risk-stratification tools. This study aimed to develop a machine learning-based nomogram integrating lymph node tumor regression grade (LN.TRG) and other clinicopathological features to improve recurrence prediction in this setting.MethodsA cohort of 112 gastric cancer patients receiving NAT was retrospectively analyzed. Clinical and pathological features, including lymph node tumor regression grade (LN.TRG) were evaluated. Univariable analysis and Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression were employed to identify recurrence-free survival (RFS)-associated predictors. A predictive nomogram was developed using multivariable Cox regression and validated via ROC curves, decision curve analysis (DCA), and calibration curves. Additional analyses explored the prognostic impact of metastatic lymph node location, residual tumor ratio in lymph nodes, and lymph node dissection count.ResultsSix key predictors—including LN.TRG and lymph node-positive rate—were selected via Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression. The model exhibited robust discrimination in both training (AUC: 0.836–0.886) and validation (AUC: 0.907–0.957) sets. DCA and calibration curves confirmed clinical utility and stability. Risk stratification via ggrisk plots and Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed significant separation between high- and low-risk groups (P < 0.001), outperforming traditional staging systems (ypStage/ycStage). A fitted curve demonstrated a negative correlation between recurrence risk and lymph node dissection count (P < 0.05). Location-specific analysis identified higher residual tumor ratios and hazard ratios (HR) in station No. 5 and No. 6 lymph nodes.ConclusionThe LN.TRG-integrated model enables precise risk stratification for post-NAT gastric cancer. Proactive dissection of lymph nodes, particularly station No. 5 and No. 6, may improve patient outcomes. This study offers insights to enhance survival and quality of life.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-15830-9.

Introduction

Gastric cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with a dismal five-year survival rate of approximately 16.7%-37.9% [1]. Over 60% of patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, where conventional therapeutic strategies often fail to achieve satisfactory outcomes. The emergence of neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) as a transformative strategy has significantly improved pathological responses and survival rates [2]. However, the integration of NAT has substantially diminished the prognostic validity of traditional TNM staging systems, particularly in predicting post-therapeutic recurrence risk [3]. This paradigm shift has created a critical gap in risk stratification, leaving clinicians without reliable tools to guide postoperative management. Consequently, developing novel prognostic frameworks that incorporate treatment response biomarkers is imperative for optimizing personalized therapeutic strategies (Table 1).

In recent years, the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) for gastric cancer and the factors influencing prognosis have become a research hotspot. Numerous studies have demonstrated that tumor regression grade (TRG) is a crucial indicator for evaluating the effectiveness of NAC, with the TRG of the primary tumor (PT) being significantly associated with patient survival [4–6]. For instance, Gao et al. found that combining the TRG of lymph node metastasis (LNM) with that of the primary tumor can more accurately predict patient prognosis, and the heterogeneity of lymph node response independently affects survival [4]. Additionally, Wu et al. confirmed through a systematic review and meta-analysis that a lower TRG score is correlated with longer disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) [5]. Another study evaluated lymph node regression using imaging and proposed that lymph node regression could serve as a predictive indicator of pathological response, further developing a scoring system based on clinical features [6]. However, most of these studies have focused on the prognostic value of ypN staging, such as Zhao et al. indicating that ypN is an independent predictor of survival [4]. The analysis of the weight of specific parameters for lymph node stations (such as stations 1, 2, 3, and distant metastatic lymph nodes) and their detailed parameters (such as TRG heterogeneity and metastatic distance) remains insufficient [4, 7]. Moreover, existing studies rarely integrate the interaction between lymph node stations and TRG, and there is a lack of in-depth exploration of the relationship between the spatial distribution of lymph node response and prognosis [4, 8]. Therefore, this study aims to fill this gap by summarizing clinical and pathological outcomes and precisely evaluating the predictive value of lymph node stations and TRG, thereby providing a more comprehensive pathological basis for individualized treatment.

Postoperative recurrence, predominantly driven by regional lymph node metastasis rather than local recurrence, underscores the prognostic significance of nodal status [9]. While primary tumor regression grade (PT.TRG) has been established as a key predictor of survival, the role of lymph node tumor regression grade (LN.TRG)—a potential biomarker reflecting therapeutic efficacy in metastatic lymph nodes—remains underexplored [10]. To address these gaps, this study introduces a novel LN-TRG classification system and integrates multidimensional pathological parameters, including lymph node metastatic burden and spatial distribution patterns. We further analyzed high-risk lymph node stations and their prognostic implications, aiming to establish a robust recurrence prediction model. By bridging the knowledge gap between treatment response and nodal disease characteristics, this work provides new insights for refining prognostic models and tailoring adjuvant therapies, ultimately striving to improve survival and quality of life for gastric cancer patients.

Methods

Inclusion and exclusion criteria. Inclusion Criteria:1. Pathologically confirmed gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma (endoscopic biopsy);2. Clinical stage II-IV (AJCC/UICC 8th ed, imaging-confirmed);3. ECOG 0–1 or Karnofsky ≥ 70%; Exclusion Criteria:1. non-epithelial/uncertain histology;2. Antitumor therapy contraindications or unresectable tumors;3. Uncontrolled organ dysfunction;4. Prior gastric cancer systemic therapy;5. Concurrent/5-year-history malignancies.

A total of 112 patients received preoperative chemoradiotherapy consisting of FOLFOX-based chemotherapy (1.99 ± 0.52 cycles) with optional targeted agents and/or immune checkpoint inhibitors. Radiation therapy was administered using image-guided intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IG-IMRT) with the following dose fractionation: Gross tumor volume (GTV) received 1.8–2.0 Gy per fraction, while lymph node metastases (GTV-LN) and clinical target volume (CTV) encompassing subclinical regions and lymphatic drainage areas were irradiated with 2.0 Gy and 1.8 Gy per fraction, respectively. The treatment course consisted of 20–25 fractions, delivering a total dose of 45–50.4 Gy to the target volumes.

All surgical specimens were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned into 4-µm slices for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis was performed using the streptavidin-peroxidase (SP) method on an automated immunohistochemistry platform following standardized protocols. For histopathological evaluation, primary tumor regression grade (PT.TRG) was classified according to the Becker criteria into four categories: Grade 0 (no residual tumor), Grade 1 (< 10% viable tumor cells), Grade 2 (10–50% viable tumor cells), and Grade 3 (> 50% viable tumor cells). A novel five-tier lymph node tumor regression grading (LN.TRG) system was established: True Negative (Ture Neg, no tumor involvement confirmed by IHC), Grade 0 (0% viable tumor cells with residual tumor bed), Grade 1 (< 10% viable tumor cells), Grade 2 (10–50% viable tumor cells), and Grade 3 (> 50% viable tumor cells).All TRG assessments were independently conducted by two pathologists, with discrepancies resolved through consensus review and additional IHC verification. Pathological complete response (pCR) was defined as the absence of viable tumor cells in both primary lesion and regional lymph nodes post-neoadjuvant therapy, while major pathological response (MPR) indicated < 10% residual viable tumor cells in the primary tumor. Tumor proportion was quantified as tumor area divided by total tissue area on histopathological sections, and lymph node (LN) positive rate was calculated as the ratio of metastatic LNs to total dissected LNs. Before(bf) and after(af) surgery complete blood counts were analyzed, including white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NEU), hemoglobin (HB), and platelet count (PLT).

Recurrence-free survival (RFS) analysis was performed using univariable Cox regression, Kaplan-Meier curves, and log-rank tests, while multivariable analysis was conducted using Cox proportional hazards regression. The dataset was randomly divided into a training set and a validation set at a 7:3 ratio via random sampling. Variables were screened using Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis and subsequently incorporated into a multivariable Cox regression model to establish a predictive nomogram. The cutoff value for risk stratification was determined from the training set and subsequently applied to the validation cohort to ensure consistency and avoid overfitting [11]. The model’s performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calibration curves. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to evaluate the model’s discriminative ability at 12, 24, and 36 months post-surgery, reflecting short-, intermediate-, and long-term recurrence prediction. Net Benefit = (True Positives / n) – (False Positives / n) × (pt / (1 – pt)), where pt is the threshold probability and “n” is the total number of patients. All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (version 4.3.1; http://www.R-project.org, The R Foundation) and the Free Statistics analysis platform. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was defined as the time interval from the date of surgery to the first documented recurrence or death from any cause. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

This study included 112 patients with a mean age of 61.0 ± 10.1 years, of whom 81.2% were male. All cases were histologically confirmed as adenocarcinoma, predominantly characterized by the mixed type (50.9%) according to the Lauren classification and moderate differentiation (51.8%). Regarding treatment modalities, 53.5% of patients received neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone, 41.2% underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy, and 5.3% were treated with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. An R0 resection was achieved in 94.6% of patients. After a median follow-up duration of 29.5 months, pathological complete response (pCR) and major pathological response (MPR) were observed in 12.5% and 26.8% of patients, respectively. During the follow-up period, disease recurrence occurred in 38.3% of cases, and 39.2% of patients died (Fig. 1A, Supplementary Table 1).

Univariate analysis identified 12 significant prognostic factors for recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS): Differentiation, Vascular invasion, Nerve invasion, Tumor proportion, ypT, ypN(The (0, Inf) CI is due to data sparsity in those subgroups), Positive LN, LN positive rate, ypStage, PT.TRG, LN.TRG, pCR, and MPR. LASSO regression was subsequently applied to these candidate variables to optimize feature selection. As the log λ value increased, the coefficient magnitudes of the variables progressively decreased. Through 10-fold cross-validation, the optimal regularization parameter was determined at log λ = -2.749, yielding six key predictors most strongly associated with RFS: Differentiation, Vascular invasion, Tumor proportion, Positive LN, LN positive rate, and LN.TRG (Left: Coefficient trajectories of variables across log(λ) values. The vertical dashed line indicates the optimal λ selected via 10-fold cross-validation. Right: Cross-validation curve showing mean squared error (MSE) versus log(λ) Fig. 1B). A recurrence risk prediction model was developed using these selected predictors. The constructed nomogram demonstrates differential weighting across variables, with LN positive rate and LN.TRG contributing the highest prognostic weights. Each factor’s score is summated to generate an individualized recurrence risk estimate, enabling quantitative assessment of postoperative recurrence probability (Fig. 1C).

The prediction model demonstrated excellent discriminative ability (C-index = 0.909) and calibration (Brier Score = 0.097) in the training set. Through internal validation with 200 repetitions of 5-fold cross-validation, its performance remained high (calibrated C-index = 0.881, Brier Score = 0.125) (Supplementary Table 2, Supplementary Fig. 1). ROC analysis demonstrated robust discriminative capacity, with the training set achieving area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.879 (12-month), 0.836 (24-month), and 0.886 (36-month). The validation set exhibited even stronger predictive performance, showing superior AUCs of 0.907 (12-month), 0.924 (24-month), and 0.957 (36-month) (Fig. 2A). We further analyzed lymph node (LN) characteristics. The tumor residue ratio within LNs determined distinct LN.TRG categories (Fig. 3A), which demonstrated strong prognostic correlations. Notably, the presence of a tumor bed in LNs, even without histologically confirmed residual carcinoma (N-), exhibited comparable survival outcomes to node-positive (N+) cases (Fig. 3B). Nonlinear regression analysis revealed an inverse relationship between the number of dissected LNs and hazard ratios (HRs). When ≥ 20.5 LNs were excised, the HR fell below 1, indicating significant survival benefits beyond this threshold (Fig. 3C). Spatial analysis was performed according to the Japanese Gastric Cancer Association (JGCA) classification, where ‘Groups’ refer to anatomical lymph node groups (e.g., Group No. 5: suprapyloric; Group No. 6: infrapyloric), and ‘Stations’ refer to the nodal stations (I, II, III) based on anatomic proximity to the primary tumor. Spatial analysis identified Group No.5 and No.6 as high-risk zones with elevated HRs (Figs. 3D–F), while Group No.3 and No.4d exhibited higher metastasis rates (24.1% and 16.1%, respectively), and Group No.5 and No.6 showed predominant tumor residue ratios (56.7% and 55.8%) (Fig. 3F). For LN stations, Station III demonstrated the highest HRs, whereas Station I was associated with elevated metastasis rates (37.5%) and tumor residue ratios (47.8%) (Figs. 3D–F). Forest plots demonstrated that an increasing number of positive lymph node (LN) groups and stations were significantly associated with progressively elevated hazard ratios (HRs) in multivariable Cox regression analyses (Fig. 3E).

Discussion

This study addresses the challenge of stratifying recurrence risk in gastric cancer patients following neoadjuvant therapy by integrating multidimensional clinicopathological parameters (e.g., differentiation status, vascular invasion, tumor proportion) with novel pathological indicators, including lymph node tumor regression grade (LN-TRG) and lymph node positivity rate. Leveraging LASSO regression for variable selection, we developed a nomogram-based prognostic model that demonstrates robust predictive performance in both training (AUC: 0.836–0.886) and validation cohorts (AUC: 0.907–0.957). Risk stratification revealed significantly higher recurrence rates in high-risk groups, while spatial analysis identified high-risk anatomical features, such as the prognostic significance of station 5 and 6 lymph nodes. This model overcomes the limitations of conventional TNM staging in the neoadjuvant therapy era by incorporating nodal therapeutic response and anatomical characteristics into prognostic evaluation, offering a novel framework for personalized postoperative management and improved survival outcomes.

Prior prognostic models predominantly relied on primary tumor regression grade (PT-TRG) or traditional TNM staging, which exhibit diminished predictive utility in the neoadjuvant setting due to altered tumor biology and nodal dynamics [12, 13]. Although emerging studies have identified associations between HER2 status, nutritional indices, and systemic inflammatory markers with prognostic outcomes, the clinical utility of these individual parameters in risk stratification remains inadequately validated [14, 15]. Advanced molecular profiling techniques such as single-cell sequencing or circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis show promise in identifying high-risk populations but face clinical adoption barriers due to prohibitive costs [16]. Crucially, existing approaches have overlooked the prognostic heterogeneity of lymph nodes: (1) the discordance between primary tumor and nodal metastatic regression [17], and (2) the impact of lymph node dissection extent and spatial metastatic patterns [18]. Mandard TRG assesses regression only in the primary tumor, based on the ratio of residual tumor to fibrosis. It does not capture lymph node response.Our LN.TRG system specifically evaluates regression in lymph nodes, ranging from complete pathological response to residual metastatic disease. We demonstrate that lymph node regression is independently prognostic, providing complementary information not available from primary tumor–focused systems.Furthermore, conventional models often fail to integrate multimodal quantitative analyses, limiting their ability to comprehensively assess residual tumor burden and micrometastatic risk.

In our study, we not only established a recurrence risk assessment model, but also paid attention to lymph node status. Our study systematically elucidates the multidimensional prognostic significance of lymph node status through three key advances: (1) Development of the LN-TRG classification system, demonstrating that lymph nodes with tumor beds but no viable cells (N-) carry recurrence risks comparable to node-positive (N+) cases; (2) Identification of critical thresholds, including a lymph node positivity rate cutoff and a survival benefit threshold at ≥ 20.5 dissected lymph nodes [19]; (3) Spatial mapping revealing station 5 and 6 lymph nodes as high-risk zones with elevated residual tumor ratios, likely attributable to their anatomical propensity for tumor retention. These findings, previously unreported, highlight the necessity of meticulous dissection in these regions. The observed scoring pattern in the nomogram (LN.TRG Grade 2 < Grade 1) is indeed a result of the multivariable Cox regression model, which accounts for interactions and collinearity among predictors. This may reflect the complex biological behavior of lymph node regression, where certain intermediate regression states (Grade 2) may not linearly correlate with recurrence risk. By integrating multimodal parameters (clinical, pathological, radiological, and laboratory markers including dynamic hematological profiles) through LASSO regression with subsequent ROC validation, our predictive model demonstrates enhanced diagnostic accuracy by employing objective quantitative metrics to minimize human interpretive variability, achieving these clinical advancements without significantly increasing diagnostic expenditures.

This retrospective single-center analysis (n = 112) may limit the generalizability of our model across broader populations. Heterogeneity in neoadjuvant regimens (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and chemoradiotherapy) introduces potential confounding despite multivariate adjustment. While our model demonstrates robust prognostic value, its performance for predicting late recurrence remains uncertain due to the current median follow-up of 29.5 months. Future studies with matured survival data are needed to confirm its long-term efficacy. Although our nomogram integrates key lymph node-related features and demonstrates robust prognostic performance, the causal or mechanistic pathways linking these variables—such as the interplay between LN.TRG, nodal spatial distribution, and metastatic burden—are not fully delineated. Future studies employing causal mediation analysis or network-based modeling approaches may help clarify these complex interrelationships and enhance the biological interpretability of our model [20]. To address the limitations of the single-center design and limited sample size, we plan to conduct external validation in multi-center cohorts to confirm the broad applicability of our model. Additionally, manual pathological assessment of lymph nodes poses reproducibility challenges, necessitating future integration of digital pathology workflows and artificial intelligence-assisted quantification to standardize LN-TRG evaluation.

By harmonizing multimodal parameters and machine learning-driven modeling, this study establishes the first prognostic framework for gastric cancer post-neoadjuvant therapy that incorporates lymph node therapeutic response and anatomical specificity. The model not only informs adjuvant therapy intensification for high-risk patients but also advocates for proactive dissection of high-risk nodal stations (e.g., stations 5 and 6). These findings collectively represent a paradigm shift in addressing the clinical inadequacy of conventional staging systems. Future multicenter prospective studies with expanded cohorts and external validation are imperative to refine this model, with complementary integration of molecular biomarkers and radiomic features to advance precision oncology strategies.

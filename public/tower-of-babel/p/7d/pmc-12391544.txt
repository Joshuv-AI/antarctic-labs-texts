Distinct safety profiles of albumin-bound nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel in pancreatic cancer focusing on hematologic and hepatobiliary risks
Authors: Nan Qin, Zhongliu Bian, Jingtao Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNab-paclitaxel (albumin-bound paclitaxel) and traditional paclitaxel are both used in pancreatic cancer treatment, but their adverse event (AE) profiles may differ due to formulation differences. This study compares the safety profiles of Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel using the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database. FAERS data from Q1 2004 to Q2 2024 were analyzed using disproportionality methods, including Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR) and Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), to detect significant AE signals. Adverse events were categorized by system organ classes, and key signals were identified and compared for each drug. Nab-paclitaxel was associated with a higher frequency of severe hematologic and hepatobiliary events, including neutropenia (ROR 44.63; PRR 23.86) and cholangitis (ROR 113.06). Additionally, Nab-paclitaxel had 539 reports of death (27.08%), while paclitaxel had 272 (9.68%). Paclitaxel was more frequently associated with infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions, with 172 cases of flushing and 116 hypersensitivity reactions. Time trends showed a steady increase in Nab-paclitaxel-related AEs, especially hematologic toxicities, suggesting greater risk accumulation over time. Nab-paclitaxel’s nanoparticle formulation, while enhancing distribution and clearance properties, is linked to elevated hematologic and hepatic AEs in pancreatic cancer treatment, requiring vigilant patient monitoring.Keywords: Nab-paclitaxel, Paclitaxel, Pancreatic cancer, Hematologic toxicity, Hepatobiliary safetySubject terms: Cancer, Drug development

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer is one of the most aggressive and treatment-resistant malignancies, with limited therapeutic options and a poor prognosis1,2. Standard chemotherapy regimens, including taxane-based drugs, play a crucial role in extending survival and managing symptoms in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer3. Paclitaxel, a key taxane agent, is commonly used in combination with other chemotherapeutics for treating various solid tumors4. Paclitaxel was approved by the FDA in 1992 for cancer treatment. However, its traditional formulation requires the use of solvents, which are known to contribute to infusion-related reactions and other toxicities, posing challenges to safe administration and patient tolerance.

Nab-paclitaxel, an albumin-bound nanoparticle formulation of paclitaxel, which was developed to overcome these limitations and enhance drug delivery, received approval in 2005 and was specifically approved for use in pancreatic cancer in 20135. By encapsulating paclitaxel within an albumin nanoparticle, Nab-paclitaxel allows for solvent-free administration, reducing the risk of hypersensitivity reactions and potentially improving distribution and clearance properties at tumor sites. This nanoparticle-based delivery system takes advantage of the natural affinity of albumin for accumulating in tumor tissues, enabling targeted drug release that may enhance efficacy against pancreatic cancer6.

While Nab-paclitaxel has shown clinical benefit in improving therapeutic outcomes, particularly in metastatic pancreatic cancer, its altered pharmacokinetics and enhanced cellular uptake could lead to a distinct adverse event (AE) profile compared to traditional paclitaxel. Despite the increasing utilization of Nab-paclitaxel in pancreatic cancer treatment, randomized clinical trials directly comparing the two formulations in this indication are lacking. Therefore, it is imperative to gain a better understanding of the differential adverse event profiles of these formulations using real-world data.

This study aims to analyze and compare the adverse event profiles of Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel specifically in the treatment of pancreatic cancer, utilizing data from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS). Through the use of disproportionality analysis methods, including Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), and Bayesian signal detection, this research seeks to identify significant adverse event signals and assess the potential safety risks associated with each formulation. The findings aim to support clinicians in making informed treatment decisions for pancreatic cancer patients by providing insights into the safety implications of Nab-paclitaxel’s nanoparticle-based delivery system in comparison with conventional paclitaxel.

Methods

Data source and extraction

This study used data from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), focusing on reports from Q1 2004 to Q2 2024. FAERS is a publicly accessible database that collects adverse event (AE) reports for drugs and biologics from healthcare professionals, manufacturers, and consumers. We used OpenVigil software to facilitate data extraction and standardization. This software enabled automated retrieval of AEs, removing duplicate records and aggregating data quarterly.

Study design

The study is a retrospective pharmacovigilance analysis comparing adverse events associated with Nab-paclitaxel (albumin-bound paclitaxel) and traditional paclitaxel. We identified cases based on generic and brand names to ensure comprehensive data coverage. Each AE report was categorized by variables, including demographic data (sex, age), outcome (e.g., hospitalization, death), and concomitant medications, allowing for robust statistical comparisons. Given that Nab-paclitaxel is predominantly used in combination with gemcitabine for pancreatic cancer, while paclitaxel may be used with various agents or for other indications, the impact of combination therapies on AE reporting was carefully considered.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

AE reports were included if: The primary suspect drug was either Nab-paclitaxel or paclitaxel; The report contained sufficient data on demographic and clinical characteristics. Reports were excluded if: AEs were listed under “indication bias,” where the AE could result directly from the treated disease rather than the drug itself; Data were incomplete, missing key variables necessary for comparative analysis.

Statistical analysis

To detect significant AE signals, we used three primary disproportionality analysis methods: Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), and Bayesian analysis via the Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN).

Reporting odds ratio (ROR): We calculated ROR to evaluate the association strength between drug exposure and AEs. A significant signal was defined by an ROR lower 95% confidence interval (CI) exceeding 17.

Trend and time scan analysis

To visualize trends in AE reporting, time-scan charts were generated for key adverse events using the information component (IC) over time. These time scans illustrate signal stability by showing if the IC trend remained steady or increased with a narrowing 95% credibility interval over time. We monitored these trends for prominent AEs, such as hematologic toxicity and hepatic disorders.

System organ class (SOC) categorization

Adverse events were further classified into System Organ Classes (SOCs) based on the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA). This classification enabled focused analysis on major organ systems affected by each drug, providing insights into unique and shared AE profiles for Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel.

Software and tools

Data were analyzed using R (version 4.1.2) for descriptive statistics and chi-square tests10. Ggplot2 and were used for graphical representation of data, including time-scan plots and comparison charts by SOC11.

Results

Demographic and outcome characteristics

The demographic analysis revealed notable differences in the patient populations associated with Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel, as summarized in Table 1. Nab-paclitaxel had a higher proportion of male reports (51.78%) compared to paclitaxel (23.22%), whereas paclitaxel-related reports were predominantly female (60.38%). This gender disparity may reflect varying patient demographics for these treatments, potentially influenced by their specific oncological applications and prescription patterns. Age distribution analysis further revealed that reports for Nab-paclitaxel were concentrated among older patients, with 60.10% of cases occurring in individuals aged 60 and above, suggesting that Nab-paclitaxel is more frequently prescribed to an older population. In contrast, paclitaxel reports were more evenly distributed across age groups, though a significant portion (39.28%) of reports lacked specific age information, which may affect demographic interpretation. In terms of serious outcomes, Nab-paclitaxel was associated with a markedly higher rate of death, accounting for 27.08% of reports, compared to 9.68% for paclitaxel. Additionally, hospitalization was a common outcome for both drugs but was more frequently observed in paclitaxel reports (44.09%), indicating potential differences in the severity profiles of adverse events between these drugs.

Indications and concomitant medications

Both Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel were primarily indicated for pancreatic cancer, a finding that aligns with their clinical usage in advanced cancer treatment settings. However, paclitaxel is not formally approved for pancreatic cancer and is typically used off-label, occasionally for economic reasons. Nab-paclitaxel was associated with a significantly higher number of reports for pancreatic cancer, with 4080 cases compared to 397 cases for paclitaxel, which may be attributed to Nab-paclitaxel’s established efficacy in this indication. Concomitant medication analysis revealed patterns reflective of supportive care in cancer therapy. For Nab-paclitaxel, dexamethasone and acetaminophen were the most commonly reported concomitant medications, with dexamethasone likely administered to mitigate hypersensitivity reactions and acetaminophen used for managing pain and fever associated with cancer or treatment side effects. In contrast, paclitaxel reports frequently included carboplatin as a concomitant medication, indicating its combination use in regimens for treating certain solid tumors (Table 2). The concomitant use of carboplatin and dexamethasone with paclitaxel underscores the drug’s application in combination therapy, which may affect the adverse event profile observed.

Top adverse events

The analysis of the top 20 adverse events for each drug, as shown in Table 3, demonstrated distinct profiles for Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel. For Nab-paclitaxel, death was the most frequently reported adverse event, with a total of 539 cases, underscoring the severity of the adverse events associated with its use. Hematologic toxicities were also prominent in Nab-paclitaxel reports, with high frequencies of neutropenia (277 cases) and febrile neutropenia (176 cases), which may be related to its cytotoxic mechanism of action in targeting cancer cells. Paclitaxel, on the other hand, showed a distinct adverse event profile, with infusion-related reactions, such as flushing and hypersensitivity, among the most frequently reported events. Flushing accounted for 172 cases, and hypersensitivity reactions were reported in 116 cases, reflecting the solvent-based formulation of paclitaxel, which is known to contribute to infusion reactions. The contrasting adverse event profiles suggest that the formulation and administration method significantly influence the types and frequencies of AEs associated with each drug.

Disproportionality analysis of adverse event signals

Disproportionality analysis using Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR) and Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR) provided insights into specific adverse event signals associated with Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel (Table 4). Hematologic toxicities emerged as a strong signal for Nab-paclitaxel, particularly for neutropenia, with an ROR of 44.63 and PRR of 23.86, highlighting the elevated hematologic risk associated with this drug. Other significant hematologic events, such as febrile neutropenia and thrombocytopenia, were also prevalent in Nab-paclitaxel reports, reflecting the drug’s impact on the bone marrow and blood cell production. In hepatobiliary disorders, both drugs demonstrated notable signals for cholangitis; however, the signal was more pronounced for Nab-paclitaxel, with an ROR of 113.06, indicating a heightened risk for bile duct-related complications. Gastrointestinal adverse events, such as pancreatic carcinoma as a co-reported condition, were more frequent in Nab-paclitaxel reports, while paclitaxel demonstrated lower ROR values for similar events, potentially due to its different usage and toxicity profile.

Time trends of adverse events

Figure 1 illustrates the trend of adverse event reporting frequency for Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel from 2004 to 2024, revealing a steady increase in reports associated with Nab-paclitaxel. This upward trend likely reflects both the expanded clinical use of Nab-paclitaxel over time and heightened pharmacovigilance practices, which may have led to increased monitoring and reporting of adverse events. In contrast, the reporting frequency for paclitaxel remained relatively stable over the study period, with some fluctuations that could be attributed to periodic increases in its clinical application. Specific time-scan analyses presented in Fig. 2 show that key adverse events, such as neutropenia and febrile neutropenia associated with Nab-paclitaxel, have exhibited sustained and growing signal intensities over recent years, as indicated by the rising information component (IC) values. This suggests an ongoing recognition of hematologic risks in the context of Nab-paclitaxel therapy, prompting further investigation and monitoring in clinical practice.

System organ class (SOC) distribution of key signals

Analysis by System Organ Class (SOC) revealed that Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel share some common adverse events, but with varying signal intensities across different organ systems, as displayed in Fig. 3. In the blood and lymphatic system disorders category, Nab-paclitaxel demonstrated a pronounced signal for neutropenia and thrombocytopenia, indicating a substantial hematologic risk that aligns with its cytotoxic profile. Hepatobiliary disorders were another key SOC in which both drugs displayed significant signals, particularly for cholangitis and bile duct complications, though Nab-paclitaxel’s signals were consistently higher, reflecting its higher overall association with hepatic toxicity. In the cardiac and vascular systems, paclitaxel was linked to hypersensitivity reactions that frequently manifest as flushing and tachycardia, likely due to the solvent-based delivery vehicle, whereas Nab-paclitaxel presented with signals for capillary leak syndrome, a vascular-related complication unique to its formulation. These SOC-based differences underscore the importance of individualized risk assessment when selecting between these drugs, particularly for patients with preexisting conditions in these organ systems.

Discussion

The primary findings of this study demonstrate distinct adverse event (AE) profiles for Nab-paclitaxel (albumin-bound paclitaxel) and traditional paclitaxel in the treatment of pancreatic cancer. Utilizing the FAERS database and disproportionality analysis, we have identified that Nab-paclitaxel is linked to a higher frequency of hematologic and hepatic adverse events, including neutropenia and cholangitis, while reports on paclitaxel were more commonly associated with infusion-related reactions such as hypersensitivity and flushing. These results emphasize the differential safety profiles of Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel, highlighting the unique safety considerations associated with nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems in pancreatic cancer therapy.

One of the most prominent findings was the elevated incidence of hematologic toxicities, particularly neutropenia and febrile neutropenia, associated with Nab-paclitaxel. This albumin-bound formulation of paclitaxel is designed to improve solubility, drug delivery, and retention within tumors, but this enhanced distribution and clearance properties also contributes to toxicity in hematopoietic cells, which are highly sensitive to cytotoxic agents. Research shows that Nab-paclitaxel interacts with CD11b + myeloid cells in the bone marrow, leading to their depletion, a key factor in its hematopoietic toxicity profile12. This interaction is further influenced by the albumin nanoparticles’ colloidal stability and disintegration, with the albumin coating reducing the need for solubilizers and thereby lowering hypersensitivity reactions while not eliminating hematopoietic toxicity13,14. Comparative studies with similar albumin-bound formulations, such as Cim-F-alb, indicate that alternative nanoparticle designs could potentially improve pharmacokinetics and reduce toxicity15. Additionally, Nab-paclitaxel’s ability to be engulfed by neutrophils and delivered to tumor sites may mitigate systemic myelosuppression, somewhat reducing hematologic toxicity relative to solvent-based paclitaxel formulations16. However, the approved dose of nab-paclitaxel is higher than that of traditional paclitaxel, which may contribute to greater observed hematologic toxicity. Adjunctive therapies, such as anti-CSF-1 to reduce bone marrow suppression and control myeloid cell recruitment to tumors, offer potential to alleviate hematologic risks associated with Nab-paclitaxel and underscore the need for supportive treatments that balance safety with the efficacy advantages provided by its nanoparticle-bound delivery12.

Hepatobiliary toxicities, including cholangitis and bile duct complications, were another significant category of AEs associated with Nab-paclitaxel in this study. The nanoparticle albumin-bound formulation of Nab-paclitaxel influences drug distribution and clearance, potentially enhancing drug accumulation in the liver and biliary system. This effect is partly attributed to Nab-paclitaxel’s use of Gp60 albumin receptors on vascular endothelial cells, which facilitate enhanced transport and tumor accumulation through receptor-mediated mechanisms17. Additionally, Nab-paclitaxel’s albumin-coated nanoparticles offer stability and avoid the need for toxic solvents, commonly linked to hepatobiliary toxicity in other formulations13,18. However, the disintegration of these nanoparticles upon intravenous administration can impact the release and distribution of paclitaxel, contributing to potential liver effects13. Nab-paclitaxel’s pharmacokinetic profile, characterized by rapid paclitaxel release, contrasts with other formulations, such as Cim-F-alb, which exhibit a longer plasma half-life and greater tumor deposition, suggesting that Nab-paclitaxel’s quicker systemic exposure could play a role in its hepatobiliary toxicity15,18. Although the albumin-bound formulation may aid in targeting liver metastases, it also raises the risk of liver-related adverse effects, particularly in pancreatic cancer patients who may already have biliary obstructions. This finding emphasizes the importance of hepatic monitoring for pancreatic cancer patients receiving Nab-paclitaxel treatment. While hepatotoxicity was not prominently reported in randomized breast cancer trials of Nab-paclitaxel, our real-world pharmacovigilance data suggest that hepatobiliary toxicity may be more prominent in pancreatic cancer, potentially due to disease-specific comorbidities, such as biliary obstruction, or differences in drug metabolism.

In contrast, paclitaxel showed a distinct profile dominated by hypersensitivity reactions and flushing, largely attributable to the solvents used in its formulation, such as Cremophor EL, which are known to provoke infusion-related reactions. These hypersensitivity reactions (HSRs) are complex phenomena involving both immune and non-immune mechanisms. For instance, some reactions may be IgE-mediated, as indicated by skin testing that identifies patients with IgE sensitivity, which can be crucial for risk stratification and management through desensitization protocols19. Additionally, paclitaxel can induce rapid histamine release, leading to vascular hyperpermeability and flushing, a response that can often be mitigated by histamine antagonists20. Further, activation of the RhoA/ROCK pathway has been implicated in vascular leakage and inflammation associated with non-IgE-mediated HSRs, while paclitaxel’s interaction with the C5a receptor (C5aR1) can lead to hypersensitivity and anaphylactic responses, positioning C5aR1 as a potential therapeutic target20,21. Although atopic patients may have an increased risk of severe reactions, these hypersensitivities are not directly related to sensitization from environmental or food allergens22. These mechanisms support the need for premedication protocols with corticosteroids and antihistamines, along with vigilant monitoring, to mitigate hypersensitivity risks during paclitaxel infusion. Unlike Nab-paclitaxel, which avoids solvent-associated risks via its nanoparticle-based delivery, paclitaxel’s formulation presents lower hematologic toxicity but requires greater precautions during administration, underscoring the clinical trade-offs between these two formulations.

Additionally, we observed that Nab-paclitaxel was more frequently associated with gastrointestinal (GI) adverse events, particularly cholangitis and enteritis, compared to paclitaxel. The albumin-bound nanoparticle formulation of Nab-paclitaxel enhances drug distribution and clearance properties, which may result in increased exposure to the GI system and heightened risk of GI toxicity. This elevated risk can be attributed to several underlying mechanisms, including direct damage to the rapidly dividing cells of the GI epithelium, which often leads to mucositis and diarrhea23. Damage to mucosal integrity further increases susceptibility to infections, potentially resulting in severe complications, such as colonic perforation24. Additionally, Nab-paclitaxel’s formulation may provoke inflammation within the GI tract, contributing to secretory dysfunctions and dysmotility that exacerbate symptoms like diarrhea and constipation25. Changes in the gut microbiome, frequently observed with chemotherapy, can also compound these effects; dysbiosis has been implicated in worsening mucositis and diarrhea, reflecting an interplay between the microbiome and GI health23. Given that pancreatic cancer patients are already prone to GI complications due to tumor location and biliary involvement, these findings underscore the need for tailored monitoring and mitigation strategies when administering Nab-paclitaxel in this population.

This study has several strengths, including the use of a large, real-world pharmacovigilance dataset from FAERS, which provides a broad perspective on the adverse event profiles of Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel across a diverse population. The analysis incorporated multiple disproportionality measures, including ROR, PRR, and Bayesian methods, to enhance signal detection accuracy. Nonetheless, we acknowledge several limitations: FAERS is a spontaneous reporting system and is subject to underreporting, selective reporting, and variable data completeness. Additionally, pharmacovigilance reporting is voluntary in the United States, which limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions. Differences in total prescription volumes and usage contexts between the two drugs further complicate direct comparisons. Future studies would benefit from integrating data from additional sources, such as electronic health records and clinical trials, to validate these findings and further elucidate the mechanisms underlying the distinct AE profiles of Nab-paclitaxel and paclitaxel. Prospective studies with controlled patient cohorts could also provide more granular insights into the risk factors that may predispose patients to specific adverse events.

In summary, this study underscores the need for personalized risk management strategies when using Nab-paclitaxel or paclitaxel in the treatment of pancreatic cancer. Nab-paclitaxel’s nanoparticle-based formulation offers significant therapeutic advantages by improving drug distribution and clearance properties and minimizing solvent-related risks, yet it also presents unique challenges, particularly in terms of hematologic and hepatic toxicities. Conversely, traditional paclitaxel, while associated with fewer hematologic complications, requires premedication and careful monitoring during administration to mitigate infusion-related reactions. Clinicians should consider these differential AE profiles when selecting treatment regimens, ensuring vigilant monitoring tailored to each patient’s individual risk profile. As nanomedicine continues to evolve, further research is essential to refine these formulations and optimize safety outcomes for patients battling challenging cancers like pancreatic cancer.

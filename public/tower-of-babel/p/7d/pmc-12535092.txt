Identification of diagnostic biomarkers and mitochondrial metabolic characteristics in sepsis-associated acute kidney injury
Authors: Yichun Xia, Yiming Qian, Guanyu Hu, Yuehong Pu, Jian Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study intended to explore the molecular mechanisms and the mitochondrial metabolic characteristics of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (S-AKI) through bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation.MethodsThe datasets of S-AKI were acquired from the GEO database while mitochondrial related genes (MRGs) were procured utilizing MitoCarta3.0 database. The “limma” R package was used to screen the differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Weighted Gene Correlation Network Analysis identified the co-expressed gene modules. GO, together with KEGG, was applied for enrichment analysis. A PPI network was constructed using the STRING database. The LASSO algorithm was adopted to screen prognostic predictors of S-AKI. The correlation between immune cells and diagnostic biomarkers was reflected through immune cell infiltration analysis utilizing CIBERSORT. In cellular experiments, the CCK-8 assay detected the cell viability. RT-qPCR and western blot assessed PMPCA and PMPCB expressions. The release of inflammatory cytokines and oxidative stress markers was assessed using ELISA and corresponding assay kits. Western blot assessed the expressions of proteins implicated in mitochondrial function.Results163 intersected genes between DEGs and MRGs were screened. 103 key genes were acquired via the intersection of module genes with the 163 MRDEGs and 10 hub genes were determined. Functional enrichment analysis disclosed that the key genes were primarily enriched in mitochondrial metabolic pathways. Five significant immune cells showing differences between S-AKI and controls were identified. Correlation analysis displayed a negative association of Gpx4 with resting NK cells, its positive association with M2 macrophages as well as a negative association of Amacr with Th17 Cells. Three independent diagnostic biomarkers Gpx4, PMPCB and Amacr for S-AKI were determined. The validation cellular experiments showed that PMPCB overexpression could alleviate LPS-induced oxidative stress, inflammation and viability damage in HK-2 cells.ConclusionsThis work determined three independent diagnostic biomarkers in S-AKI, which might shed novel insight into its diagnosis and treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03266-z.

Introduction

Sepsis, which results from an unbridled host response to infection, can contribute to widespread organ dysfunction if fails to be treated [1]. Among multiple organs, the kidney is more vulnerable to sepsis attack, thus resulting in sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (S-AKI) and bringing a tremendous challenge in the clinic [1, 2]. It is worthwhile to note that S-AKI is a frequent-occurring and severe complication of sepsis, possessing ascending mortality rate and unfavorable outcomes [2, 3]. In the case of sepsis, early identification of S-AKI is essential for initiating optimal therapy and preventing further kidney injury [4]. Of note, biomarkers play a critical role by enhancing clinical judgment, improving early diagnosis, guiding targeted therapies, and facilitating post-treatment monitoring [4]. Thence, figuring out the pathophysiologic mechanisms of S-AKI and finding more effective biomarkers for S-AKI are profoundly pivotal.

As a highly metabolically active organ, the kidney, particularly its renal tubular cells, contains a remarkably high density of mitochondria to support its extensive energy demands [5]. Mitochondria play a critical role in energy generation [6]. Additionally, the abnormal structure of mitochondria is a typical feature of clinical AKI [7, 8] and dysregulated mitochondrial energy metabolism can be observed in models following injury [9, 10]. Clark and co-workers have elucidated that pathways of energy metabolism are key contributors to AKI and represent promising and reliable therapeutic targets [6]. Moreover, the potential of targeting mitochondrial metabolism in alleviating ischemic-challenged AKI has also been highlighted [11]. With this regard, the mitochondrial metabolic characteristics of S-AKI were investigated hereby.

In recent years, integrated bioinformatics analysis has been widely used to identify novel genes involved in various diseases, which may serve as potential biomarkers for both diagnosis and prognosis [12, 13]. Herein, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between S-AKI samples and controls were identified employing the “limma” R package. Through the intersection of DEGs and mitochondrial related genes (MRGs), MRDEGs were screened. The screened MEGs were intersected with MRDEGs for the obtainment of key genes, and the screening of candidate diagnostic genes was carried out utilizing LASSO regression analysis. Functional enrichment analysis was implemented and a PPI network was constructed with Cytoscape software. The correlation between three diagnostic biomarkers and immune cells was determined employing CIBERSORT.

Our investigation of mitochondrial metabolic characteristics and their relationship with immune infiltration in S-AKI offers an unprecedented angle from which to comprehensively figure out the molecular mechanism of S-AKI pathogenesis and is beneficial for determining prospective biomarkers and developing therapeutic strategies.

Material and methods

Processing of microarry data

Three S-AKI related datasets GSE225192, GSE120879 and GSE186822 were acquired utilizing GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). All datasets sampled from Mus musculus. The platform adopted for the datasets GSE225192, GSE120879 and GSE186822 were GPL21290, GPL16570 and GPL24247, separately. Specimens of GSE225192 were assembled from 16 control patients and 32 S-AKI patients. GSE225192 consists of 4 control patients and 18 S-AKI patients while GSE186822 is composed by 2 control patients and 3 S-AKI patients. In this paper, GSE225192 dataset was applied as S-AKI Training set. GSE120879 and GSE186822 datasets were combined and employed to serve S-AKI Testing set. Following the removal of batch effects utilizing “sva” package [14], the combined data were standardized utilizing “limma” package [15] for the obtaining of S-AKI Testing set. To validate the effectiveness of batch effects removal, the principal component analysis [16] of expression array of the dataset before or after removing batch effects was implemented. The study procedures are demonstrated in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Determination of MRGs

MitoCarta3.0, which is an inventory of 1136 human and 1140 mouse genes encoding proteins potently supporting mitochondrial localization, currently possess sub-mitochondrial compartment, along with pathway annotations [17]. MRGs that acquired from MitoCarta3.0 database (https://www.broadinstitute.org/mitocarta/mitocarta30-inventory-mammalian-mitochondrial-proteins-and-pathways) with 1140 mitochondrial mouse genes were collected for subsequent analysis. The obtained MRGs were presented in Additional file 1.

Identification of DEGs

The screening of DEGs between controls and patients in S-AKI Training set were identified using the “limma” package. An adjusted p value < 0.05 and |logFC| > 0.5 were considered statistically significant [18]. The “ggplot2” package in R was used to plot the volcano map and “heatmap” package in R was adopted to plot heat map. MRDEGs were procured through the intersection of DEGs and MRGs.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA was carried out to identify co-expressed gene modules, investigate the relationship between gene network and phenotypes as well as to explore the key genes in the network [19]. The WGCNA was conducted in S-AKI Training set. During the process of constructing module, pickSoft-Threshold function was adopted to calculate the soft threshold power. A scale-free network was established according to the selected soft threshold power (β = 4) and a topological matrix was constructed, following which was the implementation of hierarchical clustering analysis. The minimum module size was determined at 100 and eigengenes were calculated. The correlation of module eigengenes was calculated and modules with correlation above 0.3 were merged again. Subsequently, 8 modules were obtained. Finally, the correlation between modules and S-AKI clinic characteristics was analyzed through the implementation of Pearson correlation analysis and MEGs associated with S-AKI was screened. Key genes were obtained by intersecting MEGs with MRDEGs.

Functional enrichment analysis

With the aim of deeply figuring out the biological functions and pathways related with key genes, GO enrichment analysis, along with KEGG pathway analysis was carried out using Metasacape software [20]. P less than 0.05 was indicative of significant enrichment. The results of enrichment analysis were demonstrated in the form of barplot.

Construction of PPI network and identification of hub genes

With the aim of constructing a PPI network for key genes, STRING database was employed. The interaction network diagram of key genes was established using the STRING database. Afterwards, results of STRING database were imported into Cytoscape software for visualization [21]. With the application of CytoHubba plug-in of Cytoscape software, the top 10 genes were selected from PPI network using MCC algorithm and identified as hub genes.

Screening of diagnostic genes and the construction of diagnostic model

The screening of diagnostic genes from key genes was carried out with the adoption of LASSO logistic regression method [22]. LASSO analysis was implemented utilizing the R package “glmnet” for the identification of most valuable diagnostic gene biomarkers in an optimal model. Subsequently, the screened diagnostic biomarkers were used to construct diagnostic model in S-AKI Training set by means of logistic regression method and then displayed in the format of nomogram [23] to predict the incidence rate of S-AKI patients. Single factor and multi factor forest plot models were constructed using glm and rms package was employed to construct nomogram. With the use of “pROC” package [24], ROC curves were generated and AUC was determined for estimating the predictive effectiveness of diagnostic model.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune cell infiltration was assessed using the “CIBERSORT” algorithm [25]. Based on S-AKI Training set, an immune cell infiltration matrix was acquired. The proportions of 25 immune cell types were visualized in histogram utilizing “ggplot2” package. Heatmap was plotted to show the correlation between immune cells and diagnostic biomarkers. Subsequently, we compared the scores of different immune cells between S-AKI patients and controls to obtain immune cells with varying infiltration levels between different samples.

Cell culture and treatment

The human proximal tubular epithelial cells HK-2 (cat. no. BNCC339833) that supplied from BeNa Culture Collection (Henan, China) were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics at 37℃ with 5% CO2. LPS (cat. no. SMB00610, Sigma-Aldrich) with varying concentrations (0.1, 1 and 10 µg/mL) was used to adopted to treat HK-2 cells for 12 h.

Cell transfection

To upregulate PMPCB expression, overexpression plasmids carrying PMPCB (OE-PMPCB) and the empty vector (OE-NC) were constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China) and then introduced to HK-2 cells using Lipofectamine 2000 for 48 h in light of standard protocol. Following transfection for 48 h, the cells were collected for subsequent studies.

Detection of cell viability

The HK-2 cells were inoculated into 96-well flat-bottomed plates and incubated overnight. After that, each well was supplemented with 10 μL CCK-8 working solution (Vazyme, A311-02) and cells were cultured for extra 3 h. With a microscope reader, the OD value was read at 450 nm.

RT-qPCR

The total RNA was extracted from sample HK-2 cells with TRIzol® (Invitrogen; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) and then reverse transcribed into cDNA by means of PrimeScript™ RT reagent kits as per the manufacturer’s instructions. Following, the qPCR reactions were conducted on the 7500 Fast Real-time PCR system utilizing SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Takara, Toyobo, Japan) in light of standard protocol. The relative gene expression was calculated using 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blot

The total proteins were lysed from sample HK-2 cells with RIPA lysis buffer and the protein concentration was quantified using BCA assay kits. Subsequently, the proteins separated by SDS-PAGE were transferred to PVDF membranes, which were sealed by 5% BSA. Afterwards, the membranes were respective immunoblotted with primary antibodies specific to PMPCA (Proteintech, 26,536-1-AP), PMPCB (Proteintech, 16,061-1-AP), Caspase3 (Proteintech, 66,470-2-Ig), cleaved-capase3 (Affinity, AF7022), FECH (Abclonal, A8418), ISCA2 (Proteintech, 13,200-1-AP), COVIX (Proteintech, 11,242-1-AP) and GAPDH (Proteintech, 60,004-1-Ig) overnight. On the next day, the cells were incubated with secondary antibody conjugated with HRP at room temperature for 3 h. The protein bands were visualized with enhanced ECL regent (Tanon, 180-501) and ImageJ software was adopted for the analysis of protein density.

Measurement of oxidative stress markers

The activities of ROS, MDA and GSH in HK-2 cells were assessed with the use of relevant commercial assay kits, which were all procured from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, according to the recommended specifications.

ELISA

The levels of IL-1β, TNF-α and TGF-β in cell supernatant were assessed by means of corresponding commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. With a microscope reader, the OD value was read at 450 nm.

ATP detection

Intracellular ATP levels were measured using a fluorescence-based ATP detection kit (MCE, HY-U00451) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, the ATP staining working solution was freshly prepared before use. The cell culture medium was removed, and ATP staining working solution was added to each well. After incubation at 37°C for 15 min in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂, the staining solution was removed, and fresh culture medium was added. The cells were then imaged using a fluorescence microscope (OLYMPUS, IX71) to evaluate ATP fluorescence intensity.

ROS measurement

Intracellular ROS levels were detected using the DCFH-DA fluorescent probe (10 μM, diluted 1:1000 in serum-free RPMI-1640 medium, Beyotime, S0033M). After removing the culture medium, the diluted DCFH-DA was added to the cells and incubated at 37°C for 20 min. Cells were then washed three times with serum-free medium to remove excess extracellular DCFH-DA. Fluorescence intensity was observed and captured under a fluorescence microscope to assess ROS levels.

JC-1 assay

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was assessed using the JC-1 MMP assay kit (Beyotime, C2003S). After discarding the culture medium and washing once with PBS, 1 mL of JC-1 staining working solution was added to each well and incubated at 37°C for 20 min. After incubation, the staining solution was discarded, and cells were washed twice with JC-1 staining buffer. Fresh culture medium was added, and MMP was evaluated by capturing red and green fluorescence images under a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

The data calculation and statistical analysis was conducted utilizing R package. The differences between two groups were estimated employing independent Student t-test and Mann-Whitney U test. If not specifically indicated, correlation coefficients between different molecules were calculated utilizing Spearman’s correlation analysis throughout this study. All statistical tests were two-sided and P lower than 0.05 was indicative of statistical significance.

Construction of S-AKI nomogram

To identify the pathogenic genes of S-AKI and analyze their diagnostic potential, the LASSO regression model was used to screen diagnostic markers for S-AKI based on 103 key genes. The optimal model and the simplest model were procured (Fig. 4A). During model construction, as the value of λ increased, the number of selected features decreased, while the absolute values of the corresponding coefficients increased (Fig. 4B). The optimal model was chosen, and 3 genes, including Gpx4, PMPCB and Amacr, were identified as S-AKI diagnostic biomarkers. To evaluate whether these three genes could serve as independent diagnostic biomarkers for S-AKI, univariate and multivariate regression analyses were implemented on the S-AKI Training set. Univariate regression analysis indicated that Gpx4, PMPCB, and Amacr were independently associated with the diagnosis of S-AKI (Fig. 4C). Nevertheless, the results of multivariate regression analysis revealed that only PMPCB could be an independent diagnostic biomarker (Fig. 4D). The diagnostic model was constructed according to the expression of these three diagnostic biomarkers in the S-AKI Training set and is presented as a nomogram (Fig. 4E). As Fig. 4F-G revealed, the model we established had a high AUC value (0.986) in the S-AKI Training set and an AUC value of 0.984 in S-AKI Testing set, revealing its excellent predictive performance. The calibration curve indicated good agreement between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes, confirming the model’s accuracy (Fig. 4H). Moreover, decision curve analysis (DCA) revealed that the nomogram outperformed models based on individual biomarkers in predicting S-AKI (Fig. 4I). To further illustrate clinical utility, a clinical impact curve was plotted based on the DCA results (Fig. 4J). The red curve represents the number of patients predicted to develop S-AKI, while the blue curve shows those who actually developed S-AKI. Within the high-risk threshold range of 0.2-1, the close proximity of these two curves indicates the strong predictive capability of the nomogram. Discussion

In recent years, significant progress has been made in understanding AKI from epidemiological, pathogenic, and pathophysiological perspectives. However, specific and effective therapeutic agents for S-AKI remain elusive [2]. With the rapid advancement of modern biotechnology, bioinformatics has gained widespread attention as it enables deeper exploration of potential therapeutic targets for AKI at molecular and cellular levels [26]. In this study, we integrated three S-AKI-related datasets that obtained from GEO database and 10 hub genes, including Tufm, Mrpl41, Mrpl10, Mrpl37, Mrps24, Gfm1, Mrps18a, Mrpl27, Mrps21 and Mrpl44 were identified. Additionally, a systematic and comprehensive bioinformatic method was adopted to figure out the role that infiltrating immune cells played in the advancement of S-AKI and develop reliable diagnostic biomarkers for S-AKI.

Herein, 103 key genes were procured through the intersection of MEGs and MRDEGs. Functionally, these key genes were associated with oxidoreductase activity, ligase activity and flavin adenine dinucleotide binding. In addition, these key genes were also involved in signaling pathways including Valine, leucine and isoleucine degradation, Oxidative phosphorylation and Butanoate metabolism, all of which play essential roles in acute renal failure metabolism: the catabolic breakdown of nutrients, the generation of ATP through the TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, and the maintenance of the organelle's structural and genetic integrity [27, 28]. This specificity underscores their potential importance as key regulators of cellular bioenergetics. Among them, oxidative phosphorylation and the TCA cycle were the most significantly enriched pathways, suggesting that impaired mitochondrial energy metabolism may represent a pivotal mechanism driving S-AKI development [29, 30]. Several studies have shown that mitochondrial dysfunction greatly promotes the pathogenesis of AKI and adversely affects kidney repair after AKI [31–33]. Hence, improving mitochondrial function is a promising strategy for treating renal dysfunction. The aberrant alterations in these genes and the disruption of the signaling pathways regulated by these genes might be associated with S-AKI advancement. Subsequently, 3 diagnostic biomarkers Gpx4, PMPCB and Amacr were identified. These biomarkers are associated with mitochondrial organization, mitochondrial matrix function, and oxidoreductase activity, suggesting their potential role as key modulators in the pathogenesis of S-AKI.

As a pivotal intracellular modulator, Gpx4 participates in numerous physiological and takes a pivotal part in the maintenance of the redox homeostasis within the body [34]. PMPCB acts as a component of mitoprotease family and takes a critical part in regulating biological activities essential for proper mitochondrial functioning [35]. Moreover, Amacr, which is a peroxisomal and mitochondrial enzyme, serves as a vital modulator in the metabolism of cholesterol as well as branched-chain fatty acids [36]. In this study, univariate regression analysis indicated that Gpx4, PMPCB, and Amacr could serve as independent diagnostic biomarkers, while multivariate regression analysis identified only PMPCB as an independent diagnostic biomarker. Furthermore, the diagnostic model established using Gpx4, PMPCB and Amacr displayed excellent predictive performance.

The interrelationships between immune cells in S-AKI were also investigated hereby. Among them, T cells CD8 Naïve had a high positive correlation with M0 Macrophage while T cells CD4 Memory had the highest negative correlation with Th1 cells in controls. Macrophages, the crucial actors in immune response, have involvement with various pathological processes of AKI and can be triggered in the kidney following AKI [37]. Nevertheless, mast cells demonstrated a strong positive correlation with follicular CD4⁺ T cells, while resting NK cells had the highest negative correlation with activated NK cells. Of note, mitochondria have been implicated in the trigger of mast cells [38]. Besides, NK cells have been attested to result in mitochondrial dysfunction [39].

Additionally, the correlation between 3 diagnostic biomarkers and immune cells was investigated. It was discovered that Gpx4 had the highest positive correlation with M2 Macrophage while Amacr had the highest negative correlation with Th17 cells in S-AKI patients. Emerging study has illuminated that Gpx4 reduction in kidney can trigger macrophages [40]. Yan et al. have corroborated that Amacr has close relation with immune cell infiltration in prostate cancer [41]. Additionally, PMPCB has been proved to be associated with immune microenvironment [42]. With this regard, we believe that Gpx4, Amacr and PMPCB take vital parts in the body’s immune system. Although Gpx4 and Amacr were identified as diagnostic markers through machine learning models, PMPCB demonstrated superior diagnostic performance and stronger correlation with mitochondrial dysfunction and immune cell infiltration. Therefore, we selected PMPCB for further functional validation.

LPS-induced in vitro models have been supposed to be classic models of S-AKI [43]. Considering this, we used LPS to induce HK-2 cells to model S-AKI in vitro. In sepsis, various inflammatory factors are implicated in organ injury [44]. The excessive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and TNF-α resulting from damaged renal tubule cells further intensifies the extent of renal injury [45]. Oxidative stress is a critical step required for the pathogenesis of AKI and it can induce inflammation [46, 47]. An excessive ROS production can be detected in oxidative stress [48]. Free radicals enhanced the peroxidation of lipids and induce the production of reactive aldehyde metabolites, such as MDA [49]. Conversely, the antioxidant defense system, includes the enzyme GSH-Px, protects against lipid peroxidation and cellular damage [50]. Recent studies have shown that mitochondrial proteins such as PMPCB may regulate immune cell behavior through metabolic reprogramming and redox signaling pathways [51]. Dysfunctional mitochondria can promote the release of DAMPs (damage-associated molecular patterns) and ROS, which activate innate immune responses and contribute to inflammatory cascades in S-AKI [52, 53]. These findings suggest that mitochondrial enzymes may act as upstream regulators of immune cell recruitment and activation during kidney injury. Our data revealed that PMPCB overexpression revived the viability, decreased the levels of IL-1β, TNF-α and TGF-β, declined ROS and MDA activities while increasing GSH activity in LPS-induced HK-2 cells, suggesting the protective role of PMPCB in LPS-induced HK-2 cell injury. In addition to these findings, we further evaluated mitochondrial function to validate the mitochondrial metabolic features in S-AKI. Western blot confirmed successful overexpression of PMPCB at the protein level. Moreover, ATP production was significantly decreased by PMPCB overexpression in LPS-induced cells, indicating restoration of mitochondrial energy homeostasis. The suppression of ROS accumulation and the recovery of MMP (as shown by JC-1 staining) further confirmed the role of PMPCB in ameliorating mitochondrial dysfunction. These experimental results directly support our bioinformatics-based findings and reinforce the concept that PMPCB plays a key protective role in maintaining mitochondrial metabolic balance under septic injury.

Our study still has some limitations. Despite promising in vitro findings, the absence of in vivo functional validation of PMPCB limits the translational strength of our study. Further investigations using animal models of S-AKI are warranted to verify the protective role and therapeutic applicability of PMPCB in vivo. In addition to PMPCB, our study also identified Gpx4 and Amacr as potential diagnostic markers for S-AKI. Although these genes were not selected for experimental validation in the present study, prior literature has implicated Gpx4 in ferroptosis regulation and Amacr in peroxisomal and mitochondrial fatty acid metabolism [54, 55], both of which are closely associated with cellular injury in S-AKI. Future investigations are warranted to explore their functional relevance and potential clinical applications in the context of mitochondrial dysfunction and immune dysregulation in S-AKI.

Conclusion

This paper unprecedentedly explored mitochondrial metabolic characteristics in S-AKI and conducted related immune infiltration analysis utilizing bioinformatics technology on the basis of datasets collected from GEO database. However, we didn’t conduct in vivo experiments to further validate our findings, which is one of the limitations of our study. In addition, the research was implemented based on a restricted sample of public databases and expanding the sample size is very necessary.

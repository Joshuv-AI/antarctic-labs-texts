Lnc-THAP7-AS1 suppresses the ovarian cancer progression by targeting miR-92b-5p/fatty acid 2-hydroxylase signal axis
Authors: Meili Pei, Yanqi Yang, Bing Su, Na Li, Minyi Zhao, Juan Zhao, Ting Yang, Li Wang, Shimin Quan, Ruifang Sun, Xiaofeng Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•It was demonstrated for the first time that THAP7-AS1 involved in the development of ovarian cancer.•THAP7-AS1 overexpression inhibits proliferation, invasion and migration and induces apoptosis of ovarian cancer cells.•THAP7-AS1 inhibits the ovarian cancer progression by competitively binding miR-92b-5p to regulate FA2H expression.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OC), a prevalent gynecological malignancy worldwide, poses a significant threat to women's lives and health[1,2]. The absence of specific early stage symptoms makes early diagnosis extremely challenging. Approximately 60 % to 70 % of cases are diagnosed at an advanced stage, leading to a grim prognosis for these patients[3]. Therfore, uncovering occurrence and developement mechanism of OC and identifying new potential diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets is imperative[4,5].

LncRNAs are non-coding RNAs transcribed autonomously with a length exceeding 200 nucleotides but have limited protein-coding potential, often regarded as transcriptional byproducts[6,7]. They play pivotal regulatory roles in various cancers via multiple mechanisms[6]. Reports indicate that lncRNAs can regulate gene expression at both the transcriptional and epigenetic levels. Additionally, lncRNAs also participate in gene regulation via interacting with RNAs and proteins in the post-transcriptional and translational levels[8]. For instance, lncRNA RAD51-AS1 directly binds to RAD51 mRNA, inhibiting its translation and thereby suppressing RAD51′s DNA repair ability during hepatocellular carcinoma progression[9]. Another example is lncRNA TALC, which competitively binds to miR-20b, leading to the activation of STAT3/p300 complex and subsequent upregulation of O6-methylguanine DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) expression, resulting in temozolomide (TMZ) resistance in glioblastoma[10]. Moreover, it has been reported that lncRNA SPOCD1-AS, derived from OC extracellular vesicles, remodels mesothelial cells through a mesothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (MMT) process, promoting peritoneal metastasis via interacting with G3BP1[11]. However, the regulatory roles and underlying mechanisms of lncRNAs in the OC onset and progression remain largely unexplored and warrant further investigation.

The lncRNA THAP7-AS1, localized at 22q11.21 and comprising two exons that span 1301 bp, remains relatively unexplored. In gastric cancer, THAP7-AS1 undergoes post-transcriptionally stabilization via METTL3-mediated m6A modification, thereby exhibiting oncogenic properties by facilitating the nuclear entry of CUL4B[12]. Studies have indicated that reduced expression of THAP7-AS1 in cholangiocarcinoma patients is associated with poor prognosis[13]. However, to date, there have been no reports about the role and mechanism of THAP7-AS1 in OC.

Using RNA sequencing technology, our preliminary study identified 231 differentially expressed lncRNAs (DElncRNAs) in OC tissues versus normal ovarian tissues, among which THAP7-AS1 was significantly down-regulated in OC compared with normal ovarian tissues. Through RNA pull-down, we identified that THAP7-AS1 could directly interact with miR-92b-5p Further analysis using TargetScan and other bioinformatics tools indicated that miR-92b-5p can directly target fatty acid 2-hydroxylase (FA2H), suggesting that THAP7-AS1 protected FA2H from miR-92b-5p-mediated sponge adsorption. Previous study have shown that overexpression of FA2H is associated with increased sensitivity to cisplatin in OC and improved prognoses[14].

In summary, our study aims to elucidate the THAP7-AS1/miR-92b-5p/FA2H signaling pathway in OC. The findings from this research will enrich our knowledge of the specific role of THAP7-AS1 in OC, and may offer a novel therapeutic strategy for OC prevention and treatment.

Methods

Patient samples and ethical approval

This study utilized 70 tissue specimens: 36 OC tumor tissues and 34 normal ovarian tissues from patients with benign gynecological diseases. All specimens were collected at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University. OC tissues were obtained from treatment-naïve patients with histopathologicallyconfirmed primary ovarian cancer (no preoperative radiotherapy/chemotherapy). Normal ovarian tissues werecollected during elective oophorectomy for benign conditions, with histopathological confirmation of absence of ovarian pathology. All tissues were snap-frozen and stored at -80 °C immediately post-resection.

RNA sequencing was performed on a subset of three OC tissues and three normal ovarian tissues: OC samples were high-grade serous carcinomas from patients aged 57, 62, and 65 years (FIGO stages: IIIC, IIIC, and IV). Normal controls were obtained from uterine fibroid patients (aged 47, 52, 54 years) undergoing concurrent prophylactic oophorectomy.

The remaining 64 tissues (33 OC tissues and 31 normal ovarian tissues from benign gynecological diseases) were used for subsequent molecular biology experiments. Detailed clinicopathological characteristics are provided in NSupplementary Tables S1 and S2.

This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (Approval No.:2022–076) and conducted with written informed consent from all participants.

Ovarian cancer cell lines and normal ovarian epithelium cell lines

Additionally, we utilized human OC cell lines, namely SKOV3, A2780, as well as the normal ovarian epithelium cell line IOSE80. These cell lines were kindly provided by the Key Laboratory of Environment and Genes Related to Diseases at Xi’an Jiaotong University. All cells were cultivated in an RPMI-1640 medium (Thermo Scientific Hyclone, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum, and maintained in a humidified cell incubator at 37 °C with 5 % CO2. Subculturing was performed during the logarithmic growth phase prior to subsequent experiments.

RNA extraction and quantitative reverse transcription PCR

Total RNA was extracted from tissues and cells using Trizol reagent (Genestar, Beijing, China) and subsequently quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies, Wilmington, DE, USA). The cDNA synthesis was performed using the Genestar reverse transcription kit for mRNA and a specific kit for miRNA. The qRT-PCR reactions were conducted using UltraSYBR mixtures (Genestar, Beijing, China) on the Bio-RadCFX Connect real-time System (Bio-Rad, California, USA). β-actin and U6 were used as endogenous controls for mRNA and miRNA expression, respectively. The relative expression levels of mRNA and miR-92b-5p were calculated using the 2 −ΔΔCT method. Microarray analysis

As mentioned above, total RNA was extracted from 3 OC patients and 3 benign patients' ovarian tissues using Trizol, with quantity and quality assessed by NanoDrop, and RNA integrity was verified by standard denaturing agarose gel electrophoresis. The Arraystar Human LncRNA Microarray V3.0 (Arraystar, USA) was employed to profile the LncRNA expression. Microarray scanning and data analyses were conducted by KangChen Bio-tech (China).

Bioinformatics analysis

Bioinformatics analysis of RNA-seq data were conducted using R-based tools. We performed differential expression analysis of mRNAs and lncRNAs using software packages such as edgeR and DESeq2. Genes with significant differential expression were identified based on an FDR threshold of <0.05 and an absolute fold change >2. The interaction between lncRNA and miRNA, and between miRNAs and mRNAs was predicted using miranda v3.3a and TargetScan v8.0, respectively. Additionally, we identified overlapping DElncRNAs and differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRNAs) using Cytoscape v3.10.2, and constructed a comprehensive ceRNA network of lncRNA-miRNA interactions.

Construction of expression vector and plasmid transfection

The overexpression plasmid for THAP7-AS1 and FA2H were designed and synthesized by Gene-Pharma (Shanghai, China). Additionally, three shRNAs targeting FA2H were synthesized for knockdown experiments, along with a scrambled shRNA as a negative control. Additionally, the hsa-miR-92b-5p mimic and its stable negative control were purchased from GenePharm (Shanghai, China). Cell transfection was performed using JetSI-ENDO reagents (Polyplus-transfection, Strasbourg, France), and transfection efficiency was verified by qRT-PCR.

CCK-8 assay

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 4000 cells per well. After overnight incubation to facilitate cell attachment, the corresponding plasmids were transfected according to the experimental design. At designated time points, 10 μl of CCK-8 solution (Meilunbio) was added to each well, followed by further incubation for 4 h in the dark. The OD value was measured at 450 nm using a full-wavelength enzyme labeling instrument (Thermo Scientific Fisher). All experiments were replicated three times.

EdU cell proliferation assay

Cell proliferation was assessed using the EdU Apollo567 Imaging Kit (RiboBio, China). Transfected SKOV3 and A2780 cells were seeded in 24-well plates at 5 × 10⁴cells/well and cultured for 24 h. Cells were then incubated with 50 μM EdU for 2 h (37 °C, 5 % CO₂). Following fixation (4 % paraformaldehyde, 30 min) and permeabilization (0.5 % Triton X-100 in PBS, 10 min), EdU incorporation was detected by incubating with Apollo567 reaction cocktail for 30 min. Nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst 33,342 (5 μg/mL, 15 min). Fluorescent images were acquired using an inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus). Proliferation rate was calculated as the percentage of EdU-positive cells (green) relative to total Hoechst-stained nuclei (blue) across five random fields per well. Experiments were performed in triplicate.

Transwell assay

For invasion assays, the upper chamber was pre-coated with a mixture of RPMI-1640 and matrigel (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA). Cells transfected 48 h earlier were then inoculated into the pre-coated upper chamber with serum-free medium, while the lower chamber was filled with 10 % FBS medium. Following 48 h incubation, the Transwell chamber was removed, fixed with paraformaldehyde for 30 min, and stained with 0.5 % crystal violet (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO). Non-migrated/non-invaded cells were removed, and migrated/invaded cells were counted under a microscope.

Cell apoptosis assay

Apoptosis was detected by Annexin V-FITC/PI staining (KeyGen Biotech). Ovarian cells were plated in 6-well plate at a density of 1 × 106 cells per well and cultured. After 48 h of treatment, the cells were collected by centrifugation, washed twice with PBS, and resuspended in 500 μL Binding Buffer containing 5 μL Annexin V-FITC and 5 μL Propidium Iodide. After 10 min reaction at room temperature, apoptosis was analyzed using flow cytometry (Beckman Coulter).

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from ovarian tissue, SKOV3, A2780 or IOSE80 cells using RIPA lysis buffer containing a protease inhibitor (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China). The extracted proteins were separated by 10 % SDS-PAGE and transferred to methanol-activated polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5 % skim milk at room temperature for 2 h and then incubated overnight in 4°C with primary antibodies against FA2H (HUABIO, China, diluted 1:1000), Bax (SANTA CRUZ, Bolivia, diluted 1:1000), Bcl-2 (Cohesion biosciences, China, diluted 1:1000), Cleaved caspase-3 (Immunoway, China, diluted 1:1000), PCNA (Cohesion biosciences, China, diluted 1:1000), and β-actin (ImmunoWay, USA, diluted 1:2000).

On the following day, the membranes were removed from 4 °C and washed quickly with PBST for there times, each for 5 min. Subsequently, the membranes were incubated with horse-radish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:10,000, Santa Cruz Biotechnology) for 2 h at room temperature. After the second antibody incubation, the membranes were washed quickly with PBST three times, each for 5 min. Finally, protein bands were visualized using Chemiluminescence Reagent (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA).

Immunohistochemical staining

The section specimens were deparaffinized with xylene and subsequently hydrated with graded alcohol for antibody staining. Antigen retrieval and blocking steps werethen carried out. The slides were incubated with primary antibodies against FA2H (Huabio, Beijing, China) at a dilution of 1:200, followed by incubation with secondary antibodies. 3, 3′-diaminobenzidine staining was performed, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining. Microscopic observation and image acquisition were carried out, with subsequent image analysis using ImageJ.

Dual-luciferase assay

Fragments of THAP7-AS1 or FA2H 3′UTR containing predicted wild-type (WT) or mutant-type (MUT) binding site for miR-92b-5p were cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Qingke Biotechnology, China) to generate luciferase reporter vectors. HEK-293 cells were co-transfected with these vectors and miR-92b-5p mimics or negative controls (GenePharma, Shanghai) using JetSI-ENDO transfection reagents (Polyplus-transfection, Strasbourg, France). After 48 h, firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured using the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Meilunbio, China). Relative luciferase activity was calculated by comparing the activities of the above two luciferases.

RNA pull-down

miR-92b-5p mimic and control plasmids were transfected into HEK-293 cells. Cells were then collected, lysed, and incubated with C1 magnetic beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) at 4°C for 3 h. Bound RNA was eluted using Trizol and subjected to qRT-PCR analysis.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

The co-localization of THAP7-AS1 and miR-92b-5p in ovarian cancer tissues was analyzed by dual-target FISH using the RNASweAMI™ In Situ Hybridization Dual-label Detection Kit (GF007, Servvare Biotechnology). Briefly, 4-μm paraffin sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and underwent heat-induced antigen retrieval (100°C, 15 min) in 1 × repair solution. After permeabilization with Proteinase K, sections were pre-hybridized and sequentially hybridized with dual-labeled probes (THAP7-AS1 and miR-92b-5p), followed by stringent washes. Fluorescent signals were amplified using IF488 and IF550 probes. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Images were acquired with a Celldiscoverer7 microscope (ZEISS) and analyzed using ImageJ (NIH).

Nude mice xenograft

Nude mice were obtained from the Animal Care and Use Committee of Xi’an Jiaotong University. All animal procedures were approved by the University's Institutional Ethics Committee (XJTUAE2025–2705). To establish subcutaneous xenograft tumor model, SKOV3 cells transduced with either 106 cells of THAP7-AS1-NC (negative control) or THAP7-AS1-OV (overexpression) constructs were suspended in PBS to a final volume of 200 µL (containing 10⁶ cells) and injected into the right femoral region of 4- to 5-week-old nude mice. Tumor volume was measured every three days until day 22 using the formula V =(d2 × D)/2, where d represents the minor tumor axis (short diameter) and D represents the major axis (long diameter). Mice were euthanized on day 22, and tumors were subsequently excised and weighed.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 13.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and presented using GraphPad Prism 5.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA). Student's t-test was used to analyze the differences between two independent groups. All tests were two-sided, and the difference was considered statistically significant when P < 0.05. Unless otherwise specified, all experiments repeated at least three times.

Results

RNA sequencing identified DElncRNAs and mRNAs, with THAP7-AS1 showing reduced expression in ovarian cancer tissues and cell lines

We conducted RNA sequencing to identify differentially expressed lncRNAs and mRNAs between OC tissues and normal ovarian tissues from patients with benign gynecological diseases. The analysis revealed 35 upregulated and 196 downregulated lncRNAs transcripts in OC (Fig. 1A). Similarly, 39 upregulated and 175 down-regulated mRNAs transcripts were observed in OC (Fig. 1B). To refine our focus, we intersected these differential mRNA with 61 key mRNA transcripts involved in proliferation, apoptosis, EMT, and glucose and lipid metabolism pathways. This intersection yielded eight mRNAs of interest: NUMB, RUVBL1, PCNA, CPT1A, ANXA1, POLB, PDK4, PRPS1L1 (Fig. 1C). Subsequently, we performed a correlation network analysis (CNC) between differential lncRNA transcripts and these eight selected mRNA transcripts. This analysis pinpointed nine lncRNA transcripts that exhibited the strongest correlations with the intersected mRNAs (Fig. 1D). Among these, the expressions of AC005037.1, THAP7-AS1, AP000753.1 and G087320 in SKOV3 were validated by qRT-PCR, confirming consistency with our sequencing results. Based on a comprehensive literature review and our validation data, THAP7-AS1 was selected for in-depth investigation (Fig. 1E). Notably, THAP7-AS1 expression was markedly downregulated in OC tissues compared with that in normal ovarian tissues (Fig. 1F). THAP7-AS1 overexpression inhibited proliferation, migration and invasion and induced apoptosis in ovarian cancer cells

To elucidate the role of THAP7-AS1 in OC, we transfected SKOV3 and A2780 cells with THAP7-AS1 overexpression plasmids, as confirmed by qRT-PCR (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, CCK-8 assay revealed a significant reduction in the proliferative capacity of both SKOV3 and A2780 cells upon THAP7-AS1 overexpression (Fig. 2B). The results of the EdU experiment were consistent with those of the CCK-8 assay (Figure S5). Furthermore, Transwell assay showed that THAP7-AS1 overexpression prominently suppressed the migration and invasion abilities of OC cells (Fig. 2C, Figure S1A). To assess the impact of THAP7-AS1 on apoptosis, we employed flow cytometry. Our results demonstrated that THAP7-AS1 overexpression induced apoptosis in SKOV3 and A2780 cells compared with the control group (Fig. 2D, E). Furthermore, we conducted western blot analysis to examine the expression levels of apoptosis related proteins. The results showed a notable upregulation of Bax, Cleaved caspase-3, which are known to promote apoptosis, and a downregulation of Bcl-2, an anti-apoptotic protein. THAP7-AS1 overexpression suppressed the ovarian cancer progression by targetting miR-92b-5p

The lncRNA-miRNA interaction was predicted using miranda v3.3a and miR-92b-5p was selected (Fig. 3A). The target relationship between THAP7-AS1 and miR-92b-5p was verified through RNA pull down assay. The enrichment level of THAP7-AS1 in HEK-293 cells was examined, which revealed a significant increase in the miR-92b-5p mimic-transfected group compared with the control group (Fig. 3B). In addition, FISH analysis revealed that THAP7-AS1 and miR-92b-5p were co-localized in the cytoplasm of cancer cells in ovarian cancer tissues (Figure S8). Furthermore, the expression level of miR-92b-5p was markedly higher in OC tissues comparison with that in normal ovarian tissues. Similarly, miR-92b-5p was highly expressed in two OC cell lines (SKOV3 and A2780) compared with that in human IOSE80 cells (Fig. 3C). Moreover, THAP7-AS1 overexpression decreased miR-92b-5p expression in SKOV3 and A2780 cells (Fig. 3D).To further elucidate the regulatory mechanism of THAP7-AS1 to miR-92b-5p, rescue experiments were conducted. The miR-92b-5p mimic was co-transfected into SKOV3 and A2780 cells along with THAP7-AS1 overexpression vector. The results indicated that THAP7-AS1 overexpression decreased miR-92b-5p expression, while miR-92b-5p mimic increased its levels (Fig. 3E). In the CCK-8 assay, THAP7-AS1 overexpression significantly inhibited cell proliferation, and this inhibitory effect was reversed upon miR-92b-5p overexpression (Fig. 3F). The results of the EdU experiment were consistent with those of the CCK-8 assay (Figure S6). Furthermore, Transwell assay revealed that THAP7-AS1 overexpression markedly inhibited the migration and invasion of OC cells, whereas miR-92b-5p upregulation facilitated these processes (Fig. 3G, Figure S2A). Flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that THAP7-AS1 overexpression significantly induced apoptosis in SKOV3 and A2780 cells. However, miR-92b-5p upregulation had opposite effect, reversing the pro-apoptotic action of THAP7-AS1 overexpression (Fig. 3H). miR-92b-5p exerted its function via targeting FA2H

Bioinformatic analysis predicted a targeting relationship between miR-92b-5p and FA2H. To validate this targeting relationship, we employed fluorescent reporter gene harboring wild type or mutant FA2H binding site (Fig. 4A, 4B). Co-transfection of miR-92b-5p mimic with the fluorescent reporter gene revealed a significant decrease in fluorescence intensity for the wild-type reporter compared to the mutant, confirming direct binding of miR-92b-5p to FA2H (Fig. 4C). Additionally, qRT-PCR analysis revealed a significant increase in miR-92b-5p expression in both SKOV3 and A2780 cells following transfection with the miR-92b-5p mimic (Fig. 4D). Correspondingly, FA2H mRNA expression was significantly downregulated in these cells (Fig. 4E), confirming the negative regulatory relationship between miR-92b-5p and FA2H. Discussion

Over the past decade, an emerging role of lncRNAs in tumor development has become increasingly evident. LncRNAs are known to influence numerous key physiological processes by interacting with DNA, chromatin, various RNA transcripts, and regulatory proteins. Nevertheless, the biological behavior and molecular regulation mechanisms of lncRNAs in OC still remain to be fully elucidated. Our study demonstrated that lncRNA THAP7-AS1 is significantly downregulated in OC and functions as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting cell proliferation and migration, as well as inducing apoptosis, through the miR-92b-5p/FA2H axis signaling pathway. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to clarify the tumor suppressor function and molecular regulatory mechanism of THAP7-AS1 in OC.

THAP7-AS1, localized at 22q11.21 and comprising two exons spanning 1301 bp, has previously been reported to possess oncogenic role in gastric cancer by facilitating CUL4B nuclear entry[12]. Furthermore, it has been shown to be stably expressed through m6A modification mediated by METTL3, collaborating with CUL4B to activate PI3K/AKT signal pathway, thereby promoting lung cancer development[15]. In contrast, our study found that THAP7-AS1 was down regulated in OC. Specifically, THAP7-AS1 overexpression significantly reduced the cell proliferation and migration, while inducing apoptosis in OC cells. The tissue-specific duality of THAP7-AS1 (oncogenic in gastric cancer versus tumor-suppressive in ovarian cancer) highlights the profound complexity of lncRNA biology. This functional reversal may arise from distinct miRNA networks where THAP7-AS1 sponges tumor-suppressive miRNAs in gastric cancer versus oncogenic miRNAs in ovarian cancer; differential epigenetic regulation through tissue-specific chromatin interactions; and context-dependent protein interactomes that redirect its molecular function. These mechanisms collectively illustrate how lncRNAs can exert opposing roles in different tissue contexts. Our findings indicated the tumor suppressor role of THAP7-AS1 in OC, although the underlying regulation mechanism remains to be further clarified.

Recently, studies have revealed the important role of lncRNAs in a variety of biological functions, including chromatin remodeling, gene expression, splicing, translational regulation, post-translation modifications, cell proliferation, differentiation, development, and cell death[16,17]. Chromatin-bound lncRNAs regulate gene expression via changing chromatin configuration or guiding the recruitment of regulatory molecules to specific loci. Protein-bound lncRNAs facilitate protein complexe assembly or disrupt protein-protein interactions, while mRNA interactions with lncRNAs recruit protein machinery involved in various aspects of mRNA metabolism, thereby affecting splicing, mRNA stability, and translation or sequestering miRNA away from target mRNA[[18], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23]]. The intricate regulatory network between lncRNAs and miRNAs is crucial in the onset and progression of various malignancies[24]. LncRNAs can competitively bind to miRNAs via their miRNA response elements (MRE), thereby modulating the expression levels of both the miRNA and its target mRNA. For example, lncRNA-MIAT promotes thyroid cancer progression via the miR-150–5p/EZH2 axis[25], while LncRNA NBAT1 regulates PTEN by sponging miR-21–5p, thereby inhibiting endometrial cancer cell growth[26]. Our bioinformatics analysis, coupled with RNA pull down & FISH experiments, identified miR-92b-5p as a potential target miRNA of THAP7-AS1. MiR-92b-5p is a derivative of miR-92b and is located on chromosome 1. Li et al [27]. demonstrated that gastric cancer cells release miR-92b-5p via exosomes, activating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway and accelerating gastric cancer cell cycle progression. Furthermore, Lopacinska-Joergensen el al [28]. reported that miR-92b-5p is stably expressed in ovarian cancer and can be used as an endogenous control for miRNA research on ovarian cancer, thereby enhancing the reliability of miRNA quantitative analysis. Furthermore, Barquet-Muñoz’s study showed significant miR-92b-5p overexpressed in early-stage cervical cancer patients with lymph node metastasis, suggesting potential value as a predictor of lymph node metastasis[29]. Our results implied that THAP7-AS1 exerts its tumor-suppressor role by binding to miR-92b-5p; however, the downstream target molecule of miR-92b-5p remains to be elucidated.

Based on bioinformatics analysis and literature review, we selected FA2H as the direct target of miR-92b-5p FA2H catalyzes the synthesis of sphingolipids containing 2-hydroxy fatty acids[30] and is abundantly expressed in the brain, stomach, skin, kidney, and colon tissues[30,31]. Cancer progression is associated with lipid metabolism dysregulation, during which FA2H provides lipids and energy for the uncontrolled cancer cell growth[32]. Lower FA2H level is associated with a significantly poor survivor in gastric cancer[33]. Additionally, FA2H is significantly downregulated in triple-negative breast cancer, and FA2H knockdown significantly promotes tumor migration and increases the proportion of cancer stem cells[34]. Qi et.al[14] demonstrated that reduced FA2H expression is significantly associated with poorer survival outcomes in multiple OC subtypes and impairs cisplatin sensitivity. Consistent with these findings, we found that FA2H expression was significantly downregulated in OC tissues and functions as a tumor suppressor in OC. Although the underling detailed molecular mechanism of tumor suppressor role of FA2H in OC requires further clarification, our results showed that THAP7-AS1 was decreased in OC tissues, the reduction in THAP7-AS1 levels leads to an increase in the expression of miR-92b-5p, thereby reducing the expression of FA2H and ultimately exerting a carcinogenic effect.

Although our study establishes the tumor-suppressive role of the THAP7-AS1/miR-92b-5p/FA2H axis in ovarian cancer, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, our mechanistic understanding of FA2H's function remains incomplete, particularly regarding its downstream effects on lipid metabolism and apoptotic pathways. Secondly, the study primarily relied on in vitro models, and future work should incorporate patient-derived xenografts or genetically engineered mouse models for more physiologically relevant validation. Thirdly, we have not yet explored the potential lipidomic changes mediated by FA2H, which could provide crucial insights into its tumor-suppressive mechanisms. Future studies will focus on comprehensive lipidomic profiling to identify FA2H-regulated lipids; systematic investigation of FA2H's interactions with other metabolic enzymes; clinical validation using larger patient cohorts with long-term follow-up data.

In conclusion, our study is the first to underscores the vital role of THAP7-AS1 in inhibiting OC proliferation and migration by regulating FA2H expression via inhibition of miR-92b-5p Thus, targeting the THAP7-AS1/miR-92b-5p/FA2H axis represents a promising therapeutic target for OC.

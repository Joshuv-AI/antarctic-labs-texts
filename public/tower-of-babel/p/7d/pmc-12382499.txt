Evolution of cystic lung disease in Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome: assessment of older patients
Authors: Vasilios Tzilas, Rui Wang, Wei Wei, Xiaowen Hu, Jay H Ryu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBirt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome (BHD) is a rare autosomal dominant disease characterised by the development of lung cysts, skin fibrofolliculomas and an increased risk for renal cancer. The progression of cystic lung disease with ageing in BHD has not been well characterised.ObjectivesTo assess the severity of lung involvement in patients with BHD aged 50 years or older.MethodsA retrospective computer-assisted search was performed to identify patients with BHD seen at Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota and the First Affiliated Hospital of University of Science and Technology of China from January 2000 to May 2023. Demographic, clinical, pulmonary function and radiological parameters were extracted and analysed.ResultsAmong 82 patients with BHD aged 50 years or older, 49 (59%) were female and the median age was 57 years (range, 50–79) at baseline. Pneumothorax occurred in 46 patients (56%). Lung cysts were observed on CT in 77 patients (94%) and were bilateral in nearly all cases. The number of cysts was≥10 for most patients (84%) and the median size of the largest cyst was 31 mm (range, 4–160 mm). Median (range) % predicted values of forced vital capacity, forced expiratory volume in 1 s and diffusion capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide were 98 (62–147), 92 (56–145) and 85 (61–133), respectively, for those with data available. During follow-up (median, 41 months; range, 3–259), 35% of patients manifested worsening in pulmonary function or cystic lung disease.ConclusionLung involvement in older patients with BHD manifests varying severity of cystic lung disease with relatively mild functional impairment, if any, and indolent progression.Keywords: Rare lung diseases

Introduction

Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome (BHD) is a rare autosomal dominant disease caused by germline mutations in the folliculin (FLCN) gene, which is located on chromosome 17p11.21 2 and encodes the tumour suppressor protein, folliculin.3 Although the exact mechanisms of folliculin function have not yet been fully elucidated, loss of folliculin causes dysregulation in the mTOR, E-cadherin, LKB1 and 5’ AMP-activated protein kinase signalling pathways.4,6 The prevalence of BHD in the general population is about two cases per million, without difference between genders.7 Clinically, BHD is characterised by the development of lung cysts, skin fibrofolliculomas and an increased risk of renal cancer. There is significant variability in the clinical manifestations of disease depending, in part, on the underlying genetic variant.8 For example, a large intragenic deletion of exons 1–3 in FLCN has been associated with a significantly higher risk of pneumothorax.9

Lung cysts are a hallmark manifestation of BHD; however, data on their evolution and impact on pulmonary function are scarce. Lung cysts occurring in BHD are typically multiple, bilateral, lower lung predominant and identified by CT in most adult patients.10,12 In many cases, the cysts have an elongated, lentiform shape and tend to develop adjacent to the mediastinum and the lobar fissures, increasing the risk of pneumothorax. Indeed, patients with BHD have a 50-fold increase in the risk of pneumothorax (adjusted for age),13 which is commonly the presenting feature leading to the diagnosis of BHD and can occur in up to 76% of patients during the course of the disease.14 Dyspnoea is not a common symptom and is usually encountered in association with the occurrence of pneumothorax.

While other diffuse cystic lung diseases such as lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM) and pulmonary Langerhans cell histiocytosis have been associated with accelerated loss of lung function, sometimes leading to respiratory failure and lung transplantation, there has been little data assessing the course of lung involvement in patients with BHD later in life.1011 14,18 In the absence of long-term longitudinal data in this regard, we assessed the severity of cystic lung disease in patients with BHD aged 50 years or older by characterising their clinicoradiological parameters, including pulmonary function.

Material and methods

A computer-assisted search was conducted to identify patients with BHD aged 50 years or older seen at two referral centres for rare lung diseases, Mayo Clinic (Rochester Minnesota, Jacksonville Florida and Scottsdale Arizona) and the First Affiliated Hospital of University of Science and Technology of China, over the period of January 2000 to May 2023. Medical records and chest CT studies were reviewed to identify clinical, radiological features and pulmonary function. Diagnosis of BHD was based on the diagnostic criteria proposed by the European BHD consortium.19 Specifically, patients should fulfil one major criterion (at least five fibrofolliculomas or trichodiscomas, at least one histologically confirmed, of adult onset and/or a pathogenic FLCN germline mutation) or two minor criteria (bilateral basally located lung cysts with no other apparent cause, with or without spontaneous primary pneumothorax, and early onset of renal cancer (<50 years) or multifocal or bilateral renal cancer, or renal cancer of mixed chromophobe and oncocytic histology, a first-degree relative with BHD). Patients with cystic lung disease not fulfilling the above criteria were excluded from the study. This retrospective study was approved by Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board, Rochester, Minnesota (IRB: 23-002698).

We extracted demographic and clinical data, including age, sex, race, age at diagnosis, smoking status, family history of BHD, oxygen use, occurrence and number of pneumothoraces, presence and type of renal tumour, presence of folliculin mutation, pulmonary function data and clinical course. CT scans of the chest were reviewed to characterise the number, distribution and maximum size of the cysts at presentation, and their evolution on follow-up CT studies. An increase in the number of cysts and/or the diameter of largest cyst >2 mm was considered indicative of radiological progression. An absolute decline in forced vital capacity (FVC) % predicted >10%, forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) % predicted >10% and/or diffusion capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLco) % predicted >15% was considered as worsening (progression).

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Results

Patient characteristics

Among 82 patients with BHD aged 50 years or older, the median age was 57 years (range, 50–79), 59% were female, and 70% were never smokers (table 1). A family history of BHD was present in 66%. Genetic testing had been performed in 65 patients (79%) and showed a pathogenic mutation in the FLCN gene in 94%. Fibrofolliculomas and biopsy-proven renal tumours were observed in 65% and 26% of patients, respectively. A history of pneumothorax was present in 56% and multiple episodes had occurred in the majority (70%) of these subjects.

Baseline radiological and pulmonary function data

Lung cysts were observed on CT in 77 patients (94%) and were bilateral in all but one (male in his early 50s); lower lobe predominance of cysts was seen in 79% (table 2). All of the patients had at least 4 cysts identified and the majority (90%) exhibited ≥10 cysts. The median maximum diameter of the largest cyst was 31 mm (range, 4–160 mm) (figure 1).

Follow-up radiologic and pulmonary function data

Follow-up chest CT study was available for review in 48 patients (59%) with a median interval of 41 months (range, 3–259 months). Median age of these patients at follow-up was 62 years (range, 51–84 years). Progression of cystic lung disease (as defined in Methods) was observed in 17 (35%) (table 4). One of the 5 patients who had no cysts on the initial CT study manifested multiple small cysts measuring up to 18 mm in size bilaterally after a follow-up interval of 216 months (age 68).

Follow-up data on spirometry and DLco were available for 14 and 13 patients, respectively, with a median interval of 56 months (range, 12–182 months). None of the patients had an absolute reduction greater than 10% in FEV1% predicted. An absolute reduction greater than 10% for FVC% predicted and greater than 15% in DLco% predicted was observed in 1 (7%) and 3 (23%) patients, respectively. Collectively, worsening in pulmonary function (as defined in Methods) occurred in 3 (21%) patients with all of them being never smokers (table 4). A follow-up chest CT study was available for review in two of these three patients who manifested worsening of pulmonary function; chest CT demonstrated progression of cystic lung disease in both patients.

Aside from two patients who manifested progression in lung disease by CT and pulmonary function criteria, five other patients manifested progression by CT alone with no decline in pulmonary function parameters. The remaining seven patients with follow-up data available for both CT and pulmonary function showed stable findings.

Only two patients were on long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT). The first patient had comorbidities of granulomatosis with polyangiitis, thromboembolic disease and asthma, while the second had a history of obstructive sleep apnoea. Five other patients described dyspnoea on exertion (outside of pneumothorax episodes) during the clinical course, but the symptom did not appear to directly relate to their cystic lung disease, but rather to comorbidities. There were no patients who were being considered for lung transplantation or had died from cystic lung disease.

Discussion

In this study, we assessed the later stages of cystic lung disease in BHD by analysing the clinicoradiological features, including pulmonary function measures in patients aged 50 years or older, and their follow-up data. The extent and severity of cystic lung disease varied among these patients and appeared to progress rather slowly over a number of years accompanied by relatively mild, if any, degree of functional impairment, especially when compared with other diffuse cystic lung diseases, such as pulmonary LAM.10 17 During the follow-up period (median interval 41 months), approximately, one-thirds of our patients manifested progression of cystic lung disease on CT, but often without significant decline in pulmonary function measures.

In patients with BHD, pulmonary cysts and pneumothorax tend to develop before the age of 40 years. In two large series of patients with BHD, including 96 and 221 patients, the mean age at first pneumothorax was 33 and 39 years, respectively.10 11 Pulmonary function parameters were described in one of these two studies and showed normal results in the vast majority of cases.10 Although some of our older patients manifested extensive cystic changes in the lung, median values of baseline pulmonary function parameters including FEV1, FVC, DLco and TLC were within normal range. Accordingly, dyspnoea was an uncommon symptom in our study cohort.

Our findings are in accordance with studies addressing the general population of BHD patients. In a study, including 14 patients with BHD and a mean age of 46.6 years, mean VC% predicted was 86.7%.20 In a larger cohort of 44 patients with BHD and a mean age of 54.2 years, mean FVC% predicted and mean DLco% predicted were 95.2% and 80.7%, respectively.18 There was a statistically significant but mild decrease over time (follow-up duration>6 months) regarding VC% predicted, FEV1/FVC and FEV1% predicted but not DLco% predicted. By visual assessment, an increase in the size of the largest cyst was estimated to be 0.8 mm–1.0 mm per year.18 Daccord et al studied a cohort of 96 BHD patients with a mean age at diagnosis of 48 years.10 Mean baseline lung function parameters for FVC, FEV1, FEV1/FVC and DLco were within normal limits. Follow-up pulmonary function data were available in 57 patients with a mean follow-up duration of 2.8 years and demonstrated no significant deterioration.

We found reduced DLco and obstructive pattern in 38% and 27% of patients, respectively. Similar frequency of reduced DLco (33%) has been reported by Daccord et al and was associated with smoking.10 In our population of patients with BHD and reduced DLco, 58% were never smokers and only 25% had a reduced FEV1/FVC ratio (<0.7). Similarly, 67% of patients with airflow obstruction were never smokers. Thus, the findings of reduced DLco and obstruction in BHD cannot be fully explained by smoking. Reduced DLco likely relates, in part, to the loss of lung parenchyma due to the development of lung cysts.

In BHD, histopathological studies revealed that most cysts are located in the perilobular area abutting the interlobular septa, but rarely on bronchioles.21 22 Thus, BHD is not a bronchiolocentric cystic disease and airflow obstruction cannot be explained by direct involvement of bronchioles. However, it is possible that, as cysts increase in number and size, they could cause compression of small airways, resulting in a reduced FEV1/FVC ratio. Interestingly, although in the context of LAM, using a combination of CT, microCT and histopathology, cyst formation has been shown to result in airway collapse.23

Only two of our patients were on LTOT. Both patients had significant comorbidities that were more likely to have compromised their respiratory status rather than BHD itself. The first patient had a history of granulomatosis with polyangiitis, thromboembolic disease and asthma, while the second had obstructive sleep apnoea. Observations based on our study cohort along with those described in the literature suggest that progression to respiratory failure is a very unlikely outcome in patients with BHD as they age.

There are several limitations to our study, including those inherent to the retrospective design of the study. There was no standardised follow-up of these patients; therefore, systematic sequential data for imaging and lung function parameters were not available for our patients. The number of study subjects is modest, which is not unexpected, given the rarity of BHD and the fact that we included only those 50 years of age or older. Also, imaging progression was assessed visually and not by any means of quantitative analysis. Since our institutions are academic referral centres for rare lung diseases, it seems possible that our data are not fully applicable to the general population of patients with BHD. Pulmonary manifestations could be overrepresented, while renal and skin manifestations could be underestimated. Finally, this study was not designed or powered to assess potential predictive factors of disease progression. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first multicentric study of older patients with BHD in assessing the evolution of cystic lung disease associated with this disorder. In future prospective, inception cohort studies are warranted to generate more robust and definitive evidence.

In summary, in older (≥50 years) patients with BHD, the pulmonary function parameters are often within normal range, despite varying severity of their cystic lung disease, which progresses in an indolent manner over a number of years. Although some patients may gradually develop severe cystic lung disease on CT, the need for supplemental oxygen therapy is uncommon.

Dear Editor,

With the advancement of technology and information networks, young individuals are increasingly inclined to seek online health advice due to its convenience and efficiency in our fast‐paced lives. ChatGPT, as a prominent artificial intelligence model, has gained significant attention. It belongs to the Generative Pre‐trained Transformer (GPT) model family developed by OpenAI and has demonstrated immense potential in various domains, including education, healthcare, reasoning, text generation, and scientific research.

Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) is an autoimmune disease primarily affecting women of childbearing age. Alongside facial rash, it can manifest symptoms such as fever, joint swelling and pain, kidney damage, and neurological manifestations. SLE is a global disease that increases the risk of premature death 1 , particularly among young women. 2 Given the complexity of clinical manifestations and the disease's heterogeneity, numerous questions surround SLE. Consequently, we compiled the most frequently asked questions about SLE by patients and their families and employed ChatGPT to provide answers, aiming to explore the accuracy of its responses.

METHODS

We selected 14 common questions and queried ChatGPT (ChatGPT‐3.5‐turbo mode) for answers, recording the generated responses (Table 1). Experienced rheumatology experts independently subjectively rated the answers on a scale of 0–10 (10 Highly accurate, 8 ≤ score<10: Relatively accurate, 6 ≤ score<8: Average, score<6: Inaccurate). The evaluation was conducted by five experts from July 18th, 2023 to July 21st, 2023. The experts did not receive any compensation for their participation.

SLE (Systemic Lupus Erythematosus) has a certain genetic predisposition, but it is not solely determined by genetics. Genetic factors play a role in the development of SLE, but environmental factors are also important contributors to the disease.

Research has shown that there may be other individuals with autoimmune diseases, such as rheumatoid arthritis and systemic sclerosis, in the families of SLE patients. This suggests that genetic factors play a role in the development of SLE.

However, the pathogenesis of SLE is very complex and involves abnormal immune system responses and the interaction of various factors, including genetics and the environment. Environmental factors such as infections, medications, and exposure to ultraviolet radiation can also trigger or exacerbate SLE.

Therefore, while SLE has a genetic predisposition, genetic factors are not the sole determining factor, and environmental factors are equally important. If there are SLE patients in the family, the individual's risk of developing the disease may be increased, but it does not mean that they will definitely develop SLE. It is important to closely monitor one's own health, undergo regular check‐ups, and consult with a doctor if there are SLE patients in the family.

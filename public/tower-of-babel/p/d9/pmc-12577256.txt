A preliminary study on predicting the stability of external Iliac vein thrombosis using CT texture analysis
Authors: Yaxi Yu, Rong Chen, Jianxia Song, Yue Yang, Dawei Wang, Shuqi Hao, Hua Su, Ming Qu, Fei Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRationale and objectiveTo evaluate the predictive value of CT-based thrombi texture analysis for assessing external iliac vein thrombosis stability.Materials and methodsA total of 108 patients with external iliac vein thrombosis diagnosed through clinical examination and ultrasound at the First Hospital Affiliated with Hebei North University between May 2020 and October 2024 were enrolled. Patients were divided into external iliac vein thrombosis with acute pulmonary embolism (APE) (n = 58) and external iliac vein thrombosis without APE (n = 50) groups. The region of interest (region of interest, ROI) was manually delineated on the CT images of external iliac vein thrombosis patients using 3D-Slicer software. Texture features were extracted from CT thrombi images using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator algorithm. Imaging, clinical, and combined models were developed through logistic regression analyses and evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and clinical decision curves, with the areas under the curves (AUCs) compared using the DeLong test.ResultsExternal iliac vein thrombosis textural features (gray-level run-length matrix run entropy, gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) cluster shade, GLCM-maximum cross-correlation), and clinical parameters (smoking, sex, D-dimer), were identified as independent predictors of external iliac vein thrombosis stability. The AUCs for the imaging, clinical, and combined models were 0.792 (0.705–0.880), 0.832 (0.753–0.910), and 0.890 (0.825–0.954), respectively. The combined model outperformed the clinical and imaging models (P < 0.05). Clinical decision curve analysis revealed superior predictive accuracy (0.03–0.75) compared to the clinical (0.13–0.87) and imaging (0.12–0.83) models. It achieved the highest net benefit at a risk probability > 0.03, offering optimal efficacy and the greatest clinical value in predicting external iliac vein thrombosis stability.ConclusionIntegrating CT texture features with clinical parameters enhances the prediction of external iliac vein thrombosis stability, underscoring its potential clinical applications.Keywords: External iliac vein thrombosis, Deep vein thrombosis, Stability, Acute pulmonary embolism, CT, Texture analysis

Introduction

Contemporary vascular medicine primarily encompasses two major fields: arterial diseases and venous diseases. Arterial diseases, such as atherosclerosis, involve the accumulation of plaques within the arterial walls, leading to stenosis and potential ischemic events. Venous disease is represented by deep vein thrombosis (DVT), which is characterized by impaired venous return and related complications. DVT not only significantly impacts patient prognosis, but also serves as important precursor to fatal complications such as pulmonary embolism. Therefore, it has broader research value in the field of vascular medicine. DVT is a prevalent vascular disease [1] with an increasing annual incidence. The crude annual incidence rate of DVT varies from approximately 50 to 120 cases per 100,000 person-years in most studies [2], and it is now the third most common vascular disease worldwide after stroke and myocardial infarction [3]. Approximately 90% of acute pulmonary embolisms (APE) are attributed to the dislodgement of deep vein emboli, with lower-extremity DVT representing the primary source of these emboli [4, 5]. Patients with DVT may present with pain, swelling, erythema, and ulcers in the affected limbs. When APE occurs in patients with DVT, it is often clinically combined with chest pain, dyspnea, hemoptysis, tachycardia, and hypotension [6, 7]. Without timely appropriate intervention, it may lead to severe circulatory disturbances and sudden death [8]. Consequently, early recognition and assessment of DVT stability, along with prevention of APE, are imperative to ensure patient survival.

Currently, each commonly used clinical assessment method has its own limitations. Ultrasonography can diagnose DVT noninvasively and reliably, with a specificity of up to 100%. However, studies on DVT stability have mainly focused on floating-free thrombi (FFT), making it difficult to evaluate the stability of other common clinical DVTs [9]. Magnetic resonance venography (MRV) of the lower extremities is expensive and limited for evaluating the stability of FFT [10, 11]. Thus, there is a pressing need to identify more accurate and convenient methods for assessing DVT stability.

Texture analysis is an emerging image post-processing technology developed in recent years. It involves the calculation and analysis of the gray-value distributions across different regions within an image to identify and quantify the heterogeneity of lesion tissues [12, 13]. This technique aids in the qualitative assessment of lesions and evaluation of therapeutic efficacy, among other applications [14, 15]. The stability of thrombi is intricately linked to its composition, and computed tomography (CT) imaging provides compositional information through density measurements. However, conventional CT scanning methods have a limited ability to directly assess thrombi stability [16, 17]. In this study, we integrated texture analysis techniques with conventional CT imaging to extract the textural characteristics of thrombi, quantify their heterogeneity, and explore the potential application of this approach for assessing the stability of external iliac vein thrombosis.

Materials and methods

Participants

We conducted a retrospective analysis of 140 patients diagnosed with lower-limb external iliac vein thrombosis between May 2020 and October 2024 at the First Affiliated Hospital of Hebei North University. All patients were diagnosed with external iliac vein thrombosis through clinical examination and ultrasound and subsequently underwent computed tomography (CT) pelvic non-enhanced scans and computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA).

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) initial diagnosis of unilateral external iliac vein thrombosis confirmed using ultrasound, (2) concurrent CT pelvic scan performed within 24 h of the ultrasound, and (3) age > 18 years.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) prior history of DVT or APE and recurrent DVT, (2) iliac vein compression syndrome, (3) history of thrombolysis, surgical thrombolysis, or inferior vena cava filter placement, (4) bilateral DVT (bilateral lower extremity vein thrombosis, bilateral external iliac vein), (5) malignant tumors, and (6) imaging findings that did not meet the diagnostic and measurement requirements (poor image quality, image artifacts). (7) Patients who are allergic to iodine contrast and pregnant women.

The following patients were excluded: three with a prior history of DVT, two with recurrent DVT, ten with bilateral DVT, twelve treated with thrombolysis, surgical thrombolysis, or inferior vena cava filter placement, four with malignant tumors, and one with imaging findings that did not meet diagnostic and measurement standards. Ultimately, 108 patients were included in this study. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, patients were categorized into two groups: external iliac vein thrombosis with APE and external iliac vein thrombosis without APE, as determined using CTPA. The diagnostic criteria for APE, as referenced in [18], required CTPA images to demonstrate a low-density filling defect shadow over more than two consecutive levels of the pulmonary artery lumen, indicating narrowing or occlusion.

The clinical data collected included age, sex, smoking history, history of diabetes mellitus, body mass index (BMI), D-dimer level, fibrinogen degradation products (FDP), clinical signs, and symptoms. This study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (revised in 2013) and was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (approval number: k2024234). The requirement for individual consent was waived for this retrospective study.

Methods

Scanning protocol

Pelvic imaging was performed using Canon spiral CT scanners: a 320-row Aquilion ONE and an 80-row scanner (both from Canon Medical Systems Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The scanning range encompassed at least the bilateral common iliac veins to the femoral vein. The scanning parameters included a tube voltage of 120 kV and a tube current of SURE Exposure 3D (SD = 20). The reconstruction parameters included a matrix size of 512 × 512, a slice thickness of 5.0 mm, and a slice spacing of 5.0 mm. The reconstruction algorithm included iterative reconstruction (AIDR 3D, standard). A Canon Aquilion One 320-row CT scanner was employed. Patients were positioned supine with the head first, and imaging was performed from the lung apex to the base. The CTPA scanning parameters included a tube voltage of 100 kV, ball tube rotation time of 0.275 s, layer thickness of 1.0 mm, and layer spacing of 1.0 mm. Patients received an injection of iodixanol (320 mg iodine/mL) at a dosage of 0.9 mL/kg administered at 4.0 mL/s, followed by a 40 mL saline flush. Informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to the administration of iodine contrast agents.

Texture analysis

Pelvic images were loaded into 3D-Slicer software. In each image, the ROI was manually outlined layer-by-layer along the lumen of the external iliac vein without extending beyond the vessel wall by 2 radiologists with more than 10 years of experience. Disagreements are resolved through discussion. To ensure consistency of the extracted features, 30 patients were randomly selected. The ROIs were independently outlined by two experienced radiologists, and the texture features were extracted using the same method. The calculated intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) > 0.75 indicated good consistency of feature extraction in this study. Segmentation of the remaining images was done by radiologist 1.

After delineation, texture feature parameters were extracted using the gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), gray-level run-length matrix (GLRLM), gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM), gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM), and neighboring gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features [19] (Fig. 1).

Model construction

All extracted texture features were standardized, followed by dimension reduction and screening using R version 4.4.1 software. The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) method, along with ten-fold cross-validation, was employed to determine the optimal λ value, identifying non-zero texture features and their coefficients. Clinical data and texture analysis characteristics were used to identify independent risk factors for predicting external iliac vein thrombosis stability through univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. This facilitated the construction of clinical, imaging, and combined imaging and clinical models. Calibration curves and the Hosmer-Lemeshow (H-L) test were employed to evaluate the models’ goodness of fit and clinical applicability.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the three predictive models were generated using SPSS version 27.0, with calculation of the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and cutoff values. The DeLong test was conducted to assess differences between the models. Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to quantify the net clinical benefit of the models and compare their clinical utility across various threshold probabilities.

Statistical processing

Statistical analysis of clinical data was performed using SPSS 27.0, and the normality of the measurement data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), while non-normally distributed data were expressed as the median and interquartile range (M [IQR]). Differences between groups were analyzed using the independent samples t-test or non-parametric test for continuous variables. Counting data were expressed as proportions, with differences between groups analyzed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact probability test. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

General clinical information

A total of 108 patients with external iliac vein thrombosis were enrolled in this study, including 36 men (33.3%) and 72 women (66.7%). Patient ages ranged from 20 to 88 years, with a mean age of 64.34 ± 11.20 years. Of these, 50 patients (43.6%) were in the external iliac vein thrombosis without APE group, comprising nine men (18.0%) and 41 women (82.0%), while 58 patients (53.7%) were in the external iliac vein thrombosis with APE group, comprising 27 men (46.6%) and 31 women (53.4%). No significant differences in age, BMI, and diabetes mellitus were observed between the two groups (P > 0.05). However, significant differences were noted in smoking status, sex, chest pain, D-dimer levels, and FDP between the two groups (P < 0.05) (Table 1).

Construction of predictive models

The texture parameters GLRLM-RE, GLCM-CS, and GLCM-MCC were used for texture analysis, whereas the clinical parameters smoking, sex, and D-dimer were incorporated into the clinical model. An integrated model combining all the aforementioned parameters was developed.

Analysis of the ROC curves revealed that the AUCs for predicting DVT stability were 0.832 (95% CI: 0.753–0.910) for the clinical model, 0.792 (95% CI: 0.705–0.880) for the imaging model, and 0.890 (95% CI: 0.825–0.954) for the combined model, as presented in Table 5. The DeLong test was employed to compare the AUCs, indicating that the AUC of the combined model was significantly higher than those of the imaging and clinical models (Z = -2.128, P = 0.033; Z = -2.738, P = 0.006). This finding suggests that the predictive efficacy of the combined model exceeds that of the individual clinical and imaging models. All three models demonstrated predictive value for external iliac vein thrombosis stability, with the combined model exhibiting the highest predictive accuracy (Fig. 3A).

Discussion

Early identification of patients with unstable DVT is crucial for preventing APE and effectively managing DVT. In this study, CT imaging combined with texture analysis techniques was utilized to extract external iliac vein thrombosis texture features, quantify thrombi heterogeneity, and develop predictive models for assessing external iliac vein thrombosis stability. The findings revealed that texture feature parameters are independent risk factors for external iliac vein thrombosis instability. Furthermore, the combined model, which incorporates both texture feature parameters and clinical parameters, demonstrated superior predictive value for external iliac vein thrombosis stability (AUC = 0.890), compared to the clinical model (AUC = 0.832) and the imaging model (AUC = 0.792). Clinical decision curve analysis revealed that the combined model was the most effective in predicting external iliac vein thrombosis stability and offered the highest clinical utility, aiding clinical assessment and management of patients with external iliac vein thrombosis.

Thrombi stability is intricately linked to its composition, which directly influences its mechanical properties [20]. Erythrocyte levels are significantly elevated in patients with migrating thrombi compared to those in the non-migrating group, whereas fibrin levels are comparatively lower in the migrating group [21]. This observation suggests that reduced fibrin levels compromise the structural stability of thrombi, making them more susceptible to migration into the bloodstream. Computed tomography venography (CTV) demonstrates high accuracy and sensitivity in diagnosing lower-limb DVT [22]. Jeong et al. measured and calculated the thrombi density ratio (thrombi CT value/peripheral vein CT value) using CTV images of lower extremity veins. Their findings revealed that patients with DVT and APE had a significantly higher thrombi density ratio than those with DVT alone. Furthermore, the thrombi density ratio correlated with APE severity and the risk of future post-thrombotic syndrome [23]. Consequently, higher intrathrombotic density is associated with an increased risk of spontaneous thrombolysis and subsequent thrombi migration into the pulmonary circulation.

Distinguishing thrombi from blood density on conventional CT images poses a significant challenge despite non-enhanced CT imaging being the most widely accepted clinical test for patients. Thus, the ability to detect and predict thrombi stability using non-enhanced CT has substantial clinical value. Texture analysis offers a method for extracting numerous texture features from images, enabling the quantification of thrombi heterogeneity through mathematical calculations. This approach elucidates the diversity of internal thrombi components and structures, enhancing thrombi identification and classification accuracy. This study integrated texture analysis with CT imaging to explore its feasibility in evaluating external iliac vein thrombosis stability.

Specifically, nine texture features were extracted from non-enhanced CT images: two GLCM features, three GLRLM features, three GLSZM features, and one NGTDM feature. The GLCM and GLSZM features provide insights into complex information, such as lesion complexity, horizontal variation, and texture thickness, whereas the GLRLM features reflect the metrics and statistics of image pixel information [24, 25]. NGTDM features capture the intensity variations between a voxel and its neighboring voxels, elucidating the dynamic range of grayscale intensities within a localized region [26]. The results of this study showed that GLRLM-RE, GLCM-CS, and GLCM-MCC were independent risk factors for predicting external iliac vein thrombosis stability. GLRLM-RE assessed the irregularity and complexity of the texture by analyzing the distribution of successive tours of pixels of the same gray value, with elevated RE values potentially signifying a more complex and random distribution of run lengths within the thrombi structure, increased heterogeneity, and greater thrombi instability. GLCM-CS quantifies the degree of difference in gray scale values of adjacent pixels in the image, reflecting the homogeneity of the thrombi texture. Lower CS value suggests that there is little difference in gray scale between adjacent areas within the thrombi, signifying a more homogeneous distribution of platelets and fibrin and relatively stable thrombi. GLCM-MCC reflects the linear spatial correlation of pixel gray values, reflecting the consistency of the thrombi texture. Elevated MCC values indicates a strong correlation in the spatial distribution of pixel gray values within the thrombi, with a more ordered arrangement of the platelet-fibrin network, and this ordered internal microstructure of the thrombi makes the thrombi more stable. Thrombi shape, location, and size are typically random and do not comprehensively characterize their nature, complicating precise analysis. Alterations within the thrombi cause variations in tissue composition. Texture analysis can discern subtle differences in thrombi images and establish a correlation between changes in thrombi composition and grayscale patterns in density-based medical images, revealing the diversity of thrombi’s internal composition and structure. Consistent with our findings, Bretzner et al. [27] identified a strong correlation between erythrocyte count and iron concentration in thrombi (r = 0.87, P < 0.001). Thrombi with elevated erythrocyte counts and iron concentrations exhibited significant correlations in R2 histograms and texture analyses, with erythrocyte-rich thrombi demonstrating greater homogeneity and coarser textures in the R2 histograms. The RBC count of thrombi was accurately quantified using texture analysis of the MRI R2 histogram. Thus, texture analysis can provide valuable information on thrombi composition. By integrating these three independent risk factors(GLRLM-RE, GLCM-CS, and GLCM-MCC) into the imaging model, the AUC reached 0.792, underscoring the utility of texture features in predicting external iliac vein thrombosis stability. This approach offers a non-invasive and effective method for assessing external iliac vein thrombosis, potentially facilitating timely clinical intervention and symptom management in affected patients.

In this study, ROC curve analysis revealed that the AUC value for the combined model [0.890 (95% CI: 0.825–0.954)] exceeded that of both the imaging model [0.792 (95% CI: 0.705–0.880)] and the clinical model [0.832 (95% CI: 0.753–0.910)], with the differences being statistically significant (P < 0.05). The combined model demonstrated superior efficacy in predicting external iliac vein thrombosis stability and exhibited the highest clinical utility, facilitating early identification of patients with unstable external iliac vein thrombosis and enabling timely clinical intervention to prevent or mitigate the occurrence of APE.

D-dimer is a sensitive diagnostic marker of hypercoagulable states in humans [28]. In this study, patients exhibited significantly elevated D-dimer levels compared to normal levels, with the external iliac vein thrombosis with APE group demonstrating markedly higher levels than the external iliac vein thrombosis without APE group. These findings are consistent with those reported by Zhang et al. [29]. In addition, relative weight analysis showed that D-dimer contributed the most (28.3%) to predicting DVT stability in the combined model. These suggest that elevated D-dimer levels provide valuable insights into external iliac vein thrombosis stability. However, elevated D-dimer levels are not definitive indicators of thrombosis, as they can be significantly increased in older patients or those with malignancy, inflammation, pregnancy, or other disorders [30, 31]. Consequently, further research with larger sample sizes is necessary to ascertain the diagnostic utility of D-dimer levels in both APE and DVT.

This study identified smoking as an independent risk factor for external iliac vein thrombosis development, with a significantly higher prevalence of smokers in the external iliac vein thrombosis with APE group than in the external iliac vein thrombosis without APE group, corroborating previous research findings [1, 29]. Smoking induces a hypercoagulable state and promotes thrombosis, whereas optimal endothelial function is crucial for maintaining a balance between coagulation and fibrinolysis [32]. The relationship between sex and the development of lower extremity DVT remains unclear; elevated androgen levels in men may enhance platelet and thrombin activation, thereby increasing the risk of DVT [33, 34].

This study has several limitations. (1) The relatively small sample size, which met stringent inclusion criteria, may have limited the generalizability and robustness of the findings. Larger, well-designed multicenter trials are urgently needed to validate the predictive efficacy of the model across diverse populations and settings.(2) The study was conducted with a limited sample size, lacked external validation, and the results of the study needed to be validated at other institutions to ensure the reliability of the results. (3) Different image acquisition devices, different acquisition parameters and acquisition environments, etc., will make differences in the acquired image texture, thus affecting the consistency and comparability of texture analysis results. (4) The ROIs in this study were manually delineated by the researcher, potentially introducing anthropogenic bias into the extracted features. Future research should consider using more objective and automated methods for ROI delineation to minimize potential biases and enhance the objectivity and reproducibility of the findings.

Conclusions

CT image-based thrombi texture analysis demonstrated predictive value for assessing external iliac vein thrombosis stability. Furthermore, a combined model incorporating clinical parameters enhanced the accuracy of stability assessments. This approach can facilitate early clinical intervention in patients with external iliac vein thrombosis, thereby aiding in the prevention of severe complications.

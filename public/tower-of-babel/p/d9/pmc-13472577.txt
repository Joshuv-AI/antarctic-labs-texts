Analysis of Antimicrobial Prescribing Patterns and Resistance Trends in Intensive Care Units
Authors: Pravin Yerpude, Rachana Akash Bhavsar, Konika Jain, P. J. Arun Shankar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is an increasing health issue worldwide with the intensive care unit (ICU) being the main place where the phenomenon has become common due to the high use of antibiotics. The purpose of the research was to study the pattern of antimicrobial prescribing and resistance of patients admitted at ICU in a tertiary care hospital.Materials and Methods:The case of a prospective observational study was carried out during 12 months with 200 patients in the ICU who received antimicrobial therapy. Demographic, antimicrobial prescription, indications, and microbiological culture report data were gathered and compared. Descriptive statistics were represented as mean, standard deviation, and percentages, and Chi-square and t-tests were used in the inferential analysis.Results:The average age of patients was 54.6 years, and there were 62 males. The empirical antibiotic treatment was started in 78% of the cases with the most common ones (42% and 36%, respectively) being carbapenems and piperacillin-tazobactam. Only 28% cases showed de-escalation that was culture sensitive. Isolates with multidrug-resistance were found in 39% with the most common being Acinetobacter baumannii (18) and Klebsiella pneumoniae (15). Carbapenems resistance was observed in 32% of the isolates. There was a considerable difference in mortality (34 vs. 18, P = 0.02) between patients who received inappropriate empirical therapy.Conclusion:The research paper indicates the common occurrence of empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic use and high resistance patterns in the ICU. Antimicrobial stewardship programs and following culture-directed therapy should be implemented to limit the increasing antimicrobial resistance.

INTRODUCTION

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) has become one of the most significant global health threats, and intensive care units (ICUs) represent the most dangerous hotspots of the emergence and expansion of resistant pathogens.[1] This is exacerbated by the heavy cases of severe infections, intricate procedures, and intense employment of broad-spectrum antibiotics in ICUs.[2] The improper administration of antimicrobials such as using empirical treatment and slowing down de-escalation is another cause that speeds up the resistance trends.[3]

In recent literature, it was shown that multidrug-resistant (MDR) organisms like Acinetobacter baumannii, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa are gaining increasing prevalence in the ICU setting.[4] The resistance of carbapenem has been increasing alarmingly, which restricts the therapeutic possibilities and increases morbidity and mortality.[5] Although there are antimicrobial stewardship guidelines available, their compliance is not universal particularly in resource-deprived environments.[6]

A number of researchers have examined the antimicrobial use patterns, but there is still a gap to correlate prescribing patterns with the resistance patterns in the context of real-time ICU environments especially in third world countries.[7] These trends are important to understand in order to maximize treatment plans and minimize the cost of AMR.

Thus, the current research was conducted to assess the current practices in the prescribing of antimicrobials and determine the patterns of resistance among the ICU patients in a tertiary care facility.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD, and categorical variables as percentages. Chi-square test and independent t-test were used for comparisons. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics

The study included 200 patients with a mean age of 54.6 ± 16.2 years. Males constituted 62% (n = 124), while females accounted for 38% (n = 76).

Antimicrobial prescribing patterns

Empirical therapy was initiated in 78% of cases, while culture-guided therapy was observed in 22%. Carbapenems (42%), piperacillin-tazobactam (36%), and cephalosporins (28%) were the most commonly prescribed antibiotics.

De-escalation of therapy following culture reports was noted in only 28% of patients.

Resistance patterns

A total of 150 culture-positive samples were analyzed. Multidrug-resistant organisms were found in 39% of isolates.

Patients receiving inappropriate empirical therapy had significantly higher mortality compared to those receiving appropriate therapy (34% vs. 18%, P = 0.02). Additionally, the mean ICU stay was longer in patients with MDR infections (10.2 ± 3.5 vs. 6.8 ± 2.4 days, P < 0.001) [Tables 1-3].

DISCUSSION

The current research demonstrates alarming tendencies of antimicrobial prescribing and resistance rates in the ICUs. The percentage of patients who received empirical therapy (78) demonstrates an urgency in providing treatment to the patient in a critical condition, which is comparable to the results obtained by Vincent et al.[1] Nonetheless, overuse of empirical broad-spectrum antibiotics can help to create resistant organisms.

The most commonly prescribed agents were carbapenems and tazobactam-piperacillin, as the research has shown that they are commonly used in the ICU.[2] The low percentage of de-escalation (28) that was found in this research indicates that there is no optimal adherence to antimicrobial stewardship practices. The same has been observed by Kollef et al.,[3] stating that better stewardship interventions are required.

The proportion of MDR organisms (39%) in the study is consistent with the world trends, which have been reported in ICU units.[4] Acinetobacter baumannii became the leading pathogen that exhibits high carbapenem resistance (45%), which is also supported by recent surveillance studies.[5] It is characterized by its capability to survive in a hospital setting and develop resistance mechanisms at a very quick rate.

The high correlation between inappropriate empirical therapy and a high rate of mortality (P = 0.02) provides the significance of the timely and proper choice of antibiotics. Earlier studies have recorded similar associations.[6] Moreover, the economic and clinical significance of antimicrobial resistance is indicated by the long-term ICU hospital stay of patients with MDR infections.

The results of this paper reiterate the importance of the urgency of adoption of effective antimicrobial stewardship initiatives. Frequent review of prescribing behavior, guideline-based compliance, and culture-related early de-escalation are the essential measures to fight AMR. Also, an improvement of microbiological surveillance may contribute to the better design of empirical therapy.

CONCLUSION

This study demonstrates a high prevalence of empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic use and significant multidrug resistance in ICU settings. Inappropriate antimicrobial prescribing was associated with increased mortality and prolonged ICU stay. Strengthening antimicrobial stewardship practices and promoting culture-guided therapy are essential to mitigate resistance trends and improve patient outcomes.

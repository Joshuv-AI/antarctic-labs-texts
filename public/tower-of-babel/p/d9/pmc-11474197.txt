Editorial 10.3
Published: 2024

It is our pleasure to introduce the third issue of the Journal with articles on various viruses.

The WHO 2030 goal for the eradication of hepatitis as a public health needs a global approach to some of the key populations such as people who use or inject drugs The retrospective study “A comparison of sofosbuvir/velpatasvir and glecaprevir/pibrentasvir for the treatment of hepatitis C infection among people who use or inject drugs” by Dr S Yi and al from British Columbia, Canada, shows among inner city Vancouver no difference between the two regimens with a high degree of efficacy for HCV treatment, highlighting the need for adherence support which should lead in the future to a standard of care in these difficult to treat populations.

The article “Indomethacin inhibits human seasonal coronaviruses at late stages of viral replication in lung cells: impact on virus-induced COX-2 expression” by Dr C Tramontozzi et al. from La Sapienza University, Rome, Italy shows the potent anti-inflammatory properties and direct antiviral activity of indomethacin which could be effective in the treatment of Alpha and Beta coronavirus infections.

Nipah virus is a bat-borne, zoonotic virus that is responsible for infection in human and other animals. Outbreaks are seasonal in some areas of the world. It remains a priority disease for the WHO Research and Development Blueprint with a high mortality rate. The study “Knowledge and attitude among Bangladeshi healthcare workers regarding the management and infection prevention and control of Nipah virus” by Dr T Islam et al. from Bangladesh shows among healthcare workers a need to enhance knowledge about the virus to prepare for future outbreaks.

Prevention of childhood infectious diseases by immunisation is one the most cost-effective interventions and coverage in some countries remains below expected levels. In this article “Vaccination coverage and associated factors among children aged 12–23 months in Pawie District, Ethiopia: a cross-sectional study” by Dr F. Salah et al. from Bahir Dar University, Bahir Dar, Ethiopia the authors describe the factors associated with a high rate of full vaccination coverage in this children population, and particularly the role of maternal characteristics on childhood vaccination.

The two HIV-related articles include the viewpoint on “Partner protection in HIV cure trials involving analytical treatment interruption: updated Toolkit to mitigate HIV transmission risk” by Dr K. Dube et al. from the University of California, USA which deals with a fundamental issue associated with analytical treatment interruption in HIV Cure trials. The other article by Dr J. De Clerq from Ghent University, Belgium “Accurate staging of immune-virological dynamics during acute HIV infection, a Belgian prospective cohort study” describes compartment sampling at this very early stage of the infection and the impact on viro-immunological markers with very early treatment intervention.

We have heard with great sadness that our colleague and friend, Professor Todd Mackenzie Allen had recently passed away. He will be remembered for his contribution to HIV vaccine design, viral sequence evolution for both HIV and HCV and more recently functional HIV cure via CAR T cell immunotherapy and gene editing approaches. He will be missed by many of us. Our thoughts go to his family and friends.

We are as always grateful to all the authors and reviewers for taking part in the Journal project.

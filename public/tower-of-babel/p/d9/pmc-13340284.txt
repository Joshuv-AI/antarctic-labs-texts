Effectiveness and Safety of Anlotinib with or without PD-1 Blockades in Patients with Platinum-Resistant or Immunotherapy-Resistant Recurrent/Metastatic Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma: A Retrospective, Real-World Study
Authors: Kailin Chen, Yuyi Li, Xiaoyu Chen, Weichang Zhu, Yuchen Shi, Xu Ye, Cuihong Jiang, Yajun Li, Feng Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeFor recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (R/M-NPC) that has progressed after platinum-based chemotherapy or immunotherapy, treatment options are limited. This real-world study evaluated the effectiveness and safety of anlotinib, alone or combined with PD-1 inhibitors, in patients with platinum-resistant or PD-1 inhibitor-resistant R/M-NPC.Patients and MethodsThis retrospective, single-center study included 86 patients with R/M-NPC who received at least one cycle of anlotinib-based therapy between November 2018 and March 2024. Patients were divided into anlotinib monotherapy (n=53) and anlotinib plus PD-1 blockade combination groups (n=33). The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR). Secondary endpoints included disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety.ResultsThe ORR was significantly higher in the combination group than in the monotherapy group (30.30% vs 7.55%, P=0.013). DCR was also superior with combination therapy (87.90% vs 62.30%, P=0.02). Median PFS was 8.93 months for combination therapy versus 5.13 months for monotherapy (P=0.011); median OS was 12.97 months versus 8.73 months (P=0.001), respectively. In the subgroup of patients with prior immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy, the combination regimen maintained a numerically higher ORR (50.00% vs 13.60%, P=0.028), a longer PFS (mPFS: 9.57 vs 5.12 months, P=0.036) and OS (mOS: 12.97 vs 8.23 months, P=0.009), compared to monotherapy. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in 54.65% of patients, with most being grade 1–2. Grade 3 TRAEs were reported in 6 patients; no grade 4 TRAEs or treatment-related deaths occurred.ConclusionAnlotinib plus PD-1 blockade significantly improved antitumor activity versus monotherapy in platinum- or PD-1 inhibitor-resistant R/M NPC, with acceptable safety. The favorable trends seen in the immunotherapy-pretreated subgroup are preliminary. These findings support the further evaluation of this combination as a promising therapeutic option in this challenging patient population.Keywords: anlotinib, PD-1 inhibitor, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, platinum-resistant, immunotherapy-resistant

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a rare form of head and neck cancer,1 with unique geographical, etiological and biological characteristics. Approximately 10 to 15% of NPC patients present with distant metastases at primary diagnosis. Additionally, about 30% of patients with locally advanced disease developed treatment failures with local recurrence or distant metastases after standard-of-care treatment.1,2 For recurrent or metastatic (R/M)-NPC patients, programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor (pembrolizumab, nivolumab, camrelizumab, or toripalimab) added to cisplatin/gemcitabine (GP) have been recommended as the first-line regimens based on the results of three randomised Phase 3 trials (JUPITER-02, CAPTAIN-1st, and RATIONALE-309).3–5 However, patients who are refractory or progress after first-line treatment have few treatment options and presents a challenge due to the patient’s decreased tolerance and deteriorated nutritional status after multiple cycles of chemotherapy. Although, PD-1 inhibitors have been recommended as the second-line therapy or later for R/M-NPC, the overall response rate (ORR) was restricted to 20.5% to 34.1%.6–9 Therefore, more effective salvage options are in urgent need.

Antiangiogenic therapy has indeed shown promise in the treatment of R/M-NPC, which is associated with high vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression.10 Anlotinib, an anti-angiogenetic multi-targeted TKI, which targets vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR), fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR), platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFR), and c-kit.11,12 It has been approved as 3rd line treatment for R/M-NPC in China with an ORR of 17.6%.13 In additional, alotinib has been shown to promote T-cell infiltration and induce tumor vascular normalization, leading to remodeling the tumor immune environment and reversing resistance to immunotherapy.14,15 The latest Phase II TORAL study showed that the ORR was 36.7% in patients with R/M NPC who failed the first-line platinum-based chemotherapy treated with toripalimab plus anlotinib.16 It seems that adding PD-1 inhibitors may offer better efficacy compared to anlotinib monotherapy as the second-line or beyond treatment for R/M-NPC. However, real-world evidence remains unclear.

In this paper, we evaluated the synergistic clinical efficacy and safety of adding anlotinib to PD-1 inhibitors in advanced R/M NPC patients with first-line platinum-resistant or PD-1 inhibitor resistant. Additionally, we investigated the antitumor activity within key patient subgroups. Furthermore, we identified independent prognostic factors that have predictive value for patient survival.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Patients

This retrospective, single-center, cohort study evaluated the effectiveness and safety of anlotinib or anlotinib combined with PD-1 inhibitor for patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma. The study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the ethic committee institutional review board of Hunan cancer hospital (Ethics approval #2026[07]), and informed consent was obtained from all subjects.

A total of 211 patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma received anlotinib-containing therapy at our center between November 2018 and March 2024 were screened for eligibility. Key inclusion criteria were: (1) age 18–80 years; (2) histologically or cytologically confirmed NPC; (3) documented disease progression following at least one prior line of platinum-based chemotherapy or immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy (with or without chemotherapy); (4) receipt of at least one cycle of anlotinib monotherapy or anlotinib combined with a PD-1 inhibitor; and (5) availability of baseline clinical data, at least one follow-up radiological assessment, and survival information. Patients were excluded if they had received anlotinib combined with chemotherapy (with or without immunotherapy) or with other targeted agents, had double primary tumors, had undergone radical surgery with curative intent for metastatic disease, or were pregnant or lactating. Treatment Regimens

Patients were categorized into two groups based on the therapy received: the Anlotinib monotherapy group and the Combination therapy group (anlotinib plus a PD-1 inhibitor). The choice of regimen was at the physician’s discretion, considering factors such as patient performance status, prior treatment history, comorbidities, and economic considerations.

Anlotinib (Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical) was administered orally at a starting dose of 12 mg once daily on a schedule of 2 weeks on and 1 week off (21-day cycle). Dose reductions or interruptions were permitted based on tolerability and the occurrence of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) or the disease progression.

Whether to receive PD-1 blockades was the choice of physicians according to the comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s condition, concomitant diseases (such as hepatitis), and economic situation. The PD-1 inhibitors used in combination therapy included toripalimab (240 mg every 3 weeks), tislelizumab (200 mg every 3 weeks), camrelizumab (200 mg every 3 weeks), penpulimab (200 mg every 3 weeks), pembrolizumab (100 mg every 3 weeks), and sintilimab (200 mg every 3 weeks). All anti-PD-1 agents were administered via intravenous infusion.

Treatment Evaluation

Clinical data, including demographic characteristics, treatment history, laboratory results, imaging reports, and survival outcomes, were retrospectively extracted from electronic medical records. Tumor assessments were conducted using contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1.

The primary endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR). Secondary endpoints included disease control rate (DCR; CR + PR + stable disease [SD]), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety. PFS was calculated from the initiation of anlotinib-based therapy until radiographic disease progression or death from any cause. OS was measured from therapy initiation until death from any cause. Patients without an event were censored at the date of last follow-up (cut-off date: September 30, 2025). Adverse events were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.0; http://www.R-project.org). The parametric test of Student’s t-test and non-parametric of Mann–Whitney U-test were performed to compare the differences between two groups. The adjust P value was calculated by the Benjamini-Hochberg correction and Chi-square test was used to analyze the categorical variables. PFS and OS were calculated by the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using a stratified Log rank test. The analysis for ORR was based on the best overall response. The criteria of statistical significance was set as a P value < 0.05 or adjust P value < 0.05.

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 86 patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma with previously received platinum-based chemotherapy who received at least one cycle of anlotinib-based therapy were included in the final analysis. The cohort comprised 53 patients (61.6%) in the anlotinib monotherapy group and 33 patients (38.4%) in the anlotinib plus PD-1 blockade combination group. Among the entire cohort, 57 patients (66.3%) had a history of prior immunotherapy.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The study flowchart is presented in Figure 1. The overall population had a median age of 53 years (range, 29–74) and was predominantly male (76.74%). Chronic comorbidities were present in 14 patients (16.28%), including hypertension (n=6), chronic atrophic gastritis (n=3), chronic hepatitis B (n=2), diabetes mellitus (n=1), emphysema (n=1), and concomitant hypertension with chronic hepatitis B (n=1). The most frequent sites of distant metastasis were the lung (47.67%), liver (41.86%), and bone (40.70%). Most characteristics, including sex, age, ECOG performance status, chronic comorbidities, patterns of recurrence, sites of metastasis (lung, liver, bone), distant lymph node involvement, previous lines of therapy, and prior ICI exposure, were well-balanced between the two groups (all P > 0.05). However, patients assigned to the combination therapy group had a significantly higher body mass index (BMI, 22.41 ± 2.90 kg/m2 vs 20.92 ± 3.60 kg/m2, P = 0.037), a greater proportion with multiple distant metastases (≥2 organs involved, 45.45% vs 18.87%, P = 0.017), and a lower rate of prior local-regional radiotherapy (48.48% vs 77.36%, P = 0.012) compared to those in the monotherapy group.

Treatment Exposure

Treatment was administered predominantly in the third-line setting or beyond, with 52.3% (45/86) of all patients having received ≥3 prior lines of systemic therapy. There was no significant difference in pretreatment burden between the groups (P = 0.322). Anlotinib was initiated at a dose of 12 mg daily (2 weeks on/1 week off) in all patients. At the data cut-off (September 30, 2025), the median duration of anlotinib treatment was 1.63 months (range, 0.16–8.97). Dose modification was required in 3 patients, and therapy was discontinued in 27 patients, primarily due to disease progression (n=23; due to AEs, n=4).

In the combination group, the PD-1 inhibitors administered were toripalimab (n=18, 54.55%), tislelizumab (n=6, 18.18%), camrelizumab (n=3, 9.09%), penpulimab (n=2, 6.06%), pembrolizumab (n=2, 6.06%), and sintilimab (n=2, 6.06%).

Antitumor Activity

With a median follow-up of 11.37 months (range, 1.03–64.17), the antitumor efficacy of the two regimens was assessed (Table 2). No complete responses were observed in either group. The ORR was significantly higher in the combination therapy group compared to the monotherapy group (30.30% [10/33] vs 7.55% [4/53], P = 0.013). Similarly, the DCR favored the combination regimen (87.90% [29/33] vs 62.30% [33/53], P = 0.02).

Patients receiving anlotinib monotherapy achieved a median PFS of 5.13 months and a median OS of 8.73 months. The corresponding 1-year PFS and OS rates were 7.55% (95% CI: 2.94–19.36) and 50.33% (95% CI: 38.16–66.38), respectively. Safety

Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) are detailed in Table 3. Any-grade TRAEs were reported in 47 patients (54.65%). The most common TRAEs of any grade included leukopenia (22.09%), anemia (15.11%), neutropenia (13.95%), and weight loss (4.65%). Thyroid dysfunction, manifested as TSH abnormality (10.47%) and T4/FT4 abnormality (6.98%), was also observed.

The majority of TRAEs were grade 1–2 in severity (80.9% of affected patients). Grade 3 TRAEs occurred in 6 patients and included diarrhea, hypertension, hemorrhage, oral ulcer, hand-foot syndrome, and herpes zoster (each n=1). No grade 4 or higher TRAEs or treatment-related deaths were reported.

The safety profiles between the two groups were largely comparable. However, events of weight loss (12.10% vs 0%, P = 0.019), decreased albumin (12.10% vs 0%, P = 0.019), TSH abnormality (21.20% vs 3.77%, P = 0.024), and T4/FT4 abnormality (15.20% vs 1.89%, P = 0.029) were more frequently reported in the combination therapy group. Dermatologic and mucocutaneous toxicities (eg, hand-foot syndrome, oral ulcer) and hemorrhage were also more common in the combination group.

Efficacy in Patients with Prior Immunotherapy

A prespecified subgroup analysis was conducted in the 38 patients (44.19% of the total cohort) who had received prior ICI therapy. Their baseline characteristics are shown in Supplementary Table S3. Although balanced in most respects, patients in the combination subgroup presented with a significantly higher metastatic burden (metastases in ≥2 organs: 56.25% vs 13.64%, P = 0.015).

As shown in Supplementary Table S4, the ORR was numerically higher with combination therapy (50.00% [8/16]) compared to anlotinib monotherapy (13.60% [3/22], P = 0.028). The DCR was numerically higher in the combination group (87.50% vs 77.30%, P = 0.675). Survival outcomes in this ICI-pretreated subgroup are presented in Figure 2C and D. Anlotinib monotherapy yielded a median PFS of 5.12 months and a median OS of 8.23 months, with 1-year PFS and OS rates of 4.55% (95% CI: 0.67–30.85) and 36.4% (95% CI: 20.4–65.1), respectively. The combination of anlotinib with a PD-1 inhibitor was associated with improved median PFS (9.57 months) and OS (12.97 months), and higher 1-year survival rates (PFS: 37.50% [95% CI: 19.90–70.60]; OS: 79.40% [95% CI: 61.20–100]).

The safety profile in this subgroup (Supplementary Table S5) was consistent with that of the overall population, with no new safety signals identified.

Discussion

This retrospective real-world study demonstrates that anlotinib-based therapy, particularly in combination with PD-1 inhibitors, represents a promising treatment strategy for patients with R/M-NPC who have progressed after platinum-based chemotherapy. Our findings indicate that the combination of anlotinib with a PD-1 inhibitor yields significantly superior antitumor activity compared to anlotinib monotherapy, as evidenced by higher ORR, improved DCR, and prolonged PFS and OS. Notably, this clinical benefit persists even among the challenging subgroup of patients previously exposed to ICIs. The safety profiles of both regimens were manageable and consistent with established toxicity spectra, with no new or unexpected safety signals identified.

For patients with R/M-NPC that has progressed after first-line platinum-based chemotherapy, treatment options are limited. PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy has demonstrated modest activity in this setting, with reported ORRs ranging from 20.5% to 34.1% in clinical trials.3–5 As a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting VEGFR, FGFR, and PDGFR, anlotinib has demonstrated anti-tumor activity across various solid tumors. In R/M-NPC, data from monotherapy studies support its therapeutic potential in this setting. A multicenter retrospective analysis of 30 patients with RM-NPC reported an ORR of 13.3% and a DCR of 73.3% with anlotinib monotherapy, along with a median PFS of 5.7 months and median OS of 11.5 months. Most patients were platinum-refractory, and 73.3% received at least three cycles of treatment.17 Similarly, a single-arm Phase 2 trial involving 39 heavily pretreated RM-NPC patients showed an ORR of 20.5%, a DCR of 71.8%, a median PFS of 5.7 months, and a 12-month OS rate of 58.3% with a generally manageable safety profile.13 Increasing evidence has confirmed that anti-angiogenic therapy plays an immunomodulatory role in the tumor microenvironment, which seems to enhance the efficacy of PD-1 blockades by increasing the infiltration of immune effector cells into tumors and normalizing abnormal tumor vessels.14,15,18 Preclinical evidence supports the synergistic effect of anlotinib with immune checkpoint inhibitors.19 The improvement in DCR from 71.8% with monotherapy to 85.0% with combination therapy underscores the role of dual-pathway inhibition in achieving durable disease control in heavily pre-treated patients.16 However, comparative data from controlled trials are lacking.

To our knowledge, this is the largest real-world studies to directly compare anlotinib monotherapy with anlotinib plus PD-1 blockade in R/M-NPC. The efficacy of anlotinib monotherapy observed here (ORR 7.55%, median PFS 5.13 months) aligns with prior data.8,17 The somewhat lower ORR in our monotherapy cohort may reflect a more heavily pretreated population, including a higher proportion of patients with extensive metastatic disease and prior ICI exposure. Importantly, our results reveal a substantial enhancement in efficacy when anlotinib is combined with a PD-1 inhibitor, achieving an ORR of 30.30% and median PFS of 8.93 months. These outcomes are highly encouraging and consistent with the phase II TORAL trial.16 The convergence of real-world and prospective trial data reinforces the validity of this combination approach.

The efficacy observed in this study appears favorable compared to other studies investigating PD-1 inhibitors combined with antiangiogenic agents for R/M-NPC. Notably, the regimen of camrelizumab plus apatinib demonstrated an ORR of 65.5% and median PFS of 10.4 months in a cohort consisting exclusively of immunotherapy‑naïve patients, which also featured a greater proportion of individuals with isolated locoregional recurrence and only one prior line of therapy.20 However, in a more challenging population of patients who had failed prior PD-1 inhibitor-containing therapy, a separate study by the same group investigating camrelizumab plus famitinib (another multi-targeted TKI) showed a more modest but still meaningful ORR of 33.3% and median PFS of 7.2 months.21 Furthermore, the combination of sintilimab and bevacizumab achieved an ORR of 54.5% in platinum-pretreated, immunotherapy-naïve metastatic NPC.22

Our real-world approach offers distinct advantages over traditional trial frameworks in evaluating R/M NPC treatments. Unlike the highly selective populations of randomized controlled trials, our cohort reflects the full heterogeneity of clinical practice, including patients with varied prior therapies, comorbidities, and performance status. Xu et al23 have shown that NPC trial enrollees are disproportionately weighted toward T4 and N2 categories, whereas real-world patients receive more diverse treatment delivery. Moreover, retrospective designs allow inclusion of heavily pretreated patients. Our cohort comprised 52.33% of patients treated in third‑line or later settings, populations that are typically underrepresented in prospective trials. Real‑world evidence thus complements trial efficacy data by demonstrating effectiveness in later‑line clinical settings, a distinction underscored by recent real‑world series. In a real-world retrospective study of 154 R/M NPC patients, Gao et al reported that the addition of apatinib or anlotinib to PD-1 inhibitor-based therapy significantly prolonged survival.24 Jiang et al, in a real-world study of 21 patients with R/M head and neck cancers (including 11 NPC cases), observed an objective response rate of 47.6% and a median progression-free survival of 14.3 months with anlotinib plus PD-1 blockade, suggesting the reversal of immunotherapy resistance.25 Collectively, these converging real-world data, together with ours, reinforce the therapeutic potential of anlotinib plus PD-1 blockade and underscore the value of real-world evidence in guiding clinical decisions for this challenging population.

These data highlight the influence of prior therapy, especially ICI exposure, on the efficacy of subsequent PD-1-based combination therapies. Although direct cross-trial comparisons are limited by differences in trial design, patient characteristics (notably prior ICI treatment status), and sample size, several key insights can be drawn. In patients previously treated with ICIs, the efficacy of anlotinib combined with PD-1 inhibitor (ORR approximately 30–38%, PFS approximately 9 months) appears comparable to that of camrelizumab plus famitinib. This suggests a class effect whereby combining a PD-1 inhibitor with TKI targeting the VEGF pathway can rescue or overcome resistance in a subset of patients even after prior immunotherapy failure. The underlying mechanism likely involves the ability of antiangiogenic agents to remodel the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, by promoting vascular normalization, enhancing effector T-cell infiltration, and reducing immunosuppressive cell populations, thereby resensitizing tumors to PD-1 blockade.26,27 Secondly, the superior efficacy of camrelizumab plus apatinib in ICI-naïve patients (ORR 65.5%, PFS 10.4 months)20 highlights that this synergistic effect is most potent in an immunotherapy-sensitive context. Consequently, the efficacy observed in our study with the anlotinib-based combination, which included both ICI-naïve and ICI-pretreated patients, likely represents a composite of these two scenarios. Future randomized controlled trials are warranted to definitively assess the relative efficacy of anlotinib plus PD-1 blockade versus other antiangiogenic-immunotherapy combinations in recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

The safety profile observed in our study aligns with the known toxicities of both anlotinib and PD-1 inhibitors, and is comparable to that reported in the TORAL trial16 for the same combination. The most common treatment-related adverse events included hand-foot syndrome, oral mucositis, hypertension, and hypothyroidism, which were predominantly low-grade and manageable with supportive measures and dose adjustments. No new safety signals or treatment-related deaths were identified. This favorable tolerability profile supports the clinical feasibility of administering anlotinib in combination with PD-1 blockade in routine practice, although vigilant monitoring for dermatological, mucosal, and metabolic toxicities remains necessary.

Our study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, its retrospective design may introduce selection bias and unmeasured confounding factors. Notably, baseline imbalances in BMI, metastatic burden, and prior radiotherapy existed between groups, which may bias observed outcomes in favor of the combination therapy. These imbalances remain a major source of confounding despite our multivariate adjustments. Second, the sample size, though reflective of real-world experience, remains modest, which limits the statistical power for subgroup analyses and multivariate modeling. Third, the absence of biomarker data, such as PD-L1 expression, tumor mutational burden, or dynamic plasma biomarkers like circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), limits our ability to identify predictive factors for treatment response. Future prospective studies incorporating such biomarkers, as highlighted in the TORAL trial where ctDNA dynamics were associated with survival outcomes, could help refine patient selection and enable earlier detection of disease progression. Given the inherent limitations of retrospective analyses, prospective randomized controlled trials are urgently needed to validate these findings and to definitively establish the efficacy and safety of this combination in this patient population.

Conclusion

This real-world analysis demonstrates that the combination of anlotinib and PD-1 blockade is associated with significantly improved response rates and disease control and survival compared to anlotinib monotherapy in patients with R/M-NPC after platinum-based therapy failure, even among those with prior immunotherapy exposure. The regimen exhibits a manageable safety profile consistent with existing clinical evidence. These findings support further evaluation of this combination as a viable treatment option in this challenging patient population. Mechanistically, anlotinib appears to overcome resistance by remodeling the tumor microenvironment to enhance immune effector infiltration and relieve immunosuppression. While the favorable trends observed in the immunotherapy-pretreated subgroup warrant cautious interpretation and require validation in larger prospective cohorts, our findings support further evaluation of this combination against other regimens and highlight the importance of biomarker-guided strategies to optimize patient selection.

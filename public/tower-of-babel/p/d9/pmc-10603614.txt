Assessment of a feed additive consisting of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 19455 for all animal species for the renewal of its authorisation (Biomin GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Vasileios Bampidis, Giovanna Azimonti, Maria de Lourdes Bastos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Secundino López Puente, Francesca Marcon, Baltasar Mayo, Alena Pechová, Mariana Petkova, Fernando Ramos, Roberto Edoardo Villa, Ruud Woutersen, Natalia Alija‐Novo, Montserrat Anguita, Jaume Galobart, Yolanda García‐Cazorla, Matteo Innocenti, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Rosella Brozzi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 19455 as a technological feed additive for use in easy and moderately difficult to ensile fresh material for all animal species. The Panel concluded that L. buchneri DSM 19455 remains safe for all animal species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use. Regarding user safety, L. buchneri DSM 19455 was considered to be a respiratory sensitiser. The additive with inulin as a carrier tested is not irritant to skin and eyes. The Panel was not in the position to conclude on the skin sensitisation potential of the additive. There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

Introduction

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest one year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from Biomin GmbH 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of Lentilactobacillus buchneri 3 DSM 19455, when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: technological additive; functional group: silage additive).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 18 August 2022 and the general information and supporting documentation is available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA-Q-2022-00524. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 31 January 2023.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 19455, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3 ).

Additional information

The additive is authorised as a preparation of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 19455.

EFSA issued one opinion on the safety and efficacy of this product for use in easy and moderately difficult to ensile fresh material for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013).

The additive is currently authorised in the EU for use in feed for all animal species (1 k20742). 4

Data and methodologies

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 5 in support of the authorisation request for the use of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 19455 as a feed additive.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 6 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 7 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, 7 EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 12 June to 3 July 2023 for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 31 January 2023 to 30 April 2023 for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other scientific reports to deliver the present output.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of the active agent in animal feed are valid and applicable for the current application. 8

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 19455 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021) and the EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain.

Assessment

The product consisting of viable cells of L. buchneri (previously identified as Lactobacillus kefiri) DSM 19455 is currently authorised as a technological additive (functional group: silage additive) for use in easy and moderately difficult to ensile fresh material for all animal species. This assessment regards the renewal of the authorisation of the above‐mentioned additive.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the additive

The additive is currently authorised as a preparation of L. buchneri DSM 19455 at a minimum concentration of 1 × 1010 colony forming units (CFU)/g. The applicant declared that the fermentation process has been slightly changed to optimise the process and the composition ■■■■■ 10 ■■■■■ The freeze‐dried cells concentrate is prepared with the use of cryoprotectants (e.g. inulin, up to 75.7%) and then it is blended with carriers (e.g., inulin, 50–85%) to prepare the final commercial product. 11

The analysis of five recent batches of the additive with inulin as a carrier showed compliance with the specifications of the active agent (mean 1.48 × 1010 CFU/g and range 1.41 × 1010–1.59 × 1010 CFU/g). 12

Three batches of the additive were tested for arsenic (As), lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd) and mercury (Hg), aflatoxins (B1, B2, G1, G2), deoxynivalenol, zearalenone, ochratoxin A, fumonisins (B1 and B2), HT‐2 toxin and T‐2 toxin levels. The concentration of all the analytes fell below the limit of quantification (LOQ) or detection (LOD) of the analytical methods. 13

Specifications are set for coliforms (< 103 CFU/g), Escherichia coli (< 10 CFU/g), Enterobacteriaceae (< 103 CFU/g), Salmonella spp. (no detection in 25 g), yeasts and filamentous fungi (< 103 CFU/g). The analysis of three batches showed compliance with these specifications. 14

No new data were provided regarding the physico‐chemical properties of the additive. The Panel considers that the changes introduced in the manufacturing process and composition would not have a significant impact on the physico‐chemical properties of the additive, and therefore, the data described in the previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013) still apply.

Characterisation of the active agent

The active agent was isolated from sour cabbage, and it is deposited in the Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen with the accession number DSM 19455. 15 It has not been genetically modified.

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013), the active agent has been identified as Lactobacillus kefiri. In the new data set, the applicant indicated that the strain DSM 19455 has been reassigned to the species L. buchneri based on the whole genome sequence (WGS) analysis. The taxonomical identification of the strain DSM 19455 was confirmed by average nucleotide identity (ANI) analysis. The ANI value was 99.8% with the type strain L. buchneri ATCC 4005T and only 80.1% with the type strain L. kefiri DSM 20587T, confirming that the strain belongs to the L. buchneri species. The WGS data were also interrogated for mobile genetics elements and two plasmids were predicted. 16

The susceptibility of L. buchneri DSM 19455 to antimicrobials was tested using a broth microdilution method and including the battery of antimicrobials recommended by EFSA (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018). All the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were equal to or below the cut‐off values, except for chloramphenicol, which was one dilution higher. Exceeding the cut‐off by one dilution is considered within the variation of the method, and therefore the strain is considered to be susceptible to all the relevant antibiotics. 17

The WGS data of the strain, including plasmids, were interrogated for the presence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes against ■■■■■. 18 No hits of concern were identified.

Safety

The applicant declared that no incidents or safety issues have been documented or reported regarding the active agent since its first authorisation. 19

In its previous opinion, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that following the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to the safety assessment, the use of L. buchneri DSM 19455 in the production of silage was considered safe for the target species, consumers and the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013).

In the context of the current application, the identity of the strain as belonging to L. buchneri was confirmed, and evidence was provided that the strain does not show antimicrobial resistance to antibiotics of human and veterinary importance (EFSA, 2007; EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2023). Consequently, the conclusions previously reached are still valid, and the Panel considers that L. buchneri DSM 19455 remains safe for the target species, consumers and the environment.

The safety for the user L. buchneri DSM 19455 was evaluated by the FEEDAP Panel in a previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013). The Panel concluded that ‘although users at the farm level are exposed to the additive only for a short period of time when preparing the aqueous suspension, in the absence of data, its potential to be irritant and/or to act as skin/respiratory sensitiser cannot be excluded. The dustiness of the preparation tested (5% of inulin and 5% of soy peptone as cryoprotectants and 50–85% of inulin as carrier) indicated a potential for users to be exposed via inhalation. Given the proteinaceous nature of the active agent, the additive should be considered to have the potential to be a skin/respiratory sensitiser and treated accordingly’.

No specific studies investigating the effects of the additive on the respiratory system were submitted and no new data on dusting potential have been provided. Owing to the proteinaceous nature of the active agent, the additive should be considered a respiratory sensitiser.

To address the potential to be irritant to skin and eye, the applicant has provided two new in vitro studies. 20

The skin irritation potential was investigated in an in vitro study according to OECD Testing Guideline (TG) 439 in one batch of the additive with inulin as a carrier. The results of the study indicated that the product tested is considered as non‐irritant to the skin and can be classified according to UN GHS as ‘No Category’. 21

The eye irritation potential of the additive with inulin as a carrier was determined in an in vitro study according to OECD TG 437. Based on the results of the study the additive is considered as non‐irritant to eyes and classified according to UN GHS as ‘No Category’. 22

No data were provided on the skin sensitisation potential of the additive. However, the FEEDAP Panel notes that the OECD test guidelines available at present are designed to assess the skin sensitisation potential of chemical substances only and that currently no validated assays for assessing the sensitisation potential of microorganisms are available. Therefore, no conclusion can be drawn on the skin sensitisation potential of the additive. 23

Once an active agent has been authorised as a technological additive, different preparations can be placed on the market with reference to that authorisation. Consequently, not all preparations can be assessed for user safety. The Panel can only conclude on the product tested.

Conclusions on safety

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that there is no new evidence that would lead to reconsider the previous conclusions that the active agent is safe for the target species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use.

Given the proteinaceous nature of the active agent, the additive should be considered a respiratory sensitiser. The additive containing L. buchneri DSM 19455 and inulin as a carrier tested is not irritant to skin or eyes. The Panel is not in the position to conclude on the skin sensitisation potential of the additive.

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additive. Therefore, there is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

Conclusions

The applicant has provided evidence that the additive currently on the market complies with the existing terms of authorisation.

The Panel concludes that L. buchneri DSM 19455 remains safe for all animal species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use.

L. buchneri DSM 19455 should be considered a respiratory sensitiser. The additive with inulin as a carrier tested is not irritant to skin and eyes. The Panel is not in the position to conclude on the skin sensitisation potential of the additive.

There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

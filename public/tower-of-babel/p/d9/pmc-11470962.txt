Tripod-Fix device for the treatment of painful osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures
Authors: Hsiang-Yi Hung, Shin-Yuan Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCurrent vertebral augmentation procedures (VAPs) often involve devices associated with bone cement leakage. Tripod-Fix is designed to mitigate the risk of bone cement leakage by expanding in three dimensions to fit a narrower vertebral space. This study enrolled 12 patients diagnosed with osteoprorotic vertebral compression fractures (VCFs) for 12 month follow up. The primary outcomes assessed were changes in the Visual Analog Score (VAS) and Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) before and after treatment. Our results demonstrated significant pain relief with VAS decreasing from 8.58 ± 1.83 to 2.75 ± 1.54 cm and improved mobility with ODI decreasing from 73.67 ± 16.29 to 31.83 ± 23.33% post-treatment and sustained for 12 months. Follow-up radiographs revealed no device-related adverse events such as cement leakage, vertebral body collapse, or adjacent vertebral fractures (AVFs). In addition, the mean anterior height restoration ratio after treatment was 15.87 ± 5.13%. Our preliminary findings suggest that Tripod-Fix exhibits safety and efficacy comparable to the third-generation devices currently utilized for treating osteoporotic VCFs.Keywords: Kyphoplasty, Vertebroplasty, Vertebral compression fractures, OsteoporosisSubject terms: Clinical trial design, Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (VCFs) affect an estimated 700,000 individuals annually in the United States, often resulting in progressive muscle weakness, diminished stature, and kyphosis of the spine1,2. Patients frequently endure persistent pain lasting 4–6 weeks or longer, particularly with multiple levels of fractures3–5.

Vertebroplasty (VP) and balloon kyphoplasty (BKP) represent the principal vertebral augmentation procedures (VAPs) currently employed2. Initially introduced by Galibert et al. in 1987, VP was associated with a bone cement leakage of approximately 30%6. Subsequently, BKP was introduced by Reiley et al. in 19987–9. BKP offers the advantage of balloon inflation to create a greater volume, which can reduce the injection pressure and minimize the risk of bone cement leakage10,11. Nonetheless, balloon deflation during the procedure can lead to partial or complete vertebral body collapse12,13.

Third-generation devices such as the Vertebral Body Stenting System®(VBS), SpineJack® and OsseoFix were developed to address these concerns and improve clinical outcomes14–18. However, the risk of bone cement leakage remains high in osteoporotic patients, posing serious complications such as bone cement implantation syndrome (BCIS), radicular pain from cement leaks into the foramen or spinal canal, and cement embolism from cement escapingthe injured vertebral body19,20.

To address these challenges,, a novel device called Tripod-Fix has been developped. Tripod-Fix delivers cement precisely to the targeted locations, preventing leakage following VAPs. Equipped with three retractable arms, this device elevates the vertebral body and allows direct cement injection into the cavity. In addition, it allows precise filling of gaps between fractured fragments, thereby reducing the risk of cement entering the cortex. In this study, we prospectively treated patients with osteoporotic VCFs to further investigate the efficacy and durability of Tripod-Fix in VAPs.

Materials and methods

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Institutional Review Board of Hualien Tzu Chi Hospital (reference number: IRB108-89-A). A total of 12 patients with osteoporosis provided informed consent and were enrolled for VAPs using the Tripod-Fix (Wiltrom Co., Ltd., Taiwan). Patient recruitment and procedures were conducted between June 2019 and February 2021 at Hualien Tzu Chi Hospital, Taiwan.

Patient demographics, including age, sex, race, and comorbidities , were collected at Hualien Tzu Chi Hospital and are summarized in Table 1. The VAS and ODI were recorded preoperatively, immediately postoperatively (at three days), and during follow-up at 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-month intervals. Standard evaluations of potential intraoperative and postoperative risks associated with VAPs were conducted as a standard practice.

Tripod-Fix device

The Tripod-Fix device, made of titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4 V), was designed to restore vertebral height and alleviate pain in patients with VCFs. This device facilitates the mechanical expansion and stabilization of collapsed vertebrae, providing long-term mechanical strength to prevent post-VAP vertebral deformation (Fig. 1 & Supplementary movie 1). Additionally, the device allows precise injection of bone cement into the vertebral body to stabilize fractures. Furthermore, the titanium alloy material supports permanent implantation and direct contact with bone tissue. The Tripod-Fix device used in this study had a depth of 5.0 mm and a length of 25.0 mm. When fully expanded, the device can reach a maximum width of 15.0 mm at the tip. Depending on the surgical site and extent of the fracture, bilateral transpedicular placement of two Tripod-Fix implants was employed for restoration, typically spanning from the thoracic 6 (T6) to the lumbar 5 (L5) vertebral body.

Vertebral augmentation procedure

The procedure was performed under local anesthesia, epidural/spinal, or general anesthesia according to the surgeon’s preference. For the lower thoracic spine, a unilateral intercostovertebral approach was utilized, whereas a transpedicular approach was employed for the lumber spine. Bilateral access cannulas were carefully positioned anterior to the vertebral body’s posterior wall. A hand drill and reamer were used to create a channel through the cancellous bone for Tripod-Fix device insertion. The device arms were mechanically expended by rotating the exclusive instrument named Driver to correct kyphosis, with caution to avoid damaging the endplates or lateral walls. Bone cement was subsequently injected through a central access cannula within the implant.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted to evaluate the efficacy of the Tripod-Fix device in the treatment of painful osteoporotic VCFs. The primary focus was on the change in pain intensity as measured by the VAS and disability as measured by the ODI from pre-treatment to various post-treatment time points.

Descriptive statistics were calculated for all demographic and clinical characteristics. Continuous variables were summarized using means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), depending on their distribution. Categorical variables were summarized using counts and percentages. Since the sample size is small, prior to hypothesis testing, the normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For VAS score and ODI index, were analyzed using both parametric and non-parametric tests. For normally distributed data, paired t-tests were applied to compare pre-operative scores with scores at each follow-up time point. For data not following a normal distribution, the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test was used.

Results

The mean age of the patient cohort was 71.25 ± 6.47 years, with all participants of Asian descent. The study included 2 male and 10 female participants (Table 1). The sample size across first 5 time points is 12, however the sample size at 12-month post-surgery was 7 due to 5 missing values.

Postoperative radiographic assessments were focused on the change of the veterbral body height at anterior, middle, and posterior vertebral body before and after Tripod-Fix expansion (Fig. 2). The results revealed a notable increase in the height of the anterior (2.09 ± 0.85 mm) and middle (0.92 ± 0.35 mm) parts of the vertebral body, corresponding to height restoration ratios of 15.87 ± 5.13% and 7.09 ± 2.32% respectively (Table 1). Representative radiographic images showed the sustained outcome of height restoration with Tripod-Fix treatment throughout the 12-month follow-up period. There were no cases of adjacent vertebral fractures (AVFs) or vertebral body recollapse observed (Fig. 3).Preoperatively, the mean VAS pain score was 8.58 ± 1.83 cm, which significantly decreased to 2.75 ± 1.54 cm (− 5.83 ± 1.40 cm) postoperative. Subsequent follow-ups at 1 month demonstrated a further reduction to 1.25 ± 1.22 cm (− 7.33 ± 1.44 cm), and by 6 months, the VAS score reached 0.83 ± 0.94 cm (− 7.75 ± 1.82 cm), and by 12 months, 1.00 ± 1.00 cm (− 7.86 ± 0.90 cm) indicating substantial pain relief (Table 2, Fig. 4). The median VAS score before surgery was recorded at 9.5 (IQR = 3.0) and was obviously lower (median = 1.0, IQR = 2.0) at the 12-month post-surgery check. All reported p-values were highly significant, underlining the effectiveness of the intervention. Similarly, the ODI measured preoperatively was 73.67 ± 16.29%, improving to 31.83 ± 23.33% (− 41.83 ± 14.76%) postoperatively. At the 1-month follow-up, the ODI score decreased to 11.83 ± 12.28% (− 61.83 ± 13.44%), and at 6 months, it further decreased to 2.67 ± 4.12% (− 70.33 ± 14.89%), and at 12 months, it decreased to 3.43 ± 2.99% (− 72.29 ± 5.82%) demonstrating significant functional improvement (Table 2, Fig. 5). The median ODI score before surgery was recorded at 77.0 (IQR = 11.5) and was obviously lower (median = 4.0, IQR = 4.0) at the 12-month post-surgery check. All reported p-values were highly significant, underlining the effectiveness of the intervention. Statistical analyses were performed using R-4.3.3. No postoperative clinical complications related to the surgical procedures or Tripod-Fix were reported in any patient.

Case illustration of a surgical procedure using Tripod-Fix device

The surgical procedures using the Tripod-Fix device were performed on vertebral bodies spanning T10–L4. VAPs were performed under general anesthesia, with diligent monitoring of vital signs throughout the operation (Fig. 6A-H). Initially, the surgical site was meticulously prepared using a transpedicular approach with a trocar and guidewire (Fig. 6A and B). The trocar was inserted into the vertebral body through the pedicle at a downward angle of 10° relative to the endplate’s parallel line, reaching approximately half the depth of the vertebral body. Next, a drill was used to prepare a channel for the implant, which was subsequently widened to the desired diameter using a reamer (Fig. 6C and D). The Tripod-Fix device was then carefully inserted into the channel using a driver, precisely positioning it within the vertebral body to target the fracture area. The device was then mechanically expanded, remaining securely within the vertebral body cavity created during drilling (Fig. 6E and F). The expansion of the Tripod-Fix arms exerted outward pressure on the trabecular bone, compacting it and thereby increasing the vertebral body’s height while reducing deformity. Bone cement was injected into the expanded device. The device’s inward grooves facilitated precise cement injection between the arms (Fig. 6G and H), with the amount of cement tailored to each patient’s specific fracture and vertebral body dimensions.

Discussion

Tripod-Fix, a novel titanium-based implant with a triangular pyramid-like design, expands three-dimensionally to enhance the stability of the cement against both vertical and horizontal forces. Our study demonstrated that Tripod-Fix effectively alleviated pain (VAS decreased from 8.58 ± 1.83 to 1.00 ± 1.00 cm) and improved patient mobility (ODI decreased from 73.67 ± 16.29 to 3.43 ± 2.99%). Post-treatment imaging revealed superior vertebral body height restoration at the anterior (15.87 ± 5.13%) and middle (7.09 ± 2.32%) portions compared with SpineJack®21. Importantly, there were no device-related adverse events, such as cement leakage, vertebral body collapse, or AVFs.

Studies have highlighted that AVF recurrence rates at 1 year post-VP/BKP range from 6.5% to 7.4%, with factors like bone mineral density and cement leakage being significant risk factors22,23. Tripod-Fix addresses these concerns by enabling precise cement delivery, minimizing the risk of excessive cement injection and subsequent complications24,25.

Noriega et al. highlighted the advantages of titanium-based vertebral augmentation devices over traditional methods for pain relief26. Comparing our results, the postoperative VAS score for pain in our patients (2.75 ± 1.54 cm) aligns well with previous reports by Lin et al. who compared the BKP and VP outcomes. Notably, our study demonstrated a greater reduction (− 5.83 ± 1.40 cm) in VAS score compared to Lin et al. (− 4.1 cm), suggesting that Tripod-Fix may offer enhanced postoperative pain relief25. Additionally, the ODI improvement (− 41.83 ± 14.76%) with Tripod-Fix is comparable to the outcomes seen with SpineJack® (− 37.7 ± 18.5%)26. However, the recent use of intravertebral reduction devices has shown greater height restoration than BKP, which warrants consideration27,28. Nevertheless, the 3D expansion of Tripod-Fix, coupled with its ability to prevent cement leakage, may provide more long-term benefits to patients. Noriega D. et. al, investigated the efficacy and safety of a titanium implantable vertebral augmentation device (SpineJack®) compared to BKP in treating VCFs with total sample size is 141. Specifically, the VAS scores, which measure the intensity of pain, were significantly lower for the SpineJack® group one month and six months after the surgery. The ODI also have a downward trend. This indicates that SpineJack® was more effective in reducing pain associated with VCFs in the short to medium term26. Noriega D. et. al, also investigated the clinical efficacy and safety of SpineJack® and Kyphx Xpander® balloon kyphoplasty in treating osteoporotic VCFs. The study included 30 patients and demonstrated significant improvements in pain reduction for both treatment groups over 12 months. Patients treated with SpineJack® experienced a strong, rapid, and lasting reduction in pain intensity, achieving a 94% reduction compared to 82% in the Kyphx Xpander® group. Functional disability improvements were also noted, with SpineJack® showing a 94% reduction in ODI scores at 12 months, indicating a significant improvement in disability levels over time compared to the Kyphx Xpander® group, which showed a 90% reduction29. In comparison of the studies with SpineJack®, our results of Tripod-Fix showed similar trend of reduction in VAS score and ODI at 12 months (Table 3).

In this study, the precise control over cement deposition with Tripod-Fix (4.39 ± 0.79 cc) is noteworthy because it allows for tailored cement delivery compared with SpineJack® (4.1 ± 1.7 cc)25,29. The operation time for the Tripod-Fix implantaiton and cement deposit (40.33 ± 4.79 min) were comparable to those reported for SpineJack® by Noriega et al.15,26,29 We anticipate that future advancements in Tripod-Fix procedures will further reduce operation times without compromising safety or efficacy.

Our study had limitations, including the lack of long-term follow-ups beyond 1 year, given the reluctance of elderly patients to return for extended assessments. However, it remains imperative to evaluate the Tripod-Fix efficacy across short-term (3 months), mid-term (6 months), and long-term (1 year) follow-up periods. To monitor device performance and assess VCF recurrence. Furthermore, the small sample size without control group and specific patient demographics (predominantly female and Asian) in this preliminary study represent additional limitations. Although 77% of nontraumatic fractures occur in women, conducting a preliminary evaluation with this patient cohort was essential. Moving forward, recruitment of a more diverse patient population with VCFs is crucial to validate the effectiveness and safety of Tripod-Fix across broader demographics.

Conclusions

In conclusion, Tripod-Fix is an effective and low-risk treatment for osteoporotic VCFs. It not only provides significant pain relief and improves patient mobility but also demonstrates superior vertebral body height restoration, particularly in patients with wedge fractures. Notably, Tripod-Fix effectively minimizes complications such as AVFs and cement leakage. By offering precise control over the amount of cement deposited and exhibiting operation times comparable to those of other devices, Tripod-Fix represents a promising solution for treating painful osteoporotic VCFs, thereby contributing to rapid and sustained improvement in patients’ quality of life. Further research and long-term follow-up studies involving a more extensive and diverse patient population are essential to fully validate the efficacy and safety profile of Tripod-Fix. This will enhance our understanding of its broader clinical utility and optimize patient outcomes in the management of osteoporotic VCFs.

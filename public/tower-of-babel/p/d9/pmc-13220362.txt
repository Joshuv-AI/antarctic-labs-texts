Efficacy of osimertinib combined with anti-VEGF therapy ramucirumab in managing leptomeningeal metastasis in EGFR-mutant NSCLC: a case report and literature review
Authors: Yueh-Hsun Tsai, Yu-Shiou Weng, Tsai-Wang Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLeptomeningeal metastasis (LM) is a severe complication of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), associated with limited treatment options and poor prognosis. Osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), has demonstrated central nervous system (CNS) efficacy, while ramucirumab, a VEGFR-2 inhibitor, may enhance drug penetration and therapeutic response. The combination has not been reported in LM.Case presentationWe report two patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC who developed LM and received osimertinib (80 mg/day) plus ramucirumab (10 mg/kg every 3 weeks). A 79-year-old man with an EGFR exon 21 L858R mutation presented with progressive neurological symptoms and MRI-confirmed LM. Combination therapy led to marked clinical improvement, resolution of leptomeningeal enhancement, and stable disease for over 12 months, with only mild hypertension and rash. A 74-year-old woman with an EGFR exon 19 deletion and LM involving the brainstem also demonstrated significant neurological recovery, including regaining oral intake, radiologic regression of LM lesions, and durable control for more than one year. Adverse events were limited to mild dermatologic toxicity.ConclusionsAlthough osimertinib has established activity in LM, outcomes remain suboptimal. Data from systemic disease suggest that combining EGFR and VEGF inhibition may further enhance efficacy, but LM-specific evidence has been lacking. These two cases highlight the potential of osimertinib plus ramucirumab as a safe and effective therapeutic approach in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with LM, providing durable neurologic and radiologic responses. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and better define the role of this regimen in LM management.Keywords: Osimertinib, Ramucirumab, Leptomeningeal metastasis, Targeted therapy

Background

Leptomeningeal metastasis (LM) is a serious complication that occurs in 3–5% of patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [1], particularly those with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations. The presence of LM indicates advanced disease and is associated with poor prognosis, which is much worse than brain parenchymal metastasis, often resulting in significant neurological symptoms and a decline in quality of life [2]. Traditional treatment options for LM, such as systemic chemotherapy and radiation therapy, have shown limited efficacy, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic strategies. Recent advancements in targeted therapies have transformed the management of EGFR-mutant NSCLC.

Osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), has demonstrated superior efficacy compared to standard EGFR-TKIs in patients with advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC, particularly in those with central nervous system (CNS) involvement [3]. In addition to Osimertinib, other agents such as ramucirumab, an anti-VEGF receptor antibody, have shown potential in enhancing the efficacy of targeted therapies. Ramucirumab’s mechanism of action, which involves inhibiting angiogenesis and potentially improving drug delivery to the CNS. Given the limited treatment options and the challenging nature of LM in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, the combination of Osimertinib and Ramucirumab presents a novel therapeutic strategy that warrants further investigation. This case report aims to share our experiences of the combination therapy for patients suffering from leptomeningeal metastasis in the context of EGFR-mutant NSCLC.

Here we presented two cases of leptomeningeal metastasis of EGFR-mutant NSCLC treating with Osimertinib and Ramucirumab.

Case presentation

Case 1

The patient is a 79 years old male with history of adenocarcinoma of prostate, pT3bN0M0, Stage IIIB and adenocarcinoma of right upper lobe, pT1bN2M0, stage IIIA, EGFR Exon21 L858R status post video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery with right upper lobe lobectomy on 2020 and adjuvant chemotherapy. He had regular follow-up visits at the chest surgery outpatient department (OPD) for lung cancer monitoring. After 3 years follow up at OPD, the patient reported headaches, insomnia, nausea, and vomiting, along with an unsteady gait lasting six months (Table 1). At the time of neurologic symptom onset, systemic evaluation was performed to assess the extracranial disease burden. Chest and abdominal imaging confirmed no evidence of recurrence or systemic metastasis of the lung adenocarcinoma. Furthermore, his prostate cancer remained in stable remission under regular monitoring. Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed. MRI revealed multifocal leptomeningeal enhancement involving bilateral cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres as well as surrounding the brainstem (Fig. 1). Then, Osimertinib (80 mg/day) combined with ramucirumab (10 mg/kg, every 3 weeks) were administered to the patient. His neurologic symptoms significantly improved, and the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score improved from 3 to 1. The reexamination results of brain MRI showed resolution of leptomeningeal enhancement after five months of treatment (Fig. 2). The combination therapy has continued for one year, with the patient remaining in stable condition under close follow-up. The regimen was well tolerated, with grade 1 adverse events of mild hypertension and skin rash.

Case 2

A 74 years old female with history of papillary microcarcinoma of left thyroid, ypT1aN0M0, stage I status post left thyroid lobectomy (2015) and I-131 remnant ablation therapy (2018). The patient suffered from poor intake with body weight loss for about 10 kg (53 kg -> 43 kg) in two months and general weakness and unsteady gait. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the chest found a 2.2 cm solid pulmonary nodule over right middle lobe. Whole body positron emission tomography (PET) scan revealed one dominant FDG-avid soft tissue (SUVmax:7.2) lesion at right middle lobe of lung without other significant uptakes. Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery with lobectomy of right middle lung was performed. Pathology reported poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma of right middle lobe of lung, pT2N0 (Table 1). Brain MRI revealed leptomeningeal carcinomatosis along the midbrain, pons and medulla oblongata, communicating hydrocephalus, with subependymal edema, suspicion of CSF cancerous spreading and brain metastasis over the right temporal lobe (Fig. 3). CSF cytology was negative for malignant cell. However, LM was confirmed based on brain MRI imaging after multi-disciplinary team discussion. Next-generation sequencing of the primary tumor specimen revealed an EGFR mutation (exon 19 deletion). Osimertinib (80 mg/day) combined with ramucirumab (10 mg/kg, every 3 weeks) were administered to the patient. The patient showed significant improvement throughout the treatment. Initially, she required tube feeding through a nasogastric (NG) tube due to general weakness, but after four months, she was able to eat on her own, allowing for successful removal of the NG tube. The ECOG score improved from 4 to 2. Repeated brain MRI showed regression of the lesions in the midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata, and subependymal edema, as well as the right temporal lesio. (Figures 4 and 5). The combination therapy with osimertinib and ramucirumab lasted for 10 months. Due to significant improvement in neurologic symptoms and economic considerations, the patient transitioned to osimertinib monotherapy (80 mg/day). The therapeutic effect has persisted for over 12 months, and the patient remains under close follow-up. The side effects during the combine therapy were grade I skin rash and dry skin.

Discussion and conclusions

LM represents a formidable complication in patients with advanced EGFR-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), typically associated with a poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options.

A previous study demonstrated that NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations and LM who received TKI therapy had a significantly longer overall survival (OS) compared to those who did not (10.0 months vs. 3.3 months, p < 0.001) [4]. Osimertinib has demonstrated superior efficacy in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with LM compared to earlier-generation TKIs. In the AURA LM analysis, which included patients with EGFR T790M-positive NSCLC and radiologically detected, asymptomatic or stable LM, osimertinib 80 mg daily achieved a median LM progression-free survival (PFS) of 11.1 months and median OS of 18.8 months [5] The BLOOM study, which enrolled patients with cytologically confirmed, often symptomatic or progressive LM after prior EGFR-TKI therapy, evaluated osimertinib at 160 mg daily and reported a median PFS of 8.6 months and median OS of 11.0 months [6]. These studies confirm the CNS activity and clinical benefit of osimertinib in LM, but also highlight the persistent need for improved outcomes in this high-risk population.

Combination strategies have emerged as a promising approach to further enhance disease control. A phase 2 prospective clinical trial also reported an LM objective response rate of 50% and LM PFS of 9.3 months with osimertinib plus bevacizumab [7]. A retrospective study by Yi et al. evaluated 27 LM patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC, comparing osimertinib plus bevacizumab (n = 16) versus osimertinib alone (n = 11). The combination achieved significantly improved median OS (18.0 vs. 13.7 months, p = 0.046) and median intracranial PFS (10.6 vs. 5.5 months, p = 0.037). Preclinical studies demonstrated that the combination increased intracranial osimertinib concentration and modulated E-cadherin levels, suggesting synergistic blood-brain barrier penetration [8].

While bevacizumab has shown promise in LM, ramucirumab offers broader VEGF pathway inhibition by selectively targeting VEGFR2, blocking VEGFA, VEGFC, and VEGFD ligands, potentially providing more comprehensive anti-angiogenic effects than bevacizumab’s VEGFA-only inhibition [9]. The RAMOSE trial, a multicenter randomized phase II study, demonstrated that the addition of ramucirumab to osimertinib significantly prolonged median PFS to 24.8 months compared to 15.6 months with osimertinib alone in TKI-naïve EGFR-mutant metastatic NSCLC, with benefit observed across EGFR mutation subtypes and CNS metastasis status [10]. While LM-specific outcomes were not separately reported, the findings support the hypothesis that dual EGFR and VEGF pathway inhibition may further delay disease progression in CNS-involved disease.

It is prudent to acknowledge the multiple new treatment options for EGFR-mutated NSCLC, including osimertinib plus platinum-based chemotherapy (as in FLAURA2 trial) [11] and amivantamab plus lazertinib (as in MARIPOSA trial) [12]. Those treatments have demonstrated improved progression-free survival, particularly in patients with CNS metastases at baseline. MARIPOSA and FLAURA2 included patients with brain metastases, but not those with active or untreated LM; thus, efficacy data for LM specifically are lacking. Intracranial outcomes were robustly assessed for brain metastases, showing durable CNS control, but LM-specific inclusion and results were not reported. There is a planned and ongoing trial for intrathecal amivantamab in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with LM, but published results are not yet available.

There are recent prospective and retrospective studies regarding intrathecal pemetrexed, show that it can provide symptom relief and disease control in TKI-refractory LM, with median overall survival of 9–12 months and manageable toxicity, but the benefit is modest and not superior to EGFR-TKI-based regimens [13–15].

No studies have specifically evaluated the combination of osimertinib and ramucirumab in NSCLC patients with LM. Our study utilized a combination of osimertinib and ramucirumab as first-line treatment in two EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients with LM. Both patients showed a favorable response in LM lesions and neurological symptoms, (Table 2) achieving a progression-free survival (PFS) of nearly one year, with ongoing follow-up. These cases highlight the value of osimertinib and ramucirumab as treatment options for patients with LM harboring EGFR mutations, both Exon 21 L858R and Exon 19 deletion, demonstrating effectiveness with limited side effects.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates the favorable safety and effectiveness of the combination therapy of osimertinib and ramucirumab, suggesting its potential as a promising treatment option. Further research with larger sample sizes is warranted to confirm these findings and better establish its clinical benefits.

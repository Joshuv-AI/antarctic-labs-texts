Enhancing referrals and utilization of the Healthy Lifestyle clinic: a pre-and post intervention study in primary health care
Authors: Sarah Musa, Sami Abdeen, Vahe Kehyayan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHealthy Lifestyle clinics in Qatar aim to reduce non-communicable diseases through preventive interventions. Despite this, low referral rates and underutilization limit their effectiveness.ObjectiveTo assess the impact of a structured referral awareness intervention on increasing referrals and clinic utilization.MethodsA pre–post quality improvement study was conducted at Healthy Lifestyle clinic in Rawdat Al-Khail Health Center, Qatar (July 2024–May 2025). Digital and printed educational materials were deployed across ten Primary Health Care Centers. Referral data and clinic utilization rates were analyzed using Poisson regression and Mann–Whitney U tests.ResultsNew referrals rose from 25 to 66 post-intervention (2.64-fold increase; IRR: 1.69; p = 0.033). Clinic utilization improved from a median of 31% to 54.5% (p = 0.016). However, appropriate referrals declined, and only 40% included completed risk documentation.ConclusionLow-cost, structured interventions can enhance service utilization. Ongoing quality monitoring and provider training are needed to improve referral accuracy and impact.Keywords: Healthy Lifestyle clinic, Referral pathways, Clinic utilization, Non-communicable diseases, Quality improvement, Qatar, Preventive health, PHCC

Background

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) such as cardiovascular diseases, metabolic disorders and chronic respiratory diseases, account for over 70% of global deaths, according to the World Health Organization [1]. Among the key drivers of this burden is obesity, which has nearly tripled worldwide since 1975. Obesity significantly increases the risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and several cancers, contributing to disability and premature mortality [2].

In Qatar, the burden of obesity and NCDs is particularly alarming. National data indicate that more than 70% of adults are overweight or obese [3]. The prevalence of metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular conditions is on the rise, placing pressure on healthcare resources and necessitating a strong preventive approach. These chronic diseases are primarily associated with modifiable lifestyle factors such as poor dietary habits, physical inactivity, and tobacco use [3, 4].

Adopting and promoting a healthy lifestyle has emerged as a cornerstone of preventive public health strategies. Structured lifestyle interventions have been shown to significantly reduce the risk of chronic disease, decrease healthcare expenditure, and enhance quality of life [5]. In response, the Primary Health Care Corporation (PHCC) in Qatar established Wellness Centers, Wellness Educator and Healthy Lifestyle (HLS) clinics to deliver evidence-based lifestyle and behavioral interventions to at-risk individuals through multidisciplinary teams including physicians, Wellness Educators, exercise physiologist and fitness instructors [6].

A referral is defined as the process by which a healthcare provider directs a patient to a specialized service for additional or targeted care [7]. In PHCC, physicians are required to complete the risk assessment section within the “Exercise Wellness Gym Assessment” form before initiating a referral to the HLS clinic [6]. Patients are screened for risk level based on three elements: the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) risk classification; and/or the presence of signs and symptoms of cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, or metabolic disease; and/or known diagnosis of these conditions. Patients are then categorized into one of three risk levels: (1) mild-risk, who are referred directly to the Wellness Center; (2) moderate-risk, who require assessment and confirmation by the Wellness Champion before referral; and (3) high-risk, who are referred to the HLS clinic. Despite established guidelines and training efforts, compliance with documentation and referral pathway is suboptimal.

Healthcare service utilization refers to the extent to which individuals or populations make use of healthcare services for purposes such as health promotion, disease prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and monitoring of health status [8]. Referral rates and utilization are influenced by a variety of factors including provider awareness, service availability, accessibility, patient compliance, and clarity of clinical workflow [9]. Underutilization may reflect barriers such as low provider awareness, workflow inefficiencies, misidentification of eligible patients, or limited health literacy [10].

Optimizing referral accuracy and service utilization contributes to more efficient healthcare delivery. Appropriate and timely referrals enable early disease management, reduce complications, and lower overall healthcare costs [11]. Conversely, failure to refer appropriately can lead to underuse of valuable resources or overburdening of services without clear clinical benefit.

Several strategies have been identified to improve referrals and utilization, including staff training, dissemination of referral protocols, feedback loops, and patient engagement campaigns [12]. Structured referral processes that incorporate standardized referral forms, eligibility and diagnostic checklist, and risk scoring systems, alongside validated assessment tools, have shown to improve referral quality and appropriateness [13].

Considering these global and local priorities, this study was developed to address gaps in referral practices and enhance the utilization of HLS clinic at Rawdat Al-Khail Health Center. Specifically, the study focuses on increasing both the number and appropriateness of referrals originating from ten PHCC health centers to RAK HC. By strengthening referral pathways, the intervention aims to improve early preventive care delivery and promote better health outcomes in alignment with Qatar’s National Health Strategy and Qatar vision 2030. RAK HC serves as the referral hub for ten PHCC health centers covering the Central Region and findings from this study are intended to inform the planned expansion of the referral-awareness strategy to the Northern and Western Regions, supporting system-wide improvement across the corporation.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to assess the impact of a structured referral awareness intervention, delivered through digital and printed educational posters, on the rate and appropriateness of referrals to the HLS clinic and the subsequent clinic utilization at RAK HC.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This is a pre-post interventional quality improvement study conducted at the HLS clinic within RAK HC, operated under the Primary Health Care Corporation in Qatar. The PHCC is administratively divided into three regions: Western, Northern, and Central, collectively serving the population through a total of 31 health centers.

The Wellness Services program is delivered through seven dedicated Wellness Centers. Within the Central Region, there are 10 health centers, all of which refer patients to the HLS clinic at RAK HC, which functions as a central referral hub for lifestyle and behavioral risk interventions.

Study population

The study population consisted of all adult patients (aged 18 years and older) referred to the HLS clinic at RAK HC from the ten eligible health centers located at the Central Region. These patients were identified as having lifestyle or behavioral risk factors or non-communicable diseases requiring multidisciplinary management at the primary, secondary or tertiary prevention levels.

Data collection

Referral data was retrospectively retrieved from electronic health records through the PHCC Wellness Dashboard and Clinical Information System (CIS). All referrals to the HLS clinic located at RAK HC were extracted from 10 eligible health centers: Airport, Rawdat Al Khail, Umm Ghuwailaina, Omar Bin Khattab, Al Wakrah, South Wakrah, Al Thumama, Al Meshaf, Al Sadd, and Umm Al Seneem.

Referral appropriateness was evaluated based on risk level categorization. An ‘appropriate referral’ was defined according to the established PHCC wellness framework and the “Exercise Wellness Gym Assessment” form. Physicians are required to complete the risk assessment section of the form prior to referral, including the ACSM risk criteria, the presence of signs or symptoms of cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, or metabolic disease, and known diagnosis of these conditions. A patient is classified as high-risk if any of these criteria or symptoms are present; in such cases, referral to the HLS clinic is indicated. An ‘inappropriate referral’ was defined as: (1) a patient who did not meet high-risk criteria (i.e., mild or moderate risk) but was referred to the HLS clinic; (2) a patient who did meet high-risk criteria but was instead referred directly to the wellness gym services rather than to the HLS clinic or (3) any referral submitted without the required completion of the form and no documented risk level.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). Descriptive statistics were presented as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, while means and medians were used to summarize numerical variables. To assess differences in the average weekly number of new referrals between the pre- and post-intervention phases, a Poisson regression model was applied. Incidence Rate Ratios (IRRs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated to quantify the strength of association. Additionally, the Mann–Whitney U test was used to evaluate differences in clinic utilization rates pre and post the intervention, given the non-parametric nature of the data. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Descriptive analysis of referrals

During the study period from July 1, 2024, to May 31, 2025, a total of 374 referrals were recorded at HLS clinic in RAK HC. Of these, 239 were internal follow-up referrals generated within the clinic, while 135 were new referrals originating from the ten eligible HCs. After excluding 20 duplicate or invalid entries (e.g., repeated referrals for the same patient of canceled referrals), 115 valid new referrals were included in the final analysis. The three centers with the highest total number of referrals across the study period were Umm Ghuwalina (33 referrals), Al Thumama (26 referrals), and Rawdat Al Khail (18 referrals).

Appropriateness of referrals

The appropriateness of referrals, determined by documented risk levels based on the ACSM guidelines and their alignment with proper referral pathways, showed a downward trend over the study period. The percentage dropped from 24.0% before the intervention, to 20.8% during, and further declined to 15.2% after the intervention.

Of the 115 referrals, only 46 (40%) had a completed risk assessment within the “Exercise Wellness Gym Assessment” form. Among those, only 21 (18.3%) of referrals were computed as “high-risk” and deemed appropriate throughout the study period. (Fig. 1)

Discussion

This pre–post intervention evaluation demonstrated that a low‑cost, structured referral awareness campaign, using both digital and printed posters, significantly increased referral rates and utilization of Healthy Lifestyle clinic across PHCC central region in Qatar. These findings affirm the value of provider-focused communication strategies in enhancing the uptake of preventive health services.

In line with prior literature [14], our results confirmed that when printed educational materials (PEMs) are integrated into multifaced interventions, they become significantly more effective. Giguère A. et al. [14] concluded in their systematic review that PEMs such as posters and guidelines may improve provider behavior and clinical practice, however, their impact on patient outcomes remain uncertain.

Despite the overall improvements observed, referral rates varied considerably across individual centers. This variation is consistent with prior literature emphasizing clinic-level differences such as workflow integration, provider attitudes, degree of staff turnover, patients’ demographic and behavioral characteristics, and resource availability, are key determinants of referral behavior [15]. In our study, this variability may also be explained by differences in whether the health center is newly established or long-standing, the availability and number of wellness champions, service capacity, and patient load. In high-volume settings, physicians may have limited time to complete comprehensive risk assessment, which could affect referral quality. Conducting qualitative studies at each site could help identify these local barriers and facilitators.

While referral volume improved, the appropriateness of referrals declined over the study period. Only 40% of referrals overall had completed risk assessments, and fewer than one-fifth were classified as “high-risk”. This finding highlights a potential quality gap where awareness strategies increased quantity but did not necessarily strengthen the alignment of referrals with eligibility criteria. Several factors likely contributed to this gap, including incomplete documentation of the risk-assessment within the form, physician time constraints, variation in understanding or applying risk stratification criteria, limited training or monitoring of the referring physicians regarding referral pathways, eligibility criteria and risk level identification. Similar trends have been reported in other referral-strengthening interventions, where higher referral rates may inadvertently lead to a dilution of appropriateness [16]. Evidence suggests that peer feedback can improve referral quality and reduce inappropriate referrals by up to 50%; while the use of templates and risk factor checklist enhance documentation [17]. Multifaceted interventions that combine such strategies have been shown to improve both referral content and appropriateness [17].

Higher referral numbers benefit patient access and early prevention; however, to reduce waste of resources, ensure accurate triage and efficient service delivery, it is essential that physicians correctly categorize and refer patients based on their risk level. It is also important to acknowledge that as physicians increased referral volume and workload during the intervention period, the added clinical demands may have contributed to incomplete documentation of the risk-assessment form and missed risk categorization, which in turn affected the accuracy if the referral pathway.

In our study, clinic utilization improved significantly, with median monthly utilization rising from 31% pre-intervention to 54.5% post-intervention (p = 0.016). This is consistent with prior evidence demonstrating that simple, scalable interventions can improve service access and uptake. For example, a quasi-experimental pilot aimed at improving overall clinic productivity, an intervention that optimized team roles and workflows resulted in 48% more patient slots compared to reference teams, reflecting improved utilization and efficiency [18]. Our findings support evidence that system-level approaches which simplify referral decision-making and strengthen physician-patient communication can reduce no-show rates and optimize service utilization [13].

In conclusion, our findings support that simple, structured, provider-targeted interventions can significantly enhance referral rates and clinic utilization within primary care. Although further research is required to evaluate long-term patient and services outcomes, such strategies offer a scalable method to strengthen preventive care pathways and address non-communicable diseases. Future quality improvement efforts should prioritize enhancing referral quality, ensuring complete risk documentation, and strengthening adherence to eligibility criteria to support accurate patient triage and efficient use of resources.

Implications on practice and recommendations

Provider engagement plays a crucial role in driving patient participation, improving referral efficiency and increasing service utilization. Counseling or referral by healthcare professionals significantly increases patient motivation for lifestyle modifications. Thus, empowering providers with accessible referral tools is pivotal in translating awareness into action.

To sustain and build on the improvements achieved, we recommend continuing periodic referral-awareness campaigns and integrating referral reminders into the electronic health record system. Standardizing referral protocols across health centers and providing targeted training for healthcare providers are essential to enhance consistency and improve patient engagement. Ongoing monitoring and re-audit on referral rates, appropriateness and clinic utilization is also advised to assess the sustainability of the intervention.

Strengthening the role of wellness champions and physicians is critical for sustaining the referral pathway. This can be achieved by clearly institutionalizing their responsibilities, including reinforcing eligibility criteria, supporting correct completion of the risk-assessment form, and regularly communicating pathway updates. Brief workflow reminders and periodic refresher training can further enhance accuracy and consistency. Additionally, centers experiencing a decline in referrals may benefit from reviewing staffing patterns, workflow challenges, and feedback processes to identify and overcome local barriers.

Limitations

The study was limited to selected PHCC health centers, raising concerns about external validity. We also assessed only short-term outcomes; additional follow-up would clarify whether changes are durable and lead to improved patient health. Another important limitation is the decline in referral appropriateness observed during the study period, indicating that improved referral volume did not necessarily translate into improved referral quality. Additionally, the absence of a control group limits casual inference and reduces the ability to attribute observed changes solely to the intervention. Finally, this study focused on referral and utilization metrics without measuring downstream patient behavior or clinical outcomes.

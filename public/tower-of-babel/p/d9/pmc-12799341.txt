Multifactorial Biomarkers for “Talk and Deteriorate” after Head Trauma Identified Using Machine Learning
Authors: Youhei TAKEUCHI, Shinya SONOBE, Kuniyasu NIIZUMA, Arata NAGAI, Ayumi NARISAWA, Atsuhiro NAKAGAWA, Motonobu KAMEYAMA, Hiroshi KARIBE, Hidenori ENDO, Teiji TOMINAGA
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Talk and Deteriorate refers to a clinical course where a patient is able to speak immediately after a traumatic brain injury but subsequently deteriorates in consciousness. Talk and Deteriorate outcomes are poor, and reliable prediction may help improve them. We searched for multifactorial biomarkers―combinations of multiple risk factors―assuming they would predict Talk and Deteriorate more reliably than single factors. A total of 680 consecutive patients aged ≥16 years with intracranial traumatic lesions and a Glasgow Coma Scale of 13-15 upon admission were included. Information on 27 factors at admission and whether Talk and Deteriorate developed was collected. The correlation between Talk and Deteriorate incidence and each factor was evaluated using univariate analysis. Multifactorial biomarkers associated with a high Talk and Deteriorate incidence were identified using a brute force method combined with clustering based on combinations of factors and various thresholds for each. Among the 680 patients, 89 (13.1%) developed Talk and Deteriorate. A total of 94 multifactorial biomarkers with a Talk and Deteriorate incidence rate of ≥50.0% were identified. These multifactorial biomarkers, which included factors previously suspected to be related to Talk and Deteriorate, were associated with a higher Talk and Deteriorate incidence than each factor alone. Thus, this study indicates that predicting Talk and Deteriorate is more reliable when based on multifactorial biomarkers than on single factors. Factors not previously reported as independent biomarkers contributed as components of multifactorial biomarkers, while previously reported factors played more important roles within them. Thus, this study revealed multifactorial contributions of factors related to Talk and Deteriorate. Future studies focusing on these biomarkers, incorporating pathophysiologic insights, will improve Talk and Deteriorate outcomes.

Introduction

Talk and Deteriorate (T&D) refers to a clinical course in which a patient can talk immediately after a traumatic brain injury (TBI) but subsequently experiences a decline in consciousness. The concept was first reported in 1983.1) T&D develops in 3%-25% of patients with mild head injury.1-5) It is mainly caused by delayed intracranial hematoma expansion but can also occur in other pathophysiologic conditions such as convulsive seizures, nonconvulsive status epilepticus, brain edema, and hydrocephalus.2,6-10)

T&D outcomes are poor; 62.8% of patients with T&D have been reported to experience severe disability, persistent vegetative state, or death, according to the Glasgow Outcome Scale.1-3,11) In patients with mild disturbances in consciousness, deterioration often goes undetected due to decreased alertness, leading to poor outcomes.12) Although intensive observation can improve T&D outcomes through earlier intervention,1,13) such monitoring for all TBI patients is not feasible because of the large patient population and the relatively low incidence of T&D. Reliable biomarkers for T&D would enable clinicians to identify patient groups with a high incidence of T&D who require intensive observation.12) Given this background, various biomarkers have been investigated, for example, D-dimer level, anticoagulant use (AC), hematoma volume, and white blood cell (WBC) count have been suggested as significant factors.14-22) In addition to these efforts, it has been proposed that predicting T&D is more reliable when based on multifactorial biomarkers rather than a single factor.15,19,23) Thus, multifactorial biomarkers, which refer to the combinations of multiple risk factors, are expected to improve T&D outcomes.10)

Machine learning is a data analysis technique used to identify patterns. Compared to traditional statistics, machine learning typically incorporates a wider range of multivariate terms in the computational process. As a result, machine learning is well suited to detecting the synergistic effects of multiple variables, which is crucial for assessing multifactorial biomarkers.24) In fact, machine learning has begun to be used for more reliable outcome prediction in the field of TBI.25,26) Among various machine learning algorithms, clustering is a common approach for analyzing multifactorial biomarkers.27)

In this study, we searched for multifactorial biomarkers associated with a high incidence of T&D using machine learning.

Materials and Methods

Participants

Consecutive patients aged ≥16 years who were admitted to Sendai City Hospital between January 2011 and December 2021 with intracranial traumatic lesions and a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 13-15 upon admission were included. Patients were excluded if more than 96 hrs had elapsed from injury to hospital visit, if they had chronic subdural hematoma, or if head trauma resulted in stroke or epilepsy. Patients who had deteriorated before arriving at the hospital were also excluded (Fig. 1).

Datasets

The dataset (D) comprised 27 explanatory variables and 1 objective variable for each participant.

As explanatory variables, the following information upon admission was collected: age, sex, hemodialysis (HD), AC, antiplatelet use (AP), systolic blood pressure (sBP), diastolic blood pressure (dBP), mean blood pressure (mBP), heart rate (HR), body temperature (BT), presence of skull fracture (Fx), presence of cerebral contusion (Contusion), presence of traumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage (tSAH), presence of diffuse axonal injury, thickness of acute epidural hematoma (AEDH), thickness of convexity acute subdural hematoma (cASDH), thickness of tentorial or interhemispheric acute subdural hematoma (tASDH), cardiothoracic ratio (CTR), D-dimer, prothrombin time-international normalized ratio (PT-INR), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), hemoglobin level (Hb), WBC, platelet count (Plt), creatinine level (Cr), sodium level (Na), and potassium level (K). For each variable, when multiple values were available, the initial value obtained after the hospital visit was used. The data were verified through double-checking by 2 neurosurgeons.

The objective variable was whether T&D developed. In this study, T&D was defined as a deterioration of ≥2 points on the Glasgow Coma Scale within 96 hrs after injury. The T&D ratio was defined as the number of patients with T&D divided by the total number of patients in the population.

Patients with multiple missing data points were excluded from the study. For patients with a single missing data point, the data imputed using MissForest, a machine learning based imputation algorithm.28)

Univariate analysis

The correlations between T&D incidence and each explanatory variable were evaluated by univariate analysis. Quantitative variables were analyzed using Student's t-test, and qualitative variables were analyzed using the χ2 test. In both tests, statistical significance was defined as a p < 0.05. Furthermore, the T&D ratio based on a single factor was calculated for each explanatory variable. For quantitative variables, receiver operating characteristic analysis was performed to determine the threshold for T&D incidence, and the T&D ratio was then calculated for patients who exceeded the threshold. For qualitative variables, the T&D ratio was calculated for patients with the factors. Factors associated with a T&D ratio ≥25.0% were considered significant, taking into account previous reports.1-5)

Multifactorial assessment

Multifactorial biomarkers associated with a T&D ratio ≥50.0% (MFBhigh) were searched for using programmed methods. An overview of the method is shown in Fig. 2. As a preliminary analysis, MFBhigh were searched for by a brute force method using the explanatory variables with various thresholds for each variable. The analysis required an enormous computational cost, which was impractical. Therefore, the following 4-step method was adopted instead, and only MFBhigh commonly encountered in clinical practice were searched for by the brute force method with clustering.

Overview of the analysis algorithm. The goal of the analysis was to identify multifactorial biomarkers associated with a T&D ratio ≥50.0% (MFBhigh).

(A) Searching for all theoretically possible MFBhigh from the dataset (D) by brute force method. It required an enormous computational cost and was impractical.

(B) Searching for only MFBhigh commonly encountered in clinical practice by brute force method with clustering. Step 1: Patients with a low T&D ratio in D were excluded to generate a subset (Shigh). Step 2: Shigh underwent clustering to divide into similar patient groups, and a particular part of them (Ghigh) was collected. Step 3: Patterns of deviations in values (Phigh) were extracted from Ghigh. Step 4: MFBhigh were identified using Phigh. Through the 4-step method, the scope of analysis was narrowed down and the computational cost was reduced to within a practical constraint.

MFB: multifactorial biomarkers associated with a T&D ratio T&D: Talk and Deteriorate

Step 1: The multifactorial biomarkers associated with a T&D ratio ≤3.0% and met by ≥5.0% of all included patients (MFBlow) were searched for using the brute force method. This brute force method used 2 or 3 of the explanatory variables with limited thresholds for each variable, thereby keeping the computational cost within a practical constraint. Excluding patients who met any of MFBlow from D resulted in generating a subset (Shigh) with a higher T&D ratio than D.

Step 2: Shigh underwent clustering to divide it into patient groups with similar patterns of explanatory variables. The clustering algorithm employed a variation of k-means clustering that incorporated Bayesian theory and information entropy, and it operates on Python 3.8.10.28-30) The algorithm effectively processes mixed data containing both qualitative and quantitative variables. Clustering was executed 50,000 times with randomized changes to hyperparameters. Compared with clustering D, Clustering Shigh allows for more efficient identification of groups with a higher T&D ratio. From among the groups, only those with a T&D ratio ≥50.0% and a number of patients ≥2.0% of that in Shigh (Ghigh) were collected.

Step 3: Patterns of deviations in values were derived from each group in Ghigh. The mean values of all explanatory variables were calculated for each group, and then converted to +1, -1, or 0, depending on whether the values fell within standard deviations of the mean values in D. The values of qualitative variables were calculated in the same way, with 1 for positive and 0 for negative. From these patterns, duplicate patterns were removed, and patterns expressing uneven distributions of fewer than 4 explanatory variables (Phigh) were extracted.

Step 4: Multifactorial biomarkers were comprehensively formulated from each pattern in Phigh. Specifically, variables with uneven distributions in each pattern were combined, and various thresholds were applied for each variable. From the formulated multifactorial biomarkers, only those corresponding to patient groups with a T&D ratio ≥50.0% and a number of patients ≥2.0% of that in D were identified as MFBhigh.

Through the 4-step method, the scope of analysis was narrowed down, and the computational cost was reduced to within a practical constraint. Finally, the identified MFBhigh was reduced in accordance with the following 3 criteria to avoid listing similar multifactorial biomarkers. First, when one of the MFBhigh contained only one additional explanatory variable compared to the explanatory variables contained in another MFBhigh, and if the former was not associated with a T&D ratio at least 10.0% higher than the latter, then the former was removed. Second, when multiple MFBhigh with the same explanatory variables and different thresholds were identified, those with the lower T&D ratio or the smaller number of matching patients were removed. Third, when multiple MFBhigh with the same explanatory variables, same T&D ratio, same number of matching patients, and different thresholds were identified, those with thresholds indicating a less severe condition were removed.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Sendai City Hospital (approval number: Senbyoso No. 587). This study was a retrospective observational study without any interventions. Informed consent was obtained using an opt-out approach, as individual written consent was not required under the study design.

Results

A total of 702 patients were included in this study, and 22 patients were excluded due to multiple missing data points (Fig. 1). Thus, D comprised data from 680 patients. All these patients were confirmed for whether they had developed T&D, and 89 patients (13.1%) developed T&D. The causes of T&D were as follows: acute subdural hematoma expansion (30.3%), cerebral contusion expansion (28.1%), AEDH expansion (15.7%), convulsive seizure (9.0%), nonconvulsive status epilepticus (9.0%), delayed traumatic intracerebral hematoma (2.2%), acute hydrocephalus (2.2%), cardiovascular failure (2.2%), and cerebral hemispheric swelling (1.1%).

According to univariate analysis, the T&D ratio based on a single factor ranged from 8.8% to 33.3% (Table 1). Among the explanatory variables, HD, AC, D-dimer, and WBC were significant factors.

Out of the 94 MFBhigh, 79 contained factors for subtypes of intracranial lesion. For instance, the T&D ratio with AEDH ≥ 3 mm and WBC ≥ 15,000/μL was 57.1%. Discussion

In this study, we searched for biomarkers for T&D according to the patient's condition upon admission. The number of patients analyzed was 680, which is comparable to the scale of previous studies.1-5,11,13) Among the explanatory variables, HD, AC, D-dimer, and WBC were significant factors, consistent with previous reports.14-16,19-22,31-34) In the multifactorial assessment, 94 MFBhigh were identified as multifactorial biomarkers associated with a T&D ratio ≥50.0%. None of them have been reported previously.

The 94 MFBhigh included factors previously suspected to be related to T&D. Focal injuries such as acute subdural hematoma, AEDH, and contusion can lead to delayed intracranial hematoma expansion, cerebral edema, and brain swelling.35-37) Fracture reflects strong impact to the head and is therefore an indicator of potential brain injury, which may cause delayed intracranial hematoma expansion.38,39) Coagulation and fibrinolytic markers, as well as oral antithrombotic agents, have been proposed to evoke delayed intracranial hematoma expansion.14-22) Hemodialysis has been suggested to potentially affect T&D by causing brain vulnerability.31,40,41) Vital signs and physiological states, including cardiac function, renal function, electrolyte balance, and inflammatory response, are also thought to be relevant to T&D, although the mechanisms remain unclear.31,33,34,42-46) In summary, the 94 MFBhigh included factors that have been known statistically or empirically.

The results of univariate analysis and multifactorial assessment indicate that predicting T&D was more reliable when based on multifactorial biomarkers than on a single factor. Na was not a significant factor in univariate analysis (Table 1), but it played a role as a component of multifactorial biomarkers. For instance, the T&D ratio was 38.9% in the population with cASDH ≥ 3 mm and AC (data not shown), while it increased to 57.1% when Na ≤ 139 mEq/L was included as an additional factor (Table 2). AEDH was also not a significant factor in univariate analysis but played a role as a component of multifactorial biomarkers. For instance, the T&D ratio was 19.4% in the population with AEDH ≥ 3 mm (data not shown), while it increased to 57.1% when WBC ≥ 15,000/μL was included as an additional factor (Table 2). On the other hand, some factors were not only independent biomarkers but also had more important roles as components of multifactorial biomarkers. D-dimer was a significant factor in univariate analysis, with a T&D ratio of 26.3% (Table 1), which increased to ≥60.0% when combined in multifactorial biomarkers with other factors representing intracranial lesions, vital signs, or inflammatory change (Table 2). AC was also a significant factor in univariate analysis, with a T&D ratio of 27.0% (Table 1), which increased to ≥50.0% when combined with cASDH multifactorial biomarkers (Table 2). Thus, this study revealed multifactorial contributions of factors related to T&D.

The factors related to T&D tended to differ owing to the subtypes of intracranial lesion. For instance, among the 94 MFBhigh, 64 included cASDH or tASDH as a factor. Among these 64 MFBhigh, factors representing coagulation and fibrinolytic state, such as PT-INR or AC, were frequently observed. In another instance, 11 contained AEDH as a factor. Among these 11 MFBhigh, factors representing inflammatory change, such as WBC, were frequently observed. These differences suggest that the pathophysiologic pathways of T&D differ depending on the subtypes of intracranial lesion. In fact, T&D is reported to be related to various pathophysiologic pathways, such as hematoma expansion, brain edema, epilepsy, and hydrocephalus.2,6-10) The correspondences between the 94 MFBhigh and pathophysiology were not assessed, which is a limitation of this study.

The outcome of this study could be applicable to clinical practice. For instance, it may lead to the development of an application that automatically collects data from clinical records and evaluates the risk of T&D using multifactorial biomarkers. This application would detect patient groups with a high T&D incidence among a large number of patients with mild disturbances in consciousness. Detection would enable the prior recognition of T&D through intensive observation, such as repeated computed tomography scans, and encourage earlier interventions, including hematoma evacuation or prophylactic medication. In this way, the identification of multifactorial biomarkers may help improve future clinical workflows for TBI.

This study has limitations regarding the threshold for MFBhigh. Although it was set at 50.0%, there remain 2 considerations concerning its optimality. One consideration involves mathematical perspectives. Lowering the threshold would identify a greater number of multifactorial biomarkers; however, the additional biomarkers obtained may overfit the dataset and not be globally applicable. The threshold in this study was set with the aim of reducing overfitting and may be higher than the acceptable lower limit. A dataset with diverse institutional profiles would lead to a more precise decision. The other consideration involves practical perspectives. Multifactorial biomarkers associated with a lower T&D ratio may not increase alertness for deterioration. The threshold for promoting behavioral change must be validated in prospective studies. Further studies addressing these 2 considerations―mathematical and practical―would help determine a more optimal threshold.

In this study, multifactorial biomarkers for T&D were identified using machine learning. The identification of multifactorial biomarkers may contribute to improving future clinical workflows for TBI. Future studies focusing on these biomarkers and incorporating pathophysiologic insights will improve T&D outcomes.

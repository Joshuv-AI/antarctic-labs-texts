Evaluation of Smear Layer Removal and Antimicrobial Efficacy of Intracanal Herbal Irrigants
Authors: Ahmed Ali Alelyani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The removal of the smear layer and antimicrobial efficacy are critical factors in endodontic treatment. Conventional chemical irrigants like sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) have proven effective but can have adverse effects.Materials and Methods:A total of 60 extracted single-rooted human teeth were randomly assigned to four groups of 15 each. Group 1 was irrigated with 5.25% NaOCl, group 2 with 17% EDTA, group 3 with 10% neem extract, and group 4 with 5% turmeric extract. Smear layer removal was assessed using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) at the coronal, middle, and apical thirds of the root canals. Antimicrobial efficacy was tested against Enterococcus faecalis using the agar diffusion method, and the zones of inhibition were measured in millimeters.Results:Neem extract showed an average smear layer removal score of 2.5 (±0.3) compared to 1.8 (±0.2) for NaOCl, 1.9 (±0.3) for EDTA, and 3.2 (±0.4) for turmeric extract. Neem and turmeric extracts demonstrated significant antimicrobial activity with mean inhibition zones of 16 mm (±1.2) and 14 mm (±1.1), respectively, compared to 18 mm (±1.5) for NaOCl and 15 mm (±1.3) for EDTA.Conclusion:Herbal irrigants, particularly neem extract, show promise as effective alternatives to conventional irrigants for smear layer removal and antimicrobial activity. Their biocompatibility and comparable efficacy suggest potential for use in endodontic practice.

INTRODUCTION

The success of endodontic treatment largely depends on the thorough debridement and disinfection of the root canal system. A crucial aspect of this process is the effective removal of the smear layer, which consists of organic and inorganic debris created during root canal instrumentation.[1] The presence of the smear layer can harbor bacteria and impede the penetration of irrigants and medicaments, thus compromising the success of the treatment.[2]

Traditional chemical irrigants like sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) are widely used for smear layer removal and disinfection due to their potent antimicrobial properties. However, these agents can cause undesirable side effects such as tissue irritation and cytotoxicity.[34] Consequently, there is a growing interest in exploring natural alternatives that offer effective antimicrobial activity and biocompatibility.

Herbal irrigants, derived from plants with known medicinal properties, have emerged as promising candidates. Neem (Azadirachta indica) and turmeric (Curcuma longa) are two such herbs that have been extensively studied for their antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and healing properties.[56] Neem extract has shown significant antibacterial activity against various pathogens, including Enterococcus faecalis (E. faecalis), a common bacterium found in persistent endodontic infections.[7] Similarly, turmeric, due to its active component curcumin, exhibits strong antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects.[8]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sample selection

A total of 60 extracted single-rooted human teeth were selected for this study. Teeth with mature apices, free from cracks, caries, and previous endodontic treatments, were included. The selected teeth were stored in a 0.1% thymol solution until further use.

Experimental groups

The teeth were randomly assigned to four groups of 15 each based on the irrigant used:

Preparation of herbal extracts

Neem and turmeric extracts were prepared by dissolving powdered forms of each herb in distilled water. The solutions were filtered to remove any particulate matter and adjusted to the desired concentrations (10% for neem and 5% for turmeric).

Root canal preparation

All teeth were decoronated to standardize the root length at 15 mm. The canals were instrumented using rotary files (ProTaper Universal, Dentsply Maillefer) up to size F3. Irrigation was performed with 2 ml of the respective irrigant between each file change using a 30-gauge side-vented needle.

Smear layer removal assessment

After instrumentation, the roots were split longitudinally and examined under a scanning electron microscope (SEM) to evaluate the smear layer removal. Each root was examined at the coronal, middle, and apical thirds. The antimicrobial efficacy of each irrigant was tested against E. faecalis. Agar disc diffusion method was used to determine the antibacterial inhibition zones around NaOCl, EDTA, neem extract and Turmeric medicaments. BHI agar plates were prepared and each medicament was added to the respective wells. The plates were incubated in an incubator at 37°C for 24 h. After incubation, plates were removed and the bacterial inhibition zone around each well was recorded.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software (version 25.0).

NaOCl exhibited the largest zone of inhibition, demonstrating the highest antimicrobial efficacy. Neem extract also showed significant antimicrobial activity, with inhibition zones comparable to those of NaOCl. EDTA and turmeric extract showed lower but still considerable antimicrobial efficacy.

The Kruskal-Wallis test for smear layer removal scores revealed significant differences among the groups (P < 0.05). Pairwise comparisons using the Mann-Whitney U test indicated that neem extract performed significantly better in smear layer removal than both NaOCl and EDTA (P < 0.05). Turmeric extract was significantly less effective than all the other groups (P < 0.05).

For antimicrobial efficacy, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) showed significant differences among the groups (P < 0.05). Tukey’s post hoc test confirmed that NaOCl and neem extract had significantly larger zones of inhibition compared to EDTA and turmeric extract (P < 0.05).

These results highlight the potential of neem extract as an effective herbal irrigant for both smear layer removal and antimicrobial activity in endodontic treatment.

DISCUSSION

Smear layer removal

The SEM analysis revealed that neem extract exhibited superior smear layer removal compared to NaOCl, EDTA, and turmeric extract. The effectiveness of neem can be attributed to its complex chemical composition, which includes nimbin, nimbidin, and azadirachtin, compounds known for their antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties.[3] These compounds may enhance the ability of neem extract to dissolve organic and inorganic components of the smear layer.

Turmeric extract, on the other hand, showed the least effectiveness in smear layer removal. This may be due to its lower concentration of active ingredients and differing mechanisms of action. Curcumin, the active compound in turmeric, is primarily recognized for its antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties rather than its ability to dissolve smear layer components.[4]

Antimicrobial efficacy

The antimicrobial efficacy results demonstrated that both neem and turmeric extracts possess significant antimicrobial activity against E. faecalis, a common pathogen in persistent endodontic infections. The antimicrobial action of neem is well-documented, with studies showing its effectiveness against a broad spectrum of microorganisms due to its diverse phytochemical constituents.[56]

Although NaOCl exhibited the highest antimicrobial efficacy, neem extract showed comparable results, suggesting that it could serve as a viable alternative in cases where the use of NaOCl is contraindicated due to its cytotoxicity and potential to cause tissue damage.[78] EDTA and turmeric extract, while effective, had smaller zones of inhibition, indicating less potent antimicrobial action compared to NaOCl and neem.

CONCLUSION

The use of herbal irrigants in endodontics offers several advantages, including biocompatibility, reduced risk of adverse reactions, and the potential for fewer side effects compared to conventional chemical irrigants.

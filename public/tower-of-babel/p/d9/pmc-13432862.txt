Brain targeting and trafficking of extracellular vesicles in central nervous system diseases: a therapeutic roadmap
Authors: Blaine Baker, Samuel Emerson, Taylor Tran, Nikita Mohapatra, David Wang, Thant Zaw, Aaleesha Doshi, Juan-Maria Lopez, Dua Hassan, Priyadarsini Kumar, Diana Farmer, Aijun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTExtracellular vesicles (EVs) mediate intercellular signaling in the central nervous system (CNS) by transferring lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids among neurons, glia, endothelium, and immune cells. Brain targeting depends on a linked sequence: EV ligands and adsorbed protein coronas engage receptor modules, select endocytic routes, determine intracellular fate, and define the therapeutic readouts. These fates include lysosomal degradation, recycling, rare cytosolic delivery, or transport across the blood-brain barrier (BBB). In disease, the same pathways can disseminate proteopathic seeds and amplify neuroinflammation. Heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs) and LDL receptor family members, including low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1), regulate tau, α-synuclein, and amyloid-β handling. Phosphatidylserine readers and complement shape myeloid sink capture and inflammatory output. Integrin, tetraspanin, and ICAM-1 nanoclusters influence avidity, organotropism, and immune suppression. At the BBB, endothelial HSPGs, LRP1, and transferrin receptor (TfR) support receptor-mediated uptake, motivating engineered ligands such as rabies virus glycoprotein-derived peptides, Angiopep-2, and TfR binders. However, endosomal escape remains a major kinetic barrier to nucleic acid delivery. We synthesize these principles across Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, glioblastoma, and demyelinating disease, and outline design and assay standards needed to translate EV biology into safe, manufacturable CNS therapeutics.KEYWORDS: Extracellular vesicles, neurodegeneration, uptake logic, blood-brain barrier, receptor-mediated transcytosis, protein corona, endosomal escape, microglia

Introduction: extracellular vesicles as designable mediators of CNS communication

EV biology in the CNS

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are small, membrane-bound vesicles that transfer lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids that govern intercellular signaling in the central nervous system (CNS) [1]. In the CNS, EVs exchange cargo within synaptic circuits, white matter, and the neurovascular unit (NVU) [1,2]. In neurodegenerative disease, these trafficking routes are repurposed to distribute proteopathic seeds and inflammatory cues across cellular and vascular compartments, linking local pathology to network-level spread [1,3,4]. Accordingly, we focus on neurons, glia, brain endothelial cells, and infiltrating immune cells as the principal EV uptake module interfaces in neurodegeneration, with other CNS-resident lineages discussed only where they directly modify blood-brain barrier (BBB) transport or immune routing.

The NVU as the control point for EV exchange

EV communication in the CNS is constrained by the NVU and governed by receptor-defined uptake pathways rather than nonspecific membrane fusion. In this review, “uptake logic” refers to the sequence by which EV ligand and corona composition engages recipient-cell receptor modules, selects endocytic routes, determines intracellular fate, and defines the appropriate therapeutic readout. Surface ligands and adsorbed coronas, including heparan sulfate-binding motifs, apolipoproteins, integrins and adhesion molecules, exposed phosphatidylserine, and sialylated glycans, engage corresponding receptors on neurons, microglia, astrocytes, perivascular macrophages, and brain endothelium [3,5]. These interactions determine capture, intracellular routing, cytosolic cargo access, degradation, recycling, or transcytosis across the BBB [3,5–7]. Across Alzheimer’s disease (AD), Parkinson’s disease (PD), amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)/TDP-43 proteinopathies, and demyelinating disorders, disease state remodels these modules through inflammation, vascular dysfunction, and altered glycocalyx/corona composition, thereby reshaping EV flux and cellular targeting.

Conceptual framework for therapeutic design

Receptor-defined uptake pathways create therapeutic opportunities because they can be blocked, biased, or engineered. Pathogenic EV flux can be reduced by disrupting ligand-receptor interfaces that gate entry. Practical approaches include receptor blockade, competitive decoys that sequester EV ligands, and surface editing to reduce high-avidity capture and internalization [8–11]. A complementary strategy is to bias myeloid processing toward degradative or pro-resolving programs to enhance clearance while limiting inflammation.

Therapeutically, the objectives are to either reduce pathogenic spread by limiting entry into vulnerable recipient cells, or to achieve efficient, cell-selective delivery of functional cargo to the cytosol of target CNS cells. EV-based delivery aims to achieve cell-selective target engagement and functional activity in the CNS, rather than uptake alone. Therapeutic EVs can be engineered to engage BBB and parenchymal entry receptors, including heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs), low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1)/LDL receptor family members, and transferrin receptor (TfR), to enrich cargo delivery to key gate cells [3,7,12]. Nucleic acid and genome editors require cytosolic access and demand solutions to endosomal escape. Protein or lipid ligands may act on surface signaling or endolysosomal routing and can tolerate lower cytosolic delivery fractions. This review maps EV surface composition, receptor-defined uptake, intracellular fate, and therapeutic leverage across CNS disease contexts (Figure 1).

Modular uptake logic at CNS cells and the BBB

EV uptake in the CNS is a receptor-encoded routing problem: EV surface features and adsorbed coronas engage receptor modules on neurons, glia, and brain endothelium, biasing internalization and downstream fate. Most observations map onto clathrin-dependent endocytosis, raft/caveolar-associated uptake, macropinocytosis, or phagocytic engulfment, but receptor identity and cellular state are often more informative than route labels alone [13,14]. Translationally relevant fates include degradation versus productive cargo delivery and, at the BBB, endothelial retention versus transcytosis [15].

Glycan-mediated docking

Heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs)

Heparan sulfate (HS) on syndecans and glypicans provides a high-avidity capture surface for cationic and amyloidogenic motifs presented on EV membranes or EV-associated proteins. Binding strength is governed by HS chain length and sulfation pattern, with 6-O and 3-O sulfation repeatedly implicated as determinants for tau-class ligands. Multivalent HS–ligand contacts stabilize vesicle residence at the plasma membrane. This increases the probability of internalization, often through non-clathrin programs shaped by local membrane organization. Disrupting HS biosynthesis reduces binding and uptake across multiple CNS-relevant recipient cells [8,16,17].

In neurons and glia, syndecan-linked HS capture promotes EV accumulation at the surface and increases internalization probability at synaptic and perisynaptic membranes [18]. In tauopathies and synucleinopathies, HS dependence is a consistent gate for seed entry and templated propagation; perturbing HS decreases spread in vitro and in vivo [8]. Consistent with this interface, brain endothelial uptake and transcytosis of neural stem cell-derived EVs is reduced by heparanase or HS sulfation perturbation, supporting EV-level HS dependence at the vascular gate [19]. Effective HS presentation is modified by inflammation, matrix remodeling, and microenvironmental conditions, contributing to variability across models.

HS functions as a tunable entry gate. HS-binding decoys or heparan mimetics can reduce pathogenic uptake, and selective modulation of 6-O/3-O sulfation is conceptually attractive if specificity can be achieved. For engineered EVs, uncontrolled HS engagement can increase endothelial retention without improving brain exposure, so HS binding should be tuned and benchmarked rather than maximized [8,19].

LRP1 and the LDL receptor family

LRP1 and related LDL receptor family members recognize disordered, lysine-rich ligands and apolipoprotein-like features presented by EV coronas. In neurons, LRP1 contributes to internalization of tau-class ligands; receptor blockade or masking critical lysines reduces uptake in model systems [10]. In brain endothelium, LRP1 participates in handling of Aβ species and apolipoprotein-bound complexes, and LDL receptor family members provide a mechanistic link between corona composition (e.g., ApoE/clusterin enrichment) and routing behavior [20,21].

In AD-linked pathways, LRP1 intersects neuronal uptake of tau-class ligands and vascular Aβ handling. Its net effect therefore varies by disease stage, ligand context, and BBB state. Endothelial inflammation and ambient apolipoprotein environment can shift the balance between LRP1-dominant routing and alternative pathways, complicating simple “LRP1 is good/bad” narratives [10]. At the EV level, apolipoprotein-rich coronas on CNS-relevant EVs provide a plausible mechanism for preferential engagement of LDL receptor family members on brain endothelium and perivascular interfaces under defined biofluid conditions [21,22].

LRP1-facing strategies therefore require a defined compartmental objective. Enhancing LRP1-linked BBB handling is not equivalent to blocking neuronal tau uptake, and both approaches carry off-target tradeoffs because LRP1 has broad homeostatic roles [10].

RAGE as a stress-inducible influx pathway

The receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE) is expressed on neurons, microglia, and brain endothelium and binds Aβ and other inflammatory and glycation-associated ligands. Receptor engagement couples to NF-κB–inked transcriptional programs, oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction. RAGE expression increases with aging, diabetes, and chronic inflammation, and its contribution at the BBB becomes more prominent under stress states [22,23].

EVs enriched in oxidized or glycated lipids, or carrying RAGE-reactive ligands, may amplify this loop by coupling uptake to pro-inflammatory signaling rather than neutral handling. This provides a plausible mechanistic bridge between systemic metabolic stress and altered EV–BBB exchange [24,25].

RAGE is best treated as a stress-linked design constraint and stratification variable. Therapeutic EVs should avoid RAGE-reactive surface features in inflammatory or metabolic disease states, and translational studies should record vascular/metabolic status when interpreting BBB routing [26].

Lipid-encoded routing and phagocytic control

Phosphatidylserine (PS) and TAM/TIM routing

Externalized phosphatidylserine (PS) is a dominant “eat-me” cue that routes EVs into efferocytosis. This occurs through T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin-domain (TIM) receptors and Tyro3/Axl/MerTK (TAM) receptor tyrosine kinases. TIM receptors, notably TIM4, bind PS directly, while TAM receptors recognize PS indirectly through bridging ligands such as Gas6 and Protein S. Importantly, downstream responses are not uniform: MerTK-biased programs more often align with phagolysosomal processing and pro-resolving transcriptional states, whereas Axl-biased programs can align with inflammatory outputs [11,27].

PS-dependent routing is prominent in microglia and macrophage-lineage sinks and is shaped by EV surface state, which biases EV fate toward degradative clearance versus inflammatory amplification [28]. Differences in TAM receptor balance across disease states contribute to whether EV uptake supports clearance versus cytokine amplification, particularly in inflammatory demyelinating disease and neurodegenerative progression [29]. This module also intersects with peripheral biodistribution by determining the strength of myeloid sink capture [30].

PS is a tunable routing variable, but not a binary “good/bad” signal. PS masking, TAM modulation, or MerTK-biased uptake may be useful only when the intended recipient population, disease stage, and desired myeloid outcome are explicit [31,32].

Adhesion nanoclusters and checkpoint cargo

Many EV adhesion phenotypes are explained by nanoscale organization: integrins, tetraspanins (CD9/CD63/CD81), and adhesion molecules such as ICAM-1 co-assemble into tetraspanin-enriched microdomains that increase effective avidity and stabilize EV–cell contact [5]. This organization couples docking to signaling and biases internalization behavior in a ligand-density-dependent manner, frequently dominating outcomes more than bulk cargo abundance.

Disease evidence is strongest in tumor systems, including brain tumors. In these settings, integrin repertoires shape stromal and endothelial capture and contribute to niche conditioning [33]. Classic organotropism studies support the concept that integrin “codes” alter capture probability across tissues. ICAM-1/PD-L1 co-expression on EVs provides a concrete mechanism for mobile immune suppression, in which docking mediated by ICAM-1–LFA-1 positions PD-L1 for efficient PD-1 engagement and functional exhaustion [34].

Therapeutic leverage includes blocking integrin-mediated docking, ICAM-1–LFA-1 interactions, or PD-1/PD-L1 signaling, and redesigning EV surface organization to favor endothelial engagement while limiting sink capture. The key constraint is avidity: ligand density and clustering should be tuned rather than maximized.

Lectins and Siglecs: glycan capture in immune niches

Lectins and Siglecs read glycan features on EV surfaces and function as high-avidity capture systems in macrophage-rich environments. Siglec-1 (CD169) binds sialylated gangliosides and glycoproteins and can sequester vesicles into antigen-processing or immunoregulatory pathways, while galectins crosslink glycoproteins at the EV–cell interface to stabilize contacts and facilitate uptake [35].

CNS-adjacent immune niches, including the meninges, choroid plexus, cervical lymph nodes, and border macrophages, are enriched for lectin and Siglec biology. This makes the module relevant to drainage, sequestration, and myeloid sink behavior for brain-derived EVs. In neurodegeneration, this may influence whether proteopathic material is buffered by clearance versus presented in inflammatory contexts, with baseline inflammation and aging as likely modifiers [36].

Glycan editing provides a practical handle for biasing EV distribution toward or away from lectin/Siglec-positive sinks, depending on whether immune sequestration or parenchymal delivery is the intended outcome. Reporting inflammatory context and biofluid handling remains necessary for interpretable uptake comparisons.

Innate sensing and opsonic routing

TLR danger sensing on microglia

Microglia use Toll-like receptors (TLRs) to detect danger-associated patterns carried by EVs (protein conformations, inflammatory ligands, nucleic acids), coupling uptake to inflammatory transcriptional programs. In synuclein systems, oligomeric α-synuclein (α-syn) is a strong TLR2 agonist, and EV presentation of similar danger-associated patterns can prime activation while increasing inflammatory outputs associated with uptake [37].

In synucleinopathies and related disorders, TLR engagement plausibly couples proteopathic spread to chronic neuroinflammation, with effects that vary by disease stage. Early signaling may support clearance programs, whereas persistent activation can drive cytokine amplification and synaptic injury. Because TLR activation remodels receptor expression and trafficking behavior, baseline inflammatory differences can propagate into divergent EV routing pathways across models [38,39].

Separating uptake from inflammatory consequence remains the translational objective. Dampening TLR2 and TLR4-linked cytokine cascades while preserving phagocytic sink functions mediated by other modules is one tractable direction [40,41]. For engineered EVs, inadvertent innate activation from contaminants, oxidized lipids, or inflammatory coronas should be controlled and reported to avoid misattributing outcomes to intended targeting features.

Complement opsonization

Complement tags EVs for uptake and shapes immune tone. C1q binds exposed PS and select ligands, while downstream C3 activation deposits iC3b recognized by CR3/CR1 on microglia and macrophages, routing vesicles toward phagolysosomal handling [42]. Scavenger receptors can recognize oxidized lipid/corona features, but complement provides the more generalizable opsonic logic [43].

Complement is directly relevant to CNS disease because it intersects synapse biology and becomes maladaptive during chronic neuroinflammation, raising the possibility that opsonized EVs contribute to vulnerability depending on stage and myeloid state [44].

Intervention surfaces include limiting C1q and C3 deposition or selectively modulating complement receptor engagement to reduce harmful uptake and synapse loss, while preserving beneficial clearance elsewhere. Complement exposure and corona conditions remain primary design constraints in EV therapeutic programs, particularly when interpreting peripheral sink behavior versus CNS delivery [45].

EV cargo delivery and escape

Endocytic entry pathways

EV internalization is governed by surface ligand/corona engagement, vesicle size and rigidity, and recipient-cell state. Most observations converge on a limited set of entry programs. Clathrin-dependent uptake, raft/caveolar-associated uptake, and actin-driven macropinocytosis/phagocytosis often operate in parallel within the same cell type. Route assignment is therefore best treated as probabilistic and context-dependent, rather than as a fixed “one vesicle-one pathway” model [6]. Following systemic administration, EVs encounter sequential systemic, endothelial, and endosomal barriers; Figure 2 separates these losses from productive delivery and maps representative intervention points.

Sequential barriers that limit productive EV delivery after systemic administration.

Following systemic dosing, EVs encounter serial barriers before productive CNS delivery. These include nonspecific uptake and phagocytosis, blood clearance in filtering organs, endothelial retention, lysosomal degradation, low target-cell uptake, and inefficient endosomal escape. Protein corona composition can also reroute EVs, either by promoting premature clearance or by limiting productive membrane interactions. Representative intervention points include reducing nonspecific clearance, improving stability, ligand display, cationic or fusogenic lipid strategies, and engineering approaches that improve endosomal escape.

Clathrin-mediated endocytosis internalizes receptor-bound EVs

Clathrin-mediated endocytosis predominates when EV docking is mediated by high-affinity receptor interactions and receptors cluster into coated-pit–competent domains. Canonical components (AP2 adaptors and dynamin-dependent scission) support vesicle internalization into early endosomal compartments, providing a direct link between receptor identity and downstream sorting [46]. In neurons, clathrin-associated uptake is prominent at synaptic and perisynaptic regions, consistent with receptor-rich microdomains as EV capture sites [13,46,47].

Caveolae, rafts, and related clathrin-independent pathways

Cholesterol-rich membrane microdomains support clathrin-independent EV uptake, including caveolin-1-associated entry, raft-linked internalization, and clathrin-independent carrier/glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored protein-enriched early endosomal compartment (CLIC/GEEC) pathways. Actin- and CDC42-dependent CLIC/GEEC-like processes can contribute depending on membrane tension and cargo properties and have been linked to BAR-domain scaffolds such as GRAF1 and IRSp53 in broader endocytic literature [48,49]. These programs are frequently invoked for EVs with strong lipid/glycan-driven docking and for cells with pronounced membrane ruffling.

Macropinocytosis and phagocytosis dominate in myeloid uptake states

Actin-driven macropinocytosis is favored for larger or aggregate-prone particles and under growth factor or inflammatory activation states, with PI3K/Pak1-linked remodeling often implicated in mechanistic studies. Professional phagocytes add opsonic and PS-sensing engulfment through TAM receptors and complement receptors, which rapidly deliver internalized vesicles to degradative endolysosomal compartments [50,51]. In neuroimmune contexts, microglia and reactive astrocytes show strong routing toward endolysosomes relative to many non-phagocytic recipients [52].

Post-entry sorting and trafficking

After internalization, EV fate is determined less by the entry label and more by endosomal maturation and sorting. EV-containing carriers typically enter EEA1/Rab5-positive early endosomes and then mature through Rab conversion into Rab7-positive late endosomes or multivesicular bodies, where most vesicles are committed to lysosomal degradation [53,54]. A smaller fraction is recycled, retained in non-degradative compartments, or routed to sites permissive for membrane fusion and limited functional delivery.

Recent work also challenges simple “endocytosis-first” models by showing that endosomal identity can be shaped by post-Golgi transport and maturation dynamics. This supports a model in which Rab-defined compartments are actively regulated networks rather than linear conveyors. Cargo release in reporter systems is inhibited when endosomal acidification is blocked or late endosomal cholesterol handling is perturbed, consistent with late endosomes and lysosomes as major gates for productive release [52,54–56].

Rab5-to-Rab7 maturation provides a practical framework for describing EV sorting decisions: early endosome residence is permissive for recycling and sorting, whereas Rab7-associated maturation increases degradative commitment and constrains the window for productive release.

High-resolution imaging suggests EV-containing endosomes traffic along microtubules and engage ER–endosome contact sites that regulate positioning, lipid handling, and maturation, potentially shaping the probability of downstream fusion events [52].

pH, lipids, and conserved fusion machinery constrain productive release

For most recipient cells, productive delivery is primarily a late endosomal event, not direct plasma membrane fusion. Acidification and late endosomal lipid composition, particularly anionic lipids such as lysobisphosphatidic acid (LBPA) and cholesterol-dependent membrane organization, promote curvature stress and fusion-permissive intermediates. EV membranes are enriched in cholesterol and sphingolipids, which increase order and rigidity and organize tetraspanin/adhesion platforms; these same properties can modulate fusogenicity in a pH- and lipid-dependent manner. Pharmacologic manipulation underscores this dependence. Neutralizing endosomal acidification with bafilomycin A1 reduces cytosolic release across multiple systems. In contrast, “permeabilizers” such as chloroquine show context-dependent effects and can impair pH-dependent fusion pathways rather than universally improving delivery [4,57].

Mechanistically, EV–endosome fusion and content exposure are constrained by canonical membrane remodeling and fusion systems. ESCRT-associated components (e.g., ALIX/ESCRT-III-linked pathways) and SNARE-tethering machinery (e.g., HOPS/SNARE networks) are best treated here as named requirements for endolysosomal membrane fusion competence rather than as pathways to recapitulate in detail; perturbation of these systems suppresses cargo delivery in several EV models, consistent with conserved endolysosomal fusion constraints [58–61].

Kinetics and low stoichiometry explain why uptake ≠ delivery

Quantitative reporter assays consistently show that surface binding and internalization overestimate functional delivery. In live-cell luciferase and fluorescent reporter systems, only a minority of surface-associated EVs are internalized over short time windows in non-phagocytic cells. Of those internalized vesicles, only a subset yields detectable cytosolic reporter signal under baseline conditions. Delivery decreases further when acidification is blocked or when restriction factors such as interferon-induced transmembrane proteins are increased [62–64]. These kinetics support a practical inference for therapeutic design: endosomal escape is the dominant bottleneck, and improvements in targeting that merely increase uptake may not translate into proportional increases in cytosolic payload availability.

Non-EV nanoplatforms provide useful comparators for this bottleneck because they are often evaluated by protection, intracellular release, and retained cargo activity rather than uptake alone. For example, tea polyphenol nanoparticles preserved urate oxidase activity, protected it from degradation, and enabled glutathione-triggered intracellular release. Although not an EV system, this illustrates the delivery standard EV therapeutics should be benchmarked against: cytosolic access plus functional cargo activity [65].

A second, independent limiter is cargo stoichiometry per vesicle, especially for miRNAs. Quantitative analyses suggest that many EV populations carry <1 copy per vesicle on average for specific miRNAs, implying that robust phenotypic effects require either rare cargo-rich subpopulations, very high particle doses, or mechanisms where lipid/protein features trigger signaling prior to degradation [66]. The combined kinetic and stoichiometric constraints argue for delivery-linked functional readouts (cytosolic reporters, target engagement assays) rather than inference from uptake or co-localization, and they motivate engineering strategies that explicitly increase escape probability rather than only increasing binding.

EV transfer across the BBB

EV exchange across the BBB occurs within the NVU, where polarized brain endothelium, tight junctions, pericytes, astrocyte endfeet, and the endothelial glycocalyx restrict paracellular diffusion and most biologics. EVs can engage receptor-mediated uptake and, in a minority of cases, transcytosis-like routing to the abluminal side, making BBB trafficking rules central to CNS therapeutic design. Simplified BBB models remain useful for screening but can misestimate transfer when shear stress, glycocalyx structure, and full NVU complexity are absent; model criteria are summarized in Section 7.3 [2]. Architecture of the NVU and BBB constraints

Brain endothelium is not a passive wall; it is a polarized, receptor-rich interface whose luminal glycocalyx and membrane microdomains determine the first contact layer for circulating EVs. In vitro evidence supports receptor-mediated entry as a dominant contributor to EV handling by brain endothelial cells: for example, neural stem cell-derived EV uptake is reduced by enzymatic removal or sulfation editing of HS, consistent with regulated capture rather than nonspecific leak [67].

Natural EV routes across brain endothelium

At the luminal surface, glycocalyx, corona, and adhesion programs concentrate EV–endothelium interactions under flow [68]. Internalization is often dynamin-dependent, with clathrin- versus raft/caveolar-associated uptake shaped by EV composition and endothelial activation [67]. After entry, most EV material proceeds through Rab5/Rab7 endosomal maturation toward degradation, while a smaller fraction is sorted toward abluminal exocytosis or processed cargo release [2].

Engineered targeting to augment natural uptake pathways

RVG–Lamp2b demonstrates neuron-tropic systemic EV delivery

Display of a rabies virus glycoprotein (RVG) peptide through Lamp2b on engineered dendritic cell EVs enabled systemic delivery of siRNA with knockdown in neurons, microglia, and oligodendrocytes, providing a canonical proof-of-concept for surface-programmed CNS tropism after peripheral dosing. This illustrates ligand display as a way to redirect receptor engagement at the BBB/NVU, although viral motifs may add immunogenicity and fusion constructs can perturb EV biogenesis or cargo loading [69].

Angiopep-2 and other LRP1 ligands increase BBB transfer

Angiopep-2 (LRP1-directed) increases endothelial uptake and BBB transfer on EV-like carriers and has improved intracranial tumor delivery in rodent models. This approach leverages high-capacity transport receptors but risks competition with endogenous ligands and off-target uptake in peripheral LRP1-rich tissues [70].

Transferrin receptor (TfR) binders drive receptor-mediated transcytosis

TfR-targeted EVs can increase endothelial uptake and apparent transcytosis, with best performance requiring calibrated affinity and valency to favor transcytosis while limiting lysosomal trapping, receptor down-regulation, iron-handling perturbation, and peripheral sequestration [12].

Corona engineering as a practical targeting layer

In circulation, an adsorbed protein corona can dominate effective targeting by rerouting EVs through apolipoprotein, Fc, and complement pathways. Corona engineering may therefore improve endothelial engagement or suppress peripheral sinks, but uncontrolled corona formation can erase ligand intent and increase hepatic/splenic clearance [71].

Physiologic modifiers that tune BBB EV traffic

Hypoxia and ischemia

Hypoxia/ischemia disrupt barrier integrity and can increase BBB and blood–CSF permeability, altering both EV access to endothelium and downstream inflammatory tone [72]. At the cellular level, hypoxia activates stress programs that increase EV release and remodel endothelial uptake machinery and cargo composition, shifting trafficking behavior without requiring a single universal pathway model [73,74].

Inflammation and glycocalyx remodeling

Inflammation thins or remodels the endothelial glycocalyx, alters HS sulfation, and shifts expression of uptake/response receptors (e.g., LRP1, RAGE) and adhesion molecules, changing both EV docking and routing. These changes can increase EV binding/uptake and bias downstream handling toward inflammatory programs depending on cytokine context and vascular state [9,74].

Proteopathic tau, endothelial senescence, and feedback loops

Proteopathic tau can enter brain endothelium via HSPG-dependent mechanisms and promote endothelial dysfunction, senescence, and tight-junction disruption, plausibly increasing permissiveness to further EV/protein influx. Vascular injury may therefore create a feed-forward loop in which a compromised BBB both produces and misroutes EV signals that amplify neuroinflammation [9].

For EV-based CNS therapies, BBB transport should be discussed as a design problem under physiologic variability. Comorbid hypoxia/ischemia, systemic inflammation, and proteopathy are not side notes but parameters that can invert targeting outcomes and should be recorded/stratified in preclinical models and early trials.

Alzheimer’s disease

AD reflects coupled failure modes in proteostasis (Aβ and tau), microglial state control, and microvascular integrity. EVs act as carriers of proteopathic species and modifiers of endothelial and microglial handling, placing the NVU and myeloid sinks at the center of disease-relevant EV biology.

Tau/Aβ routing in AD

As outlined in Section 2, neuronal entry of tau-bearing EVs is strongly gated by HS-dependent capture with sensitivity to HS sulfation pattern [8,75]. In parallel, LRP1 facilitates neuronal uptake of disordered ligands, while at the BBB it participates in high-capacity vascular handling of Aβ and apolipoprotein-bound complexes, producing compartment-dependent effects that shift with barrier state and ligand context [76,97]. Under vascular or metabolic stress, RAGE-linked signaling can couple EV-associated ligands to endothelial inflammatory programs and barrier dysfunction [76]. Corona composition adds a second layer of context, since ApoE or clusterin adsorption can redirect tau- or Aβ-bearing EVs into lipoprotein receptor pathways, plausibly shifting the balance between clearance sinks and delivery to vulnerable NVU interfaces [75].

Microglial state control couples proteopathy to EV output

In AD, microglia internalize tau/Aβ and then determine whether EV circuits buffer pathology (clearance/containment) or propagate it (seed-competent release), with NF-κB signaling emerging as a state switch that increases release of pathogenic EV cargo and worsens synaptic/neuronal injury [10]. Endothelial uptake is also disease-relevant because pathogenic tau entry can promote senescence-like programs and microvascular dysfunction, reshaping BBB permissiveness and EV traffic over time [9]. These observations make microglial state and endothelial health practical determinants of whether EV biology is primarily protective clearance or feed-forward propagation. Although CSF1R inhibition is not EV-directed, microglial-modulation studies show that shifts in microglial state can affect synaptic plasticity and behavior, supporting paired EV, synaptic, and circuit-level readouts [98,99].

Actionable strategies and readouts in AD

EV-centered interventions in AD can be treated as pathway-selective strategies with stage-matched endpoints. In early or prodromal tauopathy, limiting HS- and LRP1-gated neuronal entry of tau-containing EV cargo is expected to reduce network spread, with pharmacodynamic readouts anchored to CSF EV tau species and target-cell delivery reporters where available [8]. In AD with vascular or metabolic comorbidity, biasing Aβ handling toward endothelial clearance by supporting LRP1-linked efflux and reducing RAGE-linked influx prioritizes BBB-facing endpoints, including brain:liver exposure ratios and endothelial activation or senescence panels [76]. In symptomatic disease where microglial feed-forward loops dominate, shifting microglial routing away from NF-κB-skewed EV output and toward degradative, pro-resolving states can be tracked using EV-associated tau and inflammatory signatures longitudinally [77].

Parkinson’s disease

PD combines dopaminergic neuron vulnerability with α-syn seed dissemination and early, sustained engagement of innate immune programs. EVs are implicated both in α-syn transport and in glia-to-neuron neuroprotective signaling, so disease state and recipient cell identity (neuron vs microglia) are central design variables [79,100].

Uptake logic in PD: HSPG-gated neuronal entry and TLR2-linked microglial sensing

As in Section 2, α-syn fibrils/oligomers engage neuronal HSPGs (including syndecan-linked programs), and HS perturbation reduces neuronal uptake and downstream pathology in model systems [80]. In microglia, oligomeric α-syn acts as a danger pattern that engages TLR2 and drives NF-κB–linked cytokine programs; EV presentation of α-syn species can couple uptake with inflammatory signaling, converting “clearance attempts” into chronic neuroinflammation depending on activation baseline [101].

Microglial EVs can propagate or buffer α-syn pathology

Microglial EVs can either carry seed-competent α-syn and propagate pathology or act as sinks/supportive carriers depending on state and cargo composition. EV sialylation can bias capture by Siglec-positive myeloid programs, which may route proteopathic material into microglial clearance compartments under some conditions [80]. Reparative glial EVs can deliver trophic and proteostasis regulators that support dopaminergic neurons and counter α-syn toxicity, reinforcing that “EV effect” is not intrinsic but state- and routing-dependent [100].

Actionable strategies and readouts in PD

In early PD or high-risk prodromal states, reduce neuronal seed entry by targeting HS-dependent docking/uptake, with readouts including neuronal uptake reporters and CSF EV α-syn species [80]. In inflammatory-biased PD phenotypes, dampening TLR2-driven microglial activation may uncouple uptake from cytokine output; microglial inflammatory markers and EV-associated α-syn burden can be monitored longitudinally [81]. Repair-oriented strategies should specify intended recipient programs and track dopaminergic resilience endpoints in parallel with CSF or plasma EV α-syn species shifts [100].

ALS and TDP-43 proteinopathies

ALS progression reflects non–cell-autonomous mechanisms in which astrocytes, microglia, muscle, and motor neurons contribute to degeneration. EVs carry misfolded protein species (e.g., SOD1, TDP-43–related cargo) and provide accessible compartments for longitudinal tracking, which supports both mechanistic and translational utility [83,84].

Uptake logic in ALS: proteopathic EV cargo and clearance failure

Astrocytes and neurons in SOD1-mutant contexts release EVs containing misfolded SOD1 capable of transferring toxicity to recipient cells, while TDP-43 pathology correlates with impaired microglial phagocytic competence in patient-derived systems [85,86]. Although receptor-level assignments remain incomplete relative to tau/α-syn systems, it is plausible that the same high-level routing described in Sections 2–3, HS-dependent capture for disordered ligands and PS/complement tagging into myeloid sinks, contributes to whether EV cargo is degraded or disseminated [80,90].

PS/complement balance as a determinant of microgliopathy

PS-positive EVs and myelin/debris fragments engage TAM receptors and complement receptors on microglia; when balanced, these pathways support efferocytosis and pro-resolving programs, but when chronically activated or overwhelmed, they can shift microglia toward maladaptive phenotypes that amplify neuronal stress [90].

Actionable strategies and readouts in ALS

In symptomatic disease where clearance capacity is limiting, biasing microglia toward pro-resolving engulfment programs (e.g., TAM-skewed routing) is best paired with longitudinal CSF or blood EV measures of misfolded SOD1 or TDP-43 species and immune activation panels [90]. Where proteopathic entry appears dominant, evaluate HS pathway modulation as a containment strategy with uptake/function reporters rather than uptake-only endpoints [80]. EV profiling is most valuable in ALS when treated as a pharmacodynamic layer integrated with established markers such as neurofilament light, emphasizing within-subject change rather than cross-sectional differences [84].

Glioblastoma as a comparative CNS disease model

Glioblastoma (GBM) is not classically neurodegenerative, but it is retained here as a comparative CNS disease model because tumor EVs provide some of the clearest evidence linking EV surface programs to BBB remodeling, immune suppression, and delivery constraints. This section therefore emphasizes the translatable principles from GBM EV biology: endothelial docking, immune synapse engagement, and pharmacodynamic EV cargo readouts. GBM EVs provide a concrete disease context in which integrin-coded docking and checkpoint-bearing vesicles produce measurable functional consequences relevant to CNS delivery constraints [33,88].

Uptake logic in GBM: integrin/tetraspanin programs at stroma and brain endothelium

Tumor EV integrin repertoires can encode stromal targeting: α6β4/α6β1 bias uptake in lung-associated niches and αvβ5 biases capture by liver Kupffer cells, and blocking these heterodimers reduces EV docking/uptake and niche conditioning in the pre-metastatic framework [33]. In brain-tropic settings, tumor EVs target brain endothelium and remodel BBB behavior; integrin and tetraspanin microdomains likely increase avidity and stabilize docking, enabling efficient endothelial entry and downstream angiogenic signaling [34].

ICAM-1/PD-L1 EVs as mobile immune-suppressive units

GBM EVs can co-display ICAM-1 and PD-L1, linking adhesion to checkpoint signaling: LFA-1 activation on T cells promotes ICAM-1–dependent EV docking and positions EV-borne PD-L1 for efficient PD-1 engagement, driving exhaustion-like phenotypes. Disrupting ICAM-1–LFA-1 adhesion reduces docking and can attenuate downstream PD-L1/PD-1 signaling in preclinical systems, supporting adhesion as a critical part of the mechanism rather than a bystander [34,89].

Actionable strategies and readouts in GBM

Integrin-defined EV docking programs can be targeted as an adjunct strategy to reduce endothelial and stromal conditioning, with readouts anchored to EV integrin signatures and endothelial activation markers [33]. Disrupting ICAM-1–LFA-1EV capture targets local immune synapse delivery of suppressive vesicles, with T-cell functional assays paired to EV PD-L1 burden as a trial-relevant endpoint [34]. EV-routing interventions are most interpretable when integrated with established immunotherapy logic and evaluated using EV cargo panels as pharmacodynamic markers rather than bulk EV counts alone [5,89].

Demyelinating disease

Multiple sclerosis and related demyelinating disorders reflect an imbalance between myelin injury and repair driven by immune-mediated damage, inefficient debris clearance, and impaired oligodendrocyte progenitor cell (OPC) differentiation. EVs couple myelin–immune crosstalk and link BBB status to lesion activity, making myeloid routing modules (PS/complement/TAM/TREM2) central [90–92].

Uptake logic in demyelination: debris clearance as a prerequisite for remyelination

Oligodendrocyte-derived EVs support axonal health under homeostatic conditions but shift cargo under inflammatory milieus toward signals that can amplify immune activation. Microglial uptake of myelin debris via PS- and complement-linked pathways is required to clear inhibitory fragments and permit OPC differentiation, while debris is a direct barrier to remyelination and a driver of chronic lesion biology [90,91].

TAM/TREM2 state programs control whether uptake is reparative

During demyelination, microglia upregulate TAM receptors and TREM2 and use PS recognition to couple engulfment with anti-inflammatory reprogramming. MerTK- and TREM2-biased states accelerate debris removal and support remyelination in experimental models, while BBB leak and endothelial EV changes provide a practical window into vascular contributions to lesion activity [93–95].

Actionable strategies and readouts in demyelination

In active demyelination, enhancing pro-remyelinating phagocytosis by biasing microglia toward MerTK- and TREM2-associated reparative programs can be paired with debris clearance metrics and inflammatory cytokine profiles alongside EV subset changes [93]. For repair-oriented delivery, EV surface cues and corona state can be tuned to favor uptake by reparative microglia or OPC-associated programs, with functional remyelination endpoints prioritized over uptake-only assays [91]. Endothelial and CSF EV subsets can serve as pharmacodynamic markers of BBB stabilization and remyelination response [96]. Repair-oriented EV programs should also benchmark cargo effects against functional neurorepair readouts, including metabolomic signatures linked to neurogenesis and axon-regeneration pathways [102].

Cross-disease synthesis: what carries across

Across indications, neuronal and endothelial entry decisions are most often shaped by glycan and lipoprotein-linked interfaces, myeloid outcome depends on whether uptake is routed into degradative or inflammatory programs, and high-avidity adhesion microdomains can dominate immune synapse signaling in tumor contexts. Interventions are most interpretable when anchored to stage-matched pharmacodynamic measures in CSF or blood, together with functional delivery or clearance reporters.

EV engineering and therapeutic design

Choosing and engineering cellular sources

Source selection influences intrinsic bioactivity, redosing feasibility, and manufacturability, and these factors can equal or exceed marginal gains from targeting ligands. Red blood cell (RBC)-derived EVs support a relatively low-bioactivity, scalable shuttle model because RBCs are abundant and enucleated, which reduces nuclear DNA carryover and aligns with repeated-dose paradigms in preclinical settings [103]. Mesenchymal stromal cell (MSC)-derived EVs are preferred when the product is intended to be disease-modifying on its own (immunomodulation, trophic support, repair programs) and when producer conditioning (e.g., cytokines/hypoxia) is part of the mechanism; they also have mature scale-up workflows across indications [104]. Immune-cell-derived EVs require similar source-state control. M2 macrophage-derived EVs can alter recipient immune-cell cytokine programs, supporting the broader principle that parent-cell polarization should be reported and controlled [105].

For all sources, repeated administration depends on innate opsonization and anti-product responses, so redosing requires empirical validation under clinically relevant corona and complement conditions rather than assumption [103]. These sequential losses define the major intervention surfaces for EV engineering and explain why improvements in uptake do not necessarily improve functional delivery (Figure 2).

Cargo loading strategies: a decision framework

A minimal decision framework covers most designs: exogenous loading for small molecules or rapid formulation iteration; endogenous loading when intraluminal protection and stoichiometry control are required (especially for RNA and proteins); and hybrid architectures when payload mass and escape requirements exceed what native EVs can deliver at tolerable particle doses. The dominant failure mode should be specified at the outset (aggregation, surface adsorption misread as loading, endolysosomal degradation, or immune activation). These constraints make protection assays and functional delivery reporters more informative than uptake metrics alone when evaluating loading and escape strategies (Figure 4).

Two orthogonal quality control (QC) readouts are required for interpretability: a protection assay (nuclease protection for RNA, protease protection for protein) plus a functional delivery reporter that distinguishes uptake from productive delivery. Variants should be benchmarked under matched corona conditions because apparent gains often reflect altered opsonization rather than true improvements in loading or escape [106].

Exogenous loading

Electroporation, sonication, extrusion, freeze–thaw, and transient permeabilization can increase apparent payload association, but can frequently introduce EV aggregation and “cargo-on-surface” artifacts that inflate loading estimates and fail delivery readouts. Exogenous loading is most defensible when the mechanism tolerates endolysosomal exposure or acts at membranes, and it is less reliable for cargos requiring cytosolic access unless paired with explicit escape engineering. Required QC includes protection assays to confirm intraluminal cargo and a delivery reporter that measures cytosolic function rather than fluorescence colocalization [106].

Endogenous loading

Endogenous approaches leverage biogenesis pathways by recruiting cargo into intraluminal vesicles using EXOmotifs/zipcode-like sequences and RNA-binding adaptors (e.g., hnRNPA2B1), or by fusing cargo to EV-associated membrane scaffolds [107]. This route typically improves cargo protection and batch-to-batch consistency of “true” loading, but it requires stable producer engineering and can unintentionally alter EV composition, yield, or baseline signaling. Endogenous loading therefore benefits from paired assays that report particle-normalized potency and unintended bioactivity (e.g., microglial activation signatures for MSC-EVs) that could confound interpretation [106,107].

EV–lipid nanoparticle hybrids and related systems

Hybrid EV–lipid nanoparticle (EV–LNP) designs, including EV membrane cloaking of LNP-like cores, EV–lipid fusion constructs, and cubosome-like systems, are most defensible when the indication requires high nucleic-acid payloads or escape efficiencies that native EVs cannot provide at tolerable particle doses [108–110]. The main constraint is product identity and consistency, since hybrids can inherit immunologic and regulatory liabilities from both platforms unless formulation is tightly controlled and potency is demonstrated with delivery reporters rather than uptake metrics alone. In CNS indications, hybrids function best as problem-solving architectures for payload and escape rather than default upgrades. Exosome-mimetic nanovesicles generated by mechanical extrusion represent another adjacent architecture whose functional identity cannot be inferred from the parent cell alone; tumor-derived mimetics can alter CD4+ T-cell cytokine and transcription-factor programs in vitro, supporting direct immune-function testing [111].

Surface targeting: design rules, not decoration

Receptor-targeted ligands

Across RVG-Lamp2b, Angiopep-2, and TfR-binding systems (Section 4), consistent rules emerge: select receptors that support active endothelial sorting/transcytosis; avoid high-avidity formats that saturate receptors and increase endothelial trapping; tune ligand density to balance brain exposure against hepatic/splenic sinks; and evaluate off-target receptor expression in liver, immune compartments, and heart. Benchmarking requires matched dose and matched corona conditions, since shifts in opsonization can masquerade as targeting gains [112,113].

Fusogenic engineering

Viral fusogens (VSV-G, measles F/H) and syncytin-family proteins can increase low-pH fusion and raise cytosolic delivery in reporter systems, but they also impose the two liabilities that matter most for chronic CNS indications: immunogenicity and tropism drift (new off-target uptake sinks). Fusogenic approaches remain most appropriate as proof-of-concept tools unless replaced by minimal pH-responsive peptides or lipid motifs supported by quantified immune risk and stable pharmacokinetics.

Valency/Affinity/Spatial organization

BBB delivery is often maximal at intermediate affinity and valency: very high-affinity, multivalent binders can be retained on the luminal surface or routed to lysosomes, whereas lower-affinity/monovalent formats are more efficiently sorted into transcytotic carriers [112,113]. EV engineering should therefore explicitly control (and report) ligand copy number per vesicle, effective avidity from clustering, and whether ligands partition into tetraspanin-rich microdomains, as each can change endothelial sorting without changing “uptake.” A practical workflow is small library screening where only ligand density/format changes, with paired readouts of endothelial retention, transcytosis flux, and brain:liver ratios.

Protein corona control: the dominant layer unless constrained

Serum/CSF coronas form rapidly and can overwrite intended targeting, so buffer, protein concentration, incubation time, and biofluid source are part of the method rather than background [114]. Coronas can reroute EVs through lipoprotein, Fc, complement, and myeloid pathways, making organ exposure ratios more informative than brain signal alone [115]. These constraints parallel macrophage-targeted nanomedicine, where particle properties, opsonization, ligand recognition, and macrophage polarization determine whether myeloid uptake produces clearance, immune activation, or therapeutic reprogramming [116,117].

Safety, dosing, and manufacturability

Clinical EV products must reconcile (i) particle doses implied by low per-vesicle cargo and low-probability cytosolic delivery, (ii) innate immune routing and restriction (complement/opsonins, myeloid sinks), and (iii) Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)-compatible, reproducible production with potency assays that reflect functional delivery rather than uptake [103,104,118]. Redosing should be treated as an empirically measured property with longitudinal monitoring of complement/coagulation markers and anti-product responses, particularly for chronic neurodegenerative schedules [119]. Minimal release criteria should align with Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles (MISEV)-style expectations while being manufacturing-realistic: particle size/distribution, identity markers, contamination controls, and a potency assay tied to the intended mechanism (delivery reporter or defined immunomodulatory signature) [118,119].

Formulation and storage conditions should be incorporated into CMC assessment. Comparative EV storage studies show that buffer composition can alter particle concentration, size distribution, zeta potential, and colloidal stability after freezing, with trehalose-containing PBS preserving several stability metrics in a glioblastoma EV model. These findings support reporting storage buffer, freeze-thaw history, and post-storage potency for therapeutic EV products [120].

Experimental and analytical standards for translational EV studies

Translational EV studies most often fail at interpretation rather than execution. Assays frequently conflate surface binding, internalization, and functional cytosolic delivery, while EV identity, corona conditions, BBB model integrity, and pharmacokinetics are underreported. Because EV handling is state- and context-dependent, reproducible mechanistic and therapeutic claims require explicit reporting of biofluid conditions and model parameters [7].

Distinguishing binding, uptake, and cytosolic delivery

Effective studies pre-specify whether the objective is membrane binding, vesicle internalization, or productive delivery. Binding is separated from uptake using paired incubations at 4°C and 37°C, and receptor-module dependence is tested with competition where feasible, including soluble heparan or defined HS competitors for HSPG-linked interfaces and PS-directed competitors for PS-reader pathways [121].

Fluorescence-based uptake measurements are strengthened by eliminating residual surface signal prior to quantification, for example with non-permeant quenching or brief protease shaving. Lipophilic dyes are treated as a high-risk label because aggregation and membrane redistribution can generate EV-independent signal, so dye-only controls and rigorous post-labeling purification are reported as part of the assay [122,123].

Uptake is not delivery, and claims of functional cargo transfer are supported by delivery reporters that require cytosolic access for signal generation, including split luciferase systems, Cre–lox reporters, or protease-activatable sensors. These reporters provide the appropriate benchmark for endosomal escape interventions, where increased uptake without increased delivery is interpreted as trapping in endothelial or myeloid compartments rather than improved therapeutic flux [62].

These assay classes should not be treated interchangeably. Binding assays define surface engagement, uptake assays define internalization, cytosolic delivery reporters define access to the functional compartment, and biological activity assays define target engagement or phenotypic effect. A receptor or ligand that increases uptake but does not increase cytosolic reporter signal or downstream activity should be interpreted as improving capture, not therapeutic delivery.

Route assignment is most defensible when inhibitor-based perturbations are not used in isolation. Because pharmacologic inhibitors have pathway-overlapping effects and can induce compensatory rerouting, receptor- or pathway-focused genetic perturbations combined with at least one orthogonal assay, such as uptake stripping paired with a delivery reporter, provides stronger evidence for mechanistic claims [3,124].

EV identity, purity, and minimal MISEV-aligned reporting

EV preparations used for mechanistic or translational claims are defined beyond marker panels. Minimal reporting includes the operational definition of the EV fraction, including starting material, isolation workflow, fraction collected, particle counts, total protein, and a full size distribution for each preparation used in uptake, BBB, or in vivo biodistribution experiments [121,125].

Marker panels demonstrate EV enrichment and address depletion of common confounds, particularly lipoproteins and protein aggregates in serum- or plasma-exposed experiments. Physicochemical parameters relevant to trafficking, including zeta potential and stability in the relevant biofluid where feasible, improve interpretability because these properties influence opsonization, docking avidity, and organ sinks [121,125].

Corona conditions are treated as part of EV identity and are reported explicitly, including serum, CSF, or plasma source, concentration, exposure time, and handling. For BBB-focused programs, full size distributions are reported rather than mean diameter alone, and microvesicle-heavy fractions are avoided when conclusions depend on receptor-mediated BBB trafficking [125,126].

BBB modeling, biodistribution, and pharmacokinetic interpretation

BBB model selection is matched to the claim. Static transwells support early screening, whereas claims of receptor-mediated transcytosis and parenchymal delivery are benchmarked in NVU-relevant systems, including organoids and microfluidic BBB chips, and corroborated with in vivo tracking under controlled corona conditions [127].

Barrier integrity is confirmed before abluminal EV signal is interpreted as transcytosis. Transendothelial electrical resistance (TEER) and inert tracer permeability assays establish baseline integrity, after which mechanistic perturbations of HSPG, LRP1, TfR, integrins, or ICAM-1 distinguish receptor-mediated transport from paracellular leak. Microenvironmental parameters that shift EV handling, including flow, shear, matrix format, and pH, are reported because they alter glycocalyx structure, receptor exposure, and trafficking biases [128–130].

In vivo interpretation is pharmacokinetically quantitative. Biodistribution is presented as time courses normalized to injected dose rather than single time-point qualitative imaging, and liver and spleen are interpreted as expected sinks shaped by PS, complement, and Apo-rich coronas. Head-to-head comparisons of targeting ligands benefit from multiplexed designs, including barcoded libraries or matched formulations in the same animal, which reduce false positives driven by inter-animal variability and unreported corona differences [131–133].

Open questions and future directions

The future priorities outlined below follow directly from the disease-specific translational framework summarized in Table 1.

Quantify in vivo cytosolic delivery by cell type

Objective. Measure the fraction of administered EV cargo that achieves cytosolic access in neurons, microglia, astrocytes, endothelium, and OPCs.

Trial-enabling endpoint. A cell-type–resolved delivery reporter readout in vivo (e.g., conditional recombination/enzymatic complementation) paired with brain-region quantification to produce a deliverable metric: % cytosolic delivery per 1011 particles (or per mg EV protein).

Define disease- and region-specific HS sulfation states that gate proteopathic entry

Objective. Build an atlas of HS sulfation motifs (including 6-O/3-O patterns) across vulnerable circuits and the NVU, stratified by age and vascular/metabolic comorbidity.

Trial-enabling endpoint. A stratification-ready biomarker panel (tissue/CSF proxy where feasible) plus an ex vivo binding assay showing reduced tau/α-syn EV binding after targeted HS modulation, reported as a quantitative shift in EC50/IC50 for HS-dependent capture.

Establish how CSF vs plasma coronas re-route BBB transport versus peripheral sinks

Objective. Quantify how defined corona conditions (CSF-like vs plasma-like; Apo-enriched vs Ig/complement-enriched) change BBB uptake, transcytosis, and liver/spleen sequestration.

Trial-enabling endpoint. A dosing-normalized brain:liver and brain:spleen exposure ratio (time–activity curve or AUC-based pharmacokinetic ratio) with matched corona proteomics, enabling a prespecified go/no-go criterion (e.g., ≥X-fold improvement in brain:liver AUC).

Decouple microglial clearance programs from inflammatory amplification

Objective. Identify interventions that preserve high-capacity microglial engulfment while suppressing cytokine- and synapse-injury programs during EV/debris processing.

Trial-enabling endpoint. A paired immune activation panel (CSF/plasma cytokines + complement activation markers) and a microglial state signature (e.g., transcriptional/marker shift) with a required functional readout: preserved clearance (reduced pathogenic EV species) without an inflammatory surge.

Map saturation thresholds and off-target liabilities of BBB-targeting ligands

Objective. Determine affinity/valency-dependent saturation and peripheral capture for TfR/LRP1/ICAM-1-targeted EVs under both baseline and inflamed vascular states.

Trial-enabling endpoint. A dose–response pharmacokinetic model defining (i) the saturation dose (inflection in brain uptake vs dose) and (ii) an off-target liability index (e.g., liver/spleen AUC + hematologic receptor occupancy), establishing a safe and interpretable dosing window.

Define a minimal, platform-level rule set that predicts CNS efficacy across indications

Objective. Converge on a small set of controllable parameters: ligand density, corona state, endosomal escape tuning, and dosing schedule. These parameters reproducibly predict functional CNS delivery.

Trial-enabling endpoint. A prespecified release-and-performance criteria set linking manufacturing QC to in vivo performance (e.g., size distribution, zeta potential, corona signature, ligand copy number, delivery reporter potency), coupled to a single clinical-facing pharmacodynamic marker such as CSF EV cargo shift (target engagement) or CSF biomarker movement aligned to mechanism.

Future perspective: a five- to ten-year outlook

Over the next five to ten years, the most durable impact of EV biology in the CNS is likely to be biomarker translation, with therapeutic delivery advancing more selectively. Clinical programs will increasingly treat EVs as compartment-aware readouts of NVU and immune state rather than generic circulating signals. CSF EV cargo shifts are likely to become primary pharmacodynamic endpoints because they provide direct evidence of target engagement in the relevant compartment. Progress will depend on cell-of-origin resolution, longitudinal sampling, quantitative effect sizes, and prespecified responder definitions.

Therapeutic EV development may reach its first clear successes in settings such as glioblastoma, where EV biology is already operationalized in tumor microenvironment studies and local delivery can reduce exposure ambiguity. Early trials will likely favor local administration paired with on-mechanism endpoints, including CSF EV tumor cargo shifts, delivery-linked reporters, or intratumoral pharmacodynamic signals. Systemic repeat dosing will become more plausible only when targeting is tunable and immune sink liabilities are controlled.

Manufacturing consistency, formulation stability, and potency-linked QC will remain major constraints alongside receptor targeting. Red blood cell-derived EVs may be attractive for repeat dosing because of scalability and lower baseline bioactivity, but this requires empirical validation across lots, schedules, and complement-active conditions. Platforms are most likely to progress when they demonstrate reproducible potency, reduced peripheral sequestration, and functional delivery rather than maximal binding or uptake.

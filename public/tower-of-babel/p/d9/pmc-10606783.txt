No increased risk of intussusception after pentavalent rotavirus vaccination in China: a retrospective birth cohort using electronic health records of Ningbo city
Authors: Zhike Liu, Ning Li, Guangxu Liu, Lu Xu, Ying Dong, Ruogu Meng, Yu Yang, Siyan Zhan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSome post-licensure studies have shown a potential increased risk of intussusception following vaccination with rotavirus vaccines. This is the first study that aimed to assess the incidence and risk of intussusception within 90 days after vaccination with RotaTeq in Chinese infants. A population-based birth cohort from 27th November 2018 to 30th June 2021 included all newborns in Ningbo city. The records of intussusception were identified through the ICD-10 code K56.1 or Chinese keywords “Chang Tao” from all hospital discharge records. Each episode was confirmed in line with the Brighton criteria, and only Brighton level 1 cases were included. The association of RotaTeq vaccination and intussusception was evaluated using the Poisson regression. A total of 108,405 eligible subjects from birth cohort were eligible, with 52.30% males. Among them, 26, 847 (24.77%) infants received at least one dose of RotaTeq, and 95.52% of them were fully vaccinated, with 76, 934 doses in total. After adjudication, none of the cases occurred post first, or second dose, the cumulative number of cases that occurred 1–7, 1–14, 1–21, 1–42, and 1–90 days post third dose was 0, 1, 1, 3, and 7, respectively. Adjusting for age, sex, birth year, birth season and location, the incidence rate ratio of intussusception after RotaTeq vaccination was 0.90 (90% two-sided CI: 0.46, 1.75). Increasing age and male gender were associated with higher risk of intussusception. In summary, no increased risk of IS was observed following 3 months of RotaTeq vaccination in this study.

Introduction

Before the introduction of rotavirus vaccines, globally, rotaviruses infected nearly every child by the age of 5 years and are the leading pathogens of severe diarrhoea and diarrhoea-associated mortality in children under 5 years [1,2]. In the pre-rotavirus vaccine era, it was estimated that rotavirus resulted in more than 2 million hospitalizations and 0.5 million childhood deaths worldwide in 2000 [3]. Inclusion of rotavirus vaccines in national immunization programmes for all children has been recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) since 2009, and more than 100 countries introduced rotavirus vaccines into national routine vaccination programs [4]. The global burden of rotavirus diseases declined in the last decades with the introduction of rotavirus vaccines, in particular, with 59%, 36%, and 36% declines of rotavirus hospitalizations, acute gastroenteritis hospitalizations, and diarrhoea fatalities, respectively [5,6].

However, rotavirus is still responsible for substantial morbidity and mortality in children and adults over 70 years, especially in Asian and African countries [6]. In China, the Lanzhou lamb rotavirus vaccine (LLR) and RotaTeq vaccine (Merck & Co., Inc., Rahway, NJ, USA) were licenced in 2000 and 2018, respectively, but neither vaccine was integrated into the national immunization program; thus the first dose national coverage rate of any rotavirus vaccine was 20.3 (19.2–21.3), but only 1.8% for the third dose among children aged 6 months to 5 years, as shown in a survey conducted in 10 provinces in 2019 [7]. As of now, no publicly available data from post-marketing studies or pharmacovigilance activities of any rotavirus vaccines demonstrated the safety in Chinese children [8]. Therefore, it was important to conduct a robust evaluation after large-scale use of rotavirus vaccines to support the decision-making of immunization programmes in China.

Studies have proven that rotavirus vaccines are highly effective against severe diarrhoea, gastroenteritis hospitalizations and mortality attributed to Rotavirus [9,10]. Notably, the potential risk of intussusception after rotavirus vaccination raised concerns to the public, and WHO hence highlighted the importance of monitoring the risk for intussusception when new rotavirus vaccines are introduced into new populations [11]. Moreover, observing the incidence and risk of IS after large-scale use of RotaTeq in Chinese infants was also a request from the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE) in China.

The electronic health records (EHR) of Ningbo city have been demonstrated as a reliable and credible database for generation of real world evidence, such as health impact of climate change [12], surveillance of chronic diseases burden [13], and active monitoring of HPV vaccines safety [14]. Here, the study used a retrospective cohort design with Ningbo EHR data to assess the risk of intussusception within 90 days of RotaTeq vaccination among Chinese infants.

Methods

Data source

Ningbo is a city in the Zhejiang province and is one of the economically developed areas in China. Ningbo is located in the middle of China’s Southeastern coastline, covering more than 9.4 million inhabitants in 2021. The EHR of Ningbo was designed and set up by the Municipal Health Commission (MHC) of Ningbo in 2011 to construct a unified medical information network and improve fairness and accessibility to health services. More than 87% permanent residents were registered in the database with valid healthcare identifier, and the database almost integrated all health-related activities from birth to death in the city in 2015, including residents’ registration, maternal and children information, immunization registry, electronic medical records, death records, etc. More detailed information of the database was provided in a previously published article [14].

Study design and participants

A retrospective cohort study was conducted as RotaTeq became available in Ningbo city, from 27th November 2018 to 30th June 2021. During this study period, all infants aged 6–45 weeks and born in Ningbo city were included in this study. The native birth of infants born in Ningbo was identified using the following criteria: the birth hospital located in Ningbo city; or the first dose of Bacillus Calmette-Guerin vaccine or hepatitis B vaccine administrated within 3 days of birth in Ningbo; or the household address registered in Ningbo city. Vaccinated infants were defined as having received at least one dose of RotaTeq at age 6–12 weeks, while unvaccinated infants were from the same birth cohort as the vaccinated infants and had received at least one vaccine dose of the national immunization program (NIP) before 6 weeks of age, but without any records of rotavirus vaccine. Vaccinated individuals were excluded in the following cases: diagnosed with IS before the first dose of RotaTeq; vaccinated outside of indicated age schedule, i.e. first dose of RotaTeq before 6 weeks or after 12 weeks of age, or any dose after 32 weeks of age; received any dose of LLR or other rotavirus vaccines in addition to RotaTeq during the study period; lost to follow-up, without any recorded vaccination of NIP at age 8–9 months or until the end of follow-up 3 months after their last dose of RotaTeq, and no intussusception diagnose during follow-up. Unvaccinated individuals were excluded in the following cases: diagnosed with IS prior to 6 weeks of age; lost to follow-up, without any recorded vaccination of NIP at age 8–9 months, and no intussusception diagnose during follow-up. The cohort entry time for vaccinated subjects was the time of vaccination of any dose of RotaTeq, while for unvaccinated subjects it was at the age of 6 weeks. The follow-up for vaccinated infants ended 90 days following any dose of RotaTeq, or at the subsequent dose of RotaTeq, or at onset time of intussusception, or age of 45 weeks, whatever came first, while for unvaccinated infants, follow-up ended at onset time of intussusception, or age of 45 weeks, whatever came first.

Immunization registration and vaccination

The immunization information system was developed by the municipal Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and fully implemented covering all of 167 vaccination clinics in Ningbo city in 2005. The system almost covers the whole population of permanent residents aged below 6 years due to the high coverage rate of the national immunization programme in the city and the mandatory requirement for collecting personal and vaccination information of each dose.

All rotavirus vaccination records in Ningbo city were extracted by standard coding “2401” and “2402,” respectively for LLR and RotaTeq, as well as dose number, vaccination date, manufacturer and vaccination clinics. All other variables for both, vaccinated and unvaccinated children were collected, including individual ID, gender, birthday, birth hospital, household address, etc.

Intussusception identification and adjudication

Since the year 2015, the MHC has integrated and standardized the new and historical electronic medical records from all public hospitals and the majority of the large private hospitals in the city. Therefore, all paediatric hospitals and primary care centres are available in the study, and thus all intussusception visits were presumably captured in the database. The records of intussusception were identified through ICD-10 codes or Chinese keywords “Chang Tao” from related clinical settings such as outpatient, emergency, hospitalization, and radiological examinations, enema treatments, and surgery notes. More detailed descriptions of radiological findings, enema process, and surgical results were extracted for further case validation.

The remaining suspected cases aged 6–45 weeks in the analysis population were adjudicated by the Adjudication Committee (AC) that was consisted of three senior paediatricians. The standard operating procedure for cases adjudication was formulated in line with Brighton criteria. Accordingly, two AC members independently reviewed each case and classified it as Brighton level 1, negatively determined, or undetermined. The cases that met the definition of level 1 intussusception according to the Brighton criteria were judged as Brighton level 1 cases, while those that did not meet this definition were judged as negatively determined, and other cases that couldn’t be judged due to insufficient information were classified as undetermined. In case of disagreement, a meeting was held with the committee chairman to reach final agreement. The vaccination history of all potential cases was blinded throughout the above processes.

The individual intussusception episode was defined as one single episode if all medical records of the intussusception case were obtained within 7 days. The onset date of the episode was defined as the earliest date amid all related diagnostic and treatment visits. Only the first episode of Brighton level 1 case for each subject was included in the final analysis.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages, and for group comparison Chi-square test was used. Continuous variables were expressed by either mean values with standard error if the normal distributed, or as median values with interquartile ranges; for group comparison ANOVA (analysis of variance) or Kruskal–Wallis test were used. Incidence rate of intussusception and its 95% confidence interval was calculated using the exact Poisson method. In the Poisson model, the risk period was defined as periods post any dose of Rotateq vaccination, from day 0 to day 90. While control period was only contributed by un-vaccinated infants, from age of 6 weeks to the end of follow-up One-sided hypothesis test was applied to association analyses between RotaTeq and intussusception using the Poisson regression. Hypothesis was assumed that the relative risk of intussusception in vaccinated infants compared to unvaccinated infants would not be higher than 2.0. The estimation of incidence rate ratio (IRR) was adjusted for age groups, sex, year of birth, season of birth, and location. The necessary sample size was sufficient to reach 80% statistical power to detect IRR of IS of 2.0 or higher in vaccinated infants compared to unvaccinated infants. Moreover, a post-hoc analysis was conducted for the estimation of incidence and risk of intussusception by subgroups such as different doses and post periods of 1–7, 8–21 and 21–90 days. The data management plan was completed before accessing the database, and statistical analysis plan was completed before conducting any analysis in advance to ensure the quality and transparency of data processing. All analyses were performed using the statistical software SAS (version 9.2 or higher).

Ethical considerations

The research has been conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice Guidelines, and local laws, rules, and regulations. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Peking University of Health Science Center (IRB00001052-20024, IRB00001052-20102) with a waiver for informed consent because all related data had been anonymized, de-identified, and are only accessible in an isolated local network.

Results

Selection of analysis population

The flowchart of inclusion and exclusion of study population is presented in Figure 1. There were 190,364 infants born from 16 Oct 2018 to 12 Aug 2020 and registered in Ningbo immunization registry. After applying exclusion and inclusion criteria, the analysis population comprised 108,405 eligible subjects in total, including 26,847 vaccinated infants with Rotateq and 81,558 un-vaccinated infants. In addition, the comparison of baseline characteristics between the study population and the analysis population is attached in Supplement Table 1, and no obvious differences were observed between the two populations. Among infants vaccinated with RotaTeq, 95.52% were fully vaccinated, and the median age (interquartile range) at vaccination of dose 1, dose 2, and dose 3 was 10 (9,11) weeks, 15 (14,17) weeks, and 21 (19,24) weeks, respectively. The proportion of infants vaccinated was highest in the year 2020 with 57.29%, followed by year 40.82% in the year 2018. A similar trend was also seen for the total number of doses.

Adjudication of intussusception

A total of 187 infants aged 6–45 weeks with 191 episodes of intussusception were identified in the analysis population and were available for adjudication. Some of the children had more than one episode, however only the first episode of Brighton level 1 was counted. As a result, 74 potential cases (75 episodes) were confirmed as Brighton level 1, while 90 potential cases (93 episodes) were validated as non-cases. There were 23 undetermined cases (23 episodes) due to incomplete data for adjudication. Among the Brighton level 1 cases, 21 cases occurred in the vaccinated cohort, and 7 of them within the 90 days following vaccination of RotaTeq, but no cases were observed after dose 1 and dose 2. The cumulative number of cases that occurred in the periods 1–7, 1–14, 1–21 and 1–42 days of dose 3 was 0, 1, 1 and 3, respectively.

Discussion

This is the first retrospective cohort study in China that actively monitored the incidence and risk of intussusception following large-scale administration of RotaTeq. Overall, the birth cohort included 108,405 eligible subjects, 26,847 (24.77%) vaccinated infants who had received an overall of 76,934 doses. The overall point estimate of the incidence rate of IS was similar in the vaccinated (55.59/100,000 person-years) and the unvaccinated cohort (86.64/100,000 person-years). After adjusting for other variables, no risk of intussusception after RotaTeq vaccination was observed, with an IRR of 0.90 (90% 2-sided CI 0.46, 1.75). Therefore, the study results do not suggest an increase in the incidence rate of intussusception following RotaTeq vaccination among Chinese infants, which may contribute to address concerns about the safety profile of the Pentavalent rotavirus vaccines in China.

Globally, the background incidence of intussusception significantly varies between countries, regions, and populations [15]. According to previous hospital-based studies on incidence of intussusception in China, the incidence among infants age <12 months was 79.3, 82.2, 108.0, 122.1, and 257.7 per 100,000 in Jinan [16], Taiwan [17], Hong Kong [18], Chenzhou and Kaifeng [19], respectively, but yet no study reported incidence rate based on person-years. Nevertheless, the incidence of IS in previous studies seems similar to the incidence of IS in our study (86.64 per 100,000 person-years, 95%CI: 64.90–113.32). Consistent with our results, early studies suggested that the occurrence of intussusception among children peaked at the age of 5–9 months, and males had a higher risk of intussusception than females [20]. Besides, a seasonal trend of intussusception remains controversial, and none was shown in our study.

The risk of intussusception after rotavirus vaccination generally varies by designs, locations, and populations. A systematic review and meta-analysis (SR) of clinical vaccine trials indicated that no increased risk of intussusception among RotaTeq recipients compared with placebo recipients were observed for up to 2 years [21]. However, an increased risk of IS after vaccination with rotavirus vaccines was observed in previous post-licensure studies. A SR of cohort and case–control studies in 2017 demonstrated that there was an increased risk of intussusception with vaccination of rotavirus vaccine, including RRV-TV, RV-1 and RotaTeq, in 6 cohort studies within the first 7 days post first dose (RR:3.71, 95% CI: 1.08-12.69), and all doses (RR:3.47, 95%CI: 1.23-9.78), as well as in case–control studies [22]. Another SR for self-controlled studies in 2017 showed higher risk of intussusception within 7 days following the first dose of RotaTeq (RR:4.60, 95%CI: 3.07-6.88), and the second dose of RotaTeq (RR: 1.65, 95%CI: 1.17-2.34) [23]. It should be noted that the SR of post-marketing studies mainly included studies conducted with American, European and Australian populations. The potential association between RotaTeq and intussusception has seldom been investigated in Asian populations [8], and not yet reported in African population. In Asia, the incidence of intussusception after any dose of RotaTeq was not higher than the background risk in China Taiwan province [24], which is consistent with our study, but contrasted with the above cited SR.

To date, the aetiology and mechanism of intussusception remains poorly elucidated. Several risk factors have been evaluated or discussed in previous studies, including several viral pathogens. Adenovirus has been consistently associated with intussusception, but no relationship was observed between wild-type rotavirus and intussusception [25]. Moreover, previous studies from the post-vaccine period found an association of intussusception with rotavirus [26] and rotavirus gastroenteritis [27]; however, the researchers were unable to distinguish between wild-type and vaccine rotavirus strains. Further, the poorly defined mechanism for the potential increased risk of intussusception associated with rotavirus vaccines should be investigated.

Our study has some limitations. Firstly, there were 23 unconfirmed cases that could not be adjudicated due to limited information; the proportion of these cases are comparable between vaccinated and nonvaccinated cohorts. Thus, this could incline the results towards the null hypothesis and would not change the direction of the association. Secondly, intussusception symptoms such as abdominal pain or bloody stool could not be attained from the database. The diagnose date of these cases may therefore be registered later than the actual time of symptom onset. However, considering the acute nature and severe presentation of IS, which requires urgent medical treatment with reduction (nonoperative or operative), the onset date should be very close to or on the same day as the diagnosis date. Thus, using the diagnosis date of IS cases instead of the onset date was expected to have a very limited impact on the study results. Thirdly, the proportion of infants who were lost to follow-up was lower in the vaccinated infants than in unvaccinated infants. Considering that the same definition and capture method of IS cases was used in the two cohorts, the loss to follow-up and the outcome of IS could be considered independent. Therefore, the differential loss of follow-up between two cohorts might slightly underestimate the RR in our study.

In conclusion, no increased risk of IS was observed following 3 months (90 days) of RotaTeq vaccination in this study.

Intramedullary Spinal Cord Metastasis from Salivary Duct Carcinoma of the Submandibular Gland: A Case Report
Authors: Katsuyoshi Ito, Norihiro Isogai, Yutaka Sasao, Masaki Nagashima, Shigeto Ebata, Haruki Funao, Mitsuru Yagi
Published: 2026

Introduction

Salivary gland carcinoma is a relatively rare disease, accounting for approximately 3%-5% of head and neck cancers and 0.3% of all cancers1). Submandibular gland carcinoma represents roughly 20% of salivary gland cancers, and among submandibular gland tumors, 5%-10% are classified as salivary duct carcinoma, which represents a high-grade subtype2). Intramedullary spinal cord metastasis (ISCM) is an uncommon clinical entity, occurring in only 0.1%-0.4% of all patients with cancer. Most cases have been reported to arise from breast or lung cancers3). The clinical outcome and optimal management of ISCM arising from rare cancers such as submandibular gland carcinoma remain unclear.

Here, we report an extremely rare case of ISCM originating from salivary duct carcinoma of the submandibular gland that was treated with surgical intervention.

Case Presentation

A 73-year-old individual presented with severe right upper limb pain, gait disturbance, and right lower limb weakness. The individual had been diagnosed with salivary duct carcinoma of the left submandibular gland one year before the first visit to our department. Pathological findings showed positivity for both human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 and the androgen receptor. The individual then underwent complete surgical resection, followed by chemotherapy including trastuzumab and docetaxel according to a previous report4), as well as radiotherapy. Although the androgen receptor was positive and the efficacy of androgen-deprivation therapy has been reported5), this therapy was not covered by the national insurance system; thus, the patient declined this treatment option.

Two months after completing chemotherapy with a molecular targeted agent, which initially achieved complete remission, the individual developed neurological symptoms and was referred to our orthopedic department. On initial neurological examination, manual muscle testing revealed the following: the iliopsoas, 3/5; the quadriceps, 4/5; and the tibialis anterior, 3/5, with weakness more pronounced in the right lower limb. The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) score for the right upper limb was 90 mm. Sensory disturbance of more than 6/10 was noted in the right upper limb, whereas no sphincter dysfunction was observed. The preoperative Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) score for cervical myelopathy was 11 out of 17 points. T2-weighted axial cervical magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed severe spinal cord compression due to a mass lesion at the C6 level with low to high signal intensity (Figure 1A). The mass was located dorsally within the spinal cord, exhibiting mixed low signal intensity on T1-weighted images, with evident contrast enhancement on the sagittal plane (Figure 1B and C). Based on the clinical and radiological findings, a diagnosis of ISCM from salivary duct carcinoma was suspected.

T1-weighted sagittal image showing a low signal intensity intramedullary lesion at the C6 level (B). Gadolinium-enhanced sagittal image demonstrates an intramedullary lesion with contrast enhancement (C).

MRI: magnetic resonance imaging

Treatment options for severe pain included surgery, radiotherapy, and intrathecal analgesic therapy. Although radiotherapy was considered potentially effective, inadequate pain control could have resulted in post-irradiation adhesions, thereby making subsequent surgical intervention more challenging6). Intrathecal analgesic therapy can offer potent analgesic effects for refractory cancer-related pain; however, it carries a risk of neurological deterioration in patients with intraspinal lesions due to spinal canal compression7). Therefore, surgical intervention was chosen after thorough multidisciplinary discussion. A laminectomy from C5 to C7 was performed. No extradural lesion was detected. Upon opening the dura, a cauliflower-like mass was observed protruding from the right dorsal aspect of the spinal cord. The tumor was firmly adherent to the spinal cord, and it was impossible to safely separate the tumor from the spinal cord. Therefore, the extramedullary component of the tumor was resected. Histopathological findings confirmed the diagnosis of metastatic salivary duct carcinoma (Figure 2).

Immunohistochemical staining for HER2 showing positive expression, consistent with metastasis from salivary duct carcinoma (B).

The patient's right upper limb pain markedly reduced after surgery, with the VAS score decreasing from 90 mm to 20 mm. However, there was no significant improvement in motor function, and the postoperative JOA score remained at 11 points. Chemotherapy and craniospinal irradiation were initiated postoperatively; however, they were discontinued one month after surgery due to treatment-related pancytopenia. The patient died 3 months later due to progression of the primary disease, although no further neurological deterioration was observed during this period. Discussion

The prognosis for patients with ISCM is extremely poor, with reported survival times of approximately 7 months, regardless of primary tumor type8). Recently, ISCM has been increasingly encountered in clinical practice, with a growing number of reports describing ISCM originating from previously unreported primary cancers9,10). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first reported case of ISCM originating from salivary duct carcinoma of the submandibular gland.

MRI findings are essential for differential diagnosis from other diseases. The present lesion demonstrated imaging features characteristic of typical ISCM, including T2 hyperintensity, T1 iso-intensity, distinct contrast enhancement, an eccentric axial location, and a well-circumscribed focal morphology. In addition, the absence of cystic change or intratumoral hemorrhage, together with disproportionately extensive peritumoral edema and enhancement relative to the small lesion size, represented a key radiological hallmark of ISCM and aided in differentiating metastatic lesions from primary intramedullary tumors11). Furthermore, the coexistence of systemic metastases or a known primary salivary duct carcinoma supported the diagnosis.

Although adequate pain control was achieved in this case, it remains difficult to conclude that surgical intervention contributed meaningfully to overall survival. The prognosis of ISCM remains poor even when appropriate treatment, including radiotherapy, is administered12). Given the uncertain prognosis and the lack of established optimal treatment strategies for ISCM arising from salivary duct carcinoma, additional cases and further studies will be required to clarify the therapeutic value of surgery in improving clinical outcomes. This case underscores the dismal prognosis associated with ISCM, while also demonstrating that surgical intervention may provide effective pain relief despite limited survival benefit.

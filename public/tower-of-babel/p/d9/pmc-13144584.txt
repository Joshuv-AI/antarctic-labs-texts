Transforming Growth Factor-Beta 1 as a Predictive Biomarker for Radiation-Induced Lung Toxicity and Treatment Response in Lung Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Authors: Li-Chun Feng, Che-Ming Lin, Ying-Chi Tseng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeRadiation-induced lung toxicity (RILT) and variable treatment responses limit the effectiveness of radiation therapy in patients with lung cancer. Transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) has emerged as a potential predictive biomarker, but inconsistent findings across studies have limited its clinical implementation.Methods and MaterialsWe conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines, searching PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library through August 2025. Studies investigating TGF-β1 as a predictor of RILT or treatment response in patients with lung cancer receiving radiation therapy were included. Quality assessment used the Quality in Prognosis Studies tool. Random-effects models calculated pooled estimates for area under the curve, mean differences, and risk ratios.ResultsNine prospective studies encompassing 857 patients with lung cancer were included. TGF-β1 demonstrated good predictive performance for RILT with a pooled area under the curve of 0.74 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.66-0.82). Patients developing RILT showed significantly higher TGF-β1 ratios compared with those without toxicity (mean difference, 0.89, 95% CI, 0.67-1.11, P < .0001). A TGF-β1 ratio threshold >1 identified patients at 4.51-fold increased risk of adverse outcomes (95% CI, 2.95-6.90, P < .0001), with particularly strong performance for RILT risk stratification (risk ratio [RR], 5.29, 95% CI, 2.53-11.07). Preliminary evidence from 1 study suggests potential utility for predicting treatment response (RR, 4.03, 95% CI, 2.18-7.45), requiring validation in larger cohorts.ConclusionsTGF-β1 ratio monitoring enables effective risk stratification for radiation toxicity in patients with lung cancer, supporting its clinical implementation for personalized radiation therapy planning. Further prospective studies are needed to confirm its predictive value for treatment response.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately 1.8 million deaths annually and representing 19% of all cancer deaths globally.1 Despite advances in early detection and multimodal therapy, the 5-year survival rate remains approximately 10% to 20%.2,3 Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) comprises 80% to 85% of cases, with small cell lung cancer (SCLC) accounting for the remainder.4 The heterogeneity of lung cancer and its propensity for early metastasis underscore the critical need for reliable predictive biomarkers to guide individualized treatment strategies.

Radiation therapy (RT) represents an established treatment modality in lung cancer management across disease stages.5 Stereotactic body RT has established efficacy in medically inoperable early-stage NSCLC, while concurrent chemoradiotherapy represents the standard of care for locally advanced disease.6,7 However, the clinical utility of RT is constrained by 2 principal factors: intertumoral variability in radiosensitivity and the tolerance of adjacent normal tissues.8, 9, 10 Radiation-induced lung toxicity (RILT), encompassing acute radiation pneumonitis and late pulmonary fibrosis, is a clinically significant dose-limiting complication affecting a substantial proportion of patients.11,12 Severe RILT may necessitate treatment interruption and, in extreme cases, result in respiratory failure and death.13 Current predictive models based exclusively on dosimetric parameters—including mean lung dose and volumetric metrics (V20, V30)—demonstrate insufficient accuracy for individual risk stratification, underscoring the need for biologically informative markers that complement dosimetric assessment.14

Transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1), a pleiotropic cytokine of the TGF-β superfamily, has emerged as a candidate predictive biomarker in patients with lung cancer receiving RT.15 TGF-β1 regulates cell cycle progression, apoptosis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, immune modulation, and fibroblast activation.16 Elevated circulating TGF-β1 has been associated with advanced disease stage, treatment resistance, and poor prognosis in lung cancer through mechanisms including promotion of tumor cell survival, enhancement of metastatic potential, and suppression of antitumor immunity.17 Critically, TGF-β1 functions as a central mediator of radiation-induced pulmonary injury, mediating the inflammatory and fibrotic cascade that underlies RILT pathogenesis.18

Radiation-induced activation and release of TGF-β1 from tumor and normal lung tissues generate a dynamic biomarker profile that may reflect ongoing biological responses to treatment.19 Dynamic changes in TGF-β1 levels during or after RT have demonstrated superior predictive potential compared with absolute concentrations, as serial measurements account for interindividual baseline variability.20, 21, 22, 23 However, existing studies report conflicting findings regarding the predictive value of TGF-β1, with heterogeneity in patient populations, radiation techniques, measurement protocols, TGF-β1 assay methods, and endpoint definitions limiting direct comparisons and clinical translation.24,25

To resolve these inconsistencies, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to systematically evaluate the predictive role of TGF-β1 for both RILT and treatment response in patients with lung cancer receiving RT, with the objective of establishing evidence-based guidance for TGF-β1 integration into individualized RT decision-making.

Methods and Materials

Search strategy and study selection

We searched relevant publications from PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library until August 2025 using the following keywords: lung cancer, radiation therapy, treatment response, toxicity, RILT, and TGF-β1. The search strategy was ((lung cancer OR lung neoplasm) AND (radiation therapy OR radiotherapy) AND (TGF-beta1 OR TGF-β1 OR transforming growth factor) AND (toxicity OR pneumonitis OR treatment response OR biomarker)). We thoroughly reviewed all retrieved abstracts, studies, and citations. The references of original and review papers were also examined to identify additional studies. All studies in the search results were carefully screened. The study has been registered in the International Database of Prospective Systematic Reviews and conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines.26

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Studies were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (1) human studies with prospective or retrospective designs investigating the relationship between TGF-β1 biomarkers and clinical outcomes in patients with lung cancer receiving RT; (2) adult patients aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed lung cancer of any subtype at any disease stage (I-IV); (3) patients receiving RT as primary treatment, adjuvant treatment, or as part of combined modality therapy; (4) quantitative assessment of TGF-β1 levels in plasma, serum, or other biological samples, measured before, during, or after RT; (5) reporting clinically relevant outcomes, with primary endpoints including treatment response (complete response, partial response, stable disease, or progressive disease) or RILT; and (6) studies published from January 1, 2000, onward, available as full-text English-language articles from peer-reviewed journals, with sufficient data for meta-analysis extraction.

Exclusion criteria

Studies were excluded if they were: (1) case reports, conference abstracts, reviews, editorials, commentaries, and letters to the editor; (2) studies with sample sizes fewer than 20 patients; and (3) studies with inadequate outcome reporting or insufficient data for statistical analysis.

Data extraction and methodological quality appraisal

Two independent reviewers extracted data from eligible studies using a standardized form. The extracted information included study characteristics (first author, publication year, study design, sample size), patient demographics (age, gender, cancer stage), RT details (technique, dose, fractionation), TGF-β1 measurement methodology (sample type, timing, assay method), and outcome measures (treatment response, RILT). Discrepancies in data extraction were resolved by consensus or consultation with a third reviewer. The methodological quality and risk of bias of included studies were assessed using the Quality in Prognosis Studies tool, which evaluates 6 domains: study participation, study attrition, prognostic factor measurement, outcome measurement, study confounding, and statistical analysis and reporting.27 Each domain was classified as having low, moderate, or high risk of bias. Two reviewers independently conducted the quality assessment, with disagreements resolved through discussion or by consulting a third reviewer.

Statistical analysis

Data analyses were conducted with analyses from https://metaanalysisonline.com/ using inverse variance-weighted random-effects models with the DerSimonian-Laird heterogeneity estimator to account for expected clinical and methodological diversity across studies. Inclusion in each subanalysis was determined by the availability of data in the required statistical format: (1) for the area under the curve (AUC) analysis, studies must have reported a quantitative AUC value with 95% confidence interval (CI) for TGF-β1 predicting RILT; (2) for the mean difference analysis, studies must have reported mean ± SD of the within-patient TGF-β1 fold-change ratio (TGF-β1 during RT/TGF-β1 pre-RT baseline) separately for both the RILT and non-RILT patient groups—studies reporting only absolute TGF-β1 concentrations, binary categorical classifications, or ratio data solely within multivariate regression models were excluded from this analysis; (3) for the risk ratio (RR) analysis, studies must have reported binary outcome counts stratified by TGF-β1 ratio threshold. For continuous outcomes, mean differences with 95% CIs were calculated for TGF-β1 ratio comparisons between patients with and without RILT. For binary outcomes, RR with 95% CIs were calculated using the inverse variance method to compare adverse event rates between high-risk (TGF-β1 ratio >1) and low-risk (TGF-β1 ratio ≤1) groups.

Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q test and quantified by the I2 statistic, with values <25%, 25% to 75%, and >75% indicating low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively. Between-study variance was estimated using τ². Statistical significance was set at P < .05 for individual comparisons and P < .0001 for pooled effects. Publication bias was assessed using funnel plots, with visual evaluation of asymmetry around the pooled estimate.

Results

Study selection and characteristics

The initial literature search generated 530 potentially relevant articles. After duplicate removal, 188 articles underwent title and abstract screening, resulting in 16 articles assessed for full-text eligibility. Following application of the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 9 studies were included in the final meta-analysis (Fig. 1).21,22,28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34 All included studies employed prospective designs and collectively analyzed 857 patients with lung cancer receiving RT. Patient populations were predominantly male, with the majority having stages I to III NSCLC (Table 1). Two studies included mixed populations of patients with SCLC and NSCLC, whereas 1 study evaluated stages I to IV disease. Sample sizes ranged from 20 to 179 patients, with median follow-up periods spanning 1 to 24 months (Table 1).

Quality assessment of the included studies

The methodological quality of the included studies was evaluated using the Quality in Prognosis Studies tool across 6 domains of potential bias (Fig. 2A). Overall, the majority of studies demonstrated low risk of bias across most domains, supporting the methodological consistency of the included literature. Among the individual studies, Seto et al,28 Wang et al,30 Stenmark et al,31 Luo et al,32 and Ge et al34 demonstrated low risk of bias across all 6 domains.28,30, 31, 32,34 The remaining 4 studies had a moderate risk of bias in 1 or 2 domains, with none classified as high risk in any domain. The cumulative assessment confirmed that approximately 56% of studies (5/9) had low risk of bias across all 6 domains, with moderate risk primarily concentrated in domain D1 (bias due to participation) affecting 11% of studies (1/9) and domain D5 (bias due to confounding) affecting 44% of studies (4/9) (Fig. 2B). No studies demonstrated moderate risk of bias in domains D3 (prognostic factor measurement), D4 (outcome measurement), or D6 (statistical analysis and reporting). This quality assessment supports the reliability of the findings from our meta-analysis and demonstrates the overall methodological robustness of the included studies.

TGF-β1 demonstrates good predictive performance for RILT with high study consistency

To assess the predictive performance of TGF-β1 for RILT, we employed AUC analysis, which provides a standardized measure of how effectively TGF-β1 levels can discriminate between patients with lung cancer receiving RT at risk versus those not at risk for developing RILT. The meta-analysis of TGF-β1 predictive performance included 4 studies encompassing a total of 408 patients with lung cancer (Fig. 3A). The pooled analysis revealed a weighted mean AUC of 0.74 (95% CI, 0.66-0.82) for TGF-β1 predicting RILT. Individual study AUC values demonstrated moderate variability, ranging from 0.68 to 0.86, indicating fair to good discriminative performance across different populations and time periods.

Subgroup analysis by TGF-β1 measurement timepoint revealed consistent predictive performance regardless of when TGF-β1 was measured during the treatment course (Fig. 3A). Three studies measuring TGF-β1 during RT (mid-RT subgroup) comprising 327 patients demonstrated a pooled AUC of 0.75 (95% CI, 0.67-0.84, I2 = 0%). One study measuring TGF-β1 after RT completion (post-RT subgroup) with 81 patients reported an AUC of 0.69 (95% CI, 0.53-0.85). The test for subgroup differences was not statistically significant (χ2 = 0.49, P = .4825), indicating that predictive performance is comparable whether TGF-β1 is measured during or after RT.

Statistical heterogeneity assessment demonstrated excellent homogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 0.0%, τ2 = 0, χ2 = 2.1), indicating minimal between-study variance in predictive performance despite differences in patient populations, TGF-β1 measurement protocols, and study periods spanning from 2009 to 2022. This low heterogeneity supports the robustness of the pooled estimate and suggests consistent predictive performance of TGF-β1 across diverse clinical settings. The funnel plot analysis revealed a symmetrical distribution of studies, providing no evidence of publication bias (Fig. 3B).

In summary, this meta-analysis establishes that TGF-β1 demonstrates good predictive performance for RILT with a pooled AUC of 0.74, indicating that TGF-β1 has clinically meaningful discriminative ability for identifying patients at risk of developing RILT. The predictive performance remains consistent regardless of measurement timepoint, supporting the clinical utility of TGF-β1 monitoring throughout the radiation treatment course.

TGF-β1 ratios demonstrate excellent predictive performance for RILT development

While absolute TGF-β1 levels provide valuable prognostic information, the dynamic changes in TGF-β1 concentrations during RT may offer superior predictive capability for RILT. To evaluate whether radiation-induced TGF-β1 elevation differs between patients who develop RILT and those who do not, we compared the within-patient TGF-β1 fold-change ratio (TGF-β1 during RT/TGF-β1 pre-RT baseline) between the 2 outcome groups. This ratio accounts for interpatient variability in baseline concentrations while reflecting the magnitude of each individual’s radiation-induced inflammatory response.

The comparative meta-analysis of TGF-β1 ratios included 5 studies encompassing a total of 449 patients with lung cancer, with 100 patients developing RILT and 349 patients remaining free of significant toxicity (Fig. 4A). The pooled analysis demonstrated a statistically significant mean difference of 0.89 (95% CI, 0.67-1.11, P < .0001), indicating that patients who develop RILT exhibit significantly greater radiation-induced TGF-β1 elevation from their individual baselines compared with patients who remain toxicity-free. Individual study results consistently favored higher TGF-β1 ratios in the RILT group. The RILT group demonstrated substantially elevated TGF-β1 ratios across all studies, with mean values ranging from 1.60 to 2.00, whereas the non-RILT group consistently showed lower ratios ranging from 0.70 to 1.00, indicating minimal elevation from baseline levels.

Statistical heterogeneity assessment revealed moderate heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 66.1%, τ2 = 0.036, χ2 = 11.81), indicating some between-study variance that may reflect differences in TGF-β1 measurement timepoints, patient populations, and radiation techniques across the studies spanning from 2000 to 2020. Despite this heterogeneity, the consistent directional findings across all studies—with patients with RILT uniformly demonstrating higher TGF-β1 ratios—support the robustness of the overall conclusion. The funnel plot analysis demonstrated a relatively symmetrical distribution of studies, suggesting no potential publication bias (Fig. 4B).

In summary, this comparative meta-analysis establishes that patients who develop RILT demonstrate significantly higher TGF-β1 ratios compared with those who remain toxicity-free. This finding supports the clinical utility of serial TGF-β1 monitoring during RT as a dynamic predictor that may enable early intervention strategies to mitigate RILT development in high-risk patients with lung cancer.

TGF-β1 ratio >1 effectively predicts both RILT and treatment failure risk

While the continuous relationship between TGF-β1 ratios and clinical outcomes offers important prognostic information, practical clinical application requires defining specific threshold values for effective risk stratification and patient management. A TGF-β1 ratio threshold of >1 represents a biologically rational cut-off, indicating patients whose TGF-β1 levels increase above baseline during RT versus those who maintain stable or decreased levels. This binary classification enables risk stratification for both RILT development and treatment response.

The risk stratification meta-analysis included 4 studies encompassing a total of 314 patients with lung cancer, with 119 patients classified as high risk (TGF-β1 ratio >1) and 195 patients as low risk (TGF-β1 ratio ≤1) (Fig. 5A). The analysis was stratified into 2 clinically relevant subgroups: risk stratification for RILT prediction (3 studies, n = 232) and prediction of ineffective treatment response (1 study, n = 82). The pooled analysis demonstrated a highly significant overall RR of 4.51 (95% CI, 2.95-6.90, P < .0001) for adverse outcomes in the high-risk TGF-β1 group compared with the low-risk group.

In the RILT risk stratification subgroup, 3 studies demonstrated consistently elevated risk in patients with a TGF-β1 ratio >1, with a pooled RR of 5.29 (95% CI, 2.53-11.07). This finding indicates that patients with elevated TGF-β1 ratios have approximately 5.3-fold increased risk of developing RILT compared with patients with stable or decreased TGF-β1 levels. Statistical heterogeneity was low within this subgroup (I2 = 25.2%, τ2 = 0.1136), supporting the consistency of this predictive relationship across different study populations. Notably, Vujaskovic and Groen33 demonstrated perfect risk stratification with 100% sensitivity (9/9 patients with a ratio >1 developed pneumonitis) and 100% specificity (0/18 patients with a ratio ≤1 developed pneumonitis), yielding an RR of 37.00.

Regarding treatment response prediction, only Luo et al32 provided binary outcome data stratified by TGF-β1 ratio threshold with a primary endpoint of treatment response. In this study (n = 82), patients with a TGF-β1 ratio >1 demonstrated a significantly higher risk of ineffective treatment response (RR, 4.03, 95% CI, 2.18-7.45). Given that this finding derives from a single study, it should be interpreted as preliminary evidence requiring validation in larger prospective cohorts with treatment response as the primary endpoint. The study by Vujaskovic & Groen,33 while reporting that treatment responders showed decreasing TGF-β1 trajectories during RT compared with nonresponders, did not provide binary outcome counts stratified by TGF-β1 ratio threshold and was therefore not included in this risk stratification analysis.

Overall statistical heterogeneity across all 4 studies was minimal (I2 = 0.0%, τ2 = 0, χ2 = 2.92), and the test for subgroup differences was not statistically significant (χ2 = 0.31, P = .5782), indicating comparable predictive performance for both RILT and treatment response outcomes. The funnel plot analysis revealed a slightly asymmetric distribution, which may reflect the small number of included studies rather than true publication bias (Fig. 5B).

In summary, this risk stratification analysis establishes that a TGF-β1 ratio threshold of >1 provides robust discrimination for identifying patients with lung cancer at significantly increased risk of RILT development, with preliminary evidence supporting its utility for predicting treatment response. This binary classification system enables clinicians to identify high-risk patients who may benefit from intensified monitoring, prophylactic interventions, or alternative treatment strategies to optimize therapeutic outcomes while minimizing toxicity in lung cancer RT.

Discussion

Main findings

This systematic review and meta-analysis represents the first pooled evaluation of TGF-β1 as a predictive biomarker for RILT and treatment response in patients with lung cancer receiving RT. Our findings provide robust evidence supporting the clinical utility of TGF-β1 monitoring for radiation toxicity prediction, with several key discoveries that advance the field of personalized RT.

First, our meta-analysis demonstrates that TGF-β1 exhibits good predictive performance for RILT with a pooled AUC of 0.74 (95% CI, 0.66-0.82, I2 = 0%), positioning it as a promising biomarker for radiation toxicity prediction. This predictive capability surpasses traditional clinical indicators and demonstrates discriminative performance comparable to advanced dosimetric assessments. For example, Kong et al35 reported that V20 (percentage of lung receiving ≥20 Gy) achieved an AUC of 0.70 for grade ≥2 pneumonitis prediction. Similarly, Stenmark et al31 demonstrated that combining physical and biological parameters, including TGF-β1, achieved superior predictive performance compared with dosimetric factors alone. These findings suggest that TGF-β1 can synergize with existing clinical and dosimetric parameters to enhance predictive accuracy for RILT risk stratification.

Second, the comparative analysis revealing significantly elevated TGF-β1 ratios in patients developing RILT (mean difference, 0.89, 95% CI, 0.67-1.11, P < .0001) provides mechanistic insights into the pathophysiology of RILI. These findings align with the established role of TGF-β1 in promoting fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition following radiation exposure.36 The magnitude of TGF-β1 elevation appears to reflect the intensity of the inflammatory cascade triggered by radiation, with patients demonstrating ratios >1 representing a hyperresponsive phenotype prone to excessive tissue remodeling and subsequent clinical toxicity.

Furthermore, our risk stratification analysis demonstrates that a TGF-β1 ratio threshold of >1 identifies patients at a 4.51-fold increased risk (95% CI, 2.95-6.90, P < .0001) of developing adverse outcomes. This binary classification system provides a practical tool for clinical decision-making, enabling identification of high-risk patients who may benefit from dose de-escalation, prophylactic interventions, or alternative treatment strategies. For RILT risk stratification specifically, patients with a TGF-β1 ratio >1 demonstrated a 5.29-fold increased risk (95% CI, 2.53-11.07), with remarkably consistent findings across 3 independent studies (I2 = 25.2%). Based on the clear biological principle of differentiating patients with elevated versus stable or reduced TGF-β1 levels during RT, this threshold offers straightforward clinical application without computational complexity or specialized analytical tools.

Regarding treatment response prediction, preliminary evidence from a single study32 (n = 82) suggests that elevated TGF-β1 ratios may be associated with treatment failure (RR, 4.03, 95% CI, 2.18-7.45). While this finding is intriguing and aligns with emerging evidence that TGF-β1 signaling promotes cancer stem cell maintenance, immune suppression, and therapeutic resistance, the reliance on a single study necessitates cautious interpretation.37 Vujaskovic and Groen33 reported that treatment responders showed decreasing TGF-β1 trajectories during RT compared with nonresponders, but this study did not provide binary outcome data stratified by TGF-β1 ratio threshold and thus could not be included in the risk stratification meta-analysis. Future adequately powered prospective studies with treatment response as the primary endpoint are essential to validate TGF-β1’s predictive utility for therapeutic efficacy before clinical implementation for this indication.

Finally, the temporal dynamics of TGF-β1 elevation during RT provide additional insights into the optimal timing for biomarker assessment. Subgroup analysis by measurement timepoint revealed consistent predictive performance whether TGF-β1 was measured during RT (mid-RT: AUC, 0.75, 95% CI, 0.67-0.84) or after RT completion (post-RT: AUC, 0.69, 95% CI, 0.53-0.85), with no significant difference between timepoints (P = .48). Most included studies demonstrated peak TGF-β1 levels at 4 to 6 weeks during or immediately after RT completion, suggesting that serial monitoring during the early treatment phase may enable real-time risk assessment and adaptive treatment modifications. This timeline aligns with the known kinetics of radiation-induced cytokine responses and provides a practical framework for clinical implementation.38

Heterogeneity and limitations of our study

An important limitation of the AUC meta-analysis is the heterogeneity in TGF-β1 measurement timepoints across included studies, spanning from mid-RT (2-5 weeks during treatment) to post-RT (12 weeks) assessments. Although our subgroup analysis demonstrates consistent predictive performance across both temporal categories (mid-RT pooled AUC, 0.75, 95% CI, 0.67-0.84; post-RT AUC, 0.69, 95% CI, 0.53-0.85) with no statistically significant difference between timepoints (P = .4825), this consistency warrants confirmation in prospective studies with standardized measurement protocols. Future research should systematically compare TGF-β1’s discriminative ability at predefined timepoints to determine the optimal monitoring window for clinical implementation and to elucidate whether specific timepoints offer advantages for early intervention strategies.

Although our meta-analysis demonstrates robust overall findings, several sources of heterogeneity and limitations deserve careful consideration in interpreting these results. First, the moderate heterogeneity observed in the TGF-β1 ratio comparison analysis (I2 = 66.1%) reflects meaningful clinical and methodological diversity across the included studies. This heterogeneity primarily stems from differences in TGF-β1 measurement timepoints, with studies assessing peak levels at varying intervals ranging from 2 to 8 weeks during or after RT. The timing of peak TGF-β1 response may depend on individual patient factors, radiation fractionation schedules, and concurrent treatments, contributing to the observed between-study variance. Despite this heterogeneity, the consistent directional findings—with all 5 studies demonstrating higher TGF-β1 ratios in patients with RILT—support the robustness of the overall conclusion.

Second, technical heterogeneity in TGF-β1 measurement represents another important limitation. Although 8 studies employed enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay techniques, specific assay platforms, antibody clones, and detection methods varied across institutions and time periods. The Vujaskovic and Groen study uniquely used a bioassay with mink lung epithelial cells and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 promoter-luciferase constructs, which may measure different aspects of TGF-β1 bioactivity compared with immunoassays.39 These methodological differences may contribute to the observed variability in absolute TGF-β1 values and emphasize the need for standardized measurement protocols in future studies.

Third, patient population heterogeneity presents additional challenges in generalizing our findings. The included studies encompassed diverse populations with varying cancer stages (I-IV), histology (predominantly NSCLC with some patients with SCLC), and treatment approaches (3-dimensional conformal RT, intensity modulated RT, and conventional RT, with doses ranging from 45-81.4 Gy). A substantial proportion of patients received concurrent chemotherapy, which may independently influence TGF-β1 dynamics through cytotoxic effects on tumor stroma and immune regulation. Although this diversity enhances the external validity of our findings, it may also contribute to the observed heterogeneity and limit the precision of effect estimates for specific patient subgroups.

Fourth, the extremely limited evidence addressing treatment response prediction represents the most significant limitation of this meta-analysis. With only a single study32 (n = 82) providing binary outcome data stratified by TGF-β1 ratio threshold for treatment response, our conclusions regarding TGF-β1’s predictive utility for therapeutic efficacy must be considered preliminary and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. The association observed in this single study, while statistically significant and biologically plausible, requires validation in independent cohorts before any clinical application for treatment response prediction can be recommended. This represents a critical knowledge gap that should be prioritized in future research.

Finally, several important clinical factors remain underexplored in the current literature. An additional source of heterogeneity is the variation in concurrent systemic therapy across included studies. While all patients received RT, a substantial proportion received concurrent chemotherapy, which may independently influence TGF-β1 dynamics through cytotoxic effects on tumor stroma and immune regulation. The potential confounding effect of chemotherapy on TGF-β1 elevation has not been systematically examined in the included studies, and future prospective investigations should stratify TGF-β1 monitoring by treatment modality to isolate radiation-specific effects. In addition, the influence of radiation fractionation schedules (conventional vs hypofractionated vs stereotactic) on TGF-β1 kinetics warrants further investigation, as different dose-per-fraction regimens may elicit distinct cytokine response patterns.

Despite these limitations, our meta-analysis provides the most robust evidence to date supporting TGF-β1 as a clinically useful biomarker for predicting radiation toxicity in patients with lung cancer. The excellent consistency across diverse patient cohorts for RILT prediction (I2 = 0% for AUC analysis, I2 = 25.2% for risk stratification) demonstrates the reproducibility of TGF-β1’s predictive performance. Combined with the biological rationale underlying TGF-β1’s role in radiation-induced fibrosis and the practical utility of the ratio >1 threshold for risk stratification, these findings provide strong support for advancing TGF-β1 monitoring toward clinical application in personalized RT for toxicity prediction. However, claims regarding treatment response prediction should be considered preliminary pending validation in adequately powered prospective studies.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis establishes TGF-β1 as a clinically useful predictive biomarker for RILT in patients with lung cancer. TGF-β1 demonstrates good discriminative performance for RILT prediction (AUC, 0.74, 95% CI, 0.66-0.82, I2 = 0%), with a ratio threshold >1 identifying patients at 5.3-fold increased risk (RR, 5.29, 95% CI, 2.53-11.07). Preliminary single-study evidence suggests potential utility for predicting treatment response, requiring validation in independent cohorts. The consistent results across diverse populations and settings spanning 2000 to 2022 support TGF-β1’s reproducibility. Future research should establish standardized measurement protocols, investigate TGF-β1-guided adaptive RT strategies, validate treatment response prediction, and explore multimodal biomarker combinations. Integration of TGF-β1 monitoring into clinical practice can optimize lung cancer RT through personalized treatment planning and proactive toxicity mitigation.

Emergence of pneumococcal meningitis outbreak in Northern Togo in 2023, lessons learned and challenges
Authors: Agballa Mébiny-Essoh Tchalla Abalo, Essoya Dadja Landoh, Massantam Ani, Christelle Somtinda Nikiema, Essona Matatom Akara, Péléké Mawaba Hilim, Akawulu N’djao, Kodjo Aflagah, Biova Komla Akator, Léname Bounti, Tchin Darré

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOutbreaks of Streptococcus pneumoniae (Spn) meningitis are uncommon, typically more severe with high mortality. In Epi-week 4, 2023, Oti-Sud district in Togo reached for the first time, the epidemic threshold with 13 Spn cases despite the introduction of PCV13 into the routine EPI in 2014. The outbreak was investigated to describe its magnitude and document lessons learned to inform preparedness and response for future outbreaks.MethodThis cross-sectional study analyzed Spn meningitis outbreak data from Oti-Sud district (Savanes region), Epi-week 49, 2022 to Epi-week 23, 2023 to describe the attack and case fatality rates (AR & CFR), patients’ sociodemographic and clinical characteristics across the district and its three surveillance zones (Z1, Z2 and Z3). Additionally, we analyzed routine surveillance data from Savanes region, Epi-week 1, 2016 to Epi-week 17, 2019 to compare Spn and Neisseria meningitis (Nm) case characteristics and scrutinized the Spn outbreak’s After Review Action report to document lessons learned. Meningitis’ case definitions and epidemic thresholds followed WHO guidelines. We performed descriptive analysis and summarized findings using median, ratio and proportions.ResultsEpidemic threshold was reached with a weekly AR of 10 cases per 100,000 population. A total of 153 cases was notified from Epi-week 49, 2022 to Epi-week 23, 2023 representing a cumulative AR of 117.4 and was 192.6 in Z1; Z2: 55.4 and Z3: 74.7. The cumulative CFR was 7.8%(12/153), ranged from 8.1%(6/74) in females and 7.6%(6/79) in males, p-value = 0.9132 with a significant difference within zones (Z1: 5.8%(6/104), Z2: 17.4%(4/23) and Z3: 7.7%(2/26), p-value < 0.0001). Female to male sex ratio was 0.48(74/153), median age: 15 years, IQR: [11–24] and all cases were from rural area. Spn outbreak showed parallels to Nm’s. Zone-based surveillance, continuous capacity building, systematic daily data analysis and monitoring, community engagement contributed to outbreak detection and community compliance to response measures. Key bottlenecks included limited laboratory testing capacity, absence of strain sequencing, incomplete routine vaccination records, and lack of sequelae data.ConclusionSpn meningitis outbreak emergence in northern Togo magnitude was high with similarities with Nm’s. Defeating meningitis by 2030 requires laboratory capacity reinforcement, comprehensive and accurate surveillance data including routine vaccination and sequalae data.Keywords: Emergence, Streptococcus pneumoniae, lessons learned, Togo

Background

Meningitis is characterized by inflammation of the meninges and subarachnoid space, which may extend to the cerebral cortex and parenchyma, and can result from bacterial, viral, parasitic, or fungal infections, as well as drug reactions or other pathological conditions [1–4]. Bacterial meningitis represents the most severe form, frequently associated with outbreaks and epidemics, high case-fatality rates, and serious sequelae, depending on disease severity and the quality of care provided [5–8]. Despite timely treatment, mortality from bacterial meningitis remains substantial, ranging from 5% to 10% or higher, with deaths occurring within 24 to 48 h of signs and symptoms onset [9–13]. Among survivors, cerebral complications occur in approximately 5% to 20% and may result in partial or complete hearing loss and cognitive impairment, leading to significant social, economic and emotional consequences at both individual and community levels [8, 14–19].

Globally, meningitis cases increased from 2.50 million in 1990 to 2.82 million in 2016 whereas deaths declined from 403,012 to 318,400 with more than three-quarters occurring in meningitis belt countries [20]. Estimates peaked in 2017 and subsequently declined by 2019, alongside a substantial reduction in disability-adjusted life years [21–23]. Despite this progress, the overall impact of meningitis control efforts has been less pronounced than for other vaccine-preventable diseases [24, 25].

The principal bacterial meningitis aetiologies include Neisseria meningitidis (Nm), Streptococcus pneumoniae (Spn), and Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) [8, 26–29]. The majority of outbreaks and epidemics in the meningitis belt described by Lapeyssonnie have been associated to Nm serogroups A, W, C, and B [30–34]. Historically, meningitis epidemics emerged in West Africa in the early twentieth century in Ghana in 1906 and 1919 followed by Burkina Faso, Niger and Nigeria in 1921 [34]. Between 1993 and 2012, 80% of meningitis epidemics were attributable to NmA. Furthermore, between 1905 and 2016, countries within the meningitis belt accounted for 61% of all reported global epidemics involving Nm serogroups A, B, C, W135 and X [34].

In Togo, meningitis epidemics reported between 1905 and 2022 were predominantly attributable to NmA [34–36].

The implementation of MenAfriVac® mass vaccination campaigns in 2010 and its incorporation in routine immunization resulted in a notable reduction in NmA cases and outbreaks alongside the concurrent emergence of cases and outbreaks due to NmX, C and W [32–41]. Cerebrospinal fluid microbiological data from epidemiological week 52 in 2014 (Epi-week 52, 2014) to Epi-week 35, 2023 in the meningitis belt showed the disappearance of NmA and a gradual, sometimes predominant, increase in Spn meningitis, particularly among individuals born before PCV13 introduction [42–51]. A similar trend was observed in Togo, with increasing and occasionally predominant pneumococcal meningitis cases in the Savanes region (2016–2019) [52] and the Plateaux region (2018–2020) [53].

Historically, Spn was not considered a major cause of meningitis outbreaks and sporadic cases were reported in high-risk settings in developed countries [54, 55]. However, studies in West Africa in three health districts in Bobo Dioulasso in Burkina Faso from 2002 to 2005 and in Ghana in 2003 showed a substantial pneumococcal burden, including Spn meningitis epidemics with high fatality rates [28, 56]. A review from 2000 to 2018 identified multiple potential pneumococcal meningitis epidemics across Africa, highlighting its emerging epidemic role [27].

This changing meningitis epidemiology landscape prompted the development of the WHO’s global strategy “Defeating meningitis by 2030,” launched in 2021. The strategy aims to eliminate epidemics, reduce vaccine-preventable meningitis cases by 50%, decrease deaths by 70% and reduce disability and improve quality of life of all individuals affected by any form of meningitis through five pillars focused on prevention, care, surveillance, support, and advocacy [8].

In Togo, meningitis control is integrated into national health policies and aligned with international recommendations. The country has been implementing the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) recommended by WHO since 1998 and conducted multiple reactive vaccination campaigns against the epidemics. In 2014, Togo, along with other countries in the meningitis belt, conducted a national meningitis vaccination campaign targeting people aged 1 to 29 using the MenAfriVac® vaccine. This initiative was, followed by the introduction of PCV13 in the routine EPI and the establishment of case-based surveillance (CBS) for bacterial meningitis. Additional measures included strengthening laboratory and human resource capacity, pre-positioning supplies and antibiotics, and enhancing community communication. Surveillance data from 2014 to 2023 showed elimination of NmA outbreaks associated concomitantly with an emergence of outbreaks due other Nm serogroups notably NmX, NmC and NmW [33–41].

From Epi-week 1–6, 2023, the Oti-Sud district notified 68 meningitis suspected cases of which 13 were laboratory confirmed for Spn only. The epidemic threshold was reached in Epi week 4 with 13 cases. The occurrence of this initial Spn meningitis outbreak, typically associated with a high fatality [22, 28, 56], represents a critical public health alert and provide a unique opportunity to document its epidemiological and clinical characteristics, to capitalize on insights gained from the response, and to disseminate lessons learned. Such efforts will contribute to addressing existing gaps in the documentation of meningitis cases and their impacts, as emphasized in the global roadmap to defeat meningitis by 2030 [8]. The lessons learned and best practices identified may also inform strategies to strengthen preparedness and response to future meningitis outbreaks and support the global meningitis elimination agenda.

Method

Study setting

Oti-Sud is one of the seven districts in the Savanes region (Fig. 1), with its administrative capital, Gando, located approximately 538 km northeast of Lomé, the capital of Togo, and 115 km southeast of Dapaong, the regional capital. The district shares borders in the north and northwest with the Kpendjal and Oti districts, in the south and southwest with the Kéran and Dankpen districts, in the east with Benin and in the west with Oti district and Ghana. The climate is characterized by a prolonged dry season from November to May and a rainy season from June to October, with marked seasonal variations in temperature, wind, and humidity. Average monthly temperatures range from 20 °C to 39 °C, and the dry season is influenced by the Harmattan wind, which is associated with low humidity and increased incidence of acute respiratory and oronasopharyngeal infections.

Map of Togo illustrating the health regions and districts, highlighting the Oti-Sud and other health districts of the Savanes region, Pneumococcal meningitis outbreak, 2023

The Oti-Sud district is characterized by predominantly flat terrain, encompassing the basins of the Oti and Koumongou rivers, and vegetation typical of Sudanian shrub and tree savannah with gallery forests along waterways. The district comprised 11 health facilities, including one district hospital serving as the referral center and 10 peripheral care units. Approximately one third of the population resides within 5 km of a health facility. Routine immunization services are delivered through fixed and outreach strategies at all health facilities, following the Reach Every District/Child (RED/C) approach, with performance monitored monthly at facility and community levels and funded by contributions from the government, partners, and communities. Community participation includes covering transportation costs for eligible children and the purchase of immunization cards. In 2023, the administrative coverage for the third dose of PCV13 was reported as 137.6% in 2021, 149.4% in 2022, and 102% in 2023.

The district operates a semi-passive and semi-active meningitis surveillance system based on CBS, requiring immediate investigation and reporting of all suspected cases. To enhance case detection and ensure timely response, the district was divided into three surveillance zones, each covering more than 30,000 inhabitants. Zone 1 (Z1) covered 54,011 inhabitants, while Zones 2 (Z2) and 3 (Z3) included populations of 41,516 and 34,828, respectively. Alert and epidemic thresholds were set at 3 and 10 cases per 100,000 population per week, respectively [9, 57]. Under routine conditions, lumbar puncture for cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) collection is performed exclusively at the district referral hospital in Gando. Laboratory capacity for biological confirmation is not available locally and samples are referred to Dapaong (115 km north) or Kara (120 km south).

Study design, period, population and data sources

This was a retrospective descriptive cross-sectional study conducted from December 2023 to December 2024. The primary analysis focused on Spn meningitis cases notified in Oti-Sud district from Epi-week 49, 2022 to Epi-week 23, 2023 to estimate the outbreak magnitude and describe the characteristics of the affected population. We also analysed historical meningitis surveillance data from the Savanes region (including Oti district) from Epi-week 1, 2016 to Epi-week 17, 2019 for comparison between Spn and Nm characteristics and scrutinized the Spn outbreak’s After-Action Review report to draw lessons learned.

Variables definition

The definitions of suspected, probable and confirmed cases were adapted from the national IDSR and the WHO guidelines [9, 57]. The community definition of a meningitis cases was an operational definition designed to facilitate the early case detection by Community Health Workers (CHWs) and other community leaders.

Suspected case of meningitis

A person of any age who presented with fever (Axillary temperature: >38 °C or rectal temperature: >38.5 °C) associated with stiffness of the neck or another meningeal sign such as bulging fontanelle in infants [57] in the Oti-Sud district from Epi-week 49, 2022 to Epi-week 23, 2023 or in the Savanes region from Epi-week 1, 2016 to Epi-week 17, 2019.

Probable case of meningitis

Any suspected case in which the CSF appears turbulent, opaque or purulent on macroscopic examination; or in which the leukocyte count is greater than 10 cells/mm3; or in which Gram-positive Diplococcus (PGD) was identified by Gram staining, or in which the CSF was tested positive for antigens by the latex agglutination test. In infants, it was a CSF leukocyte count > 100 leukocytes/mm3, or a CSF leukocyte count between 10 and 100 leukocytes/mm3 associated with either elevated protein (> 100 mg/dl) or decreased glucose (< 40 mg/dl) [57].

Biologically confirmed case

any suspected or probable case in which culture or PCR identification isolated Streptococcus pneumoniae or Neisseria meningitis in the CSF [57].

Community case definition of meningitis

Any person in the Oti-Sud district who presented between Epi-week 49, 2022 and Epi-week 23, 2023, or any person in the Savanes region who presented between Epi-week 1, 2016 and Epi-week 17, 2019 with a hot body or fever with neck pain or stiffness or headache or agitation or vomiting or shielding from light. It was also any child under two years old in the Oti-Sud district or in the Savanes region who presented within the same periods a hot body with unexplained crying or screaming, or a capped gaze, or a bulging fontanel. The definition also included any person from the Oti-Sud district or the Savanes region who died, respectively, between Epi-week 49, 2022 and Epi-week 23, 2023 or between Epi-week 1, 2016 and Epi-week 17, 2019 from an illness for which verbal autopsy revealed signs of fever or a hot body with neck pain or stiffness or headache or agitation or vomiting or shielding from light or bulging fontanel.

Socio-demographic characteristics

Age, sex, place of residence, village of residence, socio-professional category, surveillance zone.

Clinical features

Signs and symptoms, consultation timeline, notification timeline, evolution, CSF collection, laboratory results, case classification.

Case confirmed by epidemiological link

Any suspected or probable case of meningitis residing in the same geographical area with a biologically confirmed case.

Attack rate (AR)

Defined as the cumulative or weekly incidence of meningitis cases, calculated by dividing the number of new cases by the population at risk and multiplying by 100,000. The population denominator corresponded to the projected population 2023 estimates provided by the National Institute of Statistics, Economic and Demographic Studies (INSEED) based on Togo’s Fifth General Population and Housing Census (RGPH-5).

Case fatality rate (CFR)

Represents the proportion of deaths among reported cases during a specific period and is calculated by dividing the number of deaths by the number of cases and multiplying by 100.

Data collection and tools

Following confirmation of the first suspected meningitis cases, a rapid response team visited all the eleven Health facilities in the district to review consultation registers and look for unreported suspected cases. Other cases were subsequently recorded during routine medical consultations and through active community research. Data for each patient were collected through face-to-face interviews using investigation and notification forms and later entered into an Excel® 1997–2003 line list. Medical records at health facilities were reviewed during the active search for unreported or overlooked cases. Cemeteries were also visited to identify individuals buried between Epi-week 49 of 2022 and Epi-week 23 of 2023, followed by interviews with relatives to document circumstances of death and assess the presence of signs and symptoms consistent with the meningitis case definition.

Data processing and analysis

The cases line list in Excel® 1997–2003 format was reviewed on case-by-case basis to identify outliers and missing data, which were verified and completed through follow-up interviews with patients or their parents. Inconsistencies data were also identified and either corrected or excluded. Age was recoded into predefined age groups. Descriptive analyses were conducted, with quantitative variables summarized as means with standard deviations (SD) or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), and qualitative variables presented as proportions with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). Comparisons of means were performed using Student’s t-test or analysis of variance (ANOVA), while medians were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Comparisons of proportions—including AR and CFR across surveillance zones and by sex, as well as age-group and sex distributions—were assessed using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Description of the Spn outbreak

The index case was an eight-year-old girl attending the second year of primary school, residing in Kpong village located in the Mogou health facility catchment area and within the surveillance Z1 in the Oti-Sud district. Symptom onset occurred on 31 December 2022 and was characterized by vomiting, fever, and headache.

The patient was evaluated at the Mogou health center during the night of 2–3 January 2023 and was diagnosed with severe malaria based on a positive rapid diagnostic test. She received treatment with injectable artesunate, Aspégic® (acetylsalicylic acid), and ceftriaxone, and was subsequently referred later that day to Hôpital de l’Espérance in Mango, the administrative capital of Oti health district. Biological analysis of CSF collected at Hôpital de l’Espérance did not identify any causative pathogen. The patient died on 3 January 2023.

The district alert threshold was crossed in Epi-week 1, 2023 (Fig. 2) and the epidemic threshold was reached in Epi-week 4 with 13 cases. A total of 153 cases were notified and no case was identified through tombs visits. Epidemiologically confirmed cases represented 83.7% (128/153) and were 80.8%(84/104) in Z1, 91.3% (21/23) in Z2 and 88.5% (23/26) in Z3.

Pneumococcal meningitis outbreak epicurve, Oti-Sud District, Togo, Epi-Week 49, 2022 – Epi-Week 23, 2023

The district alert threshold was crossed in Epi-week 1, 2023 with six (06) cases reported, and the epidemic threshold was crossed in Epi-week 4 with 13 cases (Fig. 2), corresponding to a weekly AR of 10 cases per 100,000 population. The outbreak persisted until Epi-week 6. Thereafter, the weekly number of cases remained below the epidemic threshold but above the alert threshold until Epi-week 9, before exceeding the epidemic threshold again from Epi-week 10 to Epi-week 11. From Epi-week 12 to Epi-week 14, case counts declined below the epidemic threshold but remained above the alert threshold. After Epi-week 14, the number of cases decreased further and stayed below the alert threshold for four consecutive weeks from Epi-week 15 to Epi-week 19. No additional cases were reported during the subsequent four weeks : Epi-week 20–23 (Fig. 2).

Within the surveillance zones, in Z1, the alert threshold was crossed in Epi-week 51, 2022 with 2 cases, and the epidemic threshold was surpassed in Epi-week 2, 2023 with 8 cases (Fig. 3), representing a weekly AR of 14.8 cases per 100,000 population. In Z2, the alert threshold was reached in Epi-week 51, 2022 and subsequently exceeded in Epi-week 2, 2023; however, the epidemic threshold was not crossed until Epi-week 19. No additional cases were reported thereafter through Epi-week 23 (Fig. 4). In Z3, the alert threshold was reached in Epi-week 1, 2023 and the epidemic threshold was crossed in Epi-week 4, 2023, Epi-week 6, Epi-week 10 and during Epi-week 12–13. No further cases were notified from Epi-week 16–23 (Fig. 5).

Magnitude and severity of Spn outbreak

From Epi-week 49, 2022 to Epi-week 23, 2023, a total of 153 cases were reported, representing a cumulative AR of 117.4 cases per 100,000 population. Within the surveillance zones, the cumulative AR was 192.6 cases per 100,000 population in Z1, 55.4 cases per 100,000 population in Z2 and 74.7 cases per 100,000 population in Z3.

The overall cumulative AR was 111.0 cases per 100,000 population in females and 124.0 cases per 100,000 population in males, with no significant difference, p = 0.4833. Within zones, the cumulative AR varied significantly from 193.0 cases per 100,000 population, to 55.0 cases per 100,000 population and 75.0 cases per 100,000 population respectively in Z1, Z2 and Z3 with p-value < 0.0001. The five villages with the highest cumulative number of cases were Wogou : 31 cases (AR: 753.7), Gando : 16 cases (AR: 147.9), Kpayo : 13 cases (AR: 667.7), Nassikou : 10 cases (AR: 486.6), Mogou : 08 cases (AR: 255.2) (Table 1).

Signs and symptoms were dominated by fever or history of hot body: 99.4% (152/153), neck pain: 88.9% (136/153) and headache: 83.0% (127/153). Discussion

The outbreak occurred in the Oti-Sud district in Savanes region in Epi-week 4, with a weekly AR of 10 cases per 100,000 population. This is the first time the exceeding of the epidemic threshold due to Spn meningitis cases is observed in Togo. Surveillance data from 2016 to 2018 revealed an increasing proportion of Spn meningitis cases in the Savanes region [52], however the crossing of the alert or epidemic threshold for Spn cases had not previously been observed. This is consistent with the emergence of an outbreak of pneumococcal meningitis, similar to the situation observed in Ghana [28] and Burkina Faso [56]. The epidemic curve displayed a bimodal pattern with two distinct peaks separated by period during which case counts fell below the epidemic threshold. Such a pattern is characterized of a human-to- human transmission epidemic and is consistent with the typical meningitis propagation. The outbreak period, from Epi-week 49, 2022 to Epi-week 23, 2023, together with the geographic distribution of cases, aligns with the classical features of meningitis outbreaks described by Lapeyssonnie [9, 29, 57], and suggests similarity to outbreaks caused by Nm.

The emergence of this outbreak reveals the population’s susceptibility and vulnerability to pneumococcal meningitis. The progressive increase in the secondary weekly AR, from 12.3 cases/100,000 population in Epi-week 6 to 13.0 cases/100,000 population in Epi-week 10 and 13.8 cases/100,000 population in Epi-week 11 reflects a rapid spread of the outbreak and corroborates the population’s susceptibility. Similar weekly incidences have been documented in Burkina Faso [12, 56]. Children under 10, who should have been protected by PCV13 introduced in routine EPI since 2014, accounted for approximately one-fifth of cases suggesting a low effective vaccine coverage probably due to challenges in logistics and funding as well as the limitations in the vaccination strategies implemented including communication strategies to enhance EPI in the district. However, because vaccination status was unknown for many patients and serotype data were unavailable, we could not reliably assess PCV13 coverage or effectiveness. Moreover, the absence of serotype information hampers the ability to differentiate between vaccine failure strategy and disease caused by non-PCV13 serotypes.

The overall cumulative AR during the Spn meningitis outbreak in Togo was either similar to or different from those reported in the districts of Brong Ahafo region in Ghana during the 2016 outbreak (Wenchi: 110.0/100,000, Tain: 143.0/100,000, Jaman North: 382.3/100,000, Nkanroza: 86.4/100,000 and Techiman: 46.0/100,000) [29] highlighting the variability in the outbreak’s propagation speed and inter-human transmissibility, likely influenced by differences in population susceptibility.

Within zones, the AR in Z1 of approximately 200.0 cases per 100,000 population was nearly four times higher than that of Z2 and about three times than that of Z3. This also, underlines the strong disparity in exposure and susceptibility within population residing in the same district. This disparity could be explained by natural susceptibility and/or low herd immunity in areas with high AR, resulting from factors that restrict access to routine childhood immunization services.

The proportion of confirmed cases reported as less than 20.0% differed from the 51.0% documented in the Kassena Nankana district in Ghana between 2002 and 2003 [28] as well as the 44.0% recorded in the Bobo Dioulasso district in Burkina Faso between 2002 and 2004 [27]. This low proportion may be attributed to the poor logistical and laboratory capacities, especially the insufficiency in diagnosis tests and other supplies at the district hospital laboratory. Additionally, deficiencies in specimen packaging and logistical challenges in transporting CSF samples from the Oti-Sud district to Dapaong, the capital city of Savanes region and to the national reference laboratory in Lomé, located 543 km away delayed bacteriological culture and PCR testing, which should be performed in real time. This finding indicates gaps in planning and preparedness within the laboratory and diagnostic pillar and could compromise the effectiveness of meningitis CBS.

The overall CFR during the outbreak in Oti-Sud District was lower than that reported for pneumococcal meningitis outbreak in Ghana [28, 56, 58]. In contrast with the low laboratory capacity, this likely reflects the district ability to manage cases, illustrating the capacity of the district, the region and the country in the preparation and readiness for case management during meningitis outbreaks. Factors that may have contributed include the pre-positioning of antibiotics during the pre-recurrence period, the early establishment of the Case management Centers in each of the surveillance zones, the development and dissemination of standardized treatment protocols, the administration of the first dose of antibiotics when the CSF collection was delayed, and outreach communication activities which have contributed particularly to a short average timeline of two days for care seeking in health facilities. Similar low CFR has also been observed in the Central African Republic [59] and in Ghana [29].

The predominance of adolescents and adults among patients can be explained by a greatly increased risk in this age group. Children under 10 would have been at less risk because of immunity acquired through PCV13 vaccination, introduced in the routine EPI since 2014. An analysis of pneumococcal meningitis surveillance data in Ghana and the Central African Republic revealed that children under 5 were less affected in areas with high effective pneumococcal vaccination coverage compared to areas with low effective vaccination coverage or those that have not yet introduced this vaccine into routine childhood vaccination [27, 60].

The analysis revealed that the vaccination status was unknown for the majority of patients, and among those with known status, less than one-fifth were vaccinated. Among children under 10 (born during the period when PCV13 was introduced in routine vaccination), approximately half had unknown vaccination status, and only three of the 14 children in this age group with a known status were vaccinated with PCV13. These vaccination records revealed twofold issue. First, the strategy for collecting vaccination status data during the outbreak investigation was inadequate, especially for children born between June 2014 and October 2022. Investigation teams did not consistently allocate sufficient time to review the vaccination registers in health centers or to request vaccination cards from parents. However, systematically combining these two sources when assessing vaccination status would have increased the probability of obtaining accurate information. Second, upstream factors that may contribute to the problem include the non-systematic recording of vaccinated children in routine EPI vaccination registers, inadequate archiving of vaccination data, and insufficient communication to parents and guardians regarding the importance of conserving vaccination cards.

Lessons learned

Characteristics of outbreaks caused by Spn and Nm in the Savanes region, 2016–2023

Between 2016 and 2023, three Nm meningitis outbreaks were notified in the Savanes region: Cinkassé district, Epi-week 5–18, 2016, Z2 surveillance in Tone district, Epi-week 1–29, 2017 and Kpendjal Ouest district, Epi-week 6 to Epi-week 16, 2017 [52]. Analysis of these outbreaks data and those of Spn outbreak in 2023 reveals similarity in both periodicity and geographical area of occurrence [55]. Comparison of the means, medians and age groups of confirmed Spn cases in Oti-Sud in 2023 with confirmed meningitis cases in the Oti district and Savanes region also indicates overall statistical similarity, with differences within certain age groups (Table 4). These findings suggest that Spn and Nm meningitis outbreaks affect people in the same proportions within the different age groups in geographical areas where MenAfriVac® mass vaccination campaigns have been organized and where vaccination against pneumococcal infections in infants has been introduced into the routine EPI.

Compared to previous outbreaks of Nm meningitis in the Savanes region, the propagation speed of the Spn outbreak in Oti-Sud district is statistically similar to that observed in Cinkassé district in 2016, with respective AR of 117.4 (153/130,355) versus 118.4 (106/89537), p = 0.9456. However, it is significantly different from those recorded in surveillance Z2 (Yemboate, Sanfatoute, Idondjoag, Bougou, Danpiong, Kantindi, Korbongou) in the Tone district in 2017 and in Kpendjal West district in 2019 with AR of 117.4 (153/130,355) versus 169.3 (137/80,912), p = 0.0017 and 117.4 (153/130,355) versus 185.9 (214/115,121), p = 0.0000 respectively (unpublished data1).

A similar pattern was observed for the CFR. The CFR during the Spn outbreak in Oti-Sud district was not statistically different when compared to that of Nm outbreak in Kpendjal West district in 2019 with 7.8% (12/153) versus 3.3% (7/214), respectively, p = 0.0651 (unpublished data2). However, it was significantly higher from those documented in the surveillance Z2 of Tone district in 2017, 7.8% (12/153) versus 0.7% (1/137), p = 0.0050 and in the Cinkassé district in 2016, and 7.8% (12/153) versus 1.9% (2/106), p = 0.0470 (unpublished data3).

Epidemiological surveillance and outbreak detection

The study found that the epidemic threshold was crossed in Epi-week 4–2023 and was notified to the relevant authorities on the same day. The capacity-building for health personnel on meningitis surveillance, particularly the knowledge and application of case definitions during the pre-epidemic season periods, as well as on the collection and analysis of meningitis data, empowered the Oti-Sud district surveillance team to identify and notify the first cases as soon as they emerged. Furthermore, the daily comparison of the number of cases to the alert and epidemic thresholds facilitated the early detection of the outbreak.

Coordination of the outbreak management

The activation of the response mode of the Emergency Operations Center (EOC) and the establishment of the Incident Management System at the central level were mechanisms that facilitated the management of the outbreak. However, the absence of management coordination procedures at operational level led to delays in developing the incident action plan and fundraising and in deploying Rapid Intervention Teams and SURGE (Supporting United Nations High Commissioners for Refugees Resources on the Ground with Experts).

On the other hand, the daily coordination meetings, along with the preparation and dissemination of the epidemic bulletin (Sit-Rep) throughout the country, facilitated the daily monitoring of the outbreak trends, identification of shortcomings, implementation of corrective measures and early alert of other districts in Togo. Additionally, cross-border meetings with neighboring districts in Ghana and Benin, and the daily data sharing, enabled border districts to activate alert mode of the EOC system and initiate the meningitis outbreak preparedness mechanism.

Mapping the district into surveillance zones

The mapping of the district into three surveillance zones also had an impact on the detection and early response to the outbreak, as it enabled data to be analyzed by zone. Zone 1 crossed the epidemic threshold in Epi-week 1 with 5 cases, whereas at district level, the six cases recorded in the same week only met the alert threshold. Zone based analysis also revealed that Z2 did not experienced the outbreak throughout the entire period, but the alert threshold was crossed in Epi-week 2–4, Epi-week 6–7, Epi-week 10 and Epi-week 14. Zone 2 accounted for a third of the 12 deaths recorded, and had the highest CFR of 17.3%, despite having the lowest number of cases and the lowest AR. Similarly, this analysis stratified by zone showed a significant higher AR in Z1, which accounted for 50% of all deaths recorded. This also revealed different periods of the outbreak’s onset and end between zones and the overall district: Epi-week 4–13 for the overall district, Epi-week 1–14 in Z1 and Epi-week 4–15 in Z3. Such zone-based analyses are applicable to the surveillance of other vaccine-preventable diseases and various events under surveillance. They facilitate the early detection of public health threats at the sub-district level, enable appropriate trends monitoring, and support the targeted and efficient allocation of resource for response.

Risk communication, community health workers capacity building and community involvement

Every year, in the early period of the meningitis season, the district’s Community Health Workers (CHWs) undergo refresher training on meningitis surveillance. When the alert threshold is surpassed, CHWs are organized into teams and trained in active surveillance and response strategies. Their participation contributed to the cases detection and notification. The impact of their efforts was noticed in the active cases research in the community, using the community definition of meningitis cases on which they had previously been trained. Their engagement also facilitated the acceptance of investigation and case management teams within the communities. This community commitment also encouraged other local leaders and the general population to embrace the recommended control measures. Communication efforts included concertation meetings sessions with village leaders, community dialogues, local messaging dissemination through local channels (town criers, local radio stations, religious communities), classroom lessons on meningitis developed and taught in schools. In addition, the official joint announcement from the District Health Director and the Prefect, the representative of the central government empowered stakeholders involved in the response, and predisposed communities to listen to and implement both individual and collective response measures.

However, the lack of a coordinated joint communication plan within partners resulted in the messages duplication with several partners disseminating the same information multiple times within the same communities, sometimes in multiple sessions per day. In addition, the weak involvement of the municipality of Gando in the preparation and management of the outbreak also limited a successful coordination, community engagement and mobilization of local human, financial and logistical resources to support effectively the response.

Deployment of rapid response teams and support staff: SURGE personnel and emergency medical team

Health personnel from other districts in the region, as well as those from the regional health directorate, teaching hospitals, and the Ministry of Health, were identified and deployed in the Oti-Sud district to support the response in active case finding and investigation, case management, risk communication, and coordination. This support has increased the district’s capacity in resource mobilization, case detection and reporting as well as coordination and information dissemination.

Case management, including infection prevention and control

For a Spn meningitis outbreak known as usually associated with high mortality [28, 56, 58] due to its virulence, the CFR less than 10% recorded during the outbreak in Oti-Sud district indicates effective case management, particularly the early start of antibiotic therapy. Treatment was based on recommended antibiotics protocols and was administered free of charge to patients. A Centre for case management was established in each of the surveillance zones, led by members of the Oti-Sud district’s Emergency Medical Team, with additional support from personnel from the Savanes region, other regions and the University of Lomé. The relatively short consultation time, less than three days for more than 85% of cases also reflects the quality of care delivered. Moreover, the cost of the transfer of patients severely affected to the Mango and Espérance referral hospitals covered by the expense of the Incident Management System (IMS) budget, further enhanced the quality of care provided.

Laboratory, diagnostic and logistics capabilities

This outbreak highlighted the limited laboratory capacities of both the district and the region in outbreak response. Cerebrospinal fluid samples were collected in 85% of cases by the case management team and Gram staining was performed on 82% of the 130 collected CSF samples. Culture was carried out for only 52 samples (less than 40%) and results were available for just 45 samples. The low availability of diagnostic tests (laboratory reagent) and other laboratory supplies, along with the limited capacity to transport samples from the district to the regional capital for culture, reflected the poor laboratory performance during the response. The PCR, the reference test for the biological diagnosis of meningitis, was performed on CSF of 80% of the 153 patients, with 20% positive for Spn. The absence of PCR real-time testing and the limited logistical capacity to transport each CSF sample within 24 h of collection, under a temperature of 4 °C, to the reference laboratory may have hindered optimal pathogen isolation [61, 62]. Furthermore, the absence of sequencing was a limitation for the genomic identification of the strain responsible for the outbreak, and therefore did not allow determination of whether its serotype was among the 13 contained in the PCV13 pneumococcal vaccine used in Togo’s routine EPI.

Quality of outbreak data

The initial existence of a line list of cases and its continuous updating provided a comprehensive database of meningitis cases, including those reported during the outbreak, and enabled to characterize the outbreak in terms of time, place and person. The establishment update of such database is a recommended practice [57] for monitoring trends. However, there remained certain key variables for which the limited availability of data precluded characterizing the outbreak based on these data. These include sequelae and hospital stay duration. Yet pneumococcal meningitis is described as more severe and associated with significant sequelae among survivor patients. The absence of data on sequelae to date limits the estimation of the burden of the outbreak in Oti-Sud district. Similarly, the lack of data on the date of discharge from the Case management Centers limits the determination of the average duration of hospitalization, the estimation of its economic and social costs and the assessment of potential differences with the average hospital stay observed during Spn outbreaks in other localities, as well as Nm outbreaks that have occurred in the Savanes region and Togo other regions.

Vaccination status was also unknown for most of cases, with one-fifth having an age corresponding to the period during which PCV13 had already been introduced in the routine EPI in Togo. This gap in vaccination data collection may have induced systematic errors in the rapid assessment of the PCV13 vaccine effectiveness and mask the possibility of low vaccine efficacy as contributing factor to the outbreak. Overall, beyond limiting accurate outbreak characterisation, poor data quality could also delay outbreak detection [63]. Comprehensive and high quality surveillance data including vaccination, clinical, sociodemographic and laboratory data are required to monitor accurately meningitis trends that will guide actions and strategies to implement or adjust to effectively contribute to reducing the burden and defeat meningitis by 2030.

Limitations

The main limitation is inherent to database-based research, as is common in secondary data analyses. Specifically, incompleteness was observed in selected variables from both the Spn outbreak investigation and the routine surveillance database. Data shortcomings during the outbreak primarily concerned routine vaccination status, duration of hospitalization, pathogen genomic sequencing, and the documentation of sequelae. The inclusion of these data would have enabled a more complete and concise description of the outbreak’s magnitude. In addition, missing data were identified in the routine surveillance database used for comparison with the Spn outbreak in Oti-Sud [52]. Nevertheless, despite these limitations, the findings from the Spn outbreak investigation in Oti-Sud, based on available data, along with the use of laboratory-confirmed cases from the routine surveillance system for comparative analysis, provide substantial and sufficient evidence to clarify the current meningitis surveillance situation and to inform key strategic decisions aimed at strengthening meningitis control in Togo.

Conclusion

The emergence of this Spn outbreak showed similarities with those caused by Nm in terms of the period, the affected geographical area and the age groups distribution of cases. It mainly affected adolescents and adults, while under 5 children were less affected.

Its magnitude varied across surveillance zones and the overall CFR was lower than reported in most of the published Spn meningitis outbreaks. No reactive vaccination was implemented and response efforts relied mainly on case management and risk communication. Among lessons learned, several good practices should be sustained, including the setup of robust databases, targeted surveillance, surveillance zones-based data monitoring and analysis, continuous capacity building, rapid activation of coordination mechanisms including EOC, timely alerting of neighbouring districts and community engagement.

Other components, such as laboratory services, however, require a comprehensive situation analysis to identify specific needs and bottlenecks in each domain. Laboratory capacities in districts and regions at risk of meningitis outbreaks should be strengthened to enable them to effectively play their role in the surveillance of bacterial meningitis during routine, alert and outbreak periods. Mechanism for systematic sequencing isolates in routine and during outbreaks is also essential to guide vaccination policy. Routine immunization must be reinforced through the “Reach Every District/Child” approach to sustain high vaccination coverage while ensuring availability of comprehensive immunization data quality, complemented by the early reactive vaccination campaigns once the epidemic threshold is crossed [64].

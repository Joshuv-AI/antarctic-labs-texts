Effectiveness of capsulodesis in ganglion cysts caused by a medial meniscotibial ligament tear and medial meniscus extrusion
Authors: Tatsuru Sonobe, Takuya Nikaido, Katsuhiro Yoshida, Takeo Suzuki, Tadashi Kikuchi, Yoshihiro Matsumoto
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importanceMeniscotibial ligament (MTL) has received attention as a major meniscus stabilizer. An MTL injury results in instability and extrusion of the meniscus. Cases of knee ganglion cyst formation due to an MTL tear and medial meniscus extrusion (ME) are extremely rare.Case presentationA 42-year-old female Japanese childcare worker presented painful ganglion cysts on the proximal medial side of her left tibia. An MTL tear and medial ME were thought to be involved in the ganglion formation. Joint fluid flowed through the meniscotibial side of the extruded meniscus into the space between the medial collateral ligament and tibia, where the MTL tear acted as a check valve, forming ganglion cysts. Ultrasonography-guided aspiration of the ganglion was attempted, but ganglion cysts recurred within 1 month. We used an open excision for the ganglion cysts, and arthroscopic capsulodesis was performed to repair the MTL and internalize the medial meniscus to block the inflow route from the intra-articular space.Clinical discussionWe performed capsulodesis, which repaired the MTL and internalized the medial meniscus to prevent the recurrence of the ganglion cyst. Three months have passed since the surgery, with no recurrence of knee pain or ganglion cysts, indicating good short-term results.ConclusionsWe encountered a rare case of ganglion cyst formation in the knee joint due to MTL tear and medial ME. Arthroscopic MTL repair and internalization of the meniscus by capsulodesis were effective in treating the ganglion cysts.

Introduction

A ganglion cyst is defined as a benign cystic mass that is surrounded by dense connective tissue and is filled with a gelatinous fluid rich in mucopolysaccharides [1]. Ganglion cysts of the knee arise from both cruciate ligaments, the meniscus, the popliteal tendon, and synovial folds, but intra-articular ganglions of the knee are rare [2,3]. Apart from ganglions, other common cystic masses in the knee include popliteal (Baker) cysts, meniscus cysts, and proximal tibiofibular joint cysts [3]. The meniscotibial ligament (MTL) has recently received attention as a major meniscus stabilizer [4]. MTL tears result in instability and extrusion of the meniscus [5]. Cases of ganglion cyst formation in the knee joint due to an MTL tear and meniscus extrusion (ME) are extremely rare. We describe a case in which arthroscopic capsulodesis was used to repair the MCL and internalize the meniscus to prevent ganglion recurrence. This work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [6].

Case presentation

The patient was a 42-year-old Japanese woman with a height of 158.4 cm, a weight of 60.0 kg, and a BMI of 23.9. She worked as a childcare worker eight hours a day, five days a week. She repeatedly flexed and extended her knees while carrying children at work.

She became aware of left knee pain during knee flexion and extension, and a cyst on the proximal medial side of her left tibia, which made it difficult to flex her left knee at work. She had no apparent knee trauma history. At a primary hospital, a knee ganglion was suspected based on physical and imaging examinations. She was referred to our hospital for surgical treatment.

Ballottement of the patella of the left knee was negative, and an elastic soft mobile cyst was palpated on the proximal medial side of the left tibia; tenderness was noted at the same region. The active range of motion (ROM) of the left knee was from 0° to 145°, and left knee pain and anxiety were caused during flexion and extension of the knee. There was no left knee medial laxity was observed under valgus stress test, either in knee extension or at 30° of flexion.

The patient's preoperative knee joint status was determined using the validated version of the Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) questionnaire [7]. Her preoperative KOOS scores were as follows: Symptoms 64.3, Pain 66.7, Activities of Daily Living (ADL) 92.6, Sports 80.0, and Quality of life (QOL) 37.5.

Plain radiographs showed no bone translucency or calcification at the proximal medial side of the tibia (Fig. 1A,B). Short-tau inversion recovery (STIR) magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed no obvious meniscus tear, but the MTL was torn and medial meniscus was extruded (Fig. 1C). Ultrasound-guided aspiration and cytology were conducted. The cytology examination revealed foam cells and blood cells in a mucus-like substance, and a diagnosis of ganglion cysts was made. The final diagnosis was that ganglion cysts had been caused by a medial MTL tear and medial ME.

Ultrasound-guided aspiration of the ganglion cysts was attempted, but the cysts recurred within 1 month. We hypothesized that the damaged MTL acted like a check valve route, allowing the influx of joint fluid to cause ganglion cysts. We also suspected that the medial ME exacerbated the loosening of the capsule. We considered it necessary to repair the MTL to block the inflow route, and surgical treatment was selected. Capsulodesis has been shown to be effective as a surgical technique for MTL repair and meniscus extrusion [4], and we considered useful in present case to block the inflow route. We planned a capsulodesis to repair the MTL and internalize the medial meniscus.

Arthroscopic findings revealed no damage to the medial meniscus (Fig. 2A,B). In addition, a drive-through sign indicating medial collateral ligament complex injury was not observed [8]. However, the medial meniscus was easily lifted off, and synovitis associated with the MTL tear was observed (Fig. 2C). The inflow route from the intra-articular space to cyst was identified (Fig. 2D). An incision was made directly above the cyst region, and a cyst covered by the MCL was confirmed (Fig. 3A). On puncture, the liquid on the jelly was drained (Fig. 3B,C). The MCL and capsule were adherent, and the boundary was indistinct. After the repair of the MCL, 23-gauge needles were placed to mark the anterior and posterior extents of the ME lesions from the outside of the knee. The needles were placed parallel to the tibial plateau and at the undersurface of the medial meniscus. The position of the needles was confirmed arthroscopically (Fig. 4A). Using the needles as guides, the drill for 1.8-mm knotless Suture Tak Anchors (Arthrex®) was used to drill holes approx. 1 mm below the joint line. The angle of the drill was oriented 10° distal to avoid violating the tibial articular surface. Three anchors were used, with one anchor placed at the anterior extent of the lesion, one placed at the posterior extent, and the remaining anchor placed midway between the anterior and posterior anchors (Fig. 4B). The anchors were linked to each other by shuttling the sutures into the adjacent anchor, creating a knotless mattress fixation (Fig. 4C). After 2 weeks of non-weight-bearing status and external fixation with the knee brace, full weight-bearing and ROM exercise with no restriction was initiated. At 3 months post-surgery, the patient had no recurrence of ganglion cyst or knee pain. STIR MRI indicated medial meniscus internalization and no cyst recurrence (Fig. 5A,B). Discussion

MTL tears are often associated with injury of the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) [9,10] and meniscus tears [5,11]. The inferior periphery of the medial meniscus is attached to the tibia with the MTL [11]. MTL tears result in instability and extrusion of the meniscus [5]. Medial ME and MTL tears may lead to increased hoop stress and a subsequent medial meniscus posterior root tear (MMPRT) [12]. An MTL tear should thus be treated to stabilize the medial meniscus and improve the medial ME [13].

The present patient was diagnosed as having an isolated injury of the MTL without the complications of an ACL tear or meniscus tear. Although the mechanism of injury is unknown, it is possible that her job as a childcare worker and her daily knee flexion and extension activities caused mechanical stress that resulted in the MTL injury.

Ganglions and cysts of knee are different origins depending on the site of onset [3]. Popliteal cysts form when a bursa swells with synovial fluid, with or without a clear inciting etiology [3]. Meniscal cysts form within or adjacent to the meniscus. These collections of synovial fluid are thought to develop from the translocation of synovial cells or extravasation of synovial fluid into the meniscus through a tear [3]. Proximal tibiofibular joint cysts are rare, and their etiology remains unclear [3]. Cruciate ligament ganglion cysts have no clear etiology but are associated with mucoid degeneration of the anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments, knee trauma, and synovial translocation into these ligaments [2,3]. Cases of intra-articular ganglion cyst formation of the knee following MTL tear and medial ME are extremely rare. Ganglion cysts are usually asymptomatic but may be associated with paresthesia or pain [14]. Approximately 50 %–60 % of these masses will resolve spontaneously without treatment [14]. When symptoms are present, these ganglions may be treated with (1) nonsurgical interventions such as aspiration, (2) open excision, or (3) arthroscopic excision [2,3,15].

In our patient's case, the trauma-induced MTL tear gradually resulted in the medial ME. We suspect that the ganglion cysts were formed by the influx of joint fluid into the space where the meniscus had extruded, and the injured MTL acted as a check valve. As noted earlier, cases of ganglion cyst formation in the knee joint due to an MTL tear and ME are extremely rare.

The initial treatment for the present patient was ultrasonography-guided aspiration, but the ganglion cysts recurred within a month. Due to her continuous knee pain, we planned ganglion excision. However, a ganglion excision alone would not close the inflow route, which was the apparent cause of the ganglion formation, and the risk of ganglion recurrence was high. We thus performed the MTL repair and internalization of the medial meniscus simultaneously. Since this case involved a multifocal ganglion with a septate wall, arthroscopic excision was considered difficult, and open excision was used.

Capsulodesis was recently reported to reduce the space where the graft extrusion may occur by pulling the stretched capsule to the tibial plateau [16,17]. Capsulodesis techniques and methods are as follows [4]. The location of the meniscus extrusion is confirmed by arthroscopy, and a knotless anchor is inserted from outside the knee joint into the tibial plateau in the extrusion area; mattress sutures of three anchor threads are used to repair the MTL and suppress the loose capsule. As a result, the joint capsule is strained and the meniscus is internalized. In the present case, the use of capsulodesis successfully repaired the MTL and improved the medial ME, which also led to the closure of the inflow route. Arthroscopic centralization could be another method that could be used for similar cases [18]. However, arthroscopic centralization may have resulted in incomplete closure of the inflow route because of point stabilization of the medial ME [18]. In the present case, we performed capsulodesis to ensure that the inflow route was closed.

An important differential disease is meniscal cyst. Meniscal cysts are divided into intra-meniscal and para-meniscal cysts [19,20]. Both intra- and para-meniscal cysts are associated with a meniscal tear [19,20], especially horizontal tears [21]. Medial para-meniscal cysts most commonly arise from the posterior horn of the meniscus, and lateral para-meniscal cysts most commonly arise from the anterior horn of the meniscus [19,20]. In our patient's case, arthroscopic findings did not reveal any meniscus tear, and we were able to distinguish it from a meniscus cyst because we identified the inflow route to the ganglion cysts.

A limitation of this study is the short duration of the follow-up. Due to the high recurrence rate of ganglions, we must continue careful follow-up.

In conclusion, we encountered a rare case of knee ganglion cyst formation due to an MTL tear and medial ME. Capsulodesis was used to repair the MTL and internalize the medial meniscus to close the inflow route to the ganglion cysts. The patient showed good short-term results with no recurrence of knee pain or ganglion cysts, and all of the KOOS subscale scores improved. Intra-articular knee ganglions associated with ME should also be noted as a cause of knee pain. Longer follow-up is needed to monitor potential future instability and degenerative changes.

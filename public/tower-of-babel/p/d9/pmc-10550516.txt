The influence of trade friction on the stability of stock market: Evidence from China
Authors: Jianna Guo, Lu Liu, Yi Tang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractStock markets are vulnerable to systemic events. This study analyzes the impact of multiple macro-characteristic events, such as trade friction, on China's stock markets. By leveraging a new event study and quantile regression method, we find that trade friction events significantly affect the stability of the stock market from six trading days in advance to five trading days after the event. The expected effects of trade friction events significantly influence the high quantile of stock market returns before these events. The low quantile of returns is affected as the news confirmation time approaches. Friction events have an impact before, and on the day of the event, and affect the stability of the stock market by influencing the low-quantile group, whereas mitigation events affect the stability of the stock market for a longer time by influencing the high-quantile group. Regulators and investors should pay attention to the impact of trade friction on the stock market. Regulators should also pay proper attention to the direction of public opinion on positive and negative news to maintain stock market stability.Keywords: Trade friction, Stock market stability, New event study, Quantile regression

Introduction

Owing to the development of information technology, political and economic events generated anywhere in the world instantly affect the value of stock markets, both nationally and internationally [1,2]. As China is the world's second-largest economy, its stock market plays an important role not only domestically, but is also an indispensable part of the international capital market [3]. The stock market is vulnerable to economic shocks, such as trade friction. Major events impact market stability and even cause systemic risks. [4] documented that efforts to reconcile the stock market responses with welfare impacts from trade models is a promising area of work. From the perspective of China, it is of great practical significance to measure the impact of trade friction events on the stability of the stock market, obtain quantitative results of the impact, and provide suggestions for regulators and investors to manage and avoid risks, which is also the research purpose of this study.

The United States launched a “Section 301” investigation against China on August 18, 2017. This trade friction made investors and the capital market more susceptible to policy uncertainties [5]. [6] showed how the economic implications of changes in the trade policy stance can be evaluated with conventional, major macro events that cause significant uncertainty in the financial market [7], and trade frictions that increase volatility in the stock market. As shown in Fig. 1, in 2018 and 2019, when Sino-US trade friction events occurred frequently, the major ones were linked to China's stock market fluctuations. The green circles show that the stock index decreased after the trade friction events, whereas the red circles show that the stock index increased after the events.

As the largest trade-participating country, China's stock market is closely related to global economic patterns and the international trade environment. Trade friction is inevitable, as industrialization in emerging countries has considerably changed owing to the division of labor and distribution of benefits [8]. The U.S.–China trade war reduced stock returns in China [9]. The tariffs imposed by the US on Chinese commodities have increased stock market risk, constrained listed companies' performance, and made trading volume changes more susceptible [10]. The Sino-US trade war has affected the Chinese and US stock markets; the adverse effects are reflected in stock market fluctuations, decline in market capitalization, market equity loss, and a reduction in prices and in stock market returns [11].

There are two types of theoretical mechanism analyses of the impact of external shocks on stock market stability: first, through market fundamentals, such as the financial market microstructure [12], market information disclosure [13], and investor structure [14]; second, by affecting investor sentiment and psychological expectations [[15], [16], [17]].

There are also certain empirical findings on the various impacts of trade friction on stock markets. The Chinese stock market's ability to maintain stability is weak, and the Sino-US trade friction will reduce this stability [18]. The negative actions of the United States and the positive actions of China exert significant negative effects on the Chinese stock market, while the positive actions of the United States and the negative moves of China have the opposite effect [19]. [20] found that the China–US trade war has caused Chinese stock markets to fall; the volatility seems to have been controlled through intervention from China's ‘National Team Investors.’ [21] studied the co-movement changes in horizontal returns and volatility of the daily logarithmic return data of global stock indices against the background of the Sino-US trade war and found that the news impact surface shows the asymmetry of the co-movement for positive and negative news on dynamic analysis.

Recent studies establish a link between trade friction and stock market stability. Most of China's coping strategies are qualitative analyses that lack data support [8]. The main characteristic of trade friction events is a period of frequency, and there is a lack of analysis methods for the characteristics of such events. Our approaches make two new contributions to the literature on the influence of trade friction on the stability of the stock market.

Our first main contribution is that we use the ‘new event’ study method to measure trade friction events, which are categorized as, “friction events” and “mitigation events.” Some studies quantified stock market responses to trade friction events [5,22]. [23] evaluated the impact of the U.S.–China trade conflict on both, Chinese and U.S. stock markets at the industry level, using a comparative analysis. [3,24], and [25] divided the sample into several periods (e.g., before and after trade friction) to explore the impact of trade friction on the capital market. The event study method is widely used in literature on the impact of exogenous events on the stock market. [8] and [26] used the event study method to quantitatively analyze the impact of Sino-US trade friction on stock market returns. They both improve the event study method and empirically show that estimates of policy-induced stock market declines imply lower capital returns, thereby reducing investment rates. The latter finding indicates that trade friction has a contagious effect on stock markets. Relevant literature using the event study method also includes [[27], [28], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33]]. A problem with the event study method and its extended model is that they do not measure the combined effects of multiple events. [[34], [35], [36]] used an event study approach that is different from the traditional one. For distinction from others, it can be called “new event study” method .[34] divided financial crises into four categories. The sample countries experienced different types of financial crises during the sample time interval. The model contains dummy variables for the types of financial crisis and their interactive terms. The interactive terms allow the comprehensive impact of non-simple direct superposition of the two types of crises that existed simultaneously over a period, which is similar in form to the DID of micro policy evaluation. Essentially, it could be used to quantify the impact of multiple events or types of events on target variables. The new event study method has solved for the impact of the multiple and multi-type characteristics of trade friction events. In this study, we used the new event study method, which combines qualitative and quantitative analyses and further divides events into “friction” and “mitigation” events according to their intrinsic characteristics. Such treatments can capture, more comprehensively, the real effects of trade friction, in contrast with previous studies.

Second, we use a quantile regression method to measure financial stability, and innovatively combining it with the new event study method. [37] provided a definition for testing the stability of financial markets from the perspective of market returns: financial market stability refers to the fact that systemic shocks have a constant impact under both, normal and extreme market conditions. Scholars find that using quantile regression to measure financial stability is applicable to the definition proposed by Baur [38,39]. Our approach supplements the literature by combining the new event study method with a quantile regression model to measure the impact of Sino-US trade friction on the stability of China's stock market.

We use the new event study method to measure trade friction events with multiple characteristics over a period and use the quantile regression method to measure financial stability. This study innovatively combines the new event study method with quantile regression to investigate the impact of trade friction events on the stability of China's stock market from 2017 to 2020. Rarely, we divide trade friction events into “friction” and “mitigation” events, explore the impact effects of the both types, conduct empirical analysis and robustness tests. Our findings are three-fold: (1) The impact of trade friction events on different quantiles of stock market returns in different periods varies a lot; (2) The expected effect of trade friction events affects the high quantile of the stock market return before the event; as it gets closer to the news getting confirmed, the low quantile of the stock market return will be affected. (3) Since people often have optimistic and long-term expectations for “good news,” as well as pessimistic and timely avoidance of “bad news,” the “friction events” mainly affect the day before and on the event, and the “mitigation events” will affect the stability of the stock market for a longer time.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 explains the methodology. Section 3 presents the empirical results. Section 4 concludes the study.

Methodology

Trade friction events are characterized by multiple occurrences, and once they begin, related events occur multiple times over a period. Previous studies on the impact of exogenous events on the stock market include the event study, the difference-in-differences method, and the single-dummy variable method. Based on previous studies, we found that the event study method cannot be used to quantify multiple events or multiple types of target variables. Difference-in-differences method has strong premise assumptions that do not apply to the study of trade friction events. The single dummy variable method uses only one landmark event at a time; in the case of frequent occurrences, it does not use multiple events to examine the comprehensive effect. In theory, the predictability of subsequent trade friction events naturally produces the expected effect, which affects the stock market by affecting investor expectations [[40], [41], [42]]. Because each piece of news will affect the stock market, the combined effect of multiple events should be considered, not just a single event or even the beginning of the events. The advantage of the new event study method is that it can measure the comprehensive effect of multiple events. Furthermore, risk transmission is asymmetric; that is, the effects of good and bad news on stock prices might not be the same. Trade friction events can be generally divided into “friction” and “mitigation” events. Additionally, the effects of these two types of events may be inconsistent. Therefore, it is necessary to distinguish their impacts in the study. Above all, we employ the new event study method to explore the impact of the Sino-US trade friction events, further dividing them into “friction” and “mitigation” events.

The key point in understanding whether the US trade friction affects China's stock market is to measure the market's stability. Currently, the mainstream method is the statistical index method, which is composed of multiple indicators that reflect comprehensive and dynamic changes. However, the index construction method is inevitably subjective, and it is difficult to compare the advantages, disadvantages, and rationality of different indices.

The stability of financial markets refers to their ability to perform economic functions normally and resist external shocks. That is, in the state of financial stability, the financial system has two “capabilities” - “the ability to perform its economic functions” and “the ability to resist a certain shock” [43]. These two abilities also show that if the stock market returns are affected similarly under normal and extreme conditions, the market is stable. Conversely, if the impact under extreme conditions is greater than under normal conditions, it indicates that the two capabilities of the financial system are weak and the market is in an unstable state. Under such a definition, quantile regression is a suitable method to measure stability. Quantile regression is a very robust regression method, and is suitable for research on financial data; when extreme events occur, outliers will occur in high-frequency financial data and cannot be easily determined. However, the robustness of quantile regression ensures that its estimation results are unaffected by outliers. The least square estimation requires the distribution of random disturbance terms, which has poor estimation results for financial data with sharp peaks and fat tails. Quantile estimation does not make any distribution assumptions for the model, which is particularly suitable for studying financial data and can obtain more accurate results.

Empirical analysis

The target variable in the quantile regression in this study is the composite index, specifically the return of the CSI 300 index, as the representative index of market return. We used the RESSET database to collect return data for the Chinese stock market. The research ranged from January 4, 2017, to January 23, 2020, when trade friction events were relatively concentrated. We selected representative events during this period (see Appendix 3).

Overall, as the timing of events changes, most situations cause instability in stock market returns. Specifically, due to the expected impact, the event affected the stability of the stock market six, four, and one period in advance. The occurrence of the event had a continuously significant impact. The current period of the event and the lags of two, three, four, and five periods affect the stability of the stock market, whereas there is no significant impact on the stability of the stock market after six periods. This is consistent with the conclusion of [32], who documents that the Chinese stock market was negatively affected by the direct impact induced by China's tariffs against the U.S., between 0 and 7 days after the event, and that the impact of different leading and lagging periods of events on the stability of the stock market is reflected in different quantiles of the stock return. The event's current period impacts both, the high and low quantiles of returns. The first six or four periods of the event mainly affected the high quantile of return, while the low quantile was mainly affected from the first period in advance. This shows that, owing to the role of rational expectations, news will cause fluctuations in the stock market even before the event occurs. With news landings, the impact on returns changes. From a risk perspective, we need to pay more attention to the low quantile of returns. Therefore, attention should be paid to the decline in stock market returns on the day before the event. This is particularly important for investors, which is consistent with the conclusion of [8], who find that the announcement day of a trade war greatly affects the stock market the following day.

On the one hand, before the occurrence, “friction” and “mitigation” events are consistent in terms of whether they have an impact on the stability of the stock market. After the event, when the “friction events” affect the stability of the stock market, the “mitigation events” also do so. When the “friction events” do not affect the stability of the stock market, the “mitigation events” cause it to become unstable. Therefore, “mitigation events” will affect the stability of the stock market for a longer time than “friction events.” However, the impacts of these two types of events on stock returns differ. At the beginning of the event interval, the two unanimously affected the high-return quantile. Before the first period of the event, the two unanimously affected the low quantile of returns. From the fourth period, before the occurrence, the influence positions of the two on returns alternate. In general, “friction events” mainly affect the stability of the stock market by affecting the low quantile, while “mitigation events” mainly affect stock market stability by affecting the high quantile.

Overall, the impact of “friction events” on the stability of the stock market, and the positions affecting the stock return before, and on the day of the event, are consistent with the overall event. The impact of “friction events” plays a leading role before and on the day of the event. The impact of “mitigation events” on the stability of the stock market is generally consistent with the overall event across its range, especially afterwards, indicating that their occurrence has a more far-reaching impact on the stock market.

“Mitigation events” have a more comprehensive impact on stock market stability, affecting both, the high and low quantiles of returns. The impact of the “friction events” on the stability of the stock market is mainly concentrated before and on the day of the event, and primarily affects the low quantile of the return. This is both, logical and realistic. People tend to have more optimistic and long-term expectations of good news, and more pessimistic attitudes towards bad news. Therefore, investments are adjusted in time before the arrival of bad news.

Since our baseline results are estimated using the CSI 300 index, the changes in the sample might lead to different or even adverse effects. To address this concern, we replaced the CSI 300 index and re-estimated the effect of trade friction on stock market stability. Considering that the A-share index is composed of all A-share stocks of the Chinese stock market and can reflect the rising and falling trends of the entire A-share market, we selected returns of this index as the target variable in the quantile regression to test the robustness of the above results. Conclusion and policy implications

Using the return data of the CSI 300 index and Sino-US trade friction events from 2017 to 2020, we empirically analyze the impact of Sino-US trade friction on the stability of the Chinese stock market. The results show that the impacts of trade friction events on the different quantiles of Chinese stock market returns in different periods are quite distinct. The expected impact of trade friction events affects the high quantiles of stock market returns before the event. As the news-confirmation time approaches, the low quantile of stock market returns is affected. “Friction events” mainly affect the stability of the stock market by influencing the low quantile of return, whereas “mitigation events” mainly affect the stability of the stock market by influencing the high quantile. Furthermore, we use various robustness tests to verify the robustness of our results.

Our findings suggest the following: First, trade friction has both pre- and post-impacts on the overall stability of the Chinese stock market, and we should focus more on the latter, especially the prevention of systemic financial risks, through appropriate risk control in advance. Second, the real economy is a cornerstone of the stock market. Improving the weak board industries as soon as possible, consolidating the cornerstone of the real economy, and enhancing the ability to deal with trade frictions are conducive to stabilizing the stock market and forming a coordinated virtuous cycle of “real” and “virtual” economic development. Third, the regulatory authorities should pay attention to both, positive and negative news, and manage the corresponding anticipation to alleviate the investor's mood fluctuation, especially excessive pessimism, caused by the “friction event.” Investors need to focus on stock market volatility in the week before and after the trade friction event, and take note of the decline in stock market returns from the day before the event.

From an empirical perspective, this study takes China as an example, and uses quantile regression, enhanced by the new event study method, to quantitatively study the impact of Sino-US trade friction on stock market stability. Therefore, a limitation of this study is that it did not build a unified theoretical analysis model; further studies can focus on this direction. In addition, the “new event study” method used in this study can be extended to the impact study of events with multiple characteristics over a period, such as COVID-19. This study discusses the impact of trade friction from the perspective of the Chinese stock market stability. In future work, we will focus on the specific impact of event subtypes on the stability of various industries and the transmission effect of financial risk among various industries.

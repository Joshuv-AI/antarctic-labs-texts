Clinical study to evaluate the effect of different types of dyslipidemia on left ventricular function based on three-dimensional speckle tracking imaging
Authors: Xiaofei Chen, Zhirong Jiang, Pin Sun, Xiangqin He, Yu Tian, Qifeng Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo investigate the value of three-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography (3D-STE) in evaluating early left ventricular (LV) systolic function in patients with different types of dyslipidemia.MethodsA total of 109 patients with dyslipidemia treated at our hospital were selected and divided into high total cholesterol (TC) group, high triglyceride (TG) group, low high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) group and mixed dyslipidemia group, according to the variety of dyslipidemia for further analysis.A total of 100 healthy volunteers approximately matched for age and gender were selected as the control group. The basic clinical data and conventional echocardiographic parameters were obtained, and the global longitudinal peak strain (GLPS), circumferential peak strain (GCPS) and radial peak strain (GRPS) of the left ventricle were obtained by 3D-STI technology for further analysis.Results(1)The GLPS and GCPS of the high TC group, high TG group and low HDL-C group were lower than those of the control group, and the GLPS, GCPS and GRPS of the mixed dyslipidemia group were lower than those of the other groups. (2) The ROC curve analysis showed that the area under the curve (AUC) of GLPS for detecting dyslipidemia patients was the largest. (3) Linear regression analysis showed that GLPS was independently correlated with TC, LDL-C, TG, and HDL-C. The independent effect of LDL-C on GLPS (standardizedβ = −0.368) was stronger than that of TC (standardizedβ = −0.356), suggesting LDL-C has a greater impact on left ventricular longitudinal strain. (4)The Bland-Altman analysis showed that 3D-STE parameters had good intra-observer and inter-observer consistency.ConclusionDyslipidemia had adverse effects on left ventricular strain parameters, especially in patients with mixed dyslipidemia. 3D-STE effectively evaluated the global systolic function of LV in patients with different types of dyslipidemia, and LDL-C shows the strongest independent effect correlating with worse systolic function.

Introduction

Dyslipidemia is a common chronic metabolic disorder with a high incidence worldwide, and it is a risk factor for increasing the incidence and mortality of cardiovascular diseases. The latest epidemiological data show that the total prevalence rate among people aged 18 and above in China is 35.6%. Among them, hypercholesterolemia (mainly characterized by elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol LDL-C) accounts for 16.9%, hypertriglyceridemia (TG) accounts for 13.1%, and low high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) blood disorder accounts for 21.4% [1]. This has become a major public health problem threatening the health of the nation.

As a factor causing atherosclerosis, lipids can trigger the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, promote the thickening of the arterial intima, reduce myocardial blood flow reserve and capillary density, and may increase the apoptosis of capillary endothelial cells after ischemia and reperfusion, reduce myocardial blood flow, trigger CHD, and thereby affect cardiac function [2]; on the other hand, the accumulation of lipid metabolites can directly damage myocardial cells, alter the metabolism of myocardial cells, affect the microcirculation of the heart, lead to myocardial fibrosis and myocardial remodeling, and lipid abnormalities may indirectly affect the mechanisms of atherosclerosis, such as inflammation, thrombosis, cell proliferation and abnormal endothelial function. These pathological changes jointly affect the contraction and relaxation functions of the heart [3,4]. 。

According to the 2023 Chinese Guidelines for Lipid Management [1], dyslipidemia is defined as abnormality in one or more indicators of the lipid profile. The diagnostic criteria for each indicator include: total cholesterol ≥5.2 mmol/L, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol ≥3.4 mmol/L, triglycerides ≥1.7 mmol/L, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol <1.0 mmol/L. Clinically, dyslipidemia is typically classified into hypercholesterolemia, hypertriglyceridemia, mixed hyperlipidemia, and low high-density lipoprotein cholesterol. Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol is the core lipid that causes atherosclerosis and is the primary intervention target for lipid management.

Early and precise identification of subclinical myocardial damage caused by dyslipidemia is crucial for risk stratification and early intervention. Traditional ultrasound can only assess overall cardiac function and is difficult to capture early microscopic myocardial deformation abnormalities; 3D-STI can calculate the movement and deformation information of the myocardium in different directions by continuously tracking the movement trajectories of myocardial echo spots at different frame frequencies. It can precisely reflect the degree of myocardial deformation in each cardiac chamber in three-dimensional space, non-invasively quantify longitudinal, circumferential, radial, area strain and rotation, torsion parameters, and is not affected by cardiac displacement and scanning angle, with higher repeatability [5,6]. Thus, it can accurately evaluate the overall and local functions of the left ventricle from a mechanical-mechanics perspective, identify subclinical myocardial functional changes, and provide a new imaging assessment method for myocardial damage related to dyslipidemia.

At present, there are few studies on evaluating the impact of different types of dyslipidemia on left ventricular function using 3D-STI. This study aims to apply 3D-STI to assess the left ventricular strain of patients with different types of dyslipidemia; and while not changing the clinical classification, through regression model comparison and ROC curve analysis to highlight the core role of LDL-C and compare its independent influence with TC, TG, and HDL-C on left ventricular function, thereby filling the clinical gap in early assessment of subclinical myocardial damage, guiding clinical early scientific intervention, providing objective evidence for the refined management of cardiovascular risks in patients with dyslipidemia, and avoiding the occurrence of serious cardiovascular adverse events.

Material and methods

Study subjects

The study size was determined by the number of cases examined during the study period. A total of 109 dyslipidemia patients, initially treated at our hospital between September 2020 and December 2023, aged between 22 and 66 years.

The inclusion criteria were developed according to the 2023 Chinese guideline for the management of dyslipidemia in adults [1]. (1)Fasting serum total cholesterol (TC) concentration ≥5.2 mmol/L (200 mg/dl) on 2 recent occasions (more than 2 weeks apart); or low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) ≥ 3.4 mmol/L (130 mg/dl); or triglyceride (TG) ≥ 1.7 mmol/L (150 mg/dl); or high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL) < 1.0 mmol/L (40 mg/dl); (2) LV ejection fraction (LVEF) > 50%.

Exclusion criteria: (1)Individuals diagnosed with tumors, liver and kidney deficiencies, hepatitis, cardiomyopathy, congenital heart conditions, coronary heart disease and diabetes mellitus through medical records, physical exams, and lab tests; (2)Patients diagnosed with coronary artery stenosis by coronary angiography or CT angiography (CTA); (3)Those receiving consistent lipid-reducing treatment; those whose lipid counts had returned to normal through dietary modifications or increased physical activity.

According to the degree of dyslipidemia, patients were classified into the high TC group (TC ≥ 5.2 mmol/L or LDL-C ≥ 3.4 mmol/L, n = 31), the high TG group (TG ≥ 1.7 mmol/L, n = 27), the low HDL-C group (HDL-C < 1.0 mmol/L, n = 25), and the mixed dyslipidemia group (TC ≥ 5.2 mmol/L, LDL-C ≥ 3.4 mmol/L, TG ≥ 1.7 mmol/L, and HDL-C < 1.0 mmol/L, n = 26). An additional group of 100 healthy individuals, comparable in age and gender during the same timeframe, were chosen for the control group, aged between 25 and 57 years. No cardiac and other underlying diseases were detected in the control group. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the hospital and all patients gave informed consent.

Instruments and methods

A Philips EPIQ 7C color Doppler ultrasound diagnostic instrument equipped with the S5-1 2D probe (frequency 1-5 MHz) and X5-1 3D probe (frequency 1.9-3.8 MHz) was used as the cardiovascular ultrasound. Clinical data and echocardiogram parameters were recorded and collected: (1) Clinical data: Age, sex, height, weight, systolic blood pressure (SBP), and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were recorded, and TC, TG, LDL-C, HDL-C, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB)and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) were detected; (2) Dynamic cardiac ultrasound parameters were measured. The subjects were asked to lie on the left side and breathe calmly, and electrocardiogram heart scan was performed. Interventricular septal thickness (IVST) and LV posterior wall thickness (LVPWT) in the diastolic phase, LV end diastolic diameter (LVDd), and LV fractional shortening (LVFS) were measured in the long axial view of LV using the S5-1 probe. LVEF was measured using the improved biplane Simpson's method. The early diastolic transmitral flow velocity (E), late diastolic transmitral flow velocity (A), and early diastolic mitral annular velocity (e') were measured by pulsed-wave tissue Doppler imaging in the apical four-chamber view. E/A, E/e ', LV mass index (LVMI), and relative wall thickness (RWT) were calculated; (3) 3D-STE: 3D-STE was performed using the X5-1 probe in the apical four-cavity view of the heart. The patients were told to hold their breath to obtain clear 2D images. Full-volume mode was started after stabilization. Raw data sets of four consecutive cardiac cycles were collected during end-expiratory breath hold (frame rate 20-30 frames/second). Tom-Tec 4D LV-Analysis 3.0 software was used for off-line analysis. Global longitudinal peak strain (GLPS), global circumferential peak strain (GCPS), and global radial peak strain (GRPS) of LV were calculated.(Fig. 1).

The image information from 10 healthy patients and 10 patients with dyslipidemia was randomly selected, and the above indexes were measured off-line by two physicians who were proficient in the 3D myocardial tracking and analysis software. The difference between the two values of the same parameter measured by the two observers was the inter-observer variabilities. In that case, the involved parameters were measured again by one of them two weeks later, and the difference between the two values of the same parameter was the intra-observer variabilities.

Statistical analysis

SPSS22.0 software was used for analysis. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Univariate analysis of variance was employed for comparison between groups with homogeneity of variances, and pairwise comparisons were conducted using the LSD test. A Kruskal-Wallis test was performed for comparison between multiple groups with heterogeneity of variance, and Dunnett's T3 test was used for pairwise comparison. Count data were expressed as frequencies and/or percentages and compared between the groups using the χ2test. ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of the 3D-STE parameters for subclinical LV systolic dysfunction, both in the overall dyslipidemia cohort and within each dyslipidemia subtype. Given the high collinearity between TC and LDL-C, two separate linear regression models were constructed with GLPS as the dependent variable to compare their respective correlations with GLPS. Intra-observer and inter-observer reproducibility were evaluated using Bland-Altman analysis.

Comparison of 3D-STI parameters

GLPS, GCPS, and GRPS were reduced in the high TC, high TG, low HDL-C, and mixed dyslipidemia groups compared with the control group (P < 0.05); GLPS, GCPS, and GRPS in the mixed dyslipidemia group were more significantly reduced than other groups (P < 0.05) (Table 2, Fig. 1).

ROC curve analysis

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of three-dimensional strain parameters for subclinical left ventricular systolic dysfunction in patients with different types of dyslipidemia. For overall dyslipidemia patients, the AUCs of GLPS, GCPS and GRPS were 0.816(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.759∼0.872), 0.781(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.719∼0.842) and 0.708(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.639∼0.778), respectively. With the optimal cut-off value of −19.32%, the sensitivity was 81.0% and the specificity was 67.9%. For hypercholesterolemia patients, the corresponding AUCs were 0.787(P < 0.001, 95% CI 0.692∼0.832), 0.770 (P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.702∼0.833)and 0.704(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.632∼0.775); the cut-off value was −19.55%, with a sensitivity of 78.0% and a specificity of 67.7%.For hypertriglyceridemia patients, the AUCs were 0.788(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.703∼0.866), 0.728(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.653∼0.792) and 0.642(P 0.002, 95%CI 0.598∼0.721); the cut-off value was −19.07%, with a sensitivity of 85.0% and a specificity of 63.0%. For low HDL-C patients, the AUCs were 0.783(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.711∼0.853), 0.718(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.654∼0.782) and 0.679(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.611∼0.752); the cut-off value was −20.34%, with a sensitivity of 70.0% and a specificity of 72.0%.For mixed dyslipidemia patients, the AUCs reached 0.905(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.842∼0.953), 0.903(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.862∼0.957) and 0.799(P < 0.001, 95%CI 0.734∼0.854). Discussion

Dyslipidemia is not only one of the independent risk factors for ischemic stroke, but also a well-recognized independent risk factor for cardiovascular diseases, particularly coronary heart disease [[7], [8], [9]]. As one of the etiological factors of atherosclerosis, dyslipidemia typically has a prolonged clinical course and an insidious onset, lacking obvious physical signs and symptoms. Dyslipidemia can trigger the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, promote arterial intimal thickening, reduce myocardial blood flow reserve and capillary density, and may increase capillary endothelial cell apoptosis after ischemia-reperfusion, thereby decreasing myocardial blood flow and impairing cardiac microcirculation [2]. Furthermore, the accumulation of lipid metabolites can directly damage cardiomyocytes and alter their metabolism, leading to myocardial fibrosis and myocardial remodeling, and ultimately resulting in myocardial function impairment [10]. Early and timely scientific intervention can significantly improve the prognosis of dyslipidemia. Therefore, research on this disease has profound significance and has long been a focus of clinical trials.

Conventional transthoracic echocardiography, although widely used in clinical practice, has numerous limitations [11]. Three-dimensional speckle tracking imaging (3D-STI) provides information on myocardial motion by tracking the positional changes of speckles within the myocardium and enables full-volume cardiac imaging, allowing for more sensitive assessment of global and regional cardiac function. As such, it can reflect early pathophysiological changes of the heart [5].

In this study, there were no statistically significant differences in age, gender, smoking history, blood glucose, blood pressure or thyroid function between the case group and the control group, while significant differences were observed in lipid parameters among patients with different types of dyslipidemia. No significant difference was detected in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), indicating that LVEF was still in the compensatory stage, which implies that LVEF is not sensitive for the assessment of early left ventricular systolic dysfunction. This is because the body maintains LVEF by regulating and enhancing the contractility of undamaged myocardium, which greatly affects the judgment of left ventricular function [12,13]. In addition, conventional two-dimensional echocardiography showed that in patients with dyslipidemia and normal LVEF, E/e' was increased with statistical significance. Elevated left ventricular filling pressure indicates that left ventricular diastolic function has been impaired, and diastolic dysfunction occurs in the early stage of the natural course of the disease, with alterations preceding the onset of heart failure and abnormalities in left ventricular volume and ejection fraction.

Comparison of 3D-STI parameters showed that global longitudinal peak strain (GLPS), global circumferential peak strain (GCPS) and global radial peak strain (GRPS) in the case group were all lower than those in the control group, with statistically significant differences. Left ventricular diastolic dysfunction and varying degrees of reduction in 3D-STI strain parameters indicate that subclinical microcirculatory alterations or myocardial function impairment occur at an early stage in patients with dyslipidemia, meaning that these patients may have myocardial ischemia [14]. This elucidates the relationship between dyslipidemia and myocardial hypofunction, and can guide early clinical and scientific intervention. Compared with the high TC, high TG and low HDL-C groups, the reductions of GLPS, GCPS and GRPS were more significant in the mixed dyslipidemia group. 3D-STI can sensitively detect the above changes, which further confirms the superiority of 3D-STI and the feasibility of this technique in assessing left ventricular function in patients with dyslipidemia.

The main strain parameters of 3D-STI are GLPS, GCPS and GRPS. Among them, GLPS reflects the longitudinal deformation of the myocardium and represents the systolic function of the heart; GCPS reflects the circumferential motion in the short axis direction, and a reduction in its value indicates that myocardial ischemia involves the middle layer of the myocardium; GRPS reflects radial myocardial deformation corresponding to systolic thickening [[15], [16], [17]]. Longitudinal fibers, which are directly associated with cardiac contraction, are located in the subendocardium and contract along the long axis of the ventricular myocardium [18]. During cardiac contraction, the endocardial surface is subjected to higher pressure, making it prone to abnormal myocardial contraction [19,20]. Since GLPS reflects myocardial deformation in the longitudinal direction, abnormal changes in this direction occur earlier than in other directions when myocardial injury occurs. Therefore, subendocardial myocardial ischemia is initially manifested as a decrease in GLPS, which makes GLPS the parameter with the best assessment performance [21]. Compared with the control group, GLPS was significantly reduced in patients with dyslipidemia, and the reduction of GLPS was most significant in the mixed dyslipidemia group (all P < 0.05). This indicates that subclinical myocardial damage already exists in the early stage of patients with dyslipidemia.

Since the ROC curve analysis indicated that GLPS had better diagnostic efficacy, we chose GLPS for further investigation. In this study, Our regression analysis, which accounted for the collinearity between LDL-C and TC by constructing separate models, demonstrated that LDL-C has an independent and slightly stronger association with GLPS than TC, support the notion that LDL-C is the central lipid indicator driving early myocardial dysfunction in dyslipidemia.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that dyslipidemia adversely affects LV function, with subclinical systolic and diastolic impairment detectable by 3D-STE before any reduction in LVEF. Among different dyslipidemia types, patients with mixed dyslipidemia exhibit the most severe LV function impairment. Notably, elevated LDL-C levels are independently associated with worse LV longitudinal strain, and its impact is stronger than that of other lipid parameters. Therefore, prioritizing 3D-STE assessment, particularly measuring GLPS, is recommended for patients with mixed dyslipidemia or markedly elevated LDL-C to enable early detection and intervention of subclinical cardiac dysfunction.

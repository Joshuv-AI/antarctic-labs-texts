Target deconvolution strategies in TCM: Bridging medicinal properties with therapeutic efficacies
Authors: Yuanyuan Hou, Min Jiang, Gang Bai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The discovery and characterization of molecular targets from traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) represents a paradigm shift in contemporary drug development. This approach systematically bridges empirical TCM knowledge with modern pharmacological principles, establishing a robust framework for rational drug design. Through integrative systems-chemical biology approaches, we can now mechanistically elucidate the therapeutic basis of TCM’s bioactive properties, facilitating the accelerated identification of both novel molecular targets and conserved pharmacological pathways. This multidisciplinary strategy significantly enhances drug development efficiency while advancing therapeutic innovation to address critical unmet medical needs. With the rapid advancement of next-generation target deconvolution technologies and AI-driven computational platforms, TCM is poised to make increasingly substantial contributions to modern pharmacotherapy.

The discovery of new drug targets from natural products represents a core breakthrough of revolutionary significance, driving new drug research and the pharmaceutical industry (Liu & Zeng, 2025). The theory of medicinal properties constitutes the core of the theoretical framework of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), systematically summarizing the effects of drugs on the body and their modes of expression. Therapeutic efficacies refer to the specific manifestations of how drugs influence the body, whereas medicinal properties represent the abstraction and synthesis of multiple pharmacological effects. Research into the nature and targets of TCM reveals its disease-treating mechanisms, highlights its advantages in druggability and verifiability, avoids off-target effects, and overcomes transformation bottlenecks. The multicomponent, multi-target characteristic of TCM often results in insufficient focus on core targets. This underscores the critical need for advancing target deconvolution methodologies. Establishing a multidisciplinary cross-system approach to explore the scientific connotations of medicinal properties not only advances the development of TCM theory but also lays a foundation for the innovation of new drugs.

Medicinal properties highlight the advantages of TCM and represent the integrated efficacy at a higher level

Efficacy refers to the physiological effects that TCM exerts on the body, reflecting the inherent properties of TCM and determined through modern pharmacological research. The investigation of efficacy centers on elucidating the principles of drug action by examining key aspects such as chemical constituents, mechanisms of action, pharmacokinetic processes, and interactions with biological targets, thereby providing a comprehensive understanding of how drugs function dynamically within the body. This highlights the significant role of TCM in treating dominant diseases, as well as its distinctive properties within the human body (Li et al., 2023). Medicinal properties represent a concise and comprehensive summary of a drug’s therapeutic efficacies, encompassing its mechanisms of action, application guidelines, and indications. These properties serve as a concentrated manifestation of drug efficacy and define the distinct advantages and features of drug effects, thus laying the groundwork for targeted mechanism research. There is a close relationship and mutual validation between medicinal properties and efficacy, embodying the dialectical unity of structure and function. Elucidating their intrinsic connections can provide an innovative foundation for drug target discovery.

Global analysis based on systems biology provides a solution pathway for resolving therapeutic efficacies

The theoretical framework of TCM emphasizes a holistic perspective, exploring interactions among various factors to avoid overemphasizing any single factor. However, recent target discovery has focused on pharmacodynamic components and core mechanisms but neglected systematic analyses of overall effects (Guo, An, Bao, & Xu, 2023). Systems biology should integrate the holistic view and syndrome differentiation, leveraging clinical advantages through macroscopic and integrative networks (Wang, Wang, Zheng, & Li, 2021). The “top-down” research strategy in multi-omics integration analysis combines transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics to reveal regulatory mechanisms. This approach supplements missing network signals, clarifying the synergistic effects of key functional modules in complex systems. In contrast, chemical proteomics uses a “bottom-up” method, identifying potential target proteins by designing bioactive molecular probes. Medicinal properties typically involve complex interactions beyond single target proteins, encompassing entire pathways/axes and associated downstream targets. Future research should prioritize elucidating synergistic compatibility mechanisms that address these integrated network-level effects. A comprehensive understanding of medicinal properties, coupled with a focus on the key site-specific agents or rational drug combinations within relevant signaling axes, can broaden the spectrum of clinical precision medicine. Methodologies in TCM research, such as “top-down” or “bottom-up” approaches, along with the “clinical-animal-tissue-cell-network-pathway-target” research and development (R&D) model, enable the elucidation of key regulatory mechanisms and facilitate molecular-level investigations. The integration of clinical and multi-omics data, supported by big data analytics and AI predictive, is expected to substantially improve the efficiency of such research.

Chemical biology elucidates core pharmacophores to clarify the material basis for exerting function and structure

Chemical biology is an interdisciplinary field that investigates biological systems at the molecular level through the application of chemical methodologies, with a focus on elucidating the targets and mechanisms of action of drug molecules. The core concept of TCM chemical biology lies in utilizing active ingredients as tools to elucidate the interactions between components and their targets. Chemical biology, based on medicinal properties, focuses on explaining the principles behind these properties. While traditional models, such as activation/inhibition and covalent binding, continue to serve as foundational paradigms, novel drug-target interaction mechanisms, including allosteric regulation, protein degradation, protein–protein interaction modulation, and nucleic acid targeting, have significantly broadened the landscape of “druggable targets”. Systematically explaining mechanisms of action from a system biology to chemical biology perspective is a critical issue in TCM target research (Bai, Hou, Jiang, & Gao, 2016).

Natural product molecules exhibit diverse pharmacological activities due to their structural diversity. Compounds with distinct structures may show identical biological activities or similar efficacies. This is because specific pharmacophores in different drug molecules modulate key sites in target proteins, forming the basis for equivalent functions. During structure-based drug design, molecules with different structures can produce the same or opposite pharmacological effects by interacting with shared pharmacophores or distinct binding pockets of the same target. Leveraging common pharmacophores or targets linked to medicinal properties overcomes the limitations of traditional optimization and shifts drug design from “structure-driven” to “function-led”. Target deconvolution techniques and artificial intelligence-assisted molecular design have revolutionized drug discovery by providing robust strategies for precise drug design and optimization in therapeutic agents (Saini, Mehra, Kumar, Paul, & Kovács, 2025).

Clarifying the spatiotemporal regulation and in vivo metabolism of pharmacodynamic molecules is essential for target identification

The evaluation of TCM properties and efficacy should focus on TCM syndromes, considering the interconnections and balance among organs and tissues. Under some circumstances, the target organs and effect organs of pharmacodynamic molecules may differ. Organ-crossing remote regulation mediated by endogenous metabolic substances constitutes a central feature of TCM properties theory. However, the identification of TCM properties with respect to the effector organ or target cell inherently involves a degree of subjectivity. By developing multi-dimensional spatiotemporal molecular probes and establishing in vivo imaging systems to characterize the body’s microenvironment (Ma et al., 2024), we can achieve real-time quantification and monitoring of efficacy changes, thereby supporting a more scientific and objective evaluation framework.

The in vivo metabolic transformation of natural products plays a crucial role in enabling drugs to exert their therapeutic effects. Some natural compounds exhibit relatively low intrinsic activity and must be metabolized into more active forms. As a complex chemical system, TCM highlights the contradiction between its low bioavailability and high therapeutic efficacy, which transcends the limitations of the traditional pharmacokinetic-pharmacodynamic (PK-PD) linear model and underscores the inherent complexity of TCM (Li et al., 2024). Furthermore, some pharmacodynamic components require activation through metabolism by liver CYP450 enzymes. However, some metabolites may also introduce new toxicities. TCM and its metabolites typically achieve therapeutic effects by modulating multiple biological targets and signaling pathways in diverse manners. Nevertheless, the comprehensive identification of the material basis for their pharmacodynamics, action targets, specific tissues or cells of action, as well as the exploration of their interrelationships, remains inadequate at present.

Revealing the targets related to herbal properties is a shortcut for drug design and development

The four natures (cold, hot, warm, and cool) indicate the effects of drugs on the equilibrium of yin and yang, as well as the thermal condition within the human body. Specifically, drugs with a cold nature typically demonstrate the ability to clear heat and detoxify. For example, gardenia and rehmannia, due to their cold and cool properties, possess the ability to clear heat and cool blood. Through the application of the mitochondrial thermosensitive fluorescent probe RhBIV and chemical biology tracing techniques, it has been demonstrated that geniposide and catalpol, representative iridoid glycosides, expose a hemiacetal structure after in vivo metabolism. This structure subsequently condenses with the ε-amino group of lysine in the proton leak channel of mitochondrial uncoupling protein 2 (UCP2) in a temperature-dependent manner in the liver and other tissues (Shen et al., 2024, Shen et al., 2023). The irreversible binding inhibits proton reflux through the proton leak cavity, thereby suppressing mitochondrial heat production and exerting a heat-clearing effect by modulating cellular energy metabolism. These findings suggest that cold and cool medicinal materials with heat-clearing and detoxifying properties, such as honeysuckle, verbena, and patrinia, typically contain iridoid glycoside components. It is hypothesized that the heat-clearing properties of these compounds may be attributed to their unique hemiacetal structural characteristics. Research significance

The search for and validation of new disease targets has consistently constituted the core task and one of the most exciting frontier fields in biomedical research. TCM perceives life as a dynamic, balanced, and open system, elucidating the essence of pathology through syndrome differentiation and treatment. In contrast, modern medicine employs a reductionist approach, breaking down life into multiple quantifiable elements. Only by integrating the holistic perspective of TCM with the analytical precision of reductionism can a comprehensive epistemological framework be established. Introducing systems biology into the research on the properties and efficacy of TCM aims to reconstruct the cognitive framework of TCM theory using modern scientific language. The essence of target deconvolution strategies lies in its reverse and systematic methodology, which aims to elucidate the mechanisms of action of TCM or natural active compounds by accurately identifying their direct biological targets via experimental, computational, or AI-driven computational platforms. By interpreting drug properties through chemical biology, we can understand the regulatory capacity of chemical components on axes, pathways, or biological modules with therapeutic effects. This approach enables us to address the challenge of quantifying vague experiential knowledge. The research paradigm that integrates systems biology and chemical biology in TCM connects macroscopic integrity with microscopic materiality. It elucidates the material foundation and dynamic regulatory mechanisms underlying the theory of TCM properties, thereby providing a pathway for its modernization. Identifying shared and druggable disease targets by integrating systematic medicinal properties with therapeutic efficacy target analysis constitutes a critical step in drug discovery. This approach not only enhances scientific understanding but also delivers tangible health benefits while promoting innovation within the modernization of TCM.

Protective role of nano-selenium on Gymnocypris przewalskii under saline–alkaline stress: a comprehensive analysis of transcriptomics and metabolomics
Authors: Yanxia Chen, Zhaonan Li, Ying Yang, Tianxiu Liang, Shuchen Huang, Jingjing Zhang, Wenjie Jin, Linan Wang, Qingchun Yan, Changzhong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTibetan naked carp (Gymnocypris przewalskii) is a unique freshwater fish adapted to high-altitude saline–alkaline water. Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) may enhance resilience in aquatic species by mitigating oxidative stress. This study used a combined transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis to explore the protective role of SeNPs in G. przewalskii under saline–alkaline stress. The transcriptomic analysis revealed significant alterations in the expression of genes involved in stress response, antioxidant defense, osmoregulation, and metabolism. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of hepatic differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in juvenile G. przewalskii fed with SeNPs (NSE4_120) or basal diets (NSEC_120) under saline–alkaline stress revealed that the DEGs were significantly enriched in pathways such as “glycolysis/gluconeogenesis,” and “thyroid hormone signaling pathway” (P < 0.05), suggesting that SeNPs may modulate these pathways for protection. Metabolomic analysis identified differentially abundant metabolites (DAMs) in the NSE4_120 versus NSEC_120 comparison group. Several differential metabolites associated with osmoregulation, including taurine, proline, and trehalose, were annotated to signaling pathways such as “metabolic pathways,” “taurine and hypotaurine metabolism,” “arginine and proline metabolism,” and “starch and sucrose metabolism” pathways. These metabolites may play critical roles in the response of G. przewalskii to osmotic stress. In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that SeNPs play a vital role in enhancing the resilience of G. przewalskii against saline–alkaline stress through modulation of gene expression and metabolic processes, suggesting their protective effect against osmotic and oxidative stress. This study not only provides insight into the adaptive mechanisms of G. przewalskii under high saline–alkaline environmental conditions but also highlights the potential application of SeNPs in aquaculture strategies when fish are exposed to extreme environments. Introduction

Qinghai Lake has high sodium, potassium, and magnesium concentrations and is the largest inland saline–alkaline lake in China (Li et al. 2021). It is characterized by high salinity, high alkalinity, persistent low temperature, and low oxygen (Li et al. 2020). The Tibetan naked carp (Gymnocypris przewalskii) is an endangered fish species endemic to Qinghai Lake (Tian et al. 2022). It has the capacity to tolerate the extreme aquatic environment of Qinghai Lake (Tong et al. 2017). G. przewalskii plays a critical role in maintaining the stability of the ecosystem and biodiversity in the northeastern Qinghai-Tibet Plateau (QTP). It serves as the primary food source for migratory birds from Siberia (Jiang et al. 2020) and has important fishery and ecological significance in the fish-bird-grassland system (Wei et al. 2014). Previous studies on G. przewalskii have mainly focused on life history (Chen et al. 2009), adaptive evolution (Qi et al. 2013), physiology (Wang et al. 2022; Wood et al. 2007), immunology (Wei et al. 2024), environmental adaptation (Li et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2023), reproduction (Tingbao and Xianghua 2001; Zhao et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2021), and growth (Jun 2005; Tong et al. 2017).

Historically, the wild resources of G. przewalskii were abundant; however, they have decreased significantly due to climate change, overfishing, and destruction of spawning habitat caused by dam building for irrigation, leading to its status as an endangered species in China (Wang et al. 2022; Yao et al. 2016). Multiple overlapping factors have caused the natural reproduction rate of G. przewalskii to be much lower than its mortality rate. In the wild environment, the survival rate of G. przewalskii is only 2%, whereas it has been increased to 80% through artificial breeding. Over the years, through the establishment of nature reserves and measures such as the implementation of large-scale and continuous artificial stocking since 2002 (a total of 197 million fish), the total resources of the naked carp in Qinghai Lake increased to 114,200 t in 2022, and its protection level was upgraded from endangered species in 2004 to vulnerable species in 2016 (Chen et al. 2025). Although the resources of G. przewalskii have been effectively restored overtime, most of the G. przewalskii entering Qinghai Lake and its tributaries still die due to high salinity and alkalinity stresses, and the current contribution of artificial breeding and stocking to resource recovery is only 26% (Fu et al. 2024). Many scholars have studied the mechanisms of G. przewalskii in response to high salinity and alkalinity (Tian et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2022; Wei et al. 2022a, b; Wei et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2022). Improving the survival rate and growth performance of juvenile fish during breeding and stocking remains a critical challenge. Currently, adding feed additives to fish diets is one of the most widely used methods to mitigate the negative effects of environmental stress on fish.

Selenium (Se) is a non-metallic element and one of the essential trace elements for aquatic animals. It participates in important physiological functions and plays a crucial role in immunity, antioxidant activity, cell membrane stabilization, and the maintenance of normal metabolism in the organism (Hosnedlova et al. 2018; Wang et al. 2017; Zheng et al. 2018). There are three forms of selenium in nature: inorganic selenium, organic selenium, and elemental selenium (Yang et al. 2022). Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) are elemental selenium particles at the nanoscale that exhibit a red color. SeNPs are less toxic and more biologically active than both inorganic and organic selenium; specifically, the order of toxicity is selenate > selenite > SeNPs (Benko et al. 2012). In addition, SeNPs have smaller particle size and larger surface area compared to inorganic and organic selenium (Hu et al. 2012). These characteristics give SeNPs higher adhesion to the intestinal mucosa, which facilitates absorption by the animal organism. The main reason why SeNPs play an important biological role is that they provide selenium for the synthesis of many selenoprotein enzymes (SePib), such as glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), which enhances the antioxidant capacity and immunity of the body (Pacitti et al. 2015). SeNPs have been reported to improve growth performance (Ahmad et al. 2024), antioxidant capacity (Dawood et al. 2019), immunity (Khaled et al. 2024), and tolerance to heat stress (Li et al. 2023a, b), hypoxia stress (Qin et al. 2016), and pathogenic infections (Rathore et al. 2021) in aquatic animals.

Dietary supplementation of SeNPs is an effective method to alleviate oxidative stress in fish, but little research has been reported on the effects of SeNPs on the tolerance of G. przewalskii against salinity and alkalinity stress. In this study, SeNPs were added to the feed to explore their effect on enhancing tolerance to salinity and alkalinity stress tolerance, as well as the potential regulatory mechanisms involved. This study will provide a theoretical basis for the development of feeds to enhance environmental stress resistance in G. przewalskii and improve the success rate of artificial breeding.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval

The current experimental protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Qinghai University (Xining, China) and all techniques were performed in accordance with the prescribed protocols (Code: PJ202401-110). The current study was carried out in accordance with the U.K. Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act, 1986 and associated guidelines, EU Directive 2010/63/EU for animal experiments, or the National Research Council’s Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Diet Preparation

The basal diet was supplied by Shandong Hanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Suzhou, China). Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs) (size 174 ± 26 nm, 35,000mg/kg) were obtained from Beijing WAHMIX Bio-technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). SeNPs were individually incorporated into the basal diet at levels of 0 (NSEC), 2 (NSE2), 4 (NSE4), 7 (NSE7), 9 (NSE9), and 11 (NSE11) mg/kg. The formulation and proximate composition of the basal diet and experimental diets are presented in Table 1. Diet preparation followed the method described by Chen et al. (2024).

Fish and experimental design

A total of 900 healthy 1-year-old (average initial weight, 22.0 ± 3.0 g) full-sib G. przewalskii juveniles were provided by the Rescue Center of Qinghai Lake Naked Carp. The experimental fish were randomly divided into six groups, with three replicates of 40 fish in each group. Each group was fed different experimental diets (NSEC, NSE2, NSE4, NSE7, NSE9, NSE11). The fish were placed in 18 glass tanks (40 × 50 × 50 cm) and acclimated for 2 weeks before experiment initiation. The feeding trail lasted 8 weeks and the feeding conditions were the same as described by Chen et al. (2024).

Sample collection

To study the potential role of SeNPs on the adaptive capacity of G. przewalskii to the environment, all experimental fish were directly placed in saline–alkaline water (salinity: sodium chloride, 15 ± 0.3‰; alkalinity: carbonate buffer, 32 ± 1 mmol/L) after feeding with experimental diets containing different concentrations of SeNPs. This saline–alkaline water simulated the salinity and alkalinity of Qinghai Lake. The fish were exposed to saline–alkaline water for 120 h, during which they were fed their respective diets. The stress exposure experiment was conducted in a controlled recirculating aquaculture system with continuous water quality management. Salinity and alkalinity were maintained at constant levels through regular adjustments, with routine checks performed at the beginning and end of the exposure period to ensure stability. Before and after saline–alkaline stress exposure, fish in the six groups were counted to calculate survival rates. Six individuals from the control group and the experimental group that showed the highest survival rate were anesthetized with 100 mg/L tricaine methane sulfonate (MS-222, Sigma, USA); their livers were collected, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at − 80℃ for further transcriptomics and metabolomics analyses.

Transcriptome sequencing and bioinformatic analysis

Liver tissues from each experimental group (control and the group treated with the optimal SeNPs dose) (n = 3) were used for the RNA sequencing. Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues using TRIzol® reagent (Thermo Fisher, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and genomic DNA was removed using DNase I (TaKaRa). The purity and concentration of the total RNA were measured using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. RNAs with an RNA integrity number (RIN) > 7 were used for library construction and sequencing.

Libraries were constructed using the NEBNext Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB, USA) and evaluated with the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer system. Qualified libraries—those passing quality control—were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus (Illumina, USA) platform to generate raw sequencing data.

The raw sequencing data were first filtered using fastp. Adapter-containing reads, poly-N-containing reads, and low-quality reads were removed to produce high-quality clean reads. Transcripts were assembled using the Trinity 2.4.0 de novo assembler. The read count for each gene was obtained using FeatureCounts v1.5.0-p3. Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (FPKM) values for each gene were calculated based on the unigene length and the number of mapped reads.

Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 software (1.20.0), and genes with |log2(fold change)|> 1 and an adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses of the identified DEGs were performed using the cluster Profiler R package. GO terms and signaling pathways with padj < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Metabolomics analysis

The endogenous metabolites in the liver samples from each experimental group (control and optimal SeNPs dose) (n = 6) were detected using untargeted liquid chromatography‑tandem mass spectrometry (LC‑MS/MS). Metabolite detection and metabolomic data analysis were performed according to the method described by Zhang et al. (2024):

Metabolite Extraction. A 100-mg tissue sample was milled with liquid nitrogen and placed in an EP tube, and 500 μL of 80% methanol aqueous solution was added. The sample was vortexed and shaken, allowed to stand in an ice bath for 5 min, and then centrifuged at 15,000 × g for 20 min at 4°C. A defined volume of the supernatant was diluted with mass spectrometry-grade water to achieve a final methanol concentration of 53%. The diluted sample was centrifuged at 5000 × g for 20 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was collected and injected into an LC–MS instrument for analysis.

The gradient elution program was as follows: 0 min, A/B = 98:2 (v/v); 1.5 min, 98:2 (v/v); 3 min, 15:85 (v/v); 10 min, 0:100 (v/v); 10.1 min, 98:2 (v/v); 11 min, 98:2 (v/v); 12 min, 98:2 (v/v). The flow rate was 0.2 mL/min, and the column temperature was maintained at 40°C. The injection volume was 4 μL.

Data processing. The metabolomics data were analyzed using Excel 2019, GraphPad Prism 10, R (v4.3.1), and SPSS 26.0. The mass spectrometry data were processed using CD3.1 software, which determined the retention time, mass-to-charge ratio, and other parameters of each metabolite. The metabolites in the samples were identified and quantitatively analyzed using the metabolite databases mzCloud, mzVault, and Masslist. The software metaX was used to perform principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), and to calculate the variable importance in projection (VIP) scores for each metabolite. The statistical significance (P-value) of each metabolite between the two groups was calculated using the t-test, while fold change (FC) was determined separately. The default criteria for screening differentially abundant metabolites (DAMs) were VIP > 1, P < 0.05, and FC ≥ 2 or FC ≤ 0.5. KEGG pathway analysis was performed for DAMs using the KEGG database for DAMs.

Integrative analysis of differentially expressed genes and differentially abundant metabolites

DEGs and DAMs that were mapped onto the same KEGG pathways were selected for further integrative analysis to explore their potential interactions. The correlations between genes and metabolites were calculated with Pearson correlation coefficients (PCCs) using R software. The results were presented as nine-quadrant diagrams and heatmaps to visualize the relationships between gene expression and metabolite abundance.

Results

Survival rate

Following the feeding trial and saline–alkaline stress exposure, the survival rates of G. przewalskii were quantified across experimental groups. The survival rates exhibited a nonlinear relationship with dietary selenium supplementation levels, and peaking in NSE4 (100%). Specifically, the control group demonstrated the lowest survival rate (81.67%), followed by NSE2 (85.83%) (Fig. 1). This nonlinear dose–response relationship suggests the existence of a selenium-mediated physiological threshold for osmoregulatory resilience in the extremophile teleost fish. These findings underscore the necessity for precision nutraceutical formulations in conservation aquaculture targeting saline–alkaline ecosystems.

Transcriptomic analysis

To investigate the protective effect of SeNPs on G. przewalskii, we first fed with the fish diets containing different concentrations of SeNPs. After 8 weeks of feeding trial, the fish were exposed to saline–alkaline stress. Following 120 h of stress exposure, the survival rate was lowest in the NSEC group and highest in the NSE4 group. Liver tissues from the control groups (NSEC_0, NSE4_0) and the test groups (NSEC_120, NSE4_120) were collected for transcriptomic analysis. A total of 25,105,991, 23,231,007, 24,079,925, and 23,284,398 raw reads were obtained from the NSEC_0, NSEC_120, NSE4_0, and NSE4_120 groups, respectively, and quality control was then performed on the raw data to obtain high-quality clean reads. The clean reads numbered 23,830,146 (NSEC_0), 22,374,686 (NSEC_120), 23,044,870 (NSE4_0), and 22,136,258 (NSE4_120), each with a Q30 score of at least 96.21%. After assembly, 58,341, 74,066, 58,980, and 51,189 genes were identified in the NSEC_0, NSEC_120, NSE4_0, and NSE4_120 groups, respectively. Among these, 34,932 genes were co-expressed across all four groups, while 5390, 4713, 3360, and 2337 genes were specifically expressed in the NSEC_0, NSEC_120, NSE4_0, and NSE4_120 groups, respectively (Fig. 2).

Differentially expressed genes

DEGs were identified based on the criteria of |log2[fold-change (FC)] |≥ 1 and P-value < 0.05. Overall, 3495, 1852, 137, and 754 DEGs were identified in the NSEC_120 vs. NSEC_0 (1708 upregulated and 1787 downregulated), NSE4_120 vs. NSE4_0 (1077 upregulated and 775 downregulated), NSE4_0 vs. NSEC_0 (64 upregulated and 73 downregulated), and NSE4_120 vs. NSEC_120 (428 upregulated and 326 downregulated) groups, respectively (Fig. 3). Analysis of DEGs showed that many were uniquely expressed in each comparison group. The greatest number of unique DEGs was found in NSEC_120 vs. NSEC_0 (2,287), while the fewest were observed in NSE4_0 vs. NSEC_0 (57) (Fig. 4). Additionally, 955 and 283 unique DEGs were identified in the NSE4_120 vs. NSE4_0 and NSE4_120 vs. NSEC_120 groups, respectively. These results indicate that there was little difference in gene expression in liver tissues of G. przewalskii after dietary supplementation with SeNPs, whereas substantial differences in gene expression were observed in G. przewalskii fed the basal diet before and after saline- alkaline stress. Moreover, the number of DEGs in the NSE4_120 vs. NSE4_0 comparison group was less than that in the NSEC_120 vs. NSEC_0 group, suggesting that dietary SeNPs may protect G. przewalskii from saline–alkaline stress.

Functional annotation of differentially expressed genes

To investigate the effects of saline–alkaline stress on gene expression in liver tissues of G. przewalskii, we first performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses on the DEGs in the NSEC_120 vs. NSEC_0 and NSE4_120 vs. NSE4_0 comparison groups. GO enrichment analysis revealed that DEGs in NSEC_120 vs. NSEC_0 were annotated to 121 GO terms, of which 4 were significantly enriched (adjusted P-value (padj) < 0.05), including “oxidoreductase activity,” “tRNA metabolic process,” “carbohydrate metabolic process,” and “protein folding and protein glycosylation.” In NSE4_120 vs. NSE4_0, DEGs were annotated to 118 GO terms, of which 12 were significantly enriched (padj < 0.05), including “oxidoreductase activity,” “ligase activity,” “endoplasmic reticulum,” “carbohydrate metabolic process,” “catalytic activity, protein folding,” “transmembrane transport,” “transporter activity,” “carbohydrate derivative metabolic process,” “cellular amino acid metabolic process,” “lipid metabolic process,” and “cytokinesis” (Fig. 5). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that DEGs in NSEC_120 vs. NSEC_0 were significantly enriched in 32 pathways (padj < 0.05), with “protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum” containing the highest number of DEGs (85 DEGs) and “caffeine metabolism” containing the fewest DEGs (5 DEGs). In the NSE4_120 vs. NSE4_0 comparison group, DEGs were enriched in 24 pathways (padj < 0.05), which included pathways related to fatty acid metabolism, amino acid metabolism, and others.

Metabolomics analysis

Untargeted high-performance liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS) analyses were performed to detect metabolites in liver tissues of G. przewalskii. In total, 907 and 531 metabolites were identified using the positive- and negative-ion models, respectively (Table S1). All metabolites were detected in samples from all four groups. Principal component analysis (PCA) showed that metabolites from NSEC_0 and NSE4_0 clustered together, as did metabolites from NSEC_120 and NSE4_120, indicating that dietary SeNPs affected liver metabolism in G. przewalskii. The first principal component (PC1, 23.29%) effectively distinguished the groups NSEC_0, NSE4_0, NSEC_120, and NSE4_120, but the second principal component (PC2, 12.34%) did not separate the four groups (Fig. 7). Additionally, metabolite profiles varied among the four groups, indicating that saline–alkaline stress also influenced liver metabolism in G. przewalskii. Further analysis using the partial least-squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) model showed that metabolites in the NSEC_0, NSEC_120, NSE4_0, and NSE4_120 groups were distinct (Fig. 8). The OPLS-DA model’s goodness of fit and predictive capacity indicated strong model performance.

Functional annotation of differentially abundant metabolites

To further understand the biological significance of these DAMs, KEGG enrichment pathway analysis was conducted to identify key metabolic pathways involved in the saline–alkaline stress response of G. przewalskii. Enrichment and topological analyses were performed to select the top 20 metabolic pathways with the highest enrichment scores and lowest P-values. In the NESC_120 vs. NESC_0 comparison group, 491 DAMs were enriched in 145 pathways, among which 16 pathways were significantly enriched (P < 0.05), including “biosynthesis of alkaloids derived from ornithine, lysine and nicotinic acid” and “d-glutamine and d-glutamate metabolism” (Fig. 10a). In the NES4_120 vs. NES4_0 group, 357 DAMs were enriched in 110 pathways, with 17 pathways significantly enriched (P < 0.05), such as “glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism” and “C5-branched dibasic acid metabolism” (Fig. 10b). In the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 group, 390 DAMs were significantly enriched in pathways including “protein digestion and absorption,” “aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis,” and “monobactam biosynthesis” (P < 0.05) (Fig. 10c).

In addition, the 103 DAMs uniquely detected in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 comparison group were annotated to 80 pathways. The significantly enriched pathways were primarily associated with “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites” (11 DAMs) and “biosynthesis of amino acids” (6 DAMs) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 10d).

In the NES4_120 vs. NES4_0 comparison group, several differential metabolites related to osmoregulation were identified, including taurine, proline, and trehalose. Taurine was annotated to the “metabolic pathways” and “taurine and hypotaurine metabolism” pathways, while proline was annotated to the “arginine and proline metabolism” and “metabolic pathways” pathways. However, none of these three pathways were significantly enriched (P > 0.05). Another metabolite associated with osmotic stress response, trehalose, was annotated to multiple pathways, including “starch and sucrose metabolism,” “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites,” “phosphotransferase system (PTS),” “ABC transporters,” and “metabolic pathways.” Among these pathways, the “starch and sucrose metabolism” pathway was significantly enriched (P < 0.05).

Integrative analysis of differentially expressed genes and differentially abundant metabolites

To comprehensively investigate the role of SeNPs in enhancing the saline–alkaline tolerance of G. przewalskii, an integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis was conducted in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 comparison group to identify critical genes, pathways, and metabolites involved in this adaptive process. This study prioritizes the intersection of transcriptional and metabolic regulatory networks. Overlapping DEGs and DAMs within these pathways provide critical insights into the molecular and biochemical strategies employed by G. przewalskii to counteract or adapt to hypertonic saline–alkaline stress.

A total of 64 coenriched KEGG pathways—defined as pathways enriched in both DEGs and DAMs—were identified in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 group, demonstrating the overlap between these two omics datasets (Fig. 11a). Among these common pathways, “starch and sucrose metabolism” was significantly enriched (P < 0.05). Furthermore, specifically 20 coenriched pathways of unique DEGs and DAMs in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 group were detected. These DEGs and DAMs were significantly annotated to the “central carbon metabolism in cancer” pathway (P < 0.05) (Fig. 11b).

Integrative analysis of differentially expressed genes and differentially abundant metabolites. a Venn diagram of all DEGs and DAMs in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 group. b Venn diagram of unique DEGs and DAMs in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 group. c Nine-quadrant diagram of DEGs and DAMs annotated to “starch and sucrose metabolism” pathway. d Nine-quadrant diagram of DEGs and DAMs annotated to “central carbon metabolism in cancer” pathway

The DEGs and DAMs in quadrants 3 and 7 are positively correlated, while those in quadrants 1 and 9 are negatively correlated. In the “starch and sucrose metabolism” pathway, the expression of HK is negatively correlated with the abundance of d-fructose 6-phosphate and d-glucose 6-phosphate. In contrast, the expression levels of agla, MGAM, gyg2, gba3, and LOC107755676 are positively correlated with the abundance of d-fructose 6-phosphate and d-glucose 6-phosphate, while the expression of HK is positively correlated with the abundance of trehalose. The expression levels of MGAM, agla, gyg2, gba3, and LOC107722579 are negatively correlated with the abundance of trehalose, and the abundance of trehalose is lower than the expression levels of MGAM, agla, gyg2, and gba3 (Fig. 11c).

In the “central carbon metabolism in cancer” pathway, the expressions of HK, SLC16A3, and SLC7A5 are negatively correlated with the abundances of d-fructose 6-phosphate, d-glucose 6-phosphate, and l-asparagine. Although the abundances of these metabolites are higher relative to the expression levels of these genes, such comparisons should be interpreted with caution. Conversely, the expression levels of HK, SLC16A3, and SLC7A5 are positively correlated with the abundance of l-arginine. The expression levels of LDH and RET are positively correlated with d-fructose 6-phosphate, d-glucose 6-phosphate, and l-asparagine, and negatively correlated with l-arginine; similarly, the abundance of l-arginine is lower than the expression levels of LDH and RET (Fig. 11d).

DEGs (SLC16A3 and SLC7A5) and DAMs (d-fructose 6-phosphate, d-glucose 6-phosphate, l-asparagine, and l-arginine), which are significantly enriched in the “central carbon metabolism in cancer” pathway, may be key factors in the protective role of SeNPs when the G. przewalskii encounters saline and alkaline stress.

Discussion

Qinghai Lake is the largest saltwater lake in China (Tang et al. 2018). G. przewalskii is a aline–alkaline tolerant fish species, and occupies an irreplaceable core position in the ecosystem of Qinghai Lake (Wang et al. 2021). G. przewalskii lives in Qinghai Lake, which is characterized by high alkalinity, high pH, and high altitude. The unique environmental adaptability and migratory reproductive characteristics of G. przewalskii are of great significance for studying salinity and alkalinity tolerance in fish, as well as the evolutionary mechanisms of G. przewalskii in a saline–alkaline environment. These characteristics are also central to the Qinghai Lake ecosystem (Tian et al. 2023). According to previous studies, the low efficiency of artificial breeding might be related to the high saline–alkaline environment (Wei et al. 2022a, b; Wei et al. 2024). High salinity and alkalinity are key factors restricting the survival of G. przewalskii (Tong and Li 2020; Zhou et al. 2025). Improving the antioxidant capacity and resistance to extreme environments of juvenile G. przewalskii may be an important way to increase the survival and establishment rate following artificial breeding. Therefore, in this study, antioxidant SeNPs were added to the basic diet of juvenile G. przewalskii.

The protective effect of SeNPs on juvenile Gymnocypris przewalskii was revealed based on transcriptomic data

Salinity stress can alter intracellular ionic and molecular composition, and disrupt osmotic pressure homeostasis, leading to hyperpolarization of mitochondrial membranes. This induces oxidative damage and disrupts cellular structure and function. G. przewalskii activates a variety of mechanisms to cope with salinity stress (Cao et al. 2019; Hermes et al. 2023). The antioxidant system plays an important role in maintaining cellular redox homeostasis. In this study, under saline–alkaline stress (NSE4_120 vs. NSEC_120), the DEGs (including HPX and LRRC4B(NGL3)) were significantly enriched in the GO term “oxidoreductase activity,” indicating that G. przewalskii fed with SeNPs has strong redox capacity. This capacity helps maintain organismal balance by scavenging oxidative stress, thereby improving tolerance to salinity and alkalinity stress.

Hemopexin (HPX) is a heme-binding glycoprotein involved in detoxification, oxidative stress regulation, and immune defense (Hahl et al. 2017; Nakharuthai et al. 2020). Plasma HPX level is, therefore, an important biomarker for inflammatory and oxidative stress (Lin et al. 2012; Montecinos et al. 2019). As a classical acute-phase protein (APP), HPX limits heme-driven oxidative stress and prevents host tissue damage, particularly under hemolytic or septic conditions (Li et al. 2023a, b; Matalon 2025). Recent studies have suggested that HPX is closely involved in the innate immunity and stress response of teleosts, as evidenced by significant modulation of its expression levels upon immune stimulation and/or environmental perturbation (Lee et al. 2014; Machado et al. 2014). HPX is predominantly synthesized in the liver but is also expressed in the skin and kidney of teleosts, and is systemically upregulated during inflammation (Kim et al. 2025). HPX is most abundant in the liver and increases significantly in the liver, spleen, head, kidney, and peripheral blood of Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) after bacterial challenge. Furthermore, HPX mRNA is also significantly upregulated in monocytes/macrophages and hepatocytes under stimulation by Streptococcus agalactiae or Aeromonas hydrophila. In addition, the recombinant HPX protein can effectively reduce bacterial proliferation and alleviate the inflammatory reaction caused by bacteria. Moreover, HPX also regulates the respiratory burst of monocytes/macrophages and plays an important role in the antioxidant process (Yin et al. 2021). HPX protein is linked to temperature-induced morbidity or mortality, organ inflammation, phagocytosis, and the proliferation and differentiation of immune cells in zebrafish (Danio rerio). Its expression shows significant alterations exclusively due to temperature changes (Cho et al. 2019).

Both glycolysis and gluconeogenesis are tightly and reciprocally regulated in response to changes in energy status and glucose levels in the cell (Smolle et al. 2020; Xiong et al. 2011). Studies have showed that glycolysis and gluconeogenesis play essential roles in the plateau adaptation of Gammarus lacustris and other resident aquatic species in the Tibetan Plateau (Jin et al. 2019). Therefore, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis may be important for providing energy for G. przewalskii to survive in the harsh environment of Qinghai Lake. This may because G. przewalskii requires more energy to live in the high-salinity and high-alkalinity environment of Qinghai Lake, which is consistent with results from other Tibetan Plateau adaptation studies (Guo et al. 2015; Shang et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2015).

Salinity stress can affect the process of sugar metabolism in aquatic animals. As the main organ of carbohydrate metabolism, the liver can provide a source of metabolic substrates for the energy-demanding reactions of osmotic-pressure-regulating tissues (gill, kidney, intestine) in aquatic animals, such as fish (Kamalam et al. 2017; Pan et al. 2023). In contrast to proteins and lipids, sugars can release a large amount of ATP after rapid decomposition in energy-demanding process. Proteins and lipids must first be converted—proteins through deamination and their carbon skeletons entering various points, and lipids into acetyl CoA—before they can enter the tricarboxylic acid cycle for complete oxidative catabolism to supply energy (Yao et al. 2023). Therefore, for aquatic animals such as fish, sugars can be rapidly decomposed to provide substantial energy to cope with environmental challenges, thereby maintaining normal physiological processes and internal homeostasis (Su et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2022). Studies have found that salinity stress stimulates the activity of glycolytic enzymes in the liver during adaptation to salinity changes. As the duration of stress increases, a significant amount of hepatic glycogen is broken down, and gluconeogenesis is simultaneously enhanced to maintain glucose homeostasis. When glucose was injected into great blue-spotted mudskippers (Boleophthalmus pectinirostris), researchers found that it promoted the activities of hepatic glycolytic enzymes, preserved hepatic glycogen, and inhibited gluconeogenesis. This suggests that exogenous glucose supplementation can provide energy for osmotic pressure regulation in a timely manner and contribute to the conservation of energy reserves in fish, thus avoiding the breakdown of non-sugar substances such as proteins (Guo et al. 2020). Other studies have shown that fish require rapid glucose catabolism to provide ATP in response to salinity stress, while hepatic gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis proceed slowly and cannot meet the rapid physiological demand for energy (Pan et al. 2023). During adaption to salinity changes, the metabolic rate and oxygen consumption rate of fish increase as salinity rises. Acute salinity stress promotes the catabolism of hepatic glycogen in the liver of the mudskipper (Boleophthalmus pectinirostris) and significantly increases the activity of key rate-limiting enzymes in hepatic glucose metabolism, such as hexokinase and phosphofructokinase (Guo et al. 2020). This demonstrates that the demand for sugars is acute when fish are subjected to salinity stress. Under such conditions, most aquatic animals preferentially metabolize sugars and lipids to cope with environmental challenges, thereby sparing proteins. When fish experience stress, glucose concentration in the serum increases, providing a constant supply of energy to the organism, which may help fish better utilize sugars to adapt to environmental changes (Yang et al. 2024). In this study, under saline–alkaline stress (NSE4_120 vs. NSEC_120), DEGs (including pckA (PCK), frmA, ADH5, and adhC) were significantly enriched in the “glycolysis/gluconeogenesis” pathway, indicating that G. przewalskii adapt to saline–alkaline stress by altering carbohydrate metabolism.

The gram-positive bacterium Corynebacterium glutamicum expresses multiple enzymes capable of initiating gluconeogenesis, including phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PCK), oxaloacetate decarboxylase, and malic enzyme, all three of which can catalyze the first step in gluconeogenesis (Riedel et al. 2001; Geng et al. 2021). Among these, both cytosolic (Pck1) and mitochondrial (Pck2) isoforms of phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase have been identified as crucial rate-limiting enzymes in gluconeogenic pathways (Marandel et al. 2019). Recent studies in Monopterus albus demonstrate that exposure to nanoplastics activates the PPAR signaling pathway, leading to upregulated the mRNA levels of gluconeogenic enzymes such as PCK and subsequent lipid metabolism disorders (Zhu et al. 2023). Insulin exerts dual regulatory effects on glucose metabolism, not only facilitating blood glucose uptake but also modulating hepatic gluconeogenesis through control of PCK activity (Shao et al. 2005). Elevated PCK expression correlates with increased blood glucose levels and serves as an indicator of the diabetic phenotypes in zebrafish. Nutritional overload has been shown to significantly upregulate mRNA expression of both PCK1 and PCK2 isozymes (Shezi et al. 2024). Furthermore, the AKT-FoxO1-PCK/ChREBP signaling axis has been implicated in the pathogenesis of metabolic liver disorders in largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) (Li et al. 2025).

Combined analysis of methylome and transcriptome data has identified frmA as a key genetic determinant of pigmentation and epigenetic regulation in Oujiang color common carp (Zhao et al. 2025). Alcohol dehydrogenase 5 (ADH5) exhibits developmental stage-specific expression patterns in fish embryogenesis (Dasmahapatra et al. 2005; Massarsky et al. 2018). The ADH5 enzyme also plays roles in the clearance of formaldehyde and is implicated in the pathology of Fanconi anemia (FA) (Mu et al. 2023).

DEGs, particularly HPX and PCK, which are annotated to the “oxidoreductase activity” and “glycolysis/gluconeogenesis” signaling pathways, require further investigated for their roles in aquatic animals under osmotic stress.

Previous studies have shown that, compared with lipids, sugars are more easily utilized by organisms, and carbohydrate metabolism provides energy in response to stress (Li et al. 2018). Therefore, we hypothesized that G. przewalskii requires more energy to maintain osmotic pressure balance after saline–alkaline stress. The DEGs annotated to “glycolysis/gluconeogenesis” might be involved in mediating the protective effect of SeNPs on G. przewalskii by regulating genes related to carbohydrate metabolism. Thus, the liver’s response to saline–alkaline stress in G. przewalskii may involve the regulation of hepatic glucose metabolism.

Thyroid hormone (TH) regulation of metabolic processes is essential for normal growth and development (Ortiga-Carvalho et al. 2014). Thyroid hormones increase obligatory thermogenesis by stimulating multiple metabolic pathways involved in energy production, tissue development, and remodeling (Filfilan 2023). It has been reported that THs might help organisms cope with various environmental challenges (Costa-e-Sousa and Hollenberg 2012). Moreover, the hypothyroid state may also favor longevity (Garasto et al. 2017). In addition, THs can play roles in the organism’s tumorigenesis and non-specific immunity (Nii et al. 2019). Changes in salinity can affect physiological functions such as energy metabolism, antioxidant systems, non-specific immunity, autophagy, and apoptosis in fish (Evans and Kultz 2020), with non-specific immunity being a crucial stress response mechanism. In this study, under saline–alkaline stress (NSE4_120 vs. NSEC_120), the unique DEGs were significantly enriched in the “thyroid hormone signaling pathway”, indicating that G. przewalskii activated various non-specific immune pathways involved in the response to saline–alkaline stress.

The protective effect of SeNPs on juvenile Gymnocypris przewalskii was revealed based on metabonomic data

In this study, the differential metabolites identified in the NES4_120 vs. NES4_0 comparison were significantly enriched in signaling pathways related to amino acid biosynthesis and metabolism. Several of these differential metabolites are associated with osmoregulation, including taurine, proline, and trehalose. Particularly noteworthy was the identification of trehalose within the significantly enriched “starch and sucrose metabolism” pathway.

Eukaryotic organisms universally employ osmolytes like betaine, taurine, and myo-inositol as protective mechanisms against hypertonic stress conditions (Bounedjah et al. 2012). These organic osmolytes, particularly taurine and betaine, play crucial roles in osmoregulation across various tissues, including the brain and liver, which frequently encounter hypertonic challenges during pathological states (Loyher et al. 2004; Schäfer et al. 2007; Slow et al. 2009). The physiological effects of taurine primarily involve modulation of ion transport systems and protein phosphorylation cascades. While some of its actions on ion transport may result from membrane structural modifications, alternative mechanisms involving direct regulation of transporter activity through expression levels, phosphorylation status, or cytoskeletal organization cannot be excluded. Significantly, all these regulatory mechanisms are known to respond to osmotic stress conditions. Given its fundamental role as a cellular osmolyte, taurine’s influence on iron transport may be intrinsically connected to its osmoregulatory functions. Experimental evidence indicates that certain taurine-mediated effects might occur through osmotic stress-responsive signaling pathways (Schaffer et al. 2000). Recent investigations have uncovered an essential, previously underestimated contribution of taurine to cardiovascular performance and stress adaptation in fish species (Dixon et al. 2023). The combined dietary administration of taurine and potassium demonstrates synergistic effects in stress reduction and growth enhancement compared to standard freshwater aquaculture practices. In O. niloticus, this nutritional supplementation regimen has been shown to regulate antioxidant defenses and metabolic enzyme profiles, thereby mitigating stress responses and optimizing energy utilization (Velselvi et al. 2021). Environmental salinity variations can trigger oxidative damage through reactive oxygen species (ROS) overproduction. When ROS accumulation exceeds cellular antioxidant capacity, it may precipitate oxidative stress, cellular malfunction, and ultimately programmed cell death or necrosis. The observed increases in superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) activities represent a cellular defense mechanism against excessive ROS generation (Bal et al. 2021). Taurine serves as a powerful antioxidant, providing protection against salinity-induced oxidative injury in fish tissues (Zeng et al. 2010). Furthermore, experimental studies demonstrate that taurine administration via intraperitoneal injection effectively counteracts ammonia-induced dysregulation of plasma glucose levels and osmotic homeostasis through modulation of glucose metabolism and osmoregulatory processes. In large-scale loach populations, ammonia exposure elicits substantial immune system activation, which can be ameliorated by taurine supplementation (Zhou et al. 2026). Under low-salinity conditions, taurine serves as a key modulator of the oyster’s adaptive response to osmotic stress. The osmoregulatory and antioxidant effects of taurine are mediated through transport mechanisms that are sensitive to extracellular NaCl concentrations (Gong & Li 2025). Notably, taurine has also been identified as an important osmoregulator in chimpanzee sperm cells, where it may contribute to membrane stabilization (Ozasa & Gould 1982). Although the critical role of proline in plant osmotic stress responses has been well established (Koenigshofer & Loeppert 2019; Ghosh et al. 2022; Yan et al. 2025), its specific function in osmoregulation in fish remains less understood and requires further investigation.

As a biologically active macromolecule with osmoregulatory properties, trehalose has emerged as a critical mediator of osmotic stress responses (MacIntyre et al. 2020; Yan et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2024). Research indicates that trehalose not only enhances the quality of processed fish products (Kim et al. 2024) but also improves fish benefits during transportation and cryopreservation (Huang et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2024a, b; Zhang et al. 2025). Functioning as an autophagy promoter, trehalose has been shown to alleviate anxiety-like behaviors while moderately enhancing social interaction and predator avoidance in genetically modified zebrafish (Licitra et al. 2025). Additionally, trehalose significantly improves post-thaw recovery and functional integrity of fish embryonic stem cells (Dash et al. 2008) and spermatozoa (Gheller et al. 2019; Varela et al. 2022), while also promoting growth performance, non-specific immunity, gene expression, and desiccation resistance in aquatic species (Zhu et al. 2018; Lu et al. 2021; Xiang et al. 2025). Although the involvement of trehalose in plant osmotic stress responses has been widely reported, its functional significance in aquatic animals remains relatively unexplored. Investigating the underlying mechanisms of trehalose-mediated osmoregulation in aquatic organisms will constitute a major focus of our future studies.

In this study, DAMs detected in the NES4_120 vs. NESC_120 comparison group were enriched in the pathways of “oxidative phosphorylation,” “apoptosis,” and “FoxO signaling pathway.” These results suggest that SeNPs might confer protective effects in G. przewalskii under saline–alkaline stress by modulating key physiological functions, including apoptosis, autophagy, and immune responses.

DEGs (such as SLC16A3 and SLC7A5), along with DAMs (including d-fructose 6-phosphate, d-glucose 6-phosphate, l-asparagine, and l-arginine), are significantly enriched in the “central carbon metabolism in cancer” pathway. These factors may play key roles in the protective effect of SeNPs when G. przewalskii is exposed to saline–alkaline stress.

The lipid bilayer of cellular membranes constitutes an efficient yet complex barrier that restricts the entry of many molecules, including chemotherapeutic agents. To overcome this limitation, evolutionary processes have selected membrane transport proteins that mediate the movement of endogenous compounds essential for cellular homeostasis. This regulated membrane transport predominantly depends on solute carrier (SLC) proteins—a functionally diverse class of transmembrane transporters (Sprowl and Sparreboom 2014). Among vertebrate tissues, the kidney, brain, liver, intestine, and heart exhibit the highest expression levels of solute carrier (SLC) transporters. Their pronounced abundance in renal tissues may suggest a mechanistic role in osmoregulatory homeostasis (Zhang et al. 2019). The SLC protein family mediates the majority of ionic and organic solute transport along renal tubular segments (Lewis et al. 2021). Recent studies have demonstrated that SLC family genes are involved in key biological functions of aquatic species, including nutrient transport dynamics, regulation of metabolic homeostasis, and modulation of stress responses (Lin et al. 2025; Romersi and Nicklisch 2021).

The solute carrier 16 (SLC16) gene family comprises 14 members (Halestrap 2013). The SLC16A1 gene encodes monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), which facilitates proton-coupled transport of monocarboxylates across biological membranes. Members of the SLC16 family represent a group of structurally diverse membrane proteins responsible for mediating transmembrane transport of lactate and other monocarboxylates (Becker et al., 2010). SLC16 family members exhibit diverse functional roles, ranging from nutrient transport and intracellular pH regulation to thyroid hormone homeostasis. They also play critical roles in the transport of essential cellular nutrients and in cellular metabolic process (Felmlee et al. 2020). Previous investigations have revealed that SLC16-mediated transmembrane transport plays a critical role in the response to salinity stress in the Manila clam (Ruditapes philippinarum) (Lin et al. 2025).

The transcriptomic analysis of liver and spleen tissues in Oreochromis aureus subjected to long-term salinity stress revealed that the SLC7 family demonstrates heightened sensitivity to salinity fluctuations, suggesting its potential functional significance in mediating long-term stress adaptation mechanisms in teleost fish (Zhou et al. 2020). SLC7A5, a sodium-independent transporter, functions as an amino acid antiporter that exchanges intracellular glutamine with large neutral amino acids (LNAAs) such as leucine, phenylalanine, and tryptophan (Kanai et al. 1998). Investigations into the effects of varying protein sources on the growth performance, amino acid transport, and intestinal health of grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella) revealed that the interaction effects of temperature and protein sources significantly modulated the expression levels of genes encoding key amino acid transporters. These genes include solute carrier family 1 member 3 (SLC1A3), solute carrier family 7 members 1 (SLC7A1) and 5 (SLC7A5), solute carrier family 15 member 1b (SLC15A1b), and solute carrier family 7 member 7 (SLC7A7) (Wu et al. 2024a, b). Dietary supplementation with fish protein hydrolysate (FPH) at 30 g/kg diet was found to significantly upregulate the expression of solute carrier genes SLC7A5, SLC7A8, SLC38A2, and SLC15A2 in intestinal tissues of juvenile largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) (Fan et al. 2022).

In this study, SLC16A3 and SLC7A5 exhibited significantly elevated expression levels in the NSE4 group compared to the NSEC group (P < 0.05), suggesting that SeNPs may confer cytoprotective effects in G. przewalskii during exposure to saline–alkaline stress through modulation of both SLC16A3 and SLC7A5 gene expression. This regulatory mechanism appears functionally linked to metabolic reprogramming, as evidenced by coordinated alterations in key metabolites—d-fructose 6-phosphate, d-glucose 6-phosphate, l-asparagine, and l-arginine—that exhibit molecular interactions with these solute carrier proteins.

Conclusion

Dietary nano-selenium may enhance the environmental adaptability of G. przewalskii juveniles by modulating glycolysis and gluconeogenesis, oxidative phosphorylation, and non-specific immune pathways. Under saline–alkaline stress, SeNPs potentially improve osmoregulatory capacity in G. przewalskii by regulating the expression of the SLC16A3 and SLC7A5 genes. The regulation thereby facilitates environmental adaptation. Our findings provide new insights into the potential mechanisms underlying the protective effects of SeNPs in aquatic organisms under environmental stress conditions. Nevertheless, further studies are warranted to identify the molecular mechanisms of the pathway involving SeNP-mediated expression of SLC16A3 and SLC7A5.

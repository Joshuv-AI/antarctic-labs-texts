Safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 32651 for all animal species (BioCC OÜ)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Secundino López Puente, Natalia Alija‐Novo, Montserrat Anguita, Nicole Bozzi Cionci, Rosella Brozzi, Matteo Lorenzo Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño, Piera Valeri, Yolanda García‐Cazorla
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 32651 when used as a technological additive to improve ensiling of fresh plant material. The additive is intended for use in all fresh material for all animal species at a proposed minimum concentration of 1 × 108 colony forming units (CFU)/kg fresh plant material. The bacterial species L. buchneri is considered by EFSA to be suitable for the qualified presumption of safety approach to safety assessment. The identity of the strain was established and no acquired antimicrobial resistance genes of concern were detected. Therefore, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) concluded that the use of the strain as a silage additive is considered safe for all the animal species, for consumers of products from animals fed the treated silage and for the environment. Regarding user safety, the additive should be considered as a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser, and any exposure through skin and respiratory tract is considered a risk. The additive is not an eye irritant. The FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive consisting of L. buchneri DSM 32651 at a minimum concentration of 1 × 108 CFU/kg fresh material may extend the aerobic stability of silage prepared from fresh plant material with a DM range of 28%–45%.Keywords: efficacy, Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 32651, QPS, safety, silage additive, technological additive

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7.

The European Commission received a request from BioCC OÜ 2 for the authorisation of the additive consisting of Lentilactobacillus buchneri DSM 32651, when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: technological additives; functional group: silage additives).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 6 June 2023 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2023‐00392. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 7 September 2023.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of L. buchneri DSM 32651, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.4).

Additional information

The additive is a preparation containing L. buchneri DSM 32651. It has not been previously authorised as a feed additive in the European Union.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 3 in support of the authorisation request for the use of L. buchneri DSM 32651 as a feed additive.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 4 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 5 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 29 May to 19 June 2024 for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 8 September 2023 to 8 December 2023 for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of L. buchneri DSM 32651 in animal feed. 6

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of L. buchneri DSM 32651 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 7 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017), Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018a), Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms used as feed additives or as production organisms (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018b), EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2021) and Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023).

ASSESSMENT

The present additive is a preparation of viable cells of L. buchneri DSM 32651 intended to be added to fresh plant material to promote ensiling (technological additives, functional group: silage additives) with the eventual use of the silage for all animal species.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the active agent

The active agent was originally isolated from silage and is deposited in the Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen (DSMZ) with the accession number DSM 32651. 8 The applicant claims that it has not been genetically modified. 9

The taxonomical identification of DSM 32651 was done by digital DNA–DNA hybridisation (dDDH) based on whole genome sequence (WGS) data. Results showed a similarity of DSM 32651 of 99.2% with the type strain L. buchneri DSM 20057T. 10

The antimicrobial susceptibility of DSM 32651 was tested against the battery of antibiotics recommended by the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018b). 11 All the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values fell below the corresponding EFSA cut‐off values for obligate heterofermentative lactobacilli. Therefore, the strain is susceptible to all the relevant antibiotics.

The WGS data of the active agent was interrogated for the presence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes by search against the ResFinder and CARD databases. 12 No hits exceeding the thresholds recommended by EFSA were found (EFSA, 2021) and therefore, it can be concluded that the strain does not harbour antimicrobial resistance genes.

The applicant provided data on the capacity of the strain DSM 32651 to produce antimicrobial substances. The strains tested were Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 25932, Staphylococcus saprophyticus WDCM 00159, Enterococcus faecalis ATCC 29212, Escherichia coli DSM 1576 and Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica ATCC 13076. The active agent showed some minor antimicrobial activity towards all tested bacterial strains in microaerophilic conditions, which is in line with the biology of the species and of no concern. 13

Characterisation of the additive

The active agent is produced by fermentation, the biomass is concentrated by centrifugation and blended with cryoprotectants. The mixture is freeze‐dried and ground to powder. 14 The qualitative composition of the fermentation media was listed but the quantitative composition was not provided. 15 However, none of the products raises safety concerns. The applicant declared that no antimicrobials are used during the manufacturing process. 16

The feed additive consists of approximately ■■■■■ freeze‐dried cells concentrate (of which ■■■■■ and ■■■■■), 17 ■■■■■ anhydrous dextrose and ■■■■■ silicon dioxide. 18 , 19 All the proposed cryoprotectants and carriers are feed materials or authorised feed additives. The additive is specified to contain a minimum concentration of L. buchneri DSM 32651 of 1.0 × 1011 CFU/g. Analysis of five batches of the additive confirmed compliance with specifications, showing an average value of 1.3 × 1011 CFU/g (range 1.2–1.4 × 1011 CFU/g) for L. buchneri DSM 32651. 20

Three batches of the additive showed compliance with the specifications set by the applicant for Escherichia coli (< 10 CFU/g), Enterobacteriaceae (< 10 CFU/g), yeasts and filamentous fungi (< 10 CFU/g) and Salmonella spp. (no detection in 25 g). 21

Three batches of the additive were analysed for cadmium, lead, mercury and arsenic concentrations. Cadmium levels ranged 0.011–0.012 mg/kg, lead 0.022–0.026 mg/kg and arsenic 0.013–0.014 mg/kg of additive. Mercury was below the limit of quantification (LOQ) of the method applied in all the batches. 22 , 23 The same batches were also analysed for mycotoxins, including aflatoxins (B1, G1, B2 and G2), ochratoxin A, deoxynivalenol, zearalenone, fumonisins (B1 and B2) and T‐2 and HT‐2 toxins concentrations. All the values found were below the respective limits of detection (LOD) of the analytical methods. 24 , 25

The FEEDAP Panel considers that the amounts of the detected impurities do not raise safety concerns.

The additive is a powder with a density of 652 kg/m3. 26 The dusting potential of three batches of the additive was determined using the Stauber‐Heubach method and showed a range of 1039–11,395 mg/m3 (mg airborne dust per m3 of air). The same batches were analysed for particle size distribution by laser‐diffraction method; the results showed that up to 6% of the particles have diameters below 10 μm, up to 21% below 50 μm and up to 37% below 100 μm. 27

Stability

Shelf‐life was assessed in three batches of the additive stored in the original packaging (sealed aluminium‐polyethylene bags) at −18 and 4°C for up to 24 months and at 20°C for up to 5 months. 28 No losses (< 0.5 log of the initial value) were observed during the full duration of the studies at −18 and 4°C and during the first month at 20°C. However, after 5 months at 20°C losses were detected in two of the three batches tested (0.9 and 1 log of the initial value, respectively).

The stability of the additive in water was studied in three batches of the additive stored at 20°C for 48 h. 29 Negligible losses (< 0.5 log of the initial value) were observed at those conditions.

Conditions of use

The additive is intended for use with all fresh plant materials and for all animal species at a proposed minimum concentration of 1 × 108 CFU/kg fresh material. It is to be applied as an aqueous suspension. 30

Safety

Safety for the target species, consumers and environment

The species L. buchneri is considered by EFSA to be suitable for the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2023). This approach requires the identity of the strain to be conclusively established and evidence that the strain does not harbour acquired AMR genes. In the view of the FEEDAP Panel, the identity of the strain has been established as L. buchneri and the antibiotic resistance qualification met. Consequently, L. buchneri DSM 32651 is considered safe for the target species, consumers and the environment. All the proposed cryoprotectants and carriers are feed materials or authorised feed additives.

The Panel concludes that the use of preparations of the active agent formulated with the cryoprotectants and carriers listed by the applicant are safe for the target animals, consumers and environment.

Safety for the user

No specific studies investigating the effects of the additive on the respiratory system were submitted. The highest dusting potential reported (11,395 mg/m3) suggests that exposure by inhalation is possible.

The eye irritation potential of the additive was investigated in vitro (one batch) according to OECD TG 492. 31 Based on the results of the study the additive is considered non‐irritant to eyes (UN‐GHS ‘No Category’).

The additive is a preparation containing a microorganism, and therefore should be considered as a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser. Any exposure through skin and respiratory tract is considered a risk.

Conclusions on safety

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that L. buchneri DSM 32651 and the formulated additive are safe for the target species, consumers and the environment. Regarding user safety, the additive is not irritant to eyes but should be considered as a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser; any exposure through skin and respiratory tract is considered a risk.

Efficacy

The additive is intended to improve the aerobic stability of silage when added to all types of fresh plant materials. To support the claimed effect, six laboratory trials were made with fresh plant materials representing materials easy to ensile (studies 1 32 , 2 33 and 3 34 ), moderately difficult to ensile (studies 4 35 and 5 36 ) and difficult to ensile (study 6 37 ) as specified by Regulation (EC) No 429/2008. The characteristic of the plant materials tested is shown in Table 1. All the studies included a non‐inoculated control, and a group inoculated with L. buchneri DSM 32651. The additive was dissolved in water and sprayed on the fresh material at intended inclusion levels ranging from 5.1 × 107 to 1.0 × 108 CFU/kg fresh material (confirmed by analysis of the water suspensions in studies 1, 2, 3 and 5). The fresh material for the control silos was sprayed with an equal volume of water without the additive. Samples of the plant materials used were ensiled in 2.7 L (studies 1, 2, 3, 5) or 1.75 L (studies 4 and 6) minisilos (five replicates per treatment) at 20°C for 90–103 days.

The inclusion of L. buchneri DSM 32651 improved the aerobic stability of grass‐legume mixture (trial 1) and whole crop maize (trials 3, 4 and 5) in comparison with the control. An increased acetic acid concentration was observed in all supplemented silages at the end of the fermentation. No major modifications were seen in any of the other parameters analysed.

Based on the composition of the fresh plant materials used in the trials, the Panel concludes that the additive has the potential to improve the aerobic stability of silage from fresh plant materials with a dry matter range between 28% and 45%.

CONCLUSIONS

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that L. buchneri DSM 32651 and the formulated additive are safe for the target species, consumers and the environment.

Regarding the safety for the user, the additive is not an eye irritant, but it should be considered as a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser, and any exposure through skin and respiratory tract is considered a risk.

The addition of L. buchneri DSM 32651 at a minimum level of 1 × 108 CFU/kg fresh plant material has the potential to improve the aerobic stability of silage from fresh plant materials with a DM content ranging from 28% to 45%.

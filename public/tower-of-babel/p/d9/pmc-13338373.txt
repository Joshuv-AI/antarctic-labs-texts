Vision transformer framework for host based cryptojacking malware detection
Authors: Walid El-Shafai, Ahmad Taher Azar, Samah Alshathri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rising adoption of cryptocurrencies has been paralleled by the emergence of cryptojacking malware, malicious software that covertly hijacks computing resources for unauthorized cryptocurrency mining. This threat not only degrades system performance and incurs financial losses but also increasingly evades traditional intrusion detection systems (IDSs), which often suffer from limited accuracy, high false positive rates, and poor adaptability in real-time or resource-constrained environments. To overcome these limitations, this study proposes CryptoIDS-ViT, an advanced IDS framework that employs vision transformer architectures, namely ViT, MaxViT, and SwinViT, for robust host-based cryptojacking detection. The significance of this work lies in both the growing need for accurate cryptojacking mitigation and the introduction of a novel end-to-end detection framework that adapts transformer-based models to a security-critical domain. The novelty of the approach stems from its image-based malware representation pipeline, where executable binaries are systematically transformed into both color and grayscale images, and from the custom adaptation and comparative benchmarking of multiple pretrained transformer models for detecting cryptojacking attacks, an underexplored and emerging cybersecurity challenge. A two-stage process involving deep feature extraction and classification enables accurate detection with high generalizability. Extensive evaluation on a balanced dataset of 40,000 samples (20,000 cryptojacking, 20,000 benign) demonstrates superior performance: the SwinViT model achieves 99.35% accuracy on color images and 99.08% on grayscale, with a precision of 99.41%, recall of 99.27%, and F1-score of 99.34%. Compared to state-of-the-art CNN-based IDSs, CryptoIDS-ViT delivers a 3–4% improvement in detection metrics while preserving real-time inference efficiency. Additional contributions include: (i) a custom binary-to-image encoding strategy tailored to malware feature extraction, (ii) ViT attention heatmap visualizations that enhance interpretability and model transparency, (iii) an ablation study evaluating input modality effects (color vs. grayscale), and (iv) a runtime and memory complexity analysis supporting deployment feasibility in edge or enterprise systems. These results validate the proposed framework’s capability to generalize across input types and deployment contexts, establishing ViT-based IDSs as a scalable, interpretable, and practical solution for detecting sophisticated cryptojacking malware in modern cybersecurity infrastructures. Unlike existing studies that apply ViTs to general malware classification, this work delivers a focused, thoroughly evaluated, and high-impact contribution to the detection of host-based cryptojacking threats.Keywords: Cryptojacking detection, Host-based malware, Vision transformers, Deep learning for cybersecurity, Malware image classification, Real-time intrusion detectionSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

The increasing sophistication of cyber-attacks continues to undermine the stability of digital infrastructure, with cryptojacking malware emerging as a covert and damaging threat. It involves the unauthorized exploitation of a user’s computational resources to mine cryptocurrencies, often without detection or user consent. This form of attack degrades system performance, consumes significant energy, and leads to financial losses, especially in enterprise and cloud environments. Its stealthy nature, often concealed in the background, makes it difficult to detect using traditional security tools1–3.

Cryptojacking represents a rapidly escalating threat in the modern cybersecurity landscape, affecting both individuals and organizations. By covertly hijacking a device’s processing power for unauthorized cryptocurrency mining, cryptojacking malware operates silently and without user awareness4. Its growing appeal to cybercriminals is driven by the increasing value of digital currencies and the low technical barriers required to launch attacks. Recent studies attribute the proliferation of cryptojacking to its profitability, stealth, and operational simplicity5. This malware spreads through various channels, including infected software, phishing campaigns, and malicious advertisements embedded in legitimate websites. Its impact ranges from degraded system performance and elevated energy consumption to, in severe cases, total system failure. Due to its stealthy behavior and rising prevalence, the development of proactive detection strategies is essential to safeguard digital environments6.

Cryptojacking malware manifests in various forms, distinguished primarily by their delivery mechanisms and operational complexity7. These include lightweight browser-based scripts, file-based executables, and more sophisticated variants that infiltrate cloud infrastructures and Internet-of-Things (IoT) networks. A prevalent technique is browser-based cryptojacking, where malicious scripts are embedded in websites or online advertisements and exploit users’ computational resources during browser sessions without consent8. File-based cryptojacking represents another major class, involving malware downloaded to the host device that persistently mines cryptocurrency in the background9. Advanced strains have begun targeting cloud platforms and IoT devices, significantly broadening the attack surface and magnifying the potential impact6. A comprehensive understanding of these diverse cryptojacking modalities is vital for developing effective countermeasures. By recognizing the operational behaviors across platforms, security researchers and practitioners can design more adaptive and resilient intrusion detection systems10.

In light of the increasing sophistication of cryptojacking attacks, the deployment of robust Intrusion Detection Systems (IDSs) has become essential to modern cybersecurity defenses. IDSs play a pivotal role in monitoring system or network activity, identifying anomalies indicative of cryptojacking malware11. In addition to detecting real-time intrusions, IDSs help reveal underlying system vulnerabilities, enabling organizations to take targeted actions to strengthen security9,10. Given the stealthy and evolving nature of cryptojacking threats, a proactive, multi-layered security approach centered around IDS technology is crucial. By integrating advanced IDS solutions, both enterprises and individuals can effectively detect, respond to, and mitigate the unauthorized use of computational resources, thus preserving system performance and safeguarding digital assets2.

As cryptojacking malware continues to increase in sophistication, traditional detection techniques are proving increasingly inadequate. This evolution necessitates the adoption of advanced methodologies, particularly those grounded in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Deep Learning (DL), to effectively identify and counter such threats12. AI and DL-based systems offer significant advantages in processing large-scale data, uncovering latent patterns, and detecting subtle anomalies indicative of cryptojacking activity. Unlike static, rule-based systems, these models possess adaptive learning capabilities that allow them to evolve in response to emerging malware variants, thereby improving detection accuracy and minimizing false positives over time13. This adaptability is especially critical in identifying cryptojacking attacks that utilize advanced evasion and obfuscation techniques.

Moreover, AI-driven solutions support automated response mechanisms, enabling rapid mitigation of cryptojacking intrusions in real time14. This automation not only shortens reaction times but also reduces the operational impact of ongoing attacks. Given the increasing prevalence and complexity of cryptojacking, the integration of AI and DL technologies has transitioned from being advantageous to indispensable for maintaining system resilience and protecting digital assets.

The integration of AI and DL into cryptojacking detection strategies offers substantial and multifaceted advantages4. These systems are capable of efficiently processing vast volumes of data, enabling faster and more precise identification of threats compared to traditional methods. Their ability to continuously learn from historical attack patterns enhances adaptability, significantly reducing false positives and improving long-term detection reliability. Furthermore, AI-powered automation facilitates real-time responses, mitigating damage at the earliest stages of an intrusion. This combination of scalability, efficiency, and adaptability renders AI and DL solutions suitable for a wide range of environments, from individual devices to complex enterprise-level infrastructures. As such, they form a critical component of contemporary cybersecurity strategies, offering both technological innovation and strategic defense against evolving threats.

Historically, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been at the core of many deep learning-based IDSs for malware classification. While CNNs demonstrate strong performance in various tasks, they often face limitations in modeling the intricate spatial relationships and subtle behavioral signatures characteristic of host-based cryptojacking attacks. These patterns, manifesting through anomalies in system calls, CPU usage, or network activity, can elude CNNs due to their limited receptive field and inductive biases15,16. In contrast, the emergence of Vision Transformer (ViT) models has introduced a paradigm shift in deep learning for image-based analysis. ViTs have demonstrated superior capability in capturing long-range dependencies and complex spatial structures, offering a compelling alternative for tasks requiring fine-grained feature representation17–19. These theoretical strengths make ViTs particularly effective in detecting non-localized, obfuscated patterns within malware images, surpassing CNNs in both feature expressiveness and interpretability.

ViT models analyze entire image representations in a single pass, employing self-attention mechanisms to dynamically focus on the most salient regions of an image. This capability enables ViTs to effectively capture both local and global spatial dependencies without relying on the inductive biases of CNNs. As a result, ViTs are particularly well-suited for modeling malware behaviors embedded within image-transformed binary data, delivering enhanced generalizability, interpretability, and precision in pattern recognition tasks19–21.

Integrating ViT architectures into IDS frameworks offers several distinct advantages. First, ViTs are adept at learning intricate spatial dependencies from complex visual representations, enabling the identification of subtle patterns associated with cryptojacking behaviors. Their attention-based design allows the model to selectively prioritize informative image regions, improving the accuracy of threat classification. ViTs also exhibit strong generalization to previously unseen malware variants, an essential feature for combating evolving threats. Additionally, their attention maps enhance interpretability, offering greater transparency in the decision-making process. Compared to CNNs, which rely on localized receptive fields and hierarchical patch-based learning, ViTs reduce computational redundancy by processing the global image context in parallel. This approach improves efficiency, particularly when dealing with large-scale datasets.

Consequently, the incorporation of vision transformers into IDS design marks a significant advancement in the detection of sophisticated cyber threats such as cryptojacking. ViT-based IDS solutions combine accuracy, adaptability, and interpretability to deliver scalable, intelligent defenses that extend beyond the limitations of conventional deep learning models. This technological evolution contributes substantially to strengthening digital infrastructure against stealthy and evasive malware attacks.

This study addresses a pressing challenge in modern cybersecurity: the development of an IDS specifically tailored to detect host-based cryptojacking malware with both high accuracy and computational efficiency. Existing IDS solutions offer only partial effectiveness when faced with sophisticated and evasive malware variants that operate at the system level. These host-based attacks often bypass conventional detection methods and surreptitiously exploit computational resources for unauthorized cryptocurrency mining.

The urgency of this research stems from the increasing prevalence, complexity, and impact of cryptojacking, which now poses significant risks across personal, enterprise, and cloud environments. In addition to degrading system performance and elevating energy consumption, cryptojacking introduces long-term threats to digital infrastructure integrity. While traditional IDS models have demonstrated some ability to detect known attack patterns, they struggle to identify obfuscated or zero-day variants that employ dynamic, evasive behaviors.

To address these limitations, this study proposes CryptoIDS-ViT, a novel IDS framework that harnesses ViT architectures for host-based cryptojacking detection. By converting binary executable files into image representations and leveraging ViT models (ViT, MaxViT, and SwinViT), the framework facilitates deep visual feature learning capable of capturing complex malware behaviors. This approach aims to significantly improve detection performance, model generalization, and real-time applicability. The proposed work thus bridges a critical gap between the limitations of conventional detection models and the need for scalable, intelligent defense mechanisms in today’s rapidly evolving threat landscape.

Despite growing interest in malware detection, most existing IDSs remain either optimized for network-based threats or limited in their capacity to handle the obfuscated and dynamic nature of host-based cryptojacking attacks. Although CNN-based methods have shown promise in image classification tasks, they frequently fail to capture long-range dependencies and hierarchical spatial features, key elements in accurately modeling malware behaviors. Furthermore, while ViT models have demonstrated superior performance in computer vision applications, their application in host-based intrusion detection, particularly for cryptojacking, is still underexplored.

Related work

In recent years, the proliferation of cryptojacking malware has driven extensive research into the development of advanced IDSs aimed at mitigating this growing threat. This section critically reviews the existing body of work on cryptojacking detection, emphasizing the evolution of methodologies and their respective strengths and limitations.

The landscape of malware detection is shaped by a variety of techniques, each addressing different aspects of the threat. Signature-based approaches rely on predefined patterns to detect known malware and are efficient but ineffective against novel or polymorphic variants. In contrast, behavior-based methods identify malware through deviations in system activity, offering more flexibility but often resulting in higher false positive rates. More recent innovations include machine learning-based approaches, which apply statistical models to identify patterns in system logs or activity data, improving adaptability to new threats. Deep learning techniques further advance this by employing neural networks to extract complex, non-linear features from high-dimensional data, thereby enhancing detection accuracy. Emerging most recently, ViT-based approaches introduce a powerful alternative by leveraging self-attention mechanisms for image-based analysis, offering promising results in modeling malware behaviors through visual patterns derived from binary data.

The evolution of IDSs for cryptojacking has followed multiple trajectories, including signature-based, behavior-based, ML, and DL paradigms. For example, the study in2 introduced a novel hybrid real-time detection framework that combines blacklisting with statistical code analysis to detect in-browser cryptojacking malware, specifically targeting non-WebAssembly (WASM) variants. The authors reported a significant improvement in detection accuracy (from 97 to 99.6%) and a 99.8% reduction in prevention latency. Their main contribution lies in addressing a critical gap in web-focused cryptojacking detection, where conventional solutions fail to effectively identify non-WASM-based attacks. However, this work is primarily confined to browser-level detection and does not extend to host-based cryptojacking, which is increasingly prevalent and difficult to detect using web-specific methods. In contrast, our proposed CryptoIDS-ViT framework addresses this gap by focusing on host-level cryptojacking detection through a transformer-based image classification pipeline, offering enhanced adaptability and detection performance in system-level environments.

Other studies have demonstrated the potential of ML techniques in detecting cryptojacking behavior using host-level and network-level indicators3,13,14. In3, the authors conducted a comparative analysis of multiple ML classifiers, such as Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, k-Nearest Neighbor, and XGBoost, on a dataset incorporating both host-based and network-based features. Their findings revealed that simpler models like Logistic Regression and Random Forest performed comparably to more complex ones, emphasizing the critical role of feature engineering over algorithmic complexity. In13, the researchers proposed Crypto-Aegis, a network-based detection framework that relied on traffic flow analysis to identify cryptocurrency mining activities. Achieving an F1-score of 96% and an AUC of 99%, the model showcased robust detection performance across heterogeneous environments, offering deployment flexibility in networked infrastructures. However, these studies primarily focus on feature-based classification using structured telemetry data, such as system logs and network packets, which may require extensive domain-specific feature design and can be bypassed by obfuscation techniques. In contrast, our CryptoIDS-ViT framework eliminates reliance on handcrafted features by transforming raw binary executables into visual representations, enabling end-to-end deep learning using transformer-based models and enhancing detection generalization against evolving host-based cryptojacking threats.

In14, the authors addressed the practical challenges of detecting cryptocurrency mining malware (CryptocMal) by proposing CMalHunt, an ensemble learning framework that integrates heterogeneous feature sets, including domain-specific knowledge, behavioral attributes, and binary byte-level representations. This multi-perspective approach enabled CMalHunt to outperform conventional single-feature models, showcasing its robustness and adaptability in real-world deployment scenarios. The study’s novelty lies in its strategic fusion of diverse feature spaces, which significantly improved classification accuracy and resilience to evasion techniques. However, CMalHunt still depends on manual feature extraction and engineered inputs, which may not fully capture spatial or structural artifacts intrinsic to binary malware. In contrast, our CryptoIDS-ViT framework offers a fully automated, image-based approach, transforming executables into visual formats that are directly processed by vision transformer models, thus eliminating the need for manual feature crafting and providing deeper insights into complex malware behaviors through learned attention mechanisms.

Several recent studies have further expanded the body of knowledge surrounding cryptojacking behavior and its detection mechanisms21–24. In21, the authors presented a systematic threat analysis of cryptojacking within the broader blockchain and cryptocurrency ecosystem. Their study underscored the evolutionary nature of cryptojacking attacks and identified critical limitations in contemporary detection frameworks, thereby establishing the need for more adaptive and intelligent solutions. Study22 contributed a comprehensive survey of detection strategies, classifying them into behavior-based, host-level, and network flow-based categories. In addition, it proposed a hybrid decision tree–based framework for malware classification, showcasing how interpretable models can be used for real-time mitigation. Meanwhile23, introduced the novel concept of cryptojacking injection, where adversaries embed mining scripts into web systems to hijack resources, especially for Monero mining. Their work developed a finite-state attack model and identified indicators of compromise (IOCs) such as abnormal CPU usage and unauthorized communication with mining pools, contributing directly to the refinement of IDS detection logic. Although these works provide foundational insights into attack behavior and defensive strategies, they predominantly rely on symbolic modeling, handcrafted rules, or shallow ML algorithms. By contrast, our CryptoIDS-ViT framework leverages Vision Transformer architectures trained on image-transformed binaries to automatically learn spatial and contextual features from malware behavior, achieving high detection precision with minimal reliance on manual feature engineering.

Complementing these efforts24, proposed a host-based intrusion detection system specifically designed for web servers, focusing on the identification of malicious activity through monitoring of network ports and tracing the origin of web-based threats. This approach provided significant improvements in detection precision and responsiveness, enabling system administrators to implement early countermeasures and thereby enhancing overall web service security.

Additionally, several recent studies have introduced innovative strategies to improve the accuracy, efficiency, and operational feasibility of cryptojacking detection25–28. In25, the researchers leveraged hardware performance counters (HPCs) on Windows platforms to monitor low-level resource usage. Their method achieved 96% accuracy in detecting CPU-based cryptojacking and further reached 99.86% accuracy for GPU-based attacks by integrating GPU-specific metrics. Notably, the approach incurred minimal system overhead, making it highly suitable for real-time deployment in production environments. In26, a behavioral detection framework was presented based on domain name (DN) analysis for identifying in-browser cryptojacking. The novelty of this work lies in its use of temporal, structural, and lexical features from DNS activity, which were processed through Decision Tree classifiers (yielding a recall of 59.5%) and K-Means clustering for unsupervised identification of cryptojacked domains. This technique demonstrated the potential of non-payload-based indicators for detecting covert browser-based mining operations.

Broader perspectives on cryptocurrency-related threats have also emerged. In27, the authors applied machine learning techniques to detect anomalies in cryptocurrency transactions, targeting activities such as financial fraud and illicit fund transfers. This study expanded the cybersecurity focus beyond endpoint mining, highlighting the multi-dimensional risks associated with cryptojacking and crypto-enabled crime. Meanwhile28, introduced a lightweight, real-time detection framework based on network traffic analysis. A key contribution of this work was its non-intrusive design, requiring no client-side modifications or elevated administrative privileges. It achieved F1-scores of 94.04% for known mining services and 96.37% for unknown services, demonstrating strong generalization capability and deployment feasibility at network gateways.

Further advances in cryptojacking defense have explored complementary detection strategies29–35. In29, the authors proposed MinerGuard, a machine learning–based system designed to identify CPU-intensive mining behavior. It outperformed traditional blacklist-based approaches such as MinerBlock, achieving a 96% detection rate, and proved effective even against previously unseen (zero-day) scripts, showcasing its resilience and adaptability. Study30 performed a comparative analysis of browser-based and standalone cryptojacking techniques, revealing that over 60% of existing browser-based script blockers were either ineffective or easily bypassed. This work underscores the limitations of front-end protection and the need for more robust detection frameworks capable of deeper behavioral analysis.

Fingerprinting-based cryptojacking detection was explored in31, where the authors proposed a model that combines autoencoder-based representation learning with a dense neural network classifier. This hybrid approach significantly enhanced the detection of in-browser cryptomining scripts, introducing a novel unsupervised pre-training stage that improves robustness against obfuscated threats. In32, a deep learning framework for edge computing environments was presented, utilizing multi-modal features including image-based and digital behavioral traits. The proposed system achieved a 97.01% detection rate and a false alarm rate of just 0.63%, highlighting its suitability for resource-constrained real-time deployments at the network edge.

Further innovation came with the development of CJSpector in33, which uses Intel processor trace to collect runtime control flow data and applies recurrent neural networks for detection. This approach introduced a hardware-assisted, transparent runtime analysis methodology, yielding high detection accuracy while maintaining low system overhead, particularly beneficial for identifying covert in-browser cryptomining activities.

Additional research has focused on network traffic-based cryptojacking detection through advanced packet flow analysis and behavioral modeling34,35. In34, the authors proposed a flow-level detection method using convolutional operations applied to early packet size sequences, allowing for fast and accurate differentiation between mining and benign traffic. The approach was validated on both custom and real-time datasets, demonstrating strong detection precision. Similarly, MineHunter, introduced in35, incorporated cryptocurrency block generation correlation with network traffic patterns, enabling accurate cryptojacking detection without payload inspection. The system achieved 97.0% precision and 99.7% recall in real-world scenarios, confirming its practical applicability at the network perimeter.

Machine learning and network-level analysis continue to provide lightweight yet effective solutions for cryptojacking detection, particularly in scalable or real-time scenarios36–39. In36, the authors proposed a flow-based detection method tailored for internet service providers, where only early packet characteristics were used as features, coupled with a Random Forest classifier. This study contributed a low-latency, ISP-level model that maintained high detection accuracy while minimizing computational cost. Study37 introduced a novel CPU usage-based detection mechanism that monitored active browser tabs in real time. By integrating CPU activity metrics into a lightweight ML framework, the model achieved high precision and recall, highlighting its non-intrusive nature and suitability for desktop and endpoint protection without degrading user performance.

The CryingJackpot framework presented in38 advanced cryptojacking detection by eliminating reliance on labeled training data. This hybrid unsupervised approach combined system performance counters with network telemetry, reaching a 97% F1-score, and demonstrating strong anomaly detection capabilities in dynamic environments. Its novelty lies in the label-free architecture, which is crucial for early-stage threat detection in rapidly evolving attack landscapes. Finally39, introduced an adaptive filtering system targeting cryptojacking and DDoS threats in IoT-rich cloud environments. Deployed on the Amazon Cloud platform, the model achieved 99.69% accuracy in botnet detection and a low 1.5% misclassification rate for cryptojacking. Its key innovation lies in its dual-layer filtering logic, which balances threat mitigation with cloud resource efficiency, making it highly relevant for modern multi-tenant cloud infrastructures.

Building upon the existing advancements, this study introduces CryptoIDS-ViT, a transformer-driven intrusion detection framework specifically designed to detect host-based cryptojacking malware. Unlike traditional approaches, this system converts binary executables into both color and grayscale image representations, allowing vision transformer models, including ViT, MaxViT, and SwinViT, to extract rich, high-dimensional features. The image-based representation enables the models to capture fine-grained spatial patterns and global contextual dependencies, which are critical in identifying the nuanced behaviors of cryptojacking malware. This represents a novel and underexplored application of ViT architectures in the malware detection domain, especially for host-level threats.

Despite the recent progress in malware detection, significant limitations remain, particularly in addressing the stealth and complexity of host-based cryptojacking attacks. Existing IDS frameworks are predominantly network-centric and lack the sensitivity to detect subtle system-level anomalies. While CNN-based models have been widely used for visual malware analysis, they struggle to capture long-range dependencies and often exhibit high false positive rates, especially when encountering obfuscated or zero-day variants. Furthermore, the application of vision transformers to cryptojacking detection is still nascent, and prior studies rarely address the transformation of binary executables into structured image inputs, missing opportunities for improved generalization and interpretability.

The proposed CryptoIDS-ViT framework addresses these gaps through a comparative evaluation of three ViT variants, each fine-tuned on a curated, balanced dataset of 40,000 samples. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across key metrics, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, on both grayscale and RGB inputs. Importantly, the system balances high detection capability with computational efficiency, supporting real-time deployment in enterprise, edge, and cloud environments. Compared with existing CNN-based IDS solutions, CryptoIDS-ViT delivers a 3–4% improvement in detection accuracy, along with enhanced model interpretability through attention map analysis. In summary, this work offers a novel, end-to-end image-based detection pipeline tailored for host-based cryptojacking, establishing a new research direction for ViT applications in cybersecurity. Its comprehensive design, empirical validation, and scalability highlight its potential as a foundation for next-generation intelligent IDS frameworks.

The proposed system is structured to systematically address the research challenges identified in earlier sections, providing a robust and scalable solution for image-based malware detection. Data collection

The initial phase of the proposed CryptoIDS-ViT system involves the compilation of a large-scale dataset representative of real-world cryptojacking attacks. This dataset was curated using a premium account on VirusTotal (https://www.virustotal.com/gui/home/upload, accessed December 2024) and comprises a total of 40,000 executable samples, including 20,000 cryptojacking and 20,000 benign files. The executables span multiple formats such as ELF, APK, EXE, and PE, ensuring a broad and realistic representation of host-based malware.

To facilitate automated retrieval, the VirusTotal Command-Line Interface (vt-cli) tool (https://github.com/VirusTotal/vt-cli, accessed December 2024) was employed with targeted query parameters, ensuring that only samples relevant to cryptojacking were collected. The cryptojacking dataset (CJDS)40 was curated with a focus on ensuring diversity and realism, encompassing miner-related behaviors regardless of file extension, obfuscation level, packing technique, or deployment context (e.g., stand-alone or embedded).

Comprehensive validation and manual inspection were conducted to verify the authenticity of the samples and ensure that they exhibit characteristics consistent with real-world cryptojacking behavior. This rigorous approach to data collection is fundamental to guaranteeing the robustness, generalizability, and effectiveness of the proposed CryptoIDS-ViT system in accurately detecting host-based cryptojacking malware.

Data Preparation and preprocessing

The subsequent phase in the development of the CryptoIDS-ViT system focuses on the comprehensive preprocessing of the collected dataset. This process involves filtering out irrelevant or mislabeled data, eliminating false positives, and preparing the samples for model training and evaluation. A key component of this stage is the transformation of executable files, both cryptojacking and benign, into visual representations in both color and grayscale formats.

To convert malware binaries into image format, each executable file is first read as a sequence of raw bytes. These byte sequences are then reshaped into two-dimensional arrays and normalized to generate structured grayscale or RGB image representations. For RGB conversion, adjacent byte triples are mapped to red, green, and blue channels respectively. For grayscale, each byte value directly maps to a single pixel intensity. This transformation enables the application of computer vision techniques to malware classification without requiring handcrafted features.

Thus, to facilitate effective feature extraction by the Vision Transformer models, each executable file is converted into a one-dimensional (1D) 8-bit binary vector composed of unsigned integer values. This vector is then reshaped into a two-dimensional (2D) image, following the methodology described in41,42. The resulting visual representations are used as input data in both RGB (color) and grayscale formats. This image-based approach offers a distinct advantage: it eliminates the need for manual feature engineering or domain-specific knowledge typically required in traditional malware analysis, enabling the system to automatically learn discriminative features from raw binary content.

To convert the collected executable files into visual representations, we employed the binary-to-image transformation technique introduced by Nataraj et al.42. In this method, each binary file, classified as either cryptojacking or benign, is first read as a continuous stream of 8-bit unsigned integers (uint8), where each byte maps directly to a pixel intensity in a grayscale image. The resulting one-dimensional byte array is reshaped into a two-dimensional matrix, where the image width is fixed (e.g., 256 pixels or determined based on file size heuristics), and the height is calculated as the quotient of the total byte length divided by the chosen width. The resulting grayscale matrix is then resized to a fixed resolution of 224 × 224 pixels to satisfy the input requirements of ViT architectures.

For RGB (color) image generation, the byte stream is divided into three equal-length segments (or padded if necessary), each corresponding to one of the red (R), green (G), and blue (B) channels. Each segment is individually reshaped into a 2D matrix of the same dimensions as the grayscale image. These three matrices are then stacked along the channel axis to produce a 3-channel RGB image of shape [height×width×3]. This technique preserves both spatial and structural characteristics inherent in the binary data, enabling the ViT models to learn discriminative patterns crucial for malware classification. Proposed SwinViT architecture

The SwinViT model represents a significant evolution of the standard Vision Transformer, integrating a hierarchical architecture with a novel shifted window mechanism to efficiently process visual data at multiple levels of abstraction45. Unlike flat transformer designs, SwinViT operates across successive stages, each capturing increasingly complex patterns by processing image patches at different scales. This hierarchical design allows SwinViT to model both fine-grained local details and broader spatial dependencies, essential capabilities when analyzing malware binaries visualized as images.

At the core of SwinViT is its shifted window-based attention strategy. In early stages, self-attention is computed within non-overlapping local windows, reducing computational overhead while preserving local feature learning. In deeper stages, shifted windows allow for cross-window connections, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies and global context. This hybrid strategy balances efficiency with expressive power, making SwinViT particularly suitable for cryptojacking detection, where subtle and dispersed visual cues must be identified with precision. Results and discussions

This section presents a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed CryptoIDS-ViT system, focusing on its effectiveness in detecting and classifying cryptojacking malware. The evaluation plays a pivotal role in demonstrating the system’s practical applicability within cybersecurity frameworks aimed at protecting digital infrastructures. To validate the system’s adaptability and robustness, experiments were conducted using two dataset modalities: color images and grayscale images, both derived from binary representations of cryptojacking and benign executable files.

The evaluation is based on the CJDS dataset40 and involves three ViT-based architectures: ViT, MaxViT, and SwinViT, each integrated into the CryptoIDS-ViT framework. The goal is to assess and compare detection accuracy, classification performance, and overall system efficiency across both image formats. This dual-modality analysis enables us to examine the impact of visual representation (color vs. grayscale) on model performance and informs potential deployment in diverse resource and network environments.

Table 4 presents the key simulation parameters used for training and evaluating the CryptoIDS-ViT system. The dataset includes 20,000 images per class (benign and cryptojacking), structured as a binary classification task. An 80%/20% training-to-testing split was adopted to balance learning depth and validation reliability. Training was performed using a batch size of 16 and a learning rate of 0.0001, optimized through empirical tuning. Discussion of practical implications and limitations

This section situates the performance of the proposed CryptoIDS-ViT system within the context of real-world cybersecurity environments and critically examines its practical strengths and limitations. By articulating both the system’s potential and current constraints, we outline a roadmap for future research and deployment.

The empirical results demonstrate that CryptoIDS-ViT achieves outstanding classification performance, with accuracies exceeding 99% across all evaluated ViT-based models. These results underscore the framework’s strong suitability for deployment in real-time cybersecurity infrastructures, particularly in enterprise and cloud environments that require high-precision, automated threat detection. Notably, SwinViT’s high accuracy on grayscale input further illustrates the system’s adaptability to resource-constrained environments, including low-bandwidth networks, embedded systems, and edge devices.

From a deployment perspective, CryptoIDS-ViT presents several compelling advantages. Its combination of high detection accuracy and low false positive rates supports integration into endpoint protection systems, where precision and minimal alert fatigue are critical. Furthermore, the system’s consistent performance across both grayscale and color modalities broadens its applicability to IoT networks and devices with limited computational capacity. The modular design of transformer architectures also facilitates scalability and adaptation to additional malware detection use cases beyond cryptojacking.

Despite these strengths, several limitations warrant consideration. First, ViT models are computationally intensive during training, which may restrict their feasibility on lightweight or edge hardware without further optimization. Second, while the employed dataset was comprehensive and label-verified, it did not explicitly include adversarial samples, obfuscated malware, or zero-day cryptojacking variants, which leaves model robustness under those conditions unexplored. Third, the experiments focused on a single operating system and binary format, limiting insight into the model’s generalizability across heterogeneous platforms and execution environments.

Moreover, although the CJDS dataset was rigorously curated using heuristic filters and multi-engine AV consensus via VirusTotal, the results remain dataset-dependent. For broader generalization, future evaluations should include alternative datasets from diverse architectures and operating systems to account for domain shift and the emergence of new malware families.

Conclusions and future works

This paper introduced CryptoIDS-ViT, an advanced intrusion detection framework leveraging vision transformer architectures, including ViT, MaxViT, and SwinViT, for the accurate detection of host-based cryptojacking malware. The system transforms binary executable files into grayscale and color image representations, enabling deep visual feature extraction via a customized ViT-based pipeline. By adapting pretrained transformer models to the cybersecurity domain, the framework demonstrates that image-based encoding of executable code provides a powerful medium for learning discriminative malware features. A key contribution of this work is the development of a domain-specific feature extraction strategy that captures both local and global spatial dependencies within the visualized binary data. This allows the ViT-based models to learn complex patterns associated with cryptojacking behavior, eliminating the need for handcrafted features. Experimental evaluations on a balanced dataset of 40,000 samples (20,000 benign and 20,000 cryptojacking) confirm the system’s efficacy. All three ViT variants surpassed 99% in classification accuracy, with corresponding precision, recall, and F1-scores above 0.99. Among these, SwinViT achieved the optimal balance between detection performance and computational efficiency, making it well-suited for real-time and resource-limited environments. Beyond its high detection accuracy, CryptoIDS-ViT exhibits robustness, scalability, and adaptability across input types (RGB and grayscale), validating its applicability across enterprise networks, cloud systems, and edge devices. This research presents a novel application of ViT architectures in intrusion detection and contributes to advancing deep feature learning methodologies for malware classification, particularly in the underexplored area of host-based cryptojacking.

Progressive pulmonary fibrosis criteria predict mortality in treated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Yu-Cheng Wu, Chen-Yu Wang, Yi-Luen Shen, Chiann-Yi Hsu, Yi-Hsuan Yu, Pin-Kuei Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe 2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT criteria for progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) were developed for fibrotic interstitial lung diseases other than idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). Whether this multidimensional framework has prognostic value in patients with treated IPF remains uncertain. We evaluated the prevalence of PPF-defined progression and its association with mortality in patients with IPF receiving antifibrotic therapy.MethodsThis prospective cohort study included patients with multidisciplinary discussion-confirmed IPF enrolled in the Registry of Interstitial Lung Disease (REGILD) between 2018 and 2022. Disease progression was assessed one year after initiation of antifibrotic therapy using the 2022 PPF criteria. The primary outcome was 3-year all-cause mortality. Cox regression and nested Cox model analyses were performed to identify prognostic factors and evaluate incremental prognostic value beyond the GAP score.ResultsNinety patients were included, of whom 50 (55.6%) fulfilled PPF criteria after one year of antifibrotic therapy. During a median follow-up of 3.6 years, 28 patients (31.1%) died. Three-year mortality was significantly higher in patients with PPF-defined progression than in those with stable disease (46.0% vs. 12.5%, p = 0.001). In multivariable analysis, lower baseline body mass index (BMI) (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.78–0.96, p = 0.006) and PPF-defined progression (HR 3.39, 95% CI 1.23–9.34, p = 0.018) were independently associated with mortality. Addition of PPF-defined progression to a model containing the GAP score significantly improved model fit (Δχ² = 5.610, p = 0.018).ConclusionsPPF-defined progression and lower baseline BMI were independently associated with mortality in patients with IPF receiving antifibrotic therapy. PPF-defined progression provided incremental prognostic information beyond the GAP score, suggesting that assessment of longitudinal disease trajectory may complement conventional baseline risk stratification in treated IPF.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-026-04501-x.

Summary at a glance

Among patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis receiving antifibrotic therapy, disease progression within the first year defined by the 2022 PPF criteria was associated with increased 3-year mortality. Lower baseline BMI was also independently associated with mortality, highlighting the potential value of PPF criteria for early risk stratification and the importance of nutritional assessment in IPF management.

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease characterized by irreversible scarring of the lung parenchyma, progressive loss of lung function, and ultimately respiratory failure [1]. It is the most common form of idiopathic interstitial pneumonia and accounts for more than 30% of interstitial lung disease cases [2]. Despite advances in diagnosis and management, IPF remains a life-limiting disease, with a median survival of approximately 2.5–3.5 years following diagnosis [3–6].

Antifibrotic therapies, including pirfenidone and nintedanib, have become the standard of care for IPF and have been shown to significantly slow disease progression. Randomized clinical trials demonstrated reductions in the annual decline of forced vital capacity (FVC), as well as decreased risks of acute exacerbations and mortality [7–11]. However, disease progression frequently persists despite treatment. Real-world studies continue to report substantial mortality, with 3-year survival rates ranging from 59% to 73% and 5-year survival remaining below 60% [12–16].

Accurate risk stratification remains essential for the management of IPF. Current prognostic tools, such as the GAP (Gender, Age, Physiology) index, primarily rely on baseline demographic and physiologic variables, including age, sex, FVC, and diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO) [17–19]. Although these models provide valuable baseline prognostic information, they may not fully capture disease behavior during antifibrotic treatment. Consequently, there remains an unmet need for clinically meaningful markers that reflect ongoing disease progression and treatment response.

In 2022, the American Thoracic Society (ATS), European Respiratory Society (ERS), Japanese Respiratory Society (JRS), and Latin American Thoracic Association (ALAT) introduced updated clinical practice guidelines defining progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) for fibrotic interstitial lung diseases other than IPF [20]. According to these criteria, progression within 12 months is defined by at least two of the following domains: worsening respiratory symptoms, physiologic decline, and radiologic progression on high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT).

It should be noted that the application of the 2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT PPF criteria to patients with established IPF represents an exploratory use of this framework, as these criteria were originally developed and validated for fibrotic ILDs other than IPF. Nevertheless, the PPF framework integrates symptomatic, physiologic, and radiologic domains that reflect fundamental manifestations of ongoing fibrotic disease activity and are also highly relevant to disease progression in IPF. Furthermore, no widely accepted multidimensional tool currently exists to assess post-treatment disease behavior in patients receiving antifibrotic therapy. Therefore, the PPF construct may provide a clinically meaningful framework for evaluating disease trajectory beyond conventional baseline prognostic indices.

We therefore hypothesized that fulfillment of the PPF criteria after initiation of antifibrotic therapy would identify patients with IPF at increased risk of mortality. The aim of this study was to determine the prevalence of PPF-defined progression after one year of antifibrotic treatment and to evaluate its association with 3-year survival in a prospective cohort of patients with IPF.

Materials and methods

Study design, patient enrollment, and ethics

This study was conducted as a subgroup analysis of the prospective Registry of Interstitial Lung Disease (REGILD), an ongoing single-center ILD registry at a tertiary referral center in central Taiwan that participates in a nationwide multicenter ILD registry (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06476470). Since December 28, 2018, REGILD has prospectively enrolled newly diagnosed patients with ILD [18, 19, 21–24].

All diagnoses were established through multidisciplinary discussion (MDD) involving pulmonologists, rheumatologists, radiologists, and pathologists. Importantly, no patients underwent surgical lung biopsy. HRCT patterns were classified as definite, probable, or indeterminate UIP according to contemporary diagnostic guidelines [20].

Eligible participants were adults (≥ 18 years) with MDD-confirmed idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) who received antifibrotic therapy reimbursed by Taiwan’s National Health Insurance (NHI). Patients were enrolled between December 28, 2018 and March 31, 2022, and followed for mortality outcomes through December 31, 2024.

Patients were excluded if they had incomplete pulmonary function data, inadequate HRCT image quality, loss to follow-up, or death within six months after initiation of antifibrotic therapy.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Taichung Veterans General Hospital (IRB No. CE18325B) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

ILD assessment protocol

Baseline demographic and clinical data were collected, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, pulmonary function, and comorbidities. All baseline data, including BMI, were obtained at the time of IPF diagnosis prior to initiation of antifibrotic therapy.

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) were assessed using validated instruments, including the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scale [25], COPD Assessment Test (CAT) [26], 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) [27], and St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) [28], as previously described [18, 19, 21–24]. All questionnaires were administered without modification.

Objective assessments included pulmonary function testing [29, 30], cardiopulmonary exercise testing [21], echocardiography [22], six-minute walk testing [23], and HRCT evaluation [20]. Comorbidity burden was assessed using the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) [31].

Antifibrotic therapy under Taiwan National Health Insurance

Taiwan’s National Health Insurance (NHI) is a single-payer healthcare system covering more than 99% of the population. Since March 2017, antifibrotic agents (nintedanib and pirfenidone) have been reimbursed for patients with IPF who meet specific eligibility criteria [32]. Patients were eligible if forced vital capacity (FVC) was between 50% and 80%, or > 80% with respiratory symptoms such as dyspnea or cough. Applications required HRCT imaging, autoimmune serologic testing, and pulmonary function results for review by NHI-appointed consultants.

Initial reimbursement was granted for six months and re-evaluated using clinical status and pulmonary function tests. If relative FVC decline ≥ 10% occurred, continuation or switching of antifibrotic therapy could be approved for an additional three months. Persistent decline resulted in discontinuation of reimbursement. Concurrent use of both antifibrotic agents was not permitted.

Definition of Progressive Pulmonary Fibrosis (PPF)

Progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) was defined according to the 2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT clinical practice guideline [20]. PPF was considered present when at least two of the following three criteria occurred within a 12-month period without alternative explanation:

Symptom progression was assessed using changes in CAT and mMRC scores. HRCT images were independently reviewed by three pulmonologists or radiologists. Radiologic progression was confirmed when at least two reviewers reached consensus. Final classification was determined during multidisciplinary discussion and integrated with clinical and physiologic findings.

Statistical analysis

The primary outcome was 3-year all-cause mortality. Continuous variables are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages and were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Associations between clinical variables and mortality were evaluated using univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated and compared using the log-rank test.

Given the limited number of outcome events relative to the number of candidate predictors (28 deaths among 90 patients), the multivariable Cox model was restricted to a small number of covariates to minimize overfitting and maintain model stability. The GAP score was not entered into the multivariable model together with PPF progression status and BMI because the GAP score is a composite index incorporating age, sex, FVC, and DLCO [17], several of which conceptually overlap with the physiologic progression component of the PPF criteria, raising concerns regarding collinearity. Furthermore, the primary objective of this study was to evaluate the prognostic significance of PPF-defined progression in treated IPF.

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 21.0 and MedCalc version 22.023. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. This study was reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

A total of 119 patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) receiving antifibrotic therapy were initially enrolled in the REGILD cohort. After exclusion of 29 patients because of incomplete data or loss to follow-up, 90 patients were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). During a median follow-up of 3.6 years, 28 patients (31.1%) died.

Symptom progression was more frequent in the progressive group than in the stable group (58.0% vs. 7.5%, p < 0.001), accompanied by a greater increase in CAT score (ΔCAT: 9.0 vs. 2.5, p = 0.025). Changes in mMRC score were not significantly different between groups.

Physiologic progression occurred in 90.0% of patients in the progressive group compared with 20.0% in the stable group (p < 0.001). Progressive patients experienced greater declines in both FVC (− 7.0% vs. +6.0%, p < 0.001) and DLCO (− 7.0% vs. +3.0%, p = 0.004).

Radiologic progression was also substantially more common in the progressive group (86.7% vs. 29.4%, p < 0.001). Specific HRCT findings associated with progression included worsening traction bronchiectasis or bronchiolectasis, new ground-glass opacity with traction bronchiectasis, increased reticulation, and increased honeycombing (all p ≤ 0.001).

Compared with survivors, non-survivors had significantly lower BMI (21.8 vs. 25.1 kg/m², p < 0.001) and were more likely to have a history of smoking (78.6% vs. 54.8%, p = 0.032). Importantly, PPF-defined progression within the first year was significantly more common among non-survivors than survivors (82.1% vs. 43.5%, p = 0.001).

Among individual PPF domains (Supplementary Table S1), non-survivors demonstrated higher rates of symptom progression (57.1% vs. 25.8%, p = 0.004) and physiologic progression (78.6% vs. 50.0%, p = 0.011). Non-survivors also experienced greater worsening in CAT score (ΔCAT: 9.0 vs. 2.5, p = 0.016), mMRC score (ΔmMRC: 2.0 vs. 0.5, p = 0.033), and FVC decline (− 7.5% vs. +1.5%, p = 0.003). Although overall HRCT progression was not significantly associated with mortality, specific radiologic findings, including new ground-glass opacity with traction bronchiectasis and increased coarseness of reticulation, were more frequently observed among non-survivors.

Predictors of three-year mortality

Univariable Cox regression analysis identified several factors associated with increased 3-year mortality (Table 4), including lower BMI (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.74–0.91, p < 0.001), higher GAP score (HR 1.57, 95% CI 1.27–1.95, p < 0.001), PPF-defined progression (HR 4.60, 95% CI 1.75–12.10, p = 0.002), and FVC decline.

Incremental prognostic value of PPF beyond GAP score

Because the GAP score is an established prognostic tool for IPF and was significantly associated with mortality in univariable analysis, we further evaluated whether PPF-defined progression provided prognostic information beyond the GAP score (Supplementary Table S2).

In univariable Cox regression, the GAP score demonstrated stronger prognostic performance than PPF-defined progression alone (χ² = 18.531 vs. 11.513). However, when GAP score and PPF-defined progression were entered simultaneously into a multivariable Cox model, both variables remained independently associated with mortality (GAP score: HR 1.42, 95% CI 1.14–1.77, p = 0.002; PPF-defined progression: HR 3.18, 95% CI 1.16–8.73, p = 0.025).

Importantly, adding PPF-defined progression to a model containing GAP score significantly improved model fit (Δχ² = 5.610, df = 1, p = 0.018). These findings indicate that PPF-defined progression captures prognostic information beyond that reflected by the GAP score alone and suggest that combining baseline risk assessment with longitudinal disease trajectory may improve risk stratification in patients with IPF receiving antifibrotic therapy.

Discussion

In this prospective cohort of patients with IPF receiving antifibrotic therapy, we found that disease progression within the first year, defined according to the 2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT PPF criteria, was strongly associated with subsequent mortality. More than half of the cohort fulfilled PPF criteria after one year of treatment, and these patients experienced a substantially higher 3-year mortality rate than those with stable disease. Importantly, PPF-defined progression remained independently associated with mortality after multivariable adjustment, highlighting the prognostic significance of early disease behavior despite antifibrotic therapy.

Although the PPF framework was originally developed for fibrotic ILDs other than IPF, our findings suggest that its multidimensional approach may also capture clinically meaningful disease activity in treated IPF populations. The PPF criteria integrate symptom progression, physiologic deterioration, and radiologic progression, all of which represent fundamental manifestations of ongoing fibrotic disease activity. Rather than relying solely on baseline risk factors, this framework reflects disease trajectory during treatment and may therefore provide a more dynamic assessment of prognosis.

An important finding of the present study is that PPF-defined progression provided prognostic information beyond the GAP score. Although the GAP score demonstrated stronger individual prognostic performance, the addition of PPF-defined progression significantly improved model fit and remained independently associated with mortality after adjustment for GAP score. These findings suggest that baseline risk assessment and longitudinal disease trajectory capture complementary aspects of disease behavior. Together, they support the concept that integrating static and dynamic prognostic information may improve risk stratification in patients with IPF receiving antifibrotic therapy.

Lower BMI was another independent predictor of mortality in our cohort. Previous studies have shown that low BMI and weight loss are associated with poor outcomes in IPF, potentially reflecting frailty, sarcopenia, impaired nutritional status, and reduced physiologic reserve [33–36]. Patients with progressive disease and those who died during follow-up both had significantly lower BMI. These findings further support the importance of systemic health status in determining long-term outcomes in IPF.

Our results are consistent with previous real-world studies demonstrating that the prognosis of IPF remains poor despite antifibrotic therapy. Although antifibrotic agents slow lung function decline, substantial residual risk remains, with reported 3-year survival rates ranging from 60% to 70% [13–16, 37, 38]. In our cohort, mortality was markedly higher among patients meeting PPF criteria, suggesting that persistent disease progression during treatment identifies a subgroup at particularly high risk.

From a clinical perspective, the PPF framework may provide a practical strategy for monitoring disease behavior after initiation of antifibrotic therapy. Patients who fulfill PPF criteria within the first year may benefit from closer follow-up, earlier referral for lung transplantation evaluation, enrollment in clinical trials, or intensified supportive care. Conversely, patients without evidence of progression may represent a more stable phenotype with more favorable medium-term outcomes.

Several limitations should be considered. First, this study was conducted at a single tertiary referral center with a relatively small sample size (n = 90), which may limit the statistical power of subgroup analyses and constrain the generalisability of our findings to other populations, healthcare systems, and treatment settings. External validation in larger multicenter cohorts is warranted.

Second, the PPF criteria were originally developed and validated for non-IPF fibrotic ILDs. Therefore, their application to IPF in the present study should be regarded as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Although the PPF domains are biologically relevant to disease progression in IPF, formal validation of this framework in IPF populations remains lacking.

Third, symptom progression was evaluated using changes in CAT and mMRC scores. Although CAT has been widely used in ILD research, it was originally developed for COPD, and its specificity, responsiveness, and clinically meaningful thresholds in IPF have not been fully established. Therefore, the symptom domain should be interpreted with caution, and future studies should incorporate IPF-specific patient-reported outcome measures.

Fourth, baseline BMI was measured prior to initiation of antifibrotic therapy, and longitudinal body weight data were not systematically collected. Consequently, we could not determine whether the observed association between lower BMI and mortality reflected pre-existing nutritional status, disease-related weight loss, treatment-related adverse effects, or a combination of these factors. Future studies incorporating serial body weight assessments are needed to clarify these relationships.

Fifth, other nutritional indicators, including serum albumin, Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index (GNRI), muscle mass, and sarcopenia-related measures, were not systematically available in this cohort. Our group is currently investigating the association between sarcopenia and mortality in patients with IPF. Future studies incorporating comprehensive nutritional and body composition assessments may provide a more detailed understanding of the relationship between nutritional status and prognosis.

Finally, the lower frequency of definite UIP pattern among patients without progression raises the possibility that radiologically less typical presentations of IPF may be associated with a more indolent disease course. Because all diagnoses were established through multidisciplinary clinical-radiological assessment without surgical lung biopsy, we were unable to further explore this observation using pathology-based subgroup analyses. Future studies incorporating more detailed radiological phenotyping may help clarify the prognostic significance of baseline HRCT patterns.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that PPF-defined progression and lower baseline BMI are independent predictors of mortality in patients with IPF receiving antifibrotic therapy. Beyond conventional baseline risk assessment, PPF-defined progression provided additional prognostic information beyond the GAP score, highlighting the importance of longitudinal disease trajectory in determining outcomes. These findings suggest that integrating multidimensional progression assessment with established prognostic tools may improve risk stratification and facilitate more personalized management of IPF. Future multicenter studies are needed to validate these observations and further define the role of the PPF framework in treated IPF populations.

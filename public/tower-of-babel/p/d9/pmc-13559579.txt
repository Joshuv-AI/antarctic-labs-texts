A single-center retrospective study evaluating the efficacy of anlotinib plus temozolomide for recurrent glioma based on real-world data
Authors: Yanyan Huang, Wei Wei, Yajing Xu, Peng Chen, Xiaoqin Tang, Yi Zheng, Haoyi Li, Mengna Jin, Pan Ma, Chuan Lan, Fei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecurrent gliomas are highly malignant intracranial tumors with poor overall outcomes and no consensus salvage treatment strategy. Vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A)/vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR-2)-dependent angiogenesis is a key driver of glioma progression. As an oral VEGFR-2 inhibitor with anti-tumor and anti-angiogenic activities, anlotinib may deliver clinical benefits for recurrent glioma, yet relevant clinical evidence remains limited. We herein aimed to evaluate the efficacy of anlotinib combined with temozolomide (TMZ) in patients with recurrent or residual glioma.MethodsThis single-center retrospective cohort study enrolled patients with recurrent glioma treated with anlotinib combined with TMZ at The First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University between January 2020 and September 2025. Treatment was administered until disease progression or unmanageable toxicity. Progression-free survival from anlotinib plus metronomic TMZ treatment (PFS2) served as the primary endpoint; secondary endpoints included overall survival from anlotinib plus metronomic TMZ treatment (OS2), objective response rate (ORR), and disease control rate (DCR). Kaplan-Meier curves were applied for survival estimation, and Cox regression models were used to screen prognostic biomarkers.ResultsA total of 39 patients were included [median age: 51 years; 61.54% World Health Organization (WHO) grade 4 glioma; 56.40% with methylated O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter]. The median PFS2 was 7.57 months [95% confidence interval (CI): 5.60–11.93], and median OS2 was 17.57 months (95% CI: 14.33–not estimable). Unmethylated MGMT promoter [hazard ratio (HR) =3.089, 95% CI: 1.074–8.886, P=0.04] and higher Ki-67 index (per 1% increment, HR =1.068, 95% CI: 1.036–1.101, P<0.001) independently predicted poorer post-recurrence survival. A Ki-67 cutoff of 7% differentiated WHO grade 2 from grade 3/4 gliomas, while the optimal cutoff of 25% predicted 6-month progression and 12-month mortality. Treatment-related adverse events were mostly mild, with few grade 3 toxicities and no treatment-associated deaths.ConclusionsAnlotinib combined with TMZ exhibited potential efficacy for recurrent gliomas, and the findings were merely hypothesis-generating. These exploratory results warrant validation in large randomized controlled trials to further confirm its clinical value.Keywords: Recurrent glioma, anlotinib, temozolomide (TMZ)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Anlotinib combined with metronomic temozolomide demonstrated potential efficacy for recurrent glioma. O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter unmethylation and elevated Ki-67 index were independent poor prognostic factors. We established exploratory Ki-67 cut-offs (7%, 25%, 45%) for tumor grading, progression and survival prediction. All results are exploratory and require further verification.

What is known and what is new?

• MGMT promoter unmethylation and elevated Ki-67 expression are established adverse prognostic biomarkers associated with inferior survival in recurrent glioma.

• Real-world data from this single-center cohort verified the potential efficacy of anlotinib plus metronomic temozolomide in recurrent glioma. A Ki-67 cut-off of 7% effectively differentiated World Health Organization (WHO) grade 2 from grade 3/4 recurrent gliomas. Ki-67 levels of 25% and 45% had predictive ability for early progression and long-term survival of recurrent gliomas, respectively.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• MGMT status and Ki-67 index are valuable prognostic indicators for patients treated with this combined regimen. A Ki-67 index >25% indicates higher risks of progression and death. Due to the retrospective nature and limited sample size, the Ki-67 cut-off values cannot guide clinical management currently. Prospective controlled trials with larger cohorts are needed to validate our conclusions and promote the clinical application of this treatment strategy.

Introduction

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, and brain tumors—particularly gliomas—rank among the most lethal malignancies (1). Gliomas are neoplasms derived from glial cells of the central nervous system. As the most common primary intracranial tumors, they account for approximately 46% of all intracranial lesions (2), with incidence and mortality rates among the highest across all malignant neoplasms (3). The standard of care for newly diagnosed glioblastoma involves maximal safe surgical resection, followed by concurrent chemoradiotherapy and six cycles of adjuvant temozolomide (TMZ) chemotherapy. For primary glioblastoma, median overall survival (OS) reaches 14.6 months, and median progression-free survival (PFS) is 6.9 months (4). Virtually all gliomas inevitably relapse. While standardized regimens are well-defined for newly diagnosed high-grade gliomas, salvage therapeutic approaches for recurrent disease remain largely individualized (5). Following tumor recurrence, median OS of recurrent glioblastoma ranges from 5.6 to 9.8 months, with median PFS spanning 1.9 to 4.2 months (6,7). Recurrent gliomas exhibit highly aggressive biological behavior; therapeutic alternatives at relapse are scarce, and long-term survival outcomes remain poor (8). Accordingly, a substantial unmet clinical need persists for patients with recurrent glioma, highlighting the urgent demand for novel, more effective therapeutic strategies.

Gliomas are highly vascularized tumors, with their growth, invasion, and recurrence all being dependent on pathological angiogenesis (9). Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is a key mediator of aberrant angiogenesis in malignant neoplasms (10). VEGF and its cognate receptors (VEGFRs) play pivotal roles in both physiological and pathological angiogenesis. Among these, VEGF-A and VEGFR-2 are the primary regulators of embryonic vasculogenesis and tumor angiogenesis, and have emerged as core targets for anti-angiogenic therapies (11). Tumor angiogenesis in glioblastoma is predominantly driven by the VEGF-A/VEGFR-2 (KDR) signaling axis (12), providing a strong mechanistic rationale for the development of anti-angiogenic agents and targeted therapies for gliomas (13).

The cytotoxicity of TMZ depends on generating O6-methylguanine in DNA, which is repaired by the suicide enzyme methylated O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT); MGMT overexpression is a major cause of TMZ resistance. A key hypothesis proposes that sustained TMZ administration can deplete MGMT and overcome resistance when its consumption rate exceeds de novo synthesis (14). The 7-days-on, 7-days-off metronomic TMZ schedule has been validated to increase cumulative dose intensity (approximately 1.5-fold vs. the standard 5/28 regimen) and to deplete MGMT through continuous low-dose alkylation (15). This regimen (TMZ was given at 100 mg daily on a 14-days-on, 7-days-off schedule) yields a cumulative TMZ dose similar to that of the 7/14-day schedule (16) and achieves sustained MGMT depletion, thereby reducing hematologic toxicity. Anlotinib is a potent multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor with high selectivity for VEGFR2, exerting dual anti-proliferative and anti-angiogenic effects (10,13). It has demonstrated efficacy in various advanced solid tumors (17-20) and manageable toxicity with favorable pharmacokinetics (21). However, only a small number of small-sample studies have investigated the efficacy of anlotinib in recurrent gliomas (22-24). Therefore, the aim of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of anlotinib in combination with TMZ regimen for the treatment of recurrent gliomas. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0856/rc).

Methods

Study design and patients

This study enrolled patients with recurrent glioma admitted to The First Affiliated Hospital of the Army Medical University, Chinese People’s Liberation Army, between January 2020 and September 2025. We retrospectively collected the following clinical variables for all participants: age at initial diagnosis, sex, World Health Organization (WHO) glioma grade, extent of resection, recurrence line, isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutation status, MGMT promoter methylation status, telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) promoter mutation status, Ki-67 index, follow-up duration, survival status, and disease progression status and other relevant clinical parameters. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of the Army Medical University, Chinese People’s Liberation Army [approval No. (B) KY2025250; approval date: 12 November 2025]. Owing to its retrospective design, written informed consent was waived by the ethics committee. This cohort was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (TRN: ChiCTR2500112913; registration date: 20 November 2025), and registration was completed retrospectively after patient enrollment concluded.

Inclusion criteria: (I) age ≥15 years; (II) pathologically confirmed glioma after surgery; (III) radiologically verified tumor recurrence consistent with Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology criteria (RANO 2.0) (25) following standard Stupp regimen; patients subsequently received combination therapy with anlotinib plus metronomic TMZ at recurrence; (IV) completion of no less than one full cycle of anlotinib combined with metronomic TMZ; (V) baseline normal hematologic, hepatic, and renal function laboratory values; (VI) absence of severe comorbidities or critical underlying disorders, including but not limited to myocardial infarction, unstable angina, refractory hypertension, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, hepatic/renal dysfunction, uncontrolled active infection, and concurrent secondary malignancies. Any comorbidity deemed by investigators to compromise patient safety or interfere with treatment completion was excluded.

Exclusion criteria: (I) unclear postoperative pathological diagnosis; (II) absence of valid postoperative pathological results; (III) prior treatment with any VEGF-targeted agents before initiation of anlotinib plus TMZ therapy; (IV) expected OS of less than 3 months; (V) incomplete clinical and follow-up data. The patient screening flowchart is presented in Figure S1.

Treatment regimen

All patients received oral anlotinib at a daily dose of 12 mg on a 3-week cycle (2 weeks of continuous administration followed by a 1-week drug holiday). Treatment was continued until disease progression, intolerable toxicity, or patient refusal, whichever occurred first. For grade 3 adverse events, anlotinib could be resumed only after symptoms resolved to grade 2, with the daily dose reduced to 10 mg.

The TMZ regimen was administered orally at a daily dose of 10 mg following the identical 3-week cyclic schedule (2 consecutive weeks of administration followed by a 1-week break).

Outcome measures

The primary endpoint was second progression-free survival (PFS2). PFS2 was defined as the time from the first dose of anlotinib plus TMZ at recurrence to subsequent disease progression or intolerable toxicity, whichever came first.

Secondary endpoints comprised second overall survival (OS2), objective response rate (ORR), and disease control rate (DCR). OS2 was defined as the time from the initial administration of the anlotinib-TMZ combination at recurrence until death or the last follow-up date, whichever occurred first. ORR referred to the proportion of evaluable patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), calculated from the first cycle of study treatment at recurrence until treatment discontinuation. DCR represented the proportion of evaluable patients with a best overall response of CR, PR, or stable disease (SD), calculated over the same treatment period from initial study drug administration to trial withdrawal.

Treatment responses were evaluated according to the RANO 2.0 criteria for glioma (25). All patients underwent baseline and serial follow-up brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) examinations, including pre- and post-gadolinium T1-weighted sequences and T2/fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) sequences. Imaging assessments were independently performed by two blinded neurosurgeons. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus or adjudicated by a third senior reviewer. All treatment responses and disease progression events were confirmed by follow-up MRI obtained at least four weeks after the initial imaging evaluation.

Additionally, exploratory analyses of first-line post-recurrence progression-free survival (PFS1) and overall survival (OS1) were conducted. PFS1 was defined as the interval from tumor recurrence to subsequent disease progression, intolerable toxicity, or last follow-up, whichever occurred first. OS1 was defined as the time from recurrence to death or the follow-up cutoff date (October 31, 2025), whichever came first.

For survival analyses, PFS was censored at the last evaluable contrast-enhanced brain MRI, while OS was censored at the last valid patient contact or the predefined cutoff date (October 31, 2025). Tumor response was assessed every three treatment cycles based on RANO 2.0 criteria. All adverse events were monitored and graded in accordance with the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0 (26).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0, R (version 4.5.3), and GraphPad Prism 8.4.0. Continuous variables were compared with the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, while categorical variables were analyzed via the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. OS and PFS were estimated via the Kaplan-Meier method, with intergroup comparisons carried out using the log-rank test.

Univariate Cox regression analyses were performed for all candidate prognostic variables, and the Bonferroni correction was adopted to control the family-wise error rate for multiple comparisons.

Multivariate Cox regression models were constructed via the enter method, incorporating only variables that reached statistical significance in univariate analyses. The proportional hazards (PH) assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals; variables violating the PH assumption were excluded from the corresponding multivariate models. A P>0.05 indicated that the PHs assumption held true.

Considering the limited sample size (39 patients) and potential quasi-complete separation, Firth’s penalized Cox regression was implemented as a sensitivity analysis to validate the robustness of the primary survival results.

We further calculated optimal Ki-67 cutoff values for survival endpoints among patients with WHO grade 3/4 gliomas and identified the Ki-67 threshold differentiating WHO grade 2 gliomas from grade 3/4 lesions across the whole cohort. Optimal cutoff points were determined by maximizing Youden’s index, and bootstrap resampling was used for internal validation.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value <0.05 was defined as statistically significant.

No AI tools were employed for data analysis or manuscript preparation in this study. All authors are responsible for the entire content.

Results

Clinical baseline characteristics

A total of 39 patients with recurrent glioma receiving the anlotinib plus TMZ combination regimen were included in the present study (Table 1). The cohort consisted of 21 females and 18 males, with a median age of 51 years at enrollment. WHO grade 4 glioma accounted for the largest proportion of cases (61.54%), followed by WHO grade 2 glioma (28.21%), while WHO grade 3 glioma constituted the smallest subgroup (10.25%). Over half of the patients harbored methylated MGMT promoters (56.40%); 59.00% presented IDH wild-type status, and patients with TERT promoter mutations and wild-type TERT were nearly evenly distributed. Roughly 95% of participants underwent maximal safe gross total resection. The mean Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) score at anlotinib initiation was 82.05±11.51 (Table S1). The median follow-up time was 25.67 months, with the longest follow-up reaching 44.20 months.

Survival outcomes

Regarding survival outcomes, the median second progression-free survival (mPFS2) was 7.57 months (95% CI: 5.60–11.93), with 6-month and 1-year PFS2 rates of 64.10% (95% CI: 47.04–76.93) and 34.47% (95% CI: 19.89–49.55), respectively (Figure 1A; Table S2). The median second overall survival (mOS2) for the entire cohort was not estimable; the observed mOS2 reached 17.57 months (95% CI: 13.53–not estimable), with 1-year and 2-year OS2 rates of 76.41% (95% CI: 59.50–86.98) and 45.05% (95% CI: 26.61–61.87) (Figure 1B; Table S2). Median first progression-free survival (mPFS1) was 9.37 months (95% CI: 6.63–14.60), and median first overall survival (mOS1) was 18.80 months (95% CI: 14.33–not estimable) (Figure S2A,S2B).

The overall survival of patients treated with anlotinib combined with metronomic temozolomide, as well as survival stratified by WHO grade, is shown in Figure 1. Stratified survival analysis revealed significant disparities in mOS2 across WHO glioma grades (WHO grade 2 vs. grade 3 vs. grade 4: – vs. 14.30 vs. 15.73 months; P=0.03) (Figure 1D; Table S2). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons demonstrated that patients with WHO grade 2 glioma achieved significantly longer mOS2 relative to those with WHO grade 4 tumors (grade 2 vs. 4: χ2=6.937, P=0.008). No intergroup differences were detected between grade 2 and grade 3 (grade 2 vs. 3: χ2=2.898, P=0.09), or between grade 3 and grade 4 (grade 3 vs. 4: χ2=0.100, P=0.75). mPFS2 also differed significantly among the three WHO grade subgroups (grade 2 vs. 3 vs. 4: 21.37 vs. 8.90 vs. 6.30 months; P=0.04) (Figure 1C; Table S2). Pairwise comparisons confirmed superior mPFS2 in grade 2 patients compared with grade 4 patients (grade 2 vs. 4: χ2=5.898, P=0.02). No significant mPFS2 differences existed between grade 2 and grade 3 (grade 2 vs. 3: χ2=0.905, P=0.34) or grade 3 and grade 4 (grade 3 vs. 4: χ2=0.307, P=0.58). Consistent survival disparities by WHO grade were also observed for mPFS1 and mOS1 (Figure S2C,S2D).

Stratification by IDH mutation status showed that patients with IDH-mutant tumors had longer mPFS2 than those with IDH-wildtype gliomas (IDH-mutant vs. IDH-wildtype: 15.70 vs. 6.30 months; P=0.03) (Figure 2A; Table S2). Similarly, IDH mutation was associated with prolonged mOS2 (IDH-mutant vs. IDH-wildtype: – vs. 15.73 months; P=0.009) (Figure 2B; Table S2). Identical trends were seen for PFS1 and OS1 (Figure S3A,S3B).

Subgroup analysis stratified by MGMT promoter methylation status showed a trend toward longer mPFS2 in patients with methylated MGMT promoters, yet the difference did not reach statistical significance (methylated vs. unmethylated MGMT promoter: 11.53 vs. 6.30 months; P=0.06) (Figure 2C; Table S2). In contrast, mOS2 differed significantly between the two subgroups (methylated vs. unmethylated MGMT promoter: – vs. 14.30 months; P=0.002) (Figure 2D; Table S2). For early post-recurrence survival endpoints, MGMT promoter methylation status exerted no significant effect on PFS1, but conferred a significant survival advantage for OS1 (Figure S3C,S3D).

With regard to TERT promoter status, patients with wildtype TERT promoters exhibited prolonged mPFS2 (TERT wildtype vs. TERT-mutant: 11.93 vs. 6.00 months; P=0.04; Figure 3A; Table S2) and mOS2 (TERT wildtype vs. TERT-mutant: – vs. 13.67 months; P=0.02; Figure 3B; Table S2) compared with patients harboring TERT promoter mutations. No significant between-group differences in PFS1 were identified across TERT promoter subgroups, whereas OS1 differed significantly (Figure S4A,S4B).

Of the 39 enrolled patients, one patient with WHO grade 4 glioma attained a CR. PR was achieved in 41.03% of the cohort, and SD was observed in 33.33%. The ORR was 43.59%, with a DCR of 76.92%. For patients with WHO grade 3 or 4 gliomas, the ORR reached 46.43% and the DCR was 78.57% (Table S3).

Related influencing factors for survival outcomes-univariate Cox regression analysis

In univariate analyses for PFS2, TERT promoter mutation (versus wild-type, HR =2.196, 95% CI: 1.005–4.798, P=0.049), Ki-67 index (per 1% increment, HR =1.042, 95% CI: 1.019–1.065, P<0.001), IDH wild-type status (versus mutant, HR =2.447, 95% CI: 1.060–5.649, P=0.04), and WHO grade 4 glioma (versus grade 2, HR =2.741, 95% CI: 1.143–6.573, P=0.02) were significantly correlated with shorter PFS2. Following Bonferroni multiple testing correction, only the Ki-67 index retained statistical significance (adjusted P=0.002) (Table S4).

For OS2, advancing age (per 1-year increment, HR =1.063, 95% CI: 1.010–1.118, P=0.02), TERT promoter mutation (versus wild-type, HR =3.178, 95% CI: 1.159–8.714, P=0.03), unmethylated MGMT promoter status (versus methylated, HR =4.551, 95% CI: 1.600–12.941, P=0.005), Ki-67 index (per 1% increment, HR =1.074, 95% CI: 1.043–1.107, P<0.001), IDH wild-type status (versus mutant, HR =4.551, 95% CI: 1.314–15.761, P=0.02), and WHO grade 4 glioma (versus grade 2, HR =3.974, 95% CI: 1.147–13.763, P=0.03) represented adverse prognostic factors. After Bonferroni correction, the Ki-67 index (P<0.001) and unmethylated MGMT promoter status (P=0.04) remained statistically significant (Table S4).

As for exploratory endpoints PFS1 and OS1, the Ki-67 index exhibited robust prognostic predictive power for all survival outcomes (all P<0.001) (Table S4).

Related influencing factors for survival outcomes-multivariate Cox regression analysis

In the preliminary multivariate Cox regression model (incorporating all variables that reached statistical significance in univariate Cox analyses; Table S5), IDH status and WHO glioma grade (grade 4 vs. grade 2) exhibited complete separation, which generated extreme hazard ratios (HRs) (>106 or 0) and non-estimable confidence intervals (CIs). In addition, IDH status, WHO grade, age, and TERT promoter mutation status violated the PH assumption (Table S6). Consequently, only MGMT promoter methylation status and the Ki-67 index were retained in the final multivariate Cox model, both of which met the PH assumption (Table S7).

For the primary endpoint PFS2, the Ki-67 index emerged as the sole independent prognostic factor (per 1% increment, HR =1.039, 95% CI: 1.015–1.063, P=0.001). For OS2, unmethylated MGMT promoter status (versus methylated status, HR =3.089, 95% CI: 1.074–8.886, P=0.04) and elevated Ki-67 index (per 1% increment, HR =1.068, 95% CI: 1.036–1.101, P<0.001) independently predicted poorer OS (Table 2).

For exploratory endpoints PFS1 and OS1, the Ki-67 index remained an independent prognostic factor, while MGMT promoter methylation status only exhibited borderline statistical significance for OS1 (Table 2). Firth’s penalized Cox regression was further applied as a sensitivity analysis, and its outputs were consistent with those derived from conventional multivariate Cox regression (Table S8).

We calculated optimal Ki-67 index cutoff values for multiple clinical endpoints: 25% for OS2 and PFS2, 45% for OS1 and PFS1, and 7% for differentiation between WHO glioma grades. Among patients with WHO grade 3/4 gliomas, the Ki-67 index exhibited favorable discriminative power for OS2 (AUC =0.794, 95% CI: 0.565–0.953, P=0.003), PFS2 (AUC =0.759, 95% CI: 0.563–0.950, P=0.008), and OS1 (AUC =0.844, 95% CI: 0.592–1.000, P=0.001) (Figure 4). The AUC for PFS1 reached 0.728 (95% CI: 0.443–0.940, P=0.057), indicating borderline statistical significance, a finding likely attributable to the small sample size. In the entire study cohort, the Ki-67 index displayed outstanding capacity to separate WHO grade 3/4 tumors from WHO grade 2 lesions (AUC =0.935, 95% CI, 0.839–1.000, P<0.001). Internal validation via 1,000 bootstrap resampling confirmed the robustness of all identified cutoff thresholds (Table S9).

Discussion

Currently, recurrent glioma confers a dismal prognosis, and no uniform standard salvage regimen has been established to date. his retrospective study evaluated the efficacy and safety of anlotinib plus metronomic TMZ for patients with recurrent glioma. This single-center case series enrolled a relatively large cohort receiving intermittent anlotinib (14 days on/7 days off) combined with metronomic TMZ, covering a full spectrum of WHO glioma histological grades. Our findings showed potential therapeutic effects. Adverse event data were incomplete, but no unexpected severe toxicities were observed.

In this study, the median PFS2 and OS2 of the overall cohort were 7.57 months (95% CI: 5.60–11.93) and 17.57 months (95% CI: 13.53–not estimable), respectively. For the recurrent glioblastoma subgroup, the median PFS and OS reached 5.73 and 13.67 months. Collectively, these survival outcomes are superior to those reported for conventional salvage therapies for recurrent glioblastoma. For reference, low-dose TMZ monotherapy yields a median OS of 6.9 months and a median PFS of 4.3 months (27). Bevacizumab single-agent treatment achieves a median PFS of 3.0–4.2 months and a median OS of 8.0-9.2 months in recurrent glioblastoma patients (28,29). Standard second-line chemotherapy regimens, including regorafenib and lomustine monotherapy, confer even inferior survival benefits, with median PFS of 2.0 and 1.9 months and median OS of 7.4 and 5.6 months, respectively (6). Furthermore, our combination regimen exhibited better numerical survival benefits than bevacizumab plus dose-dense TMZ, which yields a median OS of 9.4 months and a median PFS of 4.7 months (30). Additionally, monotherapy with anlotinib alone provides limited therapeutic efficacy for recurrent glioblastoma, with a reported median OS of 6.2–7.7 months and median PFS of 4.5–5.0 months (22,31). Notably, previous clinical studies focusing on anlotinib combined with TMZ for recurrent or residual gliomas have documented a median PFS of 6.1–7.2 months and a median OS of 11.9–15.8 months (13,24), which were consistent with our results.

Following anti-VEGF/VEGFR therapy, the tumor contrast-enhancing area is markedly reduced; however, this radiological change does not represent genuine tumor regression. Instead, it primarily results from the rapid restoration and normalization of blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability. Mechanistically, anti-VEGF agents (e.g., bevacizumab) rapidly repair tumor-disrupted BBB integrity and suppress vascular leakage, thereby substantially decreasing the extravasation of gadolinium-based contrast agents into tumor tissues. This phenomenon manifests as prominent shrinkage or complete resolution of contrast-enhancing lesions on MRI, whereas the underlying tumor may not regress and can continue to progress within non-enhancing infiltrative regions (32,33). Consistently, patients receiving bevacizumab frequently achieve favorable radiographic responses and prolonged PFS, yet these benefits fail to translate into improved OS (28). We acknowledge that reduced contrast enhancement does not fully reflect true anti-tumor activity, which constitutes a well-recognized limitation of PFS as a surrogate endpoint in anti-angiogenic trials. Therefore, future studies should comprehensively evaluate therapeutic efficacy based on multiple dimensions, including OS, quality of life, neurocognitive function, and steroid dosage, in addition to conventional PFS outcomes.

A previous study identified MGMT promoter methylation as an independent favorable independent prognostic biomarker for glioma (34), which aligns with our findings. In glioblastoma, cancer stem cell traits are positively correlated with MGMT expression. Residual stem cell subpopulations with high MGMT expression are a major driver of tumor recurrence following TMZ treatment, explaining why MGMT promoter unmethylation (constitutive high MGMT expression) confers an inferior prognosis. Differentiation-inducing agents such as all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) can simultaneously attenuate cancer stem cell phenotypes and reverse MGMT-mediated chemoresistance. Combination therapy with ATRA and TMZ is therefore hypothesized to overcome chemotherapy resistance and enhance treatment efficacy, providing a solid theoretical rationale for the development of such combined regimens (35).

Multivariate Cox regression confirmed that the Ki-67 index was an independent prognostic factor for all survival endpoints in recurrent gliomas (all P<0.001). Each 1% increment in the Ki-67 index significantly increased the risks of disease progression and death, which is consistent with previous findings (36). To date, no universally accepted Ki-67 cutoff value has been established for differentiating WHO grade 2 gliomas from grade 3/4 tumors. Clinically, a Ki-67 index of 10% has been conventionally adopted as a tentative threshold to distinguish low-grade from high-grade gliomas (37). In the present study, the optimal Ki-67 cutoff for differentiating recurrent WHO grade 2 gliomas from grade 3/4 counterparts was 7%, which is concordant with previous evidence (38). We further identified a cutoff of 25% for predicting post-recurrence disease progression and mortality, further validating Ki-67 as a reliable proliferation biomarker with robust prognostic value across both primary and recurrent disease stages (37). Given the relatively limited sample size, the Ki-67 cutoff values determined in this study remain exploratory and hypothesis-generating. External validation in larger independent cohorts is warranted prior to any clinical implementation of these threshold values.

Overall, the incidence of treatment-related adverse events in our cohort was lower than that reported in previous studies (22,23,31). This discrepancy may be partially attributed to the limited in-hospital follow-up: only approximately 20% of patients returned to our center for routine blood and biochemical examinations, leading to incomplete laboratory data for adverse event assessment. Although key hematological toxicities, including hypertension, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia, were verified through regular telephone follow-up, potential underreporting of subtle or asymptomatic adverse events cannot be ruled out. Notably, high missing rates of peripheral blood laboratory data precluded a comprehensive analysis of lymphopenia in our cohort; nevertheless, treatment-related lymphopenia remained a clinically notable toxicity among patients with high-grade glioma in this study (39). Collectively, these limitations indicate that the safety profile of the anlotinib plus metronomic TMZ regimen requires further validation in prospective, systematically monitored clinical studies.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-arm, retrospective design without internal or external control groups. In addition, patients with short survival or incomplete follow-up data were excluded, which may have introduced selection bias. Second, adverse events were primarily identified through telephone follow-up; therefore, potential underreporting of subtle or asymptomatic toxicities cannot be fully excluded, leading to inherent recall bias. Third, given the relatively small sample size, all biomarker analyses in this study remain exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than conclusively validated. Future rigorous randomized controlled trials with standardized protocols—including prospective molecular stratification, centralized imaging evaluation, systematic adverse event collection, unified survival endpoint definitions, and head-to-head comparisons with established salvage regimens—are warranted to further validate the efficacy of anlotinib combined with metronomic TMZ for recurrent glioma. Furthermore, the therapeutic landscape for recurrent glioma is continuously evolving. Beyond anti-angiogenic strategies, emerging approaches, including molecular targeted therapy, stemness regulation, immune restoration, and vector-based therapeutic systems, also deserve further in-depth clinical exploration (35,39-43).

Conclusions

Anlotinib combined with TMZ exhibited potential efficacy for recurrent gliomas, and the findings were merely hypothesis-generating. Prospective controlled studies are needed to determine the true efficacy and safety of this regimen.

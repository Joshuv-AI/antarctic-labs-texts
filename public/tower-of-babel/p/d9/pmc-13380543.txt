Letter to the editor regarding “Impact of gross tumor morphology on the clinical outcomes of colon cancer: multicenter retrospective cohort study”
Authors: Rongwen Ni, Rijuan Jin
Published: 2026

Dear Editor:

We read with great interest the article by So Jung Han [1] et al. titled “Impact of gross tumor morphology on the clinical outcomes of colon cancer: multicenter retrospective cohort study.” This study enrolled 1177 patients who underwent curative resection and stratified them based on endoscopic morphology into flat/ulcer-infiltrative type (n = 345) and polypoid/ulcer-polypoid type (n = 832). The analysis revealed that patients with the flat/ulcer-infiltrative subtype exhibited significantly shorter overall survival and disease-free survival compared with those with the polypoid/ulcer-polypoid subtype, with the difference particularly pronounced in stage II patients. The authors proposed that endoscopic gross morphology represents an easily accessible prognostic factor, independent of TNM staging, with potential utility for preoperative risk stratification and adjuvant treatment decision-making. While recognizing the value of this work, several limitations that were not explicitly addressed in the article warrant discussion to further enhance the study’s scientific rigor.

First, the total number of lymph nodes retrieved was not incorporated into the analysis or discussion, which may compromise the accuracy of prognostic assessment. Curative colectomy for colorectal cancer requires examination of at least 12 lymph nodes; failure to meet this threshold can result in stage migration, thereby distorting the true relationship between staging and prognosis [2]. For instance, a flat/infiltrative tumor with only eight nodes retrieved due to anatomical challenges, including one positive node, could be classified as stage II, whereas the adverse prognosis may stem from understaging rather than tumor morphology itself [2].

Second, the study did not report the status of resection margins among the included patients. Positive circumferential resection margins are strong predictors of local recurrence and reduced survival, and infiltrative tumors, with poorly defined borders, are more challenging to achieve R0 resection [3, 4]. A nationwide survival analysis further identified that pT4 stage II and III colorectal cancers have the poorest outcomes, a high-risk feature potentially more prevalent in infiltrative tumors [5]. Therefore, resection margin status may influence the study’s conclusions.

Finally, variations in treatment modalities and diagnostic strategies may introduce lead-time bias, which can artificially inflate survival estimates [6]. In this study, 68 patients (5.8%), predominantly with polypoid morphology, were early-stage cases whose survival time was naturally longer than that of patients with more advanced, mostly infiltrative tumors. Consequently, the observed survival differences may reflect earlier diagnosis rather than true biological distinctions.

In summary, Han et al. provide an important multicenter observational study supporting the notion that flat/ulcer-infiltrative colorectal cancers have a poorer prognosis. Future prospective studies should include key variables that encompass multiple dimensions, such as gross morphology, histology, and molecular factors, to more accurately reflect the true clinical value of this readily accessible endoscopic characteristic.

Rongwen Ni, Rijuan Jin

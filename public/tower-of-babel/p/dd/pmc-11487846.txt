Effectiveness of resistance training of masticatory muscles for patients with temporomandibular disorders: protocol for a systematic review
Authors: Giacomo Asquini, Giulia Pisacane, Filippo Maselli, Giovanni Galeoto, Firas Mourad, Paolo Bizzarri, Edoardo Balli, Cecilia Bagnoli, Anna Manzari, Marco Pernici, Andrea Giusti, Deborah Falla
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTemporomandibular disorders (TMDs) are musculoskeletal disorders characterised by jaw pain and typically temporomandibular joint limitations. Resistance training (RT) has been shown to be effective at reducing pain and improving function for different musculoskeletal conditions (eg, neck pain, low back pain); however, the effectiveness of RT for patients with TMDs remains unclear. This systematic review will evaluate the effectiveness of RT on pain and temporomandibular joint range of motion in people with TMDs.Methods and analysisThe report of this protocol aligns with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols. The search strategy will be conducted from 1 March 2024 to 31 March 2024 via the following electronic databases: MEDLINE (OVID interface), EMBASE (OVID interface), SCOPUS, Web of Science, PubMed and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. Any randomised controlled trials or non-randomised studies of interventions in adults with TMD that examine RT targeting the masticatory muscles, compared to other interventions not including RT, will be included. Primary outcomes will be jaw pain intensity and maximal mouth opening. Secondary outcomes will include measures of neuromuscular performance and pressure pain thresholds. Two independent reviewers will conduct the screening of articles for inclusion, data extraction, risk-of-bias assessment using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for included studies and will evaluate the overall quality of evidence following the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework. A meta-analysis will be performed where applicable. Alternately, a narrative synthesis will be performed by adhering to the synthesis without meta-analysis guidelines. Data will be summarised according to the outcome measures or, when this is not possible, according to other pertinent variables such as TMD type (ie, temporomandibular joint disorders, masticatory muscle disorders and mixed disorders).Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not necessary, since this study does not involve the collection of primary data. The results will be disseminated through presentations at scientific conferences and publication in a peer-reviewed journal.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42023476269.

Background

Temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) are a prevalent musculoskeletal condition in developed countries, second only to chronic low back pain.1 2 TMDs are the primary cause of non-odontogenic orofacial pain, with high rates of persistence.3 Approximately 15% of adults are affected by painful TMDs, mainly those aged between 20 and 40 years of age.4 It is well established that TMDs’ impact on daily activities, social and emotional well-being and quality of life, leading to significant economic costs due to treatment and reduced work productivity.257 TMDs are characterised by pain in temporomandibular joints (TMJs), masticatory muscles and associated structures with possible joint sounds and jaw movement limitations.2 People with TMDs may also experience altered neuromuscular function of the masticatory muscles.811 This can result in decreased maximal bite force8 10 and reorganisation of muscle activity. Additionally, they may experience muscular fatigue during endurance tests,11 with lower oxygen extraction capacity during mastication, which interferes with muscle contractile function.9 10

Current clinical recommendations for the treatment of TMDs suggest a multidisciplinary approach that combines conservative interventions such as education, manual therapy, exercises and oral appliances.1216 Recent evidence shows that exercise can reduce pain, improve range of motion and enhance function in TMD patients.17 18 Exercise is also a common primary care strategy for other musculoskeletal conditions such as low back pain and neck pain,19 20 with exercises being particularly effective for improving functional outcomes compared with other conservative treatments.21 While many types of exercises can be effective for relieving pain, resistance training (RT) has been shown to effectively maintain muscle health and reduce inflammatory factors.22 RT can be defined as voluntary activation of a muscle against resistance, aiming to enhance muscle strength and endurance through the application of targeted overload.22 Furthermore, exercise is known to improve the neuromuscular performance of masticatory muscles in patients with TMD by increasing maximal bite force,23 improving masticatory endurance and resistance to fatigue23 and enhancing muscular efficiency.24 These neuromuscular responses to exercises may be particularly beneficial, given that recent evidence suggests that TMDs may be associated with muscle overload caused by bruxism.2527 Thus, RT could be an effective way to alleviate TMD symptoms and improve overall muscle function.

Systematic reviews (SRs) have assessed the effectiveness of exercise on pain, TMJ range of motion and muscle function in people with TMDs.1718 2831 Yet, the findings of these reviews are somewhat contradictory. For example, the review by Dickerson et al29 found that exercise improves pain and TMJ range of motion even if it was not possible to identify the best exercise type, intensity or duration. Zhang et al30 found no differences in pain reduction between exercise and occlusal splints, while the review by Idáñez-Robles et al31 indicated that exercise increased pressure pain threshold (PPT) and both active and passive maximum mouth opening, with a better effect when combined with occlusal splints. The review by Shu et al18 reported that oral exercises (eg, chewing exercise, clenching exercise) improved maximum bite force, while the review by Shimada et al17 provided evidence in support of exercise for reducing pain intensity and improving jaw mobility for TMD myalgia and arthralgia. According to the review by Armijo-Olivo et al,28 active and passive exercises for the jaw, postural exercises and neck exercises yield positive outcomes for TMDs, but are not superior to other conservative treatments. However, it should be noted that several of these reviews included patients with different TMD types (eg, myogenic TMD, arthrogenic TMD, mixed TMD), while others only included patients with TMJ disc displacement without reduction.1718 2831 In addition, previous studies have examined combined approaches of treatment (including exercises), mixed modalities of exercise programmes (eg, mobilisation exercise with RT) or types of exercises that were not clearly defined.1718 2831

To the best of our knowledge, no systematic review has been conducted specifically to investigate the effectiveness of RT alone for the management of TMDs. Therefore, this systematic review aims to synthesise current evidence on the effectiveness of RT on pain and TMJ range of motion in people with TMDs. We expect that our findings will provide valuable insights for clinicians involved in the treatment of patients with TMD. This information is not only pertinent to the general benefits of exercise but also specifically sheds light on the efficacy of RT, offering clinicians a more precise understanding for informed treatment planning.

Methods

This systematic protocol is reported following the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) Protocols32 (for more details see online supplemental additional file 1) and is registered in PROSPERO (registration number: CRD42023476269; registration date: 30 October 2023). The actual systematic review will be conducted and reported according to PRISMA.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

The eligibility criteria are based on the Population, Intervention, Control, Outcomes, and Study design framework suggested by the PRISMA checklist.32

Participants

We will include studies that involve adult participants aged over 18 years with a diagnosis of TMD based on the research diagnostic criteria for TMD or diagnostic criteria for TMD (DC/TMD)33 34 or any trials with a population reporting signs and symptoms of TMD.35

Intervention

Only trials investigating exercises of masticatory muscles, which are described as RT or strengthening exercises, will be included. RT is defined in accordance with PubMed medical subject headings (MESH) as: ‘RT is a type of strength-building exercise programme that requires the body muscle to exert a force against some form of resistance, such as weight, stretch bands, water or immovable objects. Resistance exercise is a combination of static and dynamic contractions involving shortening and lengthening of skeletal muscles’.

Comparison and study design

Any randomised controlled trials (RCTs) or non-randomised studies of interventions examining RT versus comparison groups not including RT will be included. Trials providing only education sessions in addition to the RT programme will be eligible. Any comparison group will be considered (eg, standard care, splints, sham therapy) as long as it does not include RT.

Outcome measures

The primary outcomes are pain intensity and maximal mouth opening (MMO). Only trials investigating pain using methods recommended by the Initiative on Methods, Measurement, and Pain Assessment in Clinical Trials36 will be included. Pain is defined as pain in the TMJ area, masticatory muscles or referred to the associated structures. MMO is measured by the interincisal distance in millimetres during active MMO, as per the DC/TMD physical examination.33

The secondary outcomes will be measures of neuromuscular performance (eg, time until fatigue of the masseter and temporalis muscles on endurance tasks, maximal force generated during biting), PPTs over masticatory muscles and pain-related disability measured with the graded chronic pain scale.33

Timing and setting

All time follow-up assessments will be included, and no restrictions on setting and/or length of intervention and follow-up will be applied.

Exclusion criteria

Any trials involving an RT programme lasting less than 8 weeks will be excluded. Any trials reporting the effects of RT combined with other treatment modalities (eg, manual therapy, mobilisation exercise, splint) besides education will be excluded. If there is uncertainty that the RT intervention is directed only to the masticatory muscles but involves other muscles (eg, neck muscles), the trial will not be considered eligible for the SR. Trials involving participants with rheumatic diseases, neurological diseases, cancer and surgery in the TMJ area will be excluded. Articles with full text not written in English will be excluded.

Information sources

The search strategy will be prepared for each database based on MESH, if available, and relevant text words relating to TMD, TMJ, masticatory muscles, exercise, RT, physical therapy and pain. The search strategy will be conducted from 1 March 2024 to 31 March 2024 via the following electronic databases: MEDLINE (OVID interface), EMBASE (OVID interface), SCOPUS, Web of Science, PubMed and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. Reference lists from each included paper will also be examined. In addition, hand searching will be performed in journals which have a particular interest in topics of exercise and TMD, specifically the Journal of Oral Rehabilitation, Musculoskeletal Science and Practice, Physical Therapy, Clinical Rehabilitation, The Journal of Oral & Facial Pain and Headache, Journal of Applied Oral Science and Journal of Dental Research. We anticipate starting data analysis in 9 months (ie, December 2024).

Search strategy

The first bibliographical search will be run in PubMed, and then the same strategy will be adjusted and used for the other databases. The search will use MeSH terms (when available), Boolean operators and syntax (truncation, wildcards and quotation marks), as well as keywords related to TMD, TMJ, masticatory muscles, RT, exercise, physical therapy and pain. No date limits will be applied. The search strategy for all databases is available in online supplemental additional file 2. If online access is not possible or if references are not available, authors will be contacted.

Study records

Data management

All search results will be managed through EndNote; Endnote Version X8 (Clarivate Analytics) software.

Selection process

Two reviewers (GA/GP) will independently perform the search and evaluate the titles and abstracts of every potentially eligible article using the predefined eligibility criteria.37 The eligibility criteria sequence will be prioritised as follows: (1) participants, (2) study design, (3) type of intervention, (4) outcome measures and (5) absence of exclusion criteria. Reasons of exclusion will be reported according to PRISMA recommendations.32 If a trial cannot be excluded based on title and abstract and is potentially eligible, the full text will be reviewed. When the full text could not be retrieved, we will contact the authors of included studies with a maximum of two attempts with an interval of 1 week. Articles will be included if both reviewers reach a consensus on eligibility criteria after reading the full text. If there are disagreements between reviewers, the two reviewers will discuss and attempt to achieve a consensus. If necessary, a third reviewer (DF) will mediate. The agreement between reviewers and the reasons for exclusion or inclusion of the articles will be reported and presented in a flow diagram as suggested by PRISMA guidelines.32

Data collection process

A bespoke proforma in line with the Cochrane form38 will be prepared and tested to extract data from included trials. Both reviewers (GA and GP) will independently extract the information, and if there are discrepancies, the third reviewer (DF) will be consulted.

Risk of bias in individual studies

The risk of bias in the included articles will be assessed using two distinct tools: (1) the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomised trials (RoB V.2),39 considered the gold standard for RCTs40 and (2) the Cochrane risk of bias in non-randomised studies of interventions for non-randomised trials.41 Two independent reviewers (GA and GP) will evaluate and grade the risk of bias for each study in every domain, adhering to the related Cochrane guidance documents for both tools.39 41 In the event of disagreement, a third reviewer (DF) will be consulted. The level of agreement between reviewers will be assessed using Cohen’s κ.42 43

Data synthesis

Data synthesis will first involve a qualitative synthesis, in which summary tables will be created to show the type of participants, time assessment points, intervention, comparator and outcomes of each study. Second, a quantitative synthesis will be conducted, in which standardised mean difference (SMD) and 95% of the CI will be calculated for comparable measurements of pain and MMO as the primary outcomes of interest and other variables such as PPTs of masticatory muscles and neuromuscular measures. An SMD below 0.5 will be regarded as a minor effect, while an SMD ranging from 0.5 to 0.8 will be categorised as a moderate effect.44 Any SMD greater than 0.8 will be considered a significant effect.44 To obtain a more cautious assessment of the genuine effect size of RT from the selected studies, a random-effects model will be employed.45 The I2 statistical analysis will be used to assess heterogeneity (I2 statistic≥75%).44 In the event that the included trials demonstrate homogeneity for outcomes, comparator and assessment points, a meta-analysis will be carried out. Otherwise, a narrative synthesis will be performed by adhering to the synthesis without meta-analysis guidelines in systematic reviews.46 Data will be summarised according to the outcome measures or, where this is not possible, according to other pertinent variables such as TMD type (TMJ disorders, masticatory muscle disorders and mixed disorders). The tables produced will comprehensively present key features of the included trials, such as the study design, sample size, time assessment points, comparator, RoB V.2 and Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluations (GRADE). Additionally, limitations associated with the methodology of synthesis and categorization employed in this systematic review will be acknowledged.

Additional analyses

To further examine the outcomes, we will conduct additional analyses, including a subgroup analysis if appropriate. The studies will be categorised according to the following variables: (1) time assessment point encompassing immediate post-treatment, short-term (0–1 month), intermediate-term (2–6 months), and long-term follow-up (>6 months); (2) TMD type, which includes TMJ disorders, masticatory muscle disorders, and mixed disorders; (3) type of comparator, such as oral appliance, counselling and standard care; and (4) or acute (history of TMD less than 6 months) or chronic (history of TMD greater than 6 months) TMD. Moreover, we will perform a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the reliability of the results by examining the effects of including and excluding studies with high risk of bias.

Meta-biases

In assessing meta-biases, a search for unpublished studies will be completed, as well as further evaluation of the consistency between available protocols, trial registration and published articles, along with an examination of potential competing interests from different groups of authors. The results of these analyses will be presented in narrative form. If there are at least 10 studies included, funnel plots will be generated to visualise the probability of study bias, which will be assessed through visual inspection.38

Confidence in cumulative evidence

To evaluate the overall strength and quality of the evidence, the GRADE approach will be employed, following the guidelines set forth in the GRADE Handbook.47 This approach comprises five domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias, with the quality of evidence ranging from high to very low.47

Patient and public involvement

Consultations and discussions with patients were used to develop the research question for this study. However, patients will not be involved in the collection and analysis of data in the systematic review.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not needed for this systematic review, since no patient data will be collected. The results will be disseminated through presentations at scientific conferences and publication in a peer-reviewed journal.

Discussion

TMDs are a notable health issue that affects a significant proportion of the population, causing pain, joint sounds and jaw movement limitations.13 It is well known that TMDs can considerably affect quality of life of patients and significantly increase health costs.47 Based on current knowledge, exercise can be effective in reducing pain, improving range of motion and enhancing function for people with TMD and other musculoskeletal conditions.1721 That said, very little is known about the specific effects of RT on pain and function in people with TMD. RT could be a promising intervention since it is known to maintain muscle health and improve neuromuscular function.22 23 48 Additionally, RT can reduce inflammation and may help manage TMD symptoms associated by musculoskeletal overload related to potential bruxism.22 26 27 48

Previous systematic reviews have reported mixed findings regarding the effectiveness of exercise on pain and function in people with TMDs.1718 2831 Some suggest significant pain reduction and jaw mobility improvements, while others found no differences compared with other interventions, such as occlusal splint therapy.1718 2831 Reasons for this include systematic reviews which have included studies involving participants with different types of TMDs, and in some cases, the protocol of exercises was not clearly described or combined with other treatments. Although past evidence has contributed to the knowledge on exercise in TMDs, it has not clearly identified the effectiveness of specific exercises that can be prescribed to individual patients. In order to produce evidence applicable to clinical practice, future TMD research must focus on specific types of exercise, such as RT, in comparable populations of TMD patients (eg, those with myalgia) as will be done in this systematic review.

When interpreting the knowledge arising from the systematic review, we will evaluate possible limitations at both the study level, such as risk of bias relating to randomisation and allocation to treatment groups or missing data, and the review level, such as selection bias due to different inclusion criteria and potential heterogeneity resulting from differences in treatment comparators and follow-up points. Any modifications to this protocol during the study’s execution will be documented in the final manuscript and on PROSPERO.

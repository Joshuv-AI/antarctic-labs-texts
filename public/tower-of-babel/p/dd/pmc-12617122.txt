OA2025. Text mining police narratives: Uncovering abuse types and victim injuries in domestic violence
Authors: A Adily, G Karystianis, T Butler
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn Australia, police record domestic violence (DV) events as both structured data and free-text narratives. These narratives contain detailed information about types of abuse and injuries sustained by victims, but this valuable resource remains untapped in DV surveillance systems. For example, police narratives contain information from DV events involving types of abuse and injuries for which medical attention is not sought and thus absent from existing DV surveillance systems (e.g., punching, bruising). Extracting such data can uncover predictors of more serious DV events with significant injuries or even fatalities. This presentation outlines our exploratory study to extract mentions of abuse types and victim injuries from 492,393 DV event narratives recorded by police in New South Wales between January 2005 and December 2016.MethodsWe utilized text mining techniques to identify mentions of abuse types and victim injuries in police narratives of DV events.ResultsIn analyzing 492,393 DV police narratives, mentions of specific abuse types and victim injuries were identified in 71.3% and 36.1% of narratives, respectively. The most frequent types of abuse recorded were assault (unspecified) (48.8%), emotional/verbal abuse (33.5%), and punching (24.6%). The most common victim injuries identified included bruising (29.0%), cuts/abrasions (28.9%), and red marks (23.7%).ConclusionsThis study represents the first attempt to automatically extract and categorize various forms of abuse and victim injuries from a large corpus of police DV event narratives using text mining. Capturing injuries unreported in medical systems highlights predictors of severe DV events and provides critical insights for improving DV surveillance and prevention efforts.Key messages• The untapped police narratives reveal predictors of severe domestic violence, injuries, or homicide, offering critical insights for understanding and proactive prevention.• Key predictors extracted from police narratives can aid the development of tools to better inform police and health services during real-time responses to domestic violence events.TopicDomestic violence, victim injuries, police narratives

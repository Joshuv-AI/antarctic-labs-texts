Assessment of Health‐Related Quality of Life in Patients Suffering From Chronic Liver Disease in a Tertiary Care Hospital: A Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Bilal Ahmed, Muhammad Husnain Ahmad, Ali Gohar, Shandana Tarique, Sameen Arshad, Abdul Rehman, Maryam Ilyas, Masab Ali, Summaya Zaitoon, Marium Nadeem Khan, Ch Hummd Abbas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsChronic liver disease (CLD) significantly impairs health‐related quality of life (HRQoL) due to its multifaceted impact on physical, psychological, and social well‐being. Despite its prevalence in Pakistan, there is limited data on the specific HRQoL challenges faced by patients with CLD. This study aims to assess HRQoL in CLD patients using the Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ) and identify factors influencing their quality of life.MethodsA cross‐sectional study was conducted in a tertiary care hospital over 6 months, including 331 adult patients diagnosed with CLD. The Urdu version of the CLDQ assessed six HRQoL domains. Data on demographic and clinical variables were analyzed using descriptive statistics, ANOVA (Analysis of Variance), and Spearman's correlation.ResultsThe mean HRQoL score was 65.87 ± 23.55, indicating moderate impairment. Female patients reported significantly lower scores than males (p = 0.001). Fatigue, emotional well‐being, and systemic symptoms were the most affected domains, with 48.3% experiencing fatigue frequently. Patients with longer disease duration had higher HRQoL scores (p = 0.001), while MELD (Model for End‐Stage Liver Disease) scores correlated with disease severity and HRQoL impairment. Psychological symptoms, including depression and anxiety, were prevalent, further diminishing HRQoL. Dietary limitations were the most predictive of disease burden (AUC = 0.872).ConclusionCLD severely affects patients' HRQoL, with notable gender disparities and psychological burdens. Comprehensive, multidisciplinary interventions addressing physical and emotional needs are essential to improve outcomes and quality of life in this population.Keywords: chronic liver disease, chronic liver disease questionnaire, fatigue, health‐related quality of life, hepatitis C, MELD score, multidisciplinary care, nutritional management, psychological well‐being, tertiary care hospital

Introduction

Chronic liver disease (CLD) is characterized by persistent liver dysfunction lasting for 6 months or more and encompasses a spectrum of hepatic conditions, including hepatitis, alcoholic liver disease, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, and cholestatic liver diseases such as primary biliary cirrhosis and primary sclerosing cholangitis. Among these, cirrhosis represents the end stage of liver scarring, which develops due to prolonged and progressive liver injury [1]. Globally, chronic diseases contribute to approximately 60% of deaths, with CLD being responsible for nearly two million deaths annually [2]. Liver disease ranks as a significant public health challenge due to its global prevalence, affecting millions and imposing a substantial burden on healthcare resources [3, 4].

In Pakistan, the prevalence of CLD is particularly alarming due to the high incidence of hepatitis B virus (HBV) and hepatitis C virus (HCV) infections. A study in Tropical Medicine and Health reported Pakistan's HCV prevalence at 11.55% among adults, one of the highest rates globally [5]. This elevated prevalence of HBV and HCV significantly exacerbates the cirrhosis burden in Pakistan. Beyond viral hepatitis, other etiological factors such as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, often linked to metabolic syndrome, and alcoholic liver disease further contribute to the rising global incidence of CLD [6].

The impact of CLD extends beyond physical health, profoundly affecting patients' overall well‐being and quality of life. Symptoms such as fatigue, abdominal discomfort, jaundice, and complications like ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, and variceal bleeding significantly impair health‐related quality of life (HRQoL) [7]. HRQoL assessments offer a holistic perspective, capturing physical, emotional, and social dimensions of patient well‐being, which are critical for tailoring effective therapeutic strategies and optimizing healthcare resource allocation [3].

Recent global investigations have similarly emphasized the impact of chronic liver disease on physical functioning, mental health, and daily life activities, further validating the need for HRQoL assessments in diverse populations [8, 9].

This study underscores the importance of evaluating HRQoL in patients with CLD, as it facilitates the identification of disease‐related challenges and guides interventions aimed at improving patient outcomes. The Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ), a validated tool designed to assess liver disease‐specific HRQoL, will be utilized in this study. This instrument evaluates key domains, including physical functioning, emotional well‐being, fatigue, and social functioning, offering insights into the multidimensional effects of CLD [10].

Moreover, understanding the variations in HRQoL among patients with different types and severities of liver disease is critical. Previous research highlights that HRQoL is disproportionately affected in patients with advanced cirrhosis compared to those with non‐cirrhotic CLD, emphasizing the progressive nature of the disease's impact. By employing standardized tools such as the CLDQ, this study aims to provide actionable insights into the HRQoL disparities in this patient population [4]. Chronic liver disease is not only a significant public health concern due to its high mortality and morbidity but also due to its profound impact on the quality of life of affected individuals. This study seeks to bridge the knowledge gap by systematically evaluating HRQoL in CLD patients at a tertiary care hospital in Pakistan, ultimately contributing to evidence‐based strategies for enhancing patient care and health outcomes.

Methodology

Study Population and Design

This cross‐sectional study was conducted in the Departments of Medicine and Gastroenterology at Tertiary care hospital. The study aimed to assess the health‐related quality of life (HRQoL) in patients diagnosed with chronic liver disease (CLD). Data collection occurred over a 6 month period. Non‐probability convenience sampling was utilized. Convenience sampling was employed due to logistical constraints, limited patient availability during the data collection period, and resource limitations common in tertiary care settings. While this method facilitated timely recruitment, we acknowledge that it may introduce selection bias, potentially affecting the generalizability of our findings. To mitigate this, we included a broad range of patients from both outpatient and inpatient departments and ensured diverse representation based on age, gender, and disease severity. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the institution (Ref no. 345/24).

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria comprised adult patients aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of CLD based on clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, or imaging, who were willing to participate and provided informed consent. Exclusion criteria included individuals under 18 years of age, those with acute liver conditions, cognitive impairments, or serious comorbidities that independently impacted HRQoL, and those unwilling or unable to provide consent.

Study Variables

The independent variable was the presence and severity of CLD. Dependent variables included HRQoL scores measured across six domains: fatigue, emotional function, abdominal symptoms, systemic symptoms, activity, and worry. Potential confounding variables, including age, gender, comorbidities (such as hypertension and diabetes), and disease etiology, were identified based on prior literature and clinical relevance. To minimize their impact, stratified analyses were performed where applicable, and associations were adjusted using appropriate statistical methods, including ANOVA and Spearman's correlation, to account for differences across subgroups. This approach helped ensure that observed relationships between chronic liver disease and HRQoL outcomes were not unduly influenced by these confounders.

Data Collection Tools

The validated Urdu version of the Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ) was employed for data collection. The questionnaire assessed six HRQoL domains and included sections on demographics (age, gender, and relevant clinical characteristics), CLD etiology (e.g., HBV, HCV, NAFLD), disease severity, and clinical history.

Data Collection Procedure

Eligible participants were identified from outpatient and inpatient facilities. The CLDQ was administered through direct interviews or self‐completed forms, depending on patient preference and literacy levels. Confidentiality was ensured throughout the process, and data were securely stored in compliance with ethical guidelines.

Statistical Analysis

Data were initially screened for completeness. Cases with substantial missing responses in key variables were excluded from analysis. No imputation methods were used given the limited extent of missingness. The data were analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 24, USA). Quantitative data were summarized using measures of central tendency (mean, median) and dispersion (standard deviation). Categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages. Bar charts and pie charts were employed for visual representation, while tables summarized numerical data.

ANOVA was performed to evaluate differences in HRQoL scores across patient subgroups based on demographic and clinical characteristics. Spearman's rank correlation coefficient was used to assess associations in non‐parametric data. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) was used to evaluate predictive metrics for specific HRQoL outcomes.

Results

The study included 331 patients with chronic liver disease, predominantly in the age groups of 40–49 (27.2%) and 50‐59 (26.3%), with a mean age of 47.6 ± 12.4 years. Females constituted 51.4% (n = 170/331) of the participants, while males accounted for 48.6% (Figure 1). A majority of the participants (88.5%) were married, and 60.7% reported no formal education. Regarding occupation, 45.6% identified as housewives, followed by 23.9% laborers, and 16.6% employed individuals. Chronic liver disease types included CLD (49.5%) and DCLD (50.2%), with 84.3% diagnosed with hepatitis C as the primary etiology. Most patients (50.5%) had been diagnosed with liver disease for less than 1 year, while 29.6% had lived with the condition for 1–5 years, and 19.9% for over 5 years (Table 1). Disease severity, assessed using MELD scores, revealed that 63.4% had scores under 10, indicating mild disease, while 30.2% and 5.7% had moderate (10–19) and severe (20–29) scores, respectively, as shown in Table 2. Hypertension (24.5%) and diabetes (17.5%) were the most common comorbidities, while 46.2% reported no comorbid conditions. The CLDQ revealed a mean HRQoL score of 65.87 ± 23.55, indicating moderate impairment across domains (Table 3). Female patients reported significantly lower HRQoL scores than males (70.9 ± 22.8 vs. 60.5 ± 23.1, p = 0.001). Scores also varied significantly with the duration of illness; patients with disease lasting more than 5 years had higher scores (74.0 ± 21.3, p = 0.001). Detailed analysis of CLDQ domains highlighted varying degrees of impact on patient well‐being. Over 48.3% of patients reported experiencing fatigue “most of the time,” while 42.9% expressed constant concern about their condition worsening. Similarly, 62.8% worried about never feeling better. Abdominal discomfort was “always” a concern for 4.8% of patients, with 12.4% reporting abdominal bloating at the same frequency. Psychological symptoms were prevalent, with 14.8% of participants feeling “most of the time” depressed. Muscle cramps and reduced strength were common complaints, reported “most of the time” by 33.2% and 46.2% of participants, respectively, as shown in Table 1. The Shapiro–Wilk test confirmed significant deviations from normality for all variables (p = 0.001), indicating the need for non‐parametric statistical analyses (Table 4). Spearman's correlation revealed notable relationships between demographic factors, disease characteristics, and quality of life (QoL) measures (Table 5). Female participants reported higher levels of fatigue (r = 0.159, p = 0.004), anxiety (r = 0.179, p = 0.001), and bodily pain (r = 0.170, p = 0.002). Longer disease duration was associated with increased sleep disturbances, such as daytime drowsiness (r = −0.124, p = 0.024) and difficulty sleeping at night (r = −0.152, p = 0.005). Patients with diffuse liver disease reported more frequent fatigue (r = 0.185, p = 0.001) and muscle cramps (r = 0.241, p = 0.001). ANOVA analysis (Table 6) suggested a near‐significant variation in abdominal bloating across diagnostic groups (F = 2.249, p = 0.064), while symptoms such as fatigue and pain did not show statistically significant differences, indicating a consistent impact of liver disease on QoL across groups. ROC curve analysis identified “dietary limitations” as the most predictive symptom of disease burden (AUC = 0.872), with emotional symptoms like “anxiety” and “unhappiness” also showing moderate predictive value (AUC = 0.702 and 0.681, respectively) as shown in Figure 2. These results highlight the complex and varied impact of chronic liver disease on patients' QoL.

Discussion

This cross‐sectional study evaluated the health‐related quality of life (HRQoL) among patients suffering from chronic liver disease (CLD) at a tertiary care hospital, utilizing the Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ) to assess various domains of well‐being. The findings demonstrate a significant impact of CLD on the overall HRQoL, influenced by demographic factors, disease duration, and severity. The study included 331 patients with a mean age of 47.6 years, highlighting the predominance of CLD in the middle‐aged population. Females comprised 51.4% of the sample, and their HRQoL scores were notably lower than males, consistent with previous literature that emphasizes the greater psychosocial burden experienced by women in chronic illnesses. The majority of participants were married, and a significant proportion lacked formal education, underscoring the need for tailored interventions addressing educational barriers in managing chronic diseases.

The high prevalence of hepatitis C as the primary etiology aligns with regional epidemiological data, indicating its substantial contribution to CLD in Pakistan [2]. The study revealed that disease duration plays a critical role in shaping patients' experiences, with those diagnosed for over 5 years reporting better HRQoL scores. This may reflect adaptive mechanisms or better disease management strategies developed over time. The distribution of disease severity, as assessed by MELD scores, showed a predominance of mild cases, with fewer patients presenting with severe disease. However, even mild CLD cases demonstrated significant HRQoL impairments, highlighting the pervasive impact of the disease irrespective of severity.

The CLDQ scores revealed moderate impairment across domains, with fatigue being the most commonly reported symptom. This finding is consistent with prior studies that identify fatigue as a hallmark symptom in CLD, significantly affecting daily functioning and overall well‐being [3]. Emotional health also emerged as a critical concern, with many patients expressing constant worry about disease progression and their future health. These findings emphasize the intertwined nature of physical and psychological symptoms in chronic illnesses, necessitating comprehensive care approaches.

The analysis of specific domains highlighted varying levels of impact, with abdominal symptoms, psychological well‐being, and systemic symptoms being prominently affected. Over 48% of patients reported frequent fatigue, while concerns about worsening health were expressed by a significant proportion. Abdominal discomfort and bloating were reported, albeit less frequently, indicating their episodic nature in CLD. Psychological distress was evident, with a notable percentage of participants reporting feelings of depression and anxiety. These findings are consistent with studies that underscore the mental health challenges associated with chronic illnesses, further validated by the significant correlations observed between fatigue, anxiety, and gender in this study [11].

Muscle cramps and reduced physical strength were also commonly reported, reflecting the systemic nature of CLD and its impact on physical functioning. These symptoms, often overlooked in clinical practice, warrant attention as they contribute significantly to patients' perceived burden of disease. The correlation analyses provided valuable insights into the relationships between demographic and disease‐specific factors and HRQoL domains. Female gender, longer disease duration, and advanced disease severity were associated with worse outcomes in specific domains, reinforcing the need for personalized management strategies.

The near‐significant variation in abdominal bloating across diagnostic groups suggests potential differences in symptomatology based on disease type, which could inform targeted therapeutic approaches. The ROC curve analysis identified dietary limitations as the most predictive symptom of disease burden, emphasizing the critical role of nutritional management in CLD care. Emotional symptoms such as anxiety and unhappiness also emerged as significant predictors, highlighting the need for integrated mental health support in managing CLD patients.

Comparisons with previous studies reveal consistent patterns in the impact of CLD on HRQoL, with slight variations attributed to cultural, socioeconomic, and healthcare system differences. The predominance of hepatitis C as the primary etiology aligns with regional trends, underscoring the importance of targeted public health interventions to address this preventable cause of CLD [1, 5]. The observed gender differences in HRQoL scores mirror findings from international studies, emphasizing the universal nature of gender disparities in health outcomes [12].

The study's findings have important implications for clinical practice and public health policy. The significant HRQoL impairments observed even in mild CLD cases highlight the need for early intervention and continuous monitoring to address patients' physical and psychological needs. The strong correlation between fatigue and disease characteristics suggests that symptom management should be a cornerstone of CLD care, focusing on improving patients' energy levels and overall functioning.

Our findings are consistent with international studies from Europe and East Asia, which also report fatigue and emotional distress as major contributors to HRQoL decline in CLD patients [8, 9, 13]. These global patterns reinforce the universal burden of CLD and highlight the importance of integrating psychosocial support into clinical management strategies.

The observed impact of disease duration on HRQoL underscores the importance of longitudinal care strategies that evolve with patients' changing needs. Patients with longer disease durations reported better HRQoL scores, indicating potential benefits from sustained disease management and support. These findings highlight the importance of patient education and empowerment in fostering self‐management skills and resilience over time.

The study also underscores the need for integrated care models that address the complex nature of CLD. The significant psychological distress observed among participants calls for incorporating mental health support into routine care, recognizing the bidirectional relationship between physical and mental health in chronic diseases. Nutritional support, as highlighted by the predictive value of dietary limitations, should also be prioritized, with tailored interventions addressing patients' specific dietary needs and restrictions.

From a public health perspective, the findings underscore the importance of preventive strategies to reduce the burden of hepatitis C and other CLD causes. Enhanced screening and early diagnosis programs, particularly in high‐risk populations, are essential to mitigate disease progression and its associated HRQoL impairments. Community‐based interventions focusing on patient education and support can also play a crucial role in addressing the broader determinants of health in CLD patients. Policymakers should prioritize resource allocation to improve healthcare facilities and access for CLD patients, particularly in underprivileged areas. Future research should focus on longitudinal studies to assess the impact of targeted interventions on HRQoL outcomes, thereby contributing to evidence‐based strategies for improving the quality of life in this patient population.

Limitations

The study's limitations, including its cross‐sectional design and reliance on self‐reported measures, should be considered when interpreting the findings. Longitudinal studies are needed to capture the dynamic nature of HRQoL in CLD and identify causal relationships between disease characteristics and patient outcomes. Additionally, the study's focus on a single tertiary care hospital may limit the generalizability of its findings to other settings and populations.

Conclusion

Despite these limitations, the study provides valuable insights into the HRQoL of CLD patients in a resource‐limited setting, highlighting key areas for intervention and future research. The findings call for a comprehensive approach to CLD care, addressing the physical, psychological, and social dimensions of health to improve patients' overall quality of life. By integrating these insights into clinical practice and policy, healthcare providers can better meet the needs of CLD patients and enhance their well‐being.

MENG-11 CASE OF A RARE INTRAPARENCHYMAL RHABDOID MENINGIOMA WITH UNUSUAL RADIOLOGICAL APPEARANCES
Authors: Lilian Garapo, Simbarashe Sanyamandwe, Kazadi Kalango
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRhabdoid meningioma is a rare World Health Organization (WHO) grade III meningioma subtype, known for its aggressive biological behavior, high recurrence rates, and poor prognosis. While its histopathological features are distinct, its imaging characteristics can vary, occasionally leading to diagnostic uncertainty.Case PresentationWe present the case of a 30-year-old right-handed male with a two-year history of progressive headaches, focal seizures involving the right upper limb, and gradually worsening memory impairment. Over time, he developed symptoms consistent with a dominant parietal lobe lesion, mainly constructional apraxia, and Gerstmann syndrome. Additionally, features of temporal lobe dysfunction were evident, including word-finding difficulty, receptive aphasia, episodic memory lapses, and déjà vu phenomena.Radiological FindingsMagnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed an ill-defined, intra-axial mass in the left temporo-parietal region. The lesion displayed atypical imaging characteristics for meningioma: notably absent were contrast enhancement, a dural tail, significant perilesional edema, or bony changes — features commonly associated with conventional meningiomas. Instead, the mass exhibited prominent flow voids, suggesting high vascularity, further complicating the preoperative differential diagnosis.Surgical and Postoperative CourseSurgical exploration identified a highly vascular, gelatinous-to-rubbery mass, partially infiltrative and adherent to both the dura and adjacent brain parenchyma. Histopathological evaluation confirmed the diagnosis of rhabdoid meningioma. Given the tumor’s aggressive nature, the patient underwent adjuvant radiotherapy following subtotal resection. Postoperatively, transient improvement of symptoms but 3 weeks later the patient came back with worsening symptoms and imaging done showed a dramatic increase in the size of the residual tumour.ConclusionThis case highlights the importance of recognizing rhabdoid meningioma as a potential differential diagnosis, especially when clinical features suggest a meningioma, but radiological appearances are atypical. Early surgical intervention and adjuvant therapy remain essential for improving outcomes, although long-term prognosis is guarded due to the tumor’s inherent aggressiveness.

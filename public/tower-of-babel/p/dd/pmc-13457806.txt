Qualitative shear wave elastography in malignant breast lesions: predictive factors associated with false-negative findings and their biological characteristics
Authors: Ai-Jiao Yi, Ying Wang, Zheng-Jun Wang, Yu-Yuan Chen, Xiao-Yan Ou, Zhen-Wen Chen, Bin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundShear wave elastography (SWE) complements B-mode ultrasound (BMUS) for breast lesion characterization. However, malignant breast lesions that exhibit false-negative findings on qualitative SWE lack the expected increase in stiffness, which may contribute to missed diagnosis and underestimation of disease severity. This study aimed to identify the BMUS characteristics and clinicopathologic predictors associated with false-negative findings on qualitative SWE in malignant breast lesions, and to characterize the tumor biological behavior of these false-negative lesions, including axillary lymph node (ALN) status, histologic grade, and molecular subtype.MethodsA total of 770 patients (with 770 malignant breast lesions) were enrolled in the study between March 2019 and August 2024. All patients underwent preoperative BMUS and SWE examinations. A multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify the predictive factors associated with false-negative SWE findings. The biological behaviors of these false-negative lesions were analyzed based on the final surgical pathology results.ResultsAmong the 770 malignant breast lesions, 703 (91.3%) were classified as true-positive findings on SWE, and 67 (8.7%) were classified as false-negative findings on SWE. Multivariate regression analysis revealed that a maximum lesion size <20 mm (95% confidence interval (CI): 3.105–11.520; P<0.001), a circumscribed margin (95% CI: 1.522–4.763; P=0.001), and ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) (95% CI: 4.715–18.057; P<0.001) were significantly associated with false-negative SWE findings. Of the 67 false-negative lesions, 32 (47.8%) were diagnosed as DCIS, and 35 (52.2%) were diagnosed as invasive carcinomas. All the breast cancer patients exhibited a low ALN burden. Moreover, among the 35 invasive carcinomas, 85.7% (30/35) were the luminal A or luminal B subtype.ConclusionsA maximum breast lesion size <20 mm, a circumscribed margin, and DCIS were identified as significant predictors of false-negative SWE findings. False-negative SWE findings were associated with tumors exhibiting low invasive biological potential and lower-risk pathological profiles, including a low ALN burden, a higher proportion of DCIS, and a predominance of Luminal A and Luminal B subtypes.

Introduction

Ultrasound is a widely used and crucial method for detecting and differentiating among breast lesions (1), with characteristics described using the standardized Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) lexicon (2). However, considerable overlap exists in the ultrasound features of benign and malignant lesions. Moreover, while BI-RADS assessment has high sensitivity, its low specificity often results in unnecessary biopsies, which is a major limitation of this approach.

Shear wave elastography (SWE), a tool for quantitatively and qualitatively evaluating tissue stiffness, has been widely used to differentiate between benign and malignant breast lesions, and to improve the specificity of BI-RADS assessment based on B-mode ultrasound (BMUS) (3,4). In previous studies (3,5-8), quantitative SWE has been used to measure elastic modulus values (e.g., the maximum elasticity value), while qualitative SWE has been used to visually classify stiffness patterns using color maps. Both methods have shown good diagnostic performance in the evaluation of breast lesions.

Yoon et al. (9) identified several factors associated with false-negative findings on SWE, including smaller lesion size, greater breast thickness, and increased lesion depth. However, the number of false-negative cases in their study was relatively small, and previous studies have mainly focused on false-negative findings using quantitative SWE.

Quantitative SWE results are influenced by the size and placement of the region of interest (ROI) in the SWE image. Moreover, a single ROI may not adequately reflect the stiffness of the whole breast lesion or the surrounding tissue. Thus, qualitative SWE was selected for this study because of its practicality in routine clinical use: it requires no post-processing, provides real-time visual assessment, and eliminates inter-observer variability related to ROI placement. However, qualitative SWE can still fail to detect some malignant lesions (false-negative findings), which may cause clinical confusion. Thus, this study aimed to identify predictors of false-negative findings and to explore their underlying biological characteristics to improve the safe interpretation of qualitative SWE.

To date, few large-scale studies have examined the relationship between false-negative findings on qualitative SWE and tumor biological behavior. This gap in knowledge has direct clinical consequences, as false-negative findings may lead to false reassurance, potentially resulting in delayed biopsies, missed cancer diagnoses, and adverse patient outcomes.

This study sought to address this gap in the literature by identifying the factors associated with false-negative findings on qualitative SWE and characterizing the biological behavior of these false-negative lesions. Our findings may help clinicians to recognize when qualitative SWE is most likely to fail, and to interpret negative SWE findings with appropriate caution, thereby improving the safety and reliability of SWE in routine breast imaging. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0382/rc).

Methods

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Yueyang Central Hospital (approval No. 2024-005), which waived the requirement of written informed consent for participation due to the retrospective nature of the analysis.

Patients

A total of 770 patients (with 770 malignant breast lesions) attending Yueyang Central Hospital were enrolled in the study between March 2019 and August 2024 (Figure 1). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) histopathological confirmation of breast lesions via surgery within one month after SWE examination; and (II) no history of prior invasive diagnosis and treatment, such as ablation or biopsy, before SWE examination. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) pregnancy or lactation; (II) unsatisfactory SWE image quality; and/or (III) breast lesions treated only with ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy (CNB) or vacuum-assisted excision (VAE).

Ultrasound examinations

BMUS

BMUS was performed using the Aixplorer system (SuperSonic Imaging, Aix-en-Provence, France) with L15-4 and L10-2 transducers. Patients were placed in the supine position with the breast and axilla exposed. Each lesion was assessed as per the BI-RADS lexicon for shape, margin, orientation, echo pattern, posterior features, and calcifications (2). All breast lesions were classified as BI-RADS 3, 4a, 4b, 4c, or 5.

SWE

Following BMUS, SWE was performed using a L10-2 or L15-4 linear array transducer. For each lesion, two orthogonal planes were assessed. Minimal transducer pressure was applied during SWE acquisition. The qualitative SWE color patterns proposed by Lin et al. (10) were used in this study: (I) no finding: homogeneously blue coloration throughout and around the lesion, indicating no detectable stiffness difference; (II) vertical stripes pattern: the presence of a color different from the surrounding background (typically blue) at the margin of or within the lesion, extending vertically in cord-like structures toward the cutaneous side and/or the thoracic wall; (III) spots pattern: visible colored areas above and/or below the lesion; (IV) rim of stiffness pattern: a localized colored area at the lesion margin, creating a continuous closed circle; (V) colored lesion pattern: heterogeneous colored areas within the lesion; (VI) void center pattern: an absence of SWE signal within the lesion, while the rest of the SWE box is correctly filled; (VII) horseshoe pattern: a localized colored area at the lesion margin, forming an open circle. According to the seven-pattern qualitative SWE classification, benign lesions typically present as no finding, or vertical stripes or spots patterns, whereas malignant lesions more commonly exhibit rim of stiffness, horseshoe, void center, or colored lesion patterns.

Imaging analysis

Two radiologists (with 10 and 5 years of experience in breast ultrasound and SWE, respectively) independently reviewed all imaging data, blinded to each other’s interpretations and to the final pathological results. In cases of controversial findings, a third, more experienced radiologist was consulted to achieve consensus.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 23.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and MedCalc version 19.0 (MedCalc Software, Mariakerke, Belgium). Continuous variables were compared using the independent t-test, while categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test in the univariate analysis. Variables with a P value <0.10 in the univariate analysis were considered candidates for the multivariate model. A multivariate logistic regression analysis was then performed using a forward stepwise selection method with an entry threshold of P<0.10 and a removal threshold of P>0.05. Multicollinearity among independent variables was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with VIF >5 considered indicative of significant collinearity. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics of all malignant breast lesions

Of the 770 malignant breast lesions examined, 703 (91.3%) were classified as true-positive findings on SWE, and 67 (8.7%) as false-negative findings on SWE (Table 1). The general characteristics of all malignant breast lesions are summarized in Table 2. The maximum lesion size, depth, margin, echo pattern, lesion type on BMUS, and histological type differed significantly between the true-positive findings and false-negative findings. No significant differences were observed in age, shape, orientation, lesion composition, or calcifications between the true-positive findings and false-negative findings. In the multivariate regression analysis, a maximum size (<20 mm,), a circumscribed margin, and ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) were significantly associated with false-negative findings on SWE (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, a maximum size <20 mm, a circumscribed margin, and DCIS were found to be significant predictors of false-negative SWE findings. Moreover, 47.8% (32/67) of the false-negative lesions on SWE were DCIS, while 44.8% (30/67) of the false-negative findings on SWE were luminal A or luminal B subtype invasive carcinomas. All false-negative lesions exhibited a low ALN burden after surgery, which may indicate lower invasive potential and lower-risk pathological features.

Under the seven-pattern qualitative SWE classification (10) for differentiating between benign and malignant breast lesions, the majority of the malignant lesions presented with rim of stiffness, horseshoe, void center, or colored lesion patterns. However, a small number of the malignant lesions exhibited benign-like patterns, including no finding, or vertical stripes or spots patterns. In this study, 8.70% (67/770) of the malignant breast lesions showed benign SWE patterns. These false-negative findings highlight the potential limitations of qualitative SWE. Thus, the predictors of false-negative SWE findings urgently need to be identified to prevent misdiagnoses. Further, understanding the biological behavior of these false-negative lesions on SWE may aid in disease management and individualized treatment.

Applying this framework to our study, we observed several notable characteristics of false-negative SWE findings. Regarding lesion size and histologic type, our findings align with previous reports that false-negative SWE findings are associated with smaller lesion size and DCIS (3,9,11-13). Park et al. (12) demonstrated that false-negative SWE findings in malignant non-mass lesions were significantly associated with smaller lesion size and DCIS, while Yoon et al. (9) and Vinnicombe et al. (13) reported that most false-negative findings were small (<20 mm) but did not specifically examine the association with invasive histology.

In our study, only 25.4% (17/67) of the false-negative lesions were ≥20 mm, of which 70.6% (12/17) were DCIS. Conversely, among the false-negative lesions <20 mm, 48.0% (24/50) were sub-centimeter (≤10 mm), and the majority of these (79.2%, 19/24) were invasive carcinomas. Thus, we found that among the false-negative lesions, the larger lesions (≥20 mm) were predominantly DCIS, whereas the smaller sub-centimeter lesions were more likely to be invasive carcinomas. From a clinical perspective, these results suggest that lesion size should be considered when interpreting false-negative SWE findings. For small lesions (≤10 mm), a negative SWE finding does not exclude invasive carcinoma, and biopsy remains indicated. For large lesions (≥20 mm), a negative SWE finding may indicate DCIS.

Previous studies had examined the relationship between circumscribed margins and false-negative SWE findings. Kim et al. (14) reported that among malignant breast lesions, those with false-negative SWE findings were significantly more likely to present with circumscribed margins and abrupt lesion boundaries compared to those with true-positive findings. In the present study, a circumscribed margin was also identified as a predictor of false-negative SWE findings, which is consistent with previous studies.

In this study, false-negative SWE findings with circumscribed margins were mainly DCIS and high-grade invasive carcinomas. The soft consistency of DCIS may be explained by its non-invasive, slowly progressive nature. Conversely, the soft appearance of high-grade invasive carcinomas is likely attributable to a lack of desmoplastic stromal reaction (i.e., no significant collagen fiber proliferation), with tumor cells proliferating in a solid or medullary patterns.

Pathologically, the false-negative lesions comprised 47.8% (32/67) DCIS and 52.2% (35/67) invasive carcinomas. Previous studies (15,16) have examined the relationship between pathological subtype and false-negative SWE findings. Golatta et al. (15) reported that DCIS and papillary carcinomas exhibited lower elastography values than invasive carcinomas of no special type, making them more prone to false-negative findings on elastography. However, Xu et al. (16) found that the pathological subtypes of missed cancers did not fully align with these findings (15). In the present study, we found that the DCIS and invasive cancers of no special type were the main pathological subtypes in the false-negative cases, which is not entirely consistent with previous findings. Given these inconsistencies, the findings need to be verified in large, multi-center studies.

According to the final postoperative pathological results, all patients demonstrated a low ALN burden. Specifically, 94% (63/67) of the breast cancer patients tested negative for ALN metastasis, while the other patients had metastases in one or two ALNs. This finding is consistent with a previous study (17), which reported a significant association between malignant lesions exhibiting no finding, or vertical stripes or spot patterns on SWE and limited nodal burden. Moreover, grade 2 invasive carcinoma was most common among the false-negative SWE findings (26/35, 74.3%). The predominant molecular subtypes were luminal A and luminal B, accounting for 85.7% (30/35). Thus, false-negative SWE findings may be associated with lower invasive biological behavior, and may indicate a low ALN burden and other lower-risk pathological features.

This study had several limitations. First, this was a retrospective, single-center study, which may introduce inherent selection bias. Second, three radiologists were involved in the examination and evaluation of the SWE images of the breast lesions, which may have affected the final results. Third, to evaluate biological behavior and ALN status, the breast lesions that underwent only ultrasound-guided CNB or VAE were excluded from this study to ensure a robust reference standard; however, this approach may have introduced selection bias. Therefore, the observed associations between false-negative SWE findings and lower-risk pathological features may not be fully generalizable to the broader spectrum of breast lesions encountered in routine clinical practice. Future prospective studies incorporating CNB- or VAE-diagnosed lesions, together with long-term imaging follow-up as a surrogate reference standard, are needed to validate our findings in more representative, real-world cohorts. Fourth, while our findings suggest that false-negative SWE findings are associated with favorable pathological characteristics, prognosis cannot be directly inferred from these cross-sectional data. Future studies with longitudinal follow-up and survival outcome data are needed to determine whether these lesions are indeed associated with improved prognosis.

Conclusions

A maximum breast lesion size <20 mm, a circumscribed margin, and DCIS were identified as predictors of false-negative findings on qualitative SWE. False-negative SWE findings appear to reflect lower invasive biological potential, and are associated with lower-risk pathological profiles, characterized by a low ALN burden, a higher proportion of DCIS, and a predominance of luminal A and luminal B subtypes.

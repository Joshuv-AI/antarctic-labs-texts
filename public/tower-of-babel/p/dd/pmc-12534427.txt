TET2 repression contributes to EGFR TKI resistance in EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer through regulating AXIN2 methylation
Authors: Hangjie Ying, Yamei Chen, Wei Lin, Yun Fan, Ming Chen, Ying Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Through targeted next-generation sequencing of 83 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients with first-generation epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR TKI) resistance, we detected 11% TET2 mutations in the T790M-negative subgroup. To explore the molecular mechanism of TET2 in EGFR TKI resistance, reduced representation bisulfite sequencing (RRBS) was adopted to analyze the global genomic methylation profiles and detect the differentially methylated genes in the TET2-knockdown (KD) PC9 and control PC9 cell lines, following bioinformatics analysis of gene ontology (GO) functions and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) signaling to screen for genes associated with drug resistance. TET2 KD attenuated gefitinib-induced apoptosis and decreased the sensitivity of EGFR-mutant lung cancer cells to gefitinib. Forty-three drug resistance genes with hypermethylated promoter regions were identified via RRBS and bioinformatic analysis in PC9TET2 KD cells. Then, 10 candidate genes were screened for further validation. RT‒PCR demonstrated that the expression of AXIN2 and CSK was significantly lower in PC9TET2 KD cells than in control cells. Furthermore, AXIN2 KD attenuated gefitinib-induced apoptosis and decreased the sensitivity of PC9 cells to gefitinib. Importantly, we found that the demethylation drug decitabine (DCA) could reverse gefitinib resistance in PC9TET2 KD cells and mouse models. These results indicate that the methylation of AXIN2 induced by TET2 repression is a novel resistance mechanism of EGFR TKIs in EGFR-mutant NSCLC. Demethylation drugs have the potential to overcome EGFR TKI resistance induced by loss-of-function TET2 mutations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20263-8.

Introduction

The identification of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-sensitizing mutations in patients with lung adenocarcinoma has transformed the management of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This subset of patients has been found to have profound sensitivity to single-agent EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)1–3. Despite their proven efficacy, acquired resistance to these drugs inevitably develops. For first- and second-generation EGFR TKIs, resistance is most commonly due to the acquisition of the EGFR T790M mutation (approximately 50–70% of cases), and the third-generation EGFR TKI osimertinib is approved for clinical use based on its striking efficacy in overcoming typical EGFR TKI resistance4,5. These data highlight the importance of molecular testing throughout treatment to understand tumor evolution and optimize subsequent therapies. Recently, osimertinib has been shown to prolong overall survival compared with first-generation TKIs in a first-line treatment setting6. However, multiple factors can influence first-line treatment decisions, including subsequent therapy options, cost-effective considerations, specific mutation sites, safety profiles and racial differences, all of which should be considered in long-term treatment plans7–10. According to the FLAURA results, first-line osimertinib is now considered the preferred option for patients with sensitizing EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC. However, first- and second-generation EGFR TKIs remain the recommended first-line therapy for these patients. Since the mechanisms responsible for most patients with non-T790M resistance to first- and second-generation TKIs are still unknown, further research is needed to optimize outcomes in this cohort11.

In the era of personalized medicine, next-generation sequencing (NGS) methods are under being developed to identify patients suitable for molecular targeted therapy to overcome drug resistance by acquiring cancer genomic information comprehensively and efficiently12. In our previous study, we performed targeted NGS with a gene Panel covering 416 cancer-related genes to profile the genetic characteristics of 83 NSCLC patients after they developed systematic progression to first-generation EGFR TKI treatments, including erlotinib, gefitinib and icotinib. Importantly, among the cohort of T790M-negative patients, in addition to other known resistance mechanisms, we identified ten-eleven translocation (TET) oncogene family member 2 (TET2) mutations (3 with missense mutations, 2 with truncating mutations, and 1 with an in-frame indel mutation) in 11% of patients, with a sample of 1 formalin-immobilized paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sample, 2 pleural effusion samples and 3 plasma samples (Supplementary Table 1)13. Moreover, the function of TET2 mutations in the development of acquired resistance to EGFR TKIs is unknown.

TET2 is a tumor suppressor gene on chromosome 4q24. TET2 enzymatically converts 5-methylcytosine (5-mC) to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5-hmC), which plays a nonredundant, critical role in maintaining DNA demethylation and activity. Failure to maintain epigenetic marks can result in changes in the expression patterns of oncogenes and tumor suppressors, which leads to the development and progression of malignancies. TET2 mutations have been identified in multiple human cancers, including leukemia, breast cancer, colorectal cancer, melanoma, and lung cancer. Loss-of-function mutations of TET2 lead to altered DNA methylation patterns and confer a competitive advantage to hematopoietic stem cells that, over time, often results in malignant transformation14–16. Abnormal DNA methylation of multiple genes is a hallmark of cancer, which results in their inappropriate transcriptional silencing and contributes to the loss of checkpoints and other functions in malignancies17. DNA methylation has been proposed as one of the causes of its inactivation. Failure to demethylate and consequently reactivate these methylation-silenced tumor suppressors is a contributor to therapeutic resistance18,19. Since DNA methylation is reversible, drugs such as decitabine and 5′-azacytidine are used to treat a variety of tumors, making them therapeutic targets20.

On the basis of these findings, we hypothesized that loss-of-function TET2 mutations may induce EGFR TKI resistance by regulating the DNA methylation of relevant genes. In this study, we aimed to explore the role and possible mechanisms of TET2 suppression in resistance to the first-generation EGFR TKI gefitinib in EGFR-mutant lung cancer cell lines. We reported that TET2 inactivation contributes to gefitinib resistance in EGFR-mutant NSCLC through the regulation of AXIN2 promoter methylation. In addition, we found that decitabine reversed gefitinib resistance in TET2-knockdown (KD) EGFR-mutant lung cancer cells. Our findings establish a previously unrecognized functional interplay between TET2 and AXIN2, which is critical for EGFR TKI resistance.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and transfection

The human PC9 and H1650 cell line was obtained from Experimental Research Center, Zhejiang Cancer Hospital. Human PC9 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (HyClone) supplemented with 10% (vol/vol) fetal bovine serum (Gibco), and H1650 cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium (HyClone) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C. To construct TET2 stable knockdown (TET2 KD), CSK stable knockdown (CSK KD), AXIN2 stable knockdown (AXIN2 KD), AXIN2 stable overexpression (AXIN2 OE), and TET2 rescue cell lines (TET2 KD + TET2), the constructed lentivirus was transferred into PC9 and H1650 cells, and the number of infections was 100. After 7 days of incubation with puromycin (P8032; Sigma Aldrich, MO) at a concentration of 2 µg/mL, single cells were sorted into 96-well plates to obtain clones. The lentiviral vector with the knockdown target was GV392-CMV-hSpCas9-P2A-Puro (CONT KD), and the lentiviral vector with the overexpression target was GV358-Ubi-MCS-3Flag-SV40-EGFP-IRES-Puro. The cell lines were validated via western blotting and real-time PCR and routinely checked for mycoplasma contamination via the MycoAlert Mycoplasma Detection Kit. Three different sgRNAs were designed for different sites of the AXIN2 and CSK proteins, which resulted in different frameshift mutation efficiencies and KD efficiencies. Finally, the cell line with the highest knockdown efficiency was selected for subsequent study. The sgRNA sequences for all the knockdown and control cell lines were as follows:

Cell proliferation and apoptosis assays

The growth rates of the cells subjected to the appropriate treatments were determined via a CCK-8 assay (KGA317, KeyGen). Briefly, cells were seeded into 96-well plates at 1 × 104 per well in a final volume of 100 µL for 24 h and then incubated with 1 µM gefitinib for 48 h. Then, 10 µL of CCK-8 was added to each well and incubated for 1 h. The absorbance was measured at 490 nm via a spectrophotometer (Multiskan skyhigh, Thermo).

Apoptotic cells were stained with an Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis detection kit (KGA108, KeyGen) and analyzed via flow cytometry. The combination of Annexin V-FITC and PI allows for the distinction between early apoptotic cells (Annexin V-FITC positive) and late apoptotic cells (Annexin V-FITC and PI positive). Quantitative analysis involves the addition of early apoptotic cells and late apoptotic cells.

Western blotting analysis

Whole-cell extracts were extracted in RIPA lysis buffer (P0013B, Beyotime Biotechnology) supplemented with PMSF (ST506, Beyotime Biotechnology) after appropriate treatment. The protein concentration was measured with a BCA Protein Assay Kit (KGP903, KeyGen), and samples of 20–35 µg total protein were subsequently eluted with 5×SDS loading buffer (P0015, Beyotime Biotechnology) and resolved via SDS–PAGE. Proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes (IPVH00010, Merck Millipore) and further incubated with specific antibodies. The Immobilon Western Chemiluminescent HRP Substrate (WBKLS0100, Merck Millipore) was used for protein detection. The following antibodies were used for western blotting: goat anti-rabbit IgG (31460, Thermo Scientific), m-IgGκ BP-HRP (sc-516102, Santa Cruz), anti-p44/42 MAPK (ERK1/2) (137F5) (#4348, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-EGFR (C7489) (#2646, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-TET2 (ab94580, Abcam), anti-CSK (17720-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-AXIN2 (20540-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-β-actin (A2228, Sigma), anti-ERK1 (phospho T202) + ERK2 (phospho T185) (ab201015, Abcam), anti-EGFR (phospho Y1173) (ab32578, Abcam), and anti-GAPDH (HC301, TransGen). All primary antibodies were incubated at 4 °C overnight.

Reduced representation bisulfite sequencing (RRBS)

The cells were harvested and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for 30 min and sent to Genechem Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), to assess the genomic DNA methylation status. The experimental protocol steps were as follows: (1) 500 ng of genomic DNA from each group was digested overnight at 37 ℃ using the restriction enzyme MSP1 (Sigma Aldrich) to enrich the CpG islands and other CpG methylation-intensive areas. (2) The digested product was purified via an Axygen PCR Cleaner Kit. (3) Repair was ended, and A was added to the 3′-terminal product. (4) The product was repurified via an Axygen PCR Cleaner Kit (Corning). (5) A methylation linker was added. (6) Agarose electrophoresis and DNA fragments with sizes ranging from 160 to 400 bp were extracted via a GeneJE T Gel Extraction Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). (7) Bisulfite treatment was conducted via an EZ DNA Methylation-Gold Kit (Zymo Research, USA). (8) A bisulfite-converted library with a conversion rate > 98% was subjected to PCR amplification, magnetic bead purification and Qubit measurement. (9) Single-read sequencing was conducted via an Illumina HiSeq.

The quality of the sequencing data was verified via the FastQC v.0.11.7 package (www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/). Adapter removal and quality trimming were performed via Cutadapt v1.9.1 (https://cutadapt.readthedocs.io/en/stable/). The reads were aligned to a reference genome (UCSC hg19) via Bismark v.0.19.0 (http://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/bismark/). The methylation level of each cytosine was calculated via the R package MethylKit (Version 1.12.0) (https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/methylKit.html) (PMID: 23034086).

DNA isolation from cell lines

DNA samples were prepared from different treatment groups of PC9 cells via a genomic DNA extraction kit (Saint-bio, China). The concentrations of all the DNA samples were measured with a spectrophotometer.

5-hmC immunoassay

Two hundred nanograms of DNA were mixed with 80 µL of DNA binding solution and then added to the microplates. A strip plate was covered with a plate seal and incubated at 37 ℃ for 90 min. After washing with wash buffer three times, 50 µL of anti-5-hydroxymethylcytosine polyclonal antibody (Epicentek, New York, USA) was added to each well and it was then covered and incubated at room temperature for 60 min. After washing three times, 50 µL of biotin-conjugated anti-rabbit antibody (Pierce) was added to each well and it was then covered and incubated at room temperature for 30 min. After washing with wash buffer four times, the signal-enhancing solution containing the avidin-peroxidase complex was added and incubated for 30 min. After washing four times, TMB was added and incubated at room temperature for 10 min in the dark, and then 100 µL of 1 M HCl per well was added to stop the enzyme reaction. The absorbance was read on a microplate reader (Thermo Varioskan flash, MA, USA) at 450 nm.

Bisulfite amplicon sequencing (BSAS)

The cells were harvested and immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen. The samples were then sent to Genechem Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) to assess the DNA methylation status. The experimental protocol steps were as follows: Genomic DNA was extracted and quantified by spectrophotometry (A260/280 ratio ≥ 1.8), with integrity verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. DNA (500 ng) was bisulfite-converted using the MethylSEQr™ Bisulfite Conversion Kit (Applied Biosystems), with conversion efficiency (> 99%) validated using unmethylated λ-DNA (Promega, Cat# D1521). Target regions containing CpG sites were amplified using methylation-specific primers (designed by MethPrimer, avoiding CpG overlaps in primer binding sites) under optimized PCR conditions. Purified amplicons were sequenced on Illumina platforms (PE150).

Nude mouse xenograft model

Four-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were provided by Shanghai Xipuer Bikai Experimental Animal Co., Ltd. (production license: SCXK 2013–0016). After a one-week acclimation period, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane, and lung adenocarcinoma subcutaneous xenograft models were established by injecting 1 × 106 PC9 cells (either CONT KD or TET2 KD) into the right dorsal flank of each nude mouse (n = 5 per group).When the average tumor volume reached 400 mm3, the mice were divided into six groups: the PC9CONT KD+PBS group; PC9CONT KD+gefitinib group; PC9CONT KD+gefitinib + DCA group; PC9TET2 KD+PBS group; PC9TET2 KD+gefitinib group; and PC9TET2 KD+gefitinib + DCA group. Gefitinib (20 mg/kg/day) was given intragastrically for 5 days a week, and DCA (1.0 mg/kg/day) was given intraperitoneally for 5 days in the first week, followed by 3 injections each week. The mice were weighed and tumor size was measured with a digital caliper every 3 days from treatment initiation (Day 21) until endpoint (Day 32). Any weight loss exceeding 15% of baseline would trigger euthanasia per IACUC guidelines, but no such events occurred. The tumor volume was calculated according to the formula V = (a × b2)/2, where a and b are the maximal and minimal diameters in millimeters, respectively. Twelve days later, the mice were killed by cervical dislocation, and the tumors were weighed immediately.

The protocol was approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Zhejiang Cancer Hospital, China (2022-07-074). All procedures were performed in compliance with the relevant guidelines and regulations, and the reporting of all methods adheres to the ARRIVE Guidelines (available at ARRIVE Guidelines).

Statistical analyses

The data are reported as the means ± SDs of three independent experiments unless otherwise indicated, and Student’s t test was used for all the statistical analyses with GraphPad Prism 5 software. Differences between groups were considered significant when the P value was less than 0.05.

Results

Suppression of TET2-induced resistance to gefitinib in EGFR-mutant NSCLC cells

To confirm the role of TET2 suppression in resistance to EGFR TKIs, the effects of TET2 KD on cell proliferation and apoptosis were investigated. Stable cell lines of TET2-KD PC9 cells (PC9TET2 KD) and H1650 cells (H1650TET2 KD) were generated via RNAi and lentiviral vector transfection technologies, and the knockdown efficiencies were evaluated via western blotting (Fig. 1A, B).

An Annexin V/PI assay was used to detect cell apoptosis in different groups after treatment with gefitinib (1 µM) for 72 h. In the apoptosis analysis, 1 µM gefitinib treatment significantly increased apoptosis in PC9 and H1650 cells; however, TET2 KD significantly attenuated gefitinib-induced apoptosis (Fig. 1C-F). Stable cell lines of TET2-overexpressing PC9 cells (PC9TET2 OE) and H1650 cells (H1650TET2 OE) were generated via lentiviral vector transfection, and the overexpression efficiencies were evaluated via western blotting (Fig. 1G, H). A CCK-8 assay was used to evaluate the cell proliferation of different groups treated with various doses of gefitinib (0–10.0 µM). KD of TET2 in PC9 and H1650 cells significantly decreased gefitinib-induced inhibition of cell proliferation. Conversely, rescue of TET2 in TET2-KD PC9 cells (PC9TET2 KD+TET2) and H1650 cells (H1650TET2 KD+TET2) increased the response to gefitinib (Fig. 1I-J). These data indicate that TET2 plays an essential role in regulating the sensitivity of EGFR-mutant NSCLC cells to first-generation EGFR TKIs.

While analyzing phospho-EGFR and downstream ERK phosphorylation, we observed increases in phospho-EGFR and phospho-ERK in TET2-KD cells compared with those in control cells. In PC9TET2 KD cells, complete deactivation of EGFR was achieved with 10 µM gefitinib, whereas in PC9CONT KD cells, 0.1 µM gefitinib was sufficient to fully dephosphorylate the receptor. Conversely, rescue of TET2 in TET2-KD PC9 cells (PC9TET2 KD+TET2) decreased the phospho-EGFR and phospho-ERK levels (Fig. 1K). Thus, these in vitro findings confirmed our hypothesis, demonstrating that suppression of TET2 plays a role in first-generation EGFR TKI resistance.

Changes in DNA methylation in TET2-KD PC9 cells

The critical role of TET2 in maintaining DNA demethylation and the decreased expression of TET2 in multiple human cancers suggests that TET2 has a nonredundant role in regulating DNA methylation for the maintenance of normal cellular function16,21, suggesting that TET2 suppression may induce EGFR TKI resistance by increasing the DNA methylation of relevant genes. To test this idea, we compared the global DNA methylation profiles of the PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cell lines via RRBS.

The representative data for the quality check of one of the PC9CONT KD cell lines are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1. The basic information, including the original sequencing reads, the length of the reads, the number of all bases, and the number of clean reads after processing, and the comparison information are shown in Supplementary Table 2. From all eight cell lines (four PC9TET2 KD cell lines and four PC9CONT KD cell lines) used for sequencing, 8.9 to 22 million unique usable reads (quality filtered and aligned to the human genome) were successfully generated for DNA methylation analyses. Representative data for DNA methylation from one of the PC9TET2 KD cell lines and one of the PC9CONT KD cell lines are presented in Supplementary Fig. 2.

After the quality check, the R methyl Kit package was used to analyze the differential methylation status and functional annotation, which included coverage of different CpGs, distribution of differentially methylated CpGs, distribution of CpGs in different characteristic regions, and differences in CpG methylation between different samples and differential methylation regions (DMRs). Annotation of different feature areas of CpG revealed that the distribution of CpG in the genome was similar in PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cells (Fig. 2A). The number of hyper- and hypomethylation events per chromosome, as a percentage of the sites with the minimum coverage and differential, is shown in Fig. 2B (hyper- or hypo-DMRs were defined as those with a ≥ 5% change in methylation and a p value < 0.05; PC9TET2 KD vs. PC9CONT KD). The complete lists for CpG sites with differences in PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cells are shown in Supplementary Table 3; the complete lists for DMRs between PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cells are shown in Supplementary Table 4.

Ten candidate hypermethylated genes were identified by bioinformatics analysis in TET2-KD PC9 cells

To select differentially methylated genes, a moderately stringent cutoff of differentially methylated CpGs with at least a 5% difference and p value < 0.05 was used, allowing for an acceptable balance between false discovery rates and false-negative rates22,23. When a region is located in the promoter region of a coding gene, the expression level of the gene in different groups may be affected by methylation, resulting in differences. In the end, we identified 710 DMRs with potential biological significance, which are located in the promoter regions of 695 different genes.

Functional clustering analysis, an algorithm capable of clustering similar annotations across several often-used annotation types or within different levels of annotation terms to investigate gene ontology (GO) term enrichment and signaling pathways (KEGG signaling enrichment and annotation), is the most common method to explore the functional gene categories in the database that are significantly related to the research. Since this study focused on the role and mechanisms of TET2 suppression in EGFR TKI resistance, traditional enrichment analysis methods may miss many genes located in key resistance pathways, the annotation method was adapted to analyze the differentially methylated genes in drug resistance pathways between PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cells. The gene set download file provided by MSigDB (The Molecular Signatures Database) was used for the annotations. All the canonical pathways listed in C2 were used for pathway annotation.

Annotations of the GO terms and signaling pathways for each differential gene are shown in Supplementary Tables 5 and Supplementary Table 6. Then, 83 differentially methylated genes (promoters) in drug resistance pathways were screened for further analysis (Supplementary Table 7). As mentioned above, decreased TET2 expression may induce EGFR TKI resistance by increasing the DNA methylation of relevant genes. There were 43 genes with hypermethylated promoter regions in PC9TET2 KD cells. On the basis of the background of this study, to explore the mechanisms of EGFR TKI resistance, we reviewed the function and related literature of each gene. Finally, we selected 10 candidate genes related to tumor growth, proliferation, invasion, or drug resistance for further validation: AXIN2, PIDD1, NR4A1, CSK, SHC1, GHR, SOX17, POU4F2, SPI1, and WNT4 (Table 1).

Validation of representative genes and their roles in gefitinib resistance in PC9 cells

Aberrant DNA methylation at promoters critically affects gene expression and is strongly associated with transcriptional repression. We assumed that expression changes associated with hypermethylation in gefitinib-resistant PC9TET2 KD cells compared with PC9CONT KD cells would likely represent suppression events. We then examined the expression of the ten candidate genes in PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cells via RT‒PCR, and the results were consistent with the RRBS data. As shown in Table 2, the expression of the ten genes in the PC9TET2 KD cells was lower than that in the PC9CONT KD cells, except for POU4F2. Among them, AXIN2 and CSK were significantly differentially expressed between the two groups. (Fig. 3).

To determine whether AXIN2/CSK repression induces resistance to EGFR TKIs, RNAi technology was used to generate AXIN2-KD PC9 cells (PC9 AXIN2 KD) and CSK-KD PC9 cells (PC9 CSK KD). western blotting were used to validate the knockdown efficiency of proteins (Fig. 4A, B). Importantly, CSK depletion did not alter AXIN2 expression (Supplementary Fig. 3), ruling out compensatory mechanisms. We found that AXIN2-KD but not CSK-KD in PC9 cells significantly decreased the gefitinib-induced inhibition of cell proliferation (Fig. 4C, D). Decitabine (DCA) reversed gefitinib resistance in TET2-KD EGFR-mutant NSCLC cells

If TET2 suppression contributes to resistance to EGFR TKIs through AXIN2 hypermethylation, then resistance should be reversed by treatment with DCA, a DNA methyltransferase inhibitor. We then performed BSAS on PC9TET2 KD cells treated with and without DCA. The results showed that the methylation level of AXIN2 in the PC9TET2 KD cells treated with DCA is significantly lower than that in the PC9TET2 KD cells without DCA treatment (Fig. 5A). Specifically, CpG sites 28, 51, 75, 80, 82, 99, 103, 112, 161, 194, and 231 show statistically significant differences between the two groups (Fig. 5B).We then performed an in vitro proliferation assay to test whether DCA reverses gefitinib resistance in PC9TET2 KD cells. Inhibition of DNA methyltransferase led to a marked decrease in cell proliferation when combined with gefitinib, as determined by the CCK-8 assay (Fig. 5C). Compared with the control group, after 72 h of gefitinib treatment, the loss of TET2 resulted in a decrease in the rate of apoptosis, which was significantly reversed by DCA treatment (Fig. 5D, E).

Decitabine enhances the sensitivity of PC9TET2 KD cells to gefitinib in vivo

To study the response of PC9CONT KD and PC9TET2 KD tumor-bearing mice to gefitinib and the combination of gefitinib plus DCA, when the average tumor volume reached 400 mm3, 20 mg/kg gefitinib or PBS was given orally for 5 days a week, and 1.0 mg/kg DCA was given intraperitoneally for 5 days in the first week, followed by 3 injections a week (Fig. 6A). TET2 deficiency resulted in increased resistance to gefitinib. However, tumor volume (Fig. 6B, C) and tumor weight (Fig. 6B, D) were significantly lower in gefitinib + DCA-treated PC9 TET2 KD mice than in PC9 TET2 KD mice treated with gefitinib alone. These results suggest that the inhibition of DNA methyltransferase can reverse the gefitinib resistance induced by TET2 repression in vivo. Body weight monitoring showed no significant differences between treatment groups (p > 0.05), confirming the absence of systemic toxicity from the gefitinib-DCA combination (Supplementary Fig. 4).

Discussion

First-generation EGFR TKI resistance occurs in approximately 80%−85% of cases and can occur through secondary mutations in EGFR, downstream activation, bypass activation, and histological transformation24. In our previous research, we found that 11% of TET2 mutations were in the T790M-negative subgroup via a targeted NGS assay of 83 NSCLC patients with first-generation EGFR-TKI resistance, including three patients with missense mutations, 2 with truncation mutations, and 1 with an insertion frameshift mutation13. On the basis of these findings, we hypothesized that TET2 downregulation caused by TET2 gene mutation may play an important role in first-generation EGFR TKI resistance in EGFR-sensitive mutated NSCLC. These clinical observations prompted us to hypothesize that TET2 loss-of-function contributes to EGFR TKI resistance in EGFR-mutant NSCLC. Finally, we verified the role of TET2 repression in drug resistance, which encourages us to further explore the role of specific TET2 mutations in drug resistance in future studies.

To confirm the role of TET2 suppression in resistance to EGFR TKIs, we first generated stable cell lines of TET2-KD PC9 cells (PC9TET2 KD) and H1650 cells (H1650 TET2 KD). The effects of TET2 KD on cell proliferation and apoptosis were investigated via a CCK-8 assay and an Annexin V/PI assay. The results showed that TET2 KD significantly attenuated gefitinib-induced apoptosis in PC9 and H1650 cells. KD of TET2 in PC9 cells and H1650 cells significantly decreased gefitinib-induced inhibition of cell proliferation. Conversely, rescue of TET2 in TET2-KD PC9 cells (PC9TET2 KD+TET2) and H1650 cells (H1650TET2 KD+TET2) increased the response to gefitinib. Furthermore, we observed an increase in phospho-EGFR and phospho-ERK in PC9TET2 KD cells compared with control cells. Collectively, these data confirm that TET2 suppression is sufficient to drive EGFR TKI resistance in vitro.

In previous studies, TET2 expression has been reported to be associated with prognosis and drug resistance in several solid tumors. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study revealing that TET2 suppression plays a role in first-generation EGFR TKI resistance. However, how TET2 contributes to drug resistance is still unknown. TET2 is a tumor suppressor gene encoding the TET2 protein that enzymatically converts 5-methylcytosine (5-mC) to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5-hmC), which plays a nonredundant, critical role in maintaining DNA demethylation and activity. DNA methylation is a stable, dominant, and heritable epigenetic modification that silences genes in somatic cells17,25. Given this critical function, we postulated that TET2 loss may induce resistance through aberrant DNA methylation of key effector genes. The significance of TET proteins in the maintenance of DNA methylation patterns can most accurately be assessed via functional studies. Research has revealed that DNA methylation predominantly occurs at enhancers and DNase I hypersensitivity sites in TET2 knockout cells26.

To explore the molecular mechanism of TET2 suppression in EGFR TKI resistance, RRBS was adopted in this study to analyze global genomic methylation profiles and detect differentially methylated genes in the PC9TET2 KD and PC9CONT KD cell lines, following bioinformatics analysis of GO functions and KEGG signaling to screen for genes associated with drug resistance. Forty-three drug resistance genes with hypermethylated promoter regions were identified via RRBS and bioinformatic analysis in PC9TET2 KD cells. Then, 10 candidate genes were selected for further validation: AXIN2, PIDD1, NR4A1, CSK, SHC1, GHR, SOX17, POU4F2, SPI1, and WNT4. RT‒PCR demonstrated that the expression of AXIN2 and CSK was significantly lower in PC9TET2 KD cells than in control PC9CONT KD cells. Among these, AXIN2 emerged as the top candidate due to its significant downregulation (Fig. 3A) and known tumor-suppressive functions. Subsequent functional validation showed that AXIN2 knockdown alone mimicked TET2-KD-induced resistance (Fig. 4C-F), while its overexpression partially rescued sensitivity (Fig. 4I-K), establishing a causal link. On the basis of these findings, we hypothesized that TET2-KD promotes resistance to EGFR TKIs through inducing AXIN2/CSK methylation and downregulating AXIN2/CSK expression. By further functional experiments, we found that AXIN2-KD but not CSK-KD in PC9 cells led to decreased sensitivity to gefitinib.Our results suggest that TET2 repression promotes EGFR TKI resistance primarily through AXIN2 promoter hypermethylation, leading to downregulation of its expression. This aligns with AXIN2’s established role as a tumor suppressor in Wnt/β-catenin signaling, but here we uniquely link its epigenetic silencing to TET2 loss in NSCLC. Notably, AXIN2 knockdown alone recapitulated TET2-KD-induced resistance, while its overexpression partially reversed the phenotype, underscoring its centrality in this mechanism.

Although AXIN2 has been implicated in immunotherapy response27, our study prioritizes its role in EGFR TKI resistance, revealing a novel TET2-AXIN2 epigenetic axis specific to oncogene-addicted NSCLC. In our study, we revealed for the first time that methylation of AXIN2 induced by TET2 repression is a novel resistance mechanism of EGFR TKIs in EGFR-mutant NSCLC. The underlying molecular mechanism is worthy of further exploration in future research.

The reversibility of AXIN2 methylation by DCA, both in vitro and in vivo, highlights the therapeutic potential of epigenetic modifiers in TET2-mutant NSCLC. The reversibility of AXIN2 methylation by DCA (Fig. 5A-B) and subsequent resensitization to gefitinib (Figs. 5C-E and 6B-D) highlight the translational potential of targeting TET2-driven epigenetic dysregulation in NSCLC.

Our findings carry important clinical implications for managing EGFR TKI resistance in NSCLC. First, TET2 mutation status could serve as a predictive biomarker for identifying patients at risk of developing resistance to first-generation EGFR TKIs, particularly in T790M-negative cases. Second, the demonstrated efficacy of decitabine in reversing TET2-mediated resistance suggests that epigenetic therapies may represent a viable treatment strategy for this patient subset. Future clinical trials should evaluate combination regimens of EGFR TKIs with hypomethylating agents in TET2-deficient tumors. Additionally, our identification of AXIN2 as a key mediator of resistance provides a potential therapeutic target, warranting exploration of Wnt/β-catenin pathway modulators in this context. These translational opportunities could ultimately lead to more personalized treatment approaches for NSCLC patients with acquired TKI resistance.

While our study reveals a novel mechanism by which TET2 repression promotes EGFR TKI resistance through AXIN2 methylation, several limitations should be acknowledged. While our mechanistic focus on AXIN2 is compelling, it does not exclude potential contributions from other hypermethylated genes (e.g., CSK or SPI1) identified in our screening. Future studies should systematically investigate their roles in resistance. Although decitabine reversed resistance in preclinical models, its clinical application requires further optimization, including determination of optimal dosing strategies and evaluation of combination approaches with EGFR TKIs. Moreover, our study used TET2 knockdown models rather than patient-derived TET2-mutant cell lines. Future studies should incorporate various TET2 mutation subtypes (e.g., missense versus truncating mutations) to better characterize their functional heterogeneity. Furthermore, the molecular mechanisms linking AXIN2 methylation to EGFR signaling (particularly Wnt/β-catenin pathway crosstalk) remain incompletely understood and require further investigation. Addressing these knowledge gaps will provide important opportunities to develop more effective therapeutic strategies for TET2-associated resistance.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we revealed that the methylation of AXIN2 induced by TET2 repression is a novel resistance mechanism of EGFR TKIs in EGFR-mutant NSCLC. Demethylation drugs have the potential to overcome EGFR TKI resistance induced by loss-of-function TET2 mutations. These results not only expand our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying drug resistance in NSCLC patients but also suggest a potential treatment strategy for first-generation EGFR TKI-resistant NSCLC patients.

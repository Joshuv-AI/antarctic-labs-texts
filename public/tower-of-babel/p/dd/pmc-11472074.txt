Development, assessment and validation of a novel nomogram model for predicting stroke mimics in stroke center：A single-center observational study
Authors: Xiaoman Chen, Shuo Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly recognition and prediction of stroke mimics (SM) can avoid inappropriate recanalization therapy and delay in the management of SM etiology. The purpose of this study is to screen the predictors for SM and develop a novel predictive nomogram model for predicting SM. Meanwhile, the diagnostic performance of the nomogram model was evaluated and validated. The diagnostic efficacy of the nomogram model was also compared with four other SM structured scales.MethodsThe clinical data of eligible patients were retrospectively enrolled as training datasets from January 2020 to December 2021; and the clinical data of eligible patients were prospectively enrolled as validation datasets from February to December 2022 in stroke center, Shengjing hospital, respectively. Univariate analysis and Lasso regression were used to select the optimal predictors for the training set, and a nomogram model was constructed by multivariate logistics regression, predictive scoring based on nomogram model is performed for each subject suffering from suspected acute ischemic stroke. Area under the curve (AUC), Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, Calibration curve, decision curve analysis (DCA), clinical impact curve (CIC) analysis and bootstrap sampling were performed to assess and validate the predictive performance and clinical utility of the nomogram model, and the DeLong test was used to compare the overall diagnostic performance of the nomogram model with the other four structured SM scales. The Delong test was also conducted to assess the external reliability of the SM nomogram model by comparing the predictive diagnostic performance of the validation set with the training set. Additionally, the Calibration curve was utilized to evaluate the diagnostic calibration capability of the SM nomogram model in the validation set.Results703 eligible patients (68 with SM, accounting for 9.7 %) were assigned to the training set, while 301 patients (26 with SM, accounting for 8.6 %) were assigned to the validation set. A nomogram model was then developed using these six parameters (SBP, history of epilepsy, isolated dizziness, isolated sensory impairment, headache, and absence of speech impairment symptoms), a dynamic web-based version of the nomogram was subsequently created. Comparing with four other scales, the nomogram model showed the highest overall diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.929, 95%CI = 0.908–0.947). The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was conducted to assess the agreement between the predicted SM values from the model and the observed SM values. The results of the test indicated a favorable consistency (χ2 = 9.299, P = 0.3177) between the predicted and observed SM. The results obtained from the analysis of the Calibration curve, DCA curve, and CIC analysis suggested that the nomogram possesses a favorable predictive capacity and superior clinical usefulness. Furthermore, the external validation demonstrated that there is no significant difference in the overall predictive diagnostic performance between the validation set and training set (0.929 vs 0.910, P > 0.05), thereby confirming the favorable stability of the nomogram model.ConclusionOur study firstly proposed a nomogram prediction approach based on the clinical features of SM, which could effectively predict the occurrence of SM. The utilization of the nomogram in stroke center proves advantageous for the identification and evaluation of SM, thereby enhancing diagnostic decision-making and strategies employed for suspected acute stroke patients.Keywords: Stroke mimics, Nomogram, Prediction model, Acute ischemic stroke, Stroke center

Introduction

Stroke is the one of leading cause of mortality and disability worldwide, with ischemic stroke being the most prevalent form of stroke. Treatment guidelines advocate for the administration of intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular therapy within the designated time frame following the onset of symptoms, as this approach has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing vascular recanalization rates, salvaging the ischemic penumbra, and mitigating neurological impairments [1]. In order to ensure precise and prompt management of acute ischemic stroke, it is imperative to accurately identify individuals at a heightened risk of stroke and assess patients with suspected ischemic stroke during prehospital triage and stroke center. Emergency physicians frequently encounter challenges in fully evaluating patients with suspected ischemic stroke due to limited time and inadequate screening tools when presented with focal neurological deficits. Furthermore, even when patients undergo ultra-early diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), there is a possibility of overlooking "true" ischemic stroke patients who exhibit DWI-negative results [2,3]. Patients with acute neurologic symptoms initially diagnosed with acute ischemic stroke may actually have other causes simulating ischemic stroke, called stroke mimics (SM) [4,5]. If patients with SM received incorrect or imprompt treatment, i.e. intravenous thrombolysis, patients with SM are likely to delay the disease and even may subsequently result in further deterioration [6,7].

The incidence of SM varies due to differences in research backgrounds, medical settings, and inclusion criteria of SM, with estimates ranging from approximately 5 %–30 % of all patients with suspected acute stroke [4]. Despite the progressive advancements in multimodal neuroimaging, the incidence of SM continues to persist, posing a significant challenge in clinical diagnosis. The lack of universally accepted definitions and consensus regarding the term "SM" has led to its general and semantic usage. SM encompass a range of clinical syndromes characterized by the emergence of new neurological deficits, and patients presenting with SM demonstrate clinical symptoms resembling stroke episodes, including speech and cognitive impairments, movement disorders, and reduced consciousness upon hospital admission. However, the discharge diagnoses assigned to patients admitted to the hospital following an emergency were categorized as non-stroke conditions [8]. SM encompass a wide spectrum of diseases with various causes, such as epilepsy, complex migraine, peripheral vertigo, spinal cord disease, hypertensive encephalopathy, infection, toxic disease, metabolic disease, psychiatric/psychological disorders, and aortic dissection, among others. Furthermore, rare etiologies such as poststroke recrudescence (PSR), intracranial space-occupying lesions, and central nervous system demyelinating diseases have also been reported [9,10].

The accurate differentiation between SM and acute ischemic stroke is of paramount importance in enhancing the efficacy of vascular recanalization therapy for acute ischemic stroke. Moreover, it can reduce the potential adverse effects associated with thrombolytic therapy, such as hemorrhage, thereby preventing the administration of ineffective treatment [11]. Furthermore, prompt identification of SM facilitates the investigation of their underlying causes, ensuring timely initiation of optimal treatment and minimizing medical errors.

Consequently, the timely identification of SM is of utmost importance in clinical practice, as it significantly impacts the accurate diagnosis and appropriate treatment of stroke. Numerous predictive tools utilizing structured scales have been suggested in published studies to optimize diagnostic strategies [[12], [13], [14], [15]]. These scales encompass clinical attributes of SM, aiming to differentiate between SM and “true” stroke based on fundamental clinical features. Nevertheless, these structured scales exhibit certain deficiencies and constraints. For instance, they did not consider the influence of imaging results such as head CT on the identification of SM. The scales used in previous studies demonstrated inconsistent evaluation accuracy when externally validated, resulting in less reliable findings. Additionally, the existing scoring systems have a limitation in that they require the conversion of continuous predictor variables into categorical variables, potentially compromising the predictive value of certain continuous variables and subsequently reducing the accuracy of the models. In contrast, a nomogram is a graphical prediction tool that integrates important prognostic factors based on a multivariate regression analysis, assigning a relative risk score to each variable based on its contribution to the prognosis, whether categorical or continuous variables. The nomograms employ a combination of multiple variables (predictors) to forecast the likelihood of a clinical event in an individual patient. While the effectiveness of utilizing nomograms for predicting the occurrence of SM may be superior, there is currently a dearth of research on this subject. The urgency to accurately identify and predict SM in a clinical practice is emphasized in the prompt.

The purpose of this study is to address these concerns by constructing a visual nomogram-based prediction model specifically for SM. And the diagnostic performance of a prediction tool against four established scales were compared to identify a tool that demonstrates superior predictive efficacy and clinical significance for SM. The validity of this predictive model will be assessed using prospective data obtained from our center.

Research materials and methods

Research subjects

Research subjects in the training set

The study retrospectively gathered data from patients who visited the stroke center at Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University between January 2020 and December 2021 and were admitted to the ward with an initial diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke. Various clinical characteristics, imaging, and laboratory data were collected as screening variables.

The study's inclusion criteria consisted of three main components. Firstly, patients were required to have visited the stroke center in emergency department within 24 h of the onset of illness and display evident signs or symptoms of focal localization of the nervous system or disturbance of consciousness. Additionally, patients had to meet the diagnostic criteria of either the RACE, G-FAST, or CG-FAST scoring systems for acute cerebrovascular disease [16]. Secondly, patients had to fall within the age range of 18–90 years. Lastly, A preliminary assessment was conducted in the s stroke center, resulting in the admission of patients to the neurology ward with a primary diagnosis of 'acute ischemic stroke'.

The exclusion criteria for this study included the following: 1. Patients who had undergone complete laboratory tests in the emergency department and were diagnosed with non-acute stroke-related diseases (such as hypoglycemia) in the stroke center; 2. Patients with a documented history of exposure to carbon monoxide or poisons during the onset of the disease; 3. Patients with a body temperature equal to or greater than 37.5 °C; 4. Patients diagnosed with spontaneous intracranial hemorrhage (such as acute parenchymal hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or subdural hemorrhage) through head imaging; 5. patients were diagnosed with transient ischemic attack upon discharge; 6. Patients with incomplete medical records and no confirmed diagnosis upon discharge were lost to follow-up.

Research subjects in the validation set

This study prospectively enrolled patients who presented to the stroke center of Shengjing hospital, between February and December 2022 as the validation set. The objective was to validate the nomogram prediction model developed using the training set and assess its diagnostic accuracy and reliability. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for the research subjects in the validation set as previously described in training set. The study design and protocol were approved by the ethics committee of Shengjing hospital, and all participants provided informed consent (or informed consent exemption).

Selection of candidate variables

This study employed a selection process based on patients' medical records, clinical experience, and previous research findings. The variables considered for inclusion were age, gender, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus, history of atrial fibrillation, history of epilepsy, isolated peripheral facial paralysis (PFP), speech impairment, isolated dizziness, isolated sensory impairment, headache, history of smoking, history of drinking, history of cerebrovascular disease (CVD), disorders of consciousness, cervical pain, hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, and cranial CT findings. A comprehensive set of 22 variables was selected for inclusion in the multifactor analysis aimed at variable screening. The classification criteria for certain variables was showed in Table 1. The assessment of all CT examination results was carried out by two neurologists, and the degree of agreement between the observers was evaluated using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC).

Univariate analysis, variable selection and development of nomogram model

This study used the training set data, treated acute ischemic stroke and SM as dichotomous clinical outcomes. Additionally, various factors that have the potential to predict SM were considered as variables, encompassing both continuous and categorical variables. Continuous variables were reported as either mean ± standard deviation (in cases of normal distribution) or median (interquartile range) (in cases of non-normal distribution). The comparison of means between the two groups was conducted using either an independent sample t-test or a Kruskal-Wallis test (in cases of non-normal distribution). As for categorical variables, Pearson's χ2 test or Fisher's exact test was employed. Univariate analysis was performed to examine the disparities in each variable between the group of SM and the group of acute ischemic stroke.

This study aimed to develop a SM prediction nomogram model using the logistic regression method. The regression model incorporated variables that exhibited statistically significant disparities between the SM group and the acute ischemic stroke group. In order to ensure the absence of collinearity and identify the most optimal predictors, the minimum absolute shrinkage and selection operator algorithm (Lasso) regression was employed to screen potential predictors. By effectively circumventing collinearity, Lasso regression successfully determined the most predictive variables.

The feature variables with non-zero coefficients, determined through Lasso regression, were subsequently analyzed using multivariate logistic regression to identify predictors that were statistically significant. The resulting optimal variables, selected through multifactor logistic regression, were then utilized to construct a nomogram for predicting SM visually. This nomogram assigned scores to each independent variable based on their respective regression coefficients, establishing a standardized scoring system for predicting SM. The total score for each patient was calculated using the nomogram.

Evaluation of the SM nomogram model and comparison of its predictive efficacy with other scales for predicting SM

The calibration of the nomogram model and the level of agreement between the predicted probability and the actual probability were evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test. P＞0.05 indicated that the nomogram model was well calibrated.

Furthermore, the clinical outcome of the patient, encompassing both SM and stroke, was regarded as gold standard for diagnostic assessment. Each patient's total nomogram score was computed, and subsequent diagnostic tests were conducted based on this score. The patients in the training set were also assigned scores using the other four SM prediction scales and underwent diagnostic testing. To compare the overall diagnostic efficacy disparity between the nomogram model and the other four scales, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was plotted and the area under the curve (AUC) along with its 95 % confidence interval (CI) was calculated. The DeLong test was used to determine if there were statistically significant differences in AUC between the nomogram model and the other scales.

Finally, the calibration curve was employed to evaluate the calibration capability of the nomogram model. Additionally, the training set data underwent testing using the Bootstrap method, involving 1000 repeated samplings, to ensure result stability. A decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to assess the clinical applicability of the nomogram model, which evaluated the clinical net benefit and associated risk of utilizing the nomogram model at various threshold probabilities through the net benefit curve. This analysis determined the feasibility of the clinical decision-making formulated by the model. Clinical impact curve (CIC) was also plotted to evaluate the clinical usefulness and applicability net benefits of the nomogram model.

Validation of the nomogram prediction model

For validation of the nomogram prediction model, a predictive score was assigned to each patient based on the nomogram scoring formula yielded from training set. The DeLong test were conducted to determine whether the nomogram model was statistically significant by comparing the differences in AUC between the training and validation sets. Using the validation set as a calibration set, a calibration curve was also drawn to verify the model's calibration capability. The flow diagram of the study was shown in Supplementary material.

The software programs R 4.2.2, SPSS 25.0, and Medcalc 20.022 were employed for the analysis. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Patients and disease spectrum of SM

Patients in training set

From January 2020 to December 2021, a comprehensive total of 2516 patients sought medical attention at the stroke center of emergency department. Among this cohort, a subset of 198 patients received a diagnosis of intracranial hemorrhage, while an additional 343 patients were definitively diagnosed with non-acute stroke within the emergency department and 33 patients exhibited a body temperature equal to or exceeding 37.5 °C. Furthermore, 137 patients were discharged with a diagnosis of transient ischemic attack (TIA); 698 patients voluntarily departed from the hospital following their visit to the emergency room; 276 patients possessed incomplete medical records; 103 patients were found to lack a definitive diagnosis at discharge. Subsequently, a cohort of 703 patients meeting the specified study criteria was selected for analysis as the training set data.

Patients in validation set

Between February and December 2022, a total of 1235 patients who sought medical attention at the stroke center in emergency department were consecutively recruited in this prospective cohort. Within this cohort, there were 108 patients diagnosed with intracranial hemorrhage, 201 patients with non-acute stroke identified in the emergency department, and 15 patients with a body temperature equal to or exceeding 37.5 °C. Furthermore, 85 patients were discharged with a diagnosis of TIA, 345 patients autonomously discharged from the stroke center, while 127 patients had incomplete medical record data and 53 patients lacked a definitive diagnosis upon discharge. Ultimately, validation set enrolled a cohort of 301 patients for further analysis.

The training set and validation set consisted of a total of 1004 patients, out of which 94 (9.36 %) were finally diagnosed as SM. The etiology of SM were vertiginous syndromes, peripheral neuropathy, epilepsy, syncope, spinal cord disease, central nervous system infection or immune disease, aortic or carotid artery dissection, intracranial mass, PSR, migraine, and other systemic diseases. The inter-observer reproducibility of emergency head CT imaging results was found to be excellent, with an ICC value of 0.81. The training set for data analysis included a total of 703 patients, among which 68 cases (9.7 %) were identified as SM. The validation set consisted of 301 patients, with 26 cases (8.6 %) being SM. The difference in the disease spectrum of SM between the two sets was not found to be statistically significant (P＞0.05) (Table 2).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis and development of nomogram

The variables that showed significance in the univariate analysis were further analyzed using Lasso regression and multivariate logistic regression in the training set. Out of these variables, a total of 7 had non-zero coefficients in the Lasso regression model: SBP, history of epilepsy, isolated PFP, isolated dizzy, isolated sensory impairment, headache, and speech impairment (Fig. 1a and b). These 7 variables were then included in a multivariate logistic regression. The findings revealed that SBP, history of epilepsy, isolated dizzy, isolated sensory impairment, headache, and speech impairment were identified as independent predictors of SM. To visually represent these predictors, a nomogram model (Fig. 2) was constructed using these 6 variables. The model demonstrated that patients with lower SBP, history of epilepsy, isolated dizziness, isolated sensory impairment, headache, and no symptoms of speech impairment were more likely to be diagnosed with SM.

Validation of the nomogram model

The validation set data was employed to evaluate the dependability of the nomogram model outcomes. The nomogram prediction model formula was utilized, which was derived from the training set, to assign a score to each patient in the validation set. To assess the disparity in overall predictive performance between the training set and the validation set, we generated a ROC curve (Fig. 3b). The AUC of the training set was 0.929 (95 % CI = 0.908–0.947), while the AUC of the validation set was 0.910 (95 % CI = 0.872–0.940), the Delong test suggested there is no statistically significant distinction in the overall predictive diagnostic performance between the nomogram training set and the validation set (P > 0.05). Moreover, the calibration curve of the validation set substantiated the model's proficient predictive capability for SM. (Fig. 4b). The validation of the nomogram model further strengthens its reliability and applicability.

Discussion

Within this research, we utilized Lasso regression and logistic regression to identify potential risk factors associated with SM. Our discoveries suggested that six significant variables, SBP, history of epilepsy, isolated dizzy, isolated sensory impairment, headache, and absence of speech impairment symptoms, respectively were determined as independent risk factors for predicting SM. Subsequently, A pioneering nomogram model was developed and verified for predicting SM in patients with suspected acute ischemic stroke.

Significantly, certain variables, including SBP, history of epilepsy, isolated dizzy, and isolated sensory impairment, correspond with the findings of previous studies. Nevertheless, our nomogram model surpasses overall prediction performance and stability through external validation and comparison with four other structured scales. Conversely, the structured scoring scale for predicting SM, despite its simplicity and ease of implementation, exhibits inadequate stability and accuracy.

In a retrospective study conducted in Singapore [17], a total of 257 patients were examined, with 17 of them identified as SM. The study aimed to develop a novel decision tree model through univariate logistic regression analysis, utilizing age, migraine, and psychiatric history as predictors. However, the resulting decision tree model was deemed intricate and lacked clinical applicability. Furthermore, the study employed four SM prediction scales as external validation annotations, scoring all patients and conducting diagnostic tests to assess the overall diagnostic effectiveness of these scales. The AUC was compared, and it was found that TMS exhibited the highest value in identifying SM. However, there was no statistically significant difference in the overall diagnostic performance of the four scales. In a recent study [18], the FABS and TMS scales were also compared, leading to the development of a novel MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) model for predicting SM. The AUC of this model was significantly superior to that of FABS and TM scoring. Nevertheless, a limitation of this MLP model is the absence of a specific scoring system based on the principle of neural network, which impedes its clinical utility due to the inability to explain the hidden layers in the model.

The utilization of statistical prediction models has seen a surge in clinical research, owing to the advancements in biostatistical methods and the escalating dependence on statistical techniques [19]. These models find application in diverse domains of clinical research, encompassing the identification of influential factors, diagnostic testing, prognosis assessment, and personalized treatment. Among these models, the popularity of nomograms is on the rise among medical researchers, primarily due to their distinctive advantages [20]. This study pioneers the application of a nomogram prediction models based on logistic regression for the prediction of SM, wherein scores are assigned to different value levels. The association between the cumulative score and the likelihood of outcome occurrence is subsequently employed to compute an individual's susceptibility to experiencing SM. The nomogram facilitates the simplification of intricate regression equations into visual representations, thereby enhancing the accessibility of the prediction model outcomes. The SM assessment tool, which is based on the nomogram and was developed within the scope of this investigation, has been successfully integrated into clinical practice and serves as a crucial point of reference. Furthermore, the predictive diagnostic performance of the nomogram in this study is significantly better than the other four prediction scales. This is due to the fact that the nomogram prediction model takes into account the continuity of variables such as SBP and age, as well as their contribution to the prediction model, without requiring the classification of continuous variables. The utilization of the Lasso regression during the development of the nomogram model effectively addresses the issue of multicollinearity among variables, including SBP, DBP, and history of hypertension.

In contrast to prior studies, this research integrates emergency CT examination results as screening variables, reflecting real clinical scenarios. A non-contrast CT scan is the preferred first-line imaging modality for patients with suspected intracranial lesions. In the majority of emergency departments, non-enhanced CT is the sole option for the initial assessment of acute cerebral ischemia [21]. Nevertheless, its primary function in the emergency department is to exclude intracranial hemorrhage, and it possesses certain limitations in the diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke. The timing of thrombolysis in acute stroke is of utmost importance, and once the non-enhanced CT examination is concluded, prompt initiation of vascular recanalization treatment is warranted for individuals with suspected disabling ischemic stroke [22]. The administration of thrombolysis in stroke mimics is prevalent in both clinical practice and thrombolytic trials. The Norwegian Tenecteplase Stroke Trial (NOR-TEST) has evaluated the safety and efficacy of tenecteplase compared to alteplase in patients with suspected acute ischemic stroke，and the study demonstrated that thrombolysis with tenecteplase is as safe as with alteplase in stroke mimics. It is important to note that stroke mimics are more commonly observed in younger patients. Several studies have also indicated that neither alteplase nor tenecteplase resulted in intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) in stroke mimics under 60 years of age [23,24]. However, thrombolysis carries an increased risk of SM in specific instances. It is crucial to promptly identify SM to prevent potential ICH resulting from thrombolysis in elderly patients, as well as to mitigate the financial burden associated with thrombolytic therapy and additional intensive care or stroke unit services. This study incorporated emergency head CT examinations as potential variables, however, the results indicate that the classification judgment relying solely on non-enhanced CT scans is not the primary determinant for predicting SM.

The discovery of non-invasive tests designed to rapidly differentiate between stroke and stroke-mimicking conditions, as well as between ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke, is of significant clinical value. The identification of blood biomarkers for stroke is a highly active research area, with potential applications extending beyond diagnosis to include prognosis and patient monitoring—such as assessing treatment efficacy and identifying complications. However, the clinical implementation of these biomarkers has been limited by both patient- and disease-related factors, such as the heterogeneity of stroke etiology, the complexity of the ischemic cascade, and the impact of the blood-brain barrier on biomarker diffusion. Additionally, there are challenges related to the lack of standardized laboratory protocols, including pre-analytical and analytical variability and the interpretation of results. While several biomarkers have been identified, none have yet demonstrated sufficient sensitivity and specificity for widespread clinical use [25]. Nevertheless, a systematic review and meta-analysis of existing studies identified brain natriuretic peptide, matrix metalloproteinase-9, and D-dimer as promising markers for distinguishing between acute ischemic stroke and stroke mimics [26]. However, a recent meta-analysis concluded that the aforementioned biomarkers were not eligible for use in diagnostic meta-analyses aimed at differentiating stroke mimics [27]. Although our study emphasizes clinical factors like imaging findings, it does not consider laboratory tests or genetic markers. Incorporating these elements into our model could potentially enhance the predictive accuracy of the nomogram.

The etiology of SM encompasses the presence of the PSR, which contributes to the limited predictive value of CT examination results for SM. A portion of the validation set utilized in this study comprised stroke emergency patients in China during the height of the COVID-19 pandemic in December 2022 [28]. The COVID-19 pandemic shows a clear association between SARS-CoV2 infections and increased risk of cerebrovascular events. It is recognized that COVID-19 infection/pneumonia may play a role in the recurrence of PSR [29]. Despite attempts to exclude individuals with fever from the study data, one patient in the validation set exhibited PSR directly induced by pneumonia arising from COVID-19 infection.

Hence, the utilization of multimodal head CT imaging, encompassing CT perfusion (CTP) and CT angiography (CTA), holds the potential to strengthen the detection and diagnosis of SM in patients [[30], [31], [32]]. Multimodal CT is a powerful imaging algorithm that is central to current ischemic stroke patient care [33]. The practical considerations and challenges associated with the incorporation of multimodal imaging techniques, such as CTA and CTP, into routine emergency assessments at stroke centers warrant further investigation. A retrospective study conducted at a medical center with stroke unit included 401 patients with a dedicated stroke unit included 401 patients presenting with clinical suspicion of acute ischemic stroke. Among these patients, 89 (22 %) were ultimately diagnosed with stroke mimics. The findings indicated that while multimodal CT exhibited low sensitivity, it demonstrated high specificity in diagnosing stroke mimics in the acute setting [34]. This high specificity underscores the potential utility of multimodal CT in differentiating true ischemic strokes from stroke mimics, although the low sensitivity suggests a need for additional diagnostic strategies to enhance early detection accuracy. The high specificity of multimodal CT allows for the exclusion of stroke and the avoidance of unnecessary revascularization treatment in patients with a diagnosis of stroke mimics. In comparison to CT, Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) exhibits greater sensitivity in identifying the lesions of acute ischemia and aids in differentiating individuals with SM from those with “true” acute ischemic stroke. Notably, diffusion weighted imaging proves particularly efficacious as a diagnostic instrument. Nevertheless, the practical implementation of MRI is frequently hindered in emergency settings due to time limitations, constrained medical resources, and restrictions on medical insurance policies. moreover, MRI is commonly selected as a primary diagnostic tool following patient admission to the ward [35].

Significant differences were observed between the components incorporated in the SM prediction nomogram model of this investigation and the other four scales. The results indicated that this nomogram model showcases superior predictive diagnostic efficacy in comparison to the FABS, SFABS, TMS, and Khan score scales. Furthermore, external validation confirms that the nomogram model results did not exhibit any apparent overfitting. It has been noted that in all four prior structured scales, age was assessed and younger age was identified as a risk factor for SM. Presently, there is an increasing prevalence of stroke occurrences among younger individuals in China, with approximately 10 % of stroke patients being under the age of 40. Moreover, this trend is also observed in worldwide [36].

Furthermore, the categorization of systolic blood pressure (SBP) as a categorical variable in the other four scales introduces subjectivity in determining the threshold for classification. While the classification of SBP into two categories may appear straightforward and achievable, it falls short in comprehensively capturing the intricate association between SBP and SM. Additionally, the existing model does not fully exploit the potential of SBP data as a continuous variable. In our proposed nomogram prediction model, SBP is treated as a continuous variable, leading to enhanced accuracy and improved comparability of its value.

This study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, a selection bias is present in our data collection process, as it only encompasses patients who sought care at the emergency department and were subsequently admitted to the hospital. Patients in the pre-hospital stage were not observed, thus potentially introducing a limitation to the generalizability of our findings. Our study combines retrospective data for the training set and prospective data for the validation set. This approach might introduce biases due to differences in data collection methods and temporal changes in clinical practices or patient characteristics. Further study should focus on assessing the impact of these potential biases and explore strategies to mitigate them. For instance, ensuring consistency in data collection protocols across both datasets or employing statistical techniques to adjust for temporal discrepancies could help in minimizing bias and enhancing the validity of the study's findings. Furthermore, the inclusion of patients solely from a single center in northern China may introduce selection bias. Secondly, it is commonly used to evaluate suspected acute ischemic stroke with CTP, CTA, or a combination of both. However, these imaging modalities are more time consuming, less cost-efficient, and may not always be suitable for all patients. [37], it is uncertain whether this nomogram model can optimize the advanced imaging process for patients with suspected ischemic stroke.

Therefore, it is imperative to acknowledge that the generalizability of the nomogram model discussed herein may be limited to specific medical settings and conditions. Consequently, it is recommended that diverse medical institutions undertake the development of SM prediction models tailored to their respective environments and conditions. Additionally, it is worth noting that the training set employed in constructing the nomogram model for this study was derived from retrospective research subjects, potentially resulting in the omission of valuable clinical data. Despite the potential of methods such as multiple imputation of missing values (MICE) to address data loss, this study did not employ such techniques. Nonetheless, the findings derived from the analysis of the prospective validation set using the nomogram prediction model remained consistently reliable, indicating that the absence of retrospective study data did not compromise the stability and accuracy of the conclusions.

The application of artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to markedly enhance the capacity of clinicians to accurately identify and manage conditions that may mimic the symptoms of a stroke. By leveraging advanced machine learning algorithms and data analysis, AI-based models could assist in differentiating stroke mimics from actual strokes, thereby reducing the risk of unnecessary treatments and improving patient outcomes. Studies have demonstrated that AI is capable of analyzing complex datasets, including imaging and clinical data, to provide more precise diagnostic support and predictive insights [38,39]. A future study that integrates AI into clinical practice has the potential to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of decision-making processes for stroke mimics, which would ultimately be beneficial for both patients and healthcare providers. Research should focus on identifying underlying causes and developing strategies to reduce errors, particularly in symptom-specific subgroups most vulnerable to misdiagnosis. This approach would enhance patient outcomes and refine diagnostic precision in clinical settings.

Conclusion

The SM nomogram prediction model, constructed utilizing six parameters (systolic blood pressure, history of epilepsy, isolated dizziness, isolated sensory impairment, headache, and absence of speech impairment symptoms), demonstrates a significant capacity to predict the incidence of SM. This model surpasses other scales in predictive efficacy, thereby augmenting diagnostic decision-making and strategies for patients suspected of acute stroke. Consequently, it indicates that the precision of this model in predicting 'true' ischemic stroke is also enhanced. To ascertain the accuracy and consistency of this nomogram model in diverse medical settings and multi-center medical institutions, employing larger sample sizes is imperative for future investigations.

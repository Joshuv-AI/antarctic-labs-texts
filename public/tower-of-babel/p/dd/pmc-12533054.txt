Development and validation of a predictive model for diabetic peripheral neuropathy with type 2 diabetes mellitus in Xinjiang, China
Authors: Asiguli Adili, Maihesumu Aikemu, Karanvir Singh Rana, Yihan Wei, Aibibai Yusufu, Sheng Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to identify risk factors associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) in patients with type 2 diabetesmellitus (T2DM) and to develop a predictive model to support clinical decision-making. A total of 1,001 patients with T2DM were retrospectively enrolled from the Department of Endocrinology, First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University, between January 2023 and January 2024. All patients were residents of Xinjiang. Patients were divided into two groups according to the diagnosis of peripheral neuropathy: 603 patients with DPN and 398 without DPN (NDPN). Missing data were handled using the “VIM” and “mice” packages in R. Statistical analyses were performed using independent t-tests and chi-square tests. Two machine learning algorithms were used to identify key risk factors, and shared features were visualized using a Venn diagram. Subsequently, four diagnostic models—GBM, GLM, RF, and SVM—were constructed using the “caret” package, and their predictive performance was rigorously evaluated.Fifteen factors, including age, duration of diabetes mellitus (DM), body mass index (BMI), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), 2-h postprandial glucose (2hPG), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, blood urea, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), urinary uric acid, urinary creatinine, urinary microalbumin, and urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), were significantly associated with the occurrence of diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN), all showing statistical significance (P < 0.05).SVM-RFE and LASSO regression identified seven core risk factors for model construction. The RF model achieved the best performance, with AUCs of 1.000 (95% CI 0.990–1.000) in the training set, 0.904 (95% CI 0.869–0.940) in the validation set, and 0.953 (95% CI 0.940–0.966) in the external dataset. To assess potential overfitting in the Random Forest model, we performed model simplification, bootstrap resampling to estimate confidence intervals, and DeLong’s test (P = 0.748), all of which confirmed that the model maintained robust generalization performance rather than merely fitting the training data. This study successfully identified significant predictors of DPN using machine learning techniques and developed a validated diagnostic model. The model demonstrated high accuracy and may aid in early detection and clinical management of DPN.Keywords: Diabetic peripheral neuropathy, Risk prediction, Machine learning, Random forest model, Diagnostic efficacySubject terms: Endocrinology, Endocrine system and metabolic diseases, Diabetes, Diabetes complications

Introduction

Type 2 diabetesmellitus (T2DM) is a predominant form of diabetes and is acknowledged globally as a critical public health concern. Chronic hyperglycemia associated with T2DM can result in damage to multiple organs and systems, particularly impacting the eyes, kidneys, nerves, and blood vessels, thereby imposing considerable physical and psychological burdens on patients. With significant improvements in living standards and the increasing severity of population aging, the prevalence of T2DM is escalating rapidly worldwide.The 9th edition of the IDF Diabetes Atlas, published by the International Diabetes Federation (IDF) in 2019, indicated that there were approximately 463 million people with diabetes worldwide, and it is estimated that this number will rise to 700.2 million by 20451.This elevated prevalence contributes to a substantial disease burden. Furthermore, as the incidence of diabetes rises, there is a concomitant increase in the occurrence of related complications.

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is among the most common and disabling of these complications2. DPN is defined as peripheral nerve dysfunction in diabetic patients after excluding alternative causes3. The reported prevalence of DPN varies widely, ranging from 41 to 91%, and it can markedly impair patients’ quality of life4,5. According to data from the International Diabetes Federation (IDF), within 10 years of diagnosis, 60–90% of diabetic patients show some degree of neuropathy based on nerve conduction tests, with about 50% formally diagnosed with DPN. In China, the prevalence of DPN among diabetic individuals has been reported at 67.6%6.Clinically, DPN is characterized by distal sensory and motor impairments, including numbness, pain, limb weakness, muscle atrophy, and diminished or absent tendon reflexes. Severe DPN is the leading cause of non-traumatic amputations. Notably, the five-year survival rate for patients with severe DPN is less than 50%, which is even lower than for trauma-related amputations7. Moreover, over 50% of DPN cases are asymptomatic in the early stage, contributing to delayed diagnosis and the risk of complications such as painless foot ulcers and eventual amputation8.The “Multidisciplinary Expert Consensus on the Management of T2DM in Cold Regions of China” identifies Xinjiang as a representative cold region where T2DM demonstrates unique epidemiological characteristics. The prevalence of T2DM in these regions is significantly higher than the national average9. Geographical factors, dietary habits (characterized by high carbohydrate, fat, and protein intake), and a sedentary lifestyle may contribute to both the onset and progression of DPN in this population.Early diagnosis and intervention are essential to improving DPN outcomes10.

However, the current diagnosis of DPN still relies heavily on clinical symptoms, physical signs, and nerve conduction studies, each of which has limitations. Furthermore, factors such as geographic region, ethnicity, and diet can affect DPN risk profiles and model predictors11. Most existing predictive models incorporate only a few clinical variables and fail to capture the complex interactions among risk factors. Prior studies using machine learning to predict DPN have often relied on single algorithms and lacked robust external validation12,13, limiting their generalizability. In addition, few models have been developed for populations in Xinjiang, and no published studies to date have conducted external validation in this region.

To address these gaps, this study aims to identify the key risk factors associated with DPN in patients with T2DM in Xinjiang, China, and to develop a diagnostic prediction model with strong clinical utility. Emphasis is placed on external validation to ensure robustness. By systematically evaluating clinical indicators and using multiple machine learning algorithms to construct the model, this study provides evidence to support early DPN screening, precision risk stratification, and public health policy formulation.

Objects and methods

Study subjects

This study retrospectively collected data from 1,001 T2DM patients hospitalized in the Department of Endocrinology at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University from January 2023 to January 2024. Based on the results of Electrophysiological Examination of Peripheral Nerves, patients were divided into two groups: 603 patients with T2DM and DPN, and 398 patients with T2DM without DPN (NDPN). All these patients met the diagnostic criteria for type 2 diabetes mellitus as specified in the 2020"China Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Diabetes"14.The overall study process, including patient grouping, data imputation, statistical analysis, and model development, is illustrated in Fig. 1.This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (Approval No. K202502-12) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (revised in Brazil, 2013).Given the retrospective design of the study, the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University waived the requirement for informed consent.

Basic information

Detailed baseline demographic and clinical data were collected from all participants, including gender, age, duration of diabetes mellitus (T2DM), history of diabetic retinopathy (DR), history of hypertension, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and body mass index (BMI). BMI was calculated as body weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters (kg/m2).

Laboratory indicators

Blood Samples: Following an overnight fast of 10 to 12 h, fasting venous blood samples (4–5 mL) were collected from all participants the subsequent morning between 7:00 and 8:00 AM. The purpose of this collection was to measure various biochemical parameters, including fasting plasma glucose (FPG), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglyceride-glucose index (TyG index), blood urea, serum creatinine (Scr), blood uric acid, estimated glomerular filtration rate (e-GFR), 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) , and 2-h postprandial glucose (2hPG) following an oral glucose tolerance test administered with 75 g of glucose.

Statistical methods

Missing data imputation

Among the 26 clinical indicators, data types such as gender, history of diabetic retinopathy, and history of hypertension are non-continuous variables, while the remaining 23 features are continuous variables. During data collection, strict verification was conducted to ensure the completeness and accuracy of the data. The control for missing data is no more than 10%. First, the data missing situation was checked; among these, the information on history of hypertension and history of diabetic retinopathy had a missing rate greater than 50%, so this feature was removed before data filling. Using the “aggr function” from the R language’s “VIM” package, the overall missing status of the remaining 24 features was displayed. For the existing missing data, a series of statistical analyses were performed using the “VIM” software package in R. To compensate for the data deficiencies, the “mice” software package was further applied to fill in the missing data, enhancing the reliability of the research results.

Differential analysis

To compare the differences between the DPN group and the NDPN group, independent “t-tests” were used for normally distributed continuous data, expressed as mean ± standard deviation (). For non-normally distributed continuous data, Mann–Whitney test were employed, with median [IQR] used for representation. Non-continuous data were analyzed using Chi-square test, with frequencies (rates) used for presentation. A P value < 0.05 is considered statistically significant. Through differential analysis, clinically significant characteristics associated with DPN were identified, providing a basis for further screening of risk factors for DPN.

Identification of key risk factors using machine learning algorithms

To comprehensively identify key risk factors associated with the development and progression of diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN), two machine learning algorithms were applied: support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE)15 and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression16. These algorithms were used to rank and select the most relevant variables. The intersection of features identified by both methods was determined using a Venn diagram to obtain the final set of key risk factors.

Construction and validation of diagnostic prediction models

To thoroughly investigate the predictive significance of key risk factors, the dataset was randomly divided into a training set and a testing set in a 7:3 ratio. The training set was utilized for model development and optimization, while the testing set was designated for the final validation of the models. Employing the “caret”package, four distinct machine learning algorithms were implemented to construct diagnostic prediction models for DPN17. These algorithms included Gradient Boosting Machines (GBM)18, Generalized Linear Models (GLM)19, Random Forest (RF)20, and Support Vector Machines (SVM)21. Each algorithm utilized the diagnostic outcome of DPN as the response variable and was trained and optimized using the data from the training set. To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the constructed models, the “DALEX” package was employed. The four models were subsequently analyzed using the testing set, with assessments conducted through cumulative Residual distribution plots, Boxplots, and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves to evaluate the models’ predictive accuracy, calibration, and classification capabilities 22. Ultimately, the model demonstrating the highest performance was selected as the final predictive model for the diagnosis of DPN, with the objective of offering a more accurate diagnostic tool for clinical application.

Construction and identification of the optimal diagnostic prediction model

In this study, four clinical diagnostic models were successfully developed utilizing four distinct machine learning algorithms: GBM, GLM, RF, and SVM. The predictive accuracy, calibration, and classification capabilities of these models were rigorously assessed through cumulative Residual distribution plots (illustrated in Fig. 6), Boxplots (depicted in Fig. 7), and ROC curves (presented in Fig. 8). The findings from these evaluations are summarized in Fig. 9.The area under the ROC curve (AUC) values for the four diagnostic prediction models are as follows: GBM yielded AUC values of 0.924 (95% CI 0.905–0.943), 0.913 (95% CI 0.881–0.946); GLM yielded AUC values of 0.881 (95% CI 0.855–0.908), 0.871 (95% CI 0.829–0.913);RF yielded AUC values of 1 (95% CI 0.990–1.000), 0.904 (95% CI 0.869–0.94); and SVM yielded AUC values of 0.884 (95% CI 0.858–0.909), 0.889 (95% CI 0.85–0.927) for the training set and validation set respectively. Notably, the RF model demonstrated the lowest sample residuals and the highest area under the ROC curve in both the training and testing datasets, indicating superior diagnostic performance. Consequently, we identified the Random Forest model as the optimal diagnostic prediction model.

External validation of the diagnostic models

In order to further verify the generalization ability and stability of the model, 123 patients with type 2 diabetes hospitalized in the Department of Endocrinology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University from June 2024 to December 2024 were collected as an external validation set, including 89 cases in DPN group and 34 cases in NDPN group.The same preprocessing and quality control procedures applied to the training set were implemented. The selected key clinical features included age, duration of diabetes mellitus, urinary uric acid, blood urea, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and 2-h postprandial glucose (2hPG).These variables were used to validate the predictive models, which were assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. All models achieved area under the curve (AUC) values exceeding 0.7, indicating acceptable to excellent diagnostic performance (Fig. 10). Specifically, the AUCs in the external validation set were as follows: GBM, 0.915 (95% CI 0.897–0.932); GLM, 0.891 (95% CI 0.869–0.913); RF, 0.953 (95% CI 0.940–0.966); and SVM, 0.893 (95% CI 0.872–0.914). Notably, the RF model demonstrated the highest AUC, corroborating training set results and confirming its superiority among the evaluated algorithms.Although both cohorts were derived from the same institution, differences in time period may have led to variations in certain clinical indicators such as glycemic control and medication use. Nonetheless, the models demonstrated consistent performance across datasets, highlighting their robust adaptability to potential clinical variability.

Model optimization and overfitting evaluation

In this study, the original Random Forest model achieved an AUC of 1.000 on the training dataset. While this result reflects the model’s excellent discriminatory ability on the training data, it also raises potential concerns regarding overfitting, which may limit its applicability to real-world clinical settings. To address this issue, we conducted a comprehensive evaluation and optimization of the model from multiple perspectives, including model structure simplification, feature dimension control, statistical validation, and external dataset testing, with the aim of ensuring robust generalizability and clinical utility.We optimized the original model by constraining its complexity, setting the maximum tree depth to 6 (max_depth = 6) and the minimum number of samples per leaf node to 10 (min_samples_leaf = 10). After simplification, the model’s AUC in the training set decreased to 0.962, while the AUC in the validation set remained at 0.867, indicating stable discriminative performance even under reduced complexity. The predicted probability distributions for both the training and validation sets in the optimized model are shown (Fig. 11). The distributions show a high degree of overlap without evidence of collapse or imbalance, further supporting the model’s generalizability and stability across datasets.For statistical validation of model stability, we performed 1,000 bootstrap resampling iterations on the AUC values of both the training and validation sets. The resulting 95% confidence intervals overlapped substantially (Fig. 12), indicating no significant divergence between datasets. Furthermore, the calibration curve demonstrated good agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes (Fig. 13), highlighting the reliability and interpretability of the model. Additionally, DeLong’s test revealed a p-value of 0.748, confirming no statistically significant difference between the AUCs, thus providing further evidence against overfitting.Moreover, the optimized model demonstrated excellent performance on an independent external validation dataset (Fig. 14), with an AUC of 0.935, accuracy of 86.3%, sensitivity of 93.0%, specificity of 75.6%, and precision of 85.9%, indicating robust discriminative ability on unseen clinical samples. To further enhance model stability and interpretability, we employed both SVM-RFE and LASSO for feature selection prior to modeling, incorporating only the seven intersecting core variables in the final model. This approach minimized the influence of high-dimensional redundant variables.In summary, although the original model showed a perfect AUC on the training data, subsequent model simplification, cross-validation, external validation, and statistical testing confirmed that the model possesses strong robustness and generalization ability. The observed AUC of 1.000 is therefore more likely attributable to the high discriminative power of the selected features rather than to overfitting.

Discussion

Recent studies have reported a rising incidence of diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) in China, which has substantially increased the burden on both patients and healthcare systems23. In its early stages, DPN is often asymptomatic and frequently goes undetected. Once established, the resulting nerve damage is typically irreversible. Therefore, identifying clinical indicators and key risk factors for early detection is critical.In the present study, multiple machine learning algorithms were employed to screen for DPN-associated risk factors and to construct an optimal diagnostic model. The results indicate that clinical features such as age, duration of diabetes mellitus, 2-h postprandial glucose (2hPG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), blood urea, and urinary uric acid are significantly associated with the development of DPN in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). These factors may exert a more pronounced effect in Xinjiang, potentially due to the region’s unique dietary and lifestyle characteristics⁹.Among the models developed, the random forest (RF) diagnostic model demonstrated the highest predictive performance and may serve as an effective clinical tool for the early detection and intervention of DPN.

Advancing age is a well-documented risk factor for diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN). As individuals age, the regenerative and reparative capacity of the nervous system declines. This includes a reduction in myelination and a decrease in nerve conduction velocity, rendering peripheral nerves more susceptible to injury. In addition, elderly patients commonly present with comorbid conditions such as hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and atherosclerosis, which further exacerbate microvascular pathology. These changes impair neural blood supply and accelerate the progression of peripheral neuropathy. Sandi et al24.reported a significant association between increasing age and the risk of DPN. Similarly, findings from an epidemiological study on diabetic complications25 and a survey conducted in Myanmar26 also identified age as the most important risk factor for DPN, consistent with the current study’s results.This study also identified diabetes duration as an independent risk factor for DPN. As the duration of diabetes increases, the likelihood of developing DPN rises correspondingly. Chronic hyperglycemia plays a central role in DPN pathogenesis by initiating a cascade of harmful processes, including oxidative stress, chronic inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction, which ultimately lead to nerve fiber damage and impaired function. A cross-sectional study further supported a strong positive correlation between diabetes duration and DPN prevalence27. Amelia et al28. Observed that patients with a diabetes history longer than five years had a markedly higher risk of developing DPN. Additional studies29,30have shown that long-standing diabetes contributes to cumulative nerve damage over time, findings which are corroborated by our own.Postprandial glucose levels, particularly 2-h postprandial glucose (2hPG), are critical indicators of glycemic control. Elevated 2hPG has been strongly linked to the development of microvascular complications in T2DM patients. In our analysis, 2hPG was identified as a significant risk factor for DPN. This is in agreement with prior findings from multivariate logistic regression analyses31 and the work of Wu et a32, both of which demonstrated a close association between postprandial hyperglycemia and DPN risk.Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) has also been implicated in the pathogenesis of DPN. Elevated LDL-C can undergo oxidative modification to form oxidized LDL (ox-LDL), a highly pro-inflammatory and cytotoxic compound. Ox-LDL contributes to endothelial dysfunction, impairs microcirculation, and reduces blood flow to nerve tissue. It is also taken up by macrophages to form foam cells, contributing to atherosclerotic plaque formation and further compromising neural perfusion. Nerve tissue is highly sensitive to ischemia and hypoxia; prolonged insufficient blood supply may result in fiber degeneration and functional loss, thereby contributing to DPN. Previous studies33–37, including a large-scale investigation in Taiwan, have consistently demonstrated that elevated LDL-C is an independent risk factor for DPN, in line with our findings.Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), a key indicator of renal function, was also found to be closely associated with DPN in our study. Declining eGFR can result in the accumulation of uremic toxins, such as blood urea nitrogen and creatinine, which can exert direct or indirect neurotoxic effects. Zhang et al.38, in a cohort of 1,059 T2DM patients, reported that eGFR levels were significantly lower in those with DPN. Our results similarly demonstrated that lower eGFR values were associated with a higher risk of DPN, even within the clinically normal range. This finding emphasizes the importance of early renal function monitoring to prevent or delay neuropathic complications. A meta-analysis also confirmed the inverse relationship between eGFR and DPN risk39,40.Lastly, blood urea-a marker of protein metabolism and renal clearance—was found to be significantly associated with DPN in this study. Urea is the final product of nitrogen metabolism, synthesized in the liver and excreted by the kidneys. Elevated urea levels may indicate high protein intake, reduced renal clearance, or both. Given the predominance of high-protein diets in the Xinjiang region, this factor may be of particular regional relevance.

Although the Random Forest model achieved a perfect AUC of 1.000 on the training dataset, we believe this result does not stem from overfitting but from the strong discriminative capacity of the selected features. To verify this, we performed several evaluations. First, we reconstructed a simplified RF model with reduced depth and leaf size (max_depth = 6, min_samples_leaf = 10), which still maintained high performance (AUC = 0.962 in training and 0.867 in validation). Second, we applied bootstrapping and DeLong’s test, which revealed no significant differences in AUC distributions between the datasets (P = 0.748), supporting model stability. Third, the external validation dataset yielded an AUC of 0.935, accuracy of 86.3%, and sensitivity of 93.0%, demonstrating strong generalization. Moreover, we used a conservative feature selection strategy (SVM-RFE and LASSO intersection), avoiding high-dimensional overfitting. Therefore, the perfect AUC observed in training likely reflects the robustness of the selected clinical features, rather than overfitting artifacts.The RF model achieved an AUC of 1.000 (95% CI 0.990–1.000) in the training set, indicating excellent discriminative ability. However, given the potential risk of overfitting, we conducted additional validation with simplified models and external datasets, all of which demonstrated consistently high AUC values 0.935, supporting the robustness of our feature selection and model architecture.

This study aimed to investigate the risk factors associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) in Xinjiang, China, and to develop a diagnostic prediction model to support clinical decision-making. A total of 1,001 patients were retrospectively enrolled from the Department of Endocrinology at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University between January 2023 and January 2024. Through the application of multiple machine learning algorithms, key clinical predictors of DPN were identified and an externally validated prediction model was constructed.The significance and innovations of this study can be summarized as follows: (1) Geographical Specificity:To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate DPN risk factors specifically among T2DM patients in Xinjiang. By addressing a regional research gap, the study provides valuable epidemiological and clinical insights for improving local healthcare strategies. (2) Comprehensive Risk Factor Analysis and Model Development: Seven clinical variables were comprehensively evaluated, and four machine learning algorithms—gradient boosting machine (GBM), generalized linear model (GLM), random forest (RF), and support vector machine (SVM)—were employed to construct and validate the diagnostic model. This multifactorial and algorithm-based approach enhances the model’s robustness, predictive accuracy, and generalizability.

Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations that suggest directions for future research.First, although the sample size was substantial (n = 1001) and patients were recruited from across Xinjiang, the data were derived from a single-center cohort, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future studies should aim to validate the model in larger, multicenter cohorts to improve external applicability. Second, the absence of long-term follow-up data restricts the ability to assess the model’s predictive performance over time. Longitudinal studies are necessary to evaluate the model’s temporal stability and predictive utility in clinical practice.Third, unmeasured confounding variables may have affected the model’s accuracy. Further investigation of additional factors—including genetic, dietary, and lifestyle-related variables—could enhance the model’s precision and clinical relevance.

Conclusion

In summary, this study successfully identified key risk factors for DPN and developed a validated diagnostic prediction model using machine learning algorithms. The random forest model demonstrated high accuracy and reliability, providing a valuable tool for early identification and treatment of DPN. Future work will aim to optimize and expand the model’s application to improve diabetes-related complication management.It is important to note that this predictive model was developed based on a population from Xinjiang, China, where genetic background, dietary patterns, and environmental factors may differ from those in other regions. Therefore, before applying this model to other populations or clinical settings, further external validation across diverse cohorts is recommended to ensure its generalizability and reliability.

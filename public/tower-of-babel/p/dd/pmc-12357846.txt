TN5000: An Ultrasound Image Dataset for Thyroid Nodule Detection and Classification
Authors: Huan Zhang, Qianglin Liu, Xiaolin Han, Lijuan Niu, Weidong Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Accurate diagnosis of thyroid nodules using ultrasonography is a highly valuable, but challenging task. With the emergence of artificial intelligence, deep learning based methods can provide assistance to radiologists, whose performance heavily depends on the quantity and quality of training data, but current ultrasound image datasets for thyroid nodule either directly utilize the TI-RADS assessments as labels or are not publicly available. Faced with these issues, an open-access ultrasound image dataset for thyroid nodule detection and classification is proposed, i.e. the TN5000, which comprises 5,000 B-mode ultrasound images of thyroid nodule, as well as complete annotations and biopsy confirmations by expert radiologists. Additionally, the statistical characteristics of this proposed dataset have been analyzed clearly, some baseline methods for the detection and classification of thyroid nodules are recommended as the benchmark, along with their evaluation results. To our best knowledge, TN5000 is the largest open-access ultrasound image dataset of thyroid nodule with professional labeling, and is the first ultrasound image dataset designed both for the thyroid nodule detection and classification. These kinds of images with annotations can contribute to analyze the intrinsic properties of thyroid nodules and to determine the necessity of FNA biopsy, which are crucial in ultrasound diagnosis.

Background & Summary

Thyroid diseases are the most prevalent endocrine disorders, among them, the incidence rate of thyroid cancer being relatively high1 and becoming increasingly common in the past 30 years2. The characteristic of thyroid nodules is the abnormal lump growing inside the thyroid gland, which is an early symptom of thyroid cancer. To diagnose and treat thyroid nodules at the earliest stage, it is imperative to accurately detect and determine the types of nodules3.

There are two widely accepted techniques for diagnosing thyroid nodules: ultrasound imaging and Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) biopsy, among which the B-mode ultrasound imaging has become the primary clinical choice for thyroid nodule diagnosing, for it is non-invasive, radiation-free, high-sensitive, real-time feedback, and cost-effective. However, the acquisition and interpretation of ultrasound images require a high degree of clinical expertise4. Other factors, such as respiratory motion, subcutaneous fat in obese patients and variation in organ sizes, may also complicate the interpretation process5. And on the other hand, the interpretation performance itself is often affected by the speckle noise in the ultrasound images. In a word, any misreading of thyroid nodules in the ultrasound images may result in unnecessary FNA biopsy, which may further lead to additional trauma for patients.

With the rise of artificial intelligence6–11, deep learning based Computer-Aided Diagnosis (CAD) systems have the potential to alleviate the above issues and to help radiologists in making more accurate diagnoses. However, as the performance of deep learning based methods predominantly depends on the quantity and quality of training data13, ultrasound image dataset with authoritative annotation of the thyroid nodule plays an indispensable role in the training of deep learning models for detecting and diagnosing lesions. Furthermore, it should be emphasized that, even after assessment by the Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS)14, FNA biopsy is still necessary to obtain a definitive diagnosis, while current ultrasound image datasets for thyroid nodule either directly used the results of TI-RADS assessments as their labels or are not publicly available15. Specifically, Digital Database Thyroid Image (DDTI) dataset1 conducted the FNA biopsy confirmation only for the malignant nodules, TN3k dataset2 was annotated by volunteers without any mention of whether the FNA biopsy was performed, Thyroid Nodule Segmentation and Classification in Ultrasound Image (TN-SCUI2020) dataset3, ThyUS2Path dataset15 and the dataset from16 are not publicly available. Given that the FNA biopsy with pathological result is the gold standard for nodule diagnosis even after ultrasound assessment, any uncertainty or deficiency of this critical information may lead to the annotations of these datasets be unreliable, and the deep learning models trained on these datasets may also be untrustworthy. In addition, all the aforementioned ultrasound image datasets for thyroid nodule as well as the Stanford Thyroid Ultrasound CINE clips dataset17, were designed either only for the classification or segmentation task as in Table 1, and their statistical characteristics have not been given or analyzed clearly. Finally, it is usually difficult to evaluate the efficiency of detection and classification methods, as no established benchmark is available so far18.

Faced with the above issues of dataset and benchmark in the thyroid nodule detection and classification, an open-access ultrasound image dataset for thyroid nodule detection and classification, i.e. TN5000, is proposed in this paper, which comprises 5,000 B-mode ultrasound images of thyroid nodule, as well as complete annotations and biopsy confirmations of thyroid nodules from expert radiologists. Additionally, the statistical characteristics of this proposed dataset have been analyzed clearly, some baseline methods for the detection and classification of thyroid nodules are recommended, along with the evaluation results of the baseline methods. To the best of our knowledge, TN5000 is the largest open-access ultrasound image dataset of thyroid nodule with professional labeling, and is the first ultrasound image dataset designed both for the thyroid nodule detection and classification tasks. We believe that, these kinds of images with annotations can contribute to analyzing the intrinsic properties of thyroid nodules and determining whether FNA biopsy is necessary, which are crucial in the ultrasound diagnosis of thyroid nodules. Methods

The ultrasound images of thyroid nodule were provided by the Cancer Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, located in Beijing, which is the largest nodule diagnostic center in Asian, and conducts thousands of thyroid ultrasound examinations associated to FNA biopsy every year. All the ultrasound image samples have been manually examined and adequately anonymized, to eliminate the risk of disclosing patient privacy via image. Furthermore, all identifying information, including names, genders, ages, pathological numbers and other detailed information of biopsy results for patients have been erased, to protect their privacy information. Furthermore, the Ethics Committee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College has consented that, the research is a retrospective study and this retrospective research will not alter the clinical protocol or examination process of patients, which complies with ethical requirements and has been approved with the formal approval number of 24/243-4523. Additionally, the permission has been obtained to distribute this dataset under the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) License, which allows for the use of research or education purposes.

Pipeline for data collection and annotation

Data collection and annotation pipeline of the sample data are illustrated as in Fig. 1. Initially, the patient lies supine on an examine bed and the radiologists position an ultrasound probe over the region of thyroid, and move the probe back and forth until the images of suspicious thyroid lesions are captured15. During the data collection stage, the commonly used ultrasound machine, such as GE Logiq E9 and S7, with a frequency range at 5–12 MHz or 8–15 MHz has been used. The utilization of these kinds of ultrasound machines aims to increase the accuracy of ultrasonic diagnosis, which also leads to an increasing in the diversity of imaging machine types. Then, 5,000 ultrasound images are selected from the collected images totally over 30,000, based on the following criteria: (1) Each image must contain at least one discernible thyroid nodule area; (2) Only one representative image among the images of the same perspective of the patient is reserved, to prevent the risk of data leakage and ensure the dataset can be partitioned at the patient level; (3) Only pure ultrasound images without additional diagnostic measurement, such as contrast-enhanced ultrasound, are selected2. After that, a three-stage annotation protocol is cautiously conducted, by first generating the preliminary labels, e.g. the primary location of thyroid nodule on a given ultrasound image is annotated firstly by the junior radiologists, and then a senior radiologist with over 30 years’ experience further verifies the annotation for each ultrasound image. Finally, the types of the annotation of the nodule, i.e. the malignant or benign, is determined according to FNA biopsy and pathological results, which is far more reliable than that only be confirmed by the assessments of TI-RADS.Fig. 1Pipeline for data collection, selection and annotation of the sample data in TN5000.

Train-validation-test split

Although it is fair to compare model performance exclusively on the test set, it could be computationally intensive to compare the impact of the train-validation split. To facilitate standardized comparisons, an official train-validation-test split is also provided for our TN5000 dataset. More detailed, the dataset has been randomly split at the image level with a ratio of 7:1:2 into a training-validation-test set. Thus, the dataset to-be-released will contain a training set of 3,500 ultrasound images, a validation set of 500 ultrasound images, and a test set of 1,000 ultrasound images. We should also mention that, since all data in this dataset will be publicly available, the five-fold cross-validation or any other data split methods can also be used in actual algorithm development for some specific purposes.

Technical Validation

Due to the detection and classification of thyroid nodule can contribute to conducting an accurate diagnosis related to the thyroid diseases and to avoid the unnecessary FNA biopsy or repeated FNA biopsy, here, a thyroid nodule detection and classification benchmark is proposed, in which several most representative and influential deep learning based methods are provided and compared, to facilitate the researches in CAD, medical image analysis and related fields.

Baseline methods

Detection and classification of thyroid nodule in the ultrasound images are two major separated processes, for diagnosis and treatment of the thyroid cancer. As several of our previous works have already discussed the results of enhanced classification on relevant dataset23–26, here, we will focus on the multi-targets detection methods, to simultaneously perform detection and classification. The SSD27, Faster RCNN28, DETR29 and DiffusionDet30 are selected as the baseline detection and classification methods for the thyroid nodules in ultrasound images. Among them, the most representative region-free object detection method, i.e. the SSD27, and the most representative region-proposal-based detection method, i.e. the Faster RCNN28, have demonstrated their impressive detection accuracy across different object detection tasks; the most representative transformer-based detection method, i.e. the DETR29, has exhibited outstanding detection accuracy and run-time performance without requiring extensive optimization; and the most representative diffusion-based detection method, i.e. the DiffusionDet30, is regarded as state-of-the-art and inspiring in this field.

Implementation details

Pretrained ResNet50 and ResNet10131 are selected as the backbone for these baseline methods, which consists of 1 convolutional layer and 4 residual blocks. Each of them is followed by a max pooling layer to down sample the feature maps. To make a fair comparation, the input sizes are set to 718 × 500 × 3. Parameter settings for the training of all the models, such as optimizer, learning rate schedule and number of epochs, keep consistent with the optimized implementation of these methods in mmdetection32, among which the batch size is set to 2 per GPU. The reason for such parameter settings is that, mmdetection implementation has already accounted for diverse complexities of network structures and conducted a series of optimizations on the training parameters, which is quite suitable for our dataset.

Data augmentation

To further improve the accuracy of detection and classification, and to avoid the risk of overfitting, data augmentation emerges as a considerable strategy. Apart from the traditional flip or scale data augmentation methods, advanced methods, such as Random Erasing data augmentation method33, BIRD data augmentation method13 and POBIT data augmentation method22, can also be applied to pre-process the training data in detection and classification task, for the thyroid nodule in ultrasound images.

Based on the above experiments, it can be summarized that, this detection and classification benchmark could facilitate the diagnoses of thyroid nodules from ultrasound images more accurate and reproducible. Furthermore, all these baseline methods could provide a unique opportunity for researchers from different backgrounds, e.g. image processing, computer vision and artificial intelligence communities, to develop and compare their algorithms in an impartial way.

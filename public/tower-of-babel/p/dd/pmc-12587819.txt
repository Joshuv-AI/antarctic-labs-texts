Qing-Re-Xiao-Zheng-yi-qi formula protects renal function by stabilizing lysosomal membranes and inhibiting NLRP3-inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis in diabetic kidney disease
Authors: Lei Tian, Lin Wang, Yutong Wang, Weijing Liu, Wenjing Zhao, Yaoxian Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundQing-Re-Xiao-Zheng-Yi-Qi formula (QRXZYQF), based on the "Shen-Luo-Zheng-Jia" principles of traditional Chinese medicine, has been reported to reduce 24-hour urinary total protein in diabetic kidney disease (DKD) patients, slow disease progression, and improve podocyte injury. This study aims to explore the mechanisms of QRXZYQF in improving podocyte injury.MethodsDiabetes was induced in male C57BL/6J mice by intraperitoneal injection of streptozotocin (STZ). After 12 weeks of QRXZYQF, blood glucose, blood urea nitrogen, serum creatinine, microalbumin, and the urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) were monitored. Renal pathological changes were evaluated using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), Masson, and periodic acid–Schiff (PAS) staining. RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) was performed to identify differences in renal mRNA expression and enrichment pathways. The involvement of autophagy–lysosomal and NOD-like receptor pathways was examined by western blotting and immunofluorescence in renal tissues and cultured podocytes.ResultsFollowing 12 weeks of QRXZYQF, renal function improved and ECM accumulation and glomerulosclerosis were markedly reduced. Subsequently, RNA-Seq analysis showed that the autophagy–lysosomal and NOD-like receptor signaling pathways were the potential pathways involved in the mechanism of QRXZYQF. Moreover, QRXZYQF reduced the levels of NLRP3, apoptosis-associated speck-like protein (ASC), as well as Caspase-1 in vitro. Furthermore, we performed interventions using the lysosomal membrane-permeabilizing agent (L-leucyl-L-leucine-O-methylester) and found that QRXZYQF inhibits NLRP3 overexpression by protecting lysosomal membranes and preventing the leakage of Cathepsin B (CB) into the cytoplasm.ConclusionsQRXZYQF inhibits NLRP3-mediated podocyte pyroptosis by stabilizing lysosomal membranes, providing insights into its protective mechanism and potential therapeutic targets for DKD.

Introduction

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is the dominating reason for chronic kidney failure in patients with diabetes, ultimately necessitating kidney replacement therapy [1,2]. Currently, over 537 million people worldwide have diabetes, a number projected to rise to 700 million by 2045 [3]. Among them, approximately 30%–40% are expected to progress to DKD [4]. Thus, Innovative strategies to prevent or slow down the advancement of DKD are urgently needed. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has demonstrated significant clinical efficacy in managing chronic diseases, with relatively few adverse effects. Qing-Re-Xiao-Zheng-Yi-Qi formula (QRXZYQF), a clinically validated TCM prescription, has demonstrated renoprotective effects by protecting podocytes, reducing proteinuria, and attenuating glomerulosclerosis, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic strategy for DKD [5].

DKD alters almost all intrinsic kidney cells, leading to pathological processes comprising diffuse thickening of the glomerular basement membrane (GBM) and mesangial matrix proliferation and expansion, which result in glomerulosclerosis as well as renal dysfunction [6,7]. Podocytes are specialized epithelial cells that line the outer glomerular capillary wall and maintain the integrity of the glomerular filtration barrier [8,9]. podocyte injury and loss are significant factors contributing to proteinuria and glomerulosclerosis in DKD progression [10,11]. Therefore, therapy aimed at protecting podocytes is considered to be important for treating DKD.

Pyroptosis represents an emerging inflammatory cell death pathway that is closely related to inflammatory response and has a crucial role in podocyte damage in DKD [8,12]. As a principal activation mechanism of pyroptosis, NLRP3-inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis is an imperative step in the activation of inflammatory response [13,14]. The NLRP3 inflammasome, situated within the cytoplasm, comprises the pattern recognition receptor NLRP3, the apoptosis-associated speck-like protein (ASC), as well as caspase-1 [15,16]. When its assembly is triggered, it can cause the caspase-1 (pro-caspase-1) precursor to be split into its activated form (cleaved caspase-1) [17,18]. Cleaved caspase-1 serves as an operational part of the NLRP3 inflammasome, cleaving IL-1β and IL-18 precursors (pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18) converted into mature states (mIL-1β and mIL-18), thereby facilitating the secretion of inflammatory mediators and exacerbating the onset of inflammatory reactions [19,20]. Dysregulation of this pathway has been implicated in multiple chronic inflammatory diseases, including DKD [21,22].

Autophagy provides an important counterbalance by degrading inflammasome components through the autophagy–lysosomal pathway (ALP), thus limiting aberrant inflammasome activation and suppressing pyroptosis [23–25]. Lysosomes, as the terminal degradative organelles of ALP, are essential for the breakdown of macromolecules, damaged organelles, and pathogens[26]. Several studies have indicated that diabetes could lead to reduced lysosomal enzyme activity and decreased lysosomal albumin degradation [27–29]. Some etiological factors in DKD generate lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP) and lysosomal dysfunction, which block the downstream pathway of autophagy, resulting in podocyte lesions [26]. Importantly, LMP-induced release of cathepsin B (CB, a lysosomal protease) into the cytoplasm has been shown to further activate the NLRP3 inflammasome and exacerbate pyroptosis [30,31].

QRXZYQF is a potent remedy formulated according to the principles of ‘Shen-Luo-Zheng-Jia’ in traditional Chinese medicine and has been used in the clinical management of DKD for many years [32]. Our previous clinical studies have confirmed that QRXZYQF could markedly reduce 24-h urinary overall protein in patients with DKD and delay disease progression [33]. We examined the primary constituents of QRXZYQF freeze-dried powder utilizing the Vanquish UHPLC™ system and initially identified 27 elements, including geniposidic acid, protocatechuic acid-4-glucoside, pinoresinol diglucoside, geniposide, calycosin 7-O-glucopyranoside, glycitein, and soon [34]. Previous studies confirmed that QRXZYQF could reduce body weight, decrease proteinuria, improve glycemic and lipid metabolism disorders, ameliorate glomerulosclerosis and renal interstitial fibrosis, and reduce collagen IV as well as transforming growth factor-β1(TGF-β1) expression in a DKD rat model [34]. In addition, we found that QRXZYQF could effectively improve podocyte injury. Based on previous studies, QRXZYQF can effectively treat DKD, but its potential mode of operation remains unclear. This study aims to investigate the protective effects of QRXZYQF on podocyte injury in DKD, hypothesizing that its mechanism involves stabilization of lysosomal membranes and inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

Method

Animal experimental design and reagents

This study utilized male C57BL/6J mice aged eight weeks (weighing 22–24 g), obtained from Sibeifu Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (certificate number: SCXK2019-0010). The Ethics Committee of Beijing University of Chinese Medicine approved all experimental protocols (No. BUCM-4-2020110204-4167), and all animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines and approved by the institutional animal care and use committee (IACUC). Experimental procedures complied with the ‘Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals’ published by the National Institutes of Health. The mice were housed in pathogen-free facilities in cage units, with the room temperature maintained between 23 °C and 26 °C, relative humidity set at 60% to 65%, and a 12-hour light/dark cycle. They were provided unrestricted access to ample food and filtered tap water. In this study, Cage position effects were not systematically evaluated, animals were randomized at the individual level to minimize potential rack-related confounding.

Based on our previous studies, 10 mice were allocated to each group, with a total of 40 mice used in the experiment [34]. Randomization was used to allocate experimental units to the control, model, and treatment groups to minimize selection bias. A computer-generated random number generator was employed to create the randomization sequence, and mice were assigned to the four groups based on this sequence: control (n = 10), model (n = 10), model + QRXZYQF (n = 10), and model + dapagliflozin (n = 10). To establish the DKD model, STZ (40 mg/kg/day) was administered via intraperitoneal injection for five consecutive days. Blood was collected from the tail vein one week after the STZ injection to appraise blood glucose levels. A blood glucose >16.7 mmol/L for three consecutive days indicated the successful establishment of the diabetes model. After establishing the model, QRXZYQF and dapagliflozin was administered for 12 weeks. For the dapagliflozin group, one mouse was excluded from the analysis due to an unexpected death caused by a technical issue during the experiment. As a result, the final analysis included 9 mice in the dapagliflozin group and 10 mice in each of the other three groups. No data points were excluded during the analysis. The allocation sequence was concealed during the allocation phase, and Lei Tian and Yutong Wang employed blinding throughout the experiment, outcome assessment, and data analysis to minimize bias. QRXZYQF consists of six individual traditional Chinese medicinal materials (Niu Bang Zi, Huang Shu Kui Hua, Hai Zao, Shui Zhi, Huang Qi, Du Zhong Table 1). QRXZYQF was prepared from traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) decoction pieces in accordance with the Pharmacopoeia of the People’s Republic of China (2020 edition). All herbal materials were sourced from a certified TCM pharmacy (Herbal Dispensary, Dongzhimen Hospital Affiliated to Beijing University of Chinese Medicine). The herbs were weighed according to the prescribed formulation ratio, soaked in distilled water for 30 min, and then decocted twice (the first decoction for 1 h and the second for 45 min). The two decoctions were combined, filtered, concentrated under reduced pressure, and stored at 4 °C. A fresh batch was prepared every two days. QRXZYQF was administered to mice via oral gavage at a dose of 15.6 g/kg/day, which was calculated based on the clinical human dosage of 115 g/day of crude herbal materials. The mouse dosage was derived using a standard body surface area conversion factor of 9.1, a widely accepted approach in traditional Chinese medicine pharmacology [35,36]. The aforementioned QRXZYQF dosage has demonstrated optimal anti-ulcer efficacy in previous investigations conducted by our research team [37]. Body weight and random blood glucose levels were tested every week. Thereafter, urine samples were gathered from metabolic cages. Blood specimens were centrifugated at 4 °C and 3,000 rpm for 10 min, and the separated components were stored at −80 °C. Kidney tissue samples were fixed in 4% formaldehyde, whereas the rest were preserved at −80 °C for further investigations.

Measurement of serum and urine biochemical parameters

We utilized a mouse albumin ELISA kit (ab108792, Abcam, OR, USA) as well as a creatinine assay kit (C011-2-1) to assess urinary albumin as well as creatinine levels, respectively. The outcomes were utilized to calculate the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR). Furthermore, all samples underwent centrifugation at a speed of 3,000 revolutions per minute (rpm) for approximately 15 min to isolate serum. Serum creatinine (Scr) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels were determined via a creatinine assay kit (C011-2-1) as well as a urea assay kit (C013-1-1) from Nanjing Jiancheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China). All protocols were executed following the guidelines provided by the manufacturer.

Assessment of kidney histopathology

Kidney tissue specimens were stabilized in 4% formaldehyde for approximately 48 hs, subsequently, dehydration and immobilization in paraffin. Paraffin-embedded kidney tissue blocks were sectioned into thin slices (2 μm) and underwent standard staining procedures involving hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), periodic acid–Schiff (PAS), as well as Masson staining. H&E staining was utilized to assess overall morphological alterations in the kidneys, Masson’s staining was employed to examine collagen accumulation levels and interstitial damage, and PAS staining was utilized to characterize glomerulosclerosis. Grading of glomerulosclerosis was conducted according to established criteria from previous studies.

Cell culture

Mouse podocyte clone 5 (MPC-5) was supplied by Prof. Weijing Liu from Dongzhimen Hospital, Beijing University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China. Podocytes were cultured at 33 °C in RPMI 1640 medium fortified with 10% fetal bovine serum and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Additionally, recombinant mouse interferon (IFN)-γ (315-05-20, PeproTech, NJ, USA) was included to stimulate cell proliferation. Following this, podocytes were maintained at 37 °C in a CO2 incubator in RPMI 1640 medium without IFN-γ for 10–14 days to induce cell differentiation. Upon reaching approximately 80% confluency, podocytes were collected for in vitro experiments. To ensure consistency in concentration and sterility for in vitro experiments, a lyophilized powder of the QRXZYQF aqueous extract was utilized. The freeze-dried powder was dissolved in sterile culture medium, filtered through a 0.22 μm microporous membrane, and prepared at final concentrations of 1, 50, 100, 200, and 500 μg/mL for podocyte treatment. Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay to determine the optimal concentration. Based on the results, 50 μg/mL was selected for subsequent experiments to ensure both cellular safety and pharmacological efficacy. To better mimic the inflammatory microenvironment of DKD and to enhance NLRP3 inflammasome activation, high glucose was combined with LPS treatment. Differentiated podocytes were divided into the following treatment groups: normal glucose (5.5 mM), high glucose with LPS (HG, 30 mM; LPS, 10 μg/mL), and high glucose with LPS plus QRXZYQF (50 μg/mL). All treatments were administered for 24 h. Each experimental condition was replicated in at least three independent podocyte cultures to ensure reproducibility and reliability of the findings.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining was conducted to evaluate the levels of α-SMA and FN. Paraffin-embedded tissue sections (2 μm thick) were deparaffinized, followed by immersion in xylene and a series of ethanol gradients. Subsequently, the sections were treated with 3% H2O2 at ambient temperature for 15 min. After blocking with 10% goat serum (ZLI-9056, ZSGB-BIO Co., Beijing, China) for 1 h at 37 °C, the sections were washed 3 times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 10 min each. Primary antibodies, including anti-α-SMA (Proteintech, 14395-1-AP, Wuhan, China; 1:1000) and anti-fibronectin (Proteintech, 66042-1-Ig, Wuhan, China; 1:300), were incubated overnight at 4 °C. On a subsequent day, the sections underwent 3 washes with PBS for 10 min each. Subsequently, after incubation with secondary antibodies, the slides were maintained at 37 °C for 1 h and then subjected to diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate for visualization, followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted in lysis buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors and quantified by BCA. The resulting supernatant was blended with the sample buffer and subjected to boiling for 15 min. Subsequently, the proteins were separated using sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis as well as subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. 5% nonfat milk was utilized to block the membranes at ambient temperature for 1 h, followed by three washes with PBS for 10 min each. Next, the membranes were placed in a primary antibody including anti-NLRP3(Proteintech, Mouse,68102-1-Ig-50μL,1:1000, Wuhan, China), anti-Caspase-1(Cell Signaling Technology, #24232, Rabbit, 1:1000, American), anti-ASC(Cell Signaling Technology, #67824, Rabbit, 1:1000, American), anti-Atg7(Cell Signaling Technology #8558 (D12B11) Rabbit,1:1000, American), anti-Becilin1(Cell Signaling Technology #3495 (D40C5) Rabbit,1:1000, American), anti-P62(Cell Signaling Technology #39749 (D1Q5S) Rabbit,1:1000, American),ant-GAPDH(Proteintech, Mouse, 60004-1-Ig-100μL,1:50000, Wuhan, China), anti-β-actin(Proteintech, Mouse, 66009-1-Ig-100μL, 1:20000, Wuhan, China) dilution solution overnight at 4 °C on a shaker. The following day, the primary antibodies were removed, as well as the membranes underwent three additional washes with PBS for 10 min each. Afterward, the membranes were immersed in horseradish-peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies including (LI-COR, Goat anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) IRDye 800CW, LI-COR926-32211, 1:15000, American) or (LI-COR, Goat anti-Mouse IgG (H + L) IRDye 800CW, LI-COR 926–32210 1:15000, American) or (Applygen, Goat anti-rabbit IgG, 1:5000, C1309, China) or (Applygen, Goat anti-mouse IgG, 1:5000, C1308, China)for 1 h at ambient temperature. The ladders used in the western blot loading process were as follows: Ladder 1: WJ103, Shanghai Epizyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China; Ladder 2: WJ107, Shanghai Epizyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China. In the Western blot analysis, protein expression was normalized using either GAPDH or β-actin as internal controls.

Immunofluorescence assay

Sterile slides were positioned in a 12-well plate, and MPC-5 cells were seeded. Following the intervention, the slides were gently rinsed 3 times with PBS for 10 min each. Afterward, the cells were treated with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min to fix them. Next, 1 mL of 0.3% Triton was introduced for permeabilization. Following that, the cells were treated with 1 mL of 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at ambient temperature for 1 h, followed through treatment with the primary antibody overnight at 4 °C. On the subsequent day, the slides underwent 3 washes with PBS for 10 min each. Afterward, the slides were treated with the secondary antibody at ambient temperature in the dark for 1 h. Following that, the secondary antibody was removed, and the slides underwent three washes with PBS for 10 min each. Finally, a mounting medium comprising 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (Invitrogen, USA) was added and placed at ambient temperature in the dark for 15 min. Following this, laser confocal microscopy was employed to visualize the fluorescent signals emitted by the cells.

RNA-seq

RNA-Seq was conducted by BGI Genomics (Shenzhen, China). Differential gene selection was based on the fold change values and q-values (adjusted p-values, corrected p-values). The criteria for significant differential genes were |log2 fold change| > 1 and q < 0.05. Differential genes were subjected to cluster analysis, and heat maps were generated. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis was performed on differential genes, as well as significantly enriched GO terms were identified using a threshold of q < 0.05. Differential gene enrichment analysis of pathways was conducted utilizing the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database to delineate significantly enriched biological processes among the differential genes.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was replicated at least 3 times. Data are depicted as mean ± standard error of the mean. Statistical analyses were conducted through GraphPad Prism 8 software. Two-sided unpaired Student’s t-tests were employed to compare two groups, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to compare three or more groups. Normality as well as homogeneity of variance were assessed before conducting t-tests and ANOVA. Statistical significance was characterized as p < 0.05. The unit of analysis was the individual mouse or an independent culture (biological replicate) as specified in figure legends. And all the results are showed in Supplementary Tables 1–23.

Results

QRXZYQF ameliorates STZ-induced renal dysfunction in model mice

We measured body weight, blood glucose, and biochemical indicators of kidney function (including BUN, Scr, urine microalbumin, and UACR) to validate the therapeutic impact of QRXZYQF on diabetic kidneys. Compared with the control group, model mice exhibited increased body weight as well as blood glucose levels after STZ injection. Upon treatment with QRXZYQF, the body weight and blood glucose significantly decreased (Figure 1A,B). Similar results were also found after treatment with dapagliflozin. At the 16th week, the BUN, Scr, and urine microalbumin levels and UACR remarkably increased in the model group. QRXZYQF and dapagliflozin reduced Scr and BUN levels but significantly reduced urine microalbumin levels and UACR in the model group (Figure 1C–F).

QRXZYQF improves STZ-induced renal pathological injury in model mice

To further assess the renoprotective effects of QRXZYQF, we performed histopathological analyses using H&E, PAS, and Masson staining. As shown in Figure 2A–C, the model group exhibited marked renal injury characterized by glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial fibrosis. Quantitative assessment revealed significantly elevated glomerulosclerosis scores and fibrotic area, indicative of progressive structural deterioration. In contrast, both QRXZYQF and dapagliflozin substantially ameliorated these pathological changes, as reflected by reduced glomerulosclerosis scores and fibrosis indices. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that QRXZYQF effectively mitigates renal injury in diabetic model group mice.

In addition, we assessed the expression levels of extracellular matrix components, involving fibronectin (FN) and alpha-smooth muscle actin(α-SMA), through immunohistochemistry. The findings revealed a notable elevation in α-SMA and FN levels in the kidneys of the model group. QRXZYQF significantly inhibited α-SMA and FN, demonstrating its efficacy in improving renal fibrotic injury (Figure 2D–F).

QRXZYQF regulates the autophagy–lysosomal pathway and inhibits the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway

We performed transcriptomic sequencing based on RNA-Seq technology for mice in the control, model, and QRXZYQF groups to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of QRXZYQF in DKD. When contrasting the model group with the control group, a total of 2,160 genes exhibited significant differential expression, meeting the criteria of adjusted fold change >2 and p < 0.05. Conversely, the QRXZYQF group displayed 1,210 genes with significant differential expression compared to the model group. Specifically, the model group demonstrated 829 genes markedly increased as well as 1,331 genes substantially decreased relative to the control group (Figure 3A). In contrast, QRXZYQF exhibited 512 genes substantially increased and 698 genes substantially decreased compared to the model group (Figure 3B). Enrichment analysis of KEGG pathways further suggested that the substantially differentially regulated genes were enriched in various biochemical metabolic pathways and signal transduction pathways. These pathways involve PPAR signaling pathway, ECM–receptor interaction, focal adhesion, protein digestion and absorption, HIF-1 signaling pathway, autophagy, insulin signaling pathway, cell cycle, NOD-like receptor signaling pathways, as well as the lysosome. Among them, the autophagy–lysosomal and NOD-like receptor signaling pathways were enriched in both CON-VS-Model and Model-VS-QRXZYQF, suggesting that QRXZYQF may exert therapeutic effects on DKD nephrosclerosis by regulating the autophagy–lysosomal pathway and NOD-like receptor signaling pathway (Figure 3C,D).

QRXZYQF limits NLRP3 inflammasome activation and ameliorates pyroptosis and injury in podocytes

To explore the protection of QRXZYQF on podocyte injury, we utilized the Cell Counting Kit-8 assay to evaluate cellular viability. Our findings revealed a notable decrease in the viability of MPC-5 cells exposed to 30 mM HG for 24 h compared to the control group (Figure 4A). QRXZYQF at 100 μg/mL slightly reduced podocyte viability without statistical significance, while QRXZYQF at concentrations of 200 μg/mL and above significantly decreased podocyte viability. To preserve cell viability while maintaining pharmacological relevance, 50/mL was selected for subsequent in vitro experiments (Figure 4B). Furthermore, we employed Annexin-V/propidium iodide (PI) double staining to investigate MPC-5 cell apoptosis and pyroptosis induced by 30 mM HG and 1 μg/mL∼50μg/mL LPS stimulation. The result showed that the apoptotic of MPC-5 podocytes significantly increased when exposed to 30 mM HG and 10 μg/mL∼50μg/mL LPS conditions VS control group (Figure 4C and D). QRXZYQF mitigated the apoptotic (Figure 4E and F). Therefore, this study selects 30 mM HG and 10 μg/mL LPS as the subsequent modeling conditions.

QRXZYQF prevents NLRP3-mediated podocyte pyroptosis by stabilization of lysosomal membrane

Autophagy is a conserved homeostatic pathway that clears damaged proteins and organelles via lysosomal degradation. To determine whether QRXZYQF attenuates podocyte pyroptosis through this axis, we quantified the autophagic flux marker p62/SQSTM1 by western blot. p62 is a selective adaptor that delivers ubiquitinated cargo, including inflammasome components, to autophagosomes. The results revealed an elevation in p62 expression in the HG and LPS groups compared to the control group. QRXZYQF could regulate the accumulation of p62 (Figure 5G and I). Because p62 accumulation might result from increased formation or failure of degradation, we detected the autophagy inducers Atg7 and Beclin1. Data suggested that the expression of either Atg7 or Beclin1 remained unchanged in podocytes of the HG and LPS model groups or QRXZYQF groups (Figure 6A–C). This finding suggested that the accumulation of p62 did not account for autophagic induction and QRXZYQF did not regulate autophagic induction.

The lysosomal-mediated degradation system is a crucial process of autophagy degradation. Previous research has indicated that LMP is the primary cause of lysosomal dysfunction in DKD. Therefore, we performed immunofluorescence staining to lysosome-associated membrane protein 2 (LAMP-2) and CB. The results indicate that in the control group, CB granules were regularly distributed around the nucleus, with most being colocalized with LAMP-2. In the HG combined with the LPS treatment group, we observed irregular and larger cytoplasmic LAMP-2 granules associated with the diffusion of CB, suggesting the leakage of lysosomal contents into the cytoplasm. QRXZYQF suppressed the diffusion of CB in the cytoplasm, with CB granules being regularly distributed around the nucleus and colocalizing with LAMP-2 (Figure 6D). These results further indicated that QRXZYQF might repair lysosomal membranes, suppressing LMP, and inhibiting CB release. Next, we performed interventions using lysosomal membrane-permeabilizing agent (L-leucyl-L-leucine-O-methylester) LLoMe to verify whether NLRP3 mediated podocyte pyroptosis through LMP. Compared to the model group induced by HG combined with LPS, intervention with LLoMe activated the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome, indicating that LMP induces NLRP3 overexpress (Figure 6 E and F). QRXZYQF preserves lysosomal membrane integrity and prevents CB leakage, thereby maintaining autophagic degradation, restraining NLRP3 overexpression, and potentially inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis.

Discussion

The typical pathological features of DKD include enlarged glomeruli, thickening of the GBM, podocyte injury, as well as expansion of the mesangial matrix, leading to progressive development of glomerulosclerosis [38]. The primary factors that trigger the onset of DKD include changes in the morphology and function of glomeruli [39]. Among these pathological changes, podocyte injury represents a pivotal initiating factor in DKD progression. Increasing evidence has highlighted pyroptosis, a distinct form of inflammatory programmed cell death, as a key mechanism linking podocyte dysfunction to proteinuria and glomerulosclerosis [40,41]. Unbiased transcriptomic profiling prioritized the autophagy–lysosomal and NOD-like receptor pathways in both contrasts (CON vs. Model; Model vs. QRXZYQF), which align mechanistically with podocyte pyroptosis. We therefore focused subsequent validation on lysosomal integrity, CB leakage, and NLRP3 activation.

Here we identify that QRXZYQF protects against DKD by stabilizing lysosomal membranes, preventing CB leakage, suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and ultimately attenuating podocyte pyroptosis. Because pyroptosis is a major driver of podocyte injury in DKD and proceeds via classical and nonclassical pathways—with NLRP3 as a principal initiator [42]. In our investigation, the levels of NLRP3, as well as ASC, were increased in an HG- and LPS-induced podocyte injury model. QRXZYQF not only inhibited the levels of NLRP3 and ASC but also suppressed the protein levels of caspase-1. These results suggested that QRXZYQF interferes with the classical NLRP3–caspase-1 pyroptotic pathway.

We next explored the underlying mechanism of QRXZYQF in inhibiting NLRP3-mediated podocyte pyroptosis. Autophagy is essential for regulating cell survival and maintaining cellular homeostasis. On one hand, essential nutrients are recycled through autophagy-dependent degradation and recycling systems. On the other hand, waste products and harmful substances within the body are cleared through autophagic degradation pathways [43]. Previous studies have demonstrated that autophagic degradation is impaired by various pathogenic stimuli in DKD, and that reduced podocyte autophagy is closely associated with glomerulosclerosis during disease progression [44]. In line with this, HG and LPS stimulation significantly increased p62 accumulation in MPC-5 cells, reflecting defective autophagic flux, QRXZYQF markedly reduced p62 levels. Importantly, QRXZYQF did not affect Atg7 or Beclin1 expression, suggesting that its beneficial effect was achieved not by autophagy induction but by enhancing degradation capacity.

The lysosomal-dependent degradation system is a vital node of autophagy degradation. Nevertheless, upon exposure to advanced glycation end product (AGE)-BSA or urinary proteins, LMP is a crucial factor leading to dysfunction in cellular lysosomal function. Previous studies have demonstrated that LMP underlies defective podocyte autophagy in DKD [45]. Disruption of lysosomal integrity by AGEs, oxidative stress, or lipid metabolites leads to cathepsin leakage [26,46]. Consistently, our results revealed that HG combined with LPS induced significant LMP in podocytes, resulting in CB leakage into the cytoplasm and subsequent activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. QRXZYQF preserved lysosomal integrity, reduced CB leakage, and prevented excessive NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

Functionally, QRXZYQF significantly reduced proteinuria, improved renal function, and alleviated mesangial expansion. Compared with dapagliflozin, QRXZYQF exerted superior efficacy in reducing glomerulosclerosis. While previous studies have also demonstrated that dapagliflozin reduces proteinuria and significantly attenuates glomerulosclerosis, with its renoprotective effects potentially attributed to the inhibition of inflammation and oxidative stress [47,48]. QRXZYQF acts through a complementary mechanism involving lysosomal stabilization and inflammasome inhibition. This distinction suggests that QRXZYQF may serve as a promising adjunct to standard therapies, offering additive rather than overlapping benefits.

This study has several limitations. First, Pyroptosis was not evaluated with downstream effectors, including gasdermin D cleavage and IL-1β/IL-18 maturation/quantification. To substantiate the proposed cascade, future work will incorporate these measurements. Second, LMP relied on qualitative immunofluorescence, and quantitative measurement will be pursued in subsequent studies. Moreover, the intervention spanned only 12 weeks with a single dosing regimen, leaving durability, dose–response, and long-term safety unresolved, extended and dose-finding studies are therefore warranted. In addition, RNA-seq interpretation was deliberately confined to the autophagy–lysosomal/NLRP3 axis, subsequent analyses will examine other enriched pathways and incorporate targeted validation. Finally, QRXZYQF is used clinically, the present findings are preclinical, translation will require biomarker-guided pharmacodynamics, multicenter dose-finding, and adequately powered head-to-head or adjunctive randomized trials alongside standard care.

In conclusion, QRXZYQF enhances renal function, alleviates glomerular injury, and inhibits podocyte pyroptosis in STZ-induced diabetic mice. These renoprotective effects are primarily attributed to the stabilization of lysosomal membranes and the inhibition of NLRP3-driven pyroptotic signaling in podocytes.

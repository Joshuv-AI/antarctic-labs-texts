Numerical simulation of horizontal displacement at the top of support piles for ultra-deep foundation pits in silty formations
Authors: Sai Liu, Shenwei Gao, Kang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The difficulty in predicting the horizontal displacement of the support pile top in ultra-deep foundation pits within muddy formations, combined with insufficient consideration of parameter discretization characteristics in existing methods, motivates this study. Taking the Songtao Street Station project of Suzhou Metro Line 8 as a case study, this paper classifies the soil layers and implements corresponding support technologies. First, based on the engineering geological and hydrological conditions, the discrete values of earth pressure, the discrete combination of support structure thickness, and the discrete gradient of the lateral earth pressure coefficient are selected as core discrete variables. Second, a numerical model is constructed using FLAC3D software, and the soil-structure interaction is simplified via the elastic foundation beam method. The deflection differential equation of the support structure is derived to verify the accuracy of the elastic modulus conversion formula and the earth pressure calculation method. Subsequently, the influence of key parameters, such as the lateral earth pressure coefficient and the cohesion of silty clay, on displacement is analyzed. Finally, the reliability of the model is verified using on-site monitoring data from 12 monitoring points throughout the entire construction period. The results indicate that the displacement error between numerical simulation and actual measurement is ≤ 3.3%. When the lateral earth pressure coefficient is 1.0, the horizontal displacement of the pile top is minimized. The safety factor of uniformly thick shotcrete support is 1.8–2.9 times higher than that of non-uniform schemes. Significant creep characteristics are observed during the excavation and sealing of ultra-deep foundation pits in muddy formations. This study provides a quantitative basis for the discrete selection of support parameters for ultra-deep foundation pits in similar silty formations and improves the accuracy of displacement prediction.

Introduction

With the acceleration of urbanization, the demand for urban housing and transportation continues to increase, leading to the construction of taller buildings and deeper foundation pits. These developments have introduced new challenges in the design and construction of deep foundation pits in muddy formations. Such formations exhibit complex geological characteristics, including high water content, susceptibility to plastic flow, and low strength1, which contribute to reduced soil strength and significant deformation2. These factors pose considerable difficulties for foundation pit support3. Support piles are critical structural elements in foundation pit engineering, primarily employed to stabilize excavation sidewalls, prevent soil collapse and displacement, and ensure safe excavation and subsequent construction4. The stability and safety of the support structure are therefore paramount. However, during the excavation of ultra-deep foundation pits in muddy formations, the stress conditions of support piles become highly complex5, making the prediction and control of their horizontal displacement a major engineering challenge. Consequently, studying the horizontal displacement at the top of support piles in ultra-deep foundation pits within muddy formations is of significant importance.

Researchers in related fields have proposed various methods to accurately predict the horizontal displacement at the top of support piles. Reference15 analyzes the excavation response of prefabricated recyclable support structures in water-rich silt foundation pits through numerical simulation of a top-pipe well foundation pit in Zhengzhou6. A three-dimensional fluid-structure interaction model is established using ABAQUS software to distinguish deformation differences between rigid and flexible components in the support system. The stress and deformation characteristics of the support structure during dewatering and excavation are investigated. The results indicate that, during these processes, the stress and deformation of the main support structure remain below the design values, whereas local yielding tends to occur in the steel plates connected to the wale beams. The horizontal displacement of the support piles varies significantly during dewatering and excavation. This method can differentiate between rigid and flexible components, thereby enabling more accurate simulation of the support structure’s stress and deformation behavior during dewatering and excavation. However, establishing a three-dimensional fluid-structure interaction model requires advanced technical expertise and substantial computational resources, posing certain practical challenges. Reference3 examines the influence of pile-anchor design parameters on the deformation of deep foundation pits during the construction of an urban substation in Chengdu, China7. The excavation depth of the gravel-layer foundation pit is 24.6 m. Deformation characteristics of the foundation pit and supporting structure are obtained through detailed field monitoring. The influence of pile-anchor design parameters on foundation pit deformation is analyzed using FLAC3D software. The results show that pile diameter is the most critical factor affecting pile deformation. The influence of anchor prestress and anchor angle on the horizontal displacement of the piles is less significant than that of pile diameter and the number of anchor rows. This method clarifies the relative influence of various parameters on foundation pit deformation and provides a basis for design optimization. However, the parameter analysis is limited to specific ranges and values, and prediction accuracy decreases for parameter values outside these ranges. Reference5 studies the influence of freeze-thaw cycles on the deformation of deep foundation pits supported by pile anchors8. During winter construction in seasonal freeze-thaw regions, the soil on the pit walls often exhibits safety hazards due to frost heave and thaw settlement. Based on winter monitoring data from a deep foundation pit in Harbin, the influence of freeze-thaw cycles is analyzed. The results indicate that, during freeze-thaw cycles, the horizontal displacement in the shallow middle part of the foundation pit is significantly greater than that on both sides, demonstrating a notable spatial effect. This method targets the specific geological conditions of seasonal freeze-thaw regions and is supported by practical monitoring data, enabling accurate reflection of the impact of freeze-thaw cycles on deep foundation pit deformation. However, it is primarily applicable to deformation analysis in seasonal freeze-thaw regions and is not suitable for other regions or under special geological conditions. Reference14 proposes an analysis of foundation pit support pile deformation under horizontal load based on the Pasternak foundation model9. The Pasternak two-parameter foundation model is introduced, which fully considers the continuity of soil deformation on the pile side. A differential equation for the deflection of the support pile considering pile-anchor deformation coordination is derived, and the internal force and deformation equations of the support pile are obtained using the transfer matrix method. The displacement and bending moment results of the support pile calculated by this method are relatively accurate. However, as it remains an idealized model, deviations may exist between the calculated results and actual conditions in practical engineering applications.

Soft soil strata pose significant challenges for deep foundation pit excavation and support. Through numerical simulation methods, multiple factors such as soil properties, support structure types, and construction conditions can be comprehensively considered to predict and analyze the horizontal displacement at the top of the support piles. The discretization method serves as a key technique in numerical simulation, which transforms continuous mathematical problems into discrete numerical problems and provides an important approach for addressing practical engineering challenges.

Existing discretization research methods often focus on single parameters, lacking a multidimensional discrete-variable system that encompasses loads, structures, and mechanical properties, which makes it difficult to reflect the actual synergistic effects of multiple factors in complex geological formations. Furthermore, studies on the influence of the lateral earth pressure coefficient on displacement have mainly examined continuous variation patterns, without clarifying the discrete and abrupt characteristics near its critical value, leading to an insufficiently precise basis for parameter control in engineering practice. In discretization simulations of silty formations, the special characteristics of earth pressure calculation due to high water content and low strength, as well as the matching requirements of support structure thickness, have not been fully considered, resulting in significant simulation errors. This study aims to employ discretization methods to numerically simulate the horizontal displacement at the top of support piles in ultra-deep foundation pits within silty strata. The paper innovatively discretizes the key factors influencing the horizontal displacement of the support pile top into three core discrete variables for ultra-deep foundation pits in muddy formations and constructs a numerical simulation model to capture the synergistic effects of these discrete variables. Through quantitative analysis, it reveals the abrupt variation in the direction and magnitude of displacement when the lateral earth pressure coefficient crosses the critical value of 1.0. The impact of different discrete thickness schemes on support safety is quantified, and the elastic modulus conversion formula is validated through laboratory experiments, while the earth pressure calculation method is verified via field tests. The deviation between the simulated horizontal displacement rate at the support pile top and the actual measured value is controlled within 5%, significantly reducing the error compared to conventional methods. This study not only addresses the gap in the analysis of the critical range of the lateral earth pressure coefficient in existing research but also provides a quantitative basis for the discrete selection of support parameters in engineering practice, thereby better aligning with scenarios involving discontinuous variations in actual engineering parameters. Compared to existing research, the discrete-variable system proposed in this paper better corresponds to the engineering reality of discontinuous parameter changes in ultra-deep excavation construction, enabling precise displacement prediction under the synergistic effects of multiple factors. By simplifying soil-structure interaction through the elastic foundation beam method, the differential equation for support structure deflection is derived. Combined with field monitoring data, model validation is completed across multiple monitoring points and stages throughout the entire construction cycle. This clarifies the abrupt change mechanism in displacement direction and amplitude when the lateral earth pressure coefficient crosses the 1.0 critical value, quantifying the enhancement effect of uniform support thickness on the safety factor. This approach better meets the precision requirements for the design and construction of ultra-deep foundation pit support in silty soil formations.

This paper selects three core discrete variables: earth pressure discrete values, discrete combinations of support structure thickness, and discrete gradients of lateral earth pressure coefficients. Its innovations include:Overcoming the limitation of existing research that focuses on single discrete parameters by establishing a discrete-variable system encompassing load, structure, and mechanical parameters, thereby achieving comprehensive coverage of factors influencing the horizontal displacement at the top of support piles;Addressing the characteristics of silty soil layers with high water content and low strength, it clarifies that discrete earth pressure values require a segmented calculation logic that distinguishes actual earth pressure from additional earth pressure, that discrete combinations of support structure thickness must align with the construction characteristics of layered excavation, and that discrete gradients of lateral earth pressure coefficients must accommodate dynamic changes in soil stress states, which resolves the issue of insufficient adaptability in existing discretization methods for complex soft soil layers.

Selection of an ultra‑deep foundation pit construction project in silty formations

Project overview

To analyze the horizontal displacement at the top of the support pile in ultra-deep foundation pits within muddy formations, this study focuses on the construction project of Songtao Street Station on Suzhou Metro Line 8. The project site is located at a crossroads, with the subway station comprising three underground levels and including three entrances: Entrances 2, 3, and 4. The station has a total length of 166 m, a standard section width of 23.1 m, and a platform width of 14 m. It primarily adopts a three-story, three-span frame structure. The construction section involves silty strata characterized by high water content, low bearing capacity, and low shear strength. The on-site construction situation is shown in Fig. 1.

Hydrological conditions

To mitigate the impact or recharge of groundwater, the hydrological conditions are analyzed12. The groundwater occurrence conditions are classified as follows:Phreatic water, which is present in the shallow 1–1 mixed soil and 3 − 1 cohesive soil layers, with its abundance controlled by lithology. The initial water level and stable water level are consistent.Micro-confined water, which occurs in the 4 − 2 silty soil interbedded with silt layers. Due to the influence of terrain and topography, the initial water level and stable water level may not be entirely consistent.Confined water, which exists in the 7 − 2 silt mixed with silty soil and is characterized by good confinement conditions and moderate water-bearing capacity.

Initial support construction technology

This project adopts a layered excavation approach, primarily to ensure coordinated management of excavation and support activities and to strictly implement construction standards and principles. The technical requirement of “support first, then excavate” must be guaranteed13. Based on this requirement and the hydrological conditions of the foundation, the selected retaining and support structures are determined as follows:Retaining structure: The perimeter of the subway station is enclosed with 52 underground diaphragm walls, including eight type-A walls, 42 type-B walls, 12 L-shaped walls, and four type-D walls. The connections between each group of walls use I-beam joints, and the wall toe is embedded in the 7 − 2 silty sand layer. The wall thickness is 1.0 m.Support system: The surface section is supported by concrete. In the standard section, a total of six struts and one replacement support structure are installed14. Specifically, the first and fourth struts are reinforced concrete struts, while the second and third struts consist of 609 × 16 steel tubes. The fifth and sixth struts use 800 × 20 steel tubes.

Numerical simulation methods

Before monitoring the horizontal displacement at the top of support piles in ultra-deep foundation pits within muddy formations, it is necessary to analyze the various factors influencing the displacement of the support pile top structure. These parameters can be set and adjusted as discrete variables in the numerical simulations.

Discrete variable setting

Soil pressure distribution

Discrete variables are key parameters influencing the horizontal displacement at the top of support piles during the construction of ultra-deep excavations in clayey strata, as they exhibit discontinuous variation characteristics. Their values are determined based on variations in actual engineering and geological conditions, thereby forming a discrete distribution. The discrete variables selected in this study include discrete values of earth pressure in strata, discrete combinations of support structure thickness, and discrete gradients of lateral earth pressure coefficients.

Calculation model establishment

When constructing the stress field for an ultra-deep excavation tunnel in subway engineering, and based on on-site testing, the vertical stress is determined as 3.2 MPa and the horizontal stress as 4.6 MPa.

The model is constructed using FLAC3D software, and the vertical stress is held constant during the model design process. Different lateral pressure coefficients - specifically, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 - are input sequentially to analyze the displacement and stress changes in the initial support piles of the tunnel under varying lateral pressure conditions.

Based on the design parameters, C25 shotcrete is selected as the initial support material. Assuming a longitudinal spacing of the steel frame of 0.5 m, two forms of support pile thickness are considered: (19, 19, 19) cm and (35, 11, 11) cm. Using an equivalence method, the elastic modulus of the structural steel bars is converted to that of shotcrete. The converted elastic modulus of the concrete, which represents the elastic modulus of the initial concrete support, is calculated using Eq. (9), E is the elastic modulus of the concrete, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E_0}$$\end{document} indicates the elastic modulus of the original concrete, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${S_g}$$\end{document} represents the cross-sectional area of the steel frame, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E_g}$$\end{document} denotes the elastic modulus of the steel frame, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${S_C}$$\end{document} is the cross-sectional area of the concrete.

To validate the reliability of the formula, three sets of C25 standard concrete specimens (150 mm × 150 mm × 150 mm) are prepared, yielding an average elastic modulus of 23.2 GPa. Three standard specimens each of 609 × 16 and 800 × 20 steel sections (500 mm in length) are tested using a universal testing machine, resulting in elastic moduli of 209.5 GPa and 210.3 GPa, respectively. The equivalent elastic modulus is calculated using Eq. (9). Taking the (19, 19, 19) cm thick support as an example, the concrete cross-sectional area over a 1 m length is 0.19 m², while the cross-sectional area of the 609 × 16 steel section is 0.03002 m². Substituting these values yields an equivalent elastic modulus of 28.1 GPa. Concurrently, composite steel-concrete specimens with identical mix proportions (incorporating 609 × 16 steel cores) are fabricated and tested, resulting in an elastic modulus of 27.8 GPa. The calculated value deviates from the measured value by only 1.08%, confirming the applicability of Eq. (9).

The model employs the pore water pressure module in FLAC3D software to achieve soil-water coupled calculation. The specific procedure is as follows: groundwater is divided into three layers - phreatic water, micro-confined water, and confined water - with corresponding initial pore water pressure values assigned (35 kPa for the phreatic water layer, 60 kPa for the micro-confined water layer, and 85 kPa for the confined water layer), which are consistent with on-site hydrological survey data. Adopting the effective stress principle, the influence of pore water pressure on the soil skeleton stress is considered, and the shear strength parameters of the soil (cohesion and internal friction angle) are based on effective stress indices. Changes in the groundwater seepage field during excavation are simulated, and the additional effect of seepage force on the support pile is calculated by setting drainage boundaries - taking the support structure as an impermeable boundary and the pit bottom as a drainage boundary. This ensures that the soil-water coupling effect is accurately reflected in the earth pressure calculation. Through the above treatment, the model can effectively simulate the influence of soil-water interaction on the horizontal displacement of the support pile top under different hydrological conditions.

Calculation results and analysis

As this study focuses on the deformation behavior of deep foundation pits in soft soil, both soft soil layers and control conditions are considered in the monitoring. Excavation sections containing 5 − 1 silty clay and 7 − 2 silt mixed with silty soil are selected. The deformation results of the support pile structure are obtained using FLAC3D simulation software.

Geologically, the site comprises eight distinct soil layers from top to bottom, each with well-defined states, thicknesses, and engineering properties: 1–1 Mixed fill is loose, 1.5–2.0 m thick, with moderately high compressibility and poor engineering properties; 3 − 1 Clay is plastic to hard plastic, 2.0–3.5 m thick, with moderate compressibility and relatively good engineering properties; 3–3 Cohesive silt is slightly dense to medium dense, 1.8–2.5 m thick, with moderate compressibility and engineering properties; 4 − 2 Sandy silt mixture is medium dense, 3.0–4.2 m thick, with moderate compressibility and engineering properties; 5 − 1 Silty clay is plastic to soft plastic, 4.5–6.0 m thick, with moderately high compressibility and moderately poor engineering properties, representing the primary silt-dominated stratum; 6 − 1 Clay is plastic to hard plastic, 2.5–3.2 m thick, with moderate compressibility and engineering properties; 6 − 2 Silty clay is plastic to soft plastic, 2.0–2.8 m thick, with moderate compressibility and engineering properties; 7 − 2 Silt-sand mixture is medium dense to dense, 5.0–6.5 m thick, with low to moderate compressibility and relatively good engineering properties, serving as the bearing layer for the diaphragm wall toe. Hydrologically, groundwater includes unconfined aquifers (present in 1–1 fill and 3 − 1 clay, with stable water levels), micro-confined aquifers (present in 4 − 2 silt-clay mixture, with water levels influenced by topography), and confined aquifers (present in 7 − 2 silt-clay mixture, exhibiting good confinement). Structurally, the subway station features a three-story, three-span frame structure with a total length of 166 m, a standard section width of 23.1 m, a platform width of 14 m, and three entrances/exits. The excavation support uses a diaphragm wall system with 52 panels: 8 Type A, 42 Type B, 12 L-shaped, and 4 D-shaped walls. Each wall is 1.0 m thick, with wall toes embedded in the 7 − 2 sandy silt mixture and connected by I-beam joints. The support system consists of six strut layers and one replacement layer: the first and fourth layers use reinforced concrete struts; the second and third layers employ 609 × 16 steel beams; and the fifth and sixth layers utilize 800 × 20 steel beams. Mechanical parameters such as density and elastic modulus are specified for each support material.

For design thresholds in deep excavations (12–20 m) within silty soil strata, the horizontal displacement alarm value for the top of support piles shall be ≤ 15 mm, with a limit value of ≤ 20 mm. Upon triggering the alarm, the monitoring frequency must be increased to once every 2 h, and the pretensioning axial force of the supports must be raised by 10%−15%. If limit values are reached, excavation must be paused and grouting reinforcement performed. The lateral earth pressure coefficient must be controlled between 0.8 and 1.2; if it falls below 0.8, excess loads around the pit must be removed; if it exceeds 1.2, additional pull-out resistance piles must be installed and external dewatering enhanced. The cohesion of silty clay must be ≥ 18 kPa. If this value is insufficient, reinforcement with dual-liquid grouting (cement-water glass) at 0.3–0.5 MPa shall be applied. Safety factors for support structures must meet the following requirements: left wall ≥ 1.05, ceiling ≥ 6.0, and toe ≥ 5.0. If the safety factor of the left wall is insufficient, apply shotcrete with a thickness increase of 2–3 cm; if the ceiling safety factor is insufficient, add steel mesh with a diameter of 8–10 mm and a spacing of 15 × 15 cm.

Discussion

For ultra-deep excavations in silty-soil formations, priority should be given to identifying the core control stratum. The support system should employ a combination of “subsurface diaphragm walls + multiple rigid struts”. Diaphragm walls must be embedded into the underlying medium-dense to dense soil layer (internal friction angle ≥ 25°) by at least 3 m to prevent toe instability. If the formation contains more than 30% silt-sand mixture, a water-stop curtain with a permeability coefficient ≤ 1 × 10⁻⁶ cm/s must be installed concurrently. Core discrete variables affecting horizontal displacement at the top of support piles - lateral earth pressure coefficient, support thickness, and soil cohesion - require coordinated management. The lateral earth pressure coefficient must be controlled within the critical range of 0.8–1.2 to ensure displacement ≤ 15 mm. Concrete spraying for support must employ a uniform scheme with single-pass thickness deviation within ± 2 cm (the safety factor increases by 1.8–2.9 compared to non-uniform schemes). When cohesion falls below 15 kPa, grouting reinforcement is required to achieve ≥ 18 kPa. Stratified excavation must match soil strength: soft-plastic silty clay (density 16–18 kN/m³) should be excavated in single-lift depths ≤ 3.5 m; medium-dense silt-clay mixtures (density 19–20 kN/m³) should be excavated in single-lift depths ≤ 4.5 m, with support installation completed within 24 h after excavation.

Supports shall be preloaded with an axial force at 50%−70% of the design axial force. During the design phase, parameters shall be controlled according to the following priority sequence: lateral earth pressure coefficient > cohesion of silty clay > internal friction angle of silt-sand mixture > elastic modulus of support piles. The discrepancy between measured and simulated parameters shall be ≤ 5%.

The primary sources of error in the numerical simulation results of this study fall into three categories:Parameter error: Formation parameters (cohesion, internal friction angle) are obtained from field tests with a measurement error of ± 5%, resulting in displacement calculation errors of ± 2.1% (as determined by sensitivity analysis, where a 5% cohesion error corresponds to a 2.1% displacement error); support material parameters (elastic modulus, tensile strength) are obtained from laboratory tests with an error of ± 3%, corresponding to a displacement error of ± 1.2%.Model error: The mesh size selected is 1.0 m × 1.0 m × 1.0 m, resulting in a displacement error of 1.09% compared to a finer 0.5 m × 0.5 m × 0.5 m mesh; boundary conditions are assumed as fixed constraints, while actual engineering boundaries exhibit minor deformations, leading to displacement errors of ± 1.5%; construction error includes actual deviation in staged excavation depth of ± 0.3 m, corresponding to a displacement error of ± 1.8%, and actual deviation in pre-applied axial force of struts of ± 5%, corresponding to a displacement error of ± 2.5%.The comprehensive total error, calculated as the root mean square of individual errors, is less than the 8% allowed by specifications, indicating that the simulation results meet the required accuracy.

Conclusion

As a critical aspect of engineering construction, the safety of deep foundation pits is essential to ensure structural safety. As the primary support structure for deep foundation pits, necessary displacement monitoring of support piles represents the most important link in engineering construction. In this numerical simulation study, an in-depth analysis is conducted on the support structure of ultra-deep foundation pits in silty strata, with a focus on the horizontal displacement at the top of the support piles. Discrete variables are introduced to more accurately reflect the complexity and uncertainty of actual engineering conditions. The main conclusions are as follows:An innovative discrete-variable system was constructed, overcoming the limitation of single-parameter discretization in existing studies. This achieves comprehensive coverage of all displacement-influencing factors, with simulation errors controlled within 3.3%, significantly improving accuracy compared to traditional methods.The critical influence mechanism of the lateral earth pressure coefficient on displacement is revealed: when the coefficient equals 1.0, the pile-head horizontal displacement is minimal. Crossing the 1.0 threshold triggers a sudden displacement-direction change (outward displacement for coefficients < 1.0, inward displacement for coefficients > 1.0), accompanied by a sharp increase in displacement amplitude. This provides crucial engineering guidance for controlling lateral earth pressure coefficients.The safety factor of the uniform-thickness shotcrete support scheme is 1.8–2.9 times higher than that of the non-uniform scheme. Deep excavation and bottom sealing in silty strata exhibit significant creep characteristics, necessitating continuous monitoring of the support structure after sealing rather than immediate removal.

However, this study has certain limitations. Discrete variables do not fully incorporate dynamic changes caused by construction disturbances, and the model do not cover complex soil-water coupling effects under extreme hydrological conditions (e.g., torrential rain, groundwater surges). Furthermore, the monitoring period focuses on the 120-day construction phase, lacking long-term settlement data support. Future research will incorporate discrete parameters for construction disturbances, refine numerical models for extreme conditions, conduct long-term monitoring to analyze post-excavation deformation patterns, and explore the integration of artificial intelligence algorithms with discrete-variable simulations. This will enhance the intelligence and accuracy of displacement prediction, providing more comprehensive technical guidance for similar ultra-deep excavation projects in silty-soil formations.

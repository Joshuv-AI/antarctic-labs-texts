Safety compliance monitoring for oil tank unloading based on multimodal feature fusion and knowledge reasoning
Authors: Tiechao Liu, Chao Sun, Bo Wang, Lichao Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImproper procedures during oil tank unloading operations can lead to catastrophic accidents. To address these issues of low real-time performance and poor interpretability in existing oil tanker unloading detection systems, we propose a novel framework: YOLO-ESC. This framework monitors safety compliance via multimodal feature fusion and knowledge reasoning, enabling it to determine whether ongoing oil unloading operations comply with prescribed safety protocols and generate explainable warnings when violations are detected. Specifically, YOLO-ESC leverages a Multimodal Feature Processing Network (MFPN) with visual-semantic alignment to improve fine-grained recognition of equipment states, a Human-Equipment Interaction Semantic Modeling Module (HEISMM) to identify structured interaction events through spatial-temporal analysis, and a Compliance Judgment Expert System (CJES) that performs rule-based reasoning with explainability. It achieves real-time performance at 31 FPS, a rate that ensures seamless operation in safety-critical environments, while maintaining 92.3% compliance classification accuracy. Unlike many approaches that rely on large, complex vision-language models, YOLO-ESC instead leverages explicit knowledge encoding, making it both efficient and interpretable.Keywords: Oil tank unloading, Multimodal, Knowledge reasoning, Expert system, YOLOSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Oil tank unloading operations, which involve the transfer of flammable and explosive materials, represent one of the most critical processes in the petroleum industry1. According to industry statistics, improper operational procedures during oil unloading contribute to over 60% of fire and explosion incidents at gas stations and oil depots2, and similar risk patterns have been documented in LPG storage and transfer scenarios3. To mitigate these risks, standardized safety protocols such as the “Eight-Step Oil Unloading Method” have been established, which mandate specific sequential operations including static grounding, equipment inspection, and valve control procedures. However, human supervisors are prone to fatigue-induced attention lapses during extended monitoring periods, inconsistent judgment across different inspectors, and an inability to provide continuous 24/7 coverage4, which inevitably leads to missed violations.

Existing works can be categorized into equipment detection methods, human action recognition approaches, and compliance verification systems. Object detection methods, such as the YOLO family5,6, have established themselves as the de facto standard for real-time object detection due to their high speed. More recent transformer-based detectors, such as RT-DETR7 and DINO8, have achieved superior accuracy through attention mechanisms. However, the visual differences between an open and closed grounding clamp may span only a few pixels in typical surveillance footage, making accurate state classification challenging. Some works have explored multimodal fusion to enhance detection capabilities9, but their application to industrial equipment state recognition remains limited.

Human action recognition using skeleton-based models has emerged as a robust approach for understanding human activities10,11. Methods like ST-GCN model the human skeleton as a spatial-temporal graph, capturing both anatomical structure and motion dynamics. Pose estimation networks, such as HRNet12 and ViTPose13, provide accurate keypoint localization that serves as input to these action recognizers. However, industrial scenarios often involve non-standard body postures, equipment obscuring the body, and a large number of specific action words. As a result, the system still struggles to effectively extract the interaction context between people and equipment, leading to poor recognition results.

For compliance verification systems, traditional industrial monitoring systems typically employ rule-based pipelines with handcrafted features and threshold-based decisions14. These systems lack robustness to environmental variations. Recent large vision-language models (VLMs), such as GPT-4V15, and video understanding models like VideoMAE16 and InternVideo17, offer powerful general-purpose visual reasoning capabilities. However, these models process videos at reduced resolution and temporal sampling rates, losing the frame-level precision required for safety applications. Furthermore, they lack explicit knowledge representation for domain-specific protocols, making their reasoning processes opaque and difficult to audit.

The central idea of this paper is the observation that safety compliance monitoring requires the integration of equipment state perception, semantic understanding of human-equipment interactions, and logical verification against domain knowledge. Although the overall framework is intuitive, YOLO-ESC faces two major technical challenges. The first challenge is how to accurately recognize fine-grained equipment states under varying environmental conditions, as visual differences between states are subtle and easily confused by standard detectors. The second challenge is how to provide explainable compliance judgments that meet industrial audit requirements. Neural network-based approaches typically function as black boxes, making their decisions difficult to verify and trust.

To address these challenges, we present YOLO-ESC, a novel framework for safety compliance monitoring that integrates multimodal feature fusion with knowledge-based reasoning. Especially, we propose the Multimodal Feature Processing Network (MFPN) that leverages visual-semantic alignment through cross-attention fusion and contrastive learning, enabling accurate discrimination of fine-grained equipment states. The Human-Equipment Interaction Semantic Modeling Module (HEISMM) integrates pose estimation, spatial association analysis, temporal accumulation, and action recognition to identify structured interaction events. The Compliance Judgment Expert System (CJES) encodes the safety protocol as production rules and uses forward-chaining inference to perform logical and temporal verification with explainability. YOLO-ESC achieves real-time performance at 31 FPS while maintaining 92.3% compliance classification accuracy, making it suitable for practical deployment. It offers full explainability through symbolic reasoning, with each inference step traceable to specific rules and observations. Our major contributions are summarized as follows:

Related work

In this section, we review related works in oil tank unloading, including industrial safety monitoring and hazard detection, human pose estimation and action recognition, and vision-language models for visual reasoning.

Industrial safety monitoring and hazard detection

The petrochemical industry has experienced numerous incidents caused by improper operational procedures during loading and unloading operations. Recent advances in deep learning have facilitated the automated detection of industrial hazards. For example, Luo et al.5 proposed a lightweight-attention YOLOv8 variant for oil leakage detection in power transformers, demonstrating that attention mechanisms can enhance the detection of subtle anomalies in industrial equipment. Prakash et al.6 developed non-invasive methods for detecting hydraulic cylinder leakage using computer vision and time-frequency analysis techniques. Liu et al.7 presented a real-time welding defect detection framework based on RT-DETR, achieving both high accuracy and real-time performance. For human detection in industrial environments, Zhao et al.8 proposed VE-DINO, a visibility-enhanced DINO model designed for occlusion-heavy disaster scenarios. Chen et al.9 developed a multimodal data fusion-based method for intelligent detection of lump coal on underground conveyor belts in smart manufacturing. Alenjareghi et al.18 proposed a computer vision-enabled framework for real-time job hazard analysis in human–robot collaboration during disassembly tasks. While these methods excel at detecting specific hazards or anomalies, existing industrial monitoring systems generally do not verify compliance with multi-step operational protocols. In particular, they lack the ability to monitor human–equipment interactions or to reason logically about adherence to prescribed safety procedures.

Human pose estimation and action recognition

Understanding human activities in industrial environments requires accurate pose estimation and action recognition, which serve as the foundation for analyzing human-equipment interactions. Shi et al.12 proposed a fall detection algorithm combining enhanced HRNet with YOLO, demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating pose estimation and object detection for safety applications. More recently, Xu et al.13 introduced ViTPose++, a Vision Transformer-based approach for generic body pose estimation. While these methods perform well on benchmark datasets, they face challenges in industrial environments characterized by non-standard body configurations, equipment occlusions, and limited training data for domain-specific scenarios. Skeleton-based approaches provide solutions for action recognition. Soleymani et al.10 developed skeleton-based action recognition for manufacturing assembly tasks through graph convolution networks. Terreran et al.11 presented a general skeleton-based action and gesture recognition framework for human-robot collaboration, addressing the need for real-time action understanding in industrial settings. Mehmood et al.19 proposed an extended multi-stream temporal-attention module for skeleton-based human action recognition, which improves temporal modeling through attention mechanisms. Wang et al.20 developed a multi-scale control and action recognition framework for human-robot collaboration in new generation intelligent manufacturing. Chen et al.14 proposed knowledge distillation from handcrafted features for human activity recognition, showing that domain knowledge can enhance learning efficiency in data-scarce industrial scenarios. However, existing action recognition methods focus on classifying isolated actions, without considering the context of human-equipment interactions. For safety compliance monitoring, simply knowing that an operator is performing a “rotation” action is insufficient.

Vision-language models for visual reasoning

Recently, video foundation models have made significant progress in temporal understanding. Wang et al.16 proposed VideoMAE v2, scaling video masked autoencoders with dual masking to learn strong video representations. Wang et al.17 introduced InternVideo2, a foundation model for multimodal video understanding that achieves state-of-the-art performance across various video tasks. Moorthy et al.21 developed a hybrid multi-attention transformer for robust video object detection, improving temporal consistency through attention mechanisms.

Large VLMs have shown strong visual reasoning capabilities. Zhou et al.15 evaluated GPT-4V on the detection of radiologic findings, demonstrating that VLMs can perform domain-specific visual analysis when properly prompted. To enhance reasoning capabilities, Zhao et al.22 proposed CoT-VLA, which introduces visual chain-of-thought reasoning for vision-language-action models to improve decision-making transparency. Tan et al.23 developed Reason-RFT, using reinforcement fine-tuning to enhance the visual reasoning capabilities of vision-language models. However, these models typically process videos at lower resolution and temporal sampling rates, which leads to a loss of frame-level precision necessary for detecting subtle equipment state changes. Their computational requirements preclude real-time deployment for continuous monitoring.

Design of YOLO-ESC

To this end, YOLO-ESC consists of three main modules (see Fig. 1). The MFPN leverages visual-semantic alignment capabilities, enabling the network to better discriminate fine-grained equipment states. The HEISMM integrates human pose keypoints, equipment detection results, and action recognition outputs to identify interaction events through spatial association analysis and temporal accumulation. Performance evaluation

Experiment setup

Datasets

We evaluate YOLO-ESC on two datasets. The Occluded Human dataset27 contains 5,081 images with 13,360 annotated human instances, where each instance has 17 keypoint annotations along with bounding boxes and instance masks. Additionally, we construct a domain-specific dataset for the oil tank unloading scenario, named the Self-built Oil Unloading (SOU) Dataset. The videos are captured from different camera angles and at various occlusion levels. We extract frames from these videos and annotate them according to the COCO format. The dataset contains 900 images with 2,847 human instances and 4,521 equipment instances across 12 equipment categories, such as grounding clamps, valves, oil pipes, safety barriers, and more. For compliance classification, the 150 annotated operation sequences are split into 120 training and 30 test sequences via stratified sampling to preserve class distribution. We additionally report 5-fold cross-validation accuracy on all 150 sequences. Bounding box and keypoint annotations were produced by three trained annotators; inter-annotator agreement was evaluated on a 10% random subset, yielding a Fleiss’s for equipment state labels and a mean OKS of 0.91 for keypoint annotations, confirming label quality.

Baselines

To evaluate the compliance monitoring capability of YOLO-ESC, we compare it against several baseline methods, including Rule-based System14, Hidden Markov Model (HMM)28 for temporal sequence modeling with observation probabilities from CNN features, Decision Tree (DT)29, LSTM30, Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)31, Transformer32, TempAgg33, TimeSformer34, VideoMAE v216, InternVideo217, CLIP35, GPT-4V15, VideoMamba36, Qwen3-VL37, and LLaVA-Video38. To ensure fair and reproducible comparison, we provide the following standardized experimental settings. All methods receive the same input frames. For VLM-based methods, we use a uniform structured prompt that provides the eight-step protocol description and asks the model to classify the operation into C1–C6, with temperature fixed at 0. For neural temporal models, we fine-tune all models on our SOU training split for 50 epochs using the Adam optimizer, performing a learning rate sweep over and selecting the checkpoint with the highest validation accuracy. Latency is measured as the end-to-end wall-clock time per 30-second clip on the same RTX 4090 hardware, averaged over all 30 test sequences.

Table 6 summarises the 26 misclassified sequences in the test set, categorised by the primary failure cause. Equipment occlusion accounts for the largest failure mode, where a worker’s body partially or fully occludes the grounding clamp or valve, causing MFPN to assign low confidence to the equipment state label. This uncertainty propagates through the MYCIN-style certainty factor chain, sometimes causing CJES to abstain from issuing a RED warning when the grounding clamp state falls below the confirmation threshold. Lighting condition failures arise from sudden illumination changes. Simultaneous multi-violation scenarios reveal a priority conflict in the CJES conflict set: when two RED rules activate concurrently, the composite priority function occasionally selects the wrong rule first, causing the inference trace to report an incorrect violation type. Ambiguous action recognition occurs when the operator performs a non-standard grip posture or adopts an atypical body orientation, causing ST-GCN to output a low-confidence action prediction that does not exceed the interaction confirmation threshold, thereby suppressing a valid interaction event from the working memory. Future work will address these failures through multi-camera fusion for occlusion handling, adaptive exposure correction for lighting robustness, and richer training data for non-standard operator postures.

Conclusion

In this paper, we presented YOLO-ESC, a novel framework for real-time safety compliance monitoring in oil tank unloading operations. The framework consists of MFPN, which leverages visual–semantic alignment for fine-grained equipment state recognition; HEISMM, which identifies structured interaction events through spatiotemporal analysis and action recognition; and CJES, which encodes the Eight-Step Oil Unloading Method into production rules for logical and temporal verification. We believe YOLO-ESC deepens understanding of how neural perception can be integrated with symbolic reasoning in safety-critical applications and opens a new pathway for explainable AI in industrial monitoring. In future work, we plan to extend YOLO-ESC to multi-camera fusion scenarios commonly deployed in large-scale oil depots, which require cross-view association and consistent reasoning across multiple viewpoints. We also acknowledge the ethical implications of deploying continuous surveillance-based monitoring systems in occupational settings. In practice, we recommend that deployment be accompanied by transparent disclosure to workers, and access controls restricting inference results to safety personnel.

Assess the Effect of Detract Technique among Post-operative Toddlers with Cleft Lip and Cleft Palate
Authors: Linda Xavier, Sowndariya Velmurugan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Children who undergo surgery frequently experience postoperative pain or POP. Pain experienced by children can be addressed as a basic human right. Because hospitals can be extremely stressful places for families and their children, pain, and discomfort associated with intrusive techniques can lengthen hospital stays for children and lead them to heal more slowly than they would have otherwise. Engaging the kids in intellectual pursuits is one way to use distraction techniques to take their minds off unpleasant stimuli and lessen their suffering.Purpose:The current study’s goal was to evaluate the impact of the distract technique on postoperative infants with cleft lip and palate who were between the ages of 1 and 3 years old.Materials and Methods:The Saveetha Medical College and Hospital served as the host institution for the quantitative method with a preexperimental investigation.Sampling Method:Convenience sampling was used to select 60 postoperative children with cleft lip and palate who were between the ages of one and three.Interventions:One intervention group and a control group were randomly assigned to each of the 60 postoperative youngsters. Distract technique (tactile ball) was started for the intervention group 20 min prior to operation day and continued for 30 days. The first group (the control group) received standard care, which included analgesic medication. The second group (the intervention group) received no analgesic medication and was played with a tactile ball by a nurse who happened to be one of the researchers working in the pediatric unit where the study was conducted. Acetaminophen (10–15 mg/kg) was given to the children in the control group as an analgesic.Results:The descriptive analysis (P > 0.05) did not reveal any statistically significant differences between the groups.

INTRODUCTION

One of the main things influencing living quality in the postoperative phase is pain. There has been numerous research on the management of pain following surgery in children, but no conclusive findings have been found on pain alleviation.[12] Postoperative pain (POP) in children is still moderate to severe.[3] Therefore, more research is required to develop novel and efficient methods of treating pediatric pain.[4] Distraction tactics are a separate aspect of a nurse’s job description.[56] According to recent studies, play is a great way for kids to divert their attention. Play can be a useful diversion strategy for kids to help them deal with discomfort, worry, and anxiety.[78] For nurses, play can be very significant because it gives them the chance to deliver trauma- and family-centered care.[9] Children in hospitals engage in play activities with tactile balls. They are crucial as a source of support for kids with acute illnesses as well as kids with chronic illnesses.[10] It was discovered that using tactile balls to reduce violence in kids with attention deficit and hyperactivity was beneficial.[11]

METHODS AND MATERIALS

Study design

This was a randomized controlled trial conducted in the pediatric ward of Saveetha Medical College in Chennai. Based at least on calculations done with the G-power 3.1 Demo package program assuming an effect size of 0.8 and a power of 80%, the number of children in each group was found to be 26. Nevertheless, it was determined that each group would have 30 instances.

Study setting

The current study at SMCH evaluated the impact of the Distract Technique on postoperative children with cleft lip and cleft palate using a quantitative approach and experimental design. The pediatric ward served as the study site for four months, starting in February 2023 and ending in May 2023.

Ethical approval

The study was carried out with formal authorization from the department head of child health nursing and ethical approval from the Institutional Ethical Committee (IEC) of Saveetha Institute of Medical and Technical Science.

Study participants

A total of 60 postoperative children who met the inclusion criteria were chosen to participate in the research. Children with cleft lip and palate, POP from 0 to 3 days, and children who are 1–3 years old are among the inclusion criteria. Exclusion criteria include children who are unwilling, those who have had surgery more than three days after it began, and those who have impaired facial expression.

Sampling technique

Using the simple random sample technique, 90 postoperative children between the ages of 1 and 3 were chosen in accordance with the inclusion criteria. Hospital for Children in Eastern Ontario. The six elements on the Pain Scale in Young Children (CHEOPS) are: Cry, Facial Expression, Verbal Responses, Torso, Wound Evaluation, and Legs. This scale was created to assess postoperative aping in children. A minimum score of 1 and a maximum score of 3 are assigned to item 1 (Cry) and item 2 (Facial Expression).

Validity and reliability

The researcher (15) carried out the study’s validity and reliability, and the study’s internal consistency coefficient, Cronbach’s alpha, was found to be 0.87.

Pre-assessment form

The pre-assessment form used is the PedsQL Health Care Parent Satisfaction Scale. The children, aged 1–3 years, free of chronic illnesses, underwent cleft lip and cleft palate surgery between February and March of 2023. The researcher divided the study’s participants into three groups randomly based on the children’s hospitalization sequence.

Interventions

Of the groups, the first (Control group) received standard care (analgesic medication), and the second (Experimental Group) received no analgesic medication and instead had their parents play tactile ball with them. In accordance with the operating surgeon’s decision, the children in the intervention group did not receive analgesic medication after undergoing general anesthesia for surgery.

Placebo group

As an analgesic, the kids in the control group were given acetaminophen (10–15 mg/kg). An hour after the procedure, when the kids were fully conscious and in need of an analgesic, the intervention and scale assessments took place.

CONCLUSION

In children, the use of tactile balls effectively reduced postoperative discomfort. As an autonomous role, tactile ball can help reduce postoperative discomfort for nurses.

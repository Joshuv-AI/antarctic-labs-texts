EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A: 5-year follow-up of enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab in previously untreated locally advanced/metastatic urothelial carcinoma
Authors: J.E. Rosenberg, P.H. O'Donnell, D.P. Petrylak, T.W. Flaig, C.J. Hoimes, S. Gupta, N. Mar, T.W. Friedlander, S.T. Tagawa, M.A. Bilen, J.R. Brown, R.R. McKay, J.R. Merchan, S. Srinivas, S. Gorla, B.H. Moreno, G. Davis, H.S. Wirtz, Y. Zhu, M.I. Milowsky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDose escalation/cohort A of the phase Ib/II EV-103/KEYNOTE-869 study (NCT03288545) assessed safety and efficacy of enfortumab vedotin (EV) plus pembrolizumab (P) in cisplatin-ineligible patients with previously untreated locally advanced/metastatic urothelial carcinoma (la/mUC). Here we report updated results after 5 years of follow-up.MethodsPatients received EV 1.25 mg/kg intravenously on days 1 and 8, and pembrolizumab 200 mg on day 1 of every 3-week cycle. The primary endpoint was safety. Select secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DOR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS).ResultsForty-five patients were enrolled. Baseline characteristics were representative of a cisplatin-ineligible population; 84.4% had visceral disease and 55.6% had reduced renal function (creatine clearance <60 ml/min). The median follow-up time was 62.1 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 59.4-63.1]. There were no new safety signals, and safety was consistent with EV+P in other la/mUC studies. Confirmed ORR was 73.3%, median DOR was 22.1 months [95% CI 8.4-not estimable (NE)], median PFS was 12.7 months (95% CI 6.1-NE), and median OS was 26.1 months (95% CI 15.5-NE). The estimated 5-year survival rate was 41.5% (95% CI 26.5-56.0).ConclusionAlong with results from EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 (NCT04223856), the 5-year results further support the sustained benefit from EV+P and establish the combination as the standard of care for previously untreated la/mUC.

Introduction

First-line (1L) treatment for locally advanced/metastatic urothelial cancer (la/mUC) has traditionally been dependent on cisplatin eligibility,1 with approximately half of patients being cisplatin ineligible due to impaired renal function, poor performance status, and other comorbidities limiting treatment options.2 Treatments for cisplatin-ineligible patients have historically had limited efficacy compared with cisplatin-based chemotherapy2 or were only available as maintenance therapy to patients who had not progressed following 1L platinum-based chemotherapy,3 representing a significant unmet need in this population.

Enfortumab vedotin (EV) plus pembrolizumab (P) is the first treatment approved for patients with previously untreated la/mUC, irrespective of cisplatin eligibility.4 This broad approval was based on the randomized, phase III EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 study (NCT04223856), which included cisplatin-eligible and cisplatin-ineligible patients. The combination became the standard of care (SOC) for 1L treatment of la/mUC.

Before EV-302, US accelerated approval for EV+P was granted based on the phase Ib/II EV-103/KEYNOTE-869 study (NCT03288545).5 EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A were the first to treat patients with EV+P; long-term follow-up is ongoing.6,7 With EV+P now considered SOC, understanding long-term outcomes of EV+P treatment remains essential. This short communication presents updated long-term results from EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A after 5 years of follow-up, representing the longest follow-up to date for patients treated with EV+P and providing important insights into long-term safety and efficacy for this combination.

Methods

In patients with la/mUC, 5-year follow-up is a key landmark analysis time point. The study design and statistical methods of EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A have been previously published.6 Briefly, patients ineligible for 1L cisplatin-based chemotherapy with histologically confirmed la/mUC were enrolled. Patients had to be eligible for checkpoint inhibitor therapy and have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status ≤2. Patients received EV 1.25 mg/kg intravenously on days 1 and 8 of every 3-week cycle. Pembrolizumab 200 mg was administered intravenously ∼30 minutes after completion of EV on day 1 of each 3-week cycle. The study protocol was approved by independent review boards or ethics committees; patients provided written informed consent.

The primary endpoint was safety. Select secondary endpoints included confirmed objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DOR), disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS) per Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 by blinded independent central review (BICR) and investigator, and overall survival (OS). Results (confirmed ORR, DOR, DCR, PFS) by investigator and OS were previously published (median follow-up: 24.9 months).6

Results

Forty-five patients were enrolled to receive 1L EV 1.25 mg/kg plus pembrolizumab (dose escalation, n = 5; cohort A, n = 40). Baseline characteristics were representative of a cisplatin-ineligible population with la/mUC (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107700). Most patients had prognostically unfavorable disease characteristics at baseline, with 84.4% having visceral disease, including 31.1% with liver metastases. The most common reason for cisplatin ineligibility was creatinine clearance <60 ml/min (55.6%). At data cutoff (20 November 2023), the median follow-up time was 62.1 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 59.4-63.1]. All patients had discontinued treatment, 15 (33%) remained on study, two (4.4%) underwent surgery for urothelial cancer [one radical cystectomy, one nephroureterectomy (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107700)]. The median treatment duration for EV+P was 7.0 months (range 0.7-32.9) [median of 9 cycles (range 1-36)]. The median treatment duration with EV was 6.4 months (range 0.7-32.9) [median of 8 cycles (range 1-36)], and the median treatment duration with pembrolizumab was 6.5 months (range 0.7-28.1) [median of 8 cycles (range 1-35)]. Following treatment discontinuation, 27 patients (60.0%) received subsequent therapies: systemic therapy (48.9%), palliative radiotherapy (8.9%), surgical procedure (8.9%), and intravesical gemcitabine (2.2%) (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107700).

The most common any-grade treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were peripheral sensory neuropathy (55.6%), fatigue (51.1%), and alopecia (48.9%); the most common grade ≥3 TRAEs were lipase increase (17.8%), fatigue (11.1%), and maculopapular rash (11.1%). Seven patients (15.6%) experienced a serious TRAE; none occurred more than once. TRAEs led to dose reductions in 15 patients (33.3%) and discontinuations in 20 (44.4%). One patient (2.2%) died from a TRAE (multiple organ dysfunction syndrome with concurrent bullous dermatitis) as previously reported6; no new deaths from TRAEs have occurred.

TRAEs of special interest for EV were primarily grade ≤2 (Table 1). Treatment-related skin reactions occurred in 66.7% of patients [median onset 0.7 months, median resolution 1.2 months (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107700)]; 22.2% experienced grade ≥3 events. Fifty-nine patients (89.4%) had skin reactions that resolved, and two patients (3.0%) had events that improved. Treatment-related peripheral neuropathy occurred in 62.2% of patients [median onset 2.4 months, median resolution 8.6 months (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107700)]; 53.3% experienced grade ≥2 events, of which 4.4% were grade ≥3. Eleven patients (25.6%) had peripheral neuropathy that resolved, and 19 patients (44.2%) had events that improved. Treatment-related hyperglycemia occurred in 11.1% of patients [median onset 0.5 months, median resolution 1.6 months (Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107700)]; 8.9% experienced grade ≥3 events. Six patients (85.7%) had hyperglycemia that resolved, and one (14.3%) had an event that improved. Confirmed ORR by BICR was 73.3% (95% CI 58.1-85.4): seven patients (15.6%) achieved a complete response (CR), and 26 (57.8%) achieved a partial response (PR). Confirmed ORR by BICR was consistent with previously published investigator-confirmed ORR.6 The DCR was 84.4% (95% CI 70.5-93.5). The median DOR by BICR was 22.1 months [95% CI 8.4-not estimable (NE)] (Figure 1A), with an estimated ongoing response rate among responders of 47.0% (95% CI 27.6-64.3) at 5 years. Time to response and DOR in individual patients achieving confirmed CR or PR by BICR are shown in Figure 1B. In patients responding at 2 years, no progression events occurred at longer follow-up. Median PFS by BICR was 12.7 months (95% CI 6.1-NE) (Figure 1C), with an estimated PFS rate of 38.2% (95% CI 23.1-53.1) at 5 years. Discussion

Long-term results from EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A after 5 years of follow-up demonstrated that 1L EV+P in cisplatin-ineligible patients with la/mUC yielded durable responses and meaningful long-term survival. These cohorts were the first to treat patients with la/mUC with EV+P and are the longest follow-up data to date available for the regimen in the 1L setting. The results provide important long-term safety and efficacy insights from treatment with this combination, which is now SOC. The safety profile was consistent with that of previous analyses (median follow-up: 24.9 and 47 months).6,8 The unprecedented 41.5% estimated 5-year survival rate exceeds historical data of 1L gemcitabine plus carboplatin in cisplatin-ineligible patients (estimated 5-year OS rate: 7.6%, median follow-up: 4.5 years).9 Although comparisons to historical data should be interpreted with caution, results from EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A may provide insights relevant to the long-term follow-up for EV-302, which included a larger, broader patient population of cisplatin-eligible and cisplatin-ineligible patients.5

EV-302 demonstrated superior efficacy with EV+P, nearly doubling median PFS and OS versus platinum-based chemotherapy after ∼1.5 years of median follow-up in patients with previously untreated la/mUC irrespective of cisplatin eligibility (median PFS 12.5 versus 6.3 months, median OS 31.5 versus 16.1 months).5 In an updated analysis after ∼2.5 years of median follow-up, EV+P continued to demonstrate a survival benefit versus chemotherapy (median PFS 12.5 versus 6.3 months, median OS 33.8 versus 15.9 months).10 The long-term efficacy results from EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A may also provide more specific insights into the long-term efficacy for the cisplatin-ineligible population in EV-302.

The safety profile of EV+P is well characterized.5 In EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A, TRAEs of special interest for EV generally presented within the first few months and most improved or resolved, highlighting the importance of appropriate management including dose modifications or reductions.11

This 5-year analysis of EV-103 dose escalation/cohort A supports the safety and efficacy of EV+P in cisplatin-ineligible patients with previously untreated la/mUC, although these were small, single-arm cohorts, which can limit generalizability. These encouraging findings are corroborated by EV-302, which established EV+P as the 1L SOC for patients with la/mUC, irrespective of cisplatin eligibility.

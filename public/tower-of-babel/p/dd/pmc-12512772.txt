Propofol in combination with salvianolic acid A protect against lipopolysaccharide-induced cardiac dysfunction and ferroptosis through activating the SIRT1/FoxO1 signaling under diabetic condition
Authors: Ronghui Han, Kaijia Han, Jianyu Zhu, Anyuan Zhang, Jiaqi Zhou, Hemeng Huang, Hong Han, Liangqing Zhang, Xin Liu, Jing Tang, Bin Yi, Xiwen Ma, Qinjun Chu, Guixi Mo, Yiming Shao, Zhengyuan Xia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to investigate the therapeutic potential of a combined low-dose propofol (PPF) and salvianolic acid A (SAA) regimen in mitigating lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced cardiac dysfunction and ferroptosis in diabetic contexts, and to explore the role of the sirtuin 1 (SIRT1)/forkhead box O1 (FoxO1) signaling pathway.MethodsType 2 diabetes (DM) was induced in mice, followed by LPS administration to induce cardiac injury. The mice were randomly assigned to six groups: control, DM, control + LPS, DM + LPS, DM + LPS + high-dose PPF, and DM + LPS + low-dose PPF + SAA. Cardiac function was assessed via echocardiography, while ferroptosis was evaluated with BODIPY staining and transmission electron microscopy. In vitro, H9c2 cardiomyocytes were treated with high glucose and LPS. Ferroptosis was assessed with FerroOrange and JC-1 staining. Oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokines were measured using ELISA or flow cytometry, and protein expression of key markers was analyzed.ResultsDiabetes aggravated LPS-induced cardiac injury in mice evidenced as impaired myocardial function, which was concomitant with decreased cardiac expression of SIRT1, FoxO1, and GPX4 proteins; increased production of oxidative stress, pro-inflammatory cytokines, oxidized lipids, and damaged mitochondrial cristae as compared to NC + LPS group. The combined use of PPF and SAA enhanced cardiac SIRT1 and FoxO1 and ameliorated LPS-mediated cardiac dysfunction in diabetic mice, but these beneficial effects were abolished by inhibition of SIRT1 or FoxO1. In vitro, hyperglycemia and LPS-induced cellular injuries were ameliorated by PPF and SAA, respectively. Low dose of PPF in combination with SAA reduced the production of ROS and ferroptosis that were concomitant with increased SIRT1 and FoxO1 expression, effects that were either comparable or superior to those achieved with high-dose PPF alone, but these protective effects were reversed by silencing SIRT1 or FoxO1.ConclusionThe combination of low-dose PPF and SAA effectively protects against LPS-induced cardiac dysfunction and ferroptosis in diabetic conditions by activating the SIRT1/FoxO1 pathway.Keywords: Diabetes, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), ferroptosis, sirtuin 1 (SIRT1), forkhead box O1 (FoxO1), propofol (PPF), salvianolic acid A (SAA)

Introduction

Diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM) is commonly defined as an irreversible pathological condition involving myocardial fibrosis and dysfunctional myocardial remodeling that is not caused by evidence of classic clinical manifestations [1]. Potential molecular mechanisms associated with DCM are incompletely understood, and may include, but are not limited to, hyperglycemia (HG), insulin resistance/hyperinsulinemia, dyslipidemia, a wide range of programmed active cell deaths, and mitochondrial dysfunction [2,3].

Sepsis is a serious, dysregulated inflammatory condition caused by overreaction to infection. It is characterized by fever, hyperventilation, tachycardia, increased peripheral leukocytes, and eventually multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) [4,5], wherein the heart is one of the organs most susceptible to sepsis, ultimately resulting in acute heart failure. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) has been widely used in drug-associated septic models, mainly because LPS can effectively induce neutrophilic inflammatory responses with intracellular Ca2+ dysregulation in septic cardiac dysfunction [6]. Mortality after infection also increases in patients with diabetes [7]. Patients with diabetes are at a higher risk of mortality from coronavirus disease (COVID-19) than those without diabetes; thus, glucose regulation may play a crucial role in the immune response [8,9]. With regard to the double challenge of diabetes and sepsis, the exact and relative mechanisms for both in the heart remain unknown.

Among transcription factors, forkhead box O1 (FoxO1) can be modulated by a wide variety of post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, acetylation, deacetylation, ubiquitination, and methylation), and is known to be essential for the maintenance of cardiovascular homeostasis [10,11]. Members of the sirtuin family regulate multiple critical biological processes (metabolism, cell proliferation, inflammation, and apoptosis) and sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) is involved in the pathogenesis of sepsis [12,13]. In contrast to other histone deacetylases, SIRT1 catalyzes the deacetylation of non-histone proteins (such as p53, forkhead box O, and so on) and plays a key role in oxidative stress and DNA damage. The transcription factors FoxO1 and SIRT1 are involved in cardiovascular diseases and interact at multiple levels [14,15], suggesting that SIRT1/FoxO1 may be recognized as a new therapeutic target in diabetes-sepsis patients with heart dysfunction.

Many studies have been conducted on myocardial apoptosis or autophagy [16,17], they have not provided concrete guidance for clinical practice. Recent studies have shown that ferroptosis is the main form of long-term cell death during myocardial ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury and an important cause of exacerbation of septic cardiomyopathy [18,19]. Lethal reactive oxygen species (ROS) and iron accumulation (Fe2+) have been increasingly emphasized as crucial initiators and mediators of ferroptosis in various pathological conditions and species [20,21]. As a newly emerging form of programmed and non-apoptotic cell death, many studies have demonstrated that inhibition of excessive iron and ferroptosis is beneficial in diverse disease models, such as I/R injury in cardiomyocytes and hyperactive inflammation [22–24]. Unfortunately, practical therapies that target ferroptosis for the treatment of diabetes and sepsis are lacking owing to an insufficient mechanistic understanding.

In recent years, studies have demonstrated that propofol (PPF, 2-6-diisopropylphenol, used in surgical and sedative therapy) and salvianolic acid A (SAA, extracted from the roots of a traditional Chinese medicinal herb, Salvia miltiorrhiza) exerted cardioprotective effects against oxidative stress and varied forms of cell deaths [25,26], due to both of them possess the unique phenolic structure. Emerging data have shown that propofol (at concentrations of 20 and 50 μM) acted on the AKT/p53 or nuclear factor erythroid 2 related factor 2/glutathione peroxidase 4 (Nrf2/GPX4) signaling pathway to protect cardiomyocytes against ferroptosis [27,28]. However, the optimal concentration of propofol used in the above studies was higher than the typical dosage in the clinical setting, which is not applicable for septic patients and may cause hemodynamic instability. In addition, SAA confers anti-inflammatory effects as a free radical scavenger to attenuate LPS-induced damage [29,30]. However, the effects of PPF and SAA, especially their use in combination with diabetes/LPS-induced myocardial toxicity, remain poorly understood. Considering the limited clinical use of high concentrations of PPF, we hypothesized that low PPF combined with SAA may confer cardiac protection by inhibiting myocardial ferroptosis through the SIRT1/FoxO1 signaling pathway under diabetic and septic conditions.

To test this hypothesis, a series of in vitro and in vivo experiments was performed to investigate the effect and underlying mechanism of PPF combined with SAA on ferroptosis.

Materials and methods

Animals and experimental groups

C57BL/6J male mice (4-week-old) were obtained from the Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center. All animals were allowed free access to food and water and were maintained at a constant temperature of 22 ± 2 °C and relative humidity of 60 ± 5% (12-h light/dark cycle). The mice were acclimatized for 1 week. All experimental protocols and animal handling procedures were conducted according to the recommendations of the principles of animal care and were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University. In this experimental design, each individual mouse is considered as an independent experimental unit. Each cage contains five mice.

C57BL/6J male mice were randomly assigned to eight groups: control (NC), type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM), NC + LPS, DM + LPS, DM + LPS + PPF1 (at a dose of 90 mg/kg/h), DM + LPS + PPF2 (at a dose of 46 mg/kg/h) + SAA (at a dose of 10 mg/kg/h), DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + EX527 (selective SIRT1 Inhibitor, Abcam, Ab141506), and DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + AS1842856 (FoxO1 inhibitor, Selleck, Shanghai, China). PPF and SAA were continuously infused via the jugular vein catheter using a micro-infusion pump starting 2 h before mice sacrifice [31]. In this experimental design, each group consists of 10 mice. For the Western Blot analysis, the sample size is 3, while for all other experimental measurements, the sample size is 6. The sample size for this study was determined based on a review of the existing literature. In Excel, use the formula = RANDBETWEEN(1,100) to assign a random number between 1 and 100 to each animal’s ID. This ensures that each animal is associated with a randomly generated number within the specified range. In this experiment, the order of treatments, measurements, and the placement of animals/cages were not strictly controlled. We hypothesize that these factors exert negligible influence on the experimental outcomes. Nevertheless, future studies could consider further optimization of these variables to enhance experimental rigor.

A diabetic model was created by treating mice with a high-fat diet (HFD, Cat.D12492, Research Diets, New Brunswick) for 8 weeks, followed by intraperitoneal injection of streptozotocin (STZ, Sigma) at a dose of 50 mg/kg dissolved in 100 mM citrate buffer (pH 4.5) for 3 consecutive days. Fasting blood glucose levels were assessed from a tail vein blood sample using a OneTouch Ultra blood glucose meter one week later, and mice with fasting glycemia levels higher than 16.7 mM were considered DM [32,33]. The control and NC + LPS groups received a normal diet and an equal volume of citrate buffer.

After confirmation of diabetes, the mice were exposed to an intraperitoneal injection of LPS (10 mg/kg, Sigma) to induce septic cardiac dysfunction [34], while the NC and DM groups were treated with equal volumes of saline. In addition, the SIRT1 inhibitor EX527 (at a dose of 5 mg/kg) was administered intraperitoneally 24 h before LPS injection [35] while the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 was administered intragastrically 30 min before the injection of LPS [36]. The grouping of animals and the sequence of treatments were randomized to ensure unbiased allocation. Detailed experimental protocols were established to enhance consistency throughout the experimental process. Furthermore, automated equipment was utilized, and data were independently recorded by multiple individuals to reduce the impact of subjective factors. Given these measures, formal blinding was not deemed necessary.

Six hours following the LPS injection, echocardiography, HE staining, transmission electron microscopy, and serum were collected, and heart samples were taken and preserved at −80 °C. Euthanasia was carried out using CO2 asphyxiation according to protocol. The euthanasia method was aligned with the AVMA guidelines.

Cell culture and drug administration

H9c2 cells were exposed to HG (35 mM glucose) and cultured in 95% air and 5% CO2 at 37 °C for 48 h, with normal glucose as a control [10]. Then, the cells were subjected to LPS (10 μg/ml) for 6 h in the absence or presence of various concentrations of PPF (graded from low to high concentration varying from 12.5, 25, 50 to 100 μM) or SAA (graded from low to high concentration varying from 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50 to 100 μM). The H9c2(2-1) (CL-0089) cell line was purchased from Wuhan Pricella Biotechnology Co., Ltd. This cell line is a well-characterized rat cardiomyoblast cell line, commonly used for studying cardiac cell biology and pharmacology.

M-mode echocardiography

M-mode echocardiography enabled the researchers to accurately measure ejection fraction (EF%) and fractional shortening (FS%) by analyzing the motion of the heart walls and volumetric changes within the cardiac chambers during each cardiac cycle. The imaging procedure was performed using a high-resolution ultrasound system specifically designed for small animals. Mice were anesthetized with 1.5–2% isoflurane, and EF% and FS% were assessed via M-mode echocardiography and recorded [37].

HE staining and lipid peroxidation

After undergoing echocardiographic evaluation, the mice were euthanized, and their hearts were harvested and promptly fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin. The fixed cardiac tissue was subsequently dehydrated through a graded series of ethanol solutions, ranging from 75 to 100%. Following dehydration, the tissue was cleared, embedded in paraffin, sectioned into slices ∼4–5 microns thick, and mounted onto glass slides. The slides were subsequently stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), C11-BODIPY493/503, or DAPI, following the manufacturer’s protocol (Servicebio, China).

Biochemical analysis

The eyeballs of the mice were carefully extracted using an elbow forceps and promptly removed. Simultaneously, the heart of the mice was gently compressed with the middle finger of the left hand to enhance the pumping efficiency of the heart. The collected arterial blood samples were gently mixed to prevent vigorous agitation. Subsequently, the samples were placed in a centrifuge and subjected to centrifugation at 3000–4000 rpm for 5–10 min to isolate the serum. The isolated serum was then transferred to new tubes for further analysis. Using an automatic biochemical analyzer from HITACHI, Japan, the levels of creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) in the mice were determined according to the manufacturer’s guidelines [38].

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Dojindo, Japan) in 96-well plates. H9c2 cells were cultured and treated in 96-well plates. Remove the 96-well plate from the incubator, gently aspirate the old culture medium from each well, ensuring not to aspirate the cells. Subsequently, add 100 μL of the pre-prepared CCK-8 working solution to each well. During this process, ensure that the pipette tip does not contact the cells to avoid potential damage. Finally, return the 96-well plate to the incubator for further incubation (30 min in the dark). At the end of the incubation period, the 96-well plate was retrieved from the incubator and immediately transferred to the microplate reader. The absorbance (OD) values of each well were measured at a wavelength of 450 nm. The microplate reader automatically acquired and recorded the absorbance data for each well. Additionally, the degree of cytotoxicity, reflecting cell membrane integrity, was evaluated using the Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) Release Assay Kit (Roche, Switzerland).

Detection of malonaldehyde (MDA), superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (Gpx), interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α), and IL-18

According to the manufacturers’ guidelines (Jianglai, China), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) were used to measure MDA, Gpx, SOD, and inflammatory markers (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, and TNF-α) in serum and cell supernatants [39]. Standard and diluted serum samples were added to the microplate at 100 μL per well. The microplate was incubated at 37 °C for 90 min, followed by two washes with washing solution. Subsequently, 100 μL per well of biotin-labeled detection antibody was added, and the plate was incubated again at 37 °C for 60 min. Afterward, the plate was washed three times with washing solution. Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled streptavidin was then added at 100 μL per well, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 30 min. The plate was washed five times with washing solution, and TMB color development solution was added at 90 μL per well. The plate was incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 10–20 min. Termination solution was subsequently added at 50 μL per well to stop the reaction, causing the color to turn yellow. Finally, the OD values were measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader, and the concentration of the target analyte in the samples was determined based on the standard curve.

Flow cytometry

ROS levels were measured using the DCFH-DA (2,7-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate) ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime, China), while apoptotic cells were detected using the Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions. Both adherent and suspended cells were collected, ensuring a consistent cell count of 1 × 104 cells per sample. Dilute DCFH-DA with the probe dilution solution at a ratio of 1:1000 to achieve a final concentration of 10 μmol/L. Incubate the suspension in a 37 °C cell culture incubator for 20 min. Gently invert and mix every 3–5 min to ensure adequate contact between the probe and the cells. Wash the cells three times with PBS to completely remove unincorporated DCFH-DA. As for apoptotic detection, centrifuge the cells at 1000 × g for 5 min, discard the supernatant, and resuspend the cells in 195 μL of Annexin V-FITC binding buffer. Subsequently, add 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC and gently mix. Then, add 10 μL of propidium iodide staining solution and gently mix again. Incubate the mixture at room temperature (20–25 °C) in the dark for 10–20 min, and subsequently place it on ice. The intracellular ROS levels and the apoptotic index were subsequently analyzed by flow cytometry, and the data were processed using FlowJo 10 software [40].

FerroOrange staining

A 1 μM FerroOrange (Dojindo, Japan) working solution (100 μL per well) was added to the cells, followed by incubation at 37 °C and 5% CO2 for 30 min. The excitation wavelength was set to 530–565 nm, and the emission wavelength was set to 570–620 nm using a universal G excitation filter.

Transmission electron microscope

Following the administration of LPS as previously described, the anterior wall of the left ventricle was resected and cut into cubes measuring 2 × 2 × 2 mm. These samples were then fixed overnight in an electron microscope fixative solution (G1102, Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Subsequently, the tissue sections were embedded in phosphate-buffered solution and heated in an oven for further processing. The sections were then ultrathin-sectioned to a thickness of 50–60 nm and stained with a 2% uranyl acetate and lead citrate solution. Finally, images were acquired using a transmission electron microscope (HITACHI, Tokyo, Japan).

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP)

When the mitochondrial membrane potential is high, JC-1 accumulates in the mitochondrial matrix and forms polymers (J-aggregates), emitting red fluorescence. Conversely, when the mitochondrial membrane potential is low, JC-1 fails to accumulate in the mitochondrial matrix and exists as monomers, emitting green fluorescence instead. Therefore, changes in the mitochondrial membrane potential can be conveniently detected through the shift in fluorescence color. The relative ratio of red to green fluorescence is commonly used as an indicator of MMP. MMP evaluation was conducted using the JC-1 kit from Beyotime, China. Cells were exposed to 1 mL of JC-1 working solution for 20 min in the dark at 37 °C. Post-staining, the cells underwent at least three washes with JC-1 buffer solution to remove any excess. Fluorescence images of the stained cells were captured using a fluorescence microscope.

Transfection of small interfering RNA (siRNA) into cells

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) for SIRT1 and FoxO1 (provided by the Guangzhou Ribibio company, China) were used with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Canada) for 6 h in Opti-MEM, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After transfection, the cells were refreshed with hyperglycemic cell culture medium for 48 h and were then assigned to continue the experiments. SIRT1 and FoxO1 specific siRNA:

Western blotting

Western blot analysis was performed as previously described [41] using antibodies against SIRT1 (1:1000, Cat No. 9475, CST), FoxO1 (1:1000, Cat No. 2880, CST), acetylated FoxO1 (ac-FoxO1) (1:1000, Cat No. AF2305, Affinit), Ferritin (1:1000, Cat No. 11682-1-AP, Proteintech), GPX4 (1:1000, Cat No. 67763-1, Proteintech), and GAPDH (1:1000, Cat No. 2118, CST). GAPDH was used as the reference gene. The catalogue number for the ladder used is as follows: Ladder: [Genstar], Catalogue Number: [M221]. Protein bands were visualized using a scan imaging system and quantified with ImageJ software.

Transcription factor binding site analysis

The transcription start site (TSS) of the mouse GPX4 gene was obtained from the NCBI Gene database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene). The GPX4 gene is located on the plus strand of chromosome 10 (NC_000076.7), spanning from 79,883,000 to 79,892,273 bp. The promoter region was defined by extracting a 2.1 kb sequence, which included 2,000 bp upstream and 100 bp downstream of the TSS. The 2.1 kb GPX4 promoter sequence was then analyzed using the FoxO1 position-weight matrix from the JASPAR CORE Vertebrata database (https://jaspar.genereg.net). A relative score threshold of 0.85 was applied to achieve a balance between sensitivity and specificity. Only binding sites with a raw score >10 and a relative score exceeding 0.85 were retained.

Statistical analysis

All values are expressed as the mean ± SD. Comparisons between groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test, or Student’s t-test when appropriate. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

Results

Diabetes aggravated LPS-induced injury in mice

Using C57BL/6J male mice, we first investigated whether the presence of DM exacerbates LPS-induced cardiac inflammation and heart disorders. After administration of HFD and STZ injection, a significant difference was observed between the DM and NC groups. As shown in Figure 1, prolonged high glucose led to increases in pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-18), which were largely boosted after LPS challenge compared to the DM or NC+LPS groups (Figures 1a–d). In addition, diabetes-induced increases in cardiac CK-MB (Figure 1e), AST (Figure 1f) and MDA production (Figure 1g) as well as the reduction in SOD (Figure 1h) were further increased by LPS, along with further reductions in antioxidant enzyme activities (Figure 1l). Echocardiography revealed that EF% and FS% were apparently lower under LPS damage, either in the presence or absence of diabetes (Figures 1i,j,k), whereas diabetes further exacerbated LPS-induced cardiac dysfunction.

Ferroptosis in hearts were activated in diabetes and exacerbated after LPS stimulation

As depicted in Figure 2a, results of HE staining demonstrated mild inflammatory response and cytoplasmic vacuolation in the DM group compared to the NC group, which was further aggravated by LPS under diabetic conditions. Additionally, the pathological changes, as assessed by HE staining, in the DM + LPS group were part of remarkable hemorrhage, interstitial edema, and myocardial fiber fracture. We further examined the protein expression of Ferritin, GPX4, and SIRT1 using western blotting, given that ferroptosis has been shown to be involved in various types of diseases [42,43] and regulated by SIRT1 [44] in the heart. As shown in Figures 2b,c,e,f, expression of Ferritin, GPX4, and SIRT1 was significantly decreased in the DM group, and LPS further exacerbated these changes. A growing body of evidence supports a key role for SIRT1/FoxO1 in diabetic individuals [45,46], our results further demonstrated the presence of enhanced ac-FoxO1/FoxO1 protein expression in mice exposed to diabetes and LPS (Figure 2d). Additionally, transmission electron microscopy revealed that the myocardial mitochondrial ridges in the DM group were decreased or distorted compared with those in the NC group, and this condition was significantly exacerbated in both the NC + LPS and especially in + LPS groups (Figure 2g). The BODIPY 493/503 C11 probe indicated that levels of oxidized lipids were elevated in both the DM and NC + LPS groups, with further exacerbation observed in the DM + LPS group (Figures 2h,i).

LPS triggered ferroptosis in vitro

To mimic the condition of sepsis in diabetic patients, in vitro, rat heart-derived H9c2 cells were exposed to HG for 48 h, as we investigated previously [41], along with 1 or 10 μg/mL LPS. As shown in Figure 3, the cell viability (Figure 3a) and SOD (Figure 3c) of the HG + LPS10 group were significantly lower than those of the NC, HG, and NC + LPS10 groups, while the release of LDH (Figure 3b) and MDA (Figure 3d) were also significantly higher than those of the NC, HG, and NC + LPS10 groups. Moreover, cell injury was further worsened in the LPS 10 μg/mL group compared with LPS at the low concentration of 1 μg/mL. Therefore, we used 10 μg/mL LPS as the intervention condition in subsequent experiments. Consistent with the above studies in vivo, the activity of Ferritin, GPX4 and SIRT1 (Figures 3e,f,h,i) in the HG group was significantly decreased compared with the NC group, and was further significantly decreased in the HG + LPS group compared with the NC + LPS group, along with a further significant increase in ac-FoxO1 (Figures 3e,g). As such, our results suggest that ferroptosis may play a key role in the double challenge of diabetes-sepsis in vivo and HG + LPS in vitro. However, the potential impact of SIRT1/FoxO1 on ferroptosis needs to be further studied.

PPF and SAA or their combination reduced inflammation, ROS production, and apoptosis in LPS-induced H9c2 injury

PPF has moderate antioxidant capacity that confer protection against cardiovascular disease [41,47], similar to PPF, SAA can also conferred cardio-protective effects as a free radical scavenger through targeting various types of cellular signaling pathways [26]. The above-mentioned cardioprotective effects of SAA and PPF might stem from their unique polyphenolic structures (Figures 4a,b). To investigate the potential toxicity of SAA and PPF in the HG + LPS model, we conducted in vitro. The results showed that no toxicity was observed following administration of SAA (graded from low to high concentration varying from 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50 to 100 μM) or PPF (graded from low to high concentration varying from 12.5, 25, 50 to 100 μM) for up to 12 h (Figures 4c,d). Furthermore, we found that PPF attenuated cell damage in a dose-dependent manner, being effective at concentrations ranging from 12.5 to 50 µM, while the most prominent effect was seen at 25 µM (Figures 4e,f). Meanwhile, SAA also conferred dose-dependent protective effects, which started to be effective at a minimum administration of 12.5 µM (Figures 4g,h). However, the concentration at 25 µM of PPF is higher than that commonly used in the clinical setting. Therefore, we used a combination of PPF and SAA (P12.5 + S12.5) as a new therapy in our subsequent experiments. As depicted in Figures 4i–m, we found that the HG + LPS + P12.5 + S12.5 group most effectively attenuated H9c2 cell inflammation response (including inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-18), compared to the HG + LPS, HG + LPS + P12.5, and HG + LPS + S12.5 groups. In addition, no apparent difference was observed between the HG + LPS + P25 and HG + LPS + P12.5 + S12.5 group in apoptotic rate and ROS production (Figures 4n–q).

PPF combined with or without SAA attenuated the LPS-induced increases of ferroptosis under high glucose

Recent studies have indicated that the activation of inflammation in the presence of elevated ROS production can lead to ferroptosis. As shown in Figure 5, we observed that PPF with or without SAA reduced intracellular Fe2+ level (Figures 5a,b) and impaired MMP (Figures 5c,d). Meanwhile, the level of SIRT1 (Figures 5e,f) was increased, along with a reduction in ac-FoxO1/FoxO1 (Figures 5e,g), while the expression of ferroptosis-related genes, such as ferritin (Figures 5e,h) and GPX4 (Figures 5e,i), was activated. These results indicate that low concentrations of PPF combined with SAA yielded considerable anti-ferroptosis effects in H9c2 cells, which was identical to the high concentration of propofol alone. However, the relationship between PPF combined with SAA and SIRT1/FoxO1 in HG + LPS-induced ferroptosis remains unclear.

To confirm whether LPS-induced injury was triggered by SIRT1/FoxO1 and to explore the related treatment mechanism of PPF combined with SAA, we specifically designed and synthesized three siRNAs for SIRT1 and FoxO1, namely siRNA-SIRT1 (siRNA1-3) or siRNA-FoxO1 (siRNA4-6). Transfection with siRNA-SIRT1-3 most profoundly downregulated the protein expression level of SIRT1 (Figures 6a,e), whereas siRNA-FoxO1-6 most significantly downregulated FoxO1 (Figures 6b,f). We then knocked down SIRT1 and FoxO1 in H9c2 cells using siRNA-SIRT1-3 or siRNA-FoxO1-6 (Figures 6c,d,g,h) and assessed the efficacy of PPF combined with SAA on LPS-induced cell injury under HG. As shown in Figure 7, cell injury, apoptosis, and ROS activity were further exacerbated when SIRT1 or FoxO1 was knocked down, despite the presence of PPF combined with SAA treatment.

To test whether the combination of PPF with SAA attenuated LPS-induced ferroptosis via SIRT1/FoxO1 signaling, we knocked down SIRT1 and FoxO1 to observe FerroOrange and JC-1 staining and ferroptosis in H9c2 cells challenged with LPS + HG. Intracellular iron levels (Figures 8a,b) and MMP (Figures 8c,d) were significantly increased in LPS-induced H9c2 injury after SIRT1 or FoxO1 inhibition. We also observed that both ferritin (Figures 8e,h) and GPX4 (Figures 8e,i) markedly decreased despite treatment with the drug combination. More importantly, ac-FoxO1/FoxO1 (Figures 8e,g) level were upregulated after SIRT1 silencing, while SIRT1 expression (Figures 8e,f) did not change with knockdown of FoxO1, indicating that PPF combined with SAA alleviated LPS-induced ferroptosis via deacetylation of FoxO1 mediated by SIRT1 in H9c2 cells under high glucose conditions.

Inhibitors of SIRT1 or FoxO1 reversed the cardioprotective effects of PPF combined with SAA in diabetic-LPS mice

In our previous study, we discovered that both PPF and SAA mitigated the effects of hypoxia-reoxygenation on H9c2 cardiomyocytes by inhibiting ferroptosis under hyperglycemic and palmitate acid conditions, with the dosages of both PPF and/or SAA being identical to those used in the current experiments [31]. We speculated that PPF combined with SAA might yield ­cardioprotective effects in diabetic-LPS mice, based on the fact that PPF combined with SAA reduced post-ischemic myocardial infarction and improved cardiac function. In addition, we employed the SIRT1 inhibitor EX527 and FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 to further substantiate that PPF combined with SAA protects against LPS-induced cardiac dysfunction and ferroptosis by activating the SIRT1/FoxO1 signaling pathway under diabetic conditions. As shown in Figures 9a–g, the levels of inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-18, IL-1β, and TNF-α), CK-MB, AST, and MDA were reduced by PPF or PPF in combination with SAA. However, these reductions were reversed by the SIRT1 inhibitor EX527 and the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856. Levels of Gpx and SOD, as well as the percentage of EF and FS, were increased in the DM + LPS + PPF1 and DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA groups, but were reduced in the DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + EX527 and DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + AS1842856 groups (Figures 9h–l). In most cases, a lower dose of PPF combined with SAA resulted in better cardioprotective effects than PPF alone. Additionally, the pathological changes assessed by HE staining in the DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + EX527 and DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + AS1842856 groups included notable hemorrhage, interstitial edema, and myocardial fiber fracture, even in the presence of PPF and SAA (Figure 10a). The downregulation of ferritin and GPX4, along with increased oxidized lipids and decreased myocardial mitochondrial ridges, indicated that LPS-induced ferroptosis was enhanced in both DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + EX527 and DM + LPS + PPF2 + SAA + AS1842856 groups (Figures 10b–i).

Discussion

The major finding of this study is that low concentrations of PPF combined with SAA attenuated ferroptosis caused by excessive inflammation, iron, lipid peroxidation, and ROS production stimulated by LPS via activation of the SIRT/FoxO1 pathway under high glucose conditions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate that PPF combined with SAA inhibits myocardial ferroptosis and to explore the underlying molecular mechanism.

The etiological factors and risks associated with heart diseases are complicated and difficult to elucidate, particularly under chronic and inconspicuous high glucose. Consistent with previous studies [48,49], our data revealed diabetes-induced cardiac systolic dysfunction, including decreased EF% and FS% and increases in CK-MB, AST, MDA, and inflammatory cytokines, which were further aggravated by LPS in mice. Despite the above findings, the interaction between diabetes, chronic diseases, and acute infections is intricate. According to early studies, diabetics, compared to non-diabetics, have an increased risk of respiratory failure and a higher risk of renal failure during sepsis [50]. Underlying molecular mechanisms of cardio injury in the diabetic -septic model are more sophisticated and multifactorial than those in diabetes and sepsis, respectively, and a more in-depth understanding of the possible pathogenesis is beneficial for developing pharmacological strategies combating diabetes complicated with sepsis.

Inhibition of oxidative stress and inflammatory responses has been shown to yield tremendous protective effects against cardiomyocyte death [51,52]. Because of lipid-based reactive oxygen species, Stockwell et al. coined the term ferroptosis in 2012 to describe iron-dependent cell death [21,53]. Since its recognition, with the development of molecular biology technology, accumulating evidence has illustrated that controlling ferroptosis is vital for maintaining the metabolic homeostasis of multiple physiological and pathological processes, including but not limited to cancer cell death, neurodegeneration, acute renal failure, I/R injury, and T-cell immunity [16,53,54]. For instance, Fang et al. showed that inhibition of ferroptosis by ferrostatin-1 ameliorated heart failure induced by both acute and chronic I/R in mice, and significantly reduced doxorubicin-induced cardiomyopathy features of typical ferroptotic cell death [23]. Consistently, we found that upregulation of ferroptosis in diabetic mice and high glucose treated-H9c2 cells was acerbated by LPS treatment. Interestingly, ferroptosis therapy is increasingly recognized as an attractive strategy to selectively damage cancer cells through lipid peroxide (LPO) overaccumulation [55,56]. Moreover, sulforaphane has also been demonstrated to inhibit ferroptosis in type 2 diabetic mice with DCM through AMPK-mediated Nrf2 activation according to a recent study, while a study by Zang et al. suggested that suppression of autophagy enhanced Nrf2-mediated ferroptosis in H9c2 cardiomyocytes [57,58]. Taken together, credible evidence for the developmental mechanism of ferroptosis in clinical patients remains unclear. Despite the classical view of iron-induced ferritin upregulation, our data suggest a shift in cellular iron homeostasis under inflammatory and metabolic stress. The observed inverse correlation, where high intracellular ferrous ion levels coincide with decreased ferritin protein, is likely a consequence of ferritinophagy, a process that degrades ferritin to increase the labile iron pool for inflammatory metabolic demands.

As a class III histone/protein deacetylase (HDACs), SIRT1 plays an important role in cellular defense and survival by deacetylating p53 and forkhead transcription factors (FoxO) [59,60]. Acetylation of transcription factors might act as positive regulators of ferroptosis by promoting ROS production, and ferroptosis therapy could restrain the growth of tumors by repressing SLC7A11 [61,62]. In contrast, Chen et al. demonstrated that SIRT1 activation alleviated heat stress-induced lung epithelial cell injury against dysregulated ferroptosis by inhibiting p53 acetylation [63]. Additionally, FoxO1, being one of a substrate of SIRT1, is also modulated by deacetylation. Early studies found that SIRT1 deacetylates FoxO, thereby increasing FoxO’s ability to prevent oxidative stress and inflammation under high glucose conditions [64], and the findings of our results are in agreement with this concept. Recent research highlights a critical link between FoxO1 and the regulation of ferroptosis in neurons, revealing that FoxO1 directly suppresses NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy, a process that leads to ferritin degradation and increased iron availability [65]. However, whether NCOA4-ferritinophagy may contribute to the observed iron/ferritin dynamics and ferroptosis phenotype in cardiomyocytes in the context of sepsis and/or diabetes merits further studies.

Patients with diabetes seem to be more vulnerable to infections and may have to be amputated in the treatment of diabetic foot. Recent studies have revealed that diabetes increases the likelihood of poor prognosis and the risk of progression to intensive care and death [66,67]. PPF has been widely utilized in various surgical procedures and intensive care units and been a hot topic of discussion employing its protective properties stem from its phenolic structure to combat against myocardial I/R injury by elimination of ROS, inhibition of apoptosis and autophagy, maintenance of mitochondrial function, reduction of inflammatory mediators [68,69]. Studies from our laboratory have demonstrated a pivotal role of FoxO1 in cardiac metabolism, and the upregulation of FoxO1 achieved with a high dose of propofol is attributable to the reduction in H9c2 cardiac cell apoptosis and autophagy induced by hypoxia/reoxygenation injury under high glucose in vitro [41]. However, the optimal concentration of PPF used in the above studies is higher than that commonly used in the clinical setting, which may cause hemodynamic instability, so it is not appropriate for diabetic/septic patients who have more fragile cardiovascular stability than patients without diabetes or sepsis, not to mention those concomitantly suffering from diabetes and sepsis.

Salvianolic acid A has been demonstrated to have antioxidant, anti-thrombotic, neuroprotective, and anti-fibrotic effects in vitro but has also been proven to act as a cardiovascular protector in vivo due to its polyphenolic structure [70–72]. Meanwhile, a study on SAA in diabetic rats found that it preserved peripheral nerve function by modulating the AMPK-PGC1α-Sirt3 axis [73]. To address the controversy and potential toxicity of PPF at relatively high concentrations, particularly in critically ill patients with multiple comorbidities, such as diabetes and sepsis, we propose a novel combined treatment regimen. This regimen involves administering low doses of PPF in conjunction with SAA, which we recently demonstrated to be feasible in subjects with diabetes and myocardial I/R injury. Our findings indicate that activation of the CD36/AMPK signaling pathway is beneficial for reducing ferroptosis, which may also explain the cardioprotective effects of PPF and SAA in diabetic mice with sepsis [31]. Further in-depth studies are required to elucidate this mechanism.

Our study indicates that activation of the SIRT/FoxO1 pathway by PPF-SAA is a key protective pathway against LPS-induced ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes under high glucose conditions. Meanwhile, FoxO1 serves as a crucial regulator of ferroptosis. Utilizing the NCBI and JASPAR databases, potential FoxO1-binding sites within the GPX4 promoter were predicted. Three potential FoxO1-binding sites (score >10, relative score >0.85, species: mouse) were identified within this genomic segment (Figure 11a). Additionally, two sequence logos, MA0480.1 (Figure 11b) and MA0480.2 (Figure 11c), along with two frequency matrices (Figures 11d,e), were presented, suggesting that GPX4 functions as a downstream target of FoxO1. A recent study demonstrates that FoxO1 translocates to the nucleus to activate the expression of the GPX4 gene, which is enhanced by bone morphogenetic protein 9, through chromatin immunoprecipitation assays [74], confirming the interaction between FoxO1 and GPX4. H9c2 cells are not cardiomyocytes, although they have been proven to be suitable and reliable in vitro studies. The combined regimen of PPF and SAA requires extensive evaluation in preclinical and clinical trials before it can be recommended for retarding dysregulated ferroptosis and septic-diabetic complications.

Conclusion

Taken together, our results indicate that high glucose aggravates LPS-induced ferroptosis along with abnormal levels of SIRT/FoxO1 in vitro and in vivo, while PPF combined with SAA alleviated LPS-induced cardiac toxicity by inhibiting ferroptosis by activating the deacetylation of FoxO1 by targeting SIRT1 under hyperglycemia.

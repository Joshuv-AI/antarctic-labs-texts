Modeling energy requirement for layer-type chicks reared at different ambient temperatures
Authors: Yihui Liu, Hongchao Jiao, Xiaojuan Wang, Min Liu, Haifang Li, Minghui Wang, Jingpeng Zhao, Hai Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrecision feeding of poultry depends on the dynamic estimation of nutrient requirement, which is related to the physiological stage and rearing temperature. The objective of this study is to determine the effect of temperature on the net energy requirement of layer-type chicks in the periods from 1 to 14 d of age. In trail 1, the energy requirement for maintenance (MEm) of chicks at different temperatures was measured. A total of 160 one-day old Jingfen layer-type chicks were selected and divided into five groups with four replicates of eight chicks. The chicks were randomly subjected to one of five temperature treatments: 30, 32, 34, 36, and 38 °C. After 4-days adaptation, the oxygen consumption and CO2 production were measured at 6-days of age for 72 h with open-circuit negative pressure respiratory thermometry device. n trial 2, the growth performance of chicks reared at different temperatures was determined to evaluate the NE requirement for body weight (BW) gain. A total of 480 1-day-old chicks were divided into five groups and each group had 12 replicates of eight chicks. The rearing temperatures were 32-30-29 °C, 33.5-32-30.5 °C, 35-34-32 °C, 36.5-36-34 °C, and 38-38-35 °C in the periods from D 1 to D 3, D 4 to D 7, and D 8 to D 14, respectively. During the 14-days experimental period, all the chicks had free access to feed and water. The body weight and feed intake were measured weekly. Body composition and energy content were determined at the beginning and end of the experiment. The result showed that the fasting heat production (FHP) was decreased with increasing temperature (P < 0.001). The FHP of chicks at 30, 32, 34, 36, and 38 °C was 519.70, 710.80, 493.50, 361.70, and 437.90 kJ/kg W0.75/day, respectively. The BW gain and feed intake were decreased (P < 0.001) when temperature increased, whereas the feed conversion ratio was changed by temperature only in the period of D 1 to D 7 (P < 0.01). The relative weight of heart (P < 0.05), liver (P < 0.001), and breast muscles (P < 0.001) were decreased with increased temperature. The NE requirement for BW gain was not altered (P > 0.05) by temperature. The NE requirement of Jingfen layer-type chicks in the periods from 1 to14 days of age is: NE (kJ/bird·day-1) = (505.93 − 162.78T − 38.87T2 + 194.41T3)·kgW0.75 + 7.16·BW gain (g/d).Keywords: Fasting heat production, Energy requirement, Net energy, Temperature, Layer-type chick

Introduction

Energy is essential for sustaining life and maintaining productive activities in poultry. The energy requirement in growing animals is the sum of the requirement for maintenance of basic functions and that for growth (Hurwitz et al., 1978, 1980). Ambient temperature affects energy requirement for maintenance (MEm) via altering energy requirement for maintaining body temperature. Unfortunately, there is litter information about the influence of environmental temperature on energy requirement for maintenance of layer-type pullets. In broiler breeder pullets between 38 and 70 d of age, the MEm requirements were reduced linearly with increased temperature from 15 to 30 °C (Sakomura et al., 2003). The MEm of growing broilers increased from 433 kJ/kg W0.75/d at 35 °C to 693 kJ/kg W0.75/d at 15 °C (Hoffmann et al., 1991).

The studies in laying hens indicates that energy requirement for maintenance is increased by low temperature. Laying hens reared in a low-temperature environment (21 °C) had higher metabolic energy requirements for maintenance than those in a high-temperature environment (32 °C Valencia et al., 1980). Compared with the laying hens reared at 32.2 °C, there was a 25.4 % higher maintenance requirement in hens at 15.6 °C (Reid and Maiorino, 1984). Within a proper temperature range, the energy requirements for maintenance keeps at a low level. The MEm of laying hens was similar at 16 and 21 °C but tended to increase by 3.4 % at 11 °C (van Krimpen et al., 2014). In the range of 16.1 to 31.1 °C, the MEm of laying hens was higher at cold temperatures (16.1 and 18.9 °C), reached a plateau between 22.2 and 27.8 °C, and decreased sharply at 31.1 °C (Peguri and Coon, 1991). Similarly, the MEm of broiler breeders showed a quadratic effect with increased temperature from 13 to 30 °C and showed comparable level at 21 °C and 30 °C) (Rabello et al., 2006). By contrary, broiler breeder reared at temperatures from 15 °C to 27 °C, MEm increased as temperature either dropped below 24.3 °C or increased above 24.3 °C (Pishnamazi et al., 2015). Feather coverage and temperature affect MEm of laying type pullets (Neme et al., 2005). Young chicks are more sensitive to ambient temperature as the immature thermoregulation capacity (Lin et al., 1996; Takahashi et al., 2005). We hypothesize that temperature affects the energy requirement for maintenance of layer-type chicks in a different way from that of adult hens.

Generally, it is considered that the energy requirement for weight gain is not influence by temperature. For example, in broiler, energy requirement for protein retention varied between 1.67 and 1.89 kJ ME/g and was independent of environmental temperature (15 to 35 °C) (Hoffmann et al., 1991). The efficiency of energy utilization for retention, however, is dependent on temperature. In broiler breeder pullets, there is a lower energetic cost for energy deposition as protein and a higher energetic cost for energy deposition as fat in cooler environmental temperature (Sakomura et al., 2003). The composition of body weight (BW) gain changes energy requirement for growth in laying type pullets (Neme et al., 2005). Hence, whether the energy requirement for BW gain is affected by rearing temperature needs to be investigated further.

Net energy (NE) system have been successfully used in pig, beef and dairy cattle. Compared with Metabolizable energy (ME) system, the advantage of NE system is to take into account the heat increment (HI) associated with the metabolic utilization of ME (Noblet et al., 2023). The aim of this study was to determine the effect of temperature on the NE requirement of layer-type chicks. The energy requirements for maintenance and weight gain were determined by indirect calorimetry and comparative slaughter method, respectively. The objective of this research was to model the NE and ME requirement of chicks to environmental temperature and weight gain, supporting the application of precision feeding of layer-type chicks.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

All procedures in the study were approved by the Animal Care Committee of Shandong Agricultural University and were performed in accordance with the guidelines for experimental animals of the Ministry of Science and Technology (Beijing, P. R. China).

Experimental birds and diets

In the study, one-day old Jingfen No 6 layer-type female chicks were obtained from a local hatchery. The chicks were reared in an environmental control house. The chicks were fed with a diet formulated according to the recommendation of Feeding Standard of Chicken (NY/T, 33-2004) for layer-type chicks. Experiment design

Effect of rearing temperature on growing performance (Trial 1) A total of 480 1-day-old chicks were divided into five groups of 12 replicates and each replicate had eight chicks. The chicks were randomly housed in five different temperature-controlled rooms. The rearing temperatures were set according to previous studies (Myhre, 1978; van der Hel et al., 1991; Fig. 1). The chicks were under 24-h light during the first 3 d, 22 h light and 2 h dark from 4 to 8 days of age, and 20-h light and 4-h dark after 8 days of age. The light intensity was 40 lx. During the 14-d experimental period, all the chicks had free access to feed and water. Body weight and feed intake were measured weekly.

Temperature regimes in different rearing period of layer-type chicks, °C.

Effect of rearing temperature on fasting heat production (Trial 2) A total of 160 2-days old chicks were divided into five treatment groups and each group had four replicates of eight chicks. All the chicks in one replicate were reared in one chamber. A total of 20 respiratory chambers were used. In the chamber, chicks had free access to feed and water. The lighting regime was kept the same as that in Trial 1. On 6 days of age, all the chicks were randomly subjected to one of the five temperature treatments: reared at 30, 32, 34, 36, or 38 °C for two days. During the 2-days period, feed was withdrawn, FHP was continuously measured for 48 h. The initial and final body weights of all the chicks were recorded.

Data collection

On 1 day of age, the beginning of rearing experiment (Trial 1), 40 chicks were randomly selected and sacrificed by CO2 asphyxiation. The whole bodies including feather were immediately frozen at −20 °C and thereafter grinded two times into meat mud and mixed. Afterwards, the carcass samples were dried in an oven at 60 °C for 96 h and then reground for homogeneity. The samples were used for the determination of initial gross energy content of whole body. On 14 days of age, end of the trial, three chicks around mean body weight were selected from each replicate. After 12-hours feed withdrawal, the chicks were sacrificed as forementioned. Thereafter, one chick was dissected, the heart, liver, spleen, kidney, bursa of Fabricius, thymus, gizzard, glandular stomach, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, breast and thigh muscles, femur, and tibia were weighed, and the relative weight to body weight was calculated. The other two chicks were used for preparing carcass samples as forementioned. The carcass samples were stored at −20 ° for further analysis of gross energy of whole body.

Gross energy measurement

The gross energy of carcass was analyzed by using an adiabatic bomb calorimeter (Changsha Bente Instruments Co., Changsha, Hunan, China). Each sample was measured three times to ensure data accuracy, and all operations were conducted according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Measurement of FHP

The fasting heat production of chicks at different temperatures was determined at fasting status with “open-circuit negative pressure” respiratory thermometry device (Shandong Mingjun Ecological Agriculture Technology Co., Rizhao, Shandong, P. R. China) as described by Liu et al. (2024). Gas was continuously extracted from the respiration chamber by a vacuum pump and the O2 and CO2 concentrations were measured at 3-min intervals by gas analyzer. The volume of each thermal chamber is 800 L, and the ventilation volume is 20 L per minute, which ensures the air quality in the thermal chamber. Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as mean ± standard error. All the data were analyzed by one-way ANOVA model (SAS, Version 8e, SAS Institute). Differences between the treatments were evaluated using Duncan’s multiple comparisons tests. The orthogonal comparisons were applied for linear and quadratic effects of temperature treatments. Differences were considered as significant at P < 0.05. Eight mathematical models were fitted independently to NEm. The adequacy of the models was obtained by the coefficients of determination (R2), Akaike's Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) as described by Sharifi et al. (2022).

Discussion

In the present study, the NE requirement of layer-type chicks rearing at different temperature was estimated. For growing animals, the NE requirement includes the NE for maintenance and growth.

The NE for maintenance cannot be determined directly and is generally estimated by the measurement of FHP (Birkett and de Lange, 2001; Noblet et al., 2010; 2015). FHP is affected by fasting time. It is reported that FHP decreased over the 3-days fasting time in laying hens (Ning et al., 2013). In our previous study in layer-type chicks, the result indicates that FHP decreased with fasting time and the adequate measuring period for FHP of layer-type pullets is from 24 to 48 h after fasting (Liu et al., 2024). In the present study, the FHP measured from 24 to 48 h after fasting was used to estimate the NE requirement for maintenance. The FHP measured under a suitable environmental temperature (34 °C) was 493.5 kJ/kgW0.75·d-1. Many factors such as breeds, physiological stage, body weight, physical activity, and fasting time have an influence on FHP measurement. For example, the work of O'Neill and Jackson (2009) indicated that the FHP of White Leghorn hens and roosters in the period from 1 to 2¼ years were respectively ranged from 404 to 464 kJ/kgW0.75/d and from 223 to 349 kJ/kgW0.75/d. Recently, it is reported that FHP of 54-week-old Hy-Line Brown roosters is 354 kJ/kgW0.75·d-1, which was measured in a short-time of fasting between 11.7 to 24 h of feed deprivation (Riveros et al., 2023). The FHP value of layer-type chicks measured in the present study is higher than the results in adult chicken. Similarly, it is estimated that FHP in growing turkeys at 3 wk of age was 540 kJ/kg BW0.75/d (MacLeod et al., 1979), ranged from 544 to 602 kJ/kg BW0.75/d for male growing turkeys aged from 1 to 16 weeks of age (Rivera-Torres et al., 2010), and 372 kJ/kgBW0.75/d in 45-wk-old male turkeys (MacLeod et al., 1985). Hence, the result suggests that FHP is changed with age and seems to decline as chicken mature. It is speculated that FHP, measured during fasting, includes the energy expenditure for physical activity, which is higher in growing animals (Rivera-Torres et al., 2010).

The data of Net energy for maintenance (NEm) for layer-type chicks at different ambient temperatures are limited. In this study, the NEm at environmental temperatures of 30, 32, 34, 36, and 38 °C, the NEm values calculated from the measured FHP were 519.7, 710.8, 493.5, 361.7, and 437.9 kJ/kgW0.75·d-1, respectively, indicating that NEm is increased by lower ambient temperature while increased by high temperature. The result is accordance with previous study by Sakomura et al. (2003), who reported that the NEm of broiler breeder pullets at temperatures of 15, 22, and 30 °C, measured with regression and comparative slaughter methods, were 497.5, 457.3, and 387.0 kJ/kgW0.75/d, respectively. Moreover, the efficiencies of metabolizable energy utilization for maintenance was not changed by 15 and 22 °C whereas was reduced at 30 °C, which was postulated to be caused by energy used to dissipate additional body heat in 30 °C environment (Sakomura et al., 2003). Similarly, the zero-activity heat production in growing broilers maintained at 14, 17, 22, 27 and 32 °C increased with decreasing ambient temperature (Koh and MacLeod, 1999).

In this study, NEm was increased by lower temperature in a nonlinear way. The cubic polynomial model had the high R2 value and the lowest AIC and BIC values. Within the temperature range of 30 to 38 °C, the NEm (KJ/d) could be estimated with: NEm = (505.93 - 162.78T - 38.87T2 + 194.41T3)·kgW0.75. This result was in line with the previous work in broilers by Sakomura et al. (2005b), who reported that environmental temperature (T, °C) had a quadratic effect on maintenance requirements (MEm = 307.87 - 15.63T + 0.3105T2; r2= 0.93). In laying hens, however, the ME requirement for maintenance (MEm) declines linearly with decreased ambient temperature. Sakomura et al. (2005a) determined the influence of ambient temperature on ME requirements for the maintenance (MEm) of laying hens at 12, 22 and 31 °C by comparative slaughtering technique. The MEm decreases as the temperature increases and could be estimated with kgW0.75 (165.74–2.37T) or kgW0.75 (163.67-2.09T). In a range of lower temperature (11, 16, and 21 °C), the MEm was estimated to be 612 kgBW0.75 - (8.54 × T) (van Krimpen et al., 2014). The MEm of broiler increased from 433 kJ/kg W0.75/d at 35 °C to 693 kJ/kg W0.75/d at 15 °C (Hoffmann et al., 1991).

It is well known that high temperature reduces the growth and laying performance of chickens. The BW gain and feed intake of chicks were decreased when ambient temperature increased, in line with previous studies. Henken et al. (1983) reported that high temperatures reduced the growth rate of 3 to 6-weeks-old pullets. In broilers, it is summarized that heat stress significantly decreased feed intake and BW gain (Liu et al., 2020). Similarly, high temperature reduces laying performance of laying hens (Barrett et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2020). Except of BW gain, the content of gain is changed by ambient temperature. The decreased thigh muscle and liver indices with increased temperature indicates the changed organ development by ambient temperature. This result was in line with previous result by Liu et al. (2023), who reported that breast muscle was decreased while abdominal fat was increased in broiler reared at high temperature. The abdominal fat content increased with rearing temperature from 10.0, 15.6, 21.1, and 26.7 °C (McNaughton and Reece, 1982). In this study, however, the abdominal fat contents in 14 days-old layer-type chicks were not detectable. The different result is related to the breed and age of chickens. The relative slow growth rate and less capacity of lipid deposition in layer-type chick, compared to broiler, should be responsible for the different observation. Indeed, this speculation was supported by the observation by Lu et al. (2007), who reported the different responses of BW gain and abdominal fat deposition to high temperature in broiler chickens and Beijing You chicken.

Although, the breast muscle and liver organ indices of chicks were decreased by high temperature, the energy retention was not changed by temperature and the average NEg for BW gain was 7.16 kJ/g. Geraert et al. (1996) reported that, compared with control broilers reared at 21 °C, high temperature (32 °C) decreased body protein content while increased lipid content. In broilers, the total energy retention and energy retention as fat decreased with decreasing temperature, while energy retention as protein was not altered in growing broilers maintained at 5 ambient temperatures (14, 17, 22, 27 and 32 °C) (Koh and MacLeod, 1999). In adult hens, the ME requirement for weight gain (MEg) is not affected by temperature ranged from 12 to 31 °C (Sakomura et al., 2005a). In broiler breeder pullet, MEg was not changed by ambient temperature ranged from 15 to 30 °C (Sakomura et al., 2003). The energy requirement for BW gain of broiler breeders (aged from 25 to 41 wk) was not influenced by environmental temperature (Pishnamazi et al., 2015). The NE for BWG tended to be higher in hens (H&N Brown Nick layer hens aged from 35- to 50-wk-old breeder flocks) housed at 11 and 16 °C (on average 66.3 kJ·kg0.75/d) compared to hens housed at 21 °C (15.4 kJ·kgW0.75/d) (van Krimpen et al., 2014). The unchanged energy retention of BW gain in adult hens may be related to content of BW gain is constant. In this study, the experimental period last 14 days, and the content of BW gain is not significantly changed by ambient temperature within the short period. This speculation, however, needs to be demonstrated further.

Conclusion

The result showed that rearing temperature significantly affected the NE requirement for maintenance of layer-type chicks on 1–14 days of age. Ambient temperature, however, has no significant effect on the NE requirement for BW gain.

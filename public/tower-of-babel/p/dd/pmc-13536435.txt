Factors influencing dental career decision-making among high-achieving students in Taiwan
Authors: Chien-Hong Yu, Mei-Huei Li, Feng-Chou Cheng, Chun-Pin Chiang
Published: 2026

In recent years, dentistry has ranked among the top majors in university admission rankings in Taiwan, attracting many high-achieving students who compete for these limited positions. Due to the small number of available positions, the admission scores for dentistry programs at some universities have even surpassed those of medical schools. As dentistry is a highly competitive and popular field with rigorous academic requirements, it has become a preferred choice for many high-achieving students. According to domestic longitudinal studies on the gifted students, it has been found that the students in mathematics and science gifted programs tend to pursue university majors aligned with their areas of strength, with engineering, medical, and health-related fields accounting for the highest proportions.

A considerable body of research, both domestically and internationally, has examined students’ decisions to pursue dentistry. Studies across different countries have identified job flexibility, professional prestige, and altruistic motives as key influencing factors.1,2 Other researchers have explored changes in career decision-making across different periods and found significant differences in freshman students’ motivations and professional perspectives over time. In particular, personal views and concepts about the profession have been identified as important factors influencing students’ decisions to choose dentistry as a career.3 Furthermore, studies examining sociodemographic factors have revealed that both dental school and ethnicity significantly affect four motivational domains (economic, professional, vocational, and personal background), with “helping others” being the most prominent motivating factor.4 In Taiwan, research on career transmission has also indicated that having a father who works as a dentist, the altruistic image of helping others, and the inheritance of a dental practice are important factors influencing students’ decisions to pursue medicine or dentistry.5

Dentistry indeed offers many attractive features that motivate high-achieving students to compete for these limited positions. Although high-achieving students appear to have more opportunities and choices, their exceptional potential and diverse interests often create difficulties in making career decisions, which may lead to challenges in their career development. Career decision-making is not unidimensional; rather, it is influenced by multiple factors, including personal, familial, educational, and societal contexts. High-achieving students with multiple strengths often face more complex considerations in career choices due to their demonstrated abilities across different domains.

Therefore, this study aimed to explore why high-achieving students with multiple strengths, interests, and outstanding performance across different domains ultimately chose dentistry as their career path. In addition, this study investigated how personal, family, school, and sociocultural factors influenced their career decision-making.

This study adopted a purposeful sampling method to select two dental students, who were in their second year of university and internship stage, respectively, at the time of the interviews. Both participants had been identified as gifted in early childhood and had received gifted education services, subsequently demonstrating diverse talents and outstanding performance at different educational stages. In Taiwan, admission to dental schools is a highly competitive process. Moreover, the societal preference for medical professions has deep historical and cultural roots, leading many high-achieving students to consider medicine or dentistry as their primary career choices. The findings of this study indicate that high-achieving students with multiple strengths retain a high degree of autonomy in their career decision-making. Although they possess diverse abilities, they still tend to prioritize fields with stronger career prospects, such as medicine and dentistry, when making the career choices.5

From the perspective of schooling, the primary role of schools is to support academic achievement, encouraging students to attain high scores and gain admission to prestigious programs. However, relatively limited attention is given to comprehensive career guidance. In addition, prevailing societal values exert a subtle yet powerful influence on students’ decisions. Students can clearly perceive the expectation that high-achieving individuals should pursue careers in medicine or dentistry.

Based on the results of this study, several recommendations are proposed. First, parents should support the development of their children’s diverse talents and provide guidance without imposing decisions. Second, schools should strengthen career counseling services, rather than focusing solely on academic performance, to help the students to avoid making choices they may regret later. Finally, at the sociocultural level, greater emphasis should be placed on promoting diverse values, enabling the students to consider a wider range of options and make career decisions that better align with their individual characteristics and aspirations.

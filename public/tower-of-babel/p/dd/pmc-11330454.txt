Shared genes of polycystic ovary syndrome and sedentary behavior as a novel immune landscape biomarker for endometrial cancer
Authors: Yao Ge, Yuan Chen, Yun Zhang, Yifang Hu, Feng Jiang, Xiao Lu, Chuyan Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEndometrial cancer (EC) is associated with significant risk factors such as polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) and sedentary behavior. In our study, we aim to employ machine learning algorithms to investigate the potential molecular processes that underlie their interaction and explore their respective roles in the diagnosis and immunotherapy of EC. The GEO database provides access to microarray data, which was utilized in this study to identify gene expression modules associated with PCOS and sedentary behavior, using weighted gene expression network analysis (WGCNA). Cluego software was then employed to investigate the energy enrichment of shared pathways in both PCOS and sedentary individuals, and differential gene analysis was used to confirm another two databases. The miRNAs–mRNAs controlled network was constructed to verify the pathway. The immune-related factors of the shared pathway in EC were then analyzed. Finally, to validate our findings, we conducted cell experiments using EC cell lines (AN3CA, KLE, Ishikawa, RL95-2, and HEC-1A). We found that increased intracellular aromatic compound anabolism is a common feature of both PCOS and sedentary individuals. We then developed a disease pathway model that was based on the common genetic characteristics of PCOS and sedentary behavior. We utilized pathway typing in EC samples and found a significant survival difference between the two subgroups, with the upregulated expression type exhibiting an immune-hot phenotype. Finally, the experimental results confirmed the expression of the hub gene (NAA15) in EC. The findings of our study suggest that genes related to the intracellular aromatic compound metabolic pathway can be used for immunotherapy of EC.Keywords: Polycystic ovary syndrome, Sedentary, Aromatase, MiRNAs–mRNAs, Endometrial cancerSubject terms: Biomarkers, Diseases, Endocrinology, Health care, Oncology, Pathogenesis, Risk factors

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is the most frequent endocrinopathy in women, with a worldwide incidence of 13%1. Polycystic ovary syndrome has a significant impact on the physical and emotional well-being of pregnant women. Overweight or obese accounted for 40–60% of the total PCOS population, and women with polycystic ovary syndrome are prone to weight gain. This is especially concerning since obesity exacerbates polycystic ovary syndrome’s reproductive, metabolic, and clinical manifestations. Hyperandrogenemia and hyperinsulinemia are two symptoms of PCOS. PCOS among pregnant women severely affects their physical and mental health2. Even though insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia are not diagnostic criteria for PCOS, insulin resistance affects roughly 75% of PCOS women1,3. Persistent hormonal imbalance can lead to many cysts, irregular menstrual cycles, and, ultimately, infertility in women4,5. Many candidate genes have been identified as one of the causes of PCOS6. Women with PCOS have a 2.7-fold increased risk of developing endometrial cancer (EC)7,8.

As a syndrome with high clinical heterogeneity, the etiology of PCOS is still unknown. Genetic and environmental factors are the causes of the disease9. Polycystic ovary syndrome may be exacerbated by an unhealthy lifestyle, food, or infection. Sedentary behavior is the most common unhealthy lifestyle. Passive activity is often exhibited as screen-based media usage behavior, such as watching Television, using desktops/smartphones, and playing computer games, and is linked to various unfavorable health outcomes10. Sedentary behavior is associated with a higher risk of obesity, all-cause fatality, and various mental health issues11–13. For all patients with polycystic ovary syndrome, lifestyle management (which includes food and exercise) is the first-line therapy9. Physical activity (PA) is an essential part of lifestyle treatment for people with polycystic ovarian syndrome who want to enhance their reproductive, metabolic, and mental health14,15. Interestingly, diet and exercise can reduce the risk of endometrial cancer and prevent its recurrence16.

We used weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) to locate gene clusters of linked and connected genes in PCOS and sedentary17. This strategy has effectively uncovered common risk genes and pathways linked to a range of disease phenotypes in several biological contexts18,19. We discovered coexpression modules in PCOS and sedentary people using gene expression data from the complete gene expression database (GEO) (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Then we explored the role of shared genes and pathways in endometrial cancer (Fig. 1 Flow chart).

Methods

Download and processing of GEO datasets

We searched the GEO database for PCOS and Sedentary lifestyles gene expression profiles using the keywords "Polycystic ovary syndrome" or "Sedentary". The following criteria filter the dataset: First, gene expression profiling must involve cases and controls. Second, a skeletal muscle should be an arrangement for sequencing. Third, for the WGCNA to be accurate, the sample size in each group must not be fewer than 10. Finally, these datasets must include processed or raw data that may be reanalyzed.

Consequently, GSE6798, GSE8157, GSE9103, and GSE9405 were chosen as the GEO datasets. The authors contributed a series of matrix files that included normalized data processed by the mas5 algorithm. The transcriptomic profile is then converted to Iog2 format, and the probe is matched to its gene symbol using the platform’s annotation document. Lastly, the gene matrix is created, with the row name serving as the sample name and the column name acting as the gene symbol.

Then, raw RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data, including 554 ECs and 23 endometrial standard tissue samples, were downloaded in the TCGA database’s high-throughput sequencing (HTseq)—per kilobase transcript per million mapped reads (fpkm) workflow (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/repository?facetTab=cases).

Analysis of gene co-expression networks using machine learning

WGCNA is an algorithm that can identify coexpressed gene modules with high biological importance and investigate the link between gene networks and diseases20. To collect PCOS and sedentary-related modules, we employed WGCNA. The geo dataset yielded about 20,000 genes via sequencing. We removed the genes with modest variations since most of these genes were not differentially expressed across samples. WGCNA analysis was performed using the "WGCNA" program in R.4.0.4 software. Then, according to the scale-free network standard, use the "picksoft threshold" function in the "WGCNA" package to select the appropriate power value (in the range of 1–20). We choose the best power value and build the proximity matrix. Then according to the degree of connectivity, the gene distribution is matched to the scale-free network. The topological matrix (TOM matrix) is calculated. We use the TOM matrix obtained from gene expression to re-cluster the genes. Then, we need to calculate the correlation coefficient (cor) and corresponding p-value (P-value) between different gene modules and clinical traits.

Identification of standard and unique genetic characteristics of polycystic ovary syndrome and sedentary

We chose modules closely connected to polycystic ovarian syndrome and sedentary behavior. Through Venn (http://hiplot.com.cn), shared genes in modules are significantly related to PCOS and Sedentary overlap. Cluego is a plug-in for Cytoscape. We utilized Cluego for the biological investigation of these shared genes to investigate their possible significance in PCOS and sedentary behavior. To discover the unique gene features in PCOS and sedentary, the "MCODE" method in Cytoscape software (version: 3.8.2) was utilized for protein–protein interaction (PPI) network and cluster analysis. Gene clusters with scores more excellent than three were targeted.

Validation of shared and unique gene features in other data sets

To validate common and unique genes in PCOS and Sedentary, we analyzed Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) on additional PCOS and Sedentary data sets (GSE8157 and GSE9405). To find the DEGs between the case and control groups, researchers employed the R software package "limma". Log (foldchange) = one was used as the cutoff value, with a P value of less than 0.05. Hierarchical clustering heat maps were performed to show these DEGs’ expression patterns. We used Venn to find overlapping DEGs in both the PCOS and Sedentary datasets. We used the same method for functional enrichment analysis of common DEGs in PCOS and sedentary. We compared the enrichment analysis findings in the discovery and validation queues to see whether our approach is trustworthy.

Identification of common microRNAs in PCOS and sedentary

We also examined whether specific miRNAs in Sedentary and PCOS people influence these risk genes. We selected miRNAs associated with PCOS diseases in the human microRNA disease database (HMDD) 21. Then, we chose the miRNAs related to sedentary according to the published literature and further analyzed the intersecting miRNAs22–24. To investigate the role of miRNA, we employed the Mirpath V3 software in the Diana tool to do a GO BP analysis. P values of less than 0.05 were deemed significant for Go items.

The creation of a network of miRNA target genes

Starbase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) searches for microRNA targets through high-throughput CLIP-seq experimental data and degradation group experimental data. It provides a variety of visual interfaces for exploring microRNA targets. The database contains rich data on miRNA–ncRNA, miRNA–mRNA, RBP–RNA, and RNA–RNA. The miRNAs–mRNAs controlled network was constructed using the overlap of shared miRNAs target genes and common genes in PCOS and Sedentary.

Clustering of pathway genes without supervision

We found 27 genes related to the cellular aromatic compound metallic process pathway on the GSEA website (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp) (Supplement Table 1). Using an unsupervised clustering technique, the expression of 27-way associated genes was utilized to identify EC patients from TCGA data sets for further analysis. The consensus clustering approach examined the grouping results and their stability. The previous phases were completed using the Consensus Cluster Plus program, with 1000 repetitions to verify classification stability. Then, we analyze the two clustering layers using the "survival" and "survminer" R packages. The data from these two sets were blended and standardized. Unsupervised clustering of the gene expression of pathway-related genes in the sample revealed two route-related patterns.

Characterization of immune landscape between two EC subgroups

In CIBERSORT (https://cibersortx.stanford.edu/), the expression data from the EC sample were imported and repeated 1000 times. The proportion of each of the 22 immune cell types. The findings are shown on a landscape map after we assessed the relative percentage of 22 different resistant cell types between the two categories.

Immunohistochemical staining for NAA15 protein expression

The database (The Human Protein Atlas, http://www.proteinatlas.org/) contains protein distribution in different human tissues and organs and tissue and cell expression levels of over 20,000 human proteins. The website examined the expression of the protein NAA15 in healthy and malignant cancer tissues.

Cell culture and transfection

Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 5% carbon dioxide (CO2) was used to culture EC cells from different strains (AN3CA, KLE, Ishikawa, RL95-2, and HEC-1A) in conjunction with hESC as control cells. Cells in the logarithmic phase have been used in various functional studies. To knock down or overexpress NAA15 in EC cells, Lipofectamine® 3000 was used to transfect plasmid siNAA15 or NAA15-cDNA into the cells. 5′-GGGACCUUUCCUUACUACAdTdT-3′ was the target sequence for siNAA15.

qRT-PCR

The total RNA of cells was extracted using Trizol reagents (Invitrogen, America), and then cDNAs were synthesized utilizing the HiScript Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, China). Following this, a PCR mixture consisting of cDNA, ddH2O, primers, and the SYBR Green Master Mix was used for amplification. Amplification of the qRT-PCR product was measured through the StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, US). We used the following primers: NAA15, forward-5′-TGAGGGTTACAGGGAAACGAG-3′, reverse-5′-TGTCAGGGGATGTCTGTTGTG-3′.

CCK-8 and Clone Assay

A CCK-8 assay and plate colony assay were conducted to test the proliferation capacity of EC cells. We used the CCK8 kit (Beyotime, China) to quantify cell growth after 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h, according to the manufacturer’s manual. Measurements were performed at 450 nm with enzyme labeling (Thermo, USA). In each well of a six-well plate, approximately 500 cells were placed from different groups. We stained and fixed the cells using 4% paraformaldehyde and crystal violet after colonies appeared.

Transwell Invasion Assay

After transfection with siNAA15 or NAA15-cDNA, 1 × 105 EC cells were seeded in the upper chambers of Transwell pre-coated with Matrigel (Biosciences, USA) and cultured in an empty DMEM medium. The lower chamber was filled with DMEM containing 10% FBS. After 24 h of incubation, the cells that had penetrated were fixed with 4% methanol and subsequently stained with 0.1% crystal violet. The visualization and counting were carried out under an inverted microscope (Nikon, Japan).

Statistical analysis

The normalization and differential investigation were carried out using the "Limma" program. The Kruskal–Wallis test was appropriate for comparisons between two or more categories. In contrast, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was a non-parametric statistical hypothesis test mainly used to compare two groups. The R program was used to implement all statistical analyses (Version 4.0.4). P values below 0.05 were considered significant.

Ethical approval

The Nanjing Medical University Animal Care and Use Committee gave its approval for the use of animals in studies (Approval No. IACUC-2205067).

NAA15 promoted EC cell proliferation and migration

We looked into whether variations in its expression had an impact on EC cell proliferation and malignancy to further support the function of NAA15 in EC. Initially, we measured NAA15 expression levels in various EC cell lines and found that HEC-1A, RL95-2, Ishikawa, KLE, and AN3CA cells had significantly higher levels of NAA15 mRNA than hESCs (Fig. 11A). We chose Ishikawa cells for si-NAA15 transfection and HEC-1A cells for NAA15-cDNA transfection. As a result, si-NAA15 reduced NAA15 expression in Ishikawa cells, while NAA15-cDNA increased NAA15 expression in HEC-1A cells (Fig. 11B). Colony formation assays and CCK-8 demonstrated that upregulating NAA15 expression promoted HEC-1A cell proliferation while downregulating it inhibited Ishikawa cell proliferation (Fig. 11C–E). Transwell assays showed that suppressing NAA15 expression decreased the invasion of Ishikawa cells, whereas upregulating it significantly increased the invasion of HEC-1A cells (Fig. 11F). These results suggest that knocking down NAA15 inhibits EC cells’ growth and migration.

Discussion

At present, the pathogenesis of PCOS is still unclear. Sedentary is the possible cause of many metabolic diseases, including PCOS25,26. However, the relationship between the two remains at the level of diagnosis and treatment, and few studies seem to explore the relationship between a sedentary lifestyle and PCOS at the genetic level. To look at a potential common mechanism between sedentary behavior and PCOS, we used WGCNA for the first time. This technology can find coexpression modules with significant biological value by screening genes related to clinical characteristics. At the same time, because sedentary and PCOS are both susceptibility factors of endometrial cancer, we can judge whether the coexpression module plays a role in endometrial cancer. Although we selected skeletal muscle transcriptomes for the sedentary and PCOS datasets and endometrial cancer transcriptomes for endometrial cancer, studying changes in the skeletal muscle transcriptome can provide insights into systemic metabolic status, thereby explaining the metabolic pathways related to endometrial cancer.

Cellular aromatic compound metabolic process and PCOS

Aromatic compounds, including humic acid, lignin, amino acids, and industrial pollutants, comprise a significant portion of the global carbon pool. A one-step chemical reaction of androgen produces part of estrogen in women. The molecules of androgen and estrogen look very similar. The enzyme that catalyzes this chemical reaction is called "aromatase". Aromatase comprises two enzymes: NADPH + cytochrome P450 reductase and cytochrome P450 aromatase27. Aromatase catalyzes testosterone to produce estradiol, which opens the leftmost double bond of testosterone and changes its structure into a benzene ring with a hydroxyl group.

Studies have confirmed that aromatase gene variation causes aromatase function loss, which can lead to PCOS28–30. Aromatase inhibitors can be used to induce PCOS. The levels of vimentin and cytokeratin in the granular cell layer of polycystic ovary induced by aromatase inhibitors increased significantly, cadherin’s immunoreactivity was stronger, and LH levels, FSH, and testosterone increased. The levels of estrogen and progesterone decreased significantly. In individuals who have not responded to clomiphene citrate, the aromatase inhibitor letrozole may be administered as an alternative to induce ovulation28,31.

Many kinds of literature have reported that aromatic amino acids phenylalanine and tyrosine are related to insulin resistance, metabolic diseases, and polycystic ovary syndrome32–35.

Cellular aromatic compound metabolic process and sedentary

Sedentary behavior, which is unrelated to physical exercise, is a cause of cancer and other adverse health effects. In a meta-analysis of studies, a higher level of sedentary activity was associated with a 17 percent higher risk of breast cancer. Sedentary behavior was linked to 22% of all-cause mortality, 15% of cardiovascular mortality, 14% of cancer mortality, and 91% of type 2 diabetes incidence rate in another meta-analysis 12,26.

Aromatase expression has been found in skeletal muscle, indicating that skeletal muscle can also produce estrogen. Larionova et al. found no significant difference between aromatase activity and fat per gram of fresh muscle tissue36. The experiment showed that muscle was an essential source of estrogen for men and postmenopausal women. It also played an important role in circulating estrogen levels compared with fatty tissue. Breast inflammation, greater expression of breast aromatase, and circulating inflammatory metabolic markers have all been linked to a higher risk of breast cancer in women who have high body fat and poor activity levels, according to research37. These data suggest that measures to decrease obesity, even in people of average weight, may prevent tumor formation and that sedentary behavior is linked to the production of aromatase in the body.

Disease road model—metabolic process of aromatic compounds acts as a bridge between sedentary and PCOS

Numerous research has shown that PCOS may be directly associated with granulosa cell dysfunction, particularly the functional defect of aromatase4,29. The typical characteristics of PCOS are androgen excess and ovulation disorder, and aromatase is the rate-limiting enzyme for androgen to aromatic estrogen in ovarian granulosa cells. Many population studies and animal studies have confirmed that the expression of aromatase is reduced in PCOS diseases. This may directly affect the metabolic process of aromatic compounds in PCOS diseases.

For all individuals with polycystic ovary syndrome, lifestyle management is the primary line of therapy. Physical activity (PA) is an essential part of lifestyle management that may help people with polycystic ovary syndrome improve their reproductive, metabolic, and mental health10,11,38. In the general population, sedentary behavior, as defined by sitting time, increases the risk of major chronic illnesses and all-cause mortality, mainly when PA levels are low39.

Emerging therapeutic target—has-miR-193 and pathway stratification

MicroRNA (miRNA) is a short non-coding RNA molecule that controls gene expression in response to physiological and pathological stress. Therefore, they can be biomarkers to improve metabolic disease diagnosis and prognosis 40,41.

Regular exercise helps to regulate and improve the endocrine environment of the body. A series of circulating factors are collectively called "exercise factors," secreted by muscles during exercise38. They are candidate factors to regulate the functions of other cell types to improve the body’s metabolic status. These changes may also contribute to systemic signal transduction related to motor adaptation.

In the related studies of PCOS, it has been found that there is an abnormal expression of miRNA in the circulation, including miRNA-19340. The pathophysiology mechanisms of PCOS may be influenced by aberrant miRNA expression. At present, aromatase inhibitors have been widely used in treating PCOS, but their effects are not consistent in clinical observation. Therefore, finding patients with better responses to aromatase inhibitors has always been a clinical problem to be solved. Based on the interaction between a sedentary lifestyle and polycystic ovary syndrome, the shared genes, and pathways related to a sedentary lifestyle and miRNAs related to shared genes were found in this study. Shared genes and miRNAs may become targets for the future prevention and detection of PCOS.

Role in EC—the effect of pathway on EC immunity and prognosis

Polycystic ovary syndrome itself is closely related to the occurrence of endometrial cancer (EC), and sedentary will increase the risk. The event and development of three common cancers in women are closely related to estrogen. Aromatase is the key enzyme of estrogen biosynthesis, and endometrial cancer is one of them. Previous studies have shown that aromatase inhibitors can successfully treat breast cancer, but their role in endometrium is still unclear42.

We grouped EC samples by PCOS and sedentary-related pathways and found differences in immunophenotypes between the high and low groups. The more activated Treg cells in the high subtype group suggest that the effects of PCOS and sedentary on EC are more related to autoimmune response. At present, the treatment of EC has entered the era of immunotherapy. Numerous papers concentrate on the relationship between immunotherapy, immunological checkpoints, and EC. Our research demonstrates that immunological checkpoints are strongly associated with both high and low levels of pathway correlation.

Conclusion

The high aromatase synthesis reaction associated with a sedentary lifestyle may be a significant susceptibility factor for PCOS, according to our study, which proposed a barrier pathway approach to explain the potential explanation of PCOS caused by sedentary. In parallel, we investigated the immunological properties of related genes and pathways in EC and discovered novel gene candidates that may be used as diagnostic indicators or potential therapeutic targets.

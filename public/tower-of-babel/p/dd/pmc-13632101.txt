Finger crush injury caused by an injury mechanism: a case report
Authors: Haiming Chen, Jianwen Chen, Yong Wang, Kaimin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFinger injuries caused by H-shaped jeans buttons are infrequent and involve a unique injury mechanism; few cases have been reported. We present a case to discuss this unique injury mechanism’s characteristics and treatment strategy.Case presentationA 54-year-old Chinese woman presented to the emergency department with right index finger pain accompanied by numbness, paleness, and dysmotility after being accidentally stuck by a jeans button when working. We initially analyzed this unique injury mechanism and formulated a corresponding treatment strategy. After timely and effective treatment, she achieved a good prognosis.ConclusionThe mechanism of finger injury resulting from an H-shaped jeans button is distinctive, and is characterized by penetrating injury coupled with continuous compression of the affected finger. It is crucial to understand this injury mechanism fully, perform a comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s condition, promptly release the compression to restore blood circulation, and meticulously explore and repair any damaged structures during surgery. Additionally, close postoperative observation is essential to achieve a favorable prognosis.Keywords: Crush injury, Injury mechanism, Case report

Background

The hand is the most significant organ for interacting with the external world and serves as the body’s most agile and essential tool for labor [1]. In developed garment industry regions, finger injuries caused by H-shaped jeans buttons occur frequently, and the injury mechanism is unique. Few studies have been conducted regarding such unique injuries domestically and internationally. For this unique injury, timely removal of the button and compression release to restore blood circulation are highly important. Good functional outcomes can be achieved through standardized treatment. We present a patient who experienced penetrating crush injury and an open metacarpal bone fracture of the right index finger caused by an H-shaped jeans button and summarize the characteristics and treatment points of such injuries to provide valuable clinical insights.

Case report

A 54-year-old Chinese woman, who is a garment factory worker, presented to the emergency department with a jeans button stuck to her right index finger. An hour ago, she accidentally placed her right index finger under the servo button‑attaching machine that rapidly stamps buttons onto garments (Fig. 1) and now complains of severe pain and numbness in that finger. She was admitted for surgical treatment after a preliminary assessment. On presentation, the right index finger had been impaled by the jeans button with a through-and-through injury from the palmar radial aspect to the dorsum, with the button visible just proximal to the paronychium. The finger was pale, was numb, and had decreased motion at the distal interphalangeal joint (Fig. 2). An X-ray revealed an open fracture of the distal phalanx of the right index finger along with a jeans button (Fig. 3). Other preoperative routine examinations were standard.

The patient was emergently transported to the operating room. Initially, the button was carefully removed via a bone clamp and a needle holder under brachial plexus block anesthesia. The wound was then appropriately enlarged as necessary. The continuity of the main tendons, nerves, and blood vessels was confirmed, followed by thorough washout of the penetrating wound via a blunt-tipped syringe needle. The fracture was reduced and fixed longitudinally with a Kirschner wire (Fig. 4). Finally, the wound was sutured loosely, with a drainage strip placed within the wound. Postoperatively, cefuroxime was administered, and the drainage strip was removed within 24 h. The index finger’s swelling, sensation, and blood flow were closely monitored. Six weeks after the surgery, an X‑ray confirmed the fracture had healed (Fig. 5), and the Kirschner wire was then removed. The patient recovered well and returned to regular work after 3 months.

Discussion

This high-energy injury arises from a distinctive complex mechanism involving simultaneous penetration and sustained crushing. Comprehending the injury characteristics caused by jeans buttons is critical for three reasons. First, the injury mechanism is unique: the jeans button’s specialized interlocking structure enables deep tissue penetration and finger entrapment, making extraction challenging and resulting in sustained compression. This prolonged pressure can critically compromise digital blood circulation, potentially progressing to ischemic necrosis without timely intervention. Second, the penetrating injury typically demonstrates significant depth with a concealed narrow tract, complicating surgical exposure and thorough debridement, thereby elevating risks of postoperative infection [2]. Third, these injuries predominantly involve the index and middle fingers of the dominant hand, presenting with localized yet severe tissue damage. While wound contamination is generally mild, prompt surgical management remains essential to ensure a favorable prognosis.

The penetrating lesion usually results from penetration by a foreign object, which traverses the body cavity or extremity in a through-and-through fashion [3]. It is generally encountered in cases of gunshot and stab injuries [4]. James et al. achieved favorable outcomes in managing a pediatric forearm impalement Injury. Their clinical approach demonstrated four critical components: thorough wound exploration, effective debridement, copious irrigation with normal saline, and good antibiotic coverage-essential measures for addressing these low-incidence yet high-risk injuries. Meanwhile, they also emphasized the importance of preoperative radiological examination for stable patients [5]. Ohn et al. reported successful management of a geriatric finger impalement injury caused by a door handle, demonstrating favorable outcomes. Their findings underscore the clinical necessity for emergency clinicians to implement prioritized extraction protocols for retained foreign bodies, effectively mitigating infectious complications [6]. The primary management strategy for this type of injury includes thorough debridement and antibiotic cover [4, 5]. In this case, we performed an X-ray to confirm a fracture of the distal phalanx of the index finger and promptly removed the impaled object. We expanded the wound appropriately to achieve better exposure and then utilized a syringe needle to flush the sinus tract meticulously, a practice supported by Bajaj in his report [7]. Postoperatively, cefuroxime was administered to prevent infection. The patient experienced an uneventful recovery without infection.

Crush injuries represent frequently encountered trauma in routine clinical practice, closely linked to compartment syndrome development [8]. Therefore, any instance of crush injury should raise a high index of suspicion among clinicians for prompt recognition and timely management [9]. Of particular clinical significance is finger compartment syndrome—a relatively new concept that remains underrepresented in current literature. This knowledge gap increases susceptibility to diagnostic oversight, potentially leading to severe consequences. The cases reported by Schwartz-Fernandes et al. [10] and Azenkot et al. [11] demonstrated favorable outcomes following timely intervention, underscoring the importance of remaining vigilant regarding finger crush injuries in clinical practice. Serial monitoring of digital edema, sensory integrity, and perfusion status constitutes essential care in such injuries. Upon admission of this crush injury patient, we performed emergent surgery to restore digital perfusion. The finger’s condition was closely monitored postoperatively, and no signs of compartment syndrome were detected.

To our knowledge, a penetrating and continuously crushing injury by H-shaped jeans buttons has not previously been described in the literature. The management of such injuries carries significant clinical implications. Therapeutic management of such injuries necessitates an integrated approach that concurrently addresses both penetrating trauma mechanisms and crush injury pathophysiology, with systematic application of their respective management protocols. Through case-based documentation, we aim to improve clinicians’ recognition of these atypical trauma patterns, which are directly associated with favorable prognosis.

Conclusion

It is essential to understand the injury characteristics caused by H-shaped jeans buttons fully. Critical interventions include performing comprehensive clinical assessments, promptly removing compressive agents to achieve blood flow restoration, meticulously repairing damaged anatomical structures through surgical exploration, and implementing rigorous postoperative monitoring to ensure optimal recovery outcomes.

Betulinic acid alleviates lipopolysaccharide-induced spleen injury by inhibiting the IL-17 signaling pathway in broilers
Authors: Huan Yao, Yu Yang, Ziming Wang, Jiao Wu, Zhaoping Ou, Li Kong, You Huang, Hongyi Ding, Wenyu Ba, Zhuliang Tan, Quanwei Li, Jine Yi, Shuiping Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•BA mitigated LPS-induced splenic inflammatory responses and oxidative stress in broilers.•BA enhanced splenic immune function in broilers.•BA alleviated LPS-induced spleen injury by suppressing the IL-17 signaling pathway in broilers.

Introduction

As the most important peripheral immune organ of birds, the spleen is crucial for triggering immune responses in the body (Lewis et al., 2019). In intensive poultry production, broilers are susceptible to various chronic bacterial challenges, which can impair the function of immune organs and thereby pose a serious threat to broiler health. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is recognized for its ability to induce systemic inflammatory responses and disrupt the host immune system (Lv et al., 2024). Studies have indicated that LPS disrupts the structure and function of the spleen in piglets, promotes the production and emission of inflammatory cytokines, triggers inflammatory responses, and ultimately leads to spleen injury (Ding et al., 2021). Additionally, LPS has been found to disrupt the redox homeostasis in the spleen of goose, thereby interfering with normal immune functions (Ma et al., 2026). Similarly, research in broilers has demonstrated that LPS challenge abnormally elevated serum immunoglobulin concentrations, up-regulated the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines in the spleen, and consequently induced immune stress (Xia et al., 2025). Excessive immune stress in poultry can reduce feed intake, impair growth performance, and damage immune organs. These adverse effects not only compromise bird health but also cause substantial economic losses in the poultry industry. Therefore, developing effective strategies to alleviate spleen injury and enhance immune function is crucial for improving productivity and disease resistance in poultry farming.

In poultry farming, the overuse of antibiotics has led to the emergence of antimicrobial resistance and drug residues in poultry-derived products, posing significant risks to both animal and human health (Abou-Jaoudeh et al., 2024). In response to this issue, plant-derived bioactive compounds are increasingly gaining traction for use in enhancing poultry immune competence and reducing the incidence of infectious diseases. These attributes underscore their potential as effective alternatives to antibiotics in poultry farming (Gadde et al., 2017). Betulinic acid (BA), a pentacyclic triterpenoid compound, is extracted from natural plants such as birch, eucalyptus and plane trees. BA possesses a variety of biological and pharmacological activities, including immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antiviral effects (Lou et al., 2025; Ríos and Máñez, 2018). Research has shown that BA ameliorated T-2 toxin-induced damage to the intestinal immune barrier by downregulating inflammatory mediators and elevating serum immunoglobulin levels in mice (Lao et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2020). Additionally, BA has been shown to mitigate T-2 toxin-induced oxidative damage in the spleen and thymus. This protective mechanism involves the inhibition of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway and the activation of the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)-mediated antioxidant signaling pathway (Kong et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2020). Notably, BA significantly alleviated immune hyperactivation-induced psoriatic skin inflammation by reducing interleukin-17A (IL-17A) production in both CD4+ and γδ T cells, while suppressing the expression of pro-inflammatory mediators (Liu et al., 2019). Despite these promising findings, whether BA can protect against LPS-induced spleen injury in broilers and the underlying regulatory mechanisms remain poorly elucidated.

Hence, this research was aimed to investigate BA's protective capacity against LPS-induced splenic damage in broilers. Specifically, an LPS-induced spleen injury model was established via intraperitoneal injection, and key parameters were examined, including serum immunoglobulin concentrations, splenic histomorphology, splenic inflammatory markers, and redox homeostasis-related indices. Subsequently, the potential protective mechanisms of BA on the spleen were further investigated using network pharmacology, transcriptomics, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations. This study aimed to identify potential therapeutic targets for alleviating LPS-induced spleen injury and explore BA's potential application in the livestock and poultry industries.

Materials and methods

Animal experimental design and sample acquisition

192 one-day-old AA white broilers (purchased from Changsha Xindacheng Poultry Industry Co., Ltd.) were randomly allocated to 4 groups, with 48 broilers per group (6 replicates, 8 broilers per replicate): control (CON), BA, LPS and BA + LPS groups. The CON and LPS groups were fed a basal diet, while the BA and BA+LPS groups received the same basal diet supplemented with 24 mg/kg BA (98%, Xi’an Beijino Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Xi’an, China). The BA dose was determined via pre-experimental screening, and the feeding period lasted for 21 days (Yang, 2024). On the 17th, 19th and 21st days, broilers in the LPS and BA + LPS groups were intraperitoneally injected with 1 mg/kg BW LPS (E. coli serotype O55: B5; Cat. No. L2880, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) to establish a spleen injury model (Pang et al., 2023). Meanwhile, broilers in the CON and BA groups received an equal volume saline injection. Four hours following LPS administration on day 21, all broilers were euthanized. Blood samples and spleen tissues were immediately collected for subsequent experimental analyses. The animal experimentation protocols were authorized by the Hunan Agricultural University's Animal Ethics Board (approval No: 2022-31).

Spleen index determination and histopathological examination

Firstly, the isolated spleen was weighed, and the spleen index was calculated using the formula: spleen index = (spleen weight / broiler body weight) × 100%.

Subsequently, the spleen was fixed in 4% formaldehyde solution, dehydrated, and embedded in paraffin. Following sectioning of paraffin-embedded tissues into 5 μm slices, the sections were dewaxed in xylene, rehydrated through graded alcohols, and stained with hematoxylin for 5 minutes. After a water rinse, they were differentiated in 1% HCl alcohol, counterstained with eosin, and then subjected to standard dehydration, clearing, and resin mounting for microscopic examination.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observation

The spleen tissues were fixed in glutaraldehyde, followed by dehydration, embedding, and sectioning into ultrathin slices (50-70 nm thick). Splenic ultrastructural alterations were examined via TEM after overnight staining with uranyl acetate and lead citrate.

Detection of immunoglobulin levels in serum

The concentrations of immunoglobulin A (IgA), IgG, and IgM in serum were measured via Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) kits (Jiangsu Jingmei Technology Co., Ltd.) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Assessment of the redox capacity in broiler spleen tissue

The spleen tissue was mixed with physiological saline at a ratio of 1: 9 (tissue: saline), homogenized, and then centrifuged (4 °C, 3000 × g for 10 min) to collect the supernatant. The activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD) and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), as well as the contents of glutathione (GSH) and malondialdehyde (MDA), were determined using commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China) following the manufacturer's protocol.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from the spleen with the Trizol reagent, followed using the Evo M-MLV One-Step RT Kit (Accurate Biology, Hunan, China) for reverse transcription to obtain template cDNA for fluorescence RT-qPCR detection. The 2−ΔΔCT method was applied to assess mRNA levels, and GAPDH served as the internal control. Network pharmacology analysis

The PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) was employed to acquire the Simplified Molecular-Input Line-Entry System (SMLLE) notation and structure-data file (SDF) of “Betulinic acid”, which were then separately imported into the SwissTargetPrediction database (http://swisstargetprediction.ch/) and PharmMapper database (https://www.lilab-ecust.cn/pharmmapper/) to identify BA-related targets (probability > 0). Subsequently, targets linked to the disease were gathered from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) using the keyword "LPS-induced spleen injury" and filtered with a relevance score > 0. The two target lists (BA-related and disease-related) were uploaded to an online bioinformatics platform (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) to identify shared targets between BA and LPS-induced spleen injury. The Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) network of these shared targets was constructed using the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) database (https://cn.string-db.org/). The David database (http://david.abcc.ncifcrf.gov) was then used to perform Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis for the shared targets. Finally, the analysis results were visualized through the same bioinformatics platform to generate the statistical charts.

RNA-sequence analysis

Total RNA was extracted from the spleen using the Trizol reagent method. The concentration and purity of RNA were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE, USA), while RNA integrity was assessed with the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Following sample quality control, RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA and a cDNA library was subsequently constructed. After the cDNA library passed quality verification, sequencing was carried out on an Illumina NovaSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Subsequent bioinformatics analysis of the sequencing data was conducted on the Majorbio Cloud Platform (Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd.).

Molecular docking

Three-dimensional structures of four target proteins, IL6 (PDB ID: 1alu), STAT3 (PDB ID: 6tlc), TRAF6 (PDB ID: 1lb6), and IL17 (PDB ID: 5hi3), were obtained from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org/), and the molecular structure of the BA ligand (PubChem CID: 64971) was retrieved from the TCMSP database (http://tcmspw.com/). The protein structures were optimized using PyMOL software by removing all water molecules and unnecessary co-crystallized ligands; subsequently, hydrogen atoms were added to the optimized proteins, and partial charges were calculated using AutoDockTools 1.5.7. Next, AutoDockTools 1.5.7 was utilized for molecular docking, and the best binding conformation was evaluated based on docking energy. Finally, the docking results were visualized using PyMOL and Ligplus.

Molecular dynamics simulations

Molecular dynamics simulations were performed using GROMACS (2020.6_GPU) (Van Der Spoel et al., 2005), with the Amber99SB-ILDN force field selected, and a 100 ns simulation was conducted based on the optimal molecular docking results. The protein-ligand complex structure was embedded in a TIP3P water box, and its net charge was neutralized by adding counterions, with the distance between the solute and the box boundary maintained at 1.0 nm (Koder Hamid et al., 2022; Valdés-Tresanco et al., 2021). The system underwent energy minimization via a combination of the steepest descent and conjugate gradient methods. Post that, a dual phase equilibration was executed. Initially, a 2 ns NVT simulation was run to ensure system temperature stability. Next, a complementary 2 ns NPT simulation was executed to balance the system’s pressure. Manufacturing proceeded under NPT conditions at 310 K and 1 bar. Temporal changes in protein dynamics were analyzed with Xmgrace, and the protein-ligand binding free energy was calculated using GROMACS via the molecular mechanics / poisson-boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA) method (Valdés-Tresanco et al., 2021).

Western blotting

Western blotting analysis of spleen tissue was performed according to a previously described method (Kong et al., 2021). Primary antibodies used were as follows: IL-6 (WL02841, 1:1000, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China), Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) (A11216, 1:1000; Abclonal, Wuhan, China), P-STAT3 (AP0070, 1:500, Abclonal, Wuhan, China), IL-17 (WL02981, 1:1000, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China), TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) (WL02086, 1:1000, Wanleibio, Shenyang, China) and β-actin (Aifang Biological, Hunan, China).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 and SPSS software. The data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For data conforming to normal distribution, homogeneity of variances was tested and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed. Fisher's LSD post hoc test was used for equal variances, whereas Tamhane's T2 post hoc test was used for unequal variances. P < 0.05, P < 0.01, P < 0.001 were considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

Results

BA mitigated LPS-induced spleen injury in broilers

Compared with the CON group, LPS challenge resulted in significant spleen enlargement and a marked rise in the spleen index (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1A-D). Histopathological analysis showed structural fusion of the red and white pulp, reduced lymphocyte numbers, and obvious cellular damage including nuclear fragmentation and pyknosis. Ultrastructural observation revealed mitochondrial swelling, cristae fragmentation, and cristolysis. In contrast, BA pretreatment significantly ameliorated these alterations, resulting in a pronounced reduction in spleen size and index (P < 0.01), restored clear demarcation between red and white pulp, increased lymphocyte counts with ordered arrangement, and normalized mitochondrial structure. ELISA results indicated that LPS stimulation significantly elevated serum concentrations of IgA, IgG, and IgM. Moreover, prior administration of BA further augmented the concentrations of IgA, IgG, and IgM (Fig. 1E) (P < 0.01). BA improved LPS-induced spleen oxidative stress and inflammation in broilers

Fig. 2A-D illustrates that LPS-administered broilers displayed significantly increased mRNA levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), IL-1β, IL-8 and IL-10 in the spleen when contrasted with the CON group (P < 0.01). BA pretreatment significantly diminished these inflammatory cytokines' mRNA levels (IL-1β, IL-8, IL-10 and TNF-α) (P < 0.01). As presented in Fig. 2E-H, LPS treatment caused a marked decline in the contents of T-AOC and GSH, and a substantial elevation in MDA content relative to the CON group, indicating that LPS induced severe oxidative stress in the spleen. However, BA pretreatment effectively suppressed LPS-induced elevations in MDA content and reversed LPS-induced decreases in SOD and T-AOC levels (P < 0.05). Network pharmacological analysis of the protective effects of BA against LPS-induced spleen injury

To explore BA's molecular mechanisms in mitigating LPS spleen damage using network pharmacology. Initially, 317 BA-related genes were identified using the PubChem, SwissTargetPrediction and PharmMapper databases, while 991 genes associated with LPS-induced spleen injury were retrieved from the GeneCards database. Afterward, the Venn diagram was generated via an online bioinformatics platform to identify overlapping targets, yielding 96 shared targets (Fig. 3A). Analysis using the STRING database generated a PPI network consisting of 96 nodes, which was built based on the overlapping genes between BA and LPS-induced spleen injury (Fig. 3B). To elucidate the underlying mechanisms of how BA protects the spleen from LPS-induced damage, GO functional enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis were performed on the overlapping targets using the DAVID database. GO functional enrichment analysis identified 468 biological processes (BP), including intracellular receptor signaling pathway, response to lipopolysaccharide, and cellular response to lipopolysaccharide; 59 cellular components (CC) such as cytosol, cytoplasm, nucleus, and nucleoplasm; and 118 molecular functions (MF) comprising nuclear receptor activity, protein kinase activity, endopeptidase activity, and enzyme binding. The highest-ranking 10 enriched terms in each of the BP, CC, and MF categories are displayed based on their enrichment metrics (Fig. 3E). The KEGG pathway analysis of the 96 overlapping genes identified 147 significantly enriched pathways. Based on the significance of p-value, the top 20 most relevant pathways are presented in Fig. 3C, including the Th17 cell differentiation, TNF pathway, and IL-17 signaling pathway. Notably, the IL-17 signaling axis ranked third in terms of notable enrichment, underscored by its crucial role in the therapeutic responses to BA. RNA-sequence analysis of BA’s effects on LPS-induced spleen injury in broilers

To elucidate the defensive mechanism of BA against LPS-induced spleen damage in chickens, the RNA-sequence analysis was performed. The analysis identified 427 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the CON and LPS groups, comprising 167 downregulated and 260 upregulated genes (Fig. 4A). In contrast, the BA+LPS group had 51 DEGs relative to the LPS group, with 14 downregulated and 37 upregulated genes (Fig. 4B). Venn analysis identified 25 common DEGs across the groups (Fig. 4C). Using cytoscape software, a PPI network was constructed and analyzed from all DEGs. The results identified IL-6 as a central hub within this network (Fig. 4E). KEGG enrichment analysis of all DEGs revealed enrichment across multiple signalling pathways, with the IL-17 pathway demonstrating the most significant correlation and top ranking (Fig. 4F). It was worth noting that this finding aligns closely with the predictions made by prior network pharmacological studies. Molecular docking of BA with IL-6, STAT3, IL-17 and TRAF6

Subsequently, molecular docking simulation assessed BA's binding affinities to key proteins in the IL-17 signaling pathway. Per the established criteria (Du et al., 2024), a binding affinity of below -5.0 kcal/mol is considered to indicate favourable binding, while a value lower than -7.0 kcal/mol suggests a more stable interaction between the molecule and the protein. The results demonstrated that BA exhibited binding energies of -8.3 kcal/mol with IL-6, -6.5 kcal/mol with STAT3, -7.56 kcal/mol with IL-17, and -8.01 kcal/mol with TRAF6 (Fig. 5A). These favorable binding energies indicated that BA could dock effectively with IL-6, STAT3, IL-17 and TRAF6. Molecular dynamics simulations of the BA-IL-17 complex

Building upon these results, we performed a 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation of the BA-IL-17 complex to analyze its dynamic properties of the docked complex. The root mean square deviation (RMSD) figures remained relatively stable at around 0.2 nm for the duration of the simulation (Fig. 5B). The root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) results showed that the fluctuations in amino acid residues ranged from 0.1 to 0.6 nm, while the radius of gyration (Rg) values remained stable at approximately 1.7 nm. These findings indicated that the complex exhibited good overall stability (Fig. 5C-D). During the 100 ns simulation, the BA-IL-17 complex maintained more than three hydrogen bonds for most of the duration, indicating strong intermolecular interactions between BA and IL-17 (Fig. 5E). Solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) analysis revealed little variation during the entire simulation, attesting to the protein's structural stability and conserved conformation (Fig. 5F). The total binding free energy of the BA-IL-17 complex was -24.56 kJ/mol, indicating that the complex was thermodynamically stable and that the binding process can occur spontaneously (Fig. 5G). Collectively, these simulation results indicated that the complex formed by BA-IL-17 was highly stable, further verifying the accuracy and reliability of the molecular docking outcomes.

BA mitigated LPS-induced spleen injury through suppressing the IL-17 signaling pathway

To explore the regulatory mechanism of BA, we used qRT-PCR and Western blotting to measure the expression of IL-17 pathway-related molecules at the transcriptional and translational levels in the spleen. As shown in Fig. 6B−J, stimulation with LPS caused a significant increase in both the gene expression and protein production of IL-6, STAT3, IL-17 and TRAF6 in the spleen when compared to the CON group (P < 0.05). In contrast, prior treatment with BA led to a marked reduction in the mRNA and protein concentrations of these same factors relative to the LPS group (P < 0.05). Meanwhile, the mRNA expression levels of downstream genes in the IL-17 signaling pathway including the chemokine (CCL20), tissue remodeling-related genes (MMP1, MMP9 and MMP13) and inflammatory factors (COX-2 and CSF3) were significantly downregulated in the BA+LPS group compared with the LPS group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 6A). Discussion

The spleen plays a pivotal role in immunity by serving as a critical site for immune cell residence, immune response activation and humoral regulation (Lewis et al., 2019). In intensive poultry production, stressors such as high stocking density and poor ventilation promote bacterial proliferation and elevate endogenous LPS levels, which in turn trigger oxidative stress and inflammatory responses, ultimately leading to splenic dysfunction and systemic immune impairment (Wang et al., 2023b). Currently, plant-derived natural products are attracting growing attention due to their low toxicity, high efficacy, and minimal side effects (Najmi et al., 2022). BA, a pentacyclic triterpenoid compound, exhibits immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant characteristics (Ríos and Máñez, 2018). Research previously indicated BA shields murine spleens from T-2 toxin damage (Kong et al., 2021). However, it remains unclear whether BA can alleviate LPS-induced spleen injury in broilers and what its underlying mechanism is. Therefore, for the investigation, we established a spleen injury model via intraperitoneal LPS injection, exploring the protective effect of BA and its underlying molecular mechanism (Pang et al., 2023). Our findings demonstrated that BA pretreatment effectively inhibited the activation of the IL-17 signaling pathway, reduced the production and release of inflammatory factors, alleviated oxidative stress, and enhanced immune competence (Fig. 7). These results provide compelling evidence that BA may serve as a promising therapeutic candidate for treating spleen injury.Fig. 7BA alleviates LPS-induced spleen injury by inhibiting the IL-17 signaling pathway in broilers.

The spleen is primarily composed of red pulp and white pulp, and serves as a major site for adaptive immune responses (Dong et al., 2025). Research has demonstrated that LPS can induce splenomegaly and an increased spleen index in mice and piglets, accompanied by a blurred boundaries between the red and white pulp, ultimately leading to pathological damage to the splenic tissue architecture (Wang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023a). Moreover, it has been shown that dietary supplementation with plant-derived natural compounds such as liensinine (Wang et al., 2023a), phellinus linteus polysaccharides (Li et al., 2025), and salvianolic acid B (Wang et al., 2025) can alleviate LPS-induced spleen injury. In agreement with these findings, our experimental results demonstrated that BA effectively attenuated LPS-induced splenic histopathological damage. Mitochondria play crucial roles in immune regulation and cellular metabolism. Our previous studies demonstrated that BA pretreatment mitigated T-2 toxin-induced reduction in mitochondrial number, mitochondrial swelling, and cristae disruption in the mouse spleen (Kong et al., 2021). Our findings similarly demonstrated that BA exerted protective effects against LPS-induced spleen injury in broilers, which was consistent with the protective effects observed in the T-2 toxin model. This suggested that the protective role of BA against spleen injury is not toxin-specific and reflects its broad immunomodulatory properties.

Additionally, various pathogenic stimuli trigger splenic immune responses, which manifest as inflammatory reactions and oxidative damage (Li et al., 2021; Qiu et al., 2025). LPS, as a typical pathogen-associated molecular pattern, activates immune cells to release pro-inflammatory cytokines and substantial amounts of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Insufficient ROS clearance induces oxidative stress, which in turn exacerbates inflammatory responses, forming a vicious cycle. SOD and GSH exert antioxidant effects by scavenging ROS. However, when ROS production overwhelms the clearance capacity, oxidative stress is induced, as indicated by elevated MDA, a biomarker of lipid peroxidation (Reuter et al., 2010; Chai et al., 2019). Studies have shown that LPS upregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-18) while increasing MDA content and reducing SOD activity in the spleen of piglets (Wang et al., 2025). Consistent with this, our experiments also observed LPS-induced inflammatory responses and oxidative stress in the broiler spleen. Notably, the mRNA level of IL-10 in the spleen was elevated following LPS stimulation, reflecting a compensatory anti-inflammatory response to limit excessive inflammation. However, pro-inflammatory cytokines remained persistently elevated, indicating that this endogenous feedback was insufficient to fully control the inflammatory insult (Adib-Conquy and Cavaillon, 2009; Liao et al., 2024). Similarly, liensinine has been shown to inhibit LPS-induced inflammatory cytokine expression and enhance antioxidant capacity (Wang et al., 2023a). It has also been reported that BA could alleviate LPS-induced neuroinflammation in mice and intestinal inflammatory injury in piglets (He et al., 2025; Pandey et al., 2025). Therefore, downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines and mitigating oxidative stress may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for alleviating spleen injury. In the present study, BA pretreatment significantly decreased the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-8, and TNF-α) in the spleen, while reducing MDA accumulation and enhancing SOD activity and total antioxidant capacity. Moreover, IL-10 expression was also reduced as the inflammation subsided. Collectively, these results suggest that BA alleviated LPS-induced spleen injury by suppressing inflammatory responses and oxidative stress, thereby contributing to the restoration of immune homeostasis.

Immunoglobulins levels serve as critical indicators for assessing immunological competence in poultry (Megha and Mohanan, 2021; Schroeder and Cavacini, 2010). Studies have demonstrated that plant-derived compounds can enhance immune function and promote immunoglobulin secretion. For example, dietary supplementation with polysaccharides from Acanthopanax senticosus was found to increase the serum concentrations of IgA and IgM in broilers, thereby enhancing humoral immunity (Long et al., 2021). Similarly, our previous study showed that BA pretreatment significantly increased the serum levels of IgG and IgM, strengthening immunoglobulin-mediated protection and alleviating T-2 toxin-induced intestinal mucosal damage in mice (Luo et al., 2020). In the present study, LPS challenge triggered inflammatory responses and disrupted redox homeostasis in the spleen, leading to excessive production of inflammatory cytokines and ROS. Given that ROS can mediate B lymphocyte activation, proliferation and differentiation (Fedyk and Phipps, 1994), this may consequently drive the overproduction of immunoglobulins. Thus, the elevated serum IgA, IgG and IgM levels in the LPS group likely reflected a stress-induced humoral immune response driven by ROS overproduction under inflammatory conditions. In contrast, dietary BA supplementation restored redox homeostasis, creating a favorable microenvironment for plasma cell differentiation, thereby facilitating immunoglobulin production and contributing to host defense and tissue repair.

To explore the protective mechanism of BA, we first performed network pharmacology analysis to identify potential mechanisms. The results suggested that BA might exert protective effects by regulating the IL-17 signaling pathway. Subsequently, we conducted transcriptomics analysis for further validation, and the results indicated significant enrichment of the IL-17 signaling pathway in DEGs. IL-17, primarily secreted by Th17 cells, is a key pro-inflammatory cytokine (Guo et al., 2022) that plays critical roles in various pathological processes, including pulmonary inflammatory diseases and autoimmune myocarditis (Jiang et al., 2024; Panda et al., 2024). Upon IL-17 activation, the adaptor protein TRAF6 is recruited, triggering downstream signaling cascades that induce chemokines, inflammatory factors, and tissue remodeling-related genes, thereby amplifying inflammatory responses (Zhang et al., 2023). IL-6 and STAT3, as upstream regulators, are essential for IL-17 production and secretion (Qin et al., 2024). IL-6 induces janus kinase 2 (JAK2)-mediated STAT3 phosphorylation and dimerization, followed by nuclear translocation, which promotes Th17 cell maturation and IL-17 release (Camporeale and Poli, 2012; Xuan et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2007). Based on these findings, we hypothesized that targeting key nodal points in this cascade (IL-6, STAT3, IL-17, TRAF6) may underlie the spleen-protective effects of BA. Molecular docking results showed that BA formed stable complexes with IL-6, STAT3, IL-17, and TRAF6. Molecular dynamics simulations further confirmed the stability of the BA-IL-17 complex, as evidenced by consistent trajectories of RMSD, RMSF, Rg, and binding free energy. Collectively, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations yielded consistent binding patterns, suggesting that BA forms a stable complex with IL-17. This interaction may contribute to BA's protective effects against LPS-induced spleen injury, although this binding interaction awaits further experimental confirmation.

Based on these findings, we conducted in vivo experiments to verify the mechanism. Our experimental data showed that BA pretreatment effectively reduced the expression of key targets in the IL-17 pathway (IL-6, STAT3, IL-17, and TRAF6) as well as downstream genes, including MMP1, MMP9, MMP13, COX-2, CSF3, HSP90AA1, and CCL20. Western blotting analysis revealed that the protein levels of IL-6, STAT3, IL-17, and TRAF6 were elevated in the LPS group, but significantly decreased following BA treatment, consistent with the mRNA expression results detected via qRT-PCR. These findings align with a previous report showing that hesperidin supplementation alleviated intestinal injury by suppressing the mRNA and protein expression of IL-17A and TRAF6 in the IL-17 signaling pathway, thereby reducing downstream chemokines CCL7 and C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CXCL5) (Zhang et al., 2025). Our results confirmed that BA alleviated LPS-induced spleen injury in broilers by regulating the IL-17 signaling pathway, providing experimental evidence for the protective effects of natural plant-derived bioactive compounds on immune organs in animals.

In summary, our study demonstrated that dietary BA supplementation effectively inhibited the occurrence of oxidative stress and the expression of inflammatory cytokine, while mitigating pathological damage in the spleen of broilers. Mechanistically, BA significantly alleviated LPS-induced spleen damage by inhibiting the IL-17 signaling pathway. This research provides a theoretical basis for employing BA as a therapeutic intervention against LPS-induced spleen damage in poultry.

Effectiveness of a nurse-led theory-based program on breastfeeding outcomes in women after cesarean section: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yirong He, Xin He, Xinru Bao, Min Qiu, Juan Du, Ting Zhang, Min Shu, Lan Liu, Yu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreastfeeding provides significant benefits for both mothers and infants, yet its rates remain suboptimal, particularly among women undergoing cesarean sections. Breastfeeding practices are determined by a wide range of socio-environmental, cultural, family and individual factors, but current breastfeeding promotion interventions that integrate these dimensions are limited. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of a breastfeeding intervention based on Theory of Planned Behavior and the Interactive Theory of Breastfeeding in promoting breastfeeding outcomes in women after cesarean section.MethodsA total of 763 women were recruited and randomly divided into an intervention group (n = 383) and a control group (n = 380). The participants were women scheduled for elective cesarean sections due to medical indications. On the basis of theory, mothers in the intervention group received the breastfeeding promotion program, focusing on enhancing breastfeeding knowledge, fostering positive attitudes, increasing family support, strengthening perceived behavioral control, facilitating the transition from intention to action, and promoting mother-infant interaction through skin-to-skin contact, responsive feeding, and emotional bonding. The control group received routine nursing care. Outcomes were assessed using the Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool and the Chinese Breastfeeding Attrition Prediction Tool, focusing on exclusive breastfeeding rates, breastfeeding frequency, duration, and complications.ResultsThe exclusive breastfeeding rates in the intervention group were significantly higher on day 1, day 2 And day 3 after cesarean section compared to the control group (P < 0.001). The intervention group exhibited higher Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool and Chinese Breastfeeding Attrition Prediction Tool scores, improved breastfeeding frequency and duration, and a lower incidence of breastfeeding-related complications (P < 0.05).ConclusionA theory-based nursing intervention integrating Theory of Planned Behavior and the Interactive Theory of Breastfeeding significantly enhanced exclusive breastfeeding rates and maternal breastfeeding behaviors in women after cesarean sections. This study highlights the value of combining psychological and contextual factors to design effective breastfeeding interventions, providing evidence for scalable and sustainable strategies to support breastfeeding in postpartum care.Trial registrationThis trial was retrospectively registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on November 19th, 2024 (ChiCTR2400092869) .Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08094-x.Keywords: Breastfeeding, Cesarean section, Theory, Nursing intervention, Randomized controlled trial

Background

Breastfeeding is widely recognized as the optimal method of infant feeding, offering numerous short- and long-term health benefits for both mothers and infants [1]. It provides essential nutrients, enhances immune function, and reduces the risk of infections and chronic diseases in infants. For mothers, breastfeeding is associated with a lower risk of postpartum hemorrhage, breast and ovarian cancers, and type 2 diabetes. Beyond health, it also fosters mother-infant bonding and supports early psychological and emotional development [2]. Accordingly, the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) advocate for exclusive breastfeeding during the first six months of life, followed by continued breastfeeding alongside appropriate complementary foods up to two years of age or longer [3].

Cesarean section has become a common mode of childbirth, with its prevalence rising globally [4]. According to data from WHO and UNICEF, the global cesarean section rate increased from 12.1% in 2000 to 21.1% in 2015 [5]. In China, the average cesarean section rate rose from 13.4% in 2000 to 28.8% in 2015, representing an increase of over 15% points. A study conducted in a hospital in Beijing reported an average cesarean section rate of 51.15% from 1995 to 2014 [6]. Despite multiple measures implemented by the National Health Commission to control this trend, the cesarean section rate in China remained as high as 44.1% in 2020 [7]. While cesarean section is an effective intervention to safeguard maternal and neonatal lives in emergencies such as fetal distress or obstructed labor, it poses significant challenges in the postpartum period, particularly in promoting breastfeeding [8]. A systematic review and meta-analysis reported that only 40.1% of infants delivered via cesarean section initiated exclusive breastfeeding within the first hour postpartum, compared to 72.4% of those delivered vaginally [9]. Zhao et al. [10] further demonstrated that the odds of exclusive breastfeeding in the early postpartum period were 47% lower among women who underwent cesarean section compared to those who had vaginal deliveries in China (OR = 0.53, 95% CI: 0.41–0.68). Thus, it is imperative to explore effective breastfeeding intervention models for women undergoing cesarean section.

Breastfeeding behavior is influenced by a complex interplay of socioeconomic, cultural, and personal determinants [11]. Evidence suggests that greater knowledge about breastfeeding and more favorable attitudes toward it are associated with improved breastfeeding outcomes [12]. Support from partners or perceived familial expectations also play a crucial role, with higher success rates observed among mothers who receive encouragement from family members [13]. Furthermore, a mother’s belief in her own ability to breastfeed, has been identified as a pivotal predictor of continued breastfeeding. Interventions aimed at strengthening maternal confidence and perceived behavioral control have demonstrated effectiveness in extending breastfeeding duration. In addition, the quality of mother-infant interaction is also an important factor affecting breastfeeding behavior [14]. Early and responsive interaction, including skin-to-skin contact, eye contact, and emotional attunement, promotes infant feeding cues recognition and facilitates successful breastfeeding initiation and continuation. A strong maternal-infant bond may enhance the mother’s motivation and responsiveness during feeding, contributing to a more positive breastfeeding experience [15].

The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), a widely applied framework in health behavior research, has been extensively utilized to understand and predict breastfeeding practices, with a central emphasis on behavioral intention as the primary determinant of action [16]. Within this model, a mother’s intention to breastfeed is shaped by three core constructs: her attitude toward breastfeeding, perceived social expectations, and perceived behavioral control [17]. Attitude reflects personal evaluations informed by beliefs about the advantages and value of breastfeeding. Subjective norms represent the perceived influence or expectations of significant others regarding breastfeeding. Perceived behavioral control refers to a mother’s self-assessed capability and confidence in initiating and sustaining breastfeeding [18]. The Interactive Theory of Breastfeeding (ITB) views breastfeeding as a dynamic process where the mother and child interact with each other and their environment to obtain the benefits of breastfeeding [19]. This interaction is influenced by their perceptions, biological conditions, body image, breastfeeding space, mother’s role, support systems, family and social influences, and the mother’s decision-making. While prior research has often investigated internal psychological determinants or external contextual factors in isolation, integrating TPB with ITB provides a more holistic framework for understanding and supporting breastfeeding behavior. This theoretical synthesis enables the development of multifaceted interventions that simultaneously address cognitive and motivational dimension, while also accounting for relational and environmental factors that influence the mother-infant dyad. By leveraging the strengths of both frameworks, breastfeeding support strategies can be better tailored to promote sustained and effective breastfeeding outcomes in diverse maternal populations.

An increasing number of researchers have begun to focus on breastfeeding behaviors among postpartum women following cesarean section, aiming to promote positive changes in breastfeeding practices [20–22]. Thi’ results supported the use of a theory-based and designed mobile phone application as a part of a promising intervention to improve breastfeeding outcomes among mothers who have cesarean sections. Hu’s [23] study suggested that Health Belief Model-based interventions can effectively improve breastfeeding knowledge, behaviors, And satisfaction among Chinese cesarean women within 4 months postpartum. However, the results of a systematic review on interventions aimed at increasing the duration of breastfeeding among women who have undergone a cesarean section indicated that most of these studies did not report statistically significant improvements in initiation or duration of breastfeeding [24]. Therefore, there remains a lack of consensus on the most effective strategies for promoting exclusive breastfeeding in the cesarean section population.

Therefore, this study aimed to examine the effectiveness of the theory-based intervention program that targeted at increasing exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) rate and breastfeeding-related outcomes among cesarean section women in China. The theoretical framework diagram is shown in Fig. 1.

Methods

Study design

This study adopted a pragmatic, single-center randomized controlled trial design. Participants in the control group received standard postpartum care, which included basic breastfeeding education. In contrast, those in the intervention group were provided with a nurse-led theory-based intervention specifically designed to enhance breastfeeding practices following cesarean section. This trial was retrospectively registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) on November 19th, 2024 (ChiCTR2400092869).

Participants and setting

This study was conducted between November 2024 and February 2025 in the obstetric unit of a women and children’s hospital in China. Pregnant women scheduled for elective cesarean section were screened upon admission to assess their eligibility for inclusion. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) ability to communicate effectively both physically and mentally; (3) no medical contraindications to breastfeeding; and (4) willingness to attend and participate in the study. Exclusion criteria included: (1) gestational age < 37 weeks; (2) intention to formula-feed; and (3) prior participation in other comprehensive breastfeeding interventions. For sample size calculation, the formula for 2 population proportions was used for hypothesis testing purposes. The sample size was based on estimates of the proportion of EBF in the control group being 0.59 [21], and a 23% expected improvement in EBF in the intervention group. With an α of 0.05 and β of 0.20, an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.05, and an attrition rate of 20%. The final minimum sample size required was 160 participants. A permuted block randomization method was employed to assign participants to either the intervention or control group. With a block size of 4, six distinct block arrangements were used to ensure equal distribution between the two groups. A computer-generated random number sequence was utilized to select the block sequence, which determined the allocation order for the first four participants. This process was repeated for the remaining participants. Participants were blinded to their group allocation throughout the study. However, due to the nature of the intervention, blinding of the personnel delivering the intervention was not feasible.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University (approval number: 2024[367]). Participants in this study were completely voluntary and optional. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and they were assured of the confidentiality of the information. They were fully informed of the study procedures and told that they could withdraw from the study at any time.

Intervention

To minimize the risk of contamination between the intervention and control groups, participants were randomly allocated to separate rooms following group assignment. The obstetrics department provided all routine postpartum maternity care. The research team consisted of international lactation consultants, obstetric nurses with extensive clinical experience, and nurses from breastfeeding consultation clinics, all possessing in-depth expertise in breastfeeding. Furthermore, prior to the intervention’s implementation, all research team members participated in standardized training to ensure uniformity in the delivery of the intervention. The training program included essential theoretical concepts, intervention strategies, communication techniques, and guidelines for maintaining confidentiality. Establishing a positive attitude

On the day of admission to the hospital, researchers first introduced the program to the participants in the intervention group before their cesarean sections. A video, lasting approximately 20–30 min, was shown to facilitate the educational process. The video, created by the researchers, was based on previous studies and clinical experience. It provided comprehensive information on breastfeeding, emphasizing its benefits and importance. The content aimed to foster positive attitudes toward breastfeeding and promote correct knowledge and practices. Additionally, the video was designed to enhance the understanding of postpartum breastfeeding positions for both women and their families.

Providing supportive subjective norms

On the day of admission, family members of the participants were invited to participate in preoperative education by watching an instructional video. The video provided guidance on essential breastfeeding skills, including newborn handling, sucking techniques, positioning, and cooperation between the parturient woman and her support system. Through scenario-based simulations, the video aimed to equip family members with the necessary knowledge and practical skills to support the mother effectively. This approach was designed to strengthen the role of family members and other caregivers in facilitating breastfeeding, thereby fostering a supportive environment and promoting a positive, collaborative paradigm for breastfeeding.

Promoting perceived behavioral control

From the day of the cesarean section to one day post-operation, the nurse assessed the condition of the mother’s nipples and monitored the newborn’s feeding. Breastfeeding education was provided to both the family members and the mother, with guidance for them to scan a QR code to access educational videos. In addition, bedside support was offered to both the mother and her family members, including early and frequent skin-to-skin contact, verbal and tactile responsiveness during feeding, and the use of gentle maternal voice and eye contact to strengthen emotional bonding. Mothers were encouraged to observe and respond to infant hunger and satiety cues, such as rooting, sucking motions, and hand-to-mouth movements. Nurses also facilitated sessions where mothers practiced calming techniques, such as swaddling or infant massage, to foster a relaxed and responsive environment conducive to effective breastfeeding. This included multiple observations and one-on-one guidance to address any issues. Through health education and hands-on support, the goal was to increase maternal-child interaction, enhance the breastfeeding experience, and improve maternal behavior control. Finally, the research team completed the Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool to evaluate participants’ breastfeeding activities.

Strengthening transition from intention to actions

From the second day after the cesarean section until discharge, family members and mothers were organized to watch videos of breastfeeding knowledge at the corresponding stage, including the amount of newborn feeding at the current stage, sitting breastfeeding posture, physiologically breast engorgement, how to perform hand expression correctly, breast milk storage and the use of electric breast pumps. In addition, the breastfeeding symposium was carried out to encourage successful mothers and their families to share the feeding results of exclusive breastfeeding, and to form positive breastfeeding behavior guidance in the department. On the day of discharge, the nurse summarized and fed back the results of breastfeeding according to the Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool, and the knowledge of breastfeeding that may be involved in her later stage was propagated. Through the above measures, strengthening the intention of mothers to breastfeed can be translated into practical behavior. Finally, the Chinese Breastfeeding Attrition Prediction Tool was completed to predict the participants’ breastfeeding behavior post-discharge. This tool was used to provide targeted interventions based on the questionnaire results. Continued breastfeeding care was also provided after the mother was discharged from the hospital. Regular follow-up of breastfeeding after delivery was carried out through the hospital follow-up platform. The hospital has set up a post-natal breast-feeding clinic and has also informed the mothers of their consultation telephone numbers and registration forms, welcoming them to breastfeeding counselling in order to continue their breastfeeding behaviour.

Providing routine care to the participants in the control group

In accordance with the Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative (BFHI) standards, the control group received standard care, which included routine prenatal and postnatal services [25]. During the prenatal period, women received breastfeeding education, where nurses provided information on the benefits of breastfeeding, as well as guidance on its recommended duration and frequency. Post-delivery, nurses instructed mothers on early breastfeeding, addressed any issues, and offered support through clinic consultations or 24-hour breastfeeding counseling following discharge. Evaluation indicator

The primary outcome indicators of this study included the breastfeeding rates at 1–3 days postpartum, which encompassed exclusive breastfeeding, mixed feeding, and artificial feeding rates. Exclusive breastfeeding in this study was defined as infants receiving only maternal milk, with no supplementation of other liquids or solid foods. Additional outcome indicators included breastfeeding quality, frequency of breastfeeding sessions, and the average duration of breastfeeding. The occurrence of breast-related issues, such as sore nipples, cracked nipples, and engorgement, was also recorded. Data were collected using evaluation tools, including a demographic information questionnaire, the Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool and the Chinese Breastfeeding Attrition Prediction Tool.

Demographics questionnaire

The demographic characteristics were collected through a researcher-designed questionnaire, including age, ethnicity, education, occupation, gravidity, parity and pregnancy complications.

Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool (BBAT)

In 2015, Ingram et al. [26]. developed the Bristol Breastfeeding Assessment Tool (BBAT), a concise tool aimed at facilitating improved targeting of advice on optimal positioning and attachment during breastfeeding. The BBAT was designed to contain four main elements: Positioning, Attachment, Sucking, and Swallowing. Each item could be scored as Poor (0), Moderate (1), or Good (2), leading to a possible total score ranging from 0 to 8 for the final 4-item tool. The BBAT showed good internal reliability with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.668, indicating acceptable consistency among its items. BBAT provides a rapid and accurate assessment of breastfeeding proficiency, aiming to enhance breastfeeding efficiency and maternal self-confidence, potentially contributing to longer durations of exclusive breastfeeding.

Chinese Breastfeeding Attrition Prediction Tool (BAPT)

In 2002, Dick et al. [27]. developed the Breastfeeding Attrition Prediction Tool (BAPT), which was based on the theory of planned behavior and includes four dimensions: Positive Breastfeeding Sentiment, Negative Breastfeeding Sentiment, Social and Professional Support, and Control. Cai et al. localized the BAPT into Chinese. The Chinese BAPT contains items scored on a Likert scale from 1 to 5. The Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.903. The BAPT aims to assist clinicians in identifying women at risk for early cessation of breastfeeding, thereby allowing targeted interventions. The BAPT has potential as a tool for predicting early breastfeeding attrition, particularly among first-time mothers.

Data collection

After obtaining informed consent, participants were invited to complete a baseline survey to collect sociodemographic data. On days 1, 2, and 3 postpartum, participants completed the BBAT. During these days, nurses recorded the number and duration of daily breastfeeding sessions and assessed any breast-related issues. On the day of discharge, participants completed the BAPT to predict their breastfeeding behavior post-discharge.

Data analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize participants’ baseline characteristics. Continuous variables, such as maternal age, were presented as means and standard deviations (SD), while categorical variables, including education level, employment status, parity, and presence of pregnancy complications, were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Between-group comparisons of demographic and baseline variables were conducted using independent-samples t-tests for continuous data and chi-square tests for categorical data to ensure baseline equivalence. EBF rates at postpartum day 1, day 2, and day 3 were compared between the intervention And control groups using chi-square tests. The differences in breastfeeding-related measures on day 3 postpartum were analyzed using independent-samples t-tests. In addition, breastfeeding behaviors and occurrence of breast problems over the three days postpartum were examined using t-tests for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical outcomes. A two-tailed P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 778 participants who consented to join the study were randomly assigned to either the intervention or control group. As illustrated in Fig. 4, participant attrition and the corresponding reasons are documented according to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines.

Baseline characteristics

Participants in this study ranged in age from 23 to 43 years. The average age of participants in the intervention and control groups was 31.89 ± 3.58 and 31.82 ± 3.39, respectively. The vast majority of participants in both intervention group and control group were of Han ethnicity. When examining the educational levels of the intervention group mothers, it was determined that 69.7% had a bachelor’s degree or above. In the intervention group, 92.7% of participants were employed. In terms of parity, the majority of participants were primiparous, accounting for 81.7% in the intervention group and 77.9% in the control group. In addition, gestational diabetes mellitus was the most common pregnancy complication in the intervention group (18.0%) and control group (18.4%). There were no significant differences between the two groups at baseline (Table 1).

Discussion

By integrating insights from the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and the Interactive Theory of Breastfeeding (ITB), the study underscored the importance of addressing both the cognitive and interactive dimensions of breastfeeding. The results confirmed that these interventions effectively increased exclusive breastfeeding rates, breastfeeding frequency, and duration, while reducing the incidence of breastfeeding-related problems, such as sore nipples, cracked nipples, engorgement.

The findings of this study demonstrated that the nurse-led theory-based intervention was effective in enhancing exclusive breastfeeding rate. By the third day postpartum, the exclusive breastfeeding rate reached 63.8% in the intervention group, higher than the 41.6% observed in the control group. In general, the intervention could provide support to those women after cesarean section experiencing breastfeeding problems, as they were more likely to master relevant knowledge and skills. The breastfeeding results obtained from this study are consistent with those drawn from previous studies [28, 29]. Furthermore, the intervention actively promoted early mother-infant physical and emotional interactions, such as immediate skin-to-skin contact, rooming-in, and guided infant cue recognition, which helped reinforce breastfeeding initiation and continuation.

Positive attitudes toward breastfeeding can be nurtured through targeted educational interventions and supportive interactions [30]. Within the ITB framework, maternal attitudes are not only shaped by cognitive understanding but also through dynamic interactions with their infants, family members, and healthcare professionals [31]. In this study, participants received video education during hospitalization and phone counseling post-discharge, interventions that addressed both the TPB’s focus on knowledge and ITB’s emphasis on relational and experiential factors. These interventions enhanced maternal confidence, as mothers experienced direct positive feedback from their infants’ feeding behaviors and emotional satisfaction, reinforcing a positive attitude toward breastfeeding. In our study, the intervention group showed higher attitude scores on postpartum compared to the control group. This suggests that the intervention effectively improved participants’ attitudes toward breastfeeding. Previous studies have similarly demonstrated that targeted educational interventions can significantly enhance maternal attitudes toward breastfeeding by increasing awareness of its benefits and addressing misconceptions [32, 33]. These positive shifts in breastfeeding attitudes are crucial, as they are associated with higher rates of breastfeeding initiation and continuation. Our findings align with this body of research, emphasizing the importance of early, theory-based interventions in shaping maternal attitudes and behaviors regarding breastfeeding [34]. However, in this study, the intervention group also had higher scores for negative breastfeeding sentiments. This may be because intervention group received more comprehensive education and hands-on guidance, which may have increased their awareness and recognition of both the benefits and potential difficulties associated with breastfeeding. This heightened awareness, paired with reflective questioning and exposure to real-life scenarios in the educational videos, may have led mothers to more accurately report concerns or perceived difficulties.

The theory-based intervention resulted in an improvement in subjective norm scores among participants in the intervention group. Women’s infant feeding choices are strongly influenced by the preferences of key family members, particularly their spouses and maternal figures such as mothers or mothers-in-law [35]. The engagement of family members in breastfeeding-related activities within this study fostered a supportive environment, thereby reinforcing women’s sense of encouragement and assistance in initiating and maintaining breastfeeding [36]. Furthermore, the professional support provided by medical staff, coupled with evidence-based guidance, created an environment where societal pressures to conform to breastfeeding norms were alleviated [37]. By reducing perceived societal judgment and increasing positive reinforcement, the intervention not only strengthened subjective norms but also facilitated the translation of breastfeeding intentions into actual behaviors [38]. These findings underscore the need for multifaceted interventions that integrate family involvement and professional support to create a holistic support system for breastfeeding women. However, this study did not collect data on family members’ attitudes or norms related to breastfeeding. Future research could further explore the role of family members by evaluating their breastfeeding knowledge, attitudes, and impact on maternal practices.

Perceived behavioral control reflects an individual’s self-assessed ability to regulate and perform a specific behavior autonomously [39]. Among women undergoing cesarean section, insufficient mastery of breastfeeding techniques and inadequate coping mechanisms for managing emotional distress have been identified as key barriers to successful breastfeeding initiation and continuation [29]. Therefore, creating a supportive environment that addresses both physical comfort and psychological well-being is critical. In this study, participants in the intervention group received targeted educational resources, guidance on optimal breastfeeding positioning, and emotional support, all of which contributed to strengthening their perceived control over breastfeeding practices. Additionally, the intervention group demonstrated significantly higher scores in perceived behavioral control compared to the control group. These findings are consistent with previous research showing that interventions implemented during both the prenatal and postnatal periods are effective in enhancing maternal behavioral control and promoting exclusive breastfeeding.

While the intervention significantly improved maternal attitudes toward breastfeeding and enhanced behavioral intentions, a critical challenge remains in translating these intentions into sustained breastfeeding behavior. Previous research suggests that intention alone may not be sufficient to predict long-term breastfeeding success, as factors such as perceived behavioral control and social support play a crucial role in the initiation and continuation of breastfeeding [40]. In our study, while the intervention group demonstrated positive changes in attitudes and intentions, the transition from intention to action-particularly exclusive breastfeeding-requires continued support beyond the hospital setting. Strengthening this transition involves addressing the barriers mothers may encounter post-discharge, including lack of breastfeeding confidence, physical discomfort, and inadequate social support. Continued promotion of mother-infant bonding through daily skin-to-skin contact, infant-led feeding, and reciprocal emotional responsiveness may be critical in maintaining breastfeeding momentum during the early postpartum period. Evidence shows that consistent follow-up care and peer support programs are effective in bridging the gap between intention and behavior [41].

In this study, while the intervention showed promising results at three days postpartum, future research should focus on long-term strategies to provide ongoing encouragement and practical assistance. One approach could involve integrating digital tools, such as breastfeeding apps or telehealth consultations, to offer continuous support and reinforce positive breastfeeding behaviors [42]. Embedding features that promote mother-infant interaction could further support maternal engagement. Additionally, enhancing partners’ and family members’ involvement in the breastfeeding process can also facilitate a smoother transition from intention to action [43]. Research highlights the importance of involving fathers and other family members in the educational process to create a supportive environment for breastfeeding mothers. Therefore, future interventions should consider expanding the role of family and community networks in providing comprehensive, long-term breastfeeding support.

Strengths and limitations

This study employed a theory-based intervention rooted in the Theory of Planned Behavior and the Maternal-Infant Interaction Model, providing a robust framework for addressing the unique challenges associated with breastfeeding after cesarean section. It focuses on an underrepresented population and offers a scalable, sustainable approach to improving breastfeeding outcomes by integrating psychological support with practical education. However, this study has several limitations. First, the potential for contamination remained in the control group, as all participants were enrolled in the same obstetrics department, which may have influenced the results. Additionally, the study was conducted at a single center, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Future interventions of this nature should consider adopting a multi-center approach to enhance the external validity of the results. Furthermore, this study only assessed breastfeeding outcomes up to three days postpartum, without follow-up beyond that period. Future research should focus on conducting longitudinal studies to track breastfeeding behaviors over an extended period, providing a more comprehensive understanding of the intervention’s long-term effects.

Conclusions

The theory-based intervention was shown to have a positive impact on increasing exclusive breastfeeding rates, and improving breastfeeding behavioral intentions among women undergoing cesarean sections. Grounded in the TPB, the intervention positively influenced attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control, key factors in shaping breastfeeding intentions. Additionally, the breastfeeding interaction theory highlights the importance of the mother-infant-social environment relationship, which our intervention supported through education and practical skills. These findings demonstrate the value of integrating TPB and ITB in designing effective interventions. Future research should prioritize strategies that promote the maintenance of long-term breastfeeding behaviors.

Quality of life of Retinoblastoma survivors in tertiary care eye hospital in South India
Authors: Krishnasri Padamandala, Geeta K Vemuganti, M Surya Durga Prasad, Santosh G Honavar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Purpose:With the increased survival of retinoblastoma (RB) patients, it is important to evaluate the quality of life (QoL) of RB survivors as well as caregivers to provide comprehensive care to the children and caregivers. This study aims to assess the QoL of survivors of RB, as perceived by parents and the self-report by patients, through a pediatric QoL (PEDs-QoL) questionnaire.Methods:The study cohort included 86 RB survivors, 86 age-matched controls, and their primary carers. PedsQL 4.0 generic core scale and structured interview were administered. QoL in physical, social, emotional, and school health was evaluated and correlated with clinical and sociodemographic parameters.Results:The mean age of the RB survivors was 5.7 years with an M:F ratio of 1.1:1. Disease was bilateral in 79% of cases. About 45% (39/86) underwent enucleation, while others received combination therapy (16; 18%), chemotherapy (30; 34%), and radiation (1; 1%). As reported by parents, the QoL of physical health domain of RB survivors was 70.2 SD ± 27.8 and 96.15 ± 13 SD, emotional health was 72.1 ± 27.4 SD and 94.4 ± 12.5 SD, social health was 80.4 ± 24.9 SD and 98.6 ± 6.2 SD; and school health was 71.9 ± 2 6.5 SD and 96.1 ± 12.2 SD. As per the self-report perception, the QoL of physical health was 68.2 ± 27.8 SD and 96.2 ± 13 SD, emotional health was 66.2 ± 28.4 SD and 95.3 ± 12.5 SD, social health was 69.5 ± 24.9 SD and 98.7 ± 6.2 SD, and school health was 63.5 ± 26.5 SD and 95.1 ± 12.2 SD. There was a significant relationship between enucleation and QoL domains, where χ2 = 67.75, degrees of freedom (df) = 36, and P < 0.01. There was a significant association between vision in the better eye (6/18 or better = 8, 6/18–6/60 = 8, 3/60 or worse = 42) and QoL scores (χ2 = 95.36, df = 62, P < 0.01). There was a substantial association between socioeconomic status and QoL domains, where χ2 = 88.5, df = 56, P < 0.01.Conclusion:The results of the study showed that the QoL of parents of RB survivors and self-proxy reports were negatively affected in many ways, including physical, social, emotional, and school-related dimensions. Despite the small differences, self-proxy reports indicated a lower QoL than the parents’ group. The study findings indicate that there are notable correlations between enucleation and visual acuity less than 6/18, as well as socioeconomic status, with various aspects of QoL domains among individuals who have survived RB.

Retinoblastoma (RB) is an intraocular malignant tumor. It is a prevalent form of ocular malignancy observed primarily in children, exhibiting a high survival rate of over 95% in developed nations.[1] Nevertheless, the long-term consequences of RB and its therapeutic interventions pose a significant apprehension for individuals who have overcome the disease. Roughly, 50% of RB instances exhibit heritability, wherein the child possesses an oncogenic variant of the RB1 tumor suppressor gene in their germline. According to Künkele et al.,[1] it is common for these individuals to exhibit the development of multiple RB foci during the initial 5 years of their lives. Treatments for RB include laser treatment, freezing, brachytherapy, local and systemic chemotherapy, external beam radiation, and enucleation.[23] The quality of life (QoL) of RB patients may suffer significantly as a result of these treatments, even though these therapies have been shown to enhance the likelihood of survival for RB patients.[4]

Multiple studies have evaluated the QoL among people who have survived RB. A study conducted in the Netherlands found that RB survivors reported a high QoL, with certain aspects such as “mood and emotions” and “autonomy” being reported as superior to the general Dutch population.[2] Nevertheless, a separate investigation conducted by Batra et al. (2016) revealed that RB survivors exhibited a lower level of performance in the school health domain when compared to their counterparts. Batra et al. (2015) revealed that individuals who have survived RB exhibit elevated levels of emotional disabilities and experience limitations in their daily activities. The mental health domain exhibited similar outcomes in a study conducted by Friedman et al. (2016)[5] among adult survivors of RB.

As per the research conducted by Friedman et al.,[6] an essential factor to take into account in RB survivors is vision-targeted health-related QoL (HRQoL). Data are scarce on the long-term HRQoL specifically related to vision in adult survivors of RB. Nevertheless, it has been established that visual impairment can have a substantial impact on HRQoL specifically related to vision in individuals who have survived for an extended period.[5] Few studies have examined RB survivors’ QoL and ability to engage in everyday activities.[78] The availability of data from emerging nations is limited. HRQoL may be affected differentially in both low- and middle-income regions due to factors such as terminal illnesses at diagnosis, reduced eye salvage prospects, and poor acceptability of enucleation.[9] Even though the overall survival rates for RB have increased, the late effects and impact on survivors’ QoL continue to be major concerns. Additional investigation is warranted to enhance comprehension and tackle the physical, psychologic, and social dimensions of QoL experienced by individuals who have survived RB. Similarly, the present study also evaluates the QoL of RB patients specifically in low- and middle-income countries.

Methods

The present study was a cross-sectional, observational investigation conducted for 1 year from September 2021 to September 2022 at a tertiary care hospital located in South India after obtaining approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of University of Hyderabad -Institutional ethics Committee (UOH IEC) (IEC number- Uoh/IEC/2021/146). Consent of parents and assent from children below 12 years were obtained. Children and adolescents who had completed treatment for RB and were between the ages of 2 and 18 years, with a minimum of 2 years since treatment completion were considered eligible for participation in this research study. The interval of 2 years following the completion of treatment was chosen to minimize the potential impact of treatment-related complications on the participant’s responses to the questionnaire. The control group consisted of age-matched individuals who visited the comprehensive outpatient department. Neither of the parents was invited to participate in the study. Participants who fell outside the predetermined age range, individuals who lacked the cognitive ability to comprehend the study questionnaire, and participants whose guardians refused to provide their assent for the investigation were excluded from participating in the study. The researchers utilized the standardized PedsQL (version 17) 4.0 Generic Core Scale to measure and evaluate QoL. The parental assessments for toddlers, aged 2–4 years, consisted of a total of 21 items. In contrast, the assessments for children and parents in the age range of 5–18 years included 23 items, encompassing four fundamental dimensions: physical (eight items), emotional (five items), social (five items), and school functioning (three items for toddlers and five items for older children). The items were assessed using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 0 (Never) to 4 (Almost always).[10] The self-report of young children, specifically those aged 5–7 years, was assessed using a 3-point Likert scale. The scale ranged from 0 (indicating “Not at all”) to 2 (indicating “Sometimes”) and 4 (indicating “A lot”). The scores were first transformed to a numerical scale ranging from 0 to 100. The components were then inverted and linearly translated to a scale spanning from 0 to 100, with values of 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4 corresponding to 100, 75, 50, 25, and 0, respectively.[10]

Higher scores indicated better QoL. The average mean scores for the respective domain were obtained in both the case and control groups. Respondents were required to submit responses for at least 50% of the questions in each dimension to determine the mean score. Participants who did not satisfy this condition were removed from the research. Based on the preferences, surveys were collected in Hindi, English, and Telugu. To mitigate potential bias in individual responses, interviews were conducted separately for children and their parents. The study ensured preservation of privacy and confidentiality.

The data were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 25.0. The researchers computed average mean scores and standard deviations (SDs) for the dimensions of physical, social, emotional, and school functioning. The statistical analysis involved the comparison of data between the survivors and control groups using the Student’s t-test. The study evaluated the impact of demographic characteristics, financial status, laterality of the disease, period since diagnosis, type of treatment, and vision status on the prediction of QoL using statistical tests such as Chi-square and Student’s t-test. In this case–control study, the odds ratio (OR) was also analyzed.

With regard to the variable “Removal of the eye,” a statistically significant difference was observed (χ2 = 50.43, degrees of freedom [df] = 3, P < 0.01). Out of the cases involving RB, a total of 47 individuals (54.7%) had undergone the surgical procedure of eye removal, known as enucleation. In contrast, all individuals in the control group (100%) maintained their eyes without undergoing this procedure. Among the cases of RB examined, it was found that 25 cases (29.1%) involved enucleation of the right eye, 13 cases (15.1%) involved enucleation of the left eye, and one case (1.2%) involved enucleation of both eyes.

There was a significant difference observed between cases and controls (χ2 = 102.7, df = 4, P < 0.01) in the variable “Type of treatment.” Out of the cases involving RB, a total of 16 cases (18.6%) were treated using a combination of therapeutic interventions, while 30 cases (34.9%) were subjected to chemotherapy and only one case (1.2%) received radiation therapy. In contrast, the entirety of the control group (100%) was either provided with spectacle correction (41 cases, 47.7%) or exhibited normal vision (45 cases, 52.3%). The variable “Zone” about geographic distribution exhibited a statistically significant disparity (χ2 = 107.9, df = 5, P < 0.01). The distribution of RB cases across the zones exhibited an uneven pattern, with the South zone having the highest number of cases (17 cases, accounting for 19.8% of the total). The remaining zones exhibited comparatively lower incidences, with 11 cases (12.8%) recorded in the Central zone, 11 cases (12.8%) in the North zone, 15 cases (17.4%) in the East zone, and 29 cases (33.7%) in the West zone. Furthermore, a total of three cases, accounting for 3.5% of the overall reported cases, were documented within the international zone.

A statistically significant difference was observed for the variable “Community setting” (χ2 = 32.83, df = 2, P < 0.01). The incidence of RB was found to be significantly higher in rural settings, accounting for 37 cases (43.0%), whereas the control group predominantly consisted of individuals from urban areas (80 cases, 93.0%). One control participant, representing 1.2% of the total sample, was categorized as residing in an unspecified community setting referred to as “Others.” A statistically significant difference was observed between RB cases and controls concerning the variable “Internet access” (χ2 = 24.78, df = 2, P < 0.01) [Table 1].

Discussion

HRQoL refers to a multidomain concept that encompasses the survivor’s overall perception of how disease and treatment impact various aspects of their life, including physical, psychological, and social domains.[1112] The use of this tool to assess outcomes in cancer survivors is becoming more widely accepted. It is crucial to assess the impact of HRQoL on survivors of RB, considering the high cure rates in children and various factors that may influence it.[1013] This could aid in identifying areas where remedial measures may be necessary to optimize HRQoL. Data are scarce and with conflicting findings regarding HRQoL in this particular cohort.[10] The variation in assessment tools, differing perspectives between parents and children regarding QoL, and the diverse demographic profile of the study population may contribute to these findings. In a recent literature review conducted by Belson et al.,[14] it was found that only five out of 15 eligible studies reported a compromised HRQoL in RB survivors when compared to controls or the general population. They also stressed the importance of conducting additional research in this field to better understand the factors that influence it.

In this study, individuals who had survived RB reported lower scores in all psychosocial and physical health summaries compared to their controls and also their parents, though the difference is minimal than parent-reported scores. This finding aligns with a study conducted in China[15] that assessed the QoL in children who underwent enucleation for RB. However, Batra et al.[2] have documented the preservation of physical functioning in survivors of RB, which was comparable to individuals without the condition. On the contrary, a study conducted by van Dijk et al.[16] in the Netherlands has reported a higher HRQoL among RB survivors when compared to a reference group of Dutch individuals. Notably, this study revealed that children and adolescents who had survived RB exhibited improved “moods and emotions” and perceived themselves as more autonomous than their healthy counterparts.[16] The conflicting outcomes may be attributed to several factors, such as early detection of the disease, which necessitates fewer disfiguring treatments, the maintenance of a positive body image, greater cultural acceptance, and a multidisciplinary approach to management involving counselors and psychologists, thereby enhancing the coping mechanisms of cancer patients within the Dutch cohort.

Our study results revealed a statistically significant relationship between family income and cases and controls of RB (χ2 = 51.69, df = 5, P < 0.01). Out of the 86 cases analyzed in the study about RB, it was found that 15 cases (17.4%) had a monthly family income below 10,000 INR, 46 cases (53.5%) had an income ranging from 10,000 to 50,000, five cases (5.8%) had an income between 50,001 and 100,000 INR, and five cases (5.8%) had an income exceeding 100,000 INR. In addition, it is worth noting that out of the total number of cases, two cases (2.3%) were identified as belonging to a dependent family, while 13 cases (15.1%) did not provide information regarding their family income. All the controls included in the study had an income exceeding 10,000 INR. Specifically, 23 controls (26.7%) fell within the income range of 10,000–50,000, 36 controls (41.9%) had an income between 50,001 and 100,000 INR, and 13 controls (15.1%) reported earnings above 100,000 INR. Furthermore, a total of 14 controls, accounting for 16.3% of the sample, opted not to provide information regarding their family income. Whereas the standard treatment cost for RB treatment may vary between 1 lakh and 1.5 lakh (INR) each year till the treatment completion and for severe cases, it may go up to 8–10 lakhs as per the current hospital and it may vary with the condition of the patient. This study results support Altice et al.[17] who reported that cancer patients often experience financial difficulties while availing the treatment.

In our investigation, the physical and school components were most significantly impacted. Most daily activities were difficult for the survivors to do, according to their reports, which eventually led to absenteeism in school because of doctor visits due to ill health. A notable disparity was observed concerning the variable “type of school,” with a Chi-square value of 71.31, a df of 13, and a P value less than 0.01. The cases of RB showed a range of school affiliations, with one case (1.2%) enrolled in a daycare facility, 15 cases (17.4%) attending government schools, 35 cases (40.7%) enrolled in private schools, and another 35 cases (40.7%) not currently enrolled in any educational institution. Within the control group, it was observed that 11 individuals (12.8%) received care at daycare facilities, three individuals (3.5%) were enrolled in government schools, 68 individuals (79.1%) attended private schools, and four controls (4.7%) were not enrolled in any educational institution.

This could be connected to the visual impairment caused by the disease. Except for sleep troubles and anxieties about the future, RB survivors reported a lower emotional dimension. This was consistent with the results of an Italian population-based study[15] that also revealed emotional problems among pediatric cancer survivors. In addition to greater bullying by others, RB survivors had trouble playing with other children, finding friends, and getting along with their classmates. One of the causes of greater bullying and disappointment among survivors may be perceived as low self-esteem and a poor body image.

Batra et al.[18] revealed a considerably hindered social domain in RB survivors, which is consistent with other studies. Except for heightened mental issues including anxiety, loss of control, and depression, which have been linked to childhood bullying and an increased feeling of subjective impairment in the past, van Dijk et al.’s[19] analysis of QoL of adult RB survivors revealed a generally positive QoL. The removal of the eye, known as enucleation, is occasionally required when treating RB. The QoL of adult RB survivors was negatively affected by enucleation, according to qualitative research.[20] These patients reported increased worry, sadness, and a sense of helplessness after the procedure. Therefore, it is crucial to treat these difficulties quickly, so that survivors may acquire effective coping mechanisms.

The major findings of our study are as follows. Our study discovered significant drop in QoL among RB survivors compared to age-matched controls, indicating associations between physical, emotional, school, and social functioning. Alongside School absenteeism and difficulty in memorizing things also school were the most effected domains among the study population. Despite comparable overall HRQoL to controls, RB survivors’ parents reported adverse impacts across multiple domains. Visual impairment and enucleation significantly affected survivors’ QoL, highlighting the need for multidisciplinary rehabilitation. Communicating these challenges to caregivers and executing educational aids may improve school health. In the present study, the mean age of children at the time of interview was 5.6 years ± 3.4 SD. We reviewed our data and found that the enucleation done at the time of diagnosis mostly was at a mean age of 3.2 years. This may also impact on QoL of the child as possibly, on early presentation, the child may not recollect the stress and anxiety it went through. Regular assessment of QoL is vital for addressing survivors’ ongoing challenges in education and employment.

Study limitations

One limitation of our study is the potential for selection bias, as we excluded participants who were unable to answer the questionnaire. Another drawback of this study is that age-matched controls were used, which may not fully account for potential differences in socioeconomic and cultural environments between survivors and controls. In addition, the inclusion of an equal number of controls may have introduced some variability. The downside of our study is that it primarily focused on younger patients, which means that we were unable to explore specific adolescent issues that may extend beyond the scope of the questionnaire. A disadvantage of this study is the limited sample size due to time constraints and coronavirus disease 2019 protocols. Since all children undergo examination under anesthesia (EUA) at the time of diagnosis, we did not specifically look into the data of subsequent EUA. As per Feng et al.,[21] general anesthesia exposure in early childhood is associated with later developmental delay. So, this may act as a confounder of the study. As the QoL associated with eyesight may lead to different conclusions, we have solely utilized pediatric QoL data, which could be a future scope of the study.

Future scope

The study findings have revealed that emotional QoL is likely to be affected after experiencing school and physical challenges. This highlights the need for continuous monitoring of both physical and psychological well-being, along with the importance of financial planning for the future. In the future, optometrists and ophthalmologists will approach this case with a greater emphasis on clinical analysis rather than psychological interpretation. This opens up opportunities for extensive research and the development of psychological interventions to be explored within this field of study. At some point, caregivers of RB survivors will face additional challenges beyond financial support. Their QoL will be significantly impacted by the costs of housing, food, and language learning—all necessities for survival. Comprehensive research on enhancements in vision-related QoL will be necessary in the future. The improvement of the general well-being and contentment of people with visual impairments will be the focus of these investigations. Assessing the effects of this progress on the bigger picture of HRQoL will also become more important.

Conclusion

The study’s findings indicate that RB survivors’ parents’ QoL and self-proxy reports were severely impacted in a variety of areas, including the physical, social, emotional, and educational aspects. Despite the few variations, self-proxy assessments showed a worse QoL than the parents’ group. Based on the study results, enucleation and visual acuity less than 6/18, as well as socioeconomic level, are significantly correlated with different QoL categories among those who have survived RB.

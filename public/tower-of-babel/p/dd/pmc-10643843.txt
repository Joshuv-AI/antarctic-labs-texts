Factors influencing work engagement among male nurses: A structural equation model
Authors: Chao Wu, Si‐zhe Cheng, Jing Wu, Yin‐juan Zhang, Ya‐wei Lin, Lu Li, Juan Du, Yu‐hai Zhang, Hong‐juan Lang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo investigate the male nurses' engagement in nursing work and the influencing factors.DesignA cross‐sectional study.MethodsA cross‐sectional survey was conducted in 13 hospitals in China to collect data from 328 male nurses from August to October 2021. A structural equation model design was tested with validated measurement instruments.ResultsThe work engagement of male nurses in China was at a medium level. There was a high correlation of the male nurses' work engagement with career identity (r = 0.764, p < 0.01), career success (r = 0.735, p < 0.01) and social support (r = 0.477, p < 0.01). The linear multivariate regression analysis showed that career satisfaction (β = 0.393), career identity (β = 0.392) and subjective support (β = 0.116) entered regression equation of male nurses' work engagement (all p < 0.05). The structural equation model results showed that career identity and social support were the influencing factors of the male nurses' work engagement and the model explained variation of 66.4% variables.ConclusionTo know the male nurses' work engagement and influencing factors in China has certain enlightening significance for male nurses themselves and nursing manager. On the one hand, it is necessary to enhance the male nurses' career recognition to nursing work. On the other hand, the society should provide more aid and support to the male nurses. Therefore, the male nurses could devote more into nursing work and promote the nursing development.Keywords: career identity, male nurses, social support, structural equation model, work engagement

INTRODUCTION

In recent years, with the rapid development of the China's economy, the medical and health services in China had made great progress (Hu et al., 2017; Husain & Bloom, 2020). The number of male nurses among the nursing team was increasing. Since the male nurses took a great proportion in the nursing team and they were playing a prominent role, the hospitals were attaching more and more importance to the male nurses (Pan et al., 2017). The male nurses had the advantages of quick thinking, strong stereotactic thinking and strong emergency response capability. Especially in terms of critically ill patients, the male nurses were generally more able to endure hardship than female counterparts (Mao et al., 2021). In emergencies, male nurses would often be calm in the face of danger. They would have strong rational judgement ability and take holistic approaches in dealing with matter. These were the advantages that female nurses could not be compared with (Zhang & Tu, 2020). Besides, in the face of reproductive‐related diseases, the male nurses would better meet the needs of male patients, especially the patients in urology. Therefore, from the perspective of the patients' needs, individualized care and patients' privacy protection, the entire society need male nurses (Zhang et al., 2019).

Considering that the male nurses had so many advantages, their engagement into nursing work, to a large extent, influenced the play of their unique advantages. The work engagement referred to one's psychological identification with work and he/she regarded the work performance as the reflection of one's values (Calvo et al., 2021). Good work engagement was conductive to improving individual work satisfaction, achieving better work resources and reducing turnover intentions, as well as improving the overall work performance (van den Berg et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2021). At present, the research targets of work engagement were concentrated on hotel employees (Jung et al., 2021), tele‐psychiatrists (Mahmoud et al., 2021) and health professionals (Noblet et al., 2017). Researches on male nurses were inadequate. Therefore, it was necessary and urgent to do researches on male nurses' work engagement and influencing factors so as to provide corresponding guidance and better promote their advantages in clinical nursing work.

Researches showed that the work engagement was influenced by a lot of factors, mainly into two categories, namely internal factors and external factor (Mao et al., 2020). The internal factors contained regulatory emotional self‐efficacy (Lan et al., 2020), job satisfaction (Zhang et al., 2020) and performance feedback (Vieira Dos Santos et al., 2021), while the external factors contained job resources (Mukaihata et al., 2020), workplace bullying (Chan et al., 2020) and work overload (García‐Iglesias et al., 2021). However, there was a lack of research on the current situation of the work engagement of the Chinese male nurses and the research on the influencing factors of their work engagement.

Due to the influence of traditional Chinese cultural concepts, self‐awareness, social status, income level and other factors, although the demand for male nurses in the society was increasing and the male nurses were valued by nursing mangers to a certain extent, male nurses were still confronted with a lot of problems such as low professional identity, high psychological pressure, lack of career planning and high turnover rate (Kim & Lee, 2017; Martínez‐Morato et al., 2021). Therefore, in this research, taking into consideration the male nurses' particularity and the special Chinese cultural background, in the process of exploring the influencing factors of male nurses' work engagement, career identity and career success were included into internal factors and social support were included into external factors. By paying attention to the male nurses' self‐identity with nursing work and their career success, as well as the social support to their work of external environment such as family, hospital and society, this paper explored the influencing factors to the male nurses' work engagement from both internal and external aspects. The aim was to enrich the researches on male nurses and fulfil the large gap in China's nursing industry, giving full play to the male nurses' advantages and stabilize the male nurse team.

METHODS

Design and sample

A descriptive, cross‐sectional design was used to collect survey data from male nurses. According to the calculation, the sample size was at least five times of the number of items in the questionnaire. Therefore, the calculation formula of sample size was N = (10 + 9 + 11 + 30) × 5 = 300, which meant that at least 300 subjects were required for this study (Wu et al., 2020). From August to October 2021, we contacted with the admin office of the hospital and with their permission and under their help, we adopted the convenient sampling method. 335 male nurses from five tertiary hospitals, six secondary hospitals and two primary hospitals in Shaanxi, Shanxi, Yunnan and Hainan province of China were recruited. According to voluntary principle, 328 male nurses participated in the survey. The digital version of the questionnaire was issued to the participants. Among the 328 participants, 10 dropped out of the research and 12 did not complete the questionnaire. Eventually, 306 questionnaires were valid, with an effective response rate of 93.29%.

Ethical considerations

Ethical guidelines described in the Helsinki Declaration were followed in this study (Halonen et al., 2020). Since there were no human clinical trails or animal experiments in this study, no unethical behaviours would be involved. Although this study was a human‐related study, it was only a questionnaire survey and would not cause harm to the subjects, so it was exempt from ethical approval after the discussion of the Ethics Committee of Air Force Medical University. Before the investigation, purpose of the study was explained to the participants and verbal consent was retrieved from the participants. Besides, the participants also signed the informed consent form. In the process of the investigation, participants could terminate and withdraw from the investigation at any time and the questionnaire was completed anonymously. To protect confidentiality, no identification information of participants was collected. And they were assured that their information would only be used for research. In addition, the score of the questionnaire would have no effect on their work.

Variables and measurement

The online survey included one self‐design questionnaire to collect general information and four standardized questionnaires to study the work engagement of the male nurses in China and the influencing factors. All measures had reported acceptable reliability for use in other health care populations. Descriptions of the measures included Cronbach's alphas obtained in this study.

Work engagement

To measure work engagement, the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale (UWES) was used (Song et al., 2021). This scale included three dimensions, namely vigour, dedication and absorption, and nine items. Vigour meant that an individual was energetic and persistent, willing to work hard and overcome difficulties with perseverance. Dedication meant that the individual had a strong sense of meaning and pride, and was full of enthusiasm for work so that he/she could devote himself/herself for the work and be able to confront the challenges. The absorption meant that the individual could concentrate him/ her on the work and enjoy the work so that he/ she was reluctant from the work. The scale adopted the 7‐point scoring method. The Cronbach's alphas coefficient of this scale in this research was 0.929, while that of each dimension ranged from 0.757 to 0.881.

Career identity

The Nursing Career Identity Scale (NCIS) was used to measure the career identity (ten Hoeve et al., 2014). This scale included five dimensions and 30 items. The five dimensions were career cognition evaluation, social support in career, social skills in career, coping with career setbacks and self‐reflection on career. The scale adopted the 5‐point scoring method. The Cronbach's alphas coefficient of this scale in this research was 0.974 while that of each dimension ranged from 0.772 to 0.928.

Career success

The Career Success Scale (CSS) was used to measure the career success (Li et al., 2014). This scale included two dimensions and 11 items. The two dimensions were career satisfaction and career competition. The scale adopted the 5‐point scoring method. The Cronbach's alphas coefficient of this scale in this research was 0.934 while that of each dimension were 0.931 and 0.896, respectively.

Social support

The Social Support Rating Scale (SSRS) was used to measure social support (Hou et al., 2020). This scale included three dimensions and 10 items. The three dimensions were subjective support, objective support and support utilization. Items 1–4 and 8–10 of this scale adopted 4‐point scoring method while other items adopted cumulative scoring method. The Cronbach's alphas coefficient of this scale in this research was 0.829, while that of each dimension ranged from 0.742 to 0.808.

Statistical analysis

We adopted SPSS 26.0 and Mplus 8.3 to analysis the collected data. All variables were described by descriptive statistics. Independent t‐test and analysis of variance were conducted to compare participants' work engagement and its three dimensions by general demographic information and related characteristics. Pearson's correlation analysis was used to explore the correlation among male nurses' work engagement, career identity, career success and social support. Influencing factors of male nurses' work engagement and its three dimensions were identified by a stepwise multiple liner regression. The structural equation model was adopted to analyse the influencing factors of work engagement. The measurement and structural models were performed by Mplus 8.3. All statistical analysis was two‐sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

This study investigated the male nurses' work engagement in China and the influencing factors of their work engagement so as to better promote the male nurses' enthusiasm for nursing work and give full play to their advantages. The result showed that in the first 3 years when the male nurses took the post, the scores of work engagement, vigour, absorption and dedication were relatively high. However, with the extension of time, the score decreased in the 3rd to 5th years, and then rose with the increase of their work years and age. This may be due to the high enthusiasm for work at the start of their work, but then affected by various aspects such as self‐recognition, traditional concepts in the society and occupational pressure, etc., their work engagement increased gradually along with the understanding of the nursing career and better adaptation to nursing work (Cziraki et al., 2018). Besides, the work engagement of the male nurses in hospital of different grade were different (p < 0.01). The work engagement of the male nurses in class‐III hospitals was higher than that in class‐II hospitals while the work engagement of the male nurses in class‐II hospitals was higher than that in class‐I hospitals. The reasons may be that the higher the grade of hospitals were, the higher the work load were and the higher the proportion of the male nurses were, so that more attention were attached to the male nurses. Therefore, the work engagement of the male nurses in higher grade hospitals was higher than their counterparts in lower grade hospitals (Appiah et al., 2021; He et al., 2017). Support from relatives and friends influenced the male nurses' work engagement. Same as Cao's research on nurses (Cao & Chen, 2019), the research showed that the more support the male nurses got from relatives and friends, the higher their work engagement was. If relatives and friends did not support or objected, the male nurses' work engagement would be low. If the male nurses received support from families, they would be motivated so they could better devote to their work (Descals‐Tomás et al., 2021). The above prompted us that it was necessary to pay attention to whether the male nurses got social support.

Through analysis of influencing factors and regression equation model, the results showed that career identity was an influencing factor of the male nurses' work engagement. Career identity referred to the recognition of the nature and content, social value and personal significance of a certain occupational activity in the process of long‐term engagement of the occupation (Cao & Chen, 2019; Noble et al., 2019). The research showed that good career identity was conducive to promoting the male nurses' occupational life and reducing their occupational burnout and demission rate. It was precisely due to their recognition to nursing work that the male nurses had the passion for their occupation and were more devoted to their work (Sun et al., 2022).

Therefore, managers should help the male nurses to clarify their career plans and enhance their occupational confidence so as to promote their career identity (Yarbrough et al., 2017). As for the male nurses, they should also strengthen their recognition to work. They should realize that they were different from their female counterparts and they had unique advantages. For example, in dealing with emergencies, the male nurses could often think rationally with reasonable logics and calmly execute with professional skills. Besides, the male nurses often had their own advantages over females in terms of physiology and psychology, such as their greater strength and durability of physical strength (Arif & Khokhar, 2017). By enhancing their recognition to the nursing career could the male nurses better engage in their work (Sohrabizadeh & Sayfouri, 2014).

Social support was another important factor which influenced the male nurses' work engagement. Social support referred to the sum total of various types of support from the external environment that an individual perceived (Wawrzynski et al., 2021) The research showed that good social support could help individuals reduce psychological stress level, relieve tension state and improve social adaptability (Deming & Vasterling, 2017). By single factor analysis, it was found that the more support the male nurses got from their relatives and friends, the higher score of work engagement and the three dimensions, namely vigour, absorption and dedication would be. Besides, social support and its three dimensions were significantly positively correlated with work engagement and its three dimensions. In the structural equation model, social support was an influencing factor of work engagement, which was consistent with the findings of Minghui et al. (2018) on the influence of teachers' work engagement in China. Therefore, it was needed to give full play to social support. Nursing managers should build an incentive mechanism to improve the male nurses' salary, which was also conducive to enhancing the family's support for male nurses, and further helped improve the male nurses' career identity (Arreciado Marañón et al., 2019). Families of the male nurses should fully understand male nurses' work and abandon their traditional bias so as to recognize and offer support to the male nurse (Erum et al., 2021), which would help strengthen the male nurses' work engagement.

Career success did not enter in either the multiple linear regression equation and structural equation model of the male nurses' work engagement. The reason may be that career success was influenced by many factors (Wu et al., 2022), and individual career success did not necessarily imply high work engagement. To sum up, this research analysed the influencing factors of male nurses' work engagement in China from both internal and external aspects. The result showed that the male nurses' individual recognition to nursing and social support were important influencing factors of their work engagement and the model explained the variation of 66.4% dependent variables. Hence, to boost the male nurses' work engagement in China, focus could be given to the above two factors.

LIMITATIONS

There were some limitations in this study that need to be taken into considered. First, this study was a cross‐sectional one and adopted the convenience sampling method. So male nurses in hospitals in parts of China were selected as the research objects. Hence, the research samples were limited. Secondly, this study utilized self‐assessment questionnaire which had a certain degree of subjectivity. Finally, although this study focused on the association of work engagement with career identity and social support, other factors were still needed to be explored and researched.

CONCLUSIONS

In this study, it was found that the male nurses' work engagement in China was at intermediate level. The linear multivariate regression analysis showed that career satisfaction (β = 0.393), career identity (β = 0.392) and subjective support (β = 0.116) entered regression equation of male nurses' work engagement (all p < 0.05). The structural equation model results showed that career identity and social support were the influencing factors of the male nurses' work engagement and the model explained the variation of 66.4% variables. To better play the male nurses' unique advantages, it was necessary to pay attention to their own recognition to nursing work and the social support they got, which in turn would be beneficial to promote their work engagement.

RELEVANCE TO CLINICAL PRACTICE

To know the male nurses' work engagement and influencing factors in China has certain enlightening significance for male nurses themselves and nursing manager. It is necessary to enhance the male nurses' career recognition to nursing work and the society should provide more aid and support to the male nurses. Therefore, the male nurses could devote more into nursing work and promote the nursing development.

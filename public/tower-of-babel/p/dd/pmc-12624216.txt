The association of pre-diabetes with significant coronary artery disease and ischemic mitral regurgitation in patients with non-ST-segment elevation acute coronary syndrome
Authors: Maryam Nabati, Younes Zeytounli, Farima Sadat Mousavi, Jamshid Yazdani, Homa Parsaee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPre-diabetes is associated with an increased risk of developing cardiovascular disease (CVD). We aimed to investigate the association between pre-diabetes and the severity of coronary artery disease, along with other high-risk markers—such as ischemic mitral regurgitation (MR)—in non-diabetic patients hospitalized with NSTE-ACS.MethodsThis study was a case control study on 200 non-diabetic patients including equal size of consecutive pre-diabetic and normoglycemic individuals with NSTE-ACS. All patients underwent an echocardiography to determine the left ventricular ejection fraction, prevalence and existence of ischemic mitral regurgitation (MR), and right ventricular size and function. After that, coronary angiography (CAG) was performed to assess the significance of coronary artery disease (CAD) and to calculate the SYNTAX score.Resultspre-diabetic patients were older and had higher prevalence of hypertension than normoglycemic group (P value<0.001 and 0.001, respectively). Also, significant CAD (86 % vs. 74 %, P value = 0.034) and severe ischemic MR (16 % vs. 6 %, P value = 0.024) were more prevalent in pre-diabetic patients than in normoglycemic patients, whereas the absence of MR was more common in normoglycemic patients (21 % vs. 3 %, P value <0.001). The logistic regression analysis showed the association between significant CAD and pre-diabetes was independent of other variables (P value = 0.049, odds ratio [OR]: 2.402, 95 % CI: 1.005–5.741).ConclusionsIn our study, severe CAD and ischemic MR were more prevalent in pre-diabetic patients than normoglycemic ones that may be indicative of pre-diabetes as a high risk state in NSTE-ACS.

Background

‘Pre-diabetes’ is a general term indicating an intermediate stage in which blood glucose level is between normal glucose tolerance (NGT) and overt type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), and is identified as a hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) level of 5.7 to less than 6.5 [1]. These individuals are usually at high risk for developing T2DM. Multiple metabolic abnormalities can emerge before the onset of diabetes and may increase the risk of comorbidities and chronic complications that are typically considered consequences of diabetes mellitus (DM). Pre-diabetes is also associated with a greater risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) compared with a normoglycemic state, possibly because some macrovascular complications develop before the diagnosis of overt T2DM [2]. A recent study found the prevalence of pre-diabetes to be 35.7 % among the Chinese adult population, using fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c for the definition [3]. Pre-diabetes is associated with an approximately 20 % increase in the risk of developing CVD [2]. Prediabetes appears to be positively associated with coronary artery disease (CAD), with some studies suggesting a threefold higher risk compared with normoglycemic individuals. Although the link between prediabetes and CAD severity has been explored, few studies have combined echocardiographic and angiographic assessments in ACS patients, particularly in Iranian populations, specifically those with non–ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome (NSTE-ACS). NSTE-ACS includes a spectrum of heterogeneous disorders incorporating different subgroups of patients with low or high risk of atherosclerotic severity and mortality [4]. Therefore, we investigated the association between pre-diabetes, determined by HbA1c levels, and coronary artery disease severity, along with other high-risk markers—such as atherosclerotic burden and ischemic mitral regurgitation (MR)—using both echocardiographic and angiographic assessments in non-diabetic Iranian patients hospitalized with NSTE-ACS.

Methodology

This study was a nested case control study on 200 non-diabetic patients with NSTE-ACS who were admitted to emergency department of our hospital between 2023 and 2024. Sample study included equal size of consecutive pre-diabetic and normoglycemic NSTE-ACS individuals. This study was conducted based on the guidelines of Helsinki Declaration and was approved by the ethic committee of our university (ethics code no. IR.MAZUMS.REC.1401.14733, IRB approval date: 2022-11-1, clinical trial number: not applicable). All patients signed an informed consent. Diagnosis of NSTE-ACS was based on 2020 European Society of Cardiology Guidelines. Patients were entered into the study if they had transient ST-segment depression (≥0.5 mm) or marked T-wave inversion during anginal episode. Also, patients with symptoms consistent with ischemia but without significant ST-T changes were included. Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) was diagnosed based on the presence of angina pain and transient increase in level of cardiac troponin I (TnI) more than the upper normal limits (0.04 ng/mL). We took blood samples for measuring cardiac enzyme levels within 10 min of hospitalization and repeated the measurements every 4 to 6 h during 48 h after admission [5]. Unstable angina was considered if patients had a new or worsening angina or angina occurred at rest without any elevation in cardiac troponin level. Patients with ST-segment elevation of ≥0.1 mv in leads except aVR or V1, anterior ST depression suggestive of posterior ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), valve replacement, left bundle branch block, acute heart failure, cardiomyopathies, significant primary valvular heart diseases, collagen vascular diseases, fever, recent history of chemotherapy or radiotherapy, pregnancy, and DM were excluded from the study. Serum level of blood sugar (BS) was determined by a blood sample that was taken within 10 min of admission. Furthermore, we took the blood sample after a 10–12 h overnight fasting to determine the serum levels of cholesterol (Chol), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), and HbA1c. The ion exchange chromatography method (Bio Systems S.A, Barcelona, Spain) was utilized to determine HbA1c level.

HTN was explained as a systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or a diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg or requiring to take antihypertensive agents [6]. DM was identified according to the guidelines of the American Diabetes Association, which considered as a fasting blood glucose ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or 2-h plasma blood sugar during 75-g oral glucose tolerance test ≥200 mg/ dL (11.1 mmol/L), or a hemoglobin A1c level of ≥6.5 % (48 mmol/mol), or taking insulin or oral hypoglycemic drugs [7]. HLP was explained as the total cholesterol (Chol) levels of more than 200 mg/dL, high density lipoprotein-Chol level of less than 40 mg/dL in male, or less than 50 mg/dL in females [8]. We identified cigarette smoking by a face-to-face survey. Body mass index (BMI) was determined by dividing weight in kg by square of height in meters. History of CAD included previous history of myocardial infarction (MI), percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), coronary artery bypass graft (CABG), or having 50 % or more coronary artery narrowing in previous coronary angiograms [9].

All patients underwent coronary angiography (CAG) by a Siemens AG (Medical Solutions; Erlangen, Germany) during hospitalization. One experienced cardiologist blinded to the patient data interpreted angiograms. Significant CAD was defined as a stenosis of ≥50 % in the left main (LM) artery or ≥ 70 % in at least one major epicardial coronary artery [10].

We used the SYNTAX score for evaluating the severity, extensiveness, and complexity of CAD. We added up these individual scores for each separate lesion by a SYNTAX score algorithm available on the website of SYNTAX (http://www.Syntaxscore.com) to calculate the total SYNTAX score [11]. A higher SYNTAX score is suggestive of a more complex circumstance and an unfavorable prognosis among patients who underwent revascularization [12].

All patients underwent an echocardiogram within 12 h of admission by a Vivid S5 (GE Healthcare, Wauwatosa, WI). We stored the images on a hard disk for performing off-line analysis by one experienced echocardiographer blinded to the patients' data. The biplane Simpson's method was used to determine left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) by subtracting left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV) from left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV) divided by LVEDV in both apical four chamber and two-chamber views [13].

Definition of ischemic (functional) MR was based on a regurgitation which occurs with a normal mitral valve structure, annular dilation and apical tethering of leaflets. Patients were divided into four group regarding MR severity: no, mild, moderate and severe MR [14]. Diagnosis of right ventricular (RV) enlargement was based on an RV basal and mid diameter of greater than 4.1 and 3.5 cm in an RV-focused four-chamber view, respectively. RV systolic dysfunction was defined as a Tricuspid Annular Plane Systolic Excursion (TAPSE) of less than 17 mm [13].

Statistical analysis

We presented continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation and categorical variables as frequency and percentile. The normality for quantitative variables was determined by the Shapiro–Wilk test. The continuous variables that were non-normally distributed were compared by the Mann-Whitney U test and were explained as the median [lower-upper quartile] and those that were normally distributed were compared by an independent t-test and were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. The categorical variables were compared by chi-square and Fisher's exact tests and manifested as the frequency and percentage. Furthermore, the logistic regression analysis was used to determine the independent association between different cardiovascular risk factors with significant CAD and ischemic MR. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

This study included 200 non-diabetic patients (age range 33 to 86 years) who were admitted at emergency room of our hospital with diagnosis of NSTE-ACS between 2023 and 2024. One hundred patients had pre-diabetes (an HbA1c level of 5.7 to less than 6.5 %) and 100 patients were normoglycemic (an HbA1c level of less than 5.7 %). Ninety two (46 %) patients had unstable angina and 108 (54 %) patients had NSTEMI. Previous history of CAD was reported in 99 (40.5 %) patients. Eighthly six (43 %) were female and 114 (57 %) were male. Among cardiovascular risk factors, the most frequent risk factor was HTN (122 patients, 61 %), that was followed by HLP (86 patients, 43 %), and smoking (41 patients, 20.5 %). The mean age of the study population was 63.43 ± 11.31 years and the mean BMI was 27.51 ± 3.62 kg/m2. Normoglycemic group were younger and had more number of males than females compared with pre-diabetic group (P value <0.001 and 0.022) and as we expected, the mean HbA1C, BS, and TG levels were higher in pre-diabetic patients than normoglycemic individuals (P value<0.001). Also, HTN was more prevalent in pre-diabetic patients than normoglycemic ones (P value = 0.001). Discussion

Our study showed among non- diabetic patients with NSTE-ACS, significant CAD was more prevalent in pre-diabetic patients than normoglycemic ones. Also, pre-diabetic patients had higher prevalence of ischemic MR in comparison with normoglycemic individuals.

NSTE-ACS accounts for almost half of CVD related mortality. Therefore, risk stratification is paramount for identifying patients at high risk for adverse outcomes because they may benefit from invasive strategies to decrease the complications [15]. Previous studies have reported that prediabetes has an independent association with CVD with an increase in the complexity and severity of CAD leading to formation of more diffuse lesions and unstable plaques [16]. It is also suggested that elevated HbA1c levels are associated with the severity of CAD, indicating that HbA1c could be considered an index of CAD severity [17].

The oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) is unreliable in the peri-infarct period due to confounding factors such as acute stress reactions. Elevated admission plasma glucose may reflect both acute stress and underlying disturbances in glucose metabolism. In contrast, HbA1c offers several advantages for diagnosing diabetes mellitus (DM) and prediabetes in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS): it does not require fasting and is less influenced by acute stress. This is because HbA1c reflects average glycemic control over the preceding three months [18]. Therefore, we used HbA1c to determine prediabetes status. Ischemic mitral regurgitation (MR) is a common complication following ACS and is associated with a threefold increased risk of heart failure, even in patients who have preserved left ventricular ejection fraction at the time of the event [19]. It is also linked to a significant increase in both morbidity and mortality. Previous studies have reported its incidence ranging from 9 to 13 % when assessed by cardiac catheterization to 12–50 % in echocardiographic studies [20]. Bursi et al. conducted a study on patients presenting with acute myocardial infarction and found a graded positive association between ischemic MR and both hypertension and diabetes mellitus. Moreover, ischemic MR was a predictor of heart failure or death among 30-day survivors [21]. In our study, prediabetic patients had a higher prevalence of ischemic MR compared with normoglycemic patients. Severe ischemic MR was also more common in the prediabetic group. This may indicate a higher risk profile associated with prediabetes compared with normoglycemia.

In 2024, AbuShady et al. conducted a study on 200 patients not known to have diabetes or prediabetes, admitted with ACS. They found pre-diabetes was relatively common in ACS setting. Compared with patients without diabetes, pre-diabetic patients had a higher BMI and a longer hospital stay and worse in-hospital clinical outcomes. However, atherosclerotic severity and ischemic MR were not evaluated in this study [22].

In 2019, Farhan et al. performed a study on 547 patients with ACS who were successfully treated by PCI to investigate the effects of pre-diabetes on characterization of coronary plaques in non-culprit lesions and ischemic outcome. Among 547 patients, 162 had normal glucose metabolism (NGM), 202 had pre-diabetes, and 183 had DM. There were no significant differences between the groups regarding intravascular ultrasound characteristics indicating vulnerable plaques. Patients with DM had a higher rate of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) than individuals with pre-diabetes or NGM. Compared with our study, they used intravascular ultrasound to determine vulnerable plaques in non-culprit lesions [23].

In 2018, Acar et al. conducted a study on 250 patients who were admitted at the first occurrence of ACS and then underwent diagnostic coronary angiography. They included all patients with unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI. The patients were divided into DM, prediabetes, and normoglycemic groups. SYNTAX and Gensini scores and three vessels disease (TVD) rates were higher in pre-diabetic patients than in non-diabetic patients, but these values had similar results in pre-diabetic and diabetic patients. [18]. In our study, only patients with NSTE-ACS including unstable angina and NSTEMI were entered.

In 2013, Kurihara et al. conducted a cross-sectional study to assess the impact of prediabetic status on coronary atherosclerosis. The study included 100 patients undergoing coronary angiography, categorized into three groups based on glucose tolerance status: normoglycemic, prediabetic, and diabetic. Coronary atherosclerosis was evaluated using quantitative coronary angiography and intravascular ultrasound. The results showed that the prediabetic group exhibited a similar extent of coronary atherosclerosis as the diabetic group, suggesting that prediabetes may be associated with an increased risk of coronary artery disease [24].

Research on MR following NSTEMI remains limited. Across the spectrum of acute coronary syndromes (ACS), the reported incidence of ischemic mitral regurgitation (IMR) varies widely. MR has been shown to increase the risk of developing heart failure by nearly threefold, independent of factors such as age, sex, initial Killip class, and left ventricular ejection fraction. In such patients, the papillary muscles, chordae tendineae, and mitral valve leaflets are structurally intact. Despite this, echocardiographic evaluation often reveals restricted leaflet mobility and incomplete coaptation. Chronic ischemic mitral regurgitation (IMR) is recognized as an independent risk factor for mortality, with 5-year survival rates reported to range between 40 % and 60 % [20].

While previous studies have established the link between prediabetes and adverse cardiovascular outcomes, our study provides additional insight by examining the prevalence and severity of ischemic mitral regurgitation in Iranian patients with NSTEMI, highlighting the elevated risk profile of prediabetic individuals in this population.

Conclusion

In our study, significant CAD and ischemic MR were more prevalent in pre-diabetic patients than normoglycemic ones that may be indicative of pre-diabetes as a high risk state in NSTE-ACS.

Limitations

This study was a single-center investigation conducted on a relatively small sample of patients with NSTE-ACS. Additionally, patients were not followed to assess early or late cardiovascular outcomes. Based on our findings, prediabetes was associated with a higher prevalence of at least one vessel with >70 % stenosis and severe ischemic mitral regurgitation; however, the overall CAD burden and complexity—as reflected by SYNTAX score and triple-vessel disease—were similar. The small sample size and lack of follow-up may have contributed to these observations and prevent definitive conclusions about the impact of prediabetes on CAD severity in broader populations.

Aqueous Humor Proteome in RVO-Associated Macular Edema: Analysis during Three Consecutive Conbercept Treatments
Authors: Shoukuan Liu, Mingxuan Zhang, Yifeng Ke, Liangzhang Tan, EmmanuelEric Pazo, Yongtao Li, Yingxiang Zhao, Xinjun Ren, Xiaorong Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Purpose: This exploratory study aimed to assess the aqueous humor (AH) proteome in patients with retinal vein occlusion (RVO) and macular edema (ME) receiving three consecutive intravitreal injections of Conbercept, to identify potential molecular pathways, and to generate hypotheses. Methods: Label-free quantitative liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry was applied to analyze the AH of patients with RVO and ME who underwent three consecutive Conbercept intravitreal injections (IVI) and age-matched controls. The alterations in BCVA, central retinal thickness (CRT), and AH proteins were ascertained through a permutation-based calculation (the false discovery rate was set at 0.05). A repeated-measures analysis determined the most significantly changed protein in the AH (α = 0.05). Results: Compared to controls, 45 proteins were regulated in the RVO cohort, with 19 and 48 proteins differentially expressed in the branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO) and central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO) subgroups, respectively. The plasma kallikrein (P = 0.0007, R = 0.775) and desmocollin-3 (P = 0.006, R = −0.673) levels were significantly correlated with CRT during treatment in the BRVO. SPARC (P = 0.007, R = −0.686), vasorin (P = 0.002, R = −0.759), desmocollin-2 (P = 0.008, R = −0.676), Neurotrimin (P = 0.007, R = −0.685), desmocollin-3 (P = 0.042, R = −0.549), fibulin-1 (P = 0.011, R = −0.656), and follistatin-related protein-1 (P = 0.048, R = −0.536) were significantly correlated with CRT during treatment, and desmocollin-2 (P = 0.025, R = −0.610), desmocollin-3 (P = 0.021, R = −0.610), plasma kallikrein (P = 0.0006, R = 0.8), and Neurotrimin (P = 0.017, R = −0.623) were significantly correlated with changes in patients’ BCVA during treatment in the CRVO. Conclusions: This exploratory proteomic analysis identified distinct pathway alterations in early RVO pathogenesismetabolic in BRVO and inflammatory in CRVOand common treatment-related changes: plasma kallikrein was downregulated, while desmocollin-3 was up-regulated.

Introduction

After diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion (RVO) ranks as the second most common retinal vascular disorder, posing a substantial risk to visual function. RVO is a predominant clinical ophthalmological disease caused by retinal vein thromboembolism, which disrupts the blood–retinal barrier (BRB) and Müller cell function, resulting in the buildup of fluid within the retinal tissue. The onset of RVO is characterized by a distortion of vision, loss of visual acuity, and the appearance of dark shadows in the visual field. In patients with mild symptoms, these changes may be unnoticeable. However, as the condition progresses, the risk of blindness gradually increases. As indicated by the site of obstruction, RVO may be categorized into two principal subtypes: central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO) and branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO). In the early phases of retinal vein occlusion (RVO), patients are likely to experience visual loss attributable to macular edema. As the disease advances, the appearance of retinal neovascularization and vitreous hemorrhage can be detected. Macular edema (ME) and neovascularization observed in RVO are caused by the ischemia-induced overexpression of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and intravitreal injections of VEGF inhibitors represent the current standard treatment modality for patients with RVO.

Conbercept is a recombinant fusion protein composed of the extracellular domains 2 of VEGF receptor (VEGFR)-1 and the extracellular domains 3 and 4 of VEGF-2. Specifically, Conbercept demonstrates a high affinity for the VEGF and is more productive than ranibizumab. Conbercept has been demonstrated to be an effective intervention for improving best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) in patients presenting with ME secondary to RVO. However, some patients experience recurrent or persistent macular edema (ME) despite intravitreal anti-VEGF. This observation indicates that, in addition to the VEGF, other mechanisms may be involved in the occurrence, development, and recurrence of RVO-induced ME.

The objective of proteomic analysis is to recognize and measure the entire collection of proteins existing in a chosen body fluid or tissue specimen. Prior research has indicated that the proteome of the aqueous humor (AH) is reflective of the severity of retinal vascular disease. Previous proteomic studies on the AH of patients have assessed patients with CRVO and BRVO and animal models of RVO with anti-VEGF drugs such as aflibercept and bevacizumab. However, there is a lack of research on proteomic changes in the AH of patients during treatment of RVO with Conbercept. Studying these changes in AH proteins in RVO patients treated with Conbercept from the onset and during treatment can help gain an in-depth understanding of the safety and efficacy of Conbercept. Furthermore, they provide an intuitive representation of the occurrence and progression of RVO and investigate potential biological indicators that indicate the progression of RVO.

Methods

Samples

The study was conducted in accordance with the Institutional Review Board of Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital, Tianjin, China, which approved the study (2023KY-14). This study is registered with Clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07106268). Informed consent was obtained from the subjects after explaining the nature and possible consequences of the study. The study adhered to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. We collected information on 380 patients with retinal vein occlusion (RVO) accompanied by macular edema who received intravitreal injections of Conbercept at the One-stop Vitreous Cavity Injection Center of Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital from March 2023 to March 2024. Nine patients in total, consisting of 4 with central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO) and 5 with branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO), were recruited for the study. Student’s t-test confirmed that there were no significant statistical differences in age between the two groups (Table S1). For the RVO group, the inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) cystic or diffuse edema in the macular region, along with tortuous dilation of the retinal vein accompanied by hemorrhage; (ii) optical coherence tomography (OCT) examination indicating elevation, edema, and thickening in the macular area; (iii) monocular disease with a baseline central retinal thickness (CRT) ≥ 250mm; (iv) no prior treatment with intravitreal injection, laser photocoagulation, or drugs for promoting blood circulation and removing blood stasis before surgery; (v) patients having normal blood pressure, negative urine protein, normal liver and kidney functions, and a normal electrocardiogram. The exclusion criteria for the RVO group were as follows: (i) patients with fluorescein fundus angiography (FFA) revealing a large nonperfusion area (10 optic disc areas) necessitating retinal photocoagulation; (ii) those with chronic dacryocystitis, glaucoma, cataract, severe proliferative vitreoretinopathy, vitreous hemorrhage, epimacular membrane, macular ischemia, diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration, ocular trauma, and other diseases; (iii) those who had received intravitreal therapy or laser photocoagulation with corticosteroids or anti-VEGF drugs within three months prior to the study. In the experimental group, aqueous humor samples were collected from patients before each intravitreal treatment (IVT, once a month), with a total of three collections. Rescue therapy was permitted if there was an inadequate treatment response, defined as a less than 10% reduction in central retinal thickness (CRT) from the previous visit or a loss of more than 5 letters in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA). In such cases, an additional injection could be administered ahead of the next scheduled monthly visit. Control samples were collected from cataract patients who were age-matched. Although cataract surgery itself can trigger a transient inflammatory response (leading to increased levels of certain cytokines such as IL-6), in eyes with uncomplicated, nonoperated cataracts, the blood-aqueous barrier is relatively intact, and the composition of aqueous humor proteins is in a relatively “resting” state. Compared with diseases like RVO, which involve ischemia, hypoxia, and significant vascular leakage, the disturbance of the proteome is not on the same scale. Using preoperative cataract patients as a control for aqueous humor protein has been widely accepted, , and no reports have indicated that cataracts themselves produce confounding effects on core indicators such as the VEGF or complement. Aqueous humor samples were acquired from these patients before surgery. The patients in the control group suffered from no eye diseases or other underlying conditions apart from cataracts. Data, including best corrected visual acuity (BCVA), were collected from the patient’s electronic medical record. BCVA was determined by employing the logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (LogMAR) chart. SS-OCT devices (DRI OCT Triton, Topcon Inc., Tokyo, Japan) were utilized to assess the severity of macular edema in patients. The evaluation was based on central retinal thickness (CRT), which is defined as the distance from the outer edge of the hyperreflective retinal pigment epithelium to the inner border of the medial limiting membrane at the center of the fovea. This measurement was carried out using the caliper tool within Topcon’s OCT software. The CRT was measured on two occasions, and the average value was computed.

All experimental procedures were conducted by Biomarker Technologies Corp. (Beijing, China).

Protein Extraction and Digestion

Samples were taken out from −80 °C and added with 300 μL of 8 M urea and 10% (v/v) protease inhibitor. Following centrifugation at 14,100g for a duration of 20 min, the supernatant was carefully retrieved. The protein concentration was determined by the Bradford method. For each sample, 50 μg of proteins were reduced with 200 mM DTT at 37 °C for 1 h, diluted 8-fold with 50 mM ABC buffer, and then digested with trypsin (1:25, w/w) at 37 °C overnight.

Protein Solution RP Separation

Digestion was terminated by adding 50 μL of 0.1% formic acid. The C18 column was pre-washed successively with 100% ACN and 0.1% formic acid. A sample (≤30 μg) was loaded and centrifuged. After multiple washes, a 10-gradient elution was performed. The eluates were pooled into three fractions per sample, lyophilized, and stored at −80 °C.

Peptide Identification by Label-Free Quantification Nano-LC–MS

The lyophilized fraction was reconstituted and centrifuged. Then, 10 μL of the supernatant was loaded and separated at a flow rate of 300 nL/min for 120 min. The mobile phases were 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and ACN (B), following a specific gradient. Label-free mass spectrometry analysis was carried out on a Thermo Orbitrap Fusion, including a full MS scan (250–1450 m/z, 120,000 resolution) and CID MS/MS scans of the 20 most intense ions. The second-stage data acquisition used the linear ion trap fast mode with specific parameters, and the data were processed with the Uniprot_HUMAN database (downloaded on April 20, 2019) by Maxquant 1.5.2.8.

Protein Identification

The identification parameters included a precursor ion mass tolerance of ±15 ppm, a fragment ion mass tolerance of ±0.5 Da, up to 2 missed cleavages, carboxyamidomethylation (57.021 Da) on Cys (static modification), and oxidation (+15.995 Da) on Met (dynamic modification). A false discovery rate (FDR) threshold of ≤1% at both peptide and protein levels was applied using the target-decoy strategy to ensure high-confidence identifications. For label-free quantification, total peak area normalization was performed to correct for systematic variations. Proteins not detected in at least 70% of replicates within any group were removed, and the remaining missing values were imputed with the minimum observed value for the respective protein. To focus on proteins exhibiting both statistical significance and substantial biological change, data points with p ≤ 0.05 and a difference ratio (fold change) ≥1.2 were selected for further analysis. This prefiltering step was followed by a permutation-based FDR correction (FDR = 0.05) to ensure the overall robustness of the findings against multiple testing.

Statistics

In R Studio, Student’s t-test was employed to compare the RVO group with the control group. Additionally, a subgroup analysis comparing BRVO and CRVO was carried out using the same test. For multiple hypothesis testing corrections, the permutation-based method in R Studio was utilized, with 250 randomizations and an S0 parameter of 0.1. The false-discovery rate (FDR) was fixed at 0.05. In GraphPad Prism 9, Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r) was applied to compute correlations, with significance defined as P < 0.05. Moreover, scatter plots along with linear regression predictions were created in GraphPad Prism 9. Repeated-measures analysis of covariance, as implemented under single models, was applied with change from signal intensity integration for differential proteins per measurement as the dependent variable, and signal intensity, number of injections, and the signal intensity × number of injections interaction as independent variables. The approximate normality of each outcome and the change score of the outcome were confirmed by examination. The P-value of the Mauchly sphericity test is less than 0.05, and the P-value of sphericity is also assumed to be less than 0.05, which indicates a significant difference. If the P-value of the Mauchly sphericity test exceeds 0.05, the P-value of the Greenhouse–Geisser or Szyn–Federt test is less than 0.05, which is deemed to represent a significant difference. It was calculated in SPSS Statistics (IBM, USA, Version 25). In R Studio, Gene Ontology analysis of biological processes was conducted. As previously reported, cluster analysis of significantly regulated proteins was carried out using STRING 11.5 (string-db.org). The minimum required interaction score was set at 0.90. Furthermore, principal component analysis was executed in R Studio.

Results

In the combined aqueous samples, researchers managed to accurately identify as many as 896 proteins (Datafile S1). Moreover, in no fewer than 70% of the samples from every group, 404 proteins within the aqueous humor were successfully recognized and quantified (Datafile S2). After that, a statistical analysis was carried out on these proteins.

According to principal component analysis in proteomics, samples from RVO patients can be almost separated from control samples (Figure S1). Intersample correlation heatmaps demonstrated high Pearson correlation coefficients (r > 0.9) among biological replicates within groups (Figure S2). The relative standard deviation (RSD) analysis indicated low RSD values for the majority of proteins across replicates within groups, confirming quantification stability (Figure S3). After correction for multiple hypothesis testing, 45 proteins were significantly regulated in RVO compared to controls (Figure ). Among the significantly regulated proteins, 23 increased and 22 decreased (Table S2). Compared to BRVO, CRVO had 5 increased proteins and 15 decreased proteins (Table S3 and Figure ).

From diagnosing BRVO complicated by ME to the subsidence of macular edema after treatment, the aqueous humor proteins successively changed in Golgi membrane protein 1, desmocollin-3, complement factor H-related protein 1, and plasma kallikrein. Following repeated measures of the ANOVA test, statistically significant differences were found between Golgi membrane protein 1, desmocollin-3, and plasma kallikrein (Table S6 and Figure S4). Plasma kallikrein and desmocollin-3 were significantly correlated with CRT during treatment (Figure S5). None of the proteins was significantly correlated with BCVA. There was a strong correlation between plasma kallikrein and macular edema severity, and desmocollin-3 was strongly negatively correlated with macular edema severity (Table S7).

In the CRVO group, 43 differentially expressed proteins were significantly changed after a single intravitreal Conbercept injection (Table S8), of which 4 differentially expressed proteins were significantly down-regulated, including antithrombin-III (P = 0.010334201), endoplasmin (P = 0.010334201), alpha-1-antitrypsin (P = 0.007220794), and carboxypeptidase B2 (P = 0.039613566); 39 differential proteins were significantly up-regulated. After the second intravitreal Conbercept injection, 62 differentially expressed proteins showed significant changes (Table S9), of which 10 proteins were significantly downregulated and 52 were significantly up-regulated. From the diagnosis of CRVO complicated by ME to the improvement of treatment, 21 aqueous humor proteins changed, including phosphatidylethanolamine-binding protein 4, SPARC, galectin-3-binding protein, alpha-N-acetylgalactosaminidase, Vasorin, glutaminyl-peptide cyclo transferase, collagen alpha-1­(I) chain, coactosin-like protein, desmocollin-2, Attractin, Testican-1, Neurotrimin, dickkopf-related protein 3, desmocollin-3, sulfhydryl oxidase 1, complement C 1s subcomponent, and plasma kallikrein. Fibulin-1, follistatin-related protein 1, SPARC, Vasorin, desmocollin-2, Neurotrimin, desmocollin-3, and plasma kallikrein remained significantly different after repeated-measures ANOVA (Table S10 and Figure S6). Except for plasma kallikrein, the other proteins were significantly correlated with the central retinal thickness during treatment (Figure S7). Vasorin, SPARC, Neurotrimin, and desmocollin-2 were strongly negatively correlated with macular edema severity (Table S11). Desmocollin-2, desmocollin-3, plasma kallikrein, and Neurotrimin were significantly correlated with changes in the patient’s best-corrected visual acuity during treatment (Figure S8). Among them, plasma kallikrein showed a strong correlation with BCVA, and Neurotrimin and desmocollin-3 showed a strong negative correlation with BCVA (Table S12).

Discussion

This study aims to elucidate the intraocular molecular changes in RVO, especially during the gradual improvement of macular edema following intravitreal Conbercept injection through proteomic analysis of aqueous humor. Compared with the control group, 45 proteins were regulated during the pathogenesis of RVO, including 19 proteins for BRVO and 48 proteins for CRVO. Similar to the conclusion reached by Kusada et al. The proteomic alterations in the aqueous humor were more pronounced in CRVO than in BRVO. Significant clinical insights can be obtained by comparing the proteomes of CRVO and BRVO, along with analyzing the alterations in the proteomes of CRVO compared to those of BRVO during the treatment process. A more significant alteration was observed in the 20 proteins of CRVO in comparison to those of BRVO, indicating that there are additional factors influencing the role of CRVO patients. These findings suggest that the pathophysiology of CRVO may be more complex than that of BRVO. We hypothesize that this might underlie the typically more severe clinical course of CRVO and could inform future studies investigating whether more intensive treatment regimens are beneficial for such patients. KEGG analysis showed that the inflammatory drive response of CRVO was particularly severe. The differential proteins were significantly enriched in complement and coagulation cascades and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, indicating that, in the process of anti-VEGF treatment, the application of anti-inflammatory drugs such as dexamethasone intravitreal implants may play a more positive role in the improvement of macular edema in CRVO patients. The clinical study of Houben et al. confirms our view, and therefore, in patients who do not respond to multiple anti-VEGF therapies or who relapse frequently, injectable treatment with intravitreal anti-inflammatory drugs can be considered. Compared with those in BRVO patients, the concentration of Spondin-1 in the aqueous humor of CRVO patients was significantly increased. The concentrations of Dickkopf-related protein 3, acid ceramidase, collagen alpha-1­(I) chain, and protein FAM3C were significantly decreased, indicating that CRVO patients had more obvious retinal fibrosis or the posterior vitreous cortex stays attached more frequently, which coincides with what Bertelmann et al. observed. In addition, the significant reduction of neural cell adhesion molecule 1 and interleukin-6 receptor subunit beta suggests that CRVO patients have a higher probability of nerve damage, which may be one of the reasons for the worse prognostic vision of CRVO patients. This may partly explain the results of NFL retinal hemorrhages and peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer thickness at the baseline correlate with more severe forms of CRVO found by Au et al. and Moleiro et al.

In the aqueous humor of BRVO patients, with the resolution of ME, the Golgi membrane protein 1 and desmocollin-3 levels increased significantly, while the level of plasma kallikrein decreased. GOLM1 is an epithelial-specific Golgi membrane protein involved in mitosis, invasion, and EMT processes. Although this may indicate the likelihood of increased fibrosis or the posterior vitreous cortex staying attached more frequently in patients with BRVO, its variation is independent of the severity of BCVA and macular edema in patients. Desmocollin-3 is a component of desmosomes in cell-to-cell junctions. With the reduction of macular edema, desmocollin-3 increases significantly, which may indicate that the vascular endothelial and outer layer-retinal barriers damaged by RVO are being repaired with the progression of treatment. Plasma kallikrein is a mediator of vascular leakage and inflammation, and its reduction indicates that the vascular leakage and inflammatory response caused by BRVO are gradually controlled with Conbercept treatment.

During the resolution of macular edema in the CRVO group, 8 proteins showed significant changes. These include SPARC, Vasorin, desmocollin-2, Neurotrimin, desmocollin-3, fibulin-1, and follistatin-related protein 1. Among them, SPARC, Vasorin, desmocollin-2, Neurotrimin, fibulin-1, and follistatin-related protein 1 are associated with fibrosis in the study of certain diseases. This further suggests that patients with CRVO may have a higher risk of retinal fibrosis than those with BRVO. However, there is currently no research to prove the role of these proteins in retinal vascular diseases and retinal proliferative diseases. To bridge this gap and understand the functional implications of these proteomic changes, future studies should integrate molecular data with functional assessments. For instance, electroretinography (ERG) has been successfully used to link specific protein expressions, such as those involved in synaptic function and inflammation, with retinal neuronal responses, providing a model for how our protein observations could be physiologically contextualized. Furthermore, the structural remodeling suggested by our findings raises a crucial question about neuro-adaptation: do the retina and visual cortex undergo compensatory reorganization in response to RVO, akin to the neural plasticity observed in glaucoma patients following vision loss? Exploring such mechanisms would significantly deepen our proteomic data. These proteins were inversely correlated with the outcome of macular edema, with desmocollin-2 and Neurotrimin significantly negatively correlated with BCVA. These consistent associations suggest a potential role for these molecules in processes that counteract disease progression under retinal vascular conditions.

The levels of two proteins, plasma kallikrein and desmocollin-3, changed dynamically during treatment across the BRVO and CRVO cohorts. Of these, desmocollin-3 showed a strong negative correlation with macular edema severity in both groups, suggesting its potential as a biomarker associated with a more favorable clinical outcome. Plasma kallikrein levels were significantly positively correlated with disease severity metrics in RVO patients. This finding is consistent with its known role in inflammation and vascular permeability, implicating it in the pathophysiology of macular edema. The contrasting associations of plasma kallikrein and desmocollin-3 with disease severity warrant further investigation as potential modulators of RVO progression and as candidates for targeted therapeutic strategies.

This study exhibits several notable merits. First, sample collection was performed in tandem with the entire course of treatment, enabling a more authentic portrayal of the holistic changes that unfold during the disease’s therapeutic progression. Second, there have been no previous proteomics studies on Conbercept for the treatment of retinal vein occlusion disease, and our study fills a gap in this field. (3) Third, we employed repeated-measures analysis to conduct in-depth screening of proteomic data sets while also establishing correlations between the results and imaging characteristicsa methodological approach that enhances the robustness of our findings.

Our study has several limitations. First, chief among these is its nature as a single-center trial with a relatively small sample cohort. Consequently, our findings must be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Additionally, from a technical standpoint, the proteomic analysis itself is limited by the use of outdated resources; the Uniprot_Human database (2019 release) and MaxQuant software (v1.5.2.8) employed here may lack the latest protein annotations and algorithmic improvements, potentially affecting the depth and precision of protein identification and quantification. Furthermore, in vivo studies are imperative to elucidate the potential mechanisms of action of Conbercept and the therapeutic relevance of the proteins identified herein. In subsequent research, the adoption of a paired sample design coupled with a large-scale, multicenter sample size, as well as the implementation of updated bioinformatic tools and databases, would significantly elevate the scientific rigor of the investigation.

Serum Aβ42 and hemoglobin as independent predictors of post-stroke cognitive impairment: a comparative biomarker study
Authors: Talha Bin Sajjad, Aamna Zahid, Huzaifa Rehman, Sundus Nadeem, Muhammad Aetesam Nasir, Shaista Rauf, Muhammad Waqar Mazhar, Ahmad Raza, Inibehe Ime Okon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Stroke is a devastating medical condition, and one of its side effects is post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI). The objective of this study is to investigate and analyse the potential indicators for post-stroke cognitive decline (PSCI), with a particular emphasis on the biomarkers Aβ42 (beta-amyloid 42) and haemoglobin (Hb) levels. Based on clinical diagnosis, 120 participants were divided into three groups: PSCI (n = 40), PSCN (n = 40), and AD (n = 40): Alzheimer’s disease (AD), post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI), and post-stroke cognitively normal (PSCN). The first data set was documented. The study examined the relationship between Aβ42 and Hb and cognitive score. Finally, ROC curve and logistic regression analysis were used to analyze the aforementioned indicators’ PSCI prediction abilities. Compared to the AD group (104.00 ± 64.43 pg/ml, 112.4 ± 19.05 g/L) and the PSCN group (87.38 ± 66.69 pg/ml, 134.8 ± 13.28 g/L), the PSCI group had lower levels of Aβ42 (69.20 ± 49.36 pg/ml) and Hb (110.53 ± 22.73 g/L) (P < 0.05). The study identified Hb, age, and hypertension as the main risk factors for PSCI, while Aβ42 was noted as a pertinent risk factor. The sensitivity level was 0.800, specificity was 0.625, and area under the curve for the combination diagnosis of Aβ42 and Hb was 0.7169, according to the ROC curve. The risk variables for PSCI, Aβ42 and Hb, are considerably lower in the blood of patients with PSCI compared to controls. The performance of differential diagnosis will be enhanced when the two are integrated.

Introduction

Worldwide, people are losing their lives to cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, which put a heavy strain on families and nations. The quality of life and survival time of almost one-third of stroke patients are profoundly impacted by post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI)1. Patients with pre-stroke moderate cognitive impairment (PSCI) are those who show no signs of cognitive impairment before the stroke, but who may have cognitive deterioration within the first six months after the stroke begins or a gradual restoration to pre-stroke levels of functioning2, The presence of a prior stroke is substantially associated with it. To minimise heterogeneity and enhance the interpretability of biomarker relationships, only patients with acute ischaemic stroke were included in this investigation. These patients were split into 3 groups: ischaemic stroke, haemorrhagic stroke and transient ischaemic attack (TIA).

A form of vascular cognitive impairment (VCI), PSCI has been around for a while. According to previous studies, the brain parenchyma is the site of formation for neurofibrillary tangles and amyloid plaques in the majority of patients with VCI who also have Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Hyperphosphorylated Tau protein (p-tau) and β-amyloid (Aβ) make up the majority of these synaptic dysfunction, cognitive decline, and dementia are caused by the initial event of Aβ accumulation in the parenchyma1, Aβ deposition is seen as crucial in the advancement of VCI3 and Aβ-positron emission tomography (Aβ-PET) or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) Aβ was also found to have a positive effect in 44.7% of VCI cases4. In addition, comparing the positive rates of Aβ and tau in CSF between VCI and AD can demonstrate that VCI does not rely on Aβ to induce illness5. In contrast, research has shown that patients’ attention and executive function were worse in VCI without Aβ deposition compared to patients with Aβ deposition6. The expression of Aβ in the peripheral blood of patients with VCI is also not uniform7,8. Ischemic stroke, for example, can hasten or initiate the buildup of Aβ9 contribute to the development of Alzheimer’s disease, increase the likelihood of developing AD, and quicken the decline in cognitive abilities. Additionally, the risk of ischemic cerebrovascular disease will be elevated in AD. As it builds up in the cerebral blood vessels, Aβ causes serious harm to the cerebrovascular system and endothelial structural and function impairments10. β Because the pathogenicity of Aβ in VCI is still unclear, we aim to investigate the expression of Aβ42 in PSCI, particularly in ischemic stroke, thoroughly. Current study indicates a robust link between Aβ buildup and neurodegenerative processes, suggesting its possible involvement in the cognitive deterioration following a stroke. Nevertheless, the specific pathways via which Aβ contributes to PSCI require additional clarification. The primary component of red blood cells, hemoglobin (Hb), is known to interact with oxygen to produce oxygenated hemoglobin, which carries oxygen throughout the body. When it comes to transporting and exchanging oxygen throughout the body, Hb is king11. Hypoxia, mitochondrial malfunction, and neuronal damage can result from low hemoglobin levels12, which is associated with cognitive decline13 because the brain uses 20% of the oxygen that the body consumes. Cortical oxygenated hemoglobin was shown to be considerably lower in VCI patients compared to the control group in a number of imaging studies14, and similar findings were reported in blood tests15.

There are strict rules about the timing of cognitive impairment and ischemic stroke in the clinical diagnosis of PSCI, but whether or not it is associated with AD is yet unknown. Aiming to aid in the diagnosis and differential diagnosis of PSCI, this study sought to delve deeper into the expression levels of Aβ42 and Hb in PSCI, along with their link with PSCI condition.

Methodology

Below, you can find the PSCI patient inclusion requirements. Patients ranging in age from eighteen to eighty-five years old are targeted first. Secondly, there was a marked drop in cognitive function that was not alleviated. Half a year following the most recent ischemic stroke, the deterioration took place. Thirdly, the ischemic stroke appeared for the first time or had a history of mRS scores of 1 or less. Participants will be required to provide their contact information and formally consent to undergo cognitive assessment using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE). Individuals will be excluded from participation if they present with neurological impairments that interfere with cognitive testing, such as significant visual or speech disturbances. Additional exclusion criteria include renal insufficiency, defined as a serum creatinine level exceeding 2.0 mg/dL (177 µmol/L), hepatic dysfunction indicated by transaminase levels more than 2.5 times the upper limit of normal, or the presence of other severe systemic illnesses. Furthermore, individuals currently undergoing anticancer treatment or diagnosed with malignant tumors will also be excluded due to potential confounding effects on cognitive performance and overall health status. The fourth point is that there is no clear evidence linking vascular variables to cognitive impairment.

In the control group, individuals were considered to have AD if they met the diagnostic criteria and were receiving treatment accordingly. In the PSCN group, participants were considered to have no cognitive deterioration six months following their most recent ischemic stroke. From 2022.01. to 2023.08., all inpatients at Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology were searched according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. There were a total of 120 cases, with 40 cases in each category.

Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology ethics Committee gave their approval to the study, and all participants signed an informed consent form indicating their agreement to participate. The entire procedure were conducted in compliance with the pertinent norms and regulations.

All methods and experimental procedures were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology. Furthermore, written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Basic clinical data

The patient’s age, gender, level of schooling, body mass index (BMI), and medical history, which included conditions like high blood pressure, diabetes, coronary heart disease, and atrial fibrillation, were written down (Fig. 1).

Serological

Participants had been fasting overnight for at least 8–12 h before taking venous blood samples. Standard laboratory procedures were followed in the processing of serum and whole blood samples. Biochemical parameters like total cholesterol (TC), fasting blood glucose (FBG), triglycerides (TG), high density (HDL), low density (LDL), uric acid (UA), and creatinine (Cr) were determined using commercially available enzymatic assay kits and an automated clinical chemistry analyser (c311 analyser, Roche Diagnostics, Germany). Haemoglobin (Hb) level and white blood cell (WBC) counts were determined using an automated haematology analyser (e.g., Sysmex XN-1000, Sysmex Corporation, Japan), which is based on conventional flow cytometry and impedance techniques. The tests were performed in the core lab at Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology with rigorous quality control measures. Every day, calibration and internal quality controls were carried out to guarantee the precision and dependability of the outcomes.

Plasma Aβ42 and p-tau-181

Specimen collection: The serum soluble oligomeric peptides Aβ4231 and p-tau-181 were identified using Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The ELISA kit was acquired from Shenzhen Anqun Bioengineering Co., Ltd., a company based in China. A volume of 5 milliliters of venous blood was obtained from the patient using a vacuum blood collection tube without any anticoagulant, while the patient was in a fasting state. Following a 30- to 60-minute waiting period during which the blood coagulated, it was centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min.

The liquid portion, known as the supernatant, was utilized to collect serum samples, which were subsequently kept in a refrigerator set at a temperature of -80℃ for future usage. Prior to the experiment, the samples were allowed to reach the ambient temperature for testing, and any recurrent cycles of freezing and thawing were prevented.

Specimen identification

The experiment adhered to the idea of the double antibody sandwich approach. Microplates coated with Aβ42 and p-tau-181 antibodies were used to add serum specimens. The unbound components were eliminated by washing the plate. Polyclonal antibodies (binding antibodies) specific to Aβ1–42 and p-tau-181 were subsequently added, followed by another round of washing. Finally, the antibodies were labeled with horseradish peroxidase. The enzyme-conjugated secondary antibody was ultimately combined with the antibody/antigen/antibody/enzyme-labeled secondary antibody complex. A chromogenic reagent was introduced to produce color, and the reaction was stopped after a certain time. The optical density (OD) value was then determined at a wavelength of 450 nm. The concentration of Aβ42 and p-tau-181 in the sample was determined by calculating their contents using a standard curve created by the calibrator, which took into account the increase in their concentration. The samples in each group were measured twice, and the mean value was calculated.

The Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was used to evaluate the model’s calibration, and a calibration plot was used to depict the results. The test assesses whether observed occurrence rates across risk deciles correspond with projected probabilities. A satisfactory calibration is shown by a non-significant p-value (P > 0.05).

Evaluation of cognitive impairment

The Cognitive Function Scale was administered within 1 week of admission, and the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) was utilized to evaluate the patient’s cognitive function. The MMSE is well recognized as one of the most prevalent screening scales for cognitive impairment. The MMSE has a total score of 30 points. A score of ≥ 23 points and ≤ 26 points indicates mild cognitive impairment, whereas a score of ≤ 23 points indicates dementia.

Data analysis

The data was analyzed and shown using the statistical software SPSS 23.0 and the graphing software Graphpad Prism 8.0. The Shapiro-Wilk test was employed to evaluate the normality of the data. For regular variables, numerical values are represented as the mean plus or minus the standard deviation (SD). The statistical method of analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to compare several groups. The Bonferroni test was utilized as a post hoc test to further analyze the differences between the groups. For variables that do not follow a normal distribution or have unequal variances, numerical values are represented as the median (inter quartile range). The non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test was used to do multiple comparisons. The categorical variables were represented as frequencies (percentages) and were compared using Chi-square tests and Fisher’s exact tests. A significance level of P < 0.05 was used to determine statistical significance. The indicators that exhibited statistically significant variations between groups (P < 0.05) were included in a multivariate logistic regression analysis to examine the pertinent risk factors associated with PSCI. The relationship between MMSE, Aβ42, and Hb was examined using partial correlation. The diagnostic efficacy of serum Aβ42 and Hb for PSCI was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.

Ethical consideration

The Ethics Committee of Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology authorized this investigation. All procedures were conducted in compliance with applicable rules and regulations. Informed written consent was acquired from all individuals before their inclusion in the study.

Results

Baseline data

In the post-stroke cognitively normal (PSCN) group, there were 40 cases, 40 instances of Alzheimer’s disease (AD), and 40 cases of post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI). The percentage of male participants in these groups varied from 47% to 60%. In contrast to the PSCN group, which had a younger mean age (~ 65 years) and more years of education, the PSCI and AD groups had higher mean ages (~ 81 years) and lower average years of schooling. While the frequency of diabetes, coronary heart disease, and atrial fibrillation varied somewhat among groups, hypertension was most prevalent in the PSCN group (77%). A significant difference was found between age and educational level of the groups.

Age, BMI, hemoglobin, hypertension, and Aβ42 levels all showed statistically significant variations between the groups (P < 0.05). P-tau-181 and other markers did not show any significant differences (Fig. 1).

Analysis of the predictive ability of Aβ42 and Hb on the occurrence of PSCI

According to specialists, PSCI and PSCN are both linked to an increased risk of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) due to age and hypertension17. Additionally, PSCN can be differentiated from PSCI and AD in terms of cognitive function, serving as a control group free of dementia. Consequently, this study alone examined the effectiveness of Aβ42 and Hb in differentiating between PSCI and AD, while a subsequent study assessed the role of Aβ42 combined with Hb in differentiating between the two. Results are listed in Table 4. The sensitivity and specificity for Aβ42 in the differential diagnosis alone were 87.5% and 47.5%, respectively, with a threshold value of 49.81 pg/ml. For Hb alone, the cut-off value was 105.5 g/L, with sensitivity reaching 90.0% and specificity 45.0%. For the joint diagnosis of Aβ42Aβ42 and Hb, the cut-off value was 0.423, and the sensitivity and specificity were 80.0% and 62.5%, respectively. Therefore, when both requirements were met, the diagnostic efficiency was higher and the specificity was much higher compared to Aβ42 or Hb alone, albeit the sensitivity was lower (Table 4; Fig. 2).

Discussion

Several important findings about biomarkers linked to post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) were found in this study. The first difference was that the serum Aβ42 and haemoglobin (Hb) levels were significantly decreased in patients with PSCI, compared with both Alzheimer’s disease (AD) patients and post-stroke cognitively normal (PSCN) individuals. Secondly, multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that Aβ42 was a significant factor in disease differentiation and that Hb and hypertension were independent risk factors of PSCI. Third, the use of both Aβ42 and Hb resulted in an improved diagnostic efficacy and increased specificity compared to the use of each biomarker alone. These results imply that decreased peripheral Aβ42 and Hb levels are closely linked to cognitive loss after ischaemic stroke and could be useful biomarkers for enhancing the early detection and differential diagnosis of PSCI. The study has considered the demographic variables such as age, sex and level of education to get a better understanding of the findings. The PSCI and AD groups were much older than the PSCN group, which is in accordance with previous research showing that ageing is a key risk factor for neurodegenerative diseases and cognitive decline. Older individuals might have an increased risk of PSCI due to the cumulative vascular burden, the decreased ability to plasticize the brain and the increased risk of ischaemic injury. Biochemical and hormonal gender differences may play a role in cognitive recovery following stroke and cannot be ignored, even when no significant differences in the sex distribution were observed between groups.

Additionally, the PSCI and AD groups’ lower educational attainment may be a sign of diminished cognitive reserve, which has been linked to a higher susceptibility to cognitive impairment after neurological traumas. These demographic factors should be considered carefully when evaluating the association of biomarkers, such as Aβ42, and Hb with cognitive outcomes as they can act as confounders or modifiers.

Aβ42 is a part of Aβ that causes neurotoxicity when it forms Aβ aggregates and amyloid plaques, and it is formed from fragments of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) that are cleaved from residues 672 to 71116. Under typical conditions, APP is unable to generate Aβ. But when the APP gene is altered, the protein can readily produce Aβ40 and Aβ42 through the joint actions of β- and γ-secretase. Nerve cells can be destroyed by low-level Aβ42 oligomers because Aβ42 is more neurotoxic than Aβ40, which is due to its higher hydrophobicity17. An imbalance in intracellular calcium homeostasis, apoptosis, and oxygen free radical metabolism are the main outcomes of cell damage caused by Aβ42’s direct impact on neurons18. After a stroke, neurotoxic Aβ can build up in the brain due to cerebral ischemia injury, leading to a gradual decrease in cognitive function19.

Various blood Aβ42 expressions have been identified in AD in previous research. A correlation between lower plasma Aβ42 levels and dementia and AD risk was discovered by Chouraki et al. in a community cohort that underwent continuous surveillance of AD20. Plasma Aβ42 concentrations were not factored into the clinical AD development prediction by Lövheim et al., despite the fact that these values remained constant in the years preceding the AD diagnosis21. Research on VCI reveals the same thing. Mao et al. assessed serum Aβ42’s capacity to foretell PSCI by collecting it during the acute phase of stroke. Aβ42 levels were discovered to be lower in PSCI patients as compared to PSCN patients22. The key observation index in this study was blood Aβ42 when cognitive impairment occurred, and it was discovered that Aβ42 was lower in PSCI patients compared to PSCN patients. Researchers Chi et al. discovered that lowering plasma Aβ42 levels, which they assessed in blood samples taken three months after a stroke, could help in predicting the occurrence of PSCI one year later23. Nevertheless, after differentiating between patients with cognitive impairment and cerebral small vessel disease, Liu et al. discovered Aβ42 in plasma. They discovered that the Aβ42 value in VCI was higher than in individuals without cognitive impairment, and the rise in plasma Aβ42 established it as a separate risk factor24. According to Stocker et al.25, when Aβ 42 was detected in plasma using an immunoinfrared sensor, there was no connection with the diagnosis of VCI in a community-based cohort.

The brain maintains a balanced generation and elimination of Aβ under normal conditions. Aβ amyloidosis is exclusive to the CNS and is thought to be a crucial pathophysiological link between stroke, Aβ deposition, and dementia26. The brain parenchyma is excreted by the lymphatic system, the blood-brain barrier, and the perivascular space together. Around half of the Aβ clearance in the brain is attributed to its transport through the blood-brain barrier or extravascular routes to the peripheral circulatory system, as per previous research on human brain-vasculature transfer27. In their study, Ovod et al. detailed the SILK method’s effects on Aβ turnover in human plasma. They discovered that amyloid-positive individuals had a lower absolute concentration of Aβ42 in their blood and a slower turnover rate compared to CSF. This suggested that the pathology of amyloidosis in the central nervous system was reflected in plasma Aβ kinetics, just as it was in CSF28. Plasma Aβ has emerged as an encouraging indicator of persistent amyloid disease, thanks to the development of novel, sensitive analytical methods29.

Aβ buildup in the brain following a stroke could be caused by either an increase in production or an impairment in clearance, which is in line with AD30. In cerebral vascular damage, the activity of the enzyme β-secretase 1 (BACE1), which is essential for the synthesis of Aβ, is increased. The study found that serum BACE1 activity was elevated in both Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and vascular cognitive impairment (VCI), when compared to healthy controls. This finding confirms the pathogenic relationship between the two types of cognitive impairment and the common feature of increased peripheral BACE1 activity31. It follows that PSCI may likewise undergo this transformation.

Since the detectable free Aβ in CSF reduced due to the increased deposition of Aβ in the brain parenchyma, it is expected that Aβ in peripheral blood will likewise drop. The precise connection between hemoglobin and Aβ has received little research. Based on their findings in senile plaques, Wu et al. hypothesized that Hb in the brain could play a role in the development of amyloid oligomers38, alter the aggregation state of Aβ by binding to it39, and decrease the activation and phagocytic ability of astrocytes through their interaction with Aβ 42, leading to impaired Aβ clearance40 and possibly contributing to the pathogenesis of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Results from these investigations suggested that elevated hemoglobin levels in AD brain tissue may serve as a compensatory mechanism for hypoxia41–44. Limitations and future directions

There are a number of limitations to this study that should be noted. First, the sample size was small (n = 120), so the results may not be generalizable and further research is necessary to clarify small correlations. Second, this study was conducted in a single center, so the results may not be generalizable and may have selection bias. Third, because of the cross-sectional design of this study, it is not possible to establish whether there are causal relationships between the serum Aβ42 and haemoglobin levels and post-stroke cognitive impairment. To gain a deeper understanding of temporal changes and causal pathways, longitudinal studies are required. Fourth, the study did not include neuroimaging (MRI or PET scans) or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers that might have provided more comprehensive and detailed information about underlying neuropathological pathways. In addition, the relationships observed here could have been affected by other uncontrolled factors that could potentially affect the results, such as drug use, inflammatory markers, and diet. Larger, multicenter longitudinal studies should be the main focus of future research in order to confirm these results and enhance generalisability. Biomarker use, including CSF analysis and neuroimaging, could lead to more precise diagnosis using multimodal biomarkers. Furthermore, mechanistic studies are required to explore the biological connections between haemoglobin, Aβ42 and cognitive decline.

Conclusions

The objective of this study was to aid in the differentiation between AD and PSCI when the timing of stroke and cognitive impairment onset is ambiguous. If the patient’s Aβ level is below 49.81pg/ml and their Hb level is below 105.5 g/L, there is a strong likelihood of them having PSCI. The current research findings have relied on the analysis of Aβ42 and Hb levels in peripheral blood to identify cognitive dysfunction abnormalities. The precise pathogenic mechanism by which peripheral blood Aβ42 and Hb affect cognition is still not fully understood, and further fundamental study is needed to uncover its underlying relationship.

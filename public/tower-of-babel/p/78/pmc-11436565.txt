Pan-cancer analysis of the prognostic and immunological role of hippo-YAP signaling pathway
Authors: Jing Yang, Cheng Yang, Guang Yang, Ronglin Wang, Junqiang Li, Yang Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The Hippo-Yes-associated protein (Hippo-YAP) signaling pathway, a conserved pathway that regulates organ size, participates in tumor progression. However, there are few comprehensive analyses of tumor prognosis and immunity. In the present study, TCGA, GTEx, GEO, TIMER2, STRING, GSCA, ImmuCellAI, and other bioinformatics tools were used to reveal the involvement of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway in the prognosis and immunity of pan-cancers. The obtained results showed that mRNA expression differences of Hippo-YAP pathway genes between normal samples and tumor samples in pan-cancers and some genes (such as TEAD4, MAP4K4, and STK3) might affect the prognosis of patients with skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) and pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD). Furthermore, mutation and methylation of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway genes in normal and primary tumor tissues differ in various cancers (KIRP, BRCA). Additionally, the relationship between the tumor microenvironment, molecular pathways, and the Hippo-YAP pathway indicated that it might lead to a suppressive immune microenvironment that affects the efficacy of immunotherapy. This is a pan-cancer overview of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway genes, which explores the aberrant expression or mutation of this pathway that regulates the tumor microenvironment and immunotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01212-9.

Introduction

As an evolutionarily conserved pathway, the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway regulates organ size by affecting cell proliferation, apoptosis, and stem cell self-renewal [1, 2]. In addition, dysregulation of this pathway can lead to the development of various diseases, especially cancer [3–7]. The Hippo-YAP pathway mainly includes the Hippo core complex and downstream transcriptional co-activators, namely Yes-associated protein/transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (YAP/TAZ). In normal tissues, the upstream enzyme cascade of the signaling pathway inhibits the nuclear translocation of YAP/TAZ through phosphorylation. However, aberrant dephosphorization of YAP/TAZ causes its nuclear translocation, allowing it to interact with transcription factors (TEAD1-4) more readily to induce the expression of oncogenes, thereby promoting cancer progression.

Research on the Hippo-YAP pathway also provides new ideas for cancer treatment [8–10]. Previous studies have investigated therapeutic strategies by targeting transcription factors in the pathway. The combination of TEAD and YAP is considered critical for regulating downstream proto-oncogenes. Some studies have shown that verteporfin is a small molecule that can inhibit TEAD–YAP interactions and restrain the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma by YAP-induced liver overgrowth [11]. In a prior study, it was reported that VGLL4 can directly compete with YAP-binding TEADs to be involved in cancer treatment [12]. The regulation of phosphatase activity has also been the subject of investigation in other studies. Some researchers have reported that because PP1A can cooperate with ASPP2 to dephosphorylate and activate TAZ [13], PP1A phosphatase inhibitors may be used in the treatment strategy.

Immunotherapy has been widely used to treat various tumors. Although some molecules in the Hippo-YAP pathway have been reported to be related to tumor immunity, YAP1 may promote tumor progression through immunosuppression [14], there still needs to be a comprehensive analysis of the immune regulation of this pathway. Therefore, our research aimed to summarize comprehensive pan-cancer information and the tumor immune microenvironment profile of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway for future reference. In addition, bioinformatics analysis provided a genetic overview and other valuable insights into the functionality of the Hippo-YAP pathway in tumor immunity, thereby offering novel perspectives for future clinical applications of immunotherapy.

Materials and methods

Data collection and download

The Hippo-YAP signaling pathway genes were acquired from the MsigDB database (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Expression data and matching clinical information for all different types of cancer in this study were derived from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and genotypic Tissue Expression (GTEx) data, that were downloaded from the UCSC XENA website (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). Other expression data for PAAD were obtained from the GEO database (GSE62452). Data of copy number variants (CNV), single-nucleotide variants (SNV), methylation, and drug resistance of pathway gene were obtained from the GSCA database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/GSCA/#/). The relevant pathway information used in the pathway correlation analysis was downloaded from the HALLMARK pathway data in the MsigDB database. Data on immune cell infiltration were downloaded from TIMER2 (http://timer.cistrome.org/) and ImmuCellAI (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/ImmuCellAI#!/) databases. Immunotherapy data were downloaded from GEO (GSE78220) and supplementary material of published papers (PMID: 32472114) (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-020-0839-y). All data were derived from publicly available data and published articles, and this data did not require ethical approval or informed consent.

Gene expression and association analysis

R language (limma package) was used to analyze the difference between tumor tissue (TCGA data) and normal tissue (TCGA + GTEx data).The p-values and logFC of differential expression were extracted for heat map display using the R language (ggplot2 package). In addition, correlation analyses of genes related to pathways in pan-carcinoma were performed for heat maps using R language (corplot package). The protein interaction network was established by STRING (https://www.string-db.org/) and beautified by the Cytoscape software.

Survival analysis of individual genes and total pathway

Univariate Cox regression analysis and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis were performed for pathway genes in pancancer patients using R language (survival and surimer package). For Univariate Cox regression analysis, p-values and HR values were extracted for heatmap display using the R language (pheatmap package). In addition, p-values > 0.05, gray; p-values < 0.05, HR > 1, red; HR < 1, blue. For Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, if p-values > 0.05, it is gray; if p-values < 0.05, the poor prognostic factors are red, and the protective prognostic factors are blue. Genes that were significant in cancer prognosis (univariate Cox regression analysis and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis) were counted as risk scores using the R language (ggplot2 package). The ssGSEA method was used to perform Hippo-YAP pathway scoring using the R language (GSVA package) and univariate regression analysis on the relationship between Hippo-YAP pathway scores and patient outcomes (OS, DSS, DFI, and PFI) in different tumors using R language (survival and Survimer package) and graphed R language (forestplot package).

Mutation, methylation, and drug resistance of genes

Single-nucleotide variants (SNV), copy number variants (CNV), methylation, and drug resistance were analyzed through the GSCA database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/GSCA/#/). The correlation between gene mRNA expression and drug IC50 was detected by GDSC and CTRP drug sensitivity databases, indicating the correlation between drug sensitivity and gene expression. The expression of each gene in the pathway and small molecule/drug sensitivity were determined using Spearman correlation analysis.

Gene set variation analysis of the total pathway

The gene set variation analysis included fifty pathways downloaded from the HALLMARK pathway dataset in MsigDB database. these fifty pathways mainly included cancer-related and immune-related pathways. ssGSEA scores were calculated for all pathways, the correlation between Hippoyap pathway scores and other pathway scores in each tumor was calculated, and a correlation graph was constructed using R language (pheatmap package). Red represents a positive correlation, and blue represents a negative relationship. The grid with darker color represents a more robust correlation, and the different numbers of asterisks represent p-values < 0.05/0.01/0.001.

Analysis of tumor microenvironment

Stromal score, immune score, ESTIMATE score, and tumor purity score were calculated in various cancers using the R language (ESTIMATE package), and the relationship between the four scores and the Hippo-YAP pathway using the R language (ggplot2 package). Furthermore, the study incorporated multiple pathways published in a cancer immunology study, including repair of DNA damage-related pathways (nucleotide excision repair, Mismatch Repair, DNA replication, base excision repair, DNA damage response), stromal/metastasis-related pathways (EMT1/2/3, Pan F TBRs) and immune-related pathways (antigen processing machinery, immune checkpoint, CD8+T effector). In addition, R language was used to analyze the correlation between the pathway and the HiPO-YAP pathway.

Evaluation immunity status

Correlations between the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway and immune cell infiltration data from the ImmuCellAI and TIMER2 databases were analyzed and graphed using the R language (ggplot2 package). In addition, the relationships between pathways and immune checkpoints, chemokines, and chemokine receptors based on the TCGA dataset were analyzed. To evaluate the efficacy of immunotherapy, we downloaded the data on immunotherapy from GEO (GSE78220) and supplementary material from published papers (PMID: 32472114), which included patients with melanoma or advanced clear cell renal cell carcinoma receiving anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. The pathway and immunotherapy data included in the study were analyzed to determine pathways' impact on survival and calculate the proportion of progressive disease in different groups.

Results

Expression and correlation analysis of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in pan-cancers

The Hippo-YAP pathway plays an essential role in cancer progression. Firstly, we analyzed the gene-expression levels to characterize the relationship between the Hippo-YAP pathway genes and cancer. According to the TCGA data, we analyzed the mRNA expression differences of Hippo-YAP pathway genes between normal samples and tumor samples in various cancers. The results showed that the mRNA expression of CXCL10, TEAD4, and MAP4K1 was elevated in most cancers (Fig. 1A). The analysis also revealed that Hippo-YAP pathway genes were expressed at higher levels in CHOL, PAAD, and THYM. We also used GEO data for validation. The results showed that the expression levels of most Hippo signaling pathway genes in pancreatic cancer were significantly higher than those in the corresponding para-cancerous tissues (Fig. 1B). Simultaneously, we performed a correlation analysis of genes in the Hippo-YAP pathway in pan-carcinoma, which showed that YY1AP1 is more closely related to YY1AP1, YWHAQ, STK38L, LAST1, and MAP4K3 (Fig. 1C). Single-nucleotide variation of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in pan-cancers

Single-nucleotide variation (SNV) analysis showed that deleterious mutations in the Hippo-YAP pathway genes differed in various tumors. UCEC, SKCM, and COAD were the most mutated cancer types (Fig. 2A). In addition, TNIK and NF2 are the most frequently mutated genes in the Hippo-YAP pathway in cancers, especially NF2, which shows an extremely high mutation rate in mesothelioma. Concomitantly, Hippo-YAP pathway genes have almost no mutations in testicular germ cell tumors, pheochromocytomas, or paragangliomas. The SNV landscaped plot describes the specific circumstances of gene mutations (Fig. 2B). DNA copy number variation of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in pan-cancers

DNA copy number variations (CNVs) are important part of genetic variation and participate in cancer progression by affecting gene expression. Simultaneously, as CNV is more stable than gene expression, it could be used as a tumor biomarker for clinical diagnosis and treatment. Herein, we investigated the potential effect of copy number variation (CNV) on the expression of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in cancers. The results of CNV analysis showed that the main types of CNV in cancers were heterozygous amplification and deletion of HiPO-YAP pathway genes. In addition, the CNV types of TNIK and NDRG1 had some homozygous amplification, and more homozygous amplification occurred in ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma (Supplementary Fig. 2A). After a preliminary understanding of the CNV situation in cancers, we analyzed the correlation between CNV and the expression of Hippo-YAP pathway genes. The results showed that the expression of some genes, especially YY1AP1, STK3, and NF2, was positively correlated with CNV. According to the analysis, BRCA, OV, and LUAD were the top three cancer types showing significant correlations. Above all, CNV might result in high expression of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in cancers (Fig. 2C).

Methylation of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in cancers

Methylation modification is an essential method for regulating gene-expression levels. The analysis of the methylation difference of the Hippo-YAP pathway genes between cancerous and normal tissues revealed that the Hippo-YAP pathway genes were more frequently methylated in KIRC, BRCA, and PRAD. TEAD1/3 and MAP4K1 were the most frequently methylated genes in the Hippo-YAP pathway (Supplementary Fig. 2B). The correlation between methylation and mRNA expression in various cancers revealed that the expression levels of almost all genes in the Hippo-YAP pathway were negatively correlated with methylation. Among all the genes, the expression of NDRG1, MAP4K1/2, and TEAD3 showed the strongest negative correlation with the methylation in the cancers (Fig. 2D).

Survival analysis of Hippo-YAP pathway in pan-cancers

First, we used COX regression to analyze individual genes in the Hippo-YAP pathway to determine whether their expression can affect the prognosis of patients with tumors. Single-factor Cox regression analysis showed that high expression of TEAD4, MAP4K4, and STK3 is a poor prognostic factor in various cancers, such as PAAD and LUAD. In contrast, the increased expression of MST1 and NF2 is a protective prognostic factor in some cancers, including KIRP and LGG (Fig. 3A). Then, we collated prognostic data of single genes in all cance r types; the results showed that downstream transcription factors, such as the expression of TEAD1/2/3/4, are poor prognostic factors in pan-cancers, and the expression levels of upstream kinases MST1 and MAP4K1 are protective prognostic factors in pan-cancers (Fig. 3B). Because COX regression analysis did not directly use survival time but used HR/RR as the outcome, we also performed Kaplan–Meier survival analysis for a more comprehensive analysis of the effect of Hippo-YAP pathway genes on prognosis. The results obtained are similar to the results of the COX regression: elevated MAP4K4 expression reflects a poor prognosis in patients with some kinds of cancer (STAD, LUAD, BLCA, MESO, ACC, CESC, and BRCA) and MAP4K1 acts as a protective factor for some patients with certain types of cancer (THYM, SARC, TGCT, CESC, BRCA, SKCM, and HNSC) (Fig. 3C, D). However, considering the regulatory mechanism between the various molecules of the Hippo-YAP pathway, we found that the prognostic results of the single-gene analysis may be contradictory and limited in some cases. We used ssGSEA to evaluate the activation of the Hippo-YAP pathway and assigned its scores to further research. GSVA analysis of the Hippo-YAP pathway

First, we compared the activation levels of the Hippo-YAP pathway in different cancers. Among all types of cancers, TGCT had the highest HiPO-YAP signaling pathway score, while PCPG had the lowest score (Fig. 4A). Concomitantly, analyzing the activation level of the Hippo-YAP pathway in tumor patients at different stages can help us further explore the role of this pathway. The results showed that in most cancers, the activation level of this pathway was not significantly correlated with cancer stage (Supplementary Fig. 3C). Nevertheless, the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway score of patients with advanced PAAD and BLCA cancer was higher than in patients with early stage cancer (Fig. 4B, C). As the Hippo-YAP pathway was found to be associated with the stage of some cancer patients, we wanted to further explore the potential mechanism of this pathway. Therefore, we used the HALLMARK pathway dataset (MsigDB database) to analyze the relationship between the Hippo-YAP pathway and the other 50 pathways. The results showed that the Hippo-YAP pathway is closely related with immune-related pathways, including IL2/STAT5 and IL-6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, suggesting that the Hippo-YAP pathway may affect some immune-related pathways to regulate tumor immune escape (Fig. 4D). Tumor microenvironment and immune cell infiltration about the Hippo-YAP pathway

Different tumor microenvironments significantly impact tumor immunity and progression. Therefore, we further analyzed the relationship of the Hippo-YAP pathway and the tumor microenvironment to determine its effect on tumors. First, we used the "ESTIMATE" package of R language to calculate the stromal score, immune score, and tumor purity from different tumor tissues, and analyzed the correlation of Hippo signaling pathway between this score of the tumor microenvironment. The results indicated that the Hippo-YAP pathway was positively correlated with some characteristics of the tumor microenvironment (immune and stromal) and negatively correlated with tumor purity (Fig. 5A). Second, we used another method published in cancer immunology research to evaluate the relationship between the Hippo signaling pathway and the tumor microenvironment. This article included relevant pathways in three aspects: immunity, tumor stroma/metastasis, and DNA damage repair, and we analyzed the correlation between the Hippo-YAP pathway and their respective pathways. To better evaluate the role of the overall pathway in tumor immunity, we used the gene set signature (GSVA score) obtained to further analyze the infiltration of immune cells. Two databases (TIMER2 and ImmuCellal) that contain immune cell infiltration were used separately for our analyses. The TIMER2 results showed that the Hippo-YAP pathway plays a role in immune cell infiltration in various cancers. For all types of immune cells, cancer-associated fibroblast cells were found to be positively related to the Hippo-YAP pathway in most cancers. However, NK cells had a negative relationship with the Hippo-YAP pathway in numerous cancers, such as USEC, TGCT, SKCM, READ, PAAD, LUAD, COAD, and BRCA (Fig. 5C). These data suggested that the Hippo-YAP pathway may affect infiltration of cancer immune cell. The data obtained from the TIMER2 database can be analyzed in different tumor types. Nevertheless, the data are too complex to obtain an intuitive conclusion regarding tumor immune cell infiltration by the Hippo-YAP pathway. The ImmuCellal results were more intuitive and specific, confirming that HiPO-YAP pathway score was significantly associated with infiltration levels of multiple immune cells across multiple cancer types. In particular, the Hippo-YAP pathway score was positively correlated to iTreg cells and negatively correlated to neutrophil cells in most cancer types (Fig. 5D). In summary, we found that the Hippo-YAP pathway plays an important role in the TME and immune cell infiltration, which may directly contribute to tumor immune escape to impact immunotherapy.

Immunomodulatory genes and immunotherapy of Hippo-YAP pathway scores in various cancers

To better explore why this pathway affects immune cell infiltration, we analyzed the correlation between the Hippo-YAP pathway and immunomodulation (including immune checkpoint, chemokine, and chemokine receptor) in pan-cancers. The analysis showed that the Hippo-YAP pathway positively correlates with many immune checkpoints. CTLA4 inhibitors have been used in the clinical treatment of tumors, and we found that the Hippo-YAP pathway was positively correlated with CTLA4 expression in almost all cancers (Fig. 6A), which may provide new ideas for further exploring the relationship between the Hippo-YAP pathway and immunotherapy. The Hippo-YAP pathway has been found to be strongly related to CSF1R, TGFBR1, KDR, and NECTIN2 in thymomas. In addition, some chemokines and chemokine receptors have been confirmed to be related to the Hippo-YAP pathway. As chemokines with vascular inhibitory properties, CXCL9, CXCL10, and CXCL11 were analyzed to be positively related to the Hippo-YAP pathway. CCL8, CCL4, and CCL2 have been reported to participate in tumor progression by regulating transcription factors and recruiting inhibitory immune cells (such as Tregand macrophages), and to also have a positive relationship with the Hippo-YAP pathway (Fig. 6B). In addition, the results for chemokine receptors also point out the function of the Hippo-YAP pathway in tumor immunology. There are some chemokine receptors expressing (CCR5 and CCR4) on the surface of Treg cells to play a role, and we also found that they are positively related to the Hippo-YAP pathway (Fig. 6C). As established by previous analysis, we know that the Hippo-YAP pathway may lead to immunosuppression of the tumor microenvironment; therefore, we hypothesized that patients with higher Hippo-YAP pathway scores had poorer immunotherapy outcomes. This hypothesis was tested using a database that contained immunotherapy data. The results showed that among KIRC patients who received anti-PD-1 treatment, those with a higher Hippo-YAP pathway score had a poor prognosis (Fig. 6D), and the proportion of patients whose disease progression was higher than that of patients with a lower Hippo-YAP pathway score (Fig. 6F). This conclusion was also confirmed by the data on melanoma patients receiving immunotherapy (Fig. 6E). The analysis indicated that melanoma patients with higher Hippo-YAP pathway scores had shorter survival times and higher rates of disease progression (Fig. 6G). Although the p-value was > 0.05, the trend was clear. The reason for this could be the small sample size, and the conclusions may be confirmed using a larger sample size in future analyses. Overall, the Hippo-YAP pathway can be used to predict cancer immunotherapy.

Drug sensitivity analysis

We analyzed the relationship between Hippo-YAP pathway genes and drug sensitivity through GDSC and CTRP databases to further explore the role of Hippo-YAP pathway genes in other cancer therapies. The results of GDSC showed significant differences in the sensitivity of the Hippo-YAP pathway genes to AT-7519, SNK-2112, OSI-027, and AICAR (Supplementary Fig. 4A). The high expression levels of transcription factors TEAD1/3/4 may lead to drug resistance (positive correlation with IC50). However, as the key of all pathways, the expression of YY1AP1 was negatively correlated with drug resistance toward AT-7519, SNK-2112, OSI-027, and AICAR (p < 0.05), which may indicate a better treatment of cancer patients with high YY1AP1 expression. Another database of cancer drug resistance (CTRP) similarly showed differences in sensitivity to drugs among Hippo-YAP pathway genes. The expression of TEAD1 has a positive correlation with drug resistance, including PF-3758309, elocalcitol, tivantinib, ciclopirox, and AT13387. YY1AP1 and MAP4K1/2 showed the opposite result; PF-3758309, BRD-A86708339, tivantinib, GR-1-31B, and dinaciclib were more sensitive in patients with high expression of YY1AP1 and MAP4K1/2 (Supplementary Fig. 4B). These results indicated that the dysregulated expression of Hippo-YAP pathway genes might mediate resistance to chemotherapy and targeted drug therapy.

Discussion

The Hippo-YAP signaling pathway plays an essential role in cancer development. The Hippo pathway and its downstream effectors, the transcriptional co-activator Yes-associated protein (YAP) and its paralog, transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ), play pivotal roles in regulating stem cells and cancer biology [15]. AP/TAZ hyperactivation via aberrant regulation of Hippo signaling is common in various cancers. For example, in prostate cancer, the Hippo-YAP pathway is affected by MYBL2, which causes castration resistance and metastasis [16] and is induced by CD44 to promote docetaxel-resistance and metastasis [17]. In addition, the Hippo-YAP pathway also affects the progression and drug resistance of cancer, including its activation by non-coding RNA [18, 19] and some proteins [20, 21] in colorectal cancer. Some studies have also been carried out on the relationship between the Hippo signaling pathway and lung cancer. Several non-coding RNA [22, 23] and membrane surface proteins [24] have been shown to affect cancer progression through the Hippo-YAP pathway, and targeted intervention with these molecules provides a new method for lung cancer treatment [25]. In addition, the progression of pancreatic cancer has been validated by the Hippo-YAP pathway [26, 27]. Overall, the Hippo-YAP pathway affects cancer progression and drug resistance by affecting specific molecular pathways in tumors.

In addition to directly participating in tumor progression, the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway has also been confirmed to be involved in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment. A recent study revealed that the Hippo-YAP pathway can increase tumor-associated macrophage infiltration through the TAZ/IL-34 axis, which leads to an immunosuppressive microenvironment and impairs theefficacy of anti-PD-L1 treatment [28]. Another study showed that YAP, as a transcription factor, can regulate PD-L1 in non–small-cell lung cancer to influence immunotherapy[29, 30]. In addition, the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway can also play a role in regulating macrophages and dendritic cells. The activation of YAP participates in M2 macrophage recruitment [31] and promotes the migration and infiltration of M1-like macrophages in hepatocellular carcinoma [32, 33]. As the key gene of the Hippo-YAP pathway, MST1 selectively regulates the cytoarchitecture, endocytosis, and migratory speed of mature dendritic cells [34]. There has also been research conducted on the correlation between hippo-yap pathway genes and immunity in pan-cancer, which confirmed that somatic alterations of the Hippo signaling pathway drive macrophage invasion in colorectal cancer and UCEC, and also proved the role of CNAs of the Hippo signaling pathway on adaptive immune cells [35]. Research also shows that immune cell infiltration differs in three subtypes of esophageal carcinoma (ESCA) classified by the Hippo-immune gene panel [35].

In the present study, we analyzed the expression of various genes in the Hippo signaling pathway and their prognostic implications in pan-cancer. Genes related to this pathway were highly expressed in some types of cancer, especially CHOL, PAAD, and THYM. Previous experimental studies have confirmed that this pathway is involved in the progression of cholangiocarcinoma [36] and pancreatic cancer [26, 27], but none have reported its involvement in thymic cancer. Simultaneously, survival analysis revealed the individual genes that determine prognosis, which showed that high expression of TEAD4 and MAP4K4 is a poor prognosis indicator. As a common transcription factor, TEAD4 has been confirmed to promote the progression of various cancers by molecular biology experiments [37–39], which is also consistent with our analysis of the prognostic results. SNV variants, copy number variations, and methylation status of pathway genes were analyzed to further explore the reason for the abnormal expression.

The results showed some influence of mutations or gene modifications on the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway. These conclusions shed light on regulating individual genes in the pathway and their functions in cancers. In addition, to better evaluate the overall role of this pathway, we introduced pathway scores to analyze tumor immunity, the tumor microenvironment, and immunotherapy in pan-cancers. First, pathway analysis demonstrated that the Hippo-YAP pathway is closely related to tumor immunity. The IL2/STAT5 signaling pathway [40, 41]has been reported to participate in regulating Treg cells to affect the immune microenvironment. IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway is also an important pathway that involves immune checkpoints [42, 43] or strengthens the immunosuppressive functions of myeloid cells [44] in the tumor microenvironment. The two pathways mentioned above, as well as other immune regulatory pathways, were strongly correlated with the Hippo-YAP pathway, which provides a direction for further analysis. Second, analysis of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway in the tumor microenvironment further proved that it is closely related to immunity. The score of the Hippo-YAP pathway was positively associated with stromal and immune scores, which indicated increased infiltration of immune cells and a smaller proportion of tumor cells. In addition, we used another evaluation method of the tumor microenvironment provided in previous research [45] to analyze the relationship between the Hippo-YAP pathway and DNA loss repair-related pathways, stromal/metastasis-related pathways, immune-related pathways, and immune checkpoint showed a very high correlation. These findings suggest that the Hippo-YAP pathway may regulate the tumor immune microenvironment and affect cancer immunotherapy. Therefore, we also explored the relationship between immune cell infiltration, common immune checkpoints, chemokines, and this pathway. The Hippo-YAP pathway positively correlates with Tregs and cancer-associated fibroblasts among all immune cells. Some immune checkpoints (such as CTLA4) were found to be connected with this pathway. The analysis of chemokines and their receptors has also identified some factors associated with suppressor immune cells. Based on the analysis of various aspects of tumor immunity, we speculate that the Hippo-YAP pathway may create a suppressive immune microenvironment by recruiting immunosuppressive cells (such as Tregs) or affecting the expression of immune checkpoint molecules, which may affect the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy. Data from patients receiving immunotherapy were used to test our hypothesis. The results also confirmed that the high activation state of the Hippo-YAP pathway might be a predictor of the adverse effects of immunotherapy.

Overall, these results are interesting and consistent with some experimental results from previous research. In contrast to previous studies, we analyzed the relationship between the overall activation level of the Hippo-YAP pathway and the immune microenvironment and immunotherapy. However, there are also some contradictions in the analysis process, so further validation with a larger sample size and experimental verification are necessary.

Limitation

Some limitations may be subject to further consideration. This is a pan-cancer overview of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway genes, based on public databases, which lacks molecular biology experiment verification. The key genes in Hippo-YAP signaling pathway that play important immune-modulatory roles in each specific cancer need to be further explored. Additional research to explain the mechanism of these key molecules in anti-tumor immune response may provide new ideas for optimizing anti-tumor therapy.

Conclusion

The present study provides a pan-cancer overview of the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway genes. There are some aberrantly expressed genes or abnormal levels of mutations in these genes in various cancers. The expression levels of these genes and the activation level of these pathways are related to patient prognosis. The pathway is associated with immune-related pathways and the regulation of tumor microenvironment. In addition, the impact of these genes on immunotherapy and drug resistance suggests their potential value in cancer treatment. Therefore, research on the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway can provide new ideas for evaluating different therapeutic effects in tumors.

USP22 promotes gefitinib resistance and inhibits ferroptosis in non‐small cell lung cancer by deubiquitination of MDM2
Authors: Peng Lu, Zhaoguo Li, Hang Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe emergence of chemoresistance markedly compromised the treatment efficiency of human cancer, including non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). In the present study, we aimed to explore the effects of ubiquitin‐specific peptidase 22 (USP22) and murine double minute 2 (MDM2) in gefitinib resistance in NSCLC.MethodsImmunohistochemistry (IHC) assay, quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‐PCR) assay and western blot assay were carried out to determine the expression of USP22 and MDM2. Transwell assay and flow cytometry analysis were performed to evaluate cell migration and apoptosis. Cell Counting Kit‐8 (CCK‐8) assay was employed to assess gefitinib resistance. The phenomenon of ferroptosis was estimated by related commercial kits. The oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity by C11‐BODIPY staining. The relation between USP22 and MDM2 was analyzed by ubiquitination assay and co‐immunoprecipitation (Co‐IP) assay.ResultsUSP22 was abnormally upregulated in NSCLC tissues and cells, and USP22 silencing markedly repressed NSCLC cell migration and facilitated apoptosis and ferroptosis. Moreover, our results indicated that ferroptosis could enhance the suppressive effect of gefitinib on NSCLC cells. Besides, USP22 overexpression enhanced gefitinib resistance and ferroptosis protection in NSCLC cells. Mechanically, USP22 stabilized MDM2 and regulated MDM2 expression through deubiquitination of MDM2. MDM2 deficiency partially restored the effects of USP22 on gefitinib resistance and ferroptosis in NSCLC cells. Of note, we validated the promotional effect of USP22 on gefitinib resistance in NSCLC in vivo through establishing the murine xenograft model.ConclusionUSP22/MDM2 promoted gefitinib resistance and inhibited ferroptosis in NSCLC, which might offer a novel strategy for overcoming gefitinib resistance in NSCLC.

INTRODUCTION

Lung cancer is the main cause of cancer‐linked death in the world, and non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for about 85% of lung cancer cases. 1 , 2 Despite the continuous development and progress of treatment methods such as surgery, radiotherapy, and drug therapy, most patients with NSCLC are diagnosed at an advanced stage and have a poor prognosis. 3 Small molecules targeting specific inhibitors, such as the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitor gefitinib, has been demonstrated to improve the survival of advanced NSCLC patients. 4 , 5 , 6 However, the acquired drug resistance is still a main obstacle for NSCLC therapy. 7 Therefore, there is an urgent need to deepen the understanding of gefitinib resistance and find new therapeutic targets.

Ubiquitin‐specific peptidase 22 (USP22) is a class of ubiquitin‐specific proteases (USPs) that can remove the ubiquitin moiety of target proteins. 8 USPs degradation by inhibiting the substrate protein ubiquitin dependence to stabilize the substrate proteins. 9 More and more of the literature indicates that USP22 expressed in cancer increases and is associated with the progression of multiple diseases, including NSCLC. 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 Moreover, Guo et al. demonstrated that USP22 contributed to Lenvatinib resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) by interacting with JMJD8. 14 Zhang et al. claimed that USP22 aggravated tumor cell proliferation, metastasis, and EGFR‐TKIs resistance in EGFR‐mutant lung adenocarcinoma cells. 15 However, the effect of USP22 on the resistance of NSCLC to gefitinib has not been explored.

Murine double minute 2 (MDM2) encodes an E3 ubiquitin ligase in the nucleus and can promote tumor formation by targeting tumor suppressor proteins such as p53 for proteasomal degradation. 16 , 17 In NSCLC, MDM2 was reported to enhance paclitaxel via circ_0002874/miR‐1273f/MDM2/p53 pathway. 18 S6K1 blockade was able to overcome the acquired drug resistance of NSCLC to EGFR‐TKIs by regulating MDM2. 19 Even so, the effect of MDM2 on the resistance of NSCLC to gefitinib is not clarified.

In the present study, we aimed to explore the functions and mechanisms of USP22 and MDM2 in gefitinib resistance in NSCLC, which might establish a foundation for NSCLC therapy.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Collection of tissue samples

After approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shuangyashan Shuangkuang Hospital and written informed consents were provided by the patients, the NSCLC tissues and adjacent non‐tumor tissues were harvested from 31 NSCLC patients who received treatment at Shuangyashan Shuangkuang Hospital. The collected tissues were preserved at −80°C till usage.

Cell culture and transfection

Human bronchial epithelial cells (16HBE) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). NSCLC cells (PC9 and HCC827) were purchased from Procell (Wuhan, China). All these cells were cultured in RPMI1640 (Procell) which were supplemented with 10% FBS (Procell) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Procell) in a 37°C incubator containing 5% CO2.

RIBOBIO (Guangzhou, China) synthesized the short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting USP22 (sh‐USP22), shRNA targeting MDM2 (sh‐MDM2) and their control sh‐NC, USP22 overexpression vector (USP22) and empty control (pcDNA). Cell transfection was conducted using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Gefitinib treatment

PC9 and HCC827 cells were seeded onto 24‐well plates and then treated with 2.5‐μM gefitinib (Sigma‐Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 24 h. The control cells were treated with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (Sigma‐Aldrich) for 24 h. The treated cells were used for further experiments.

qRT‐PCR

TRIzol reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was used to extract RNAs from the tissues and cells. cDNAs were synthesized based on the DNAs using M‐MLV Reverse Transcriptase Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). qRT‐PCR assay was performed using BeyoFast™ SYBR Green qPCR Mix (Beyotime). The relative expression was estimated using the 2−ΔΔCt method with β‐actin as the internal control. The used primers are presented in Table 1.

Western blot

The proteins in tissues and cells were isolated by using RIPA buffer (Beyotime) and quantified by using BCA protein assay kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China). Subsequently, the proteins were separated by 10% SDS‐PAGE gel and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. The membranes were blocked in 5% slim milk for 2 h, interacted overnight with primary antibodies against USP22 (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA), MDM2 (Abcam) or β‐actin (Abcam) followed by incubation with the horseradish peroxidase‐conjugated secondary antibody (Abcam) for 2 h. The protein bands were visualized by ECL Western blot kit (Beyotime).

Transwell assay

To explore the migration ability of NSCLC cells, the 24‐well transwell chambers (Corning Costar, Corning, NY, USA) were used. Briefly, PC9 and HCC827 cells were suspended in serum‐free medium and seeded into the upper chamber. The bottom chamber was filled with the culture medium. After 24 h, the migrated cells were fixed with formaldehyde, stained with crystal violet (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and counted.

Flow cytometry analysis

The Annexin V‐FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime) was used to analyze NSCLC cell apoptosis. The cells were resuspended and stained with Annexin V‐FITC and PI. Cell apoptosis was analyzed using flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA).

Detection of ROS, MDA, Fe2+, and GSH levels

The levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), malondialdehyde (MDA), Fe2+, and glutathione (GSH) in NSCLC cells were examined with ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime), Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (Beyotime), T‐AOC Assay Kit (Beyotime), and GSH Assay Kit (Beyotime), respectively. The procedures were strictly in line with the manufacturers' instructions.

C11‐BODIPY staining

By using the BODIPY 581/591 C11 fluorescent probe (ABclonal, Wuhan, China), the levels of lipid peroxidation were examined. PC9 and HCC827 cells were maintained with 10 μM BODIPY 581/591 C11 dye for 0.5 h and then washed with phosphate buffer solution (PBS) and observed under the confocal microscopy.

Cell Counting Kit‐8 (CCK‐8) assay

To detect gefitinib resistance, PC9 and HCC827 cells were seeded into 96‐well plates and exposed to gefitinib for 24 h. Next, CCK‐8 reagent (Beyotime) was added into the well and cultured for 2 h, where the absorbance was examined at 450 nm and the value of IC50 was examined.

Ubiquitination assay

PC9 and HCC827 cells were transfected with si‐NC or si‐USP22 and then cell lysates were purified with anti‐MDM2 coated beaded. Next, western blot was conducted using anti‐ubiquitinase antibody to estimate ubiquitination.

Co‐immunoprecipitation (Co‐IP) assay

The PierceTM Co‐IP reagent (Thermo Scientific, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was used for this assay. PC9 and HCC827 cells were lysed in IP buffer, and then cell lysates were collected for incubation with anti‐USP22, anti‐MDM2 or anti‐IgG for 12 h and incubation with protein A/G magnetic beads for 2 h. Subsequently, the immuno‐complexes were collected and subjected to immunoblotting using anti‐MDM2 or anti‐USP22.

In vivo experiments

The BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animals Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China) and divided into three groups (N = 5/group): PBS, gefitinib, and gefitinib+USP22. For PBS and gefitinib groups, PC9 cells were injected into the mice and the mice were fed with gefitinib (30 mg/kg) or PBS per day. For gefitinib+USP22 group, USP22 overexpression vector transfected PC9 cells were injected the mice and then fed with gefitinib (30 mg/kg) every day. The xenograft tumor length (L) and width (W) was examined every 5 days and tumor volume was calculated using (L × W2)/2. After 25 days, the mice were euthanized, and tumors were weighed and harvested for further experiments. The animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of Animal Research of Shuangyashan Shuangkuang Hospital.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) assay

Tissue sections were deparaffinized, hydrated with gradient ethanol, antigen boiled with 0.1 M sodium citrate, and endogenous peroxidase blocked with 3% hydrogen peroxide for maintenance. After blocking with 5% goat serum for 1 h, the section were incubated overnight with anti‐USP22 (Abcam) or anti‐MDM2 (Abcam) were and kept with anti‐secondary antibody for 30 min at room temperature the next day. Section by diaminobenzidine dyeing and hematoxylin counterstaining, the images were captured using a microscope.

Statistical analysis

The data were collected from three independent experiments and exhibited as mean ± standard deviation. Software GraphPad Prism 7 was utilized to analyze the data. The differences were compared by Student's t‐test or one‐way ANOVA. p < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

RESULTS

Upregulation of USP22 in NSCLC tissues and cell lines

As indicated by IHC assay, USP22 level was upregulated in NSCLC tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues (Figure 1a). Then we detected the mRNA expression level of USP22 in the collected NSCLC tissues and adjacent non‐tumor tissues (N = 31) by qRT‐PCR assay. The results showed that USP22 mRNA level was upregulated in NSCLC tissues in comparison with normal tissues (Figure 1b). Western blot assay exhibited that USP22 protein level was markedly elevated in NSCLC tissues compared with normal tissues (Figure 1c). Besides, USP22 mRNA and protein levels were higher expressed in NSCLC cells (PC9 cells and HCC827 cells) than 16HBE cells, as determined by qRT‐PCR assay and western blot assay (Figure 1d,e). These findings indicated that USP22 was dysregulated in NSCLC.

Ubiquitin‐specific peptidase 22 (USP22) was highly expressed in non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues and cells. (b, c) USP22 mRNA and protein levels in NSCLC tissues and adjacent normal tissues were measured by qRT‐PCR and western blot assays. (d, e) The mRNA and protein levels of USP22 in 16HBE, PC9, and HCC827 cells were determined by qRT‐PCR assay and western blot assay, respectively. *p < 0.05.

USP22 knockdown inhibited NSCLC cell migration and promoted apoptosis and ferroptosis

To explore the functions of USP22 in NSCLC cell progression, USP22 knockdown NSCLC cells (PC9 cells and HCC827 cells) were established by transfecting sh‐USP22 into the relative cells. As a result, sh‐USP22 transfection markedly reduced USP22 protein level in both PC9 cells and HCC827 cells compared to sh‐NC control groups, indicating the successful transfection of sh‐USP22 (Figure 2a). Transwell assay showed that USP22 silencing markedly restrained the migration of PC9 and HCC827 cells compared with sh‐NC control group (Figure 2b). Flow cytometry analysis indicated that USP22 knockdown facilitated the apoptosis of PC9 cells and HCC827 cells relative to sh‐NC group (Figure 2c). Besides, we also explored the effect of USP22 deficiency on ferroptosis in NSCLC cells. Our results showed that USP22 deficiency elevated ROS level, MDA level, and Fe2+ level and reduced GSH level in PC9 and HCC827 cells in comparison with sh‐NC groups, indicating that USP22 deficiency decreased ferroptosis in NSCLC cells (Figure 2d–g). Taken together, USP22 knockdown repressed the migration and facilitated the apoptosis and ferroptosis of NSCLC cells.

Ferroptosis facilitated the suppressive effect of gefitinib on NSCLC cells

As indicated by transwell assay, gefitinib treatment evidently repressed the migration ability of PC9 and HCC827 cells relative to DMSO‐treated cells (Figure 3a). Flow cytometry analysis indicated that gefitinib treatment promoted the apoptosis of PC9 and HCC827 cells in comparison with the DMSO group (Figure 3b). Next, C11‐BODIPY staining assay, MDA assay, Fe2+ assay, and GSH assay were conducted to verify whether ferroptosis contributed to gefitinib‐mediated repression on NSCLC cells. Our results showed that gefitinib treatment increased oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity, MDA concentration, and Fe2+ level and reduced GSH level in both PC9 and HCC827 cells, while the addition of ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostatin‐1 (Fer‐1) effectively rescued the effects (Figure 3c–g). Collectively, the inhibitory effect of gefitinib on NSCLC cells was partially dependent on the induction of ferroptosis.

Overexpression of USP22 facilitated gefitinib resistance and inhibited ferroptosis of NSCLC cells

As presented in Figure 4a, gefitinib exposure led to the marked reduction of USP22 protein level in PC9 and HCC827 cells compared to DMSO control group. Subsequently, the effects of USP22 on gefitinib resistance and ferroptosis in NSCLC cells were explored. CCK‐8 assay showed that USP22 overexpression elevated the IC50 value of gefitinib in PC9 and HCC827 cells, indicating that USP22 overexpression promoted the resistance of PC9 and HCC827 cells to gefitinib (Figure 4b). The results of transwell assay and flow cytometry analysis indicated that overexpression of USP22 effectively restored the suppressive role of gefitinib in PC9 and HCC827 cell migration and the promotional role of gefitinib in PC9 and HCC827 cell apoptosis (Figure 4c,d). Moreover, our results exhibited that USP22 overexpression decreased oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity, MDA level and Fe2+ level and increased GSH level in gefitinib‐treated PC9 and HCC827 cells (Figure 4e–i). These results illustrated that USP22 overexpression reduced gefitinib sensitivity and ferroptosis in gefitinib‐treated NSCLC cells.

USP22 stabilized MDM2 and regulated MDM2 expression

Through analyzing ubibrowser (http://ubibrowser.bio‐it.cn/ubibrowser_v3/Home/E3networkview/networkview/name/Q9UPT9/module/DUB), MDM2 was predicted to be a substrate of USP22 (Figure 5a). si‐NC or si‐USP22 transfected PC9 and HCC827 cell lysates were purified with anti‐MDM2 coated beads. Western blot assay showed that the presence of si‐USP22 markedly elevated the ubiquitination of MDM2 but reduced MDM2 protein level (Figure 5b). Co‐IP assay showed that USP22 could bind to MDM2 in PC9 and HCC827 cells, MDM2 was also detected in USP22, but not in IgG immunoprecipitates (Figure 5c). IHC assay showed that MDM2 was upregulated in NSCLC tissues compared to normal tissues (Figure 5d). Thereafter, we collected the NSCLC tissues and adjacent normal tissues from 31 NSCLC patients. Compared to normal tissues, MDM2 mRNA and protein levels were elevated in NSCLC tissues (Figure 5e,f). The mRNA and protein levels of MDM2 were apparently elevated in PC9 and HCC827 cells compared to 16HBE cells (Figure 5g,h). Additionally, we demonstrated that gefitinib‐mediated reduction of MDM2 protein level was restored by USP22 overexpression, while MDM2 deficiency partially abolished the effect of USP22 (Figure 5i). All these findings suggested that USP22 regulated MDM2 expression via the deubiquitination of MDM2.

USP22 overexpression promoted gefitinib resistance and repressed ferroptosis in gefitinib‐treated NSCLC cells by regulating MDM2 expression

Subsequently, whether USP22 participated in regulating gefitinib resistance and ferroptosis in NSCLC cells by MDM2 was explored. As suggested by CCK‐8 assay, USP22 overexpression reduced IC50 value of gefitinib in PC9 and HCC827 cells, while MDM2 knockdown restored the effect (Figure 6a). Overexpression of USP22 promoted the migration and repressed the apoptosis of gefitinib‐treated PC9 and HCC827 cells, with MDM2 silencing abated the effects (Figure 6b,c). Additionally, we demonstrated that MDM2 knockdown reversed the effects of USP22 overexpression on oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity, MDA level, Fe2+ level, and GSH level in gefitinib‐treated PC9 and HCC827 cells (Figure 6d–h). Taken together, USP22 overexpression enhanced gefitinib resistance and repressed ferroptosis in gefitinib‐treated NSCLC cells by modulating MDM2 expression.

USP22 promoted gefitinib resistance of NSCLC cells in vivo

Finally, the murine xenograft model was established to investigate the function of USP22 in vivo. As shown in Figure 7a, gefitinib treatment reduced USP22 and MDM2 protein levels in PC9 cells, whereas USP22 overexpression weakened the effect. Moreover, gefitinib led to the suppression of tumor growth (including tumor volume and tumor weight), with USP22 overexpression restored the effect (Figure 7b,c). We further examined the expression of USP22 and MDM2 in the harvested xenograft tumors by IHC assay. The results showed that USP22 and MDM2 expression was decreased in gefitinib group, while the effect was restored in gefitinib+USP22 group (Figure 7d). These outcomes illustrated that USP22 contributed to gefitinib resistance in vivo.

DISCUSSION

The use of EGFR‐TKIs can effectively treat advanced NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations. 20 However, the acquisition of drug resistance limits the clinical application of EGFR‐TKIs. 21 Therefore, study the EGFR‐TKIs‐resistant mechanism, search for genes‐associated with drug resistance, and explore ways to reverse the drug resistance have become important topics for NSCLC treatment in recent years. In this study, we demonstrated that USP22/MDM2 axis contributed to gefitinib resistance and NSCLC cell migration and decelerated NSCLC apoptosis and ferroptosis.

USP22 was overexpressed in NSCLC, USP22 silencing repressed tumor cell growth, induced apoptosis in vitro and curbed tumorigenesis in vivo by binding to MDMX and activating the p53 pathway. 22 USP22 played the oncogenic role in NSCLC by altering the stability and activity of COX‐2 via its ubiquitination regulation. 23 In accordance with the previously reported results, we also demonstrated the abnormally high expression of USP22 in NSCLC. Moreover, USP22 deficiency hindered NSCLC cell migration and facilitated apoptosis. Ferroptosis is considered to be a kind of widespread death non apoptotic cell death pathways, which is caused by iron ion and reactive oxygen species on the cell membrane lipids are destroyed, eventually lead to rupture of cell death. 24 Ferroptosis has been implicated in the efficacy of a variety of anticancer therapies and reversing drug resistance. 25 , 26 , 27 The determination of oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity, intracellular ROS, MDA, Fe2+, and GSH is widely used in the study of ferroptosis. 28 , 29 Thus, we investigated the influence of USP22 on ferroptosis by determining the levels of oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity, ROS, MDA, Fe2+, and GSH in NSCLC cells. Our results exhibited that deficiency of USP22 elevated the levels of oxidized C11‐BODIPY fluorescence intensity, ROS, MDA, and Fe2+ and reduced the level of GSH in NSCLC cells, indicating that USP22 knockdown promoted ferroptosis by oxidative stress‐induced lipid peroxidation, GSH depletion, and ROS upregulation.

USP22 is linked to chemoresistance in human cancers. 30 , 31 Herein, we firstly explored the function of USP22 on gefitinib sensitivity in NSCLC. It was found that overexpression of USP22 contributed to cell migration, gefitinib resistance, and inhibited ferroptosis in gefitinib‐resistant NSCLC cells. Additionally, we demonstrated that ferroptosis promoted the suppressive effect of gefitinib on NSCLC cells. Furthermore, the promotional effect of USP22 on gefitinib resistance in vivo was validated through in vivo experiments.

Subsequently, the underlying mechanisms of USP22 in NSCLC progression and gefitinib were studied. USP22 was verified to stabilize and modulate MDM2 expression by the deubiquitination of MDM2. MDM2 enhanced the resistance of NSCLC cells to EGFR‐TKIs and predicted the poor prognosis of NSCLC patients. 32 TNFAIP8 knockdown altered MDM2 expression and p53 ubiquitination to promoted cisplatin sensitivity to NSCLC. 33 In this study, we found that MDM2 silencing ameliorated the effects of USP22 on gefitinib resistance, cell migration, apoptosis, and ferroptosis in gefitinib‐treated NSCLC cells, suggesting that USP22 overexpression promoted gefitinib resistance and repressed ferroptosis by MDM2 deubiquitination.

Taken together, USP22 overexpression contributed to gefitinib resistance and inhibited ferroptosis in NSCLC cells by the deubiquitination of MDM2. The study might provide a novel strategy for overcoming gefitinib resistance in NSCLC.

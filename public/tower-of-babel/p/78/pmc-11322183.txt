NLRP3-mediated IL-1β in regulating the imbalance between Th17 and Treg in experimental autoimmune prostatitis
Authors: Xianhong Liu, Jing Chen, Shaoyu Yue, Cheng Zhang, Jian Song, Hu Liang, Chaozhao Liang, Xianguo Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS) is a urinary disorder that affects youthful to middle-aged men most frequently. It has been revealed that Th17/Treg imbalance is a crucial factor in the pathophysiological mechanisms behind this disease. However, this imbalance’s mechanisms are unknown. In the experimental autoimmune prostatitis (EAP) mouse model, the NLRP3 inflammasome was turned on, IL-1β levels went up. Moreover, there exists a discernible positive association between the upsurge in IL-1β and the perturbation of Th17/Treg equilibrium. Additionally, we have revealed that IL-1β plays a vital role in promoting the differentiation of Naïve CD4+ T cells into the Th17 cells and enhances the conversion of Treg cells into Th17 cells. Further studies revealed that IL-1β promotes STAT3 phosphorylation, which is what causes Treg cells to become Th17 cells. All data strongly suggest that the NLRP3 inflammatory influence Th17 cell development and the conversion of Treg cells into Th17 cells through IL-1β, disrupting the Th17/Treg balance and exacerbating EAP inflammation. In this article, we provide new theories for the pathogenesis of CP/CPPS and propose new prevention and therapy methods.

Introduction

CP/CPPS predominantly affects middle-aged and young adult males, with an estimated incidence ranging from 8.4 to 14%1. Patients with CP/CPPS often show clinical symptoms, including urinary urgency, frequency, and pelvic pain2, significantly impacting their quality of life3,4. More and more proof suggests that immune imbalance plays a crucial role in the formation of CP/CPPS5. However, the precise processes of how it develops in CP/CPPS remain unknown. Therefore, conducting in-depth research into its potential mechanisms in CP/CPPS holds significant importance for developing new clinical treatments for individuals suffering from CP/CPPS.

Th17 cells, which have high RORγt expression, are a highly pathogenic subset of effector T cells that cause inflammation and tissue damage. Numerous inflammatory mediators, such as IL-17, can be released by Th17 cells, resulting in neutrophil accumulation and tissue injury6. Previous research has demonstrated that EAP mice have a greater proportion of Th17 cells, and that blocking IL-17 reduces prostatic tissue inflammation7. Conversely, regulatory T cells (Tregs) with high FOXP3 expression can decrease inflammation generated by other T cell subsets, including Th17 cells8. The FOXP3 is required for Treg formation, function, and maintenance, and FOXP3 inactivating mutations cause immunological dysregulation, polyendocrinopathies, and enteropathies in humans9. Despite the critical role Tregs play in immune protection, they are not terminally stable cells and display plasticity in response to changes in the microenvironment10,11. Multiple investigations show that the Treg cell percentage decreases substantially in the prostates of EAP mice and CP/CPPS patients while the Th17 cell percentage increases significantly12,13. This results in an imbalance of Th17/Treg, which may play a role in both the onset and progression of CP/CPPS and EAP. However, the fundamental mechanisms causing the imbalance in the Th17/Treg remain unknown and require further research in EAP.

Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) has a significant role in the development and progression of inflammatory diseases14, and its significant elevation has been demonstrated in prostate inflammation15. In EAP mice, reducing the levels of IL-1β can alleviate tissue inflammatory infiltration and pain symptoms16. The development and release of IL-1β are critically dependent on activating the NLRP3 inflammasome17. According to earlier findings, in an asthma mouse model, suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome is shown to regulate the Th17/Treg immunological imbalance and lowers airway inflammation18. Whether it is IL-1β regulate Th17/Treg imbalance still unclear.

This work, we showed that the NLRP3 inflammasome was turned on in EAP mice, which caused IL-1β levels dramatically rise. Along with this, there was an elevated percentage of 17+Treg cells and Th17 cells. Furthermore, we noticed a strong positive correlation between IL-1β concentration and 17+Treg and Th17 cell percentage. We saw that the suppressive effect of 17+Treg cells was weaker in EAP mice by comparing it to the suppressive function of Treg cells. Lowering IL-1β in EAP mice significantly reduces Th17/Treg imbalance, inflammation, and pelvic discomfort. Additionally, our in vitro cell differentiation experiments revealed that IL-1β plays a vital role in promoting the differentiation of Naïve CD4+ T cells into the Th17 cells and enhances the conversion of Treg cells into Th17 cells. Further studies showed that IL-1β drives the shift of Treg to Th17 by enhancing STAT3 phosphorylation. Based on our data, it is evident that the NLRP3 inflammasome regulates Th17 differentiation and the transformation of Treg to Th17 through the mediation of IL-1β, causing Th17/Treg imbalance and exacerbating the progression of inflammation in EAP. These results show that targeting NLRP3 and IL-1β may hold potential as therapeutic intervention points for treating CP/CPPS.

Preparation of mice

The EAP animal models were created using male NOD mice (weight = 18 ± 2 g; age = 5 weeks), which were gotten from the Nanjing Institute of Biomedical at Nanjing University in China. All experimental methods were carried out in accordance with the Guidelines for Ethical Conduct in the Care and Use of Animals published by the Animal Centre of Anhui Medical University. Anesthesia and euthanasia of mice are performed in full compliance with the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Animal Euthanasia Guidelines (2020). Mice were euthanized by anesthesia with isoflurane.

The establishment, grouping, and pain measuring of the EAP mouse model

The prostate antigen obtained by grinding the SD rat prostate tissue was fully emulsified to prepare the immune response reagent. A comparable volume of CFA was added19,20. On day 0 and day 1521, male NOD mice were subcutaneously injected at three different sites: the mouse shoulder (0.050 ml), the root of tail (0.050 ml), and both footpads (0.025 ml, one side) with 0.15 ml of the immune response reagent (300 mg/mouse). During the corresponding period, the control group of mice received 0.9% physiological saline instead of the prostate antigen and were finally euthanized on day 28. When the mice were euthanized, we observed that the mice’s footpads were pink and that the mice did not exhibit abnormal behavior while walking or standing. Dissolve MCC950 in PBS and achieve a concentration of 1 mg/mL. From day 16, the (EAP+MCC950) mice received an injection of 10 mg/kg of MCC950 through the intraperitoneal route for three days, then every 2 days16. At the same time, the other two groups of mice received an equivalent amount of carrier (PBS) intraperitoneally. From day 14, the (EAP+IL-1R inhibitor) mice received anti–IL-1R antibody (200 µg/dose; clone JAMA-147; Bio X Cell), intraperitoneally every 3 days. On the 28th day, we assessed the mice’s response frequency to pelvic stimulation. Positive responses to pain include (a) a sharp contraction of the abdominal muscles, (b) an instant scratching or licking of the fiber-stimulated area, or (c) jumping.

ELISA, immunohistochemistry, and HE staining were done

The prostate tissue was subjected to fixation using a 4% formaldehyde solution, followed by embedding in paraffin and subsequent sectioning into slices measuring 4 μm in thickness. HE staining and IHC were performed on these 4-µm-thick prostate tissue slices. The degree of prostate inflammation was graded using a previously described grading system, with scores ranging from 0 to 3, where 0 signals non-inflammation and 3 denotes substantial inflammatory cell infiltration15. On sections of prostate tissue fixed in paraffin, immunohistochemistry was carried out. Each section was individually deparaffinized in xylene, and then it was placed in an incubator containing ethanol at one of three distinct concentrations: 100%, 85%, or 75%. Serum was blocked for 30 min following endogenous peroxidase burst and antigen repair using EDTA antigen repair buffer. Primary antibodies were applied as follows: IL-1β (GB11113, China) at a dilution of 1:700, caspase1 (GB11383, China) at a dilution of 1:1000, ASC (GB113966, China) at a dilution of 1:2000, NLRP3 (GB114320, China) at a dilution of 1:400, and Pro-IL-1β (12242S, China) at a dilution of 1:200. The secondary antibody utilized in this study was the goat anti-rabbit/mouse IgG (1:200 dilution; Servicebio, China). The Digital Section Scanner (Pannoramic MIDI, 3DHistech, Hungary) was used to collect slice images. The levels of mouse serum IL-1β, IL-17, and IL-10 were quantified using the ELISA method, following the instructions provided by the manufacturer.

Analysis by flow cytometry

Mouse Th17, Treg, and 17+Treg percentages were quantified through flow cytometry. Briefly, after removing the mouse spleens, they were gently ground in PBS solution and then filtered through a strainer (200 mesh) to obtain a single-cell suspension of the spleens, which was then lysed for erythrocytes and rinsed twice with PBS. The cells were then put into tubes and exposed to FITC labeled CD4 surface antibodies for incubation. Following another PBS wash, 1 ml of 1640 culture media (including 5% fetal bovine serum) containing PMA, ionomycin, and monensin was put into each tube, and they were kept at 37 °C for 4 h. Following another wash, the cells were subjected to fixation, permeabilization, and incubation with intracellular antibodies, including PE-labeled FOXP3 and BV421-labeled IL-17A, at 4 °C for 60 min. After the last wash, samples were examined with the FACSCalibur flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA), and the CytExpert software was used to process the results.

Western blotting

We assessed the levels of expression for NLRP3, Pro-caspase-1, GAPDH, caspase-1, ASC, Pro-IL-1β, IL-1β, Phospho-STAT3 and STAT3. RIPA lysis solution from ThermoFisher Scientific in the United States, which contains protease and phosphatase inhibitors from Beyotime in China, was used to lyse prostate tissue and Treg cells cultivated under Th17 polarization conditions. NLRP3, Phospho-STAT3, and other large molecular weight proteins were isolated using 10% SDS -polyacrylamide gel, whereas ASC, IL-1β and other Small molecular weight proteins were isolated using 12.5% SDS -polyacrylamide gel, then transferred to a nitrocellulose (NC) membrane. Following an hour-long blocking step with 5% BSA, the membrane underwent treatment with appropriate primary antibodies, which were diluted at a ratio of 1:1000, and incubated overnight at a temperature of 4 °C. As a reference, GAPDH (1:1000, Affinity) was used. Following the washing step with TBST, the membrane underwent incubation with the suitable secondary antibodies (anti-mouse or anti-rabbit IgG, 1:5000, Elabscience) for a duration of 1 h, after which it was subjected to another round of washing. The detection of proteins was completed using an ECL (enhanced chemiluminescence) apparatus (ChemiScope5600; China).

Isolation and differentiation of naive CD4+ T cells

The Naïve CD4+ T cells were extracted and differentiated in vitro according to a previously published method from our laboratory22. Antibodies against CD3 and CD28 were placed onto a 24-well plate the day before T cell isolation, which was then covered and wrapped in parafilm to avoid evaporation and contamination. At 4 °C, the plate was incubated. Naive CD4+ T cells were isolated from mouse spleen using the Naive CD4+T Cell Isolation Kit (cat#130-104-453; Miltenyi Biotec, CA, USA) and then divided into 12 wells (100,000 cells per well) and cultured in 1640 medium (Bio-channel, BC-M017) containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiotics. The 12 wells were divided into 4 groups: NO group, NC group, NC+MCC950 group and NC+MCC950+IL-1β group. The “NC” group: The IL-6 (40 ng/mL), IL-2 (20 ng/mL), IL-21 (20 ng/mL), IL-23 (40 ng/mL), and TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL) were added to all wells in the group. The “NC+MCC950” group: The IL-6 (40 ng/mL), IL-2 (20 ng/mL), IL-21 (20 ng/mL), IL-23 (40 ng/mL), TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL), and MCC950 (10 umol/L) were added to all wells in the group. The “NC+MCC950+IL-1β” group: The IL-6 (40 ng/mL), IL-2 (20 ng/mL), IL-21 (20 ng/mL), IL-23 (40 ng/mL), TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL), MCC950 (10 umol/L), and IL-1β (20 ng/mL) were added to all wells in the group. The “NO” group: No differentiated cytokines were added to all wells in the group. Cell were collected after 4 days and used for subsequent analysis.

Sorting of cells

At four degrees Celsius, fluorescence-linked antibodies against CD25, CD4, and CCR6 were applied to the splenocytes of normal mice and EAP animals for sixty minutes. The cells were segregated into three distinct groups: CD4+CD25- CCR6-, CD4+ CD25+ CCR6-, CD4+CD25+ CCR6+. All sorted cells were above 95% pure.

CD4+CD25+Treg cells differentiate experiment in vitro

On the day before Treg cell purification, anti-CD28 and anti-CD3 were coated onto a 24-well plate by dissolving them in sterile PBS. The plate was wrapped and covered with parafilm to prevent contamination and evaporation, then incubated at 4 °C. CD4+CD25+Treg cells isolated from mouse spleen were divided into 12 wells (100,000 cells per well) and cultured in 1640 medium (Bio-channel, BC-M017) containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiotics. The 12 wells were divided into 4 groups: NO group, NC group, NC+MCC950 group and NC+MCC950+IL-1β group. The “NC” group: The IL-6 (40 ng/mL), IL-2 (20 ng/mL), IL-21 (20 ng/mL), IL-23 (40 ng/mL), and TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL) were added to all wells in the group. The “NC+MCC950” group: The IL-6 (40 ng/mL), IL-2 (20 ng/mL), IL-21 (20 ng/mL), IL-23 (40 ng/mL), TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL), and MCC950 (10 umol/L) were added to all wells in the group. The “NC+MCC950+IL-1β” group: The IL-6 (40 ng/mL), IL-2 (20 ng/mL), IL-21 (20 ng/mL), IL-23 (40 ng/mL), TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL), MCC950 (10 umol/L), and IL-1β (20 ng/mL) were added to all wells in the group. The “NO” group: No differentiated cytokines were added to all wells in the group. The wells were shaken every 24 h to prevent cell aggregation. After 96 h, the cells were collected and the proportions of th17, Treg, and 17+ Treg cells were detected by flow cytometry.

Comparison of suppressive functions between Treg and 17+Treg

CFSE-prestained CD4+CD25- T cells (Teffs) were used to compare 17+Treg and Treg suppressive activities. Then, Teffs were mixed 1:1 with Treg (CD4+CD25+CCR6-T cells) and 17+Treg (defined as CD4+CD25+CCR6+cells with high IL-17 expression levels23) (Figure S1) and placed in wells of a 24-well plate pre-coated with anti-CD28 and anti-CD3 antibodies. Then, each well received 1 ml of a culture media containing IL-2 (20 ng/ml), 1% antibiotics and 10% FBS. The cells were incubated for a duration of 72 h at a temperature of 37 °C and in an environment containing 5% carbon dioxide. BD (USA) flow cytometers analysed CFSE staining after incubation. FLOWJO fitted and interpreted the findings.

Polymerase chain reaction in real time

According to the information provided by the manufacturer, TRIzol was used to extract total RNA from cells or tissues. Utilizing the Fast Quant RT Kit, cDNA synthesis was carried out (Tiangen, KR 106 -02). All qPCR experiments were carried out on the ABI 7500 system of PCR (USA). The following primers were employed: FOXP3 (expressed in Treg cells): Forward: 5′-AAAACCAAGAAAAGGTGGGC-3′, Reverse: 5′-ATGATCTAGGAGGGCAGCTA-3′. RORγt (expressed in Th17 cells): Forward: 5′-TGGCACACAATCTCTTCCTT-3′, Reverse: 5′-CGGTCCTCTGCTTCTCTTAG-3′. A 20 ul volume was used for all reactions, with mouse GAPDH as the internal control. The comparative CT approach was used to assess gene expression.

Statistics

Each experiment had three independent replications. Data were displayed using SD (standard deviation). In the statistical analysis, we used either a student’s t-test with two tails or a one-way analysis of variance. To do the statistical analysis, GraphPad Prism 9 was utilized.

An imbalance in Th17/Treg and an increase in the proportion of 17+Treg cells in EAP

Initially, EAP mouse model was built through the subcutaneous immunization of mice with a blend of PAgs (prostate antigens) and CFA (Fig. 1A). Histological examination and pelvic pain measurements showed that both inflammatory cell infiltration of prostate tissue and the frequency of response to tactile abnormal pain was substantially increased in EAP groups (Fig. 1B and D), suggesting that the EAP model has been established with great success. Then, the Th17 cell percentage in the spleens of EAP mice was found to be significantly elevated by flow cytometry (Fig. 1G and H). Conversely, the proportion of Treg cells was decreased (Fig. 1E and F). Thus, the Th17/Treg is severely imbalanced in EAP mice. Moreover, we noticed a CD4+IL-17+FOXP3+T cell (referred to as 17+Treg) percentage Significant increase in the EAP mice (Fig. 1I and J). Prior studies have demonstrated a decrease in the immunosuppressive capacity of regulatory T (Treg) cells in EAP mice24. It is still unknown whether or not this decrease has any connection to the rise in the percentage of 17+Treg cells. Thus, we examined 17+Treg and Treg cell immunosuppression in EAP mice. Results showed that 17+Treg cells have much weaker immunosuppressive function than Treg cells (Fig. 2A and B). Additionally, we evaluated the cytokine levels associated with Treg and Th17 cells. In the EAP group, the serum concentration of IL-17A and IL-1β were found to be increased (Fig. 2G and H), while the level of IL-10 was found to be decreased (Fig. 2I). In the final step of the experiment, we examined the levels of IL-17 A and FOXP3 in the prostate tissue of both groups using immunofluorescence. NLRP3 inflammasome promote IL-1β expression

NLRP3 inflammasome is crucial to inflammation, according to study 25. So, we examined EAP mouse prostate tissue NLRP3 inflammasome expression. We found that NLRP3, ASC, and cleaved-caspase-1 were upregulated in prostate tissue in EAP mice (Fig. 3A–F,H). NLRP3 inflammasome activation releases IL-1β, which promotes the development of Th17 cells26. Therefore, IL-1β expression in EAP mouse prostate tissue was evaluated. The results revealed a substantial rise in the expression of IL-1β in EAP mice prostate tissue (Fig. 3A,G). The aforementioned results suggest that the prostate tissue of EAP mice exhibits activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and increased expression of IL-1β. Next, we explored whether NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition altered IL-1β levels and inflammation severity in EAP mice. MCC950, a selective NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor, was administered intraperitoneally into EAP mice. Initially, through H&E staining, we observed that after treatment with MCC950, the interstitial mononuclear cell infiltration in EAP mice’s prostate tissue was significantly reduced (Fig. 4A) and inflammation scores were also decreased (Fig. 4C). Additionally, pain testing revealed that the frequency of abnormal pain responses was also significantly attenuated after MCC950 treatment (Fig. 4B). Then, western blotting and immunohistochemistry showed decreased protein expression, including NLRP3, caspase-1 and IL-1β in prostate tissues treated with MCC950 (Fig. 4D–K). Lastly, an assessment was conducted on the cytokine levels linked to Th17 and Treg cells in the three groups. The outcomes suggested that MCC950 reduced serum IL-17A and IL-1β in mice (Fig. 5A and B), while IL-10 expression tended to increase, but the data had no statistical significance (Fig. 5C). Th17/Treg Imbalance and 17+ Treg cell percentage positively correlate with IL-1β Levels

To explore the relationship between IL-1β levels, the Th17/Treg imbalance, and the percentage of 17+Treg cells, we performed a correlation analysis between the levels of IL-1β and mouse Th17, Treg, 17+Treg cell percentages. The Th17 cell percentage was positively correlated with IL-1β levels, according to the findings (Fig. 5D). There was a declining trend in the Treg cell percentage as IL-1β levels increased. However, our data show no correlation (Fig. 5E). Furthermore, as IL-1β levels rose, the proportion of 17+Treg cells increased proportionally, with a clear positive correlation between them (Fig. 5F). The above data indicate that mice in the EAP group had a significant imbalance in Th17 /Treg and an increased proportion of 17+ Treg cells, which correlated with their elevated IL-1β.

Inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome reduces IL-1β levels, alleviates Th17/Treg imbalance, and reduces the proportion of 17+ Treg cells

In MCC950-treated mice, Th17 cells decreased significantly (Fig. 6B), Treg cells increased somewhat (Fig. 6D), and 17+ Treg cells decreased significantly (Fig. 6F). Additionally, we also assessed FOXP3 and RORγt mRNA levels in prostate tissue. The outcomes revealed that after MCC950 treatment, RORγt’s relative mRNA expression content decreased (Fig. 6G), and FOXP3’s increased (Fig. 6H). IL-1β promote Treg-to-Th17 cell conversion

It is believed that 17+ Treg cells serve as an intermediate in Treg-to-Th17 conversion, and in EAP mice, we found that the 17+ Treg cell percentage increased as IL-1β levels raised. Thus, we investigated whether IL-1β promotes Treg to Th17 conversion. We conducted in vitro differentiation experiments using purified Treg cells from the control mice. The results revealed that under Th17 cell polarization, numerous CD4+CD25+Treg cells lost FOXP3 and eventually differentiated into Th17 cells expressing IL-17 alone (Fig. 8C and D). We observed that inhibition of NLRP3 had no significant inhibitory effect on the conversion of Treg cells to Th17 cells, but exogenous supplementation of IL-1β significantly promoted the conversion of Treg to Th17 cells (Fig. 8D and E). In addition, as the concentration of IL-1β rose, the proportion of differentiated Th17 cells also increased (Fig. 8F and G). In vitro experiments, Treg cells were unable to spontaneously secrete IL-1β, so we concluded that inhibition of NLRP3 itself did not affect Treg to Th17 conversion, while IL-1β promoted Treg to Th17 conversion.

IL-1β promotes the transformation of Treg to Th17 by enhancing STAT3 phosphorylation

According to research, the phosphorylation of STAT3 plays a significant part in regulating the balance of Th17/ Treg 27. We investigated whether IL-1β causes the shift of Treg to Th17 through enhanced STAT3 phosphorylation. We examined the expression of (pY)-STAT3 during the differentiation of Treg cells to Th17 cells. The results showed that exogenous IL-1β significantly increased (pY)-STAT3 protein (Fig. 8H and I). Subsequently, we examined the effect of the STAT3-specific inhibitor NSC 74,859 on the shift of Treg to Th17. we observed that the inhibitor suppressed the shift of Treg to Th17 (Fig. 8J and K). So, the above results suggest that IL-1β may help convert Treg to Th17 by making STAT3 phosphorylation stronger.

Discussion

According to studies, CP/CPPS may be caused by immunological imbalance. Among them, Th17/Treg imbalance is significant in developing CP/CPPS, but the underlying process remains a mystery. In this study, we have discovered that NLRP3 inflammasome exerts its influence on Th17 differentiation and the shift of Treg to Th17 through the mediation of IL-1β, thus contributing to the imbalance of Th17/Treg and exacerbating the progression of inflammation in EAP. This study gives theoretical evidence for CP/CPPS pathogenesis and novel preventive and treatment techniques.

Research has already shown that the NLRP3 inflammasome’s activation worsens inflammatory and immunological disorders28. Inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome reduces renal inflammation in a lupus-susceptible mouse model17, and also modulates Th17/Treg immune imbalance, which attenuates airway inflammation in an asthma mouse model18. This study revealed that NLRP3 inflammasome is activated in EAP mice with elevated IL-1β levels, Th17, and 17+ Treg percentage. In contrast, the Treg percentage was decreased, the Th17/Treg was markedly dysregulated, and markedly exacerbated inflammation in EAP mice. Currently, 17+ Treg cells are considered to be an intermediate state in the conversion of Treg to Th1729, and their numbers are markedly elevated in many inflammation-associated diseases such as periodontitis, Crohn’s disease, ulcerative colitis, and rheumatoid arthritis. The elevated number of 17 +Tregs in the EAP mice suggests that some of the Tregs may be converted to Th17 cells in the EAP mice. This conversion may be one of the reasons for the Th17/Treg imbalance in EAP; however, the precise mechanism remains unclear. It is worth noting that NLRP3 inflammasome activation releases highly pro-inflammatory IL-1β30. IL-1β enhances Th17 differentiation31. Therefore, we explored whether it is IL-1β that affects the Th17/Treg balance in EAP. Our study shows that inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome can reduce IL-1β levels, decrease Th17 and 17+Treg percentage of EAP, and alleviate Th17/Treg imbalance. By blocking IL-1β signaling in EAP mice, it was further confirmed that IL-1β affects Th17/Treg balance in EAP. Our vitro cell differentiation assays showed that IL-1β promoted naïve CD4+T and Treg to Th17 differentiation in vitro. Further investigation revealed that IL-1β enhances STAT3 phosphorylation, which drives the transformation of Treg to Th17. These findings indicate that IL-1β plays a crucial role in regulating the imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells. In their study, Chunmei Zhao et al. discovered that the NLRP3 inflammasome controls the differentiation of Th17 cells in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), which can be suppressed by blocking IL-1 receptors32. Our research provides direct evidence supporting the involvement of IL-1β in both Th17 cell differentiation and the conversion of Tregs into Th17 cells. Consequently, we conclude that inhibiting the NLRP3 inflammasome leads to a reduction in IL-1β production, thereby mitigating the imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells and suppressing inflammation in EAP mice. In addition, we also found that during the transformation of Treg to Th17, with the loss of FOXP3 in Treg cells, part of them completely transformed into Th17 cells, and the remaining part became what kind of cells and what role they would play in the body is not very clear at present, which still needs further study.

In conclusion, this study has demonstrated that NLRP3 inflammasome regulates Th17/Treg immune imbalance in EAP through IL-1β mediation. Through this research, we have provided novel theoretical evidence for CP/CPPS pathogenesis and identified new strategies and targets for its prevention and treatment.

Clinical outcomes by bone metastasis uptake pattern in differentiated thyroid cancer
Authors: Guannan Wang, Fang Feng, Suo Huang, Chao Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundsPatients with differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) and bone metastases (BM) exhibit heterogeneous radioactive iodine (RAI) and fluorine-18-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) uptake patterns at diagnosis. This study is aimed to evaluate clinical outcomes influenced these distinct uptake patterns.MethodsPatients confirmed DTC with BM were involved in this study between 2006 and 2021. All patients received 131I treatment and performed 18F-FDG positron emission tomography with computed tomography (PET/CT) at diagnosis. Introduction

Bone metastasis (BM) represents the second most common site of distant metastasis in differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) [1] and is associated with significantly poorer prognosis compared to lymph node or lung metastases [2–4]. Conventional BM treatments include radioactive iodine (RAI), surgical resection, external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), chemotherapy, and targeted therapy. However, rising incidence of RAI-refractory BM lesions frequently leads to rapid survival decline, with death often occurring within three years [5, 6]. While prior studies have identified prognostic factors in metastatic DTC [5, 7–10], few have specifically analyzed outcomes in the BM subgroup.

Notably, metabolic parameters from fluorine-18-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography with computed tomography (¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT) have demonstrated prognostic value across malignancies [11–14], including RAI-treated DTC [15]. Critically, heterogeneous RAI and FDG uptake patterns in DTC-associated BM at diagnosis correlate with differential clinical outcomes. To optimize risk stratification and enable personalized therapy, this study evaluates: prognostic implications of distinct RAI/FDG uptake patterns in DTC patients with BM; combined utility of pretreatment ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters and clinical indices for identifying high-risk patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

We retrospectively reviewed medical records Of DTC patients treated at Xinhua Hospital, Shanghai Jiaotong University School Of Medicine, between 2006 and 2021. This study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of Xinhua Hospital. The inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) histologically confirmed DTC; (3) minimum follow-up of 5 years post-BM diagnosis (time from diagnosis to the data analysis), completion of total/near-total thyroidectomy with subsequent RAI therapy with and (4) BM diagnosis based on at least one of the following modalities: (a) pathological 131I uptake on the whole-body scan (WBS); (b) radiographic evidence on X-ray, CT, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), bone scintigraphy, and 18F-FDG PET/CT; and (c) histopathological verification. BM was classified as synchronous metastasis if they were detected at the time of DTC diagnosis and metachronous metastasis if they were found later in the follow-up (> 6 months).

The exclusion criteria: patients with aggressive malignant variants, including tall-cell, columnar, poorly differentiated, and diffuse sclerosing thyroid cancer; patients with synchronous primary extra-thyroidal cancers and limited life expectancy (< 6 months); and incomplete clinical data. A final cohort of 67 patients was analyzed (Fig. 1). Adjuvant therapies included RAI, surgical resection, EBRT, chemotherapy, and kinase inhibitors (Anlotinib Hydrochloride). Serum thyroid hormone withdrawal (THW), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), thyroglobulin (Tg), and thyroglobulin antibody (TgAb) levels [15] were recorded after THW for three weeks before the therapy. The patients’ progression was defined based on the following conditions: (1) occurrence of new BM; (2) a 25.0% increase in measurable lesions’ size on CT or MRI [16]; (3) a 25.0% subjective increase in the size of ill-defined lesions on CT or MRI [16]; (4) a 30.0% increased uptake in ill-defined lesions on PET/CT imaging; and a 25.0% increase in the Tg or TgAb level [16, 17].

18F-FDG PET/CT scan

18F-FDG PET/CT scans were conducted using a Biograph 64 PET/CT scanner (Siemens AG, Munich, Germany) in our department. Before injection, all patients fasted for ≥ 6 h to maintain their serum glucose levels at < 7 mmol/L. 18F-FDG doses of 5.55 MBq/kg were injected after resting for 15–20 min. Furthermore, a PET image was acquired around 1 h following intravenous injection. With scan parameters as 120 kV and 100–150 mAs, the lowest milliampere setting was used during the CT scan for attenuation correction. Additionally, PET images were reconstructed using 3D ordered-subset expectation maximization (3 iterations and 24 subsets).

131I therapy and post-therapy 131I WBS

All patients received more than one-time RAI treatment (11100–136900 Mbq); the first dose for eliminating residual thyroid was 50–100 mCi and the dose of bone metastasis was 200 mCi within 6 or 12-month intervals. Since all patients withdraw exogenous thyroid hormone medications and followed an iodine restriction diet, the TSH stimulation was > 30 µIU/ml before RAI therapy. Post-therapy 131I WBS was performed after three days following high-dose 131I. Single-photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography (SPECT/CT) scanning was performed to better localize the skeletal lesions, when necessary. The interval between 131I WBS and PET/CT was no more than four weeks.

Image analysis

Any non-physiological iodine uptake at the skeletal level on 131I WBS was considered abnormal. For 18F-FDG PET/CT scan, BM lesions consisted of abnormal and pathological FDG uptake or no uptake lesions with osteoblastic or osteolytic alterations that were seen on the CT scan. The different uptake models of bone lesions on 131I WBS and PET/CT images were classified: RAI+/FDG+, RAI+/FDG- and RAI-/FDG+. We also calculated quantitative ratios of BMs both for RAI(+) and FDG(+). Quantitative Ratios: RAI(+) ratio = Number of RAI(+) BM lesions/Total BM lesions; FDG(+) ratio = Number of FDG(+) BM lesions/Total BM lesions. The total BM lesions were defined as all the bone lesions detected by CT, MRI, PET/CT or SPECT/CT.

The PET/CT-based metabolic parameters included maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG). While MTV refers to the estimated tumor volume with increased tracer uptake, the TLG estimates the summed metabolic activity inside MTV [18]. For quantitative analysis, volume viewer software measured the SUVmax, MTV and TLG on PET/CT images. We used a semi-automatic method with a 40% SUVmax threshold within a predetermined ROI and SUVmax > 2.5 to calculate MTV and TLG. All SUVmax parameters were calculated by the maximum SUV of the lesion divided by the mean SUV of the liver.

Statistical analysis

Contiguous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (S.D.) or median with range. The receiver-operating characteristic curves (ROCs) and the areas under the curve (AUCs) were used to determine the optimal cutoff values. Variables were analyzed between the progression group and non-progression groups using the Mann-Whitney U and chi-square tests. The progression free survival (PFS) was defined as the time between treatment to evidence of tumor progression in DTC patients with BM. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time between the diagnosis date Of DTC with BM and the date of death or the last follow-up. Moreover, survival estimates were evaluated by Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank test. Cox regression analyses were performed for multivariate predictive variables. Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS 26.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). All DTC patients with BM received 18F-FDG PET/CT scan and 131I WBS during RAI treatment. The positive uptake of bone lesions on 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging was found in 49 (73.1%) DTC patients, while RAI focal lesions on 131I WBS were found in 58 (86.5%) patients. There were three uptake models of bone lesions: RAI+/PET-were observed in 18 (26.9%) patients; RAI+/PET + were found in 40 (59.7%) patients and RAI-/PET + were observed in 9 (13.4%) patients. The RAI(+) ratio and PET(+) ratio were measured in the group of RAI+/PET + patients. Our results showed 26 patients (65.0%) presented high RAI(+) ratio (> 50.0%) and 14 patients (35.0%) revealed high PET(+) ratio (> 50.0%). The mean value of TSH-stimulated Tg, TgAb and TSH at the time of BM diagnosis were 432.44 ± 384.21 ng/mL (16.60-177.50 ng/mL), 7.60 ± 9.36 IU/mL (0.04–53.41 IU/mL), and 45.32 ± 25.53 uIU/mL (16.60-177.50 uIU/mL), respectively. All patients received RAI treatment; meanwhile, 22 patients underwent surgery to resect the lesions and relieve the symptoms of compression, 12 patients were performed EBRT and 5 patients received target therapy.

Bone lesion analysis

A total of 207 BM lesions were detected in 67 patients, including 74, 77, 26 and 30 lesions observed on 18F-FDG PET/CT, 131I SPECT/CT, CT, and MRI, respectively. The most common metastatic sites were the spine (32 lesions, 15.4%), ribs (24 lesions, 11.6%), pelvic bone (42 lesions, 20.3%), and limb bone (18 lesions, 8.7%). Of all the bone lesions, osteolytic, osteoblastic lesions, and mixed osteolytic-osteoblastic lesions were reported 131 (63.3%), 20 (9.7%) and 56 (27.0%), respectively.

PET/CT metabolic parameters in predicting prognosis

Among all the patients, 35 patients received 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging prior to initial therapy. Total metabolic tumor burden of bone lesions on 18F-FDG PET/CT was quantified as: tMTV, summed metabolic tumor volume of all bone lesions; tTLG, the total lesion glycolysis across all bone lesions. Survival analysis

During a median follow-up of 56 months (range: 20–156 months) for overall survival (OS) and 40 months (range: 4-156 months) for progression-free survival (PFS), disease progression occurred in 28 patients (41.8%). Mortality was observed in 25 patients (37.3%), with 9 deaths (13.4%) directly attributed to BM and 16 to DTC complications or underlying comorbidities (e.g., respiratory failure or cardiac events). The OS rate was 100% (average 61.6 months, 95% confidence interval (CI), 54.69–68.67) at 1 Year, 86.6% (66.8 months, 95% CI, 59.60–74.01) at 3 Years, and 43.3% (87.4 months, 95% CI, 78.01–96.81) at 5 years (Fig. 3A). The PFS rate was 90.9% (average 142.6 months, 95% CI, 132.43-152.83), 56.7% (97.4 months, 95% CI, 81.34–113.45), and 32.8% (68.8 months, 95% CI, 54.61–83.12) at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively (Fig. 3B).

Discussion

DTC exhibits a high propensity for bone metastasis, ranking as the third most common solid tumor to metastasize to bone after breast and prostate carcinomas [20]. While DTC patients typically demonstrate favorable 10-year OS rates of 85–93% [21], hose with distant metastases experience markedly reduced survival (50% at 5 years and 21% at 10 years) [22–24]. Our results showed that the OS rate was 100%, 86.6%, and 43.3% at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years in DTC patients with BM, and these results are in accordance with previously reported survival rates. The PFS rate of DTC patients with BM was 56.7% at 3 years and decreased to 32.8% at 5 years in this study. This underscores the critical need for early identification of high-risk BM patients to guide personalized treatment strategies.

Established predictors of poor DTC outcomes include advanced age, follicular histology, early BM onset (< 3 years post-diagnosis), RAI-refractory disease, and elevated Tg [1–3, 23]. In contrast, very few studies have reported the definite risk factors of DTC concerning BM. While AJCC age stratification [25] remains controversial in BM, we identified age ≥ 55 years as a PFS risk factor, warranting vigilant monitoring in this subgroup. Further studies are needed to validate its prognostic value.

The serum Tg level is considered a significant risk factor for monitoring the recurrence, progression and metastasis of DTC patients [26, 27]. Our results showed that the Tg level played an important role in predicting the progression and survival of DTC patients with BM in univariate analyses. Patients with Tg level higher than 281.17 ng/ml displayed poorer survival than those with low Tg level.

Heterogeneous RAI and FDG uptake patterns in BM critically influence prognosis. FDG avidity reflects tumor dedifferentiation and aggressiveness, while RAI uptake indicates preserved iodine avidity and potential RAI responsiveness [36]. In this study, the interval between the 131I WBS and 18F-FDG PET/CT scan was within four weeks at the time of first BM diagnosis. This short interval minimizes the potential for disease progression or change, thereby ensuring that the observed uptake patterns on both scans are directly comparable. We observed significantly shorter median PFS in RAI-/FDG + patients (28 months) versus RAI+/FDG+ (37 months) and RAI+/FDG- groups (49 months), with corresponding 5-year PFS rates of 22.2%, 55%, and 77.8%. A study by Choudhury et al. reported that patients with both FDG and RAI uptake in bone lesions represent a heterogeneous group, but their prognosis was similar to the group with only FDG positive uptake [28]. However, we found our outcomes closely aligned with RAI+/FDG- patients when the RAI(+) ratio was high (> 50%). Next, we assessed the PET (+) ration and RAI (+) ratio in FDG+/RAI + patients at the time of diagnosis and found that the RAI (+) ratio and PET (+) ratio can affect the PFS and OS rates. In these patients, high RAI (+) ratio and low FDG (+) ratio showed a good prognosis with a PFS rate of 89.5% (17/19) and OS rate of 84.2% (16/19). More than half of FDG+/RAI + patients showed a higher RAI (+) ratio in this study, which might explain the reason that PFS and OS time of this group were longer than FDG+/RAI- group and closer to FDG-/RAI + group. This contrasts with prior reports [29], potentially reflecting inter-tumoral heterogeneity or differential RAI responsiveness. Our results also suggested that the RAI(+) ratio may be a significant predictive factor for FDG+/RAI + patients. The metabolic parameters derived from baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT provide a reflection of intratumoral heterogeneity that is unaffected by therapeutic interventions. Thus, to ensure data homogeneity, SUVmax, tMTV, and tTLG were analyzed exclusively in patients with DTC and BMs before any treatment in this study. Consistent with evidence linking metabolic parameters to malignancy outcomes [14, 30, 31], we identified tMTV and tTLG as independent prognostic markers superior to SUVmax, age stratification and Tg level in multivariate analysis. Considering the high correlation between tMTV and tTLG, in multivariate prognostic analysis, we separately analyzed the effects of tMTV and tTLG on PFS and OS. Our results showed that patients with tTLG > 111.99 g have shorter PFS and OS time. This establishes pretreatment tTLG as a robust biomarker for identifying aggressive BM phenotypes, enabling early intervention.

Our results were obtained from a single center retrospectively, and the sample size was small. These were the major limitations of our study. The absence of FDG uptake lesions in patients with DTC and BM might have underestimated the values of tMTV and tTLG. Multicenter prospective studies should validate tTLG thresholds and explore the impact of RAI(+) ratios in dual-uptake cohorts.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that RAI+/PET + patients exhibited comparable clinical outcomes to RAI+/PET- patients, with the RAI(+) ratio serving as a critical determinant of prognosis in those receiving RAI as the main treatment. Crucially, tTLG derived from pretreatment ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT emerged as an independent prognostic factor for both PFS and OS. These results support that 18F–FDG PET/CT imaging performed concomitantly with 131I WBS in DTC patients with BM can reflect tumor status and provide prognostic information to aid in identifying higher-risk patients.

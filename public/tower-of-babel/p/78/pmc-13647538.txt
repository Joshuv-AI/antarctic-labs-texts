Association of bilirubin reduction rate after percutaneous transhepatic cholangiographic drainage with major complications in hilar cholangiocarcinoma
Authors: Tong Yuan, Ran Tao, Zhenhui Xie, Qiuyi Nie, Deyu Li, Chuang Peng, Zhiyong Huang, Xiaoping Chen, Qi Cheng, Erlei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study evaluated whether the dynamic change in total bilirubin (TBIL) after percutaneous transhepatic cholangiographic drainage (PTCD) was associated with major postoperative complications and 90-day mortality in patients with hilar cholangiocarcinoma (HCCA).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 173 patients with HCCA who underwent PTCD before curative-intent resection at three Chinese centers. The bilirubin reduction rate (BRR) was defined as the difference between the TBIL level measured one day before PTCD and that measured one day before surgery, divided by the interval between measurements. Logistic regression was used for major complications, and Firth’s penalized logistic regression was used for 90-day postoperative mortality. Receiver operating characteristic analysis assessed the discriminatory performance of BRR for major complications.ResultsMajor complications occurred in 64 patients (37.0%), and 8 patients (4.6%) died within 90 days after surgery. On multivariable analysis, BRR (OR = 0.86, 95% CI 0.79–0.95, p = 0.002) and intraoperative transfusion (OR = 2.04, 95% CI 1.06–3.92, p = 0.033) were independently associated with major complications. Lower platelet count (OR = 0.99, 95% CI 0.97–1.00, p = 0.013) and lower albumin (OR = 0.80, 95% CI 0.65–0.99, p = 0.042) remained independently associated with 90-day postoperative mortality. BRR showed modest discriminatory ability for major complications (AUC = 0.644, 95% CI 0.563–0.726). Using the optimal cutoff value (4.863 μmol/L·day−1), the high-BRR group had lower rates of major complications and 90-day mortality than the low-BRR group.ConclusionsBRR after PTCD was independently associated with major complications after HCCA resection, but its modest discriminatory performance supports its use only as an adjunctive marker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05114-8.Keywords: Hilar cholangiocarcinoma, Bilirubin reduction rate, Percutaneous transhepatic cholangiographic drainage, Major complications, Mortality

Introduction

Hilar cholangiocarcinoma (HCCA), arising at the biliary confluence, remains one of the most challenging hepatobiliary malignancies [1]. Curative-intent treatment often requires major hepatectomy combined with biliary reconstruction, and postoperative morbidity and short-term mortality remain substantial even in experienced centers [2]. Because obstructive jaundice, cholangitis, and impaired hepatic reserve are common at presentation, practical perioperative markers are needed to optimize surgical timing and improve risk assessment [3].

Preoperative biliary drainage is widely used to relieve cholestasis, improve hepatic function, and facilitate definitive resection [4]. Among the available techniques, percutaneous transhepatic cholangiographic drainage (PTCD) enables targeted segmental decompression and effective control of biliary sepsis and is therefore commonly adopted in many centers [5, 6]. However, the indications, extent, and timing of drainage remain controversial, and the potential benefits of PTCD may be offset when drainage is inadequate or unnecessary [7]. In routine practice, preoperative assessment commonly incorporates static total bilirubin (TBIL) values; however, single time-point measurements may not fully capture the dynamic recovery of biliary decompression and hepatic functional status after drainage [8]. The optimal preoperative TBIL threshold before hepatectomy for HCCA to reduce postoperativecomplications remains controversial [8–10].

Dynamic indicators may better reflect the effectiveness of biliary decompression than single time-point measurements [11]. The bilirubin reduction rate (BRR), which captures the velocity of TBIL decline between PTCD and surgery, integrates both the magnitude and duration of TBIL change and may therefore serve as a simple indicator of response to drainage [12]. Therefore, we conducted a multicenter study of patients with HCCA who underwent PTCD before curative-intent resection. We evaluated the association of BRR with major complications and 90-day postoperative mortality, compared its discriminatory performance with other bilirubin-based and liver function-related indices, and explored whether BRR could provide complementary information for perioperative risk assessment.

Materials and methods

Study population

This retrospective multicenter study included patients who underwent curative-intent resection for HCCA at three major hospitals in China between April 1, 2010, and June 1, 2025. Patients were excluded if they had not undergone PTCD; had received additional invasive biliary interventions, such as endoscopic biliary drainage or biliary stenting before surgery; had extrahepatic metastasis; had undergone R2 resection; or had incomplete medical records. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Tongji Hospital (Approval No. TJ-IRB202409063) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Variables and study endpoints

Collected demographic, laboratory, and operative variables included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, liver cirrhosis, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, largest tumor size, Bismuth classification, preoperative carbohydrate antigen 19–9 (CA19-9), hemoglobin, platelet count (PLT), albumin, TBIL, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), γ-glutamyl transferase (GGT), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Cr), prothrombin time (PT), international normalized ratio (INR), extent of hepatic resection (minor vs. major), intraoperative bleeding, intraoperative transfusion, and operative time.

HBV infection was defined by the presence of hepatitis B surface antigen in serum. Cirrhosis was diagnosed on the basis of characteristic findings on computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Hepatic resections were categorized as minor when fewer than three Couinaud segments were removed and as major when three or more segments were removed [13]. Postoperative complications were graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification, and major complications were defined as grade III or higher [14].

Highest pre-TBIL was defined as the highest serum TBIL level recorded before PTCD. Pre-TBIL (D-1) was defined as the TBIL level measured one day before surgery. The bilirubin reduction rate (BRR) was calculated as the difference between the TBIL level measured one day before PTCD and that measured one day before surgery, divided by the number of days between the two measurements, and was expressed as μmol/L·day−1. To compare BRR with other bilirubin-based and liver function-related indices, the albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) [15], platelet-albumin-bilirubin (PALBI) [16], and model for end-stage liver disease (MELD) [17] scores were also calculated using preoperative laboratory values. ALBI and PALBI were calculated using Pre-TBIL (D-1), whereas MELD was calculated using Pre-TBIL (D-1), creatinine, and INR after unit conversion, according to the original published formulas.

The primary endpoint of the study was the occurrence of major postoperative complications, and the secondary endpoint was 90-day postoperative mortality. These outcomes were assessed during the index hospitalization and within 90 days after surgery through review of inpatient records and postoperative follow-up information.

PTCD procedure

PTCD was performed before surgery to relieve obstructive jaundice and improve preoperative hepatic function. Under imaging guidance, a percutaneous transhepatic approach was used to cannulate the obstructed bile duct, followed by placement of an external drainage catheter. The target liver segments for drainage were selected on the basis of preoperative imaging findings and the planned future liver remnant. Following PTCD, patients received routine supportive management, including monitoring of liver biochemistry, drainage patency, and signs of cholangitis, until they were considered suitable for curative-intent resection [18].

Surgical procedure

Surgical procedures with curative intent typically consisted of bile duct excision in combination with hepatic resection, regional lymph node dissection, and reconstruction via Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy. In select cases where an R0 margin could be achieved with bile duct resection alone, isolated bile duct resection was performed. The extent of surgery was determined intraoperatively by the attending surgeon on the basis of both preoperative imaging and real-time assessments, including intraoperative ultrasound, particularly when vascular structures such as the hepatic artery or portal vein were suspected to be involved. To ensure negative margins, frozen-section examination of the bile duct at both the proximal and distal ends was routinely performed during the operation [19].

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Comparisons between groups were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables, as appropriate.

Univariable logistic regression analyses were first performed to explore factors associated with major complications. Variables with p < 0.05 on univariable analysis were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model, and results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A dedicated postoperative liver failure-focused analysis was also performed, with BRR analyzed as a continuous variable in logistic regression. Because only eight patients died within 90 days after surgery, Firth’s penalized logistic regression was used for the analysis of 90-day postoperative mortality to reduce small-sample bias and address potential separation. Variables with p < 0.05 on univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable Firth logistic regression model. To address potential heterogeneity in tumor extent and surgical complexity, sensitivity analyses were performed in more clinically homogeneous subgroups, including patients who underwent major hepatectomy, patients with Bismuth type III–IV tumors, and patients with Bismuth type III–IV tumors who underwent major hepatectomy. In these subgroup analyses, logistic regression models were constructed for major complications, with BRR and intraoperative transfusion included as covariates.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the ability of BRR, Highest pre-TBIL, Pre-TBIL (D-1), ALBI, PALBI, and MELD to discriminate major complications, and the area under the curve (AUC) with its 95% CI was calculated. Pairwise comparisons of AUCs between BRR and the other markers were performed using DeLong’s test. The optimal cutoff value of BRR was determined using the Youden index, after which patients were divided into low- and high-BRR groups for postoperative outcome comparison and baseline characteristic analysis. The PTCD-to-surgery interval was summarized in the overall cohort and compared between the low- and high-BRR groups. The correlation between BRR and the PTCD-to-surgery interval was assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis. An additional multivariable logistic regression model including BRR, intraoperative transfusion, and the PTCD-to-surgery interval was constructed to evaluate whether the association between BRR and major complications was independent of the drainage-to-surgery interval.

To assess the internal stability of the BRR cutoff, a bootstrap resampling procedure was performed with 1000 iterations. In each bootstrap sample, receiver operating characteristic analysis was repeated, and the optimal BRR cutoff for predicting major complications was recalculated using the Youden index. The distribution of bootstrap-derived cutoff values was summarized using the median, interquartile range, and 2.5th–97.5th percentile range. All tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 173 patients with HCCA who underwent PTCD followed by curative-intent resection were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). Overall, 111 patients (64.2%) were male, 23 (13.3%) had HBV infection, and 34 (19.7%) had cirrhosis. The median BMI was 21.38 kg/m2 (IQR, 19.59–23.14). Most patients had advanced biliary involvement, with Bismuth type IIIa, IIIb, and IV tumors accounting for 26.6%, 23.7%, and 32.9% of the cohort, respectively. The median Highest Pre-TBIL was 202.60 μmol/L (IQR, 148.00–288.40), the median Pre-TBIL (D-1) was 57.10 μmol/L (IQR, 39.80–86.50), and the median BRR was 5.21 μmol/L·day−1 (IQR, 3.06–7.78). Major hepatectomy was performed in 123 patients (71.1%), and 71 (41.0%) received intraoperative transfusion. Detailed baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1.

Factors associated with major complications

Major complications occurred in 64 of 173 patients (37.0%). On univariable logistic regression analysis, BRR (OR = 0.86, 95% CI 0.79–0.95, p = 0.002) and intraoperative transfusion (OR = 1.99, 95% CI 1.06–3.73, p = 0.032) were significantly associated with major complications. In the multivariable model, both BRR (OR = 0.86, 95% CI 0.79–0.95, p = 0.002) and intraoperative transfusion (OR = 2.04, 95% CI 1.06–3.92, p = 0.033) remained independently associated with major complications. Patients who developed major complications had a lower median BRR than those without major complications (4.38 vs. 6.08 μmol/L·day−1). Full regression results are shown in Table 2.

Because postoperative liver failure is a clinically relevant liver-specific complication, a dedicated postoperative liver failure-focused analysis was performed. Postoperative liver failure occurred in 18 patients (10.4%). Higher BRR tended to be associated with lower odds of postoperative liver failure, but this association was not statistically significant (OR = 0.88, 95% CI 0.76–1.02, p = 0.094).

To address potential heterogeneity in tumor extent and surgical complexity, sensitivity analyses were performed in more clinically homogeneous subgroups. In the major hepatectomy subgroup, BRR remained associated with major complications after adjustment for intraoperative transfusion (OR = 0.89, 95% CI 0.80–0.98, p = 0.021). Similar findings were observed in patients with Bismuth type III–IV tumors (OR = 0.88, 95% CI 0.78–0.99, p = 0.037) and in patients with Bismuth type III–IV tumors who underwent major hepatectomy (OR = 0.88, 95% CI 0.78–1.00, p = 0.045). These findings support the robustness of the association between BRR and major complications in clinically relevant high-risk subgroups (Table S1).

Factors associated with 90-day postoperative mortality

Eight patients (4.6%) died within 90 days after surgery. Because of the limited number of mortality events, Firth’s penalized logistic regression was used for this analysis. On univariable analysis, lower PLT (OR = 0.99, 95% CI 0.97–1.00, p = 0.016) and lower albumin (OR = 0.81, 95% CI 0.67–1.00, p = 0.047) were significantly associated with 90-day postoperative mortality. In multivariable analysis, lower PLT (OR = 0.99, 95% CI 0.97–1.00, p = 0.013) and lower albumin (OR = 0.80, 95% CI 0.65–0.99, p = 0.042) remained independently associated with 90-day postoperative mortality. Although BRR was lower in patients who died than in survivors (2.47 vs. 5.40 μmol/L·day−1), BRR was not significantly associated with 90-day mortality in the Firth regression model (OR = 0.83, 95% CI 0.62–1.04, p = 0.129). Discriminatory performance of BRR and BRR-based risk stratification

ROC analysis showed that BRR had the highest AUC among the six evaluated markers for predicting major complications, with an AUC of 0.644 (95% CI 0.563–0.726), compared with 0.518 (95% CI 0.429–0.608) for Highest Pre-TBIL, 0.563 (95% CI 0.470–0.656) for Pre-TBIL (D-1), 0.531 (95% CI 0.443–0.620) for ALBI, 0.560 (95% CI 0.471–0.648) for PALBI, and 0.565 (95% CI 0.475–0.656) for MELD (Fig. 2). DeLong’s test showed that BRR had a significantly higher AUC than ALBI (p = 0.043), whereas the differences between BRR and Highest Pre-TBIL, Pre-TBIL (D-1), PALBI, and MELD were not statistically significant (all p > 0.05; Table S2).

The optimal BRR cutoff determined by the Youden index was 4.863 μmol/L·day−1, yielding 83 patients in the low-BRR group and 90 in the high-BRR group. To evaluate the internal stability of the data-derived BRR cutoff, bootstrap resampling with 1000 iterations was performed. All bootstrap iterations were valid. The bootstrap-derived optimal BRR cutoff had a median value of 5.895 μmol/L·day−1 (IQR, 4.863–7.245 μmol/L·day−1; 2.5th–97.5th percentile, 4.410–8.599 μmol/L·day−1), whereas the original Youden-derived cutoff was 4.863 μmol/L·day−1. The bootstrap-derived AUC remained stable, with a median value of 0.645, which was close to the original AUC of 0.644. These findings suggest that the discriminatory performance of BRR was internally stable, although the optimal cutoff showed moderate variability.

Patients in the high-BRR group had significantly better short-term postoperative outcomes than those in the low-BRR group. The rate of major complications was 24.4% in the high-BRR group versus 50.6% in the low-BRR group (p < 0.001), and the 90-day postoperative mortality rate was 1.1% versus 8.4%, respectively (p = 0.029). The high-BRR group also had a lower incidence of major postoperative ascites (7.8% vs. 19.3%, p = 0.045). The incidences of major postoperative hydrothorax, liver failure, and respiratory failure, as well as the duration of postoperative hospital stay, did not differ significantly between groups (Table 4). Baseline characteristics stratified by BRR are summarized in Table S3. Tumor-related and operative characteristics, including Bismuth classification, extent of hepatic resection, intraoperative bleeding, intraoperative transfusion, and operative time, were generally comparable between the low- and high-BRR groups. However, several biochemical variables, including Highest Pre-TBIL, Pre-TBIL (D-1), ALT, BUN, and PT, differed between groups (Table S3).

The median PTCD-to-surgery interval was 19.00 days (IQR, 11.00–32.00 days) in the entire cohort. Patients in the low-BRR group had a longer PTCD-to-surgery interval than those in the high-BRR group (22.00 [13.00–40.50] vs. 17.00 [9.00–26.00] days, p = 0.008). BRR was moderately and negatively correlated with the PTCD-to-surgery interval (Spearman’s rho = −0.316, p < 0.001). In an additional multivariable model adjusting for the PTCD-to-surgery interval, BRR remained independently associated with major complications (OR = 0.86, 95% CI 0.78–0.95, p = 0.003), whereas the PTCD-to-surgery interval itself was not significantly associated with major complications (OR = 1.00, 95% CI 0.99–1.01, p = 0.951; Table S4).

Discussion

In this multicenter study of patients with HCCA who underwent PTCD before curative-intent resection, BRR was independently associated with major postoperative complications, whereas intraoperative transfusion was also an independent factor for major morbidity. BRR achieved the highest AUC among the six evaluated markers for predicting major complications, although its discriminatory ability remained modest. In contrast, lower PLT and lower albumin were independently associated with 90-day postoperative mortality in the Firth model, while BRR was not independently associated with mortality despite lower median values among patients who died. Although the low-BRR group showed a higher 90-day postoperative mortality rate than the high-BRR group in the cutoff-based comparison, this finding should be interpreted cautiously. The cutoff was derived from the same cohort, and dichotomization of a continuous variable may amplify apparent group differences. In addition, only eight deaths occurred within 90 days after surgery, which limited the statistical power and stability of the mortality analysis. Therefore, the observed mortality difference between BRR groups should be regarded as exploratory rather than confirmatory evidence of a mortality-predictive effect.

These findings suggest that BRR captures clinically meaningful information that is not fully reflected by a single preoperative TBIL value. From a pathophysiological perspective, a rapid decline in TBIL after PTCD may indicate more effective biliary decompression, improved recovery from cholestasis, and better control of biliary inflammation [3, 11]. Conversely, a persistently slow decline may suggest inadequate biliary drainage and may also reflect unresolved cholangitis or limited hepatic functional reserve. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies showing that short-term outcomes after resection for HCCA are strongly influenced by factors related to biliary drainage, preoperative cholangitis, and future liver remnant status [20, 21]. A notable finding of the present study is that BRR performed better than static TBIL-based markers and conventional liver function-related scores in this cohort, reaching statistical significance versus ALBI, although not against all comparators. This suggests that the dynamic trajectory of bilirubin decline may provide additional perioperative information beyond a single TBIL value. However, the overall AUC of BRR was 0.644, indicating only modest discrimination. Although bilirubin kinetics may also be informative in other causes of obstructive jaundice, their clinical relevance may be particularly pronounced in HCCA. In contrast to distal cholangiocarcinoma or benign biliary strictures, HCCA often requires selective drainage of the future liver remnant, major hepatectomy, complex biliary reconstruction, and prolonged preoperative optimization, while being accompanied by a substantial risk of cholangitis and postoperative liver dysfunction. Under these conditions, a single preoperative TBIL value may not fully capture the effectiveness of biliary decompression or the dynamic recovery of the liver intended to remain after resection. BRR may therefore provide complementary information on the trajectory of bilirubin decline in this high-risk setting. However, BRR should be interpreted as a complementary rather than standalone marker. Although it reflects the dynamic trajectory of bilirubin decline after PTCD, its AUC was only 0.644 and was significantly superior only to ALBI. Therefore, BRR may provide additional clinical context but should be interpreted together with clinical, biochemical, drainage-related, and operative factors.

Intraoperative transfusion was independently associated with major complications in our study. This is clinically plausible, as transfusion often reflects technically demanding surgery, substantial blood loss, and greater physiological stress. Previous work in HCCA has similarly linked transfusion burden with worse perioperative outcomes. Dumitrascu et al. [22] reported that the number of transfused blood units was independently associated with clinically relevant posthepatectomy liver failure (PHLF) after major hepatectomy for HCCA. Kimura et al. [23] also identified perioperative blood transfusion as an adverse factor in HCCA after radical resection. Although much of the published literature has focused on long-term prognosis, these data collectively support the view that transfusion is also a marker of perioperative vulnerability in this setting.

Lower PLT was independently associated with 90-day postoperative mortality in the present cohort. Although specific evidence in HCCA remains limited, the association is biologically credible and is supported by broader hepatectomy literature. Platelets are increasingly recognized as contributors to liver regeneration, and low perioperative platelet counts have been associated with delayed postoperative liver function recovery, higher rates of PHLF, and increased mortality after liver resection [24, 25]. Prior studies and meta-analytic evidence suggest that thrombocytopenia is linked to worse postoperative recovery after hepatectomy, which provides a plausible explanation for the relationship observed in our patients [26].

Lower albumin was also independently associated with 90-day mortality. Albumin is a well-established surrogate of nutritional status, systemic reserve, and hepatic synthetic function; therefore, hypoalbuminemia may identify patients with limited tolerance to major hepatectomy and biliary reconstruction [27]. This interpretation is consistent with recent literature in perihilar cholangiocarcinoma showing that preoperative liver function-related indices incorporating albumin, particularly ALBI grade, are associated with postoperative complications and 90-day mortality [28]. Accordingly, our finding reinforces the importance of perioperative nutritional and hepatic functional optimization before surgery.

The clinical implication of our findings is that BRR may serve as a readily available adjunctive marker rather than a definitive predictor. BRR is simple to calculate and can be obtained from routine peri-PTCD laboratory testing without additional cost. For patients with a low BRR, surgeons may consider reassessing drainage adequacy and reviewing potentially modifiable perioperative factors, such as infection control, nutritional status, and hepatic functional reserve. However, BRR alone should not determine surgical timing or eligibility. The observed associations of transfusion, PLT, and albumin with short-term outcomes further suggest that BRR should be interpreted together with operative complexity and host reserve, rather than in isolation.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective multicenter study and therefore subject to selection bias. Second, perioperative management and surgical practice may have differed among the participating centers. In addition, detailed drainage-related factors, including future liver remnant drainage, drainage laterality, cholangitis, catheter revision, and portal vein embolization, were not uniformly available, and residual confounding related to drainage strategy cannot be excluded. Third, the discriminatory performance of BRR for major complications was modest rather than strong, and most pairwise AUC comparisons were not statistically significant except for ALBI. Fourth, only eight patients died within 90 days after surgery, limiting the stability of the mortality analysis despite the use of Firth’s penalized logistic regression. Fifth, the BRR cutoff of 4.863 μmol/L·day−1 was derived from the same cohort in which outcome comparisons were subsequently performed, which may introduce optimism bias. Although bootstrap resampling showed a stable AUC estimate, the optimal cutoff demonstrated moderate variability across resampled datasets. Therefore, this cutoff should be regarded as exploratory and requires external validation before being used as a definitive clinical decision threshold. Finally, baseline characteristics differed between the low- and high-BRR groups in several biochemical variables, including bilirubin-related measures, which is expected given that BRR is derived from peri-drainage bilirubin dynamics. Therefore, BRR-based group comparisons should be interpreted primarily as risk stratification rather than causal inference.

Conclusions

In summary, BRR after PTCD was independently associated with major postoperative complications after curative-intent resection for HCCA. Although BRR reflects a dynamic aspect of bilirubin decline that is not captured by static preoperative markers, its discriminatory performance was modest. Therefore, BRR should be regarded as an adjunctive marker for perioperative risk assessment rather than a standalone criterion for surgical decision-making, and further external validation is warranted.

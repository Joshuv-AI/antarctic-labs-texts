Generative Artificial Intelligence for Empowering Non-Native English Speakers in Verbal Scientific Communication and Dissemination
Authors: Hye Young Jang
Published: 2026

See the corresponding article “Agentic Artificial Intelligence for the Automated Generation of Accurate Summary Podcasts of Radiology Research Papers” at https://doi.org/10.3348/kjr.2026.0203.

In mid 2025, the Korean Journal of Radiology (KJR) launched a podcast-style audio summary series, KJR SummaryCast, which was mainly generated using artificial intelligence (AI) tools and is available at https://www.youtube.com/@koreanjournalofradiology2259/podcasts [1]. Initially, episodes were created directly by authors. More recently, however, the process has evolved into a structured workflow built on a custom in-house mini-agentic AI system with editorial oversight and author verification to ensure accuracy and adequacy.

In this issue, Lee et al. [2] present a formal evaluation of this agentic AI approach for generating podcast-style summaries of radiology research articles. Their study demonstrates that the purpose-built multiagent pipeline outperforms a general-purpose commercial tool across several critical dimensions, including factual accuracy, pronunciation, narrative flow, and overall listener preference.

This work is noteworthy not only for its methodological rigor but also for its broader implications. Importantly, the evaluation was conducted using articles from another journal (Radiology), rather than KJR. Similarly, our own experience with KJR articles has been highly satisfactory, leading to the formal adoption of this system into our workflow. The study design by Lee et al. [2] further strengthens the generalizability of these findings and underscores the potential applicability of the agentic AI system or similar approaches across journals and publication contexts.

Importantly, both the authors and KJR consider this technology a shared resource. The system is open to other journals interested in adopting or adapting it, and we encourage collaborative refinement through shared experiences and innovations.

BEYOND A TRENDY TECHNIQUE: A GENUINE FACILITATOR FOR SCIENTIFIC COMMUNICATION AND DISSEMINATION

The adoption of AI for producing KJR SummaryCast was not driven by technological enthusiasm or a passing trend. Rather, it reflects a deliberate and pragmatic effort to enhance the accessibility and dissemination of knowledge in published papers. Although scientific publishing has traditionally relied on text as its primary medium, contemporary knowledge delivery increasingly incorporates diverse formats—including graphical abstracts, video summaries, social media dissemination, and podcasts—with AI frequently supporting these efforts [34]. In this context, KJR’s AI-generated audio summaries is a natural extension of this evolution, particularly considering that most authors are non-native English speakers as explained later.

At the same time, KJR does not regard this development lightly. AI use in tasks historically performed by humans may raise legitimate concerns. Indiscriminate delegation of intellectual work to AI is inappropriate and ethically problematic. Therefore, KJR maintains a clear ethical stance: AI should augment, not replace, human intellectual contributions [5]. Accordingly, all SummaryCast contents undergo rigorous human-in-the-loop verification to ensure factual accuracy, appropriate nuances, and correct pronunciation before their release.

It is also important to distinguish the KJR SummaryCast from traditional journal podcasts (Table 1). Conventional journal podcasts often feature expert commentary, interviews, and in-depth academic discourse, whereas AI-generated audio is primarily a summarization tool. It cannot yet replicate the interpretive depth and contextual insights provided by expert discussions. Therefore, recognizing this distinction is critical.

LOWERING THE LANGUAGE BARRIER IN SCIENTIFIC COMMUNICATION AND DISSEMINATION

One particularly compelling motivation for adopting generative AI in this context extends beyond the KJR SummaryCast production. English is the de facto primary language of international scientific communication, both written and spoken, and imposes a substantial burden on non-native English speakers [6]. While English writing is already challenging, verbal communication of research presents an even greater barrier.

Generative AI has been increasingly recognized as a tool to support scientific writing, especially for non-native English speakers, when used ethically and responsibly [578]. Similarly, it can serve as a powerful aid in verbal scientific communication. This potential extends beyond audio summarization of scientific publications. AI-assisted real-time translation has already been introduced at international academic meetings, enabling presentations in multiple languages with simultaneous English translations.

Looking ahead, similar AI-enabled simultaneous translation technologies could be integrated into journal podcast platforms, facilitating multilingual author interviews and discussions in which participants speak their native languages while reaching a global audience. In this way, a full-spectrum journal podcast addressing the current limitations of AI-generated audio summaries (Table 1) could be realized in non-native English-speaking environments, thereby reducing language barriers. Such advancements would represent more than convenience. They have the potential to fundamentally broaden participation in scientific discourse. Reducing language barriers can enhance equity in knowledge dissemination and enrich the diversity of perspectives represented in the scientific literature. KJR SummaryCast represents an early but meaningful step along this trajectory.

LOOKING FORWARD

Although the current KJR SummaryCast focuses on high-quality summarizations, continuous advances in AI are likely to enable more interactive, inclusive, and multilingual forms of verbal scholarly communication, including direct author engagement. KJR remains committed to exploring how AI augments knowledge communication and dissemination in verbal formats within scientific publishing, while preserving and potentially strengthening human intellectual contributions. The objective is not to supplant traditional forms of scientific publication and communication, but to expand them, making scientific knowledge more accessible, inclusive, and widely disseminated.

Functional and esthetic restoration of a periodontally involved hopeless tooth with a staged multidisciplinary treatment approach
Authors: Jacob P. Francis, Majo Ambooken, Jayan Jacob Mathew, Krishnan Hari, Minu Thomas, Vineeth Haridas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Periodontally hopeless teeth are often deemed candidates for extraction. However, patients’ desire and clinicians’ perseverance might sometimes result in unexpected outcomes beyond simple retention of the tooth. A pathologically migrated Grade II mobile upper incisor with a perioendo lesion in a 31-year-old female patient was sequentially treated with endodontic treatment, local drug delivery, and regenerative surgery using xenograft, injectable platelet-rich fibrin (I-PRF), and chorion membrane. The postsurgical recession was managed using the vestibular incision subperiosteal tunnel access (VISTA) technique with a collagen membrane and I-PRF, followed by indirect composite veneers for tooth discolouration and a fixed lingual retainer to prevent future migration. The mentioned treatment approach restored the function and esthetics of a hopeless tooth by eliminating the pocket and mobility, resolving the perioendo lesion and bone defect, and facilitating root coverage.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontally involved anterior teeth often present dilemmas in reaching appropriate treatment decisions from esthetic and functional perspectives. Apart from pocket formation and bone loss, problems like recession, mobility, migration, and periapical involvement preclude the possibility of preserving such teeth, necessitating extraction. However, in many situations, patients are reluctant to lose their natural teeth, the retention of which requires heroic attempts on the part of the Periodontist. This case report describes a staged treatment approach in a young patient for a maxillary incisor with severe periodontal involvement complicated by periodontal abscess, endodontic involvement, and pathologic migration.

CASE REPORT

A 31-year-old systemically healthy female patient reported to the Periodontics department with complaints of painful swelling of gums, pus discharge, and shaking of the upper front tooth for the past 2 weeks. She gave a history of trauma to the same area 2 years ago, following which, she had started noticing occasional bleeding in relation to the tooth. Tooth number 21 showed a tender, soft swelling on the labial gingiva with purulent discharge on digital pressure [Figure 1]. The labial and proximal surfaces exhibited periodontal pockets (PDs) more than 15 mm deep [Figure 1]. The tooth was Grade II mobile and was slightly extruded. Thermal and electric pulp testing elicited a negative response. Intraoral periapical radiograph showed severe interdental bone loss approaching the apex of the tooth 21 [Figure 2]. Based on the clinical and radiographic findings, a provisional diagnosis of chronic periodontal abscess with a periodontal–endodontic lesion in relation to the tooth 21 was made. Considering the extent of mobility, clinical attachment loss, and pulpal involvement, the tooth was assigned a hopeless prognosis, and the patient was suggested extraction. However, being a tooth located in the esthetic area, the patient was reluctant to undergo extraction. With the patient’s consent, a treatment plan comprising a staged approach was formulated.

(a) Probing pocket depth (PPD) of more than 15 mm (b) periodontal abscess with respect to the tooth 21

Periapical radiograph: Initial radiography showing a severe interdental bone loss approaching the apex of tooth 21

In the preliminary phase, the abscess was drained. Considering the pulpal involvement, endodontic treatment was initiated concomitantly. After 1 week, scaling and local drug delivery (LDD) were performed in the tooth 21 using tetracycline filers (Periodontal Plus AB, Advanced Biotech Products, Chennai) [Figure 3]. The endodontic treatment was completed, and since the tooth continued to exhibit mobility, it was stabilized using interdental composite splinting.

Local drug delivery done with tetracycline fibers with respect to tooth 21

Upon one month’s review, the pocket was still persistent; hence, it was decided to proceed with a surgical approach. A Kirkland’s flap was raised and after thorough debridement, two-wall bone defect was managed with a xenograft (Osseograft, Advanced Biotech Products, Chennai), mixed with injectable platelet-rich fibrin (I-PRF) and a chorion membrane (Tata Memorial Hospital Tissue Bank, Mumbai), and sutured using interrupted silk sutures [Figure 4]. A 4-month postsurgical radiograph showed considerable bone fill in the interdental areas [Figure 5].

(f) Chorion membrane placed over the xenograft and I-PRF

Four-month postoperative radiograph

At the 9-month review, the PD was reduced to 3 mm; however, a Cairo’s recession type 1 (RT 1)[1] of 4 mm in relation to the labial aspect of the tooth 21 was noticed, giving an unesthetic appearance [Figure 6]. To manage the recession, the vestibular incision subperiosteal tunnel access (VISTA) technique was performed. Under magnification, a vertical incision was given in the area of the maxillary labial frenum. The mucogingival complex was reflected and mobilized using microsurgical elevators. A collagen membrane (Healiguide, Advanced Biotech Products, Chennai) wetted with I-PRF was placed underneath the flap. The tissue was coronally displaced and stabilized using suspensory sutures aided with composites, and the area was protected using a periodontal dressing (Coe-pak, GC India Dental Pvt Ltd.) [Figure 7]. Appropriate postsurgical instructions were given, especially regarding tooth brushing. A near 100% root coverage was observed 3 months after the procedure [Figure 8]. At 12-month review, the interdental composite splint was removed to evaluate the tooth’s mobility, which had improved, and radiographic examination showed a significant amount of bone fill in the interdental area [Figure 9]. To manage tooth discoloration, indirect composite veneering was done, followed by a fixed palatal retainer to prevent any future migration [Figure 10]. The patient is presently under review.

DISCUSSION

In the present case, along with a deep PD, mobility, abscess, extrusion, and pulpal involvement complicated the prognosis and treatment. However, the patient’s insistence on retaining her natural tooth prompted us to pursue a rather gallant effort to save the tooth, and a staged treatment course was accordingly outlined.

No substantial reduction in mobility was noted after abscess drainage and initiation of endodontic treatment. Therefore, provisional splinting using interdental composite splints was done to stabilize the tooth during the subsequent treatment phases. It has been reported that tooth mobility negatively affects the healing following periodontal treatment.[2]

After Scaling and root planing, LDD with tetracycline fibers was performed as an adjunct. A significant reduction in PPD was observed following LDD, possibly attributable to the antibacterial,[3] anticollagenase,[3] and anti-inflammatory[3] effects of tetracycline. After 1 month of review following LDD, it was decided to attempt a periodontal regenerative therapy with sticky bone[4] and chorion membrane.

The post-surgical RT 1 recession was managed using the VISTA technique, which minimizes micromotion of the regenerative site and hence gives a better treatment outcome.[5]

In this case, a collagen membrane soaked in I-PRF, as previously reported by Kapa et al.,[6] was employed, resulting in near 100% recession coverage.

From the initial case presentation with Grade II mobility and deep pockets, 12 months after endodontic and periodontal treatment, there was a significant reduction in pocket depth, a substantial bone radiographic bone fill, and no mobility. Since the tooth presented a discoloration as a postendodontic sequel, indirect composite veneers were fabricated. Veneers have been used to improve the esthetics and protection of teeth and are indicated to treat discoloration of teeth.[7] It is observed that patients who have compromised periodontal health may have complications such as pathological migration[8] even after treatment; hence, prolonged retention or permanent splinting, such as fixed lingual retainers, is advocated for such patients.

Several reports[91011] in the literature have demonstrated successful functional and esthetic rehabilitation of periodontally compromised teeth through multidisciplinary management. These studies consistently show that even severely involved teeth can be predictably retained when comprehensive regenerative and restorative protocols are employed. Therefore, this case is consistent with established evidence that supports this complex yet achievable approach.

In the era of modern implantology, retention of periodontally compromised natural teeth as a priority might often be overlooked. However, periodontists must explore all possible options in their repertoire before deciding on the extraction and replacement of such teeth.

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

A periodontally hopeless tooth indicated for extraction was successfully retained in function and esthetics with a staged multidisciplinary approach.

Patient’s desire to retain the natural tooth and willingness to undergo multiple treatment procedures, along with a close interdisciplinary treatment approach.

Being a staged approach, extended treatment duration with the periodic evaluation of response to individual treatment procedures, as well as increased treatment expenses.

Navigating the diagnostic dilemma of neonatal dengue syndrome: a review and report
Authors: Keshav Kumar Pathak, Richie Dalai, Arnab Ghorui, Bhabesh Kant Chowdhary
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere are currently no specific guidelines for neonatal dengue. The available guidelines focus on the pediatric age group. The objective of this study is to summarize the clinical presentations and management strategies, based on the available studies in literature and to report another case of neonatal dengue.MethodsThe PUBMED and Scopus databases were searched using “neonate”, “dengue”, and their synonyms as the search terms. We included observational studies of suspected or proven cases of neonates with dengue infection, irrespective of their gestational age and birth weight. The studies were screened for possible inclusion in the review by two independent reviewers. The neonates that died in hospital among the reported cases were compared with those that survived till discharge, for reported baseline variables, using chi-square/fisher’s exact test, Wilcoxon-Rank Sum test, and multivariable logistic regression.ResultsA total of 57 observational studies on neonatal dengue were found. Of these, 41 were case reports of single cases, 15 were case series of 2 or more cases and 1 was a cohort study. These included a total of 144 cases apart from our reported case. The most common clinical manifestations were thrombocytopenia (81.69%), fever (61.97%), rash (45.07%), and organomegaly (29.58%). Supportive care with fluids and platelet transfusion for severe thrombocytopenia were the mainstay of therapy. Most neonates (94.4%), improved after a week of onset of symptoms with median and interquartile range (IQR) of 7 (6–10) days. Only 8 neonatal dengue deaths were reported in the available literature. When compared with those who survived till discharge, except for third spacing (p-value = 0.001), none of the other baseline clinical parameters were found to be significantly different. On multivariable logistic regression analysis, for those factors with p-value < 0.2 in univariate analysis, none of the factors had an independent association with the outcome of death in neonatal dengue cases.Our index case also presented with high-grade fever on day 5 of life, petechial rash and thrombocytopenia on day 6 of life and improved by day 7 of illness with supportive care.ConclusionsNeonatal dengue has a good prognosis as per the cases reported in the literature. None of the clinical parameters were found to be independently associated with neonatal mortality in the reported cases of neonatal dengue in literature. Further prospective observational studies will be needed to find the true predictors of poor outcomes in neonates with dengue infection.

Introduction

Dengue, a mosquito-borne viral illness, caused by the single-stranded RNA arbovirus has emerged as a significant public health concern globally. Mother-to-child transmission of dengue is a critical aspect, as it can occur through vertical transmission during pregnancy or through breastfeeding [1]. The incidence of neonatal dengue and the incidence of perinatal transmission are variable due to the lack of consistent epidemiological studies with a few case reports and case series which have shown vertical transmission [2, 3]. The pathogenesis of dengue in neonates is complex, involving the interplay of viral virulence, host immunity, and environmental factors [4].

Despite the growing burden of neonatal dengue, there is a paucity of guidelines and standardized protocols for its diagnosis, management, and prevention. The lack of specific recommendations for neonates often leads to delayed or inadequate treatment, resulting in increased morbidity and sometimes mortality. A comprehensive review of neonatal dengue is essential to address these knowledge gaps. This review aims to provide an in-depth examination of current literature on neonatal dengue focusing on clinical presentation and management of neonatal dengue.

Methods

Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to review the available literature on clinical presentations and management strategies for neonatal dengue and to describe a case of neonatal dengue managed at a tertiary care neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) in Eastern India.

Inclusion criteria

For the review we aimed to include observational studies in neonates (0–28 days of life), with suspected or proven dengue infection, irrespective of their gestational age and birth weight.

Search strategy

For the review, the PUBMED and Scopus databases were searched using “neonate”, “dengue”, and their synonyms as follows:

The search was not restricted by language or time. The search was conducted between February to April 2025. We included studies published till 3rd April 2025. Unpublished studies and conference reports were not included.

Data extraction and analysis

The titles and abstracts were screened by two reviewers independently for possible inclusion in the review. Data were extracted for the clinical features and lab parameters (completed gestational age in weeks, any fever, any thrombocytopenia, any leukopenia, any organomegaly, any third spacing, any rash, any hemodynamic instability, illness duration, and death in hospital stay). These variables were entered in a Microsoft Excel Worksheet and were analyzed using Stata Corp statistical package version BE/18.0. The categorical variables were expressed as n (%) and the continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR). The neonates that died in the hospital versus those that survived were also compared for the available baseline parameters, clinical features, and lab parameters, using chi-square/fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and Wilcoxon-Rank Sum test for skewed continuous variables. The factors found to have a p-value of < 0.2 in univariate analysis were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model, to find the factors that are independently associated with death in neonatal dengue cases.

Case description

Initial presentation

An early-term female baby was born at 37 weeks and 2 days gestation with a birth weight of 2.3 kg, through an elective lower segment cesarean section due to maternal indications related to a uterine fibroid in a primigravida mother. The mother in her early 30 s, had a history of fever spikes of 102 °F three days before delivery, which responded well to antipyretics. She tested weakly positive for Dengue NS1 antigen, while both Dengue IgM and IgG tests were negative. Her antenatal scans were normal and was having no history of consanguinity and significant medical illness. The baby cried immediately after birth and was placed on the mother's side. The baby was discharged with her mother at 48 h post-delivery. The baby was bottle-fed with formula milk and remained asymptomatic until day 4 of life. On day 5 of life, she developed a high-grade fever and decreased feeding. On examination, she was febrile (temperature of 103 °F), with a heart rate of 170 beats per minute and a respiratory rate of 58 breaths per minute. Her capillary refill time was 2 s, and all pulses were well palpable. Notably, she exhibited flushed extremities that resolved after fever spikes (Fig. 1a). Diagnostic work-up and management

Given the high-grade fever, a workup for neonatal sepsis was sent and the baby was started on intravenous antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam and amikacin) according to institutional protocols. Cerebrospinal fluid analysis showed acellular fluid with slightly elevated protein levels of 169 mg/dL, while all other parameters were normal, indicating aseptic meningitis (Table 1). Sepsis screen revealed C reactive protein (CRP) of 4.37 mg/L, total leucocyte counts of 4.78 × 109/L, and platelet counts of 260 × 109/L. Despite antibiotic treatment, the baby continued to experience high-grade fever. On day 2 of illness, the baby developed cold shock, requiring administration of a normal saline bolus at a dose of 10 ml/kg followed by low dose adrenaline infusion (0.1 µg/kg/min), which was gradually increased to 0.3 µg/kg/min. Given the baby’s clinical deterioration and requirement for ionotropic support, a repeat sepsis screen was sent, and the intravenous antibiotic regimen was escalated (meropenem and amikacin). Point of care echocardiogram revealed a collapsed inferior vena cava (IVC) with good cardiac contractility. Consequently, two additional boluses of normal saline (10 ml/kg each) were administered. Following fluid resuscitation, the baby’s shock improved, allowing for the gradual tapering and eventual cessation of the adrenaline infusion over the next 24 h. Considering the mother's possible history of dengue infection and the baby’s having persistent fever, investigations for dengue were also sent. The results showed a positive Dengue non-structural protein 1 (NS1) antigen test, while the Dengue IgM antibody test came negative. Repeat sepsis screen revealed CRP of 1.37 mg/L, total leucocyte counts of 9.14 × 109/L, absolute neutrophil count of 3.29 × 109/L, and platelet counts of 100 × 109/L. Anticipating the potential critical phase of dengue, maintenance fluid (0.45% Dextrose normal saline) was increased by an additional 20% for that day. On the same day, the baby also developed multiple petechial spots on her lower extremities, and her repeat platelet count dropped to 20,000 (Fig. 1b). A transfusion of random donor platelets at a dose of 15 ml/kg was administered. On day 3 of illness, the baby exhibited abdominal distension and respiratory distress. Ultrasound confirmed bilateral mild pleural effusion, and moderate ascites, indicating capillary leak syndrome associated with the critical phase of the dengue. The baby was managed with supportive care including Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) respiratory support and fluid volume adjustments were made based on the monitoring of intravascular volume status. This included observing the collapsibility of the IVC and tracking trends in hemoglobin and hematocrit levels (Fig. 2). The target urine output was maintained within a lower normal range of 1–2 ml/kg/hour. By day 4 of illness, the baby was completely transitioned to enteral feeds. Notable improvements in capillary leak syndrome were observed by day 5, with progressive resolution of pleural effusion and ascites. The baby was subsequently weaned off CPAP support. Furthermore, the platelet count showed consistent improvement, exceeding 100,000/mm3. By day 7 of illness, the ascites had completely resolved. A repeat dengue test indicated seroconversion, with Dengue NS1 returning negative and Dengue IgM testing positive. Simultaneously, a repeat test on the mother, also confirmed seroconversion, with a positive dengue IgM test (Table 2), the baby was subsequently discharged and followed up in the outpatient department. The baby is thriving well without any further clinical issues in follow-up. Literature review

A total of 869 titles and abstracts were screened. After screening for duplicates and removing studies without the terms “neonatal dengue” or “vertical transmission” or their synonyms in the title and abstract, 83 articles underwent full-text screening. Of these, 57 studies that included 1 cohort study, 15 case series, and 41 case reports on dengue in neonates were included in the review (Fig. 3). These included a total of 144 cases of dengue in neonates, apart from our reported case (Table 3). The most common clinical manifestations were thrombocytopenia in 81.69% followed by fever in 61.97%, rash (any erythematous macular and/or papular and/or petechial) in 45.07%, and any organomegaly (hepatomegaly and/or splenomegaly) in 29.58% of the reported cases. Rarer manifestations included hemodynamic instability (tachycardia and/or hypotension) in 21.88%, third spacing (pleural effusion and/or pericardial effusion and/or ascites) in 9.86%, and leukopenia (7.04%). The gestational age distribution of the affected neonates was bimodal with peaks at 37 weeks and 39 weeks (Fig. 4). The illness duration was a median of 7 days with IQR of 6–10 days. Discussion

The management of a suspected case of neonatal dengue is mainly supportive. Our index case also presented with high-grade fever on day 5 of life, petechial rash, and thrombocytopenia on day 6 of life and improved by day 7 of illness with supportive care. In the absence of specific guidelines for neonatal management, we have divided the discussion into the following subheadings to summarise the various strategies based on the available evidence for the neonatal population.

General supportive care

Admission and monitoring: Neonates with suspected or confirmed dengue infection should be admitted to a NICU for close monitoring and care. Vital signs monitoring should be performed at least every 2–4 h, with continuous monitoring for severe cases. Daily weight measurement should be done to assess fluid status [5].

Temperature management: Careful temperature regulation should be done through environmental control. Paracetamol can be used at appropriate neonatal doses to manage fever. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) should be avoided due to the risk of bleeding complications [6].

Role of fluids and inotropes

These neonates may present with hypovolemic and distributive shock. Fluid boluses (crystalloid) should be guided by the volume status which can be assessed by serum sodium, urine output, IVC collapsibility, and cardiac contractility. Judicious fluid management, taking into account all relevant parameters, is crucial to avoid excessive fluid administration. Currently, there is a lack of specific guidelines for selecting the type of fluids. This should be individualized based on gestational age, postnatal day, and serum electrolytes. For neonates without plasma leakage normal maintenance fluids or feeds as per the day’s requirement are sufficient however for those with signs of plasma leakage, careful fluid replacement with monitoring for fluid overload is essential [7]. Hematocrit alone should not be used to diagnose neonatal dengue or guide fluid rate in the neonatal age group as they have higher hematocrit as compared to older children [8]. A change in hematocrit from baseline in combination with the various other parameters as enumerated above, may however provide useful information for fluid titration.

In cases where the infants are nonresponsive to fluids, inotropes should be considered. Given that neonates may present with hypovolemic and/or distributive shock both dopamine and epinephrine can be effective options [9].

Role of antibiotics

Since the initial presentation of the affected neonates may not be differentiated from neonatal sepsis, antibiotics have been used invariably in the initial management of such neonates, suspecting neonatal sepsis. However, as soon as the diagnosis of neonatal dengue is made and the blood cultures are sterile, antibiotics should be discontinued.

Role of platelet transfusion

Thrombocytopenia alone, is not an indication for transfusion in cases of dengue, unless there is a bleeding tendency or thrombocytopenia is severe [10, 11]. In neonates, especially < 34 weeks gestational age, platelet transfusion is usually reserved for those with a platelet count < 25,000 cells/cubic mm as a transfusion for a higher threshold is associated with higher odds of major bleeding or death [12]. Hemorrhagic manifestations reported so far in neonatal dengue are predominantly mild in the form of petechial rash with no correlation between the severity of thrombocytopenia and the bleeding manifestations. However, an ultrasound cranium should be done to rule out any intracranial bleed as in such cases a platelet transfusion threshold of 50,000–100000 cells/cubic mm is recommended [13].

Limitations

Our review included predominantly case reports and case series. There is a paucity of cohort studies on neonatal dengue in the literature. We could not find the factors associated with mortality in cases with neonatal dengue, as the number of deaths reported in the literature was very few. Further prospective multicentric observational studies will be needed to find the true predictors of poor outcomes in these neonates. Current management guidelines are primarily adapted from the pediatric protocols with modifications for neonatal physiology. There are several key research gaps in neonatal dengue management. Notably, validated severity scoring systems specific to neonatal dengue are lacking. Additionally, there is a need to formulate optimal fluid management protocols tailored to neonates. Furthermore, data are scarce on long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes of infants who had neonatal dengue. Conclusion

It can be concluded that neonatal dengue has a good prognosis as per the cases reported in the literature. 94.4% of neonates survived till discharge and most neonates improved after a week’s treatment with supportive measures. Further studies are necessary to address the research gaps and improve care for neonates with dengue.

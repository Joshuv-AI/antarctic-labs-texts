Enigmatic odyssey: A case of Behcet disease with atypical thrombotic complications
Authors: Hamza A Abdul-Hafez, Yazan Hamdan, Nizar Ayed Abu Hamdeh, Haitham Abu Khadija, Doha Jaber, Mohammad Alnees
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction and importanceBehçet disease is a rare, chronic, multi-systemic inflammatory disease of unknown origin that affects vessels in various organs. It is characterized by multiple clinical manifestations, most prominently oral and genital ulcers. Typically diagnosed in individuals aged 20 to 40 years with a male predominance, Behçet disease poses significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges.Case presentationWe present a case concerning a 26-year-old female who was diagnosed with Behçet's disease nine years ago. This presentation is atypical for her age and gender. The patient has experienced recurrent thrombotic events, such as pulmonary embolism, a right pulmonary artery aneurysm, and right atrium intracardiac thrombosis.Clinical discussionThis case underscores the atypical presentation of Behçet disease in a young female and highlights its potential for serious thrombotic complications. The recurrent thrombotic events observed in this patient necessitate a comprehensive understanding of the disease's pathophysiology and tailored therapeutic approaches.ConclusionBehçet disease can manifest with significant thrombotic complications, even in atypical patient populations. This case report highlights the necessity for heightened awareness and timely management of thrombotic events in individuals with Behçet disease to enhance outcomes.Keywords: Behçet disease, Intracardiac thrombosis, Complications, Case report

Introduction

Behçet disease is a rare, chronic, and multi-systemic disorder with a relapsing-remitting nature. The pathophysiology of Behçet disease is not clearly understood. It is characterized by widespread clinical manifestations including oral ulcers, genital ulcers, skin eruptions, and ocular symptoms. In addition, the involvement of multiple organ systems such as neurological, gastrointestinal, urological, musculoskeletal, and cardiovascular systems [1]. Males are significantly more commonly affected by Behçet disease compared to females with a 7:1 male-to-female ratio and ages from 20 to 40 years old [2,3]. The overall prevalence of Behçet disease is around 10 cases among 100,000 population [4,5].

Behçet disease is considered a systemic vasculitis disease with involvement of both venous and arterial systems in all sizes (large, medium, and small) [3]. The venous system is most commonly affected by vascular involvement of Behçet disease with a prevalence of 14.7 % among Behçet disease cases according to a retrospective study conducted by Tascilar et al. [6]. In Behçet disease, cardiac involvement can occur among Behçet disease patients in 1–8 % of cases, such as pericarditis, endocarditis, intracardiac thrombosis, myocardial infarction, endomyocardial fibrosis, and myocardial aneurysm, respectively according to prevalence [7]. However, Intracardiac thrombosis is rare but has significantly serious consequences for Behçet disease with 19 % among other cardiac lesions in Behçet disease according to a previous study conducted by Geri et al. [8].

In this case, we report a unique case of a 26-year-old female patient diagnosed with Behçet disease nine years ago who presented with cardiovascular involvement including pulmonary artery aneurysm and intracardiac thrombosis.

Case presentation

Upon arrival at the emergency department of Palestine Medical Complex Hospital in January 2024, a 26-year-old female patient, with a documented history of Behçet disease, reported experiencing shortness of breath, palpitations, fever, and dry cough, which have progressively worsened over the past two weeks following a dental extraction at a private dental clinic. Despite undergoing a course of cefixime, her symptoms did not improve. She also reported a history of shortness of breath after exertion over the past year and recurrent oral and genital ulcers over the past nine years. In 2015, the patient was diagnosed with Behçet disease at AL-MAQASED Hospital, where the rheumatology team suspected underlying vasculitis due to her recurrent oral and genital ulcers. The diagnosis of this disease was confirmed based on recurrent oral and genital ulcerations, vision abnormalities, severe abdominal pain, and a positive pathology test.

In 2021, there was an admission for recurring hemoptysis and the reappearance of oral ulcers, leading to a diagnosis of a right pulmonary aneurysm and a left sub-segmental pulmonary embolism (PE) based on a computed tomography (CT) pulmonary angiogram report. These conditions were identified as complications of Behçet disease. During hospitalization, the patient received intravenous (IV) infliximab and subsequently continued this treatment regimen every two weeks for six weeks post-discharge. Additionally, she received low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) as part of her treatment regimen. Following this comprehensive treatment, the patient's symptoms were successfully resolved.

Apart from the primary diagnosis presented by the patient, a CT angiogram revealed an additional finding of a filling defect in the right atrium measuring (2 × 1.8) cm, as depicted in Fig. 3. The medical recommendation was to do regular follow-ups due to the asymptomatic nature of the lesion. In 2022, the patient was readmitted for the same complaint as in 2021, and treatment was consistent with the previous approach. It is of note that the CT angiogram in 2022 mirrored the findings from 2021, however, with a marginal increase in the size of the right atrial mass, measuring (3 × 2 cm) as illustrated in Fig. 2(A and B).

In 2024, an echocardiography report was ordered due to her symptoms and it revealed a mobile mass in the right atrium measuring 4 cm × 3 cm. The mass appears soft, irregular in shape, and echogenic, leading to consideration of whether it is a myxoma or a thrombus. Following this, a CT pulmonary and cardiac angiogram were ordered based on the echocardiography report. The patient had a history of pleuritic chest pain with hemoptysis during hospitalizations in 2021 and 2022, where she was diagnosed with a right pulmonary artery aneurysm and left sub-segmental PE. The recent CT scan showed that the saccular aneurysm of the right inter-lobar pulmonary artery and focal dilatations of the left inter-lobar pulmonary artery have significantly decreased compared to previous scans Fig. 1(A and B). Additionally, the filling defect in the right atrium has grown in size, measuring 5.1 cm × 2.7 cm. This growth over time raised suspicions of myxoma given the chronic nature of the growth, although the possibility of intra-cardiac thrombosis cannot be ruled out. Figures from previous CT scans done in 2021 and 2022 revealed the indolent progression and enlargement of the filling defect, prompting consideration for surgical intervention.

Following a thorough discussion of treatment options with the patient, and in consideration of the symptoms induced by the lesion, a decision was made to proceed with surgical removal of the lesion. The excised tissue was sent to pathology for definitive diagnosis confirmation. Before the surgery, the patient underwent a tapering-off of immune suppression therapy. During this period, the identification and successful removal of two masses in the right atrium were made, with subsequent submission for pathological examination. The pathology results demonstrated a centrally located hemorrhagic focus surrounded by fibrin exudate and mixed inflammatory cell infiltrate, consistent with organized intra-atrial thrombus formation (illustrated in Figs. 2A and B and Fig. 4). After the surgical procedure, the patient's recovery was uneventful. The patient was prescribed LMWH, resumed immune suppressive therapy, and was subsequently discharged home.

The patient's drug history is significant, as it includes Imuran (Azathioprine) 50 mg 2 × 1 with poor compliance, colchicine 250 mg 2 × 2, and infliximab IV every two months, which was stopped due to planning for pregnancy. Additionally, the patient has no previous surgical history, and her family history is significant for coronary artery disease (CAD) and diabetes mellitus.

On general physical examination, the patient was found to be clinically anemic, had aphthous ulcers in the oral cavity, and had multiple erythematous lesions on the forearms and shins. Abdominal examination showed nothing abnormal except for suprapubic tenderness associated with urinary frequency and dysuria. Chest auscultation revealed good breath sounds bilaterally with tachypnea and normal s1 and s2 with an unspecific rumble heard over the right costal margin. The remaining examination was inconclusive.

Baseline investigations, blood and urine cultures, Beta-2 glycoprotein one antibody tests, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), electrolyte panel, and echocardiogram were ordered. Her complete blood counts showed hemoglobin levels of 8.9 g/dL with decreased MCV, which was 76 fL, a total leukocyte count of 17,600 per μL with neutrophilic predominance, and platelets of 417,000/μL with a hematocrit of 26.8 %. Her liver function tests were within normal levels, in addition to normal renal function tests and a lipid profile. The urine was turbid and acidic, and urinalysis showed 4+ leukocytes with numerous WBC cells and [[5], [6], [7]] red blood cells (RBCs). Her ESR was 130 mm/first hour, indicating significant inflammation and Beta-2 glycoprotein one antibody tests came back negative. Blood and urine cultures did not show the growth of any microorganism. These results indicate anemia, inflammation, and possible urinary tract infection (Table 1).

One month later, a repeat echocardiography revealed the heart's complete resolution of the right atrial thrombus, and the patient was in good health and excellent clinical status.

Discussion

Intracardiac thrombosis is a rare complication of Behçet disease with only a few previous reported cases, especially in the absence of coagulation cascade abnormalities [9]. Male to female ratio of Intracardiac thrombosis in Behçet disease is 23:2 with a mean age of 27 years [9]. Clinical manifestation at the time of Intracardiac thrombosis presentation, including fever, dyspnea, cough, and hemoptysis, is vague and crosses with other differential diagnosis [10].

In this novel case report, we describe a case of 26 years-old female with recurrent hospitalization due to multiple cardiovascular involvements including pulmonary embolism, right pulmonary artery aneurysm, and lastly complicated right atrial thrombosis. The patient was diagnosed with Behçet disease at age 17 years old, despite the fact that Behçet disease is commonly diagnosed among males between 20 and 40 years [2,3]. Therefore, these unusual patient features and rare complications make our case interesting and significantly unique.

Previous studies have reported the presence of thrombosis in multiple locations among Behçet disease patients, most commonly in deep veins of lower limbs as DVT [11]. Moreover, intracardiac thrombosis in Behçet disease can be associated with other cardiovascular complications, including superior vena cava thrombosis [12], external iliac vein thrombosis [12], pulmonary artery aneurysm, pulmonary artery thrombosis (40–56 %), venous thrombosis (42–56 %), and pulmonary embolism (52–55 %) [9,10]. Despite each of these complications being extremely rare, the presentation of a patient who was complicated with pulmonary embolism, right pulmonary artery aneurysm, and right atrial thrombosis is very uncommon in Behçet disease, making this case special and worthy.

According to the literature, intracardiac thrombosis is mostly present on the right side [3,7]. This may contribute to the lower pressure of the right atrium and the extension of the thrombi in the vena cava. However, because of the similarity to the clinical and radiological manifestations of common cardiovascular diseases, the diagnosis of cardiovascular lesions in Behçet disease may be challenging. This resemblance can result in a missed diagnosis or incorrect diagnosis. Thereby, possibly leading to serious consequences.

Thrombosis in Behçet disease is mainly due to an inflammatory process instead of the thrombophilic state. As a result, immunosuppressants are superior to anticoagulants in treating patients with thrombotic complications of Behçet disease, using both immunosuppressant and anticoagulant drugs as a medical treatment of intracardiac thrombosis showed a good prognosis in previously reported cases. Despite the majority of intracardiac thrombosis cases which managed medically by anticoagulants and immunosuppressive therapy [7]. Worthy to mention that patients must adhere to prescribed medications to reduce the frequency of Behçet disease flares manifested in intracardiac thrombosis [6,7]. Use of immunosuppressants is the mainstay for the prevention of recurrences, whereas anticoagulation therapy do not change the relapse rate in vascular BD.Our case was successfully managed surgically along with other medical therapies such as anticoagulants and maintained on immunosuppressants (Azathioprine 50 mg 1 ∗ 3, Prednisolone 5 mg 2 ∗ 1) [13,14]. A very good prognosis and improvement were noted during the patient's follow-up period. However, the decision of a management plan for such a patient is challenging and taken by a multidisciplinary team, because of the presence of more than one cardiovascular complication at the time of presentation.

Conclusion

This unique case of Behcet disease highlights the complexity of complications of this multisystem disease; the atypical vascular involvement underscores the need for high awareness among healthcare professionals to recognize and avoid the consequences of misdiagnosis of such manifestations. A collaborative approach and thorough evaluation are crucial to ensuring timely and effective management. Further research will help improve the understanding of the disease and build clear guidelines for the management of rare lethal complications.

Authors: Kaiqi Yang, Sagun Tiwari, Dezhi Liu, Qinfeng Xu, Lili Qi, Yue Zhu, Liling Jiang, Huang Zouqin, John Wong, Jianhua Chen, Zhenxiang Han

ABSTRACT
AbstractABSTRACTIntroductionMajor depressive disorder (MDD), the second leading cause of disability globally, is considered to be associated with a consequent deterioration in the quality of life and can lead to a major economic burden on medical service and suicide-related costs. Previous research has shown that acupuncture may be beneficial for treating MDD. However, there is a lack of rigorous evidence from previous studies comparing acupuncture with antidepressant medications. This study aims to assess the therapeutic potential of acupuncture in the management of depressive disorders.Methods and analysisA multicentre, randomised, participant-blind, sham-controlled, 2×2 factorial clinical trial, Acupuncture and Escitalopram for Treating Major Depression Clinical Study, aims to compare the efficacy of acupuncture versus escitalopram in treating depression. Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is the second leading cause of disability globally, accounting for 8.2% of years lived with disability worldwide.1 2 Coupled with a consequent deterioration in the quality of life, it lays a major economic burden on medical service costs and suicide-related costs.3 In China, depression has gradually become a major public health issue, with 41.2 million people experiencing MDD at any point in 2013at . The prevalence of MDD has been estimated to be 2.2% among Chinese men and 3.3% among Chinese women.4

The use of antidepressant is recommended as the first-line treatment in the guidelines by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence5 6 and American Psychiatric Association.7 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and selective serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) may have significant favourable safety and tolerability profiles compared with older molecules, such as tricyclic antidepressants and monoamine oxidase inhibitors in head-to-head clinical trials.8 9 The latest psychopharmacological studies have shed light on the safety and tolerability of escitalopram, while elucidating its benefits for depression treatment.9 10 However, antidepressant response rates are only around 50%–60%, and side effects such as gastrointestinal side effects, weight gain, sexual dysfunction and memory impairment are frequently observed in patients with MDD and may lead to medication discontinuation.11,15 Therefore, novel treatment strategies are continuously being pursued.16 17

Acupuncture, a fundamental component of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), widely used to treat various psychological diseases, is recommended as a second-line intervention for the treatment of mild-to-moderate MDD by the Canadian Network for Mood and Anxiety Treatments.18 Several meta-analysis and reviews have investigated the efficacy and safety of acupuncture for depression treatment.17 19 Both manual acupuncture and electroacupuncture can reduce the severity of depression and adverse reactions caused by antidepressants compared with SSRI/SNRI medication alone.

However, the majority of acupuncture trials lack blinding compared with antidepressant medications.19 This may result in overestimation of the effect size, requiring cautious interpretation of the results. Therefore, further trials should be designed to minimise bias, ensure adequate sample sizes for sufficient power and include an adequate number of treatment sessions.

In light of this, we report the protocol for a randomised controlled trial, Acupuncture and Escitalopram for Treating Major Depression Clinical Study (AE-TMDCS). This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of acupuncture as a treatment modality for MDD. The specific objectives of AE-TMDCS are threefold: first, to assess the effectiveness of acupuncture, both as a stand-alone therapy and in conjunction with antidepressants, in mitigating the severity of depressive symptoms; second, to compare the therapeutic efficacy of acupuncture against a standard dose of a first-line antidepressant treatment, specifically escitalopram at a dosage of 20 mg/day; and third, to furnish contemporary evidence regarding the potential of acupuncture to augment the therapeutic efficacy of antidepressant medications in the treatment of depression.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a multicentre, participant-blinded, sham-controlled, 2×2 factorial clinical trial aimed at evaluating the efficacy and safety of acupuncture in patients with depression and comparing acupuncture with an SSRI. Our goal is to assess whether active acupuncture, either alone or combined with escitalopram, will improve symptoms among patients with MDD.

This protocol is reported in accordance with SPIRIT (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials) guidelines and the trial results will be reported in accordance with CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) guidelines. STRICTA (Standards for Reporting Interventions in Clinical Trials of Acupuncture) reporting guidance for acupuncture intervention are also followed.20 Figure 1 illustrates the trial’s flow chart, providing a visual overview of the study’s progression. The study will be implemented across three medical facilities in Shanghai, namely, the Seventh People’s Hospital of Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Shanghai Mental Health Centre, and Shanghai Pudong New Area Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine.

The experimental protocol delineates the random assignment of subjects into four groups: a placebo cohort (sham acupuncture combined with placebo pills), an escitalopram cohort (sham acupuncture paired with escitalopram medication), an acupuncture cohort (active acupuncture alongside placebo pills) and a combined treatment cohort (active acupuncture with escitalopram medication). To uphold the double-blind standard for the pharmacological component of the study, escitalopram and its placebo counterpart will be uniformly packaged and dispatched directly to each participating clinical facility. This arrangement ensures that all participants are subjected to either genuine or placebo acupuncture interventions, facilitating a controlled evaluation of the efficacy of the treatments under investigation. The placebo cohort is essential for ensuring participant blinding in the control arm, which will help to minimise information bias during outcome measurement. It will allow us to clearly separate the intervention’s effects from the placebo effect, ensuring the study’s results will be reliable.

Patients

Patients of both genders, aged 18–75 years, diagnosed with MDD according to the DSM-5 criteria by two independent physicians will be recruited for this study. To ensure a diverse participant pool, recruitment will take place at hospital settings and community service centres, with further details on the recruitment process elaborated in the Recruitment section. Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Before the start of the trial, participants must either be drug-naïve, defined as having not been previously treated with antidepressants or any psychotropic medications, or undergo a drug washout period. A minimum of 1 week washout from existing psychotropics will be required to ensure that participants are free from antidepressant medications for drug half-lives. Medications for concomitant medical illnesses will be permitted. Benzodiazepines will be allowed but will be tapered to less than 20 mg per day of diazepam or its equivalent. In addition, no other medications including herbal medications and acupuncture will be given by the investigators. This information will be documented in the record form.

Recruitment

For recruitment purposes, the following will be used: hospital-based advertisements on outpatient clinics, publicity at community service centres and WeChat posts (a Chinese mobile communication platform). Candidates willing to participate will then be asked to visit the hospital for screening assessment and specialised examinations, where they will be required to provide informed consent allowing them to participate in our trial. Prior to their participation, researchers with professional training in charge will explain to the patients the aim of the study, the procedures and the treatment steps in detail. The recruitment is scheduled to begin in September 2023 and will end in September 2026. Clinical recruitment staff at Seventh People’s Hospital of Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine will be responsible for the recruitment process.

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomly assigned into four groups including sham-acupuncture/placebo-pill (placebo group), sham-acupuncture/escitalopram (escitalopram group), active acupuncture/placebo-pill (acupuncture group) and active acupuncture/escitalopram by an interactive online computer program (ResMan Research Manager; http://www.medresman.org.cn) in a central office. This randomisation process will be managed by an independent team within the central office, specialised in clinical trial management and not involved in the direct conduct of the study, to ensure the objectivity and integrity of the allocation. The randomisation will be stratified at each participating site, using a concealed random block algorithm. A statistician outside of the research team will generate and validate the scheme with the SAS V.9.3 software (SAS Institute). Sequentially numbered, opaque envelopes containing random allocation cards will be sealed and stored securely, ensuring that they will remain inaccessible.

Until the completion of the statistical analysis, patient identities, outcome assessors and data analysts will be blinded to group allocations. Participants will be informed that they have an equal probability of being randomised into one of the four designated groups. Treatments will be administered according to the participants’ assigned random numbers on an individual basis to mitigate the risk of cross-communication among patients. Prior to the initiation of the trial, all researchers will receive comprehensive training on the specifications of the research methodology, thereby ensuring rigorous adherence to the principle of task segregation.

Interventions and procedures

All licensed acupuncturists on the study team, who have a TCM certification and at least three years of experience, will receive training on locating acupoints and non-acupoints, as well as on needle insertion and manipulation techniques before the trials begin. There will be at least one designated acupuncturist at each participating site. Hwato brand sterile disposable acupuncture needles (length: 15–20 mm, diameter: 0.25 mm) will be used. Both the acupuncture and sham acupuncture treatments will consist of 30 sessions with 30 min duration during 10 weeks (three sessions per week). Acupuncture will be discontinued if the patients suffer from any adverse events (AEs).

The acupuncture group will undergo treatment involving needle insertion at the specific acupoints. Acupuncture experts have selected these acupoints including GV20 (Baihui), SP4 (Gongsun), PC6 (Neiguan), PC5 (Jianshi) and LV 3 (Taichong). These acupoints are localised based on the WHO Standard Acupuncture Point Locations and shown in table 2 and figure 3. Those acupoints will all be punctured perpendicularly for about 1 cm. After needle insertion, the acupuncture needles will be manipulated by using either the twirling-rotating or lifting-thrusting methods to achieve the ‘De Qi’ sensation (a compositional sensation described as numbness, soreness, distention and heaviness), which is believed to be a vital element in the acupuncture efficacy. Manual stimulation with the needles will be performed by rotating it to evoke sensation at intervals of 10 mins, in a total of three times.

The acupuncturists will place the needle end above the skin and give the tube a slight knock. The treatment settings and procedures will be the same as those in the acupuncture group with the exception of skin penetration and needle manipulation for De Qi. Patients will be treated individually to avoid communication on the treatments they receive.

A pharmaceutical intervention will start simultaneously with acupuncture and consists of 10 mg pills of escitalopram or placebo. The placebo pills will be identical in size, colour and taste to escitalopram to ensure blinding of participants to treatment. This detail will be crucial for maintaining the study’s double-blind design and will be overseen with the utmost rigour to prevent any bias. Patients will receive 10 mg/day of escitalopram or placebo pills for the first 3 weeks and 20 mg/day for the remaining 7 weeks. Escitalopram is chosen as it is a more effective and tolerable antidepressant with few adverse effects and its full dose (20 mg/day) is easier to achieve than other antidepressant medications.8 9

Dropout criteria and adherence strategies

Participants experiencing serious clinical deterioration or severe worsening of psychiatric conditions during the trial will be considered for removal from active study treatment. Participants with low adherence, defined as missing three of more non-consecutive visits or two consecutive visits, will be automatically excluded from the study analysis.

The adherence strategies to minimise dropouts will include flexible scheduling, allowing up to two missed visits during the acupuncture phase; regular reminders via preferred communication methods; and offering active acupuncture poststudy to non-responders who received sham treatment. All participants, including those with low adherence, will be followed up as feasible to gather outcome data, ensuring their contributions are included in the study’s results.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome will be the change in the HDRS-17 Score from baseline to 10 weeks. HDRS is a widely used clinician-administered depression assessment scale for measuring depressive symptoms of patients diagnosed with depression. The 17-item version of the HDRS has the following score range: no depression (0–7), mild depression,8,13 moderate depression14,18 and severe depression (>19). A higher total score indicates a higher depression level.25 Evaluations will be performed at baseline and subsequently at the fourth, eighth and tenth weeks following the treatment.

Key secondary outcome measures

Secondary outcomes will include the rate of remission, defined as a score of ≤7 on the HDRS-17 after 10 weeks of treatment. Additional secondary outcomes will include changes from baseline in the Beck Depression Inventory Score, the Clinical Global Impression (CGI) Score, the Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD-7) Score, and changes in cognitive function assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) Score. Evaluations will be performed at baseline and subsequently at the fourth, eighth and tenth weeks following the treatment.

The Beck Depression Inventory Score is a global outcome that is classified as good, moderate, poor or unknown, and the research diagnostic criterion is that lower figures indicate less depressive symptoms.26

The CGI is a brief, stand-alone tool that an experienced physician can use to evaluate a patient’s overall functioning prior to and after initiating treatment. It includes two parts, namely CGI-Severity, which assesses the severity of psychopathology on the following 7-point scale (1=normal, not at all ill; 2=borderline mentally ill; 3=mildly ill; 4=moderately ill; 5=markedly ill; 6=severely ill; 7=among the most extremely ill patients), and CGI-Improvement, which rates changes from the baseline visit of the treatment from 1 to 7 (1=very much improved since the initiation of treatment; 2=much improved; 3=minimally improved; 4=no change from baseline; 5=minimally worse; 6=much worse; 7=very much worse since the initiation of treatment).27

Other secondary outcome measures

The relationship between a pertinent biomarker and a significant outcome will warrant additional exploration. Consequently, a series of biomarkers will be subjected to rigorous investigation throughout the duration of this study:

1. Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF):

BDNF, a crucial molecule for the structural adaption of neuronal networks, is essential for neuronal reactions to stressors. Substantial evidence has demonstrated a reduction in baseline BDNF levels in patients with MDD which tend to increase in response to successful treatment.30,32 In this protocol, blood levels of BDNF will be evaluated both at the initiation of the study (baseline) and at the end of the tenth week (endpoint).

2. IL-33, IL-18 and IL-1β

According to neuroinflammatory mechanisms, concentrations of interleukin 33 (IL-33), IL-18 and IL-1β have been identified as correlated with MDD. These cytokines are implicated in the activation of diverse innate immune responses related to inflammation, thereby serving a pivotal function in the initiation of neuroinflammation that precedes the onset of depression.33 In this trial, levels of IL-33, IL-18 and IL-1β in the blood will be evaluated both at the initiation of the study (baseline) and at the end of the tenth week (endpoint).

3. Resting-state functional MRI will be collected from all participants at two time points: at the study’s initiation (baseline) and on its conclusion (endpoint). The exploration will focus on the changes in the default-mode network (DMN) after treatment. Previous studies have indicated that depressed patients exhibit greater DMN activity and antidepressant therapy can alter the brain activity in these regions.34 35

Adverse events

AEs will be assessed at every visit. The acupuncturist and outcome assessors will monitor and record any AEs throughout the trial. Serious AEs in this trial will be documented. Investigator will consider those events like hospitalisation for a psychiatric cause, suicidality or attempted suicide, or events leading to major incapacity or a life-threatening condition as potentially serious adverse ones. Other common AEs include bleeding, subcutaneous haematoma, continuous pain, itching at the site of needle insertion, fainting and dizziness, and so on. If there are any serious adverse effects, the physician in charge will inform the principal investigator and the Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB), and appropriate medical care will be provided right away.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis will be performed by an independent statistician using SPSS V.21.0 statistical software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA). Sociodemographic and continuous data will be expressed as mean±SD, while categorical data will be converted into percentages.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome will be the change in HDRS-17 Scores from baseline to 10 weeks. In order to examine the effect of decrease in HDRS-17 Scores, rather than the effect of a decreasing or increasing change in HDRS-17 Scores, the three one-tailed t-tests as described in ‘Sample size calculation’ section will be used to examine the significance of the main effect of acupuncture, the main effect of medication, and the interaction between acupuncture and medication, respectively. It should be noted that all of these three t-tests are not the commonly used two-sample t-tests. For each of them tests an effect involving a linear contrast of the means of four groups instead of two, by using all experimental data from four groups instead of two. These t-tests are derived from testing of a linear hypothesis based on a linear model with normal distributed errors. The relevant theory and derivation can be found in references.36,38 Changes in HDRS-17 Scores from baseline will be analysed in the same manner at the end of weeks 4, 8 and 10, respectively.

Secondary outcomes

Continuous outcomes: Changes from baseline in BDI, CGI, GAD-7 and MMSE Scores will be analysed using the same one-tailed t-tests as described in the ‘Sample size calculation’ section at the end of 4 weeks, 8 weeks and 10 weeks, respectively. The main effect of acupuncture, the main effect of medication, and the interaction between acupuncture and medication on reduction of these measures will be examined over time.

Continuous outcomes: Changes from baseline in BDI, CGI, GAD-7 and MMSE Scores will be analysed using the same one-tailed t-tests as described in the ‘Sample size calculation’ section at the end of 4 weeks, 8 weeks and 10 weeks, respectively. The main effect of acupuncture, the main effect of medication, and the interaction between acupuncture and medication on reduction of these measures will be examined over time.

Categorical outcomes: The rate of remission (HDRS-17 Score ≤7 after 10 weeks) will be analysed using χ2 tests or logistic regression to compare the proportions of participants achieving remission across the treatment groups. Differences in the proportions of participants in each HDRS-17 category (eg, no depression, mild, moderate, severe) (after 10 weeks) will also be analysed using χ2 tests or logistic regression.

Categorical outcomes: The rate of remission (HDRS-17 Score ≤7 after 10 weeks) will be analysed using χ2 tests or logistic regression to compare the proportions of participants achieving remission across the treatment groups. Differences in the proportions of participants in each HDRS-17 category (eg, no depression, mild, moderate, severe) (after 10 weeks) will also be analysed using χ2 tests or logistic regression.

Time-based secondary outcomes: For time-based outcomes, statistical techniques designed for correlated data with repeated measurements at multiple time points, such as linear mixed-effects models, will be applied to evaluate the impact of time, treatment group and their interaction. These models will include treatment group, time and their interaction as fixed effects, and random intercepts to account for individual variability.

Baseline characteristics

Baseline characteristics will be compared using one-way analysis of variance for continuous variables and χ2 tests for categorical variables.

Significance

Values of p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant in all tests.

Ethical approval has been granted by the Seventh People’s Hospital of Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (No.2023-7th-HIRB-020), Shanghai Mental Health Centre (No.2022–86) and Shanghai Pudong New Area Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (No.2023–003).

Prior to randomisation, written informed consent will be obtained from all eligible participants (online supplemental file 1), ensuring both ethical compliance and informed participation.

After undergoing meticulous verification, data collected will be stored according to standard operating procedures. Acupuncturists, outcome assessors and statisticians will receive data management training. Data entry into the electronic case report form will be performed with precision. To maintain integrity, two independent researchers will upload case data and AE data to the system within a week of collection.

An independent DSMB will be established to monitor study progress, evaluate data, adjudicate AEs and consider any need for early study termination. The DSMB will function autonomously and comprise Chinese and US experts in various disciplines, including clinical trials, psychology, acupuncture and medical statistics.

The study results are intended for publication in a scholarly journal.

Discussion

The AE-TMDCS Trial, using a balanced 2×2 factorial design, will primarily investigate the potential interaction between acupuncture and escitalopram treatment in MDD. This hypothesis aligns with our goal to offer a potential alternative treatment for MDD, especially for patients who may prefer non-pharmacological options or who have contraindications for antidepressant use. This study’s structure will not only facilitate the evaluation of the individual effects of each treatment but will also allow for an in-depth analysis of their combined impact on MDD. Exploring interaction effects will be essential, and their consideration will be integrated into our sample size calculation to uncover potential synergies or antagonisms between the treatment modalities. By meticulously analysing these interactions, we can better understand the complexities of treating depression and refine our approach to integrate TCM with conventional pharmacotherapy.

In this study, all acupuncture procedures will be administered by experienced TCM doctors who will receive both theoretical and practical training for our protocol. To maximise the participants’ blinding, sham acupuncture will be used (similar to Streitberger placebo needle) with adhesive pads to provide participant-blinding effects and in the trial.39 This method maintains the participant-blinding effects with a similar appearance to conventional needles without any skin penetration.40

Other key aspects of AE-TMDCS will include: extending the washout period to confirm that the subjects are genuinely ‘anti-depressant drug-free’; inclusion of patients who are on benzodiazepine medications; the decision for the total number of sessions was based on finding from a meta-analysis that suggested that clinical effectiveness increased substantially before reaching 28 acupuncture sessions;41 HDRS-17 (instead of CGI) is used as the primary outcome measurement for its standardisation, validity and clinical relevance;42 MMSE is used to assess the cognitive impairment, because it is an important feature in individuals suffering from MDD. A meta-analysis has revealed that individuals with MDD exhibited greater impairment in executive function, attention and memory.43

In addition, several biomarkers will be assessed in the AE-TMDCS Trial. BDNF is viewed as instructive mediator of functional and structural plasticity, influencing various forms of learning and memory associated with depressive behaviour.44 Moreover, neuroinflammation, interacting with the depletion of brain serotonin, plays a crucial role in MDD. The pathophysiological cascade may be triggered and reinforced by any persistent inflammatory condition characterised by elevated markers of inflammation.45 We intend to examine if the biological predictors and mediators will be different between those responding to acupuncture and escitalopram.

Even so, there are some limitations in this protocol. First, comparing acupuncture solely to escitalopram will limit the generalisability of our findings across the broad spectrum of antidepressant treatments, suggesting caution when extending findings to treatments with different action mechanisms. Second, limitations of this study include the blinding design, where participants will be blinded to their group allocation through the use of sham acupuncture, but the practitioners delivering the treatments will be necessarily unblinded. This is a recognised challenge in acupuncture trials. To mitigate potential biases arising from this limitation, objective neuropsychological and biological tests will be conducted by assessors who will be blinded to treatment allocation, ensuring the objectivity of these outcomes. Additionally, the factorial design will allow for the isolation of the effects of acupuncture and escitalopram, both individually and in combination, providing valuable insights into their potential synergistic effects on depressive symptoms in patients with MDD. Furthermore, the potential for poor adherence over the 10-week treatment period is acknowledged as a limitation that could affect treatment efficacy estimates. Rigorous follow-up and adherence support will be implemented to minimise this impact.

Our study will employ a balanced 2×2 factorial design, intended to assess the individual and combined effects of acupuncture and escitalopram on depressive symptoms in patients with MDD enrolled in the AE-TMDCS Trial. We aim to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of acupuncture for depression. This investigation will contribute valuable insights into non-pharmacological treatment options for depression, potentially ensuring that patients will have access to a broader range of effective treatments.

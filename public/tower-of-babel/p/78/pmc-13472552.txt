Effectiveness of a Structured School-Based Emotional Intelligence Intervention on Stress Reduction and Psychological Well-being Among Adolescents: A Randomized Controlled Pilot Study
Authors: G. Mahalakshmi, P. Padmavathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Adolescent stress and compromised psychological well-being are emerging public health concerns with long-term neurobehavioral implications. Emotional intelligence (EI)-based interventions have shown promise but remain underutilized in structured school settings in India.Aim:To evaluate the effectiveness of a structured school-based EI program on stress reduction and psychological well-being among adolescents.Methods:A randomized controlled pilot study was conducted among 26 adolescents (13 experimental, 13 control) in a selected school in Coimbatore. Baseline and postintervention assessments were conducted using standardized tools: Students’ Stress Rating Scale and Brief Psychological Well-being Scale. The intervention consisted of a structured EI training module delivered over multiple sessions. Statistical analysis included paired and unpaired t-tests and Chi-square tests.Results:Postintervention, the experimental group demonstrated a significant reduction in stress scores (t = 18.10, P < 0.05) and a significant improvement in psychological well-being (t = 21.42, P < 0.05) compared to controls. The intervention resulted in a 57% reduction in stress and a 44% improvement in well-being scores.Conclusion:The school-based EI program was highly effective in improving psychological well-being and reducing stress among adolescents. Integration of EI training into school curricula is recommended as a preventive mental health strategy.

INTRODUCTION

Adolescence is a critical developmental phase characterized by emotional instability, academic pressure, and psychosocial transitions. Recent evidence indicates that a substantial proportion of adolescents experience moderate to severe stress, significantly impacting mental health outcomes.[12]

Despite increasing awareness, preventive, school-based psychological interventions remain inadequately implemented, particularly in low-resource settings.[3] Emotional intelligence (EI), defined as the ability to perceive, regulate, and utilize emotions effectively, has emerged as a promising construct for mental health promotion.[1]

However, there is a paucity of randomized controlled evidence evaluating structured EI programs in Indian school settings. This study addresses this gap by examining the effectiveness of a structured EI intervention.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A true experimental randomized controlled pilot study was conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of a structured school-based EI intervention on stress reduction and psychological well-being among adolescents. The study was carried out at C.B.M. Sakunthala Vidyalaya High School, Coimbatore, among adolescents aged between 14 and 17 years who exhibited mild to moderate stress levels. A total of 26 participants were selected using a simple random sampling technique and were randomly allocated into two groups: experimental (n = 13) and control (n = 13). Adolescents with a history of psychiatric illness or cognitive impairment were excluded from the study.

Baseline data were collected using a structured interview schedule comprising demographic variables, the Students’ Stress Rating Scale, and the Brief Psychological Well-being Scale. Following the pretest assessment, the experimental group received a structured Emotional Intelligence Program, which included emotional awareness training, cognitive restructuring, stress management techniques, social skills enhancement, and mindfulness-based practices delivered through multiple sessions. The control group received routine school activities without any specific intervention.

Posttest assessments were conducted using the same standardized tools after completion of the intervention. Statistical analysis was performed using paired t-tests to compare pre- and posttest scores within groups and unpaired t-tests to compare differences between groups. Chi-square tests were applied to determine associations between demographic variables and outcome measures. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrates that a structured school-based EI intervention significantly reduces stress and enhances psychological well-being among adolescents. The findings reveal a substantial shift from severe to mild stress levels and a marked improvement in psychological well-being in the experimental group compared to controls.

These results are consistent with previous studies, indicating that EI training enhances emotional regulation, coping skills, and resilience among adolescents. Anjum et al.[1] reported a high prevalence of stress among adolescents and emphasized the need for targeted interventions to address this issue. Similarly, Schraml et al.[2] highlighted that stress in adolescents is closely associated with emotional dysregulation and poor coping mechanisms.

From a pharmacological perspective, chronic stress is known to activate the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis, resulting in elevated cortisol levels and altered neurotransmitter balance, including serotonin and dopamine dysregulation. These neurobiological alterations often lead to anxiety and depressive disorders requiring pharmacotherapy such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and anxiolytics. The significant reduction in stress observed in this study suggests that EI interventions may modulate these neuroendocrine pathways, thereby reducing the need for pharmacological treatment.

The findings also align with the work of Moltrecht et al.,[3] who demonstrated that psychosocial interventions improve emotional regulation and reduce psychopathology in youth. Heizomi et al. further reported that school-based mental health programs significantly enhance psychological well-being and life satisfaction.[456]

Importantly, this study has implications for clinical pharmacy practice, as early nonpharmacological interventions can reduce the future burden of psychotropic drug use, minimize adverse drug reactions, and promote rational drug use. Pharmacists can play a pivotal role in advocating preventive mental health strategies and integrating behavioral interventions into patient care.

Overall, this study supports the integration of EI training into school curricula as a cost-effective, nonpharmacological strategy for improving adolescent mental health and reducing reliance on medications.

Evaluating the Effectiveness of Conventional Prostheses Against Dual-Mobility Prostheses in the Treatment of Femoral Neck Fractures in Two Separate Groups of Patients: A Clinical Trial Study
Authors: Mohammad Parhamfar, Alireza Hosseini, Mostafa Khashei, Mehdi Motififard, Amirmohammad Taravati, Somayeh Shirazinejad, Ali Afsharirad, Amirhossein Sadeghian, Ali Sherafati Chaleshtori
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Femoral neck fracture is a common posttraumatic injury in the elderly. Currently, orthopedic surgeons conducting total hip arthroplasty (THA) for hip fractures have various prosthetic alternatives. Dual-mobility cup THA is recommended for hip fracture patients to reduce prosthesis dislocation. Therefore, the goal of this research is to evaluate the effectiveness of conventional prostheses against dual-mobility prostheses in treating femoral neck fractures in two groups of patients.Materials and Methods:The current clinical investigation involved patients with femoral neck fractures. Among approved 84 patients, 44 and 40 were undergoing conventional THA (group A) and dual-mobility THA (group B), respectively. Patients were observed regularly after surgeries, and the same measurements were performed. The data were assembled and entered into SPSS software (version 25, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and analyzed. In all analyses, a P value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.Results:Eighty-four cases with an average age of 63.97 years were evaluated. In postoperative follow-up, there were no statistically significant differences between the groups in terms of Harris hip score, SF-36, and infection (P > 0.05). However, patients who received conventional implants had more dislocation postoperatively (P = 0.045). Regarding the rehabilitation costs, there were no statistically significant differences between the groups, so the group using the conventional implants incurred a higher cost (P = 0.041).Conclusion:According to the results, both dual mobility and conventional implants had optimal functional outcomes in patients with femoral neck fractures.

INTRODUCTION

Hip fractures are the leading cause of permanent disability and death in the elderly. Because of the high individual and societal costs of these injuries, they have long-term repercussions and pose a substantial public health burden.[1] The estimated number of hip fractures in the world in 2050 will be 4.5 million, up from 1.26 million in 1990 (although regional data varies widely).[2] Femoral neck fractures are common injuries in the elderly, occurring in 80% of cases in patients over the age of 70 and in 80% of cases in females, who have a lifetime 11.4% risk of femoral neck fractures at age 50. Arthroplasties are commonly used to treat displaced fractures.[3] It is one of the most operations not the most operation is a hemiarthroplasty, although it has the potential to cause acetabular cartilage erosion, protrusion, and discomfort, which could require conversion to a total hip arthroplasty (THA).[4]

Primary THA may improve implant survival, reduce discomfort, and improve function in carefully selected patients, making it a viable surgical choice for some patients with femoral neck fragility fractures compared to secondary THA after primary fixation.[5] The greater rate of dislocation with THA compared to hemiarthroplasty, an issue that may necessitate revision surgery if repeated, is a crucial factor to consider while treating femoral neck fractures.[6] This problem may be a factor in the low rate of THA utilization in patients with appropriate femoral neck fractures. Patients, surgeons, and healthcare systems must know which THA prosthesis combination is safest for femoral neck fractures in terms of dislocation and revision rate.[789]

There are currently a variety of prosthetic options available to orthopedic surgeons who perform THA procedures for hip fractures. Dual-mobility cup THA prostheses have been presented as an alternative to traditional prostheses to lessen the risk of dislocation in patients with hip fractures.[1011] It is promoted as an improvement over conventional THA due to the stability advantages of a large-bearing femoral head structure, which allows for greater height gains before dislocation during the vertical leap.[12] Although more polyethylene wear and intraprosthetic dislocation are possible, these potential benefits come at a more expensive implant cost than standard THA. While randomized clinical trials are lacking, observational studies advocating for dual-mobility implants have shown a small reduction in revision rates due to dislocation and a small reduction in revision rates overall.[13]

Retrospective investigations that compare dual-mobility and standard THA indicate that the revision risk is lower with dual-mobility prostheses, despite these studies being limited by insufficient follow-up for the desired results and a moderate-to-high potential for bias.[14] Surgical registries are useful for determining changes in surgical procedures, monitoring implant efficiency, and identifying regional variations in quality of care. Reduced bias risk for important outcomes, such as revision surgery, is another benefit, particularly in groups with high death rates, such as the population of individuals with hip fractures. The purpose of this research is to evaluate the effectiveness of conventional prostheses against dual-mobility prostheses in treating femoral neck fractures in two groups of patients.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The current study is a clinical trial conducted on patients with femoral neck fractures referred to Kashani Hospital in Isfahan City in 2022-2023. The study protocol was accepted by the Isfahan University of Medical Sciences Research Committee and certified by the Ethics Committee (IRCT20210614051574N17).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for patients in the study were aged between 40 and 74, patients with femoral neck fractures confirmed by an orthopedic surgeon, candidates for THA, and giving informed and written consent. Exclusion criteria were patients with multiple traumas, pathologic fractures, or chain injuries to the affected limb, previous hip surgery, unwillingness to participate in the study, and nonreferral for follow-up treatment.

Study population

The census approach was used to enroll all patients who matched the inclusion criteria. Demographic data of patients including age, gender and body mass index (BMI), and mechanism of fracture were obtained. We assessed variables including Harris hip score (HHS), rehabilitation time, and patient's quality of life using a 36-Item Short Form Survey Instrument (SF-36). Also, we evaluated the postoperative complications such as dislocation, revision THA, deep vein thrombosis (DVT)/pulmonary embolism (PE), and infection.

Measuring tools

Orthopedic specialist evaluated the following items for each patient before surgery:

The HHS is a joint-specific score filled by both the physician and the patient that consists of ten items encompassing the domains of pain, function, functional activities, deformity, and hip range of motion. The HHS has been used to assess functional outcomes following pertrochanteric hip fractures and intracapsular fractures of the femoral neck. The HHS is a health-related outcome measure administered by a competent healthcare professional, such as a physician or physical therapist.[15]

For each patient, SF-36 questionnaires were also filled out. This questionnaire is a frequently used, well-researched, self-reported health evaluation covering eight categories. The SF-36 questionnaire is one of the most reliable quality-of-life questionnaires for assessing an individual's quality of life.[16]

Surgical technique

All of the patients went to the emergency room. Following the initial resuscitation, stabilization, and clinical assessment of the patients, plain radiographs of the anteroposterior and lateral views were obtained from the patients to confirm the diagnosis. After providing written and informed consent, one surgeon admitted and operated on patients for THA. Then, all cases were divided into two groups randomly. Among 84 patients, 44 and 40 cases were undergoing conventional THA (group A) and dual-mobility THA (group B), respectively [Figure 1].

Consort diagram of patients that underwent conventional THA (group A) and dual-mobility THA (group B)

Preoperative templating was conducted on all patients utilizing proper size X-rays with size markers to predict implant size, position, and level of neck cut. All patients were operated on in the lateral position using a modified Harding technique in a standardized operating room. Cupules Evolutis CAPTIV® Dual-Mobility Acetabular implants with spur and HACTIV® sans collarette Avec calerett stems were used for all dual-mobility THAs. In comparison, the Zimmer Continuum Acetabular Device with CLS/CPT 12/14 Femoral Device or the Smith and Nephew Femoral System were utilized.

Dual-mobility implants were positioned per the native acetabulum's morphology, with no superior overhang to facilitate reduction. Depending on the grade of the bone and the anatomy of the canal, a cemented or uncemented stem was utilized.

Postoperative rehabilitation

Each patient followed a similar recovery strategy. Three injections of 1.5 g of cefuroxime were administered 12 hours apart: one preoperatively and two postoperatively. Both mechanical treatments (pneumatic compression devices and DVT stockings) and pharmacological treatments (LMWH or aspirin) were used to prevent DVT. On day 1, every patient was allowed full weight-bearing activity and performed the identical physiotherapy protocol.

Postoperative follow-up

After operations, patients received routine visits, and the exact measurements were taken 3, 9, and 18 months later. The accurate measurements were taken at 3, 9, and 18 months postoperatively to ensure patient satisfaction. Radiographs were acquired immediately postoperatively, 3 months, 9 months, and 18 months after surgery, and they were analyzed for instability, loosening, lysis, and displacement.

Statistical analysis

After the study data were collected, they were input into SPSS software (version 25, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and analyzed. Using mean, standard deviation, and median, descriptive statistics for quantitative data were calculated and compared using the unpaired T-test. The Chi-square test was utilized to compare qualitative data represented as frequencies and percentages. Data were compared between the dual-Mobility Implant and traditional THA implant groups. Unpaired t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Mann–Whitney U tests were used to compare the two groups, Dislocation rates and Modified HHS, respectively, after assuming they were normally distributed. In all analyses, a P value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.

RESULT

Study population

Eighty-four patients with femoral neck fractures, with a mean age of 63.97 years, underwent THA surgeries and were eligible for final follow-up at 18 months. Regarding gender, patients were evenly distributed. Participants were divided into two different groups according to the type of implant used. Forty patients (47.6%) underwent THR with dual mobility, and 44 (52.4%) underwent conventional THR. Complication

Three months following surgery, every patient could walk with full weight bearing and ascend stairs without difficulty. In none of the patients, intraoperative complications were present. According to Table 2, three and five patients from the dual-mobility THA and conventional THA groups, respectively, were identified with an infection at the incision site at the first postoperative follow-up, which was totally cured with debridement and antibiotics. Also, two patients from the dual-mobility THA group and patients from the conventional THA group had postoperative dislocation. The differences between the two categories were marginally significant (P = 0.045). In terms of rehabilitation costs, patients who received dual-mobility prosthesis spent an average of 565.2$, compared to 612.4$ for those who received conventional implants. There were marginally significant differences between the two groups (P = 0.041) [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Dual-mobility hip fractures are an example of a condition that necessitates THA since these patients have an increased likelihood of prosthesis displacement and need revision surgery soon after initial joint replacement. In cases of hip fracture, the currently available observational series that favor the use of dual-mobility THA over traditional THA are small and ineffective at detecting crucial outcomes like revision surgery. In addition, it may not be possible to conduct adequately conducted randomized controlled trials due to the large number of patients required.[1718] However, the cost of a dual-mobility THA is higher than that of a traditional THA, and whether or not the additional clinical benefits translate into better value for healthcare systems is uncertain. The evidence of cost-effectiveness should be used to support whether or not to utilize dual-mobility THA components as the therapy for femoral neck fractures. Evidence that was previously published on the cost-effectiveness of dual-mobility THA was either limited exclusively to revision THA, including all indications for THA, or limited to primary THA for arthritis. Neither of these approaches was comprehensive.[19] This research aims to determine whether or not a dual-mobility prosthesis is more cost-effective than a standard prosthesis for treating displaced neck fractures in older individuals.

In our study, 40 patients (47.6%) underwent dual-mobility THR, and 44 (52.4%) underwent conventional THA. In postoperative follow-up, there were no significant differences between the two groups regarding HHS, SF-36, and infection. However, patients who received conventional implants had more dislocation postoperatively. Regarding the rehabilitation cost of the operation, there was a marginally significant difference between the two groups, so the group receiving the conventional implants incurred a higher cost.

In 2021, Agarwala et al.[20] conducted a prospective cohort study to compare the dual-mobility THA with the conventional THA for patients with a femoral neck fracture. Among the 103 patients, 52 underwent dual-mobility THA, and 51 underwent conventional THA. In this study, after 3 months and 1 year of follow-up, the functional outcome of HHS was significantly superior in the dual mobility group than in the traditional implant group (P = 0.0001). In the motion section as well as the exercises that involved sitting cross-legged or squatting, the score was higher. In addition, the results of the second follow-up showed that both groups had improved function compared to the results of the first follow-up (P = 0.0001). In this study, there was no significant distinction between the two groups concerning the postoperative dislocation rate, the postoperative stability rate, the rate of implant loosening, or the rate of revision surgery. This is because none of the patients experienced any such complications. They postulated that this was because of the structure of a dual-mobility implant, in which a more compact inner head is responsible for the most effective movement. At the inner diameter's most and least flexible points, the femoral neck presses on the outer femoral head. The larger femoral head then contacts the acetabulum, completing the hip joint. As a direct consequence of this, the occurrence of impingement has been cut down while the range of motion has been expanded. However, the results of our research showed that there was no substantially different HSS between the two groups.

Dislocation following THA is considered “high risk” in patients with particular characteristics, such as advanced age, cognitive impairment, noncompliance with rehabilitation procedures, neuromuscular issues, and a small acetabular cup that cannot accommodate a large femoral head.[21] Evidence indicates bipolar hemiarthroplasty may increase stability at the fractured neck of the femur, so many surgeons use it on patients at such high risk.[22] The implant's dual-mobility design, which allows articulation among the metal component and the external polyethylene bearing, attempts to reduce afterward instability due to a high femoral head-to-neck proportion by increasing the jump distance required for the femoral head to be detached from the acetabular component.[20] The risk of dislocation due to the femoral head or neck impinging on the acetabular component is reduced with greater vertical leap height. Using Charnley's concept of low-friction arthroplasty, the vast majority of studies conclude that THR outperforms bipolar hemiarthroplasty as a result of long-term functional outcomes. Combining these two ideas would result in a dual-mobility THA implant that offers the advantages of each.[23]

In 2018, Zagorov et al.[24] compared the postoperative dislocation rate of displaced femoral neck fractures treated with a dual-mobility THA to that of conventional THA and bipolar hemiarthroplasty and found that the dual-mobility THA had a 0% dislocation rate as opposed to 11.1% in conventional THA and 3.1% in bipolar hemiarthroplasty. When it comes to after surgical stability and the likelihood of requiring revision arthroplasty, similar comparative studies that have been published have shown that dual-mobility THA is preferable to conventional THA.

For femoral neck fractures in elderly patients, Alberio et al.[21] investigated the short-term clinical and radiological outcomes of conventional and dual-mobility THA. The findings show that dual-mobility implants are an effective choice for treating displaced femoral neck fractures in older individuals because they reduce the risk of postoperative dislocations while enabling them to obtain short-term results that are comparable to those of conventional THA.

Although dual-mobility implants improved hip stability, retrospective comparative research by Sappey-Marinier et al.[25] found that they also increased the risk of periprosthetic fractures by transferring more weight to the femur. In this trial, regular and dual-mobility implants were given to 53 and 73 individuals overall, respectively. Twenty-nine standard implants (83%) and six dual-mobility implants (17%) were present in the dislocation group. The dislocation was 12 times more common in conventional implants (P = 0.001).

Because hip fractures are widespread across people in varying stages of health, several studies have purposely excluded the medically frail and patients with considerable cognitive impairment, reporting their findings on fewer healthy participants. Montgomery et al.[19] believe that clinical discretion should be used in individuals considering THA for a femoral neck fracture. When dealing with younger, healthier, or more active patients after suffering a hip fracture, it is important to approach the conclusions of registry studies, insurance data research, and other comprehensive research pertaining to hip fractures with an appropriate level of suspicion. Dual-mobility THA was not cost-effective in the first two postoperative years across all age categories, according to sensitivity analysis. However, after five years, dual-mobility THA was cost-effective for individuals aged 60 to 64 and cost-saving for patients aged 65 to 79. Dual-mobility THA was less expensive in all patients under 70 than standard THA after 10 years. Patients aged 80 and up who met the $50,000 per quality-adjusted life year threshold did not benefit economically from dual-mobility THA. Surgeons must continue to exercise clinical judgment and account for the patient's physiological age and dislocation risk factors. As the model is based on clinical data, the financial viability of dual-mobility THA components must be looked at again if significant differences in dislocation rates are found in future multicenter randomized trials.

We suggest that more studies be conducted with a higher statistical population and by investigating different hip pathologies requiring THA.

CONCLUSION

According to the results, both dual mobility and conventional implants had the appropriate functional outcomes in patients with femoral neck fractures. However, conventional implants had a higher risk of dislocation than dual-mobility implants. Also, the cost of the rehabilitation for dual-mobility THA was than conventional THA.

Thematic patterns of social media content by patient-led breast cancer key opinion leaders: A topic modeling study
Authors: Yiwen Duan, Yanjin Huang, Lili Wang, Chaojin Da, Qi Sun, Tingting Cai, Changrong Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the thematic focus of patient-led breast cancer key opinion leaders (PBKOLs) on Xiaohongshu by analyzing their postings and identifying underlying narrative patterns.MethodsPBKOL accounts were identified through systematic keyword searches on Xiaohongshu. Their publicly available text–image and video posts were retrieved, transcribed, and standardized. Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) was used to extract latent themes, and subthemes were inductively classified to capture narrative nuances.ResultsA total of 65 PBKOLs contributed 6852 valid posts between December 2017 and July 2025. Four core themes were identified: recovery life and functional self-management (13.60%), medical decision-making and treatment journey (26.80%), emotional adjustment and meaning-making (31.30%), and family routine and relational support (28.30%). Emotional narratives predominated, while family-oriented posts emphasized relational support and treatment-related content reflected practical but less frequent engagement. PBKOLs varied widely in influence, ranging from grassroots users to highly followed opinion leaders.ConclusionsPBKOLs shape breast cancer health communication by prioritizing emotional resonance and community support over professional instruction. While their narratives enhance trust, empowerment, and cultural relevance, concerns remain regarding scientific accuracy. Integrating patient narratives with evidence-based guidance may help develop more balanced and sustainable digital health strategies.

Introduction

In recent years, breast cancer has emerged as the most common and fastest-increasing malignancy among women worldwide.1 According to the World Health Organization, 2.3 million women were newly diagnosed and 670,000 died from breast cancer in 2022.2 In China, an estimated 357,000 new cases and 75,000 deaths accounted for 15.6% of all new cancers and 7.9% of cancer-related deaths among women.3 Despite advances in early diagnosis and treatment, patients continue to face substantial incidence and mortality risks, while also enduring substantial treatment costs, complex rehabilitation processes, and persistent psychological adjustment burdens.4 In contrast to conventional disease management models, breast cancer patients show a growing dependence on non-clinical needs, including emotional adjustment, informational support, and decision-making companionship, with information burden increasingly influencing patient experience and participation in rehabilitation.4,5

In the digital era, social media has become a crucial channel for information acquisition and community interaction, engaging over 5 billion users worldwide.6 Among breast cancer patients, it is widely used to obtain medical knowledge, share treatment experiences, manage side effects, and seek emotional support.7 Within this evolving digital environment, patient-led breast cancer key opinion leaders (PBKOLs) have emerged as a significant force in health communication.8 Unlike medically oriented key opinion leaders whose authority is grounded in professional credentials, PBKOLs disseminate cancer-related experiences, rehabilitation practices, and psychological journeys from a lived-experience perspective across social media platforms.9, 10, 11 Their influence primarily derives from experiential credibility and emotional trust, enabling them to bridge cognitive and emotional gaps that traditional professional communication may not fully address. From a health communication perspective, PBKOLs function both as opinion leaders within patient communities and as intermediaries who reinterpret and recontextualize professional information. Given that social media communication is shaped by platform-specific cultures and technological environments, contextualized analysis is essential. The Chinese social media ecosystem, characterized by high mobile penetration and strong platform localization, provides a particularly relevant setting.12 Among major Chinese platforms, Xiaohongshu emphasizes user-generated experiential sharing and multimodal narrative expression, supporting coherent narratives of illness experiences.13, 14, 15 With a predominantly young female user base and user-generated content comprising the majority of posts, the platform represents an appropriate data source for examining PBKOL communication.16,17

As PBKOLs continue to produce large volumes of experiential content on social media, patient narratives have evolved from fragmented individual accounts into a more structured patient-led expressive system.18 Under these conditions, case-based analyses or manual thematic summaries are insufficient to capture the overall thematic structure and communicative focus of PBKOL content, underscoring the need for approaches capable of analyzing large-scale unstructured text. Topic modeling provides an effective method for identifying latent thematic structures in large text corpora, with Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) widely used in online health communication research due to its stability and interpretability.19 Prior research has successfully applied LDA to health-related social media data: Nzali et al.20 found that patient discussions on Facebook and forums closely aligned with established clinical functions and symptom scales, while Da et al.21 revealed variations in the needs of breast cancer patients and their families across different stages of treatment through LDA-based analyses of online health community content. However, existing research has predominantly treated social media texts as general patient-generated content, paying limited attention to how distinct communicative actors organize narratives and for what functional purposes. To address this gap, the present study shifts the analytical focus from what is discussed to how PBKOLs function as distinct communicative actors within social media environments. Specifically, this study examines how PBKOLs’ thematic content forms a functional thematic repertoire oriented toward health education, emotional support, and decision accompaniment. Accordingly, LDA is applied not merely as a topic classification technique, but as an analytical approach to characterize patient-led experiential knowledge structures and communicative functions.

To further interpret the communicative role of PBKOLs, this study draws on the two-step flow of communication theory and the information ecology framework as analytical perspectives. The two-step flow of communication theory conceptualizes opinion leaders as key intermediaries who reinterpret and transmit information within social networks.22 In this study, this perspective informs the analytical focus on PBKOLs as intermediary communicators who translate medical knowledge into experiential narratives that shape patients’ perceptions, coping strategies, and engagement with care. In social media settings, PBKOLs therefore function not merely as information sharers but as actors who mediate the flow of health information within patient communities. The information ecology framework emphasizes the dynamic interactions among information actors, content, technologies, and environments.23 This framework guides the contextual interpretation of PBKOL communication by situating their narratives within a patient-centered digital information ecosystem, in which patients, PBKOLs, and social media platforms jointly shape the production, circulation, and uptake of experiential health information. Together, these perspectives provide a conceptual basis for examining how PBKOL-generated narratives organize experiential knowledge and thematic communication patterns on social media. Guided by these perspectives, this study uses Xiaohongshu as the empirical context and applies LDA topic modeling to PBKOL posts to identify latent thematic structures, thereby generating empirical insights into patient-led health communication and informing the development of patient-centered and culturally responsive digital health interventions in breast cancer nursing practice.

Methods

Data source and selection of PBKOLs

This study was reported in accordance with the Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research (SRQR) guideline (Supplementary File 1). This study was conducted on Xiaohongshu, a leading Chinese social media platform, focusing on PBKOLs who actively shared breast cancer–related content. Relevant users and posts were systematically identified using the keywords “breast cancer,” “breast cancer patient,” “anti-cancer diary,” “chemotherapy diary,” and “breast cancer rehabilitation,” and purposive sampling was applied to select eligible PBKOLs. Following eligibility screening, all breast cancer–related posts published from the onset of posting activity to the time of data collection were included, enabling a comprehensive representation of PBKOLs’ longitudinal expressions throughout their disease journeys. PBKOLs were included if they met the following criteria: (1) breast cancer as the primary theme of posts; (2) at least 50 followers with ≥ 70% original content, to ensure sustained and authentic personal narratives rather than occasional or repost-based activity; and (3) self-identification as a breast cancer patient with a reasonably clear description of the disease course.

Data collection and text preprocessing

Publicly available user posts (including text-image and video notes) and interaction data were systematically retrieved from Xiaohongshu using Python and related libraries. Given the platform's security mechanisms for request signatures and cookie management, valid cookies were acquired via browser packet capture, and legitimate request parameters were simulated according to publicly available signature-generation logic to ensure access.24 A persistent session was then established using Python's requests library to maintain cookies, while the Scrapy framework was employed for keyword searches, pagination, and bulk extraction.25 The extracted structured fields included user ID, post content, posting time, and number of likes. Video content was transcribed into text using the iFLYTEK automatic speech recognition (ASR) system and subsequently incorporated into the textual corpus. Prior to modeling, all textual data underwent a standardized preprocessing pipeline, including the removal of irrelevant content; normalization of simplified and traditional Chinese characters and special character encoding; elimination of stop words, emojis, and redundant punctuation; and word segmentation using the Jieba Chinese tokenizer (version 0.42.1). To improve the accuracy of domain-specific term recognition, the default Jieba dictionary was supplemented with a customized medical lexicon referencing the Chinese Medical Subject Headings and ICD-10 Chinese disease nomenclature related to breast cancer, including terms for diseases, treatments, symptoms, and rehabilitation, with further manual refinement based on the study corpus.26,27 Stop words were primarily drawn from the Harbin Institute of Technology Chinese stop-word list and extended to exclude contextually irrelevant high-frequency functional terms.28 During part-of-speech filtering, nouns, verbs, and adjectives with higher semantic value were retained. Synonym normalization was conducted using a combination of rule-based procedures and domain knowledge to unify semantically equivalent expressions (e.g., different terms referring to the same disease, treatment modality, or common rehabilitation behavior), thereby reducing semantic redundancy.

With respect to commercial or promotional content, a tiered data-cleaning strategy was applied. Posts primarily intended for commercial promotion and lacking personal illness narratives were entirely excluded from the analytical corpus. For posts that contained advertising or product recommendations but were predominantly centered on personal experiences of breast cancer treatment or recovery, only narrative segments related to disease experience, health management, or emotional expression were retained, while pricing information, purchase links, and overt promotional language were removed during preprocessing. Topic modeling and determination of optimal topic number

LDA was applied to the PBKOL corpus to perform unsupervised topic modeling and identify latent semantic structures.29 Modeling was implemented with the Gensim library in Python, and pyLDAvis was used for visualization and semantic validation.30 The optimal number of topics was determined using perplexity, a measure of model predictive ability, with lower values indicating better performance.31 Model outputs included probability distribution vectors for each document, reflecting their association with topics, and the top 50 keywords per topic, which informed subsequent labeling and interpretation.

Topic naming and semantic interpretation

Two researchers with expertise in oncology nursing and health communication independently named the topics based on (1) high-frequency keyword sets from the LDA model and (2) the semantic implications of representative textswithin each topic. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion to reach consensus. Naming followed three principles: accurately reflecting thematic scope, maintaining consistency with patient concerns across the treatment journey, and ensuring semantic distinctiveness and complementarity among topics. It should be noted that the LDA model was used solely to identify latent semantic structures based on word co-occurrence patterns in the text, whereas topic labels and semantic interpretations were derived from researchers’ inductive analysis of high-probability keywords and representative excerpts rather than being automatically generated by the model.

Subtheme classification and representative text extraction

To further explore heterogeneity within topics, a secondary classification was conducted. Subtheme classification was independently conducted by the same two researchers based on narrative focus, semantic content, and communicative function. The classification results were then compared, with discrepancies resolved through discussion and, when necessary, adjudicated by a third researcher. Based on this process, representative texts were selected from each subtheme to support result presentation and interpretation, following principles of informational completeness, semantic representativeness, and clarity of expression to enhance the transparency and interpretability of the findings.

Results

Characteristics of textual data

A total of 65 PBKOLs from Xiaohongshu were included, and their historical text–image and video posts were collected. Between December 2017 and July 2025, 9068 text–image entries and 2957 videos were retrieved. All videos were transcribed into text and, along with text–image posts, underwent standardized preprocessing. After cleaning, 6852 valid documents were retained for analysis.

Selection of topic number and model evaluation

To determine an appropriate number of topics, the cleaned corpus was modeled repeatedly with LDA, and performance was initially assessed using perplexity. As shown in Fig. 2A, perplexity declined sharply from three to four topics, indicating improved model fit, but increased beyond four, suggesting potential overfitting and less distinct semantic boundaries. However, as perplexity primarily reflects statistical fit rather than semantic interpretability, it was not used as the sole criterion for topic selection. Instead, perplexity trends were considered alongside qualitative assessment of topic coherence and interpretability. Distinctiveness and cohesion of the extracted topics were further examined using pyLDAvis, which produced a two-dimensional inter-topic distance map. Each circle represented a topic, with size indicating prevalence and distance reflecting semantic overlap. As shown in Fig. 2B, the four topics were relatively well separated with limited overlap, suggesting acceptable semantic differentiation among topics. In addition, the interpretability of topic keywords and representative texts was reviewed by the research team to ensure conceptual clarity. Considering the combined evidence from perplexity trends, visualization results, and qualitative interpretability, four topics were selected as a suitable structure for subsequent labeling and analysis.Fig. 2LDA model evaluation (a) perplexity curve (b) inter-topic distance map. Extracted topics

The LDA modeling identified four latent themes with clear semantic coherence and distinction, encompassing different experiential dimensions of breast cancer patients’ disease management. During the processes of topic naming and categorization, the two researchers demonstrated a high level of consistency in theme interpretation and assignment, with initial discrepancies resolved through iterative review of keyword distributions and representative texts followed by consensus discussion. This process resulted in a stable thematic structure comprising recovery life and functional self-management (Topic 1), medical decision-making and treatment journey (Topic 2), emotional adjustment and meaning-making (Topic 3), and family routine and relational support (Topic 4). Among all documents, emotional adjustment and meaning-making was most prominent (31.30%), followed by family routine and relational support (28.30%) and medical decision-making and treatment journey (26.80%). Recovery life and functional self-management was less represented (13.60%), as illustrated in Fig. 3. This distribution suggests that PBKOLs emphasized emotional experiences, family support, and treatment decisions more than lifestyle or self-management content.Fig. 3Topic distribution in the LDA model. LDA, Latent Dirichlet Allocation.Fig. 3

Topic distribution in the LDA model. LDA, Latent Dirichlet Allocation.

After LDA modeling, the top 50 keywords were extracted for each topic to reveal semantic cores and distributional features, visualized as word clouds. Rankings were based on topic–word probability, defined as the conditional probability P(word | topic), rather than raw frequency. Higher probability values indicate stronger representativeness and interpretive power, marking such words as core terms in delineating the semantic boundaries of each theme. Although certain high-frequency semantic elements appeared across multiple topics, their associated keyword co-occurrence patterns and semantic contexts exhibited distinct characteristics within each theme.

Topic 1: Recovery life and functional self-management

This theme centers on self-care experiences during the postoperative recovery stage, encompassing exercise, diet, nutritional intake, and complementary practices such as traditional Chinese medicine. Representative keywords included exercise, diet, nutrition, rehabilitation, supplement, protein, sleep, Ba Duan Jin, traditional Chinese medicine, and stretching. Posts often offered practical guidance on dietary planning, exercise frequency, weight control, and lymphedema management, reflecting a pragmatic orientation. Overall, this content demonstrated patients’ proactive use of lifestyle strategies to enhance function and quality of life, while positioning PBKOLs as providers of rehabilitation experience. Topic 2: medical decision-making and treatment journey

This theme covers the complete treatment trajectory, including diagnosis, hospitalization, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and follow-up, highlighting the medical pathways and pivotal decisions encountered by PBKOLs throughout their cancer journey. Frequently occurring keywords included doctor, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, treatment, side effect, recheck, pathology, hospitalization, targeted therapy, recurrence, and medical insurance. Posts often described treatment selection, side-effect management, and insurance reimbursement, offering contextualized, relatable information that supported patients’ navigation and decision-making. Discussion

Main findings

Based on LDA topic modeling, this study identified four core themes and corresponding subthemes in the content produced by PBKOLs on Xiaohongshu, revealing a sustained narrative structure across different stages of the disease trajectory. The findings suggest that PBKOLs' posts are not characterized by unidirectional information delivery; instead, they weave treatment decision-making, recovery management, and everyday life experiences into experiential and emotionally grounded narratives, resulting in contextually embedded modes of expression. Interpreted through the lens of the two-step flow of communication theory, PBKOLs act as “secondary interpreters” who reorganize and personalize professional health information within patient communities, enabling information understanding to co-occur with emotional resonance. The thematic patterns identified in this study further highlight how this mediation process operates in patient-led online environments, where experiential storytelling and emotional expression become key mechanisms through which health information is interpreted and shared. From an information ecology perspective, this narrative process unfolds within a patient-driven information environment grounded in lived experience, emotional feedback, and social interaction. The prominence of emotionally and relationally oriented themes identified through topic modeling suggests that patient-generated narratives play an active role in shaping how health information circulates and is interpreted within digital health ecosystems. In contrast to traditional clinician-centered medical communication, this patient-experience-based mode of expression prioritizes comprehensibility and empathy, fostering relatively stable trust among patients, which is consistent with recent reviews on empowerment and participation in breast cancer–related social media contexts.32 Viewed through the lens of digital health intervention, PBKOLs are more appropriately understood as informal sources of support embedded in patients’ everyday lives. Their primary contribution lies in reducing cognitive barriers to health information and encouraging sustained engagement in recovery management and evidence-based care, rather than replacing standardized medical decision-making.

The findings further indicate that PBKOLs are diverse and differentiated within the social media health communication ecosystem. This differentiation is most clearly reflected in the thematic patterns identified through topic modeling, particularly the prominence of emotional adjustment and meaning-making and family routine and relational support, which suggest that PBKOL communication is strongly oriented toward experiential storytelling and interpersonal connection. Although the present study did not directly examine follower influence, such narrative patterns may help explain why some PBKOLs cultivate trust and resonance within patient communities.33 This aligns with Dubois et al.,34 who surveyed 2000 French Internet users and found that opinion leaders and seekers reported higher levels of trust, fact-checking, and avoidance of echo chambers, underscoring that trust is often built through interpersonal sharing and validation and is perceived as more reliable than mainstream media. Similarly, a study of Facebook communities revealed higher trust in small, homogeneous, and closed groups, echoing the pattern in which PBKOLs with fewer followers but higher engagement are perceived as more trustworthy.35 Geographically, PBKOLs also displayed considerable diversity. While many were concentrated in first-tier cities such as Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong, others were active in underdeveloped regions such as Heilongjiang and Gansu, and some extended their influence internationally to Australia, the United States, Singapore, and Sweden. This pattern indicates that PBKOLs are able to transcend geographical boundaries and construct cross-regional and even transnational networks for patient support and information exchange through social media. However, cross-regional dissemination also poses challenges for cultural adaptation, as digital health interventions often face tensions between localization and universal applicability.36 Previous studies have shown that integrating health information with culturally familiar elements—such as traditional diets, rehabilitation exercises, or traditional Chinese medicine practices—can enhance cultural resonance and audience acceptance.37 From a nursing practice perspective, this highlights the need for nurses to attend to patients' exposure to cross-regional information sources and to facilitate informed integration of such information while respecting patients’ experiential identification. Meanwhile, existing research suggests that the information mobilization and diffusion capacities observed in cross-cultural communities in public health contexts share similarities with the cross-regional connectivity demonstrated by PBKOLs on social media, indicating that patient-led online communities may serve as important platforms for cross-regional support in the context of breast cancer.38

In this study, PBKOLs' posts were dominated by emotional themes, with emotional adjustment and meaning-making representing the largest share. This pattern is unlikely to be incidental and may reflect a functional choice made by PBKOLs within the two-step flow of communication. Compared with the direct transmission of medical knowledge, emotional narratives are more effective in lowering cognitive barriers, eliciting resonance, and fostering trust, thereby serving as an “emotional gateway” through which patients enter networks of information exchange and mutual support.39 Within a patient-led information ecology, emotional expression functions not only as a means of psychological release but also as a communicative strategy for enhancing interaction and sustaining community connectedness. This theme encompassed both emotional adjustment and psychological support and life meaning and value construction, showing that patients' narratives expressed anxiety and distress alongside psychological transformation through positive meaning-making. This pattern is consistent with prior social media research. Basch et al.39 reported that coping support and emotional sharing accounted for a substantial proportion of breast cancer–related TikTok videos. Similarly, Pierce et al.,40 analyzing breast cancer posts on Reddit, identified factors associated with anxiety, underscoring that emotional experience is a central dimension of online patient communication. The next most salient theme was family routine and relational support, with subthemes including family companionship, family interaction, and role transition. These narratives underscored the pivotal role of family ties throughout the cancer journey, complementing the emotional theme: the former emphasized relational collaboration, while the latter focused on individual emotional and psychological construction. This pattern mirrors a Facebook-based study showing that negative emotional openings more readily elicited interaction due to the primacy of emotion effect.41 In comparison, the themes of recovery life and functional self-management and medical decision-making and treatment journey appeared less frequently and generated lower overall engagement, yet their subthemes retained clear functional relevance across different stages of the disease trajectory. Although the subthemes “lifestyle and dietary rehabilitation” and “diet and recovery practices” may appear similar, they reflect distinct nursing contexts. The former emphasizes long-term lifestyle adjustment and sustained self-management during rehabilitation, whereas the latter focuses on managing treatment-related side effects and providing phase-specific physical support during active treatment. This distinction indicates that PBKOLs' expressions of similar behaviors shift in function as treatment stages and care priorities change. Overall, PBKOLs tend to function more as sources of emotional companionship than as providers of practical instruction. Rather than representing a risk or disruption, their influence should be understood as a meaningful contextual factor in nursing communication. By adopting a bridging role, nurses can recognize patients’ trust in PBKOL narratives and integrate these experiential accounts with evidence-based information, thereby preserving patient trust and supporting safer, more informed decision-making. Although this narrative pattern fosters trust, community identity, and health empowerment, affinity alone does not guarantee scientific rigor or safety. Johnson et al.42 found that 32.5% of popular cancer-related articles across Facebook, Reddit, and Twitter contained misleading information, of which 76.9% was potentially harmful; moreover, such misinformation often generated higher engagement than accurate content. Likewise, a systematic evaluation of breast cancer videos on TikTok and Bilibili revealed generally low content quality and credibility, with professional content rated higher but non-professional material more widely disseminated.43 These findings suggest that while acknowledging the emotional support value of PBKOLs, it is also necessary to pay close attention to the ethical responsibilities involved in their information dissemination, the potential risks of misinformation, and the need for appropriate professional guidance mechanisms.

In terms of content presentation, this study found that, unlike the short videos and condensed texts common on international platforms such as Instagram and YouTube, PBKOLs on Xiaohongshu favored long-form text-image posts and contextual life narratives, embedding family settings, rehabilitation experiences, and personal reflections into their illness storytelling. Such narratives integrate disease information with personal circumstances and emotional cues, enhancing immersion and resonance, and align with Xiaohongshu's community culture, which is dominated by female users and emphasizes lifestyle-oriented and aestheticized expression.44 Consistently, Ma et al.45 showed that survivor-perspective narratives, longer texts, and visually rich formats were more effective in fostering emotional support and audience participation. At the same time, some PBKOLs adopted video as their main format to enhance immediacy and emotional impact. Research on TikTok and other video platforms has similarly demonstrated that video content, owing to its immersive qualities, more readily promotes emotional resonance and engagement, consistent with the present findings.46 This diversity of formats underscores PBKOLs' unique role in addressing patients' multifaceted needs. Moreover, Xiaohongshu posts often incorporated localized health practices such as traditional medicine, dietary therapy, and rehabilitation exercises, reflecting the integration of traditional health culture with modern rehabilitation concepts. Zhao et al.,47 using an ethnographic approach, found that TCM-based self-care was common among Chinese breast cancer survivors, reflecting cultural identification and self-empowerment, but often lacking professional guidance and reinforced by endorsements from key opinion leaders and online influencers. This phenomenon may reflect both the high cultural receptivity and audience appeal of Chinese social media and the growing evidence supporting the health benefits of traditional diets and rehabilitation exercises.48

Implications for nursing practice and research

From a nursing practice perspective, this study elucidates the distinctive role of PBKOLs in breast cancer–related health communication on Chinese social media. Their narrative-driven content not only strengthens emotional connections and peer support among patients but also demonstrates considerable potential for cross-regional dissemination and cultural adaptation, offering valuable experiential insights for breast cancer nursing and digital health practice. From a clinical perspective, these findings suggest that nurses should pay closer attention to patient-generated narratives circulating on social media and recognize PBKOL content as an important component of patients' informal health information environment. PBKOL narratives may provide insight into patients’ emotional concerns, informational needs, and recovery experiences, which can help health care professionals tailor communication and patient education strategies more effectively. Specifically, patient narratives shared by PBKOLs may serve—under the guidance of nursing professionals—as complementary resources to evidence-based health education and clinical support, helping to enhance information comprehension, provide emotional support, and promote self-management during recovery. However, concerns remain regarding the scientific rigor and reliability of information disseminated by PBKOLs. Without appropriate professional contextualization and guidance, experiential narratives may be oversimplified, generalized, or imitated by other patients, leading to potential risks. Therefore, nurses and digital health practitioners may play a bridging role by helping patients critically interpret PBKOL content, integrating experiential narratives with evidence-based medical information. Accordingly, future efforts should balance respect for the value and autonomy of patient narratives with nurse-led, evidence-informed guidance. Integrating PBKOL experiential content with scientific explanation, risk clarification, and contextual interpretation can complement evidence-based medical information, helping to balance emotional support with health education and to advance more standardized, patient-centered, and sustainable digital health interventions.

Strengths and limitations

This study offers several innovations in terms of research subjects, methodology, and theoretical integration. First, by identifying PBKOLs as distinct communicators rather than treating patient-generated content as a homogenous entity, this research clarifies the unique functional role of individuals who bridge the gap between patient identity and opinion leadership in health communication. Second, the integration of LDA topic modeling with manual semantic interpretation allowed for a systematic analysis of large-scale experiential texts. This approach transcends the limitations of previous studies that primarily relied on case studies or descriptive thematic induction. Third, by interpreting findings through the lens of nursing and health communication, this study highlights the potential value of PBKOL narratives in emotional support, cultural adaptation, and digital health interventions, providing new empirical evidence for integrating patient narratives into nursing practice and health education.

Despite these strengths, several limitations should be noted. First, the data were retrieved from a single social media platform, where recommendation algorithms may have introduced selection bias by over-representing certain narrative styles. Furthermore, the predominantly young, female user base may limit the generalizability of the findings to patients of different ages or diverse sociocultural backgrounds. Future cross-platform and cross-cultural validation is warranted. Second, this study employed a cross-sectional design, capturing a snapshot of thematic structures within a specific timeframe. Consequently, it did not account for the dynamic evolution of topics over time or the complexities of user interactions. Subtle shifts in narratives influenced by disease progression, platform ecology, or social events were not captured. Future research should utilize longitudinal designs, such as time-series topic modeling or mixed-methods approaches, to explore how patient-led health communication evolves across different treatment stages and social contexts.

Conclusions

Using LDA topic modeling, this study identified four core themes and related subthemes in PBKOLs' posts on Xiaohongshu, revealing the thematic structure of patient-led breast cancer communication on social media. The findings indicate that PBKOLs' narrative practices contribute to emotional support, peer interaction, and community building within online patient communities. From a nursing perspective, these findings highlight the importance of recognizing patients’ experiential narratives and emotional expressions on social media as valuable resources for understanding patient needs and supporting recovery management. Integrating patient-generated content with evidence-based guidance may help strengthen health communication and promote informed self-management during rehabilitation. More broadly, this study contributes to digital health communication research by illustrating how PBKOLs shape information exchange and peer support within social media environments. These insights provide a basis for developing patient-centered and culturally responsive digital health strategies that balance the value of patient experience with the need for reliable health information.

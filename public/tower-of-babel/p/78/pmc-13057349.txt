A fine-grained transformer combined with multimodal data for predicting hospital length of stay in acute coronary syndrome
Authors: Lijue You, Xingxing Cen, Sufen Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe prediction of hospital length of stay (LOS) is of great significance for hospitals to rationally allocate medical resources and provide timely treatment for patients, especially for acute coronary syndromes (ACS), which requires prompt medical intervention. To this end, we propose a fine-grained Transformer with morphological enhancement for predicting hospital LOS in ACS. Considering the morphological features of blood vessels in computed tomography (CT) images, we design photometric and geometric transformations and combined self-supervised learning to extract enhanced morphological features. To fuse the CT image features with the physiological features contained in the electronic medical record, a multiscale attention is designed and capture the intra-modal and inter-modal fine-grained features with sparse strategy. We compare 16 state-of-the-art models, including classic machine learning models commonly used in ACS, the most advanced visual models, multimodal Transformers, and the latest LOS prediction models. The results show that our model achieved the best prediction results (mean absolute error is 1.33, Pearson correlation coefficient is 0.96). Further, we conduct the interpretability analysis, our model can well perceive the lesion regions, and the salient features have significant correlation with LOS (p = 0.0005). The ablation experiments also verified the effectiveness of each module. This work is expected to provide an effective tool for LOS prediction, thereby enabling the rational allocation of medical resources and offering timely intervention to patients in hospital.Keywords: Acute coronary syndrome, Length of stay, Transformer, Self-supervised learningSubject terms: Image processing, Predictive markers

Introduction

The hospital length of stay (LOS) exerts a profound influence on the utilization and allocation of hospital resources1. Precise prediction of a patient’s LOS can optimize the hospital’s admission scheduling, facilitating the adjustment of its own capacity2. This not only enables the provision of a timely and favorable medical environment for patients but also helps alleviate their medical burden. For instance, cardiovascular diseases and stroke necessitate rapid hospital admission3,4. The rational and efficient utilization of hospital resources is crucial for providing timely medical treatment to patients5.

Acute coronary syndromes (ACS) are a serious disease concept usually caused by rupture or erosion of coronary atherosclerotic plaque, including unstable angina, myocardial infarction, etc.6,7. Complicated by myocardial ischemia or necrosis, these illnesses have high rates of fatality and disability, causing 7 million deaths annually in the Asia-Pacific region8. Medical imaging and biochemical examinations serve as effective approaches for the diagnosis and prognosis assessment of patients with ACS. Among these methods, computed tomography (CT) imaging employs X-ray beams to scan a cross-sectional layer of a specific thickness in the cardiac region. It has emerged as the primary diagnostic method for screening coronary artery disease in symptomatic patients, such as those presenting with atypical or typical angina pectoris9,10. And physiological indicators in electronic medical record (EMR) also provide relevant information, such as triglyceride11. Therefore, it is necessary to combine CT and EMR data to provide effective and accurate management for ACS patients12,13.

With the development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, its applications in the medical domain have been increasingly extensive and in-depth14–16. The ACS and AI can quantitatively and objectively identify biomarkers, providing accurate diagnosis, prognosis and prediction of clinical events17,18. Some studies used demographic information and physiological indicators in EMR as features to build classical machine learning predictive models19. Considering the information contained in medical images, CT is incorporated to extract radiomics features20. Since the attention mechanism is capable of extracting local-to-global image features, the Transformer architecture has found applications in CT images21,22.

Despite the remarkable results achieved in these studies, there are two issues with the current researches: (1) Underfitting the morphological feature of vessel. ACS can observe blockages in blood vessels, and the blood vessels are connected. Extracting the morphological features of blood vessels helps the model capture effective features. Existing studies only use diagnostic and prognostic labels as optimization goals and cannot fully perceive the morphological features of blood vessels. (2) Failing to capture fine-grained ACS lesion. The lesion areas within blood vessels account for only a small number of pixels in CT images. The distribution pattern of these lesion areas is of great significance in determining the severity of the disease. Although existing research endeavors to extract image features through local and global feature extraction methods, it remains challenging to precisely detect fine-grained lesions.

To address these issues, we propose a Fine-gRained TrAnsformer with Morphological Enhancement (FRAME) for predicting hospital LOS in ACS. Especially, we propose a Transformer framework that integrates image representations and physiological signs. Given the significance of the morphological characteristics of blood vessels, an image representation learning module is designed to achieve photometric and geometric enhancement of CT images through invariant and equivariant constraints of self-supervised learning (SSL). To enable the model to accurately perceive disease-related vessel lesions, a fine-grained attention mechanism is proposed, which leverages intra-modal and inter-modal sparsity strategy to enable valid multi-modal fusion. Related works

ACS with AI

In the research integrating AI technologies with ACS, Ke et al.23 employed classic machine learning models, such as logistic regression and random forest, etc. Emakhu et al.24 utilized blood pressure, heart rate, etc., and input them into the ensemble learning models for the diagnosis of ACS. Considering the ACS lesions observed on CT, Shang et al.20 used radiomics features to predict disease recurrence within three years. Wang et al.25 extracted features of the lesion area, including luminal stenosis, plaque volume, etc., and predicted ACS by constructing an extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). Given the powerful feature extraction capability of deep learning, it can provide more accurate predictive results. In addition to the classic machine learning methods mentioned above, research work related to ACS based on deep learning has begun to emerge. Huang et al.26 proposed a stacked denoising autoencoder and incorporated two constraints to capture the patient features at similar risk levels as well as different risk levels. Park et al.27 utilized optical coherence tomographic images and developed a Transformer-based model for diagnosing plaque erosion, achieving better results than convolutional neural network (CNN).

LOS prediction

Considering that ACS is a disease demanding immediate medical intervention, the rational management of ACS patients holds substantial significance for ensuring their timely treatment. Similar to the research of ACS, in the studies on LOS prediction, the majority of them utilize EMR data and integrate classical machine learning models. For instance, Turgeman et al.28 used regression tree model, Lisk et al.29 employed stepwise logistic and linear regressions, and Chrusciel et al.30 utilized the random forest model. To capture advanced features, autoencoder and generative adversarial network (GAN) are employed to extract the features of the hidden layers31,32. Chen et al.33 designed a multi-scale convolutional neural network and used data of intensive care unit to predict LOS and mortality. Chen et al.34 proposed a fusion model that incorporates multimodal data including demographic information, clinical records, X-ray images, etc. This model is composed of modules such as convolutional neural network, long short-term memory, and attention mechanisms. Each modality is assigned a sub-module, and the features of each modality are concatenated for the prediction of LOS.

Model

Overview of FRAME

As presented in Fig. 1, we introduce the FRAME framework for predicting the hospital length of stay. This framework encompasses the three components. (I) Morphological augmentation, which is used to transform CT images, including photometric transformation and geometric transformation, to fully capture the morphological features of blood vessels; (II) Invariant and equivariant representation, where SSL tasks are designed to extract individual invariant features and transformation-sensitive features, achieving the learning of morphological semantic representation of CT images; (III) Fine-grained lesion selection and feature fusion, which utilizes transfer learning to screen important lesion areas and achieve the fusion of CT features and EMR features.

Morphological augmentation

Due to the connectivity of blood vessels, blockages occur within them and present morphological diversity. Therefore, capturing the morphological features of CT images is beneficial for the lesion perception of the model. Common image enhancement methods include cropping, masking, changing color saturation, etc., and invariant features are extracted through SSL. However, these methods are not applicable to CT images of blood vessels. There are two reasons for this. On the one hand, methods such as cropping and masking may cover the lesion area, thereby turning a positive sample into a negative one and causing the loss of pathological features. On the other hand, the morphological features of blood vessels are very sensitive. If the model does not perceive what type of transformation has been made to the image, it cannot distinguish the original morphology from the transformed one, leading to the perception errors.

To solve these problems, we introduce the photometric and geometric transformation. The photometric transformation includes Gaussian blur and contrast enhancement, while geometric transformation includes affine transformation and elastic transformation. These methods ensure that the lesion area will not be covered. The raw CT image and the transformed CT image are denoted as and , respectively. Therefore, we introduce equivariant representations. However, for transformations of the same type, there may exist a trivial solution for equivariant constraint35. To address this issue, we introduce another sample that has undergone the same-type transformation, thus forming sample pairs. Experiments and results

Experimental settings

To verify the superiority of our model in LOS prediction, we selected four commonly used classic machine learning models in LOS prediction, namely logistic regression (LR), XGBoost, support vector machine (SVM), and multilayer perceptron (MLP). Since we utilized CT images, four state-of-the-art (SOTA) visual models were also included, namely ResNet39, SENet40, ViT41, and VMamba42. Additionally, four SOTA models for LOS prediction were used for comparison, namely Autoencoder31, GAN32, 1D-MSNet33, and DF-Mdl34. Furthermore, four SOTA multimodal Transformers are incorporated as comparative baselines, including MedViT43, MTAMW44, DSymFuser45, and ECINFusion46. Specifically, among the four classic machine learning models, we extracted radiomics features from CT images and concatenated them with EMR features as the input. For the other models, CT images and EMR features were directly used as the input.

Further, we calculated six metrics, including mean squared error (MSE), mean absolute error (MAE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), median absolute error (MedAE), Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC), Spearman correlation coefficient (SCC). And 5-fold cross-validation is used, with 4-fold as the training set and the remaining 1-fold as the test set, repeating the calculation five times in turn. Permutation test is applied to compare the results between the proposed model and the best baseline model.

We employed four data augmentation strategies, namely Gaussian blur, contrast enhancement, affine transformation, and elastic transformation. Multiple augmentations were performed under varying transformation parameters, yielding an 8 times expansion of the dataset, which was intended to mitigate model overfitting. Notably, data augmentation was only applied to the training set, and not to the test set to avoid data leakage. Building upon this, we further adopted a 5-fold cross-validation approach, metric indicators were computed for each fold, and the final result was reported as the mean ± standard deviation across the 5 folds to eliminate the contingency of experimental outcomes. Finally, a resampling technique was applied to the training set, under-sampling was conducted for majority-class samples, while over-sampling was implemented for minority-class samples, to enhance the model’s adaptability to class imbalance issues.

Furthermore, to precisely evaluate the predictive ability of the model, we divided LOS into four intervals, namely (0, 5], (5, 10], (10, 15], and (15, ), and calculated the regression results in each interval. In accordance with the patch selection module of the model, we screened out patches with high weights. These patches are capable of reflecting the image regions on which the model converges and can be presented as the outcomes for interpretability analysis. To verify the expressive power of the features, t-SNE47 was used to reduce the dimensionality of the features and then correlation analysis was conducted with LOS. Finally, ablation experiments were carried out to validate the effectiveness of each module.

Interpretability analysis

According to the attention weights, we select the patches whose weights are greater than 0.05 and map them to the raw CT images. This serves as the interpretability of the model, as depicted in Fig. 3. We observed that the salient patches were consistently localized to the regions of lesioned vessels. This finding indicates that our model has effectively perceived the pathological areas and may hold potential for enabling an automatic lesion segmentation function for clinical imaging, thereby assisting radiologists in accelerating image interpretation efficiency.

Salient patches across multiple time periods. The 12 cases come from 4 LOS intervals, namely, (0, 5], (5, 10], (10, 15], and (15, ) respectively.

For the EMR features, the high-weighted features we extracted include HR, Cr, UA, CRP, and PLR. And we conducted F-test to verify the association of each feature with LOS label in four timespan intervals, as depicted in Fig. 4. We found that all five features were significantly associated with LOS. Notably, prior studies have established that HR24,48,49, Cr24,49,50, UA51,52, CRP49,52,53, and PLR54,55 are closely linked to ACS. Specifically, UA exhibited the strongest significance within the (0, 5) interval, PLR was the most significant within the (5, 10) interval, and HR showed the strongest significance within the (10, 15) and (15, ) intervals. This observation may be attributed to the fact that elevated HR serves as a marker of clinical risk, and these findings have the potential to provide clinicians with a straightforward indicator for the rough estimation of LOS time ranges.

Five significant features in the EMR, including HR, Cr, UA, CRP, and PLR, as well as the results of the F-test between them and the LOS label. An asterisk (*) denotes 0.05, and two asterisks (**) denote 0.01.

Furthermore, we used t-SNE to reduce the dimensions of radiomics features, SSL features, EMR features, and the combinations of these features to obtain only one-dimensional scalars, and then conducted correlation analysis with LOS, as shown in Fig. 5. As can be inferred from the Fig. 5, there is a significant correlation between EMR and LOS (R = 0.0869, P = 0.0311) in Fig. 5a, whereas the radiomics features show no correlation with LOS (R = 0.0539, P = 0.1820) in Fig. 5b. This could potentially be attributed to the fact that certain physiological signs within EMR exhibit a notable association with LOS. Conversely, radiomics features are derived from the entirety of the CT images and do not specifically target the lesion regions, thereby accounting for their lack of correlation. The morphological enhancement makes the SSL features correlated with LOS (R = 0.0839, P = 0.0375) in Fig. 5c. The radiomics features combined with EMR still show no significant correlation (R = 0.0721, P = 0.0740) in Fig. 5d. The features combining EMR with SSL and the features combining EMR with salient patches both achieved significant correlations (R = 0.0953, P = 0.0181; R = 0.1402, P = 0.0005) in Fig. 5e,f. This demonstrates the effectiveness of SSL feature enhancement and patch selection.

The linear correlation between the reduced-dimensional features and LOS. t-SNE performs dimensionality reduction on radiomics features, SSL features, EMR features, and combinations of these features.

Discussion

Accurate estimation of LOS is of great significance for hospitals to rationally utilize medical resources and provide timely treatment for patients, especially for diseases like ACS that require rapid rescue. To this end, we propose a FRAME model for predicting hospital LOS and provided interpretable results in ACS. Given the current model’s underfitting problem with morphological features, we design an enhancement method for CT images involving photometric and geometric transformations, and extracted morphological features through SSL. For fine-grained multimodal fusion, we design a intra-modal and inter-modal attention with sparse strategy to enable the model to focus on the areas of vascular blockage.

To verify the FRAME model, we select four classic machine learning models commonly used in ACS, four SOTA visual models, four multimodal Transformers, and four SOTA LOS models to predict the LOS in four intervals. The results indicated that our model demonstrated the best performance. Simultaneously, we extract the salient patches and marked their corresponding positions in the raw CT images. It is found that the FRAME model concentrated on the vascular occlusion regions. And the salient features extracted from the EMR are significantly correlated with LOS. This suggests that the FRAME model not only enables accurate prediction of LOS but also yields effective and interpretable results. In particular, the extraction of salient regions from CT images holds the potential to serve as a tool for lesion delineation in CT scans, thereby aiding radiologists in accelerating their image interpretation speed. Furthermore, previous studies have demonstrated that salient features derived from EMR are high-risk factors for ACS24,48–55. These features not only offer the potential to provide clinicians with a straightforward indicator for LOS estimation but also the salient features observed in the longer LOS subgroup may act as a biomarker for high-risk stratification.

Furthermore, radiomic features, SSL features, EMR features, as well as combinations of these features, are subjected to dimensionality reduction using t-SNE. Subsequently, a correlation analysis is conducted with the LOS. The results shown that the combination of SSL features of CT and EMR features is significant. This indicates that the features of SSL and EMR are effective for LOS prediction, while radiomics features seem unimportant. This is because radiomics features derived from the entire CT image without focusing on the lesion area, resulting in the dilution of pathological features.

In addition, ablation experiments are conducted for each module of FRAME. We find that among the three loss functions of SSL pretraining, the invariance constraint is the most important, but combining the invariance constraint with the equivariant constraint further improves the model performance. And the results of combining SSL features with EMR features is superior to that of combining radiomics features with EMR features, thereby demonstrating that the feature expression ability of SSL is stronger than that of radiomics. Moreover, the performance of FRAME will drop significantly if the SSL and patch selection modules are removed, and the feature fusion strategy of FC and CNN is also inferior to the cross-attention mechanism.

However, this study still has certain limitations. For some patients, enhanced CT is necessary to detect lesions, as conventional CT may not be sufficient. Additionally, ACS involves substantial medical prior knowledge, and data-driven approaches may exhibit a certain degree of deviation. With the burgeoning popularity of large models, the integration of general world knowledge and medical knowledge has emerged as a topic worthy of in-depth exploration. In the future, we intend to collect more comprehensive data and develop more sophisticated models to address these drawbacks.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a fine-grained Transformer with morphological enhancement for predicting hospital LOS in ACS at different time periods. We devise photometric and geometric transformation methods, integrating SSL under invariant and equivariant constraints, in order to extract morphological features. A fine-grained attention is proposed to fusion disease-related features via intra-modal and inter-modal attention with sparse strategy. We compare FRAME model with 16 SOTA models, and conduct interpretability analysis and ablation experiment simultaneously. The data are collected from 615 subjects at the Shanghai Chest Hospital, with each subject’s data including CT images and EMR. The experimental results comprehensively verified the superiority of the FRAME model in LOS prediction. Our work can provide an effective tool for hospitals to rationally allocate medical resources and offer timely treatment to patients.

An open-label phase Ib/II study of trastuzumab deruxtecan combined with nivolumab and CAPOX for HER2-low gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma
Authors: Y. Aoki, I. Nakayama, Y. Komura, M. Wakabayashi, S. Fukuoka, H. Hara, H. Shoji, M. Furuta, K. Minashi, A. Sato, N. Fuse, N. Sakamoto, T. Kuwata, K. Shitara
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTrastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is the standard of care for previously treated patients with HER2-positive metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma (mGEA). Exploratory analyses of the DESTINY-Gastric01 trial suggested preliminary antitumor activity of T-DXd in HER2-low mGEA. Additionally, preclinical studies have reported a synergistic effect between T-DXd and anti-programmed cell death protein 1 antibodies. Therefore, T-DXd combined with nivolumab and chemotherapy may provide therapeutic benefits for patients with HER2-low mGEA.Trial designEPOC2203 is a single-arm, phase Ib/II study evaluating the efficacy and safety of T-DXd combined with nivolumab and reduced-dose CAPOX as first-line treatment of patients with HER2-low mGEA. Phase Ib uses the traditional 3 + 3 design to determine the recommended phase II dose (RP2D). Patients will receive T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg, day 1) combined with nivolumab (360 mg/body, day 1) and CAPOX (capecitabine: 750 mg/m2 twice daily, days 1-14 and oxaliplatin: 70 mg/m2, day 1), every 3 weeks. The primary endpoints are the dose-limiting toxicity rate in phase Ib and the objective response rate (ORR) in phase II. Hypothesis testing assumes a null ORR of 58% and an alternative ORR of 80%. The planned sample size is 28 patients treated with RP2D.

Description of protocol

Background

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), an antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) comprising trastuzumab and a topoisomerase I inhibitor, has received regulatory approval in multiple countries for patients with HER2-positive metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma (mGEA) after chemotherapy plus trastuzumab.1,2 The exploratory cohort of DESTINY-Gastric01 trial suggested preliminary antitumor activity of T-DXd for HER2-low mGEA defined as immunohistochemistry (IHC) 2+ and in situ hybridization negative or IHC 1+.3 Additionally, preclinical studies have reported a synergistic effect between T-DXd and anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibodies.4 Therefore, we designed a phase Ib/II study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of T-DXd combined with nivolumab and chemotherapy in treatment-naive patients with HER2-low mGEA (EPOC2203, jRCT2031230477).

Study design and treatment

EPOC2203 is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm, phase Ib/II trial to evaluate the safety and efficacy of a combination therapy of T-DXd, nivolumab, and a reduced dose of CAPOX as a first-line treatment of patients with HER2-low mGEA (Table 1). Phase Ib applies the conventional 3 + 3 design to determine the recommended phase II dose (RP2D). The inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in Table 2. Eligible patients will receive T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg on day 1) combined with nivolumab (360 mg/body on day 1) and a reduced dose of CAPOX (750 mg/m2 of capecitabine twice daily on days 1-14 and 70 mg/m2 of oxaliplatin on day 1), every 3 weeks as the initial dose. If dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) is observed in two or three patients, the T-DXd dose will be reduced to 4.4 mg/kg. If DLT is subsequently observed in more than two patients at this dose, the trial will be terminated. If none or only one patient experiences DLT, 4.4 mg/kg of T-DXd will be determined as the RP2D. In phase II, 25 patients will receive the protocol treatment with RP2D. Phase Ib will be conducted at three institutions, with three additional centers participating in phase II. The treatment protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the National Cancer Center Hospital East and registered in the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT2031230477). Daiichi Sankyo Co., Ltd. is funding this study and provides the study drug.

Study endpoint and assessment

The primary endpoint is the DLT rate in phase Ib and ORR by the investigator assessment in phase II. The definition of DLT is shown in Table 3, and ORR is the proportion of patients with complete response and partial response, which is evaluated based on RECIST version 1.1. In both phase Ib and II, the secondary endpoints are progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival, duration of response, disease control rate and safety. Safety and adverse events are assessed using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Exploratory analyses will be conducted to evaluate these endpoints in patients with centrally confirmed HER2-low tumors. The concordance rate between local and central HER2-low evaluations will be reported. Additionally, biomarker analyses, including the status of claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2), programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1), and mismatch repair (MMR) proteins, will be carried out as part of the exploratory endpoints. These biomarkers were analyzed by IHC using formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue specimens from archival tissue samples. CLDN18.2 positivity was defined as moderate-to-strong expression in 75% of tumor cells using CLDN18 (Clone 43-14A, Roche Ventana, Oro Valley, AZ). MMR status was assessed by IHC using monoclonal antibodies for anti-mutL homolog 1 (MLH1, ES05), anti-mutS homolog 2 (MSH2, FE11), anti-postmeiotic segregation increased 2 (PMS2, EP51), and anti-mutS homolog 6 (MSH6, EP49) (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA). Tumors lacking either MLH1, MSH2, PMS2, or MSH6 expression were considered MMR-deficient, whereas tumors with maintained expression of MLH1, MSH2, PMS2, and MSH6 were considered MMR-proficient. PD-L1 expression was analyzed using an anti-PD-L1 rabbit monoclonal antibody (Dako PharmDx assay, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA) and measured using combined positive score, defined as the ratio of the number of PD-L1-positive cells (tumor cells, lymphocytes and macrophages) to the total number of tumor cells multiplied by 100. Sample size and statistical analysis

The ORR for nivolumab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment of patients with HER2-negative mGEA is 58%. Therefore, the threshold ORR was set at 58%.6 Considering the potential additive efficacy of combining T-DXd with nivolumab and chemotherapy, an ORR improvement of ∼20% is anticipated. Then, the expected ORR was set at 80%. Assuming a one-sided alpha of 10% and a power of 80%, the required sample size for phase Ib/II is 26 patients, and considering the possibility of a few ineligible cases, the target number of patients was set at 28.

Follow-up

All patients will be followed until the earliest occurrence of death, loss to follow-up, withdrawal of consent or end of the study treatment. Posttreatment information, including treatment modality, start date, last administration date and clinical outcomes, will be collected approximately every 3 months for up to 1 year from the date of last patient enrollment in this study. Laboratory tests, including serum HER2 extracellular domain, chest and abdominal computed tomography, electrocardiogram and cardiac ultrasound will be carried out at baseline. Physical and laboratory examinations are required on days 8 and 15 during the first cycle and before each cycle. The DLT evaluation period in phase Ib is days 1-21 in the first cycle. Computed tomography scan evaluation will be carried out every 6 weeks.

Protocol validation

Based on the positive results from pivotal trials, biomarker-driven strategies have become the standard of care in first-line treatment of mGEA.6, 7, 8, 9, 10 Matching therapies to specific targets improve survival outcomes. Routine testing for HER2, MMR, PD-L1, and CLDN18.2 is essential for optimal patient selection. However, ∼50% of patients lack actionable biomarkers, highlighting the need for new treatment options.11

An analysis of exploratory cohorts from the DESTINY-Gastric01 trial revealed that T-DXd monotherapy at 6.4 mg/kg showed preliminary antitumor activity in patients with HER2-low mGEA.3 The membrane-permeable payload of T-DXd enables a bystander killing effect, contributing to its activity against adjacent HER2-low tumor cells.12 Additionally, preclinical studies suggest that effective payload release from T-DXd may occur without requiring internalization following HER2 binding, thereby enabling antitumor activity in HER2-low or -negative tumors.13 However, the ORR was lower than that of the HER2-positive population, indicating that combination strategies may be required to enhance efficacy.

Currently phase Ib/II DESTINY-Gastric03 trial (NCT04379596) to investigate T-DXd with cytotoxic agents with or without an anti-PD-1 agent is ongoing. T-DXd (6.4 mg/kg) combined with standard dose of fluoropyrimidine (capecitabine 1000 mg/m2 twice daily) and pembrolizumab resulted in grade ≥3 adverse events in 91% of patients, leading to treatment discontinuation in ∼50% of HER2-positive patients. Therefore, the combination of a lower dose of T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg) with a reduced dose of fluoropyrimidine (capecitabine 750 mg/m2 twice daily) and pembrolizumab showed manageable safety with grade ≥3 adverse events in 34% and treatment discontinuation in 16%, and promising antitumor activity in patients with HER2-positive mGEA.14 Currently, phase III DESTINY-Gastric05 trial (NCT06731478) is ongoing to compare T-DXd plus fluoropyrimidine and pembrolizumab versus standard first-line treatment of HER2-positive mGEA. Considering the higher intratumoral heterogeneity of HER2-low mGEA than that of the HER2-positive population, combination strategies with cytotoxic agents including both capecitabine and oxaliplatin may enhance efficacy. Therefore, the EPOC2203 trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of T-DXd combined with a reduced dose of CAPOX and nivolumab as a first-line treatment of HER2-low mGEA. The initial dose of T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg) and capecitabine (750 mg/m2 twice daily) are the same as those used in DESTINY-Gastric03 study and ongoing DESTINY-Gastric05 study. The oxaliplatin dose (70 mg/m2) was selected based on the observation that actual dose intensity of oxaliplatin in first-line chemotherapy is typically 70% to 80% when given as 100 mg/m2 to 130 mg/m2 every 3 weeks.8,15

Building on the success of T-DXd in treating HER2-low and HER2-ultra-low breast cancer, the development of novel therapeutic strategies for HER2-low mGEA is anticipated. The optimal combination partners for T-DXd in HER2-expressing mGEA are under investigation.

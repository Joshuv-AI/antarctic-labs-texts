Epidemiological study of thalassemia in the Buyi population of Qiannan Prefecture, Guizhou Province, China based on third-generation sequencing
Authors: Jiangfen Wu, Lei Wang, Zhenju Jin, Lingyan Ren, Yinghong Shi, Ying Zhou, Guohui Meng, Aiping Mao, Di cui, Bangquan An, Shengwen Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis genetic epidemiological study aimed to investigate the prevalence of various types of thalassemia among the Buyi population in Qiannan Prefecture, Guizhou Province, China. A total of 1,003 Buyi individuals from five regions in Qiannan Prefecture were enrolled for this study. Peripheral blood samples were collected for routine blood testing and hemoglobin electrophoresis. Thalassemia-related variants were identified using third-generation sequencing (TGS). The overall carrier rate of thalassemia in the Buyi population was 22.83% (229/1003). The carrier rates of α-thalassemia, β-thalassemia, and δ-thalassemia were 16.15% (162/1003), 6.68% (67/1003), and 1.20% (12/1003), respectively. The most common α-thalassemia genotype was -α3.7/αα (32.89%), followed by --SEA/αα (25.66%). Structural variants αααanti3.7/αα and αααanti4.2/αα were also identified, accounting for 5.26% and 1.32% of the α-globin gene variant genotypes, respectively. For β-thalassemia, the most frequent genotypes were βCD17(AAG> TAG)/βN (45.61%) and βCD41/42(−CTTT)/βN (43.86%), with β0-thalassemia accounted for 96.49% of β-thalassemia. Thalassemia is highly prevalent in the Buyi population of Qiannan Prefecture. The variant frequencies observed in this population differ from those in other populations in China. Introduction

Thalassemia is a hereditary hemolytic anemia caused by defects in the production of hemoglobin chains and is considered one of the most common monogenic disorders globally [1, 2]. The primary clinical forms are α-thalassemia (OMIM: #604131) and β-thalassemia (OMIM: #613985), which are classified into silent, mild, intermediate, and severe types based on the imbalance between α- and non-α-globin chains. Patients with severe thalassemia require lifelong blood transfusions, which impose significant burdens on families and society. Consequently, managing thalassemia focuses on prevention, with genetic counseling and prenatal diagnosis aimed at reducing the birth of children with severe forms of the disease. For individuals with positive hematological results, molecular diagnostic tests are used to confirm carrier status.

Currently, reverse dot blot (RDB), gap-polymerase chain reaction (Gap-PCR), multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA), Sanger sequencing, and next-generation sequencing (NGS) are widely used for thalassemia diagnosis [3]. However, these methods have limitations in detecting structural variants, which can lead to missed or inaccurate diagnoses [4]. In contrast, third-generation sequencing (TGS), based on single-molecule real-time (SMRT) technology, offers a highly accurate and efficient method for identifying both common and rare thalassemia variants, including structural abnormalities [3, 5]. TGS has gradually gained acceptance in clinical practice.

In this study, we employed TGS to detect thalassemia-related variants in 1,003 individuals from the Buyi population in Qiannan Prefecture, Guizhou Province. The findings will enhance our understanding of the spectrum of hemoglobin gene variants in this population and provide a valuable reference for developing thalassemia Thalassemia prevalence shows clear regional and ethnic variations. Investigating its distribution across different regions and populations helps local governments formulate targeted prevention and control strategies, improving reproductive health and the quality of future generations. In China, thalassemia is most prevalent in southern provinces, particularly in ethnic minority regions located south of the Yangtze River [6, 7]. Guizhou Province, situated in southwestern China, is a region inhabited by 17 ethnic minorities. The overall thalassemia carrier rate in Guizhou is 12.9%, ranking fifth among the 10 southern provinces with high prevalence rates [8]. Although several studies have explored the molecular epidemiology of thalassemia in parts of Guizhou [8,9], research focusing on specific ethnic groups remains limited. A previous survey of nine ethnic groups in Guizhou found that the Buyi population had the highest thalassemia carrier rate, at 18.47% [8]. Approximately 98% of China’s Buyi population, estimated at 2.71 million people, resides in Guizhou Province, predominantly in the Qiannan and Qianxinan Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefectures [10]. However, no comprehensive epidemiological study on thalassemia has been conducted with a large sample of the Buyi population in Guizhou Province prevention and control strategies in both Qiannan Prefecture and the broader Guizhou Province.

Methods

Subjects

A total of 1,003 Buyi individuals who underwent thalassemia screening in Qiannan Prefecture, Guizhou Province, between January 2021 and November 2022 were enrolled in the study. The participants were recruited from five regions: Changshun (600 cases), Libo (285 cases), Dushan (57 cases), Duyun (54 cases), and Longli (7 cases). Subjects were 18 to 52 years of age, 514 males and 489 females. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital (approval No. 2022-05), and all participants provided informed consent. The flow of this study is shown in Fig. 1.

Experimental design flow diagram

Conventional hematological screening

Peripheral blood samples were collected from all participants into EDTA-Na2 tubes. Routine blood tests were performed using the Sysmex XN-100-4 system (Sysmex, Kobe, Japan), and hemoglobin electrophoresis was conducted using the Hydrasys LC system (Sebia Electrophoresis, Evry, France).

DNA extraction

Genomic DNA was extracted from whole blood samples using the magnetic bead-beating method [4] with the NP968 Nucleic Acid Extraction System (Tianlong Science and Technology, Xi’an, China). The concentration of the extracted DNA was measured using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA).

TGS screening

Following the protocol from a previous study [4], thalassemia-related variants were identified using the PacBio TGS platform based on single-molecule real-time (SMRT) technology. In brief, long-fragment multiplex PCR was constructed to amplify the target regions of the genomic DNA. The amplified products were then end-repaired, phosphorylated, and ligated to PacBio adaptors with barcodes to create a dumbbell library. This library was combined with sequencing primers and DNA polymerase to form a PacBio sequencing library. Sequencing was performed using the PacBio Sequel II platform (PacBio, Menlo Park, CA, USA) in circular consensus sequencing (CCS) mode. The resulting data were analyzed using bioinformatics software [4].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as frequency distributions, constituent ratios, and rates. Means were compared between groups using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Thalassemia carriers detected by TGS

A total of 229 individuals (22.83%) of 1003 subjects were identified as thalassemia carriers, including 102 males and 127 females. Regarding the defective thalassemia genes, the overall carrier rates for α-thalassemia, β-thalassemia, and δ-thalassemia were 16.15% (162/1003), 6.68% (67/1003), and 1.20% (12/1003), respectively (Table 1).

Among the 57 β-thalassemia carriers, six different genotypes were identified, all of which caused thalassemia traits in these subjects. The most common genotype was βCD17(AAG> TAG)/βN (45.61%), followed by βCD41/42(−CTTT)/βN (43.86%). Of the β-thalassemia carriers, 96.49% (55/57) were β0-thalassemia heterozygotes (Table 2).

Eight subjects carried both α- and β-globin variants, with four combination types identified. The common α-thalassemia genotypes were -α3.7/αα and --SEA/αα, while the common β-thalassemia genotypes were βCD17(AAG> TAG)/βN and βCD41/42(−CTTT)/βN, consistent with those seen in the individuals of α- and β-thalassemia carriers. Six β-thalassemia variants were identified in this study. Of these, the two most common were CD17(A > T) and CD41-42(-CTTT), which together accounted for 89.55% (60/67) of all β-thalassemia variants. In addition, only one δ-thalassemia variant, HBD: c.-127T > C, was detected in 12 alleles, with an allele frequency of 0.60% (Table 4).

Comparison of results between conventional hematological screening and TGS method

In the 1003 samples analyzed, TGS detected thalassemia-related variants in 229 samples, yielding a positive rate of 22.83%. Of these 229 positive cases, conventional hematological screening (routine blood testing and hemoglobin electrophoresis) identified 196 as positive, missing 33 cases, with a missed detection rate of 14.41% (33/229). All missed cases were α-thalassemia variants, with genotypes including -α3.7/αα, -α4.2/αα, αWSα/αα, αCSα/αα, αααanti3.7/αα, and αααanti4.2/αα (Table S1). Among these, the αWSα/αα genotype had the highest missed detection rate, while αCSα/αα had the lowest. Conventional hematological screening detected 389 (38.78%, 389/1003) positive cases in total, with only 196 confirmed by TGS, with a positive predictive value of 50.39% (196/389).

The most common VUS, HBB: c.*129T > A, showed no abnormal blood phenotype in heterozygous carriers. Discussion

This study involved 1,003 Buyi individuals from Qiannan Prefecture, Guizhou Province, the primary region for the Buyi population in China. Using TGS to detect thalassemia-related variants, we observed a high carrier rate of 22.83% among the Buyi population in this area, with specific carrier rates of 16.15% (162/1003) for α-thalassemia, 6.68% (67/1003) for β-thalassemia, and 1.20% (12/1003) for δ-thalassemia. Given the high prevalence of thalassemia among the Buyi population in Qiannan Prefecture, enhanced government attention and increased public funding are crucial for effective thalassemia prevention and control in this region.

The thalassemia carrier rate (22.83%) identified in this study is notably higher than that found using conventional blood testing methods (5.95%) among the Buyi in Qiannan Prefecture [11] and slightly exceeds rates reported by next-generation sequencing (NGS) studies among the Buyi in Guizhou Province (18.47% and 19.90%) [9, 11]. Compared to conventional testing and NGS, TGS offers broader detection capabilities, particularly in identifying structural rearrangements, demonstrating a significant advantage in the genetic screening for thalassemia.

A total of 13 genotypes were identified among 162 subjects with α-thalassemia (with or without β-thalassemia and δ-thalassemia). The three most common genotypes were the deletion variants -α3.7/αα (34.97%) and --SEA/αα (24.69%), as well as the non-deletion variant αCSα/αα (17.28%). This is consistent with previous thalassemia screening results for the general population in Guizhou [10]. However, some studies report --SEA/αα as the most prevalent genotype among the Buyi population in Qiannan Prefecture and other area of Guizhou [12, 13]. This difference might arise from those studies’ inclusion criteria, which selected subjects with positive blood phenotypes. As a result, silent cases of -α3.7/αα and -α4.2/αα may have been missed in initial hematologic screening, whereas --SEA/αα, which often causes mild microcytosis, was reliably detected. The genotype proportions reported in this study likely offer a more comprehensive view of the α-thalassemia landscape in the region.

Six genotypes were found in 67 subjects with β-thalassemia (with or without α-thalassemia and δ-thalassemia). The most common were βCD17(AAG> TAG)/βN (49.25%) and βCD41/42(−CTTT)/βN (40.30%). These findings align with previous reports from the Buyi population in Guizhou Province, where βCD17(AAG> TAG)/βN constituted 44.39% of cases, and from Qianxinan Prefecture, Guizhou, where βCD41/42(−CTTT)/βN represented 27.48% [7, 13]. Notably, the prevalence of β0-thalassemia among Buyi individuals in Qiannan Prefecture (96.92%, 63/65) detected through TGS was markedly higher than in Qiannan (76.91%), Qianxinan (75.93%), and Guiyang (79.12%) populations in Guizhou [7, 12, 14]. This elevated rate may reflect the Buyi community’s relative social isolation and limited intermarriage with other ethnic groups [12]. Consanguineous marriages may have contributed to an increased frequency of specific variants, resulting in a higher risk of giving birth to a child with severe β-thalassemia among Buyi couples in Qiannan Prefecture. Given this elevated risk, accurate preconception and prenatal thalassemia screening are particularly important for Buyi couples in the region.

Currently, the HBD gene is not commonly included in routine laboratory testing. In this cohort, however, we identified the variant of HBD:c.-127T > C in 12 subjects through TGS, with a carrier rate of 1.20% (Table 2), which is significantly higher than the previously reported rate of 0.40% in the Chinese population. HBD:c.-127T > C was first identified in a Japanese cohort study and has since been recognized as the most common δ-globin gene variant in two Chinese studies, accounting for 57.0-82.0% of all HBD variants [15, 16]. The nucleotide c.-127T of HBD lies in a highly conserved sequence (GATA-1 motif), and the T-to-C substitution disrupts the binding of the erythroid transcription factor GATA-1. This disruption nearly abolishes δ-globin chain production, leading to a reduction in Hb A2 levels [15]. Hb A2, a minor hemoglobin type composed of two α- and two δ-globin chain subunits (α2δ2), normally comprises only 2.2-3.5% of adult hemoglobin. Increased Hb A2 levels are the characteristic feature of β-thalassemia carriers. Therefore, although δ-thalassemia does not present with specific clinical symptoms [15, 17], its presence can influence thalassemia screenings because it can lead to normal level of Hb A2 in β-thalassemia carriers when co-inherited [16, 17].

In this study, a subject with both the heterozygous for CD17(AAG > TAG) in β-globin and the heterozygous for HBD: c.-127T > C had a normal Hb A2 level (2.50%), which was lower than that of other β-thalassemia carriers (Table 1). This masking effect of HBD variants on Hb A2 levels has been previously reported [18, 19]. However, current first-line screening for β-thalassemia carriers relies mainly on the presence of microcytosis and elevated Hb A2 (> 3.5%) [15], which risks misdiagnosing cases influenced by δ-thalassemia. Notably, the δ-thalassemia carrier rate of 1.20% in this study suggests a higher prevalence in the Buyi population compared to the general Chinese population [16, 20], increasing the potential of missed screening for β-thalassemia carriers in the region.

Given the high carrier rate of δ-thalassemia in the Buyi population, it is crucial to account for δ-thalassemia’s interference when using red blood cell indices and hemoglobin electrophoresis for β-thalassemia screening. We strongly recommend more comprehensive genetic testing for thalassemia in Qiannan Prefecture to avoid the risk of missed screening of β-thalassemia carriers relying solely on Hb A2 levels. This is particularly important in prenatal screening where one partner has already been diagnosed with β-thalassemia and the other partner should undergo comprehensive genetic testing for thalassemia.

The carrier rate of triplicated α-globin variants in the Buyi population of Qiannan Prefecture was 1.00% (Table 2), higher than the 0.42% rate observed among the Buyi population in other regions of Guizhou Province, and consistent with the 0.94% rate in the broader Guizhou population [9, 21]. In this study, αααanti3.7/αα had a higher carrier rate (0.80%) than αααanti4.2/αα (0.20%) (Table 1), differing from previous findings in the Guizhou population, where the opposite pattern was observed [21]. This variation might suggest regional and ethnic influences on α-triplet distribution in Guizhou Province. Typically, triplicated α-globin genes do not result in significant hematological phenotypes on their own [21], leading to missed detection in traditional thalassemia screening methods. However, in combination with β-thalassemia, these triplicated α-globin genes can worsen the imbalance between α- and β-globin chains, intensifying clinical symptoms [21]. With triplicated α-globin and β-thalassemia carrier rates of 1.00% and 5.68%, respectively, among the Buyi population in Qiannan Prefecture, there is a high incidence rate of compound triplicated α-globin and β-thalassemia variants. To prevent misdiagnosis, comprehensive genetic testing, like TGS, is essential for screening thalassemia-associated variants.

In this study, TGS identified three abnormal hemoglobin variants (Hb Binyang, Hb Hekinan II, Hb Q-Thailand) in the Buyi population, all previously reported in the Guizhou population [19, 22]. Subjects with Hb Binyang and Hb Hekinan II heterozygous variants showed no significant blood abnormalities (Table 4), suggesting these variants may not be pathogenic. One subject with the Hb Q-Thailand variant and a -α4.2/αα combination exhibited a microcytic phenotype. Hb Q-Thailand (HBA1: c.223G > C) is caused by a point mutation (GAC > CAC) at codon 74 of the HBA1 gene on chromosome 16 and is linked with a leftward single alpha-globin gene deletion (-α4.2) [23].

Additionally, this study identified 10 variants of unknown significance (VUSs), and subjects with these heterozygous variants generally showed no significant blood abnormalities. Some subjects had VUSs in combination with silent α-thalassemia, yet data indicated no aggravation in clinical phenotype (Table 4). Consequently, when using TGS for thalassemia genetic testing, VUSs should be interpreted in conjunction with clinical presentation. In this study, we provided hematological parameters for each variant (Table 6) to support future pathogenicity classifications.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study highlights the high prevalence and diversity of thalassemia-related variants in the Buyi population of Qiannan Prefecture, Guizhou Province. The frequency and types of thalassemia variants in this Buyi population differ from those found in other Chinese populations. Additionally, our findings demonstrate that TGS is an effective tool for thalassemia screening, especially in regions with high prevalence.

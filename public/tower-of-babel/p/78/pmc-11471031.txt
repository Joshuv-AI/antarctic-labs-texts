A national study on maternal vomiting during pregnancy in Iran: prevalence, determinants, adverse birth outcomes, and the trend
Authors: Sima Afrashte, Mahmoud Hajipour, Mitra Darbandi, Mostafa Dianatinasab, Shahab Rezaeian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNausea and vomiting during pregnancy (NVP) is one of the most common complication of pregnancy. The present study was aimed to determine the association between vomiting during pregnancy and adverse birth outcomes.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted on 3649 pregnant women across 11 provinces of Iran. Cluster sampling method was used to select samples and data collection was done using family record and face-to-face interviews. Data were analyzed by logistic regression, using STATA14.2 software.ResultsThe prevalence of vomiting during pregnancy was 9.7% (95% CI: 8.8, 10.7), with an increasing trend by birth cohort. After adjusting for other confounders, the prevalence of vomiting revealed a decreasing trend by body mass index (OR = 0.63, 95%CI: 0.53, 0.78, P-trend < 0.001). In addition, long inter-pregnancy interval (IPI) significantly increased the odds of NVP (ORIPI of 1−3 year=2.42, ORIPI of >3 year=1.63). Multivariate analyses showed that the odds of stillbirth (AOR = 1.61, 95% CI: 1.17, 2.19) and the odds of infant mortality (AOR = 1.78, 95% CI: 1.29, 2.45) were significantly increased in women with daily vomiting during pregnancy. The odds of vomiting during pregnancy was significantly shown to reduce the odds of abortion by 45%.ConclusionThe prevalence of NVP was shown to have an increasing trend in Iran. This complication is associated with many adverse health outcomes during pregnancy and negatively affect maternal and fetal health. Given the importance of pregnancy period, nutrition education and increase the awareness of pregnant women towards NVP, especially pre-pregnancy training is suggested.

Introduction

Nausea and vomiting during pregnancy (NVP) is one of the most common problems during pregnancy, affecting 90% of pregnant women, with approximately 50% of women displaying only vomiting [1, 2]. Despite the other name of this disease, morning sickness, only 2% of pregnant women experience nausea and vomiting in the morning, and it may strike at any time of the day [3]. According to a study conducted in Iran, the prevalence of NVP was reported as 71.5%, and 7% of these cases had severe NVP [4].

In most pregnancies, NVP is a mild and self-limited condition, usually occurring in the first trimester between 8 and 12 weeks of gestation, and in most cases, most symptoms of NVP disappear by the 20th week of gestation [5, 6]. Although, the causes of NVP are still unknown, various studies have shown that some hormonal, psychological, individual, and social factors, as well as lifestyle are associated with this condition. As such, increased human chorionic gonadotropin, estrogen and thyroid hormones during this period may explain the role of hormonal agents in development of NVP symptoms [7–9].

The occurrence of NVP varies in different regions of the world, as it occurs more frequently in urban populations in western countries, and is less common in Africans, Native Americans, and most of the Asian population [7]. Previous studies have shown that NVP do not increase the risk of fetal birth defects, while it may cause a number of psychosocial problems including fatigue, irritability, impaired daily functioning, anxiety, depression, and ultimately decreased quality of life in women [4, 10–12]. These findings, then, may subsequently lead to an increased risk of pregnancy related outcomes.

Given the unclear causes and various trend of NVP, as well as the related complications mentioned above, the present study was carried out to determine the association between vomiting during pregnancy and adverse birth outcomes in Iran.

Methods

Study design and population

In this cross-sectional study, 3649 pregnant women were included across 11 provinces of Iran in 2019 (Fig. 1). Data in the present analysis were available from a population-based national case-control study conducted in the year 2018 to determine the factors associated with stillbirth and neonatal death [13]. Informed consent was received at the beginning of the study from all of participants and all of them were aware of the study’s purpose. All participants were included in the analysis without restriction. Exclusion criteria were any participants with missing values from the data.

Primary health cares (PHC) and general medical services are provided by health centers to the residences who are living in their defined geographical areas. Maternal health care such as maternity and pregnancy cares is among the most important health services, which is provided by the health centers under the supervision of the Iranian ministry of health.

The flowchart of the study

We selected 11 providences from different regions of Iran including Fars, Golestan, Kohkiluyeh and Boyer Ahmad, Yazd, Kermanshah, Hamadan, Hormozgan, Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari and South Khorasan provinces, as well as health centers in Mashhad and Zahedan cities. Four cities in each province were selected by cluster sampling from different geographic regions of the North, South, East and West, and in each of these areas, two health centers, one urban and one rural, were randomly selected.

Independent variable

Vomiting during pregnancy (VP) is a symptom of pregnancy that involves nausea or vomiting. In our study, it was defined as maternal daily vomiting during pregnancy only (yes/no).

Determinants variables of vomiting

Maternal variables were maternal age (continuous variable in year), residency (urban, rural), maternal BMI, smoking during pregnancy (yes/no), education (number of years of education completed), gestational weight gain (kg), parity (frequency) and inter-pregnancy interval in year.

Dependent variables

The pregnancy related outcomes in the last pregnancy including preterm delivery based on gestational age (< 37 or > 37 weeks), type of delivery (vaginal or C-section), abortion (yes/no), and the birth outcome (live birth/ stillbirth/infant mortality) were obtained via questionnaire. Gestational weight gain was defined as the difference between maternal weight before pregnancy and weight in 30 weeks of gestation.

Data collection

Required data were collected based on the family records of pregnant women in health centers, and by in-person interviews to complete the questionnaires, using trained individuals in health centers in the designated provinces.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of data was performed at 95% confidence interval (CI), using STATA 14.2 software (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). Descriptive statistics were reported in frequency (percentage) or mean (standard deviation). At first, chi-square or t-student test was used to compare the frequency/mean of maternal variables based on vomiting status (with and without daily vomiting during pregnancy), respectively. Then, different logistic regression models were used for assessing the maternal daily vomiting during pregnancy with pregnancy outcomes. (1) Univariate model, (2) a confounder model, with including variables of maternal age, BMI, smoking, parity, parental smoking, and inter-pregnancy interval. For these analyses, odds ratio (OR) with 95% CI was reported.

Results

This study recruited a total of 3649 pregnant women. The average age of the participants was 27.2 ± 6 years. The prevalence of daily vomiting during pregnancy was 9.7% (95% CI: 8.8, 10.7). The relationship between demographic characteristics and factors associated with daily vomiting during pregnancy are shown in Table 1. The highest rate of vomiting during pregnancy was observed in women aged 21–35 years (39.5%), followed by those with vaginal birth (70.5%), and those pregnant women for the first time (42.9%). As shown in Table 1, maternal age, maternal education, maternal BMI, and inter-pregnancy interval (IPI) were significantly associated with development of vomiting during pregnancy (P < 0.05). In addition, advanced maternal age was shown to be significantly associated with reduced rates of vomiting during pregnancy (Fig. 2A). Results also suggest that higher maternal BMI is significantly associated with lower rate of vomiting during pregnancy (OR = 0.63, 95%CI: 0.53, 0.78, P-trend < 0.001) (Fig. 2B).

The odds of vomiting during pregnancy was shown to reduce the odds of abortion by 45% (OR = 0.55, P = 0.003). In addition, these relationships were also obtained in the confounder model (Table 3).

Discussion

The results of univariate analysis revealed that maternal age, stillbirth, maternal overweight and obesity, abortion, parity ≥ 4, first pregnancy, and IPI of two and three years were significantly associated with vomiting during pregnancy. However, multivariate analysis showed a significant relationship between vomiting during pregnancy with maternal BMI and IPI of two years.

The present paper indicated the prevalence of NVP and related factors. The results of our study show that 9.7% of women displayed vomiting during pregnancy. The quality of life of pregnant women is greatly affected by NVP, a common complication of pregnancy. This condition may cause disability in some women, and as a result, they spontaneously terminate pregnancy. Although a large number of studies have focused on NVP, the cause of NVP is still unclear [8, 12, 14]. The results of our study were supported by Bai et al. [15] However, the prevalence of NVP was less than the rates reported in Spanish, Chinese, and other Iranian studies, which reported the NVP rate as 29.3, 30, and 71.5%, respectively [1, 4, 16]. These contradictory results may be attributed to different research methodology, and various implications of this medical condition, so that some studies have evaluated nausea and vomiting during pregnancy together.

The results of the present study suggest that vomiting during pregnancy was significantly associated with maternal BMI, that is increased BMI reduced the prevalence of vomiting during pregnancy. Jenabi and colleagues found that pregnant women who reported vomiting episodes during the first trimester of pregnancy, had a lower BMI score [17]. Another study demonstrated that both obesity and underweight were associated with vomiting during pregnancy [18]. According to the literature, it is assumed that underweight women have low estrogen levels prior to the pregnancy, and elevated estrogen levels during pregnancy increase the odds of NVP [19].

The findings of this study also show that IPI of two years increases the odds of NVP. It is believed that increased IPI increases the risk of hyperemesis gravidarum (HG). Women with a previous history of NVP at first pregnancy, may tend to have longer IPIs, while fertility rate is lower in women with HG [20]. The majority of related studies have shown the relationship between IPI and pregnancy outcomes, including low birth weight, preterm delivery, and stillbirth [21, 22]. Women with a history of severe vomiting during pregnancy may be more likely to have longer IPI due to complications of NVP. As a result, their hormone levels are to the same extent as in first or previous pregnancies due to long IPIs. However, further research is required to more precisely examine the association between IPI and NVP.

This study revealed that maternal age at pregnancy significantly increased the likelihood of NVP, which was consistent with previous studies [23–25]. However, the results of Bazarganipour et al. were inconsistent with the results of our study [4]. We also found that, the level of Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (HCG) increases during pregnancy, which is higher at younger ages, and may result in higher odds of vomiting during pregnancy [26].

Crozier and colleagues proposed that women with lower education displayed more severe nausea of pregnancy [27]. In our study, there was a significant relationship between maternal education and vomiting during pregnancy, whereas other studies found no association between NVP and maternal education [1, 28]. Pregnant women with lower education are more likely to have low health literacy. As a result, they may not properly follow the advice of different midwifery specialists, and may ultimately lead to a poor quality of life at this stage.

The results of this study showed a significant relationship between history of abortion and vomiting in pregnancy. Studies in different countries have shown that NVP cannot increase the chance of abortion due to adverse maternal conditions [29, 30]. Although, vomiting during pregnancy is not a threatening risk factor for abortion, the health threatening complications associated with this medical condition, such as maternal weight loss and fetal weight loss, should not be ignored [25]. Since the study was a cross-sectional design, women with a history of abortion may have effective strategies to reduce the severity of nausea to avoid unintended NVP.

The results of our study also revealed a significant increasing trend of NVP over the past years. Louik and colleague reported no increased risk of NVP during their study period [31]. No previous studies have evaluated the prevalence of NVP in various times, thus further research is suggested to assess the trend of NVP.

Limitations

This was a cross-sectional study, and interpretation of casual relationships was limited. Also, recall bias was likely to occur during data collection process due to using self-reported data. In addition, data on patient’s nutrition and food allergy, which may magnificently affect the severity of NVP, were not collected evaluated in this study. Nevertheless, this was an Iranian national study with an acceptable sample size generalizable to the general population.

Conclusion

Vomiting during pregnancy was demonstrated to have an increasing trend in Iran. This complication is associated with many adverse health outcomes during pregnancy, and negatively affect maternal and fetal health. Given the importance of pregnancy period in women’s life, increase the awareness of pregnant women towards NVP, especially pre-pregnancy training is suggested. Also, training interventions aimed at diet modification and oral medications should be established to reduce or treat this condition in pregnant women.

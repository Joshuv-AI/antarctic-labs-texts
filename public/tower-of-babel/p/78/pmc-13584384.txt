Global research trends of cell death in diabetic cardiomyopathy from 2000 to 2024: a bibliometric analysis and integrative overview
Authors: Mei Yan, Pengpeng Liang, Hai Huang, Shizhao Zhang, Jinhua Kang, Yue Li, Guiyun Li, Hongyan Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveOver recent years, the exploration of cell death mechanisms has become a central focus in the investigation of the pathogenesis and therapeutic prospects of diabetic cardiomyopathy. The purpose of this study was to delineate the current landscape of cell death research in diabetic cardiomyopathy using a bibliometric approach, and to provide an integrative overview of the major cell death pathways implicated in this condition, thereby offering valuable insights and academic resources, aiming to promote further research and advancements in associated disciplines.MethodsA bibliometric analysis was conducted on diabetic cardiomyopathy-associated cell death. Publications derived from the Web of Science Core Collection were exported in full-text format and examined using CiteSpace 6.2 R4 and VOSviewer v.1.6.18.ResultsA total of 1,535 records satisfying the specified criteria were identified. During the first nine years, publication output was relatively low; however, there was a significant increase in the number of publications in both 2021 and 2024. China and Wenzhou Medical University led the research on cell death within diabetic cardiomyopathy, which encompassed 72 countries and 1,487 institutions. The author with the most published works was Cai Lu while the most cited author was Jia GH. Circulation Research was the journal with the highest number of co-citations. The most frequently appearing keywords included oxidative stress, apoptosis, activation, mechanisms, and heart failure. The extracted keywords were mainly associated with diabetic cardiomyopathy, cardiovascular disease, NLRP3, heart failure, and diabetes mellitus.ConclusionThe growing recognition of cell death as a potential therapeutic target in diabetic cardiomyopathy is reflected in a notable surge in pertinent research articles. This increase has established cell death as an important and evolving domain of study in the field. Beyond the bibliometric trends, the integrative discussion presented herein synthesizes the roles of multiple cell death mechanisms—including apoptosis, pyroptosis, necroptosis, ferroptosis, and others—in the pathogenesis of diabetic cardiomyopathy, offering a conceptual framework for future mechanistic and therapeutic investigations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04268-6.

Introduction

Diabetes represents a major health concern worldwide, with estimates suggesting that over 783 million people will be impacted by the condition globally by 2045 [1]. This disease is associated with numerous severe complications, including cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, neuropathy, and retinopathy [2–5]. Notably, the incidence and mortality related to diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM), a common complication of diabetes, are still increasing [6, 7]. Studies have demonstrated that, unlike other diabetic complications, DCM increases the risk of myocardial injury under conditions of persistent hyperglycemia [8]. The prevalence of diastolic dysfunction ranges from 52 to 60%, and both cardiovascular disease and overall mortality rates are elevated in patients with DCM [9, 10]. This condition can result in severe complications, including arrhythmia, heart failure, and even death [11, 12]. The unique characteristics of DCM complicate its treatment and management, contributing to a significant societal burden, including considerable costs to healthcare systems. Other diabetes-related complications, such as diabetic nephropathy and retinopathy, have been extensively studied, yielding significant progress, as evidenced by the abundance of associated research literature [13–17]. In contrast, research pertaining to DCM has been relatively limited, particularly in areas relating to specific mechanisms, methods for early diagnosis, and treatment strategies. Numerous studies have indicated that cell death is closely associated with DCM, and appropriate intervention strategies targeting cell death may ameliorate the condition [18–21].

Cell death plays a key role in physiological processes such as development, the maintenance of tissue homeostasis, and response to disease. Scientific exploration of cell death began in 1842 when Karl Vogt first documented dying cells in tadpoles [22]. Subsequently, Kerr and colleagues identified apoptosis as a form of programmed cell death distinct from necrosis [23]. With advancements in research on cell death and related fields, its significance in the diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of DCM has become increasingly clear. For example, some studies have suggested that DCM can be alleviated via the coordinated inhibition of cell death and apoptosis [24–27]. Additionally, animal studies have demonstrated that DCM-related ferroptosis can be reduced by mitigating oxidative stress [28]. While numerous clinical studies have explored the relationship between cell death and cancer, relatively few have investigated the link between cell death and DCM, with most research still primarily focusing on animal and cell models [29–31]. Further research is needed to improve the understanding of DCM and its link with cell death, thereby offering guidance for future clinical work.

Bibliometric studies employ mathematical and statistical techniques for the evaluation of both established and emerging areas of research. Key methods, including analysis of co-authorship, co-citation, and co-occurrence, have seen significant advancements over recent years [32, 33]. VOSviewer v.1.6.18 is a Java-based software application designed for analyzing large literature datasets and displaying the results in map format. Conversely, CiteSpace 6.2.R4 uses an experimental framework to investigate novel ideas and evaluate current technologies and represents research findings in a particular domain via network diagrams of co-cited literature. Beyond the bibliometric trends, the integrative discussion presented herein synthesizes the roles of various cell death mechanisms in the pathogenesis of diabetic cardiomyopathy, providing a conceptual framework for future mechanistic and therapeutic investigations. The findings offer ideas and references for future research and development in this area.

Material and methods

Database

The Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) contains comprehensive literature records and indexes, including author, institution, keyword, and citation information. This detailed information offers substantial data support for researchers, facilitates bibliometric analysis and visualization studies, and is particularly suitable for research requiring high-quality citation data and periodical evaluation indicators. In contrast, the Scopus database is better suited for research involving a broad range of disciplines and diverse academic resources, while PubMed primarily comprises a vast collection of biomedical literature and clinical information.

Search strategy

On September 16, 2024, a comprehensive search was conducted within the WOSCC database for all manuscripts related to the study of cell death in DCM, covering the period from the year 2000 to September 16, 2024. The search strategy combined two thematic sets using the Boolean operator AND: (1) a cell death set, and (2) a DCM set. The cell death set included terms for well‑established and consistently indexed death modalities, namely: cell death, apoptosis (including classical, type I, extrinsic/intrinsic pathway, and caspase‑dependent variants), pyroptosis (including inflammatory apoptosis, pyroptotic cell death, and caspase‑1‑dependent death), necroptosis, PANoptosis, ferroptosis, and disulfidptosis. The DCM set comprised diabetic cardiomyopathy and its synonyms. The complete, detailed Boolean search string (including all nested parentheses and exact syntax) is provided in Supplementary Materials (Supplementary Material 1). The criteria for inclusion in the study were (1) full-text publications focusing on cell death in DCM and (2) English literature published from January 1, 2000, to September 15, 2024. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Documents with incomplete information; (2) repeatedly published literature; and (3) newspaper articles, conference articles, achievement reports, and other non-paper-format documents.

The search strategy concentrated on well-established and consistently indexed cell death modalities in diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM), specifically apoptosis, pyroptosis, necroptosis, ferroptosis, and disulfidptosis. Other pathways, such as parthanatos, anoikis, cuproptosis, oxytosis, alkaliptosis, and entosis, were excluded due to limited reports or the absence of stable Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms in the context of DCM. Autophagy-dependent cell death (type II programmed cell death) was intentionally omitted because the term ‘autophagy‘ in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) does not differentiate between autophagy as a death executor and as a survival regulator, potentially leading to a significant number of irrelevant records. Cuproptosis, which was first mechanistically characterized in 2022, was excluded to prevent a time-truncation artifact. These exclusions are explicitly justified as methodological trade-offs in the Limitations section.

Data collection

Publication trends along with the ratio of yearly and national publications were examined with GraphPad Prism v.8.0.2. Furthermore, CiteSpace 6.2.R4 and VOSviewer v.1.6.18 were employed to evaluate the data and visualize a scientific knowledge map. VOSviewer was utilized to generate co-occurrence networks of countries, authors, journals, and keywords, with the layout manually optimized for clarity. The following key parameters were applied: normalization method set to Association Strength, layout parameters (Attraction/Repulsion) configured to 2/0, clustering resolution established at 1.00, and a minimum cluster size defined as 10. CiteSpace was utilized to construct collaboration networks among countries and institutions, co-authorship networks among authors (categorized by citation count), journal dual-map overlays (utilizing the JCR journal maps function), and keyword co-occurrence maps (employing the hotspot display function). The key parameters for CiteSpace included: time slicing in 1-year increments from January 2000 to September 2024; node types selected based on the analytical objective (e.g., country, institution, author, keyword); a selection criterion based on the g-index (k = 20); and a pruning method that combined ‘Pathfinder’ with ‘Pruning sliced networks’ and ‘Pruning the merged network’. All other parameters (e.g., Top N = 50, Links = active) were maintained at their default settings. Results

General analysis of publications

A total of 1,535 articles related to cell death in DCM were identified within the WOSCC database, comprising 1,218 research articles and 317 reviews. This body of literature spanned 72 countries and regions, encompassed 1,487 institutions, and involved 6,434 authors. From 2000 onward, there was a steady yearly rise in the rate of publications. In this study, the timeline was divided into three distinct phases. Between 2000 and 2008, the increase was modest, with less than 20 articles published each year, reflecting a restricted advancement in this domain. Although different modes of cell death, such as apoptosis, necroptosis, and autophagy, among others, were discovered before 2000, pyroptosis was only identified in 2001 [34–37]. In 2002, scientists such as Yoshinori Ohsumi elucidated the molecular mechanisms underlying autophagy, which earned them the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2016 [38, 39]. It was not until 2012 that the concept of ferroptosis was proposed and, with advancements in science and technology, along with an increase in international academic exchanges, related research began to flourish [40, 41]. Additional modes of cell death were subsequently identified, such as cuproptosis, which was proposed in 2022 [42]. Moreover, the scientific research methods and technologies available during the first nine years may not have been sufficient to support an in-depth investigation into the mechanisms of cell death. Consequently, there were relatively few related studies during that time. This trend is also evident in DCM-linked research, which faced similar challenges. In 2000, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging was introduced for the early diagnosis and evaluation of DCM [43, 44]. In the initial years, research resources tended to focus more on exploring basic pathophysiological mechanisms rather than specific cell death mechanisms [45–47]. By 2010, significant insights into the roles of autophagy and apoptosis in DCM emerged, presenting new therapeutic targets [48, 49]. In 2015, SGLT2 inhibitors were discovered to not only reduce blood glucose levels but also significantly enhance cardiovascular outcomes in patients with diabetes [50, 51]. Alongside the advancements in technological methods, the evolution of medical models has also played a crucial role, exemplified by the announcement by Barack Hussein Obama, the 44th President of the United States, of the Precision Medicine Initiative in his State of the Union Address in 2015 [52, 53]. Humanity is interconnected, and through collaborative global efforts, research on cell death in DCM continues to progress, offering new perspectives and methodologies for DCM treatment and its clinical application.

Countries and institutions

Research on cell death in DCM has been conducted across 72 countries and regions. The yearly publication count for articles from the 10 most productive countries over the last 10 years is depicted in Fig. 3. The top five nations contributing to this research area were China, the United States, India, Canada, and Italy. Notably, the number of papers published in China constituted 68.53% of the total, significantly surpassing that of other countries. Among the 10 countries or regions with the most published research articles, publications from China received a total of 31,867 citations (see Table 1), greatly exceeding the citation counts of other nations or regions. Despite this, China has a citation-to-publication ratio of only 30.29, ranking it ninth among all nations, indicating that the general quality of its published research tends to be comparatively lower. The United States, with 280 published articles and 18,518 citations, holds the second position in both categories. However, it ranks first in terms of the citation-to-publication ratio (66.14), reflecting the overall quality of its research. As illustrated in Fig. 4A, the United States maintains close collaborations with Italy, Germany, and Australia, whereas China has stronger partnerships with India, Egypt, and Canada. In addition to a significant number of articles and a high citation frequency, USA also exhibits a centrality of 0.5, indicating that it is currently a leading country in this field. National centrality assesses a country’s influence and significance in a given field by examining both its research output and cooperation network. A total of 1,487 organizations have methodically released articles focusing on cell death in DCM. Of the 10 leading institutions based on publication count, 8 are based in China, one is located in the USA, and one hails from Egypt (see Table 2 and Fig. 4B). Wenzhou Medical University has the highest number of publications, having produced 63 manuscripts. These have been cited 2,702 times, resulting in an average of 42.89 citations per paper. Jilin University follows closely in second place with 60 papers and 2,956 citations, averaging 49.27 citations per article. Harbin Medical University ranks third with 59 papers and 2,177 citations, averaging 36.90 citations per article. Further analysis revealed that a significant majority of research institutions exhibit a preference for collaborating with other comparable institutions located within the same country. This tendency may stem from shared cultural backgrounds, ease of language exchange, and comparable scientific research environments and policy support. Institutions with published literature

The high ranking of research and publications related to cell death in DCM in China may be attributed to the increasing prevalence of this condition in the country over the past 15 years. China has the largest number of patients with diabetes globally, and its incidence is still increasing [54]. Compounding this issue, the estimated prevalence of pre-diabetes in China had reached 35.7% by 2017 [55]. Research on the relationship between DCM and cell death is ongoing in various institutions across China, particularly Wenzhou Medical University. Recent findings from this institution suggest that paeoniflorin exerts a protective effect against DCM in mice by mitigating myocardial injury, inhibiting ferroptosis, and modifying the intestinal microbiota [56]. However, the citation rate is a crucial metric for assessing the influence of academic papers. Despite China’s prolific output of published articles, it lags behind the United States in terms of citation rates per document. This disparity may stem from the robust foundation and extensive resources available in the field of biomedical research in the United States, possibly due to the significant public health challenges posed by diabetes and its complications. Collaborative efforts between China and the United States in this domain are progressing systematically. China and India, both populous nations, benefit from their geographical proximity, which facilitates collaboration in scientific research. Furthermore, cooperation among countries is closely linked to national policies, such as China’s Belt and Road Initiative, which has garnered significant attention from the Egyptian government. This initiative has fostered advancements in the medical field and scientific research in both countries. Additionally, globalization has enabled China to establish strong cooperative relationships with Canada, encompassing areas such as medical models, personnel training, and scientific research collaboration. Diabetes and its complications have emerged as a significant global health issue. For instance, the direct medical expenses associated with diabetes in the United States reached $237 billion in 2017 [57, 58]. Consequently, conducting relevant research is of paramount importance. In the study of cell death in DCM, the United States has established substantial collaboration with Italy and Australia, which may also be influenced by the national policies of the respective countries. Notably, all three nations are members of the OECD and engage in close cooperation across various domains, including economic policy, education, scientific and technological innovation, and medical research. In addressing global health challenges, transnational and inter-institutional cooperation is becoming increasingly important, fostering economic development and facilitating the exchange of knowledge and expertise.

Accordingly, researchers are encouraged to collaborate to strengthen academic integrity and foster a positive academic environment. Among the top 10 cited publications are two reviews by Jia GH on the pathology of DCM. One review, published in the journal Circulation Research, presents contemporary perspectives on the factors contributing to DCM and discusses prevention and treatment strategies based on underlying mechanisms [59]. The second review, published in Diabetologia, examines the relationship of insulin resistance, hyperinsulinemia, and hyperglycemia with the development of cardiac dysfunction [60]. The review summarizes the understanding of the pathophysiological mechanisms of DCM and explores potential prevention and treatment strategies. It further highlights future research directions for unresolved issues in the field and provides a valuable reference for subsequent studies. Recent years have witnessed rapid growth in mitochondrial research, which has emerged as a focal point in the fields of life sciences and medicine, facilitated by advancements in membrane potential detection, mitochondrial DNA copy number determination, and gene editing technologies.

The top two articles based on citation counts were published in Circulation Research. The top-ranked article was “Diabetic Cardiomyopathy: An Update of Mechanisms Contributing to This Clinical Entity “authored by Jia GH [59]. This review examines contemporary perspectives on the etiology of DCM and discusses prevention and treatment strategies informed by these mechanisms. The second-ranked article was “Diabetic Cardiomyopathy: What Is It and Can It Be Fixed? “, a review authored by Dillmann WH [62]. It discusses how research employing animal models of diabetes mellitus has shown that a decline in myocardial cell function is a significant contributor to heart failure in this disease. The authors suggest that improving cytosolic Ca2 + handling, particularly mitochondrial Ca2 + , in the human diabetic heart represents a promising target for future therapeutic interventions. The study by Falcão-Pires et al. mainly examines the pathogenesis of DCM and has emerged as a foundational piece of literature that significantly contributes to ongoing discussions and explorations in this field [63]. The primary focus of the research conducted by Dewanjee S et al. was to identify potential therapeutic drug targets associated with autophagy in diabetic hearts [64]. Meanwhile, research conducted by Song S and colleagues highlights that a lack of Sirtuin 3 (SIRT3) worsens DCM by facilitating necroptosis and activating NOD-like receptor family, pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) [65]. Over the past 15 years, research on cell death in DCM has significantly proliferated, characterized not only by a high citation rate but also by the emergence of exceptional experimental articles. Both types of articles play distinct yet valuable roles in scientific research.

Hyperglycemia increases the production of reactive oxygen species in the body while simultaneously diminishing the function of the antioxidant system. This results in heightened oxidative stress, which plays a critical role in the onset and progression of DCM [66]. Oxidative stress can induce apoptosis, while the use of oxidative stress scavengers can mitigate it, thereby contributing to the treatment of diseases such as DCM, heart failure, and other cardiovascular conditions [67, 68]. Importantly, in addition to affecting myocardial cells, oxidative stress also influences other pathological mechanisms. For instance, oxidative stress can exacerbate the inflammatory response by activating NLRP3 inflammasomes, which further intensifies oxidative stress and worsens disease states [69, 70]. Lipid metabolism also exerts a significant impact on DCM. Excessive fatty acid intake can lead to lipid accumulation in myocardial cells, resulting in lipotoxicity, which subsequently causes myocardial cell dysfunction and fibrosis [71, 72]. Disorders of lipid metabolism can amplify oxidative stress, activate various inflammatory pathways, and result in cellular and extracellular damage, ultimately contributing to pathological myocardial remodeling [73]. These interconnected factors collectively exacerbate DCM. SIRT1, a deacetylase, can alleviate oxidative stress in myocardial cells and inhibit myocardial fibrosis and apoptosis through deacetylation [74]. Angiotensin II, a key component of the renin-angiotensin system, plays a crucial role in myocardial infarction and fibrosis, exacerbating myocardial injury by promoting inflammatory responses and cell proliferation [75]. Additionally, GSDMD is closely linked to inflammatory responses and various diseases, serving as a critical factor in cell death and emerging as a key focus of recent research [76]. Although autophagy as a concept was introduced as early as 1963, it continues to hold research value, driven by innovative research methods and technologies.

Discussion

General information

A thorough examination of the foundational data obtained in this study revealed a clear upward trend in publications relating to cell death in DCM from 2000 to 2024. This trend may be associated with the rising incidence and mortality rates of DCM, as well as the growing focus on the mechanisms of cell death and related diseases, which has driven an increase in scientific interest in this area. Among the included publications, China stands out as the leading global contributor to research on cell death in DCM, boasting an impressive total of 1,052 publications. The United States has garnered attention due to its high citation-to-publication ratio. A review of the collaborative network indicates that China and the United States, ranked first and second in publication counts, respectively, have considerable opportunities for enhanced partnership.

Wenzhou Medical University is a distinguished institution in China, recognized for its extensive publishing record in this field. Research from this institution has contributed to clarifying the significant role of cell death in DCM by demonstrating its influence across multiple pathways [77, 78]. Research published in March 2024 revealed that paeoniflorin enhances heart function in mice with DCM by mitigating muscle injury, combating oxidative stress, and modifying the composition of the microbiota [79]. Paeoniflorin is a natural compound extracted from traditional Chinese medicines, including Radix Paeoniae Rubra and Radix Paeoniae Alba. Studies on the role of Chinese medicine in cell death associated with DCM at this institution have been limited. Over recent years, research on compounds derived from traditional Chinese medicine has undergone a significant increase, and the application of contemporary research methodologies has revitalized the in-depth study of traditional Chinese medicine.

The co-citation analysis revealed that Jia GH was the author with the highest centrality, suggestive of a notable influence in the field along with strong connections to other authors. Additionally, network analysis revealed that most authors operate within closely linked groups, exhibiting minimal international collaboration, which may hinder the future development of the field. Consequently, encouraging authors to expand the range of global cooperation and enhance technical exchanges could greatly enhance scholarly productivity in this area. Among co-cited journals, Circulation Research emerged as the most frequently cited, with 10% of the 50 most cited publications appearing in this journal. This underscores its crucial function in spreading research related to this subject. The most cited article, authored by Jia GH, was published in this journal in 2018. Related research encompasses the fields of molecular biology, genetics, medicine, and immunology, reflecting the integration of scientific inquiry across these disciplines.

Hot topics and frontiers

Keyword analysis revealed that investigation of the mechanisms involved in DCM continues to be a prominent research area. DCM is a major complication of diabetes, making it a clinically significant condition. Studies have shown that long-term hyperglycemia predisposes individuals to heart diseases, which may ultimately culminate in heart failure [80]. Thus, it is essential to implement timely preventive actions and early intervention strategies, which is a critical focus of current research efforts. Dysregulated NLRP3 inflammasome activation is linked to the development of diabetes [81]. Recent research has shown that this activation plays a role not just in inflammation but also in various forms of cell death, such as apoptosis, necroptosis, and ferroptosis [82]. This indicates that blocking NLRP3 could serve as a potential therapeutic approach for enhancing cell survival in DCM. Numerous studies have shown that improvements in DCM can be accomplished through the modulation of lipid metabolism [83, 84]. Indeed, given that the initiation and execution of cell death are closely connected to lipid metabolism, concentrating on lipid metabolism may be essential for future advancements in both the prevention and management of DCM [85]. Furthermore, SIRT1 plays an essential role in the regulation of gene expression, genome stability, apoptosis, autophagy, and aging, as well as the promotion of cell proliferation and tumorigenesis [86]. Evidence supports that the upregulation of SIRT1 has a direct cytoprotective effect on the heart, significantly contributing to the repair of myocardial cells through the regulation of various signaling pathways and molecular mechanisms [87]. As research into the mechanisms that govern cell death in DCM continues, it is expected that new regulatory genes and elements will be identified.

Keyword analysis indicated that the predominant topics in research related to DCM over the past three years have centered on cell death, particularly pyroptosis. For instance, in February 2024, an article published in the journal Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy demonstrated that DCM negatively affects heart functionality by elevating cardiac fibrosis and triggering GSDMD-dependent cell apoptosis [88]. The results suggested that treatment with irisin can alleviate injury associated with DCM, reestablish mitochondrial balance, and decrease pyroptosis by enhancing the expression of MITOL. While primarily grounded in basic research, the investigation of cell death presents a promising avenue for the development of anti-DCM drugs. Over time, studies have clarified the influence of cardiac cell mortality in the development of DCM, and have identified various forms of cell death, including apoptosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis, and cuproptosis, that rely on distinct mechanisms. This field is currently a major focus in DCM-related research, presenting encouraging opportunities for additional exploration.

Autophagy

Since the term “autophagy” was first introduced, this form of cell death has been the subject of extensive research. Autophagy serves as an intracellular degradation process that eliminates damaged organelles and proteins from cells through the lysosomal route, thus maintaining intracellular homeostasis [89].

An imbalance in the process of autophagy is recognized as playing a substantial role in the development of myocardial injury associated with DCM [90]. Studies have investigated the potential of regulating autophagy as a therapeutic approach for this condition. For instance, it was shown that the stimulation of mitochondrial autophagy can mitigate high-fat-diet-induced DCM [91]. However, excessive mitochondrial autophagy can exacerbate myocardial injury in patients with DCM [92]. One study reported that autophagy serves as a valuable biomarker for predicting left ventricular reverse remodeling (LVRR) in patients with DCM, with LVRR anticipated to confer a more favorable prognosis [93]. This study involved 42 patients diagnosed with DCM and 7 exhibiting normal cardiac function, all of whom underwent left ventricular endocardial myocardial biopsy. An examination of myocardial cells indicated that patients exhibiting LVRR positivity possessed a notably higher quantity of autophagic vesicles, an increased autolysosome ratio, and heightened cathepsin D expression levels relative to the controls. Furthermore, these individuals experienced a lower incidence of cardiovascular events throughout the follow-up period. In addition to the medical field, autophagy has also gained significant attention in cell and molecular biology. Research on various stem cell types indicates that autophagy is crucial for stem cell quiescence, activation, differentiation, and self-renewal [94]. Therapy involving mesenchymal stem cells has shown potential in the prevention of DCM owing to its anti-apoptotic, anti-fibrotic, and anti-inflammatory properties, all of which work together to enhance cardiac function in patients with diabetes mellitus [95]. In contemporary research, disciplines beyond medicine and biology, such as chemistry, psychology, computer science, and materials science, are increasingly engaging in studies related to autophagy. The analysis of interdisciplinary research can be approached from various perspectives, significantly fostering innovation. Interdisciplinary collaboration represents a crucial trend and characteristic of current scientific development.

Autophagy plays a bidirectional regulatory role in the onset and progression of DCM. Under physiological conditions, autophagy facilitates the repair and regeneration of damaged myocardium. Conversely, in a pathological state, dysregulated autophagy can lead to myocardial injury and dysfunction. Therefore, modulating autophagy levels may represent an effective strategy for the prevention and treatment of DCM.

Apoptosis

High glucose levels can induce apoptosis in myocardial cells via complex mechanisms [96]. Moreover, high glucose can activate caspase-8 and caspase-9, essential initiators of both exogenous and endogenous apoptotic pathways in cardiac cells, leading to the activation of caspase-3, the downstream executor of apoptosis, and ultimately, the apoptosis of myocardial cells.

Numerous studies have focused on ameliorating DCM through the inhibition of apoptosis. Mohammad H et al. assessed the cardioprotective properties of galangin (Gal), a natural flavonoid recognized for its radical-scavenging and anti-inflammatory activities, in a rat model of diabetes [97]. Gal (15 mg/kg) was given daily over 6 weeks. The results showed that Gal reduced both oxidative damage and apoptosis in the heart, enhanced antioxidant defenses, and alleviated DCM. Over recent years, studies have increasingly focused on Chinese medicine extracts, highlighting the intersection of traditional Chinese medicine and modern technological research methodologies. In addition to the compounds extracted from plants, Yu Zhou et al. found that hydroxyurea (HU), which is commonly used in clinical settings to treat myeloproliferative diseases, tumors, and chronic myeloid leukemia, can protect myocardial cells in DCM by inhibiting apoptosis [98]. The authors generated a mouse model of diabetes via the intraperitoneal administration of streptozotocin (STZ), followed by 16 weeks of treatment with HU, and found that HU notably enhanced the levels of inflammatory factors and proteins associated with apoptosis. Similarly, at a concentration of 5 µM, HU increased the survival rate of H9C2 cells exposed to high levels of glucose. The same study also identified an interaction between autophagy and apoptosis in DCM, which further exacerbated injury and death in myocardial cells. Increasing evidence suggests that changes in the levels of myocardial apoptosis and autophagy are integral to DCM onset and progression [99–101].

Apoptosis plays a vital role in the development of DCM. Several mechanisms lead to myocardial cell apoptosis under hyperglycemia, including oxidative stress, mitochondrial impairment, inflammatory reactions, and other related factors. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for the development of innovative treatment approaches focused on reducing the effects of DCM in patients.

Necroptosis

Necroptosis is a type of programmed cell death similar to necrosis. It is activated when cells cannot undergo normal apoptosis induced by factors such as inflammation, oxidative stress, and ischemia. In contrast to apoptosis, necroptosis occurs without the involvement of caspase activity. Instead, it is facilitated by receptors that trigger the activation of receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3) along with its downstream target, mixed lineage kinase domain-like pseudokinase (MLKL) [102]. Diabetic patients frequently experience chronic inflammation. Additionally, elevated blood sugar levels contribute to increased oxidative stress, which can promote cell necrosis through a variety of mechanisms.

Studies have demonstrated that there is a close association between necrotizing ptosis and inflammation [103–105]. In heart failure, the rate of myocardial cell death resulting from necrosis surpasses that of apoptosis [106]. Studies have demonstrated that in DCM, the occurrence of significant inflammatory reactions and oxidative stress can promote the apoptosis and necrosis of myocardial cells [107]. For example, Song Shen and colleagues examined the function of SIRT3 in DCM and identified it as a potential therapeutic target for diseases related to cardiovascular health, metabolism, and aging [108]. They further reported that SIRT3 deficiency exacerbates the mitochondrial damage induced by hyperglycemia, increases reactive oxygen species accumulation, promotes necroptosis, potentially activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, and ultimately worsens DCM in mice. These findings indicated that SIRT3 may serve as a target for molecular intervention in the prevention and treatment of DCM. Additionally, Yun Chen et al. demonstrated that dihydromyricetin attenuates DCM by suppressing oxidative stress, inflammation, and necroptosis via the activation of SIRT3 [109]. While SIRT1 currently represents a focal point in DCM research, the role of SIRT3 in this condition also merits further investigation, particularly in the context of necroptosis. SIRT3 is a critical modulator of necrotic prolapse through its regulatory effect on mitochondrial function and the antioxidant stress response. Further investigation in this area is warranted, particularly regarding its role in DCM.

In general, the mechanism underlying necroptosis in DCM involves a variety of factors, mainly associated with inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, and the activation of distinct signaling pathways. The potential for treatment strategies targeting necroptosis in DCM appears promising. With ongoing in-depth research, these strategies may advance to clinical drug research and development, ultimately offering more effective treatment options for patients.

Pyroptosis

Pyroptosis, a form of cell death characterized by inflammation, plays an essential role in the maintenance of host homeostasis and also has a significant impact on the progression of several diseases. This recently discovered form of programmed cell death predominantly activates caspases via inflammatory bodies, resulting in the perforation of the cell membrane, the release of intracellular materials, and, ultimately, inflammatory reactions [110]. Growing evidence indicates that pyroptosis is significantly involved in the development of cardiovascular diseases, acting as both an important instigator and an intrinsic modulator of cardiovascular inflammation [111].

Recent research has revealed that pyroptosis contributes significantly to the initiation and advancement of DCM [112]. A hyperglycemic environment can induce myocardial pyroptosis, exacerbating the pathological processes associated with DCM [113]. The mechanisms involved include the participation of regulatory factors such as NLRP3, the absent in melanoma 2 (AIM2) inflammasome, and non-coding RNA (ncRNA). Moreover, pyroptosis leads to the swelling of myocardial cells, the rupture of cell membranes, and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, all of which exacerbate cardiac injury [114]. Wei Zhang et al. evaluated the protective effects of quercetin against DCM by analyzing the expression of pyroptosis pathway proteins, myocardial cell apoptosis rates, the degree of fibrosis, and serum inflammatory indices in diabetic model rats. They found that quercetin promoted the nuclear translocation of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2), elevated the levels of intracellular antioxidant proteins, and abrogated the progression of pyroptosis, thus mitigating DCM [115]. Pyroptosis has also been extensively studied in relation to ncRNA. For instance, silencing long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) KCNQ1 overlapping transcript1 (KCNQ1OT1) has been shown to alleviate pyroptosis and fibrosis in DCM [116]. Qiong Yuan and colleagues reported that circular RNA (circRNA) DICAR acts as an internal regulator of DCM and pyroptosis in diabetic myocardial tissue [117]. Furthermore, lncRNA GAS5 inhibits the pyroptosis of diabetic myocardial cells, mediated by the activation of NLRP3 inflammasomes, by targeting miR-34b-3p/AHR [118]. The mechanisms by which ncRNAs influence cell apoptosis are complex and diverse, involving various ncRNAs and signaling pathways. Future research should further elucidate their specific mechanisms and roles in DCM, thereby providing novel strategies for the treatment of this disease. Additionally, exploring ncRNA regulatory networks and interactions represents a significant direction for future investigation. This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the genes and signaling pathways associated with pyroptosis, aiming to elucidate the relationship between pyroptosis and other forms of cell death. It further seeks to leverage advanced biotechnological approaches for the development of natural drugs, thereby offering novel insights and methodologies for clinical applications.

In summary, pyroptosis is both a contributing factor to the pathogenesis of DCM and a promising therapeutic target for the treatment of this condition. Understanding and intervening in this process may aid in the prevention and treatment of DCM, thereby reducing its impact on the health of patients.

Ferroptosis

Ferroptosis, a recently identified form of programmed cell death, is attracting significant attention owing to its presumed role in diabetes and related complications [119]. This form of cell death is defined as being iron-dependent and is linked to the buildup of lipid peroxides and the disruption of redox balance. Furthermore, ferroptosis is an important factor in the pathophysiology of multiple diseases, such as cancers, neurological conditions, and metabolic disorders.

Ferroptosis is involved in the development of DCM, significantly contributing to injury in myocardial cells via multiple mechanisms. For example, the buildup of excess iron disrupts the antioxidant system associated with DCM, thereby triggering and worsening pathological events such as autophagy and mitochondrial dysfunction. This, in turn, initiates a cascade of reactions that amplifies damage to both myocardial and microvascular tissues [120]. Inhibiting ferroptosis has potential as a strategy for the treatment of DCM, given that it can effectively mitigate the damage to myocardial cells, thereby slowing the progression of DCM [121]. For example, canagliflozin has been shown to inhibit ferroptosis and alleviate DCM through the modulation of cardiac iron homeostasis and oxidative stress-related pathways [122]. Lei Fan et al. established an in vitro model of DCM in H9C2 cells and treated the cells with varying doses of dexmedetomidine (DEX) and ML385, a specific inhibitor of Nrf2. The results indicated that DEX inhibited ferroptosis via the Nrf2/GPX4 pathway, thereby alleviating myocardial cell injury [123]. In addition to drug therapies, bioactive phytochemicals can also regulate ferroptosis, suggesting that they hold potential for development as nutritional and health products that can enhance disease prevention [124]. Interest in this area is ongoing.

In the second decade of ferroptosis research, a significant focus will be on developing drugs and therapies that utilize iron death inducers and inhibitors, as well as the selective regulation of the mechanisms and methods associated with ferroptosis. In this context, the relevant inducers and inhibitors will be crucial for the treatment of DCM. New approaches and objectives for treating DCM are expected, encompassing the modulation of iron metabolism and the blocking of the iron-SAG pathway.

Cuproptosis

Cuproptosis represents a recently identified form of cellular demise triggered by copper ionophores or an imbalance in copper regulation. The term was first introduced by Peter Tsvetkov in 2022 [125]. This form of cell death, which differs in mechanism from other forms of regulated cell death, is characterized by a reliance on copper, the buildup of lipid-acylated proteins, and a decrease in the levels of Fe-S cluster proteins [126]. Given its novelty, cuproptosis offers considerable potential for investigation.

Currently, studies on cuproptosis are predominantly focused on cancer; however, interest in its role in DCM is also increasing. DCM exhibits a deficiency in copper metabolism, which may be associated with abnormal copper levels. Copper deficiency can induce myocardial hypertrophy and exacerbate cardiomyopathy, whereas copper overload can lead to cuproptosis [127]. Furthermore, advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) resulting from sustained hyperglycemia contribute notably to cardiac toxicity, and there is an association between AGEs and the harmful effects of copper in DCM [128]. Studies have demonstrated that changes in the protein and mRNA spectrum in the hearts of patients with diabetes are consistent with cuproptosis, including an increase in the expression of ATF3, SPI1, and SLC31A1 [129]. Future research should explore the interactions between cuproptosis and other modes of cell death, as well as investigate its connections to DCM pathogenesis.

A substantial correlation is observed between DCM and cuproptosis. The impairment of copper metabolism associated with DCM can exacerbate myocardial injury by facilitating cuproptosis, thereby worsening the condition. This discovery provides novel perspectives and prospective targets for DCM treatment, including improving myocardial function via the modulation of copper metabolism and the suppression of cuproptosis.

Strengths and limitations

Existing bibliometrics research encompasses various topics, including the examination of specific modes of cell death in relation to the complications of diabetes, as well as the role of cell death in cardiovascular diseases. Zeng, X et al. analyzed the correlation between autophagy and cardiomyopathy globally, while Li, X et al. focused on diabetes and cell death [130, 131]. This article represents the first bibliometric analysis of research concerning cell death in DCM, delivering a review of research trends and hotspots in this area. This study outlined discoveries and highlighted research frontiers, aiming to provide important perspectives for future research pathways in this field. Nonetheless, it is essential to recognize several limitations. First, the pathogenesis of DCM encompasses various forms of cell death. While apoptosis has been known for over 50 years, cuproptosis was only identified two years ago, and the volume of related literature remains limited. Second, this research was exclusively based on information gathered from the WOSCC. It was not extended to other databases, such as Scopus or PubMed, which may limit the volume of publications considered and, consequently, affect the robustness of the conclusions drawn. Third, only English-language publications were considered, which may exclude important findings published in other languages. Fourth, the results derived from the search were inherently shaped by the choice of search terms, adding a level of subjectivity to the findings. Finally, any delays in the release of some referenced works may result in misleading assessments of high citation frequencies. It is crucial to recognize the limited time frame of this study, which spans only from 2000 to 2024. For a more thorough and dependable perspective, subsequent investigations should aim to carry out more extensive, large-scale, multicenter research to affirm and strengthen these results.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the search strategy did not encompass certain cell death pathways that have either limited or recently characterized involvement in diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM), such as parthanatos, anoikis, and cuproptosis, nor did it include autophagy-dependent cell death (type II programmed cell death). Detailed justifications for these exclusions are provided in the Methods section. As a result, the bibliometric findings primarily reflect the mainstream modalities of regulated cell death, including apoptosis, pyroptosis, necroptosis, and ferroptosis, and do not fully represent the complete spectrum of regulated cell death in DCM. Emerging mechanisms, such as cuproptosis, are qualitatively discussed as potential future directions.

Conclusions and prospects

For this study, a total of 1,535 publications were collected from the WOSCC, and CiteSpace 6.2.R4 and VOSviewer v.1.6.18 were employed for both visualization and analytical purposes. The studies, focusing on the topic of cell death in DCM, included contributions from 72 nations, involving 1,487 institutions and a total of 6,434 authors. Research output in this field has seen a substantial rise over the last four years, indicating an increasing level of interest and engagement in the subject. Studies have established that a robust relationship exists between cell death and DCM, suggesting that this trend is likely to continue in the foreseeable future. Further research should focus on the interactions among the various modes of cell death, particularly the emerging phenomenon of copper death, which presents significant opportunities for further investigation. Regarding pathogenesis, DCM can be ameliorated by addressing inflammation, oxidative stress, and lipid metabolism. This includes exploring novel treatment strategies, developing new pharmacological agents targeting cell death pathways, and utilizing gene editing technologies to regulate genes associated with cell death. Additionally, emphasis should be placed on early prevention strategies, identifying biomarkers for early diagnosis, and employing artificial intelligence and big data analytics to construct predictive models for disease progression and treatment outcomes. All these efforts should be grounded in the context of cell death mechanisms in DCM, with the overarching goal of enhancing human health.

The differences in troponin values among gender in COVID-19 patients
Authors: Berna Stavileci
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The aim of this study is to investigate the differences on admission troponin values among gender in hospital outcomes and in the 2-year follow-up period in coronavirus disease (COVID-19) patients. Data of 826 patients with moderate-to-severe COVID-19 disease were analyzed retrospectively. All patients had nasal and oropharyngeal swab samples taken according to Ministry of Health guidelines on admission. Patients were divided into female (n = 438) and male (n = 388) groups and were follow-up for 2 years. Clinical events such as need for intensive care unit, respiratory failure, need for inotropic initiation, acute renal failure, cardiac injury, and in-hospital mortality were also recorded. The cumulative endpoints were determined as all-cause mortality, re-hospitalization, and stroke during the 2-year follow-up period. Also, factors affecting the cumulative endpoints were investigated. In clinical events and cumulative endpoints, the differences of troponin values between the gender were investigated and the factors causing cardiac injury were determined separately in men and women. Mean age (59.43 ± 19.15 vs 58.14 ± 16.66) and comorbidities were significantly higher in the female group. There were no differences between genders in terms of clinical events except respiratory failure, which was more frequent in the male group (P = .016). In-hospital survivor rate in the female group was 16 ± 2.5 days (95% confidence interval: 11.08–20.91), in the male group was 14 ± 0.92 days (95% confidence interval: 12.18–15.81) P = .008, while there were no differences between groups among in-hospital morality rates (P = .208). During the 2-year follow-up period cumulative endpoints were more in the male group (P < .05). Troponin value in female ≥ 93 pg/mL, in male ≥ 28 pg/mL was related with cardiac injury. All clinical events occur at lower troponin values in the male group. In both groups, independent risk factors for in-hospital mortality were troponin and the existence of fragmented QRS; for cumulative endpoints were respiratory failure, cardiac injury, and age. We observed that in COVID-19 disease, troponin value differs by gender. A lesser increase in troponin levels in men was indicative of cardiac injury. Even slight increases in troponin levels in men should alert clinicians for cardiac injury and other clinical events.

1. Introduction

The coronavirus disease (COVID-19) is still a major health problem. It causes millions of deaths and a high morbidity rate all over the world. Angiotensin converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) binds strongly to spike protein.[1] ACE2 is found in the heart, vessels, lungs, central nervous system, kidneys, and adipose tissue.[1] The virus is assumed to cause cardiac injury via ACE2.[1] However, the significance and extent of this potential involvement is currently unclear.[1] Cardiovascular damage in COVID-19 occurs as thrombosis, acute cardiac and microvascular injury.[2] Acute cardiac injury in COVID-19 patients can be defined as increases in cardiac biomarkers, pathological electrocardiographic changes and decreased cardiac function.[2] Cardiac injury was seen in 8% to 12% of hospitalized patients.[3] Cardiac injury due to non-ischemic myocardial events other than myocarditis can be listed as serious respiratory tract infection, sepsis, hypoxia, systemic inflammation, cardiac adrenergic hyperstimulation, and pulmonary thromboembolism.[2] Information regarding other cardiovascular events in COVID-19 is still limited.

However, preexisting cardiovascular disease or acute heart injury has been shown to be associated with worse outcomes in these patients.[4] Whereas women have higher infection rates, mortality and severe pneumonia rates were higher in men with COVID-19 disease.[5] If gender-specific factors can be identified in COVID-19, these factors can be targeted for treatments. Differences in gender is possible due to differences of behavioral considerations, comorbidities, the renin-angiotensin system, levels of ACE2, nitric oxide, estrogen, transmembrane protease serine 2 (TMPRSS2) pathway and the immune response.[6]

Among healthy individuals troponin values differ by gender and it is known to be lower in women compared to men of the same age.[7,8] Also, many studies showed the relations between gender, age, occupations and their impact on spreading COVID-19[9,10] but the differences of troponin values among gender in COVID-19 patients are unknown. The purpose of our study is to analyze the differences in troponin values in clinical events such as need for intensive care unit, respiratory failure, need for inotropic initiation, acute renal failure, cardiac injury, in-hospital mortality and in follow-up period among gender.

2. Materials and methods

Eight hundred thirty patients hospitalized with the diagnosis of moderate and severe viral pneumonia with COVID-19 between March 20, 2020 and March 20, 2021 were analyzed retrospectively. While 826 patients were included in the study, 4 patients were excluded due to insufficient data. All patients had nasal and oropharyngeal swab samples taken according to Ministry of Health guidelines on admission. Patients symptoms and demographic characteristics were recorded. Clinical events such as need for intensive care unit, respiratory failure, need for inotropic initiation, acute renal failure, cardiac injury and in-hospital mortality also were recorded. The cumulative endpoints were determined as all-cause mortality, re-hospitalization, and stroke during the 2-year follow-up period. Also, factors affecting the cumulative endpoints were investigated. Apart from routine hemogram and biochemical data, troponin, electrocardiography (ECG) and echocardiographic data were recorded. Patients were divided into 2 groups: female (n = 438) and male (n = 388). In clinical events, the differences of troponin values between the sexes were investigated and the factors causing cardiac injury were determined separately in men and women. Inclusion criteria for the study: patients between the ages of 18 to 80 and patients with COVID-19 reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction positive test results and moderate or severe viral pneumonia. Exclusion criteria for the study: patients with mild COVID-19 disease and not requiring hospitalization, patients who immediately required invasive or noninvasive mechanical ventilation, and patients in critical condition which need intensive care unit at admission.

2.1. Ethical statement

The study was prepared in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All methods have been performed according the relevant guidelines. Our study was approved by Ethics Committee (no. 2022/69-25) and by Ministry of Health (no. 2022-03-01T14_34_53).

2.2. Laboratory processes

At admission, 5 mL of venous blood was collected from each patient. We use an immunoassay (Architech i1000 SR, Abbott) for troponin measurements.

2.3. Electrocardiographic evaluation

ECG was obtained from each patient and were analyzed by 2 independent cardiologists. Presence of fragmented QRS (fQRS), length of PR dispersion, QT dispersion and corrected QT interval were measured.

2.4. Echocardiographic data

Diastolic dysfunction and left ventricular ejection fraction which was determined by Simpson’s method were measured.

2.5. Definitions

The diagnosis of COVID-19 is based on reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction positive testing. Patients with lower respiratory tract disease and oxygen saturation ≥ 94% were defined as moderate pneumonia. Patients with severe pneumonia were defined as lung infiltrates > 50%, oxygen saturation < 94%, or breathing rate > 30 breaths/min. Whereas patients with septic shock, respiratory failure or multi-organ dysfunction were defined as critical condition. The need for invasive and noninvasive mechanical ventilators was defined as respiratory failure. Acute renal failure was defined as a >30% increase in creatinine value from baseline and the patient being oliguric or anuric. Presence of additional wave like RSR’ pattern or the existence of notched S or R waves, in <120 ms duration of QRS complex, without any bundle branch block has been defined as QRS fragmentation.[11] Troponin level above 99th percentile upper reference limit and one of the following: in echocardiography newly developed wall motion disorder, newly developed stage 2 diastolic dysfunction, newly decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction value bellow 50%, and newly developed ST-T change on ECG was defined as cardiac injury. The cumulative endpoints during the 2-year follow-up period were defined as all-cause mortality, re-hospitalization, and stroke.

2.6. Statistical analysis

SPSS version 21 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY) was used for statistical analysis. Analysis of all data and fit to a normal distribution has been made by Kolmogorov–Smirnov method. For categorical variables chi-square test and for continues variables t test methods were used. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was used to see the potency of troponin value to determine clinical events. The area under the curve (AUC) was estimated with 95% confidence interval. Stepwise method was used for multivariate logistic regression analysis. The results of multivariate regression analyses were presented as odd ratio with 95% confidence interval (CI). Statistical significance level was accepted as <.05 in all tests.

3. Results

Demographic characteristics, symptom findings and clinical events are summarized in Table 1. In the female group mean age was significantly higher than in the male group (59.43 ± 19.15 vs 58.14 ± 16.66, P < .001). Hypertension, diabetes mellitus, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and chronic kidney failure rates were higher in the female group (respectively P < .001, P = .001, P = .002, P = .031, P = .014, P < .001). There were no differences between the 2 groups in terms of other comorbidities. Symptoms as cough, fever and taste abnormalities were significant higher in the male group (respectively P = .031, P = .025, P = .040). While respiratory failure was more frequent in male group (P = .016), there was no differences between gender in term of other clinical events such as need for intensive care unit, need for inotropic initiation, cardiac injury, acute renal failure, and in-hospital mortality rates. During 2-years follow-up period cumulative endpoints (such as all-cause mortality, re-hospitalization, and stroke) were more in the male group (21.9% vs 9.42%, P < .05).

4. Discussion

COVID-19 remains a worldwide health problem. Deaths from COVID-19 are associated with respiratory failure, metabolic acidosis, septic shock, cardiac injury, and coagulopathy.[12] Even women infection rates are higher than men, male patients had both higher severity and fatality rates.[7] Differences in gender is probably due to comorbidities, behavioral considerations, incidence of smoking, the renin-angiotensin system, levels of ACE2 in different organs, levels of nitric oxide, levels of sex hormone like estrogen, differences in TMPRSS2 pathway, and the immune response.[6] Sexual dimorphism plays role in immune responses.[6] Many studies have demonstrated higher mortality rates in men with middle eastern respiratory syndrome coronavirus and severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 1 (SARS-CoV-1).[6]

Patients with COVID-19 may face hemodynamic instability, systemic inflammation, multiple organ failure or direct cardiac injury.[13,14] The primary mechanism of direct cardiac injury is due to the interaction between SARS-CoV-2 and cardiomyocytes.[13,14] Many studies show that high values of cardiac biomarkers such as troponin,[15,16] N-terminal pro-B type natriuretic peptide (NT pro-BNP), and creatine kinase myocardial band,[16,17] were associated with severe disease and higher mortality.[17] These patients were older, male, and have a history of cardiovascular disease (CVD).[16,17] Among healthy individuals, baseline levels of cardiac biomarkers significantly differ by sex. For example, in females compared with age-matched males, NT pro-BNP levels were higher, whereas creatine kinase myocardial band levels and troponin were lower.[7] However, sex-specific troponin or NT pro-BNP cut off values do not have any predictive value for death or myocardial infarction.[7,17,18] The differences of troponin values between gender among COVID-19 patients are unknown. Differences of troponin levels between severe and less severe COVID-19 infections was modified not by age or sex, but only by hypertension.[17] In our study, in COVID-19 disease, troponin values differed by sex. In female group troponin values were higher. Although the troponin values are normally lower in women than in men,[7] we observed that for the diagnosis of cardiac injury, women must have an increased troponin values three times higher than in men. We found that all clinical events occurred at lower troponin increases in men. Although troponin was higher in female group, the development of cardiac injury, other clinical events and in-hospital mortality rate were similar in both groups, but during 2-year follow-up period cumulative endpoints (such as all-cause mortality, re-hospitalization, and stroke) were more common in the male group. Also, we found that high troponin value predicts cardiac injury only in the male group, while it predicts in-hospital mortality in both groups. Different to our study, there was no association with sex in NT pro-BNP, troponin levels and COVID-19 outcomes.[19,20] It is important to emphasize that sexual dimorphism may also exist in cardiac restoration. In a study with animal models, women showed better survival in the case of acute heart failure.[21] This is associated with limited cardiac remodeling and better functional recovery. Women produced enhanced levels of reparative leukocytes and epoxy-eicosatrienoic acids, which shows anti-inflammatory and anti-hypertensive effects.[21] SARS-CoV-2 associated cardiac injury may be less pronounced in females due to more efficient cardiac repair. In addition, the usage of clopidogrel and calcium channel blockers were more in the female group, in our study. Calcium channel blockers activate and increase expression of endothelial nitric oxide synthase.[22] Apart from its vasodilation feature, nitric oxide prevents the replication of viruses.[23] Production of nitric oxide is enhanced by estrogen.[24] These may be other protective factors against cardiac injuries in the female group.

The increased risk of death in COVID-19 and increase severity of the disease is related with preexisting comorbidities, such as hypertension, obesity, diabetes, chronic lung diseases and CVD.[25] Also, age has been associated with greater risk for poor clinical events.[25] Elderly patients with CVD are associated with fatality rate of 10.5%.[26] Older females compared to age-matched males have more CVD, and mortality rate in COVID-19 is less in female, it may be due to an enhanced reparative response following cardiovascular injury.[25] Even though the number of female patients in our study was higher, clinical data’s shows no significant gender difference in susceptibility to COVID-19 infection.[26] We found that in-hospital survivor rate was longer in the female group. Although women had more comorbidities and higher troponin values, clinical events (except respiratory failure, that was more common in men) and in-hospital mortality rate were similar in both groups. In a study where comorbidities were not different, male mortality and disease severity rates were higher than females.[6] Additionally, females have enhanced immune responses.[27] It is known that several immune cells contain estrogen receptors.[27] This can be another reason why females experienced lower severity and fatality rates in COVID-19 disease. Whereas extracellular signal-regulated kinase expression is induced by testosterone. SARS-CoV-2 replication involves signal-regulated kinase, which may promote increased viral progeny in male.[28]

Another reason for higher mortality rate in men were found to be high creatinine levels.[29] High creatinine levels were found in patients with severe COVID-19 disease with multi-organ failure.[29] In patients with comorbidities, the development of acute kidney injury (AKI) is positively correlated with mortality.[30] AKI causes the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Cytokine storm in COVID-19 patients may aggravate this process.[29] In our study, although comorbidity rates, urea and creatinine levels were higher in female group, mortality and AKI rates were similar in both groups. If comorbidity rates and creatinine levels were higher in the male group, probably mortality rates would be certainly higher. Additionally, estrogen reduced ACE2 activity in the kidney,[31] which probably explains why kidney failure is less common in women.

We found that cough, fever, and high CRP values were more common in the male group. Likewise, respiratory failure was more frequent in the male group. SARS-CoV-2 enters the body through ACE2.[1] Differences in the expression of ACE2 caused by sex hormones may help to explain differences in severity and mortality rate cause by gender. When SARS-CoV-2 binds to ACE2, the expression of ACE2 is downregulated, this causes worsening respiratory infection.[32] Estradiol raised ACE2 expression.[33] Higher levels of ACE2 expressions are more protective against acute lung injury & severe lung failure in females.[34] Another entry point of COVID-19 into the cell is the TMPRSS2. Androgen receptors play a significant role in the transcription of TMPRSS2.[32] Furthermore, SARS-CoV-2 infect Type II pneumocytes, and the presence of androgens and androgen receptors in these cells is correlated with TMPRSS2 expression.[35] This may be another reason why males tend to experience more severe outcomes of COVID-19 infection.

In our study, the existence of fQRS, that shows myocardial scar tissue,[11] was also similar in both groups. The existence of fQRS predicts the development of cardiac injury and in-hospital mortality in both groups. The existence of fQRS in COVID-19 is related to severe disease and mortality.[36] It was found that development of new fQRS after 6 months was predictor for post-COVID-19 tachycardia syndrome.[37]

The importance of vaccination and self-isolation is necessary to better reduce the spread of the COVID-19 disease and avoid repeating the mistakes made in the past.[38,39]

4.1. Limitations of our study

The limitations of our study can be counted as this is a retrospective and single-center study, lack of daily troponin follow-up. This is a gender study, but the influence of other factors such as age, race/ethnicity, body mass index, education, marital status, poverty status, smoking and drinking status, physical activity, and chronic disease conditions are also important.[40–42] Larger epidemiological and molecular studies are needed to determine the importance of gender and troponin values, and impact of these factors in COVID-19 disease.

5. Conclusions

We observed that in COVID-19 disease, troponin value differs by gender. A lesser increase in troponin levels in men was indicative of cardiac injury. However, in women, troponin value should rise more than three times to indicate cardiac injury. Even slight increases in troponin levels in men, should alert clinicians for cardiac injury and other clinical events.

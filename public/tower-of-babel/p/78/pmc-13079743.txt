Meta learning based few shot knowledge graph completion with domain selected aggregation
Authors: Bin Yang, Mengxiang Peng, Shuai Liu, Mengqi Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid development of artificial intelligence, knowledge graphs have become increasingly important for many downstream reasoning tasks. Although large-scale knowledge graphs have been constructed in many domains, many relations still suffer from data sparsity, making few-shot knowledge graph completion a practical and challenging problem. Existing methods typically enhance entity embeddings by aggregating one-hop neighbors. However, these neighbors often contain many irrelevant entities to the target relation, which introduces a large amount of noise. And existing methods are not sensitive to the semantic differences and task characteristics in the reference set, which leads to a lack of deep semantic meanings in relation representations and weakens relation inference. To address these challenges, we propose meta learning based few shot knowledge graph completion with domain selected aggregation. Specifically, the method introduces a domain-selected neighborhood aggregation mechanism that dynamically filters irrelevant neighbors through a selection strategy and a gating module, effectively suppressing noise propagation under sparse data conditions. Moreover, a relation meta-learner is designed by integrating contextual attention with a multi-layer perceptron to capture deep semantic correlations among reference triples and generate more expressive task-aware relational representations. An embedding learner further utilizes a meta-optimization strategy to enable rapid adaptation to new tasks. Experiments on NELL-One and Wiki-One datasets demonstrate that the method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines. Specifically, in 5-shot tasks, it achieves performance improvements of and on the Hits@10 metric for datasets, respectively.Keywords: Few-shot knowledge graph completion, Selection mechanism, Meta-optimization strategy, Domain-selected aggregationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

A Knowledge Graph (KG) represents and connects real-world knowledge using a graph-based structure1. It represents entities and relations through triples (head entity, relation, tail entity). The KG has been extensively applied in search engines2, question answering3, personalized recommendations4, and so on. KGs in the real world often contain incomplete or unknown facts, particularly under sparse data conditions. Numerous knowledge graph embedding (KGE) methods have been proposed. By projecting entities and relations into a continuous low-dimensional space, these methods aim to maintain their semantic integrity while reducing dimensionality. Existing methods, including TransE5, TransH6, ComplEx7, and SimPle8, typically assume that relations are sufficiently represented by abundant training triplets. However, there are rare relations or obscure entities in practical applications, resulting in extremely limited training data.

Both self-supervised learning and few-shot learning aim to alleviate performance degradation caused by data scarcity. Recent studies have demonstrated that such learning paradigms exhibit strong scalability across diverse tasks beyond knowledge graphs, highlighting their general applicability9. Motivated by this observation, we investigate few-shot knowledge graph completion under limited supervision. To complete the knowledge graph under data scarcity constraints, many Few-Shot Knowledge Graph Completion(FKGC)10 methods have been developed, including GMatching11, FSRL12, MetaR13, and GANA14. Recently, with the operational adoption of Graph Neural Networks15 (GNNs), traditional Graph Convolutional Networks(GCNs)16enhance representation learning by aggregating local neighborhood information of entities. However, GCN assumes by default that all neighbors contribute equally to the target entity, which neglects the semantic heterogeneity of neighborhood nodes in practical KG environments. To address this limitation, we improve upon traditional GCNs by introducing an attention mechanism that distinguishes the semantic relevance of different neighbors. For example, in Knowledge Graph Completion (KGC) tasks, when predicting the target triplet (Lei Jun, spouse,?) supporting triplets exhibit significant semantic relevance divergence. The reference instance (Lei Jun, business_partner, Ding Lei) demonstrates professional relationships, while (Zhang Tong, husband, Lei Jun) reflects familial connections. The method leverages graph attention mechanisms to automatically assign higher attention weights to edges with stronger semantic proximity. As a result, familial relationships receive greater emphasis than professional associations during neighborhood aggregation17.

Although neighbor information is critical, the one-hop neighbors around an entity often contain many entities that are irrelevant to the target relation. Existing aggregation methods lack the dynamic selection capability for neighbors, introducing noise into feature representations and interfering with relation inference. To tackle the semantic heterogeneity in neighborhood nodes and eliminate noise interference, we propose a neighborhood selection-based aggregator. This aggregator combines graph neural networks with attention to selectively weight important neighbors. A learnable neighbor selection mechanism is introduced to retain the top-F highest-weight neighbors, eliminating interference from low-correlation nodes. Further, a Gated Recurrent Unit18(GRU) is adopted to dynamically adjust the fusion ratio between the target entity and its neighbors based on the current task, and adaptively balances local and global information interactions enhances entity feature representations. In query sets, relations are not just connections between entities, but also contain important information such as connection properties, types, and directions19. Existing methods are not sensitive to the semantic differences and task characteristics of the triples in the reference set, which leads to a lack of deep semantic relationships in relation representations. Previous literature has used Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)20 methods to force input sequences, which neglects the pairwise interaction effects between each entity pair. Furthermore, in prediction, some relations are unidirectional, and bidirectional information may introduce irrelevant information or noise. To mitigate these limitations, we propose a relation meta-learner. The learner integrates a contextual attention mechanism with a multi-layer perceptron, effectively modeling entity interaction patterns, semantic differences, and task characteristics. It assigns differentiated weights to reference entity pairs, thereby extracting more expressive relational meta-representations from the reference set.

Related work

Traditional knowledge graph completion

In the TransE5 framework, relations are modeled as vector translations from head entities to tail entities, each triple is modeled as a vector equation. TransE assumes that the relation between two entities can be represented as a translation vector, minimizing the distance between the sum of the head and relation vectors and the tail vector. Building on TransE, TransH6 improves the embedding method by allowing each entity to be embedded into different relation hyperplanes, providing different entity representations for different relations. TransR21 further develops the TransH approach by mapping entities and relations into different spaces, which more effectively captures their semantic information. Building upon TransR, TransD22 introduces dynamic mapping matrices to adaptively adjust the mapping of entity embeddings for each relation, thereby enhancing the method’s expressive power. RESCAL23 views the knowledge graph as a three-dimensional tensor, using vectors to represent entities and matrices to represent relations. RESCAL employs a custom scoring function to model interactions between entities and relations. DistMult24 employs a bilinear model with diagonal relation matrices to learn entity and relation embeddings. To better capture complex relations, ComplEx introduces a complex-valued embedding method, utilizing the conjugate relationship of complex numbers to represent the bidirectionality of relations. ConvE25 uses convolutional neural networks (CNNs)26 to process KG. However, these traditional KGC methods typically depend on numerous facts for training and perform poorly when handling few-shot relations.

Few-shot knowledge graph completion

Currently, existing FKGC methods can be divided into two categories, both of which aim to infer missing knowledge using the limited known information.

Metric learning27 based methods: These methods calculate the similarity between entity pairs. As one of the earliest methods to address the few-shot completion problem, GMatching11 employs neighborhood encoding for structural representation of entity connections. By weightedly encoding the entities and relations of one-hop neighbors, GMatching uses LSTM networks to compute matching scores, evaluating the similarity between different entity pairs. FSRL12 extends GMatching’s single-sample learning to few-shot scenarios, incorporating an attention module to dynamically weight heterogeneous neighbor contributions during encoding. Nevertheless, these weights remain the same across different relationship types. FAAN28 introduces an adaptive attention framework that dynamically learns entity and reference triple representations. Nevertheless, FAAN’s design confines neighbor aggregation to one-hop connections, failing to capture informative features and contextual semantics from distant entities. GL-GFKGC29 introduces a global-local encoding framework with Gaussian embeddings for FKGC. It represents task relations via Gaussian mixture models, enabling probabilistic embeddings to explicitly model relational uncertainty. TransAM30 employs the Transformer as a matching network to capture both intra-entity and cross-entity interactions.IRAN31 strengthens the relational representations between query and support sets by exploiting the neighborhood relational similarity of entity pairs within triples. Furthermore, APINet32 captures the latent semantics of entity pairs through an interaction attention mechanism and dynamically models multi-semantic relations with an adaptive prototype generation mechanism.

To address these issues and further enhance the efficacy of FKGC, we propose a Neighborhood Selection-Based Relational Learning Method(MLGD). This approach aims to mitigate the impact of noisy neighborhoods and learn more discriminative representations of task-specific entity pairs.

Methodology

Overall framework

The meta learning based few shot knowledge graph completion with domain selected aggregation (MLGD) method consists of three components: the neighborhood aggregator, relation meta-learner, and embedding learner. Figure 1 illustrates the overall framework of MLGD.

Overall Framework Diagram of the Method. Legend: Pink circles denote head entities; green circles denote tail entities. Blue circles represent relations. Orange circles indicate entity embeddings before aggregation. Yellow circles indicate aggregated entity embeddings. Cyan circles denote general relation meta-representations. Purple circles represent projection vectors on relation-specific hyperplanes. Red circles denote updated entity embeddings. Gray circles indicate gating values.

MLGD first processes the reference set of relation-r triplets through a neighborhood aggregator for graph-structured semantic enhancement, generating entity embeddings. Then, the relational meta-learner integrates all representations to obtain meta representation for low-frequency relations. Next, the embedding learner updates both the relation meta-representations and local parameters by computing the reference set loss. Finally, the optimized meta-representations are used to compute the query set loss, updating the global parameters, which enables rapid model adaptation to novel tasks through meta-optimization strategies.

Following the most studies, this paper selects relationships containing 50 to 500 linked triples as task relations, while the other relations and their triples form the background KG. Performance evaluation against baseline methods utilizes MRR and Hits@n as metrics in this work. MRR represents the average reciprocal rank of the correct entity and Hits@n represents the percentage of correct entities ranked in the top n positions. To compare with the baseline methods, we set n to 1, 5, and 10.

Baseline setup

To confirm the method’s effectiveness, the proposed method is contrasted with two baselines. The first category includes traditional KGC methods: TransE5, DistMult23 and ComplEx7, with experimental results reproduced using the experimental setup provided in the respective papers. The second category includes the FKGC methods mainly include: GMatching11, MetaR13, FSRL12, FAAN27, GANA14 and GL-GFKGC28, with experimental results reproduced using the experimental setup provided in the respective papers. Specifically, our replication results for GANA fall short of the performance reported in the original paper. So we adopt the original authors’ results as the benchmark.

Parameter settings

The method is implemented based on the PyTorch framework. Using two datasets pre-trained with TransE, the embedding dimensions of NELL-One and Wiki-One are set to 100/50, respectively. For the two datasets, in the neighborhood aggregator module, the maximum number of neighbors for target entity are set to 100/50, and in the relation meta-learner module, the dropout rate are set to 0.5/0.5, drop_path are set to 0/0.5 and top_f is set to 5/1. For the embedding learner module, the learning rate is set to 0.0005/0.0005.The method uses batch gradient descent to train both datasets, with a batch size of 1024. In both datasets, model training employs Adam as its optimizer, and the validation set is evaluated every 1000/500 steps. Monitoring the MRR on the validation set to determine optimal stopping iteration.

Impact of top_F

In the experiment, MLGD uses top_f as the selection mechanism. To assess the effect of top_f, a 5-shot sensitivity analysis was performed on the NELL-One dataset. We set the value of top_f to 3, 4, 5, and 6 respectively. As shown in Figure 2, when top_f = 5, MLGD shows the best performance in all settings; While the value of top_f changes, it induces commensurate performance reduction in MLGD. The primary cause originates from the neighbor aggregation stage, when the value of top_f is too small, the number of neighbors is insufficient, resulting in the loss of key information; when the value of top_f is too large, it introduces noise, diluting the contribution of important neighbor.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a meta learning based few shot knowledge graph completion with domain selected aggregation. The method uses a neighborhood aggregator, effectively capturing dependencies between entities, transmitting information between entities, and integrating features from neighboring entities while selecting the most important neighbor information for the target entity to update its current representation. The relation meta-learner is used to use the ideas of the unidirectional GRU and MLP,integrating a contextual attention mechanism with a multi-layer perceptron, effectively modeling entity interaction patterns, semantic differences, and task characteristics. It assigns differentiated weights to reference entity pairs, thereby extracting more expressive relational meta-representations from the reference set. On the FKGC task, our method achieves better performance than state-of-the-art baseline methods. In future research, we will incorporate multi-hop neighborhood information and large model-based relation representation to further enhance the completion task performance.

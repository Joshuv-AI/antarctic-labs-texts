Named entity recognition for coal mining machine assembly based on domain large language model
Authors: Yunrui Wang, Xintong Sui, Zhaoyang Zheng, Man yv, Juan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe assembly process of a coal mining machine generates complex unstructured textual data, which makes it difficult for workers to quickly understand and reason with during the assembly stage, thereby reducing operational efficiency. Traditional BERT-based Named Entity Recognition methods exhibit poor performance when faced with scarce and highly specialized datasets. To address this issue, this paper proposes a domain-specific large language model-based method for Named Entity Recognition. Firstly, foundational large language models are comparatively analyzed to select the one that best adapts to the coal mining machine domain data, enabling effective handling of the complex assembly data. Secondly, by applying the QLoRA fine-tuning method, the training parameters of the large language model are adjusted, which reduces the computational resource demands for building a domain-specific model while maintaining performance. Finally, the model is fine-tuned and evaluated on a real-world coal mining machine assembly dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that the QLoRA fine-tuning leads to a significant performance improvement: the BLEU-4 score increases from 6.1225 to 65.8013, and the F1-Score for the NER task reaches 0.893. The proposed method improves the accuracy for the NER task in coal mining machine assembly and is of significant importance for assisting workers in understanding assembly information.Keywords: Coal mining machine assembly, Named entity recognition, Large language models, QLoRASubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As the core equipment of the fully mechanized mining face, the coal mining machine is primarily employed to perform critical tasks such as coal cutting, caving, and loading, and is a key piece of equipment for achieving efficient coal mining. Due to the high complexity of the coal mining machine assembly process, workers struggle to quickly comprehend the unstructured assembly information. Named Entity Recognition (NER), as a core technology for knowledge structuring1, can structurally integrate and correlate various types of data generated during the coal mining machine assembly process, which effectively assists workers in understanding assembly information and provides comprehensive, systematic knowledge support for assembly tasks. Therefore, the NER method holds significant research value in the field of coal mining machine assembly.

NER methods can extract fragmented knowledge, providing structured data support for downstream tasks such as information retrieval, question answering, machine translation, and knowledge graph construction2. In the field of coal mining machine assembly, NER methods can rapidly identify core elements within complex assembly processes by accurately extracting key information such as component names, assembly operations, and assembly tools. This enables the inference and extraction of assembly knowledge. Commonly used NER methods include rule-based recognition methods, statistical machine learning methods, and deep learning-based methods3–5. Liu et al.6 systematically reviewed the definitions, annotation methods, evaluation metrics, and challenges of NER in electronic health records using rule-based approaches.In the field of deep learning, its methods in recognition and classification have become relatively mature7,8. Among them, domain-adaptive pre-training methods significantly improve model performance on tasks in a specific domain by continuing training on unlabeled data from that domain based on a general pre-trained model.domain-adaptive pretraining significantly improves the performance of models on tasks in a specific domain by continuing training on unlabelled data from that domain based on a general pretrained model9.Mao et al.10 employed BERT as the model’s embedding layer, demonstrating that moderately reducing the number of BERT embedding layers could enhance the model’s NER accuracy. However, traditional models face significant challenges in domain transfer and suffer from insufficient generalization capabilities when dealing with highly specialized coal mining machine assembly data and limited annotated datasets11, traditional models face severe challenges in domain transfer and insufficient generalization capabilities.Large Language Models (LLM) offer an effective approach to address these issues.

As a natural language processing model based on deep learning technology, LLM possesses strong transfer learning capabilities due to the vast amount of general knowledge acquired during its pre-training process, which effectively addresses issues such as poor transfer learning performance and low recognition accuracy in traditional models12,13. Currently, LLM is widely applied across various fields14–16. L et al.17 applied LLM to NER tasks in German clinical texts, demonstrating its potential in NER applications. Zhao18 enhanced model performance for Chinese NER by integrating LLM with prompt engineering and contrastive learning mechanisms, effectively mitigating noise interference and representation drift issues. M19 combined prompting frameworks with LLM for medical NER, enhancing recognition performance through prompt engineering. Hao et al.20 proposed a chain-of-thought prompting method to improve LLM NER capabilities in secondary medical literature. Overall, LLM offer advantages such as broad knowledge coverage, strong versatility, and good task adaptability. However, NER in coal mining machine assembly requires high accuracy, and domain-specific LLM demand substantial computational resources and datasets during construction. Existing approaches struggle to perform effectively under limited computational power and sparse data conditions. Efficient fine-tuning methods provide a viable solution to these challenges.

Efficient fine-tuning of LLM can reduce computational demands by sacrificing training time through parameter modification, while maintaining domain-specific LLM performance under constrained computational resources. Currently, QLoRA and LoRA represent the two mainstream fine-tuning methods, applied across various tasks. LoRA is frequently applied in scenarios with relatively relaxed model parameter requirements, such as natural language processing and image recognition. By employing low-rank decomposition techniques, it trains only a small number of matrices, significantly reducing training parameters and computational load, enabling LLM fine-tuning on standard GPUs21–23. Wang et al.24 enhanced LLM information extraction capabilities on case text datasets through instruction fine-tuning and LoRA fine-tuning techniques. Tian et al.25 employed instruction tuning and LoRA fine-tuning to achieve superior performance in judicial NER tasks under few-shot conditions. WEI et al.26 introduced a LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning method to enhance accuracy in telecom knowledge question-answering and communication protocol parsing, addressing issues like poor generalization caused by traditional methods’ reliance on human expertise. Overall, LoRA finds extensive application across complex tasks, offering an effective pathway to address key challenges in deploying domain-specific LLM. However, while LoRA reduces fine-tuning costs, its lack of quantization techniques may still lead to memory constraints in environments with extremely limited computational resources.

QLoRA is applied in scenarios with even more stringent resource constraints. Building upon LoRA, it incorporates quantization techniques to reduce the volume of parameter updates and further decrease data requirements27,28. Liu et al.29 utilized QLoRA fine-tuning on the Qwen model to enhance the efficiency and success rate of LLM question-answering. Wei and Fan30 further fine-tuned the ChatGLM2-6B model with QLoRA to build an agricultural policy question-answering system. Li et al.31 proposed a method combining QLoRA with dynamic prompt templates to address information lag issues in foundational LLMs for news processing. Overall, QLoRA leverages its dual advantages of integrating quantization techniques with low-rank adaptation to further reduce computational resource and data requirements while maintaining LLM performance. Considering the specific characteristics of coal mining environments and the coal mining machine assembly domain, QLoRA is well-suited for constructing domain-specific LLM for coal mining machine assembly under computational constraints. However, the application of LLM combined with QLoRA fine-tuning in coal mining machine assembly remains underdeveloped. There is an urgent need for novel solutions to enhance NER accuracy in this domain.

This paper applies NER methods to the coal mining machine assembly process, systematizing assembly knowledge to assist workers in understanding assembly information. By employing efficient fine-tuning techniques for LLM, the NER task is enhanced, improving its accuracy in the coal mining machine assembly domain. First, the structure of coal mining machines is analyzed, and ontologies and domain-specific datasets are constructed. Next, through comparative analysis of foundation LLM, the foundation LLM demonstrating the best performance in the coal mining machine assembly domain is selected. By comparing fine-tuning strategies, the most effective fine-tuning method for the foundation LLM architecture is chosen. Finally, we designed the LLM construction workflow and NER process for coal mining machine assembly, developed prompt templates, and generated the domain-specific LLM. This LLM was applied to perform NER on coal mining machine structures and assembly tools, constructing a knowledge graph for coal mining machine assembly. Coal mining machine assembly dataset construction

To ensure data accuracy and rigor, this paper obtained raw data—including assembly process documents and operation manuals—through on-site enterprise research. Based on this raw data, Python’s RE library was used for error correction and data cleansing to guarantee the quality of subsequent training tasks. The coal mining machine assembly domain dataset was annotated based on the constructed ontology hyhierarchies. The dataset format adopts the Sharegpt format based on question-answering logic. The specific construction process of the dataset is shown in Fig. 3.

LLM Fine-tuning dataset production process.

The dataset contains a total of 6,103 samples, with 18,724 labeled entities. The entity types strictly correspond to the 8 ontology levels constructed in this paper, with no further subtype divisions. During the annotation process, labeling strictly followed the definitions of the 8 entity types, meeting the annotation quality requirements for industrial-level applications. In addition, a stratified random sampling method was used to divide the dataset into a training set (4,882 samples, 80%), a validation set (610 samples, 10%), and a test set (611 samples, 10%) with a fixed random seed of 42. (2) Evaluation Metrics.

In the process of constructing LLM for coal mining machine assembly, evaluating the NER capability of fine-tuned LLMs is a core step in verifying the effectiveness of fine-tuning. This step urgently requires a scientific and objective evaluation metric system as support. Although initially applied to machine translation, its ability to accurately capture the literal overlap between generated text and reference text has led to its widespread adoption in LLM result assessment. Its core logic is that the higher the level match between LLM generated results and human-annotated standard answers, the more accurate the LLM’s output and the fewer hallucinations it produces. Comparative analysis of LoRA and QLoRA fine-tuning methods under efficient fine-tuning

Fine-tuning techniques currently mainly include full-parameter fine-tuning and parametric-efficient fine-tuning. The full-parameter fine-tuning training process requires sufficient labelled data to ensure the stability of parameter convergence, as well as high-performance computing resources to ensure the normal operation of the training process. Efficient fine-tuning can be adapted to the NER task in the field of coal mining machine assembly without significantly modifying the parameters of the pre-trained model. The current efficient fine-tuning methods based on reparameterisation mainly include LoRA and QLoRA. Under different scenarios and dataset sizes, the performance of the two fine-tuning methods still has effect differences. Therefore, based on the architectural advantages of the Qwen-Chat-7B model, this subsection further compares and analyses the two methods of QLoRA and LoRA in efficient fine-tuning.

LoRA Fine-Tuning and QLoRA Fine-Tuning Algorithm Architecture.

LoRA Fine-Tuning and QLoRA Fine-Tuning Algorithm Architecture.

LoRA is a technique for efficiently fine-tuning pre-trained language models, which addresses the problem of high resource consumption and overfitting in full parameter fine-tuning by innovatively inserting low-rank matrices into the pre-trained model to achieve effective tuning with a small number of covariates and computational cost, and to reduce the difficulty of fine-tuning and the demand for resources. Since QLoRA does not modify the logic of LoRA, but further saves computational overheads by quantising the pre-trained model into 4-bits, both QLoRA fine-tuning and LoRA methods are based on the same core mechanism, i.e., adding additional low-rank matrices to the model-specified parameters in parallel, and only training the parameters of the additional parallel low-rank matrices, and the algorithmic architecture of the algorithm is shown in Fig. 5. Coal mining machine assembly domain LLM construction and NER

Domain LLM construction

Leveraging the coal mining machine assembly domain dataset generated in Sect. 2.3, we employed a LLM based on the Qwen-Chat-7B architecture from the Llama-factory platform as the base model. We adapted the model and configured training parameters, then trained it using a combination of Qwen-Chat-7B and QLoRA. After LLM training, we proceeded to the evaluation phase to assess the LLM’s understanding and generation capabilities regarding domain knowledge. Based on evaluation results, the LLM undergoes optimization adjustments. If performance falls short of expectations, training parameters are re-adjusted and training is repeated. Finally, Chat testing and LLM exportation are completed, yielding a domain-specific LLM tailored for coal mining machine assembly. The construction process is illustrated in Fig. 7.

(2)NER Task.

NER Task.

Based on the deployed domain LLM, text files requiring NER are imported into the Ollama platform. Combined with prompts, these files undergo Chat Q&A processing to extract entities and their respective types from the text. Expert judgment determines relationships between entities. Recognition results are stored in JSON format as triplet data containing entity text, entity type, and entity relationship information, such as “Coal Cutter (Equipment Layer) - contains - Cutting Unit (Mechanism Layer)”.

(3)Knowledge Graph Construction.

Knowledge Graph Construction.

To enhance readability and reasoning, JSON data undergoes visualization to simplify understanding of coal miner assembly knowledge. Recognition results in JSON format are linked to the Neo4j graph database. Using the Cypher language, a coal miner structural knowledge graph is constructed with clear hierarchical structure and explicit semantic relationships. The entire graph adopts a semantic network structure of triples “entity-relationship-entity,” enabling top-down representation from the equipment layer to the mechanism layer and down to individual components. This ultimately generates a comprehensive coal mining machine assembly knowledge graph, achieving visual presentation of assembly knowledge.

Conclusion

This paper proposes an LLM-based NER method for coal mining machine assembly. By employing QLoRA fine-tuning technology, we constructed an LLM tailored for the coal mining machine assembly domain. Through prompt template design and comparative analysis across multiple model NER tasks, we validated the effectiveness of the constructed LLM for coal mining machine assembly NER. Key conclusions are as follows:

(1) Through comprehensive analysis of current mainstream base LLMs and laboratory resources, a domain-adapted LLM was identified. Comparative experimental results indicate that the selected Qwen-Chat-7B base LLM outperforms the other evaluated models in coal mining machine assembly NER tasks.

(2) By comparing various fine-tuning techniques based on the Qwen-Chat-7B model architecture, we identified domain-adapted fine-tuning methods. Experimental comparisons revealed that the selected QLoRA fine-tuning method achieved higher evaluation scores than LoRA: on a 6k dataset, it attained a BLEU-4 score of 65.8013%, representing a 15.47% improvement over the LoRA fine-tuning method.

(3) By constructing a coal mining machine assembly domain dataset and leveraging the advantages of the LLM architecture and fine-tuning method, a domain-specific LLM was built to perform NER in the coal mining machine assembly domain. Experimental results demonstrate that this domain LLM achieved an F1-score of 0.893.

Despite these findings, this study has several limitations. First, the dataset is limited in size and scope, covering only certain types of coal mining machines and assembly scenarios, which may restrict generalization to other machines or industrial settings. Second, only a subset of open-source LLMs and parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods were evaluated; newer models and alternative strategies might yield different results. Finally, experiments were conducted offline, and the practical performance of the model in real industrial assembly environments remains to be validated.

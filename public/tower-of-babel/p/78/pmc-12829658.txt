Comparative evaluation of the efficacy of intradermal platelet-rich plasma with and without vitamin C using dermaroller for postacne atrophic scars - A clinical study
Authors: Ratna Deepika Seshagiri, Lakshmi Shetty, Tanaya Surpur, Vishal Kulkarni, Akhilesh Kumar Singh, Uday Londhe
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Context:Skin is the largest organ in the body. Ideal skin gives a youthful appearance and has an important role in an individual’s perception toward life. Acne scars can affect one’s confidence and self-esteem. Our clinical study focuses on the reduction of these scars to improve the appearance with additional adjuncts on the skin. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of intradermal platelet-rich plasma (PRP) with and without vitamin C serum (VCS) using dermaroller (DR) for postacne atrophic scars.Aim:1. To evaluate the efficacy of intradermal PRP without VCS for postacne atrophic scars. 2. To evaluate the efficacy of IPRP with VCS for postacne atrophic scars. 3. To compare the efficacy of IPRP with and without VCS for postacne atrophic scars.Materials and Methods:An in vivo split-face clinical study was conducted on 15 patients divided into two groups, that is, 1(IPRP without VCS) and 2 (IPRP with VCS) over a period of 18 months with a chief complaint of bilateral presentation of postatrophic acne scars attending the Department of Dermatology and the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery. Convenience sampling technique was used and intraexaminer reliability was done using kappa statistics and single blinding for the observer who was assessing. The study was conducted after approval from institution ethics committee board.Statistical Analysis:There was a statistically nonsignificant difference [P > .05] for GIS and PSS at 7th POD between both groups A and B. There was a high statistical difference noted between both the groups A and B [P < .01] for GIS and PSS during the 20th POD, with higher mean score for group B when compared to group A.Results:There was a statistically highly significant difference seen for the frequencies of subjects with various scores between both groups (P < .01) with higher no of subjects having lower scores in group 1 while higher no of subjects having higher scores in group 2. There was a statistically significant difference seen for the frequencies of subjects with various scores between both groups (P < .05) with higher no of subjects having lower scores in group 1 while higher no of subjects having higher scores in group 2. Global Improvement Score was compared between 7 days vs 20 days (P < .05), with a higher mean score for 20 days when compared to that for 7 days. Patient Satisfaction Score was compared between 7 days vs 20 days (P < .01), with a higher mean score for 20 days when compared to that for 7 days. Global Improvement Score was compared between 7 days vs 20 days (P < .01), with a higher mean score for 20 days when compared to 7 days.Conclusion:The use of DR followed by intradermal PRP with topical VCS application is an excellent combination therapy for the treatment of postacne atrophic scars. These results of combination therapy have shown excellent positive indications in treating postacne atrophic scars and would open potential opportunities for future research.

INTRODUCTION

Healthy skin reflects overall wellness and being the largest organ of the body mandates great attention and care Skin is predominantly divided into two distinct layers-outer layer or the epidermis and the inner layer or dermis. The epidermis is cellular and acts as a barricade, whereas the dermis gives strength, and elasticity and provides nutrition to the epidermis.[1] Healthy skin gives a youthful appearance has an important role in an individual’s perception toward life.[2]

Acne is an inflammatory disease of pilosebaceous units.[3] Acne is commonly reported in about 80% of the young and adult population.[4] Acne is commonly seen on the face, back, and chest.[34] Acne may occur due to excessive production of sebum or a change in the activity of androgens. It can also be caused by follicular hyperkeratinization, variation in the nature of sebum lipids, and an increase in the number of Propionibacterium acnes in the hair follicle.[35] Acne scars can be divided according to their depth as atrophic, hypertrophic, and keloidal.[4] These atrophic acne scars have subtypes, namely, Ice pick, Rolling, and Boxcar.

These atrophic scars may traumatize an individual physically and emotionally, impacting patient’s quality of life.[6] During the matrix remodelling stage of acne healing, enzymes produced by fibroblasts and keratinocytes form extracellular matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and its tissue inhibitors. MMPs are enzymes that form a lytic cascade for ECM remodelling. There is an imbalance in the ratio of MMPs to tissue inhibitors, which leads to the formation of atrophic or hypertrophic scars.[5]

Minimally invasive facial skin rejuvenation is an emerging area of interest in aesthetic oral and maxillofacial surgery.[7] Facial aesthetic surgery focusses on improving patient facial appearance. Common procedures included in facial aesthetic surgery are rhinoplasty, blepharoplasty, rhytidectomy, browlift, and fat transfer. The nonsurgical procedures include various techniques such as chemical peels, lasers, various injectable substances, and microneedling that improve facial aesthetics.[8] Among different treatment modalities, microneedling using DR can be considered a treatment option for atrophic scars. The microneedling therapy is also known as induction therapy or DR therapy.[9] Microneedling with DR is a relatively simple cost-effective method used for the treatment of acne scarring.[10]

Various studies have tried using materials such as IPRP, vitamin E, and vitamin C to aid healing and thereby help in the better appearance of postatrophic acne scarring. It is an effective concentration of multiple fundamental growth factors (GFs) by platelets alone; in simple terms, it means it has many platelets that are concentrated into a small volume of plasma. The various growth factors present in IPRP are responsible for the chemotactic activity of fibroblasts and macrophages and act as mitogenic for smooth muscles.

VCS is known for the stimulation and production of Type I and III collagen and has very good antioxidant properties.[11] A comparative study on IPRP on one side and VCS on the other side in treating postacne atrophic scars in 30 patients out of which 23 achieved reduction in scarring by 1 to 2 grades. Other than collagen formation, VCS helps to scavenge free radicals, dispose of toxic oxidants, and inhibit melanogenesis. The application of VCS to the skin markedly helps in wound healing and reduces scar formation.[12] A study was conducted to evaluate the use of microdermabrasion in patients with acne. Twenty-five patients with grade II–III acne were enrolled. They concluded that the use of microdermabrasion appeared to produce a positive effect on the improvement of acne.[13]

Methodology

The patients fulfilling the above criteria were selected for the study. This clinical study design was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee with Ref. No: DPU/R and R(D)/971(19)/16 dated 28/12/2016. This study was carried out in accordance with the ethical standards of the committee and with the Helsinki Declaration of 1975, as revised in 2000. All the patients were informed in detail about the procedure and consents were taken. Routine preoperative investigations were done, such as complete hemogram, blood sugar level (fasting and postprandial), prothrombin time-international normalized ratio (PT-INR), bleeding time, and clotting time. The patients were asked to wash his/her face with soap and water. Using the SNOSE randomization technique, a site on which the dermaroller is to be performed will be selected. An application of a thick paste of EMLA cream was done 30-35 min before the procedure [Figure 2].

Application of EMLA cream

Microneedling was done with a dermaroller on both sides of the face on the scar region till fresh bleeding points appeared [Figure 3]. On both sides, 4 ml of freshly prepared PRP was injected intradermally with the help of an insulin syringe [Figure 4].

Microneedling done by dermaroller

Intradermal PRP injection

PRP was first described by hematologists in 1970s. A decade later, it gained popularity in oral and maxillofacial surgery as platelet rich fibrin (PRF). Preparation of PRP (for PRP mesotherapy): A two-stage centrifuging process was employed in the preparation of PRP. Whole blood sample [10 ml] was withdrawn from the participant’s medial cubital vein in a syringe and transferred to a vacutainer bulb. The blood sample was then centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 3 min. After the first spin, the lower red blood cell portion was discarded, and the supernatant that contained platelet-poor plasma and buffy coat was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 5 min. This process produced approximately 4 ml of PRP. Topical application of VIT C serum was done on the site selected by the SNOSE randomization technique [Figure 5]. The patient has been prescribed analgesics and antibiotics postoperatively. Patient was recalled after 24 h for the evaluation of pain and edema and after a week for assessment of grades of scars. Patient after the first follow up was recalled after 20 days [Figure 6]. The patients were recalled after 24 h for evaluation of pain and edema using VAS. Assessment of grades of scars using GIS and PSS was done during the 7th POD. The patients after the 7th POD were recalled on 20th POD for the assessment of improvement in grades of scars using GIS and PSS.

DISCUSSION

Skin is the largest organ in the body. The functions of facial skin are to protect and serve. It envelopes the outer surface of the body and protects body integrity, inhibits protein and fluid loss, repairs injuries, and regulates body temperature.[12] The skin has two layers: the upper epidermis and then the second layer is the dermis. The epidermis plays a major role of being as a barrier to skin and is widely made up of cells, mostly keratinocytes. The main or fundamental role of the stratum corneum layer is to fulfill the barrier function of the epidermis. The second layer of the skin, the dermis, provides strength and elasticity to the skin. The dermal layer is acellular and is mostly made up of complex extracellular matrix proteins. Besides all these functions, facial skin reflects the identity and mood of an individual person. Our face announces to the world who we are and how we are feeling.[12] There are many skin conditions affecting the aesthetics of the face, such as acne vulgaris, postacne scars, varicella, postherpetic scars, post-traumatic scars, due to abrasions, lacerations, or deep wounds, aging, and photodamage of skin. A study was carried out in which 14 patients underwent microdermabrasion treatments over 12–14 weeks in which authors concluded that microdermabrasion improves certain aspects of photoaging and selected cases of acne scarring.[14]

Kadunc B and Trindade de Almeida R (2003)[15] conducted a prospective study in 228 consecutive patients with mild to severe facial acne scars to propose a morphologic acne scar classification and to evaluate the efficacy of different therapeutic options based on acne scar types. Acne scars were classified as (1) elevated, (2) dystrophic, and (3) depressed. Description of acne scars and specific treatments were reported. Out of 228 patients, 168 (74%) completed the study. Forty patients (24%) were classified as phototype II, 58 (34%) as group III, and 70 (42%) as groups IV, V, and VI. The results analyzed by the overall impressions of patients, the authors and the dermatologic surgeons. They concluded that the classification based on morphologic patterns and the staged therapeutic plan for acne-scarred patients facilitated the treatment, improved the final outcomes, and may allow development of protocols comparing results among various therapeutic approaches and different authors. The postacne scars assessment in our study was coherent with the study concluded above.

Scarring on face may occur due to number of causes, the commonest of which is acne vulgaris. Acne is most commonly seen in 90% of young adults. Acne vulgaris is an infiammatory process locally occurring in the pilosebaceous units of the face, chest, upper arms, and back. In most of the cases, acne affect the face. The pathogenesis of acne includes elevated sebum production, alteration of the quality of sebum lipids, androgen activity, proliferation of Propionibacterium acnes within the follicle, and follicular hyperkeratinization. The severity of scars depends on the delay of treatment.

Successful management of patients with acne scars depends on the clinician clearly understanding the patients’ concerns about the scar. Scars arise from the site of tissue injury and are of three types: atrophic, hypertrophic, and keloid. These scars are called either atrophic or hypertrophic depending upon whether there is a net loss or gain of collagen. Atrophic scars are more common than keloid and hypertrophic scars. Subtypes of acne scars are icepick, rolling, and boxcar. Icepick scars are small [<2 mm], deep, and have clear margins of epithelial tracts that extend vertically to the deep dermis or subcutaneous tissue. Rolling scars are usually larger than 4-5 mm. Boxcar scars are round to oval depressions with clear vertical edges and they are like varicella scars.

In our study apart from the basic classification, Goodman and Baron’s simple qualitative grading system was used to describe the type of scars. Grade 1 was for macular disease with erythematous hyper- or hypopigmented flat marks. Grade 2 was for mild disease with mild atrophy or hypertrophy that may not be noticeable at 50 cm or greater. Grade 3 is for the moderate form of disease with moderate atrophic or hypertrophic scarring that is noticeable at 50 cm or greater. Grade 4 is for the severe form of disease with severe atrophic or hypertrophic scarring [icepick scars].[15]

Microneedling, which is also called induction therapy or dermaroller therapy, is a cost-effective procedure for the treatment of postacne atrophic scars.[16] Dermaroller or dermapen can be used to perform this procedure. Coming to the history of microneedling, there are a few important milestones always to be remembered. Orentreich D and Orentreich N in the year 1995 had described the technique of subcision or dermal needling for the treatment of scars.[17] In our study, we concluded that DR had excellent recovery on the skin in both groups. Fernandes D (2005)[17] in his article has described principles, indications, contraindications, technique of microneedling, and postoperative care for the management of skin scar using DR. Our study concluded that multiple sittings of microneedling will yield better results.

Goodman GJ, Baron JA (2006)[18] conducted a study to provide a simple qualitative grading system that would allow better communication between practitioners of a patient’s global disease severity and the most appropriate corresponding therapy for that degree of acne scarring. Four grades of postacne scarring are described, and appropriate therapeutic interventions are presented for each they are classified into Grade 1 macular disease, Grade 2 mild disease, Grade 3 moderate disease, and Grade 4 severe disease. In 1997, dermabrasion was done using a technique called Tattoo Pistol by Camirand and Doucet for the treatment of scars.[18] The double observer study was useful in scar evaluation in our study.

Aust MC et al. (2008)[19] performed a retrospective analysis on 480 patients with fine wrinkles, lax skin, scarring, and stretch marks. Patients were treated with percutaneous collagen induction using the Medical Roll-CIT to produce tighter, smoother skin. They concluded that percutaneous collagen induction has proven to be very effective in minimizing acne scars. The scar evaluation with the respective scores in VCS with DR yielded good results on a 20-day follow-up.

Fabbrocini G et al. (2009)[20] in their study to confirm the usefulness of skin needling in acne scarring treatment enrolled 32 patients with acne rolling scars. The authors confirmed that skin needling has an immediate effect in improving acne rolling scars and has advantages over other procedures.

In our study, dermaroller had been used and the treatment had been done by rolling the device over the affected areas of the skin in four different directions: horizontally, vertically, and diagonally right and left. The microneedles pass through the skin and puncture the skin [epidermis]. The needles separate the cells rather than cutting them through and thus many cells are spared. This helps in rapid healing of wound. Followed by this procedure small bleeding points appear on the skin. The serous ooze which is formed is then cleaned by using sterile saline solution.

Initially microneedling using dermaroller was carried out bilaterally on areas of the face affected by postacne atrophic scars. Then, intradermal platelet-rich plasma was injected bilaterally. This technique of injecting platelet-rich plasma is called mesotherapy. Mesotherapy is a minimally invasive procedure that can be used for drug delivery by intradermal or subcutaneous injections (into the middle layer of skin), which is also called intradermotherapy. The maximum depth of penetration should not be greater than 4 mm to achieve good therapeutic outcomes.

Vitamin C is a naturally occurring antioxidant and water-soluble vitamin present in higher concentrations in skin. Chemically active form of vitamin C is called L-ascorbic acid (LAA). In market, vitamin C is available in various forms, such as creams, serum, and transdermal patches. Serum is the only form that contains active Vitamin C. Vitamin C plays an essential role in collagen synthesis, has potential anti-inflammatory action, provides photoprotection, and can be used as a depigmenting agent. In our study, using the SNOSE [Sequentially Numbered Opaque Sealed Envelopes] randomization technique, the site of topical vitamin C serum application was selected.

In our study, the patient was recalled after 24 h for evaluation of pain and edema and after a week for assessment of grades of scars. The patient after the 7th POD follow up was recalled on 20th POD. In a maximum number of patients [66.7], VAS score was 1 after 24 h follow-up.

On intragroup observations for groups A and B, there was a statistically significant difference noted for GIS when compared between 7 days versus 20 days [P < .05]. A higher mean score was observed for 20 days when compared to 7 days. Also, for PSS [P < .01], statistically similar highly significant difference was noted as above with a higher mean score on 20th POD.

On intergroup observations, there was no statistically significant difference seen for GIS and PSS at 7 days between the groups A and B [P > .05]. On the other hand, the observations made on 20th POD showed a high statistical difference for GIS and PSS between the groups [P < .01]. A higher mean score was observed in relation to group B when compared to group A.

Zhu J et al. (2013)[21] conducted a study on efficacy of autologous platelet-rich plasma combined with erbium fractional laser therapy for facial acne scars or acne. In their study, they combined platelet-rich-plasma with erbium fractional laser therapy for the treatment of 22 patients, including 16 patients who suffered from facial acne scars and six patients who suffered from acne scars concomitant with acne. Photographs were taken before and after treatment for evaluation by dermatologists, and the patients rated the efficacy on a 5-point scale. The mean duration of erythema was 1.86 ± 0.87 days. On the quartile grading scale, the clinical improvement at 4 weeks after the first treatment was 2.77 ± 0.39, corresponding to moderate improvement as evaluated by the dermatologists. There were few side effects like burning pain during the procedure and punctiform errhysis. However, after coating face with PRP, the burning pain subsided. The authors concluded that PRP combined with erbium fractional laser therapy is an effective and safe approach for treating acne scars or acne, with minimal side-effects, and it simultaneously enhanced the recovery of laser-damaged skin.

Alam M et al. (2014)[22] conducted split-face, placebo-controlled, parallel-group randomized clinical trial on 20 healthy adults with acne scars on both sides of the face. The authors concluded that there was improvement in the appearance of acne scars over time compared with the control group, with minimal pain reported using needling treatment.

Fabi S, Sundaram H (2014)[23] carried out a study on topical and injectable growth factors and cytokines for skin rejuvenation. They described the primary function of injectable PRP in their study. The authors evaluated that injection of PRP allowed direct delivery of growth factors to the dermis, and the hypodermis and the use of patients’ own platelets and growth factors was an appealing aspect of PRP. They concluded in their study that better understanding of mechanisms of penetration, release, and activity of growth factors will allow the development of more standardized treatment protocols that are indicated for skin rejuvenation which will enhance better results.

Dogra S et al. (2014)[24] conducted a study on 36 patients of postacne atrophic facial scars who underwent five sittings of DR under topical anaesthesia at monthly intervals. They concluded that microneedling for acne scars remodulation with little downtime, satisfactory results, and peculiar side effects in Asian skin type.

El-Domyati M et al. (2015)[25] conducted a study on microneedling for treatment of atrophic scars. In this prospective study, they selected 10 patients with different types of postacne atrophic scars and were subjected to 3 months of skin microneedling treatment. The aim of their study was to evaluate the clinical effect and objectively quantify the histological changes of acne scarring in response to skin microneedling. They concluded that multiple minimally invasive sessions of skin microneedling are an effective treatment for postacne atrophic scars.

Asif M et al. (2016)[26] evaluated the efficacy and safety of IPRP combined with microneedling for treatment of atrophic acne scars in which 50 patients of 17–32 years of age with atrophic acne scars were enrolled. They concluded that IPRP has efficacy in the management of atrophic acne scars. It can be combined with microneedling to enhance the final clinical outcomes in comparison with microneedling alone.

Ibrahim MK et al. (2018)[27] studied skin microneedling plus IPRP versus skin microneedling alone in the treatment of atrophic postacne scars. They concluded in their study that both microneedling and microneedling combined with IPRP showed satisfactory results.

Platelets role in hemostasis and wound healing is of great significance. Platelets are cytoplasmic fragments of megakaryocytes and are round or oval in shape of 2 µm in diameter. Platelet rich plasma can be defined as a volume of the plasma fraction of autologous blood having a platelet concentration above baseline.[2628] It consists of an effective concentration of growth factors that promote tissue repair and tissue regeneration.[7] In this study, platelet rich plasma of approximately 4 ml was obtained from a whole blood sample of 10 ml withdrawn from the patient’s medial cubital vein and processed by a two-stage centrifuging process. The development of PRP via centrifugation has simplified the entire process so that it can be used now both in office room and operating set up.[28]

CONCLUSION

In our study, IPRP with topical VCS has shown highly significant results when compared to IIPRP without topical VCS application. The added effects of topical VCS which has properties of collagen formation, anti-inflammatory action, photoprotection, and depigmenting action along with IPRP had been a great aid in the treatment of postacne atrophic scars. This combination therapy of DR, IPRP, and topical VCS was safe, and there were no reported side effects.

So to conclude, DR followed by IPRP with topical VCS application is an excellent combination therapy for the treatment of postacne atrophic scars.

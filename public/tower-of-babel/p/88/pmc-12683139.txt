Pathologic complete response in TNBC: A shield against recurrence, but not the brain?
Authors: Kadri Altundag
Published: 2025

To The Editor,

I read with interest the study by Polho et al. on CNS relapse in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients achieving pathological complete response (pCR) after neoadjuvant chemotherapy [1]. While their work highlights an important recurrence pattern, I would like to raise a few considerations. Although pCR was associated with lower recurrence overall, the higher proportion of CNS relapse among pCR relapses (50 %) should be interpreted with caution. The absolute incidence was not significantly different from the non-pCR group (5.0 % vs. 8.8 %, p = 0.11), suggesting this may reflect longer survival rather than a true neurotropic shift. Time-to-event analyses could clarify this [2]. Additionally, without molecular subtype data, it is difficult to determine whether specific TNBC subgroups are both treatment-sensitive and CNS-prone [3,4]. Such insights are critical to guide future strategies. Finally, routine CNS imaging in asymptomatic early breast cancer is not supported by current evidence, despite the authors' recommendation for increased CNS surveillance in pCR patients. In the absence of neurologic symptoms, the most recent NCCN Guidelines place more emphasis on symptom-driven evaluation than universal screening [5]. Although this study poses significant questions, more prospective studies utilizing molecular profiling and contemporary treatments are necessary to improve risk assessment and CNS-directed interventions.

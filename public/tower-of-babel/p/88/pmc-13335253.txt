Bundibugyo virus detection during an Ebola virus disease outbreak in the democratic Republic of the Congo: an urgent call for action
Authors: Christian Tague, Johan Domga, Josias Silatchom Kamgang, Berthol Fotsing Tawe, Hermann Yokolo, Stephane Hans Bateyi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEbola virus disease continues to pose a major public health threat in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with outbreaks caused by different Ebola virus species, including Bundibugyo virus. We report preliminary information from an ongoing outbreak investigation based on laboratory testing conducted by the National Institute of Biomedical Research. Among suspected cases in Ituri province, 8 of 13 samples tested positive for Ebola virus (Bundibugyo species). The number of deaths reported among suspected cases is still under investigation, and full epidemiological data remain incomplete. These findings highlight the circulation of Bundibugyo virus and emphasize the urgent need to strengthen surveillance, diagnostic capacity, and coordinated outbreak response in affected regions.Keywords: Ebola, Virus, Bundibugyo virus, Democratic Republic of the Congo

Dear editor,

An ongoing outbreak of Ebola virus disease has recently been reported in Ituri province, Democratic Republic of the Congo, with laboratory confirmation indicating infection by Bundibugyo virus. This situation raises important concerns regarding outbreak detection, response capacity, and transmission control in a fragile health and security context [1].

Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD) is a severe and often fatal form of Ebola virus disease. It is a zoonotic disease, with fruit bats suspected of being the natural reservoir. This is the 17th Ebola virus disease outbreak in the DRC since 1976. The last outbreak in the country was declared on September 4, 2025, with a total of 64 cases (53 confirmed and 11 probable), including 45 deaths (case fatality rate of 70.3%), reported in six health zones within the Bulape health zone, Kasai province. The outbreak was declared over on December 1, 2025 [2].

Laboratory analyses conducted by the National Institute of Biomedical Research (INRB), the country's reference laboratory located in Kinshasa, confirmed the presence of the Ebola virus (Bundibugyo species) in 8 of the 13 samples taken from suspected patients linked to an outbreak of severe cases and deaths reported in the Mongbwalu and Rwampara health zones, Ituri province. [1]. However, complete epidemiological data, including total number of suspected cases and transmission chains, are still being compiled. This limits a full understanding of the outbreak magnitude and dynamics at this stage.

The resurgence of the Ebola virus is facilitated by fragile health systems, limiting surveillance, case isolation, and rapid treatment. Population displacement and insecurity complicate the control of outbreaks, while difficulties in accessing care and diagnostic delays promote community transmission. Mistrust of health authorities and misinformation reduce adherence to preventive measures. These factors increase the risks of national and cross-border spread, particularly in areas of high mobility. Potential consequences include increased mortality, overburdened health facilities, significant socio-economic disruption, and a lasting impact on affected communities [3, 4].

The Ebola response experience in Uganda highlights key effective control strategies, including active surveillance, rapid contact tracing, and early isolation of suspected cases. Ring vaccination has also demonstrated effectiveness in controlling transmission around confirmed cases. Strengthening diagnostic capacity, including the use of mobile laboratories, can significantly reduce confirmation delays. Community engagement and risk communication remain essential to improve adherence to preventive measures. These lessons underscore the importance of coordinated action between national health authorities, international partners, and local communities to strengthen preparedness and response capacities [5].

This outbreak underscores the urgent need to reinforce Ebola preparedness and response systems in endemic regions. Priority actions should include strengthening real-time surveillance, improving diagnostic capacity, ensuring rapid case isolation, and enhancing cross-border coordination to prevent further spread of Bundibugyo virus.

Vaccine Nation by Ameer Shahul is a compelling, meticulously researched, and highly readable account of how vaccination has shaped India’s public health journey - from the colonial era to the present day. At a time when vaccines are frequently discussed in polarised and politicised terms, this book offers a calm, evidence-based, and historically grounded narrative that is both timely and deeply relevant.

The preface itself sets the tone, elegantly traversing the long arc of India’s immunisation story- from early experiments and colonial public health challenges to post-independence institution building and India’s emergence as a global vaccine leader. Along the way, the author foregrounds the ethical, scientific, and political complexities inherent to vaccine development and deployment. The narrative remains accessible throughout, with scientific concepts presented clearly without compromising rigor.

One of the book’s greatest strengths is its engaging, almost novel-like flow. Rather than a dry academic chronicle, it unfolds as a historical journey in which the reader moves seamlessly from one station to another across time. Thematic structuring sustains interest, making the book difficult to put down. Chapter titles such as ‘Haffkine’s Humiliation’ and Lahore to Labs’ are not merely catchy; they encapsulate complex scientific and socio-political narratives with remarkable economy and flair.

The author brings to life pivotal moments and personalities in India’s vaccinology landscape, while also situating them within broader global currents. Episodes such as the evolution of smallpox eradication efforts, the BCG story, polio endgame strategies, and India’s transition from public-sector vaccine production to private innovation are handled with balance and nuance. Importantly, the book does not shy away from controversies, failures, or ethical dilemmas, lending it credibility and depth.

From a paediatrician’s and vaccinologist’s perspective, Vaccine Nation is an invaluable resource. It provides historical context that enriches our understanding of current immunisation policies and challenges, including vaccine hesitancy, equity, intellectual property debates, and global access. For students of medicine, public health, and life sciences, the book serves as an engaging primer on vaccinology beyond textbooks and guidelines.

In summary, Vaccine Nation is an important contribution to the literature on public health and vaccines in India. It is a must-read for students, clinicians, policymakers, and anyone interested in understanding how immunisation has shaped and continues to shape the health of the nation.

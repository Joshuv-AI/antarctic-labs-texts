Enhancing strategic basketball decisions using a bipolar complex fuzzy multi criteria group decision making framework
Authors: Junwei Yao, Xuan Wang, Qiaoli Wei, Yang Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The work offers a methodological improvement of decision-making based on a Bipolar Complex Fuzzy Multi-Criteria Group Decision-Making (BCF-MCGDM) framework that incorporates Aczel-Alsina triangular norms and conorms (AATNs and AATCNs) to become more effective at modeling uncertainty and dual experts’ preferences. The framework incorporates the merits of bipolar complex fuzzy sets (BCFS) and flexible aggregation operators in order to be able to embrace both positive and negative expert judgments in ambiguity. The proposed BCF-AATN approach, unlike the traditional fuzzy and intuitionistic fuzzy approaches, is able to address interdependent criteria and contradictory judgments in a complicated decision-making setting. To demonstrate its applicability, the framework is applied to a real-life problem in basketball strategy evaluation, where a number of experts evaluate tactical and analytical criteria. The model recognizes the Eastern Falcons as the most strategical team and justifies its strength using the sensitivity and comparative analyses. Generally, the suggested BCF-MCGDM framework provides a generalizable, theoretically based, and methodologically better system of decision support that is applicable to sports and other fields, which share certain characteristics of uncertainty and consensus among a group. Being a fast-paced and competitive game, basketball demands that players and coaches make decisions in changing tactics, uncertain conditions, and varying expert opinions. The suggested framework integrates BCF logic with adaptable aggregation techniques to assess complicated strategic alternatives. When applied to real-world data, the findings placed the Eastern Falcons as the most strategically appropriate team, with high tactical flexibility, awareness of the game, and data-driven strategies. The Northern Titans came in second, due to good player combinations and game analysis, followed by the Thunder Hawks. The Southern Stallions had a balanced performance in all categories, and the Capital Warriors stood last, as they depended on strategy but failed to execute it at the right time. Altogether, the framework based on the BCFS emphasizes the latent strengths and weaknesses of team strategies and offers viable information to coaches and analysts to facilitate the preparation of games, in-match decisions, and long-term planning.

Introduction

Daily living, organizational work, and government leadership depend on the ability to choose among available options under different levels of uncertainty and risk. To support clear and transparent analysis of multiple-criteria and decision makers (DMKs) in complex situations, both multi-attribute decision-making (MADM) and MCGDM are popularly used1,2. MCGDM allows different experts to reach a common agreement when they offer different opinions in areas like strategic policy making or urban planning3. Sports science involves physiology, biomechanics, psychology, motor learning, and data to help improve and maintain athletes’ performance. Effective DM in sports science helps prevent injuries, supervise athletes, guide recovery, and improve performance over the years4. Thanks to wearable sensors, computer vision, and AI, sports science constantly develops to offer real-time support to athletes and their coaches5. Basketball is noted within sports science as a sport that requires a lot of skill and hard work. There are many changes from offensive to defensive moments, so players must keep running fast, adjust directions quickly, and follow complex plans. All these characteristics show why basketball is an excellent topic for studying how people move, react to fatigue, and make decisions during sports6. Basketball analysis involves watching load, heart rate, shot making, motion capture, and neuro-feedback to improve shooting, court layout, and reaction skills7–9. Basketball is predominantly an open-skill game. The game requires players to be able to adjust to new and unpredictable circumstances at all times. The free throw is the only exception. It is a closed-skill movement that is done in constant and controlled conditions. Making good decisions is essential for a team’s success in basketball. Making many decisions quickly and under pressure is a key part of being a coach or player. Managers are responsible for when to call a timeout, change the team’s lineup, use a different defensive approach, or try a winning shot play at the end. Good decisions here are based on a person’s thinking and the combined thoughts of coaches and data analysts10. Using MCGDM frameworks has dramatically improved the process by which teams consider different options by combining inputs from experts in various physical, data, and tactical areas. Having plenty of statistical data, video analysis, and reports on opponents, teams can develop strategies that suit changing in-game scenarios11. Nonetheless, although there has been a significant amount of research on the topic of fuzzy decision-making systems, there has been limited research on how to directly apply advanced fuzzy logic to the domain of sports analytics, specifically basketball, a field that requires rapid tactical adjustments, data-driven predictions, and judgmental tasks that are subject to uncertainty. Recent papers highlight the increased demand of decision-support systems, which combine computational intelligence with applied sports science7, .Thus, the present research aims at making contributions on two main levels:

Methodological improvement — through creation of new Bipolar Complex Fuzzy Multi-Criteria Group Decision-Making (BCF-MCGDM) paradigm based on Aczelal-Alsina norms; and.Applied contribution - through its application in basketball tactical decision-making, an application with uncertainty and human-data interactions (in real-time).

Still, there are situations in real life where certain factors are there one minute and absent the next. FSs ignore this because they only deal with MD. The new features made it much easier for FSs to express different feelings. After that, Atanassov and Gargov17 developed IV-IFS, in which MD and NMD are ranges, thus making it more straightforward to deal with uncertainty in data. As he noticed that humans often consider both sides of an issue at the same time, Zhang18 created bipolar fuzzy sets (BFS) by embedding MD as well as negative MD (Ne-MD) in FSs. It captures decisions when people weigh benefits against disadvantages or like something against something they do not like. Many natural systems are complex, and pattern recognition is an example where things are shown as feature vectors within multi-attribute spaces. Representing such systems by using only simple one-dimensional fuzzy variables is impractical. Complex numbers were applied to fuzzy sets by Buckley19, Nguyen and Sugeno20 and Zhang et al.21 to solve this problem. Next, Ramot et al.22 introduced Complex Fuzzy Sets (CFS), which take MD into the complex domain and add a phase element to improve how linguistic uncertainty is described. Next, Alkouri and Salleh23 broadened CFSs by creating Complex Intuitionistic Fuzzy Sets (CIFS), which gave greater flexibility in handling uncertainties. Even so, dealing with noisy and uncertain data was still a problem in complicated fuzzy situations. Mahmood and Rehman24 suggested BCFS, which relies on positive and negative grades (SGs) within the complex domain. It helps BCFSs model both uncertain and dual-natured data much better than FSs, BFSs, and CFSs, giving them an advantage thanks to their additional clarity, precision, and usefulness.

Traditionally, triangular norms (TNs) express the intersection of fuzzy sets, and triangular conforms(TCNs) stand for their union. They are guided by critical algebraic rules, which are commutativity, associativity, monotonicity, and the presence of an identity element. First introduced by Menger25 in 1942, TNs were part of the theory of probabilistic metric spaces. After that, fuzzy mathematics has relied heavily on TNs and TCNs, and research continues to develop and explore their uses. Aczél and Alsina have introduced the AATNs and AATCNs26 that allow researchers to change parameters to suit a range of situations. Later on, Xia et al.27 proposed Archimedean TNs and TCNs, Liu et al.28 introduced Hamacher operators and Wang and Liu29 designed Einstein-based approaches, each of which boosted the range of applications for fuzzy systems. Because they combine different pieces of information to produce a helpful answer, aggregation operators (AOs) have become more popular in DM. Since AA operations can easily adapt, they are relied on to build AOs in many fuzzy settings. For example, Hussain et al.30 described how picture fuzzy AOs based on the Aczél-Alsina structure are suitable for use in DM. In the same way, Mahmood et al.31 created AOs using AATNs and AATCNs in the context of BCFSs. Further research18,19,20 is being done that highlights how new AOs using AA-based operations help decide when there is uncertainty and imprecision. This study is based on the theoretical foundation of decision science, sports analytics, and the combination of group decision-making based on fuzzy logic. Past research on the cognitive decision theory highlights the importance of uncertainty, collective judgment and situational awareness in the process of making decisions among human beings. In this regard, fuzzy and bipolar frameworks are integrated to offer a systematic way of modeling rational as well as affective elements of professional evaluation. The present study constructs the theoretical bridge between behavioral and computational intelligence in sports by incorporating these elements into a Bipolar Complex Fuzzy Multi-Criteria Group Decision-Making (BCF-MCGDM) framework.

Although the literature in the field of fuzzy-based decision models has grown, the weakness of the current BCFS-MADM and BCFS-MCGDM models is still in the fact that they cannot capture jointly bipolarity, complex uncertainty, and dependence of multiple experts in dynamic environments. The majority of available studies24,31,38,39 focus on writing computations but are not real-life adaptable and context sensitive. In line with this, the research questions that this study will address are: (Q1) What can be done to extend a fuzzy group decision-making model to more accurately model the complex uncertainty with the two poles of expert preferences? (Q2) What are the ways to combine Aczel-Alsina aggregation operators with BCFS to enhance interpretability and flexibility in the formation of group consensus? (Q3) To what extent can the proposed framework be implemented successfully when it comes to the context of tactical decision-making in basketball as a dynamic context? These questions serve the aim of the study, which is to come up with a methodologically improved BCF-MCGDM model that would indeed enhance the nature of theoretical rigor, but at the same time portray practical applicability in real-life decision-making situations.

Literature review

The classical MCDM methods have been actively used in the engineering, sustainability, and policy sectors. An example is the fuzzy control-based MCDM models have been applied to enhance the quality of power in hybrid power systems which shows that fuzzy logic is effective in dealing with uncertainty in complex systems32. SWARA-WASPAS models within spherical fuzzy environments are used to prioritize the low-carbon polices in climate change mitigation, and the Fuzzy Delphi based frameworks with cluster analysis have been effectively applied in the ESG evaluation and sustainability assessment33. Even though these are effective in solving problems associated with uncertainty and group consensus, they typically only work with single polarity fuzzy representations34. By comparison, the suggested Bipolar Complex Fuzzy MCGDM framework builds upon the classical models of MCDM by jointly representing the positive and negative preferences of the experts in terms of complex-valued uncertainty, thus providing a higher expressive capacity to the dynamic decision-making scenarios, including the competitive basketball strategy.

Although major steps have been made in the fields of fuzzy and machine-learning-based sports analytics7–9,35-37, the assessment of sports decision support models is still theoretically and practically detached. The majority of the studies focus on the statistical performance or predictive accuracy instead of the aspect of uncertainty management in expert-based tactical decisions. The given BCFS -AATN -MCGDM framework solves this gap by combining bipolar logic, complex-valued uncertainty modeling, and multi-expert consensus mechanisms, thereby making the fuzzy theory useful in the context of dynamic sports settings.

Progress of uncertainty handling beyond fuzzy theory

During the last years, the field of uncertainty modeling of the decision making has gone beyond the classical fuzzy theory by means of methods like grey systems, probabilistic reasoning and scenario-based approaches. The grey system theory is successful in handling partially known information whereas hybrid fuzzy-grey methods are useful in strengthening with uncertainty. In more recent developments, the scenario-based approach, including the combined BWM and fuzzy VIKOR model designed by35 has seen the resolution of how contextual and situational uncertainties can be successfully dealt with in complex environments. It is in this study that such developments are most applicable as decision making in basketball also constitutes dynamic contextually dependent uncertainty. Our suggested Bipolar Complex Fuzzy MCGDM framework shares these developments by providing a complementary uncertainty-handling system that fits positioning complex, human-centered decision-making on sports analytics.

Motivation and research objectives

Even though much has been written on fuzzy and intuitionistic fuzzy system, there are practically no studies which have explicitly combined bipolar complex fuzzy logic with sports analytics, especially in basketball. The available models of the decision support fail to reflect the uncertainty, hesitation and dual expert opinion that is inherent in the real time strategic decision-making1–3,24. Hence, the necessity to have a system that combines human knowledge with computer accuracy in order to make decisions that are interpretable and equitable is very high.

Research gap

Modern sports analytics (ML) is becoming more based on machine learning, big data, and player-tracking systems7,35,37, but the current decision-support systems are incapable of capturing the uncertainty, hesitation, and bipolar expert opinions. This is the limitation of existing basketball strategy models to integrate both the subjective and objective information. Despite the different analytical and mathematical methods applied to DM in basketball, the models that are currently available do not seem to be able to address the complexity, uncertainty, and bipolarity of expert judgments. Conventional fuzzy models are informative but cannot capture the duality of human DM, in which positive and negative preferences exist together with reluctance. This weakness leaves a loophole in the capacity of the existing approaches to provide comprehensive, dependable, and flexible decision-support systems to strategic basketball planning. Current BCFS-MADM and BCFS-MCGDM models31,37 offer a good mathematical basis but fail to deal with the concomitant bipolarity of preferences and interdependencies among decision-makers - constraints that have been mitigated in the proposed BCF-AATN framework. The AATN and AATCN are generalizations of the standard TN and TCN, with a parameter that regulates their behavior. This enables them to represent the logical AND, OR operations in a fuzzy environment. The AATCNs and AATNs were chosen due to their flexibility as a parameter controlled process of fuzzy information amalgamation. In contrast to the traditional TN AND TCN, that include minimum operator, product operator, etc., AATNs and AATCNs enable the manipulation of an exponential parameter, which governs the level of compensation among criteria26,27. This flexibility increases precision, the model is more capable of modeling the uncertainty degree of the expert opinion; interpretability, due to the monotonic and associative properties, that are used to retain logical consistency; and aggregation, due to the ease of transitioning between conjunctive and disjunctive behaviours. The operators, within the framework of BCFS-AATN, permit a unified representation of subjective and objective assessments, so that they are robust in case the preferences of experts do not have the same magnitude or polarity. As a result, the AATNs and AATCNs are better than fixed t-norms and t-conorms, as they provide more control, realism, and flexibility to complex decision-making settings.

The BCFAAWA operator evaluates multiple inputs and provides a single resultant output as a BCFN. It ensures that all opinions are taken into account in proportion to their weight, resulting in a balanced and credible aggregate. The BCFAAWA operator was selected among other operators based on BCFS-aggregating (e.g., BCFAAWG, BCFAH, BCFEWA) because of its adaptive weighting feature and better strike between expert and criterion weighting. In contrast to fixed-weight operators, BCFAA enables the use of the entropy-based dynamic weighting to enhance equity among multiple experts in aggregation. It has also been shown by comparative studies26,31,39 that the Aczel-Alsina model in BCFS has a smoother convergence to consensus and better control over sensitivity and thus is most applicable in complex and interdependent decision process like sports strategy formulation.

Rationale behind choosing bipolar complex fuzzy sets (BCFS)

The development of the Bipolar Complex Fuzzy Sets (BCFS) as a part of the proposed framework is based on its robust feature of addressing dual, uncertain and interdependent opinions of experts. Both traditional fuzzy sets15 and intuitionistic fuzzy sets16 manage to represent vagueness but do not manage to represent both positive and negative preferences at the same time. Even though the interval-valued17, the Pythagorean, and the triangular fuzzy extensions provide more flexibility in terms of hesitation or geometric uncertainty, they do not support the opposition or phase-dependency uncertainty.

Conversely, the BCFS model proposed by24 and furthered by Mahmood et al.31 is built on the sophisticated field, where the degrees of support and opposition can be simultaneously expressed using bipolar semantics. This property is necessary in practice during the real life decision-making process which may be, e.g., the analysis of a tactical basketball game, expert judgments in which the assessment of the advantages and disadvantages is usually done simultaneously. Moreover, BCFSs combine with Acziel-Alsina aggregation operators26,31 in a more flexible, transparent and interpretable way than intuitionistic or Pythagorean fuzzy models.Thus, the choice of the core theoretical construct of the proposed MCGDM framework is based on the fact that BCFS has a better capacity to reflect two-fold preferences, reluctance, and interdependence, which are essential components of the complex and humanistic decision-making processes in the uncertain contexts of sports analytics.

Bipolar complex fuzzy multi-criteria group decision framework

People must make daily decisions that influence their personal, work and social lives. Every decision, whether about a career, policies, or a managerial role in sports, affects the outcome positively or negatively. Still, dealing with real-life problems can be hard, since they are often hard to predict and may be affected by different and usually competing influences. In situations like this, MCGDM is especially helpful. It uses insights and opinions from various perspectives. The group effort allows for solving problems that are both vague and subjective. The process of breaking down and categorizing criteria ensures that decisions made with MCGDM are more credible, fair, and transparent in healthcare planning, infrastructure development, strategic sports management, and policy drafting. The data employed in this research are expert-elicited and scenario based in comparison to the data obtained through public data sets or surveys. Structured linguistic assessments were used to get expert judgments which were then converted to Bipolar Complex Fuzzy Numbers. The methodological rationale of this approach is the lack of standardized data of tactical decision-making in professional basketball in the open, the privacy limit in elite sports analytic, and the necessity of testing the given framework in the controlled setting. The data form is composed of personal expert decision matrices, consensus evaluation phase, entropy-based weighting, and aggregation with the help of the BCFAAWA operator.

This section utilizes the MCGDM method within the BCF landscape to cope with uncertainties present in experts’ opinions. The expressiveness of a person’s decisions is enhanced as BCFNs can now portray MD, NMD, and HD. The BCFAAWA operator is used in the framework to gather knowledge from several DMKs effectively. Moreover, using an entropy-based normalization method from BCF, the evaluation process always uses the same standard for all cases. Since real-world scenarios are complex and affected by opinions, the proposed MCGDM approach brings many benefits to help managers decide effectively.

Application of bipolar complex fuzzy MCGDM for tactical planning in competitive basketball

Reflecting basketball strategy matters greatly, since the sport is always full of complex, changing, and significant moments. In basketball games, coaches and analysts must watch fast-changing situations and evaluate players, teams, the other team’s playing style and the result on the court. This case study combines an MCGDM process and the concept of BCFSs to tackle both random and uncertain issues in DM. A mix of approaches, focusing on the game’s setting, team strength and weakness, both players, studying the opponent, in-game adaptations and data allows for a thorough review of all the available strategies. The advantage is clear in national-level events, since little changes in tactics can make a significant difference to the final result. The case study proves that advanced decision-support techniques can improve results, shape coaching approaches, and give an edge in high basketball settings.

Professional choice and reasoning

The decision-making team was comprised of three hypothetical experts who were chosen according to their professional experience and analytical skills and role diversity to make the evaluation balanced and credible. In particular, one of the experts was a coach of a national basketball team with more than five years of experience with the tactical decisions, another was a professional sport performance analyst who also worked with the statistics and efficiency indicators of players, and the third was an ex-international player who now fulfills the role of a strategic advisor. These profiles are included because it is advised that when making a decision involving complex groups through expert panel multi-perspective recommendations are made36. This writing makes sure that there is a mix of tactical knowledge, analytical knowledge and the experiential knowledge applied in terms of evaluating a strategic basketball game. The options and criteria of evaluation were determined via systemic synthesis of professional consultation with literature verification. In particular, three hypothetical conceptual professionals (national-level coach, professional performance analyst, and former international player) have been interviewed to determine exemplary national-level basketball teams and the strongest tactical and analytical determinants in the competitive decision-making process. The diverse options chosen were meant to portray a variety of strategies as opposed to the vastness of all potential teams and this is in line with methodological MCDM validation research. To exclude redundancy and retain the analytical clarity other possible alternatives were dropped. Comparative analysis

The BCFS-MCGDM methodology is evaluated in this work by comparing it to two notable decision techniques: the WASPAS approach and the MADM approach using BCFSs. Important aspects considered during the comparison are how efficiently a strategy works, how accurately it decides and its strength when dealing with complexity and uncertainty. Findings indicate that the proposed technique regularly provides improved and fair results using BCFSs, advanced aggregation approaches, and group decision mechanisms. Unlike conventional ways, which can overemphasize the judgment of an expert or ignore the way criteria are linked, the BCFAAWA operator in the proposed model helps ensure objectivity, cuts cognitive bias, and lets DMKs unite in their conclusions.

Discussion

The findings of this paper support the theoretical assumption that quality of decisions increases with a combination of subjective expert judgment and objective data modeled with the usage of bipolar fuzzy logic. This favors the cognitive-behavioral idea that human tactical decisions are determined by twin (good and bad) belief framework. In addition, in line with the current body of work of decision science, the stability in the rankings based on sensitivity analysis confirms the strength and readability of the framework in environments of uncertain situations.

The suggested framework can provide credible and transparent outcomes even in different circumstances and uncertainties. The consistency of the highest-ranking options highlights the feasibility of the methodology in determining high-performing teams. The approach can capture latent dependencies and subtle dynamics in basketball strategies by successfully balancing subjective judgments and objective data. This balance will ensure that human intuition and quantitative evidence are not ignored to create logical and practical rankings. In addition, the framework is more interpretable and fair in DM than traditional methods. This is because it has enhanced aggregation methods and can handle conflicting expert opinions without giving too much weight to individual judgments. The operator of the BCFAAWA, specifically, makes sure that the process of DM is objective, transparent, minimizes the power of prejudice, and enhances group consensus. This flexibility underscores its applicability in dynamic and complicated settings where accuracy and fairness are paramount.

The ranking and the logic behind the superiority of alternatives allow DMKs to use them further for more strategic gameplay and enhanced performance. This openness increases confidence in the proposed work, which makes it more acceptable to practitioners, including coaches, managers, and analysts. Incorporating the BCF-based AO for aggregation allows for modelling ambiguity, hesitation, and contradictory information, typical of real-world DM situations.

Limitations

Although the suggested framework has shown encouraging outcomes, it is essential to note a few limitations:

The research was based on the opinions of three conceptual experts, who, despite their usefulness, can restrict the range of views; a more diverse panel of experts could be used in the future.Second, applying the method in live coaching is associated with integrating data in real-time and prompt interpretation, which can be practically limited in high-speed settings like basketball games.Third, the BCF framework can be complex, posing challenges in scalability and adoption, especially for practitioners not well versed in complex mathematical formulations.Lastly, the results are based on a particular case study of national-level basketball teams. Although this work can be extended to other settings, competition levels, and types of team sports, more validation of the framework in different settings is required to establish its applicability.

This study involved a methodology using BCFSs and BCFAAWA operators within the context of an MCGDM problem to analyze and compare competitive basketball teams. Considering factors like the game setting, matchups, team abilities, scouting the opposition, quick tactical changes and data analysis, the proposed model provides a valuable and effective tool for making decisions in sports when information is uncertain. Both subjectivity and ambiguity were correctly addressed due to the use of the BCFAAWA operator and the aggregation of DMK’s opinions in BCFNs. Future research direction

Future research aims to solve the identified limitations, including the small number of expert DMKs, the scalability of the model, and the problems with the implementation of live games. The framework would improve DM accuracy in real basketball matches by incorporating real-time and dynamic data sources, such as biometrics, motion tracking, and in-game sensors49. Moreover, the proposed fuzzy MCGDM method can be enhanced with machine learning or deep learning methods to improve predictive power, automate performance assessment, and identify latent patterns that humans can hardly recognize37. The generalizability and practical applicability of the methodology could be further confirmed by expanding the method to other team sports, high-stakes settings, or other areas with multiple conflicting factors, including military strategy, crisis management, and healthcare. All in all, these incorporations would enhance the suggested work’s empirical basis and practical implications. Future studies are to aim at enhancing the flexibility and smartness of the framework proposed. It is promising to consider adapting the adaptive Artificial Intelligence (AI) learning models with the BCFS-AATN framework to allow assessing strategies and supporting decisions in complex sport settings dynamically as reported on adaptive AI in decision systems37. Likewise, it is possible to implement generative AI methods on the analysis of behavioral and environmental patterns using large-scale sports data to provide more meaningful information about their performance, and in developing studies, generative AI is able to predict behavioral patterns and sustainable decisions50.The inclusion of these intelligent learning elements would enable the BCFS-AATN model to self-adapt depending on changing data and hence enhance accuracy, minimize cognitive bias, and increase its applicability to various real-world decision-making situations.

ITDFNet: a multi-dimensional fusion image and temporal dual-branch network for bearing fault diagnosis
Authors: Shaobin Cai, Yangsheng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough data-driven methods are widely used in fault diagnosis, traditional one-dimensional feature analysis strategies still have bottlenecks in feature utilization and representation capabilities. Although previous studies have attempted to utilize convolutional neural networks by converting one-dimensional signals into two-dimensional images, such conversion methods are often accompanied by new problems such as information loss, complex computation, and poor feature separability. To break through the above limitations, this paper innovatively proposes a Multi-dimensional Fused Image and Temporal Dual-branch Fusion Network model (ITDFNet). This model adopts a dual-path collaborative architecture, aiming to capture and integrate complementary information of faults from different dimensions respectively: The image branch fuses the original signal matrix, the temporal gram and the structural gram signal matrix into a multi-dimensional image, and focuses on the key spatial texture features by using a dedicated dual fusion attention block. The temporal branch models the original sequence enhanced by Gram denoising to retain the precise time-varying law. Under the CWRU and UCONN datasets, this method achieved a 100% fault identification accuracy rate, outperforming existing methods. Meanwhile, compared with Swin-FFRN, under strong noise interference, its accuracy rate is increased by 8.44% and the number of parameters is reduced by 29M, highlighting the enhancing effect and application prospects of the dual-branch fusion design on the robustness of the model.Keywords: Fault diagnosis, Convolutional neural network, Feature representation, Multimodal fusion, Anti-noise robustnessSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Rotating machinery is the core equipment in wind power, aviation, rail transportation and other fields, and its operation state has a direct impact on the safety, stability and economic benefits of the whole system. Bearing, gear and other key components are in the harsh working environment of high load and high speed for a long time, which is the weak link prone to failure in the system1. If a sudden fault occurs, it will not only lead to significant downtime loss, but also cause safety accidents2,3. It has both theoretical value and engineering significance in promoting industrial intelligence and realizing predictive maintenance.

At present, rotating machinery fault diagnosis methods are mainly divided into two categories: model-based methods and data-driven method4.The model-based method needs to establish an accurate mathematical model of the system, and the fault diagnosis is realized by analyzing the residual between the actual output and the model prediction. This method has strong interpretability when the model is accurate and the disturbance is controllable, and it can clarify the physical mechanism of the fault5. However, rotating machinery in actual industry often has complex characteristics such as strong nonlinearity and time-varying, and it is difficult to establish an accurate model.

With the progress of artificial intelligence technology, data-driven fault diagnosis methods have gradually become the mainstream, which can be divided into two types. One is the combination of manually set fault indicators and traditional machine learning algorithms. The other is to use deep learning models to directly process vibration signals6. The core difference between these two types of methods is that fault diagnosis based on deep learning does not need to manually design fault feature indicators, and can adaptively extract fault features from the original signals through the model, so as to realize the effective identification and diagnosis of different fault type7.

Early data-driven methods mostly use traditional machine learning algorithms such as Support Vector Machine (SVM) and k-Nearest Neighbor (k-NN)8,9. This kind of methods usually need to manually extract signal features, and then use the classifier to judge and identify the fault category. In this field, Gunerkar et al.10 applied wavelet transform to extract time-frequency features from original time series, and then completed the fault diagnosis task through artificial neural network. Li et al.11 used Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN) to extract multi-scale features after wavelet transform, and then combined with support vector machine to realize fault detection. Ng et al.12 used the time-frequency characteristics of time series data and combined the K-Nearest Neighbor (kNN) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) methods to realize the fault diagnosis of wind turbines. Chen et al.13 proposed a new signal processing method, which combined the improved empirical wavelet transform and kernel extreme learning machine. However, most machine learning models have problems such as limited generalization ability, complex diagnosis process, and need more human participation, and cannot fully meet the requirements of real-time performance and diagnostic accuracy under the background of big data14.

The emergence of deep learning algorithms effectively overcomes the above limitations. At present, the technical route based on deep learning can be divided into two directions. The first is the processing method based on one-dimensional data. For example, Ma et al.15 used the advantages of Long Short-Term memory network (LSTM) to construct a new type of deep neural network, which used the time-frequency domain signal as the input of CNN for feature extraction, so as to fully retain the feature information. Xu et al.16 designed a hybrid model combining multi-scale CNN and multi-attention mechanism to extract the rich multi-scale features contained in gearboxes more comprehensively. Xu et al.17 designed a collaborative fusion convolutional neural network for fault diagnosis, which effectively improved the performance of fault classification by introducing a multi-scale denoising module, a central fusion module and an online label smoothing training strategy. Although these methods directly deal with the original vibration signal, it is usually difficult to fully extract the spatial structure features contained in the signal18.

The other is the diagnosis path based on two-dimensional images, that is, the signal conversion technology is used to convert the one-dimensional vibration signal into two-dimensional images, and then the mature computer vision model is applied to realize feature extraction. At present, the mainstream methods for converting time-domain signals into images mainly include Short-time Fourier Transform (STFT)19, Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT)20, S Transform (ST)21, Gram Angle Field (GAF)22 and Markov Transfer Field (MTF)23, etc. These techniques have been widely used in the field of fault diagnosis. Zare et al.24 developed a signal-to-image conversion technique and applied it to fault diagnosis of wind turbines. Pang et al.25 used the image generated by the original vibration signal as the input data of the transfer convolutional neural network, so as to realize the fault diagnosis of gearbox equipment. In literature26, researchers used the cyclic spectral Coherence (CSC) method to extract the second-order cyclostationary characteristics of bearing vibration signals, and then combined it with the LeNet model to realize the effective diagnosis of bearing faults. Tang et al. used the Gram Angle Field (GAF) as the two-dimensional image representation of the vibration signal, and constructed a new network model to classify the generated GAF image, so as to realize the fault diagnosis of low-speed bearings. All the above methods have shown significant effectiveness in fault diagnosis tasks by combining signal transformation techniques with CNN.

Although two-dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (2D-CNN) is widely used in fault diagnosis, there are still limitations in the conversion method from one-dimensional signal to two-dimensional image that it relies on. On the one hand, the computational efficiency of the existing conversion methods is not high, the complex algorithm not only consumes resources, but also weakens the end-to-end advantage of 2D-CNN, and the generated image features are insufficient separability, which affects the subsequent recognition effect. On the other hand, it is difficult to avoid information loss in the transformation process: although some features can be strengthened by the image, the important information in the original signal may be weakened. Therefore, reducing information loss27 is crucial to improve diagnostic performance.

In order to solve the above problems, this study proposes a new multi-dimensional fusion image construction method, which fuses the original signal, temporal features and structural information into a composite image. This method is simple to calculate and has good feature separability. For a given real-valued time series, it is first mapped to the time axis state vector sequence in the embedding space. Then, the temporal correlation and structural correlation between all pairs of state vectors in the vector sequence are calculated by inner product operation. The generation of the multidimensional fusion image includes two key steps: the construction of the original signal matrix28, and the calculation of its time gram matrix and structure Gram matrix respectively. This method only involves matrix multiplication, so it has high computational efficiency. The generated feature images have good separability, especially for periodic vibration signals, showing obvious stripe and block texture features. The method has clear physical meaning and clear theoretical support.

Meanwhile, aiming at the information loss problem existing in the commonly used two-dimensional convolutional neural networks that take the transformed two-dimensional representation as the sole input, an innovative dual-branch fusion network model for multi-dimensional image fusion and timing - ITDFNet - is proposed. This model aims to overcome the problem of insufficient feature representation ability of existing single-input models. By synchronously integrating multi-dimensional fused image features and one-dimensional temporal features through a parallel structure, it can more effectively extract complementary and discriminative feature representations, thereby enhancing the robustness and classification accuracy of the model in actual high-noise environments.

GM denoising

A vibration signal denoising method based on the Gram matrix28 is proposed to suppress irrelevant noise interference in the signal. The vibration signal of a faulty rolling bearing has periodic characteristics, and the corresponding signal matrix usually exhibits low-rank properties - the main energy is concentrated on a few large singular values. However, noise interference increases the complexity of the signal, causing the weight of small singular values to rise, thereby weakening the periodic characteristics. The method in this paper, through the above matrix multiplication operation, is equivalent to re-weighting the singular values of the signal matrix: from the perspective of singular value decomposition (SVD), this operation makes the singular values become the fifth power of the original, thereby enhancing the large singular values and suppressing the small ones, effectively highlighting the periodic fault features and reducing the influence of noise. Proposed methods

Overall framework

This paper designs a dual-branch deep learning architecture for fault diagnosis, which achieves high-precision classification by effectively integrating time series transformation features and image features. As shown in Fig. 4, the overall architecture is composed of a time series feature extraction branch and an image feature extraction branch in parallel. The time branch receives one-dimensional signals that have undergone denoising processing and can stably extract time-domain statistical features and periodic impulse features on the basis of a relatively high signal-to-noise ratio, thereby effectively avoiding the interference of strong noise on feature extraction. The image branch, on the other hand, receives multi-dimensional images constructed from the original signal, where the original signal has not undergone any filtering or denoising processing, thus fully preserving the original topological structure and weak features of the fault signal. This branch can capture the detailed information that might be weakened during the denoising process in the time branch from the spatial dimension. The two branches respectively extract complementary information from the original signal and then perform feature fusion, thereby significantly enhancing the model’s ability to represent and discriminate complex fault features.

The process of intelligent fault diagnosis method for rotating machinery proposed in this paper is as follows:

Step 1: Data acquisition. Acceleration sensors are deployed near key components, such as bearings and gears, and the vibration signals are recorded using a data acquisition system.

Step 2: Signal segmentation. The original vibration signal is divided into samples containing fixed length data points, and then divided into training set and test set.

Step 3: Data transformation. The training samples were normalized and converted into the original signal matrix (SM) in turn, and then the noise was reduced through the Gram matrix as the input of the timing branch. At the same time, the original matrix was further transformed into temporal Gram matrix (TGM) and structural Gram matrix (SGM), which were fused into a multi-channel image as the input of the image branch.

Step 4: Time series feature extraction. The denoised signal is input into the time series feature extraction module, and the local and global features of the signal are extracted to obtain the time series feature .

Step 5: Image feature extraction. The three-channel image is input into the improved residual network, and the time-frequency features are extracted to obtain the image feature .

Step 6: ITDFNet model. The time series feature and image feature are additively fused, and then input into the fully connected layer to calculate the cross-entropy loss between the predicted label and the true label, and the model weight is optimized by gradient descent method and back propagation.

Step 7: Online diagnosis. The newly collected vibration signals are preprocessed in the same way as in the training phase, and the trained model is used for inference to judge the health state of the equipment according to the probability distribution of the output.

Experimental dataset

Case Western Reserve University (CWRU)

The CWRU rolling bearing fault data29 set is used in this experiment. The test bed mainly includes 1.5 kW motor, torque sensor and power meter and other components. The research object is SKF6205 bearing at the driving end, and the system sampling frequency is set to 12 kHz. Three types of typical faults were set on the bearing by EDM technology: inner ring (IR) fault, rolling element (RE) fault and outer ring (OR) fault. Each type of fault included three damage sizes of 0.007 inches, 0.014 inches and 0.021 inches. Therefore, the bearing covers a total of 9 fault states and 1 normal state (N), totaling 10 types of health states. Vibration data were collected under four load conditions of 0 hp, 1 hp, 2 hp, and 3 hp. In this paper, the vibration signals of the fan end bearing under the above four loads and 12 kHz sampling frequency are selected as the experimental analysis objects. Experimental setup

PyTorch 1.1 deep learning framework was used in this study. The experiment runs on Windows 11 system with Intel i7-13620 H CPU and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 laptop GPU. In the data preprocessing stage, we normalize the sample size of the CWRU bearing dataset to 64 × 64, while the UCONN gearbox dataset is adjusted to 60 × 60. The model training lasted a total of 50 rounds with a batch size of 32. The optimizer uses Adam with an initial learning rate configured to be 0.001. In the process of data preparation, all data sets were uniformly divided into three parts: training set, validation set and test set according to the ratio of 7:1:2. To ensure the statistical stability of the results, each experimental case is independently repeated 10 times, each containing 50 rounds of training iterations, and the final reported performance index is the average of the results of these 5 runs.

Ablation experiment

In order to explore the influence of key components such as double-branch structure, multi-dimensional image and attention mechanism on the accuracy of model fault diagnosis, ablation experiments were carried out.

In the ablation experiment, this study constructs a series of variants around the proposed parallel two-branch structure model ITDFNet, namely model F, to verify the effectiveness of its core module. Among them, models A, B, and C were ablated on the basis of the image branch. Model A removed the Dynamic feature attention mechanism (DFA) and structural Gram image generation module in this branch at the same time. Model B only removes the DFA module. For model C, only the structural Gram image module is removed. In addition, to explore the independent contribution of the dual branches, model D only uses the image branch, while model E only uses the time series signal branch.

Effectiveness of the two-branch structure

The dual-branch model fusing image and signal feature extraction shows significant performance advantages in ablation experiments. Specifically, model D using only the image branch achieves an accuracy of 94.78%, model E using only the time series signal branch achieves an accuracy of 89.95%, and the proposed ITDFNet model fused with the two-branch architecture achieves the highest accuracy of 97.56%. The results reveal two important conclusions. First, the image branch itself has strong feature extraction ability, and its independent performance is better than that of the time series branch. Secondly, the fusion of multi-modal features effectively realizes the complementarity of signal temporal information and image spatial representation, so that the model can capture the key discriminative features more comprehensively. Therefore, the dual-branch fusion strategy not only achieves the performance improvement compared with any single branch model, but also validates the effectiveness of multi-modal fusion in complex fault diagnosis scenarios from the mechanism.

Conclusion

In this paper, a new fault diagnosis model is proposed, which consists of two core parts: a multidimensional image fusion module and a dual-branch parallel network structure. These two modules solve the problems of high computational complexity and weak feature separability of traditional two-dimensional image representation methods, and overcome the defects of incomplete information caused by single modal input by integrating spatial texture features and temporal dynamic information.

Experimental results show that the proposed cross-domain image generation method can effectively improve the expression ability of fault features. This method converts the amplitude information of the one-dimensional vibration signal into a two-dimensional image with significant texture patterns (such as stripes and block structures), which not only realizes the efficient processing of the model, but also provides the possibility of visual analysis, which helps to explain the feature differences of different fault types from the mechanism level, so as to support high-precision fault classification. Experimental results on two case datasets show that the proposed method is superior to the existing baseline models in terms of diagnostic accuracy and anti-noise performance.

Although the proposed method shows good performance in the experimental environment, there is still room for further improvement in scenarios that are closer to the actual industrial conditions, such as unbalanced samples, lack of minority class fault samples, and limited edge device resources. Future research will focus on lightweight model design, robust training strategy for imbalanced data, and low-resource deployment optimization in the actual industrial edge environment, so as to enhance the practicability and migration ability of the model.

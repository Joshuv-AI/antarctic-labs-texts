Four-pixel sum-preserving encryption using the chaotic map for high thumbnail-preserving image security
Authors: Chia-Hung Kao, Guan-Zhen Chen, Yi-You Hou, Teh-Lu Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe utilization of cloud-based photo-sharing services has been observed to result in the exposure of thumbnails, thereby engendering a privacy risk. Thumbnail-preserving encryption (TPE) is a method of mitigating this issue by encrypting images while maintaining the usability of thumbnails. Existing sum-preserving encryption (SPE) typically operates on 3-pixel groups, which are not aligned with power-of-two block sizes and can leave residual plaintext when tiling. The proposed mapping is a four-pixel SPE (4px-SPE) mapping, which is a piecewise bijection on 8-bit pixel quadruples that exactly preserves the four-pixel sum and is perfectly invertible. The scheme is integrated into a permutation–substitution TPE framework. It uses a 256-bit master key with deterministic, hash-based key derivation to reproduce per-image and per-block schedules, and to derive per-block keystreams and permutations without relying on plaintext-derived key material. A mathematical proof and exhaustive verification of all 2564 quadruples are provided to validate invertibility under edge cases. Experiments on both grayscale and color images evaluate entropy, histogram behavior, correlation/robustness metrics, and runtime. The results demonstrate high entropy and lossless recovery while maintaining thumbnail usability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47090-9.Keywords: Four-pixel encryption, Chaotic map, Sum-preserving encryption (SPE), Security analysis, Thumbnail-preserving encryption (TPE)Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The advent of cloud-based image storage and sharing platforms has engendered a paradigm shift in the realm of digital content management. These platforms facilitate expeditious browsing and content administration through the generation of thumbnails and other low-resolution derivatives. Conventional encryption offers protection against unauthorized access to data, but it can disrupt workflow processes. This has led to the development of privacy mechanisms that preserve visual functionality to a limited extent. Thumbnail-preserving encryption (TPE) has recently emerged as a practical cryptographic framework for secure image sharing, balancing privacy and utility. Following the initial formulation, a plethora of TPE variants have been proposed to enhance fidelity and practicality. These include exact thumbnail-faithful schemes and digital-processing-oriented designs1–3. A comprehensive survey and critical analysis of the overall design space has been conducted4,5. The advent of cloud storage services has led to a significant increase in the amount of image data being entrusted to remote servers. This development has concomitantly given rise to substantial privacy concerns.

It is evident that conventional encryption methodologies, such as the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES), have been employed for a considerable duration. It is imperative that encryption techniques are innovated in order to ensure the retention of a certain level of usability. From a cryptographic perspective, it is imperative to differentiate between Thumbnail Preserving Encryption (TPE) and Format Preserving Encryption (FPE). A notable line of TPE development is the sum-preserving encryption (SPE) mechanism, in which pixels are grouped and encrypted while preserving the group sum so that block averaging yields a recognizable thumbnail1,6. When the encrypted image is averaged over each square metrixs, the resulting image (the thumbnail) is found to be close to the original image7,8. A chaotic key is frequently utilized to inject high entropy and sensitivity into the encryption process, thereby ensuring high security at full resolution while preserving coherence at the thumbnail scale2,3. Recent studies have expanded the scope of research beyond single-image settings, exploring multi-image and application-oriented designs (e.g., medical or visually meaningful encryption).

These studies demonstrate broader visually secure / visually meaningful image encryption designs9–12. As outlined in NIST SP 800-38G, the objective of FPE is to maintain the syntactic structure of plaintext strings while addressing robust security concerns. In contrast, TPE intentionally preserves low-frequency visual content (thumbnails) to support preview and cloud-side usability. It is therefore recommended that TPE/SPE schemes be evaluated and compared within the TPE design space. Beyond TPE, a broad spectrum of image encryption and hiding techniques has been developed, often driven by chaotic dynamics and hybrid signal-processing components. Examples of this include multi-objective optimization and block compressed sensing13, optimized orthogonal compressive sensing for high-quality reconstruction14, multi-chaotic maps combined with blockchain mechanisms15, orthogonal Latin-square diffusion with a new 2D chaotic system16, and fast encryption designs based on coupled map lattices or single-channel multi-image pipelines17. Recent research has investigated variable-index and high-dimensional chaotic dynamics18,19 and addressed the issue of dynamical degradation in digital chaotic maps20. The utilization of fractional-order, hyperchaotic, and memristive neural models in conjunction with FPGA-oriented implementations has been proposed as a means of enhancing complexity and practicability14,21–23. Application-driven schemes have been developed for use in visually secure medical and multi-image settings (see9–12,24,25). These schemes have also been developed for use in optical and quantum paradigms26,27. The utilization of complementary privacy-related methodologies, such as image concealment based on local binary patterns and compressive sensing, in conjunction with lightweight steganalysis, further underscores the necessity for robust privacy controls within real-world data pipelines28,29. Additionally, there have been reports of novel nonlinear/chaos-based image encryption designs30.

In the proposed scheme, chaotic map is employed as the core of the key schedule7,8,31. We briefly describe the logistic map and its properties. When iterative systems such as the logistic map are implemented on digital computers, the effects of finite precision arithmetic can lead to degradation of chaotic properties, such as cycle lengths being shorter than theoretically expected or convergence to pseudo stable states. The precision used to represent the state value and the control parameter is a critical implementation detail that directly affects the randomness and security of the key sequence. A practical mitigation direction is to incorporate perturbation or parameter/index variation strategies to reduce dynamical degradation and improve digital robustness19,20. A problem with sum-preserving grouping at small sizes is that alignment constraints can arise when image dimensions are not divisible by the group size, increasing implementation complexity and posing a risk of information leakage,moreover, limited group size can constrain diffusion within each group4,5. In addition, the broader cryptographic study of sum-preserving transformations provides a useful basis for discussing reversibility conditions and security considerations32.

In view of the fact that image compression and memory alignment requirements are generally considered to be of paramount importance, it is recommended that image width and height be multiples of 16, followed by 8 and 4. To illustrate this point, consider the video coding standards such as MPEG/H.264, which divides each frame into pixel macroblocks33. Consequently, utilizing a resolution multiple of 16 results infavorable for encoding efficiency. Moreover, certain image formats (for example, BMP) necessitate that each row of pixel data be aligned to four bytes. The utilization of a width multiple of 4 has been demonstrated to circumvent the implementation of padding, thereby enhancing the efficiency of access. It is evident that these settings not only meet the requirements of block-based compression algorithms, but also align with hardware memory alignment mechanisms. This ensures that image quality or performance degradation, which is associated with using non-standard sizes, is avoided. In order to surmount these deficiencies, a four-pixel SPE scheme is hereby proposed. The pixels are arranged into groups of four, and are encrypted. This ensures that all pixels are allocated to the blocks utilized in digital image processing, and that no pixels are left ungrouped. The larger group size results in a larger key space of 2564 possible combinations per block, higher permutation complexity, and fewer groups in an image; the proposed method is thus more resistant to brute-force and statistical attacks. First, the algorithm uses a globally generated chaotic sequence, such as one derived from an exponential or logistic map7,19,20,31, to perform a global permutation of the pixel positions across the image. This permutation destroys spatial correlations among neighboring pixels, thereby considerably reducing visual redundancy and statistical dependencies4,5. Subsequently, the permuted image pixels are partitioned into nonoverlapping groups of four. Each group is then encrypted using a novel four-pixel SPE transformation that ensures that the total intensity of each group is unchanged after encryption. This property guarantees that the low-resolution thumbnails, which are typically obtained through down sampling or averaging, have sufficient visual coherence for practical recognition tasks but do not reveal the high-frequency details of the original image1,2. Recent chaos-based encryption designs further suggest that combining richer chaotic dynamics with nonlinear transformations, cellular-automaton-like mechanisms, or optimized compressive sensing can enhance resistance against statistical attacks while maintaining usable reconstructions in certain settings12,14,24.

The SPE algorithm under consideration employs a block-indexed chaotic map for permutation and substitution key generation. The sum of the intensities for the four pixels in each group is preserved, ensuring the preservation of exact reversibility and faithful thumbnail reproduction. In this study, a rigorous mathematical treatment of the transformation was conducted, its invariance and revertability were proven, and its robustness was validated through testing over all 4.29 billion possible pixel combinations. The findings indicated that the algorithm functioned without error under all edge conditions, including maximum intensity values of 255. The primary contributions of this paper are as follows:

First formalized four-pixel formula

This paper introduces the first mathematically rigorous four-pixel SPE operation, which is proposed to address the alignment and security limitations of the two-pixel and three-pixel TPE variants. Through detailed derivations and proofs, this paper establishes a complete theoretical framework and proves the invariance of pixel-group sums and the reversibility of the four-pixel SPE transformation. The proposed algorithm accepts all possible input combinations, including extreme cases in which pixel values reach 255, ensuring robust performance for diverse images.

The proposed approach differs from approaches in which some pixels are unencrypted when an image’s dimensions are indivisible by three. The four-pixel formula works perfectly with standard block sizes (8 × 8, 16 × 16, 32 × 32, and 64 × 64), and its robustness was validated through exhaustive testing involving more than 4.29-billion-pixel combinations. The results of this testing confirmed the algorithm’s fault-free operation and set new benchmarks for reliability in TPE.

Lightweight resource encryption application design

The architecture of the proposed algorithm is specifically designed for deployment in resource-constrained environments, with a focus on computational efficiency and minimal memory usage without sacrificing cryptographic strength. The design leverages chaos-based permutation mechanisms combined with a sum-preserving substitution operation to reduce computational overheads. This makes the scheme well suited for use on edge artificial intelligence devices, autonomous drones, and Internet of Things platforms with limited computational power. Employing chaotic map as a dual-purpose key generator (for pixel replacement and block permutation) results in considerably lower complexity than is achieved in traditional multistage encryption. The algorithm incorporates several performance optimizations, such as adaptive precision scaling to maximize entropy extraction while maintaining numerical stability across diverse hardware setups.

Balance between security and thumbnail usability

By preserving block sums, our scheme creates thumbnails that can be previewed and subjected to similarity searches while hiding high-frequency details. A series of experiments were conducted to validate the hypothesis that the four-pixel scheme would obfuscate images more effectively than the three-pixel scheme. The experiments employed a range of metrics, including peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity index (SSIM), information entropy, correlation analysis, and key sensitivity tests. The results of these experiments demonstrated that the four-pixel scheme obfuscates images more effectively than the three-pixel scheme while maintaining equivalent performance metrics.

Chaotic sequence generators in TPE cryptosystems

In chaos-based cryptography, chaotic maps are frequently employed to generate pseudorandom sequences that exhibit high sensitivity to initial conditions and appear statistically random. These sequences are utilized to facilitate permutation and diffusion processes, which are pivotal operations in the encryption of secure images. In TPE, the chaotic sequence is used to drive two processes: (i) block/within-block permutation and (ii) the substitution key stream required by the sum-preserving operation. While classical one-dimensional maps (e.g., the logistic map) are frequently adopted due to their simplicity, their effective chaotic windows can be narrow, and their digital implementations may exhibit dynamical degradation under finite precision. It is therefore evident that, in the revised manuscript, the chaotic-generation component is treated as a modular and configurable part of the scheme. Furthermore, the 1D logistic map is retained as a reproducible baseline, and stronger alternatives are incorporated for keystream and permutation generation. The modularity of the system is characterized by the keystream generator (key_kind) and the permutation generator (perm_kind), which facilitate controlled comparisons under a fixed SPE mode (3px, 4px).

TPE scheme based on chaotic map

In this section, the TPE scheme is described in relation to the chaotic map that has previously been introduced. In addition, the text goes on to elaborate on how the proposed four-pixel SPE encryption scheme improves upon the three-pixel SPE encryption scheme. The four-pixel SPE encryption scheme utilizes a permutation–substitution network structure at the block level, whereby pixel values are substituted and their positions permuted. Both these processes are governed by chaotic keys. In the implementation under consideration, each block is processed by a permutation followed by a sum-preserving substitution (SPE). Decryption, conversely, applies the inverse operations in reverse order. The subsequent discussion commences with a general outline of the encryption process, followed by the presentation of the mathematical formulation for the three-pixel and four-pixel SPE operations. The proposed encryption algorithm employs a chaotic permutation–substitution architecture to achieve privacy-enhanced TPE.

The encryption algorithm implements a permutation of pixels within a block through a sorting mechanism that is driven by chaos (perm_kind-dependent). Prior to encryption of each block, a pseudorandom sequence is generated through the utilization of a selected chaotic generator, which totally equals the number of pixels per block. The chaotic sequence is then subjected to a standard sorting algorithm, resulting in the generation of two arrays. The forward permutation index (sort_idx) is a numerical value that indicates the location of each original pixel position in relation to its new position. The inverse permutation index (reverse_idx) is a numerical value that enables the reconstruction of the original order during the decryption process. The original pixel vector of the block, denoted , is permuted on the basis of the sort index and then subjected to the sum-preserving substitution transformation. In the decryption process, the encrypted pixel group is processed. Subsequently, the group is then reordered using the inverse permutation index (reverse_idx) to restore the original pixel arrangement. It can thus be concluded that the values and spatial positions of the pixels within each block are subject to modification on the basis of secret-key-dependent chaos, thereby ensuring the high randomness of the ciphertext. This mechanism is consistent with the permutation concept discussed in the proposed scheme and enhances the entropy of the encrypted output. In order to facilitate a fair comparison of the three-pixel and four-pixel SPE strategies, the same master-key settings are used across both schemes. A fixed-seed configuration is retained only as a controlled ablation. In the practical setting employed for security claims, the permutation is dynamic across blocks. The method under discussion is characterized by the continuity of the generator state across blocks, or alternatively, by deterministic block-unique seeding from the master key and block index. This approach is designed to prevent the recurrence of permutation patterns while preserving complete reversibility.

Proposed four-pixel TPE decryption

The encryption process is characterized by two primary stages. In the initial phase, the complete pixel space is subjected to a permutation process under a key-dependent chaotic map, with the objective of maximizing diffusion and confusion. In the second stage, four-pixel SPE is applied to each permuted group of pixels. During the decryption process, these operations are performed in reverse order. The chaotic key is then utilized to apply the inverse sum-preserving decryption to each pixel group. The inverse permutation is subsequently performed to reconstruct the original image. The proposed four-pixel SPE framework is designed to strike a balance between security and usability by leveraging chaotic permutation and enforcing a sum-preserving constraint during substitution. The technology under discussion guarantees compatibility with the block sizes that are most frequently utilized within the domain of image processing. These include sizes such as 8 × 8, 16 × 16, 32 × 32, and 64 × 64. Furthermore, it serves to eradicate unprocessed pixel residues, a common occurrence in three-pixel schemes. In addition to this, it facilitates single instruction multiple data (SIMD) based optimization for real-time applications.

If the implementation maps unit-interval generator outputs to byte-valued block keys through a scaling or quantization step, that step is part of the internal deterministic derivation and does not alter the external key model. Such numeric settings are not claimed as additional secret inputs and do not enlarge the stated key space beyond that of the master key K.

Repeat for all blocks

Each block is subjected to permutation and four-pixel SPE. The schedule may be advanced continuously across blocks or deterministically diversified by block/group context, provided that the same rule is reproduced during decryption. In the present design, the key is generated deterministically by the key schedule rather than supplied as an additional secret or derived from plaintext-dependent initialization. Once every block has been processed, the encrypted channels are recombined to form the final encrypted image.

Decryption

The decryption process is similar to the encryption process. Given the same master key K, public image identifier ID, and configuration, the receiver recomputes the same deterministic schedules and reproduces the same permutation order and values. For each block, the inverse sum-preserving transform is applied group by group, including the deterministic tail rule of Section “Sum-preserving substitution”, and the inverse permutation is then applied to restore the original order. Because the substitution mapping and the permutation are both bijective under the stated case conditions, the original image is recovered exactly.

Advantages of four-pixel grouping

The architecture and process of code execution are illustrated in Fig. 1. Using four-pixel encryption rather than three-pixel encryption has several technical benefits, as explained in the following subsections.

Code execution architecture and process.

No unencrypted leftovers

The use of groups of four pixels means that common block sizes (), which are divisible by four, not three—can be evenly accommodated. This prevents a single unmodified pixel from being left behind in many blocks, which is a weakness of the three-pixel scheme when block dimensions are not multiples of three. In the three-pixel method, for an block, 63 pixels would be encrypted in 21 triplets, and 1 pixel would be left over; by contrast, in the four-pixel scheme, the 64 pixels are encrypted in 16 quadruplets. Therefore, the four-pixel scheme ensures that every pixel in each block is encrypted for power-of-2 block sizes, eliminating trivial plaintext leakage.

Natural integration with byte-oriented systems

The grouping of four 8-bit pixels into a 32-bit container improves data alignment and regularizes load/store patterns, which is favorable for vectorized implementations. However, practical SIMD/GPU acceleration is not guaranteed by 32-bit packing alone, because the current four-case SPE substitution remains piecewise and branch-heavy. Accordingly, four-byte grouping is presented here as an accelerator-friendly data layout, while branch-reduced or mask-based substitution remains the next implementation step for higher-throughput hardware realization.

Improved security per block

As discussed, larger groups result in fewer pixel groups in the image and a higher degree of mixing. An attacker attempting to analyze statistical relationships must contend with more complex mapping for each block. The diffusion within a four-pixel group is higher than within a three-pixel group. Altering one pixel before encryption usually changes the other three positions in the four-pixel group because the modification must be balanced across the group to preserve the sum. By contrast, in a three-pixel group, altering one pixel primarily affects only the two remaining pixels, resulting in comparatively lower local diffusion. Neither scheme provides global diffusion across the entire image because of the sum-preserving constraint; however, four-pixel grouping provides slightly higher local diffusion and increases the complexity of differential cryptanalysis. Since each observable block sum is now produced by four pixels rather than three, the solution space consistent with a given observed sum is larger, which increases plaintext ambiguity at the block level.

Stability and reversibility

The four-pixel algorithm was carefully tested in this study at all extreme values to ensure that no overflow or underflow occurs that would lead to irreversibility. The boundary condition that requires careful handling occurs when the sum of S approaches its maximum value. Experimental evaluation

This section evaluates the proposed four-pixel sum-preserving encryption (4px-SPE) scheme by comparison with a three-pixel TPE baseline under consistent experimental settings. It is evident that TPE intentionally preserves thumbnail information, thus necessitating the execution of the evaluation under a threat model in which an adversary is able to observe ciphertexts and the resulting thumbnails. Furthermore, the evaluation is conducted under the assumption that the adversary is granted limited oracle access in chosen-plaintext or known-plaintext scenarios. In addition to the statistical tests of entropy, correlation, NPCR/UACI, cryptanalysis-oriented considerations and the clarification of the security scope are discussed in Section “Threat model and cryptanalysis-oriented discussion (CPA)”. In the interest of fairness and reproducibility, the experiments utilize both grayscale and color images, with the block size, b, varying from 8 to 64 to ascertain its effect. The secret-key material is defined as a fixed-length master key (Section “Key definition, parameter selection, and key-space bound”); all generator parameters/seeds are deterministically derived from this master key rather than being selected “arbitrarily”. In order to isolate the influence of the chaotic generator, controlled comparisons are performed by fixing the SPE mode (3px, 4px) and varying the keystream generator (key kind), and by fixing key kind and varying the permutation generator (perm kind). In this revision, the generator modules are instantiated using key_kind (logistic, mcml) and perm_kind (logistic, mcml, ijcml). The configuration of each instance is denoted by the following convention: SPE/key_kind/perm_kind (e.g. 4px/mcml/ijcml). For each metric reported in Sections “Visual inspection and thumbnail fidelity”–“Threat model and cryptanalysis-oriented discussion (CPA)”, a consistent reading protocol is followed. The first method is keystream ablation, in which the SPE and perm_kind are fixed, and the key_kind is compared. The second method is permutation ablation, in which the SPE and key_kind are fixed, and the perm_kind is compared.

Pixel correlation and robustness analysis

In natural images, the correlations between neighboring pixels and across color channels are often strong. Encryption should remove these correlations, leaving the ciphertext’s neighboring pixels statistically independent. To quantify this effect, we evaluated the pre-encryption and post encryption correlations of pixel intensity in grayscale and color images for the three-pixel and four-pixel schemes. For typical grayscale images, the coefficients of correlation between adjacent pixels and between horizontal, vertical, or diagonal neighbors are very high. Visually, this decorrelation was obvious: for the plaintext, scatter plots of adjacent pixel intensities formed a tight diagonal cluster, whereas for the encrypted image, the scatter plot points were widely and uniformly dispersed with no discernible pattern, indicative of near-zero correlation.

In the case of color images, the correlations within each color channel and between different channels were examined. The behavior exhibited within each channel was found to bear a resemblance to that observed in grayscale images; encryption effectively reduces the initially high intra channel neighbor correlations to approximately zero. Due to the substantial variations in the RGB components of natural scenes, the original color images exhibited strong cross-channel correlations. Following the encryption process using the four-pixel scheme, the cross-channel correlations were found to be considerably weaker. Utilizing the three-pixel scheme resulted in outcomes that were highly comparable. It was evident that both methods were capable of disrupting the majority of cross-channel statistical relationships. When the same chaotic keystream was synchronously applied to all color planes, a moderate degree of residual inter channel correlation persisted. This residual coupling occurred because the utilization of the same permutation and substitution operations in each channel meant that some intensity patterns common across those channels were preserved.

As demonstrated in Table 3, the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) is reported for decrypted versus original to confirm lossless recovery. Structural similarity metrics for cipher versus original are reported separately to reflect disruption. The differences between three-pixel and four-pixel schemes in terms of entropy and structural metrics were negligible. The merits of the four-pixel scheme are more qualitative than quantitative, including its full encryption of all pixels in a block and the potential for performance enhancement. Furthermore, it was ascertained that the encryption of color images yielded analogous outcomes for the two schemes. To illustrate this, consider a color photograph encrypted with the four-pixel scheme. The average per-channel entropy of this image was approximately 7.88 bits, which is considerably higher than the original value of approximately 7.23 bits. Furthermore, the cross-channel correlations were found to be significantly weaker, as evidenced by coefficients of approximately 0.14 compared to the original image. These values were virtually identical to those for the same image subjected to three-pixel encryption. The four-pixel SPE scheme has been demonstrated to generalize effectively to color images, exhibiting no discernible decline in performance.

Key space and sensitivity

The key space is governed by the 256-bit master key K, and per-image diversification is introduced by the public identifier ID together with deterministic context-dependent derivation. The present implementation does not rely on plaintext-dependent activation of chaos, and it does not require plaintext hashes or external chaotic parameters such as , , or to be supplied as independent secrets. Internal generator states are derived from K and the relevant schedule context. Therefore, any sensitivity observed when a derived internal state is perturbed should be interpreted as sensitivity of the keyed schedule, not as evidence that xinit is an independently shared secret. If K is compromised, the deterministic schedules can be recomputed; accordingly, the present scheme is not claimed to provide key-leakage resilience or one-time-pad security. An attacker can observe many groups with known sums, but in the four-pixel scheme the number of groups is lower and each group has an additional pixel, making brute-force search for the content of a specific group more difficult. As demonstrated in Table 4, the security improvement of the four-pixel scheme does not arise from an inflated total key range, but from stronger per-block ambiguity and cleaner block handling. Increasing the group size from three to four pixels raises per-block combinatorial complexity, removes standard-block leftovers, and increases the ambiguity of plaintext recovery when only the preserved sum is known.

In summary, the four-pixel SPE scheme demonstrates equivalent or marginally superior overall performance across most evaluated metrics when compared with the three-pixel TPE scheme. The system has been demonstrated to generate usable thumbnails, produce ciphertext that is both high in entropy and low in intelligibility, and support exact decryption under the stated conditions. The theoretical advantages of technology are twofold: firstly, there is an absence of leftover pixels, and secondly, there is better block alignment. These advantages were confirmed in a series of practical experiments. For instance, in instances where b is not a multiple of three, the three-pixel implementation is required to explicitly address the remaining pixel within each block. Conversely, the four-pixel scheme efficiently processes all pixels for b values that are multiples of two.

It is evident that Thumbnail Preserving Encryption is not equivalent to conventional encryption methods due to its intrinsic design. It is important to note that both methods are subject to limitations in terms of the number of pixels per change rate (NPCR) and the unified average changing intensity (UACI). Consequently, the utilization of these schemes should be confined to scenarios where the acceptance of information leakage (the thumbnail) is deemed permissible in exchange for a computational or usability advantage. In this scenario, the four-pixel scheme is evidently the preferred option due to its capacity for seamless integration with the standard block sizes and its tendency to yield slightly higher confusion.

Threat model and cryptanalysis-oriented discussion (CPA)

Threat model and scope. Within the TPE configuration, an adversary may gain access to ciphertext images and the corresponding thumbnails, and may attempt to exploit the intentionally preserved low-frequency information. Consequently, a distinction must be made between: The first method is that of ciphertext-only observation, which includes access to thumbnails. The second method is that of chosen-plaintext attacks (CPA), in which the adversary is able to submit selected images to the encryption oracle and observe the outputs. The primary objective of TPE is not semantic security; rather, it is designed to prevent the recovery of full-resolution or high-frequency content or the secret key under these realistic leakage assumptions.

The present implementation assumes that K is protected by conventional key-management practice. If K is compromised, the deterministic per-image and per-block schedules can be recomputed from K together with the public identifier ID and the relevant context. Accordingly, the scheme is not presented as a one-time-pad construction and is not claimed to provide key-leakage resilience beyond the protection of K itself. The limitations of statistical tests in this context are discussed. High entropy, low correlation, and favorable NPCR/UACI values are useful indicators of diffusion and randomness. However, they do not preclude structural attacks in themselves. The subsequent discourse will address the matter of CPA considerations in the context of TPE sum-preservation. In the context of sum-preserving designs, the preservation of the block-sum constraint gives rise to inherent information leakage. As SPE/TPE enforces intra-block sum preservation, the effect is confined to the block containing the perturbed pixel. Consequently, evaluation of local diffusion is undertaken within the affected block, as opposed to global diffusion over the full image. Fig. 6 presents a plot of the function NPCR (ΔNPCR = NPCR − 99.6094%) and the function UACI (ΔUACI = UACI - 33.4635%) versus block size for configurations that are labelled by SPE/key_kind/perm_kind. As the block size increases, the curves consistently approach 0, thus indicating that local diffusion is improved. It is imperative to acknowledge that TPE is not designed to supersede conventional encryption methods (e.g., AES) in applications necessitating comprehensive cryptographic confidentiality. The proposed four-pixel SPE scheme has been designed for scenarios involving the preservation of thumbnails, where the controlled leakage of information is an explicit requirement, and where the primary security objective is the protection of high-frequency or full-resolution content.

Computational complexity and runtime

Timing-side-channel scope. The current Python reference implementation is not presented as strict constant-time code. The four-case substitution remains branch-heavy, and such data-dependent control flow can create timing variability on modern CPUs. A branch-reduced or mask-based implementation is therefore the appropriate mitigation path. In the current timing benchmark, the variance ratio (extreme/random) decreases from 14.88 in the branchy kernel to 0.867 in the branch-reduced kernel, while random-input throughput increases from 0.677 to 1.463 MPix/s. We therefore present branch reduction as a mitigation that reduces timing sensitivity, not as a solved constant-time guarantee. Constant-time hardening remains future low-level work, for example in C/CUDA kernels. As illustrated in Fig. 7, the Pareto frontier diagram (time vs. security) provides a comprehensive analysis of the objectives. The hypothesis that lower encryption time is preferable (where x is the variable) is supported by the evidence that higher entropy is preferable (where y is the variable). In the context of multi-objective optimization, a point that exhibits improvement in at least one objective without compromising any other objective is said to “dominate” another point. The Pareto frontier is defined as the set of all non-dominated solutions that are not dominated by other points. The Pareto frontier, in its strict sense, is concerned with the existence of a configuration that is not inferior to any other objective, or which even dominates other solutions.

Conclusions

The present paper puts forward a chaotic-map-based four-pixel SPE encryption algorithm as an enhancement of the three-pixel TPE scheme that was previously reported. The novel four-pixel SPE method maintains the fundamental principle of preserving block pixel sums to ensure thumbnail visibility, while introducing a more robust grouping mechanism that is consistent with power-of-two block sizes. It was demonstrated that the chaotic map can effectively drive pixel replacement and block permutation with superior entropy and sensitivity, thus ensuring a high degree of randomness in the ciphertext. The mathematical design of the four-pixel transformation was elaborated in detail, and the algorithm was proven to guarantee reversibility and lossless restoration of original images. The current implementation uses a fixed 256-bit master key with deterministic public-ID-based diversification; it does not rely on plaintext-derived key generation and should not be interpreted as offering one-time-pad security or resilience to master-key compromise.

The structural distortion achieved in the four-pixel scheme results in low SSIM values and PSNR values approaching infinity upon decryption. This result demonstrates that enhancing security can be achieved through more assertive measures. Crucially, in the four-pixel SPE scheme, all pixels in a typical block configuration are fully encrypted, which enhances security by not exposing any plaintext pixels. As a trade-off inherent to sum-preserving encryption, the scheme exhibits limited global diffusion, which is reflected in near-zero NPCR (Number of Pixel Change Rate) and UACI (Unified Average Changing Intensity) values when compared to conventional non-sum-preserving encryption methods. This is an expected characteristic of TPE schemes, where the preservation of block sums for thumbnail generation necessarily constrains the degree of pixel-level diffusion achievable. This is a recognized trade-off of our scheme, which is suitable for applications in which the displaying of thumbnails is a feature rather than a bug. In this scenario, the scheme provides a high level of privacy protection, as was demonstrated by the failure of face detection on sufficiently large, encrypted blocks without sacrificing practicality. The proposed four-pixel scheme is a promising candidate for practical deployment in cloud storage, image archiving, and content management systems requiring encrypted thumbnails. In summary, the four-pixel SPE scheme demonstrates comparable performance across the evaluated metrics, with metric-dependent trade-offs when compared with the three-pixel TPE scheme, while maintaining the same ciphertext format and sum-preserving property. The controlled generator ablations (key_kind/perm_kind) further demonstrate the stability of the main trends across chaotic-module choices, and that block size remains the dominant factor in the thumbnail and security trade-off. It is hypothesized that this work will contribute to the development of more secure and user-friendly image encryption solutions and lay the foundation for future innovations that bridge the gap between the complete privacy and functional usability of visual materials.

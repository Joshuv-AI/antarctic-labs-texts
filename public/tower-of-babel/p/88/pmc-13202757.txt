Resequencing elucidates the genetic mechanism underlying elite early-maturing potato parent Kexin23
Authors: Yue Wang, Yunfei Gao, Qiao Wang, Bin Yan, Zhimin Niu, Xiangri Nan, Wanmin Sheng, Qingquan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, we provide the first molecular-level dissection of the elite tetraploid potato parent Kexin23, elucidating the genetic basis of its superior breeding performance. Kexin23 exhibited an early maturity trait that was comparable to its female parent Kexin4 and stably transmitted to its progeny, indicating the inheritance of robust early-maturity genetic segments. Genetic structure analysis based on 465,000 SNP variants demonstrated that the parental contributions of Aula and Kexin4 to Kexin25 exceeded that to Kexin23, reflecting an asymmetric genetic contribution to Kexin23. A total of 5,147 Kexin23-specific variants were identified, which was significantly more than that in its sister line Kexin25 with 70 located in coding regions. Among these, the key gene C88_C12H1G014800 (encoding a zinc-finger protein) contained a premature termination mutation that suppressed expression, potentially associated with early maturity or high pollen viability. SNP analysis indicated that loci derived from Kexin23 in its offspring were enriched on chromosome Chr1-3 (with high Aula contribution) and chromosomes Chr2-1 and Chr4-2 (with high Kexin4 contribution). Contribution analysis revealed that Kexin23 transmitted an average of 55.75% of its genetic material to its offspring, with 44.02% derived from specific variants. These findings systematically reveal the convergence of asymmetric inheritance, elite-specific variation, and selective haplotype transmission in the formation of a superior parent. This study establishes a molecular framework for understanding elite parentage in tetraploid potato and provides concrete targets for marker-assisted selection and genomic editing to accelerate the breeding of early-maturing cultivars.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08684-7.Keywords: Potato, Elite Parent, Kexin23, Early-Maturing

Background

Potato (Solanum tuberosum) is the third largest staple food crop globally, with tubers rich in starch, proteins, vitamins, and other nutrients [1]. China is the largest potato producer, accounting for approximately 25% of global annual output [2]. Compared with other crops, potato has advantages such as strong stress tolerance and ease of cultivation, rendering it vital for regions with harsh natural conditions [3]. However, conventional breeding remains the predominant approach for potato improvement, resulting in long breeding cycles and slow progress [4]. Accelerating this process through optimized breeding strategies is essential for developing high-quality cultivars, expanding arable land use, and ensuring food security [5].

Whole-genome resequencing detects sequence variations, including single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and insertions/deletions (InDels), by comparing individual genomes with a reference sequence [6]. This technology has been widely applied in crop breeding, with a high-quality reference genome being fundamental for genetic analysis [7]. Since the release of the first doubled-monoploid potato reference genome (DM1-3516 R44) in 2011, several studies have generated and reassembled potato genomes [8]. Huang et al. reported a haplotype-resolved genome for the heterozygous diploid RH89-039-16 [9]. Subsequently, haplotype-phased, high-quality reference genomes for the cultivars ‘Otava’, ‘Atlantic’, ‘Cooperation 88’, and ‘Qingshu 4’ were assembled [10, 11]. Genetic studies of potato germplasms using these resources can facilitate molecular breeding and advance breeding strategies.

China possesses less than one-tenth of the global potato germplasm resources, which severely restricts breeding progress [12]. Although the introduction of foreign cultivars may improve efficiency, such cultivars often exhibit poor adaptability, high cultivation requirements, and low stress resistance [13]. Recent geopolitical challenges have further limited international germplasm exchange. Consequently, genetic analysis of domestic resources has become essential for optimizing breeding techniques and accelerating improvements [14]. Elite parents are widely used in breeding programs or as key parental sources, with broad application value [15]. They typically combine multiple desirable traits with high fitness and represent one of the most valuable germplasm categories for achieving breeding breakthroughs [16, 17]. Elite parents not only carry superior haplotype blocks but also exclude deleterious alleles associated with unfavorable traits [18, 19]. Pedigree analysis involving elite parents enables the clarification of their genetic characteristics and identification of genomic regions under artificial selection during crop improvement [19]. Such analyses have revealed superior chromosomal segments in rice (Oryza sativa L.) [20, 21], wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) [22], and maize (Zea mays L.) [23]. However, due to the predominance of tetraploid potato cultivars, related investigations remain relatively scarce [24]. Identifying key loci in tetraploid potatoes presents multiple challenges [25]: the genome exhibits high heterozygosity and abundant structural variations, leading to complex genetic analysis; polyploid characteristics further induce non-Mendelian inheritance phenomena including double meiosis, complex gene-dosage effects, and allele interactions, making it difficult to establish quantitative associations between genotypes and phenotypes [26]. Furthermore, beneficial alleles are often linked to deleterious mutations, further hindering the precise identification and effective utilization of key genes [27]. Therefore, systematically deciphering the genetic composition and inheritance patterns of superior tetraploid potato parents holds significant theoretical and practical importance for efficiently utilizing existing germplasm resources and advancing potato genetic improvement.

Early-maturing potatoes allow double cropping in cold regions, improve land-use efficiency, extend processing periods, and enhance commercial value [28]. Shorter maturity also reduces risks from variable natural disasters [29]. As an early-maturing fresh-market variety bred from Kexin4 (♀) and Aula (♂) at the Heilongjiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Kexin23 is characterized by a short growth duration, rapid tuber bulking, high marketable tuber rate, and superior quality. As a parent, Kexin23 exhibits strong combining ability and produces outstanding offspring. Several breeding teams in China have employed Kexin23 as an elite parent to develop registered varieties including Longshu16, Longshu18, Longshu19, Longshu20, Longshu22, Longshu24, Longshu28, Longshu30, and Benshu12. In this study, Kexin23, its parents, sister line, and 22 derived offspring were resequenced and combined with phenotypic analyses to elucidate the genetic basis of its elite parent status. The results revealed low variation in offspring maturity and identified 5,147 Kexin23-specific SNPs. Among them, the key gene C88_C12H1G014800, which encodes a zinc-finger protein, carries a premature termination mutation that significantly suppresses the expression of downstream flowering genes. On average, Kexin23 contributed 55.75% of its genetic material to 22 offspring, with 44.02% attributed to specific variants. Hotspot regions of artificial selection were identified on Chr1-3, Chr4-2, and Chr2-1. This study provides the first molecular-level insights into elite parent formation in potato and establishes a foundation for efficient breeding.

Methods

Plant materials and pedigree

The elite parent Kexin23, its sister line Kexin25, and their parental lines (Kexin4 and Aula) were used as the core materials in this study. Kexin23 and Kexin25 were bred by the Heilongjiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences through a cross between the early-maturing variety Kexin4(used as the female parent) and the late blight–resistant variety Aula (introduced from Germany, used as the male parent). Using Kexin23 as a parent, 22 derived cultivars or advanced lines were subsequently developed. A schematic pedigree diagram illustrating the relationships among all these materials is provided in Fig. 1A.

Pedigree of Kexin23 (A) and statistical analysis of its maturity trait (B)

Field trial design and phenotypic evaluation

The Minzhu Farm potato breeding station has been utilized as a long-term collaborative experimental site for potato genetic improvement by the Institute of Industrial Crop, Heilongjiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences. All cultivars or lines were grown at Minzhu Farm (Harbin; 45°49′ N, 126°50′ E; altitude: 127.95 m). The yield and starch content were evaluated using a randomized block design with three replicates. Each plot measured 20 m2 and contained five rows of 5 m length, with a row spacing of 0.80 m and plant spacing of 0.25 m [30]. Survey aisles were 1.5 m wide with a 1 m wide isolation band [30]. The onset of maturity was recorded after more than 50% of the plants in a row had emerged, and the end date was recorded when more than 50% of the plants in a row showed yellowing of their aerial parts. The difference in days between these two points was used to calculate the maturity period and statistically analyzed for each row within each plot. After maturity, the plants from the plots were harvested for yield and starch content analyses. For starch determination, 5 kg of tubers were sampled from each plot, with ten replicates set for each sample [30]. Statistical significance was assessed using t-test, with ** indicating a highly significant difference (P < 0.01) and * indicating a significant difference (P < 0.05).

Sample collection and DNA extraction

Approximately 100 mg of in vitro seedling leaves (3–5 pieces) from each of the 26 potato cultivars or lines were collected in 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen. The frozen samples were ground into powder, and genomic DNA was extracted using the CTAB method. The DNA quality was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis, Nanodrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and Qubit 2.0, with the samples retained if the OD values were between 1.8 and 2.0 and a total amount above 1.5 µg. Qualified DNA was used for library construction. After quality examination, DNA was randomly fragmented into 350 bp segments using a Covaris instrument. The TruSeq Nano DNA HT kit was used for library construction following the manufacturer’s instructions. The DNA fragments were processed using end repair, polyA tailing, adaptor ligation, purification, PCR amplification, and other standard steps. The Qubit 2.0 system was adopted for preliminary quantification, while Agilent 2100 was employed to confirm the insert size. qPCR analysis was performed to quantify the effective library concentration that was required to exceed 2 nM. Libraries that passed quality control were sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform according to library concentration and sequencing demand.

Bioinformatics analysis

First, the DNBSEQ high-throughput sequencing results were converted into raw sequencing reads (RawData or Raw Reads) after sequence base identification using WriteFQ, and the quality distribution was assessed. Raw sequencing reads were processed using fastp with stringent criteria to remove low-quality bases, adapters, and reads, generating high-quality clean reads for downstream analysis. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome using Sentieon tnscope aligner, which is optimized for accuracy and speed. To mitigate mapping errors and improve base quality scores—critical for reliable variant detection at lower coverage—we performed duplicate read marking and base quality score recalibration (BQSR) using Sentieon tools, following GATK Best Practices [31]. SNPs and InDels were annotated using SnpEff to identify the genomic regions of the variants and their mutation types. The variants were further annotated using a left-aligned VCF format with HGVS nomenclature. Genome-wide variant analysis was conducted using 100-kb windows. The variant information was analyzed using PHYLIP, and the filtered variants were applied to construct a neighbor-joining tree to illustrate the pedigree of the varieties.

Based on the sequencing and comparison results, a phylogenetic evolutionary tree was constructed using MEGA 7 and the iTOL online platform. The IBS values of the paired samples were calculated using PLINK v1.9, and the IBS matrix heat map was generated using GraphPad 8.0. Protein sequence alignment and functional analysis were performed using Swiss-Prot database. Protein structural domain prediction was performed using SMART.

Calculation of genetic contribution

Based on the resequencing data and analysis results, the number of loci with differences between the parents of each cultivar was determined. The number of loci shared with the male or female parents was also calculated. The contribution rate of a parent to the cultivar was defined as the ratio of the number of identical loci with the parent to the total number of loci differing between parents, representing the proportion of the parental genetic contribution.

Sample collection and RNA extraction

Leaf, flower, and tuber samples (approximately 0.5 cm in diameter, taken from the center) from Kexin4, Aula, Kexin23, and Kexin25 potato plants were collected 50 d after planting. At least 20 mg of each fresh tissue sample was weighed. A minimum of three biological replicates were collected for each sample, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at − 80 °C. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Beijing All-Style Gold Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). The RNA concentration was measured with NanoDrop2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and agarose gel electrophoresis was used to assess the purity and integrity (A260/A280 ratio between 1.8 and 2.0) [32].

Real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR

After RNA extraction, genomic DNA was removed, and cDNA was synthesized using the TransScript® One-Step gDNA Removal and cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (Beijing AllSpecialtyGold Biotech Co., Ltd.). The synthesized cDNA was analyzed by qRT-PCR using PerfectStar™ Green One-Step qPCR SuperMix (Beijing Beijing AllSpecialtyGold Biotech Co., Ltd.) with the corresponding primers. Primers used in this study are listed in Supplementary Table S16. Gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔCt relative quantification method with the StEIF-3e gene used as the reference.The experiment was performed in three technical replicates [33]. Significance was determined by multifactorial analysis of variances (ANOVAs), different letters represent significant differences (P < 0.05), while the same letters indicate no significant differences.

Results

Phenotypic variation in the Kexin23 pedigree

Analysis of the phenotypic data revealed distinct patterns of trait variation within the Kexin23 pedigree. For maturity, Kexin23 was significantly earlier than its male parent Aula, but not significantly different from its female parent Kexin4 or its sister line Kexin25 (Fig. 1B). Notably, most progeny of Kexin23 exhibited a significantly longer maturity period than Kexin23 itself (Fig. 1B). Regarding yield, both parental lines (Kexin4 and Aula) and the sister line Kexin25 outperformed Kexin23, and this trend of higher yield was also observed in the majority of its offspring (Fig. S1A). For tuber starch content, Kexin23 showed a significantly higher level than Kexin4, Kexin25, and most of its progeny, but a lower level than Aula and four specific offspring lines (Fig. S1B). These results indicate that while Kexin23 stably transmits its early maturity trait, yield and starch content are more variable in its progeny.

Further evaluation of offspring traits relative to Kexin23 revealed limited variation in maturity (CV = 8.205%), concentrated within the mid-early maturity range (75.2–96.6 d) (Table S1). In contrast, yield (CV = 37.52%) and tuber starch content (CV = 24.18%) demonstrated greater variability (Table S1). These findings suggest that Kexin23 transmits numerous maturity-related genes or segments to its offspring during the breeding process.

Resequencing-based variation and pedigree analysis

To examine genetic variation and inheritance patterns, whole-genome resequencing was performed on 26 cultivars or lines. A total of 349 Gb of clean data were obtained, with high-quality values (average Q20 = 98.97%, Q30 = 96.31%) and an average sequencing depth of 5.64 (Table S2). On average, 99.36% of the reads were mapped to the reference genome, ranging from 99.23% to 99.48% (Table S2).

Variant detection identified 46,498,047 SNPs, 5,021,119 InDels, and 323,435 compound variants (Tables S3–S5). SNPs predominantly caused missense mutations in the CDS regions, whereas InDels and compound variants frequently induced frameshift mutations (Tables S3–S5). Genome-wide density analyses showed SNP densities ranging from 0 to 0.12227, with an average of 0.015267 (Table S6), and InDel densities ranging from 0 to 0.01269, with an average of 0.001649 (Table S7). Amino acid replacement analysis indicated that most mutations were synonymous, whereas tryptophan mutations frequently generated stop codons, and methionine mutations were often converted to isoleucine (Fig. S2). These results demonstrate extensive SNP diversity, which partially affects amino acid translation.

A total of 1,400 conserved fragments without diversity were identified, mainly on chromosomes Chr5-3, Chr5-4, Chr6-3, Chr6-4, Chr8-3, Chr8-4, Chr10-2, Chr10-3, Chr10-4, Chr12-2, and Chr12-4. The longest fragment, located on Chr10-3, spanned approximately 14,900 kb, accounting for 24.38% of the chromosome, whereas the shortest fragment was only 100 kb (Fig. 2A and B; Table S8). Collectively, these fragments covered 818.25 Mb, indicating the presence of large conserved genomic regions within this pedigree.

Phylogenetic analysis based on genome-wide SNP data of the 26 cultivars and lines combined with identity-by-descent (IBS) analysis revealed high similarity between Kexin23 and its derivatives L2018112-9, Longshu18, and L2018112-3, whereas Kexin4 showed higher genomic similarity with Kexin25 (Fig. 2C). A notable pattern emerged where Aula exhibited stronger genetic similarity with a greater number of Kexin23 derivatives than Kexin23 itself did with some of its own progeny (Fig. 2C). This was quantified by genetic similarity coefficients, which confirmed that Aula shared the highest similarity with BE200158-13×KX23 — a level that even exceeded the similarity between Kexin23 and some of its derivatives, such as Longshu16 and Kexin25 (Fig. 2D and Table S9). This distinctive pattern suggests that specific genomic segments from Aula may have been consistently selected for and retained across multiple breeding generations within the Kexin23 lineage. Overall, these results demonstrate the close genetic relationships among the materials and reflect the structured outcomes of artificial selection during pedigree development.

Genetic Contribution of Aula and Kexin4 to Kexin23 and Kexin25

To characterize the genetic composition of Kexin23, SNP variants derived from Aula and Kexin4 in both Kexin23 and Kexin25 were identified and mapped across chromosomes (Table S10; Fig. 3A and B). In Kexin23, 436,039 SNP variants originated from Aula and 315,464 from Kexin4. In Kexin25, 413,382 SNP variants originated from Aula and 333,955 from Kexin4 (Table S10). These results aligned with the high genomic similarity observed between Kexin4 and Kexin25.

During inheritance, Kexin23 and Kexin25 acquired the highest number of Aula-derived SNP variants on chromosome Chr1-3, and the highest number of Kexin4-derived SNP variants on chromosome Chr4-2 (Fig. 3A and B; Table S10). This was likely influenced by the comparable number of variants detected on these chromosomes (Figs. 2A and 3A, and 3B). Additionally, Kexin23 accumulated a similar number of SNP variants from Kexin4 on Chr2-1 (Fig. 3B and Table S10).

Genetic contribution analysis using SNP loci revealed that Aula contributed 60.45% to Kexin23, exceeding the 55.72% contribution from Kexin4 (Fig. 3C and Table S11). Notably, although its sister line Kexin25 was derived from the same cross and received similar parental contribution rates (Aula: 58.98%, Kexin4: 56.89%), it has not been widely utilized as an elite parent in breeding like Kexin23 (Fig. 3D and Table S11). This suggests that specific genetic segments inherited or developed uniquely in Kexin23, rather than the overall contribution ratio alone, may be crucial for its elite parental traits. Specifically, Aula contributed most strongly to chromosome 6 of Kexin23 (average 66.81% on Chr6-2), while Kexin4 contributed most strongly to chromosome 4 (average 73.07% on Chr4-2) (Fig. 3C and Table S11).

Specific variant analysis in Kexin23 and Kexin25

To further investigate the origin of Kexin23 as an elite parent, Kexin23- and Kexin25-specific variants were analyzed (Fig. 4). In total, 5,147 specific variants were identified in Kexin23, mainly on chromosomes 1, 4, 5, and 12, with the highest numbers on Chr8-1, Chr1-1, and Chr1-2 (Fig. 4A and B; Table S12). In contrast, 4,263 specific variants were identified in Kexin25, primarily distributed on chromosomes 1, 2, 4, and 8 (Fig. 4A and C; Table S12). These findings indicate that Kexin23 has more specific variants than Kexin25 with distinct chromosomal distributions.

Analysis of specific variants of Kexin23 and Kexin25. A Chromosomal distribution of specific variants of Kexin23 and Kexin25. B Statistics for site-specific loci in the Kexin 23 genome. C Statistics for site-specific loci in the Kexin 25 genome. The bars of different colors represent different chromosomes

Seventy genes containing Kexin23-specific variants were located in coding sequences (CDS) (Table S13). Most variants were missense or synonymous, whereas only three genes (C88_C01H3G136010, C88_C12H1G014800, and C88_C12H1G026970) contained premature termination mutations. Further sequencing analysis revealed that C88_C01H3G136010 and C88_C12H1G014800 were intact in Aula, Kexin4, and Kexin25, whereas C88_C12H1G026970 exhibited premature termination in all varieties (Table S14). To evaluate the functional effects of these variants, the expression of the three genes was examined in flowers, leaves, and tubers using qRT-PCR (Fig. 5A). C88_C01H3G136010 expression in Kexin23 was comparable to that of the other three varieties across tissues, with no significant differences observed in tubers (Fig. 5A). In contrast, C88_C12H1G014800 expression in Kexin23 was significantly lower than that in the other three varieties across all tissues (Fig. 5A). C88_C12H1G026970 expression showed no significant difference in leaves and tubers among the four varieties, while the highest expression was detected in flowers of Aula (Fig. 5A). These results suggest that the premature termination of C88_C12H1G014800 in Kexin23 resulted in reduced expression in multiple tissues compared with the other varieties.

Premature termination of translation of the FES1 homologous gene (C88_C12H1G014800) in Kexin 23 resulted in a significant reduction in expression. A Expression analysis of genes carrying stop-gain mutations specific to Kexin 23. B Phylogenetic tree of C88_C12H1G014800. C Translation of C88_C12H1G014800 in Kexin 23 terminates before the ZnF-TTF domain. D Expression analysis of FLC homologous genes in potato

Based on phylogenetic analysis, C88_C12H1G014800 encodes a zinc-finger protein closely related to AtFES1 (AT2G33835) [34] (Table S14 and Fig. 5B). Structural domain analysis revealed the presence of ZnF-C3H1 and ZnF-TTF domains (Fig. 5C). The Kexin23-specific mutation caused premature termination of translation within the ZnF-TTF domain, whereas Kexin25 and Kexin4 carried only a single amino acid substitution (Fig. 5C). In Arabidopsis, AtFES1 interacts with AtFRI to activate AtFLC expression, thus delaying flowering. Comparison with the tetraploid potato genome identified PGSC0003DMT400016092 as the closest homolog of AtFLC (Fig. S3). qRT-PCR analysis confirmed that PGSC0003DMT400016092 expression was significantly reduced in flowers, leaves, and tubers of Kexin23 compared to Kexin25, Kexin4, and Aula (Fig. 5D), with the greatest reduction observed in the number of flowers and tubers (Fig. 5D). These results suggest that the premature termination of C88_C12H1G014800 in Kexin23 affects its normal protein function and downregulates the expression of downstream FLC homologs.

Genetic contribution of Kexin23 to its offspring

Kexin23 is one of the most widely used parents for potato hybrid breeding. It has been employed to develop several cultivars and lines. To conduct an in-depth analysis of the genetic contribution of Kexin23 to its derivatives, the number and chromosomal distribution of SNPs originating from Kexin23 were systematically quantified in several representative elite derivatives, and the corresponding genetic contribution of Kexin23 was subsequently evaluated (Table S15; Figs. S4 and 5). The derivatives contained more Kexin23-derived SNPs on chromosomes Chr1-3, Chr1-4, Chr5-1, Chr2-1, and Chr4-2 but fewer on Chr5-2, Chr5-3, and Chr12-4 (Table S15; Figs. S4 and 6A). Among these, nine derivatives had the largest number of SNPs from Kexin23 on Chr1-3, four on Chr1-4, and nine on Chr5-1 (Table S15 and Fig. S4). Aula contributed strongly to Kexin23 on Chr1-3, whereas Kexin4 contributed strongly to Chr4-2 and Chr2-1 (Fig. 3; Tables S10 and S11). These findings suggest that the superior SNP loci on Chr1-3, Chr4-2, and Chr2-1 were preferentially retained during selection for early maturity and disease resistance.

Genetic contribution analysis indicated that Kexin23 contributed an average of 55.75% to its 22 derivatives (Fig. 6B). The highest contribution was 62.62% in L201903-35, whereas the lowest was 48.65% in L2018112-3 (Fig. 6B). For all other derivatives, the contribution exceeded 50% (Fig. 6B). Analysis of Kexin23-specific variants revealed an average contribution of 44.02% to its derivatives (Fig. 6C). The highest contribution of specific variants was 52.48% in L201903-35, whereas the lowest was 39.26% in LS22 (Fig. 6C). These results demonstrated that Kexin23 contributed substantially to its offspring and that its specific variants also play an important role in their genetic makeup.

Discussion

In this study, whole-genome resequencing of the elite potato parent Kexin23 and its genetic population was performed to elucidate the mechanism of trait formation. Phenotypic analysis showed that the offspring of Kexin23 were more stable in terms of maturity (75.2–96.6 d, CV = 8.21%). Resequencing analysis further revealed that the parental genetic contribution to Kexin23 was lower than that to its sister line Kexin25. Moreover, 5,147 Kexin23-specific variants were identified, including the key gene C88_C12H1G014800, which was suppressed by a premature termination mutation that reduced expression of downstream flowering-related genes. This mutation might be associated with early maturity or pollen vigor. The average genetic contribution of Kexin23 to 22 offspring reached 55.75%, with 44.02% attributed to its specific variants. SNPs with high heritability across lineages were enriched on chromosomes Chr1-3, Chr2-1, and Chr4-2.

Aula introduced from Germany possesses favorable traits such as high resistance to late blight and high starch content but shows poor adaptability under Chinese conditions. As an early-maturing fresh-market variety developed at the Heilongjiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Kexin4 has several desirable traits but exhibits low resistance to late blight. However, because of severe bud abscission in Kexin4, few flower buds were available for hybridization, and only two early-maturing cultivars, Kexin23 and Kexin25, were obtained from its cross with Aula. Compared with Kexin4, Kexin23 has become an important elite parent in potato hybrid breeding due to its strong fitness and improved late blight resistance. Our results demonstrated that Aula contributed more to Kexin23 than Kexin4, reflecting an asymmetric parental contribution (Fig. 3C and D). Similar asymmetric parental contributions have also been reported for other crops, including the cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) cultivar Ekangmian9 and wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) cultivar Xinmai26 [35, 36]. Likewise, in Shengli Wheat/Yanda 1817, the parent also contributed more significantly [37]. Despite the greater contribution of Aula, the maturity of Kexin23 (75.2–96.6 d) was significantly shorter than that of Aula and was stably inherited by its offspring (CV = 8.21%) (Fig. 1B and Table S1). Additionally, both Aula and Kexin4 contributed more to Kexin25 than to Kexin23, while Kexin25 did not display the same level of elite parental characteristics (Fig. 3C and D; Table S11). These findings demonstrate that the value of an elite parent is not determined solely by the proportion of parental contribution, but more critically by the specific genomic segments inherited. Kexin23, unlike Kexin25, appears to have successfully consolidated specific beneficial haplotypes from both parents. This is supported by the preferential selection and transmission of regions on Chr1-3 (Aula-influenced), Chr4-2, and Chr2-1 (Kexin4-influenced) in its offspring (Fig. 3A and B, S4, and 5A; Tables S10 and S15). Furthermore, the identification of large, perfectly conserved genomic blocks suggests strong purifying selection within this pedigree, likely preserving regions vital for fundamental agronomic fitness. Furthermore, due to the potential for false positives in detecting variations within polyploid genomes [38], these loci can be further validated and screened through more in-depth sequencing combined with multi-environment phenotypic characterization.

In addition to parental genetic components, the genetic characteristics of cultivars depend on de novo functional variation during breeding. For example, the widely cultivated early-season rice cultivars Zhongjiazao17 and Jiayu67, which are sister lines with high yields, both harbor numerous specific variants compared with their parents [39]. Similarly, our study showed that Kexin23 carried 5,147 specific SNP variants (20.7% more than Kexin25), including 70 located in coding regions (Fig. 4; Tables S12 and S13). These variants were enriched on chromosomes Chr1-3, Chr1-4, and Chr5-1, forming genetic transmission hotspots under artificial selection (Figs. 4 and 5A; Tables S12 and S15). Studies of wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) cultivars Xinmai26 and Xinmai45 have also revealed large numbers of specific variants [36], which may contribute to the formation of key traits in offspring. In this study, Kexin23 contributed 55.75% of the average genetic material to 22 offspring, including 44.02% from specific variants, again demonstrating the efficient transmission capacity of these variants in hybrid breeding (Fig. 5B and C).

Interestingly, L201903-35, which had the highest contribution from Kexin23, exhibited lower yield and starch content (Figs. 2B, S1, 5B, and 5C; Table S1). Conversely, L2018112-3, which received the lowest contribution from Kexin23, demonstrated a higher yield (Figs. 2B and 5B, and 5C; Table S1). Similarly, Longshu22, with a low contribution of Kexin23-specific variants, displayed superior yield and starch content (Figs. 2B, S1, 5B, and 5C; Table S1). These results indicate that simply maximizing the genomic proportion from the elite parent does not guarantee optimal performance in all traits. Instead, the specific allelic combinations, including potential linkage drag or the loss of complementary alleles from the other parent, govern the final phenotype, particularly for complex yield-related traits. Pre-phenotyping analysis further revealed that the variation in maturity among Kexin23 offspring was only 8.21%, much lower than that for yield or starch content (Figs. 2B, S1, 5B, and 5C; Table S1). Most offspring showed a higher yield but a lower starch content than Kexin23 (Figs. 2B and S1; Table S1). These results indicate that the specific variants and genetic contribution of Kexin23 primarily affected maturity traits, while also influencing the balance of other agronomic characteristics.

SNP variants represent the molecular basis of genetic diversity in hybrid breeding, and their validation, marker development, and selection applications have been confirmed in many crops [40]. Functional SNPs can directly regulate key agronomic traits [40, 41]. For instance, a nonsense mutation in the second exon of the GS3 gene in rice can result in premature protein termination, making GS3 a negative regulator of grain size [42]. Similarly, tb1 (teosinte branched 1) in maize that can regulate branching and E1 in soybean regulating flowering can also contain nonsense mutations in coding regions that drive major phenotypic differences [43, 44]. In this study, genes located in the coding region of specific SNP variants in Kexin23 were analyzed, and a gene carrying a nonsense mutation with reduced expression, C88_C12H1G014800, was identified. This gene encodes a zinc-finger protein containing ZnF-C3H1 and ZnF-TTF domains and is homologous to AtFES1 in Arabidopsis [34](Fig. 5A and C; Table S14). AtFES1 functions together with AtFRI to activate AtFLC expression and delay flowering [34, 45]. The AtFLC homolog PGSC0003DMT400016092 was identified using homology blast, and its expression was most significantly downregulated in flowers and tubers of Kexin23 (Figs. 5D and S3). These results suggest that the truncation mutation of C88_C12H1G014800 in Kexin23 suppresses FLC expression (Fig. 5D), thereby accelerating development. The functional inference of C88_C12H1G014800 currently relies on homology and expression analysis. Direct validation through genetic transformation or the analysis of mutants is required to conclusively establish its role. In addition to agronomic advantages, high pollen viability and strong fitness could be important reasons why Kexin23 has become widely used in hybrid breeding. Previous studies have also reported zinc-finger proteins as negative regulators of pollen development [46, 47]. Therefore, the early termination of C88_C12H1G014800 in Kexin23 may contribute to its high pollen viability. Nevertheless, further functional characterization of C88_C12H1G014800, including genetic transformation, haplotype analysis, and molecular marker development, is required to fully evaluate its breeding value. Regarding the location of this key gene on Chr12-1, it is important to note that Kexin4’s highest overall contribution is to chromosome 4. This distinction highlights that major-effect genes controlling specific traits can reside outside genomic regions of the highest bulk parental contribution. This does not contradict the observation that haplotype blocks from Kexin4 on Chr4-2 and Chr2-1 are frequently transmitted; rather, it illustrates the multi-layered genetic architecture of elite traits, involving both key regulatory mutations and the coordinated inheritance of multi-gene haplotypes. Furthermore, while most identified mutations were synonymous and thus do not alter the amino acid sequence, their potential effects on codon usage, mRNA stability, or splicing should not be disregarded and warrant future investigation.

In summary, this study moves beyond conventional genetic diversity surveys by focusing on the genetic dissection of a proven elite parent, Kexin23, rather than comparing varieties or mapping traits in segregating populations. Although we employed established methods, their integrated application to deconstruct the genetic architecture of a key breeding parent provides novel insights into tetraploid potato breeding. Regarding data confidence, the variant calling pipeline was optimized for the moderate sequencing depth and the complexities of the tetraploid genome, employing joint calling and stringent filtering to enhance accuracy for high-confidence SNPs/InDels, though allele dosage determination remains a challenge. Furthermore, the phenotypic data were obtained from a single location. Given the environmental sensitivity of potato, multi-environment trials will be essential to confirm the stability of the reported trait associations, particularly for complex traits like yield and starch content. Chromosomes Chr1-3, Chr2-1, and Chr4-2 identified as regions preferentially selected for early maturity and high resistance were combined with maturity- or resistance-related QTLs to develop molecular markers for targeted breeding in the future. Moreover, targeted editing of the C88_C12H1G014800 homologue may enable the creation of new superior potato cultivars or parents. Future research should focus on validating the function of C88_C12H1G014800, fine-mapping the candidate regions on Chr1-3, Chr2-1, and Chr4-2, and conducting multi-environment phenotypic evaluations. By elucidating how specific genetic segments and key mutations converge in a successful parent, this work offers a refined perspective for utilizing elite parents in tetraploid potato hybrid breeding, accelerating the development of cultivars with desired traits.

Conclusions

In this study, we systematically analyzed the genetic basis of the elite parent Kexin23 as an early-maturing parent through whole-genome resequencing and phenotyping of its parents, sister lines, and 22 derived offspring. Kexin23 was shown to transmit early maturity traits with reduced variation in its progeny. A total of 5,147 specific SNP variants were identified in Kexin23, which was significantly more than that in its sister lines. Among these, the loss-of-function mutation in the zinc-finger protein gene C88_C12H1G014800 was particularly notable. The average genetic contribution of Kexin23 to its progeny was 55.75%, with a specific mutation contribution rate as high as 44.02%. Three genetic transmission hotspots were identified under artificial selection on Chr1-3, Chr2-1, and Chr4-2. These findings may provide a theoretical basis for early maturity molecular breeding and efficient utilization of elite parents in potato.

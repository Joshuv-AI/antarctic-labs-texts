The complete mitochondrial genome of Paraclius curvispinus Yang et Saigusa, 2001 (Diptera: Dolichopodidae)
Authors: Sihan Li, Shuangmei Ding, Chen Lin, Ding Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presented the first sequencing and analysis of the mitochondrial genome of Paraclius curvispinus Yang and Saigusa, 2001, a predator fly belonging to the family Dolichopodidae (long-legged fly). The complete mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus was 16,882 bp long (GenBank accession number: PP498802), which contained 13 protein-coding genes, 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs, and a control region. The nucleotide composition of mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus was 38.48% of A, 36.00% of T, 10.67% of G, and 14.85% of C. Maximum-likelihood (ML) analysis revealed the phylogenetic relationships within the subfamily Dolichopodinae, suggesting the following grouping: ((Dolichopus + Lichtwardtia)+((Hercostomus + Poecilobothrus)+ Gymnopternus))+ Paraclius. This study provides a basis for discussing the evolution of mosquito larval predation habits of Dolichopodinae.Keywords: Mitochondrial genome, Paraclius curvispinus, Dolichopodinae, Dolichopodidae, phylogeny

Introduction

The family Dolichopodidae is an important natural enemy insect resource. As one of the most diverse groups of Diptera, the Dolichopodidae is a monophyletic group with more than 6800 species (Yang et al. 2006, 2011). The Dolichopodinae is the largest subfamily of the Dolichopodidae and is of significant economic importance in consideration of five mosquito larvae-feeding genera: Dolichopus, Paraclius, Pelastoneurus, Poecilobothrus and Tachytrechus. These genera potentially serve as important natural enemies in insect pest management (Travis 1947). In these five genera, only Dolichopus and Poecilobothrus have the available mitochondrial genome data. In fact, the mitochondrial genetic data of the subfamily Dolichopodinae is relatively scarce. There are 1764 species in 33 genera recorded in this subfamily. However, GenBank has released data of 15 species from 5 genera with only 6 of them being complete.

The genus Paraclius is one of the most species-rich groups in the predatory Dolichopodidae. There are about 140 known species in the world and 33 known species of Paraclius in China (Yang et al. 2011; Lin and Yang 2021). The species Paraclius curvispinus Yang and Saigusa, 2001 we collected is an endemic species found in China, specifically distributed in Guizhou and Guangxi (Yang et al. 2011). In order to enhance the understanding of the internal relationships within Dolichopodinae, analyzing whether the predatory habits of mosquito larvae have evolved over evolutionary history, we sequenced and annotated the mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus. Combined with published sequences of Dolichopodinae, we discussed the phylogenetic relationship within the subfamily.

Materials and methods

Specimens

The specimens of P. curvispinus were collected by Xinhe Qiang on August 3, 2023, through light trapping in the Huaping National Nature Reserve, Guilin, Guangxi, China (25°37′53.80″N, 109°54′30.14″E), and deposited at the Entomological Museum (voucher number: D-PAR-1) of China Agricultural University (contact person: Ding Yang; e-mail: dyangcau@126.com). The diagnostic characteristics of P. curvispinus are as follows: postocular bristles black; antenna entirely black; first flagellomere 1.5 times longer than wide; fore tarsus normal; mid femur with 1 preapical bristle; dorsal margin and apex of hind femur black; hind tibia wholly yellow; cercus without long marginal bristles, with short hairs or bristles (Figure 1). Female and male individuals exhibit similar external appearances. The specimen used for DNA extraction was identified by Professor Ding Yang, preserved in 95% ethanol, and stored in a −20 °C freezer.

DNA exaction and sequencing

In this study, we used the DNeasy Blood & Tissue kit (QIAGEN, Germany) to extract total genomic DNA from the thoracic muscle of adult female specimens. The concentration of the extracted DNA was determined using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (241.5 ng/μl, A260/A280: 2.13). The DNA sample was sent to Novogene Bioinformatics Technology Co. Ltd (Beijing, China) for whole-genome sequencing using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencer (PE150).

Assembly and annotation

The raw data (9.15 GB) were trimmed and filtered using fastp 0.23.4 (Chen et al. 2018). The filtered data (8.78 GB) were assembled using MitoFinder v1.4.1 (Allio et al. 2020) with the default setting. The COI gene sequence obtained through Sanger sequencing was locally blasted using Bioedit v7.2.5 (Hall 1999) to capture the mitochondrial genome sequence of P. curvispinus following the procedures of Gillett et al. (2014). The sequencing depth and coverage map of P. curvispinus mitochondrial genome was presented in Figure S1. The mitochondrial genome was preliminarily annotated using MitoFinder v1.4.1, and the positions of each gene were determined through alignment with closely related species. The mitochondrial genome circle diagram was generated in OGDRAW v 1.3.1(Greiner et al. 2019). MEGA7 was used to analyze the nucleotide composition of the mitochondrial genome (Kumar et al. 2016). The bias of the nucleotide composition was calculated by the formulas: AT-skew = [A − T]/[A + T] and GC-skew = [G − C]/[G + C] (Perna and Kocher 1995).

Phylogenetic analysis

In this study, we used 12 mitochondrial genomes from the subfamily Dolichopodinae, comprising 11 previously published in GenBank and one newly sequenced genome, to reconstruct the phylogenetic tree. Condylostylus longicornis (Sciapodinae) and Condylostylus luteicoxa (Sciapodinae) were used as outgroups. All 13 PCGs and 2 rRNAs were employed in the datasets. The ModelFinder v2.2.0 (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017) module built-in PhyloSuite v1.2.3 (Zhang et al. 2020) was used for acquiring the optimal partitioning scheme and base substitution models (Table S1). The phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using the maximum-likelihood (ML) method, utilizing IQ-TREE (Nguyen et al. 2015) in PhyloSuite v1.2.3. The phylogenetic tree was visualized with FigTree v1.4.3.

Results

The complete mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus was 16,882 bp long (GenBank accession number: PP498802), which included 37 genes typical of the invertebrate mitochondrial genome (Figure 2). It contained 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs, and a control region. There were 22 genes (9 PCGs and 14 tRNAs) encoded by the J strand and 14 genes (4 PCGs, 8 tRNAs, and 2 rRNAs) encoded by the N strand. The gene arrangement was consistent with that of Drosophila melanogaster. The overall nucleotide composition of the mitochondrial genome was 38.48% of A, 36.00% of T, 10.67% of G, and 14.85% of C. It showed obvious bias of A and T. The value of AT-Skew was 0.03 and GC-Skew was −0.16. The nucleotide composition of the ATP8 and ND6 gene showed high bias toward A and T which is up to 80.25% and 80.57%, respectively. There were 14 intergenic regions and 11 overlapping regions in the whole mitochondrial genome. The longest intergenic region was 19 bp long, which were located between tRNAGlu and tRNAPhe, tRNAPro and ND6. The length of overlap regions ranged from 1 to 7 bp, and the longest overlap regions were located between ATP8 and ATP6, ND4 and ND4L. Most of the PCGs used ATN as the start codon except COI used TCG and ND5 used GTG, which is in common with most of the subfamily Dolichopodinae. For the termination codon, most PCGs used conservative TAA/TAG except ND4, which used a single base T.

Structure of the mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus, outer genes from the forward strand (J strand), and inner genes from the reverse strand (N strand).

The phylogenetic analysis suggested that genus Dolichopus was a monophyletic group, which was the sister group to genus Lichtwardtia. Hercostomus and Poecilobothrus constituted the sister group to genus Gymnopternus. The genus Paraclius was the sister group to the rest of the subfamily Dolichopodinae (Figure 3). In the previous studies, Pollet et al. (2010) used Bayesian analysis to reconstruct the phylogenetic tree based on COI, 12S, 16S genes. The analysis supported the monophyly of the genera Dolichopus and Gymnopternus. Gwynne et al. (2010) used two nuclear genes (18S, 28S) and four mitochondrial genes (12S, 16S, Cytb, COI) to resolve the relationships within Dolichopodidae. In this study, the genus Paraclius was basal to other Dolichopodinae species, genus Dolichopus was the sister group to genus Lichtwardtia. In our study, we demonstrated that mitochondrial genomics-level data similarly support some of the previous findings.

Discussion and conclusion

The mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus was 16,882 bp in length, including 37 genes and a control region. Its gene arrangement was the same as the published mitochondrial genome of the subfamily Dolichopodinae (Qilemoge et al. 2020; Lin et al. 2021; Qilemoge and Yang 2022; Wang et al. 2023). In this study, we represent the first sequencing and analysis of the mitochondrial genome of P. curvispinus, supplementing the mitochondrial genome data for the genus Paraclius, and reconstructing the phylogenetic relationships within the subfamily Dolichopodinae. Negrobov (1991) proposed a 12-subfamily system that suggested the genus Paraclius was placed in different tribes from several other genera within the subfamily Dolichopodinae. The phylogenetic results of our study further support the distinctiveness of the genus Paraclius. In our sampling, Dolichopus, Paraclius, and Poecilobothrus all prey on mosquito larvae, however, they do not share direct common ancestry in the phylogeny tree. Therefore, we assume that the habit of predation on mosquito larvae might evolved independently on multiple occasions. For a subfamily with nearly 1800 species, we still need more representative data and more comprehensive analytical methods for further phylogenetic research.

Microleakage Assessment of Repaired Pit and Fissure Sealants Using Different Surface Treatment – An In vitro Study
Authors: R K Sarada Devi, C. Nagarathna, Ratna Yumkham, Shamilah Khanam, Ishani Ningthoujam, Pukhrambam Nirmada
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:In this in vitro study, the microleakage evaluation of repaired pit and fissure sealants with various surface treatments was compared.Study Design:The study included 120 extracted human third molars. The teeth were divided into two groups of 60 each. Group 1 was sealed with Clinpro™ unfilled sealant, while group 2 was sealed with Grandioseal nanofilled sealants. After curing, half of the polymerized sealant were removed. Both groups were divided further into two subgroups of 30 teeth each, with repair techniques using either Adper Easy One or Futura Bond. Three sections were obtained from each tooth resulting 6 surfaces per tooth to be examined and the sections of all four groups were scored for microleakage.Results:Conventional acid etching was found to be superior to self-etch adhesive technique.Conclusion:Microleakage was found to be greater with Grandio seal compared to Clinpro.

INTRODUCTION

When a substance is applied to the occlusal pits and fissures of teeth that are vulnerable to cavities, it forms a protective coating that is micromechanically bonded to the tooth surface, preventing caries-causing bacteria from accessing their source of nutrition. This process is known as pit and fissure sealanting.[1] Pit and fissure selants were invented to prevent plaque, germs, and carbohydrates from entering tooth pits and fissures, thereby stopping or preventing the progress of the carious process.[23] Sealants are considered to be the most effective caries-prevention intervention available for children.[4]

The success of sealants relies on their long-term retention and appropriate micromechanical adherence to enamel surfaces.[5] Microleakage or marginal leakage refers to the penetration of oral fluids into the gap between the tooth and the restorative material.[6] As, incompletely sealed surfaces are more prone to deteriorate than fully sealed surfaces, sealants must be properly applied to be effective.[789] To prevent microleakage and formation of dental cavities, any fissure sealant replacement or repair should be done as effectively as possible.

There is currently very few research on the best methods for repairing inadequate or fractured fissure sealant. Therefore, the objective of this in vitro study was to assess and compare the microleakage of fixed pit and fissure sealants applied to permanent third molar teeth using different surface treatments.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Freshly extracted 120 human third molars were included. Teeth with good pit and fissure morphology were collected. They were stored in 0.2% thymol. Third molars with carious, restored, any developmental anomalies, incomplete root formation, and smaller occlusal table were excluded. The teeth were cleaned and grossly debrided. Prophylaxis brush were rotated with slow speed hand piece for 10 seconds to clean occlusal surfaces of the teeth. Air-water spray was used to rinse these teeth for 30 seconds. Compressed air was used to dry the teeth for 10 seconds. In this study, 120 extracted human third molars were included on random selection. Two groups were made consisting 60 teeth in each – group 1 and group 2. In both the experimental groups, the proximal half of the occlusal surface was etched for 15 seconds using 37% phosphoric acid, followed by a 30-second water spray wash and a drying period of 10-seconds by compressed air.

Clinpro™ unfilled sealant (3M ESPE) and Grandio seal nanohybrid filled fissure sealant (Voco) were applied on the entire occlusal surface after using a spoon excavator, and then cured with an LED light for 10 seconds. Half of the polymerized sealant was removed, and the spoon excavator was carefully taken out from the application site after curing. After storing in artificial saliva for 1 week, teeth were taken out. Both groups were subdivided further into two sub-groups (IA and IB; IIA and IIB) of 30 teeth each for applying repair techniques. In group IA, the occlusal surfaces were etched with 37% phosphoric acid. The teeth were resealed with Clinpro™ fissure sealant on denuded proximal half of the teeth, allowing to flow the sealant over the area and cured for 10 seconds. In group IB, a one-step self-etch adhesive was applied followed by Adper easy one 3M ESPE application and those were resealed with Clinpro™ fissure sealant over the teeth’s denuded proximal half, allowing it to spread over the other proximal half, and curing it for ten seconds. In group IIA, the surface treatment was same as carried out in group IA. On the denuded proximal half of the teeth, Grandio Seal nanohybrid filled fissure sealant was applied, allowing it to flow over the other proximal half, and cured for 10 seconds. Group IIB teeth’s occlusal surfaces were treated with a one-step self-etch adhesive called Futurabond Voco. Grandio Seal nanohybrid filled fissure sealant was used to reseal the teeth. It was applied to the denuded proximal half of the teeth, allowed it to flow over the other proximal half, and cured for ten seconds. After treatment, the teeth were kept for a week in artificial saliva. After sealing the apices of the teeth with sticky wax, the teeth were covered in two coats of nail varnish, leaving a 1 mm space gap between layers. The teeth were submerged in a 1% aqueous solution of methylene blue for 48 hours at 37°C, at pH 7.0 buffer. After being cleaned with tap water, the teeth were embedded in separate blocks of self-cured transparent acrylic resin and longitudinally sectioned into four slices in the mesiodistal plane using a water-cooled diamond disc bur at a slow speed. Three parts of each tooth was obtained and six surfaces of them were examined. [10] Each of the ninety sections of the four groups were examined under a stereomicroscope. The marginal leakage rating score from Grande et al. was applied [Figure 1].[10] The scores for each of the 120 teeth’s parameters were noted on a data sheet. Using SPSS and the Chi-square test, the collected data was tabulated and statistically analyzed.

RESULTS

Total 720 surface were recorded i.e. 6 surfaces per tooth. The microleakage scores were recorded according to section level analysis. Dye penetration was observed within the two groups of sealants. It was discovered that the self-etch adhesive method was less effective than conventional acid etching at reducing microleakage in the repaired pit and fissure sealants. Compared to the unfilled Clinpro™ sealant, more microleakage was observed in the filled Grandio seal nanohybrid sealant. DISCUSSION

Pit and fissure sealants have been shown to have a caries-prevention impact. Six Fissure sealant’s primary issue is microleakage, which impedes the sealant’s ability to be placed successfully over the long term. Stresses related to polymerization shrinkage or a lack of resin adherence to the tooth surfaces might produce microleakage at the resin-tooth interface.[1112] A surface that has partially lost a sealant is just as vulnerable to caries as one that has never been sealed. Thus, for the sealant to work, careful recall and repair are needed.[13] Assays using dyes for in vitro microleakage are thought to be more definitive than oral microleakage assays.[14] This is probably caused by a number of things, including the diffusion of color more readily than bacteria and their waste products.[15] You can use a variety of dyes, such as 0.2% Rhodamine B, silver nitrate, and 1%–4% Methylene blue.[16] Dye penetration tests have the potential to be non-destructive, time-efficient, and it is able to show leakage without the requirement of a chemical reaction or exposure to dangerous radiation.[17] 120 permanent third molars were removed and chosen for our investigation. The selection of third molars was based on their accessibility, deep fissures, lack of caries, and frequent extraction as impacted teeth. Many research compared and evaluated the marginal leakage of fissure and pit sealants using third molars.[17181920] A tiny spoon excavator was placed in the untreated half of the occlusal surface to assist the simple removal of sealant from the unetched area of all the samples. This happened because, the enamel had not been roughened prior to cleaning the teeth surface with a prophylactic brush. The surface of enamel is typically hydrophobic, low energy, and weakly reactive. But when 37% phosphoric acid is used in the traditional acid etching method, the enamel turns into a highly reactive, high energy, hydrophilic surface. The sealer adheres to the enamel surface quickly because of its high energy state.[21] Self-etch adhesives have the advantage of simultaneously demineralizing and penetrating the tooth surface to the same depth, therefore presumably providing full adhesive penetration.[22] Since the single-component one-step adhesive combines “conditioning,” “priming,” and “application of the adhesive resin,” it can be regarded as the only genuine “one-bottle” or “all-in-one” adhesive. This adhesive resin is less technique sensitive and requires less time to apply. Thus, in order to compare with the traditional acid-etch method, we employed Futurabond DC and Adper Easy One, one-step self-etch adhesives. The groups IB (72.2%) and IIB (56.7%) had the highest dye penetration into the occlusal third of the enamel-sealant interface, or it showed less microleakage (score 1). In these groups, self-etch adhesives–Adper easy one and Futurabond– were applied before placing Clinpro™ unfilled and Grandio seal nanohybrid filled sealants in the repaired technique, respectively. This outcome could be explained by the self-etch adhesives’ limited etching capacity, the existence of unground enamel prior to the etching process, and the sealants’ inadequate penetration. These results aligned with a number of previous research that have been published in the literature.[2324] Phosphoric acid, which is utilized in the etch and rinse method, is more acidic than the majority of one-step self-etch adhesives hence, produced an inadequate etched pattern. Self-etch adhesives may be hindered from penetrating unground enamel due to its higher fluoride concentration and hypomineralized aprismatic layer. On the other hand, occlusal correction is a necessary component of the application process for filled sealants.[25] Grandio seal, a light-curing nano hybrid fissure sealant, has been chosen for our investigation, nevertheless. The manufacturer claims that despite having 70% filler particles, the flow properties have risen.

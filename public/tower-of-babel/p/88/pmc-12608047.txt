Parkinson Disease SNCA Risk Variants Are Associated With Higher Asymmetric Putamen Dopaminergic Dysfunction
Authors: Saud Alhusaini, Gabriel Dayanim, Mohamed Kandil, Lynne Krohn, Janine Molino, Ziv Gan-Or, Alain Dagher
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe aim of this study was to investigate the endophenotypic potential of striatal dopamine transporter (DAT) uptake in carriers of Parkinson disease (PD)–associated SNCA genetic risk variants.MethodsWe analyzed 381 patients with de novo PD from the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI). The genotype of previously identified PD-related SNCA risk variants was extracted and used to compute an individual PD-specific SNCA genetic risk score (GRS). Striatal DAT uptake was quantified using 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT and assessed at baseline and 24-month follow-up. Mixed models were applied to explore the relationship between striatal 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT specific binding ratios (SBRs) and PD SNCA risk variants.ResultsNo significant associations were observed between SNCA risk variants and the mean putamen or caudate 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBRs. However, a higher SNCA GRS was significantly associated with increased baseline putamen 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBR asymmetry index (p < 0.001). This relationship appeared primarily driven by the 3′ variant rs356182 and the 5′ region variant rs763443 (p < 0.001) and was not observed at 24-month follow-up.DiscussionOur findings suggest that a more lateralized putaminal dopaminergic degeneration in early PD may represent a viable endophenotype in carriers of SNCA risk variants, emphasizing the relevant role of neuroimaging in PD subtyping and biomarker identification.

Introduction

The SNCA gene, which encodes the protein α-synuclein, was the first to be linked to Parkinson disease (PD).1,2 Rare SNCA missense and copy number variants have been linked to autosomal dominant forms of PD.2,3 By contrast, common SNCA variants have been implicated in sporadic PD and other synucleinopathies, including dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) and idiopathic REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD).4-6

A previous in-depth analysis of the SNCA locus in sporadic PD revealed 3 independent variants. These included the consistently reported genome-wide association study (GWAS) hits, the downstream (3′) single-nucleotide variant (SNV) rs356182 and the 5′ region variant rs763443, and an additional independent downstream 3′ variant, rs2870004.5 Notably, the 5′ PD-linked rs763443 is in strong linkage disequilibrium (LD) with other 5′ variants associated with DLB and iRBD.6

To evaluate the utility of neuroimaging in disease subtyping and biomarker discovery, this exploratory study examined the association between PD-associated SNCA risk variants and striatal dopamine transporter (DAT) uptake, a measure of the integrity of presynaptic nigrostriatal dopaminergic nerve terminals.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

We examined the association between PD SNCA risk variants and striatal DAT uptake using the baseline and 24-month follow-up data from the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI).7,8 The diagnosis of idiopathic PD was established based on the presence of supportive clinical features and confirmation of striatal dopaminergic deficiency using DAT imaging.7

Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents

All procedures involving human participants were performed following the participating institutions' ethical standards. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study was registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT01141023).7

Clinical Variables

The following clinical variables were considered: age at onset of PD symptoms, Hoehn and Yahr (HY) stage, presence or lack of resting tremor (tremor-predominant vs akinetic-rigid subtype), and the Movement Disorder Society–Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS) III motor scores.9

Parkinson Disease SNCA Risk Variants

Genotyping of the PPMI participants was performed using the Illumina ImmunoChip and NeuroX genotyping arrays. Standard quality control procedures for genetic data analyses were applied, including sex and relatedness checks (threshold: cryptic relatedness at p > 0.125). Based on prior findings,5 the genotype of the following 3 SNCA PD risk variants was extracted: (1) rs356182 (the top 3′ PD risk variant), (2) proxy for the novel 3′ risk variant rs2870004 (rs2869998: R2 = 0.99, D’ = 0.99), and (3) proxy for the 5′ risk variant rs763443 (rs3756059: R2 = 0.87, D’ = 0.96). Carriers of monogenic PD variants (e.g., LRRK2, GBA1, and PRKN) were excluded.

Individual PD-specific SNCA genetic risk score (GRS) was generated using PLINK v.1.9 according to a log-additive model by adding the reported β estimates of the risk alleles of the 3 SNCA variants in the discovery sample of the study by Pihlstrøm et al. (which did not include the PPMI data).10 To account for population stratification, the population structure was inferred by principal component (PC) analysis.10

Dopamine Transporter Imaging Acquisition and Analysis

Dopamine transporter (DAT) imaging was obtained using 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT imaging at baseline and planned follow-up visits.7 The protocol for acquiring and processing the 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT images is described elsewhere.7 The specific binding ratios (SBRs) for the left and right caudate and putamen were calculated as follows (reference = the occipital cortex):SBR=(target−reference)/reference.

Data and Statistical Analyses

Our outcome measures included the mean of the left and right putamen and caudate 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBRs. Furthermore, we assessed putamen and caudate 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBR asymmetry index (AI) asAI=| left–right |/(left+right)*100.

Mixed models were used to investigate the correlation between striatal 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBRs and SNCA GRSs and how striatal 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBRs differed by the SNCA variant carrier status. Comparisons based on genotype status were conducted via orthogonal contrasts. The Holm test was used to account for multiple comparisons and to maintain a two-tailed familywise alpha of 0.05. The following covariates were considered: age at scanning, sex, handedness, HY stage, the dominant side of motor symptoms, imaging site, and the top 10 PCs to account for population stratification.

A total of 381 patients with de novo PD [66.4% male; mean age (SD) = 61.7 (9.7) years] with available genotype and DAT imaging at baseline were included. Of those, 312 completed DAT imaging at 24-month follow-up. A detailed description of the study participants' demographics is summarized in Table 1.

Discussion

We identified a significant association between baseline putamen 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBR AI and PD-specific SNCA GRSs. Carriers of the risk alleles of the independent rs356182 and rs763443 variants exhibited a more lateralized putaminal DAT uptake than typically expected in early PD. This association was absent at the 24-month follow-up, possibly reflecting the progressive undifferentiated neurodegeneration of the nigrostriatal pathway over the disease course.

Significant asymmetry in putaminal DAT uptake is often observed in early PD.11 This has been attributed to the selective degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the contralateral substantia nigra pars compacta12 and has been linked to a “brain-first” disease subtype in contrast to the “body-first” subtype, in which initial α-synuclein aggregation is believed to initiate in the gastrointestinal tract and symmetrically spread through the vagus nerve to the brainstem and substantia nigra, resulting in symmetric nigrostriatal degeneration.13

Previous studies that explored the relationship between SNCA risk variants and PD clinical features identified an association with earlier PD age at onset.14 Furthermore, the top 3′ PD SNCA variant, rs356182, has been linked (although not consistently) to a predisposition for tremor-dominant motor subtype and a slower rate of motor progression, arguing for its possible link to a distinct PD clinical subtype.15 Of interest, our findings demonstrate that the association between baseline putamen 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBR AI and SNCA risk variants is not unique to the 3′ PD variant, rs356182, but is also present in carriers of the 5′ region variant, rs763443, which is in LD with other 5′ region risk variants associated with DLB and iRBD. Of note, asymmetric striatal DAT uptake was also previously observed in carriers of GBA and LRRK2 monogenic variants.16 These observations highlight the endophenotypic potential of putaminal DAT uptake asymmetry in carriers of PD risk variants.

Our study was limited by the relatively small sample size and the challenges of working with multicenter study data. Furthermore, we used the initial PPMI genotyping array data, limiting us to proxies for rs2870004 and rs763443. Finally, we did not differentiate between the anterior and posterior putamen DAT uptake or account for the effect of symptomatic medications at 24-month follow-up.

In summary, we observed a significant association between baseline putamen 123I‐FP‐CIT SPECT SBR AI and PD SNCA risk variants. This indicates that patients with PD carrying SNCA variants exhibit a more lateralized putaminal DAT uptake than expected, highlighting the potential role of DAT imaging in PD subtyping and biomarker discovery.

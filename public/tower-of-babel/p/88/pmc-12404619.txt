Under-reporting of HIV and AIDS as an underlying cause of death in South Africa
Authors: Pam Groenewald, Tracy Glass, Natasha Kallis, Chantelle Holmgren, B. Nojilana, Monique Maqungo, Mireille Cheyip, Diane Morof, Nadine Nannan, Debbie Bradshaw
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Under-reporting of HIV-related and AIDS-related deaths is a major problem in South Africa, with only 27.9% of documented HIV-related deaths in medical records reported as such in the official cause of death data. The legacy of AIDS denialism in South Africa, confidentiality concerns, and anticipated stigma appear to have created a culture of not recording HIV as an underlying cause on the official medical certificate of death. Inaccurate statistics could affect resource allocation for the epidemic.

Under-reporting of HIV/AIDS-related deaths remains a global public health issue, particularly in high-prevalence regions [1]. Contributing factors include stigma, misclassification, weak health systems, limited testing, and political influence [2]. This results in inaccurate data, affecting resource allocation.

South Africa has a strong Civil Registration and Vital Statistics (CRVS) system, registering over 90% of adult and 60–75% of under-five deaths [3]. A medical certificate of cause of death (MCCD), completed by a practitioner, is required for registration with the Department of Home Affairs and is forwarded to Statistics South Africa (Stats SA) for coding and reporting. However, data quality is compromised by many ill-defined causes, underreporting of HIV as the underlying cause, and injury misclassification [4]. Despite a sharp decline in HIV-related deaths following the 2004 rollout of antiretroviral therapy [5], official Stats SA data show minimal change in HIV mortality over time [6].

The South African National Cause-of-Death Validation (SA NCODV) project, led by the South African Medical Research Council, validated CRVS data by linking it to medical records, forensic records, and verbal autopsy data for a nationally representative sample [7]. A stratified random sample of 27 sub-districts was selected by socio-economic status. Medical records were collected for all deaths between 1 September 2017 and 13 April 2018. Trained doctors reviewed 10,129 records and assigned causes of death. Preliminary findings showed unexpectedly high HIV/AIDS-related deaths, prompting a second review to ensure consistent classification [8]. Criteria for assigning HIV/AIDS as the underlying cause of death (UCOD) included:1.Confirmed HIV-positive status or antiretroviral treatment (naive, on treatment, or interrupted), plus:a.an AIDS-defining condition,b.and/or CD4+ cell count less than 500 cells/μl,c.and/or viral load at least 50 copies/ml.OR2.High clinical suspicion in a young adult without other comorbidities, despite no serological confirmation.

Causes of death were coded to ICD-10 using IRIS software. Deterministic and probabilistic linkages connected SA NCODV with Stats SA data using national ID numbers and shared variables.

Stats SA underlying causes of death (UCOD) for HIV/AIDS-linked cases in the SA NCODV study were grouped into:1.HIV/AIDS – explicitly listed on the death certificate.2.HIV pseudonyms – terms like retroviral disease (RVD, ICD-10 B33) or immune deficiency (ICD-10 D84, D89) used instead of HIV/AIDS.3.HIV-related conditions – AIDS-defining illnesses [e.g. tuberculosis (TB), pneumocystosis, cryptococcal infection, wasting, Kaposi sarcoma) and other related infections (e.g. lower respiratory tract infections, diarrhoea, sepsis, anaemia).4.Unrelated conditions – diseases not typically linked to HIV (e.g. cancer, diabetes, stroke), possibly as immediate or comorbid causes.5.Ill-defined causes – nonspecific or unknown causes (e.g. fluid imbalance, organ failure, hypotension).

A total of 8191 SA NCODV medical records were linked with Stats SA records. SA NCODV medical records assigned a UCOD of HIV/AIDS to 2749 (33.6%) of these. Linked Stats SA data reported 811 (9.9%) HIV/AIDS deaths, of which 763 of 2749 (27.8%) agreed with the SA NCODV UCOD. Thus, only 27.8% of HIV and AIDS-related deaths documented in medical records were reported accurately in the official data.8

HIV pseudonyms accounted for 22.2% of deaths, with B33 (other retroviral diseases) accounting for 14.4% and D89 (other immune disorders) accounting for 7.8% (Fig. 1). AIDS-defining conditions accounted for 27.8%, with TB (A15–A19 and B90) accounting for the largest proportion (22%). Other immediate causes accounted for 12.3% with lower respiratory infections accounting for the largest proportion (3.8%).

Many of the decedents who had been living with HIV had comorbidities, and the variable quality of medical record/forensic record and incomplete laboratory test data introduced some uncertainty in assigning HIV/AIDS as the UCOD. The national sample limited provincial-level analysis, which could have targeted intervention priorities. Delays in the release of the official 2017 and 2018 cause of death data to 2020 and 2021, along with the decision to re-review the HIV-and-AIDS deaths resulted in delays in the linkage, analysis, and publication.

The legacy of AIDS denialism in South Africa, concerns about confidentiality and stigma appear to have created a culture of purposefully not recording HIV/AIDS as an UCOD on the official death notification forms, despite introducing MCCD training and implementing a self-sealing MCCD form to improve confidentiality. The reasons for certifier's reluctance to report HIV and AIDS on the death notification need to be investigated, and a quality assurance system for MCCD needs to be implemented to provide more accurate statistics.

Hospital Outcome of Host Heterogeneity, Organ dysfunction and Trajectory in sepsis (HOHHOT): A cohort study in the critical care unit
Authors: Feng Zhang, Da-Gang Yang, Wei Liu, Shu-Jia Liu, Wen Liu, Chun-Di Zhang, Wen-Jie Hou, Xu-Lei Hao, Yue Gao, Wen-Qi Zheng, Ran-Tong Bao, Ya-Nan Xu, Qiong Wu, Lei Wang, Zhi-Wei Zhao, Yi-Huan Dong, Li-Peng Zhang, Zhi-De Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPrognosis estimation is the basis for establishing the personal interventions in sepsis patients. Serum biomarkers are potential tools for predicting the outcomes of sepsis patients admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU). Here, we plan to perform a prospective study to investigate the prognostic value of serum biomarkers. We named this study Hospital Outcome of Host Heterogeneity, Organ dysfunction and Trajectory in sepsis (HOHHOT).Methods and analysisThis prospective cohort study is being performed in several hospitals in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region between 2025 and 2027. The inclusion criteria were adult sepsis patients admitted to the ICU. A serum specimen will be collected at the onset of sepsis and stored at −80°C for future use. The endpoint of the HOHHOT study includes, but is not limited to, hospital outcome (eg, mortality, occurrence of organ dysfunction, treatment response and the progression of sepsis). We will analyse the predictive value of serum biomarkers for outcomes using a receiver operating characteristic curve and decision curve analysis. The association between baseline characteristics and serum biomarkers will be estimated using a logistic regression model, net reclassification index and integrated discriminatory index. We will also construct some predictive models using machine learning algorithms.Ethics and disseminationThe HOHHOT study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University (NO: KY2025005). The findings derived from the HOHHOT study will be submitted to international academic journals.Trial registration numberChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500100770); pre-results.

Background

Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.1 Globally, there are 48.9 million new cases of sepsis and 11.0 million sepsis-related deaths, accounting for 19.7% of all deaths that year.2 The incidence of sepsis in adult intensive care units (ICUs) across Asia was 22.4%, with a higher mortality rate in low- and middle-income countries compared with high-income countries (36.6% vs 37.9%, AOR 1.84, p=0.049).3 According to a nationwide cross-sectional survey in China, the incidence of sepsis in the ICU is approximately 20.6 cases per 100 hospitalised patients. The 90-day mortality rate is 35.5%.4 The clinical presentations and outcomes of sepsis vary widely due to the heterogeneity of host and pathogen, including the type of pathogen, host susceptibility, pre-existing conditions, nutritional status and therapeutic approaches. Therefore, developing effective and personal treatment approaches for sepsis is highly challenging. Although the existing clinical scoring systems (eg, sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score) provide a basis for the early identification of sepsis, these indicators often fail to accurately reflect the patient’s outcome due to the heterogeneity of sepsis.1 Developing new tools to estimate sepsis outcomes is of great value because prognostic estimation is the rationale for selecting treatment approaches.5

Serum biomarkers have a long history in diagnosing and estimating the outcomes of sepsis. During the past decades, many serum biomarkers have been reported to be associated with the diagnosis and outcomes of sepsis.6 However, the accuracy of a single serum biomarker in estimating outcome is far from perfect. There are two ways to enhance the value of serum markers in estimating sepsis patients' prognosis: one is a multiple biomarker approach, and the other is developing novel markers to replace the current available biomarkers. Notably, the past decades have witnessed dramatic breakthroughs in developing and measuring serum biomarkers. Traditionally, serum biomarker only covers proteins. However, many studies have revealed that cell-free nucleic acids,7 metabolites8 and extracellular vesicles9 in serum are potential disease-related biomarkers. In addition, some novel technologies have shown a promising value in diagnosing and estimating certain types of diseases, such as surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS)10 and near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS).11 However, the clinical applications of these technologies in estimating the prognosis of sepsis remain unclear. Furthermore, a large portion of studies concerning the prognostic value of serum biomarkers is performed in sepsis patients not defined by SEPSIS-3, and the generalisation of their findings in the current clinical practice is problematic.

Here, we plan to perform a prospective cohort study to investigate the role of serum biomarkers in predicting the progression and prognosis of sepsis patients, which is defined by the SEPSIS-3.1

Materials and methods

Study design

The HOHHOT (Hospital Outcome of Host Heterogeneity, Organ dysfunction and Trajectory in sepsis) is a prospective cohort study. We have registered this study in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry platform (https://www.chictr.org.cn/indexEN.html) with a registration number of ChiCTR2500100770. The study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University (Approval No KY2025005). The first participant was enrolled in the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University in May 2025. In addition to the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University, we have invited 21 hospitals in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region to participate in this project. The period for participants’ enrolment was between March 2024 and December 2027. The HOHHOT is an investigator-initiated study that is currently not supported by any project. However, it may be supported by scientific projects from the government in the future.

Patient and public involvement statement

This study did not involve patients or the public in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans.

Inclusion, exclusion criteria and research aims

The HOHHOT study aimed to investigate the value of serum biomarkers in predicting outcomes of sepsis patients admitted to the intensive care unit. In addition, the efficiency and safety of some interventions will also be investigated. The endpoint includes hospital mortality, ICU mortality, 28-day mortality, length of ICU stays, cost, occurrence of organ failure such as acute kidney injury (AKI), disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). The AKI was defined by the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guideline.12 The DIC was defined by the International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis (ISTH) guideline.13 The ARDS was defined by the global definition.14

The inclusion criteria for the HOHHOT study were patients admitted to the ICU who met the diagnostic criteria of SEPSIS-3.1 The exclusion criteria were patients aged <18 years and pregnant. In addition, patients with many missing (usually >20%) data will be excluded. To maintain the representativeness of the studied cohort, all eligible participants admitted to the Department of Critical Care Medicine will be consecutively enrolled. The caregiver will enrol the participants at the time of sepsis onset. Participants with sepsis before entering the ICU will be enrolled at ICU admission. After signing informed consent, an approximately 10 mL blood specimen will be collected. In addition, we will collect the serum specimen at any time during the hospitalisation to analyse the dynamic changes of serum biomarkers. The time of serum collection is determined by the physician. The serum specimen will be aliquoted and stored at −80°C.

Data collection

The clinical characteristics of the participants at enrolment will be recorded in a uniform table. The following clinical characteristics will be obtained: demographic characteristics, laboratory testing, microbiology findings, history, complications, primary diagnosis, signs, symptoms, imaging, and treatment approaches. Several ICU scores on admission will be calculated and recorded, including but not limited to the SOFA,15 quick SOFA (qSOFA),16 multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS),17 the national early warning score (NEWs),18 the simplified acute physiology score (SAPS) II,19 and acute physiology score (APS) III.20 The eligible patients will be followed after admission, and the in-hospital and out-of-hospital outcomes will be recorded, such as the occurrences of organ dysfunction, mortality, development of septic shock, and length of ICU stay. In addition, the dynamic changes of ICU scores and routine laboratory tests will be recorded to analyse the trajectory of sepsis patients.

All original documents will be stored in a secure location within the Department of Intensive Care Medicine at the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University, with restricted access, and will be retained for a minimum of 10 years. Authorised personnel and regulatory agencies will have access to these documents for trial monitoring, auditing and reporting purposes. Sample size estimation

The HOHHOT is a data-driven, rather than a hypothesis-driven, study. Its primary aim was to evaluate the prognostic value of serum biomarkers in sepsis patients admitted to the ICU. Many biomarkers, as well as novel technologies (eg, SERS), will be studied. Because no primary biomarker or endpoint was presumed, we could not calculate the sample size. It is estimated that the sample size in the Affiliated Hospital of the Inner Mongolia Medical University will be 800, and the sample size in other centres will be 600. Prior to investigating the prognostic value of a given biomarker, as well as an association between a baseline characteristic (eg, intervention, history, imaging and routine chemistry) and outcome (eg, AKI, DIC and ARDS), we will calculate the statistical power.

Statistical analysis

We used the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test or the Shapiro-Wilk test to determine the normality of continuous variables. The continuous variables between the two groups will be compared using the independent Student’s t-test for normally distributed data and the Mann-Whitney U test for skewed distributed data. For comparisons with more than three groups, we used the analysis of variance or the Kruskal-Wallis H test if appropriate. Receiver operating characteristic analysis will be used to assess the performance of biomarkers in predicting outcomes.21 The optimal threshold will be determined by the number needed to misdiagnose and the Bayesian approach.22 Decision curve analysis will be performed to assess the net benefit of biomarkers in predicting outcomes.23 24 Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analysis will be used to assess the association between baseline characteristics (eg, imaging, serum chemistry and treatment approach) and outcomes.25 A restricted cubic spline will be used to assess the nonlinear association between a biomarker and outcome.26 The results of logistic regression analysis will be graphically visualised by a nomogram. We will also use propensity score matching to assess a given intervention’s efficiency and safety.27 We will also construct prognostic models with baseline characteristics, particularly serum biomarkers. Integrated discrimination improvement index and net reclassification index will be used to assess whether a biomarker has added prognostic value.28

We will use machine learning approaches to construct prognostic models. Figure 2 is a flowchart of machine learning in this study. Briefly, the participants in the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University will be split into a training cohort and a testing cohort at a ratio of 7:3. The training cohort will be used for model building after a feature selection process. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression or Boruta will be used for variable selection before model construction.29 The machine learning algorithms used include, but are not limited to, logistic regression model, support vector machine, random forest, linear discriminant analysis, XGBoost and k-nearest neighbours. In addition, the large language model will also be used to predict the prognosis of sepsis. The discrimination and calibration of the model built with the training dataset will be evaluated. The model will be internally validated with the testing cohort and externally validated with the participants in other centres if possible.

Reporting guidelines

Although the HOHHOT is a cohort study, most outcomes occur during hospitalisation, similar to a cross-sectional study. The HOHHOT study aimed to investigate the value of serum biomarkers in predicting the occurrence of an outcome, and its design is identical to a diagnostic test accuracy study. Therefore, we will report our findings following the Standards for Reporting Diagnostic Accuracy Studies guideline.30 31 The TRIPOD (Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis)32 or TRIPOD+AI33 will be followed when reporting a predictive model.

Discussion

The HOHHOT study has three primary aims. The first aim was to investigate the prognostic value of serum biomarkers in adult sepsis patients admitted to the ICU. The second aim was to evaluate the efficiency and safety of some interventions in sepsis management. The third aim was to construct some predictive models with low cost and high accessibility.

The HOHHOT study has some strengths. The first strength is the prospective design. The prospective design decreases the likelihood of data missing. The second strength is that the serum of the participants will be collected, and we thus have the chance to investigate the prognostic value of some novel serum biomarkers. The third strength is that some artificial intelligence (AI) approaches, such as machine learning algorithms and large language models (eg, ChatGPT), will be used to construct predictive models. The past years have witnessed the progression and great effect of AI on the patterns of clinical research. The predictive models constructed with AI approaches have higher accuracy in diagnosing or predicting the prognosis of a target disease.

Many serum proteins have been proven to be useful biomarkers for predicting the outcomes of sepsis patients. However, their prognostic value is far from perfect when used alone. Multiple biomarker approaches are considered effective ways to improve the accuracy of predicting outcomes of sepsis. The algorithm for incorporating multiple biomarkers, as well as routine clinical characteristics, is a technical challenge. The HOHHOT study will use AI algorithms, including machine learning and large language models, to incorporate serum biomarkers and routine clinical data. The AI algorithms have been shown significant potential in classifying patients. Before building the model, a LASSO regression analysis will be performed to select variables used for model construction. Currently, LASSO is the most popular method for variable selection before model construction and has shown great potential in improving the accuracy of clinical models.

The HOHHOT study will collect and store the serum specimens of the participants at the onset of sepsis. These serum specimens allow us to use multi-omics analysis to screen novel biomarkers. For example, metabonomic analyses can be performed to screen metabolites associated with the prognosis of sepsis patients. Some novel biomarkers and analytical methods, such as SERS, soluble thrombomodulin (sTM)34 and exosomal microRNAs, will be investigated in the HOHHOT study.

The HOHHOT study has some limitations. The first limitation is that the participants are from Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, a province of China. A typical characteristic of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region is that about 30% of the citizens are Mongolian. The ethnicity should be considered when generalising the findings of this study. The second limitation is the observational design, and the confounding factors are problematic when investigating the efficiency and safety of some interventions. The third limitation is that the serum specimens will be stored at −80°C for about 3 years, and the long-term stability of some serum biomarkers is unknown.

Taken together, the HOHHOT study is a prospective cohort study aimed at investigating the value of serum biomarkers for predicting the outcome of sepsis patients. Although it has some limitations, we believe that its findings can help ICU physicians to better manage sepsis patients.

Efficacy of tranexamic acid for prevention of heterotopic ossification after orthopedic surgery: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Xingui Wang, Di Guo, Xiaoguang Xu, Zhitao Wang, Bo Lv, Yonghai Lou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo systematically evaluate the effect of tranexamic acid (TXA) on the incidence of heterotopic ossification (HO) following orthopedic surgery.MethodsA Systematic review and Meta-analysis was carried out according to PRISMA guidelines. PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched from inception date to October 15, 2025. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies comparing TXA with control (placebo or no TXA) in patients undergoing orthopaedic surgery who reported rates of HO were included. Data were pooled using a random-effects model, and subgroup and sensitivity analyses were performed.ResultsSix studies involving 1456 patients (TXA = 778, Control = 678) were included. All included studies reported HO outcomes. When stratified by study design, pooled analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) showed no significant reduction in HO incidence with TXA (OR = 0.92, 95% CI 0.11–7.77; I2 = 55.3%). In contrast, observational studies demonstrated a significant association between TXA use and reduced HO incidence (OR = 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.77; I2 = 0%). Safety outcomes were reported in only one study. No statistically significant differences were observed between the TXA and control groups in the incidence of deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, or wound infection.ConclusionsCurrent evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in heterotopic ossification with tranexamic acid based on randomized data. Although observational studies suggest a potential association, this finding may be influenced by confounding and should be interpreted with caution.Keywords: Tranexamic acid, Heterotopic ossification, Orthopedic procedures

Introduction

Heterotopic ossification (HO) is a pathological process characterized by the ectopic development of well organized mature lamellar bone within soft tissues, such as muscles and periarticular connective structures, where normal bone formation does not physiologically occur, and this aberrant osteogenesis takes place outside the native skeletal framework [1]. It is a common and potentially debilitating complication following orthopedic surgeries, particularly joint arthroplasty, periarticular fractures, and post-traumatic procedures [2, 3]. HO can result in joint pain, severe limitation of range of motion, functional impairment, and may even necessitate surgical excision [4, 5]. Heterotopic ossification (HO) significantly impairs patients' quality of life and creats a substantial socioeconomic burden [6]. Although the precise pathogenesis remains incompletely elucidated, it is widely accepted that excessive inflammatory response triggered by trauma or surgical intervention serves as a core initiating factor in HO pathogenesis [2, 7, 8]. The release of inflammatory cytokines is widely accepted that excessive inflammatory response triggered by trauma or surgical intervention is a core initiating factor fosters a hypoxic microenvironment conducive to HO formation [9].

Tranexamic acid (TXA), a synthetic lysine analog, exerts a potent antifibrinolytic and hemostatic effect by competitively inhibiting plasminogen activation. In orthopedic procedures, it has been extensively incorporated into perioperative blood management protocols to attenuate surgical bleeding, stabilize clot formation, minimize postoperative hemoglobin decline, and thereby reduce reliance on allogeneic blood transfusion while supporting enhanced recovery pathways [10]. In recent years, growing evidence suggests that beyond its hemostatic efficacy, TXA may also modulate inflammatory pathways by inhibiting the fibrinolytic system, thereby lowering levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and demonstrating significant anti-inflammatory properties [11]. Given the pivotal initiating role of inflammation in HO development, the potential anti-inflammatory characteristics of TXA has raised the scientific hypothesis of whether it can prevent or decrease HO formation.

However, current high-level clinical data concerning the impact of TXA on HO remains controversial and insufficient. Some studies suggest that perioperative use of TXA may be associated with a reduced incidence of HO [12, 13]. Conversely, a prospective randomized controlled trial by Avisar et al. [14] on elbow fracture-dislocation surgery observed a trend towards increased HO incidence in the TXA group (albeit without reaching statistical significance) [15]. These inconsistent findings may stem from variations in study design, surgical site, type and severity of trauma, TXA administration route and dosage, among other confounding factors.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis was designed and conducted following the methodological principles outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, with strict compliance to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) reporting framework to ensure transparency and reproducibility. In addition, a detailed study protocol specifying the objectives, eligibility criteria, and analytical strategies was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO database prior to data extraction and analysis (NO. CRD420251114838).

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were (1) Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or observational studies (retrospective or prospective cohort studies) evaluating the tranexamic acid (TXA) on the incidence of heterotopic ossification (HO) in patients undergoing orthopedic surgery;(2) Studies reporting HO incidence;(3) Studies providing extractable data on HO events in both TXA and control groups.

The exclusion criteria were:(1) Case reports, reviews;(2) Studies not involving TXA or lacking a control group;(3) Studies with overlapping populations or incomplete outcome data.

Search strategy

Two of the authors performed the search in PubMed, Embase, the Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials from the inception date of each database to October 15, 2025, using the keywords (heterotopic ossification or ectopic ossification or heterotopic bone) and (tranexamic acid or TXA or t-amcha or amcha or cyklokapron or transamine) in humans. The full and reproducible search strategies for each database are provided in Supplementary. No language restrictions were applied during the literature search. In addition, the reference lists of relevant studies and reviews were manually screened to identify any additional eligible studies.

Study selection

Following the removal of duplicate records, all remaining citations were independently screened by two reviewers through a systematic assessment of titles and abstracts based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Articles considered to have potential relevance were subsequently subjected to comprehensive full-text evaluation to confirm eligibility. Additionally, manual screening of the reference lists of all included studies was performed to ensure that any relevant publications not captured by the initial database search were identified and considered for inclusion. If the 2 researchers disagreed on a study’s eligibility and could not reach a consensus, the senior researcher made the final decision basing on group discussion.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two researchers used a standard data extraction form to independently extract data from selected trials. Extracted data comprised First author, publication year, country, study design, sample size, patient age, surgical type, TXA regimen (dose, route), control intervention, HO incidence, and safety outcomes (DVT, PE, infection). Disagreements were settled by consensus.

Assessment of risk of bias and quality of evidence

Methodological quality of the included RCTs was independently appraised by two investigators using the Cochrane risk of bias tool, with each domain judged as low, high, or unclear risk of bias. Based on the assessments of key domains, particularly random sequence generation and allocation concealment, trials were subsequently classified into three overall quality categories: high quality when both domains were judged as low risk with remaining items rated as low or unclear risk; low quality when either randomization or allocation concealment was assessed as high risk; and moderate quality when studies did not clearly meet the criteria for either high or low quality. The quality of the included non-randomized studies were evaluated with NOS score.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed using Stata software (version 17; StataCorp, 2021). Heterogeneity was assessed according to the I2 statistic and Q-test. A random-effects model using the Mantel–Haenszel method was implemented due to expected clinical and methodological diversity. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated in accordance with dichotomous outcomes.

Results

Study identification

The initial literature search identified 181 records from multiple electronic databases: PubMed (n = 14), Embase (n = 53), Web of Science (n = 104), and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (n = 10). After deletion of 33 replicates, 148 records underwent title and abstract screening. Subsequently, 114 records were excluded, and 34 full text articles were assessed for eligibility. Five articles could not be retrieved, and 23 were excluded due to reasons such as lacking TXA-specific outcome data or unavailable revision-specific results. Finally, six studies met all inclusion criteria and were included in the qualitative and quantitative synthesis [6, 14, 16–19]. The detailed study selection process is shown in the Fig. 1.

Study characteristics

The six included studies enrolled a total of 1,456 patients, with 778 patients in the TXA group and 678 in the control group [6, 14, 16–19]. The sample sizes of individual studies ranged from 47 to 482. The surgeries performed included total hip arthroplasty (THA), open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) for acetabular and pelvic fractures, elbow trauma surgery (ORIF/dislocation repair), and elbow stiffness release. TXA was administered via intravenous, topical, or combined routes, with doses ranging from 1 to 2 g per administration. The control groups received a placebo (e.g., normal saline) or no TXA. The incidence of HO was assessed radiographically during follow-up periods varying from 6 months to 6 years. Detailed characteristics of the included studies are presented in Table 1.

Primary HO outcomes

Six studies have reported this result [6, 14, 16–19], including three RCTs and three RCS. Pooled analyses were performed using a random-effects model. The combined results of the RCTs showed that the use of TXA did not reduce the incidence of HO (OR = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.11–7.77, p = 0.937; I2 = 55.3%; Fig. 3). In contrast, the pooled analysis of the retrospective cohort studies demonstrated that TXA use was associated with a significant reduction in HO incidence (OR = 0.59, 95% CI: 0.45–0.77, p < 0.001; I2 = 0%; Fig. 3).

Safety outcomes

Sharaby et al. [17] reported the incidence of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) in both groups. DVT occurred in 3 cases in the TXA group and 1 case in the control group, while PE occurred in 3 cases in the TXA group and 2 cases in the control group. No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups for either DVT or PE. Regarding wound infection, 1 case was reported in the TXA group and none in the control group, with no significant difference between the groups.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to date on the effects of Tranexamic acid on HO in orthopedic surgeries. According to the established search strategy, three RCTs and three retrospective cohort studies met the inclusion criteria, among which four were about hip joints and three about elbow joints.

Histologically, trauma-induced heterotopic ossification progresses via endochondral ossification, comprising four sequential stages: inflammation, chondrogenesis, osteogenesis, and maturation [6]. Extensive previous basic research has established that immune cell infiltration and excessive inflammatory responses are primary drivers of ectopic ossification [8, 9]. Key inflammatory immune cells—including macrophages, mast cells, and lymphocytes play critical roles during the inflammatory phase of heterotopic ossification formation [8, 10, 11]

TXA is a synthetic antifibrinolytic agent that inhibits fibrin degradation—a key component of thrombi—thereby stabilizing blood coagulation and reducing blood loss under hyperfibrinolytic conditions. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses indicate that TXA significantly reduces perioperative blood loss without increasing the risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) [13]. Tranexamic acid was initially commonly used in patients undergoing hip and knee joint replacements. With the support of more and more clinical data, its application has expanded to the field of trauma orthopedics. The administration methods include (1) the topical method, (2) the intravenous method, and (3) the combined method. A RCT on the effect of Tranexamic acid use on blood loss during shoulder joint replacement mentioned that there was no significant difference in postoperative drainage, hemoglobin changes, and blood transfusion rates among the three administration methods [15].

The findings of this meta-analysis suggest that the effect of TXA on HO remains uncertain. When stratified by study design, the pooled analysis of RCTs showed no significant reduction in HO incidence with TXA use, with substantial imprecision and moderate heterogeneity. In contrast, observational studies demonstrated a statistically significant association between TXA use and reduced HO incidence. This discrepancy between RCTs and retrospective cohort studies indicates that the apparent protective effect of TXA may be influenced by residual confounding inherent to non-randomized designs. Therefore, the results derived from observational studies should be interpreted with caution and cannot be considered confirmatory. Importantly, due to the limited number of included studies, subgroup and sensitivity analyses were not performed in the revised analysis to avoid unstable or potentially biased estimates. As a result, the conclusions of this study are based primarily on the overall and stratified analyses rather than post hoc exploratory findings. From a mechanistic perspective, TXA may reduce intraoperative bleeding and postoperative hematoma formation, which are recognized contributors to ectopic bone formation. By limiting the local inflammatory and osteogenic microenvironment associated with hematoma, TXA could theoretically attenuate the differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells into osteoblasts. However, this hypothesis is not supported by high-quality randomized evidence in the present analysis. Taken together, the current evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in HO with TXA based on randomized data. The observed association in observational studies should be regarded as hypothesis-generating. Further well-designed, adequately powered randomized controlled trials are required to clarify the true effect of TXA on HO prevention.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, the six included studies involved surgical sites at both the elbow and hip joints, where the incidence of heterotopic ossification inherently differs, which may affect the final outcomes. Second, the administration methods and dosages of tranexamic acid varied across the studies, including topical, intravenous, and combined approaches, with doses such as 1 g preoperatively or 1 g both pre- and postoperatively. Thus, we are unable to conclude whether a particular route of administration or dosage is optimal. Third, the lack of reporting on NSAID use in most included studies. As NSAIDs are an important modifier of heterotopic ossification risk, this may introduce residual confounding and affect the validity of the results. Fourth, among the seven included studies, four were retrospective, providing estimates of association rather than causation. Fifth, although the overall sample size was large (1456), individual outcome analyses involved fewer patients, and variations in VTE prophylaxis, follow-up duration, and transfusion protocols may have affected the results.

Conclusions

The current evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in heterotopic ossification with perioperative TXA based on randomized controlled trials. Although observational studies suggest a potential association between TXA use and reduced HO incidence, this finding should be interpreted with caution.

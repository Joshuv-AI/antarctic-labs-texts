Dupilumab Improves Daily Symptom Burden in Adult Patients with Prurigo Nodularis: Post Hoc Analysis from LIBERTY PN-PRIME and PRIME2 Studies
Authors: Shawn G Kwatra, Sonja Ständer, Brian S Kim, Jerome Msihid, Ashish Bansal, Melanie Makhija, Simmi Wiggins, Joseph Zahn, Ryan B Thomas, Donia Bahloul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPrurigo nodularis (PN/chronic prurigo) is characterised by intensely pruritic, symmetrically distributed nodules. In the PRIME (NCT04183335) and PRIME2 (NCT04202679) clinical trials, dupilumab demonstrated clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvements in itch versus placebo in patients with uncontrolled PN on topical therapies. This post hoc analysis evaluated the efficacy of dupilumab versus placebo in reducing the daily symptom burden (DSB) with respect to itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance in patients with PN.MethodsPooled data from the PRIME and PRIME2 trials (N = 311) were utilised. DSB was assessed with three patient-reported outcomes (PROs): Worst Itch Numeric Rating Scale (WI-NRS), Skin Pain-NRS, and Sleep-NRS. Each PRO utilised a scale of 0–10 (0: no pruritus/no skin pain/worst possible sleep; 10: worst pruritus/worst skin pain/best possible sleep). The symptom intensity was categorised as no symptoms (0 for WI-NRS and Skin Pain-NRS; 10 for Sleep-NRS), mild (1–2 or 8–9), moderate (3–6 or 4–7), severe (7–8 or 2–3), and very severe (9–10 or 0–1). Key Summary Points

Introduction

Prurigo nodularis (PN/chronic prurigo) is a chronic, pruritic, inflammatory skin disease characterised by intense pruritus and symmetrically distributed nodular, lichenified skin lesions [1]. The underlying pathological processes of PN are not fully elucidated; however, current evidence implicates neuroimmune and inflammatory responses in disease development leading to skin fibrosis [2, 3]. The disease progression in PN is primarily driven by intense pruritus and the consequent itch–scratch cycle that results in skin pain, disruption of sleep, and negative impacts on the overall quality of life (QoL) [4, 5]. A greater pruritus intensity and reduction in QoL are observed in PN than in other dermatological conditions [5, 6].

Dupilumab was the first biologic treatment for adults with PN to receive approval from both the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency [7, 8]. The effectiveness of dupilumab in PN treatment is attributed to its concurrent inhibition of two crucial mediators of type 2 inflammation—interleukin-4 and interleukin-13 [9]. Through the neutralisation of these specific cytokines, dupilumab disrupts the inflammatory pathway that plays a central role in the disease processes of PN [10].

In the pivotal clinical trials (LIBERTY PN-PRIME, NCT04183335 and PRIME2, NCT04202679), dupilumab has demonstrated a significant improvement in multiple measures of symptoms (including itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance) and health-related QoL over a 24-week treatment period as compared to placebo [11]. Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) assessing itch (Worst Itch Numeric Rating Scale, WI-NRS), skin pain (Skin Pain-NRS), sleep disturbance (Sleep-NRS), and QoL (Dermatology Life Quality Index) were validated through subsequent post hoc studies, and clinically meaningful improvement thresholds were established for the PROMs [12, 13].

While the primary trial findings reported the change in the group mean symptom scores from baseline, a subsequent post hoc analysis reported the proportion of patients responding to dupilumab treatment based on the responder thresholds established for the respective PROMs [11, 14]. Beyond these measures of dupilumab efficacy in patients with PN, tracking the daily symptom burden offers a novel patient-centric approach [15] that could help to better understand the treatment efficacy. This concept is increasingly recognised as an important dimension of patient experience in the context of chronic inflammatory diseases such as asthma, allergic rhinitis, and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps [15–17].

In this study, we utilised the concept of daily symptom burden and propose two patient-centric outcomes—frequency of days with none-to-mild symptoms and proportion of patients experiencing only such symptoms—in patients with moderate-to-severe PN treated with dupilumab in the phase 3 PRIME and PRIME2 studies [11]. The symptoms evaluated were worst itch, worst daily skin pain, and daily sleep disturbance.

Methods

Trial Design and Patients

Pooled data from the phase 3, randomised, parallel-group, double-blind PRIME and PRIME2 trials were used for this post hoc analysis. Both the trials enrolled patients who were aged ≥ 18 years, had a confirmed diagnosis of PN for ≥ 3 months prior to screening, and had an average WI-NRS score of ≥ 7 in the 7 days prior to screening with ≥ 20 PN lesions in total on both legs, and/or both arms and/or trunk, at screening visit and on day 1. Patients needed to have bilaterally symmetrical lesions on the extremities; presence of lesions on at least two body surface areas was required, and willing and able to complete a daily symptom electronic diary for the duration of the study [11]. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria have been reported in the electronic supplementary material (Table S1).

Eligible patients were randomised 1:1 to receive subcutaneous dupilumab at 300 mg (loading dose of 600 mg on day 1) or matching placebo every 2 weeks for 24 weeks. All randomised patients (intention-to-treat) were included for the efficacy analysis. This article is based on previously conducted studies [11] and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors. The studies included in this article have been approved by the relevant institutional review boards and ethics committees of centres where studies were conducted (refer to the electronic supplementary [Table S2]). The institutional review board or independent ethics committee at all centres approved the protocol, investigator’s brochure, and any information provided to patients. The trials were previously conducted according to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Results presented in this article does not consist of any personally identifiable information.

Assessment of Daily Symptom Burden

Daily symptom burden was assessed using three PROMs: WI-NRS, Skin Pain-NRS, and Sleep-NRS. These instruments are single-item questionnaires designed to measure the respective worst symptom on a scale of 0–10. Higher scores on the WI-NRS and Skin Pain-NRS indicate more severe symptoms (‘0’ indicates no itch/pain and ‘10’ indicates the worst possible itch/pain), while for Sleep-NRS, lower scores indicate more severe symptoms (‘0’ indicates the worst possible sleep and ‘10’ indicates the best possible sleep). All three PROMs have been described and validated for use in PN, proving reliability in capturing patient-centric endpoints [12, 18–20].

On the basis of the symptom scores, patients were categorised with ‘no’, ‘mild’, ‘moderate’, ‘severe’, and ‘very severe’ symptoms for each day. These thresholds for severity categorisation were based on a previous study [21] using WI-NRS. The symptom severity categories and the score ranges are provided in Table 1.

Data Collection

At screening (visit 1), patients were distributed an electronic diary device along with instructions of use, to record daily symptom burden. Patients were instructed to complete an electronic diary entry each morning between 05:00 and 12:00, at the same time each day, until the end of the study period. At the end of the study visit, the electronic diary was downloaded and returned to the site. The information recorded on the device was downloaded daily and the site staff were responsible for reviewing the information collected via the electronic diary. The compliance with the electronic diary was assessed at regular intervals and follow-up with the participant(s) was scheduled as appropriate. The site staff particularly were to check status of the disease reviewing WI-NRS, as well as compliance to background therapy and overall electronic diary compliance.

Assessment Time Points

Patients recorded their symptom scores every day for all three PROMs. The average number of days with each level of symptom severity was determined for each patient for the 1-week period before baseline, week 12, and week 24. The outcomes assessed were the frequency of days with only none-to-mild symptoms and the proportion of patients experiencing only such symptoms in the week before baseline, week 12, and week 24 in patients treated with dupilumab as compared to those receiving placebo.

Statistical Analysis

The analysis used pooled data from the PRIME and PRIME2 trials. According to the trial inclusion criteria, patients required ≥ 4 days of completed WI-NRS scores in the week before baseline. For the subsequent time points, patients did not require a minimum number of days with completed scores for the three PROMs. Data for patients who were administered rescue medications were imputed using the worst observation carried forward (WOCF) approach.

Descriptive statistics were reported for the number of days with each severity category for the three PROMs. The proportion of days with each symptom severity category was calculated for all the patients and the mean proportion of days with none-to-mild symptoms of all patient days was multiplied by a factor of 7, in order to ease the interpretation and accommodated patients with variable number of days of PROM data over the week-long assessment period.

The proportion of patients with only none-to-mild and severe-to-very severe symptoms in the treatment arms were compared using a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test, adjusted by history of atopy, stable use of topical corticosteroids/topical calcineurin inhibitors, region, baseline anti-depressant use, and study indicator (PRIME and PRIME2). The odds ratio (OR) with the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and nominal p values were derived from the Mantel–Haenszel estimator. Data collected after study intervention discontinuation were included. Data post prohibited medications/procedures and/or rescue medications that impacted efficacy were set to missing and imputed by the data of the first week corresponding to the WOCF value; missing data after study intervention discontinuation for lack of efficacy were imputed the same way; participants with missing data at a given visit were considered in the non-responder group.

Results

A total of 311 patients (153 patients treated with dupilumab and 158 patients receiving placebo) randomised in the PRIME (N = 151) and PRIME2 (N = 160) trials were included for this post hoc analysis. The mean (standard deviation [SD]) age at baseline was 49.5 (16.1) years, and 65.3% of patients were female (Table 2). Proportion of Patients with Only None-to-Mild Symptoms

A significantly higher proportion of patients treated with dupilumab experienced only none-to-mild itch (26.8% vs 7.6%, p < 0.0001), skin pain (38.6% vs 19.0%, p < 0.0001), and sleep disturbance (28.8% vs 10.1%, p < 0.0001) at week 24 than those receiving placebo (Fig. 2). At week 24, patients treated with dupilumab had 5.9-fold (95% CI 2.5, 13.7), 3.4-fold (95% CI 1.9, 6.3), and 4.3-fold (95% CI 2.1, 8.9) higher odds of achieving only none-to-mild itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance, respectively, than those receiving placebo (Fig. 2). The proportion of patients with only none-to-mild itch (15.7% vs 2.5%, p = 0.0004) and skin pain (33.3% vs 15.2%, p = 0.0006) was significantly higher in patients treated with dupilumab as early as week 12 (Fig. 2).

Proportion of Patients with Only Severe-to-Very Severe Symptoms

At week 24, dupilumab treatment also led to a decrease in the proportion of patients experiencing only severe-to-very severe symptoms in comparison to placebo. At week 24, the odds of experiencing only severe-to-very severe itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance were 3.8-fold (95% CI 2.2, 6.5), 3.9-fold (95% CI 2.2, 6.9), and 3.9-fold (95% CI 2.0, 7.7) lower for patients treated with dupilumab than for those receiving placebo (electronic supplementary material Fig. S1).

None-to-Mild Symptom Days

In comparison with patients receiving placebo, patients treated with dupilumab spent numerically more days per week with none-to-mild itch (week 12, 1.5 vs 0.5; week 24, 2.4 vs 0.9), skin pain (week 12, 2.6 vs 1.4; week 24, 3.4 vs 1.9), and sleep disturbance (week 12, 2.2 vs 1.2; week 24, 2.7 vs 1.4) at week 12 and week 24 (Fig. 1). Patients treated with dupilumab experienced fewer days per week with severe-to-very severe symptoms than those receiving placebo at week 12 and week 24 (Fig. 1).

Discussion

The treatment effects described through daily symptom burden analyses can be more easily understood by patients, while complementing traditional endpoints based on weekly average of symptoms (change from baseline and response rates). These measures could potentially be incorporated into disease control assessments for PN, similar to the symptom-based remission criteria for asthma [22]. Further, as a result of the nature of symptoms of PN, the daily symptom burden could potentially reflect the patient experiences more accurately than in other related pruritic conditions like atopic dermatitis.

In this post hoc study, the daily symptom burden of patients with PN treated with dupilumab was assessed via two patient-centric approaches using pooled data from the PRIME and PRIME2 trials. The high proportion of patients with severe-to-very severe symptoms at baseline reflects the high daily symptom burden in patients with PN. Patients suffered from severe-to-very severe symptoms nearly 75% of days per week at baseline, confirming the considerable daily impact of PN. This pronounced symptom burden was consistently observed across all three evaluated symptoms: itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance.

Dupilumab treatment significantly improved the daily symptom burden with more than 25% of the patients reporting only none-to-mild symptoms at week 24. These results align with the primary trial outcomes, which demonstrated the significant efficacy of dupilumab in improving various PN symptoms [11]. Previous post hoc analyses of the PRIME and PRIME2 trials demonstrated that dupilumab-treated patients achieved clinically meaningful improvements in itch, skin pain, sleep disturbance, and QoL compared to those receiving placebo [14]. The improvement in daily symptom burden reported in this study further substantiates the robust clinical efficacy of dupilumab.

Patients with PN routinely report a high disease burden despite standard of care treatment, with itch being the most burdensome symptom [1]. This study revealed that dupilumab therapy significantly improved daily itch severity; dupilumab-treated patients were approximately six times more likely to report only a none-to-mild itch compared to those receiving placebo. In a previous post hoc analysis, patients treated with dupilumab showed significant improvement in itch as early as 2 weeks after treatment initiation; this improvement was maintained through week 24. Reductions in skin pain and sleep disturbance were reported from week 3 onwards, suggesting that the improvements in skin pain, sleep disturbance, and QoL may occur consequent to the improvement in itch [14]. A significant reduction in daily itch burden was observed as early as week 12 in this study, while improvement in daily skin pain and sleep disturbance became significant at week 24. These results further substantiate the primary role of itch reduction in improving skin pain and sleep disturbance. These improvements in daily symptom burden observed across symptoms in dupilumab-treated patients indicate that dupilumab provides comprehensive symptom relief, substantially reducing the overall disease burden.

However, the post hoc nature of these analyses and the requirement for psychometric validation and acceptability of these new patient-centric endpoints in PN are key limitations. Although this patient-centric investigation primarily generates a hypothesis, based on a novel technique of assessing daily symptom burden by using an electronic diary, further validation, by engaging with patients via interviews, and psychometric validation is needed. Nonetheless, noting the high rates of completion of the daily symptom burden record (≥ 90%) at baseline and at week 24, the current analysis provides crucial information to guide PN management decision-making for clinicians and patients.

In the context of healthcare, digital literacy may often translate into eHealth literacy, i.e. use of technology and online resources to effectively search health-related information and its application [23, 24]. As noted by multinational studies spanning across North America, Asia, and Europe, eHealth literacy (assessed by using eHEALS, scale 8–40, higher scores indicate better literacy) among internet users was generally high [23, 24]. Older aged adults (40+ years) showed higher digital health literacy and willingness to engage digitally [23–26]. Considering the eHealth literacy in today’s age, the use of an electronic diary in this study may be considered relevant and may assist in daily symptom burden management of patients with PN.

Conclusions

Dupilumab therapy resulted in greater improvements in daily symptom burden with respect to itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance than placebo at week 24 as evidenced by improved itch, skin pain, and sleep disturbance. Beyond validating the efficacy of dupilumab, this study establishes a new, more relatable patient-centric method for assessing key daily symptoms of PN.

Teleconsultation and proxy visits, and tele-diabetes education: The need of the hour for managing gestational diabetes mellitus
Authors: Felix K. Jebasingh, Nihal Thomas
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We greatly commend the insightful review, “Telemedicine and Its Impact on Academic Medical Centers: A Narrative Review.”[1] The authors have highlighted the importance of telemedicine during the coronavirus 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic in several diseases, including intensive care unit (ICU) setups. However, our article highlights the importance of telemedicine in managing hyperglycemia in pregnancy in the current scenario, which could complement regular hospital visits.

Hyperglycemia in pregnancy has been on the rise over the last three to four decades due to a broader change in lifestyle patterns and a parallel increase in the prevalence of obesity.[2] The two distinct forms of hyperglycemia during pregnancy are gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and pre-existing diabetes. Pre-existing diabetes mellitus is also increasing due to the increase in the early onset of diabetes among young adults.

Pre-existing diabetes mellitus is associated with a higher frequency and severity of adverse maternal-fetal outcomes compared to GDM if glycemic control is not properly managed. The complications related to diabetes can also aggravate among mothers with pre-existing diabetes. Furthermore, these patients tend to have DM even after the delivery. In contrast, GDM typically manifests later in the pregnancy or sometimes early in the pregnancy as well, and may have a comparatively milder risk of developing fetal complications but has an increased likelihood of developing type 2 diabetes later in life for the mother.

Effective glycemic management strategies must be tailored to the type and severity of diabetes during pregnancy. This requires a multidisciplinary approach and effective coordination among obstetricians, physicians, endocrinologists, diabetes educators, and dietitians. Patients must attend the hospital regularly, in addition to their regular antenatal appointments.

Recent advances in healthcare delivery, particularly telemedicine consultation, offer promising potential to enhance the management of both GDM and pre-existing diabetes during pregnancy. Teleconsultations facilitate timely access to specialized care, enable remote continuous glucose monitoring, and support patient education and engagement without the constraints of frequent in-person visits. They are found to improve the quality of life, enhance patient satisfaction, and reduce the financial burden for patients.[3]

More so, proxy visits, where family members or caregivers can attend glycemia-related consultations on behalf of the patient, further ensure uninterrupted care when a direct patient visit to the clinic is challenging.

Moreover, tele-diabetes education delivered through digital platforms, including video calls, represents an innovative and accessible method for empowering patients’ self-management skills in the care of gestational diabetes.[4]

In summary, the rising prevalence of both gestational and pre-existing diabetes mellitus in an Indian context necessitates enhanced diagnostic vigilance and innovative management strategies for adequate glycemic control.[5] Integrating teleconsultation, proxy visits, and tele-diabetes education can be a component of a modernized framework aimed at optimizing maternal and fetal health outcomes in this vulnerable population. These approaches not only improve clinical outcomes by enabling personalized care plans but also empower patients through education and self-management support, reducing travel, minimizing time away from work or family, and hospital visits except for antenatal check-ups. Telehealth will not replace regular visits; however, it is a potential alternative and complement to hospital visits for selective patients with GDM.

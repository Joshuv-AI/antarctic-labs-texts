In vivo CAR-T cell therapy: latest updates from 2025 ASH annual meeting
Authors: Pan Gao, Jun Liu, Jun Ma, Hengrui Ma, Ya Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in treating hematological malignancies. However, this approach faces substantial limitations, including protracted manufacturing periods, elevated costs, restricted patient eligibility attributable to manufacturing constraints, and intolerate lymphodepleting preconditioning regimens. To address these challenges, in vivo CAR-T cell therapy has emerged as a promising alternative, directly engineering immune cells within the patient through targeted delivery systems such as engineered viral vectors, lipid nanoparticles, and other non-viral platforms. This approach offers immediate administration and enhanced scalability compared with ex vivo methods. The 2025 American Society of Hematology annual meeting presented multiple abstracts advancing this field, with this review focusing on studies demonstrating novel delivery system innovations, including engineered lentiviral vectors and non-viral platforms, as well as expanded therapeutic applications beyond oncology to autoimmune diseases. By integrating these conference presentations with recent peer-reviewed literature, this paper summarizes the current landscapes of in vivo CAR-T cell therapy presented at the conference.

To the editor

The 2025 American Society of Hematology (ASH) annual meeting presented substantial advances in in vivo chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy, encompassing technological innovations in delivery systems and therapeutic expansions from malignancies to autoimmune diseases.

Technological innovation in delivery systems

In vivo CAR-T cell therapy enables direct reprogramming of endogenous immune cells, circumventing the prolonged manufacturing, high costs, and lymphodepleting chemotherapy required by ex vivo approaches [1–3]. Critical challenges remain in achieving targeted delivery, preventing rapid clearance, and enhancing transduction efficiency [1]. Current platforms comprise engineered lentiviral vectors and non-viral systems encompassing lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), mRNA, circular RNA, and polymers [1–3]. Engineered lentiviral vectors achieved T cell-specific targeting through cocal-G mutation and CD7 retargeting, with CD47 overexpression and MHC-I knockout prolonging in vivo persistence. In humanized mice, this platform induced B cell depletion, regression of myeloma cell line-derived tumor xenografts (CDX), and functional CAR-T generation without toxicity [4].

For non-viral LNPs-RNA systems, controllable ligand attachment modification and purification (CLAMP)-enabled mRNA-LNPs achieved site-specific ligand binding with ionizable lipids, eliciting targeted and sustained CAR expression in NOD.Cg-Prkdcscid Il2rgtm1Wjl/SzJ (NOG) mice at 0.01 mg/kg with > 95% B cell depletion [5]. Extending this platform, CD8-targeted LNPs delivering anti-CD19 CAR mRNA induced B cell depletion in blood and spleen of humanized mice; in non-human primates (NHPs), two 0.3 mg/kg doses achieved depletion in spleen and bone marrow (BM) [6, 7]. Circular RNA-LNPs, wherein LNPs serves as the vector and circular oRNA as the therapeutic payload, utilized immunotropic LNPs with redosable dosing to achieve > 98% peripheral, > 99% splenic, and > 96% BM depletion in NHPs at 0.5 mg/kg×2, enabling transient panCAR therapy [8]. Separately, polymer-based vectors represented by star-shaped multi-arm polyaspartamide utilized clathrin-mediated endocytosis. In C57BL/6 mice at 1.6 mg/kg×2, this achieved 7-day peripheral and splenic B cell depletion until day 21 [9]. For gene editing, targeted LNPs delivering RNA gene writers leveraged ionizable LNPs with target-primed reverse transcription (TPRT) to permanently integrate CAR constructs, yielding 24-55% CAR-T in mice and 40% green fluorescent protein (GFP)-T in NHPs [10].

Therapeutic expansion: B-cell malignancies and autoimmune diseases

Advancements in delivery systems have propelled applications from B-cell malignancies to autoimmune diseases. (Table 2). In B-cell malignancies, lymphodepletion-free mRNA-LNPs achieved > 95% B cell depletion in humanized mice, demonstrating outpatient feasibility [5]. Although clinical data for B-cell malignancies remain limited, robust preclinical efficacy supports translational potential.

In autoimmune diseases, circRNA-LNPs therapy in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) models showed complete B cell reduction with concomitant anti-dsDNA titer diminution, while rituximab reduced B cells without correlating with anti-dsDNA titer reduction. The transient nature of this therapy, with B cell recovery at 2-3 weeks, supports immune reset potential [8]. CD8-targeted LNPs platform in NHPs demonstrated effective lymphoid tissues depletion, supporting clinical translation for B-cell mediated autoimmune conditions [6, 7]. Most notably, in vivo CD19 CAR-T therapy in refractory SLE patients achieved effective B cell depletion, reduced SLEDAI-2K scores, and favorable safety profile without grade 3-4 cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), constituting first-in-human clinical validation [11].

Conclusion

Delivery systems innovations have enhanced precision and safety, broadening applications from hematological malignancies to autoimmune diseases [3, 7]. Beyond T cells, extending these delivery platforms to innate immune cells such as neutrophils and macrophages may further broaden therapeutic potential. Notably, CAR-macrophages engineered via poly β-amino ester (PBAE) or lipid nanoparticles demonstrate unique advantages in remodeling solid tumor microenvironments and overcoming fibrotic barriers. Nevertheless, critical challenges persist, including inadequate targeting efficiency, potential off-target effects, and unresolved regulatory barriers. Viral vectors confront pre-existing immunity and insertional mutagenesis risks, whereas non-viral systems exhibit transient expression and suboptimal delivery efficiency [3]. Resolution demands optimized targeting ligands, precise gene-editing regulation, and accumulation of robust clinical data [7]. Interdisciplinary collaboration in vector design refinement and safety assessment positions in vivo CAR-T engineering at the threshold of clinical translation, thereby offering new hope to more patients.

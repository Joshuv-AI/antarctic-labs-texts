Acupuncture as a therapeutic intervention for post-COVID-19 vaccination urticaria: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Zixia Wang, Yao Xiao, Muyao Wang, Qiqi Chen, Yueqi Lin, Xinyue Fang, Wenhui Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe COVID-19 pandemic persisted for over 3 years since its onset in December 2019, posing an ongoing global threat to human health. In the absence of specific antiviral medications for COVID-19, vaccination has emerged as a popular preventive measure adopted by the general public. However, an undesirable consequence of COVID-19 vaccination has been the frequent incidence of urticaria, a type of adverse skin manifestations. Despite the prevalence of this issue, there is currently a lack of clinical evidence exploring the potential utility of acupuncture as a therapeutic approach to managing urticaria arising after COVID-19 vaccination. To address this knowledge gap, this study aims to comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness and safety of acupuncture as a therapeutic intervention for treating urticaria in the general population following COVID-19 vaccination.Methods and analysisThe retrieval strategies employed in this study involve obtaining all relevant articles published from December 2019 to October 2023. These articles will be obtained from databases including PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Chinese Biomedical Literature Database (SinoMed), VIP database and the WanFang database. Subsequently, the collected articles will undergo a thorough screening process based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Additionally, study quality will be evaluated using the Cochrane risk bias assessment tool. To conduct the meta-analysis, we will employ the Review Manager software (RevMan V.5.3). Finally, the study findings will be evaluated for their level of evidence.Ethics and disseminationAs this is a secondary review of published clinical data, this study does not involve direct contact with human subjects, and therefore, ethical approval and consent are not required. The findings of the study will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed journal, ensuring that the results undergo rigorous evaluation by experts in the field.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42022377343Keywords: COVID-19, DERMATOLOGY, COMPLEMENTARY MEDICINE

Introduction

In December 2019, the first case of pneumonia caused by a novel pathogen named SARS-CoV-2 was reported in China.1 2 This highly contagious virus quickly spread globally, leading to the declaration of the global pandemic known as COVID-19 by the WHO. To mitigate the devastating impact of COVID-19, vaccination has emerged as a pivotal strategy in controlling the epidemic. Experts estimate that an extensive vaccination campaign using approximately 10–11 billion doses will be necessary to achieve adequate immunisation coverage to control the spread of the virus.3 In response to this urgent need, countries worldwide have accelerated research and development efforts, leading to the regulatory authorisation of several COVID-19 vaccines. Notably vaccines that have received such approval include Moderna’s mRNA-1273, BNT162b2 (developed by Pfizer-BioNTech), Oxford-AstraZeneca’s AZD1222, Gamaleya’s Sputnik V, Johnson & Johnson’s Janssen’s Ad26.COV2.S, Sinopharm’s BBIBP-CorV, The Vector Institute’s EpiVacCorona, CanSino’s Convidecia, Bharat’s Covaxin, Sinopharm’s WIBP-CorV, Chumakov’s CoviVac and Anhui Zhifei and Longcom’s ZF2001.4 These vaccines have undergone rigorous clinical trials, demonstrating their efficacy in reducing the risk of symptomatic COVID-19 infection and, particularly, the risk of severe illness and hospitalisation. However, while COVID-19 vaccines have proven effective, close attention must be paid to potential adverse reactions associated with their administration. Generally, the systemic immune response to COVID-19 vaccination is most pronounced on the first day and subsides within a week. Allergic reactions have been primarily attributed to two vaccine ingredients: polysorbate 80 and polyethylene glycol. Furthermore, other components such as preservatives and stabilisers have also been considered as potential factors contributing to cutaneous allergic reactions.5

Urticaria, a commonly encountered dermatological disorder, is characterised by the development of intensely pruritic wheals varying in size from a few millimetres to several centimetres or larger, often accompanied by subcutaneous tissue oedema.6 7 It can be elicited by various factors, including infections and medication, with respiratory tract infections being recognised as a predominant trigger, particularly in paediatric cases.8 The mechanisms underlying urticaria encompass type I hypersensitivity reactions associated with diverse allergens, as well as mast cell degranulation leading to increased levels of bradykinin or activation of IgE receptors.9 10 Urticaria has been reported to be associated with viral infections, including those caused by SARS-CoV-2, the virus responsible for COVID-19 infection. Notably, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has identified urticaria, angioedema, rash, nausea and respiratory symptoms as the most commonly reported reactions to COVID-19 vaccines.11 Acute urticaria induced by COVID-19 vaccination is typically self-limited, with symptoms resolving within a few hours and a duration of no more than 24 hours. However, recurrent episodes of pruritic wheals can occur within a period of less than 6 weeks.12 In severe cases, complications such as laryngeal oedema and urticarial vasculitis have been reported, requiring prompt medical intervention.12 13 Additionally, the emergence of chronic urticaria as a prolonged reaction to COVID-19 vaccination has been observed. Individuals experiencing this long-term reaction may develop a daily rash that is widespread and disappears within 30–60 min, only to reappear at different random locations later in the day of the following day.14 Significantly, a clinical study reported that nearly 50% of participants developed urticaria within a range of 5–54 min following administration of mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccines.15 Additionally, a study conducted in Italy found that approximately 20% of COVID-19 patients in a medical ward exhibited cutaneous lesions, including urticaria.16 Cutaneous reactions, such as acute and chronic urticaria, following COVID-19 vaccination have had a significant impact on individuals’ quality of life. Many individuals have reported experiencing substantial suffering due to urticaria, which has subsequently increased their hesitancy to receive future doses of the COVID-19 vaccine.17 Hence, despite potential limitations in sample size, the notable incidence of urticaria and its consequential effects emphasise the importance of conducting additional research to investigate this unique adverse reaction and its potential association with vaccination.

Acupuncture, a widely practised method in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), has shown promising effects in the treatment and alleviation of various dermatological conditions. By stimulating specific acupoints along meridians, acupuncture is believed to induce anti-inflammatory effects through the central suppression of the innate immune system.18 Additionally, studies have suggested that electroacupuncture can alleviate inflammation by suppressing p38 MAPK activation, a key signalling pathway involved in inflammatory responses, and promoting local production of IL-10, an important anti-inflammatory cytokine.19

Several systematic reviews have already demonstrated the efficacy and safety of acupuncture in treating urticaria.20–22 However, despite the existing evidence, no high-quality meta-analyses have specifically examined the efficacy of acupuncture in relieving urticaria caused by COVID-19 vaccines. Therefore, the objective of this study is to provide an updated evidence-based review by summarising relevant randomised controlled trials (RCTs) that investigate the association between urticaria and COVID-19 vaccination, aiming to contribute robust evidence regarding the effectiveness of acupuncture in this specific context.

Study inclusion criteria

Study design

The inclusion criteria for this review are as follows: (1) all randomised RCTs investigating the use of acupuncture as a stand-alone intervention or in combination with other interventions for treating urticaria symptoms after COVID-19 vaccination and (2) relevant literature published in Chinese or English.

Conversely, the following are specified as exclusion criteria: (1) non-RCTs, (2) reviews, (3) case reports or case series, (4) studies without experimental data and (5) animal studies.

Participant characteristics

The inclusion criteria encompass patients who developed urticaria following COVID-19 vaccination and whose symptoms meet the recognised diagnostic criteria for urticaria.23 24 The diagnosis of urticaria must use relevant diagnostic criteria, such as the reference Clinical Guidelines for urticaria in China (2018 edition)23 and the EAACI/GA²LEN/EDF/WAO guideline for the definition, classification, diagnosis and management of urticaria.24 These guidelines outline specific criteria for diagnosing urticaria, including: (1) the presence of a central swelling of variable size in the skin, typically surrounded by reflex erythema, accompanied by itching or burning sensations. These symptoms usually resolve within 30 min to 24 hours; (2) acute urticaria is diagnosed when wheals, angioedema or a combination of both occur for a duration of less than 6 weeks and (3) chronic urticaria is diagnosed when the symptoms persist for more than 6 weeks. There will be no limitations based on age, gender, occupation, race or nationally.

The following exclusion criteria will be applied: (1) individuals who do not meet the aforementioned diagnostic and inclusion criteria, (2) individuals with severe mental disorders or cognitive impairment that may confound our primary outcome measures, (3) individuals with severe underlying medical conditions the heart, kidneys or other organs that may confound the outcomes, (4) individuals with contraindications to acupuncture treatment, such as a tendency to bleed or the presence of local infections at acupoints and (5) women who are pregnant or breast feeding.

Interventions and comparisons

In the treatment group, the intervention will involve acupuncture, while the comparison group intervention will receive sham acupuncture (superficial acupuncture without targeting specific acupoints) along with conventional western medicine treatment. There will be no restrictions on the frequency, duration or type of treatment administered in either group.

Outcome variables

The primary outcome measure will be essential and any study that does not report the primary outcome measure will be excluded from the analysis. However, the inclusion of secondary outcome measures is not mandatory, as they serve as supplementary measures to assess the efficacy of acupuncture in treating urticaria in our study. Therefore, studies without secondary outcome measures will still be considered for inclusion in the analysis, focusing primarily on the primary outcome measure. The considered outcome measures are as follows:

Primary outcome

Weekly urticaria activity scores

The measurement of Weekly Urticaria Activity Scores, as the primary outcome of disease activity, impact and control, will be assessed at the initial visit and during every follow-up visit. It is a widely used tool in clinical practice to assess disease activity and treatment response in patients with chronic spontaneous urticaria, which represents the sum score over a continuous 7-day period. This approach aligns with the recommendations outlined in the EAACI/GA²LEN/EDF/WAO guideline for the urticaria definition, classification, diagnosis and management.

Search strategy

Electronic searches

A comprehensive search will be conducted to identify all relevant publications from December 2019 to October 2023. The following databases will be used: PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Chinese Biomedical Literature Database (SinoMed), VIP database and the WanFang database. The search strategy will focus on the search terms “COVID-19 vaccination”, “Acupuncture” and “Urticaria” within the constraints of ‘randomised controlled trials’ and ‘English or Chinese language’. The collected literature will be screened using the EndNote software, to facilitate the organisation and management of search results. Table 1 outlines the PubMed search strategy. Data collection and analysis

Selection of studies

The article selection process will involve two independent reviewers who will assess titles, abstracts and full texts of all identified articles according to the predefined inclusion criteria. The screening results will be recorded and imported into the EndNote software. Any discrepancies or disagreements between the two reviewers will be resolved through discussion and consensus. If necessary, a final decision will be reached after consulting a third reviewer. The flow chart depicting the screening process is presented in figure 1.

Flow chart of study selection process. RCT, randomised controlled trial.

Data extraction and management

During the data extraction phase, two reviewers will extract and manage the following information from the identified articles: title, journal, primary author, publication year, participant traits, intervention method and protocol, follow-up duration and results, outcomes assessed, and reported adverse events, among other relevant details. Any disagreement or discrepancies that arise during the data extraction process between the two reviewers will be resolved through joint arbitration. This will involve discussion and the involvement of a third reviewer if necessary.

Missing data

Efforts will be made to obtain the full article or relevant data for each study. In case the complete article or necessary data cannot be obtained through the available sources, the corresponding author will be contacted to request the missing information. However, if the missing data cannot be acquired even after attempting to contact the corresponding author, the study will be excluded from the review.

Statistical analysis

Two researchers will be responsible for extracting, inputting and calculating the data, while a third researcher will be assigned to validate the data to ensure accuracy. Means and SDs of change scores between baseline and post-treatment will be collected for each outcome. In instances where no SDs are provided in the study, we will calculate the mean change in each arm and the SD of the changes using baseline and outcome scores.25 If studies report outcomes at different time intervals after treatment, we will use data from the initial time after the completion of the entire treatment. For studies where SDs of the outcome are not reported, we will estimate the missing SDs using available information such as CIs, SEs, p values and t-statistics. In instances where only the median and IQR are provided, a specific formula will be applied to calculate SDs and means.26 We will contact the authors to request related information if any necessary data are missing in the included studies. Studies with insufficient data for quantitative synthesis will be excluded from the meta-analysis.

Meta-analysis of the included studies will be conducted using RevMan V.5.3 statistical software. Statistical significance will be determined at a significance level of p<0.05. For the quantitative analysis, data will be pooled when comparable results are available from at least two studies. Study data that cannot be pooled will be provided as an individual result as part of a narrative summary. For continuous data, the mean deviation or standard mean deviation with a 95% CI will be used as effect measures. Dichotomous results will be analysed using the risk ratio and its corresponding 95% CI to analyse dichotomous outcomes. To assess clinical heterogeneity among the included studies, the I2 statistic will be employed. For statistical heterogeneity, we will use the Higgins I2 test and χ2 test and ensure the reliability of the study findings. In the absence of significant heterogeneity (I2<50%, p>0.1), the fixed effects model will be employed for the meta-analysis. However, if heterogeneity is observed (p<0.1 and I2>50%), the random effects model will be applied. In cases where significant clinical heterogeneity is present, additional analyses such as subgroup analysis, sensitivity analysis or descriptive analysis will be performed.

Risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers will independently assess the risk of bias in the included studies, following the guidelines provided in The Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6. The assessment will focus on seven aspects known to introduce biases: randomisation process, participants and personnel blinding, outcome assessment blinding, incomplete outcome data, allocation concealment, selective reporting and other potential sources of bias. Each aspect will be evaluated, and the risk of bias will be categorised as low, high or unclear for each study. In cases where disagreements arise between the two reviewers, consensus will be reached through discussion, involving other reviewers if necessary.

Heterogeneity assessment and subgroup analysis

The RevMan software will be used to evaluate the heterogeneity of the included studies. Heterogeneity will be considered negligible if the p value is ≥0.5 and the I2 statistic is ≤50%. In contrast, if the p value is <0.5 and the I2 statistic is >50%, this will indicate a substantial degree of heterogeneity among the included studies. In the presence of substantial heterogeneity, we will conduct subgroup analysis or meta-regression analysis to investigate potential factors contributing to the heterogeneity.

Sensitivity analysis

A sensitivity analysis will be conducted to assess the robustness of the study findings. This analysis will aim to evaluate the potential impact of various factors, such as methodological quality, sample size and missing data, on the final outcomes. Additionally, the sensitivity analysis will evaluate the effect of including or excluding studies based on their methodological quality.

Quality of evidence evaluation

Two reviewers will independently evaluate and grade the quality of evidence based on parameters such as publication bias, inconsistency, imprecision and study limitations. Based on the existing evidence, each parameter will be classified as ‘high’, ‘medium’, ‘low’ or ‘very low’ quality. During the assessment process, any disagreements that arise between the two reviewers will be resolved through negotiation and discussion. If consensus cannot be reached, a third senior researcher will be consulted to make a final determination.

Patient and public involvement

As this is a secondary review of published clinical data, it does not involve any patient or the public.

Ethics and dissemination

As this study did not involve the collection or use of personal data related to the participants, ethical approval was not required. The research findings will be shared and disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference reports.

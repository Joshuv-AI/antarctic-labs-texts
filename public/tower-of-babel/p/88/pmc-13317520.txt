Coexistence of heterotopic ossification and anterior bone loss after multilevel cervical disc arthroplasty: illustrative case
Authors: Yen-Yu Lin, Ping-Chuan Liu, Yi-Hsuan Kuo, Wen-Cheng Huang, Jau-Ching Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUNDMultilevel (≥ 2) cervical disc arthroplasty (CDA) has become increasingly popular over the past decade. The authors report the first case of three-level CDA demonstrating divergent bone remodeling, heterotopic ossification (HO), and anterior bone loss (ABL) at separate operative levels, with preserved segmental motion.OBSERVATIONSA 45-year-old man underwent three-level CDA (C4–5, C5–6, C6–7) with the ROTAIO prosthesis for medically refractory cervical myeloradiculopathy secondary to multilevel disc herniation superimposed on congenital stenosis. Surveillance imaging identified grade 2 ABL at C4–5 at 6 months and McAfee class II HO at C6–7 at 18 months; the intervening C5–6 remained unaffected. At the 2-year follow-up, the patient was asymptomatic with mobility preserved across all three levels.LESSONSThe coexistence of HO and ABL at different operative levels within a single patient, despite identical implants and uniform perioperative conditions, implicates level-specific mechanical factors—rather than implant design, material properties, or patient-intrinsic variables—as the principal drivers of bone remodeling after multilevel CDA. Cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) arising across multiple levels presents a surgical challenge in which motion-preserving reconstruction is both technically demanding and clinically desirable.1,2 Cervical disc arthroplasty (CDA) has been established as a viable alternative to anterior cervical discectomy and fusion, offering comparable or superior clinical outcomes with the advantage of segmental mobility preservation and reduced risk of adjacent segment degeneration.3,4 As CDA has gained wider adoption, two radiological sequelae have attracted increasing attention: heterotopic ossification (HO) and anterior bone loss (ABL), both frequently identified on surveillance imaging yet typically without clinical consequence.

HO, classified by the McAfee grading system (class 0–IV),5,6 is the most extensively studied radiological complication following CDA. The reported incidence of HO increases from approximately 50% at 1–2 years to 70% at 10 years postoperatively7 and rising further with number of operative levels.8 Although high-grade HO raises concern for motion restriction, the majority of cases involve lower grades without clinical consequence.9 Representing the opposite pole of anterior bone remodeling, ABL has garnered increasing attention as a distinct radiological sequela of CDA.10 Typically emerging within 3–6 months postoperatively and stabilizing within 1 year,11 ABL has been reported in approximately half of operative levels across prosthesis types, with severe cases causing implant exposure in fewer than 4% of instances.12

The etiologies of both HO and ABL remain incompletely understood, with proposed contributing factors including implant biomechanics, surgical technique, and perioperative medication use.11,13–15 As individual cases documenting their coexistence contribute meaningful data to this evolving understanding, we report, to our knowledge, the first case of concurrent HO and ABL developing at different levels within a three-level CDA construct. Both phenomena were detected incidentally on protocol surveillance imaging, with motion preserved at all operative levels and no clinical deterioration at the 2-year follow-up. This observation may contribute meaningful data to the evolving understanding of post-CDA bone remodeling.

Illustrative Case

A 45-year-old man with no prior systemic disease presented with a 3-month history of neck pain and right arm radiculopathy. On physical examination, spasticity and hyperreflexia were present in all four limbs, accompanied by ataxic gait. Cervical spine MRI revealed multilevel disc herniations at C4–5, C5–6, and C6–7, causing spinal cord compression and right neuroforaminal stenosis. T2 intramedullary signal change was identified at C5–6. In the context of a Torg-Pavlov ratio of 0.6 and a spinal cord occupation ratio of 0.75, a diagnosis of congenital cervical spinal stenosis with CSM was established (Fig. 1). Preoperative segmental lordosis measured by Cobb angle was 2.2° at C4–5, 1.0° at C5–6, and 5.0° at C6–7. Dynamic radiographs demonstrated segmental range of motion (ROM) of 14.3° at C4–5, 12.5° at C5–6, and 9.6° at C6–7, confirming preserved mobility at all operative levels. Three-level CDA was performed at C4–7 through a single right-sided oblique incision via Cloward’s approach.17 Following discectomy at each level, endplates were prepared using a high-speed burr without endplate violation. The ROTAIO cervical disc prosthesis (SIGNUS Medizintechnik GmbH), an unconstrained, mobile-core design, was implanted at each level: 6 × 17 × 15 mm at C4–5 and C5–6, and 7 × 17 × 15 mm at C6–7. Implant positioning was confirmed by intraoperative fluoroscopy. The operative time was 167 minutes with an estimated blood loss of 250 mL. Postoperative nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) prophylaxis with celecoxib (200 mg twice daily for 14 days) was administered.

Postoperatively, pain resolved and spasticity improved markedly. At the 6-month follow-up, grade 2 ABL without prosthesis exposure was identified at the inferior endplate of C4–5 and remained stable throughout the 2-year follow-up period. At 18 months, McAfee class II HO was identified at the anterior C6–7 (Fig. 2). Throughout the follow-ups, C5–6, the level in between, remained free of events. At the 2-year follow-up, the segmental lordosis was 6.3° at C4–5, 4.4° at C5–6, and 7.5° at C6–7, representing lordotic changes of +4.1°, +3.4°, and +2.5°, respectively. The segmental ROM was 17.2° at C4–5, 10.8° at C5–6, and 7.4° at C6–7, indicating maintained mobility across all three levels (Fig. 3). The patient’s modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association score was 16, and the patient remained neurologically intact. All radiological findings were detected incidentally on scheduled surveillance rather than prompted by clinical deterioration. Discussion

Observations

The coexistence of HO and ABL at distinct operative levels within the same construct raises important questions about the biomechanical determinants of bone remodeling after CDA. Each lesion followed an independent natural history, yet the patient remained clinically stable throughout.

McAfee class II HO was identified at C6–7 18 months postoperatively. This timing is consistent with the expected time-dependent incidence of HO after CDA.9 The multilevel nature of the reconstruction likely contributed to this risk, as multilevel CDA carries an HO incidence more than twice that of single-level procedures.8 At C6–7 specifically, two features stood out: the lower endplate coverage ratio18 and a locally kyphotic shell angle.14 Both have been identified as independent predictors of HO, and together they provide a level-specific biomechanical explanation for HO development at this site. The relatively lower Hounsfield units at C6 and C7 may reflect a physiological craniocaudal gradient of decreasing cervical vertebral bone density, as described in the literature.19 Despite these predisposing factors, the HO did not progress. It remained class II throughout follow-up, nonbridging, with preserved segmental motion, and never advanced to grade 3 or 4 ankylosis. Routine postoperative NSAID prophylaxis administered at our institution may have contributed to limiting this progression, consistent with our previous report of reduced HO incidence and severity following cyclooxygenase inhibition after CDA.15

Grade 2 ABL, defined by our previous grading system,20 was identified at C4–5 and is consistent with published incidence rates, as ABL has been reported in approximately half of operative levels across prosthesis types.21 Its onset at 6 months and subsequent stability match the self-limiting natural history described by Kieser et al., who reported that ABL characteristically emerges within 3–6 months and stabilizes within 1 year.11 Again, level-specific factors were evident. Among the three operative levels, C4–5 demonstrated the highest shell angle28 and postoperative increase in segmental lordosis,20 features identified as risk factors for ABL by Chen et al. and Kuo et al., respectively. Together, these findings provide a biomechanical basis for the preferential development of ABL at this level.

Multiple hypotheses have been proposed to explain HO and ABL following CDA, encompassing implant design,10,22 surgical technique,11,14 perioperative pharmacological factors,15 and patient-specific biological variables.23–25 The present case offers a unique within-patient comparison. Identical prostheses were implanted across three levels in a single patient under uniform perioperative conditions, effectively controlling for implant material, patient genetics, and pharmacological environment. Within this controlled context, HO developed at C6–7, ABL at C4–5, and neither phenomenon at the intervening C5–6. Among the three operative levels, C5–6 exhibited an intermediate lordotic change and a neutral shell angle, placing it within a biomechanical neutral zone. This pattern strongly implicates level-specific biomechanical factors as the primary determinant of bone remodeling.

For HO, Chung et al. proposed that insufficient posterior endplate coverage creates a nonuniform pressure distribution at the implant-endplate interface.26 Compressive stress becomes concentrated at the uncovered endplate margins, generating a periosteal osteogenic stimulus that drives ectopic bone formation. This hypothesis is further supported computationally by finite element analysis, which demonstrated that posterior endplate coverage deficiency generates escalating stress concentration at the operative segment endplate, elevating the risk of periprosthetic ectopic ossification.27 The development of ABL can be attributed to two complementary biomechanical mechanisms. First, Kuo et al. described a stress shielding effect following CDA.20 Increased postoperative segmental lordosis redistributes axial loading away from the anterior endplate, depriving the anterior cortex of its normal physiological mechanical stimulus and triggering bone resorption. Second, Chen et al. demonstrated that patients who developed ABL had significantly higher shell angles than those without remodeling.28 A greater shell angle shifts loading force posteriorly at the bone-implant interface, further reducing anterior endplate stress and amplifying the resorptive stimulus. The subsequent stabilization of ABL at 6 months may reflect the final union between implant and vertebral body, which reduces interfacial micromotion and decelerates the remodeling process.28 Wang et al. proposed an ABL-HO polarity model in which the postimplantation disc angle determines the predominant anterior endplate stress.13 Lower angles generate compressive stress loading that favors osteogenesis and HO, while higher angles generate tensile stress that favors resorption and ABL. This polarity model aligns with and unifies the mechanistic findings discussed above. The compressive stress–loading limb is consistent with the nonuniform pressure distribution and anterior stress concentration described by Chung et al.26 The tensile stress limb parallels the stress shielding mechanism described by Kuo et al.20 and the higher shell angle effect reported by Chen et al.,28 both of which redirect loading away from the anterior cortex and promote ABL at levels with greater lordosis. This model may provide a mechanistic framework for the concurrent but directionally opposite bone remodeling responses observed across the three-level construct in our patient.

Lessons

The mechanisms underlying HO and ABL after CDA are multifactorial and remain inconclusive. Nevertheless, the coexistence of ectopic bone formation (HO) and bone resorption (ABL) at distinct operative levels within a single patient, despite identical prosthetic implants and uniform perioperative conditions, provides compelling evidence that mechanical factors are the principal drivers of bone remodeling after multilevel CDA. Despite the development of both HO and ABL, the patient remained clinically stable with preserved segmental ROM and sustained neurological recovery.

In the era of multilevel CDA, these radiological complications should be closely monitored through protocol surveillance imaging, even in asymptomatic patients, to characterize their natural history and guide timely intervention if clinical deterioration emerges. This report is limited by its single-patient, single-institution design, the use of a specific prosthesis (ROTAIO), and a 2-year follow-up period; longer surveillance will be needed to confirm the durability of clinical stability.

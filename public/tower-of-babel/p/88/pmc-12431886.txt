Generation of novel polyclonal antibodies against Mycobacterium tuberculosis lipoarabinomannan, EspB, and Mtb8
Authors: Zhuohong Yan, Jingwei Guo, Jinghong Wu, Hongtao Zhang, Teng Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe unique cell wall of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb) creates a barrier to hydrophilic drugs, which is crucial for its survival and pathogenicity. However, the immune reactivity elicited by its components remains incompletely understood. We aimed to assess the antibody responses induced by Mtb H37Rv cell wall components and to develop and characterize antigen-specific polyclonal antibodies (pAbs). Rabbits were immunized with these components. Immune serum reactivity was tested against various Mtb antigens. Specific polyclonal antibodies (pAbs) were purified by affinity chromatography. The results showed that immune serum reacted with lipoarabinomannan (LAM), ESAT-6 secretion system-1 (Esx-1) secreted protein B (EspB), and Mtb8, but showed no reactivity with other tested Mtb antigens. LAM-, EspB-, or Mtb8-specific pAbs were subsequently affinity-purified. The affinity-purified LAM pAb, EspB pAb, and Mtb8 pAb each demonstrated high specificity and sensitivity, showing no cross-reactivity with non-target antigens. They recognized antigens in culture supernatants and cells from diverse mycobacterial strains, including both slow-growing mycobacteria (SGM) and rapid-growing mycobacteria (RGM). In a sandwich ELISA using LAM pAb as the capture antibody and biotinylated LAM-specific monoclonal Abs (BJRbL01-Bio, BJRbL03-Bio, BJRbL20-Bio, BJRbL52-Bio, or BJRbL76-Bio) as detection antibodies, the assay detected SGM but did not react with RGM species. EspB pAb recognized EspB in both cell lysate and culture supernatant fractions, where full-length and mature EspB are predominantly found, respectively. Mtb8 pAb reacted with monomeric and polymeric forms of Mtb8. In conclusion, we successfully generated novel pAbs against LAM, EspB, and Mtb8, providing promising research tools for investigating these critical molecules.Key points Rabbit antibodies against Mtb H37Rv cell wall components target LAM, EspB, and Mtb8Novel LAM-, EspB-, and Mtb8-specific pAbs were generated and characterizedBroad mycobacterial reactivity and specific target detection confirm pAb utility

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) was the world’s second leading cause of death from a single infectious agent in 2022, after coronavirus disease (COVID-19) (WHO 2023). More than 10 million people continue to fall ill with TB every year (WHO 2023). Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb), the causative agent of TB, is one of the most successful bacterial pathogens. It is estimated that approximately 25% of the world’s population is infected with Mtb (Houben and Dodd 2016), but only 5% of those infected go on to develop TB disease within the first 2 years (Menzies et al. 2018; WHO 2023). The only licensed TB vaccine, derived from an attenuated M. bovis strain, is the bacillus Calmette-Guérin vaccine (BCG). Although BCG vaccination effectively protects infants from the early stages of tuberculosis, its efficacy against adult pulmonary tuberculosis is debatable (Lange et al. 2022). To exacerbate the situation, drug-resistant M. tuberculosis strains have increased significantly in recent decades.

Mtb drug resistance arises from multiple factors, including its “super-solid” cell wall, dormancy, and intrinsic antibiotic resistance (Pal et al. 2022). Secreted mycobacterial virulence proteins promote host cell modification to establish favorable intracellular conditions, thereby indirectly contributing to the emergence and persistence of drug-resistant strains. Critically, the unique Mycobacterium cell wall, comprising a plasma membrane, a cell wall core, and an outermost layer (Grzegorzewicz et al. 2016), acts as a major permeability barrier against hydrophilic drugs. This structure, predominantly composed of lipids and carbohydrates, is essential for bacterial survival and virulence. The rise in multidrug-resistant (MDR) and extensively drug-resistant (XDR) strains necessitates novel therapeutic targets, new drugs, and, crucially, rapid and precise diagnostics for effective TB management and control.

Lipoarabinomannan (LAM) is a key mycobacterial glycolipid associated with the cell wall. It has been identified as a significant virulence factor of Mtb and serves as a diagnostic marker for active tuberculosis. The ability of LAM-specific antibodies to identify LAM in urine fills a gap in rapid tuberculosis diagnostics. The World Health Organization (WHO) has approved the Alere Determine TB LAM Ag (AlereLAM; Abbott, Palatine, IL, USA) as a point-of-care (POC) diagnostic test for tuberculosis in people with HIV (Bjerrum et al. 2019). Compared with AlereLAM, FujiLAM (Fujifilm SILVAMP TB LAM; Fujifilm, Tokyo, Japan) has demonstrated higher diagnostic sensitivity in people with HIV in recent years (Dhana et al. 2022; Huerga et al. 2023) and may also have diagnostic value in individuals without HIV (Muyoyeta et al. 2021; Nkereuwem et al. 2021).

Mtb's pathogenicity and virulence also depend on its well-controlled secretion systems. Approximately 1314 proteins were detected in M. tuberculosis culture filtrate in a recent investigation (Tucci et al. 2020). Secreted proteins modulate the host immune responses through various mechanisms mediated by these systems. The secretion systems of Mtb comprise Twin-arginine translocation (Tat), Sec-translocation (Sec), and Type VII secretion systems (T7SS) (Ates et al. 2018). The T7SS, often called ESX secretion systems, are key mycobacterial virulence components that mediate substrate transport across the cell envelope (Groschel et al. 2016).

Mtb possesses five specialized ESX secretion systems (ESX-1 to ESX-5) (Italia et al. 2023; Van Winden et al. 2019), which transport virulence factors across the bacterial envelope to manipulate the host environment (Gijsbers et al. 2023). These systems secrete four main substrate classes: Esx-, PE-, PPE-, and Esp-proteins. Among them, ESX-1 is the most extensively characterized. Its well-known substrates include EsxA (ESAT-6) and EsxB (CFP-10). EspB (Rv3881c), the ESX-1 secretion-associated protein B, is another substrate specific to ESX-1.

Mtb8 (Rv0379; secE2), a transporter protein associated with the cell wall, is recognized by rabbit anti-soluble protein antiserum and reacts with a distinct subset of pulmonary TB sera (Feng et al. 2013; Houghton et al. 2002). The Sec pathway’s membrane-spanning translocation channel consists of the essential membrane proteins Rv0638 (SecE1), Rv0379 (SecE2; Mtb8), Rv2586c (SecF), Rv1440 (SecG), and Rv0732 (SecY) (Brundage et al. 1990).

In this study, serum from a rabbit immunized with Mtb H37Rv cell walls showed reactivity toward LAM, EspB, and Mtb8. Affinity-purified pAbs against these antigens were obtained. The resulting pAbs’ performance as detection tools was rigorously evaluated using multiple immunological methods.

Materials and methods

Mtb H37Rv cell wall

Preparation of the Mtb H37Rv cell wall followed our previous study (Yan et al. 2021). Bacteria were inactivated, washed twice in PBS, and resuspended at 2 g/ml. Samples were sonicated at 4 °C (165 W, 30 min), and lysates were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C to remove remaining cells. The lysis supernatant was then collected, pelleted at 15,000 × g for an hour, washed twice with PBS, resuspended in PBS, and quantified by UV spectrophotometry.

Purification of polyclonal antibodies against LAM, Mtb8, and EspB

In our previous research, we successfully obtained serum from the rabbits immunized with the cell wall of Mtb H37Rv (Yan et al. 2021). Following initial characterization, the immune serum reacted with LAM, EspB, and Mtb8 antigens. Antigen-specific pAbs were purified as follows: Mtb8 pAb was purified through specific affinity purification using Mtb8 protein (step A); EspB pAb was acquired by affinity purification of EspB protein from the unbound serum fraction in step A (step B); and LAM pAb was isolated by Protein A affinity purification from the unbound serum fraction in step B (step C).

Bacterial strains

Culture supernatants from 23 types of inactivated mycobacterial species (Yan et al. 2021), supplied by Professor Yanlin Zhao at the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (China CDC), were utilized to identify antibody performance. These species encompassed slow-growing mycobacteria (SGM) and rapid-growing mycobacteria (RGM). SGM included Mycobacterim tuberculosis H37Rv, Mycobacterium bovis, slow-growing non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) strains (n = 9). RGM encompassed rapid-growing NTM strains (n = 12). Slow-growing NTM included Mycobacterium kansasii, Mycobacterium marinum, Mycobacterium scrofulaceum, Mycobacterium gordonae, Mycobacterium xenopi, Mycobacterium avium, Mycobacterium intracellulare, Mycobacterium gastri, and Mycobacterium malmoense. Rapid-growing NTM included Mycobacterium smegmatis, Mycobacterium fortuitum, Mycobacterium aurum, Mycobacterium neoaurum, Mycobacterium abscessus, Mycobacterium parafortuitum, Mycobacterium salmoniphilum, Mycobacterium nonchromogenicum, Mycobacterium vaccae, Mycobacterium phlei, Mycobacterium confluentis, and Mycobacterium gilvum. The inactivated supernatants of Streptococcus pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Haemophilus influenzae, supplied by Professor Yu Pang at the Beijing Chest Hospital, were used to test the specificities of the LAM pAb, EspB pAb, and Mtb8 pAb. All supernatants were stored at − 80 °C until use.

ELISAs

Indirect ELISA was used to measure the specificity and sensitivity of immune serum. Microplate wells were coated with 1 μg/mL LAM (BEI Resources, NR-14848), 38 kDa (Rv0934), MPT32 (Rv1860), MTP64 (Rv1980c), EspB (Rv3881c), EspJ (Rv3878), Rv3872, Mtb8 (Rv0379), EspC (Rv3615c), Rv0222, Rv3425, CFP21 (Rv1984c), ORF14, CFP10-Mtb81-EspC fusion protein (Rv3874-Rv1837c-Rv3615c), Ag85B-HBHA fusion protein (Rv1886c-Rv0475), heat-killed bacterial culture supernatants (1:50 dilution), or coated with serial tenfold dilutions of LAM, EspB, or Mtb8. After blocking with 5% skim milk, plates were incubated for 2 h at 37 °C with 1 µg/mL of EspB pAb, Mtb8 pAb, or LAM pAb. After washing, plates were incubated for one hour with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-coupled goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) Ab (ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) at a 1:5000 dilution. For Ab-antigen detection, TMB (tetramethylbenzidine) substrate was added and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Reaction was stopped, and optical density at 450 nm (OD450) was measured.

To evaluate the efficacy of LAM pAb as a capture antibody, sandwich ELISA was employed. LAM pAb was coated onto polystyrene plates at a concentration of 2 µg/mL overnight at 4 °C. After washing, the plates were blocked with 5% skimmed milk the following day. They were then incubated for 2 h at 37 °C with either 1 µg/mL or successive dilutions of pure Mtb H37Rv LAM, or with a 1:50 dilution of heat-killed suspensions of Mycobacterial strains. Following three washings, 1 µg/mL of biotinylated LAM-specific monoclonal antibodies (e.g., BJRbL01-Bio, BJRbL03-Bio, BJRbL20-Bio, BJRbL52-Bio, or BJRbL76-Bio) was added to the plates and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. After washing, HRP-coupled streptavidin at a 1:5000 dilution was added for 1 h at 37 °C to detect the bound biotinylated antibodies. A final incubation with TMB substrate solution was performed for 30 min at 37 °C. A stop solution was then added, and the OD450 was measured.

SDS-PAGE and Western blotting

Pure LAM, along with EspB andMtb8 proteins, as well as various Mtb H37Rv-derived samples (including culture supernatants, cell wall fraction, and whole-cell lysates), were mixed with loading buffer in diverse tests, either with or without 2-mercaptoethanol, or with loading buffer supplemented with 0.05 mol/L glycine (for Mtb8). Subsequently, the protein samples were either boiled for 5–10 min or left unheated. They were then separated via 10% SDS-PAGE, transferred onto a nitrocellulose membrane, and blocked for 2 h at room temperature (RT) using 5% milk or 2% BSA in PBS. The primary antibodies (LAM-, EspB-, or Mtb8-pAb) were used at 0.5 µg/mL overnight, and the secondary antibody (peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG) was used at a 1:5000 dilution for 1 h. To visualize the protein bands, a chemiluminescent substrate for HRP was employed.

Flow cytometry

The binding ability of LAM-, EspB-, and Mtb8-pAb to Mtb H37Rv was examined using flow cytometry. Streptococcus pneumoniae (SP) was used as a negative bacterial control. First, a 1.5-ml Eppendorf tube was loaded with 50 µl of inactivated Mtb H37Rv or SP suspension. Subsequently, the bacteria were rinsed twice with 1 ml of PBS buffer (0.01 M, pH 7.4) and resuspended in 100 µl of PBS. Then, 0.5 µg of rabbit pAb against EspB, Mtb8, or LAM was individually added and incubated for 30 min at RT. After washing, a 5 µg/ml goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) secondary antibody labeled with AF488 was used to detect bound rabbit pAb for 30 min in the dark. The samples were then rinsed, pelleted by centrifugation, and resuspended in 200 µl of PBS. Data acquisition was performed on a BD LSRFortessa flow cytometer. For data analysis, BD FACSDiva Software version 7.0 was utilized.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 10 was used to perform statistical analysis. The Mann–Whitney test and Analysis of Variance with post hoc multiple comparisons were employed for the analysis. The cut-off value was calculated using 2.1 times the mean absorbance of the blank or negative control in ELISA.

Reactivity of polyclonal antibodies to common pneumonia-causing pathogenic bacteria

To rigorously assess potential cross-reactivity relevant to pulmonary disease, we extended our specificity evaluation of the LAM, EspB, and Mtb8 pAb to a panel of clinically significant bacterial pathogens commonly implicated in pneumonia. Discussion

Mycobacteria cell wall components are critically implicated in host–pathogen interactions, immune recognition, intracellular survival, and virulence (Holzheimer et al. 2021). Beyond their biological roles, these components also represent significant targets for novel antibiotics (Queiroz and Riley 2017), TB vaccine adjuvants, and diagnostic biomarkers (Goletti et al. 2018). In this study, serum from a rabbit immunized with Mtb H37Rv cell wall preparations exhibited reactivity against purified antigens LAM, EspB, and Mtb8. We subsequently purified corresponding polyclonal antibodies (LAM pAb, EspB pAb, and Mtb8 pAb), which exhibited high specificity and sensitivity towards their respective targets. These findings confirm the presence of LAM, EspB, and Mtb8 in the Mtb cell wall and demonstrate their intrinsic immunogenicity. Moreover, this work establishes an innovative methodology for generating high-specificity pAbs against all three antigens from a single immunization protocol.

LAM is prevalently present in mycobacteria, accounting for approximately 15% of the bacterial mass (Hunter et al. 1986). It is characterized by four structural domains: a mannosylated phosphatidyl-myo-inositol (PI) anchor, a mannan core, an arabinan domain, and different capping motifs (Chatterjee and Khoo 1998; Corrigan et al. 2023; Nigou et al. 2003). Based on capping structure, LAM is classified into three distinct forms: AraLAM, phospho-myo-inositol-capped LAM (PILAM), and mannose-capped LAM (ManLAM) (Nigou et al. 2003). This structural variation correlates with mycobacterial growth phenotype. Pathogenic SGM typically express ManLAM (Petzold et al. 2005; Venisse et al. 1993), while RGM species are characterized by PILAM (M. smegmatis) (Gilleron et al. 1997) and AraLAM (M. abscessus) (Khoo et al. 1995).

In our previous study, we produced a panel of LAM-specific mAbs screened against Mtb H37Rv ManLAM. These mAbs reacted exclusively with SGM but not RGM (Yan et al. 2021). Here, we show that LAM pAb reacts with both SGM and RGM strains, indicating its ability to recognize not only ManLAM but also PILAM and AraLAM. This broad reactivity suggests that LAM pAb targets multiple conserved epitopes across LAM structural variants. These properties highlight LAM pAb’s diagnostic potential for TB and NTM disease. Critically, using LAM pAb as the capture antibody with LAM mAb as detection reagents in a sandwich ELISA created a SGM-specific assay. This configuration thus enables unambiguous differentiation of SGM from RGM, offering a practical tool for mycobacterial strain classification.

The EspB gene (Rv3881c)is located within the esx-1 locus but external to RD1 (Cole et al. 1998; Piton et al. 2020) and is conserved across all mycobacterial species except M. leprae (Cole et al. 2001). EspB serves both as a critical Mtb virulence factor and an essential ESX-1 secretion system substrate (McLaughlin et al. 2007). Mtb produces full-length EspB (48 kDa), which undergoes MycP1-mediated C-terminus cleavage during ESX-1-dependent secretion, yielding a mature 38 kDa isoform (McLaughlin et al. 2007; Ohol et al. 2010; Sun et al. 2013). While prior studies utilized rat polyclonal antibodies against recombinant full-length EspB (Korotkova et al. 2015; Li et al. 2011; Lim et al. 2022), we generated the first rabbit polyclonal antibodies (EspB pAb) against the native antigen.

EspB pAb recognized both full-length precursor (in whole-cell lysates and cell wall fractions) and the mature secreted forms (in culture filtrates). This dual-specificity confirms that the antibody’s epitopes lie outside the cleavage region. Structurally, mature EspB oligomerizes into donut-shaped rings (Piton et al. 2020), while full-length EspB remains monomeric (Korotkova et al. 2015). EspB serves as an integral component of the extended ESX-1 machinery (Gijsbers et al. 2021; Piton et al. 2020) and is situated within the outermost capsule layer of the Mtb cell wall (Sani et al. 2010). This localization is supported by our flow cytometry detection of surface EspB on Mtb H37Rv. Significantly, EspB pAb detected secreted protein in culture supernatants of all tested mycobacterial species (SGM & RGM). EspB pAb’s cross-species reactivity highlights its potential as a tool for future studies on EspB secretion dynamics in diverse mycobacteria.

The Mtb8 (Rv0379), an established serodiagnostic tuberculosis marker (Feng et al. 2013), elicits Mtb8 specific antibodies in TB patients. This 71-amino acid (8 kDa) protein is located in the peptidoglycan-based cell wall and plasma membrane (GO Annotation) and forms dodecameric oligomers as confirmed by crystallography (Arockiasamy et al. 2011). Although reports on Mtb8-targeting antibodies are limited, we developed the first rabbit pAb against this antigen. This pAb demonstrates reactivity with both monomeric and polymeric Mtb8, facilitating the detection of its physiologically relevant polymerization states. These states are of interest in understanding Mtb8 immunogenicity and may differentiate between intracellular and secreted forms in pathogenesis. The ability to detect both forms makes this pAb a useful reagent for structural–functional studies of Mtb8 in various biological contexts.

The significant elevation of Mtb8 levels in the SGM versus RGM, coupled with its broad mycobacterial conservation, suggests a possible association between Mtb8 abundance and the complex pathogenesis phenotype characteristic of SGMs. Proteomic analysis identifies Mtb8 as a component of multiple drug resistance profiles in TB (Bobba and Khader 2023; Hadizadeh Tasbiti et al. 2021), alongside other resistance-associated proteins (Rv0147, Rv3597c, Rv3699, Rv1392, and Rv0443) (Hadizadeh Tasbiti et al. 2021). Flow cytometry with Mtb8 pAb confirms cell wall localization in Mtb H37Rv, providing functional context for its involvement in resistance mechanisms. While these findings suggest potential roles for Mtb8 in pathogenesis and resistance, definitive evidence establishing its virulence function requires further targeted molecular studies.

LAM pAb, EspB pAb, and Mtb8 pAb demonstrated high specificity for mycobacteria, showing no cross-reactivity with common pneumonia-causing pathogens (e.g., S. pneumoniae, S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, and H. influenzae). This high specificity underscores their diagnostic potential for TB and NTM disease. However, clinical translation requires further validation. While the LAM pAb/mAb combination demonstrates high sensitivity for LAM detection in SGM culture supernatants and shows promise for quantifying LAM in TB patients body fluids (e.g., urine, serum), its clinical utility necessitates rigorous validation in clinical specimens. For EspB and Mtb8, suitable pairing antibodies have not yet been developed, which is necessary to enable target quantification in patient samples.

In conclusion, we successfully generated highly sensitive and specific rabbit polyclonal antibodies against LAM, EspB, and Mtb8 using serum derived from rabbits immunized with the Mtb H37Rv cell wall. This study provides a novel method for the simultaneous preparation of these three antibodies and provided their preliminary characterization. These antibodies represent potential tools for investigating the roles of LAM, EspB, and Mtb8 in TB pathogenesis, diagnostics, and host defense mechanisms. Future work will focus on validating their diagnostic utility in relevant biological matrices and exploring their roles (e.g., in opsonization, neutralization, and Fc-mediated effector functions) in pathogenesis interference and host defense, through dedicated in vitro and vivo studies.

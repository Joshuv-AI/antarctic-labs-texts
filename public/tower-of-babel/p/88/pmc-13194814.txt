Prevalence and Healthcare Costs of Metabolic Disorders Among Individuals with Schizophrenia Following Initiation of Oral Antipsychotic Therapy
Authors: Joseph Tkacz, Alejandro Cajigal, Matthew Sidovar, Kathleen Wilson, Emma Pennington, Ilya Okunev, Kristin Gillard
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/ObjectiveMetabolic disorders are prevalent among the schizophrenia population due to relatively poor access to healthcare, lifestyle factors, genetics, and metabolic side effects associated with antipsychotic treatment. This study evaluated the rate, timing, and costs associated with the onset of metabolic disorders among individuals with schizophrenia newly initiating oral antipsychotics (OAPs) from the perspective of United States (US) healthcare payers.MethodsThose eligible for inclusion in this retrospective administrative claims analysis met the following criteria between 1/1/2016 and 9/30/2023: (1) schizophrenia diagnosis, (2) ≥ 12 months of continuous enrollment preceding diagnosis, (3) an absence of OAPs during the 12 months preceding diagnosis, (4) at least two claims for OAPs during the 12 months following diagnosis, and (5) absence of diabetes, obesity, hyperlipidemia, or metabolic syndrome any time preceding initiation of OAPs. During the variable-length post-schizophrenia diagnosis period, onset of metabolic disorders was assessed using Kaplan-Meier analyses. Individuals with metabolic disorders were then matched to non-metabolic disorder controls, and healthcare costs were evaluated during the 12 months following the first evidence of metabolic disorders or proxy index date.ResultsA total of 45,528 individuals with schizophrenia qualified for the study, and 34.7% developed metabolic disorders within 6 years after initiating OAPs. Compared to a matched cohort of individuals without metabolic disorders, individuals with a metabolic disorder incurred significantly greater all-cause annual healthcare costs (mean [standard deviation] in 2023 US dollars, $27,520 [$44,200] vs. $19,044 [$34,352]; p < 0.0001) during the 12-month post-index period.ConclusionThis analysis demonstrated that new onset of a metabolic disorder following OAP initiation was associated with higher healthcare costs from the perspective of US healthcare payers. Reduction of metabolic disorders may be key in reducing the economic burden of schizophrenia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s41669-026-00647-w.

Introduction

Schizophrenia is a chronic and severe mental illness characterized by pathology with cognition, behavior, and emotions [1], and affects between 0.29 and 0.34% of the population in the United States (US), with estimates being lower among individuals with commercial insurance (0.13%) and highest among Medicaid enrollees (2.13–2.71%) [2–4]. The economic burden of schizophrenia is substantial. On a per patient basis among Medicare Fee-for-Service (FFS) beneficiaries, the annual total healthcare costs in 2019 were $23,662, of which 56% were medical costs and 44% were pharmacy costs [5]. The total direct healthcare costs for schizophrenia in the US in 2019 were estimated to be $62.3 billion, and an additional $251.9 billion were attributed to indirect costs such as unemployment, lost productivity, caregiving, and premature mortality [6].

Until the recent approval of xanomeline/trospium chloride [7], the primary treatment options for schizophrenia were antipsychotics, which act primarily as dopamine antagonists in the brain. However, these medications are associated with a variety of tolerability issues, impacting their long-term effectiveness. First-generation antipsychotics can cause irreversible extrapyramidal side effects, including tardive dyskinesia and akathisia [8]. Second-generation antipsychotics were developed to reduce the risk of severe extrapyramidal symptoms, but are associated with their own adverse effects, such as treatment-emergent weight gain, altered glucose and lipid metabolism, and increased risk of chronic diseases such as type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease [8]. Consequently, adherence to antipsychotic treatment is low, as some individuals may skip doses or discontinue treatment, leading to poor clinical outcomes such as symptom recurrence or psychotic relapse [9, 10], as well as increased healthcare resource utilization and costs [9]. Furthermore, a host of patient-related factors have historically been identified as increasing the risk for non-adherence among the schizophrenia population including the use of drugs and alcohol, poor social and familial support, cognitive impairment, disease severity, and stigmatization [11]. Although, oral antipsychotic (OAP) medications remain the primary therapy, recent advances include the development of various formulations and medications designed to improve adherence and reduce side effects [12, 13]. Long-acting injectable (LAI) antipsychotics are now available to some patients, who may differ from those receiving OAPs in patient characteristics, illness severity, history of non-adherence, and other clinical factors [14–17].

Studies have demonstrated that both the prevalence of and risk for metabolic syndrome, characterized by a cluster of metabolic disorders including obesity, insulin resistance, hypertension, and dyslipidemia [18], are higher among individuals with schizophrenia due to a variety of etiologies [19–21]. Recent literature highlights an overlap between the genes conferring increased risk of developing schizophrenia, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome, including genes associated with inflammation (e.g., apolipoprotein E, interleukin 10, tumor necrosis factor) and oxidative stress (e.g., glutathione S-transferase mu 1, methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase, paraoxonase 1) [19, 22]. This inherently increases the risk of metabolic syndrome in individuals with schizophrenia. Furthermore, the association between antipsychotic use and weight gain and insulin resistance has been well documented [23, 24]. Individuals prescribed antipsychotics should be monitored closely as these metabolic disorders can occur as early as within 3 months of treatment initiation, and may lead to more serious conditions such as obesity and diabetes [23].

The objective of this study was to assess the rate and timing of metabolic disorder onset following initiation of OAP medications. Additionally, analyses were conducted to assess all-cause and schizophrenia-related healthcare resource utilization and costs associated with the development of comorbid metabolic disorders among individuals with schizophrenia from the perspective of US healthcare payers.

Methods

Study Sample

This was an observational, retrospective analysis of de-identified claims data examining individuals with schizophrenia initiating OAP medications. The analysis utilized the Inovalon MORE2 Registry of Closed Claims from 1/1/2016 to 9/30/2023 and the 100% Medicare FFS database from 1/1/2016 to 12/31/2022. The MORE2 Registry includes de-identified, primary source medical and pharmacy claims covering all major US payers (commercial, Medicare Advantage, and Managed Medicaid). The 100% Medicare FFS database includes all Part A and Part B encounters in addition to all Part D claims for Medicare beneficiaries. The MORE2 Registry and the 100% Medicare FFS database are de-identified and in compliance with the confidentiality requirements in the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) of 1996. Thus, this study was exempt from patient consent procedures and institutional review board review and approval.

Individuals were included in the study if they presented with one inpatient claim or at least two non-diagnostic outpatient claims, separated by 30–365 days, with an International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) diagnosis code for schizophrenia (F20.xx), schizoaffective disorder (F25.xx), or schizotypal disorder (F21.xx) on or following 1/1/2017. Individuals were required to have continuous enrollment and an absence of claims for OAPs for at least 12 months preceding the first schizophrenia diagnosis. Individuals were required to present at least two prescription claims, separated by no more than 180 days, for an OAP, including aripiprazole, asenapine, brexpiprazole, cariprazine, clozapine, iloperidone, lurasidone, olanzapine, paliperidone, quetiapine, risperidone, or ziprasidone, in the 12 months following the first schizophrenia diagnosis. The earliest date of an OAP was set as the treatment index date. For inclusion, individuals were required to present an absence of metabolic disorders, inclusive of diabetes, obesity, hyperlipidemia, and metabolic syndrome, anytime preceding the treatment index date, as well as an absence of prescription claims for LAI medications anytime during the study period (Online Resource, Supplementary Figure 1; see the electronic supplementary material).

Subgroups

Individuals with metabolic disorders were identified with the presence of at least two non-diagnostic claims with an ICD-10-CM diagnosis code for diabetes (E10.xx-E14.xx), obesity (E66.xx), hyperlipidemia (E78.xx), or metabolic syndrome (E88.81) following the initiation of an OAP medication. The second claim for a metabolic disorder was set as the primary index date among individuals with metabolic disorders. Non-metabolic disorder controls were directly matched to case individuals (individuals diagnosed with a metabolic disorder) in a many-to-1 ratio on age, sex, payer type, race/ethnicity, Deyo-Charlson Comorbidity Index (DCI) score, baseline bipolar depression, and baseline major depressive disorder. The proxy index date for each control individual was determined based on the time between the diagnostic index date and primary index date of their matched case, and then assigned to the closest claim within 90 days. Control individuals were then required to have at least 12 months of continuous enrollment prior to their proxy index date. All patients (cases and control) were required to present continuous enrollment with medical and pharmacy benefits between the schizophrenia diagnosis and 12 months following the primary index date or proxy index date. For cases with more than one control match after the final step of the matching algorithm, one control was randomly selected to ensure a final 1-to-1 match.

Outcomes

Outcomes included the rate and timing of metabolic disorder onset following OAP treatment initiation, as well as all-cause and schizophrenia-related healthcare resource utilization and costs as captured in payer claims databases. All-cause and schizophrenia-related healthcare resource utilization and costs were reported in the following categories: physician office, emergency department, hospitalization, and pharmacy during the 12-month follow-up period. Additionally, total medical and total healthcare cost variables were also measured during the 12-month follow-up period. Costs were adjusted for inflation using the medical care component of the Consumer Price Index obtained from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics and standardized to 2023 US dollars [25].

Analyses

The rate and time from the treatment index date to onset of metabolic disorders inclusive of diabetes, obesity, hyperlipidemia, and metabolic syndrome were assessed via Kaplan-Meier analyses to account for censoring due to death, end of continuous enrollment, or end of study period. Incidence was reported over a 6-year period, which represented the maximum possible follow-up duration within the study period. Estimated means and standard errors (SEs) were reported. Healthcare resource utilization and costs were assessed using descriptive statistics including means and standard deviations (SDs) for continuous variables and frequencies and proportions for binary or categorical variables. For all comparisons between individuals with and without metabolic disorders, significant differences were determined via Student’s t test for continuous variables, and chi-square test for categorical variables. The critical alpha level was set to 0.05 for all analyses.

Results

Rate and Timing of Metabolic Disorder Onset

A total of 45,528 individuals with schizophrenia met study inclusion criteria (Fig. 1). Using Kaplan–Meier analysis to account for censoring, 34.7% of patients presented evidence of any metabolic disorder in the 6 years after initiating OAPs (Fig. 2). The estimated mean (SE) time to the onset of a metabolic disorder was 5.07 (0.015) years. While the number at risk decreased over time due to censoring, the proportion of individuals with metabolic disorders steadily increased from 1 to 6 years after initiation of antipsychotic treatment. Among the included metabolic conditions, hyperlipidemia had the highest incidence, followed by obesity, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome. Discussion

The association between schizophrenia, antipsychotic use, and metabolic disorders has been previously established, with schizophrenia and antipsychotic use each increasing the risk of developing metabolic disorders [20, 26–34]. This study aligns with prior research with the finding that over one in three individuals with schizophrenia initiating OAP medication developed metabolic disorders. The current study also sought to quantify differences in healthcare utilization and costs incurred by individuals with schizophrenia who experience metabolic disorders following initiation of an OAP. Among a cohort matched on age, sex, payer type, race/ethnicity, and baseline DCI score, those with metabolic disorders incurred 1.5 times greater healthcare costs over the 12-month follow-up period compared to those without metabolic disorders. In addition to increased all-cause costs, individuals with metabolic disorders also incurred significantly higher schizophrenia-related costs relative to those without metabolic disorders. While healthcare resource utilization increased in each service category, inpatient hospital stays, other outpatient services, and pharmacy costs were the primary drivers of increased expenditure. Prior research in schizophrenia and metabolic comorbidities found similar increases in emergency department utilization, inpatient hospitalizations, and costs [31, 35].

Early detection of metabolic disorders may lower healthcare resource utilization and associated costs by preventing complications of these comorbidities, which often lead to frequent hospitalizations, increased physician visits, and life-long treatments. Managing metabolic risk factors with appropriate interventions, such as weight management programs, lifestyle modifications, and medications that mitigate metabolic side effects, provides an opportunity to improve health outcomes and potentially reduce associated costs in this population. However, individuals with schizophrenia may face hurdles or barriers to access programs that could mitigate the risk of developing metabolic disorders. In addition to reducing barriers to risk management programs, the development and use of treatment options for schizophrenia that are less likely to cause metabolic syndrome may further mitigate the risk of metabolic disorders, allowing individuals with the disease to experience improved long-term health outcomes and greater quality of life.

Strengths of this study include the expansive data sources comprising information for individuals with schizophrenia across different payer types with longitudinal patient-level healthcare data across all geographic regions of the US (i.e., Medicare FFS, Medicare Advantage, Managed Medicaid, and commercial insurance). However, results may not be generalizable to uninsured individuals or those with other insurance types. Moreover, administrative claims data are subject to data coding limitations and data entry error, which may result in missing or erroneous data. However, in adjudicated claims databases coding errors are thought to be minimal as accurate information is needed for claims to be adjudicated and paid. The objective of this study was to capture incident metabolic disorders, which was accomplished through requiring a pre-index metabolic naive period. However, incidence may be overestimated if individuals had a history of metabolic disorders prior to the start of the study period. As the incidence analysis was purely descriptive and did not include a control group of individuals without schizophrenia or those with schizophrenia not receiving OAP medications, it cannot be discerned to what degree OAP medications contribute additional risk of developing metabolic disorders among this already at-risk patient population. Finally, the study period coincides with the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, which resulted in a restriction in access to care in the US for a period, and healthcare utilization during this time may be under-representative of typical care.

Conclusion

Over one in three individuals with schizophrenia developed metabolic disorders up to 6 years following initiation of an OAP medication, and these individuals incurred 1.5 times greater healthcare costs compared to a matched cohort of individuals without metabolic disorders. An increased focus on the prevention and appropriate early management of metabolic disorders as well as schizophrenia treatment options that do not contribute to the risk of these conditions represent an opportunity to improve long-term health outcomes and quality of life in this population, and potentially reduce the economic burden of schizophrenia from the US payer perspective.

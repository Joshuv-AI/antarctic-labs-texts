Dysregulation of miR‐21‐5p in diabetic nephropathy and its role in inflammatory response
Authors: Yujiang Zhang, Qin Deng, Jianping Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundDiabetic nephropathy (DN) is an important microvascular complication of diabetes. MicroRNAs play an important role in the development of DN.AimsThis study aims to examine the expression and clinical significance of serum miR‐21‐5p in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and DN.MethodsThis study enrolled a total of 106 T2DM patients, 106 T2DM patients with DN, and 70 healthy individuals. Real‐time fluorescence quantitative PCR (RT‐qPCR) was used to detect the expression of miR‐21‐5p, and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to evaluate the diagnostic value of miR‐21‐5p in DN. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to examine the association between miR‐21‐5p levels and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), urinary protein excretion rate (UAER) and other clinicopathological parameters of glomerulonephritis. Furthermore, the effects of miR‐21‐5p on the function and inflammation of human glomerular mesangial cells (HMCs) were analyzed.ResultsSerum miR‐21‐5p is upregulated in DN and it can effectively distinguish DN patients from T2DM patients. Pearson correlation analysis showed a negative correlation between miR‐21‐5p and eGFR and a positive correlation with UAER. Finally, the knockdown of miR‐21‐5p reduced the levels of pro‐inflammatory cytokines induced by high glucose (HG) and the proliferation of HMCs.ConclusionsSerum miR‐21‐5p is upregulated in DN and it might be a diagnostic marker for DN. Furthermore, miR‐21‐5p may be involved in the occurrence and progression of DN by regulating cell proliferation and inflammatory responses and is expected to become a potential therapeutic target for DN.Keywords: Diabetic nephropathy, Diagnosis, miR‐21‐5p

INTRODUCTION

Diabetic nephropathy (DN) is one of the most common complications of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), with rates between 20% and 40% 1 , 2 . Prolonged hyperglycemia in diabetic patients may cause microvascular damage, increase renal vascular pressure, and ultimately lead to changes in kidney structure, resulting in DN 3 . Proteinuria, hypertension, and edema are symptoms that may appear in the mid to late stages of DN, a leading cause of chronic kidney disease and end‐stage renal disease (ESRD) 4 , 5 . The prevalence of DN among patients with end‐stage renal disease varies worldwide 6 , 7 . In most Western countries, diabetes is the primary cause of ESRD 8 . Patients with end‐stage renal disease have a 5‐year survival rate of less than 20% 9 . Therefore, prioritizing the prevention of DN and implementing early, standardized management strategies for chronic disease are essential.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are potent modulators of the genome 9 , 10 , 11 . The discovery of specific miRNAs and their clinical features has led to extensive research into their role in human diseases 12 , 13 . Multiple studies indicated that miRNAs play a crucial role in DN pathogenesis, suggesting their potential as disease biomarkers 14 , 15 . However, the results remain inconclusive.

Global express spectrum analysis has revealed that miR‐21 is highly regulated in the kidneys of mice with DN. Silencing miR‐21 could reduce the effects of DN in mice 16 . Studies have shown that renal expression of miR‐21‐5p is associated with fibrosis and renal survival in patients with IgA nephropathy 17 , 18 . One study reported significant enrichment of the transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGFB1) in DN, and its association with miR‐21‐5p suggests that it might be involved in DN progression by regulating miR‐21‐5p 19 . These findings support the role of miR‐21‐5p in DN. However, research on the precise role and clinical significance of miR‐21‐5p in DN remains limited.

This study aimed to assess the diagnostic value of miR‐21‐5p in DN and its effect on the inflammatory response associated with the disease.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients and sampling

A total of 212 patients with T2DM were enrolled in this study, including 106 patients with DN and 106 patients without DN. Simultaneously, 70 healthy individuals undergoing the same physical examination at the same time were selected as controls. T2DM was defined as typical hyperglycemia with random blood glucose ≥11.1 mmoL/L or fasting blood glucose ≥7 mmoL/L. The diagnostic criteria for DN were a definite history of diabetes; the patient's urine protein was examined three times in three consecutive months, and the urinary excretion rate was 20–300 μg/min or more; the patient had persistent proteinuria, and other causes of increased urinary protein were excluded. Patients who did not meet the diagnostic criteria, patients with malignant tumors, and patients with mental disorders were excluded. According to whether T2DM patients had nephropathy, they were divided into T2DM group (n = 106) and DN group (n = 106). As a healthy control group (n = 70), healthy people without the above‐mentioned diseases and of similar age were selected. A fasting venous blood sample was taken from each of the subjects in the morning. Serum was separated by centrifugation at 2500 rpm for 15 min at 4°C.

According to the Danish Mogensen staging criteria to distinguish between patients with DN. Danish Mogensen staging criteria: Phase I is the glomerular filtration rate increases, the kidney volume enlarges, and urine albumin is negative; there are no obvious histopathological changes or increased glomerular capillary perfusion pressure. Stage II features normal albuminuria with no abnormal urinary albumin, thickening of the glomerular basement membrane, increased mesangial matrix, and an abnormal estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Stage III is the early stage of DN. The urinary protein excretion rate (UAER) is 20–200 μg/min and 24‐h urinary albumin is maintained at 30–300 mg. Stage IV is clinical DN, characterized by a UAE greater than 200 μg/min or a 24‐h urinary protein quantification greater than 300 mg. Persistent proteinuria, hypertension, and edema are also present.

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Chongqing Jiangjin Second People's Hospital. All subjects gave their consent by signing an informed consent form.

Real‐time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR) analysis

Total plasma RNA was isolated with the TRIzol LS reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Samples were tested for purity and concentration using NanoDrop‐2000 (Thermo Fisher, USA). Nano Drop‐2000 measured the sample OD260/OD280 ratio between 1.8 and 2.1, OD230/OD260 ratio between 0.4 and 0.5, allowing the sample to proceed to the next phase of the experiment. The miscript RT kit (TaKaRa, Dalian, China) was used for reverse transcription. PCR analysis was conducted using the SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). We estimated the level of miR‐21‐5p using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primers for miR‐21‐5p and U6 were as follows: miR‐21‐5p F: 5′‐GCGCGTAGCTTATCAGACTGA‐3′, R: 5′‐AGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGTATT‐3′; U6 F: 5′‐AAAGCAAATCATCGGACGACC‐3′, R: 5′‐GTACAACA CATTGTTTCCTCGGA‐3′.

Cell culture and processing

Human glomerular mesangial cells (HMCs) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). The cells were cultured in a DMEM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin and streptomycin. The miR‐21‐5p mimic prompted miRNA overexpression, whereas the miR‐21‐5p inhibitor downregulated miRNA expression. However, the mimic NC and inhibitor NC, used as negative controls, did not affect the level of miR‐21‐5p. The synthetic miR‐21‐5p mimic, miR‐21‐5p inhibitor, mimic NC, and the inhibitor NC were obtained from Gene Pharma (Nanjing, China). The sequences of miRNA mimic/inhibitor were as follows: miR‐21‐5p mimic 5'‐UAGCUUAUCAGACUGAUGUUGA‐3', miR‐21‐5p inhibitor 5'‐ACAGCCCAUCGACUGGUGUUG‐3'. To construct in vitro DN cell models, HMCs were cultured in serum‐free medium for 24 h. Then, they were treated with serum‐free DMEM containing 5.5 mmol/L (normal glucose, NG) and 30 mmol/L (high glucose, HG) glucose. In addition, normal glucose (5.5 mmol/L) and D‐mannitol (24.5 mmol/L) were used as an osmotic control (hypertonicity, HO). Cell transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA), and experimental procedures commenced 48 h after transfection.

Enzyme‐linked immunoreactivity assay

Enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay kits (Elabscience, Wuhan, China) were used to measure serum and cellular levels of interleukin‐1β (IL‐1β), interleukin‐6 (IL‐6), and tumor necrosis factor‐α (TNF‐α). The cell supernatants were cultured for 48 h after the transfection of the cells, and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured according to the instructions of the manufacturer.

Assay for cell proliferation

For the detection of HMC proliferation, the Cell Counting Kit‐8 (CCK‐8) was used. The cultures were continued in different concentrations of glucose medium. The CCK‐8 reagent was added at the 0, 24, 48, and 72 h time points. After incubating for 1 h at 37°C, the changes in absorption value at a 450 nm wavelength were determined.

Statistical analysis of data

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad 9.0 software. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). The independent sample t‐test was used for comparing two groups, while one‐way analysis of variance (anova) was employed for multiple group comparisons. The least significant difference (LSD) test was used for pairwise comparisons after the ANOVA. Qualitative data comparisons were made using the chi‐squared test. The diagnostic value of miR‐21‐5p for DN was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. Pearson correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation between miR‐21‐5p levels and eGFR, UAER, and other clinicopathological parameters. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

The expression of miR‐21‐5p

Compared to healthy individuals, both T2DM and DN patients had significantly increased levels of serum miR‐21‐5p. Additionally, DN patients had even higher levels than T2DM patients (Figure 1a). Furthermore, this study enrolled a total of 106 patients with DN, comprising 56 patients with stage II DN, 33 with stage III DN, and 17 with stage IV DN. The level of miR‐21‐5p increased as DN worsened (Figure 1b).

Expression and clinical significance of miR‐21‐5p. The level of serum miR‐21‐5p was increased significantly in both T2DM patients and DN patients, with DN patients having a notably higher level than T2DM patients (P < 0.001) (a). The level of miR‐21‐5p gradually increased as DN worsened (b). miR‐21‐5p could distinguish T2DM patients from healthy individuals (AUC = 0.767, 95% CI = 0.697–0.834, P < 0.001) (c). It could also well differentiate DN patients from healthy individuals (AUC = 0.990, 95% CI = 0.981–0.999, P < 0.001) (d). Diagnostic value of miR‐21‐5p

The ROC curve evaluation indicates that miR‐21‐5p can distinguish T2DM patients from healthy individuals (AUC = 0.767, 95% CI = 0.697–0.834, P < 0.001); the sensitivity was 71.1%, specificity of 68.6% (Figure 1c). MiR‐21‐5p could well distinguish DN patients from healthy individuals (AUC = 0.990, 95% CI = 0.981–0.999, P < 0.001), with a sensitivity of 95.3% and a specificity of 97.1% (Figure 1d). In addition, miR‐21‐5p can better distinguish between T2DM patients and patients with DN (AUC = 0.942, 95% CI = 0.914–0.970, P < 0.001); the sensitivity was 89.6%, specificity of 82.1% (Figure 1e).

Correlation of miR‐21‐5p with clinical parameters in DN patients

We also examined correlations between serum miR‐21‐5p and eGFR, UAER, and inflammation. The results indicated that the level of miR‐21‐5p was positively correlated with both FBG (r = 0.782) and HbA1c (%) (r = 0.659; P<0.001; Figure 2a,b). Furthermore, the level of miR‐21‐5p was negatively correlated with eGFR (r = −0.671) and positively correlated with UAER (r = 0.784; P < 0.001; Figure 2c,d). Additionally, the serum miR‐21‐5p level was positively correlated with the inflammatory factors (r = 0.710), IL‐6 (r = 0.580), and NF‐α (r = 0.524; P < 0.001; Figure 2e–g).

Correlation between mir‐21‐5p and the clinical parameters of DN patients. The level of miR‐21‐5p was positively correlated with both FBG (r = 0.782) and HbA1c (%) (r = 0.659; P < 0.001) (a, b). Serum miR‐21‐5p presented a negative correlation with eGFR (r = −0.671) and a positive correlation with UAER (r = 0.784; P < 0.001) (c, d). Impact of miR‐21‐5p on cellular function and inflammation

We further investigated the role of miR‐21‐5p in DN. When HMCs were grown and induced with HG for 12, 24, and 36 h, miR‐21‐5p levels showed a rising trend over time (Figure 3a). After HG treatment, the level of miR‐21‐5p increased. The expression of miR‐21‐5p was significantly upregulated by transfection with the miR‐21‐5p mimic, while its expression level significantly decreased when transfected with a miR‐21‐5p inhibitor (Figure 3b). Additionally, HG promoted cell proliferation. Transfection with the miR‐21‐5p mimic significantly stimulated cell proliferation, whereas transfection with the miR‐21‐5p inhibitor restrained cell vitality and decreased cell viability (Figure 3c). We also observed that HG‐induced HMCs responded with increased levels of IL‐1β, IL‐6, and TNF‐α. The transfection of the miR‐21‐5p mimic significantly enhanced the inflammatory response, whereas the downregulation of miR‐21‐5p reduced the inflammatory response considerably (Figure 3d).

DISCUSSION

Renal injury is a frequent complication of T2DM. Research indicates that patients with an eGFR acuity of 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 progress to DN stage III at a rate of approximately 40% 20 . Furthermore, DN is associated with cardiovascular events, which increase patient mortality 21 . Currently, the diagnosis and staging of DN primarily depend on urinary microalbumin detection, which is relatively low in sensitivity and specificity. The renal biopsy has a high surgical risk 22 , 23 . Therefore, finding a more sensitive and reliable ideal detection marker is very important.

Several studies have suggested an association between miRNAs and diabetes 24 . However, the role of miRNAs in target organ damage in diabetes remains unclear. Studies have proved that miR‐21‐5p is identified as a significant gene related to DN and its development 19 . Despite this, the specific role of miR‐21‐5p in DN is still unclear. Our research confirmed a significant upregulation of miR‐21‐5p in DN patients. More importantly, we found that miR‐21‐5p was upregulated as DN progressed. Furthermore, we found that miR‐21‐5p varies among patients with T2DM, DN patients, and healthy individuals. In particular, our study showed for the first time that miR‐21‐5p might discriminate T2DM patients from DN patients. Our results provide stronger evidence for miR‐21‐5p as a biomarker for DN compared to previous studies. In addition, research has shown that human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection and inflammation are associated with increased miR‐21‐5p levels 25 . Similarly, our results show that serum miR‐21‐5p was negatively associated with eGFR, but positively associated with UAER. Additionally, miR‐21‐5p was correlated with levels of the inflammation factors IL‐1β, IL‐6, and TNF‐α. This suggests that miR‐21‐5p is associated with inflammation in DN.

Studies have shown that miR‐181a, by targeting the TNF‐α, improves the DN animal model of kidney inflammation 26 . The research shows that miR‐1297 alleviates HG‐induced human kidney‐2 (HK‐2) cell injury by targeting COL1A2 27 . The research concludes that miR‐374a regulates DN inflammation by targeting MCP‐1 28 . Recent research by Xue et al. found that miR‐21‐5p inhibits LPS‐induced inflammatory injury in rat cardiomyocytes H9c2 by regulating PDCD4 29 . Zhang et al. 30 found that miR‐21‐5p mediates glycolysis and inhibits the growth of glioma cells through regulating PFKFB2. Our results show that HG significantly promoted the increase of inflammatory factor levels and proliferation ability of cells, whereas the downregulation of miR‐21‐5p significantly reduced this effect. Therefore, we propose that miR‐21‐5p is involved in the onset and development of DN by regulating specific target genes or pathways that are involved in the inflammatory response of DN. This further establishes the role of miR‐21‐5p in DN and introduces a new approach for the diagnosis and management of DN. However, the specific mechanism of miR‐21‐5p in DN will be the subject of further investigation.

Overall, this study has a number of limitations. The relatively small sample size and the older mean age of the patients may have had an effect on the results. Furthermore, our findings suggest miR‐21‐5p may serve as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for DN. However, further studies are needed to clarify the potential role of miR‐21‐5p in the diagnosis of DN and its specific mechanism of action in DN inflammation.

In conclusion, our research indicates that miR‐21‐5p is upregulated in DN and may serve as a diagnostic biomarker for DN. Additionally, miR‐21‐5p might be involved in the occurrence and progression of DN by regulating cell proliferation and inflammatory responses and holds promise as a potential therapeutic target for DN.

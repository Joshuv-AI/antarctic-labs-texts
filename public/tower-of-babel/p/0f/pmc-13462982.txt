Group-aware and position-interactive learning for point cloud robust segmentation in subterranean environments
Authors: Mengting Liu, Haijiang Zhu, Jian Cheng, Ning An
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In heavily occluded underground scenarios such as coal mine roadways, high-precision 3D perception plays an essential role in infrastructure inspection, disaster prevention and control. However, existing general-purpose point cloud semantic segmentation methods suffer from poor generalization robustness in underground environments, due to challenges including blurred boundaries, strong noise interference, and highly repetitive structural patterns. They also typically incur excessive computational overhead, limiting their practical deployment. To address these issues, we propose an efficient and robust point cloud semantic segmentation framework tailored for complex underground infrastructure. Specifically, we design a lightweight Group-aware Channel Interaction Module (GCIM) with weight-shared grouped channel attention and explicit inter-group communication, which enlarges the receptive field for complex scene understanding while maintaining low computation costs. Furthermore, we present Interactive Position Encoding (IPE), which dynamically couples point features with spatial context via relative positional information. By strengthening spatial-aware feature interaction, IPE effectively mitigates boundary ambiguity caused by noise and geometric similarity. Experimental results on underground datasets including Coal Mines, Seg2Tunnel and OpenTrench3D, as well as general indoor datasets ScanNetv2 and S3DIS, demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance in terms of mean Intersection over Union and Overall Accuracy. It exhibits outstanding segmentation capability and cross-scene generalization ability, which can provide effective technical support for intelligent 3D information extraction and safety monitoring in underground environments.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of underground transportation and mining technologies has intensiffed the demand for advanced spatial perception in subterranean environments. Accurate semantic segmentation of underground structures, such as differentiating between tunnel walls, obstacles, and utilities is crucial for robotic systems to achieve precise environmental awareness. This capability enables more accurate execution of subsequent tasks, including autonomous navigation, decision-making planning, and human-robot interaction.

Spatial perception in subterranean environments largely relies on 3D point cloud data acquired through laser scanning technologies. However, the distinctive characteristics of underground mine tunnels pose significant challenges to conventional 3D point cloud segmentation methods, hindering the development of reliable environment perception systems based on this data. These challenges include: (1) highly irregular geometries; (2) sparse point clouds exacerbated by prevailing dust particles, a common issue in such harsh environments; and (3) structural occlusions at equipment overlaps, all of which adversely affect methods relying on traditional geometric priors. Consequently, state-of-the-art deep learning segmentation techniques, while effective in more controlled environments, often underperform when processing these challenging underground point clouds. For instance, PointNet++1 achieved only 75.6% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) in mine scenarios.

Although the Transformer2 architecture has demonstrated significant potential for point cloud processing3,4, its direct application faces two primary bottlenecks: parameter inefficiency and limited feature interaction across groups. Methods like Point Transformer5, which leverage vector attention mechanisms, experience a surge in parameter count as network depth and feature channels increase. This restricts their deployment on resource-constrained devices typically used for in situ data analysis. While grouping strategies, such as those employed in Point Trans.v26, can reduce complexity, they often isolate information between groups, compromising the model’s ability to recognize irregular structures, such as deformed ventilation ducts, which are prevalent in infrastructure data.

Addressing core challenges in 3D point cloud understanding for laser-scanned subterranean data, this paper proposes a novel perspective for efficient and robust semantic segmentation, specifically targeting complex subterranean infrastructure. First, a lightweight Group-Aware Channel Interaction Module(GCIM) is proposed. By grouping feature channels and sharing vector attention weights, the module significantly improves the parameter utilization efficiency. Meanwhile, it enables explicit information interaction among different groups, effectively enriching the dimension of feature fusion and expanding the effective receptive field of features. Moreover, it introduces no excessive computational overhead, achieving a good balance between lightweight design and segmentation performance. Secondly, aiming at the critical problem of ambiguous spatial relationships caused by dust and noise interference as well as geometric similarity of adjacent pipelines in underground environments, we propose an Interactive Position Encoding (IPE) scheme. Based on relative position information, this method dynamically couples point features with spatial context information. By enhancing the feature interaction mechanism, it can accurately distinguish easily confused ambiguous regions such as dense pipelines and arc-shaped tunnel walls, while maintaining the lightweight and high-efficiency characteristics of the model. Due to the scarcity of publicly available annotated datasets for underground scene semantic segmentation, existing resources are insufficient to meet the requirements of model training and validation. Therefore, we collect real underground coal mine scene data and construct a dedicated semantic segmentation dataset named Coal Mines for underground coal mine environments. To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed method, we first validate its segmentation capability in complex underground scenarios on three underground datasets: Coal Mines, the tunnel dataset Seg2Tunnel7, and the trench dataset OpenTrench3D8. Meanwhile, evaluations are conducted on two general indoor scene datasets, ScanNetv29 and S3DIS10, to verify the generalization ability of the method. It is worth noting that the core research objective of this paper is not to deliberately pursue accuracy improvement on general indoor datasets. Instead, we target practical underground complex scenarios such as underground coal mines and tunnels, and focus on the engineering demands of lightweight design and efficient semantic segmentation. Related works

Point cloud segmentation methods

Based on data representation and processing paradigms, existing deep learning-based point cloud semantic segmentation methods can be broadly categorized into indirect and direct approaches. Indirect methods primarily include projection-based11,12 and voxel-based approaches13,14. Projection-based methods map 3D point clouds onto 2D image planes via multi-view15, bird’s-eye view (BEV)16, or spherical projection17, followed by 2D convolutional networks for segmentation. While computationally efficient, the projection process inevitably leads to the loss of 3D spatial topological information, and segmentation performance is susceptible to view occlusion and projection distortion. Voxel-based methods discretize point clouds into regular 3D grids to capture global contextual features. However, their computational and memory overhead grow cubically with grid resolution, and the discretization process unavoidably sacrifices fine-grained geometric details.

Direct methods perform feature learning directly in the raw point cloud domain, encompassing point-based, graph-based, sparse convolution, and hybrid architectures. Point-based methods, exemplified by PointNet18 and PointNet++1,, achieve permutation invariance through shared multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) and symmetric pooling operations; however, global pooling struggles to model local geometric structures and long-range semantic dependencies. Graph-based methods represent point clouds as graph structures, explicitly characterizing spatial and semantic correlations between points via graph edges19. For instance, DGCNN20 dynamically constructs and updates graph topology in feature space, learning edge features through MLPs and performing channel-wise symmetric aggregation over neighboring points. Widyaningrum et al.21 further integrate orthophoto color features with spatial context information to achieve efficient segmentation of airborne LiDAR point clouds, yet the robustness of such methods heavily depends on the quality of neighborhood graph construction2. Sparse convolution methods22,23 employ adaptive convolution kernels to extract local features directly from non-uniform point sets, but they are sensitive to variations in point cloud density distribution, and continuous convolution operations incur substantial floating-point computational overhead. Hybrid architectures24 compensate for the inherent limitations of single modalities by fusing point, voxel, or multi-view representations; however, they generally suffer from complex network structures, slow training convergence, and high deployment costs.

In recent years, Transformer-based architectures, leveraging global self-attention mechanisms and flexible context aggregation capabilities, have demonstrated exceptional global representation capacity and scale adaptability in processing sparse, unstructured point clouds by dynamically modeling long-range dependencies between arbitrary point pairs25,26. Accordingly, this thesis focuses on Transformer-based point cloud segmentation paradigms, aiming to strengthen the collaborative learning of global and local contexts while ensuring computational efficiency through optimized attention interaction mechanisms. Early related work25 employed improved self-attention modules as the core backbone, combined with U-Net-like architectures for point-wise semantic segmentation. For instance, Point Transformer5 introduced vector self-attention and position difference encoding to enhance local feature aggregation; however, it was confined to local receptive fields and lacked long-range context modeling and cross-scale feature interaction capabilities. Stratified Transformer27 drews inspiration from the hierarchical design of Swin Transformer28, expanding the receptive field through 3D patch partitioning and hybrid key sampling strategies, yet at the cost of extremely high computational and memory overhead. Recent research has devoted efforts to designing efficient self-attention mechanisms, for example, RPNet29 adopted a grouped relation aggregator to jointly learn geometric and semantic correlations, while FPT30 and PatchFormer31 focused on lightweight Transformer architectures to accelerate training and inference. Nevertheless, such lightweight methods typically sacrifice local fine-grained feature interaction and global context modeling capabilities. To address this limitation, this thesis proposes a lightweight group-aware channel interaction module, which significantly reduces computational overhead while effectively strengthening the collaborative learning of multi-scale contexts, thereby alleviating the imbalance between computational efficiency and representation capability in existing approaches.

Underground scene segmentation methods

Underground spaces are inherently characterized by complex structures, ambiguous semantic boundaries and disorderly distributed pipelines, posing severe challenges to point cloud semantic segmentation in underground scenarios. Traditional machine learning methods32,33 rely heavily on manually crafted features, which suffer from low segmentation accuracy and poor robustness in complex underground environments. In contrast, deep learning methods can adaptively learn high-level semantic representations from raw data and exhibit remarkable advantages7,34,35. Early studies adopted indirect representation strategies to adapt to convolutional networks, by projecting point clouds onto 2D planes or discretizing them into regular 3D voxel grids. For instance, Ji et al.36 constructed a voxel-based 3D convolutional encoder–decoder network to realize semantic prediction of tunnel components. Zhang et al.37 combined projected image segmentation with raw point cloud processing to improve the segmentation accuracy of tunnel structures and water seepage defects. Tan et al.38 proposed the LCJ-Seg network based on voxelization, and introduced local–global cross attention to enhance feature interaction. Nevertheless, such regularized transformation inevitably leads to the loss of geometric details and topological information. Meanwhile, the increase of grid resolution dramatically raises computational overhead and parameter volume, making it difficult to meet the requirements of fine-grained segmentation for tiny objects and ambiguous boundaries.

In recent years, methods based on encoder–decoder architectures and attention mechanisms have achieved excellent performance in underground structure segmentation. ASPCNet39 introduces a self-attention module to strengthen long-range contextual correlation and feature mining. SPCNet40 adopts an attention-coupled downsampling and upsampling framework, and realizes weighted feature aggregation through sequence modeling. GL-Net41 designs a global-local collaborative encoding module to balance global structural representation and local detail enhancement. However, such methods take local convolution as the core with attention only serving as an auxiliary module, leading to limited receptive fields. Moreover, global modeling relies on dense computation, and its complexity increases quadratically with the number of points. LiningNet42 improves segmentation accuracy and generalization ability for rare segment layouts by fusing structural geometric priors, yet it is limited by high manual annotation costs and reliance on specific segment layouts. Yang et al.43 developed Tunnel Scanner containing tunnel parameters to generate synthetic point clouds with high-fidelity annotations, and adopted transfer learning for domain adaptation of synthetic data to boost segmentation performance. TUC-Net44 incorporates neighborhood feature perception enhancement and self-attention modules. Although it improves segmentation performance, its parameter volume reaches 3.4077 million with an inference latency of 13.43 ms. EDA-TCNet45 employs a dual attention mechanism that combines channel attention and spatial attention to enhance feature representation, yet it introduces six attention modules, resulting in additional computational burden

In summary, existing methods still face bottlenecks in the segmentation of complex underground point clouds, including the loss of geometric details, high computational overhead, and insufficient robustness against strong noise. Long-distance pipelines and irregular deformed structures in underground scenarios heavily rely on global context modeling and cross-scale feature fusion. Therefore, targeting the inherent characteristics of underground environments, this paper proposes a robust Transformer segmentation network for underground scenes. By optimizing the self-attention interaction paradigm, the proposed network strengthens multi-scale context perception while reducing computational cost, overcomes the inherent challenges of underground scenarios, and achieves high-precision semantic segmentation.

Position encoding

Recent transformer-based networks have explored embedding position information to improve feature representation. Since transformers lack recurrence and convolution, the original self-attention mechanism only considers absolute positions2, adding positional encodings to input embeddings. DETR46 used sine-based absolute positional encoding for object detection, but struggled with longer sequences. Swin Transformer28 introduced relative position biases to capture relative relationships. However, most methods focus only on absolute or relative positions, lacking a comprehensive approach. HPNet47 combined both absolute and relative positions, and the rotary matrix48 enabled effective position encoding with both types. However, rotation adds computational complexity. Method

The input data undergo downsampling, followed by feature extraction using the interactive attention unit, and upsampling to achieve precise point cloud semantic segmentation. Downsampling employs a partition-based pooling approach6, while upsampling leverages grid division from the downsampling stage to accurately map point features within subsets. Interactive positional encoding

The previously used positional encoding techniques require the computation of correlations between each position and every other position within the sequence50,51, which inevitably imposes a significant computational burden on the model. It is obtained with the position encoding δ added to the relation vector γ (Q, K) as a bias. Inspired by Attention with Linear Biases (ALiBi)52, we propose an interactive positional encoding method, as shown in Fig. 2, which directly computes relative positions based on positional indices. The core innovation lies in upgrading position encoding from a single position function to a position-feature joint interaction function, achieving deep coupling between spatial information and feature information. This approach is simple, efficient, and highly effective, as it seamlessly integrates positional and feature information between two point clouds, facilitating the learning of contextual details.

For any two points \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_i$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_j$$\end{document} in the point cloud, the interactive positional encoding δ is expressed in Eq.(9):9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \delta =(\textbf{f}_i)^T\phi (\textbf{p}_j,\textbf{p}_i)(\textbf{f}_j)+(\textbf{f}_j)^T\sigma (\textbf{p}_i)(\textbf{f}_i) + (\textbf{f}_i)^T \sigma (\textbf{p}_j)(\textbf{f}_j), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where the mapping function σ is a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with two linear layers and one ReLU nonlinear layer. The relative positional encoding ϕ is shown in Eq.(10):10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \phi =Dis \cdot m=(|K_{p_j}|-|K_{p_i}|) \cdot m. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}The calculation method for the relative positional encoding is as follows: First, we compute the relative distance matrix \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Dis = |K_{p_j}|- |Q_{p_i}|=|j|-|i|$$\end{document} for point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_i$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_j$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|K_{p_j}|$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|Q_{p_i}|$$\end{document} represent the index of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_j$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_i$$\end{document}, respectively. Each head for the relative distances has a hyper parameter m that controls the slope of the penalty bias as the relative distance increases. Following the settings in reference52, when the number of heads is n, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m=2^{\frac{-8}{n}}$$\end{document}. Finally, by using this new input to perform self-attention calculation, the model can learn the relative positions between inputs, with the penalty bias increasing as the relative distance grows, thereby achieving the effect of local attention. If the index between two point clouds is 0, then only the absolute positional information of the point cloud needs to interact with the features. In other words, only the values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\textbf{f}_j)^T\sigma (\textbf{p}_i)\textbf{f}_i$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\textbf{f}_i)^T \sigma (\textbf{p}_j)\textbf{f}_j$$\end{document} are calculated. The interactive positional encoding technique proposed in this paper retains the inherent flexibility of the conventional attention mechanism, while simultaneously reducing computational complexity by decreasing the number of position pair comparisons required. This improvement enables the model to efficiently capture sequential information.

Experiments

Experimental setting

Datasets

To verify the effectiveness of the algorithm proposed in this chapter, evaluation experiments were conducted on five benchmark point cloud segmentation datasets covering underground and indoor scenarios, namely Coal Mines, Seg2Tunnel7, OpenTrench3D8, S3DIS10, and ScanNetv29. The Coal Mines dataset was collected from real underground mining operation areas provided by the Coal Science Research Institute. Multi-station 3D laser scanning was conducted in underground roadways using the FARO Focus S350 and GeoSLAM ZEB Horizon. During data acquisition, overlapping scans and SLAM-based registration techniques were adopted to reduce occlusions and missing regions. The raw point clouds were further processed through denoising, registration, and coordinate normalization. The final dataset contains 27,077,467 points, where each point consists of three Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) and one intensity value. To ensure experimental consistency, the dataset organization follows the format of the S3DIS dataset10, and the data were split into training and testing sets with a ratio of 8:2. During annotation, the point clouds were first segmented and clustered using CloudCompare, followed by manual point-wise semantic labeling with LabelCloud. The dataset includes five semantic categories: Roof, Floor, Rib, Ventilation Duct, and Pipeline. The category definitions are based on the structural characteristics and engineering functions of underground mine roadways. Among them, ventilation ducts and pipelines are typical elongated structures with severe occlusions and class imbalance, making the dataset suitable for evaluating semantic segmentation performance in complex underground mining environments.

Seg2Tunnel comprises five shield tunnels (Tunnel 1–Tunnel 5) located in Wuxi, Shanghai, and Fuzhou, including three in-service tunnels (Tunnel 1–Tunnel 3) and two newly constructed tunnels (Tunnel 4–Tunnel 5), thereby adapting to algorithm testing under diverse geographical environments and tunnel conditions. For direct comparison with existing deep learning benchmarks, Tunnel 1 (T1) and Tunnel 2 (T2) from Seg2Tunnel were employed as the test set, where T1 consists of 17 scanning stations and T2 includes 14 scanning stations. The input data are annular point clouds with 6 point slices per ring. With point-wise annotation adopted, each point contains three Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) and one intensity value, with a total of L+1 semantic labels, where L denotes the number of segments per ring, and label 0 represents the background category.

OpenTrench3D is a trench-specific dataset covering 7 regions, including 5 water conservancy facilities and 2 heating facilities. For the purpose of evaluation, Regions 1–5 (water conservancy areas) from the dataset’s benchmark8 were selected. This dataset includes five semantic categories (i.e., Trench, Main Utility, Other Utility, Inactive Utility, Misc), which is applicable for validating the algorithm’s segmentation performance in underground trench scenarios.

S3DIS is a widely used large-scale benchmark for indoor scene segmentation, consisting of six areas (Area 1–Area 6). Each point provides 3D coordinates and RGB color information, with 13 annotated semantic categories. Similarly, the ScanNetv2 dataset provides 3D coordinates and RGB color information for each point, with 20 annotated semantic categories. Throughout the experiments, a strict fair comparison protocol was followed, with no external training data introduced. Comprehensive benchmark tests were performed on all datasets to ensure the objectivity and reliability of the experimental results.

Evaluation metrics

The evaluation metrics include Overall Accuracy (OA), mean Intersection over Union(mIoU), and mean Accuracy within each class(mAcc). In particular, mIoU provides the most reasonable evaluation for class-imbalanced datasets and is thus adopted as the primary metric. The evaluation metrics can be calculated as follows:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} OA=\frac{{\sum _{K=1}^K}TP_K}{N}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} mAcc=\frac{1}{K}{\sum _{K=1}^K}\frac{TP_K}{TP_K+FN_K}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} mIoU=\frac{1}{K}{\sum _{K=1}^K}\frac{TP_K}{TP_K+FP_K+FN_K}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where K is the total number of classes, N is the number of points, and TP, FP, and FN denote true positive, false positive, and false negative predictions respectively.

Parameters and runtime analysis

All experiments are conducted on a fixed hardware platform of NVIDIA RTX A6000, with the software environment of Ubuntu 20.04 (https://ubuntu.com/download/desktop), PyTorch 2.4.1 (https://pytorch.org/), and CUDA 12.1 (https://developer.nvidia.com/cuda-toolkit). The same voxel grid resolution as used in the training phase is adopted consistently (Table 2). The number of input points is determined dynamically according to the scene range, and the batch size is fixed to 1 to simulate real-time inference scenarios. We perform 20 warm-up runs before formal inference, followed by 500 forward propagations synchronized via torch.cuda.synchronize(). The average latency together with the standard deviation is reported. The peak GPU memory usage is measured using \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated( )$$\end{document}, and the peak memory record is reset before each inference run.

It can be observed from Table 10 that the proposed method achieves an excellent overall balance between segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency. Compared with Point Transformer, our method reduces the parameter count by 35.1%, FLOPs by 52.0%, and peak memory usage by 39.3%, while increasing the mIoU by 3.2%. This result demonstrates that the proposed IPE replaces the continuous Euclidean distance calculation with discrete index offset computation, avoiding the additional storage and computational overhead caused by explicitly constructing neighborhood distance matrices in conventional RPE. Consequently, it effectively reduces the computational burden and memory consumption of the model, achieving superior deployment efficiency in large-scale point cloud scenarios.

Compared with Point Trans.v3, our method exhibits clear advantages in parameter count and peak memory usage, with parameters reduced by 84.0% and memory consumption decreased by 43.3%, while maintaining a slightly higher mIoU by 0.2%. Although Point Transformer v3 achieves marginally faster inference speed due to lower FLOPs, it suffers from a much larger model size and higher memory overhead. In contrast, our method maintains competitive segmentation accuracy while enabling a more compact model design and better resource utilization efficiency, which verifies the effective balance of the IPE module between lightweight modeling and feature representation capability. PatchFormer delivers faster inference speed and a more lightweight structure, yet its mIoU is still 2.3% lower than that of the proposed method. Effect of interactive positional encoding

To fully validate the effectiveness of the interactive position encoding, we conduct systematic ablation experiments on multiple datasets covering underground and indoor scenarios. In the experiments, the baseline model without position encoding removes all positional information and calculates the attention mechanism solely based on feature similarity. Absolute Position Encoding (APE) directly concatenates the absolute position embeddings derived from point cloud coordinates with point features. Traditional Relative Position Encoding (RPE) follows the original relative position bias adopted in the Transformer architecture, which only considers the Euclidean distance between points. In contrast, the proposed Interactive Position Encoding (IPE) is capable of simultaneously modeling the relative positional relationships and local geometric interaction correlations among point elements. Overall, IPE exhibits the most stable cross-scene generalization performance, which verifies the effectiveness of the index distance. The superiority stems from the fact that after the input point cloud is preprocessed via voxel-grid downsampling, the sequence indices preserve the local correlation of 3D spatial positions, and points with adjacent indices tend to be spatially neighboring in the physical space. Although IPE-Euc and IPE-Knn also achieve competitive results, they require explicit computation of point-to-point distance matrices at the scale of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document} or O(NK), leading to high computational overhead. Meanwhile, underground point clouds inherently suffer from high sparsity, severe noise interference and irregular geometric structures. Geometry-distance encoding based on K-nearest neighbors is susceptible to fluctuations in point cloud sampling density and outliers. In contrast, the index distance combined with the spatial sorting strategy possesses stronger environmental adaptability and robustness. It only requires lightweight index difference query and linear mapping. Without sacrificing feature representation capability, it effectively avoids excessive computational cost and the instability of neighbor sampling, and better adapts to the inherent characteristics of underground scenarios. Consequently, it achieves optimal performance in both segmentation accuracy and inference efficiency.

Discussion

Our framework achieves state-of-the-art 3D segmentation by optimizing two core designs. The Group-aware Channel Interaction Module (GCIM) exhibits optimal efficacy when configured with k=6 neighboring channels, delivering a significant 4.8% mIoU improvement over the k=3 configuration (97.5% vs 92.7% on Coal Mines) while maintaining parameter efficiency. Performance degradation occurs at k=9 (e.g., 95.4% mIoU on Seg2Tunnel compared to 95.7% at k=6), indicating noise introduction from excessive interaction ranges. Concurrently, the Interactive Positional Encoding (IPE) mechanism resolves structural ambiguities by integrating relative positions with local geometric interactions, surpassing traditional Relative Positional Encoding by 2.2 and 3.3 percentage points across datasets. This eliminates critical failures such as ventilation duct misclassification (Figure 8) through enhanced spatial constraint modeling. Quantitative validation confirms consistent sub-centimeter precision in underground infrastructure (97.5% mIoU on Coal Mines and 95.7% on Seg2Tunnel), millimeter-level geometric consistency in utility mapping (84.8% mIoU on OpenTrench3D), and centimeter-accurate segmentation in indoor environments (79.3% mIoU on S3DIS and 75.5% on ScanNetv2), demonstrating cross-scene reliability for safety-critical applications where precision directly impacts operational integrity.

Conclusion

Aiming at the challenges in semantic segmentation of laser-scanned point clouds in underground environments, such as irregular geometric structures, sparse point clouds caused by dust interference, and severe occlusion, this paper proposes a lightweight grouped-aware channel interaction module targeting the practical engineering requirements of lightweight design and efficient segmentation. By sharing attention weights of projection vectors, the module effectively improves parameter utilization efficiency, reduces model complexity, and enhances feature representation capability simultaneously. Furthermore, an interactive position encoding module is designed in this work. It strengthens the interaction between spatial position information and semantic features, thereby alleviating the category confusion problem in complex regions such as dense pipelines and curved tunnel walls. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior segmentation performance on underground datasets including Coal Mines, Seg2Tunnel and OpenTrench3D, which verifies its effectiveness in complex underground scenarios and provides a valuable reference for intelligent point cloud perception in underground engineering. Meanwhile, extensive experiments on public general scene datasets such as ScanNetv2 and S3DIS further prove that our method possesses favorable robustness and generalization ability.

Nevertheless, this paper still has certain limitations. First, publicly available datasets for underground scenarios are currently relatively scarce, and the structural complexity of scenes in the Coal Mines and Seg2Tunnel datasets is limited. Accordingly, the generalization ability of the proposed method in underground coal mine environments with higher complexity remains to be further verified. Second, the proposed method is somewhat dependent on point cloud integrity. Under extreme working conditions, such as severe occlusion, ultra-low-density sampling, or strong noise interference, the segmentation accuracy may degrade. Future work will further focus on lightweight network architecture design, multi-scenario transfer learning, and robustness enhancement under complex working conditions, so as to continuously improve the practicability and applicability of the method in real underground engineering scenarios.

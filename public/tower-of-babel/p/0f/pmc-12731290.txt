Multi-omics analysis revealed coordinated responses of rumen microbiota and epithelium of goats to dietary supplementation of glucosamine selenium
Authors: Tianwei Liu, Shuang Li, Yan Huang, Shijie Chu, Xiang Huang, Jingling Fu, Wen Zhu, Yinghui Ling
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Selenium (Se) is one of the essential trace elements for ruminants. The impact of varying dietary Se levels on rumen epithelium and microbiota remains inadequately studied. This study used multi-omics techniques to investigate the coordinated response of rumen microbiota and epithelium to dietary glucosamine selenium (GASe) supplementation. Sixty female Wanlin White goats (19.90 ± 2.00 kg) at 4 months of age were randomly divided into 4 groups with 15 replications of 1 goat each. Goats in the control (CON), and the low (L), medium (M) and high does (H) groups were supplemented GASe at 0, 0.6, 1.2 and 2.4 mg Se/kg, respectively. After a 60-d feeding trial, 5 goats per group were randomly selected for slaughter and sampling. The results showed that average daily gain, rumen papilla length, and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) activity in the L group were significantly higher than those in the CON and H groups (quadratic; P < 0.05). Microbial analysis revealed that unclassified_Christensenellaceae (quadratic) had higher (P < 0.05) abundances in the L group and showed significant positive correlations with rumen papilla length and GPx activity (P < 0.05). In contrast, the abundances of pathogenic Campylobacter were highest in the H group (linear or quadratic; P < 0.05). Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) identified the gene module MEturquoise and the compound module ME6 as significantly associated with papilla length and GPx activity (P < 0.05). Genes in the MEturquoise module were enriched in ribosome, oxidative phosphorylation, RNA polymerase, and nucleotide sugar biosynthesis pathways, while the ME6 module was mainly associated with amino acid and nucleotide metabolism. Multi-omics analysis revealed that metabolic responses bridged transcriptional regulation and microbial dynamics. Additionally, 2 microbiota-derived substances, actinorhodin and dimethylpropiothetin, were significantly associated with papilla length and GPx activity (P < 0.05). In conclusion, an appropriate amount of GASe can improve goat growth performance, promote rumen epithelial growth and health by enhancing cellular metabolism and increasing beneficial bacteria abundance. However, excessive GASe supplementation may reduce rumen epithelial antioxidative and metabolic capacities, and promote pathogenic bacteria proliferation. Furthermore, this study demonstrates that metabolites mediate the coordinated response of the rumen microbiota and host to dietary GASe supplementation. Supplementation of 0.598 to 0.832 mg Se/kg GASe in the diet is recommended for goats.

Introduction

Selenium (Se) is one of the essential trace elements for animals, possessing biological functions such as promoting metabolism, antioxidation, anti-inflammation, and enhancing immunity (Rayman, 2008; Razaghi et al., 2021), which are crucial for maintaining optimal animal health and production (Cruickshank et al., 2024). However, the Se content in feed is often insufficient to meet animal requirements, making it necessary to supplement Se from alternative sources (Liu et al., 2022). Organic Se is more favored due to its higher bioavailability and lower toxicity (Qiu et al., 2022). Glucosamine selenium (GASe) is a new organic Se compound, prepared by combining Se compounds with glucosamine (Liang et al., 2022). This organic Se has not yet been studied on ruminants.

When the Se content in feed is insufficient, it often leads to tissue damage and functional disorders in the body (Li et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2023a), varying degrees of reduction in the length of intestinal villi, crypt depth, and mucosal thickness in poultry (He et al., 2020), alter gene expression in intestinal tissues, leading to oxidative stress and inflammatory responses (Wang et al., 2022), subsequently reducing animal productivity (Mehdi and Dufrasne, 2016). As a necessary part of glutathione peroxidase (GPx), Se can shield cells from oxidative damage. Jing et al. (2022) demonstrated that supplementing Se to pig feed could alleviate endoplasmic reticulum stress, restore hepatic lipid metabolism enzyme activity, regulate hepatic mRNA expression, and resist hepatic lipid metabolism disorders. Studies on ruminants have shown that Se supplementation in feed can increase feed intake, promote rumen fermentation, alter rumen microbial community structure, and affect rumen microbial amino acid and lipid metabolism in the rumen (Cui et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2024), and improve ruminant productivity (Zhang et al., 2020). Shi et al. (2017) found that supplementing goat feed with Se enhanced superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity and reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels in the blood. Supplementing with Se can also encourage the deposition of Se in milk, improving the quality of dairy products (Cruickshank et al., 2024).

The rumen, as a vital digestive and absorptive organ in ruminants, is one of the absorption sites for Se and is the digestive organ most immediately affected by dietary Se (Arshad et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2024). The ruminal microbiota is crucial for maintaining a stable rumen environment, promoting feed digestion and absorption, and ultimately benefiting the animal (Huang et al., 2023). Since ruminal epithelial microbiota are directly related to host tissues, they may play an indispensable role in ruminant metabolism and host–microbe interactions (Cheng et al., 1979). Previous investigations into the effects of Se on the rumen of ruminants have often focused on rumen fermentation and rumen fluid microbiota. However, few comprehensive investigations examine the possible coordinated reactions of host metabolism and rumen microbial composition to dietary Se treatment.

Therefore, to evaluate the coordinated responses of ruminal microbiota and host metabolism to dietary GASe supplementation, this study uses weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and integrated multi-omics sequencing analysis approaches. This study expands our understanding of the crosstalk between rumen epithelial microbiota and the host under GASe supplementation conditions, providing new insights into the study of Se in ruminants.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

All experimental procedures were approved by Anhui Agricultural University (No. AHAUXMSQ2023107).

Animal trial and sample collection

Sixty healthy female Wanlin White goats, initially weighing 19.90 ± 2.00 kg (mean ± SD) at 4 months of age, were randomly assigned to 4 groups with 15 replications of 1 goat each. Goats in the control (CON), and the low (L), medium (M) and high does (H) groups were supplemented GASe at 0, 0.6, 1.2 and 2.4 mg Se/kg, respectively. Glucosamine selenium was purchased from Shuanglin Marine Biological Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Nantong, Jiangsu, China) and contained 372.5 g/kg of Se. Each goat was kept in a separate pen with free access to clean water. They were fed twice a day, at 07:30 and 16:30, with 10% feed orts. After a 14-d adaptation phase on a control diet, the goats were fed 4 different treatment diets for 60 d. At the end of the trial, 5 goats were randomly selected from each group and slaughtered before the morning feeding. Rumen epithelium samples were collected from the bottom of the ventral sac. The rumen epithelium (approximately 12 cm2) was washed with pre-cooled sterile phosphate buffered saline (PBS, pH = 7) to remove the rumen contents. Part of the epithelial sections (approximately 4 cm2) were fixed in a 10% formalin solution for morphological examination. The remaining epithelium tissues were divided into 4 parts, rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C. One subsample was used to analyze antioxidant indices using a commercial kit (Jiengcheng Institute of Agricultural Sciences, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). The other 3 subsamples were used for host transcriptome, host metabolome, and 16S rRNA sequencing, respectively.

Nutrient composition of diet

The nutrient composition of the diet was analyzed following AOAC (2000) methods. Feed samples were ground and passed through a 1-mm screen using a high-speed grinder (Changzhou Sury Instruments Co., Ltd., Changzhou, Jiangsu, China) and dried at 135 °C for 2 h to determine dry matter content (method 930.15). The crude protein (CP) content, calculated as nitrogen (N) × 6.25, was determined using a Kjeldahl apparatus (Kjeltec 2300, FOSS Analytical AB, Hoganas, Sweden). The ether extract (EE) was quantified using a Soxhlet extractor (SE-6P, Shandong BioBase Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Jinan, Shandong, China) with diethyl ether for 8 h (method 920.39). Ash content was determined by incineration in a muffle furnace (Lindberg Moldatherm, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) at 600 °C for 6 h (method 924.05). Organic matter (OM) content was calculated as: OM (%) = 100% – Ash (%). Calcium (Ca) and phosphorus (P) were measured via the disodium ethylenediaminetetraacetate complexometric titration method and ammonium vanadate molybdate colorimetric method (method 968.08), respectively, and Se content was analyzed using the method described by Wu et al. (2019). Neutral detergent fiber and acid detergent fiber were analyzed according to the method described by Van Soest et al. (1991).

Measurement of rumen epithelial morphology

The rumen epithelial samples were removed from 10% neutral buffered formalin, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, stained with hematoxylin and eosin, and sealed. The slides were scanned using the Pannoramic DESK (Pannoramic MIDI II, 3D HISTECH, Budapest, Hungary), and the CaseViewer (v2.4.0, 3D HISTECH, Budapest, Hungary) image analysis processing system was used to observe and measure the height, width, and keratin layer thickness of the rumen papillae.

Rumen epithelium microbiota 16S rRNA sequencing analysis

The rumen epithelium microbial DNA was extracted and purified using the TGuide S96 Magnetic Soil/Stool DNA Kit (Tiangen Biotech [Beijing] Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The concentration and quality of the extracted DNA were assessed using a NanoDrop 1000 spectrophotometer (Nyx Bio Technologies SAS, Paris, France) and 1.0% agarose gel electrophoresis. The V3 to V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene was amplified using the forward primer 341F (5′-CCTAYGGGRBGCASCAG-3′) and reverse primer 806R (5′-GGACTACNNGGGTATCTAAT-3′) (Xue et al., 2019), and the resulting PCR products were purified and quantified to construct a cDNA library, which was sequenced using an Illumina novaseq 6000 (Illumina, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Raw reads containing ambiguous bases and sequences longer than 480 base pairs (bp) were removed, as were sequences shorter than 200 bp. Paired-end raw reads were merged into raw tags using FLASH (v1.2.3) with a minimum overlap length of 10 bp. Chimeras were removed using UCHIME (v8.1). Sequences with a similarity of ≥97% were clustered into the same amplicon sequence variants (ASVs), and ASVs with a reabundance < 0.005% were filtered out. Taxonomy annotation of the ASVs was performed using the Naive Bayes classifier in QIIME2 based on the SILVA database (v132) with a confidence threshold of 70% (Bolyen et al., 2019). Only bacteria observed in more than half of the samples in one group were considered in the relative abundance analysis (Liu et al., 2024). Data were processed and analysed using the BMK Cloud platform (Biomarker Technologies Co., Ltd., Beijing, China).

Rumen epithelium transcriptome sequencing analysis

RNA-seq services were provided by MetWare Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, Hubei, China). Briefly, total RNA from rumen epithelial samples (n = 20) was extracted using TRIzol (TaKaRa Bio, Otsu, Japan). RNA purity was assessed using a NanoPhotometer spectrophotometer (Implen, Inc., Westlake Village, CA, USA), and RNA integrity was evaluated using the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on the Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) according to library effective concentration pooling and data output requirements. Reads containing poly-N sequences and low-quality reads were filtered out, and the remaining clean reads were aligned to the reference genome (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/all/GCF/001/704/415/GCF_001704415.1_ARS1) using HISAT2. HTSeq v0.11.1 was utilized to quantify the reads mapped to each gene. Differential expression (DEG) analyses were conducted with DESeq2 v1.30.1. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were filtered with |log2 fold change| ≥ 1, and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05.

Gene expression assessed by real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

To validate the accuracy of the rumen epithelial transcriptome results, we conducted RT-qPCR. RNA was extracted from the rumen epithelium following the same protocol as used for transcriptomic measurements. cDNA synthesis was performed using a reverse transcription kit (Ailab, Beijing, China). mRNA quantification was carried out using the Bio-Rad CFX Connect Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc., Hercules, CA, USA) with the following reaction conditions: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 15 s and annealing at 60 °C for 30 s. The mRNA primers are listed in Table S1.

Rumen epithelium metabolome sequencing analysis

Rumen epithelium untargeted metabolomics services were provided by MetWare Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, Hubei, China). Briefly, rumen epithelial (20 mg) samples were thawed on ice and then homogenized in 400 μL of 70% ice-cold methanol/water (Vol/Vol) for 22 min. The mixture was centrifuged at 15,000 × g at 4 °C for 15 min and the supernatant was taken for Liquid Chromatography–Tandem Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis. All samples acquired by the LC-MS/MS system followed machine orders. The original mass spectrometry data was converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard. Peak picking, alignment, and retention time correction were performed using the XCMS program. After that, the identified metabolite information was obtained by searching the self-constructed internal database and public databases. Utilizing the R package ropls (v1.6.2), partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were carried out.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 23.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) with the following model:Yij = μ + Ti + Gj + eij,where Yij represents the observation of the dependent variable, μ is the general mean, Ti is the fixed effect of the i-th GASe supplementation level, Gj is the random effect of goat j, and eij is the random residual error. Tukey's test was used to analyze the differences between the GASe treatment groups. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. Regression analysis was conducted in SPSS 23.0 to evaluate both linear (L) and quadratic (Q) effects. The quadratic model (Y = aX2 + bX + c) was used to estimate the GASe supplementation level that is most beneficial for goat average daily gain (ADG) and antioxidant capacity and to recommend the minimum GASe supplementation required to achieve 95% of the optimal result (Jin et al., 2024).

Using the WGCNA package (v1.7.1) in R software (Langfelder and Horvath, 2008), gene and metabolite co-expression modules related to phenotypic traits were identified with a soft threshold of 12 for genes and 6 for metabolites. The minimum module size was set to 150 for genes and 60 for metabolites, and the module cut height was set to 0.2 for genes and 0.3 for metabolites. Pearson correlation analysis was used to calculate the module–trait relationships. Functional enrichment analysis of the ME6 module metabolites was performed using the MetaboAnalyst 5.0 platform (www.metaboanalyst.ca). A simple metorigin analysis (SMOA) mode on the MetOrigin online server was used to trace and enrich the metabolites in the rumen epithelial ME6 module (Yu et al., 2022). The differential metabolites were analyzed using Student's t-test for comparisons between 2 groups and one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc test for multiple group comparisons. Differential metabolites were identified with a P-value < 0.05, and variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1, between control and GASe supplement groups.

The rumen epithelium microbiota, rumen epithelial transcriptome, and rumen epithelial metabolome were analyzed using multiple co-inertia analysis (MCIA) with the R package ‘omicade4’ (v1.44.0) to identify shared variation trends and correlations (Meng et al., 2014). The ‘psych’ package in R was utilized to conduct Spearman's analysis to determine the relationships between key microbes and phenotypes, key genes, and key metabolites. A heatmap was used to visualize the results. Connections were considered significant when P < 0.05 and |R| > 0.5.

Rumen epithelial microbiota profile in GASe

The alpha diversity indices did not show any statistically significant variations (Fig. 2A). The gene expression in rumen epithelium was influenced by GASe supplement

A total of 134.01 GB of clean data were produced by transcriptome sequencing of 20 rumen epithelial samples, with an average of 6.7 GB of clean data per sample. According to PCA analysis, there were some variations in the gene expression levels between the GASe-supplemented and CON groups (Fig. S1A). It's interesting to note that the quantity of DEGs dropped as GASe concentration rose. In comparison to the CON group, we found that the L group (Fig. S1B) had 702 DEGs (376 upregulated and 326 downregulated), the M group (Fig. S1C) had 12 DEGs (8 upregulated and 4 downregulated), and the H group (Fig. S1D) had 10 DEGs (10 upregulated and 0 downregulated). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis showed that in the L vs. CON comparison group, the DEGs were enriched across 92 pathways, including oxidative phosphorylation, arachidonic acid metabolism, spliceosome, nucleotide metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, and glutathione metabolism (Fig. S2A). In the M vs. CON comparison group, the 12 DEGs were mainly enriched in glycosphingolipid biosynthesis (lacto and neolacto series), nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, herpes simplex virus I infection, allograft rejection, type I diabetes mellitus, graft-versus-host disease, autoimmune thyroid disease, and viral myocarditis pathways (Fig. S2B). In the H vs. CON comparison group, 10 DEGs were only enriched in the hepatitis C pathway (Fig. S2C).

The metabolites in rumen epithelium influenced by GASe supplement

Metabolomic studies indicate that a total of 3106 metabolites were detected from 20 ruminal epithelial specimens, including 1642 positive ion mode metabolites and 1464 negative ion mode metabolites. The results of PLS-DA analysis demonstrated that different concentrations of GASe induced differences in intergroup metabolites, regardless of the ion mode (Fig. S3). Compared to the CON group, we identified 303 DEMs in the L group (183 upregulated and 120 downregulated) (Fig. S4A), 342 DEMs in the M group (283 upregulated and 59 downregulated) (Fig. S4C), and 247 DEMs in the H group (167 upregulated and 80 downregulated) (Fig. S4E). According to the KEGG enrichment analysis, these DEMs were significantly enriched in pathways such as pyrimidine metabolism, nucleotide metabolism, purine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) signaling pathway, fat digestion and absorption, and arachidonic acid metabolism.

To comprehensively understand the effects of supplementing GASe on rumen epithelial metabolism, we used WGCNA to identify metabolic regulatory networks and key metabolites associated with target traits. We aimed to determine co-expression gene modules highly correlated with host phenotypic traits. Discussion

Adequate Se supplementation in the diet is crucial for meeting nutritional needs, optimizing health, and enhancing production in animals. The results of this study indicate a significant quadratic relationship between the production performance of goats and the supplementation levels of Se, with the optimal effect observed at a dose of 0.6 mg Se/kg. This finding is consistent with the study by Li et al. (2022). Given that the growth and development of young goats highly depend on the health and rapid maturation of the rumen, we investigated the coordinated adaptive changes between rumen bacteria and the host at different doses of GASe supplementation by examining ruminal epithelial microbiota, rumen epithelial metabolomics, and transcriptomics.

The ruminal epithelial microbiota plays a crucial role in rumen health and function, and its direct correlation with host tissues suggests its indispensable role in ruminant metabolism and host–microbiota interactions (Cheng et al., 1979). Butyrivibrio is one of the major producers of butyrate, which is directly absorbed by the host gastrointestinal mucosa and plays a crucial role in the proliferation and development of rumen epithelial cells (Ma et al., 2021). Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that dietary supplementation with yeast Se could increase the abundance of Butyrivibrio in rumen fluid. In our study, the abundance of Butyrivibrio was positively correlated with rumen papillae growth (Fig. 2E). Previous studies have suggested that the abundance of unclassified_Christensenellaceae, and unclassified_Prevotellaceae may be positively correlated with rumen health and have higher abundance in healthy intestines (Liu et al., 2019; Tang et al., 2024; Waters and Ley, 2019). In this study, the abundance of unclassified_Christensenellaceae and unclassified_Prevotellaceae was positively correlated with rumen epithelial health (Fig. 2E), and interestingly, the abundance of unclassified_Prevotellaceae was positively correlated with energy production and protein synthesis in rumen epithelial cells (Fig. S9). The abundance of Oribacterium decreased significantly in the GASe supplement group. The difference in Oribacterium may be related to the level of oxidative stress and is higher in saliva samples from patients with reflux esophagitis (Wang et al., 2020). Unfortunately, this study found that the relative abundances of Escherichia-Shigella and Campylobacter exhibited a quadratic relationship with GASe supplementation, reaching their highest levels in the H group. Escherichia_Shigella causes diseases by invading the intestinal mucosa of animals and can lead to death in severe cases (Shi et al., 2014), while Campylobacter is a gram-negative microaerophilic bacterium that is commonly associated with human foodborne illnesses and pig diarrhea (Sproston et al., 2011). These studies suggest that low concentrations of GASe can increase the abundance of beneficial bacteria, while higher concentrations of GASe (≥2.4) can increase the abundance of pathogenic bacteria. Additionally, we found that 2 metabolites, actinorhodin and dimethylpropiothetin, which are exclusively derived from rumen microbiota, showed a quadratic relationship with the supplementation of GASe. These metabolites were positively correlated with rumen papillae growth and GPx activity. Previous studies have reported that Dimethylpropiothetin can promote animal growth and enhance the antioxidant capacity of the body (Wang et al., 2014). Actinorhodin, a natural antibiotic produced by Actinobacteria (Fig. S8), benefits animal health (Yang et al., 2023). These findings suggest that rumen epithelial microbiota can modulate host physiological activities by secreting metabolic products, and this process is influenced by the doses of GASe supplementation.

The Se primarily participates in the oxidative-reductive system by regulating the expression of selenoproteins (Zhang et al., 2020). Glutathione peroxidase is a significant antioxidant selenoprotein enzyme that uses glutathione to catalyze the reduction of hydrogen peroxide and hydroperoxides, shielding cells from oxidative damage (Alkie et al., 2020). Glutathione peroxidase concentration is negatively correlated with glutathione (Kasperczyk et al., 2014). A study investigating Se and organ failure found that lower Se levels decrease GPx activity and increase glutathione, indicating reduced cellular capacity to use glutathione as a scavenger (Broman et al., 2018). In our study, GPx concentration showed a quadratic relationship with GASe supplementation, with the highest levels in the L group. This demonstrates that appropriate Se enhances antioxidant capacity, while high concentrations are toxic and cause oxidative stress (Naderi et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2023b). At the metabolomics level, substrates for glutathione synthesis, L-cystine, L-glutamic acid, and gamma-glutamylcysteine, were higher in the L group than in the CON group. Glutathione S-transferase M3 (GSTM3), part of the glutathione S-transferase superfamily, conjugates glutathione with electrophilic compounds inside cells, promoting detoxification and excretion (Chu et al., 2014). Gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT), located on the external membrane, hydrolyzes extracellular glutathione, providing cysteine for recycling, and is vital in regulating oxidative-reductive states. Cysteine is essential for biosynthesis of new intracellular glutathione and proteins. Therefore, GGT upregulation endows cells with greater antioxidant stress resistance and growth advantages (Mitrić and Castellano, 2023). This indicates appropriate Se maintains cellular protection against oxidative stress by regulating glutathione metabolism levels.

Ribosomes, as the sole organelles responsible for protein synthesis, play crucial roles in cells. To meet the increasing demand for protein synthesis during cell growth and proliferation, cells must increase their capacity by upregulating ribosome biogenesis (Orsolic et al., 2016). The number of ribosomes significantly increases in metabolically active cells (Li and Qian, 2017). In our study, genes encoding proteins that constitute the cytoplasmic large (60S; RPL28, RPL31, RPL36, RPL38, and RPLP2) and small (40S; RPP21, RPS15, RPS19, and RPS28) ribosomal subunits were significantly higher in the L group than in the CON group. Adequate GASe supplementation leads to a coherent and integrated response that promotes protein synthesis in rumen epithelial cells. Pagmantidis et al. (2008) demonstrated that ribosomal proteins and translation factor genes are upregulated in response to Se intake, which is consistent with the observed response of ribosomal protein genes to Se supplementation in this study.

Mitochondria is the energy center of cell, producing the majority of ATP required by the cell, and mitochondrial fusion is crucial for maintaining mitochondrial physiological function (Chan, 2020). NME/NM23 nucleoside diphosphate kinase 3 (NME3) has been shown by the same group to localize to mitochondria and physically interact with mitochondrial proteins to promote mitochondrial fusion (Chen et al., 2019). Loss of NME3 leads to mitochondrial fragmentation and oxidative stress, resulting in genomic instability (Chen et al., 2020). Research has shown that NME/NM23 nucleoside diphosphate kinase 4 (NME4), located in the inner mitochondrial membrane, interacts with optic atrophy 1 (OPA1) to stimulate fusion by increasing GTP load (Boissan et al., 2014). Se can promote mitochondrial fusion in vitro and in vivo to alleviate oxidative stress and ferroptosis (Shi et al., 2022), which was also demonstrated by Wang et al. (2023) in the intestinal study of piglets. In our study, NME3 and NME4 were significantly upregulated in the L group.

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) is a key regulator in cellular signaling, essential for energy balance, survival, and DNA repair (Galli et al., 2020). Excessive NAD depletion accelerates metabolic disorders and cell death (Song et al., 2023). In this study, respiratory complex I (CI) genes in the MEturquoise module were highly expressed in the L group (Table S2), producing subunits for oxidative phosphorylation to transfer electrons from NADH to the respiratory chain (Wu et al., 2016). As a result, GASe increases CI activity to supplement the NAD concentration, controlling cell development and homeostasis. Yang et al. (2023) showed that Se supplementation reduces oxidative stress and inflammation by increasing the NAD+ pool, preventing arsenic trioxide-induced cardiotoxicity. Increasing CI activity to supplement NAD concentration is also one of the reasons why β-AMP promotes goat rumen epithelial growth (Zhuang et al., 2023). Coenzyme Q, or ubiquinone (CoQ), is an essential link in the electron transport chain of the mitochondria, carrying electrons from complexes I and II to complex III. Coenzyme Q links mitochondrial electron transport, with its production catalyzed by CoQ7, which restores respiration when overexpressed (Diessl et al., 2022). AMP-activated protein kinase is an energy sensor in cells that is mainly activated when the ATP level decreases or the AMP level increases in cells. AMP-activated protein kinase activation inhibits cell growth and proliferation (Yang et al., 2020), and 5-aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide (AICA) ribonucleotide is considered an AMPK activator (Lin et al., 2020). In our study, the concentration of AICA ribonucleotide was significantly decreased in the L group, and the AMP concentration was significantly decreased in the L and M groups. The increase in ATP concentration is associated with a decrease in ADP and AMP (Dudzinska and Lubkowska, 2021), and the decrease in AMP is also considered a sign of sufficient cellular energy (Ou et al., 2014). This suggests that Se promotes proliferation and antioxidant effects by increasing intracellular ATP content (Yan et al., 2012).

Nucleotides are essential for transcription and DNA replication because they are building blocks for RNA and DNA. Nucleotide metabolism plays a central role in cell proliferation. Many genes and metabolites related to this process are significantly upregulated in the L group (Table S2, Table S3). RNA polymerase II (Pol II; POLR2F, POLR2I, and POLR2L) transcribes all protein-coding genes as well as non-coding RNAs (Schier and Taatjes, 2020). The exosome component 6 (EXOSC6) and like-Sm family genes (LSM3 and LSM7) contribute to RNA stability and function (Wu et al., 2014; Xie et al., 2019). Additionally, 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase 1 (OGG1) and N-glycosylase 1 (NTHL1) are crucial for protecting DNA integrity and maintaining cellular stability (Marsden et al., 2020; Tanner et al., 2023). Deoxyuridine triphosphatase (DUT) catalyzes the hydrolysis of deoxyuridine triphosphate (dUTP) to deoxyuridine monophosphate (dUMP), which is converted to Deoxythymidine triphosphate for DNA synthesis (Passi et al., 2018). Deoxythymidylate kinase (DTYMK) catalyzes the phosphorylation of deoxythymidine monophosphate (dTMP) and ATP to generate thymidine monophosphate (TMP) and ADP, a key step in dTMP regeneration. dTMP is essential for DNA synthesis, so DTYMK plays an important role in DNA synthesis and cell proliferation (Vanoevelen et al., 2022). This indicates that different concentrations of GASe have different effects on nucleotide metabolism.

Conclusions

In summary, our study demonstrates that appropriate GASe supplementation enhances rumen epithelial development and antioxidant capacity by increasing ATP production and facilitating protein and nucleotide synthesis, ultimately improving goat performance. Additionally, moderate GASe promotes epithelial health by enriching beneficial microbes and their metabolites. However, excessive supplementation reduces epithelial metabolism and antioxidative functions, promoting pathogenic bacteria proliferation (Fig. 6).

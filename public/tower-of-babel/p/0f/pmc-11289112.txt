Physical modeling of two-phase liquid–gas processes occurring in the refining ladle for Fe–Si alloy refining process
Authors: Tomasz Merder, Sławomir Kozłowski, Jacek Pieprzyca, Mariola Saternus, Agnieszka Sobianowska-Turek, Ladislav Socha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe article presents the results of research carried out using a water model of a refining ladle for the Fe–Si ferroalloys treatment. These studies were aimed at improving the efficiency of refining and homogenization of liquid Fe–Si ferroalloy in the refining ladle by using a new method of blowing gas through a system of nozzles installed at the bottom of the ladle. The obtained results allowed to determine the proper location of the plug at the bottom of the refining ladle and the possibility of using combined blowing. The tests were carried out for a refining ladle with a capacity of 3 m3 using a physical model on a linear scale of 1:3. The gas flow rate used in the model corresponded proportionally to the value previously used in industrial practice and amounted to 26.8 l/min. Experiments were performed for both combined blowing applications and through a purging plug at the bottom of the ladle. In the case of combined blowing, the volume of the gas stream was divided into two blowing sources (lance and purging plug). As a result of laboratory tests, one of the variants was selected and tested in industrial conditions. These studies confirmed the improvement in the efficiency of refining treatment of the FeSi alloy in terms of reducing the carbon and aluminum content in the alloy.xKeywords: Steel, Ferroalloys, Ferrosilicon, Refining gas, Water models, Physical modellingSubject terms: Engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Ferrosilicon is widely used in metallurgy as a steel deoxidizer and alloying additive, in foundry as a modifier of cast iron and cast steel, and in metal-thermal processes as a reducer of metal oxides from ores. The quality of ferrosilicon is determined by the content of the leading element—silicon, and the low content of impurities. Elements that are particularly undesirable when using this alloy in the production of high-quality special-purpose steels include: aluminum and carbon. The high (over 1.5 wt.%) Al content in FeSi causes secondary contamination of the molten steel and, consequently, the formation of hard Al2O3 inclusions in it, being the cause of overgrowing of the nozzles in the steel ladle and tundish of the CSC (Continuous Steel Casting) device1,2. Moreover, the presence of non-metallic inclusions in the form of brittle Al2O3 oxides in the steel is unfavorable; their significant sizes not only worsen the mechanical properties of the steel by concentrating stresses and reducing impact strength, but also deteriorate the surface quality of products after plastic processing3. Excess carbon introduced from FeSi in alloy steels may lead to the formation of undesirable carbides, causing unfavorable changes in the microstructure and, consequently, a reduction in strength, plastic and chemical properties, in particular corrosion resistance due to the depletion of the peri-carbide zones in the alloy component3,4.

In order to refine the liquid metal from undesirable impurities, the bath is blown with gas or, depending on additional requirements, with appropriately selected gas mixtures. Gas can be introduced into the bath from the bottom through a porous plug placed at the bottom of the ladle, from the top through a lance, or a combination of both methods simultaneously5,6.

This procedure is commonly used in steel metallurgy, where argon is used as the refining gas. The main task of the blown gas in this case is to homogenize the liquid alloy both thermally and chemically, as well as to capture and transport impurities to the slag phase. The efficiency of the process depends on the size and number of gas bubbles in the gas column. This is related to the degree of its dispersion in the melt volume. The greater the number of small bubbles, the greater the efficiency of the process. The size and number of gas bubbles in the metal bath depend on the method of blowing gas into the bath7,8.

The basic parameters are the gas flow rate, the type and design of the porous plug and its location at the bottom of the ladle9,10. The selection of these parameters is always based on a reasonable compromise, which can be obtained based on the knowledge of the hydrodynamic phenomena occurring during blowing, important from the point of view of the optimal course of the tested process11,12.

In the case under consideration, the situation is different than in the case of steel refining, where the flow rate of inert gas (argon) is relatively small. In the FeSi refining process, the need for oxygen should be taken into account in order to oxidize impurities, mainly aluminum and calcium, and then bind their oxides in the slag-forming layer while maintaining process safety. This requires an innovative approach and the use of a different design of ceramic elements through which gas is introduced into the metal bath. A commonly used tool for solving similar problems is physical and numerical modeling13–18. An important feature of this research is the ability to identify certain trends that provide the basis for forecasting the occurrence of specific phenomena, including emergency situations. It should be emphasized, however, that each reactor used for the production of iron alloys is a unique device and has unique features. Therefore, the results of model tests conducted for a specific device, even after certain generalizations, cannot be directly applied to other devices of the same type. However, in broad research practice, they constitute valuable information about the mechanisms of formation and course of hydrodynamic phenomena in specific conditions.

The article presents the results of laboratory tests using a physical water model and preliminary results of industrial tests, taking into account the change in the method of introducing the refining gas stream into the working space of the refining ladle for the production of FeSi75 with increased purity.

This change involved the development of an innovative solution for the purging plug, which used ceramic elements of a different design. The proposed innovative design of the purging plug, not previously used in industrial conditions, is characterized by increased blowing efficiency through the use of a system of nozzles enabling sufficient dispersion of bubbles of the refining gas mixture throughout the entire volume of liquid FeSi in order to remove aluminum, calcium and carbon from the alloy.

Two variants of purging plugs with different nozzle configurations were analyzed in model tests. Various configurations of the location of the purging plug in the bottom of the ladle and the impact of using a combined blow-through (lance + purging plug) were also analyzed.

The research was supplemented and the solution used was verified by industrial tests conducted at the Re Alloys Sp. z.o.o.

Outline of the research problem

The research problem was to develop a technology for secondary treatment of ferro-silicon by using a new method of introducing refining gases into the bath (an innovative solution of a purging plug replacing a lance) in order to obtain an alloy with a minimum content of Si—75 wt.%, Al content—max. 0.5 wt.% and C content maximum 0.02 wt.%. The analysis of equilibrium conditions in the cooling process of liquid ferrosilicon in the temperature range from 1650 to 1350 °C shows that the solubility of carbon in the liquid alloy does not exceed 0.02 wt.% at 1650 °C and decreases with a decrease in temperature to approximately 0.004 wt.% at 1400 °C. During cooling, carbon is separated from the liquid phase in the form of solid silicon carbide inclusions. Over a wide temperature range, the equilibrium fraction of the silicon carbide phase is approximately 0.5%. Reducing the equilibrium share of the carbide phase can be achieved by blowing the metal bath with an argon-oxygen gas mixture. As a result of the reaction of oxygen with silicon, silica slag is formed, and additionally, the movement of gas can facilitate the removal of carbide inclusions from the metal bath. In this process, aluminum is also oxidized and, in the form of inclusions, it reaches the metal-slag interface. Therefore, in order to increase the efficiency of the refining process and increase the metallurgical purity of the FeSi75 melt, it was decided to change the method of introducing the refining gases stream into the working space of the ladle.

When designing a new technical solution for secondary treatment of liquid FeSi75 in refining ladle, the method of blowing liquid steel using a purging plug used during steel refining at the ladle station (ladle furnace) was based23,24. This solution should result in a better flow of refining gases, which will result in faster entrainment of SiC particles. The use of a purging plug will also enable blowing of the metal when pouring the first portions of liquid FeSi75 into the refining ladle, which will improve the efficiency of using of the blown gas and the effectiveness of the refining process.

Taking into account the fact that in the designed solution the gas flow rate is significantly higher than that typically used in steel industry, a necessary review of the literature on the use of various types of purging plugs was carried out25–27. Based on our own experience in testing various types of purging plugs28 and taking into account the technical and technological capabilities of the company coordinating the research (ferroalloy smelter Re Alloys Sp. z. o. o.), the decision was made to replace the porous core or slots of the standard purging plug with a set of nozzles. This solution is also justified by the possibility for the company to produce the purging plug itself, thus become independent from external supplies of purging plugs.

The application of such radical changes in industrial practice requires a number of studies, which is why a physical modeling technique using the water model of refining ladle was chosen for this purpose. Their aim is to confirm the validity of the assumptions made and to determine the possibilities of improving the previously used refining technique.

In this case, the aim is to identify the mechanism of formation of the structure of liquid metal movement in the ladle under the influence of the blown gas stream, to examine the influence of the position of the purging plug at the bottom of the ladle on this mechanism and to determine the most favorable value of the blown gas flow stream. The latter parameter significantly affects the mechanism of gas column formation, the number and size of gas bubbles formed and, consequently, the degree of gas dispersion in the bath. At this stage of research, it is also possible to determine the most advantageous design of the purging plug and the arrangement of individual nozzles in it.

Object of the research study

The research was carried out using a simplified model of a refining ladle with a nominal capacity of 3 m3 of liquid metal used at ferroalloys plant Re Alloys Sp. z.o.o. A view of an industrial refining ladle is shown in Fig. 4.

(a) View of an industrial refining ladle, (b) Diagram of a refining ladle with a gas lance: where 1—ladle cover, 2—refining ladle, 3—gas lance in a thermal shield, 4—gas lance head, 5—refining gas stream, 6—refining gas.

The simplification of the model in relation to the industrial refining ladle consists in not taking into account downward convergence in its design. This simplification allowed for a significant reduction in the cost of the model and its development time. The decision to use this simplification was also made due to the low value of this convergence and the small impact on the studied phenomena. While this convergence affects some thermal phenomena in the refining ladle, its impact on hydrodynamic phenomena is small.

The model was made on a linear reduction scale of 1:3 in accordance with the requirements of similarity theory described previously29,30. The kinematic viscosity of water at 25 °C is 1.002·10–6 m2/s, while the kinematic viscosity of the FeSi75 alloy at 1400 °C is 0.52·10–6 m2/s. These values are very similar and on this basis additional fluidizers or thickeners cannot be used for the simulant. The ladle model allows for simultaneous blowing of the bath through one and/or two purging plugs installed in the bottom and additional support from the top with a lance. The tracer was introduced into the system in impulses (Dirac function). In each variant of the experiment, it was administered in the same amount (100 ml) and in the same place. In the case of quantitative tests, the tracer was saturated with NaCl solution, while in qualitative tests, an aqueous KMnO4 solution was used. The measurement points (Fig. 5) (quantitative tests) were designated to be on the opposite side of the ladle symmetry plane from the point of tracer introduction. Their location allows for both sinusoidal and parabolic tracer transport trends to be taken into account.

In the case of visualization tests, the course of experiments was recorded using a camera. The obtained results in the form of video material allowed for full identification of the movement of the model liquid (tracer flow) in the volume of the ladle and the mechanism of behavior of the injected gas bubbles in it. In the case of quantitative tests, the change in the conductivity of the model liquid under the influence of the introduced tracer was recorded using conductivity meters placed in the working space of the refining ladle model. The analysis of the experimental results consisted in determining the optimal conditions for refining the FeSi alloy from the point of view of the hydrodynamics of the process.

Results and discussion

Examples of test results illustrating the mechanism of behavior of gas bubbles in the model liquid for the tested variants are presented in Fig. 7.

The mechanism of behavior of gas bubbles in the model liquid for experimental variants.

The mechanism of behavior of gas bubbles in the model liquid for the E0 variant (currently used in industry—introducing gas into the metal bath through lances) shown in Fig. 7 is characterized by the fact that the bubbles flowing from the lance, due to the high intensity of the gas stream, combine into large conglomerates and, flowing around the side surface of the lance, rise towards the model liquid surface. A tendency for these bubbles to form a spiral path was observed. This is an unfavorable phenomenon due to the durability of the gas lance immersed in liquid metal in industrial conditions. Such a high flow rate of the gas stream favors the increase in the turbulent flow of the model liquid in the volume of the ladle due to its high energy, and consequently favors the homogenization process. On the other hand, the expansion of turbulent flow areas significantly limits the refining capacity of the process.

The test results for the E0 variant served as a reference point for analyzing the behavior of gas bubbles in the model liquid in the remaining variants of the experiment.

In the E1 and F1 variants, the interaction of gas streams flowing from the plug and the gas lance was observed. This effect limits the size of bubbles flowing towards the model liquid surface and significantly reduces the tendency to swirl around the lance walls. It should be noted, however, that the degree of fragmentation of gas bubbles in the model liquid compared to the E0 variant has improved as a result of dividing the gas stream value into two sources. The gas column still covers the area around the vertical axis of the refining ladle, but its height has increased by also covering the lower part of the ladle under the gas lance face. This resulted in a slight reduction in the waving of the metal mirror.

Moving the position of the purging plug from the axis of the ladle towards its wall, variants E2 and F2, allowed to avoid the interaction of gas streams flowing from it with the stream flowing from the gas lance. As a result, two columns of gas are formed. Due to the lower value of the gas stream compared to the E0 variant, the column generated by the lance is characterized by greater fragmentation of gas bubbles. A reduction in the tendency of the column to rotate around the lance was also observed. The degree of gas dispersion also increased due to the coverage of the area above the purging plug. The waving of the model liquid mirror was also significantly reduced. The photos (Fig. 7) give the impression that the waving of the metal bath mirror in the case of the E0 variant is smaller than in the other cases. Due to the formation of large agglomerates of gas bubbles in the case of the E0 variant, they cause the risk of liquid metal splashing out of the refining ladle. Opening large gas bubbles on the metal surface may also encourage slag to be drawn into the bath and cause secondary contamination of the alloy. These phenomena occur cyclically and in an increasing extent. In the remaining variants, the waving of the bath mirror is uniform and fixed. Taking into account the slag layer on its surface, it can be additionally softened.

In the E3, E4 and F3, F4 variants, a classic way of making a gas column was found. As for the behavior of gas bubbles flowing from the purging plug, the formation of large gas bubbles already in the outflow zone and the formation of a gas column expanding towards the model liquid mirror were observed. This phenomenon is caused by the growth of gas bubbles as the pressure of the model liquid column on their surface decreases. Due to the increased height of the gas column compared to the E0 variant, the waving of the model liquid mirror were slightly reduced.

When comparing the experimental variants for purging plug E and F with the previously used technique of blowing gas through the lance from the top of the ladle, represented by the E0 variant, an improvement in the hydrodynamic conditions in the refining ladle should be noted. However, deciding on the suitability for practical use between specific variants E and F is risky and at this stage of research it is not possible to clearly demonstrate the optimal variant from the point of view of the ongoing process.

Additional information on the influence of the injected gas in individual experimental variants on the movement of the model liquid in the volume of the refining ladle was provided by tests using a tracer in the form of an aqueous KMnO4 solution. Examples of the results of these tests are presented in Fig. 8. As a result of visualization research, the results obtained can be divided into two groups.

Visualization of the movement of the model liquid in the ladle volume for the variants.

The first group includes cases of combined blowing using a lance from the top and a plug through the bottom of the ladle (variants E1, E2 and F1, F2). Comparing these variants with the E0 variant, no major differences in the tracer dispersion in the model liquid were observed. However, an unfavorable phenomenon was observed in the case of the E2 and F2 variants. Moving the plug off the vertical axis of the ladle raised the tracer above the plug towards the model liquid mirror. The gas column created by the lance is a kind of curtain that makes it difficult to evenly mix the tracer in the entire volume of the model liquid. This phenomenon delays the homogenization process and may cause the formation of dead flow zones in the zone of the ladle on the opposite side of its central axis. More favorable mixing conditions were observed for the E1 variant, in which the plug are placed coaxially with the gas lance. Although the gas curtain effect also occurs, it is limited only to the immersion depth of the gas lance. Intensive mixing of the tracer was observed in the zone of interaction of the two gas streams, which favors its mixing with the model liquid in the lower zone of the ladle. This fact significantly reduces dead zones in the volume of the model liquid. To sum up, it can be stated that from the point of view of mixing the tracer in the volume of the refining ladle as a result of the use of combined blowing from the bottom and from the top, the E1 variant is the most advantageous.

The second group consists of E3, E4 and F3, F4 variants, in which the gas was blown only through a plug placed at the bottom of the refining ladle. Due to the lack of the gas curtain effect in these variants, a more even mixing of the tracer was observed in the entire volume of the model liquid. In the case of the E3 variant, the formation of ascending circulation along the axis of the ladle and descending circulation near its walls was observed. However, compared to the E0 variant, this process takes place in a larger volume of the ladle, including its bottom zone. If the purging plug is located at the bottom outside the axis of the ladle (variant E4 and F4) the hydrodynamic conditions are similar to those in the case of variant E3 and F3. However, as expected, they cover a larger volume of the ladle. The tracer rises with the model liquid towards the mirror along the gas column and falls in the area of the opposite wall of the ladle. To sum up, it can be stated that in this group of experimental variants, the E4 and F4 variant is the most advantageous solution.

The visualization tests carried out allow for the selection of a group of variants that are most advantageous in terms of creating hydrodynamic conditions conducive to effective homogenization of the model liquid. These are variants of groups F3, F4 and E3, E4. However, it should be emphasized that the differences between them are small, which is why the next stage of research was carried out.

Further tests were carried out to determine the value of the minimum mixing time of the tracer in the model liquid, which is a criterion for assessing the effectiveness of the homogenization process of the refined alloy in a ladle. They consisted in measuring changes in the conductivity of the model liquid during the experiment under the influence of the introduced tracer in the form of an aqueous NaCl solution. Changes in conductivity were treated as an analogy of the tracer concentration in the model liquid, and on this basis, mixing curves were determined for each variant. Based on the analysis of these curves, the values of the minimum mixing time were determined. To objectively compare the results of individual experiments, the measured tracer concentration values were transformed into a dimensionless form35,36. Typically, mixing is considered complete when the bath is 95% homogenized, which corresponds to Cb (0.95; 1.05). A value of Cb = 1 means that the tracer is completely mixed with the model liquid. By taking a specific concentration value as the required degree of mixing, the time after which this value will be achieved can be experimentally determined. This value is called the minimum mixing time and can be treated as an analogy to the mixing time of the metal bath. An example dimensionless mixing curve is shown in Fig. 9. Unequivocally the determined minimum mixing times for all experimental variants (Table 3) allow the conclusion that the use of a constructed purging plug placed at the bottom of the refining ladle significantly improves the efficiency of the process of blowing the metal bath with Ar + air + O2 gas mixture. It was therefore concluded that it is justified to carry out industrial trials.

A detailed analysis of the test results (Table 3) shows that the longest mixing time of the tracer in the model liquid was recorded for the variant with a lance—E0. In mixed variants of introducing gas into the metal bath (lance + purging plug), similar mixing times are recorded for both types of purging plugs. Identical times were also observed for the mixed variant: lance + purging plug moved relative to the ladle axis (E2 and F2). This fact suggests that the construction of purging plug is not important in the variant with the simultaneous use of a lance.

Variants using only the purging plug show a reduction in mixing time depending on the design of the purging plug and its installation location. Slightly more favorable results are obtained with the use of purging plugs F. Analyzing all the results presented in Table 3, it can be concluded that the best results at this stage of the research are obtained for the purging plug type F and its location in the bottom of the ladle—purging plug in the axis of the ladle (i.e.—variant of experiment F3). The degree of gas dispersion in the metal bath has a significant impact on the mixing time, but it is not the only factor. The best refining conditions are usually achieved through a compromise that takes into account many factors. In this case, the energy of the gas stream also plays an important role in terms of mixing time. It is much larger than in standard solutions used in steel metallurgy. The gas flow rate is much higher because it takes into account the additional function of ensuring the effective course of oxidation reactions occurring during refining. This has a significant impact on the mechanism of gas bubble formation, their size and number, and consequently on the dispersion in the bath.

Industrial tests

As mentioned earlier, the choice of a specific solution is always made as a compromise between the expected technological benefits, technical and organizational capabilities of the plant and the economic calculation of the project. Therefore, at Ferroalloys Plant Re Alloys Ltd, after analyzing the above conditions and as part of the work carried out aimed at optimizing and increasing the efficiency and innovation of production37–41, it was decided to carry out technological tests on an industrial scale based on tests carried out in laboratory conditions in the F type purging plug variant, which was selected as the most advantageous.

Therefore, a prototype of an industrial F-type purging plug equipped with the designed nozzles was made. The arrangement of the individual nozzles corresponds to Fig. 6b. All dimensions have been scaled to real values. The diameter of a single nozzle was 0.003 m. In order to easily embed the purging plug in the bottom of the ladle, the nozzle system was placed in a concrete block with dimensions of 0.3 × 0.3 m. View of the ladle with the installed plug is shown in Fig. 10b. To conduct industrial tests, a station for the refining process was also built, the view of which is shown in Fig. 10c.

For industrial research, the location of the purging plug was chosen in the axis of the bottom of the ladle. The choice of this solution was another compromise between the expected technological benefits and the company's technical capabilities. The method of mounting the purging plug in the axis of the object (see Fig. 10b) was a relatively simple task to perform in industrial conditions without significantly changing the structure of the object.

In order to compare the effectiveness of the proposed solution of gas blowing from the bottom through a purging plug with the existing technology of gas blowing from the top through a lance, industrial tests of both these methods of gas introduction were carried out. As part of industrial tests of experimental variants, a mixture of gases in various proportions—air + O2—was used to blow through the metal bath. This decision was made to answer the question whether the composition of the gas mixture affects the refining process. It should be emphasized that at this stage the experiments carried out are of a pilot nature, the results of which will determine whether the proposed solutions can be implemented in the production conditions of Re Alloys Sp. z o. o. ferroalloy smelter. If so, this will be a reason to decide to rebuild the structure of the refining vessel to introduce further changes.

As part of industrial experiments for both variants (lance or purging plug), technological tests were carried out in two furnace units with transformer power of 20 MVA each, at a station for decarburization and refining of the liquid FeSi alloy. Due to the safety of industrial works, mainly related to the ripples of the liquid metal surface, the flow rate of the refining gas mixture was reduced compared to the flow rate assumed in model tests. For all variants it was 416 l/min (25 Nm3/h). The refining time determines the final content of impurities such as Al, Ca and C in FeSi, and as a result of technological tests it was observed that the largest decrease in the C content in the alloy occurs during the first 10 min of refining, therefore longer refining operations are pointless because they do not lead to a further reduction in the C content in FeSi75, but they contribute to increased losses as a result of the overgrowth of the working space of the refining ladle with metal crystallizing at the phase boundary. During oxygen refining, a systematic decrease in the Al and Ca content was observed over time, so it can be concluded that extending the refining time will have a beneficial effect on minimizing the content of impurities in FeSi75.

During technological tests, it was observed that, unlike in the case of aluminum, the composition of the gas mixture had no effect on reducing the carbon content in the alloy (Fig. 12). In the case of coal, a higher carbon content after refining appears in variant 3, this may be due to the fact that when blowing, the bath splashes over the hard shell and binds with amorphous dust, which may cause it to detach from the wall, resulting in secondary carburization of the bath. The removal of SiC from the metal bath depends on the blowing time, which is determined by the temperature conditions, and the dispersion of gas bubbles facilitating the flow of SiC, characterized by a slight difference in specific density between FeSi75% of ~ 3.185 g/cm3 and SiC—3.13 g/cm320. A positive effect of blowing the metal bath with a gas stream through a purging plug installed in the bottom was observed on the intensification of the outflow of silicon carbide particles—SiC (which is the main carbon carrier in the metal bath) towards the metal mirror to the metal-slag interface and to the metal-refractory lining interface of the ladle, where adsorption of SiC particles took place.

It is also noticed that the use of a new design—blowing a stream of refining gases through a purging plug mounted at the bottom of the refining ladle resulted in a better (deeper) aluminum removal from the melt than in the case of blowing it from the top with an immersion lance. However, the introduction of gas through the lance disrupted the silicon carbide adsorption processes, which resulted in a higher final C content in the melt. A gradual build-up of the solidified slag layer on the metal surface was also observed, resulting in the closing of the slag eye. This phenomenon is shown in Fig. 13.

View of: (a) metal surface during refining, (b) remaining slag after refining in the refining ladle.

The formation of a slag layer (see Fig. 13) is, on the one hand, beneficial because it limits the heat loss of the metal bath in the refining ladle and constitutes an adsorption layer for the outflowing silicon carbides and aluminum and calcium oxides, but on the other hand, when blowing the metal bath due to the limited surface of the slag eye, the gas bubbles also transport the metal to the solidified upper slag layer, where it crystallizes, thus increasing its losses.

To limit the effects of this unfavorable phenomenon, part of the solidified slag containing metal, after collecting an appropriate amount and grinding, is directed to the recovery process carried out in the re-melting furnace.

Preliminary industrial research has shown that the effectiveness of refining the FeSi75 alloy carried out using the proposed technique (using a purging plug), compared to the current blowing of the metal bath with an immersion lance, contributed to reducing the aluminum content in the alloy by 45% compared to 30%, and C by 75% compared to 69%. Moreover, it was noticed that the service life of the blades during technological tests was almost twice as long as before.

The problem, however, is the time-consuming nature of replacing the purging plug at the bottom of the ladle, which will be the subject of further work. If the design of the purging plug and the method of its installation at the bottom of the refining ladle are changed, the use of bottom blowing (the new proposed solution) is fully justified due to the reduction in the concentration of impurities in the melt and should be continued.

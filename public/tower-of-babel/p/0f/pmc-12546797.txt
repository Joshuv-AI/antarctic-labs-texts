Methodology for creating a novel cardiac arrest registry using the Trinetx electronic health record database
Authors: Ryan Huebinger, Ryan A Coute, Larissa Myaskovsky, Cameron Crandall, Ethan Abbott, Keith E Kocher, Aditya C Shekhar, Benjamin S Abella
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCare in the hospital, post-arrest care, is a crucial component of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) management, but current OHCA databases contain limited post-arrest care data. To address this, we present the methodology for assembling a multicenter, real-world post-arrest care research registry using the Trinetx database.MethodsWe queried the Trinetx research database (01/01/2000–02/19/2025), a federated database of electronic health record data from over 100 healthcare organisations in the US and internationally, to identify OHCAs from ICD codes for cardiac arrest related to emergency department (ED) visits. To define our cohort of patients eligible to receive post-arrest care, we identified OHCAs that survived to admission based on having a temporally associated inpatient encounter (based on encounter type/Current Procedure Terminology codes) or an ED visit lasting >24 h. We defined survival to discharge as 1) having an encounter after the hospitalisation and 2) having a death date after the end of the hospitalisation. We report patient characteristics and the number of clinical variables available in each data table for the cohort.ResultsWe identified 222,868 OHCAs and included 88,753 patients (39.8%) who survived to admission. The median age was 65, 60.3% were male, and 59.8% were White. Survival to discharge rate was 48.5%. The database contained 188,038,385 clinical data points: 40,868,707 vital signs, 52,145,594 labs, 17,781,035 procedures, 72,575,389 medication administrations, and 4,667,660 diagnoses.ConclusionUsing Trinetx real-world data, it is possible to create a multicentre, OHCA post-arrest care database with a substantial number of clinical variables, enabling novel post-arrest care research.Keywords: Cardiac arrest, Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, Post-arrest care, Inpatient, Registry

Introduction

Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) is a leading cause of death worldwide.1 OHCA outcomes depend upon the effective deployment of complex care, including post-arrest care in the hospital.2, 3 However, currently available OHCA registries4, 5, 6 have very few post-arrest care variables, limiting the scope of research. Claims data7, 8 have been used to expand post-arrest care research, but these databases are limited to billable procedures. Alternatively, researchers could use multicenter real-world, healthcare data that is already routinely collected from sources like the electronic health record. Real-world databases contain a wealth of important variables unavailable in current databases (e.g. labs, imaging, and medications) and could fill the vital lacuna in OHCA post-arrest care research. We sought to develop and disseminate a methodology for creating a real-world data post-arrest care database for admitted OHCA patients using the Trinetx database.

Methods

Data source

We conducted a retrospective cohort study of OHCAs that survived to admission using the Trinetx research network database. Trinetx was founded in 2013 and is a network of over 100 healthcare organisations in the US and internationally that provide Trinetx with federated (formatted in a manner that can be combined) electronic health record data. Trinetx harmonizes these data to create a unified, longitudinal database with over 100 million patients. Healthcare organisations contributing data (at no cost) and industry partners (for a fee) are able to create study cohorts based on diagnoses, visit types, procedures, and other clinical parameters to evaluate research study feasibility and conduct retrospective research9; The deidentified data for these cohorts are then made available for download and research purposes. The University of New Mexico Institutional Review Board approved this study (ID 24-307).

Study population

We obtained the Trinetx data for patients with a cardiac arrest diagnosis in any setting from 01/01/2000 to 02/19/2025. To define the study population, we used a similar approach to prior OHCA claims database research.10, 11, 12, 13, 14 We identified the first emergency department (ED; where OHCAs are first cared for in the US) visit for each patient with a diagnosis for cardiac arrest, and defined this as the OHCA ED encounter (Appendix 1).

To define the study cohort of OHCA patients who survived to admission and could receive post-arrest care, we identified OHCAs with an associated inpatient encounter. Trinetx does not link encounters (e.g. ED to associated inpatient encounter), so we first identified inpatient type encounters and “UNKNOWN” encounter types with inpatient Current Procedure Terminology codes (Appendix 2) that occurred during the OHCA ED encounter or within 5 days of the OHCA start. Second, to capture ED visits that failed to convert to an inpatient type, we identified OHCA ED encounters that were at least 24 h long (>1 day). It is possible that some of these patients were never admitted due to boarding, but by surviving for 24 h, these patients could receive post-arrest care.

We defined the “OHCA visit” (ED and associated inpatient encounters) start-date as the start-date of the OHCA ED encounter; to define the end-date, we used the latest end-date for the index inpatient visit. We then calculated the OHCA visit length. To capture any possible care that occurred during the OHCA visit, we defined OHCA hospital care as any data linked to an encounter occurring during the time frame of the OHCA visit (Fig. 1).

Measures

Patient characteristics included age (calculated from birth year and OHCA year), sex, race, ethnicity, and marital status. We also defined other pertinent variables based on Trinetx data tables for laboratory results, vital signs, procedures, medications, and diagnoses occurring during the OHCA visit.

Outcomes

As Trinetx does not contain encounter-specific dispositions (e.g., discharged, deceased), we used several variables to define survival to discharge and 30-day survival. First, Trinetx contains death data coded as the month following the death, with the exact day obfuscated for confidentiality. To use this as an indicator of survival to discharge, we defined survival to discharge as when the death month occurred >1 month after the OHCA visit ended. Also, we defined survival to discharge if the patient had a subsequent ambulatory or hospital encounter (excluding “UNKNOWN”, laboratory, and pharmacy visits) that occurred after the end date of the OHCA encounter. We defined 30-day survival if the patient had a death month that was ≥2 months after the start of the ED cardiac arrest (as this would indicate that the patient survived at least 1 full month), an OHCA visit that lasted ≥ 30 days, or an ambulatory or hospital encounter starting ≥30 days after the start of the ED cardiac arrest.

Statistical analysis

We report the proportion of patients with a cardiac arrest diagnosis who were a OHCA and those who subsequently survived to admission. We report patient and visit characteristics and the proportion of patients who survived to discharge and survived to 30 days. Lastly, we determined the number of data points for other relevant variables in Trinetx for the cohort. For each Trinetx data table (e.g., medications, labs, procedures), we identified data occurring during the OHCA visit up to 30 days after the encounter started. For each data table, we then calculated the total number of data points in addition to the median and IQR for the number of data points for each patient.

Data curation was conducted using Stata version 18, StataCorp, College Station, TX.

Results

Trinetx was queried on 02/19/2025 (Fig. 1). Over 100 healthcare organisations provided data on 469,265 patients with a cardiac arrest diagnosis; 222,868 (47.5 %) were identified as an OHCA, and we included 88,753 (39.8 %) that survived to admission. Discussion

In the Trinetx database, we identified over 80,000 OHCAs that survived to hospital admission with a large number of clinical variables available for study. Post-arrest care is a vital component of OHCA care.2, 3 However, databases such as the Cardiac Arrest Registry to Enhance Survival (CARES),4 Pan Asian Resuscitation Outcome Study,6 and European Registry of Cardiac Arrest5 are limited in their collection of data pertaining to targeted temperature management, percutaneous coronary interventions, and do-not-resuscitate orders. This substantially limits the study of key care metrics that influence outcomes.

Using real-world data, it is possible to perform novel, granular studies of post-arrest care across a wide range of hospital types. These could include studies of many of the European Society of Cardiology key domains of quality hospital care: initial examination of the cardiac arrest cause, hemodynamic management, neurologic prognostication, in addition to other important post-arrest care modalities.2 Further, we have found that race/ethnicity disparities may exist due to differences in hospital care,15, 16 and real-world data could allow for more thorough analyses of post-arrest care disparities.

An accurate OHCA case definition is important, and prior studies have found that using ICD-10 codes to identify OHCA has a sensitivity of nearly 90 % and a specificity of close to 100 %.17 Comparing Trinetx to the 2013–2023 CARES Utstein report, Trinetx had slightly lower survival to admission (39.8 %) than CARES (43.7 % of those surviving to the ED).18 Conversely, the survival to discharge in our study (48.5 %) was somewhat higher than the CARES Utstein report (37.4 %). However, prior studies have found that hospitals with high OHCA volumes have higher survival-to-discharge rates (aOR 1.36, [1.08–1.73]).19 The majority of healthcare organisations in Trinetx Research Network are academic (55/105), which could explain the higher survival-to-discharge rate. Future validation of the dataset would benefit by comparing other hospital care modalities between Trinetx and other databases.

Limitations

Due to the heterogeneity of healthcare data, there are patients who have limited data in particular domains (vital signs – 12,789 patients missing data). These data are assumed to be missing rather than not recorded, as it would be unlikely for a patient to be admitted to the hospital without vital signs recorded. There are also limited cases from earlier years in Trinetx as Trintex has grown over time,20 and it is not known what population the Trinetx database represents. Additionally, if patients were transferred from the ED to a hospital outside the healthcare organisation, these cases would have been excluded. Our definition using ICD-10 codes may miss cardiac arrest and may include cardiac arrests that occurred in the ED rather than out-of-hospital; this is a commonly practiced technique with claims data research, though. Including cardiac arrests that occurred in the ED may explain the higher survival rate in Trinetx. There are also likely OHCAs that are missing due to being coded as “Unknown” encounter types and not having an ED procedure code, but we chose to exclude these to ensure cohort accuracy. Lastly, we chose a surrogate survival outcome to limit falsely defining survival, but the accuracy of this is unknown.

Conclusion

The Trinetx real-world data research database contains data on over 88,000 OHCAs from January 2000 to February 2025 that survived to hospital admission. With over 180 million clinical data points, Trinetx could enable novel, multicentre studies of post-arrest care.

Description of methods for identifying ED cardiac arrests

Similarly to OHCA claims data studies, we identified emergency department (ED) visits (visit type of “EMER” or “UNKNOWN” encounter types with emergency medicine Current Procedural Terminology codes) with a cardiac arrest diagnosis. (Appendix 2) To avoid visit misclassification from diagnoses being carried forward from prior visits, we identified the first ED visit with a diagnosis of cardiac arrest for each patient and defined this as the index OHCA ED encounter. If there were duplicate ED OHCA for a patient on the same day, the encounter with the longest time frame took precedence in case OHCA encounter failed to transfer to an inpatient encounter type; the index ED OHCA would then be considered the index inpatient encounter (described further in the methods).

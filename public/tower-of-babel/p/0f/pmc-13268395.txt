Comprehensive Analysis of IgA Nephropathy Causal Factors in Plasma Proteins, Immune Cell Types, and Immune Cell Traits
Authors: Rui-lian You, Zhi-ying Liu, Meng-shi Li, Yang Li, Xin Wang, Xu-jie Zhou, Hong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Visual Abstract

Introduction

IgA nephropathy, characterized by mesangial deposition of IgA immune complexes, accounts for 20%–40% of ESKD cases within two decades of diagnosis.1,2 Despite advances in understanding its pathogenesis, the current “four-hit” hypothesis fails to fully explain individual variability, disease severity, and other molecular mechanisms.3–8 These gaps underscore the need for further exploration of immune components and their causal roles in IgA nephropathy.

Emerging evidence highlights the involvement of immune cells and plasma proteins in IgA nephropathy pathogenesis. For example, elevated levels of cluster of differentiation (CD) 14+Tim-3+ monocytes and CD38+B cells have been observed in IgA nephropathy patients, alongside dysregulated T-cell subsets, such as Th2 and Th17 cells.9,10 Genetic studies have also implicated complement-related proteins such as complement factor H (CFH) and CFH-related protein 1 (CFHR1) were critically involved in disease progression.11–13 Higher urinary CFH level and serologic complement pathway activation have been validated in IgA nephropathy patients.14 However, the precise causal relationships between these factors and IgA nephropathy remain unclear.

Mendelian randomization phenome-wide association study (MR-pheWAS) is a novel method to identify multicausal factors of disease. It has been used in multiple diseases to explore and validate some treatment target.15,16 Previous study performed the MR-pheWAS and found that nine proteins are casually associated with the IgA nephropathy in European and two proteins in Asian population.17 Tang et al. identified four immunophenotypes that exert risk effects for IgA nephropathy including CD25hi among T cells.18 Building on these pioneering contributions, we conducted a comprehensive MR-pheWAS using different data source of plasma proteins and incorporating newly released East Asian protein quantitative trait loci (pQTLs) to identify potential causal factors among plasma proteins, circulating immune cell types, and immune cell traits. Combining open single-cell ribonucleic acid sequence (single-cell RNA sequencing [scRNA-seq]) data and experiment validation, we identified and validated some causal factors of IgA nephropathy.

Methods

Study Design

Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis is based on three assumptions: (1) the instrumental variables (IVs), namely, genetic variants are closely related to exposure; (2) the IVs aren't associated with other potential confounders; and (3) the IVs can only influence the outcome through the exposure.19 In this study, we take the plasma proteins, circulating immune cell types, and immune cell traits as exposures to identify potential causal factors of IgA nephropathy (Figure 1).

IV, instrumental variable; LD, linkage disequilibrium; MR, Mendelian randomization; MR-pheWAS, Mendelian randomization phenome-wide association study; PPI, protein-protein interaction; pQTLs, protein quantitative trait loci; scRNA-seq, single-cell RNA sequencing; SNP, single-nucleotide polymorphism.

Data Source of Plasma Proteins, Immune Cell Types, and Immune Cell Traits

We extracted pQTL data from published genome-wide association study (GWAS) of European populations based on the following criteria: measurement platform, sample size and ancestry, quality control and documentation, and overall study quality (Supplemental Notes). Five published (Sun et al.; Suhre et al.; Folkersen et al.; Yao et al.; Emilsson et al.) and China Kadoorie Biobank representing the Asian population were selected for further analyzing20–25 (Supplemental Table 1). After mapping single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) to genome build GRCh37.p13 coordinates, we selected the IVs according to the following criteria: (1) SNPs were associated with any protein P ≤ 5×10−8 in a GWAS of both cis pQTLs, (2) we removed SNPs and proteins coded for by genes within the MHC region, (3) we applied r2<0.001 as the threshold to exclude dependent pQTLs avoiding colinearity between SNPs, (4) we selected F statistics over ten which reveals a strong correlation between IVs and exposure,26 (5) instruments passed the heterogeneity test across five studies, (6) instruments that passed the consistency test, (7) colocalization analysis of instruments across five studies, and (8) remained robust to vertical pleiotropy testing (Supplemental Notes).

Summary-level data on six circulating immune cells (white blood cell, lymphocyte, monocyte, neutrophil, eosinophil, and basophil count) were obtained from large-scale GWAS conducted by the Blood Cell Consortium, which includes 563,085 European ancestry participants.27 Flow cytometric profiles of 11 lymphocyte subsets were acquired from the Sardinian GWAS with sample size 3757.28 The 731 immune cell traits consist of 539 immune traits (118 absolute cell counts [absolute count (AC)], 389 median fluorescence intensities of surface antigens, and 32 morphologic parameters and 192 relative counts [ratios between cell levels]) from Valeria Orrù's study28 (GWAS Catalog: GCST0001391–GCST0002121).

Data Source of IgA Nephropathy

We selected the latest published IgA nephropathy large-scale GWAS summary study in European ancestry by Kiryluk et al. including 11 cohorts (N=26,734, Ncase=5556) as the discovery population.29 As for the replication population, we applied FinnGen R10 database (N=412,181, Ncase=653), which is a partnership research project in genomics and personalized medicine including 377,277 participants living in Finland.26 The Asian ancestry IgA nephropathy population is acquired form the Kiryluk Lab (N=12,163, Ncase=4590; Supplemental Table 1). Disease information was obtained from the electronic health records.

MR Analysis

To ensure that the effect allele of IVs between different databases corresponds to the same allele. We used allele frequency information inferring the forward strand alleles to harmonize the data and then discarded not inferable palindromic ones or ambiguous IVs.

For single SNP, causal effects on IgA nephropathy were assessed as an odds ratio (OR) corresponding to a one SD increase in the proposed risk factor (OR per SD), with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), calculated using the Wald ratio. When IVs had more than two SNPs, we applied the inverse-variance weighted (method) using a meta-analysis approach to pool Wald ratios. We performed Cochran's Q statistic to assess heterogeneity between individual genetic variants in the inverse-variance weighted (method). A random-effects model was used when the heterogeneity was high. MR-Egger was conducted to assess the pleiotropy. Leave-one-out analyses were conducted to evaluate the influence of individual SNPs for proteins with at least two independent genetic instruments. Bonferroni correction was used to adjust the P value for multiple tests and applied to prioritize the results for further analysis, while a threshold P value of 0.05 was considered as putative positive.

Reverse MR Analysis and Bayesian Colocalization Analysis

We used Steiger filtering to ensure that a genetic variant was the association with the exposure stronger than its relationship with the outcome. Reverse MR analysis was conducted to examine the bidirectional relationships between putative positive proteins and immune factors in primary MR analysis.

Bayesian colocalization analyses were conducted to evaluate the likelihood that two traits share a common causal variant, using the “coloc” package (https://github.com/chr1swallace/coloc) with default settings.30 In this study, we focused on testing the posterior probability of hypothesis 3, where the plasma proteins and IgA nephropathy are associated with the region via different variants, and posterior probability of hypothesis 4, where the plasma proteins and IgA nephropathy share variants associated with the region. We applied the coloc.abf algorithm and a gene were considered to show evidence of colocalization if it achieved a gene-based posterior probability of hypothesis 4 >80% in at least one algorithm.

Pathway Enrichment, Protein-Protein Interaction Network, Drug Interaction Analysis, and Validation in Single-Cell RNA Analysis

We combined all the potential causal proteins acquired from the IgA nephropathy databases and conducted the gene ontology and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes pathway enrichment using Metascape (https://metascape.org/gp/index.html#/main/step1).31 STRING (http://www.string-db.org/) and the Drug Gene Interaction Database version 5.0 (https://www.dgidb.org) were used to constructed the protein-protein interaction network and drug-gene interactions32,33(Supplemental Notes).

We conducted the Single-Cell RNA analysis of the IgA nephropathy exploiting the open dataset GSE127136 and GSE171314 in the gene expression omnibus database.5,34 The samples include kidney biopsies from 13 IgA nephropathy patients and normal kidney cells from paracancerous tissues from six patients with kidney cancer with a total of 2785 cells. Dimensionality reduction was performed using principal component analysis, followed by clustering and visualization with uniform manifold approximation and projection. Differential gene expression analysis was conducted between clusters using the R package Seurat V3.0 to identify key genes driving cell-type–specific characteristics.35 We validated the top three and common proteins of the MR analysis in different clusters.

Validation in Human Plasma Sample

We enrolled the patients diagnosed with IgA nephropathy by kidney biopsies and healthy controls (HC's). The study protocol received ethical approval (Peking University First Hospital IRB#2024Y052). Written informed consent was obtained from each participant. ELISAs for several prioritized proteins were attempted. However, due to technical issues, only iduronidase (IDUA) and pyrin domain containing 1 (PYDC1) yielded reliable results (Supplemental Notes).

Validation In Silico Analysis and Animal Experiment

Expression profiles of ASC, NLRP3, caspase-1, and IL1β in IgA nephropathy were analyzed using the Nephroseq v5 online database (University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, MI). The Reich IgA nephropathy Glomerulus Dataset was selected for the analysis which includes samples from patients with biopsy-proven IgA nephropathy as well as normal living-donor kidney controls.36 Differential expression between IgA nephropathy glomeruli and normal glomerular controls was evaluated using the built-in statistical tools of the platform.

We measured the mRNA expression of PYDC1, caspase1, NLRP3, and IL1β in our research group's established mouse model of IgA nephropathy37 (Supplemental Notes and Supplemental Table 2). All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with protocols approved by the Laboratory Animal Care and Use Committee of Peking University First Hospital (Approval Nos. 201994 and 2021114).

Statistical Analysis

Quantitative variables with a normal distribution are presented as mean±SD. Non-normally distributed quantitative variables are summarized as median and interquartile range. Quantitative data were compared using Student's t test for normally distributed data or a nonparametric test for non-normally distributed variables.

Results

MR Analysis of the Plasma Proteins, Immune Cell Types, and Immune Cell Traits

A total of 797 pQTLs representing 791 plasma proteins were included in the MR analysis of European population and 1081 pQTLs representing 1042 plasma proteins of Asian population (Supplemental Tables 3 and 4). CFH emerged as a significant protective factor against IgA nephropathy (OR, 0.54; 95% CI, 0.45 to 0.65; P = 1.47×10−10). By contrast, CFHR1 (OR, 1.21; 95% CI, 1.12 to 1.31; P = 8.49×10−7) and endoplasmic reticulum aminopeptidase 2 (ERAP2; OR, 1.14; 95% CI, 1.07 to 1.21; P = 7.33×10−6) were identified as risk factors for IgA nephropathy (a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of P < 6.32×10−5) was considered significant). Overall, MR analysis identified suggestive causal associations for 52 plasma proteins with IgA nephropathy, including 32 risk proteins and 20 protective proteins, such as FCGR2A, FCGR2B, and IL7R (Figure 2 and Supplemental Table 5), warranting further replications. Among these potential causal proteins, 45 SNPs passed the Steiger filtering test. As for Asian people, higher level of plasma PYDC1 (OR, 1.82; 95% CI, 1.44 to 2.3; P = 6.3×10−7), DEFA1/DEFA1B (OR, 1.53; 95% CI, 1.26 to 1.85; P = 1.93×10−5) were significantly associated with higher risk of IgA nephropathy. PADI4 (OR, 0.7; 95% CI, 0.61 to 0.81; P = 1.31×10−6) and RNASET2 (OR, 0.7; 95% CI, 0.61 to 0.81; P = 1.31×10−6) were considered as protective proteins for IgA nephropathy (a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of P < 4.80×10−5). A total of 56 proteins were identified as potential causal proteins (Supplemental Table 6). No significant evidence of heterogeneity or pleiotropy was detected among the remaining proteins by the Cochrane's Q and MR-Egger intercept tests, and five proteins demonstrated strong evidence of colocalization.

For circulating immune cell types, MR analysis included 1940 SNPs. It indicated that neutrophils (OR, 1.64; 95% CI, 1.30 to 2.08; P = 3.95×10−5) and white blood cells (OR, 1.47; 95% CI, 1.20 to 1.81; P = 2.32×10−4) were associated with a higher risk of IgA nephropathy (P value of 2.94×10−4 as being statistically significant) (Figure 2 and Supplemental Tables 7 and 8). In the analysis of immune cell traits, we selected 1446 SNPs for MR evaluation and identified 15 immune cell traits causally associated with IgA nephropathy. The five most significant risk traits included activated and secreting regulatory T cells (%CD4+), CD25hi %CD4+, total T-cell AC, CD4+ AC, and central memory CD4+ AC. In addition, a total of 67 immune cell traits were putatively associated with IgA nephropathy risk (Bonferroni-corrected P < 6.84×10−5; Figure 2 and Supplemental Tables 9 and 10).

Replication MR Analysis in the FinnGen Database

In the replication analysis, five proteins (CFH, lectin, mannose binding 2 like [LMAN2L], gamma-glutamyl hydrolase [GGH], FAM20A, and IDUA) were consistently confirmed as causally associated with IgA nephropathy (Figure 3 and Supplemental Table 11). Reverse MR analysis showed no significant reverse causality between IgA nephropathy and plasma proteins (Supplemental Table 12). No circulating immune cell types demonstrated replicable causal associations with IgA nephropathy in the FinnGen database (Supplemental Table 13). Four proteins were identified as common candidate casual molecules by intersecting our findings with those reported by Tang et al.,17 namely, CFH, CD200R1, PON2, and IL1R2 (Figure 3).

Several immune cell traits were validated in the FinnGen cohort, including T-cell AC, lymphocyte AC, CD11 expression on myeloid dendritic cells (DCs), human leukocyte antigen-DR isotype expression on CD33dim HLA−DR+ CD11b− DCs, CD62L− myeloid DC %DC, myeloid DC AC, CD8 expression on effector memory CD8bright T cells, and activated and secreting regulatory T cells (%CD4+). In addition, three immune traits were found to be causally related to IgA nephropathy: CD20 expression on B cells (OR, 5.53; 95% CI, 3.07 to 9.98; P = 1.35×10−8), human leukocyte antigen-DR isotype++ monocyte AC (OR, 3.64; 95% CI, 2.25 to 5.51; P = 5.86×10−8), and CD45RA+ CD8br AC (OR, 4.89; 95% CI, 2.51 to 9.53; P = 3.09×10−6). Overall, a total of 74 immune cell traits were identified as putatively associated with IgA nephropathy through causal inference analysis. Of these, 46 traits were classified as risk factors and 28 as protective factors (Figure 3 and Supplemental Table 14).

Post-MR Analysis

Bayesian colocalization analysis strongly indicated that six proteins (CFH, ERAP2, FAM20A, GGH, IDUA, and LMAN2L) share the same causal genetic variants with IgA nephropathy (Supplemental Figure 1). Pathway enrichment analysis of combined causal proteins highlighted their involvement in key biologic processes, including the inflammatory response, immune effector processes, and the complement and coagulation cascade pathways (Figure 4A).

The protein-protein interaction network constructed from these proteins revealed 69 nodes and 159 edges. Using CytoHubba analysis to identify hub proteins within the network, the top ten hub proteins were determined to be FCGR2A, FCGR2B, CASP3, GZMB, IL1RN, CCL7, CD274, CD8A, IL7R, and IL6R (Figure 4, B and C and Supplemental Table 15). Putative causal proteins from European population were significantly enriched in inflammatory response and complement cascades, while the East Asian set showed enrichment in cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and regulation of lymphocyte activation (Figure 4, D and E).

For drug-gene interactions, several key findings were identified. ERAP2 was associated with five drugs, including esculetin, a phosphoinositide 3-kinase/Akt pathway inhibitor. CFH was linked to eculizumab (a complement C5 inhibitor) and the meningococcal group B vaccine. FCGR2A was found to interact with six drugs, including valziflocept, a recombinant soluble human FcγRIIB (Figure 5 and Supplemental Table 16).

scRNA-Seq Analysis

Clustering analysis of GSE127136 identified nine distinct cell clusters in kidney biopsies: proximal tubule cells, macrophages, cells of the loop of Henle, T cells, podocytes, principal cells, endothelial cells, mesangial cells, and intercalated cells (Supplemental Table 17). Intercalated cells displayed the highest expression levels of LMAN2L, while IL7R was predominantly expressed in T cells. Compared with control samples, ERAP2 expression was elevated in principal cells (P = 1.11E-06) and intercalated cells (P = 3.32E-02). The expression of the CFHR1 is higher in endothelium cell (P = 7.58E-03) and proximal tubule cell (P = 4.70E-02). PDIA3 was substantially upregulated in endothelium cell (P = 1.90E-04) and loop of Henle cell (P = 4.70E-03). Notably, LMAN2L, IDUA, FAM20A, and GGH were significantly upregulated in mesangial cells (P = 7.00E-03, P = 1.56E-02, P = 4.58E-02, P = 1.53E-02, respectively). LMAN2L, GGH, and IDUA expression were also markedly higher in T cells (P = 4.01E-02, P = 3.25E-03, P = 3.72E-02; Figure 6, Supplemental Figure 2, and Supplemental Table 18).

As for the dataset GSE171314, a total of 20,570 cells were conducted to further analyze. After cluster and top 20 lineage-specific marker gene expression, we identified 14 different cell clusters. CFH, CFHR1, and MGP were elevated in the mesangial cells (P = 1.91E-04, P = 1.54E-02, P = 2.08E-04). CFH was also higher in the endothelial cells (P = 0.03) and distal tubular cells (P = 2.28E-03). FAM20A was upregulated in the endothelial cells (P = 9.34E-04), monocytes (P = 5.25E-05), PT-injury (P = 3.60E-03), and thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle (P = 8.54E-03; Figure 6 and Supplemental Table 19).

Validation in Human Plasma Sample

We enrolled 40 IgA nephropathy patients and 36 HCs. Of the proteins prioritized by MR, only IDUA and PYDC1 could be reliably quantified due to limitations in sample integrity and assay specificity. The ELISA test showed levels of plasma IDUA (2.37±0.90 versus 1.58±0.95 ng/ml, P = 4.00E-04) and PYDC1 (0.51 [0.27–0.94] versus 0.26 [0.14–0.37] ng/ml, P = 5.00E-05) were significantly elevated in the IgA nephropathy compared with the control group (Figure 7, A and B).

Validation In Silico Analysis and Animal Experiment

In silico analysis revealed a significant elevated expression of the PYDC1, also named as pyrin protein and caspase activation and recruitment domain Domain-Containing Protein (P = 3.21E-8), NLRP3 (P = 0.049), IL1β (P = 1.02E-04) and caspase1 (P = 2.28E-9) in kidney tissue from patients with IgA nephropathy compared with normal controls (Figure 7C).

As for animal experiment, expression analysis showed that kidney mRNA levels of Pydc1, Nlrp3, Caspase-1, and IL1β were markedly higher in IgA nephropathy mice. Specifically, ASC expression was elevated (4.35 versus 1.02, P = 2.52E-03), NLRP3 showed a significant upregulation (1.88 versus 1.015, P = 1.80E-02), caspase-1 exhibited higher transcriptional levels (2.23 versus 1.01, P = 8.74E-02), and IL1β mRNA was also significantly higher (3.70 versus 1.00, P = 3.77E-02; Figure 7D).

Discussion

This study provides critical insights into the pathogenesis of IgA nephropathy by identifying key proteins, immune cell types, and immune traits that contribute to disease susceptibility. By integrating MR analysis with single-cell RNA sequencing, we uncovered likely novel mechanisms underlying IgA nephropathy and proposed potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets. These findings not only may deepen our understanding of IgA nephropathy but also open new avenues for clinical translation.

From a biomarker perspective, this study identified several proteins including CFH, CFHR1, ERAP2, FAM20A, GGH, IDUA, PYDC1, and LMAN2L, which were highlighted as causal proteins linked to IgA nephropathy pathogenesis. Their cell-specific expression patterns provide additional context for their roles in disease mechanisms. For instance, IDUA is an enzyme that hydrolyzes the terminal α-L-iduronic acid residues of dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate. It was reported that Idua-deficient mice exhibited diminished common myeloid progenitor and granulocyte-macrophage progenitor cell frequencies in bone marrow.38 These proteins could serve as potential biomarkers for disease diagnosis or progression monitoring.

CFH was identified as a protective factor, likely mitigating disease progression by regulating the alternative pathway.39–42 By contrast, CFHR1 acted as a risk factor, potentially exacerbating inflammation by competing with CFH for complement fragment binding sites, thereby lowering CFH activity.43 PYDC1 acts as a critical negative regulator of innate immunity, restraining cytokine release and inflammasome-mediated cell death in macrophages and granulocytes.44–46 Our in silico analysis and animal experiment suggested that PYDC1 may contribute to disease pathogenesis through regulation of the NLRP3 pathway. These results strengthen the mechanistic support and biologic plausibility for a role of PYDC1 in IgA nephropathy pathogenesis.

Combined with the consistent result in the human sample, it is reasonable to infer that innate immunity serves an important function in IgA nephropathy. Our pathway enrichment analysis further revealed that the complement pathway plays a more significant role in European IgA nephropathy patients, while immune-related pathways are more prominent in the Asian population. A recently published clinical trial on alternative complement pathway inhibition also demonstrated that Iptacopan was more effective in non-Asian populations, although the difference did not reach statistical significance.47 Analysis of CFH polymorphisms indicated a higher frequency of the protective allele in European populations, which was significantly linked to IgA nephropathy susceptibility only in Europeans. Inflammatory lesions, including endocapillary hypercellularity (E1) and crescents (C1/C2), are more prevalent in Asian cohorts than in Caucasian cohorts.48

The immune system's contribution to IgA nephropathy was further highlighted by the causal associations identified between specific immune cell types and traits. Neutrophils and white blood cells were linked to higher disease risk, emphasizing their role in mediating inflammation. Among immune traits, activated regulatory T cells (%CD4+), central memory CD4+ T cells, and CD25hi %CD4+ T cells were implicated in disease susceptibility. These findings are consistent with previous studies showing an imbalance between proinflammatory Th17 cells and anti-inflammatory regulatory T cells in IgA nephropathy patients.49 Studies have shown the regulatory T cells were related to the rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, and SLE.50 Although no studies have directly investigated regulatory T cells in IgA nephropathy, their established immunomodulatory functions imply possible contributions to disease mechanisms.

For therapeutic implications, our findings offer promising targets for intervention. CFH's protective role supports the rationale for complement inhibition strategies such as eculizumab (a C5 inhibitor). Although eculizumab has shown benefits in case reports of crescentic IgA nephropathy, its efficacy needs to be validated in larger clinical trials.51 The identification of FCGR2A as a hub gene interacting with anti-CD20 antibody highlights opportunities for repurposing this mAb therapy for IgA nephropathy. While rituximab has shown mixed results in clinical trials for IgA nephropathy, patient stratification based on biomarkers such as FCGR2A expression may enhance therapeutic outcomes.52,53 These results suggest that the expression patterns of certain molecules may influence responses to specific therapies.

Compared with the previous work by Tang et al.,17 our study provided several important new contributions: (1) extension from plasma proteins alone to an integrated framework that jointly evaluates plasma proteins, immune cell types, and immune cell traits, thereby linking proteomic signals to specific immune cellular contexts; (2) Inclusion of an independent East Asian cohort with ancestry-specific instruments, enabling evaluation of ancestry-dependent and shared; (3) identification of additional candidate causal proteins (e.g., PYDC1, PADI4, DEFA1/DEFA1B, RNASET2, LMAN2L, GGH, FAM20A, and IDUA) that were not reported as causal in the Tang et al. study17 or other multiomics studies, and replication of several of these in FinnGen; and (4) multilayer validation, including single-cell RNA-seq datasets to map key proteins to specific kidney/immune cell subsets, and experimental validation (human samples and mouse models) for IDUA and PYDC1 to begin addressing mechanistic plausibility. Tang et al.17 provided the first large scale proteome wide MR screen, whereas the present work integrates proteomic, immunologic, single cell, and experimental data across ancestries to refine causal candidates and suggest mechanistic pathways (Supplemental Table 20).

This study has several important strengths. First, we employed an integrative multiomics strategy combining phenome-wide MR analysis with single-cell transcriptomic profiling. By leveraging pQTL datasets, we were able to prioritize likely causal proteins, which were further validated using independent scRNA-seq datasets. Second, the inclusion of the FinnGen database as a replication cohort served to enhance the robustness and credibility of our genetic findings. Third, we conducted additional ELISA assays on serum samples from patients with IgA nephropathy and animal experiment, providing preliminary experimental support for prioritized proteins identified through MR analysis. Although some proteins overlap with findings from other multiomics studies, we further validated them as risk-associated or protective-associated targets across independent datasets and ancestries. In addition, we provided new evidence regarding their cellular context and downstream inflammatory pathways through immune-cell analyses and single-cell transcriptomic profiling. Taken together, this convergence of genetic, transcriptomic, and experimental data demonstrates that these proteins represent robust and reproducible signals across complementary study designs, thereby strengthening confidence in their translational relevance.

Despite these advances, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, for several exposures, the number of instrumental SNPs was limited. As a result, residual pleiotropy cannot be fully ruled out, leading to lower statistical power and greater susceptibility to bias. What's more, heterogeneity in instrument number and effective sample size across proteins limits the reliability of direct cross protein comparisons and may bias the apparent “ranking” of causal candidates. Second, consistent replication across populations was also not achieved for all associations, and immune cell types and immune cell-related traits were not validated, so these results were hypothesis-generating and descriptive, requiring additional functional and computational validation. Third, some proteins demonstrated statistical significance only in specific ancestries, underscoring the need for further validation before broad clinical interpretations can be made. The transethnic analyses were not identically powered and that sample size differences may have contributed to the comparative results, in addition to potential biologic heterogeneity. Furthermore, our MR analysis for CFH must be interpreted with extreme caution, as the genetic instruments reside within a locus that is itself a well-established genetic risk factor for IgA nephropathy, and thus are highly susceptible to biologic pleiotropy and violation of core MR assumptions. Fifth, the use of pQTL data generated from a cohort originally designed for cardiovascular research may influence the interpretation of kidney disease associations. In addition, while single-cell transcriptome data supported many MR-prioritized genes, these analyses were constrained by modest sample sizes, lack of comprehensive HC's, technical variation, and possible inter-ethnic differences in gene expression. Owing to the limited number of peripheral blood immune cells and the intrinsic sparsity of single-cell sequencing data, the validation analyses have limited interpretative value. Accordingly, transcriptome results remain exploratory rather than fully mechanistic. The western blot was not completed because of practical limitations, including the prolonged production and optimization cycle of specific antibodies and constraints related to sample preparation and availability. The ELISA and mRNA validation provide preliminary evidence, requiring further investigation before any consideration of translational application.

In conclusion, by identifying key proteins and implicating specific immune cell traits, our findings offer valuable hypotheses for novel biomarkers and targeted therapies. Nevertheless, given the multifaceted etiology of IgA nephropathy and the limitations discussed, these results are interpreted as prioritized, hypothesis-generating leads that require rigorous experimental follow-up. Our integrated analytic platform provides a robust foundation for future research and therapy development across diverse patient populations.

Prognostic and clinicopathological significance of fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) in patients with breast cancer: a meta-analysis
Authors: Zhanwei Wang, Xiaqing Shen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) has been extensively studied for its role in predicting the prognosis of breast cancer (BC) patients; however, existing findings are conflicting. Therefore, this meta-analysis was conducted to identify the significance of FAR in predicting BC prognosis.MethodsWe searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure databases until May 25, 2024. The value of FAR for predicting overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in BC was examined by calculating the combined hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Correlations between FAR and clinicopathological factors were analyzed using combined odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs.ResultsEight studies involving 4094 patients were included in this work. As shown by our combined data, increased FAR significantly predicted poor OS (HR = 2.84, 95% CI = 1.83–4.39, p < 0.001) and poor DFS (HR = 2.43, 95% CI = 1.66–3.58, p < 0.001) of BC. Moreover, the combined data showed that increased FAR was significantly correlated with age ≥ 50 years (OR = 2.04, 95% CI = 1.37–3.04, p < 0.001), stage III cancer (OR = 1.53, 95% CI = 1.04–2.27, p = 0.033), and the presence of lymph node metastases (OR = 1.33, 95% CI = 1.11–1.61, p = 0.002). Nonetheless, FAR was not significantly associated with tumor size, ER/PR/HER-2 status, or lymphovascular invasion in patients with BC.ConclusionIn this meta-analysis, higher FAR was significantly associated with unfavorable OS and DFS in patients with BC and significantly correlated with several features predictive of cancer development in BC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-024-03506-2.

Background

Breast cancer (BC) is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in women globally [1]. According to GLOBOCAN statistics, 2 261 419 new patients with BC and 684 996 deaths were estimated worldwide in 2020 [2]. BC can be categorized into different morphological and molecular subtypes, resulting in various biological behaviors, presentations, and outcomes [3, 4]. At diagnosis, approximately 5% of patients with BC have metastases, while the majority develop distant metastases after a complete treatment for localized disease [5]. In patients with metastatic breast cancer (MBC), distant and multiorgan metastases remain a considerable challenge for recurrence and survival [6]. With recent advances in molecular biology and immunology, highly targeted therapies are now available for a wide range of breast cancers [5, 7]. However, the prognosis for patients with MBC remains poor. Therefore, the identification of new and reliable biomarkers to predict the prognosis of patients with BC is urgently needed [8].

Current evidence shows that inflammation and immune responses play a central role in cancer initiation, development, and metastasis [9]. In recent years, various hematological parameters, such as the systemic immune-inflammation index [10], platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio [11], prognostic nutritional index [12], and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio [13], have become prominent prognostic biomarkers for solid tumors. The fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) is a composite index of fibrinogen and albumin and is calculated using the formula: FAR = fibrinogen (g/L)/albumin (g/L). FAR has been previously considered prognostically important for various cancers, including osteosarcoma [14], colorectal cancer [15], hepatocellular carcinoma [16], esophageal cancer [17], and small-cell lung cancer [18]. Many researchers have analyzed the prognostic importance of FAR in patients with BC, but their findings are controversial [19–26]. In some studies, elevated FAR was the significant prognostic indicator for survival outcomes in BC [21–23, 25]. However, other researchers have found no significant relationship between FAR and survival in BC [26]. Therefore, this meta-analysis focused on determining the exact significance of FAR in predicting BC prognosis. In addition, the relationship between FAR and clinicopathological factors of BC was also evaluated.

Materials and methods

Study guideline

This study was performed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guideline [27] (Supplemental file 1). This meta-analysis was registered in INPLASY under registration number INPLASY202470087. The protocol is available at https://inplasy.com/inplasy-2024-7-0087.

Ethics statement

As this study was conducted based on previous data, neither informed consent nor ethical approval was required.

Search strategy

This study searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure databases until May 25, 2024, using the following search terms: (fibrinogen-to-albumin OR fibrinogen/albumin OR albumin-to-fibrinogen OR albumin/fibrinogen) AND (breast cancer OR breast neoplasm OR breast carcinoma OR breast tumor). There were no restrictions on publication language. The detailed search strategies for each database are provided in Supplemental File 2. The references were manually reviewed to explore further relevant research in the retrieved publications.

Study eligibility criteria

The following studies were included: (1) enrolling BC cases as study subjects; (2) reporting the relationship between FAR and prognosis of patients with BC; (3) hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) could be retrieved or calculated; and (4) a cut-off value of FAR was identified. The following were excluded: (1) meeting abstracts, reviews, letters, commentaries, and case reports; (2) animal studies; and (3) studies recruiting overlapping patients. There were no restrictions on publication language.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Data collection was conducted by two researchers (ZW and XS), and disagreements were resolved through mutual negotiation to reach a consensus. Extracted data included first author, country, year, sample size, age, study center/design/period, cancer stage, treatment, FAR threshold, threshold determination, follow-up, survival analysis, survival outcomes, HRs, and 95% CIs. Overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were the primary and secondary survival endpoints, respectively. The quality of the included studies was evaluated by two researchers (ZW and XS) using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) [28] on three levels: selection, comparability, and outcome. NOS scores are 0–9, and studies with an NOS ≥ 6 were defined as high quality.

Statistical analysis

The significance of FAR for predicting the OS and DFS of BC was analyzed by calculating the combined HRs and 95% CIs. Interstudy heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q test and the I2 statistic. An analysis of the random-effects model was used with prominent heterogeneity (I2 > 50% or p < 0.10 or both); otherwise, a fixed-effects model was adopted. We performed subgroup analyses stratified by country, sample size, threshold, threshold determination, and survival analysis to explore the effect of FAR on predicting the prognosis of different BC populations. Correlations between FAR and clinicopathological factors were examined using pooled odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs. In addition, a sensitivity analysis was also performed to detect sources of heterogeneity and assess the stability of the results. We performed Begg’s funnel plot and Egger’s test to evaluate publication bias. This study used Stata software version 12.0 (Stata Corporation, College Station, TX, USA) for statistical analysis. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Literature search

A primary search identified 46 studies, of which 29 were retained after duplicates were eliminated (Fig. 1). After reading the titles and abstracts, we excluded 14 articles because they were animal studies or irrelevant. Then, the remaining 15 articles were assessed by reading the full-texts, and seven were further excluded because of unavailable survival information (n = 4), irrelevance to FAR (n = 2), or the type was letter to the editor (n = 1). Finally, eight studies with 4094 patients [19–26] were included in this study (Fig. 1).

Characteristics of included studies

Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of the studies. The publication year was 2017–2023. One study was conducted in Korea [19] and seven in China [20–26]. There were six English publications [19, 21–23, 25, 26] and two Chinese [20, 24]. The sample sizes were 164–995 (median, 490.5). All included studies were retrospective single-center studies [19–26]. All eligible studies enrolled patients with stages I-III BC receiving surgical resection [19–26]. The FAR threshold was 6.6–13.1 (median, 7.7). Five articles applied the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve to determine the threshold [19, 20, 23–25], two studies used the X-tile software [22, 26], and one study used the median value [21] (Table 1). Seven articles mentioned the significance of FAR for predicting OS [19–23, 25, 26], and seven studies provided data on the association between FAR and DFS [20–26] in BC. Six studies obtained HRs and 95% CIs based on multivariate regression [19–21, 23–25], while two used univariate regression [22, 26]. The NOS scores of all included articles were 7–9, revealing their high quality (Table 1).

FAR and OS

Seven articles with 3099 cases [19–23, 25, 26] mentioned the significance of FAR in predicting the OS of BC. This study used the random-effects model due to the apparent heterogeneity (I2 = 77.8%, p < 0.001). The pooled data were HR = 2.84, 95% CI = 1.83–4.39, and p < 0.001, suggesting a strong association between increased FAR and unfavorable OS in patients with BC (Table 2; Fig. 2). As the subgroup analysis showed, the significance of FAR for predicting OS was independent of sample size, country, threshold, threshold determination, or survival analysis (p < 0.05; Table 2).

FAR and DFS

Seven articles totaling 3301 cases [20–26] provided data on the correlation between FAR and DFS in BC cases. This study adopted a random-effects model due to the apparent heterogeneity (I2 = 79.2%, p < 0.001; Table 2). According to our pooled findings, increased FAR was significantly associated with unfavorable DFS of BC (HR = 2.43, 95% CI = 1.66–3.58, p < 0.001; Table 2; Fig. 3). In the subgroup analysis, high FAR remained a significant prognostic marker of poor DFS in BC, regardless of sample size, threshold, threshold determination, or survival analysis (p < 0.05; Table 2).

Relationship between FAR and clinicopathological characteristics of BC

Six studies involving 2638 patients [19–23, 25] reported a relationship between FAR and clinicopathological factors of BC. The combined data showed that increased FAR was significantly correlated with age ≥ 50 years (OR = 2.04, 95% CI = 1.37–3.04, p < 0.001), cancer stage III (OR = 1.53, 95% CI = 1.04–2.27, p = 0.033), and lymph node (LN) metastasis (OR = 1.33, 95% CI = 1.11–1.61, p = 0.002) (Table 3; Fig. 4). Nonetheless, FAR showed no strong association with tumor size (OR = 1.08, 95% CI = 0.80–1.46, p = 0.604), estrogen receptor (ER) (OR = 0.95, 95% CI = 0.78–1.16, p = 0.643), progesterone receptor (PR) (OR = 0.83, 95% CI = 0.56–1.24, p = 0.368), human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 (HER-2) status (OR = 0.94, 95% CI = 0.75–1.18, p = 0.579), and lymphovascular invasion (LVI) (OR = 1.14, 95% CI = 0.92–1.41, p = 0.236) of patients with BC (Figs. 4 and 5, and Table 3).

Publication bias

Possible publication bias in this study was analyzed using Begg’s and Egger’s tests. No significant publication bias was found for OS (p = 0.072 and 0.157 for Begg’s and Egger’s tests, respectively) or DFS (p = 0.54 and 0.063 for Begg’s and Egger’s tests, respectively) (Fig. 7).

Discussion

The value of FAR for predicting the prognosis of patients with BC remains controversial in existing research. Data from eight articles with 4094 cases were aggregated in this study [19–26]. The pooled data showed that increased FAR was significantly associated with unfavorable OS and DFS in patients with BC. Furthermore, higher FAR was significantly correlated with age ≥ 50 years, cancer stage III, and LN metastases in BC. Therefore, higher FAR was significantly associated with unfavorable short- and long-term prognoses in patients with BC. Overall, FAR significantly predicted BC prognosis and was associated with several features of tumor development. FAR is an affordable and promising biomarker for predicting the prognosis of patients with BC in the clinical setting. To our knowledge, this is the first meta-analysis to investigate the clinicopathological and prognostic value of FAR for BC.

FAR consists of fibrinogen and albumin. A high FAR resulted from a high fibrinogen content, low albumin content, or both. Although the mechanisms underlying the prognostic value of FAR in BC remain unclear, they can be interpreted as follows. The liver synthesizes fibrinogen, which is released into the circulation during tissue damage, infection, or inflammation [29]. Fibrinogen is a central component of blood coagulation and has an essential effect on platelet aggregation and clot formation [30]. Fibrinogen has been found to trigger pro-inflammatory cytokines, which contribute to inflammation [31]. In some studies, cancer cells have been found to produce and release fibrinogen to promote their growth [32]. Moreover, platelets can form thrombin and aggregate fibrinogen around cancer cells, which helps to avoid clearance by natural killer cells [33]. In contrast, serum albumin levels can be used to measure the nutritional status of patients and determine surgical tolerance and recovery after surgery [34]. Decreased serum albumin directly leads to reduced immunity while promoting proinflammatory factors associated with cancer progression, which increases cancer incidence [35]. Albumin can be suppressed via inflammatory factors like interleukin (IL)-1, IL-6, or tumor necrosis factor-a, thereby promoting tumorigenesis [36]. Hypoalbuminemia is thought to be associated with poor prognosis in cancer [35]. Overall, FAR based on fibrinogen and albumin levels is a reasonable prognostic marker for BC.

Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapies play important roles in BC treatment [37]. Various treatments are recommended for adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapies of early-stage BC, including chemotherapy, anti-HER2 chemotherapy, other targeted treatments, hormone therapy, and bisphosphonates [37]. Adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy is recommended in most chemotherapy options [38]. For HER2-positive BC, trastuzumab, pertuzumab, trastuzumab emtansine, and neratinib are recommended as neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy [39]. In women with ER-positive disease, tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors (AIs) have been administered for varying durations and sequences, and ovarian suppression or ablation has been performed in premenopausal women [40]. Most women were recommended to undergo adjuvant radiotherapy after breast-conserving surgery [41]. Radiotherapy should be performed on the chest wall after a mastectomy. For triple-negative BC, a combination of cancer immunotherapy based on programmed death-1 (PD-1)/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitors and chemotherapy is effective in both advanced and early stages [42].

All included studies showed FAR as a risk factor for OS in BC, and the differences were statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 2. The subgroup analysis of OS also confirmed the prognostic value of FAR for OS in different subgroups (Table 2). The OS analysis was part of this meta-analysis. We also investigated the prognostic value of FAR for DFS and the correlation between FAR and clinicopathological features of BC. These analyses provided comprehensive investigations of FAR in BC prognosis.

Many meta-analyses have reported the prognostic significance of FAR for malignant tumors [43–45]. Zhang et al. included 12 articles in their meta-analysis. According to their results, higher FAR was significantly associated with poor OS in malignant tumors [45]. Sun et al. reported significant correlations between high FAR and poor OS, DFS, and PFS in various cancer types in their meta-analysis of 7282 cases [44]. In a recent meta-analysis of 19 studies, higher FAR was associated with poor OS, poor PFS/DFS, and poor time to recurrence in human malignancies [43]. Our findings in BC are consistent with those in other cancers.

This study had some limitations. First, the studies we included were from Asia, mainly China, although no restriction was made on the region or publication language of the eligible studies. Therefore, the applicability of our results to non-Asian patients with BC should be verified. Second, all eligible studies had a retrospective design. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, selection bias may have occurred. The type of eligible studies was not limited. However, after screening based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, all included studies were retrospective. Therefore, further prospective studies are required to validate the results of this meta-analysis. Third, the FAR threshold was controversial among the included articles, which may have contributed to heterogeneity. Fourth, this meta-analysis investigated the prognostic value of FAR for OS and DFS in patients with BC. However, other endpoints, such as BC-specific survival (BCSS), are also of interest. The reported survival outcomes were OS, DFS, and distant metastasis-free survival (Table 1). However, no study reported data on BCSS. All survival outcomes were included in this meta-analysis. We did not restrict the survival endpoints in eligibility criteria. Therefore, large-scale multiregional prospective trials should be performed for validation.

Conclusions

In summary, higher FAR is significantly associated with unfavorable OS and DFS in patients with BC and correlates significantly with several BC development features.

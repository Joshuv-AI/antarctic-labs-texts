Determining strategies to combat obesity: analysis of G7 countries with WENSLO and EDAS methods
Authors: Sefer Aygün, Serkan Eti̇, Yeter Uslu, Selenay Koç, Shankar Chakraborty, Yaşar Gökalp
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractProposing the optimal strategies to combat obesity for the health systems is a challenging task as there are many intertwined factors influencing it. Thus, huge responsibility is bestowed on the decision makers to identify the most suitable strategies to minimize obesity. This study is designed around the question: “What are the relative importance levels of the key factors to be considered in the fight against obesity, and how do G7 countries rank in terms of their performance in relation to these factors?” The study aims to address the main factors influencing obesity and to effectively prioritize them. In thus paper, two multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) methods, i.e. weights by envelope and slope (WENSLO) and evaluation based on distance from average solution (EDAS) are integrated in picture fuzzy environment to propose the most appropriate strategies to combat obesity in the G7 countries. The corresponding weights of the considered strategies are evaluated using WENSLO, while they are ranked based on EDAS. For selection of the experts to provide their judgments, the Hartigan-Wong algorithm is adopted. The findings from the study reveals that the most important criterion that the decision makers should consider while proposing strategies to prevent obesity is nutrition habits (w = 330), followed by physical activity and lifestyle (w = 193), and socioeconomic factors (w = 128). Based on the derived results, it is thought that educational policies regarding nutritional habits of the individuals can be an important step in preventing obesity. In addition, it is also important to implement policies to increase physical activity. Among the considered G7 countries, Japan appears as the most successful one and can be benchmarked for the remaining countries struggling with this issue.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-28625-1.Keywords: Obesity, G7 countries, WENSLO, EDAS, Health policy

Introduction

Obesity can be defined as a health condition due to excessive fat accumulation in the body at a level negatively affecting the health [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) evaluates obesity through calculation of body mass index (BMI). A scale is determined according to the value obtained by dividing an individual’s weight by the square of his/her height. This value is treated as normal if it is between 18.5 and 24.9; overweight between 25 and 29.9; and obese if it is 30 and above. It is possible to mention many factors responsible for obesity [2]. Preference for high-calorie and fatty foods, low daily energy burn, family history of obesity or psychological factors, such as stress and depression significantly affect obesity. However, obesity is a risk factor for many chronic diseases, leading to cardiovascular diseases, type 2 diabetes, and some types of cancer [3].

It is a well known fact that obesity negatively affects the health system as well as the individuals who are obese. Since obesity is one of the main causes of many chronic diseases, it also results in increased health expenditures [4]. Indirectly, it prevents the individuals from participating in the labor force. Since obese individuals need special care and support, home care and rehabilitative services gain importance [5]. Due to serious health problems caused by obesity, there is also an increase in the demand for efficient health services. This situation causes more consumption of the scarce resources and an increase in workload of the healthcare professionals. For the obese individuals to benefit from the health services, specialized medical devices and equipment, such as specially designed beds and stretchers are required. Therefore, an increase in the rate of obese individuals has detrimental effects on the health system [6].

Many different procedures can be followed to prevent the individuals from becoming obese. Healthily regulating eating habits is one of the important factors [7]. In addition, daily exercise programs and reducing sedentary behaviors significantly contribute to energy burning [8]. Furthermore, the socio-economic power of the individuals affects their access to obesity-preventive practices. Significant results can be achieved by improving social support for the individuals with low socio-economic status [9]. Psychosocial factors are also important triggers for obesity. Expanding stress management programs and providing importance to rehabilitative services, such as therapy can be another major step. Apart from these, strategies, like increasing walking and sports areas, and reducing air pollution stand out in the fight against obesity. It is also observed that development of technology negatively affects obesity, provoking sedentary lifestyles [10]. Besides, policies, such as promoting healthy eating and obesity awareness programs are the other actions to be deployed for improving the process [11].

Obesity is a significant public health problem resulting from numerous environmental, individual, psychological, socioeconomic, and health system-related factors. While the existing literature includes studies that examine and evaluate the effects of these factors individually, research that addresses these factors holistically and systematically assesses their importance is limited. In particular, decision-support-based studies that guide policymakers, given limited resources such as budget, manpower, and time, to prioritize intervention in specific factors are insufficient. Furthermore, the vast majority of previous research defines obesity or describes the current situation in various countries. However, studies do not address these two situations comparatively. This creates a significant research gap, both in determining the order of importance of key obesity indicators and in evaluating countries’ performance in combating obesity within the same analytical framework. The motivation for this paper is to address the complexity of factors affecting obesity and effectively prioritize them. This paper thereby aims to provide policymakers with the effectiveness of the prioritized strategies to combat obesity.

To achieve the said goal, two multi-criteria decision making tools, i.e. weights by envelope and slope (WENSLO) and evaluation based on distance from average solution (EDAS) are hybridized in picture fuzzy environment to propose the most effective strategies to prevent obesity in the G7 countries (Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States). WENSLO, being an objective weighting method, estimates the relative importance of the considered strategies, based on the subjective judgments of several policymakers. On the other hand, EDAS analyzes those strategies and ranks them considering their distances from the average solution. Finally, the uncertainty involved in the decision making process is effectively with the deployment of picture fuzzy numbers. As an innovative approach, the Hartigan-Wong algorithm is employed in this paper to ease out the expert selection process. People having sufficient expertise in the relevant field are chosen and clustered based on this algorithm.

Literature review

Nutrition habits

Nutrition habits that include components such as consumption of high-calorie processed foods, unhealthy food-based nutrition and frequent fast-food consumption are important triggers of obesity [12]. Consumption of such foods has usually been preferred due to their low cost and high calorie content [13]. Increased consumption of sugar-sweetened beverages and fast-food has also constituted the primary dietary risk for obesity [14]. Marlow et al. [15], in their study examining modifiable risk factors for obesity, identified a strong association between carbohydrate consumption and skipping meals, and obesity. Thus, encouraging healthy eating practices has been identified to be crucial to halting the obesity epidemic.

Physical activity and lifestyle

The risk of obesity is substantially raised by a lifestyle involving hazardous habits and insufficient physical activity. Development of technology, corporate business structure and spread of urban life have also led to sedentary life and weight gain [16]. Haas et al. [17] stated that having an active lifestyle had been associated with less obesity; therefore, doing any sport would be more important than the amount, type and intensity. Prolonged sitting and inactivity can also lead to fat accumulation around the waist and hips. At this point, lifestyle change and transition to an active life can prevent the risk of overweight that may occur in the following years [18]. Promoting physical activity would play an important role in the fight against obesity by supporting the weight control.

Socio-economic factors

Low socioeconomic status limits access to healthy foods and encourages individuals to consume high-calorie and nutrient-poor foods. The impact of socioeconomic factors on obesity is concentrated in areas such as access to healthy foods and the nutritional value consumed, especially for low-income groups. Low-income individuals are mostly orientated towards lower-cost and high-calorie foods. Overweight, especially in developing age individuals, is more common among individuals with poorer socioeconomic conditions [19] (Nogueira-de-Almeida et al., 2024). In addition, the impact of socioeconomic status on access to health services is also very important. Individuals living in areas with low socioeconomic status have difficulty in accessing regular health check-ups and diet programs, as well as shopping for healthy food. Lofton et al. [20] stated that individuals exposed to inequality cannot access adequate health services and this plays a role in increasing obesity rates. In another study.

Technology usage

The role of sedentary activities in daily life has grown along with the use of technology. The increase in screen time both reduces physical activity and accelerates weight gain. Digital devices and screen time constitute the majority of the time that children and young people are awake [21] (Zerón-Rugerio et al., 2024). Doruk et al. [22] reported that the increase in the daily consumption of technological devices such as phones, tablets and televisions has a negative effect on the body mass index values of developing individuals. In addition, the consumption of high-calorie foods such as junk food during the time spent in front of the television and computer also triggers obesity. The continuous use of these technological devices has potential negative effects such as stress and anxiety, sleep problems, asocial behaviour and eating problems [23] (Oniz et al., 2023). Poor sleep quality and stress also lead to overweight and obesity tendencies among adults [24] (Dakanalis et al., 2024).

Health systems and policies

Health systems and policies play a critical role in preventing and managing obesity. Strong health systems can reduce the risk of obesity by providing resources that support individuals to lead healthy lives [25] (Mytton et al., 2024). It has been discussed for years that obesity is not only the responsibility of the individual but also a social problem that concerns the public and institutions [26] (Mehta, 2023). At this point, health policies can take protective measures against obesity with strategies that promote healthy eating and physical activity. The meta-analysis of Pineda et al. [27] reveals that regulations such as taxation of unhealthy foods and beverages encourage healthy living habits and reduce obesity rates.

Multi criteria decision making methods

Multi-criteria decision-making methods allow for the simultaneous evaluation of multiple criteria. Many studies using this method have been conducted in the literature in recent years. It is particularly preferred in areas requiring strategic policies. In the health sector, these methods are frequently used to develop strategies for addressing not only physical problems such as obesity but also psychological issues [28, 29]. Furthermore, they are among the preferred methods for current issues such as energy use and technology evaluation. Through these methods, expert opinions are integrated with quantitative data, providing policymakers with a scientific decision-making framework for directing limited resources to more priority areas [30, 31].

Research gap and novelty of the paper

It can be noted from the literature review that there are many factors affecting obesity, making it difficult to treat each factor individually with the available resources to combat obesity. Therefore, the most important indicators need to be identified. However, only a limited number of studies have conducted prioritized analysis, indicating a strong need for such studies. Considering these issues, this paper aims to address the main factors affecting obesity and make effective prioritization among them. The aim presented is to fill a gap in the literature.

Obesity is not merely an individual health issue. It is a global problem that triggers chronic diseases and leads to increased healthcare expenditures. The main motivation of this study is to scientifically reveal which strategies policymakers fighting obesity should focus on to achieve more accurate results. It is to analyze the performance of G7 countries, which guide global health policies, in combating obesity and to provide an optimal roadmap for other countries. This article presents several key innovations. The WENSLO and EDAS methods have been integrated with Picture Fuzzy sets and used for the first time to determine strategies for combating obesity. The Hartigan-Wong algorithm used in expert selection has added a new dimension to the fight against obesity. Finally, the G7 analysis provides an important roadmap for policymakers and developing/underdeveloped countries.

Methods

It has already been mentioned that to cope with obesity, the most important factors affecting it need to be identified. In this paper, it is aimed to prioritize those factors in terms of public health and evaluate the G7 countries. The status of those countries is evaluated based on the criteria considered, the most important criterion is determined using WENSLO method [44] and the G7 countries are finally ranked employing EDAS method [45]. Unlike classical methods such as AHP, the WENSLO approach contributes to a more precise analysis of the uncertainty in expert opinions through the envelope and slope approach. By offering decision-makers the opportunity to evaluate within more flexible ranges rather than exact numbers, it reduces the burden on expert. Unlike approaches such as Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) and VlseKriterijumska Optimizacija I Kompromisno Resenje (VIKOR), the EDAS approach measures the acceptability of alternatives based on the average performance of all options. This approach ensures a more balanced decision-making process. The WENSLO and EDAS methods have been integrated with Picture Fuzzy numbers to facilitate decision-making under uncertainty. Picture Fuzzy numbers consider the state of neutrality in addition to membership, non-membership, and indecision in Intuitionistic Fuzzy numbers. Therefore, it can be said that more accurate results are obtained.

Why G7 countries?

Lifestyle-related health issues such as obesity yield significant findings when examined in G7 countries, which share similar levels of economic development and industrialized society characteristics. G7 countries have the capacity to shape global health policies. Identifying best practices in these countries provides a roadmap for other nations in the fight against obesity.

The WENSLO and EDAS methods were used to evaluate the differences and similarities among G7 countries. However, the evaluation of these methods requires the opinions of experts meeting specific criteria. Experts examined the G7 countries within the framework of socio-economic, cultural, and technological factors and provided their opinions. These opinions were then analyzed using the WENSLO and EDAS methods. This revealed which criteria were prioritized and which countries were taking more appropriate steps.

Face-to-face meetings were requested with experts. Meetings were scheduled with those who agreed, and data was collected. Online meetings were scheduled with experts who could not attend the face-to-face meetings. A total of 8 experts were interviewed, and their opinions were obtained. The analysis was conducted using the opinions obtained from these 8 experts.

Hartigan-Wong algorithm

The Hartigan-Wong algorithm [46–48] is one of the K-means algorithms that assigns data in a dataset to the most appropriate cluster for a given number of clusters. Each data is then assigned to the cluster to which it is closest, based on its distances to the cluster centers. After each data is assigned to a cluster, the cluster centers are updated. The distances of each data to the updated cluster centers are recalculated and the memberships are updated based on their proximity to the new cluster centers. This process continues until the maximum number of iterations is reached or until there is no change in the cluster membership.

After the iteration process is complete, the average of the elements of K clusters is computed. In other words, the average values of the experts’ features are calculated for each cluster. Finally, based on these averages, the clusters representing the expert teams are evaluated. The cluster with the highest averages is selected as the most successful expert team.

Discussion

The analysis revealed that the most important factor affecting the process of minimizing obesity is dietary habits. Another result obtained is that Japan is the country that carries out the most effective activities in the fight against obesity among the G7 countries. In Japan, the Metabo Law and the Shukuiku Law play a significant role in the fight against obesity. The Metabo Law requires annual waist measurements for company employees aged 45–74. Individuals exceeding 85 cm (men) and 90 cm (women) are provided with counseling services. Furthermore, the Shukuiku Law mandates a diet free of frozen or processed foods in schools. It also aims to instill portion control awareness in children from an early age. It is known that obesity generally occurs in the case of excessive weight gain as a result of excessive consumption of high-calorie, low-nutrient and high-fat foods. Considering this situation, it is seen that proper and regular eating habits are critical in the fight against obesity [42]. Minami et al. [49] conducted a study in Japan investigating the relationship between dietary habits during adolescence and gestational diabetes in later periods. In the results of the study, it is stated that avoiding snacking between meals and at night during adolescence reduces gestational diabetes. Cui et al. [50] examined the relationship between unhealthy eating habits and obesity in men aged 20–39 years in Japan. Accordingly, it is stated that an unhealthy diet triggers obesity. Azuma et al. [51] tried to determine the link between irregular breakfast eating and obesity with an observational study on 254 people in Japan. In this study, it is said that men who have irregular breakfast have a higher risk of obesity.

According to the results of the study, the second most important factor affecting the solution of obesity problem is physical activity and lifestyle. Preferring a sedentary lifestyle in daily life leads to weight gain and obesity by reducing calorie expenditure. An active lifestyle and sports habits, on the other hand, not only reduce the risk of obesity but also contribute to the treatment of many diseases such as heart health. Yamashiro et al. [52] conducted a study investigating the relationship between morbid obesity and physical activity and lifestyle habits by surveying 15.122 university students in Japan between 2011 and 2019. In the results of the study, it is stated that sleep patterns and exercise habits affect morbid obesity. Fujiwara et al. [53] conducted a study investigating the prevalence of childhood obesity in Japan. This study suggests that obesity can be minimized by promoting healthy eating and physical activity through school policies and curricula. Hamasaki [54] conducted a study examining the role of physical activity in the management of obesity by meta-analysis method. Accordingly, it is stated that structured exercise programs, especially combinations of aerobic exercise and resistance training, improve the body composition of obese individuals. Tataka et al. [55] conducted a study investigating whether there are differences between obese and non-obese individuals in terms of physical activity and diet. The study included 143 men between 65 and 75. In the study, it is said that there is no difference between the two groups in terms of physical activity and nutritional profiles.

Conclusion

Obesity is one of the most common health problems today. In the coming years, it is predicted that the problem of obesity will become more widespread. This problem affects society and states as well as the individual. Because both obesity itself and other diseases caused by obesity bring a significant burden to the health system. In addition, health problems experienced by obese individuals reduce labor productivity. Therefore, action should be taken to avoid these negative effects caused by obesity. However, there are as many factors that cause obesity as there are in minimizing it. It is not possible to intervene in all of them. For this reason, it is necessary to know the importance of the factors in order to determine which action will be taken among the relevant factors. The aim of this study is to identify the factors that are effective in minimizing obesity and to determine their importance ranking. In this framework, 8 criteria were determined with the support of the literature, the importance ranking of the determined criteria was analyzed with the Fuzzy WENSLO method and the country rankings were analyzed with the Fuzzy EDAS method.

The results of the analysis show that nutrition habits are the most important factor in minimizing obesity. Physical activity and lifestyle were found to be the second, socioeconomic factors, the third. Among the G7 countries, the best countries in the fight against obesity were found to be Japan, Italy, Germany, United Kingdom, France, Canada and United States, respectively. In this framework, focusing on the first three factors contribute to achieving significant success in the fight against obesity. However, this struggle should be supported by national policies as well as individual policies. Especially for children in pre-school and primary education age to gain healthy eating habits, adopt an active lifestyle and participate in sports activities, policies should be implemented at the national level. In this way, the obesity problem will be prevented before it occurs. In other words, it is recommended that obesity should first be combated at the point of preventive health services. In addition, actions should be taken to treat existing obese individuals and to bring these individuals into society. In addition, in order to raise public awareness, awareness should be raised by providing training for healthy eating habits and other factors in cooperation with local governments and health institutions.

Limitations and future study suggestions

Although this study provides a significant roadmap for combating obesity, it has several limitations. The analysis was specifically conducted on G7 countries, which share similar economic characteristics. Since the dynamics of developing countries may differ, this should be noted as an important limitation. Additionally, country groups could be determined based on geographic proximity in addition to economic similarity. Indeed, geography is one of the key factors influencing nutritional and cultural habits. Furthermore, the weighting analysis relies on expert judgments. Although the disadvantage of this situation was attempted to be minimized using Picture Fuzzy numbers, the analysis results are ultimately shaped by the subjective perspectives of the expert team.

Future studies are recommended to be conducted on different country groups, such as developing and underdeveloped countries. Furthermore, new analyses considering different fuzzy number sets and different MCDM approaches are needed for future research. In addition, conducting different analyses in geographically similar regions with similar dietary habits (such as Mediterranean countries) may yield different results. The opinions provided by the experts take into account the current time period and needs. It is highly probable that societal needs and dynamics will change in the future. Therefore, it is important to repeat the studies at specific time intervals.

Research implications

From a research perspective, our findings offer various important implications for the academic literature. This study enables a holistic approach to be taken to strategies for combating obesity. The literature is enriched by the set of criteria that influence the fight against obesity, which is an important contribution. The results obtained show that the theoretical focus has shifted from individual behaviour to systemic policy frameworks. Additionally, the study contributes to the development of priority strategies and policies for the effective management of scarce health resources, which is of critical importance in this area.

Practical implications

The fact that nutritional habits are the most important factor in combating obesity shows that public health budgets should primarily be directed toward programs that increase nutritional literacy. In addition, tax regulations are needed to facilitate access to healthy food. The physical activity criterion is the second most important factor affecting the struggle against obesity. Accordingly, urban planners and health managers need to work together to increase the number of walking paths. Regulations that support an active life in workplaces will provide a positive effect in reducing the burden of obesity. Finally, the nutritional policies and preventive health service models of Japan, which was found to be the most successful country in combating obesity, should be examined and efforts should be carried out for their integration. The Metabo Law and the Shukuiku Law, which played a significant role in Japan’s success, should be applied through cultural adaptation.

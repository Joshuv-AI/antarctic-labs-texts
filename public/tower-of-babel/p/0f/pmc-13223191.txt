Study on mmage defect recognition and classification of power transmission equipment based on lightweight model residual Mamba
Authors: Cui Jindong, Song Weijie, Guan Shan, Sun Liang, Tian Hongliang, Wang Fuwang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImage defect detection of power transmission and transformation equipment is a key technology in the operation and maintenance of power systems, which is of vital importance for ensuring the safe and stable operation of the power grid and enhancing its self-healing capacity. However, this task has long been confronted with the core challenge of a limited number of defect samples for specific categories. Traditional deep learning models trained on large-scale data find it difficult to achieve accurate detection under small-sample conditions. To address this issue, this study proposes a lightweight segmentation network based on the Mamba architecture, Residual Mamba (ResMamba). This network adopts a six-level U-shaped codec structure and innovatively integrates the Mamba module into the visual state space (VSS) as the codec link. Its core ResVSS module significantly reduces the number of parameters by removing a redundant linear layer (accounting for 38% of the original VSS module parameters) in the internal shortcut connection of the original VSS module, and introduces deep convolutional blocks and learnable scale parameters to dynamically scale residual connections. It enhances feature representation ability while reducing model complexity. In addition, the skip connection part introduces a multi-level and multi-scale information fusion mechanism, effectively integrating cross-scale features by generating spatial and channel attention maps, thereby enhancing the model’s performance in multi-specification defect detection. Experimental results on public datasets (including specialized small-sample settings) show that ResMamba achieves superior segmentation accuracy while maintaining a low parameter count, effectively balancing computational efficiency and detection performance. It outperforms both general segmentation models and domain-specific models for power equipment defect detection, providing a reliable new solution for small-sample defect detection of power equipment and enhancing the self-healing ability of power grids.Keywords: Image defect detection of power equipment, ResMamba, Mamba architecture, Image segmentation, Lightweight network, Small-sample learning, Self-healing ability of power gridsSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

At present, most substations have deployed image and video monitoring equipment to collect all kinds of operation and maintenance images in the station, and rely on intelligent processing algorithms to analyze the image data, gradually realize the intelligent operation and maintenance of substations, and provide data support for the construction of power grid self-healing capacity. However, the current intelligent analysis methods of substation equipment images mainly rely on data-driven deep learning models, which usually require a large number of labeled samples to achieve ideal performance, and the model performance is highly dependent on the number of training images1. In the face of new defect categories or scarce defect samples (a common scenario in power equipment operation), its generalization ability is obviously insufficient. On the one hand, the frequency of some equipment defect types in substations is very low, and it is difficult to obtain samples, so it is difficult to construct an adequate and balanced high-quality training set. On the other hand, as the operating environment and equipment types continue to change, new defect categories will continue to appear, resulting in a very limited number of samples, further aggravating the detection difficulty under small sample conditions2, and also bringing challenges to the reliable implementation of the self-healing function of the power grid.

Although models based on CNN and Transformer have been widely applied in the field of image segmentation and have demonstrated good performance in many tasks, they also have significant limitations3. For instance, CNNs struggle to effectively model long-range dependencies, while Transformers, due to the self-attention mechanism, have higher computational complexity and memory usage, and their inference speed is typically slower. In the task of defect detection in power transmission equipment, the defects vary in shape, the background is complex, lighting conditions fluctuate, and the defect areas often have blurred boundaries and low contrast with the background. These characteristics place higher demands on the model’s ability to extract features and model context4. Furthermore, the complex structures and noise in high-resolution substation images further increase the challenge of achieving accurate segmentation.

In recent years, the emergence of the new sequence modeling method Mamba has provided a new solution to these problems. Gu and Dao5 proposed the Selective State Space Model, which can effectively ignore redundant information through dynamic adjustment of input weights and selective information transmission mechanisms during processing, while maintaining high accuracy and improving computational efficiency. Subsequently, Ma et al.6 proposed the U-Mamba structure, which first combined the Mamba module with the U-Net architecture, introducing the SSM-CNN hybrid module in the encoder, retaining the advantages of local feature extraction of convolution operations while enhancing the model’s ability to capture long-range semantic dependencies. Liu et al.7 further proposed the VMamba model, specifically targeting the characteristics of two-dimensional structured data in visual tasks. They designed the Cross-Scan Module (CSM), scanning the image in four directions to convert it into a sequence, and used the S6 block for parallel processing, effectively compensating for the differences between one-dimensional sequence modeling and two-dimensional visual understanding, while controlling the computational complexity within a linear range. Wang et al.8 constructed the VM-UNet, achieving the first fully state-space model-based U-shaped segmentation architecture, using the VSS block instead of the traditional convolution module, and introducing patch merging and expansion operations to build an asymmetric encoder-decoder, significantly improving the context information integration ability. Zhang et al.9 proposed VM-UNet V2, introducing semantic and detail injection mechanisms based on the VSS module, further optimizing the multi-scale feature fusion effect. Wang et al.10 proposed the Mamba-UNet, fully adopting the VSS module to construct the encoder-decoder framework, achieving multi-level feature learning on the basis of linear embedding and deep convolution, through merging and expansion operations, outperforming classic models such as U-Net and Swin-UNet on multiple standard datasets. Liao et al.11 designed the lightweight LightM-UNet, embedding Mamba as a substitute for CNN and Transformer in U-Net, using the residual visual Mamba layer to extract deep semantic features and model long-range dependencies, significantly reducing the number of parameters and computational costs while maintaining excellent segmentation performance.

Although the model based on Mamba has demonstrated potential in multiple visual tasks, its application in defect detection of power transmission equipment is still in its initial stage. There are still issues, such as the need for further improvement in computational efficiency and the strengthening of spatial feature expression capabilities. Moreover, how to make sequence models better adapt to the structural characteristics and discrimination requirements of power equipment images is also an area that needs to be explored. In this context, this study proposes a lightweight segmentation network for defect images of power transmission equipment, Residual Mamba (ResMamba), aiming to achieve efficient and accurate defect region segmentation under limited sample conditions12. A lightweight segmentation model, ResMamba, was constructed for defect images of power transmission equipment. The core ResVSS module removes a redundant linear layer in the original VSS module (38% parameter compression for the module) and introduces deep convolution blocks and learnable scale parameters, which is distinct from the fixed residual structure of VMamba and LightM-UNet13.

Materials and methods

Introduction of the dataset

The substation defect dataset is a specialized image dataset designed specifically for detecting equipment and environmental defects in substations. This dataset contains 8,307 labeled images aimed at identifying and classifying various defects in substations using machine learning and computer vision techniques, such as equipment damage and abnormal behavior15,16. The dataset provides two annotation formats: XML and TXT, which can be directly used for training YOLO series object detection models. The dataset covers a variety of defect types in substation equipment, including but not limited to equipment damage and abnormal behavior. Detailed annotations: Each image comes with detailed annotation information, including the object category, bounding box coordinates, etc. Strong compatibility: The dataset provides both XML and TXT annotation formats, making it suitable for traditional XML-based annotation methods and convenient for direct use with the YOLO model. Dataset acquisition address: https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/720591905. In this study, a subset of 1,836 images was selected from the substation defect dataset according to predefined sample selection and annotation quality control criteria. First, only images belonging to nine common substation defect categories relevant to this work were retained, as shown in Table 1, while samples from other categories were excluded. Second, annotation validity was ensured through manual inspection, and samples with incomplete, inconsistent, or ambiguous annotations were removed. Third, image quality control was performed by discarding images with severe blur, heavy occlusion, or extreme illumination conditions to ensure that defect regions were visually distinguishable. Finally, highly similar images captured under nearly identical conditions were filtered out to reduce redundancy and dataset bias. After this quality control process, the remaining samples were used for training, validation, and testing. Nine common substation defect categories are: blurred dial, damaged dial, damaged meter housing, abnormal meter reading, color change in silica gel, damaged silica gel bucket, abnormal oil level, abnormal box door closure, and insulation breakage. Among these, blurred dial, damaged dial, damaged meter housing, and abnormal meter reading are meter-related defects, color change in silica gel, damaged silica gel bucket, and abnormal oil level are respirator-related defects, and abnormal box door closure and insulation breakage are box door-related defects and insulation-related defects, respectively. Small-sample Experimental Design

To verify the model’s performance under small-sample conditions, a “category-stratified sampling” strategy was adopted to construct the experimental scenario: Scarce defect categories: Select 3 defect types with the least samples in the dataset (insulation breakage: 326 images, silicone color change: 412 images, blurred dial: 508 images) as the target scarce categories. Sample size gradient: Set 4 small-sample gradients for the scarce categories: 5-shot (5 labeled samples per category), 10-shot (10 labeled samples per category), 20-shot (20 labeled samples per category), 50-shot (50 labeled samples per category). Auxiliary categories: The remaining 6 defect types are used as auxiliary categories, with all samples retained to simulate the actual scenario where “most categories are sufficient while individual categories are scarce”. Evaluation method: Use mIoU, DSC, ACC, Sen, Spe as evaluation metrics, and compare with classic small-sample learning methods (ProtoNet, MAML) and transfer learning methods (ResNet50 pre-training + U-Net fine-tuning) to verify the model’s small-sample adaptation ability.

Overview of ResMamba

Mamba theory

The State Space Model (SSM) originated from the classic Kalman filtering model17 and has gained significant attention in sequence modeling tasks in recent years. SSM models the dynamic evolution of the system through state equations and output equations. Its core lies in learning a reasonable state representation h(t) to effectively combine the input sequence to predict the output sequence. Specifically, SSM describes the internal state of the system’s evolution over time through the state equation and maps the hidden state to observable variables through the output equation, as shown in Eqs. A: State transition matrix, dimension D×D, value range [-1,1], governing the dynamic evolution of the state. B: Input projection matrix, dimension D×C, value range [-1,1], mapping input to state space. C: Output projection matrix, dimension C×D, value range [-1,1], mapping state to output. D: Direct transmission term, scalar, value range [0,1], representing the direct contribution of input to output. The core objective of SSM is to achieve end-to-end mapping from the input sequence to the output sequence through parameterizing A, B, C, D, while explicitly modeling the temporal continuity of the internal state of the system.

Gu et al. proposed the Structured State Space Model (S4) based on SSM. To better adapt to the environment of deep learning, S4 and Mamba discretized the continuous system18. Specifically, they used the Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) technique for sequence discretization, converting A and B into discrete parameters A and B. The principle is that the value is maintained until a new discrete signal is received. The duration of storing this value is represented by a new learnable parameter called the step size ∆. When there is a continuous signal as input, a continuous output can be generated, and the values are sampled only based on the time step of the input. This sampled value is the discretized output, which can be expressed as Eqs. (3) and (4). Parallel training is achieved through loops or convolutions, and the HiPPO technique is introduced to solve the problem of long-range dependencies, improving the storage and computing efficiency of the model.

Furthermore, to address the issue of long-distance dependencies, S4 incorporates HiPPO (High-Order Polynomial Projection Operator) technology, enabling the model to efficiently store and update information over long periods. The introduction of this technology significantly enhances the model’s storage efficiency while combining methods such as recurrent neural networks (RNN) or convolutional neural networks (CNN) for parallel computing, greatly improving training speed and computational efficiency19. Mamba, as an upgraded version of S4, further optimizes the computational process, reduces complexity, and enables higher efficiency and stronger expressiveness in long-sequence modeling tasks. Compared to S4, Mamba introduces a gating mechanism that controls the flow of information at different time steps, allowing key information to be more effectively propagated. Similar to the gating units in long short-term memory networks (LSTM), this mechanism enables the model to more flexibly model long-term dependencies. It combines the advantages of both RNNs and CNNs, can model sequence dependencies cyclically, and supports parallel computing, improving training speed. Let n denote the sequence length. In Transformer-based self-attention, pairwise interactions are computed between all tokens, resulting in a computational complexity of O(n²). In contrast, the proposed model adopts a Mamba-based selective state-space formulation, where the hidden state is updated recursively as h_t = A h_{t − 1} + B x_t. Each time step involves a constant amount of computation, leading to an overall complexity of O(n) with respect to sequence length n. Parallel scan operations further enable efficient training without increasing asymptotic complexity. At the same time, Mamba inherits the HiPPO technology from S4, optimizes the state update method, solves the issue of long-distance dependencies, and enables the model to maintain stable information storage capabilities over long sequences, achieving low computational cost and efficient parallel training20.

ResMamba architecture

This section provides a detailed introduction to the proposed ResMamba network model. As shown in Fig. 2, this model combines the Mamba structure with the U-Net encoder-decoder structure, adopting a symmetric six-level U-shaped design. The number of channels for each level is {8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64}, ensuring that the model can extract effective features at different scales21. After the input image is fed into the encoder part, it primarily extracts hierarchical features from the original image. The encoder structure is divided into six stages, using a progressive downsampling method. At each level, the number of channels in the feature map doubles, while the resolution is halved, gradually extracting more abstract and high-level feature information. In stages 1–3, a convolution module is used to extract shallow features, where each layer includes a standard 3 × 3 convolution kernel and a max-pooling operation. In stages 4–6, Mamba is embedded into the visual state space and integrated into the encoder-decoder. After each stage, the number of channels in the feature map doubles, while the resolution is halved. In the skip connection part, the skip features from each encoding stage are processed by the Spatial Attention Bridge Block (SAB) component. The decoder then receives features from the Channel Attention Bridge Block (CAB) and performs further feature extraction. The sequential arrangement of the two bridge attention modules follows the AB priority order, which gradually integrates multi-level information—first extracting features from the spatial dimension and then further strengthening key features from the channel dimension. This process generates more accurate spatial and channel attention maps, effectively improving the feature representation ability. In the overall architecture, the ResVSS structure is primarily used for deep feature extraction at the encoder end, drawing on the Visual Mamba structure and combining deep convolution, VSS modules, and residual connections to enhance the remote spatial modeling ability, thereby improving feature representation without adding additional computational costs. SAB is mainly used to enhance spatial feature expression by generating spatial attention maps that highlight the feature expression of spatial regions. CAB is mainly used to enhance channel feature expression by generating channel attention maps that adaptively adjust the channel weights. It further integrates the skip connection features processed by SAB and the deep features extracted by ResVSS, emphasizing important channels through channel attention and reducing the interference from redundant information. SAB and CAB modules are fused with the RM Layer in the skip connection, optimizing features from both the spatial and channel dimensions to maximize information utilization. Different modules work collaboratively at different stages to form the encoding-skip connection-decoding mechanism, effectively improving the global modeling ability and segmentation accuracy of the model22.

Residual Mamba layer

The Residual Mamba Layer (RM Layer) is a crucial encoding-decoding unit in the ResMamba network. Its core objective is to efficiently extract both global and local features, while alleviating the gradient vanishing problem in deep networks and enhancing the model’s learning capability23. Figure 3 illustrates the specific structure of the RM Layer. This module integrates the Residual Visual State Space (ResVSS) and the convolutional neural network, combining state space modeling to optimize feature representation. The ResVSS module is an important component of this architecture, primarily responsible for extracting the global features of the input data, which effectively captures the feature representations over a large range. After extracting the global features, a 3 × 3 convolution is applied to further explore the finer-grained local features in the data. Then, the batch normalization layer and the ReLU activation function are applied, adjusting the data distribution to a relatively stable range, accelerating the model’s convergence speed, and alleviating the gradient vanishing problem to some extent. The ReLU activation function enhances the non-linear expression ability of features and improves computational efficiency. Finally, the data is further processed through MaxPooling and skip connections. The MaxPooling operation reduces the dimensionality of the data through downsampling, removes redundant information, reduces computational costs, and prevents overfitting of the model. Skip connections enable the network to better share and fuse feature information between different layers, which helps improve the model’s performance and stability.

Residual visual state space Module

By using ResVSS for image depth semantic feature extraction, residual connections and adjustment factors are further employed to enhance the remote spatial modeling capability of SSM without introducing new parameters or increasing computational complexity. Firstly, local features of the image are extracted using deep convolution, maintaining channel independence while reducing the computational burden. Then, the VSS module is used to capture long-range spatial correlations. Finally, the nonlinearity and stability of the model are achieved through instance normalization and the ReLU activation function. Based on Visual Mamba, this study introduces the VSS block as the main component of the ResVSS architecture and incorporates it into two parallel branches. The key optimization of ResVSS compared to the original VSS module (from VMamba) is the removal of a redundant linear layer in the internal shortcut connection. The original VSS module’s shortcut connection contains two consecutive linear layers: the first is a dimension conversion layer (C→D), and the second is a feature mapping layer (D→C) with no adaptive parameter adjustment function, accounting for 38% of the original VSS module’s total parameters (0.042 M). Removing this redundant layer reduces the module’s parameters to 0.026 M, a compression ratio of 38.1%, and the overall model parameters are reduced by approximately 22%. In addition, ResVSS introduces a deep convolution block (3 × 3 grouped convolution) and learnable scale parameters α (value range [0.1, 1.0]) to dynamically scale residual connections, which is fundamentally different from VMamba’s fixed residual weights and LightM-UNet’s pure residual structure. Figure 4 shows the structure comparison between ResVSS and existing core modules. In the first branch of ResVSS, adding a deep convolution block after the linear layer improves the performance of the VSS block. Using the SiLU activation function enables more stable learning of complex features. Then, connecting the core module SSM and LayerNorm normalizes the different dimensions of the input features. In the second branch, linear layers are still used to expand the feature channels, after which the SiLU activation function is applied. Subsequently, the VSS module uses the Hadamard product to aggregate features from the two branches, ensuring that the input and output dimensions remain the same.

Spatial attention bridge

The SAB mainly operates on the spatial dimension and is used to learn the importance of the input feature map at different spatial positions. First, the input feature map undergoes maximum pooling (max F) and average pooling (avg F) operations to generate two channel features, which are then concatenated. Next, a dilation convolution with a dilation rate of 3 and a kernel size of 7 is applied to capture larger-scale spatial information. After that, a Sigmoid activation function is used to generate the spatial attention map for each stage. Results and discussion

Classification results

The evaluation metrics used in this study include the average intersection-over-union (mIoU), Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), accuracy (ACC), sensitivity (Sen), and specificity (Spe) to comprehensively assess the performance of different models27. mIoU is used to evaluate the degree of overlap between the model’s segmentation results and the true labels. DSC is used to measure the similarity between the ground truth and the predicted segmentation maps28. ACC is mainly used to calculate the percentage of correct classifications. Sen measures the percentage of true positives among all positives (true positives + false negatives). Spe measures the percentage of true negatives among all negatives (true negatives + false positives). Additionally, the parameter “Params” represents the number of parameters, with the unit in millions (M). The computational complexity is calculated based on the number of floating-point operations (GFLOPs). The model’s parameters and GFLOPs are measured using an input size of 256 × 256. The dataset is randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets with a ratio of 70% : 15% : 15%. The training set is used for model learning, the validation set for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping, and the test set is strictly reserved for final performance evaluation. The programming language used in this experiment is Python 3.8, the deep learning framework is PyTorch 2.0, and the CUDA version is 11.8. The processor is an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8255 C, and the graphics card is an NVIDIA RTX 3080 Ti. Training and testing are conducted on a Linux operating system. During training, AdamW is used as the optimizer, with an initial learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 8, and 300 epochs. Table 3 shows the detection results for different defect categories. To alleviate overfitting under limited sample conditions and improve model generalization, data augmentation was applied only to the training set during model training. The adopted augmentation operations include random horizontal and vertical flipping, random rotation within ± 15°, random scaling in the range of [0.9, 1.1], and brightness/contrast jittering. These augmentations preserve the structural characteristics of defect regions while introducing reasonable appearance variations. No data augmentation was applied to the validation or test sets.

Compared to methods such as U-Net, TransFuse, MALUNet, VM-Unet, VM-UNetV2, and LightM-UNet, ResMamba has the following advantages in the field of image segmentation:

Lightweight and efficient

ResMamba adopts the Mamba structure and introduces the ResVSS module as the core of the encoder, which effectively reduces the redundant parameters of the model by removing redundant linear layers. Compared to LightM-UNet, which also emphasizes lightweight design, ResMamba further reduces the computational burden during training and inference by optimizing the model structure34.

Multi-scale information fusion and enhancement

ResMamba introduces a multi-level multi-scale information fusion module, generating spatial and channel attention maps to enhance the fusion of multi-scale features. This enables the model to better capture detailed information and improve segmentation accuracy when processing defect images in complex scenarios. Compared to U-Net, although U-Net enhances multi-scale information through skip connections, it lacks the fine attention mechanism like ResMamba, which makes ResMamba more effective at handling complex and detailed images35.

Residual connections and learnable scale parameters

In residual connections, ResMamba combines learnable scale parameters, which can flexibly adjust and scale features, thereby improving segmentation accuracy. Compared to VM-Unet and VM-UNetV2, although these methods also apply visual information enhancement, ResMamba enhances feature representation through a more efficient residual connection design. This is especially advantageous when dealing with small sample defect images36.

Optimization for small sample problems

Traditional deep learning models, such as TransFuse, enhance the representation ability of images through the fusion of transformers and convolutional neural networks. However, they are prone to overfitting or poor performance when dealing with small sample defect images. ResMamba reduces redundant parameters and optimizes the network structure, enabling better adaptation to small sample cases and avoiding the risk of overfitting.

Balance between segmentation accuracy and performance

Experimental results on multiple public datasets show that ResMamba achieves a good balance between segmentation performance and parameter quantity, demonstrating high segmentation accuracy and low computational consumption. Compared to models such as MALUNet, ResMamba not only performs excellently in processing accuracy but also has a smaller model size and lower computational resource consumption, making it suitable for efficient deployment in practical applications.

From the ablation experiment data, it can be seen that after introducing the SAB and CAB modules based on U-Net, the model’s performance in the task of defect detection for power transmission equipment has improved. SAB and CAB enhance the feature representation ability in the spatial and channel dimensions, respectively. By constructing spatial attention maps and channel attention maps, the multi-scale feature fusion effect is improved. However, since these two modules mainly function during the feature extraction stage and do not deeply optimize the overall network architecture, the performance improvement is limited. On the other hand, SAB adopts dilated convolution and a dual pooling branch structure, while CAB uses fully connected layers to generate channel attention. Although this increases the number of parameters, it optimizes the information transmission path through the attention mechanism, reduces redundant computations, and halves the actual computational complexity. After further introducing the RM Layer, the model’s performance improvement becomes more significant. This module is based on the state-space model (SSM) and enhances the modeling ability of long-range dependencies through ResVSS in deep feature extraction, alleviating the gradient vanishing problem and improving the stability of defect recognition for power transmission equipment such as insulator damage, bushing cracks, and wire rust. The RM Layer adopts a grouped processing strategy, dividing the input features into four groups for parallel computation, reducing the number of channels to one-fourth, and compressing the parameter scale from C² to C²/4 through linear projection before SSM. This significantly strengthens the long-range context modeling ability with almost no change in computational cost. Moreover, this module dynamically generates discretized parameters through multiple layers of perception, replacing traditional static parameter storage, significantly reducing the parameter quantity while ensuring accuracy. When SAB, CAB, and the RM Layer are jointly embedded in U-Net, the model achieves optimal performance: SAB and CAB strengthen multi-scale feature fusion in the skip connection and suppress information loss; ResVSS simplifies the model structure by removing redundant linear layers, reducing parameters while enhancing the state-space modeling ability. Experimental results show that the improved model can achieve more complete and coherent defect region segmentation in power transmission equipment defect images, maintaining high detection robustness and accuracy in complex backgrounds, multi-scale defects, and noise interference environments37.

In the original VSS design, multiple linear projection layers are introduced inside shortcut connections to expand and compress feature dimensions. However, in visual segmentation tasks, we observe that these linear layers mainly serve channel transformation purposes and partially duplicate the function of surrounding convolutional operations. Therefore, in ResVSS, we remove the redundant linear layers in the internal residual paths and retain only the core state-space modeling module. This simplification reduces parameter redundancy and computational overhead, while preserving the long-range dependency modeling capability of the VSS block. Discussion

In recent years, deep learning–based methods have been extensively investigated for industrial defect detection and segmentation due to their ability to automatically extract discriminative features and improve inspection efficiency. A number of systematic reviews have summarized trends and challenges in this area, highlighting the broad applications of convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures for surface defect inspection across various industrial domains38,39. Furthermore, industrial defect detection has increasingly leveraged deep learning–based semantic segmentation models to automatically identify and localize defects in diverse industrial environments such as weld seams, transmission lines, and surface structures. U-Net and its variants continue to serve as core architectures in many defect segmentation studies due to their balanced trade-off between localization accuracy and architectural simplicity. For example, deep learning methods based on U-Net and attention mechanisms have been applied for weld line detection (Lukács et al.40 and two-stage YOLOv5s–U-Net frameworks have been proposed for defect localization and segmentation in overhead transmission lines (Li et al.41. These works demonstrate that encoder–decoder architectures with multi-scale feature representation can effectively capture defect structures across scales, which is critical for industrial inspection applications. While numerous industrial defect detection approaches are rooted in convolutional encoder–decoder designs, a growing trend in deep learning research is the exploration of alternative backbone architectures that can better balance computational efficiency and long-range dependency modeling. The Mamba architecture, grounded in selective structured state space models (SSMs), offers linear computational scaling with sequence length compared to the quadratic scaling of traditional self-attention mechanisms, and has demonstrated competitive performance in vision tasks such as semantic segmentation and object detection, while also achieving improved computation and memory efficiency relative to Vision Transformers42. Such properties make Mamba and its variants attractive candidates for resource-constrained and high-resolution visual tasks.

In this study, the proposed method is designed with the objective of achieving accurate defect segmentation under limited training data and computational constraints, which are common in practical substation inspection scenarios. Building upon the widely adopted U-Net encoder–decoder framework, this work introduces lightweight Mamba-based residual components to enhance feature modeling capacity while maintaining a compact parameter budget. First, the integration of ResVSS blocks enables the model to capture long-range contextual dependencies beyond the receptive field of standard convolutional layers. Unlike self-attention mechanisms that incur quadratic computational complexity with respect to spatial resolution, the state-space formulation employed in Mamba allows linear-complexity sequence modeling. This property is particularly beneficial for defect segmentation tasks involving elongated structures or spatially dispersed defect regions, while remaining computationally efficient. Second, the removal of redundant linear projection layers within the ResVSS design contributes to a noticeable reduction in model parameters without sacrificing representational capacity. This structural simplification is consistent with the lightweight design goal of the proposed network and supports stable training even with a relatively small number of parameters. Combined with residual connections, the network maintains effective gradient propagation and convergence behavior during training. Furthermore, the introduction of learnable scale parameters in residual paths allows the network to adaptively modulate feature magnitudes at different stages, providing finer control over information flow during feature fusion. This design improves flexibility in handling defects of varying sizes and contrasts, which is particularly important in substation images where defect appearances can be subtle and heterogeneous. In addition, the proposed SAB and CAB are incorporated to enhance multi-scale feature fusion. By operating on spatial and channel dimensions separately, these attention modules selectively emphasize informative regions and discriminative feature channels across different resolution levels. This mechanism improves the robustness of the model to background clutter and illumination variations commonly encountered in real substation environments.

Overall, the proposed architecture balances accuracy, efficiency, and robustness through a combination of state-space modeling, lightweight residual design, and attention-guided feature fusion. Compared with conventional U-Net variants and recent lightweight segmentation models, the method demonstrates improved segmentation performance under small-sample conditions while maintaining low computational cost, making it suitable for practical industrial defect inspection applications.

Conclusion

This study addresses the precise segmentation requirements for image defect detection of power transmission equipment under conditions of small sample sizes for specific categories and proposes a lightweight segmentation network named ResMamba based on the Mamba architecture. By deeply integrating VSS and the U-shaped encoding-decoding structure, and combining residual connections with learnable scale factors, the network enhances its ability to model remote spatial information. As a result, the feature expression capability of the model is significantly improved without increasing its parameters or computational complexity. The multi-stage and multi-scale information fusion mechanism, designed for the skip connection, effectively integrates cross-scale spatial and channel attention, enhancing the model’s performance in perceiving various defect specifications. Experimental results on the public dataset of power transmission equipment defect images show that ResMamba achieves superior segmentation performance compared to existing mainstream methods, with extremely low parameters (0.134 M) and computational costs (0.067 GFLOPs). The results include mIoU (87.96%), DSC (91.23%), ACC (97.67%), Sen (92.34%), and Spe (98.63%), Specialized small-sample experiments (5-shot, 10-shot, 20-shot, 50-shot) and cross-scenario generalization tests verify that ResMamba has excellent performance and strong adaptability under small-sample conditions, demonstrating good robustness and accuracy in various typical defect tasks, such as insulation rupture, dial damage, and abnormal oil levels. ResMamba provides a reliable new solution for small-sample defect detection of power equipment, which is conducive to promoting the intelligent operation and maintenance of power systems and enhancing the self-healing ability of power grids. Future research will focus on expanding the defect category coverage of the dataset and optimizing the model’s real-time performance in edge device deployment.

Literature review: Current trends and future prospects of digital vaccine supply chain support technology
Authors: Wenxue Ran, Zhaoxia Li, Yun Xue, Dandan He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe digital vaccine supply chain (DVSC) harnesses modern information technology to track, monitor, and manage the entire vaccine process in real-time, ensuring vaccine quality and safety while enhancing the transparency and efficiency of the supply chain (SC). This paper undertakes a systematic literature review of research papers on DVSC support technology, adopting the Bibliometric-Systematic Literature Review (B-SLR) method. It addresses the gaps in previous research in this area and offers new ideas and perspectives for further applications. The results suggest that the DVSC can gain advantages from the perspective of supporting technology and identify research opportunities for the digital-technology community. Further research in this field holds promise, and we propose intriguing research directions. This study clearly presents the research theoretical framework of DVSC support technology. We expect that, to some extent, it can predict the future development direction of DVSCs.KEYWORDS: Supply chain management, vaccine, digital vaccine supply chain, digital technology, public health

Introduction

In recent years, the global public health system (PHS) has been confronted with severe challenges due to multiple outbreaks of infectious diseases worldwide, the accelerating aging of the global population, and the economic development and improved medical standards in emerging market countries. However, the vaccine supply chain (VSC) has unique characteristics: high uncertainty in supply and demand; misalignment of goals and decentralized decision-making among suppliers, public health organizations, and end customers; The crucial importance of complex political decisions regarding allocation and timely decision-making and action.1 Moreover, the three main issues in the VSC – vaccine quality, demand forecasting, and trust among stakeholders – persist, leading to low efficiency.2 People also know very little about the impact of digital technology on self-efficacy in responding to public health crises.3

It is worth noting that VSC practitioners are experimenting with available technologies to address supply, demand, and behavioral challenges.4 Although these highly correlated intelligent and interconnected health technologies, such as the Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI) (including machine learning (ML) and big data analytics), as well as robots and blockchain, cannot address the issues related to pandemics overnight and may not be the only solution to crises, they can provide a deeper understanding of diseases and support frontline work in preventing and controlling pandemics.5 International organizations such as the World Health Organization (WHO) are also actively promoting the digital vaccine supply chains (DVSC) by formulating relevant standards and norms to facilitate collaboration and cooperation within the global VSC. For instance, the WHO released the “Global Strategy on Digital Health (2020–2025)” in 2020, aiming to guide countries in leveraging digital technologies to improve health services, including the management of VSCs. Governments of various countries have introduced relevant policies to support the development of DVSCs and promote the research and application of related technologies. For example, in the “Outline of the 14th Five Year Plan for National Economic and Social Development of the People’s Republic of China and the Long Range Objectives by 2035,” the Chinese government clearly emphasizes the need to vigorously cultivate and expand emerging digital industries such as AI, big data, and blockchain, and carry out pilot demonstrations in key areas such as smart healthcare.

Currently, there exists a substantial body of review studies on VSCs. These studies predominantly encompass three key aspects: research on the integration and design of SCs, the resilience of SCs and their response to disruptions, and the application and optimization of emerging technologies. (Table 1 offers a comprehensive summary of the review studies within this domain.) In the realm of SC integration and design research, Yadav et al.6 put forward a decision-making framework for the integration of VSCs with other health-commodity SCs. Lemmens et al.7 delved into the applicability of model-based design within VSCs. By comparing the similarities and differences with other pharmaceutical SCs, they furnished a theoretical foundation for the integration and design of VSCs. Duijzer et al.1 in alignment with the WHO’s priorities for establishing a flexible and resilient VSC, identified the distinct characteristics of VSCs. When it comes to SC resilience and response to disruptions, Golan et al.8 conducted an in-depth analysis of the existing gaps in general SC resilience modeling, with a particular emphasis on VSC resilience modeling. In the field of emerging technology application research, Arora et al.9 concentrated on the application potential of AI in COVID-19-related scenarios. Their work explored how AI could be harnessed to improve vaccine-related research, such as in vaccine development, demand forecasting, and distribution optimization. Ng et al.10 and Fiore et al.11 both investigated the application of blockchain technology within the health-care domain. Their findings indicated the latent value of blockchain in VSC management, such as enhancing the transparency and traceability of vaccine distribution. Regarding optimization research, Lopes et al.12 explored the application of optimization methods in VSCs and logistics based on multiple studies. Although they discovered that research on vaccine inventory management and logistics optimization was lacking, their work was still valuable as it paved the way for subsequent optimization research.

Recent literature review studies on VSC.

Although review studies on DVSCs have been conducted in the past, with the first reviews focusing on the applications of AI and blockchain in VSCs emerging around 2021, existing research related to digitalization remains predominantly focused on single technologies. These studies fail to explore the synergistic application of multiple technologies, such as the integration of AI + blockchain + IoT across the entire VSC. Additionally, insufficient efforts have been made to link digital technologies with specific segments of VSCs. Consequently, a comprehensive panorama of the current trends and future prospects of enabling technologies for DVSCs has not yet been presented. To address these gaps, we conducted this review to address the following research questions:

Which digital technologies are of significant importance to the development of VSCs?

What are the current trends in enabling technologies for DVSCs?

What are the future prospects of digital technologies in VSCs?

To address the aforementioned research questions, we conducted a systematic literature review based on the Bibliometric-Systematic Literature Review (B-SLR) method. In this study, the Web of Science (WoS) was selected as the primary database for comprehensive data extraction, while Scopus was utilized as an auxiliary database to cross-validate the accuracy and integrity of the final data. The focus of our review and discussion centered on DVSCs. Our aim was to systematically organize the current status of literature on DVSCs and identify the present trends and future prospects of DVSCs. In addition, the B-SLR method also has limitations, such as high methodological complexity and technical requirements, constraints related to data quality and database coverage, the difficulty in completely eliminating subjective judgments, and uncertainty regarding theoretical contributions. Therefore, on the basis of fully recognizing the advantages and limitations of this method, this paper reasonably applies the B-SLR method in combination with specific research questions and practical situations, aiming to improve the quality and effectiveness of the research.

This research is expected to clearly present the research framework of DVSC support technology. We hope that, to some extent, it can provide insights into the future development direction of DVSCs, offer entry points for subsequent research, and furnish valuable references for relevant parties such as vaccine manufacturers, logistics service providers, centers for disease control and prevention, end-point medical institutions, and scientific research departments, thereby guiding their decision-making and practices.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the review methods. Subsequently, the results of this paper are presented, covering bibliometric analysis and the theoretical framework of DVSC support technology. Section 4 is dedicated to the discussion. Section 5 elaborates on the future prospects and challenges. The final part presents the conclusions and limitations of the research.

Review methodology

In this study, we used B-SLR to focus on research related to DVSCs, and in the review, we followed the stages and guidelines established by Marzi et al.13 The specific process is as follows (see Figure 1):

The framework for studying DVSC support technologies using the B-SLR method.

Research questions

Informal literature scanning

Initially, we perused the classic and recently highly cited papers in the field of DVSCs, such as the research by Arora, Ng, Fiore, and others,9–11 to gain an understanding of the core concepts, key issues, and research hotspots in this area. By retrospectively examining the cited literature and prospectively searching for new studies that cite these papers, we gradually expanded the scope of the literature and determined the research direction to be the current trends and future prospects of DVSC support technology.

Defining research questions

After preliminary scanning, although existing research has focused on the application of digital technologies in VSCs, the panorama of DVSC support technology has not been fully presented. Thus, the research questions were defined as: Which digital technologies are of significant importance to the development of VSCs? What are the current trends in enabling technologies for DVSCs? What are the future prospects of digital technologies in VSCs?

Defining research boundaries

We clearly defined the research focus on the application of digital technologies in VSCs, excluding traditional SC management technologies and non-vaccine-related digital supply chain research. The research time-frame was set for the past 10 y (considering the rapid development of digital technologies in this field, literature from the past 10 y can better reflect the current trends). The literature type was restricted to academic journal articles to ensure the scientificity and reliability of the research.

Search strategy

Decomposing research questions into concepts

The research questions were decomposed into key concepts such as “digital support technologies” (including specific technologies like the IoTs, blockchain, ML, AI, etc.), “VSC links” (production, distribution, storage, traceability, etc.), “SC,” “management,” and “public health emergencies.”

Identifying keywords and considering variants

Based on the above-mentioned concepts, initial keywords were determined, such as “digital technology AND vaccine supply chain,” “IoT AND vaccine distribution,” “blockchain AND vaccine traceability,” etc. Meanwhile, variants of keywords were considered, for example, “digitalization” and “digitization” are synonyms, and “vaccine logistics” is related to “VSC.”

Constructing search strings

Boolean operators were used to construct search strings, and preliminary searches were conducted in two databases (Scopus and WoS). The search strings were continuously adjusted and optimized according to the search results to ensure comprehensive retrieval of relevant literature. Finally, our search keywords are combined as (“vaccine”) AND (“digitization” OR “digital” OR “blockchain” OR “IOT” OR “AI” OR “ML”) AND (“supply chain” OR “management”).

Considering database characteristics

Scopus and WoS are commonly used databases in management research, both providing extensive literature resources. Vieira et al.14 found through the analysis of research achievements of typical universities in Portugal that there is a certain complementarity between the two databases in terms of the scope of covered journals. Scopus covers more disciplinary fields, including a large number of social science and engineering and technology literatures, while WoS is renowned for its high-quality journal collection and powerful citation indexing function, which helps to trace the academic context of research.

Pilot extraction and evaluation

Pilot extractions were carried out in the two databases using the constructed search strings, respectively. By comparing the extraction results, it was found that Scopus had more literature related to digital technologies than WoS, but the proportion of highly cited literature in WoS was relatively higher.

Determining database selection strategy

After comprehensive consideration, WoS was selected as the main database for comprehensive data extraction, and Scopus was used as an auxiliary database for cross-validating the accuracy and integrity of the final data. Meanwhile, to obtain high-quality relevant literature, we focused on studying the Social Science Citation Index (SSCI) and Science Citation Index Extended (SCIE) literature in the WoS index. Because SCIE not only includes high-quality academic journals in the global natural science field but also provides a network of citation relationships between these literatures, allowing researchers to track academic progress in specific fields and evaluate the impact and importance of research results. SSCI focuses on indexing academic journals and papers in the field of social sciences. Similar to SCIE, SSCI also provides a network of citation relationships between these literatures.

Data analysis

Data screening

Based on the search strings, a large number of literatures were initially retrieved from the WoS database. And according to the pre-defined research boundaries, the criteria for literature inclusion are as follows:

Articles and reviews published in the following subject categories: Immunology, Multidisciplinary Science, Healthcare Science Services, Management, Business, Economics, Transportation, Mathematics, and Computer Science.

Language: English

Source type: Journal

All non-academic journal articles, conference subject categories: papers, reports, letters, editorial materials, chapters, recensions, and summaries of the meeting are included in the exclusion criteria, and duplicate articles need to be removed.

After the above steps, 227 articles were obtained.

Data cross-checking

The literature records screened in the WoS database were retrieved again in the Scopus database using the same search strings to check the consistency of the literature in the two databases. Key information such as the number of citations, authors, and publication years of the literature was focused on. If any differences were found, further verification was carried out to determine the accurate information.

Manual literature screening

The literatures extracted from the WoS database were manually screened one by one according to the research questions and inclusion/exclusion criteria. The titles, abstracts, and keywords of the literatures were carefully read to preliminarily judge their relevance. The “A/B/C logic” was adopted to classify the literatures. The literatures that are highly relevant to the research questions and provide key data or viewpoints were marked as category “A” (particularly relevant); the literatures that may be relevant to the research questions but require further analysis or confirmation were marked as category “B” (potentially relevant); and the literatures that are clearly irrelevant were marked as category “C” (irrelevant). For category “B” literatures, the research team members were organized for discussion, and according to the discussion results, they were finally classified into category “A” or category “C.” After reading the titles and abstracts in line with the review objectives, we removed the irrelevant and low-relevance literatures and obtained 123 articles.

For the literatures that were difficult to determine, the full-text was further read for evaluation. After reading the full-text in line with the review objectives, the total number of articles ultimately selected for B-SLR is 76, the PRISMA flow diagram is shown in Figure 2.

The PRISMA flow diagram.

Data export

After completing the literature screening, the finally determined literatures were exported from the WoS database, ensuring that the data contains key information of the literatures, such as titles, authors, abstracts, keywords, publication years, journal names, and the number of citations. At the same time, all the initially retrieved literature records (including the screened-out literatures) were saved as supplementary materials, and the decision-making basis during the screening process was recorded for possible subsequent review or further analysis.

Bibliometric analysis

In this study, the co-citation analysis function of VOSviewer was used to analyze the citation relationships between literatures, identify the research achievements with significant influence in the field of DVSCs and their interrelationships. Meanwhile, through keyword co-occurrence analysis, the research hotspots and topic trends in this field were revealed.

Defining themes through in-depth literature reading

For each cluster generated by the VOSviewer analysis, the research team members were organized to independently read the literatures included in it (at least read the abstracts, and if the cluster size is small, read the full-text) and deeply understand the research themes represented by each cluster.

Discussing and determining cluster labels

After the research team members proposed theme labels for each cluster, respectively, a discussion meeting was organized to compare, merge, and optimize the labels. Ensure that each cluster label accurately and clearly reflects the core research content of the literatures it contains, while avoiding duplication or confusion between labels. During the process of determining the labels, the comprehensibility and universality of the labels were fully considered so that readers can intuitively understand the research fields represented by the clusters.

Adjusting VOS viewer parameters to optimize the analysis

According to the actual situation of clustering, starting from the default settings in VOSviewer, gradually adjust the resolution (increase or decrease) and observe the changes in clustering results, so as to achieve the effect that each cluster has high homogeneity internally and there are obvious differences between different clusters. Finally, the settings of VOSviewer are as follows: extract terms from the fields of abstracts and keywords, ignore structured abstract labels and copyright statements, and adopt the binary counting method; set a minimum occurrence of 6 times for a term, among 2856 terms, 69 meet this threshold; from these 69 terms, select the 41 most relevant terms based on the relevance score, and finally manually screen out 6 weakly relevant terms, namely thing, issue, help, term, paper, and world.

Theoretical framework of DVSC support technology

The theoretical framework of supporting technologies for the DVSC (see Figure 5) integrates a variety of advanced technologies and management concepts to ensure the safety, efficiency, and traceability of the entire vaccine chain from production to vaccination. This framework consists of five components: technical direction, core business, management concept, technical foundation, and participating entities. In current literature, research on supporting technologies mainly focuses on four directions: end-to-end integration, intelligence, super automation, and trusted circulation. Among them, end-to-end integration encompasses VSC integration and VSC coordination; intelligence includes intelligent prediction, vaccine hesitancy management, and vaccine distribution; super automation involves digital space automation and physical space automation. The core business corresponds to the specific businesses under the technical direction. The management concept clarifies the specific implementation methods and principles for each core business. The technical foundation covers AI, ML, IoT, blockchain, 5 G, big data, cloud computing, digital twin, digital image, encryption technology, etc. Participating entities include vaccine manufacturers, logistics service providers, centers for disease control and prevention, end-point medical institutions, scientific research departments, etc.

Theoretical framework of DVSC support technology.

There are relationships of technical foundation support, technical direction guidance, and participating entity collaboration among these five components. The technologies in the technical foundation provide underlying support for the upper-level technical direction, core business, and management concept. The technical direction defines the scope of the core business and guides its development direction. The core business conducts specific operations under the guidance of the management concept, which elaborates in detail how to use technologies to achieve the goals of the core business. Participating entities operate in coordination within the above-mentioned technical and business framework. Through the mutual cooperation of all parts, this framework enables the efficient operation of the VSC.

End-to-end integration

The digital transformation of the VSC, end-to-end integration remains the primary focus. With the completion of point applications in the SC, literature research has increasingly focused on optimizing the integration of the SC, that is, under the guidance of market demand, integrating digital tools throughout the entire process of design, planning, manufacturing, delivery, and operation. Currently, many studies are focused on the integration and coordination of VSC.

VSC integration

Establishing a disaster SC structure within an institutional framework and integrating Industry 4.0 information and digital technology into it can be used for vaccine distribution, monitoring, regulation, and effective implementation.15 By leveraging a smart VSC management architecture that incorporates blockchain, IoT, and ML, trust among stakeholders is ensured through blockchain’s transparency, vaccine quality is safeguarded by IoT’s real-time monitoring, and vaccine requirement forecasting and sentiment analysis for vaccine reviews are facilitated by ML.2 The implementation of mobile/network-based digital information platforms in two regions of Uttar Pradesh, India, can improve and stabilize SC performance.16 An information physical platform framework that applies Internet of Everything and Digital Twin technologies can promote information fusion and provide intelligent services for different stakeholders in cold chain logistics.17 A modular and sustainable SC integration optimization design is proposed for the safety supervision mechanism of Class II vaccines. This design has deep traceability and disintermediation, can track and monitor the quality of each stage of the entire SC, and has features such as intelligence, scalability, and compatibility.18 Unlike most commercially available clinical trial information platforms that are often protocol-driven, the Division of AIDS Enterprise Infrastructure of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases in the United States is designed to exchange information with these platforms, assisting scientific project leaders in guiding research efforts and keeping pace with the complex and ever-evolving global HIV/AIDS pandemic.19 Emerging network science tools such as AI, stress testing, and Digital Twins will help support optimal operational performance after disruptions in response to regulatory, economic, and other threats to VSCs.8

VSC coordination

At present, the global spread of COVID-19 is causing a heavy blow to the lives of people worldwide. To cope with this serious pandemic, it is urgent to improve and strengthen the coordination of VSCs.20 Blockchain technology is considered an effective solution to vaccine safety, but from the perspectives of SC operations and consumers, how to build blockchain is a topic that needs to be discussed. Vaccine manufacturers and vaccination units jointly undertake blockchain construction by signing cost sharing contracts. When certain conditions are met, the two tariff contracts can achieve blockchain based VSC coordination.21 When the proportion of problematic vaccines is low, blockchain based VSC coordination can also be achieved by signing revenue sharing contracts or two-part tariff contracts between two vaccine manufacturers and vaccination units.22 Meanwhile, when the profit sharing ratio meets certain conditions, the fixed fee scheme is more conducive to coordinating the VSC than the proportional fee scheme.23 After studying factors such as the number of vaccinated individuals, positive and side effects of vaccines, vaccine attenuation rates, revenue sharing/cost sharing ratios, and commission ratios, the VSC should estimate the difficulty of consumers obtaining certified vaccine sources and the size of production planning and demand forecasting errors before adopting a combination of AI and blockchain technology.20 The study of the impact of blockchain technology on the regulation of Class II vaccines, as well as the potential driving factors of reliability theory and fuzzy dynamic decision-making methods, provides theoretical basis and technical support for embedding blockchain information and communication technology into multi-agent collaborative regulation of Class II vaccines.18

Intelligence

Many studies in the literature focus on the intelligent direction of DVSC based on traditional ML applications, including intelligent prediction, vaccine hesitation management, and vaccine distribution.

Intelligent prediction

ML has become an important asset in life sciences and medicine.24 Based on the overall framework of methods in ML and optimization, the composition of influenza vaccine specific to a region or a country can be specified according to the historical data on the transmission rate of major viruses, so as to reduce the incidence rate and mortality.25 The creation of a unified database FluPRIMT to explore the cellular and molecular basis of successful antibody responses to influenza vaccines has promoted the application of ML algorithms in data mining.26 ML models can accurately predict seasonal antigen changes of influenza A (H3N2) using genetic data.27 The LBCEPred model, an ML approach for predicting linear B-cell epitopes, utilizes a random forest classifier combined with statistical moment-based descriptors to forecast B-cell epitopes within protein sequences.28 Variable learning rates can be used to train artificial neural networks and predict binding/non binding agents for small datasets.29 A hybrid model for drug development recommendation that combines association rule learning, collaborative filtering, and content-based filtering methods can be used to recommend and/or predict drug groups suitable for development by a single pharmaceutical company.30 The construction of distributed lag inspired ML can be used to predict changes in COVID-19 hospitalization and intensive care unit admission rates caused by vaccines.31 By using a novel ML-based aggregation method, researchers create a single prediction called MIT-Cassandra, which helps decision-makers predict how COVID-19 cases and true prevalence rates will develop in different regions in the coming months.32 Based on stacked ensemble learning, optimized through genetic algorithm, it can be used for epidemic prediction and health decision support processes to accurately predict the outbreak of a pandemic.33 As the acute phase of the pandemic comes to an end, the biggest challenge currently faced by most governments is the lack of tools to demonstrate citizens’ immune status and the risk of COVID-19 virus transmission related infections. A distributed Information technology tool, VPassport, can assist in large-scale test execution/result management.34

By utilizing a large number of COVID-19 vaccine recipients, ML methods can be used to provide high-precision personalized predictions of the most common adverse side effects after injection of six different COVID-19 vaccines based on personal and health-related characteristics.35 In terms of vaccine administration performance, a hybrid approach consisting of data envelopment analysis and ensemble ML methods can be used to predict vaccine efficiency and understand the importance of variables in the prediction.36 Rule based ML techniques, namely association rule mining, can identify common symptoms and define patterns in the discovered rules.37 A key part of vaccine development is phase III clinical trials, where a novel, accurate, strategy driven ML model, DELPHI, can evaluate the safety and efficacy of vaccines in preventing COVID-19.38 A two-stage optimization mathematical model and popular supervised ML method for designing a COVID-19 VSC network and estimating COVID-19 vaccine demand under uncertain demand and delivery times.39 To help response agencies prepare for recent traffic patterns, an innovative traffic prediction model based on a combination of ML and graph theory can be used.40 Daily access to individual level vaccine data from the National Immunization Management System enables UK Health Security Agency to quickly and accurately estimate vaccine coverage and provide the world’s first vaccine efficacy estimate.41

Vaccine hesitancy management

Since the first batch of vaccines were introduced, hesitation has been a major issue – the WHO considers it one of the most serious global health threats.42 Heterogeneity analysis based on ML on different information responses shows significant differences in vaccine hesitancy among countries.43 A framework combining laboratory experiments, crowdsourcing, and ML was introduced to evaluate whether the low vaccination rate of COVID-19 vaccines in the United States was attributed to misinformation on social media, and the answer was negative.44 The use of supervised non parametric ML techniques (random forest) has examined that the success of vaccination will depend on two dimensions: a) addressing a broader range of risk perception factors, not just health-related issues and b) gaining sufficient social and institutional trust at the beginning of vaccination campaigns.45 Using ML methods to train several classifiers, it is possible to identify the author’s possible gender (female or male) and the stance of specific posts on the COVID-19 vaccine (neutral, in favor or against).46 Using models such as naive bayes, logistic regression, support vector machine, decision tree, and k-nearest neighbor ML classifiers, sentiment analysis can be performed to determine the popularity of each vaccine47; It can also be used to analyze discussions on social media to identify hotspots during disease outbreaks, providing health policy information for the planning and management of vaccine hesitancy in Africa.48 Applying ML algorithms and network analysis to extract hidden and latent patterns from unstructured data, and developing a COVID-19 vaccine hesitancy amplification model based on social media big data samples, can help communication strategists and stakeholders design effective communication strategies using digital platforms.49 Long short-term memory and recurrent neural network models outperform traditional ML models in detecting vaccine hesitancy in social media.42

Vaccine distribution

During the pandemic, distributing vaccines fairly is a serious challenge, and preferences between governments and suppliers may make the problem even more complex.39 In the digital age, operations can be improved through the wise use of information and technological tools.50 The adoption of the IoTs has a positive impact on VSC in distributing COVID-19 vaccines, and there is a positive correlation between vaccine products, supply, demand, and social behavior during adoption. 4. A meta heuristic optimization method that can be used for classical simulation optimization settings and utilizes simulation models in feedback loops to address the significant challenges of distributing newly developed vaccines and reduce the negative impact of pandemics.51

Using deep learning, ML, digital image processing, and cloud solutions to address the increasing number of COVID-19 cases and their priority allocation for vaccination.52 By combining COVID-19 cases, predicting and determining the true prevalence rate, and incorporating this prevalence rate into an optimized model, vaccines can be effectively and fairly distributed within operational constraints.32 With the help of the SC, the safe and reliable distribution of vaccines can be ensured through blockchain technology, where any faults in the chain can be identified by the trust value of the function point method and the value of the Markov chain.53 By using ML algorithms to determine customer priority based on their value for vaccine allocation, and optimizing waiting queues based on mathematical models to reduce customer waiting time, loyal customer satisfaction can be improved and customer churn can be prevented.54 By combining geospatial data analysis and AI methods, assess vulnerability factors at the local level and allocate vaccines based on updated vulnerability and dynamic mechanisms of vaccination.55 Due to potential obstacles, the full adoption of blockchain technology in vaccine distribution and management has not yet been achieved. The most prominent obstacle is the requirement for organizational structure and policy change, while the most influential obstacle is the need for large-scale IoT infrastructure and a lack of technical expertise.56

In addition, in any pandemic situation, effective distribution and timely delivery of vaccines to the public require reliable and efficient vaccine cold chain management. By implementing the ML tool “k-means clustering” algorithm, it emphasizes that factors such as cold chain infrastructure, vaccine storage guidelines, and availability of skilled professionals are most important for this cold chain management.57 The IoT has ushered in a paradigm shift in vaccine information and storage monitoring, achieving 24/7 continuous tracking.58 A quality management platform based on edge cloud blockchain and Internet of Everything can achieve low latency and fast response for sensor data collection, authentication, consistency, and transparency in cold chain logistics.59 Government supervision will promote the improvement of the logistics market environment, society, and governance equipped with digital twin services, and the public will become the driving force for cold chain logistics service providers to refuse to invest in digital twin services.60

Super automation

Many studies in the literature are driving the DVSC to achieve SC business automation through the combination of multiple technologies, thereby improving the digital and physical space efficiency of the VSC. Building automation applications based on technologies such as AIe and business process optimization in digital space automation; Promote the integration and collaboration of new mobile logistics devices and logistics management software in physical space automation.

Digital space automation

The application of AI technology in the VSC is meaningful when the difficulty of consumers obtaining certified vaccine sources and the error terms of production plans and demand forecasts meet specific intervals.20 AI mainly focuses on medical image detection, genomics, drug development, and dissemination prediction, so it still has great potential in this field.61 The use of AI can support clinical diagnosis of patients to determine how each cytokine contributes to the severity of COVID-19, which may lead to the identification of several cytokines that can aid in treatment decision-making and vaccine development.17 A unified pandemic management architecture, which uses the IoTs and edge computing paradigm to automatically put forward suggestions on vaccine distribution, dynamic locking, mobile scheduling and pandemic trend prediction.62 Use Google DeepMind’s AI to closely monitor features and reduce the quantity and dissemination of visual content related to vaccine misinformation.63

With the help of intelligent IoT, AI, ML, and 5 G technology, cloud solutions and mobile applications, as well as personal phones and personal data, can be used for self-diagnosis, self-monitoring, and self-management of COVID-19.64 Without the need for event processing and smart contract language experts, the development environment integrating blockchain with complex event processing can automatically invoke smart contracts when event pattern conditions are met.65 A novel multi task speech corpus for COVID-19 research, utilizing classic ML methods and deep learning techniques to provide benchmark tests, facilitates monitoring of general infectious diseases.66 Utilizing the functionality of smart contracts in the Ethereum blockchain to govern and manage interactions between senders and receivers, for managing transportation conditions, automatic payments, legitimizing receivers, and refunding in case of violation of predefined conditions.67 By training a support vector model classifier based on behavioral features, an automatic malware detection system is developed to improve the accuracy of automatic detection.68 Introduce a decision model for evaluating and making decisions on IoT platforms suitable for the logistics and transportation process of COVID-19 vaccines, in order to select and deploy the correct IoT platform.69 By making intelligent decisions based on AI to address the spread of COVID-19 suspects with different symptoms, and observing and monitoring them to see the severity of symptoms.70 Although existing data-driven architectures operate in specific application domains and attempt to achieve decision automation, they have not captured the multifaceted interactions between computational models, communication infrastructure, and data.62

Physical space automation

Although there is limited evidence on the impact of digital interventions on health outcomes, the digitization of the Last Mile Electronic Logistics Management Information System can reduce child mortality by increasing coverage of life-saving hygiene products.71 In response to the issue of damage during vaccine transportation, a blockchain driven intelligent container infrastructure (CryptoCargo) can monitor the condition of goods and detect any violations that may damage their contents.72 The comprehensive electronic immunization registration logistics management information framework, including inventory notifications, can reduce the probability of vaccine shortages over time 16. Using a mobile application as the user interface, communicating through Arduino and message queue telemetry transmission, integrating temperature sensors, and employing a mini portable cooling box for universal delivery of drugs/vaccines.73 The unmanned aerial vehicle communication network monitored by the ground controller station provides 5 G haptic internet based services to reduce the round trip delay of the vaccine production warehouse.74 Wearable devices can provide an objective and continuous method for predicting and monitoring moderate to severe vaccine side effects, which may improve clinical trials by automatically classifying vaccine severity.75 The digital near field communication pendant worn on the black line and voice call reminder mechanism improved the adherence of vaccine recipients to Diphtheria, Tetanus, and Pertussis Vaccine within 1 month after Diphtheria, Tetanus, and Pertussis Vaccine administration.76 The South African Department of Health has launched the inventory visibility solution, a mobile application aimed at capturing and monitoring drug inventory levels, including vaccines, using mobile phones.77

Trusted circulation

Due to the sensitivity of data in the VSC and the common issues of authenticity, correctness, and transparency in data exchange, trustworthy circulation technology can be used to ensure the authenticity and secure exchange of SC data. Many literature study the layout of digital transformation in key technologies such as blockchain technology and data control technology.

A transparent, immutable, and secure VSC can improve the overall performance, safety, and efficiency of VSC management.78 An effective VSC mechanism needs to be established to address issues such as counterfeit vaccines, reduced vaccine waste, and falsified vaccine records. A blockchain based VSC that ensures the correctness, transparency, credibility, and immutability of vaccines, improving the efficiency of vaccine distribution in the cold chain.79 A model called “VaccineChain” is a new type of secure VSC based on checkpoint assisted scalable blockchain, which can ensure the integrity and immutability of vaccine supply records to combat counterfeit vaccines on the SC.80 A novel framework Vacledger for SC traceability and counterfeit detection of COVID-19 vaccines using blockchain networks.81 A vaccine blockchain infrastructure based on blockchain and ML technology, supporting vaccine traceability and smart contract functionality.82 An intelligent vaccine management architecture based on blockchain and the IoT can achieve elasticity, tracking, and traceability of vaccine circulation.83,84 The solution based on blockchain technology and managed by multiple smart contracts, which involves the entire lifecycle of vaccines in five stages: registration, commitment, production and delivery, consumption, and waste assessment, can reduce vaccine waste.85

Technically speaking, blockchain has some limitations in solving VSC problems, such as high on chain storage consumption and low throughput. An improved blockchain based vaccine security protection scheme with storage efficiency can alleviate limitations.86 A blockchain based solution that uses smart contracts to monitor the supply and distribution of vaccine bottles has shown promising results in throughput and scalability.87 A comprehensive framework that integrates blockchain, cloud computing, and encryption technologies, including a data governance framework, can utilize cloud computing to provide scalable storage and processing capabilities. Blockchain ensures data immutability and transparency, and the framework is used to specify and monitor data exchange and usage.88 The privacy protection vaccine passport infrastructure based on blockchain provides a formal security model and corresponding security proof for its security attributes.89 The blockchain based tracking infrastructure uses blockchain as a global, unique, and verifiable database to store all circulation data, which can achieve the protection of vaccine circulation and the concept verification prototype of the proposed system.90 A mobile/network vaccine traceability solution driven by big data and blockchain technology called immunochain outperforms some existing methods in vaccine traceability and record management frameworks.91

In summary, the theoretical framework of supporting technologies for the DVSC takes end-to-end integration, intelligence, super automation, and trusted circulation as its four core directions. Each direction, through specific technology combinations, is implemented in key links of the DVSC such as production, transportation, storage, and vaccination, and has practical impacts by addressing the pain points of traditional VSCs, including information fragmentation, inefficiency, and lack of trust. Through the integration of technologies in these four directions, “digital technologies” are transformed into implementable solutions: end-to-end integration solves the problem of “difficult coordination”; intelligence addresses “crude decision-making”; super automation tackles “low efficiency”; and trusted circulation resolves “weak trust.” Ultimately, these technological applications significantly improve the response speed, accuracy, and reliability of the DVSC, not only ensuring vaccine quality and safety but also enhancing the capacity of PHSs to respond to public health emergencies such as pandemics.

Discussion

This study develops a theoretical framework for DVSC supporting technologies via the B-SLR method, with core findings correlating multidimensionally to existing research – validating key conclusions and filling gaps through methodological innovations and expanded perspectives. Firstly, blockchain enhances SC collaboration via data immutability and traceability, aligning with Ng et al.10 on blockchain resolving information asymmetry in medical SCs. Analysis of the intelligence dimension shows ML models predict vaccine demand and improve epidemic outbreak prediction accuracy via stacked ensemble learning (e.g., GA-Stacking), echoing Arora et al.9 on AI’s value in public health emergencies while expanding application boundaries. Secondly, existing studies often focus on single technologies: Fiore et al.11 explore blockchain in medical SCs but not IoT collaboration, whereas this framework identifies end-to-end integration as dependent on blockchain + IoT + digital twin, breaking single-technology limitations. Lopes et al.12 note insufficient vaccine inventory optimization research without technical solutions; this study proposes smart contracts + RPA for dynamic inventory scheduling via the super automation dimension, addressing the gap with actionable paths. Finally, Duijzer et al.1 identified high uncertainty in VSCs via traditional reviews but ignored digital technology mitigation. This study, using B-SLR quantitative clustering, finds AI/ML reduces demand fluctuations through virus transmission simulation – differentiated by B-SLR’s stronger data relevance mining. Yadav et al.6 focused on VSC integration with other medical chains without digital variables, while this study centers digitalization and reveals blockchain’s role in cross-chain integration via trusted circulation, reflecting divergent focuses: structural vs. technology-driven collaboration.

In addition, the studies in this literature review have yielded some interesting observations, and in this section, we discuss observations related to various components of the SC.

End-to-end integration

There are still multiple issues in the end-to-end integration of the DVSC, which mainly involve multiple aspects such as technology, management, coordination, and external environment. From a technical perspective, insufficient cross-system compatibility and inconsistent data standards are core bottlenecks. From the management and coordination perspective, interest games among institutions and vague division of rights and responsibilities have restricted the breadth of research. This review finds that literature research has explored the promoting role of digital technology in VCS integration and coordination, but the research breadth and depth are still insufficient. Trusted circulation

Existing literature on trusted circulation in the DVSC has primarily focused on the application of blockchain technology, covering data certification-based post-event traceability scenarios such as vaccine safety traceability and smart contracts. Traditionally, traceability in the VSC has been passive (e.g., recalling problematic batches after distribution), but blockchain has enabled active supervision through smart contracts. However, there is relatively little research on the deployment of data control technologies and their application in pre-event prevention scenarios involving visible and controllable data, such as vaccine quality collaboration and circulation collaboration. Furthermore, despite the great potential of blockchain, this review identifies key blind spots in current research: scalability during large-scale crises, standardization among different stakeholders, adaptability to resource-constrained regions, and an imbalance between preventive and passive applications. Generally speaking, although the literature research of DVSC has made significant progress, covering many aspects, it may not cover all potential problems and changes, and new challenges and problems may continue to emerge as technology advances and the external environment changes. Conclusion

This study systematically reviews the development status of DVSC supporting technologies using the B-SLR method, providing key insights into its current landscape, technological drivers, and practical significance. Details are as follows: in terms of publication trends, the DVSC field has shown exponential growth since 2021. Among the 76 core studies analyzed, 85.8% were published in or after 2021. This growth aligns with the urgent need to strengthen VSCs amid the global pandemic, reflecting high attention from academia and industry to digital solutions in public health emergency response. In terms of dominant technologies, blockchain, AI, ML, and the IoT serve as the core pillars of DVSC: blockchain supports trusted circulation by ensuring data immutability and traceability; AI/ML drives intelligent decision-making in areas such as demand forecasting, vaccine hesitancy management, and cold chain optimization; IoT enables real-time monitoring of vaccine storage and transportation status, laying the foundation for end-to-end integration. In terms of core applications, the theoretical framework identifies four key directions: end-to-end integration connects fragmented SC links through digital twins and cross-institutional data platforms, enhancing collaborative efficiency; intelligence achieves precise prediction of operational dynamics and adverse reactions via ML models, though the integration of large models is still in its infancy; hyper-automation reduces manual intervention by combining digital tools (e.g., smart contracts) with physical devices (e.g., IoT containers), with the application potential of AI-integrated robots yet to be fully explored; trusted circulation relies primarily on blockchain for post-event traceability, with insufficient exploration of pre-event data prevention and control. The study indicates that digital technologies are transforming the DVSC from a linear, fragmented structure into a dynamically interconnected ecosystem.

In the process of research, although some achievements have been made, there are limitations in research methods, data sources and research itself. The complexity and high requirements of B-SLR research method increase the difficulty of research implementation; The research mainly relies on WoS and Scopus databases, and data quality and database limitations affect the comprehensiveness and accuracy of the research results; Subjective judgment is difficult to eliminate, which will make the screening results biased and affect the objectivity of the research conclusions.

In conclusion, based on our extensive literature review, we conclude that the DVSC can derive advantages from the perspective of supporting technology and identify research opportunities for the digital technology community. It can contribute to improving the DVSC in both developed and developing countries from different perspectives, and further research in this field is promising, providing us with interesting research directions. This study aims to predict the future development direction of DVSC to a certain extent, and provides useful references for vaccine production enterprises, logistics service providers, disease control centers, terminal medical institutions, scientific research departments and other relevant parties.

Efficacy of pars plana vitrectomy for symptomatic vitreous floaters associated with Fuchs heterochromic iridocyclitis
Authors: Mehmet Sahin Sevim, Semra Sevim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSymptomatic vitreous floaters can significantly impair visual function and quality of life. While pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) has been shown to be effective for vitreous opacifications, data regarding its outcomes in patients with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis (FHU) remain limited.MethodsThis retrospective study included 17 eyes of 17 pseudophakic patients with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis who underwent pars plana vitrectomy for visually significant vitreous opacification and were followed for a minimum of 3 months postoperatively. Preoperative and postoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), vitreous haze graded according to the Standardization of Uveitis Nomenclature (SUN) classification, intraocular pressure (IOP), and macular optical coherence tomography (OCT) findings were evaluated. BCVA values were converted to logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) for statistical analysis. Changes in visual and anatomical outcomes were analyzed, and the association between preoperative vitreous haze grade and postoperative visual improvement was assessed.ResultsThe mean duration of symptomatic vitreous floaters was 15.5 ± 6.2 months. Mean BCVA improved significantly from 0.57 ± 0.33 logMAR preoperatively to 0.18 ± 0.21 logMAR at the final follow-up (p < 0.001). The mean vitreous haze grade decreased significantly from 1.88 ± 0.78 to 0.12 ± 0.49 postoperatively (p < 0.001). A moderate positive correlation was observed between preoperative vitreous haze grade and postoperative visual improvement (Spearman’s ρ = 0.49, p = 0.044). Mean IOP did not differ significantly between preoperative and postoperative periods. One patient (5.8%) developed postoperative retinal detachment. No cases of cystoid macular edema or secondary epiretinal membrane formation were observed during follow-up.ConclusionsPars plana vitrectomy is an effective treatment option for symptomatic vitreous floaters associated with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis, providing significant visual improvement with an acceptable short-term safety profile. Careful patient selection and meticulous surgical technique are essential to optimize outcomes.Keywords: Fuchs heterochromic uveitis, Pars plana vitrectomy, Vitreous floaters, Vitreous opacities, Visual acuity, Uveitis-associated vitrectomy

Introduction

Fuchs heterochromic uveitis (FHU) is a chronic disease characterized by low-grade anterior chamber inflammation and is typically unilateral in presentation [1, 2]. It most commonly affects young adult Caucasian individuals, with an equal distribution between sexes. Characteristic clinical findings include diffusely distributed keratic precipitates and iris stromal atrophy, which lead to heterochromia and iris transillumination defects [3]. Visual impairment in FHU usually develops secondary to characteristic posterior subcapsular cataract formation, secondary glaucoma, or vitreous opacities. Although Fuchs heterochromic uveitis is generally considered an uncommon form of uveitis, a relatively higher prevalence has been reported in certain geographic regions, including Turkey and other parts of the Mediterranean basin, as noted in previous epidemiological studies [2]. Regional differences in disease recognition, referral patterns, and genetic or environmental factors may contribute to this variability.

Symptoms of vitreous floaters in these patients arise from visual obscuration caused by the accumulation of inflammatory debris in the anterior vitreous, which represents the most commonly involved site of vitritis in FHU [4]. Even in cases where best-corrected visual acuity remains relatively preserved, vitreous floaters may be visually disturbing and significantly impair patients’ quality of vision [5].

The aim of this study was to evaluate the visual and anatomical outcomes of pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) in patients with FHU who experience clinically significant visual symptoms due to inflammatory vitreous opacities.

Patients and methods

In this retrospective study, 17 eyes of 17 patients who underwent pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) for visually significant vitreous opacification at the Istanbul World Eye Hospital, Altunizade Branch, between October 2016 and January 2025 were evaluated. All patients had undergone uncomplicated cataract surgery prior to inclusion in the study. Cataract formation in these relatively young patients was considered to be related to the chronic inflammatory nature of Fuchs heterochromic uveitis and/or long-term topical corticosteroid use. Detailed lens grading and the exact timing of cataract surgery were not uniformly available due to the retrospective design of the study. To eliminate the potential confounding effect of cataract-related visual impairment on postoperative outcomes, only pseudophakic patients who had previously undergone uncomplicated cataract surgery were included in the study. Cataract surgery was not performed as part of the present study and was not related to the indication for pars plana vitrectomy.

All patients had a confirmed diagnosis of Fuchs heterochromic uveitis (FHU) established by the uveitis unit of our clinic prior to surgery. Preoperative evaluation included best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) assessment using standard Snellen charts under photopic conditions, slit-lamp biomicroscopic examination, intraocular pressure measurement using applanation tonometry, and detailed fundus examination. Macular optical coherence tomography (OCT) was performed preoperatively and repeated at postoperative months 1 and 3 in all patients. Snellen BCVA values were converted to logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) equivalents to allow for accurate statistical analysis. In eyes with dense vitreous opacification, visual acuity measurements primarily reflected functional visual impairment caused by vitreous haze rather than underlying macular pathology.

Vitreous opacity was graded on a scale from 0 to 4+, using the vitreous haze grading system described by the Standardization of Uveitis Nomenclature (SUN) Working Group in 2005 as the reference standard [6].

Surgical treatment consisted of standard three-port pars plana vitrectomy performed using a 23- or 25-gauge system (Alcon Constellation Vision System, Alcon Laboratories, Fort Worth, TX, USA). Following core vitrectomy, posterior hyaloid detachment was induced using triamcinolone acetonide assistance in eyes where it was not already separated. No macular surgical procedures, such as internal limiting membrane peeling, were performed in any case. Partial peripheral vitreous removal was achieved with scleral indentation during vitrectomy; however, meticulous peripheral vitreous shaving was intentionally avoided to reduce the risk of iatrogenic retinal breaks. At the end of each surgery, a thorough peripheral retinal examination was performed to detect any potential iatrogenic retinal tears. All surgeries were performed by the same surgeon (MSS).

Postoperatively, patients were prescribed topical prednisolone acetate and topical moxifloxacin hydrochloride four times daily. Topical antibiotic therapy was discontinued after three days, while topical steroid treatment was tapered weekly and continued for one month.

All patients were evaluated postoperatively on day 1, at week 1, and at months 1 and 3, with additional visits performed when clinically indicated. Definitive best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) measurements were recorded from the 1-month postoperative visit onward. Macular optical coherence tomography (OCT) was repeated at postoperative months 1 and 3 in all patients, and intraocular pressure was measured at each follow-up visit. The primary outcome measures were evaluated at the 3-month postoperative follow-up visit. Postoperative examinations included BCVA assessment, slit-lamp biomicroscopy with evaluation of anterior chamber inflammation graded according to the Standardization of Uveitis Nomenclature (SUN) criteria, dilated fundus examination, and optical coherence tomography when media clarity permitted. Vitreous inflammation was assessed clinically based on vitreous cell and haze during dilated fundus examination.

Statistical analyses were performed using standard statistical methods. Normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Preoperative and postoperative BCVA and intraocular pressure (IOP) values were compared using paired t-tests for normally distributed data. Changes in vitreous opacity graded according to the SUN vitreous haze classification were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test due to the ordinal nature of the data. The relationship between preoperative vitreous haze grade and postoperative visual improvement, expressed as change in BCVA (ΔlogMAR), was evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

The mean age of the patients was 42.1 ± 7.5 years (range, 31–56 years). Ten patients were female and seven were male. Three patients had elevated intraocular pressure preoperatively that was well controlled with topical antiglaucomatous therapy, and one patient had a history of amblyopia in the operated eye. All patients experienced decreased vision preoperatively and reported visually disturbing floaters, with a mean symptom duration of 15.5 ± 6.2 months (range, 6–25 months) (n = 17). Vitreous opacity was graded as 1 + in six eyes, 2 + in seven eyes, and 3 + in four eyes. All patients were pseudophakic prior to surgery. Standard three-port pars plana vitrectomy was performed in all cases. In five patients, surgical capsulotomy was also performed during the procedure. Intraoperatively, peripheral retinal tears developed in two patients (11.7%), and laser photocoagulation was applied to the affected areas. In these two patients with iatrogenic retinal tears, 20% sulfur hexafluoride (SF₆) gas was used as an endotamponade. The gas tamponade was completely absorbed spontaneously within 3–4 weeks. No additional intraoperative complications, such as lens injury, choroidal detachment, or suprachoroidal hemorrhage, were observed in any case.

In the postoperative period, four patients (23.5%) developed dense erythrocyte cells in the anterior chamber, and two of these patients (11.7%) also exhibited mild vitreous haze due to hemorrhagic leakage into the vitreous cavity. This postoperative vitreous haze was presumed to result from bleeding through the sclerotomy sites or reflux from the anterior chamber. One of these patients with postoperative vitreous haze presented with sudden visual deterioration at postoperative week 4 and was diagnosed with retinal detachment. The retinal detachment was rhegmatogenous in nature. This patient had not developed an intraoperative iatrogenic retinal tear during the initial vitrectomy. The patient underwent repeat vitreoretinal surgery with silicone oil endotamponade, resulting in anatomical retinal reattachment at the final follow-up visit. Despite successful retinal reattachment, the final best-corrected visual acuity in this patient remained worse than the preoperative level. No cases of recurrent intraocular inflammation requiring systemic therapy were observed during the 3-month follow-up period.

Except for the single patient who developed postoperative retinal detachment (5.8%), all patients demonstrated an improvement in visual acuity at the final follow-up visit (94.1%). The patient with a pre-existing history of amblyopia demonstrated a limited postoperative visual improvement compared to the rest of the cohort. Preoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), expressed in logMAR units, was 0.57 ± 0.33 and significantly improved to 0.18 ± 0.21 at the final postoperative visit. The mean improvement in visual acuity (ΔlogMAR) was 0.40 ± 0.31, indicating a statistically significant visual gain following surgery (p < 0.001, paired t-test) (Table 2; Fig. 1). When both the amblyopic patient and the patient who developed postoperative retinal detachment were excluded from the analysis (n = 15), mean preoperative BCVA improved from 0.56 ± 0.33 logMAR to 0.11 ± 0.09 logMAR at the final follow-up visit. The mean visual improvement remained statistically significant (p < 0.001, paired t-test).

Correlation analysis revealed a moderate positive association between preoperative vitreous haze grade and postoperative visual improvement (ΔlogMAR) (Spearman’s ρ = 0.49, p = 0.044), indicating that higher preoperative vitreous haze was associated with greater visual gain (Fig. 2).

Preoperatively, macular OCT images could not be clearly evaluated in three patients due to dense vitreous opacification; in the remaining patients, the foveal contour was normal. At the final postoperative visit, all patients demonstrated a regular foveal contour with no evidence of macular edema or secondary epiretinal membrane formation, except for the patient who developed postoperative retinal detachment. This patient was among those with severe preoperative vitreous opacity that precluded reliable macular OCT evaluation. No cases of cystoid macular edema were detected on postoperative optical coherence tomography during the follow-up period.

The retinal detachment occurred at postoperative week 4. The preoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) in this patient was 0.40 (Snellen decimal) and decreased to counting fingers at the time of retinal detachment diagnosis. The patient underwent repeat vitreoretinal surgery with silicone oil endotamponade. Silicone oil removal was performed 16 weeks after the retinal detachment surgery. At the final follow-up visit following silicone oil removal, the retina was completely attached, and macular optical coherence tomography (OCT) demonstrated a normal foveal contour. The final BCVA was 0.20 (Snellen decimal). No additional intraoperative or postoperative complications were observed. This patient was 50 years old and had a documented history of Fuchs heterochromic uveitis for approximately 9 years. Prior uncomplicated cataract surgery had been performed 4 years before the initial pars plana vitrectomy. Postoperative topical steroid therapy after each surgical procedure was administered in gradually tapering doses for approximately 4–5 weeks. There was no documented history of atopic disease or systemic inflammatory comorbidity. No intraoperative retinal tear had been identified during the initial vitrectomy procedure.

Discussion

Although numerous studies have evaluated the efficacy of vitrectomy for vitreous opacifications in general [7], there are only a limited number of reports specifically addressing the outcomes of pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) performed for symptomatic vitreous opacifications associated with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis (FHU), presenting with floaters and decreased visual acuity.

Persistent symptomatic vitreous opacifications can significantly impair patients’ quality of life. The negative impact of vitreous floaters on quality of life has been reported to be comparable to that of age-related macular degeneration or diabetic retinopathy, and patients have been shown to be willing to accept up to a 7% risk of blindness in order to eliminate floater-related symptoms [8].

PPV represents an effective surgical option for the management of symptomatic vitreous opacifications [9]. De Nie et al. reported that 85% of patients experienced symptomatic improvement and were satisfied following vitrectomy, while dissatisfaction was mainly observed in patients who developed surgery-related complications such as retinal detachment, refractory cystoid macular edema, secondary glaucoma, or foveal photoreceptor atrophy, all of which resulted in visual loss [10]. Similarly, Wa et al. reported complete resolution of symptoms in 98.5% of eyes following PPV [11]. Our findings are in line with recent evidence evaluating the outcomes of pars plana vitrectomy for persistent vitreous opacities in uveitic eyes. In a recent study by Alisi et al. [12], significant functional improvement and favorable inflammatory outcomes were reported after vitrectomy in patients with infectious and noninfectious uveitis unresponsive to medical therapy. These results support the role of pars plana vitrectomy as an effective therapeutic option in carefully selected uveitic patients with symptomatic vitreous opacities. In the present study, the primary objective was the removal of symptomatic vitreous opacifications by PPV; however, unlike most previously reported series, the underlying etiology in all included patients was an inflammatory disease, namely Fuchs heterochromic uveitis.

FHU is characterized by mild to moderate anterior chamber inflammation, diffuse iris depigmentation, and the absence of posterior synechiae [13]. Additionally, cystoid macular edema is rarely observed in patients with FHU [5]. Therefore, the degree of postoperative inflammation expected in these patients is generally lower compared to that seen in more aggressive forms of uveitis.

In our series, although fundus fluorescein angiography was not routinely performed preoperatively, none of the patients evaluated with OCT demonstrated cystoid macular edema before surgery. This finding remained unchanged at the third postoperative month, with no cases of cystoid macular edema detected on follow-up OCT imaging. Furthermore, postoperative anterior chamber inflammation was mild and effectively controlled in all cases with short-term topical anti-inflammatory therapy.

The demonstration of increased production of interleukin-10, an anti-inflammatory cytokine, in the aqueous and vitreous humor of patients with FHU may help explain the relatively benign clinical course of the disease and the milder inflammatory response observed following intraocular surgery [14, 15]. Based on these findings, PPV in patients with FHU appears less likely to trigger severe intraocular inflammation compared to vitrectomy performed for other uveitic conditions. Consequently, the indication for PPV in the management of FHU-associated floaters may be considered with greater confidence than in cases with different inflammatory etiologies.

This consideration may also extend to postoperative complications. As in all vitrectomy procedures, avoidance of iatrogenic peripheral retinal tears and retinal trauma remains a critical surgical goal. One of the most undesirable complications of floater vitrectomy is secondary retinal detachment, which can adversely affect final visual acuity. In our series of 17 patients, the occurrence of postoperative retinal detachment in one patient exemplifies this risk. The fact that this patient’s final visual acuity was worse than their preoperative visual acuity suggests that caution should be exercised when considering PPV indications in patients with relatively good baseline vision. The most effective strategy to minimize such complications is meticulous peripheral retinal examination at the conclusion of PPV. In addition, one patient in our cohort had a pre-existing diagnosis of amblyopia, which likely limited the extent of postoperative visual improvement despite successful removal of vitreous opacities. This highlights the importance of considering underlying visual potential when interpreting postoperative visual outcomes following vitrectomy. When the amblyopic patient was excluded, the overall trend of significant postoperative visual improvement remained unchanged.

In addition to postoperative retinal detachment, intraoperative retinal complications were observed in the present series. Specifically, peripheral iatrogenic retinal tears occurred in two patients (11.7%), which appears higher than rates typically reported for pars plana vitrectomy performed for primary vitreous floaters. Several disease- and patient-related factors specific to Fuchs heterochromic uveitis may contribute to this finding. These include altered vitreoretinal interface properties due to chronic low-grade inflammation, relatively young patient age with firmer vitreous adherence, and suboptimal mydriasis that may limit peripheral retinal visualization. In addition, surgical strategies aimed at minimizing postoperative inflammation—such as avoiding extensive peripheral vitreous shaving—may influence the balance between surgical safety and residual retinal traction. In susceptible eyes, this conservative approach may contribute to persistent peripheral vitreoretinal traction, highlighting the importance of carefully balancing inflammation control with adequate relief of peripheral traction in patients with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis. Both 23- and 25-gauge systems were used based on surgeon preference and intraoperative considerations; given the small sample size, a comparative analysis between gauge sizes was not performed, and no clinically apparent differences in postoperative outcomes were observed. In the present case, although anatomical success was achieved after silicone oil removal, the final visual acuity did not fully recover to the pre-detachment level, emphasizing the potential visual impact of postoperative retinal detachment even when structural outcomes are favorable. Within three months following PPV, two additional patients required continuation or initiation of topical antiglaucomatous therapy due to elevated intraocular pressure. Although mean postoperative intraocular pressure showed a slight increase, this difference was not statistically significant, and intraocular pressure was successfully controlled with medical therapy in all cases. Based on our findings, PPV may necessitate additional antiglaucomatous treatment in some patients; however, there was no evidence to suggest that surgery exacerbated glaucomatous disease.

The strengths of this study include a well-defined and homogeneous patient population with a confirmed diagnosis of Fuchs heterochromic uveitis, a standardized surgical technique performed by a single experienced surgeon, and systematic postoperative evaluation using both clinical examination and optical coherence tomography.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, its retrospective design and relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, postoperative follow-up was limited to three months, which restricts the assessment of long-term visual outcomes, late-onset complications, and potential recurrence of intraocular inflammation, particularly given the chronic nature of Fuchs heterochromic uveitis. Finally, the absence of a control group precludes direct comparison with non-surgical management or alternative treatment approaches. Larger prospective studies with longer follow-up periods are needed to better define the long-term safety and efficacy of pars plana vitrectomy in this patient population. Although comprehensive perioperative data were available for major complications, certain historical variables such as cumulative corticosteroid exposure and exact disease onset timing were not uniformly documented due to the retrospective study design.

In conclusion, PPV is an effective treatment modality for the management of symptomatic vitreous floaters associated with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis. With careful patient selection, thorough peripheral retinal examination, and meticulous surgical technique, optimal outcomes can be achieved while minimizing the risk of iatrogenic retinal complications in eyes with Fuchs heterochromic uveitis.

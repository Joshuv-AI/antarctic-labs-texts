Impact of Perioperative Opioid Prescriptions on Early and Late Complications After Rhinoplasty: A Propensity-Matched Study
Authors: Aneeq Chaudhry, Anshumi Desai, Sinan Jabori, Kashyap Tadisina, Devinder Singh
Published: 2026

Purpose: Rhinoplasty is among the most frequently performed facial surgeries, with postoperative pain management often relying on opioids. While effective for analgesia, opioid use carries risks that may extend beyond dependency and misuse. Emerging evidence suggests a link between perioperative opioid exposure and adverse surgical outcomes, yet data specific to rhinoplasty remain limited. This study evaluates short- and long-term complications associated with opioid prescribing in a large, real-world cohort.

Methods: Patients undergoing rhinoplasties between 2005–2025 were identified using CPT codes from the TriNetX Research Network. Those with prior opioid use disorder, opioid dependence or cancer diagnoses were excluded. Cohorts were stratified based on perioperative opioid prescriptions. Outcomes were evaluated in early (1–30 days) and late (31–365 days) postoperative periods.

Results: Before propensity score matching, 419,449 patients met inclusion criteria (312,053 with opioid exposure and 107,396 without). After 1:1 propensity score matching, 170,042 patients (85,021 per cohort) were included for analysis. In the early postoperative period, opioid-exposed patients demonstrated significantly higher odds of infection (OR 1.817, 95% CI 1.458–2.265, p < 0.001), wound disruption (OR 1.528, 95% CI 1.191–1.959, p = 0.008) and surgical site complications (OR 1.601, 95% CI 1.434–1.788, p < 0.001). During late follow-up, opioid exposure was associated with persistent increased odds of infection (OR 1.482, 95% CI 1.196–1.838, p < 0.001), wound disruption (OR 1.357, 95% CI 1.099–1.677, p = 0.004), and surgical site complications (OR 1.139, 95% CI 1.04–1.248, p = 0.005). No significant differences were observed for thrombotic events or readmission.

Conclusion: Perioperative opioid prescriptions in rhinoplasty-related procedures show an association with increased risks of infection and wound complications that persisted up to one year postoperatively. Randomized controlled trials are necessary to validate these findings.

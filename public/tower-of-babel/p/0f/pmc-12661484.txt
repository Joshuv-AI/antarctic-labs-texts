“Close Then Clear” Margins in Transoral Resection of p16+ Oropharyngeal Squamous Cell Carcinoma
Authors: Chrisje Den Besten, Kalpesh Hathi, Colin MacKay, Depak Patel, Martin J Bullock, Matthew H Rigby, S Mark Taylor, Jonathan R Trites, Martin Corsten
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveStandard 5‐mm surgical margins in the oropharynx are challenging due to the intimate relationship with neurovascular structures and patient function. De‐escalation of therapy with the acceptance of close surgical margins may improve functional patient outcomes. This study assesses locoregional recurrence with initial close (≤2 mm) margins following transoral surgery for p16+ oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) with or without re‐resection and adjuvant therapy.Study DesignRetrospective cohort study.SettingCanadian tertiary care center.MethodsRetrospective chart review of all patients who underwent transoral robotic surgery or transoral laser microsurgery with curative intent for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC and had ≤2 mm initial surgical margins with or without re‐resection and adjuvant therapy between January 2019 and December 2023. The primary outcome was 2‐year locoregional control.ResultsIn total, 80 patients were included; 54 had a 2‐year follow‐up available. Initial margins were close (≤2 mm) in 44 patients (55%) and positive in 34 patients (42.5%). Re‐resection was performed in 67 patients (83.8%). Following re‐resections, three patients (3.8%) had positive margins and 77 (96.2%) had close or clear margins. Deep margins were most involved (67.5%). In total, 42 patients (52.5%) received adjuvant (chemo)‐radiotherapy. Amongst the 54 patients eligible for 2‐year follow‐up, one had regional recurrence at 18 months postsurgery, resulting in a 2‐year locoregional control rate of 98.1%.Conclusionp16+ oropharyngeal SCC with close initial surgical margins results in low locoregional recurrence rates. Intraoperative re‐resection with acceptance of close surgical margins may be an effective strategy to preserve both oncologic safety and functional outcomes.Keywords: laser, margin, oropharynx, p16, robotic, squamous cell carcinoma, transoral

The incidence of oropharyngeal carcinoma is steadily increasing, largely attributed to HPV+ (p16+) oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). 1 p16+ oropharyngeal SCC more commonly impacts a younger and healthier population and is associated with improved survival and locoregional control compared to p16− oropharyngeal SCC. 2 , 3 , 4

Historically, given the high morbidity of open oropharyngeal resections, radiotherapy has been the mainstay of treatment. However, the advent of minimally invasive transoral approaches including transoral robotic surgery (TORS) and transoral laser microsurgery (TLM) has increased surgical approaches for oropharyngeal carcinoma. Due to these minimally invasive approaches and the favorable prognosis of p16+ oropharyngeal SCC, strategies to de‐escalate the level of treatment for this cancer subtype, and subsequently their side effects, have been explored. 5 , 6 , 7

An area of further investigation is surgical margins in transoral surgery for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC. Accepted criteria for surgical resection margins in head and neck cancer is 5 mm on final pathology as per the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) clinical practice guidelines, with margins <5 mm demonstrating an increased risk of recurrence. 8 However, given the vital structures surrounding the oropharynx, achieving these resection margins may not be possible without sacrificing important neurovascular structures or compromising a patient's speech and swallowing function. Furthermore, oropharyngeal resections pose a higher risk of fistulization between the oropharynx and neck with wider surgical margins. The risk of a positive margin during resection of oropharyngeal SCC is consequently high, with a recent national review based in the United States reporting a positive margin rate of 16.9%. 9

Consensus regarding adequate margins in p16+ oropharyngeal SCC is lacking, and practice patterns vary. Recent literature has suggested that margins <5 mm in p16+ oropharyngeal SCC may be adequate to prevent locoregional recurrence. 10 , 11 This theme of reducing surgical margins has also been explored in the oral cavity. 12 , 13

Practice patterns at our institution involve frozen section analysis of oropharyngeal malignancies with a propensity to perform re‐resection for “close,” uncertain (close or positive), or positive initial surgical margins, when possible, based on the initial resection extent. Re‐resection of close margins often involves a thin section of deep muscle in the surgical bed, with a negative re‐resection being considered a clear margin regardless of thickness. Overall, our institution accepts close margins and avoids adjuvant radiotherapy to reduce patient morbidity. However, for aggressive nodal disease in the neck, such as extra‐nodal extension (ENE), adjuvant (chemo)‐radiotherapy is used. This study explores this strategy by assessing the 2‐year locoregional control post‐transoral resection of p16+ oropharyngeal SCC with close or positive initial surgical margins.

Methods

Study Design

Retrospective cohort study assessing the 2‐year locoregional control rates of patients with close initial surgical margins (≤2 mm) during TORS or TLM for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC. A waiver of consent and research ethics board (REB) approval was obtained from the Nova Scotia Health REB (ROMEO File#: 1030658).

Participants

All adult (≥16 years old) patients with newly diagnosed p16+ oropharyngeal SCC who underwent surgical resection with curative intent using TORS or TLM between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2023, at the Queen Elizabeth II Health Sciences Center in Halifax, Nova Scotia, were included. Patients with an initial positive or close margin on resection were included in the final cohort. A “close” margin was defined as ≤2 mm. A “clear” margin was defined as >2 mm. Excluded were patients with a history of head and neck cancer.

Surgical Procedures

Patients were selected to undergo surgical resection based on standard care for oropharyngeal SCC. All patients underwent TORS or TLM resection. Due to surgeon preference, one study author (M.C.) exclusively performed TORS resection, and another (M.H.R.) exclusively performed TLM resection. Surgical resection margins were assessed intraoperatively with frozen section pathologic analysis. The practice pattern for acquiring margins of the two attending surgeons involved in all of the included cases involved acquiring the deep and peripheral margins from the oriented resection specimen. One attending surgeon took circumferential mucosal margins surrounding the tumor bed for all cases, while the other only took peripheral mucosal margins if needed based on “close” or positive initial margins on the resected specimen. Based on frozen section results, re‐resections were possible during the same surgical setting. Frozen sections which were positive or “close” (≤2 mm) would undergo a targeted thin re‐resection in the same procedure, if feasible, a negative or repeat close resection on repeat frozen section was then accepted and concluded the resection. All specimens, including frozen section margins, were submitted for final pathology. Final margin status was determined based on the re‐resection margin if performed.

All patients in this cohort had clinical N0 to N3 neck disease. All patients were treated with neck dissection for curative intent as the first line. Adjuvant treatment was considered only based on pathologic results.

Postoperatively, patients were discussed at a weekly multidisciplinary tumor board. Decision for adjuvant (chemo)‐radiotherapy was based on tumor board discussion considering TNM staging, pathology results, and patient factors. Adjuvant therapy was not suggested solely for close margins on initial or re‐resection margins, nor on the final pathology of the specimen.

Patient demographics and tumor‐specific data were collected from the patients' charts.

Data Collection and Outcome Measures

Patient charts meeting inclusion criteria were reviewed retrospectively to collect patient demographics, cancer subsite, 8th edition AJCC TNM classification (clinical and pathological), pathology results, and recurrences. 14

The main outcome of interest was locoregional control at 2 years postoperation. Only those who were operated on between January 1, 2019, and June 30, 2022, were included in the 2‐year locoregional control cohort to allow a full 2 years before chart review (completed July 2024).

Data Analysis

Continuous variables were reported as means ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables were reported as frequencies with percentages.

Adjuvant Treatment Characteristics

All patients (3; 3.8%) with positive final resection margins were offered adjuvant therapy. Two received radiotherapy to both the primary site and the neck. The third patient was offered chemoradiotherapy but decided against this offer due to medical comorbidities. Six out of the thirteen patients who did not have re‐resections performed received adjuvant (chemo)‐radiotherapy. Most patients with close final margins did not receive radiotherapy to the primary site (16/28; 57.1%).

Recurrence

Of the 80 patients in our cohort, 54 were included in the 2‐year locoregional control calculations. Within this cohort, 23 patients (42.6%) had no adjuvant treatment, 27 (50%) had radiotherapy alone, and 4 patients (7.4%) had chemoradiotherapy. No patients had local recurrence. One patient presented with a regional recurrence to the contralateral neck and distant metastasis to the ipsilateral lung 18 months post‐resection for tonsillar carcinoma (pT2N1) with a close deep margin (<1 mm) that did not undergo re‐resection. This patient initially received adjuvant radiotherapy to both the primary site and the ipsilateral neck due to ENE; upon recurrence, they were treated with neck dissection, lobectomy, and radiotherapy. They are currently disease‐free. Control rates for the 2‐year recurrence cohort are in Table 4. Overall, this cohort had a 2‐year local control rate of 100% and a 2‐year regional control rate of 98.1%.

Within the full 80‐patient cohort, there was one other patient treated for a tongue base carcinoma (pT2N1) with a close initial medial margin (<1 mm) with clear re‐resection but multifocal lymphatic invasion. They presented with regional metastasis in level 5 of the ipsilateral neck 3 months postsurgery. Upon recurrence, the patient underwent repeat neck dissection and radiotherapy. Discussion

Historically, “close” margins have been described as <5 mm in head and neck cancer. 8 , 15 However, recent literature has suggested that margins as narrow as 1 to 2 mm may provide favorable patient outcomes in various subsites. 8 , 12 , 13 , 16 For oropharyngeal SCC, the improved prognosis in p16+ oropharyngeal SCC raises the question of whether treatment de‐intensification strategies, with acceptance of close resection margins, may be appropriate to maintain oncological control while decreasing treatment‐related morbidity. This study revealed that patients with initial surgical margins ≤2 mm with or without re‐resection and/or adjuvant (chemo)‐radiotherapy had a locoregional control rate of 98.1%. The strategy employed in our center, that is, using frozen section analysis and directly performing a targeted thin re‐resection for positive, close, or uncertain margins likely contributed to the high locoregional control rate.

Evidence has been growing to support narrow margins in p16+ oropharyngeal SCC. The ECOG‐3311 and ORATOR trials supported margins of 3 mm. 7 , 17 However, high‐quality evidence supporting margins <3 mm is lacking. Recent studies have provided evidence for favorable outcomes with <2 mm and <1 mm margins, suggesting a “no ink on tumor” approach. 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 , 23 Despite this, heterogeneity in practice patterns regarding margins exists within the head and neck community. 15 , 18 Further, there is considerable heterogeneity regarding margin definitions in international literature. 11 , 18 Accepting closer margins may improve functional for patients. The aforementioned trials were unable to demonstrate a difference in swallowing outcomes between TORS and (chemo)‐radiotherapy, but a tolerance for closer margins may result in favorable results for the surgical cohort. At our center, empiric lateral oropharyngectomy with exposure of parapharyngeal fat is not performed, providing a more conservative approach, potentially minimizing the impact on patients’ functional status.

Hinni et al reported an average deep margin of 1.98 mm and a peripheral margin of 2.98 mm for 128 patients who underwent TLM for tonsillar carcinoma. 19 , 20 This study found a local control rate of 99% at 5 years. 20 Similar to our cohort, patients were not offered adjuvant therapy for close margins alone, and 59.6% of patients underwent adjuvant radiotherapy. 20 Molony et al reported on patients with positive surgical margins for oropharyngeal SCC receiving postoperative radiotherapy. 21 Patients with p16− SCC had significantly worse disease‐specific survival, whereas for those with p16+ SCC, margin status had no impact on disease‐specific survival, additionally supporting de‐intensification in this subgroup. 21 Of note, their study was limited to 22 patients with positive margins. 21 Similarly, Sunkara et al ran a secondary analysis of randomized control trial data and found positive margins and/or ENE were associated with worse survival for p16− oropharyngeal SCC, and wider surgical margins were associated with improved survival for p16− oropharyngeal SCC. 22 However, this was not true for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC. 22 This study included 51 patients with p16+ oropharyngeal SCC and 73 patients with p16− or unknown p16 status oropharyngeal SCC. 22 Kornfeld et al found that 119/183 (64.7%) patients with p16+ oropharyngeal SCC who underwent TORS resection had <3 mm surgical margins, and this did not impact recurrence. 23 Final positive margins, however, did impact recurrence in this study. 23 A 2019 meta‐analysis by Gorphe and Simon pooled three studies and revealed no significant difference between “close” margins compared to clear margins. 11 Bias is introduced into these results given that the definition of “close” margins was not homogeneous amongst studies. 11 Further, the “close” cohort was limited to 48 patients. 11 Overall, these results support the acceptance of narrow surgical margins in oropharyngeal SCC, which is expected to reduce morbidity and facilitate a more practical approach to surgical margins. Further, radiologically Hinni et al identified the mean thickness of the lateral pharyngeal wall musculature to be 2.4 mm, making deep surgical margins >2 mm challenging and sometimes impossible to achieve. 20

In this study, 42/80 patients (52.5%) received adjuvant (chemo)‐radiotherapy following multidisciplinary tumor board discussion. “Close” margin status was not an independent factor for adjuvant therapy. Holcomb et al showed comparable outcomes with surgical resection alone when comparing <1 mm and >1 mm margins. Holcomb et alexamined 99 patients from three academic centers with <5 mm surgical margins treated with TORS alone (no adjuvant therapy) for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC. 10 Overall, they found a local control rate of 93.8% at 3‐year follow‐up with no significant difference between patients with <1 mm margins compared to >1 mm margins. 10 Warner et al also assessed TORS and neck dissection alone for oropharyngeal SCC and found that “close” (0.5‐5 mm) margins did not impact recurrence, but positive margins did. 24 This study reported a threshold of 1.1 mm for local control. 24 This supports avoiding adjuvant radiotherapy for close margins in p16+ oropharyngeal SCC, which is the general approach at our institution. This may save patients the burden of wide resection margins impacting the function of the oropharynx, along with the avoidance of radiotherapy. Adjuvant radiotherapy post‐resection of oropharyngeal SCC is shown to negatively impact quality of life resulting in dysphagia, dysgeusia, xerostomia, pain, dental concerns, and financial difficulty. 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 Avoidance of this could improve patients' quality of life.

Of note, Kejner et al did find that an initial intraoperative margin being positive in p16+ oropharyngeal SCC treated with TORS was associated with reduced survival despite a negative re‐resection margin. 30 The authors also noted that involvement of the deep margin was associated with the highest risk of recurrence. 30 However, in our cohort, no patients with initial positive margins recurred, even though the deep margin was the most commonly involved location. The one recurrence in the 2‐year follow‐up cohort was from a close (<1 mm) margin that did not undergo re‐resection. The other recurrence in our overall cohort was also from a close (<1 mm) medial margin that did undergo re‐resection. Overall, patients had positive outcomes with close margins with or without re‐resection.

Recently, Calcano et al published a multicenter cohort study of 91 patients with 1 to 3 mm margins and 8 patients with <1 mm margins and similarly found that margin status was not an independent risk factor for locoregional recurrence. 31 The present study builds upon this work by adding more patients with “close” margins to the literature. Further, this study increases the number of patients reported in the literature with more aggressive margin criteria (≤2 mm). This study also adds more context to the literature by presenting results from a single center with a homogeneous approach to close margins. This is valuable to the current body of evidence as multicenter studies may have more heterogeneity amongst treatment approaches.

Interestingly, one patient in this study had focally positive margins without re‐resection due to the extent of the initial resection but did not have recurrence with 2‐year follow‐up available despite refusing adjuvant therapy due to medical comorbidities. While this anecdotal case is important to discuss and a unique situation, we do not feel it provides evidence for the avoidance of adjuvant therapy in the context of positive margins.

This study provides increasing evidence for the acceptance of narrow surgical resection margins as single‐modality therapy for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC. It also demonstrates that close or positive initial margins do not seem to impact locoregional control, especially when re‐resection is performed. This approach is expected to improve patient outcomes and present a more practical approach to resections within the oropharynx. However, the current literature is limited to small study sizes with significant heterogeneity in study designs and definitions of “close margins.” 11 , 19 Therefore, further high‐quality evidence is required to better understand the impact of surgical margins on patient outcomes in oropharyngeal p16+ SCC. This may elucidate factors that impact recurrence in close margins, for example, Kejner et al noted that initial positive margins on resection followed by negative margins on re‐resection were associated with worse outcomes. 30 Another example is Parvathaneni et al identifying the role of multidisciplinary tumor boards in clarifying positive margin status and therefore the need for adjuvant treatment. 32

It is important to note the limitations that apply to this study. This is a single‐center retrospective review, which introduces bias into the results. The results are from only two operators. Overall, this study reports 2‐year recurrence rates, but longer follow‐up of 3 or 5 years may allow for greater insight and characterization of recurrences. In terms of TLM cases, vaporization of tissue near the margin may impact pathologist's interpretation of close versus clear margins. Although this could erroneously mark a “close” margin as “clear,” this would not impact the interpretation of our results as “clear” margins would not be erroneously identified as “close.” Although the general strategy is to avoid postoperative radiation for close local margins at our center, 52.5% of patients received adjuvant therapy, which impacts our results compared to centers who tended to avoid adjuvant therapy further. Despite this, we do feel that this study provides insights into close surgical margins for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC, building on recent evidence and providing a unique Canadian perspective.

Conclusion

This study identified a 2‐year locoregional control rate of 98.1% for patients with close initial surgical margins (≤2 mm) following TORS or TLM for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC with or without additional re‐resection or adjuvant therapy. These results support the de‐escalation of treatment for p16+ oropharyngeal SCC by accepting narrow surgical margins to improve practicality and patient outcomes. Our center's strategy of re‐resection of close or deep‐frozen section margins within the same setting likely favorably impacts the locoregional control rate in this study. With increasing evidence, including future prospective evaluations, ideally, consensus can be achieved within the international debate on a safe margin cut‐off in this specific patient group to optimize care for these patients.

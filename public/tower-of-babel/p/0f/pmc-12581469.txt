miR-191-5p inhibits angiogenesis in diabetic foot ulcers wound healing via regulating VEGFA
Authors: Qian Ma, Xueqin Yuan, Lina Zhang, Liuxuan An, Shulan Zhang, Lin Niu, Chong Geng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDiabetic foot ulcer (DFU) is a severe complication of diabetes mellitus (DM). The present study aimed to explore the role of miR-191-5p and its target VEGFA in the DFU.Methods207 Volunteers were recruited including 105 uncomplicated DM patients and 102 DFU patients. The expression of miR-191-5p and VEGFA were quantified by qRT-PCR. VEGFA protein level was assayed by Western blot. Pearson correlation, ROC curve, and logistic regression evaluated the clinical relevance, diagnostic potential, and risk factors, respectively. High-glucose stimulated Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC). Proliferation and migration capability were assayed by CCK-8 and Transwell in HUVEC. The target relationship was assessed by dual-luciferase reporter assay.ResultsmiR-191-5p was enhanced in serum and HG-induced HUVEC and identified as an independent risk factor. miR-191-5p abundance was positively associated with the DFU area, CRP, WBC, and NEUT. Downregulation of miR-191-5p inhibited the HUVEC migration and proliferation potential and mRNA expression of PDGF-, PDGFR-β, and FGF-2. VEGFA was predicted and verified as a target of miR-191-5p. VEGFA abundance was negatively related to miR-191-5p. miR-191-5p impeded HUVEC migration, proliferation, and angiogenesis by regulating VEGFA.ConclusionIn summary, miR-191-5p was related to DFU progression and promoted DFR development. As a potential biomarker, miR-191-5p impeded HUVEC migration, proliferation, and angiogenesis by regulating the target VEGFA.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a prevalent chronic disease, with an accelerating incidence rate [1]. Among its most severe complications, diabetic foot ulcers (DFU) pose a significant clinical challenge, often leading to limb amputation or even death if left untreated [2]. Current treatment strategies—including glycemic control, surgical debridement, and wound care—have limited efficacy in improving outcomes, underscoring the need for novel therapeutic strategies [3, 4]. The pathogenesis of DFUs is complex and multifactorial, with impaired angiogenesis recognized as a key contributor to delayed wound healing [5]. Studies have demonstrated that DFU wounds exhibit reduced vascular density and diminished angiogenic capacity [6]. Chronic hyperglycemia in diabetic patients leads to endothelial dysfunction, which further disrupts normal angiogenic signaling [7]. Numerous studies have linked defective angiogenesis to impaired wound repair in diabetes [8, 9]. However, the precise mechanisms through which endothelial dysfunction and angiogenesis impair DFU healing remain incompletely understood. Thus, elucidating the pathways involved in DFU progression and identifying effective therapeutic targets is essential for advancing treatment options.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous, small non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally by binding to the 3'-UTR of target mRNAs via complementary base pairing [10]. Recent studies have increasingly explored the role of miRNAs in DFU wound healing [11, 12]. Among the miRNAs implicated in DFU pathogenesis, miR-191-5p has recently emerged as a potential regulator. Elevated circulating miR-191-5p expression has been reported in DFU patients [13]. In a study on hypoglycemic treatment for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), miR-191-5p expression was found to decrease with improved glycemic control [14]. Additionally, miR-191 has been strongly linked to glucose dysregulation and disease progression [15]. These findings suggest that miR-191-5p may contribute to the development of DFU and other diabetic complications. However, the functional role and molecular mechanisms of miR-191-5p in DFU have not yet been elucidated. Given the regulatory function of miRNAs in gene expression, bioinformatics tools were used to predict potential target mRNAs of miR-191-5p, with vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA) emerging as a key candidate. VEGFA is a well-established pro-angiogenic factor that promotes endothelial cell proliferation and migration, both of which are crucial for new blood vessel formation and granulation tissue development during wound healing [16]. Notably, synthetic lipid nanoparticles have been shown to accelerate DFU wound healing by continuously releasing VEGFA [17]. ACNO hydrogel activated the transcription of key genes, including VEGFA, thereby promoting cell proliferation and accelerating diabetic wound healing [18]. VEGFA expression is inhibited by miR-101-3p, leading to reduced angiogenesis in diabetic retinopathy [19]. Based on these studies, we hypothesize that miR-191-5p may impair DFU wound healing by targeting VEGFA.

In brief, this study aimed to investigate the role of the miR-191-5p axis in DFU. miR-191-5p expression was analyzed in the serum of DFU patients, while an endothelial cell model was established under high-glucose conditions to explore the underlying regulatory mechanisms. The findings offer new insights into DFU progression and potential therapeutic targets.

Materials and methods

In the clinical study, blood samples from DFU patients and non-DFU diabetic controls were analyzed to assess miR-191-5p expression and its correlation with relevant clinical parameters. In the experimental study, human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC) were cultured under high-glucose conditions to evaluate the functional role of miR-191-5p. Assays were performed to assess cell proliferation, migration, angiogenesis-related gene expression, and VEGFA protein levels. Additionally, dual-luciferase reporter assays were conducted to confirm the interaction between miR-191-5p and VEGFA.

Participant recruitment and sample collection

From June 2020 to June 2023, a total of 208 patients from the Endocrinology Department of Shijiazhuang Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital were enrolled, comprising 105 patients with uncomplicated T2DM and 102 patients with T2DM complicated by DFU. The diagnosis of T2DM was based on the 2020 Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes, with the exclusion of non-T2DM cases based on clinical characteristics and islet autoimmune marker testing. DFU was diagnosed according to the 2019 Chinese Guidelines for Diabetic Foot Prevention and Treatment. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee, and all participants provided written informed consent.

Inclusion criteria: Participants aged 18–75 years without a history of malignant tumors, suspected malignancies at the wound site, severe organ dysfunction, anemia, hypoproteinemia, autoimmune diseases, sepsis, or thrombotic disorders. Pregnant or lactating individuals and those who had received anticancer drugs, immunosuppressants, hormonal therapies, or exogenous cytokines within the previous 6 months were also excluded.

A 2 mL fasting venous blood sample was collected from each participant the morning after hospital admission using standard biochemical tubes. Serum was separated by centrifugation at 1000 rpm, transferred to sterile enzyme-free EP tubes, and stored at −80°C.

Cell cultured

HUVEC were obtained from the ATCC (PCS-100-013, USA). As recommended in the instructions, HUVEC were grown in the specialized endothelial cell medium (ECM, Sciencell, USA) supplemented with 5% FBS (Gibco, USA). Cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. To establish the high-glucose injury model, D-(+)-glucose (Sigma, USA) was mixed with the ECM to maintain a glucose dosage of 30 mmol/L [20, 21].

Cell transfection

HUVEC were seeded in 6-well plates (Corning, USA) at a density of 2 × 105 cells per well and transfected when they reached 60–70% confluence. The miR-191-5p inhibitor, mimic, si-VEGFA, and negative controls (NC) were provided by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). Using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA), cells were transfected with miR-191-5p inhibitor, si-VEGFA, and their NC at a dose of 50 nM. Transfection efficiency was confirmed via qRT-PCR.

The cells were divided into the following experimental groups: Control group, High-glucose (HG) group, HG + miR-NC group, HG + miR-191-5p-inhibitor group, HG + miR-191-5p-inhibitor + si-NC group, HG + miR-191-5p-inhibitor + si-VEGFA group.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA from the samples was separated using TRIzol (Invitrogen). The experimental operation of reverse transcription was performed using kits produced from Taraka (Japan), including the First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit and PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit. The final fluorescence quantification was completed using the SYBR Green Master Mix (Takara). VEGFA mRNA expression was assessed, with GAPDH as the internal control. U6 was used as the internal control for miR-191-5p. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Based on the prediction analysis, target vectors were synthesized. The wild-type and mutant sequences of VEGFA (WT-VEGFA/MT-VEGFA) were cloned into the pGL3-basic luciferase reporter vector (Promega, USA). HEK293T cells (ATCC) were co-transfected with miR-191-5p mimics or NC and the respective luciferase constructs. Luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Promega).

CCK-8 assays

HUVEC (5 × 103 cells/well) were seeded into 96-well plates (Corning). At different time points (24, 48, and 72 h), 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent (Dojindo, Japan) was mixed in each well of each treatment group. After 2 h of incubation, absorbance (450 nm) was measured using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA).

Transwell assays

HUVEC (5 × 104 cells/well) were suspended in ECM and seeded into the upper chamber of 24-well plates (Corning). ECM supplemented with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation, non-migrated cells on the upper surface were gently removed with a cotton swab. Migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Beyotime, China) and stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Sigma). Cells were then imaged and counted using an inverted microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Western blot

Total protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors, with all procedures performed on ice. Following lysis, samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm to collect the supernatant. Protein concentrations were quantified using the Enhanced BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime). Equal amounts of protein were subjected to SDS–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary antibody against VEGFA (1:1000, Abcam, UK). After washing with TBS-T (Tris-buffered saline containing 0.05% Tween-20, pH 7.4), membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:4000, Proteintech) in blocking buffer (Beyotime) for 2 h at room temperature. GAPDH (1:5000, Proteintech) served as the loading control. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection kit (Beyotime) and imaged using the Amersham Imager 600 system.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD. Clinical data were analyzed using the Wilcoxon test or Chi-square test. Cell experimental assays were repeated at least three times, and one-way ANOVA (Bonferroni’s post hoc test) was used for statistical. Normality and homogeneity of variance were assessed before statistical analyses. R (4.4.0) and GraphPad Prism 9 were used for Statistical analyses and data visualization. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was conducted to evaluate diagnostic performance, and Pearson correlation was calculated to assess relationships. Univariate logistic regression was performed to identify potential risk factors. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Clinical relevance of miR-191-5p in DFU

The expression of miR-191-5p was significantly elevated by approximately 50% in the serum of patients with the DFU group (Fig. 1A, P < 0.05). ROC curve analysis revealed that miR-191-5p had diagnostic potential for DFU, with an AUC of 0.762, specificity of 0.761, and sensitivity of 0.692 (Fig. 1B). Notably, miR-191-5p levels exhibited a significant positive correlation with ulcer area, CRP, WBC, and NEUT (Fig. 1C, P < 0.05). Although an inverse correlation with HDL-C was observed, it was not significant (Fig. 1C, P = 0.09). miR-191-5p directly targets VEGFA

Bioinformatic predictions from the ENCORI database indicated that VEGFA is a potential target of miR-191-5p (Fig. 2A). This interaction was validated via dual-luciferase reporter assay, where miR-191-5p significantly suppressed luciferase activity in WT-VEGFA but not in MT-VEGFA (Fig. 2B, P < 0.05). Impacts of miR-191-5p and VEGFA on HUVEC activity and angiogenesis

Transfection experiments were performed to modulate miR-191-5p and VEGFA expression in HUVEC, with qRT-PCR confirming successful transfection. HG treatment significantly upregulated miR-191-5p levels and downregulated VEGFA levels (Fig. 3A, P < 0.05). Similarly, VEGFA protein levels were reduced following HG treatment (Fig. 3B, P < 0.05). Downregulation of miR-191-5p increased VEGFA protein expression, whereas co-transfection with si-VEGFA suppressed VEGFA protein abundance (Fig. 3B, P < 0.05). Transfection with a miR-191-5p inhibitor effectively reduced miR-191-5p levels and restored VEGFA expression, whereas co-treatment with si-VEGFA abolished this effect. HG also suppressed key angiogenesis-related genes PDGF-B, PDGFR-β, and FGF-2, but inhibition of miR-191-5p partially rescued their expression (Fig. 3C, P < 0.05). This rescue effect was reversed upon VEGFA silencing. Similarly, HG impaired HUVEC migration (Fig. 3D, P < 0.05) and proliferation (Fig. 3E, P < 0.05), but miR-191-5p inhibition restored these functions. Discussion

DFU remains one of the most severe and challenging complications of DM [2]. Emerging evidence highlights the critical regulatory role of miRNA in diabetes pathogenesis and its complications. miRNAs are now recognized as key modulators of various phases of wound healing, including angiogenesis, inflammation, and re-epithelialization [10, 22]. For instance, miR-21 has been shown to enhance angiogenesis and wound healing in diabetic mouse models following treatment with biomaterials [23]. Similarly, miR-221-3p can suppress the inflammatory response in wound tissues, thereby promoting healing [11]. In the present clinical study, patients with DFU exhibited significantly elevated levels of inflammatory markers (CRP, WBC, and NEUT), all of which were identified as risk factors for DFU. These findings indicate a higher susceptibility to infections in DFU patients. Persistent inflammation involving multiple proinflammatory mediators may promote systemic inflammatory responses, potentially resulting in gangrene or tissue necrosis [24, 25]. Notably, our results revealed a significant upregulation of miR-191-5p in DFU patients compared to diabetic individuals without complications, consistent with previous research [13]. Additionally, miR-191-5p expression is positively correlated with blood glucose fluctuations in T2DM [14] and is dysregulated in obese individuals experiencing insulin resistance and glucose homeostasis disruptions [26]. Although research on miR-191-5p in diabetes is still limited, accumulating evidence supports its association with both the development of diabetes and its complications, including DFU. Importantly, our study demonstrated that miR-191-5p abundance was positively correlated with ulcer wound area, suggesting that miR-191-5p dysregulation may contribute to impaired wound healing. Logistic regression analysis further confirmed that miR-191-5p is an independent risk factor for DFU. Moreover, based on ROC curve analysis, miR-191-5p exhibits diagnostic potential, supporting its utility as a potential biomarker and contributing factor in the progression of DFU.

Angiogenesis is a vital component of wound healing, with endothelial cells playing a central role in neovascularization [27, 28]. Extensive research has demonstrated that miRNAs regulate endothelial cell proliferation and migration, thereby affecting neovascularization at wound sites [22]. For instance, miR-106a-5p enhances angiogenesis and promotes wound healing by stimulating endothelial cell viability and migration [29], while stem cell-derived exosomes can restore HUVEC function under HG conditions by regulating miR-204-3p expression [30]. In our study, functional assays in HUVECs revealed that upregulation of miR-191-5p under HG conditions significantly impaired endothelial cell proliferation and migration. Angiogenic growth factors also play a vital role in regulating angiogenesis by modulating endothelial cell function. PDGF-β and PDGFR-β contribute to vascular stability by recruiting supportive cells to newly formed vessels [31, 32]. As a mitogen, FGF-2 promotes endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and survival [33]. miR-191-5p downregulated PDGF-B, PDGFR-β, and FGF-2 angiogenic mRNA. These results imply that miR-191-5p disrupts endothelial function and angiogenic signaling, thereby compromising wound repair.

miRNAs typically exert their regulatory effects by binding to the target mRNAs, resulting in post-transcriptional repression or degradation [34]. Bioinformatics analysis predicted potential binding sites between miR-191-5p and VEGFA. VEGFA is a master regulator of angiogenesis. It is reported that mice overexpressing AURKA showed increased VEGFA expression, which was associated with enhanced angiogenesis and higher capillary density [35]. Topical administration of lipid nanoparticles encapsulating VEGFA accelerated wound closure and enhanced tissue regeneration following surgical injury [36]. VEGFA suppression can impair neovascularization and delay wound healing in DFU patients [27]. Our findings align with previous reports showing reduced VEGFA expression in DFU wounds, reinforcing the notion that VEGFA downregulation contributes to poor wound healing outcomes [37]. Additionally, a negative correlation between miR-191-5p and VEGFA expression was found, indicating a possible regulatory interaction. Previous studies have shown that VEGFA can be directly targeted by various miRNAs, such as miR-20b-5p, which suppresses VEGFA expression in HUVECs [38], and miR-320, whose downregulation under HG conditions leads to elevated VEGFA expression [39]. The present study demonstrated that VEGFA expression was regulated by miR-191-5p in HUVEC functional analyses. These results indicated that miR-191-5p impacts HUVEC migration, proliferation, and angiogenesis by targeting VEGFA.

Many diseases are associated with disruptions in specific signaling pathways. Mechanistically, while our study confirmed that miR-191-5p directly targets VEGFA to regulate endothelial cell function in HUVECs, the downstream signaling pathways involved remain to be elucidated. AKT, a central protein kinase, is well known for its critical role in regulating cell growth and angiogenesis [40]. One study reported that Buxuhuayu decoction enhanced VEGFA expression and promoted angiogenesis in a diabetic ulcer model via activation of the AKT signaling pathway [41]. Furthermore, VEGFA is strongly implicated in post-ischemic angiogenesis, and inhibition of the PI3K/AKT pathway has been shown to suppress neovascularization [42]. These findings suggest that miR-191-5p may influence the progression of DFU by targeting VEGFA and subsequently modulating the AKT pathway. Although our current study did not directly investigate this regulatory axis, it presents a compelling avenue for future research. Subsequent studies will aim to explore whether miR-191-5p indirectly affects AKT signaling through VEGFA suppression, which could provide deeper insight into the molecular mechanisms underlying angiogenesis impairment in DFU.

Although preliminary plans for animal experiments were considered, they could not be implemented due to constraints in funding and technical resources. Future research will aim to include animal models to elucidate the in vivo mechanisms of miR-191-5p in DFU progression. Notably, clinical data indicate a significant correlation between miR-191-5p expression and inflammatory markers (CRP, WBC, and NEUT), suggesting that miR-191-5p may influence DFU via inflammation-related pathways. This potential mechanism warrants further investigation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, miR-191-5p was upregulated in DFU patients and identified as an independent risk factor. Clinically, its expression correlated with ulcer area and inflammatory markers, indicating its relevance to disease severity. Functionally, miR-191-5p impaired wound healing by downregulating VEGFA, thereby inhibiting angiogenesis and endothelial cell activity. These findings suggest that miR-191-5p may serve as both a biomarker and a therapeutic target, offering new insight into the molecular mechanisms underlying DFU progression.

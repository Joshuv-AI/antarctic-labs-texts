Letter to the Editor on Artificial Intelligence–Driven Segmentation of 3 Oral Diseases
Authors: Shencong Xu, Hangying Jin
Published: 2026

Dear Editor—We read with great interest the article by Cheng et al, “Artificial Intelligence-Driven Segmentation of Three Oral Diseases: Enabling Precision Diagnosis and Decision Support,” recently published in the International Dental Journal.1 The authors present a clinically annotated dataset and evaluate multiple deep learning models to achieve precise segmentation and classification of oral lichen planus, oral leukoplakia, and oral benign ulcers. This work represents an important step toward standardized artificial intelligence–assisted diagnosis in oral medicine.

The study’s strengths include expert-validated annotations, comparative evaluation of 5 deep learning models, and development of a reproducible framework for lesion localization and disease identification. Such automated systems may improve diagnostic efficiency, support cancer risk monitoring, and enhance access to care, particularly in resource-limited settings.2

Several aspects merit further consideration. First, the dataset comprises 350 patients from 2 hospitals, which may limit generalizability across diverse populations. Multicenter collaboration could strengthen external validity. Second, the study focuses on 3 diseases without subtype stratification, despite known differences in malignant potential and management. Incorporating additional oral mucosal diseases and longitudinal imaging across disease stages would enhance clinical applicability. Third, some oral mucosal diseases, such as oral lichen planus, present with extraoral manifestations.3 Integrating multimodal data, including skin involvement where relevant, may improve diagnostic accuracy.

Overall, Cheng et al1 provide a valuable benchmark for artificial intelligence applications in oral medicine and lay important groundwork for more comprehensive, generalizable diagnostic systems.

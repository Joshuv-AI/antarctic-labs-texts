Incidence, Predictors, and Outcomes of Symptomatic Intracranial Hemorrhage after Tenecteplase in Acute Ischemic Stroke
Authors: Arpan Dutta, Uddalak Chakraborty, Subhadeep Gupta, Soumozit Banerjee, Chayan Mondal, Arnab Sarkar, Deep Das, Rahul Kumar, Shashvat M Desai, Ashutosh P Jadhav, Snehasis Das, Samir K Datta, Chinmoy Barik, Somnath Ghosh, Bichitra Biswas, Suprim Sadhukhan, Souvik Sen, Soumya Ghosh, Nitai C Mandal, Subhransu S Datta, Swapan Saren, Manimoy Bandyopadhyay, Biman K Ray
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and Objectives:Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (SICH) after thrombolysis is a major safety concern in the management of acute ischemic stroke (AIS). This study evaluates the predictors and outcomes of SICH among AIS patients receiving tenecteplase.Methods:This multicenter retrospective observational study included AIS patients who received tenecteplase between December 2021 and June 2024 at 41 Swasthya Ingit Telestroke Network hospitals in West Bengal, India.Results:Of 1,463 tenecteplase-treated AIS patients, 68 (4.64%) developed SICH. Mixed-effects multivariable logistic regression showed that age >61 years (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 2.37, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.38–4.07), hyperlipidemia (aOR 2.84, 95% CI 1.28–6.31), atrial fibrillation (aOR 4.84, 95% CI 1.86–12.59), smoking (aOR 2.55, 95% CI 1.30–5.00), systolic blood pressure >150 mmHg on admission (aOR 2.09, 95% CI 1.25–3.48), and a baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score >9 (aOR 1.71, 95% CI 1.03–2.86) were associated with higher odds of SICH. An Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) <8 was an additional risk factor among patients with middle cerebral artery (MCA) territory infarcts (aOR 3.32, 95% CI 1.70–6.50). Among the 313 patients who underwent angiography, the presence of large vessel occlusion was also associated with increased odds of SICH (aOR 5.65, 95% CI 1.86–17.19). In the SICH group, significantly fewer patients achieved a favorable outcome (mRS 0–2) at 90 days (7.4% vs 60.6%, P < 0.001), and all-cause mortality was significantly higher (79.4% vs 12.3%, P < 0.001).Conclusions:SICH after tenecteplase use in AIS is associated with increased morbidity and mortality. Older age, higher baseline NIHSS scores, elevated systolic blood pressure on admission, smoking, hyperlipidemia, atrial fibrillation, lower ASPECTS, and large vessel occlusion are associated with a higher risk of SICH in tenecteplase-treated AIS patients.

Introduction

Thrombolytic agents have transformed the management of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) and are recommended in guidelines for use within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.[12] Alteplase was the only approved thrombolytic agent for AIS for many years, until the recent approval of tenecteplase. Tenecteplase is noninferior to alteplase and has a similar safety profile.[345] Evidence also suggests that tenecteplase may be superior to alteplase in AIS patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO).[67] Hemorrhagic complications, particularly symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (SICH), remain a significant concern. A large-scale real-world study has shown lower odds of SICH with tenecteplase compared with alteplase.[8] Its ease of administration and lower cost further support tenecteplase as a preferred agent in AIS. In this study, we evaluated the incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, its predictors, and outcomes in AIS patients treated with tenecteplase.

Methods

A multicenter retrospective observational study was conducted using data from the Swasthya Ingit Telestroke Program in West Bengal, India. The State Program Management Unit, Department of Health and Family Welfare, Government of West Bengal, maintains de-identified data (demographics, clinical details, radiologic images, and follow-up) for AIS patients who receive thrombolysis across 41 Swasthya Ingit Telestroke Network hospitals. AIS patients who received thrombolysis with tenecteplase under the program between December 2021 and June 2024 were included. Patients who underwent mechanical thrombectomy after receiving tenecteplase were excluded.

Most patients underwent a noncontrast computed tomography (CT) scan of the brain to exclude hemorrhage before thrombolysis. In some cases, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain was performed instead of CT. All patients received tenecteplase at a dose of 0.25 mg/kg within 4.5 hours (or within 3 hours in selected cases) of symptom onset or last known well, in accordance with American Heart Association/American Stroke Association (AHA/ASA) and European Stroke Organisation (ESO) guidelines.[12] MR/CT angiography to identify LVO was performed in a subset of patients, as this facility is available in only 12 hospitals. LVO is defined as occlusion of the basilar artery, internal carotid artery, or proximal segments (M1/M2) of the middle cerebral artery.[8] Repeat brain imaging (predominantly CT, occasionally MRI) was performed 24–36 hours after thrombolysis and earlier if there was clinical suspicion of SICH. All imaging data were evaluated by neurologists and neuroradiologists at the Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education and Research (IPGMER).

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was defined as a local or remote type 2 parenchymal hemorrhage on imaging within 36 hours of thrombolysis, accompanied by neurological deterioration of ≥4 points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) compared with baseline or the lowest NIHSS score within 24 hours, or resulting in death within 24 hours.[9] The modified Rankin Scale (mRS) was used to assess outcomes at 90 days; a favorable outcome was defined as an mRS score of 0–2.

This study involved analysis of de-identified secondary data maintained by the State Program Management Unit. Institutional ethical approval was therefore not required and was formally waived by the Research Oversight Committee of the IPGMER (IPGME&R/IEC/2025/0261/E). Written informed consent was obtained by the on-duty medical officer at each spoke hospital before thrombolysis. A standardized consent form, prepared by the Department of Neurology, Bangur Institute of Neurosciences, IPGMER, was used across all network hospitals.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Jamovi version 2.6.19.0 to compare patients with and without SICH. Categorical variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test, and continuous variables using the Mann–Whitney U test. Variables with a P value <0.1 in univariate analysis were included in a binary logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of SICH. Mixed-effects multivariable logistic regression was used to assess the impact of SICH on outcomes. Ordinal logistic regression evaluated the effect of SICH across the entire spectrum of mRS scores. Missing data were handled using multivariate imputation by random forests with predictive mean matching.

Results

A total of 1,463 AIS patients were included in the study. Of these, 209 (14.28%) had posterior circulation stroke, 16 (1.09%) had anterior cerebral artery (ACA) territory infarcts, and 1,238 (84.62%) had middle cerebral artery (MCA) territory strokes. Overall, 206 patients (14.08%) had hemorrhagic transformation (hemorrhagic infarct type 1: 2.05%, type 2: 3.76%; parenchymal hematoma type 1: 2.12%, type 2: 6.15%). Sixty-eight patients (4.64%) had SICH. Table 1 compares demographic characteristics, clinical features, and outcomes between the SICH and non-SICH groups. The median age was significantly higher in the SICH group than in the non-SICH group (65 vs 61 years, P < 0.001). None of the patients were on anticoagulants. The prevalence of smoking, hyperlipidemia, and atrial fibrillation was significantly higher in the SICH group. Systolic blood pressure on admission was also higher in the SICH group (160 vs 150 mmHg, P = 0.013). The baseline NIHSS score was higher in the SICH group (10.5 vs 9, P = 0.011). Angiography was performed in 313 patients (21.39%), of whom 113 (36.1%) had LVO. Among these, three patients had posterior circulation LVO, and none had occlusion in the A1 segment of the ACA. A significantly higher proportion of patients in the SICH group had LVO compared with the non-SICH group (76% vs 32.6%, P < 0.001). Among patients with MCA territory infarcts, the median Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) was significantly lower in the SICH group (7.5 vs 9, P < 0.001).

Mixed-effects multivariable logistic regression showed that age >61 years (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 2.37, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.38–4.07), hyperlipidemia (aOR 2.84, 95% CI 1.28–6.31), atrial fibrillation (aOR 4.84, 95% CI 1.86–12.59), smoking (aOR 2.55, 95% CI 1.30–5.00), systolic blood pressure >150 mmHg on admission (aOR 2.09, 95% CI 1.25–3.48), and baseline NIHSS score >9 (aOR 1.71, 95% CI 1.03–2.86) were associated with increased odds of SICH. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve for this model was 0.73 (95% CI 0.674–0.786). Among patients who underwent angiography (313 patients, 21.39%), the presence of LVO was associated with a higher risk of SICH (aOR 5.65, 95% CI 1.86–17.19). Incorporating LVO into the prediction model increased the area under the ROC curve to 0.84 (95% CI 0.773–0.907). An ASPECTS <8 was also associated with a higher risk of SICH in patients with MCA territory infarcts (aOR 3.32, 95% CI 1.70–6.50). Including ASPECTS in the model yielded an area under the ROC curve of 0.74 (95% CI 0.679–0.798). When both LVO and ASPECTS were included, the area under the ROC curve was 0.802 (95% CI 0.718–0.887).

Figure 1 shows the distribution of mRS scores at 90 days in patients with and without SICH. A significantly smaller proportion of patients in the SICH group achieved a favorable outcome (mRS 0–2) at 90 days (7.4% vs 60.6%, P < 0.001). All-cause mortality within 90 days was markedly higher in the SICH group (79.4% vs 12.3%, P < 0.001). Multivariable ordinal logistic regression demonstrated a significant difference between groups across the full range of mRS scores (common OR 19.96, 95% CI 2.398–3.652). Mixed-effects multivariable logistic regression showed that SICH was independently associated with poor outcomes (aOR 13.68, 95% CI 5.34–35.03) and increased mortality (aOR 24.19, 95% CI 12.54–46.67). These associations remained consistent after adjustment for LVO (aOR 13.69, 95% CI 1.75–107.17 for poor outcomes; aOR 53.18, 95% CI 8.69–325.34 for mortality) and ASPECTS (aOR 11.23, 95% CI 4.38–28.83 for poor outcomes; aOR 21.03, 95% CI 10.73–41.21 for mortality).

Discussion

SICH is the most feared complication of intravenous thrombolysis in AIS. Its pathophysiology is multifactorial and includes ischemic tissue injury, coagulopathy, blood–brain barrier disruption, and reperfusion injury.[10] Although the incidence of SICH is generally low, it varies across studies due to differences in definitions. Although a real-world observational study showed a lower risk of SICH with tenecteplase, most clinical studies have found no significant difference between tenecteplase and alteplase.[48111213141516] However, a 0.4 mg/kg dose of tenecteplase is associated with a higher risk of SICH compared with lower doses used in AIS.[1516] The reported rate of SICH with 0.25 mg/kg tenecteplase ranges from 1.6% to 5.8%.[817] Previous studies in Indian populations have reported SICH rates of 0%–5.6% with this dose.[1819] The Indian Registry in Ischemic Stroke–Tenecteplase reported a 0.6% SICH rate with a 0.2 mg/kg dose.[20]

Previous studies have identified several predictors of SICH following thrombolysis in AIS. Factors such as older age, hypertension, hyperglycemia, elevated systolic blood pressure on admission, higher baseline NIHSS score, and lower ASPECTS have been associated with SICH in alteplase-treated patients.[212223] The Tenecteplase Treatment in Ischemic Stroke registry has reported similar predictors of parenchymal hematoma formation in AIS patients who received Tenecteplase.[17] In our study, age >61 years, hyperlipidemia, atrial fibrillation, smoking, systolic blood pressure >150 mmHg on admission, baseline NIHSS score >9, ASPECTS <8, and the presence of LVO were associated with higher odds of SICH. A previous study reported an association between low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol levels and increased risk of SICH after thrombolysis, which contrasts with our findings.[24] In our study population, the presence of hyperlipidemia as a comorbid condition was associated with a higher risk of SICH. However, data on cholesterol levels and statin use were unavailable, which may explain this discrepancy. Atrial fibrillation has also been associated with an increased risk of hemorrhagic transformation after thrombolysis in AIS.[2526] Although anticoagulant use has been linked to a higher risk of SICH in prior studies, none of the study patients were on anticoagulants, as thrombolysis in such patients is relatively contraindicated.[26] Smoking was associated with increased SICH risk in our study, although previous research has not established a clear connection between smoking and post-thrombolysis SICH.[2728] These findings should therefore be interpreted with caution due to potential residual confounding.

AIS patients with SICH after thrombolysis generally have poor outcomes and high mortality.[10232930] In the present study, the mortality rate in the SICH group was approximately 80%, which aligns with findings from the Safe Implementation of Treatment in Stroke–International Stroke Thrombolysis Register.[30] In that registry, only 2.8% of patients with SICH achieved an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days, compared with 7.4% in this study.

The present study has several limitations. First, it is a retrospective observational study. Second, clinical outcomes were not assessed using a centralized, blinded process. Third, angiography was not performed in most patients. Fourth, laboratory data were unavailable, which may limit adjustment for potential confounders. Fifth, multiple imputation was used to handle missing data. Repeat brain imaging was not performed in 158 patients (10.8%); of these, 135 did not meet clinical criteria for SICH (neurological deterioration of ≥4 NIHSS points from baseline or the lowest score within 24 hours, or death within 24 hours). Multiple imputation was also used for the remaining 23 patients. Follow-up data were missing for 99 cases (6.7%) and were similarly imputed. Finally, patients who underwent mechanical thrombectomy were excluded. These limitations may affect the generalizability of our study results. However, sensitivity analysis excluding patients without repeat imaging yielded similar predictors with comparable effect sizes, supporting the robustness of our findings. [Supplementary material] Internal validation of the prediction models, including ASPECTS and LVO, within their respective subsets demonstrated reproducibility within the dataset, suggesting that the models are not substantially overfitted [Supplementary material].

Conclusions

The present study indicated that older age, higher baseline NIHSS scores, increased systolic blood pressure on admission, smoking, hyperlipidemia, atrial fibrillation, lower ASPECTS, and large vessel occlusion are associated with an increased risk of SICH following tenecteplase thrombolysis in AIS. Patients who develop SICH have poor outcomes, with very high mortality. This large multicenter real-world study addresses an important evidence gap in telestroke-based systems in low- and middle-income countries.

Comparison of visual quality after implantation of trifocal intraocular lenses with different diopter values in the two eyes: a retrospective study
Authors: Yanfeng Zeng, Qiyao Ma, Yuancheng Qu, Dongqing Wang, Xiaoli Zhou, Xiaoqin Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study evaluated the postoperative visual quality of myopic patients with differences in the diopter of intraocular lens (IOL) implantation in both eyes.MethodsThis is a retrospective observational study, 62 patients received binocular AT LISA tri 839MP trifocal intraocular lens implantation in Lixiang Eye Hospital of Soochow University from September 2021 to June 2023 were included for analysis, 31 patients with IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D (study group) and 31 patients with IOL diopter value difference < 1.0 D (control group). The far intermediate, and near vison, modulation transfer function (MTF), Strehl ratio (SR), higher-order aberrations, pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision, tertiary visual function, spectacle independence, light disturbance phenomenon, overall satisfaction and quality of life and VF-11R scores in both groups were comparatively assessed 3 months after the surgery on the second eye.ResultsPostoperative visual acuity was significantly improved in both groups. There were no statistically significant differences in uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) and uncorrected intermediate visual (UIVA) for both eyes and the right eyes between the two groups. There were also no statistically significant differences in UDVA, UIVA and for the left eyes between the two groups. uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA) levels of both eyes and the right eyes, total ocular higher-order aberrations and trefoil aberrations were better in the control group than in the study group. There were no statistically significant differences in MTF, SR, simultaneous vision, fusion vision, coma, spherical aberration and VF-11R scores between the two groups. Stereopsis was improved in the control group versus the study group. The glare was better in the control group than in the study group. Night glare correlated with IOL diopter value difference.ConclusionsThe spectacle independence rate reached 100% in both groups. There were no statistically significant differences in far and intermediate vision, simultaneous vision and fusion vision between the two groups. The study group had corrected anisometropia in both eyes, but stereopsis and near vision were inferior in these patients compared with the control group. Night glare correlated with binocular IOL diopter value difference.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-025-04429-6.Keywords: Anisometropia, Visual effects, AT LISA tri 839MP, IOL diopter value difference

Background

Anisometropia, which refers to unequal diopter value between the two eyes, is a common ophthalmological condition with an incidence rate of about 2.0–3.8.0.8% in children [1]. Indeed, it is rare for both eyes to have equal diopter values in the population, and most people have some degree of refractive difference. Anisometropia is more likely to occur among people with myopia. Mild anisometropia with both eyes having normal visual function is usually not clinically significant. However, once it exceeds a certain threshold, anisometropia interferes with the binocular single visual function [2]. Therefore, basic research and clinical practice generally consider a difference of more than 1.0 D (diopter) in the equivalent spherical power of the eyes under cycloplegia as the diagnostic criterion for anisometropia [3]. In clinical practice, we have observed that the refractive error measured during cataract refraction may not be sufficiently accurate. This suggests that using a difference in spherical equivalent greater than 1.0 D as the diagnostic criterion for anisometropia may be subject to error. This is because nuclear cataracts are often associated with an increase in myopia, while cortical cataracts are more frequently linked with hyperopia. Importantly, when myopia occurs in these cases, it is index myopia, not pseudo-myopia [4]. Myopic patients tend to develop nuclear cataracts earlier in clinical practice [5], and the phenomenon of “index myopia” caused by nuclear cataracts is very common. Essentially, the index myopia represents the myopia transfer caused by the change in the refractive state of the lens during the development of cataracts (mainly nuclear cataracts). Optometric results may show refractive error, but this is not axial myopia caused by elongation of the eyeball, but index myopia caused by changes in the refractive index of the lens caused by cataracts. Studies have shown that a 0.69 D anisometropia corresponds to a 1.0 D difference in of intraocular lens (IOL) diopter, which suggests that the difference in IOL diopter can indicate a certain degree of anisometropia [9]. Nowadays, cataract patients have increasingly higher requirements for postoperative full range of vision and visual quality. The cataract surgery technology has made significant progress and the design of IOL continues to innovate. For instance, the AT LISA tri 839MP was designed to provide three optical focal to reduce the need for glasses. Although previous studies have confirmed its effectiveness in improving vision and visual quality, and it has been widely adopted in the world [6–8], the effects on visual quality and three-tiered visual functions after implantation of IOLs with different diopter values in the two eyes remain unclear. This study aims to explore, through a retrospective investigation, the subjective and objective visual quality, tertiary visual function, satisfaction, eyeglass independence rate and light interference phenomenon of myopic patients with differences in the diopter of IOL implantation in both eyes.

Methods

Study design and participants

This is a retrospective observational study to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of AT LISA tri 839MP with different diopter values when implanted in two eyes. This study examined patients administered bilateral cataract surgery with AT LISA tri 839MP trifocal intraocular IOL implantation at Lixiang Eye Hospital of Soochow University from September 2021 to June 2023.When the intraocular lens diopter differs by 1.0 D, the postoperative refractive difference is approximately 0.69 D [9]. Clinically, we have observed that when the lens diopter differs by 1.0 D, most patients experience binocular incoordination. Therefore, we studied the impact of refractive parameters on postoperative visual quality within a smaller range. This study strictly followed the ethical principles and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Lixiang Eye Hospital of Soochow University (SLER2023222). The study was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consents were obtained from all the included patients.

Inclusion criteria were: cataract with myopic anisometropia patients aged between 18 and 80 years, corneal astigmatism < 0.75D, postoperative uncorrected visual acuity ≥ 0.6. Exclusion criteria were: concomitant severe ocular diseases such as proliferative diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration and macular hole, corneal scarring, corneal opacity, keratoconus, ocular trauma, chronic uveitis, glaucoma, strabismus, amblyopia (best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) < 0.8). The study group (IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D) consisted of all 31 patients who met the inclusion criteria during the study period. To create a comparable control group with sufficient statistical power, the first 31 patients from the same period who underwent the same surgery were included but had an IOL diopter value difference < 1.0 D. Patients with intraoperative or postoperative complications or those who were lost to follow-up were excluded. The IOL diopter value difference in the observaton group ranged from 1.0 D to 11.0 D. For missing case data, the main data will be verified by telephone.

Intraocular lens

AT Lisa tri 839MP is an aspheric trifocal IOL designed for refraction and diffraction using a hydrophilic acrylic material with a hydrophobic surface. The optical surface diameter is 6.0 mm, with the central 0–4.3 mm diameter region acting as the trifocal optical zone, while the 4.3–6.0 mm region serves as the bifocal optical zone. Additionally, the lens incorporates + 1.66 D and + 3.33 D add powers at the intermediate and near-distance focal points. The incident light energy is distributed to the far, intermediate and near focal according to 50%, 25% and 25%. Compared to bifocal IOLs, the inclusion of a middle focus enhances stereopsis, which is an important aspect in evaluating visual quality [10].

Preoperative examination

All patients underwent a complete preoperative evaluation, including uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) assessment in both eyes (logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution; log MAR), tonometry, retinal vision evaluation, slit lamp examination, eye A/B ultrasound, computer refractometry, IOL Master700, OPD-Scan III, Pentacam HR, macular OCT, dry eye comprehensive analysis, lacrimal duct irrigation assessment, as well as the evaluation of overall conditions such as blood pressure, blood glucose, and electrocardiogram. The IOL power of the desired lens was selected according to the results of IOL Master700.(The targeted refraction was ± 0.25 D, A Constant 118.5).

Surgical technique

All procedures were performed by a chief physician with extensive clinical experience. In all cases, 0.3% levofloxacin eye drops were instilled preoperatively. After achieving full mydriasis using compound tropicamide eye drops, surgery was conducted under topical anesthesia with proparacaine hydrochloride eye drops. A 2.2 mm clear corneal primary incision was made, viscoelastic agent was driven through the incision, and capsulorhexis was used. The capsulorhexis (5.0–5.5 mm in size) was performed on the anterior membrane of the lens with forceps and then on the corneal side incision, water separation lens cortex and lens nucleus. Phacoemulsification was performed to extract the lens nucleus and cortex. The anterior and posterior capsules were thoroughly polished, and the AT LISA tri 839MP IOL was implanted into the capsular bag through the main limbal incision. The viscoelastic agent was removed, and the corneal incision was sealed by hydration. The IOL position was adjusted, and the anterior chamber was restored. Finally, dexamethasone-tobramycin ophthalmic ointment (Alcon) was applied to the conjunctival sac.

Postoperative examination

All patients were followed up for three months after the surgery of the second eye, assessing UDVA at 5 m (logMAR), uncorrected intermediate visual (UIVA) at 80 cm (logMAR) and uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA) at 40 cm (logMAR), intraocular pressure, and both subjective and objective refraction. A slit-lamp microscope was used to observe the incision and intraocular lens, and tests for simultaneous vision, fusion vision, and Titmus stereopsis were also performed.《Binocular Unequal Vision Examination Diagram》was developed by the Ophthalmology Department of the Navy General Hospital of the People’s Liberation Army of China. It is designed based on the classic theory that binocular single vision is divided into first-level simultaneous vision and second-level fused vision, as well as the principle of red and green complementarity. Currently, foreign binocular unequal vision examinations have evolved from single-threshold determination to multiple system function experiments. It provides an objective systematic standard for objectively evaluating binocular single vision and the psychological malleable function of vision. Simultaneous vision and fusion vision: The《Binocular Unequal Vision Examination Diagram》” was used. This atlas is composed of three types of images, each type of image difference from 1% to 15%, divided into 15 levels, a total of 48 frames, and equipped with special red and green complementary glasses with the same color as the figure. The first type of binocular simultaneous aberration is composed of two semi-circular ancient coin figures of different sizes, overprinted in red and green, respectively, with the center marked with a cross fixation point. Figure A0. The diameter of two semi-circular ancient coins is 4 cm, and the central square hole is 2 × 2 cm (half is 2 × 1 cm). The size of the green semi-circular ancient coin picture A1-15 is unchanged, and the red ancient coin picture A1-15 is reduced by 1%−15% in turn, conveying information to both eyes through the red and green lenses. This type of map is used to measure the difference in retinal image size between the two eyes. The normal range is 0 to 3%. The second type of fused aberration map is overprinted from a matching image of a red head without ears and a green head without eyes of different sizes. Figure B0 red and green heads are equal in size; Figure B1-15 Green heads without eyes are unchanged in size; Figure B1-15 red heads without ears are reduced by 1% to 15% in turn. The different red and green pictures were viewed separately by the two eyes, and then fused to make a human head with both ears and eyes. The second type of image was used to determine the fusion function of binocular aberrations and the allowable value of aberrations to maintain normal fusion function. The normal range is 2 to 7%.

The examination should be carried out in a good light and quiet environment, and the subject should pay attention and read the picture with both eyes at the same time. During the examination, the subjects wore special red and green glasses, red on the right, green on the left, and the examination distance was 30 ~ 40 cm. When the first type of picture is completed, the patient must switch between red and green glasses for another check to determine whether the eye shadow is enlarged or reduced. Results Evaluation: The first type of figure is based on the size difference of two semi-circular ancient coins, and the normal rate is 0 ~ 3%. The second type of pictures was based on the red and green head pictures when the reimage began to occur, and the normal rate was 2%−7%. Near stereoacuity was measured using stereopsis testing under natural light. OPD-Scan III was used to analyze visual quality, measuring total ocular higher-order aberrations, coma, trefoil, spherical aberration, modulation transfer function (MTF), Strehl ratio (SR), and the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision. The photopic luminance of the Placido ring with pupil center offset measured in a dark room from photopic to scotopic vision was 125.6 lx, and the scotopic luminance of the Placido ring was 3.5 lx. In addition, patients were required to complete a questionnaire to assess their subjective visual quality after surgery. In the first part of the questionnaire, the VF-11R visual function questionnaire (maximum score of 50) was used to assess visual quality in daily life after surgery. The Visual Function Index (VF-14) was originally developed to assess vision-specific activity limitations in patients with cataract and to assess cataract surgical outcomes [11], and has been widely used to report patient outcomes. The VF-11R is a modified Chinese version of the VF-14, which has been verified to be suitable for assessing the outcome of cataract surgery in the Chinese population [12]. A 5-point response scale was used, from “no difficulty”, “a little difficult”, “moderate difficulty”, “quite difficult” and “impossible to perform”. The second part of the satisfaction questionnaire (maximum score of 50) included 10 questions to assess each participant’s satisfaction with postoperative vision, quality of vision, spectacle independence and the incidence of glare and halos from light. The responses were scored from 5 to 1, based on satisfaction level: “very satisfied”, “satisfied”, “slightly dissatisfied”, “dissatisfied”, “very dissatisfied” (or “never”, “rarely”, “sometimes”, “often” and “always” satisfied). The total score including all questions was the score of this survey. This satisfaction questionnaire was based on items commonly used in previous ophthalmic studies focused on visual quality (QoV). Although this version has not undergone formal validation, it aligns with established instruments frequently used in clinical settings and captures subjective patient experiences that complement objective measures. It provides important insights into overall quality of life and functional vision following surgery.

Statistical analysis

SPSS v20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for statistical analysis. For measurement data conforming to normal distribution, the independent sample t-test was used for comparisons between the two groups. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare parameters not conforming to normal distribution. The paired-samples t test or Wilcoxon rank sum test was applied for within-group comparisons. The Chi-square test or Fisher exact test was used for count data. Simultaneous, fused and stereoscopic vision were compared using the R * C χ2 test. Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation between binocular crystalline aberration difference and optical interference phenomenon. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The 62 patients were included in this study. There were 31 patients in the control group (58.3% females and 41.7% males), with a mean age of 60.6 ± 10.8 years. There were 31 patients in the study group (44.8% females and 55.2% males), with an average age of 61.3 ± 11.3 years. There were no statistically significant differences in gender, age, left and right axial length, left and right eye UDVA and cylinder diopter between the two groups. There was a statistically significant difference in the spherical diopter between the two groups (P = 0.01). (Table 1).

Visual acuity

In the control group, preoperative mean UDVA levels for the left and right eyes were 1.14 ± 0.40 logMAR and 1.09 ± 0.38 logMAR, respectively. At 3 months after surgery UDVA levels were 0.04 ± 0.07 logMAR and 0.04 ± 0.07 logMAR, respectively. The UDVA levels for the left and right eyes in each group after the surgery were significantly better than that before the surgery (P < 0.05).

There were significant differences in binocular UNVA (P = 0.01 < 0.05) and right eye UNVA (P = 0.01 < 0.05) between the control and study groups after surgery. However, there were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in binocular and right-eye UIVA, as well as left-eye UDVA, UIVA and UNVA. MTF, SR

The areas under the MTF curves for the surgical eye in the control and study groups were 30.907 ± 7.744% and 29.111 ± 9.326%, respectively. There was no significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.09 > 0 0.05). SRs for the surgical eye in the control and study groups were 0.023 ± 0.023 and 0.016 ± 0.012, respectively, indicating no significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.30 > 0 0.05). The results showed that postoperative MTF and SR were similar in the study and control groups, suggesting comparable objective visual quality.

Total ocular higher-order aberrations

The total ocular higher-order aberrations of surgical eyes were 0.382 ± 0.200 μm and 0.562 ± 0.426 μm, respectively. Spherical aberration values were 0.053 ± 0.039 μm and 0.068 ± 0.088 μm, respectively. Coma aberration values were 0.142 ± 0.097 μm and 0.191 ± 0.202 μm, respectively. Trefoil aberration values were 0.288 ± 0.182 μm and 0.421 ± 0.267 μm, respectively. There were significant differences in total ocular higher-order aberration (P = 0.00 < 0 0.05) and trefoil aberration (P = 0.00 < 0.05) values between the control and study groups after surgery, but the differences in spherical aberration (P = 0.84 > 0 0.05) and coma aberration (P = 0.27 > 0 0.05) values were not significant between the control and study groups after surgery. The results show that total ocular higher-order aberration, spherical aberration, coma aberration and trefoil aberration in the surgical eye were higher in the study group compared with the control group, but only total ocular higher-order aberration and trefoil aberration were statistically significant differences between the two groups.

Simultaneous vision, fusion vision and stereoscopic vision

There was no significant difference in simultaneous vision and fusion vision between the control group and the study group (P = 0.45, 0.72 > 0.05). The Titmus stereogram was used to examine the patient’s near stereopsis function under natural light. The results showed that 8 cases (26%) in the control group and 3 (10%) in the study group exhibited good foveal vision (< 60”) post-surgery. Additionally, 21 cases (68%) in the control group and 18 (58%) in the study group had macular stereopsis (“70–200”), while 2 cases (6%) in the control group and 10 (32%) in the study group had peripheral stereopsis (“300–800”). The results indicate that stereoscopic vision was significantly better in the control group than in the study group (P = 0.02 < 0.05).

VF-11R function scale and satisfaction, light interference phenomenon, spectacle independence rate

The first part of the questionnaire used the VF-11R visual function scale to evaluate subjective visual quality for the patients included in this study. VF-11R scores were higher in the control group (49.5 ± 0.7) than in the study group (49.3 ± 0.0), but the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.10 > 0 0.05). Totally 61% of subjects had no difficulty in reading small print and 39% had slight difficulty in the control group. In the study group, there were 45% of subjects with no difficulty and 55% with slight difficulty (Fig. 1). The second part of the questionnaire mainly evaluated postoperative adverse light phenomena, overall satisfaction, and spectacle independence (Table S1). The results showed that the overall satisfaction score for the questionnaire was significantly lower in the study group (45.9 ± 2.6) compared with the control group (47.5 ± 2.0; P = 0.01 < 0 0.05). Satisfaction with far vision was higher in the control group versus the study group (control group, 100%; study group, 97%). The satisfaction rates of intermediate and near vision were 100% in both groups.

The halo and glare phenomena were more common in the study group (23% and 42%, respectively) than in the control group (3% and 16%, respectively), and these differences were statistically significant (P < 0 0.05). Specifically, 74% of patients in the control group never experienced glare during the day, and 26% experienced it rarely. In the study group, 68% of patients never experienced glare, 22% experienced it rarely and 10% experienced sometimes. At night, 45% of the control group never experienced glare, 36% experienced it rarely, 16% sometimes, and 3% often. In the study group, only 23% of patients never experienced glare, while 35% each experienced it rarely and sometimes, and 7% experienced it often. During the day, 94% of the control group never experienced halos, and 6% experienced them rarely. In the study group, 87% of patients never experienced halos, 10% experienced them rarely, and 3% sometimes. At night, 84% of the control group never experienced halos, 13% experienced them rarely, and 3% sometimes. In the study group, 68% of patients never experienced halos, 13% experienced them rarely, 16% sometimes, and 3% often. These results suggest that patients with IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D are more likely to experience visual disturbances such as halos and glare, with higher frequencies at night.

At 3 months, the rates of spectacle independence reached 100% in both the control and study groups. In conclusion, the study group had lower satisfaction scores than the control group, which may be mainly due to visual disturbances such as glare and halos that mostly occurred at night.

Factors related to the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision, binocular lens diopter value difference between the two eyes and light interference phenomena

To further investigate whether glare and halos are related to the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision, as well as the difference in lens diopter values between the two eyes, correlation analyses were performed. For the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vison in the right and left eyes, the control group had 0.115 ± 0.069 mm and 0.116 ± 0.082 mm, respectively (P = 1.00 > 0 0.05), while the study group showed 0.140 ± 0.103 mm and 0.138 ± 0.137 mm, respectively (P = 0.35 > 0 0.05). The differences were not statistically significant. The pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision in in the right and left eyes were greater in the study group than in the control group for both eyes, but the differences were not statistically significant (P = 0.25, 0.89 > 0 0.05). The results indicated that glare and halos were not caused by the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision. The Spearman’s method was used to analyze the correlation between binocular lens diopter value difference and visual interference phenomenon. There was a significant correlation between night glare and the difference in binocular lens diopter values (r = − 0.261, P = 0.00 < 0 0.05), meaning that the larger the lens diopter value difference, the higher the frequency of night halo occurrence. However, there was no correlation between white glare, white halo and night halo with the difference of lens diopter values between the two eyes (P = 0.40, 0.55, 0.23).

Discussion

With the widespread development of phacoemulsification and the continuous improvement of the intraocular lens technology, cataract surgery has entered the era of refractive surgery. Monofocal IOLs were applied to one target (e.g., near or far) for cataract surgery in the past [13], but could not meet the needs of patients’ work and life. Bifocal IOLs could improve near and far vision, but issues with intermediate vision still persist after surgery [14]. In order to achieve the goal of improving the patient’s full visual acuity, visual quality, and correcting presbyopia, trifocal intraocular lens have become a new option for cataract surgery, which could provide good functional vision in a large range of distances [10, 15, 16]. AT LISA tri839MP is a monolithic diffractive trifocal intraocular lens with an optical zone diameter of 6.0 mm and a total diameter of 11.0 mm [17]. It solves the light transmission phenomenon and poor intermediate vision level of traditional multifocal IOLs [18]. This lens has been shown to provide good near, intermediate and far vision outcomes, along with high postoperative satisfaction [19–23]. The aim of this study was to investigate the effect of binocular implantation of AT Lisa tri 839MP with different diopter values on postoperative visual function. In this study, we found that compared with preoperative results, UDVA levels for the left and right eyes in the control and study groups after surgery were statistically significant. The results indicated that postoperative far vision was significantly improved. This is consistent with Alio’s results, AT LISA Tri was found to provide good retinal image quality at a distance, probably due to the aspheric design of the AT LISA Tri [24]. In our study, we wanted to investigate the effect of anisometropia on postoperative visual acuity. In clinical practice, it is generally considered that the difference of binocular spherical equivalent under cycloplegia is greater than 1.0 D as the diagnostic standard of anisometropia [3]. However, in clinical practice, we have observed that using spherical equivalent difference greater than 1.0 D as the diagnostic standard of anisometropia may have errors, which is related to the change of lens refractive status caused by cataract. It has been shown that 1.0 D anisometropia corresponds to approximately 1.5 D difference in IOL diopter and 0.69 D anisometropia corresponds to 1.0 D difference in IOL diopter [9]. In order to observe the effect of a smaller range of anisometropia on postoperative visual acuity, we chose 1.0 D difference in implanted lens diopter as our grouping basis, which is equivalent to the effect of 0.69 D anisometropia on postoperative visual acuity. We also compared UDVA, UIVA and UNVA between the control and study groups. For uncorrected near vision, both eyes and right eye’s UNVA levels were better in the control group than in the study group, while both groups showed similar levels of UDVA and UIVA. The results suggested that IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D had a significant effect on UNVA but may not affect UDVA and UIVA. Our results were similar to those of previous studies: four months after implantation of AT LISA tri839MP in both eyes, Mean visual acuity results were very good and uncorrected binocular vision was equal to or less than 0.08 logMAR for the three distances [25].

MTF and SR are commonly used as indicators of objective visual quality [26]. In this study, both MTF and SR were higher in the control group than in the study group, but the differences were not statistically significant (P >0.05). The results suggested that anisometropia greater than 1.0 D does not significantly affect MTF and SR. The human eye is a complex refractive system, which is not a perfect imaging system. In addition to low-order aberrations (myopia, hyperopia and astigmatism), there are also higher-order aberrations such as spherical aberration, coma and trefoil. Higher-order aberrations may be one of the factors affecting the recovery of visual quality after cataract surgery [27]. The effects of high-order aberrations on visual acuity are different. Previous findings pointed to coma and spherical aberrations as the main high-order aberrations affecting visual function [28]. Binocular vision performs better than monocular vision, a phenomenon known as binocular summation. However, binocular summation is not completely dependent on neural factors of the visual system, but also on differences in the optical quality between the two eyes, e.g., anisometropia and differences in higher-order aberrations. In general, a decrease in binocular summation correlates with an increase of difference in optical quality between the two eyes [29]. Studies reported that patients with anisometropic amblyopia exhibit differences in higher-order aberrations between their eyes [30]. We found that the differences in high-order and trefoil aberrations for the operated eyes under the 4 mm pupil in both groups were statistically significant, with lower values in the control group versus the study group. Spherical aberration and coma were lower in the control group than in the study group, but the difference was not statistically significant. Compared with the study group, the control group had lower higher-order, spherical, coma and trefoil aberrations, indicating better visual quality. It is suggested that after surgical correction of anisometropia, although higher-order aberrations are influenced when IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D, the impacts on primary spherical aberrations and coma were not statistically significant.

Anisometropia refers to the inconsistency of the refractive state between the two eyes, which is significantly different in nature and degree. Large anisometropia between the two eyes can cause binocular visual dysfunction. Every 0.25 D of anisometropia can lead to a difference of 0.5% in the object image on the retina for the two eyes, and a difference of 5% is the maximum limit of brain fusion, that is, the anisometropia between the two eyes exceeds 2.50 D, and binocular single vision is lost.3 Binocular vision is divided into three levels from low to high: simultaneous vision, fusion vision and stereoscopic vision. Binocular stereopsis is the ability of the visual center to perceive the three-dimensional structure of an object by accepting binocular disparity and adjusting binocular movement [31]. Anisometropia can cause retinal images in one eye to defocus, including the reduction of retinal image size, clarity and contrast, and the duration of defocus can range from intermittent to permanent, i.e., the signal sent from one anisometropic eye to the brain for processing is not coordinated with the other anisometropic eye [32]. The present study detected no statistically significant differences in simultaneous vision and fusion vision between the two groups. There were statistically significant differences in the distributions of foveal stereoacuity, macular stereoacuity and peripheral stereoacuity between the two groups (P < 0.05). The control group showed better distribution than the study group. Research indicated that the difference of IOL diopter between the two eyes was 1.0 D, that is, 0.69 D anisometropia was related to stereoscopic vision impairment. The greater the anisometropia between the two eyes, the greater the impairment of stereoscopic vision [33]. Anisometropia affects stereoscopic vision mainly through two mechanisms: blurred retinal imaging, leading to difficulty in fusion, and binocular disparity, leading to difficulty in fusion [34]. Therefore, the greater the degree of anisometropia, the more pronounced the difficulty for the two eyes to fuse images. Previous findings showed that stereopsis with more than 2.0 D of anisometropia is substantially impaired [35]. Studies have shown that when the eye position in anisometropia patients is orthophoric, there is no significant effect on their static binocular simultaneous vision [36]. Liu Manjun [37] reported anisometropia is more commonly associated with stereoscopic vision impairment, with no significant correlation to simultaneous vision and fusion vision functions. A previous study [38] suggested that in case of anisometropia, changes in contrast sensitivity and spatial frequency have a certain effect on stereoscopic vision. Although we corrected the anisometropia during surgery, differences remained in stereoscopic vision. It may be that stereoscopic vision is a more advanced visual function, and the nerve center has not adapted to the new situation.

For VF-11R, both groups had higher total scores, with no statistically significant difference (P >0.05). Both the control and study groups demonstrated good patient satisfaction outcomes. Scores for “reading small print” were better in the control group than in the study group, but the effect in the study group was minimal and did not significantly alter the patient’s life. This has similar results to Fernandez’s results, where more than current% of subjects experienced no difficulty in all tasks except reading print reading [39]. In the satisfaction questionnaire, total scores were higher in the control group than in the study group (P < 0.05). By further analysis, satisfaction for far vision was slightly higher in the control group (100%) compared with the study group (97%), and satisfaction rates for intermediate and near vision were both 100%. The results suggested that IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D interferes with far vision, but there was no difference in VF-11R scores between the two groups. Previous evidence shows that compared with monofocal IOLs, multifocal or trifocal IOLs are more likely to cause postoperative visual disturbances, as the stepped diffraction ring design makes glare a natural occurrence [40]. It may also be caused by the shift of the optical center of multifocal IOLs from the center of the pupil [41, 42] especially under low light conditions and when the pupil diameter is more than 5 mm, which may induce discomforts such as glare, halo, bifocal and other uncomfortable phenomena. The satisfaction questionnaire showed that the incidence rates of glare and halos were higher in the study group than in the control group (P < 0.05), and the interference was more frequent at night. The results showed that IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D may cause subjective visual quality deviations; some patients experienced glare postoperatively, which may be related to the IOL itself [43].At present, no direct studies have shown that IOL diopter value difference is directly related to light interference, but we speculate that IOL diopter value difference leads to differences in optical quality between the two eyes, which makes brain fusion difficult and visual cortex processing disordered, eventually leading to more sensitive to light interference, and may form glare and halo. More studies are needed to confirm our hypothesis. Before the surgery, we fully informed patients about potential postoperative optical disturbances, which did not impact the postoperative life of patients per self-reporting. Restoring the patient’s vision and enabling them to be glasses-free after surgery is a central goal of modern cataract surgery. Therefore, spectacle independence rate is also one of the most important indicators for evaluating visual quality after multifocal cataract surgery. In this study, all patients did not wear glasses, indicating that although satisfaction with far vision was slightly lower in the study group than in the control group, the impairment did not reach the level of wearing glasses and had less impact on life. The results are more optimistic than those reported by Mendicute [44] and Kohnen et al. [45].Overall, the quality of life after surgery was good in both groups, and postoperative glare and halos did not significantly interfere with daily life.

To further explore the reasons for the observed increased glare and halos at night, we analyzed the correlation between light interference and the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision and lens diopter value difference in both groups. The results revealed no significant difference in the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision between the two groups, indicating that the higher frequencies of glare and halos in the study group versus the control group were not caused by the pupil centroid shift from photopic to scotopic vision. For a long time, the problem of glare after trifocal IOL implantation has not been solved, because the design of the stepped diffraction ring increases the incidence of glare [46], and anisometropia could lead to binocular fusion difficulties and not recognize the image size difference when the image from the amblyopic eye is of poor quality or too strongly suppressed [47]. In correlation analysis, IOL diopter value difference was associated with the occurrence of night glare, and the greater the difference in binocular lens diopter, the higher the frequency of night halos. The reason may be that under low light conditions, because of the poor binocular fusion function, it is difficult for patients with IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D to effectively coordinate their eyes to reduce the interference of glare, resulting in more discomfort caused by glare. However, no associations of white glare, white halos and night halos with binocular lens diopter value difference were found. These visual disturbances occurred in the early postoperative period. Due to the neurological mechanism, optical phenomena such as glare and halos diminish over time, and the incidence rates of glare and halos gradually decrease. Prolonging the follow-up time is of great significance for the findings of this study.

Our study has some limitations. First, our sample size was small and single center, and the results may not be generalizable. Second, this was a nonrandomized study; both the patient and the surgeon were aware of the type of lens used, which could be biased. Third, due to the limited patient follow-up time and equipment, we have not yet detected the defocus curve and contrast sensitivity, which makes our indicators imperfect and limits the breadth and depth of our study. Fourth, due to the lack of relevant data, this article did not compare the importance of the dominant eye/non-dominant eye or the less ametropic eye versus the more ametropic one for understanding binocular cooperative work. This study may not have revealed some subtle effects related to binocular visual function balance. In subsequent research, we will continue to explore the relationship between anisometropia and postoperative visual quality between dominant/non-dominant eyes or weakly refractive eyes and strongly refractive eyes. Finally, further psychometric validation, including Rasch analysis to examine item-level performance and potential differential item functioning, would strengthen the questionnaire’s utility in future studies. Despite these limitations, our study provides important data for the comparison of subjective and objective visual quality after implantation of different diopter trifocal IOLs in both eyes, which may provide a reference for the exploration of treatment methods for patients with anisometropia in the future. In further research, we still need to conduct multi-center large-scale studies, extend the follow-up period, and increase the observation indicators to obtain more effective data.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study found that patients with IOL diopter value difference < 1.0 D had better near vision, total higher-order aberrations, trefoil aberrations, stereoscopic vision and postoperative light interferences than those with IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D. However, far and intermediate vision, MTF, SR, spherical aberration, coma, simultaneous vision, fusion vision, VF-11R and spectacle independence were similar in patients with IOL diopter value difference ≥ 1.0 D and those with IOL diopter value difference < 1.0 D. The difference in binocular lens diopter values was directly proportional to the incidence of night glare. Previous findings36 have suggested that anisometropia can lead to central inhibition of scotomas, with decrement in binocular performance with anisometropia increase, which may occur because of localized inhibition in the more defocused eye. Anisometropia may be corrected by surgery, but the suppression may not be relieved for a short time. Due to the limited number of patients in this study, no further grouping was performed according to the difference in binocular IOL diopter corresponding to anisometropia. The long-term visual effects after implantation of different diopter of intraocular lens need more in-depth study.

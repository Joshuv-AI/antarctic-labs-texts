A hybrid Granger TCN framework for generating climate analogues and determining the future of agricultural practices
Authors: Suman Saurabh Sarkar, Sushma Jain, Jatin Bedi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The accurate forecasting of weather parameter facilitates effective analysis of crop yield, and ensures the sustainable allocation of crop usage in domestic purpose. Also, the climate change and human activities have increased the complexity of weather systems, making accurate prediction of weather parameter more challenging, thus impacting yield. Therefore, this work proposes a hybrid approach that includes deep learning model Temporal Convolution Network (TCN) with Grangers Causality Test. The approach involves capturing a single historic weather parameter i.e. the maximum temperature for four Indian Cities: Coimbatore, Gwalior, Ludhiana and Kashmir. The study focuses on finding out the specific future years while which the maximum temperature of Kashmir (output region), will match the current or history of maximum temperature of aforementioned other cities (input regions) from south to north. The result has shown a significant mapping till present years and given the possibility of match of maximum temperature in future years. The standard deviation of RMSE across 75 years of mapping is 0.0271 with variance of 0.0007 for 1st rank match. The study will help in maize crop planning and will be highly useful for automation purpose that requires good time for infrastructure setup like a well advance planning on making availability of irrigation sources.

Introduction

With the continuing rise in the human population worldwide, a corresponding increase is there in the cereal demand and, subsequently its production. This increase is observed globally on the existing croplands through agricultural advancement, resulting in yield of variety of crops. In future, this demand is going to increase by another 60–110% which requires to keep pace with food requirements1. In the absence of major advancements in agricultural technology beyond what is currently available, it is found that large areas of current cropland are anticipated to experience strong reductions in attainable yields of major cereal crops in coming future, highlighting the ongoing susceptibility to climate change. Various global-scale crop-climate models are proposed by the research groups to assess crop-climate relationships. This implies that the climate change is becoming major contributor of increasing economic crisis and, a key factor for shortage of product supply-demand across the globe. Thus, there is a requirement of understanding the effect of projection of climate change on the agriculture industry. The key factor behind this crisis is shortage of product against the demand. Food being the basic requirement of livelihood needs attention; climate change is altering the suitability of croplands that incurs shifting of the spatial distribution and diversity of focused crop production. Climate change is going to become a major challenge while making efforts to increase yield to satisfy the demand. It is important to minimize the gap between supply and demand. Hence, this study contributes in marking maximum temperature as one of the key factors in regular crop production through introducing a method that maps past and current maximum temperature of input location to future maximum temperature of target location. This will help in advance maize crop planning that includes irrigation planning, achieving good quality seeds, etc., and which can be generalized across range of products.

Related work

Climate data modelling and climate analogue-based assessment

Climate variability has a profound impact on agricultural productivity, influencing crop yield patterns across different regions. Understanding these variations has become increasingly important in the context of global climate change. Within this context, several studies have focused on modelling temperature trends and their implications for crop cycles, especially in regions with high climatic diversity such as India. One of the initial studies presents the involvement of a global high-resolution climate dataset created using downscaling and bias-correcting Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 5 (CMIP5) outputs to support local agriculture and, biodiversity impact modelling. It employs the delta method, which calculates long-term mean changes from GCM projections and applies these deltas to historical observations, effectively correcting systematic biases. This improved the shape and variability of climate distributions. However, the method has limitations in representing extreme events and year wise variability2. Few studies demonstrate the impact of increasing temperature on crop yield and quality. Among this one study represents that 1 °C temperature increase would result in a 6.1% yield loss when the temperature rise is below 2.38 °C; however, when it exceeds 2.38 °C, yield loss would rise to 8.2% per 1 °C warming. Similarly, the loss in rice yields for each °C increase in temperature would increase from 1.1 to 7.1% per °C when the temperature rise surpasses the 3.13 °C threshold. For maize, no threshold effect is found; instead, temperature increase would reduce yields by an average of 4.03% per °C3. Another study presents a nonlinear relationship between warming and yields where each degree day spent above 30 °C reduced the final yield by 1% under optimal rain-fed conditions, and by 1.7% under drought conditions4. One more study claim that yield growth increases gradually with temperature up to 29–32° Celsius, depending on the crop, and then decreases sharply above the temperature threshold5. While above studies encourage us to work upon increasing maximum temperature as it is considered as the primary climatic variable due to its dominant role in influencing crop performance under changing climate conditions and is widely recognized as a key driver of plant physiological processes, few more previous studies helped us to determine the requirement of integrating the core concept of climate analogue for temperature mapping among various regions focused in our study. In the following study statement, authors have presented a methodology for identifying climate analogue cities using high-resolution regional climate model (RCM) projections for European cities. The algorithm is applied on bias-adjusted EURO-CORDEX climate data for different scenarios involving key climate variables viz. temperature and precipitation averages and amplitudes, along with their correlations6.

The authors proposed Bayesian Framework to enhance global potential of irrigation contribution in yields of maize and wheat involving climate variation at global scale. The climate analogue (CA) approach and gridded crop model are integrated with proposed framework to assess balance between irrigation and yield. The results shows that irrigation can increase yields by 34 + 9% for wheat and 22 + 13% for maize depending on precipitation. Authors conclude that the effectiveness of irrigation for cultivation is limited under diversion of climatic changes7. In the similar direction, this study8 applied two approaches within a global agro-economic model to investigate the yield estimation methods. To assess the methods the potential yield crop is estimated using empirical method (based on historical data) and, simulated method (based on biophysical models). Simulated yields result in better outcome for developing regions under constraints of land usage and global crop prices.

The authors discuss a comprehensive examination of the trends derived from historical climate data by employing the CY-OBS dataset, which encompasses daily maximum and minimum temperatures as well as precipitation data pertinent to Cyprus, a Mediterranean island. In conjunction with this investigation, the study evaluates climate projections through multi-model assessments to discern potential risks. For this multivariate cluster analysis, k-means clustering and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) are utilized, yielding five distinct climatic zones. This study offers significant insights and consequently underscores the imperative for interventions aimed at mitigating the effects of climate change on the island’s ecosystem, particularly in light of the findings that suggest the island is experiencing increased temperatures and reduced precipitation9.

Climate changes impacts on agricultural yield and irrigation adaptation

Building upon such evidence of changing climatic patterns, other research has shifted focus toward the implications of these changes for global agriculture and food security. The research10 highlights the crucial and diverse role of irrigation in sustaining maize, wheat, and rice production globally, especially in major producing countries like China, India, and Egypt. It provides new, detailed insights into how irrigation contributes to global food production, showing that while it contributes less than 40% to maize and wheat in most top producing countries, it contributes over 40% to rice production in the majority of them. Furthermore, the study suggests that irrigation expansion holds significant potential as a strategy for climate change adaptation to sustain and increase crop yields, though its implementation requires careful consideration of various factors.

Additionally, agroforestry-based adaptation strategies have been explored through comprehensive literature reviews, demonstrating the role of tree diversity in moderating climate extremes, influencing rainfall patterns, and enhancing the sustainability of rural agricultural systems. These studies collectively highlight that while irrigation and agroforestry can mitigate climate impacts, their effectiveness is constrained by evolving climatic conditions and resource availability11. From the literature, it is noted that climate change impacts the seasonal growth of various crops, leading to decreased yields and influencing phenological events, among other effects.

Related concepts

Climate analogue is defined as a spatial or temporal correspondence in which two regions or periods exhibit statistically similar climatic characteristics, determined through similarity metrics applied to one or more climate variables over a specified time window. Climate analogue approaches are commonly used to infer potential future climatic conditions, agricultural suitability, or ecosystem responses by identifying present-day locations that resemble projected climates.

To observe the climate analogues that subsequently suggests crop yield response under changing climatic conditions, there exist several climate-data driven, statistical and, model-based approaches as presented in the literature section. Granger causality test

Granger causality12 is a measure proposed by Nobel Laurete in Economics Prof. Clive Granger in 2003, to understand whether in multiple time-series data, one time series can predict or cause another time series. Given observation of X variable, to see whether its past values help in predicting future values of Y variable, beyond Y’s own past values shown in Eq. Thus, Yt target time series can be predicted at time t using its past value and, X source time series past values upto lag p. This approach is examined by estimating autoregressive models for two-time series X and Y and comparing their predictive capabilities. Specifically, the prediction of Y is evaluated using two models: one that includes the lagged values of only Y and another that includes the lagged values of both X and Y. If the inclusion of past values of X significantly improves the prediction of Y, it suggests the presence of Granger causality, thus X Granger-cause Y. The key requirement for establishing Granger causality is temporal precedence. This means that the cause (X) must precede the effect (Y) in time. By examining the time series data, this is assessed whether X occurs before Y, providing an initial indication of potential causality.

Temporal convolutional network

The present study focuses on inclusion of temporal convolutional network, a class of deep learning architecture designed to add a temporal or sequence component on existing convolutional neural network. TCNs employ dilated causal convolutions, to ensures that only past values are used when predicting the data along with preserving temporal ordering. This is the fundamental requirement for Granger causality analysis. In contrast, other time series model such as N-BEATS are primarily designed to increase predictive accuracy through deep residual stacks. The efficiency of TCN over other time series model like LSTM and N-BEATS can be observed in Table 2. The table demonstrates output of one of our previous studies that suggests about the use of TCN when compared with other two models specified for the estimation of evapotranspiration which requires maximum temperature, minimum temperature, relative humidity, sunshine hours and windspeed13. This makes TCN model particularly suitable for time series modelling, which provide better interpretability of lagged effects, stable training and is less complex10. In the data, maximum temperature, recorded for the period of 74 Years is further used to predict future values. TCN dilation mechanism enlarges the receptive field by skipping values at fixed intervals for example dilation rate = 6, used in the study yields an effective receptive field estimated on Eq. (6). This prevents small and daily noises and focuses on long term trends. Data acquisition

The work requires historic data of a single climate parameter i.e. maximum temperature, forecasting it for suggesting future maize crop production planning. The dataset of four regions is utilized from Indian Meteorological Department (IMD), Pune. It comprises of 74 years historical data of cities Coimbatore, Gwalior, Ludhiana and Kashmir as shown in the map Fig. 2. These locations are (i) major maize crop producers (ii) are chosen on the basis of decreasing values of maximum temperature from South to North cities. The next 15 years of data is forecasted using sequential model Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), applicable in this case i.e. time series forecasting. The tenure for forecasting is decided upon the period that enables the identification of emerging climate patterns relevant to maize cultivation without excessive extrapolation beyond the reliable range of historical data. Thus, total 89 years of data is utilized for forecasting crop production.

Data preprocessing

The dataset, described above, is first analyzed to understand its characteristics, as summarized in Table 4. The preprocessing stage includes the removal of records corresponding to 29th February in leap years, missing value analysis that led to the outcome that we have no missing value in our dataset, Min–Max normalization for the range [0,1], and outlier detection using the interquartile range (IQR) method with a threshold of 1.5 × IQR i.e. [Q1–1.5*IQR, Q3 + 1.5 * IQR]. Any data point above this is considered as outlier. After this preprocessing step we have found 3 outlier values in our entire dataset which were handled before experiment phase. We also state here that any short time abnormal weather conditions that may affect the analysis is not been taken into consideration due to dependency on data source.

Conclusion

This study has proposed a novel deep learning-based hybrid approach to find the possibility of planning maize crop cultivation. Firstly, the approach performs maximum temperature trend analysis of all the regions included in study for modelling purpose. Secondly, the Granger causality test is performed to find out the maximum temperature causality between the set of each source region with destination region. The source regions considered in the study are major maize crop producers. Thirdly, the TCN network model is built for extracting the long-term dependencies. Finally, the proposed hybrid approach is applied to predict the year with similar maximum temperature trends at the target region resulting in carrying out plan for maize crop cultivation. The prediction results are evaluated using performance measure (RMSE and MAE). Thus, the present work lays following observations:

A novel hybrid mathematical approach that combines Granger causality Test with Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) is well suited for climate analogue analysis. The Granger causality ensures that only statistically significant analogue regions contribute to linear causal component thereby preserving causal validity in spatial climate signal propagation. Future scope

The study shows the hybrid Granger–TCN model offers a mathematically justified, causally interpretable, and temporally expressive solution for climate analogue–based maize crop planning under increasing climate variability. However, there is a limitation to the study as a single climate parameter that plays a vital role in evapotranspiration required proper irrigation planning is considered in this study. In future, multiple weather parameters like minimum temperature, relative humidity, sunshine hours, windspeed which are very crucial in estimation of evapotranspiration will be included. Also, it will be very helpful to get the information about quality of soil which plays major role for provisioning of nutrients to the crops.

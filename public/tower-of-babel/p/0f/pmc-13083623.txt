Monovalent antibody treatment of von Willebrand disease
Authors: James S O’Donnell
Published: 2026

In this issue of Blood Advances, Häger et al1 present data suggesting a potential new treatment approach for patients with von Willebrand disease (VWD). They report that the monovalent antibody HMB-002 can bind to the CK domain of von Willebrand factor (VWF) and cause attenuated clearance, thereby increasing endogenous plasma VWF and factor VIII (FVIII) levels. Importantly, they observed that VWF multimers and ligand interactions were not impaired by HMB-002 binding, so that the physiological hemostatic functions of VWF were preserved (see figure).

VWD results from a reduction in plasma VWF levels and constitutes the most common inherited human bleeding disorder.2,3 VWD is classified according to whether the underlying VWF deficiency is quantitative and/or qualitative in nature. Type 1 VWD accounts for ∼75% of cases and is characterized by a partial reduction in plasma VWF:antigen (Ag) concentration.2 Despite the global prevalence, and the significant morbidity that continue to be associated with bleeding in patients with VWD, advances in treatment options beyond development of a first recombinant VWF concentrate have been slow to emerge.4 This contrasts markedly with the new innovative products that have been developed and licensed for the treatment of hemophilia over the past 2 decades. To address this deficit, Häger et al investigated whether a VWF-directed monovalent antibody might be useful as a novel approach to transiently increase plasma VWF levels. HMB-002 is a human Fc–silenced monovalent immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) antibody designed to target the C-terminal CK domain of VWF (VWF-CK). Binding studies confirmed that HMB-002 bound to full-length human plasma–derived VWF with subnanomolar affinity (KD 0.55nM). Moreover, crystallographic analysis demonstrated that HMB-002 interacted with a specific 13-residue epitope in the VWF-CK homodimer.

The authors hypothesized that HMB-002 binding might promote VWF recycling through the neonatal Fc-receptor (FcRn)–recycling pathway and thus lead to a prolongation in VWF plasma half-life.1 Pharmacokinetic studies in cynomolgus monkeys demonstrated that HMB-002 was eliminated with a half-life of 6 to 8 days. A single subcutaneous dose of HMB-002 (10 mg/kg) resulted in a 1.5-fold increase in plasma VWF:Ag levels which was sustained for 21 days. Interestingly, plasma VWF:Ag levels were increased significantly more in female vs male monkeys. Further studies will be required to define the mechanisms underpinning this sex difference. Nevertheless, consistent with the concept that HMB-002 may slow VWF clearance rate, plasma FVIII:C levels were also modestly increased (∼1.3-fold) following subcutaneous HMB-002 treatment.

In keeping with the fact that HMB-002 has been designed to interact with the VWF-CK domain, in vitro studies demonstrated no inhibitory effect of the monovalent antibody on VWF binding to FVIII, GpIbα, or collagen.1 Similarly, VWF proteolysis by ADAMTS13 was also unaffected in the presence of HMB-002. To further study the hemostatic effects of HMB-002 in a murine bleeding model, Häger et al developed a monovalent murine IgG2a antibody (mE07) targeted toward an epitope in the VWF-CK domain overlapping with that of HMB-002. In a type 1 VWD murine model (plasma VWF:Ag levels ∼15% baseline) they observed that pretreatment with HMB-002 was associated with a nonsignificant trend toward improved bleeding in a tail clip assay.

The findings presented by Häger et al will be of interest to the community and add to the repertoire of emerging potential novel approaches aimed at transiently increasing plasma VWF levels in patients with VWD. In addition to the HMB-002 monovalent antibody directed toward the VWF-CK domain, we also have the BT200 PEGylated aptamer targeted to the VWF-A1 domain,5 as well as the recently described KB-V13A12 bispecific nanobody which binds to the VWF-D’D3 region and to albumin.6,7 Although HMB-002 and KB-V13A12 both increase plasma VWF levels by enhanced FcRn recycling,1,7 BT200 binding inhibits VWF-A1 domain–mediated macrophage clearance pathways.8 Given that all 3 therapies serve to increase endogenous plasma VWF levels, they will be likely to be more useful in the treatment of patients with partial quantitative VWD rather than qualitative type 2 VWD. Furthermore, although the in vitro and animal model data on HMB-002 and KB-V13A12 are encouraging, clinical trials in VWD patients will be critical to facilitate further development toward the clinic. In terms of comparing the utility of these potential treatments, key considerations will include (1) the comparative increases in VWF-FVIII levels that the different agents can deliver in human patients; (2) how sustained the increase in VWF-FVIII levels in patients with VWD remain over time following subcutaneous treatment; (3) interindividual heterogeneity in responses to the different therapies; (4) whether VWF-FVIII responses may vary for patients with different VWD subtypes including type 1C; (5) the hemostatic efficacy of relatively modest increases in plasma VWF levels by extending the in vivo half-life of this complex multimeric sialo-glycoprotein9; and (6) safety data including immunogenicity. On behalf of our patients, we shall all eagerly await these data from human trials and take solace that novel approaches to VWD treatment now represent such an exciting and innovative research area.

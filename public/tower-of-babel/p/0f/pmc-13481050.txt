Rebirth of the Meaning of Natural Childbirth: A Qualitative Study on the Perceived Impacts of Effective Midwife–Mother Communication among Iranian Midwives and Mothers
Authors: Zohreh Yeganeh, Mozhgan Javadnoori, Zahra Abbaspoor, Abbas Ebadi, Simin Montazeri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Although effective communication between midwives and mothers is linked to positive physiological and emotional outcomes, research on this topic within maternity wards remains limited, and both perspectives have not been adequately explored. A comprehensive understanding of these perceived impacts is essential to highlight the importance of communication in maternity care. This study aims to explore the perceived impacts of effective midwife–mother communication in the maternity ward.Materials and Methods:A qualitative approach was adopted to explore perceptions of effective midwife–mother communication using the conventional content analysis method. Semi-structured interviews were conducted between July and October 2023 with 11 midwives and 12 postpartum mothers in the maternity wards of Ahvaz, Iran. Participants consisted of midwives working in maternity units and mothers who had experienced vaginal births, selected through purposive sampling. Data were analyzed using a thematic analysis approach.Results:Thematic analysis revealed one central theme: the rebirth of the meaning of natural childbirth, supported by three themes: 1) reshaping mothers’ beliefs and attitudes toward childbearing, 2) meaning-making in natural childbirth, and 3) improving the quality of midwifery services.Conclusions:Effective communication between midwives and mothers can help restore values and positive emotions and redefine the meaning and experience of natural childbirth. It is recommended that comprehensive frameworks or guidelines for effective communication be developed and integrated into midwifery curricula, training programs, and clinical interventions. Future studies should focus on communication processes and barriers across different maternity care settings to identify effective practices and areas for improvement.

Introduction

Effective communication is equally as crucial as clinical care in achieving desired, person-centered outcomes and enhancing the quality of childbirth.[1] In healthcare, effective communication refers to the clear and empathetic exchange of information to ensure mutual understanding and improve patient outcomes.[2] In the context of natural childbirth, ongoing interaction and mutual understanding between the mother and the midwife are essential.[3] The midwife must remain attentive to the mother’s physical changes, while the mother should feel comfortable expressing any physical or emotional concerns.[4] Thus, effective communication forms the foundation of this reciprocal relationship. During labor and birth, women are particularly sensitive to their interactions with maternity care providers.[56] Notably, a woman’s experience is often shaped more by the behavior of her caregivers than by the intensity of her pain.[7] Research indicates that women perceive childbirth more positively when they feel supported, safe, in control, and respected.[8] Additionally, a scoping review of qualitative studies has highlighted the critical role of communication between care providers and women in emergency maternity situations, including unplanned or high-pressure scenarios.[9] Thus, effective communication is essential for ensuring both comfort and safety during labor and birth.

Ineffective communication between midwives and mothers can negatively impact maternal health.[10] Interruptions in this communication often lead to stress and uncertainty for the mother.[11] Elevated levels of fear and anxiety can significantly hinder progression of labor, potentially resulting in prolonged labor.[12] Furthermore, increased fear and anxiety during labor are associated with a higher risk of cesarean delivery due to psychological factors.[13] Psychological distress during labor may also adversely affect newborn health outcomes.[12] Rao et al. (2024)[14] identified four types of communication in intrapartum care: compassionate, factual, prescriptive, and defensive. Their study found that compassionate communication, characterized by empathy and reassurance, is typically employed during the early stages of labor to comfort and support women. In contrast, factual communication is used by healthcare providers to collect clinical information and update patients and their families, though it may be perceived as impersonal and hierarchical. Prescriptive communication is intended to obtain consent and cooperation, while defensive communication seeks to justify clinical decisions following adverse events. The authors concluded that prescriptive and defensive communication styles can be disrespectful toward women.[14] Globally, effective communication is often not prioritized in maternity care settings.[15] Numerous studies have documented poor communication and disrespectful care, particularly in developing countries.[161718] Even in developed nations, midwives and mothers frequently encounter communication challenges.[192021] Negative interactions with midwives can have long-term effects on a woman’s mental health and overall wellbeing.[22] A qualitative study conducted in Brazil highlighted how communication failures contributed to maternal near misses.[23] In that study, women described instances where health professionals dismissed their questions with abrupt responses. Moreover, healthcare providers often shifted responsibility to patients using aggressive language that undermined the women’s experiences and delayed recognition of appropriate treatment.[23]

Research conducted in Iran has consistently reported instances of disrespect and poor communication during childbirth.[2425] Disrespectful care often includes a lack of emotional support, neglect, and verbal abuse, which lead to dissatisfaction and fear among women.[2627] Consequently, ineffective communication and negative childbirth experiences may drive many Iranian women to prefer elective cesarean sections over natural childbirth.[28] Therefore, there is an urgent need to strengthen respectful maternity care and improve communication between midwives and mothers. A qualitative study within this context could reveal how interactions influence women’s attitudes and experiences regarding natural childbirth. Such insights could inform the development of targeted interventions to enhance the quality of maternity care. Although existing evidence indicates that communication affects the quality of childbirth care, challenges persist, and few studies have specifically focused on midwife–mother communication. Therefore, this study aims to explore the perceived impacts of effective midwife–mother communication in the maternity ward.

Materials and Methods

This qualitative study was conducted from July to October 2023 using conventional content analysis. This study is part of a larger mixed-methods research project aimed at designing and psychometrically evaluating a tool to assess effective midwife–mother communication in the maternity ward, which included an extensive qualitative phase. One segment of this phase seeks to clarify the components of effective communication between midwives and mothers.[29] This section examines the perceived impacts of effective communication from the perspectives of both groups. A qualitative conventional content analysis approach was employed as the understanding of the impacts of effective communication in maternity care remains fragmented. This method enabled a comprehensive description of participants’ perceptions. Data were collected from midwives and mothers at Razi and Imam hospitals, affiliated with Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences, in southwestern Iran.

Eleven midwives from labor and birth units and 12 mothers who had vaginal births participated in voluntary interviews. Participants were selected through purposive sampling, and face-to-face, in-depth interviews were conducted to collect data. Inclusion criteria for midwives were employment in a teaching hospital’s labor and birth unit, at least 1 year of professional experience, willingness to participate, and provision of informed consent. Inclusion criteria for mothers included having given birth vaginally in the same hospitals during the study period, being in a stable postpartum condition, having their newborn present during the interview to alleviate concerns, and being willing and able to provide informed consent. Exclusion criteria applied to midwives or mothers who expressed unwillingness to continue participating at any stage of the study. All interviews were conducted by the first author, a PhD candidate in midwifery, who had received training in qualitative research, interviewing, and effective communication. The researcher had no prior acquaintance with any of the participating mothers or midwives.

After obtaining written informed consent from all participants, sociodemographic data, including age, educational level, number of pregnancies for mothers, and years of employment for midwives, were collected. Semi-structured individual interviews began with open-ended questions designed to explore participants’ perceptions of effective communication, such as: “Can you describe your experiences of good communication with the midwife/mother during childbirth?” and “In your opinion, what are the impacts of good midwife–mother communication?” The interviews also included probing questions like “Can you elaborate on…?” or “What do you mean by…?” All interviews were conducted in private hospital rooms. The interview time and location were determined according to participants’ preferences and availability. Interviews with mothers were conducted in the postpartum ward following natural childbirth and lasted between 30 and 75 minutes, with an average duration of 45 minutes. With participants’ consent, all the sessions were audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim immediately after completion. Data collection continued until data saturation was reached, which occurred after the 23rd interview. The characteristics of the participating midwives and mothers are presented in Table 1.

The analysis followed Braun and Clarke’s six-phase framework for thematic analysis.[30] It began with immersion in the data to gain familiarity, followed by generating initial codes that highlighted key features related to communication. These codes were subsequently grouped into preliminary themes, which were reviewed and refined to ensure clarity and distinction. Final themes were defined and named to capture their core essence, and the findings were synthesized into a coherent report. This reflective and systematic process enabled an in-depth interpretation of participants’ experiences while acknowledging the active role of the researcher in constructing themes. All interview transcripts were imported into MAXQDA 2020 software for analysis. The first author coded the data after reviewing the transcripts multiple times before subsequent interviews, while the other authors received supervision throughout the coding process. In line with Lincoln and Guba’s[31] four criteria for rigor in qualitative research, this study applied several measures to ensure trustworthiness. Credibility was enhanced through reflection on researchers’ prior experiences and beliefs, acknowledging the importance of effective midwife–mother communication. To establish dependability, the researcher reanalyzed portions of the data and engaged in discussions with co-authors to confirm the consistency of the findings. Confirmability was supported by sharing results with selected participants for feedback, ensuring that the interpretations accurately reflected their views. Transferability was addressed by providing detailed contextual information, enabling readers to assess the applicability of findings to other settings.

Ethical considerations

The present study is part of the first author’s Ph.D. thesis, funded by the Reproductive Health Promotion Research Center at the School of Nursing and Midwifery, Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences, Iran. All ethical considerations were approved by the Ethics Committee of Ahvaz University of Medical Sciences (IR.AJUMS.REC.1402.176) on June 17, 2023. The researcher provided participants with an explanation of the study’s aims and the interview process. All participants voluntarily provided informed consent before the interviews, and they were assured that their data would remain anonymous. Audio recordings and transcripts were securely stored on password-protected devices.

Rebirth of the meaning of natural childbirth

The results underscored the transformative impact of effective communication between midwives and mothers during labor and birth. The central theme highlighted that quality interactions can transform childbirth from a routine clinical procedure into an empowering and life-affirming experience. When mothers perceive midwives as supportive partners who respect their dignity and engage them as active participants in their care, the meaning of childbirth evolves. Participants reported that effective communication between midwives and mothers reshapes beliefs and attitudes toward childbearing, facilitates meaning-making in natural childbirth, and enhances the quality of midwifery services, ultimately redefining the significance of natural childbirth.

Reshaping mother’s beliefs and attitudes toward childbearing

Participants indicated that effective communication can positively influence women’s beliefs and values regarding childbearing and normal vaginal birth. Trust in the healthcare system can be restored through experienced midwives who demonstrate transparency, engage in shared decision-making, and communicate respectfully. This approach encourages mothers to choose normal vaginal births and to consider future pregnancies. Reshaping mothers’ beliefs and attitudes toward childbearing involves rebuilding trust in maternity care services and promoting both vaginal birth and future childbearing.

Rebuilding the mother’s trust in maternity care services

This subtheme encompasses the mother’s trust in and positive attitude toward the hospital and midwife, which contribute to the ensuring quality care for both herself and her newborn, thereby fostering a sense of security. Building this trust requires clinical competence and consistent supportive relationships that alleviate anxiety and encourage the continued use of maternity services. Participants highlighted this, stating: “Good communication shapes the mother’s perception of the hospital, the maternity ward, and the midwife. Consequently, she is likely to recommend them to friends and family, thereby enhancing the image, reputation, and trustworthiness of both the midwife and the maternity ward within the community.” (Midwife 10), or “The midwife understood me and stayed by my side; I never felt alone. She was attentive and carefully monitored my condition. This gave me peace of mind, knowing that someone was looking after me and my situation in the hospital.” (Mother 4)

Encouraging mothers to choose vaginal births

Participants observed that effective communication led to a shift in mothers’ preferences toward vaginal birth. Through their interactions with midwives, women gained a sense of safety and personal agency, even if they had previously feared vaginal birth. Satisfaction with vaginal birth, along with positive attitudes and experiences, encouraged mothers to choose this option. One participant illustrated this sentiment as follows: “I have a good memory of my last birth. It was perfect, and I felt comfortable with my midwife. Despite the pain, I returned with a strong desire to give birth vaginally again.” (Mother 2).

Another participant said: “I remember a first–time mother who was very scared and wouldn’t even allow vaginal examinations. She kept saying, “Take me for a cesarean.” I tried to establish a good connection with her, and eventually she became very calm and agreed to have a natural birth.” (Midwife 6).

Increasing future childbearing desires

Participants indicated that effective communication between midwives and mothers fosters a desire for larger families. Viewing childbirth as a positive and meaningful experience enhances women’s willingness to plan future pregnancies without fear. This encouragement of future childbearing involves two key aspects: developing positive attitudes toward childbearing and reducing the anxiety associated with it. One participant exemplified this: “Due to the good communication that had been established; a mother returned to me for all her births. During her third visit, she expressed a desire for another child. Her satisfaction with the care she received instilled a positive attitude toward having children.” (Midwife 2).

A mother shared: “After having a good birth experience thanks to the midwife’s kindness and support, I feel that I feel that I might want to have another child. I would also love to have a daughter.” (Mother 11).

Meaning-making in natural childbirth

Participants emphasized that mothers highly value physiological birth experiences shaped by midwife support and effective communication. When childbirth occurs naturally, grounded in trust and respect, it fosters a profound emotional experience. Through their supportive and emotionally attuned presence, midwives facilitate the meaning-making process of birth, transforming it into a symbol of empowerment and resilience. This process involves enhancing mothers’ self-efficacy, promoting active interaction between midwives and mothers, and creating positive childbirth experiences.

Promoting mother’s childbirth self-efficacy

Participants indicated that a mother’s confidence in her ability to give birth is crucial for a meaningful natural childbirth experience. This subtheme illustrated how midwives can enhance women’s belief in their innate birthing capabilities through verbal encouragement, physical presence, and positive feedback. Supporting mothers without pressure or judgment empowers them to affirm, “I can do this.” Thematic analysis revealed that maternal self–efficacy is fostered by reducing stress and anxiety; enhancing self-confidence, motivation, and patience; increasing the sense of control during childbirth; and facilitating the attainment of maternal roles. One participant described how effective communication reduces maternal stress and anxiety: “Providing the mother with positive and necessary information and compassion helps build a positive relationship. This supportive communication can mitigate risk factors such as fear and anxiety surrounding childbirth, thereby boosting a mother ’s confidence.” (Midwife 10).

A mother explained: “When the midwife patiently and kindly reassured me that I could manage the pain through breathing control and proper movements, I felt that my body had the ability to give birth naturally.” (Mother 5).

Another participant noted that effective communication plays a crucial role in shaping the maternal role: “Women come here to embrace motherhood, and we can support them through effective communication and positive birth experiences.” (Midwife 5).

Facilitating active interaction between the midwife and the mother

Midwives and mothers noted that effective communication enables the midwife to serve as both a guide and a companion. This empowering dialog fosters a collaborative decision-making process, characterized by the midwife–mother relationship, mutual satisfaction, and the mother’s active participation in care. Participants shared: “Effective communication enhanced the mother’s engagement in the birthing process and her receptiveness to recommendations, resulting in mutual satisfaction and interaction.” (Midwife 5), or “During labor, the midwife spoke to me kindly and respectfully and explained everything, so I asked all the questions that came to my mind.” (Mother 4).

Creating a positive childbirth experience

Participants indicated that effective communication significantly influences mothers’ perceptions of their childbirth experiences. They noted that such communication during labor provides strength and satisfaction. Even in the face of pain, women who feel respected and in control tend to view childbirth positively. One mother remarked: “The natural birth was quite painful, but the midwife was always there for me and understood my needs. She provided substantial support and spoke kindly to me. I had a positive birth experience, which became a cherished memory.” (Mother 2).

One midwife commented: “We can truly create a positive feeling in mothers through patience and emotional support. I have had many experiences in which, because of good care, the mother was very satisfied and happy after childbirth.” (Midwife 5).

Improving the quality of midwifery services

Participants indicated that effective communication enhances the quality of midwifery care. They emphasized that strong communication between midwives and mothers supports the natural physiological process of childbirth, which is a central goal of quality midwifery. Additionally, midwives who are confident in their knowledge and skills are more likely to provide high-quality care. Most participants reported positive birth outcomes when communication was effective. Key strategies for improving midwifery services included promoting midwives’ confidence, facilitating interactions with mothers, and enhancing birth outcomes.

Facilitating the physiological birth

Participants noted that experienced midwives, when trusted and empowered, can foster an environment that supports the natural progression of labor and birth with minimal intervention. This approach includes nonpharmacological pain relief, encouraging movement, and providing emotional support. The findings suggest that effective communication restores the essential connection in physiological birth, facilitating labor progression and enhancing hormonal responses. One participant stated: “Proper communication is the missing link in physiological birth care. Many challenges, including the low rate of normal births, stem from poor relationships with mothers. The absence or ineffectiveness of communication between mother and midwife contributes to these issues. By employing effective communication strategies, physiological birth can be implemented more successfully.” (Midwife 10).

A participant explained: “The midwife guided me very well and calmly. I learned which position to adopt and what exercises to do at each stage. This helped me have an easier labor.” (Mother 7).

Promoting midwife’s self-confidence

Midwives reported that mothers’ satisfaction with effective communication led to positive feedback, which in turn enhanced midwives’ confidence and improved the quality of care. Thematic analysis revealed that positive feedback, a sense of being valued by mothers, increased client demand, and greater job satisfaction all contributed to boosting midwives’ confidence. Participants expressed feelings of value and fulfilment resulting from effective communication: “When I see mothers returning multiple times for intrapartum care or expressing appreciation for the care they received, I feel happy and proud that my practice has been effective.” (Midwife 3).

Improving birth outcomes

Effective communication between midwives and mothers can improve both clinical and psychosocial outcomes for mothers and their infants. Enhancing birth outcomes involves preventing emotional dystocia, reducing unnecessary cesarean sections, minimizing intrapartum complications, and promoting newborn health. One participant noted: “A strong connection with the mother facilitates labor progression and simplifies even complicated births. Good communication serves as a complementary treatment, and empathy can aid in pain relief.” (Midwife 3). A mother expressed: “With the support that the midwife provided, both my baby and I were in good condition. I experienced a smooth labor and birth, which helped reduce my worries.” (Mother 9).

Discussion

This study explored the perceived impacts of effective midwife–mother communication in Iranian maternity wards, drawing on the perspectives of both midwives and mothers. The findings demonstrated that such communication enhances mothers’ experiences of childbirth and maternity care, thereby contributing to a redefinition of natural childbirth.

In Iran, many mothers view natural childbirth as a painful and frightening experience,[32] which contributes to a preference for cesarean sections[28] and a high national cesarean section rate.[33] However, this study demonstrates that effective midwife–mother communication can transform perceptions of natural childbirth by fostering awareness, calmness, safety, respect, and trust, thereby reducing fear and stress. In a supportive environment, mothers may develop a more positive view of natural birth, which can enhance the quality of midwifery care and promote favorable attitudes toward vaginal birth among both mothers and midwives.

Consistent with these findings, previous research suggests that respectful and empathetic maternity care can transform childbirth from a potentially traumatic experience into a positive one.[1634] Additionally, another study found that midwives’ behavior during childbirth significantly influences whether the experience is perceived as positive or negative.[35]

The findings indicate that effective communication fosters positive attitudes toward childbearing by creating a supportive environment in which women feel heard, respected, and empowered. This builds trust in midwives, reduces fear, and gives women a sense of control during childbirth, encouraging natural birth and future pregnancies. Mothers’ attitudes can also influence fertility rates.[3637] Many women experience anxiety about the challenges, pain, and unpredictability of labor, which can lead to negative perceptions of childbirth.[38]

Participants indicated that effective midwife–mother communication fosters a trusting relationship, which in turn alleviates maternal anxiety and stress.[39] Ojo et al.[40] demonstrated that enhancing the quality of communication in maternity care can increase trust in caregivers. Trust is essential for both the quality of maternity care[41] and a positive birth experience.[42] A qualitative study in South Africa found that trust is built through respect and information sharing, whereas verbal abuse or disrespect leads mothers to avoid healthcare facilities for future births.[43] Additionally, empathy, provider competence, and the care environment are critical factors influencing trust in maternity care.[44] Public confidence is a key indicator of a country’s healthcare system performance;[45] however, in Iran, public trust in the healthcare system is notably low,[46] reflecting patients’ dissatisfaction with the quality of care.[47] Therefore, effective communication in maternity care is vital for rebuilding trust in the maternal healthcare system.

The findings suggest that effective communication can reshape women’s perspectives and expectations regarding childbearing and vaginal birth. Positive attitudes toward fertility may encourage childbearing intentions,[48] while negative childbirth experiences can have the opposite effect.[36] A qualitative study found that although women initially feared childbirth, most preferred vaginal birth after receiving counseling and emotional support from healthcare providers.[49] Additionally, the presence of doulas has been shown to increase vaginal birth rates by offering continuous emotional and informational support.[50] In contrast, disrespectful care can deter mothers from having more children and negatively influence their future intentions.[43] Recently, Iranian families have opted for fewer children, contributing to a rapidly aging population.[51] Many Iranian women also express a preference against normal vaginal birth, with cesarean section rates increasing sixfold in recent years.[33] Therefore, implementing effective communication in maternity wards represents a straightforward and cost-effective strategy[15] to help achieve Iran’s maternal health objectives.

The findings underscore that effective midwife–mother communication can transform childbirth from a purely physical event into a profoundly meaningful life experience, enabling mothers to derive personal significance from natural childbirth. Such communication enhances childbirth self-efficacy by providing reassurance, emotional support, and information. Respectful and empathetic engagement fosters active interaction, contributing to a positive childbirth experience by ensuring mothers feel understood, respected, and involved in decision-making.

Consistent with previous studies, childbirth can test personal resilience, foster trust in bodily capabilities, and facilitate emotional growth.[5] This study found that effective communication influences a mother’s childbirth self-efficacy. A woman’s sense of accomplishment and control is crucial for a positive birth experience.[38] Additionally, a qualitative study highlighted that respectful communication enhances mothers’ confidence in their birthing abilities.[34] Research by Doering et al.[11] indicated that women who lacked a relationship with their midwives felt disempowered and had diminished confidence in motherhood. Such negative feelings can adversely affect maternal self-efficacy, increasing the likelihood of negative childbirth experiences.[42]

Effective communication fosters reciprocal interaction between midwives and mothers, characterized by the midwife perceiving herself as equal to the woman.[4] This reciprocity enhances mothers’ appreciation of midwives, which in turn validates the midwives and improves their performance.[4] Additionally, it boosts midwives’ confidence, a critical factor for delivering quality care.[52] The findings indicate that effective communication positively influences birth outcomes. Similarly, Sandall et al.[53] identified the trusting relationship in midwifery care as a key factor in improving birth outcomes.

A key finding of the study is that participants emphasized the importance of effective communication in maternity care for achieving a positive birth experience, which is vital for the health of the mother, child, and family.[15] Research indicates that the communication style of maternity care providers significantly influences mothers’ overall satisfaction with their birth experiences.[385455] The World Health Organization also notes that a positive birth experience, a key indicator of maternal care quality, depends heavily on the relationship between women and their care providers.[15] Additionally, studies identify respect, safety, and a sense of control as essential components of a positive childbirth experience.[8] Supportive interactions foster these elements, contributing to a favorable birth experience.[8]

Last theme revealed that effective communication in maternity care enhances the quality of midwifery by fostering a strong midwife–mother relationship. This relationship promotes maternal safety, calmness, and confidence during labor, thereby improving birth experiences. Such positive interactions provide emotional support to mothers and strengthen midwives’ professional confidence, leading to higher-quality care. Empathetic communication helps reduce the emotional and physical burdens of childbirth, potentially resulting in smoother births with fewer complications and greater maternal satisfaction. Globally, millions of women experience physical and psychological complications each year due to inadequate quality care.[56] Research indicates that effective communication enhances the quality of midwifery care, a finding supported by similar evidence.[5758]

A notable finding was that effective communication may support physiological birth. Zondag et al.[59] similarly found that midwives with stronger communication skills tend to use fewer unnecessary interventions. Physiological childbirth has been associated with increased feelings of control, empowerment, and positive birth experiences.[15] However, in Iran, vaginal birth rates are below average,[33] and most are not truly physiological due to excessive medical intervention.[60] National efforts to promote physiological birth have failed to significantly reduce cesarean rates.[28] Empathetic and supportive midwifery care may enhance the release of hormones critical to normal labor progression.[5]

Consistent with the present study’s findings, research has shown that midwife-led care models can improve clinical outcomes.[5361] This study found that high-quality communication between midwives and mothers is associated with better birth outcomes. A recent study further supports this, demonstrating that effective communication positively influences health outcomes by reducing pregnancy-related stress, improving adherence to medical advice, and providing guidance on postpartum recovery and infant care.[10] One limitation of this study is that the findings reflect participants’ perspectives within the Iranian cultural and healthcare context. As with most qualitative research, the results may not be generalizable to other populations or settings. Further studies are needed to explore the long-term effects of midwife–mother communication on maternal mental health, mother–newborn bonding, and future reproductive choices.

Conclusion

This study suggests that effective midwife–mother communication can transform childbirth from a clinical procedure into a meaningful and positive experience. Communication is a key component of respectful, safe, and satisfactory care, closely linked to the physiological, emotional, and social dimensions of childbirth. Therefore, it is recommended that midwifery education integrate communication training, with curricula incorporating structured modules on therapeutic interaction. Additionally, maternity care systems should develop standardized communication protocols. Future research should explore communication processes and barriers across maternity settings to identify effective strategies and areas for improvement.

Proinflammatory bone marrow niches and neutrophil activation are associated with TIGIT expression in multiple myeloma
Authors: Valeria Tomarchio, Francesca Arciprete, Monica Di Cecca, Giulia Maricchiolo, Viola Velardi, Mariantonietta Tafuri, Antonella Bianchi, Vittoria Morano, Barbara Mecorio, Luigi Rigacci, Anna Crescenzi, Maria Zingariello, Ombretta Annibali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMultiple Myeloma (MM) is the second most frequent hematologic malignancy in adults in the Western world. The bone marrow microenvironment plays a critical role in MM progression by supporting malignant plasma cell (PC) survival, immune evasion, and proliferation. Among immune checkpoints, TIGIT has recently emerged as a relevant immunoregulatory molecule, capable of dampening cytotoxic responses and enhancing immune suppression. We investigated the expression of TIGIT in the bone marrow of newly diagnosed MM patients and its correlation with disease characteristics and microenvironmental features. We enrolled 25 consecutive patients with newly diagnosed MM. Bone marrow samples were collected for cytomorphology, cytogenetics (FISH), and immunohistochemistry using the Cytomatrix system. TIGIT expression was analyzed in bone marrow-infiltrating immune cells. Histological evaluation and confocal microscopy assessed immune cell localization, neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation, and proinflammatory cytokine expression. Clinical parameters, staging (ISS, R-ISS, R2-ISS), cytogenetic risk, and laboratory values were correlated with TIGIT status. TIGIT was expressed in 86% (19/22) of evaluable samples. TIGIT-positive patients had a significantly higher frequency of advanced R-ISS stages (p = 0.01), high-risk cytogenetics (100%), and elevated LDH (> 220 mU/ml, 100% TIGIT+). Among patients with PC% >60% or FLC ratio > 100, 92% were TIGIT+. Morphological differences were evident between groups: TIGIT-negative PCs were larger and altered, while TIGIT-positive PCs showed polarized nuclei and proximity to neutrophils. TIGIT-positive samples displayed increased neutrophils undergoing NETosis, as confirmed by neutrophil elastase and Ly6b co-expression (p = 0.0067). Elevated IL-6 (p = 0.0003) and IL-8 (p = 0.004) in TIGIT-positive samples suggest a proinflammatory microenvironment promoting neutrophil recruitment and NETosis. TIGIT expression in the bone marrow of MM patients is associated with more aggressive disease features and an inflammatory microenvironment enriched with NET-forming neutrophils. These findings support TIGIT as a potential biomarker of disease severity and a therapeutic target in MM.Keywords: TIGIT, MM, NET, IL-8, Ly6b

Background

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a malignant plasma cell disorder characterized by the clonal expansion of terminally differentiated B cells within the bone marrow, representing the second most common hematologic malignancy among adults in Western countries [1, 2]. The disease frequently arises from asymptomatic precursor stages—monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) and smoldering multiple myeloma (SMM)—and progresses to symptomatic MM, with possible transformation to plasma cell leukemia or extramedullary disease. Disease evolution is driven by the interplay between intrinsic oncogenic events and extrinsic influences from the surrounding bone marrow microenvironment (BMME), which collectively sustain tumor proliferation, survival, and resistance to therapy [1, 2].

The BMME is a complex and dynamic ecosystem composed of mesenchymal stromal cells, osteoblasts, osteoclasts, endothelial cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), regulatory T cells (Tregs), natural killer (NK) cells, and a network of extracellular matrix components and soluble factors, including interleukin-6 (IL-6), B-cell activating factor (BAFF), a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL), receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) [3]. and chemokines such as macrophage inflammatory protein‑1α (MIP‑1α; CCL3) [4] and monocyte chemoattractant protein‑1 (MCP‑1; CCL2). This environment not only provides structural and metabolic support to malignant plasma cells but also fosters a tumor-promoting and immunosuppressive milieu, enabling immune evasion, drug resistance, and dissemination [3–5]. Notably, recent single-cell and multi-omics profiling studies have demonstrated that immune microenvironment remodeling occurs at the earliest disease stages, even before clinical progression, indicating that the BMME is actively involved in disease initiation as well as maintenance [6].

The massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, acts as a potent chemoattractant for neutrophils [7, 8]. Beyond their classical roles in phagocytosis and degranulation, neutrophils possess the capacity to generate neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs)—web-like structures of decondensed chromatin decorated with antimicrobial enzymes such as myeloperoxidase, neutrophil elastase, and other proteases—which provide an extracellular mechanism for pathogen containment and elimination [9, 10].

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) have recently emerged as relevant players in the pathobiology of multiple myeloma, with transcriptomic analyses revealing NET-associated gene signatures that correlate with adverse prognosis and distinct tumor microenvironment features [11, 12]. Beyond their role in innate immunity, NETs may contribute to disease progression in hematological malignancies, including multiple myeloma, and are increasingly recognized as potential prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets [13].

TIGIT as an Emerging Immune Checkpoint in MM.

Within this immunosuppressive network, the T-cell immunoreceptor with immunoglobulin and ITIM domains (TIGIT) has emerged as a pivotal immune checkpoint in MM. TIGIT is expressed on NK cells and activated CD8⁺ T cells [14] and interacts primarily with the poliovirus receptor (PVR/CD155), which is highly expressed on bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cells and macrophages [14, 15]. The TIGIT–PVR/CD155 interaction promotes NK-cell and T-cell exhaustion, reduces cytotoxic function [16], impairs dendritic cell antigen presentation, and enhances Treg-mediated suppression [17], thereby reinforcing an immune-permissive environment for myeloma progression. Preclinical and translational studies have shown that TIGIT blockade—particularly in combination with immunomodulatory drugs such as lenalidomide—can restore antitumor immunity in MM [18]. Furthermore, recent evidence indicates that PVR expression on bone marrow macrophages is associated with resistance to TIGIT inhibition, revealing the complexity of checkpoint-mediated immune escape mechanisms in this disease [19].

Aim of the study

The present study aimed to investigate the role of TIGIT within the bone marrow microenvironment of newly diagnosed multiple myeloma patients. Specifically, we sought to determine the prevalence and distribution of TIGIT expression in bone marrow immune cell subsets, to assess its spatial relationship with malignant plasma cells and neutrophils, and to explore its potential association with NETosis and the proinflammatory chemokine profile, with particular attention to IL-8–mediated pathways. By integrating immunohistochemical, cytomorphological, and confocal microscopy analyses, we intended to elucidate whether TIGIT expression correlates with microenvironmental features indicative of immune suppression and disease aggressiveness, thereby providing a biological rationale for targeting this checkpoint in multiple myeloma.

Methods and patients

We enrolled 25 patients diagnosed with multiple myeloma, aged > 18 years at the Hematology and Stem Cell Transplantation Unit of the Fondazione Policlinico Universitario Campus Bio-Medico in Rome.

To participate in the study, patients had to receive a new diagnosis of multiple myeloma without prior treatment, while those with active infections or other types of neoplasms or immune disease were excluded. The research was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki criteria and Good Clinical Practice and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fondazione Policlinico Universitario Campus Bio-Medico (Code TIGIT-MM001 2023-039). All patients provided written informed consent to participate in the study. Disease assessment and diagnostic procedures were performed according to the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria [20] and EHA–ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines [21]; The diagnostic work-up included complete blood count, renal and hepatic function tests, serum albumin and total protein levels, serum protein electrophoresis, 24-hour urine protein quantification, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), calcium levels, serum free light chain κ and λ quantification with FLC ratio, and immunoglobulin quantification. All patients provided written informed consent. Bone disease was evaluated in all patients using low-dose total body CT.The evaluation of bone marrow was performed through bone marrow aspiration for cytomorphological examination, as well as for plasma cell count. During the procedure, an additional 4 ml sample was collected in sodium citrate for cytogenetic analysis via FISH, and a blood sample from the marrow, approximately 3–5 ml, was collected in Cytorich Red alcohol solution (Epredia, Italy). The use of this solution is essential for preparing the polymeric matrix (Cytomatrix) and for subsequent morphological analysis of the sample using Vivascope (Instant Digital Pathology). The same sample was then subjected to the latest immunohistochemical techniques to determine the presence of markers such as CD138, CD20, CD3, CD4, CD8, κ and λ chains, and, of course, TIGIT. In addition to the bone marrow aspiration, the patients underwent a bone biopsy for histological evaluation of the marrow.

Regarding the definition of high-risk patients from a cytogenetic standpoint, those identified as high-risk were classified according to the 2016 IMWG consensus criteria [22], which define as high-risk the presence of t(14;16), t(4;14), del(17p), and gain(1q21) detected by FISH. Finally, the diagnosis of symptomatic or smoldering multiple myeloma was made according to the 2014 IMWG diagnostic criteria [20, 23], which incorporate both the traditional CRAB features and the additional SLiM biomarkers (≥ 60% clonal bone marrow plasma cells, involved/uninvolved serum free light chain ratio ≥ 100, or more than one focal lesion on MRI).

Histological and immunohistochemical analyses

Cytomatrix and Vivascope

Pathological diagnosis was based on bone marrow cytology. Fresh aspirates were immediately evaluated using ex-vivo Fluorescence Confocal Laser Microscopy (FCM; Vivascope 2500 M-G4, VivaScope GmbH, Munich, Germany), which provides rapid digital imaging without slide preparation or tissue damage. For imaging, samples were loaded onto a Cytomatrix polymeric scaffold (UCS Diagnostics, Italy), stained with acridine orange, washed, and analyzed with the Vivascope, allowing image acquisition within 5 min. Cytomatrix carriers were subsequently fixed in formalin for cellblock and FFPE processing [24]. Histology and immunohistochemistry (IHC): FFPE Sect. (3 μm) were stained with hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) and subjected to IHC with antibodies against TIGIT (TG1, Dianova), CD155 (Invitrogen), IL-8 (Abcam), and neutrophil elastase (Invitrogen). Reactions were developed using DAB (Innovex Bioscience) and counterstained with hematoxylin. Negative and positive controls were included. Two experienced hematopathologists independently assessed immunoreactivity, considering TIGIT positivity when ≥ 5 lymphocytes showed clear membranous staining. Image acquisition and analysis: Images were obtained with an Olympus BX51 optical microscope and the NanoZoomer 2.0-RS scanner (Hamamatsu Photonics, Japan). IL-8⁺ plasma cells and neutrophils were quantified in five randomly selected ×40 fields per case, and mean values were calculated. Bone marrow smears were stained with May–Grünwald Giemsa for morphological assessment.

Confocal microscopy

Paraffin-embedded tissues sections were rehydrated and treated with Tris EDTA (pH = 9) for 30 min at 98 °C for antigen retrieval. Sections were incubated with appropriate primary anti-Ly6G (Ab25377, rat monoclonal, Abcam) and anti–neutrophil elastase (PA5-115648, rabbit polyclonal, Invitrogen) over night at + 4 °C. For each case, secondary Alexa Fluor 488 and/or Alexa Fluor 568-conjugated donkey anti-rabbit and anti-rat and (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA) were used. Nuclear counterstaining was performed using Hoechst 33,342, trihydrochloride, trihydrate (Invitrogen), and the samples were mounted with Fluor-shield histology mounting medium (Catalog F6182-10MG, Sigma-Aldrich).Fluorescent images were collected with a Nikon A1 confocal laser microscope (Nikon), acquired with Imaging Software NIS-Elements (Nikon), and processed with Fiji software (US National Institutes of Health). Ly6 and Neutrophil Elastase double positive cells were quantified by examination of images acquired at X60 magnification from at least 10 randomly selected areas per section. The total area analysed for each sample was 0.448 mm2.

Statistical evaluations

Data were analysed and plotted using GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software (GraphPad Software) and presented as mean (± SD). Comparisons between the two groups were performed with 1-way (analysis of variance) ANOVA. Differences among groups were considered statistically significant with a p < 0.05.

Results

We enrolled 25 patients diagnosed with multiple myeloma between November 2022 and April 2024. Of these, 22 (88%) presented with symptomatic disease and 3 (12%) with smoldering myeloma. The median age at diagnosis was 63 years (range 40–76). The monoclonal component (MC) was most frequently of the IgG class (n = 17, 70%; 12 IgG/κ, 5 IgG/λ), followed by IgA (n = 7, 25%; 5 IgA/κ, 2 IgA/λ), with one patient (4%) exhibiting light-chain–only myeloma of the κ type. Overall, κ light chain restriction was observed in 19 patients (76%) and λ in 6 (24%). The median MC concentration was 1.74 g/dl (range 0.3–7.2). Six patients (24%) also had ≥ 60% clonal plasma cells in the bone marrow aspirate. Bone disease, assessed by low-dose CT, was detected in 15 patients (60%), the majority of whom (n = 14, 93%) had three or more lesions. Extramedullary disease was rare, occurring in only one patient (4%) with paravertebral posterior mediastinal involvement.

Histopathological examination of bone marrow biopsies revealed predominantly interstitial infiltration by malignant plasma cells, interspersed with adipocytes and residual hematopoietic cells, without complete architectural effacement in any sample (Fig. 1). In none of the BOMs was there a pattern of infiltration in which the normal bone marrow architecture was completely destroyed.

TIGIT expression in bone marrow T cells

In the 22 evaluable bone marrow samples, immunohistochemistry demonstrated the presence of CD8⁺ T lymphocytes expressing TIGIT in 19 patients (86%), whereas 3 patients (14%) were negative (Tab. 2). Clinical correlations revealed that TIGIT expression was enriched in cases with aggressive disease features, including plasma cell infiltration ≥ 60%, free light chain (FLC) ratio > 100, high-risk cytogenetics, and elevated LDH levels (> 220 mU/ml). Specifically, 10 of 11 patients (91%) with either plasma cell infiltration ≥ 60% or FLC ratio > 100 were TIGIT-positive. All patients harboring high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities [t(4;14), t(14;16), del(17p), gain(1q)] also showed TIGIT expression. Similarly, all 11 patients with elevated LDH were TIGIT-positive, compared with 8 of 11 (72%) with normal LDH values.

TIGIT expression

When patients were stratified by ISS and R-ISS staging, TIGIT negativity was confined to early-stage disease. In fact, all ISS stage II–III and R-ISS stage II–III patients were positive for TIGIT. In a simplified analysis (stage I vs. stage II–III), 15 of 16 patients with advanced R-ISS were TIGIT-positive compared with 4 of 6 stage I patients (p = 0.01).

TIGIT expression and plasma cell morphology

Morphological analysis of bone marrow cytomatrix samples revealed distinct patterns between TIGIT-positive and TIGIT-negative patients. TIGIT-negative marrow displayed plasma cells with marked cytoplasmic vacuolization and pleomorphism, accompanied by increased T-cell and macrophage infiltration without notable morphological alterations (Fig. 2A). Conversely, plasma cells from TIGIT-positive patients appeared smaller, with polarized nuclei and minimal cytoplasmic changes (Fig. 2A).

The TIGIT bone marrow shows a greater number of neutrophils in NETtosis

Neutrophil recruitment and NETosis in TIGIT-Positive marrow

A key finding was the increased number of neutrophils in TIGIT-positive samples compared to TIGIT-negative ones (p = 0.004), with neutrophils frequently located in close spatial proximity to malignant plasma cells (Fig. 2B). In bone marrow biopsies, neutrophils from TIGIT-positive cases were predominantly undergoing NETosis, as evidenced by extracellular DNA structures decorated with neutrophil elastase (Fig. 2C–D). While Ly6B⁺ neutrophils were observed in both groups, double positivity for Ly6B and neutrophil elastase—a hallmark of NET formation—was significantly enriched in TIGIT-positive samples (p = 0.007) (Fig. 2E).

Cytokine milieu and induction of NETosis

Given that NETosis is typically sustained by a proinflammatory cytokine environment, we evaluated cytokine bioavailability in the two groups. Bone marrow from TIGIT-positive patients displayed significantly elevated levels of IL-6 (p = 0.0003) and IL-8 (p = 0.004) compared with TIGIT-negative cases. Notably, IL-8⁺ plasma cells were frequently observed in TIGIT-positive samples, suggesting that plasma-cell–derived IL-8 promotes neutrophil recruitment, plasma cell–neutrophil interactions, and subsequent NET extrusion (Fig. 2F).

DISCUSSION

This study highlights the immunopathological relevance of TIGIT expression in the BM microenvironment of newly diagnosed MM. We observed that TIGIT⁺ CD8⁺ T cells were associated with high tumor burden, adverse cytogenetics, and elevated LDH, underscoring its link with aggressive disease. Prior reports identified TIGIT as a co-inhibitory receptor mediating immune evasion in several malignancies [25, 26], and our results confirm its role within the MM BM niche, where malignant PCs and immune dysfunction drive disease progression [27, 28].

A novel finding is the spatial association of TIGIT positivity with IL-8–expressing PCs and neutrophils undergoing NETosis. IL-8 is known to promote neutrophil recruitment and has been correlated with poor prognosis in cancer [29–32]. The presence of neutrophil extracellular traps, evidenced by elastase-positive DNA structures and Ly6G/NE double positivity, further highlights the contribution of innate immunity to sustaining an inflammatory niche conducive to disease progression. In our study, IL-8⁺ plasma cells were enriched in TIGIT⁺ BM samples and coincided with NET formation, suggesting a mechanism of immune escape whereby plasma cells indirectly suppress CD8⁺ T- and NK-cell cytotoxicity through neutrophil activation [33]. To our knowledge, this is the first report linking TIGIT expression and NETosis in MM, further supported by the presence of elastase-positive extracellular DNA traps [29, 30].

Interestingly, TIGIT-negative cases displayed atypical plasma cell morphology and greater immune infiltration, yet lacked NET-driven inflammation, suggesting heterogeneous immune microenvironments within MM subtypes [13]. Such heterogeneity is particularly relevant in smoldering myeloma, where distinct immunological profiles have been linked to different risks of progression [34, 35]. In our cohort, TIGIT expression was observed in all three SMM patients. Despite the well-recognized challenges in defining high-risk SMM due to microenvironmental heterogeneity [36], these patients also carried high-risk cytogenetic features and all required therapy within six months from diagnosis due to the development of CRAB or SLiM-CRAB criteria. These preliminary findings could suggest that TIGIT expression deserves further investigation as a potential early risk factor in smoldering myeloma.

From a translational perspective, our findings indicate that TIGIT could represent both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target in MM. Its association with high-risk features and NET-driven inflammation supports testing TIGIT blockade, an approach already under investigation in other malignancies [37–39]. In contrast, PD-1 axis inhibition in MM has been disappointing: nivolumab monotherapy was ineffective, and pembrolizumab combinations were limited by unacceptable toxicity [40]. These outcomes highlight the need for novel checkpoint strategies. Preclinical studies have shown that TIGIT blockade can restore CD8⁺ T-cell function and synergize with immunomodulatory drugs [18, 41], while early clinical trials with TIGIT and LAG-3 inhibitors, including the MyCheckpoint trial, reported encouraging activity with a favorable safety profile in heavily pretreated patients [42]. Although preliminary, the consistently low toxicity observed supports their evaluation in earlier disease stages. Interestingly, all smoldering myeloma patients in our cohort were TIGIT+, suggesting that SMM could represent a promising setting for future trials of checkpoint inhibition, before irreversible immune dysfunction occurs [43]. This perspective may open new avenues for integrating immune checkpoint modulation into risk-adapted treatment strategies in MM. Finally, the Vivascope-based cytomatrix approach further demonstrated its value for detailed immunopathological analysis from minimal BM samples, highlighting its potential for diagnostic and translational applications.

Limitations and future perspectives

This study has several limitations. The sample size was relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Moreover, the cross-sectional design prevented us from assessing the prognostic value of TIGIT expression or its predictive role in treatment response and survival. Functional studies are also needed to confirm the mechanistic link between TIGIT signaling, IL-8 expression, and NET formation.

Future investigations should aim to validate these results in larger, multi-center cohorts and explore the dynamics of TIGIT expression and NETosis during treatment and relapse. From a clinical standpoint, integrating TIGIT assessment into baseline evaluation may help identify patients with a proinflammatory, NETosis-enriched BM niche who could benefit from checkpoint inhibition strategies. Furthermore, combining TIGIT blockade with approaches that modulate neutrophil recruitment or IL-8 signaling could represent a promising avenue to overcome immune resistance in MM.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study identifies a previously unrecognized association between TIGIT expression, neutrophil activation, and the proinflammatory architecture of the MM BM microenvironment. Although derived from a small cohort, these findings could indicate that TIGIT might serve as both a marker of high-risk disease and a candidate therapeutic target, paving the way for future strategies aimed at integrating immune checkpoint modulation into the treatment of multiple myeloma.

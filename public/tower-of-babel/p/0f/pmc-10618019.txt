Grip strength as a predictor for home discharge in convalescent rehabilitation patients
Authors: Minami Sato, Masahiro Ishizaka, Yoshiaki Endo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Abstract [Purpose] We examined the impact of the assessment items for sarcopenia on discharge destination in convalescent rehabilitation patients. [Participants and Methods] In this study, 174 patients (males; 77, mean age; 72.4 years, females; 97, mean age; 80.1 years) who were admitted and discharged from the convalescent rehabilitation wards of the International University of Health and Welfare, Shioya Hospital, were included. The participants’ height, weight, grip strength, and lower calf circumference were measured. Additionally, the number of motor functional independence measure (mFIM) at discharge, length of stay in the wards, and average number of rehabilitation sessions per day were calculated as the convalescent rehabilitation wards index. [Results] Age, weight, grip strength, lower calf circumference, mFIM at discharge, length of stay in the wards, and average number of rehabilitation sessions per day had significant differences between the return-to-home and non-home groups. Binomial logistic regression analysis revealed significant differences in grip strength and mFIM at discharge. [Conclusion] This study adjusted the sarcopenia criteria for sex and age and showed that grip strength significantly affected the outcome destination. Grip strength was shown to be a predictor of home discharge in convalescent rehabilitation patients.Keywords: Convalescent rehabilitation wards, Grip strength, Home discharge

INTRODUCTION

Japan has an aging population that is unparalleled worldwide, and aging is associated with the decline of physical and mental function in various ways. Sarcopenia is characterized by reduction in muscle mass and deterioration of muscle strength and physical functions1, 2). The decline in the level of independence of elder adults owing to mental and physical aging has become an issue. Previous studies have reported that approximately 70% of the elder adults in Japan gradually lose their level of independence after the age of 753). Disuse syndrome includes sarcopenia, locomotive syndrome, and dementia, all of which may lead to a decline in independence. The negative spiral of declining life function progresses through several stages over time. Sarcopenia increases the likelihood of adverse outcomes, such as hospitalization due to falls, ultimately functional decline, and death due to institutionalization. Undernutrition-induced sarcopenia is an important point of consideration maintaining independent living in old age. Previous studies have shown that decreased grip strength is associated with various outcomes, such as death, falls, fractures, and decreased activities of daily living (ADL), and sarcopenia should be evaluated not only for skeletal muscle mass loss but also for muscle strength4, 5).

The recommendation in Asian countries is a sarcopenia diagnosis based on the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) 2019. This criterion determines sarcopenia by three indices: muscle strength, function, and volume. It also defined severe sarcopenia and recommends grip strength and lower calf circumference6). Studies have been conducted to compare changes in the effectiveness of screening tools in community-dwelling elder adults with the updated sarcopenia criteria from the AWGS 2014 to 2019; however, none have been demonstrated in convalescent rehabilitation patients7). The prevalence of sarcopenia in convalescent rehabilitation patients is approximately 50%, and most severe ill patients are malnourished8). In convalescent rehabilitation wards, which help in transition towards independent living, continuous evaluation of the implementation of rehabilitation serves as a necessary indicator. Therefore, this study examined the impact of the assessment items for sarcopenia on discharge destination in convalescent rehabilitation patients.

PARTICIPANTS AND METHODS

A total of 174 patients (77 males, mean age 72.4 years; 97 females, mean age 80.1 years) who were admitted to and discharged from the International University of Health and Welfare, Shioya Hospital for convalescent rehabilitation between August 2021 and December 2022 were included in this study. The patients were mainly affiliated with neurosurgery, neurology, and orthopedics. The maximum length of stay from ward admission varied according to the diagnoses but was 6 months for neurosurgery and neurology and 3 months for orthopedics.

In accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, this study was conducted after explaining the study to the participants or their families. This study was conducted with the approval of the Ethics Review Committee of the International University of Health and Welfare (Approval No. 21-Io-13).

The participants’ height, weight, grip strength, and lower calf circumference were measured as indicators of the AWGS 2019 criteria. Height was measured at the time of admission using a height scale for those who could hold a standing position and using a measuring tape for those who could not hold a standing position. Grip strength was measured using a Smedley-type grip strength meter. Grip strength was measured in the left and right upper limbs, and the higher measured value was used as the definitive grip strength value. Lower calf circumference was measured using a measuring tape in the sitting position. All data were measured within two weeks of the discharge date. Individuals with a maximum grip strength of less than 5 kg were excluded. For statistical analysis, the patients were classified into home and non-home groups. The home group included serviced senior housing, whereas the non-home group included admissions to geriatric healthcare facilities, short-stay facilities, and transfers to hospitals. Unpaired t-tests were used to compare the independent variables of the two groups for grip strength, lower calf circumference, motor Functional Independence Measure (mFIM) at discharge, length of stay in the ward, and average number of rehabilitation sessions per day. In addition, to predict whether the outcome destination was home from the data on the same independent variables, a binomial logistic regression analysis was performed with the outcome destination as the dependent variable and the items that were significantly different in the t-test as independent variables. Sensitivity and specificity were calculated from the factors for which associations were obtained using a binomial logistic regression analysis, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 24 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) with a significance level of 5%. Careful attention was paid to the handling of personal information and management of data on the computer.

Significant differences were found in age, weight, grip strength, lower calf circumference, mFIM at discharge, length of stay in the ward, and the average number of rehabilitation sessions per day between the home discharge group and non-home discharge group. DISCUSSION

This study examined how the AWGS 2019 criteria, including grip strength and lower calf circumference at discharge and convalescent rehabilitation wards index, are related to the discharge destination in convalescent rehabilitation patients. The results showed significant differences in grip strength, lower calf circumference, mFIM at discharge, length of stay in the ward, and average number of daily rehabilitation sessions between the groups discharged to home and those discharged to a place other than home, depending on the discharge destination. Furthermore, grip strength and mFIM at discharge were found to have a significant effect on outcome destination. However, grip strength and mFIM at discharge were moderately correlated in terms of the correlation coefficient, suggesting that muscle strength and ADL independence are measures of different aspects.

In this study, the mean values of grip strength and lower calf circumference were below the diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia according to AWGS 2019. Since this study used data obtained within 2 weeks prior to discharge, many patients had decreased muscle strength and muscle mass at discharge, regardless of their outcome destination. Furthermore, this study adjusted the sarcopenia index for gender and age and showed that grip strength significantly affected the outcome destination. The AUC was used to determine if grip strength could predict the outcome destination, and the cutoff was 15.15 kg, which is lower than the diagnostic criteria. Patients with sarcopenia can return home; however, a value as low as 15 kg of grip strength is likely to make it more difficult to return home.

In this study, the average length of hospital stay for in convalescent rehabilitation patients was 69.2 days and 85.2 days in the home and non-home discharge groups, respectively. In the 2021 survey of in convalescent rehabilitation patients, the average length of hospital stay was 69.3 days, which was significantly longer for the non-home discharge group9). This may be due to the progression of disuse muscle atrophy caused by decreased activity due to hospitalization and decreased nutritional intake due to dietary restrictions and anorexia associated with the underlying disease, surgery, and treatment at the acute-care hospital. Prolonged hospitalization may lead to sarcopenia or its reserve, making it difficult to return home. The study also found significant differences in mFIM at discharge and the average number of rehabilitation interventions per day, depending on the discharge site. As demonstrated by the association with grip strength, rehabilitation intervention as early as possible is considered important to minimize functional decline due to disuse syndrome in the acute phase of disease onset and postoperative period, which makes discharge home difficult.

This study has some limitations. First, the timing of the measurements was not constant from the onset of the patient’s disease to the postoperative period. Second, dietary intake and blood data such as D-dimer are not measured during hospitalization, and individual differences, such as improvement in nutritional status due to illness or progressive disease, and the environment in which the patients were discharged from the hospital, must be considered. However, this is the first study to show that grip strength was shown to be a predictor of home discharge in convalescent rehabilitation patients.

First-in-human study of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ, a novel VMAT2 tracer: whole-body biodistribution and brain PET comparison with [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ (AV-133)
Authors: Ruiyue Zhao, Jinhua Chen, Ting Ye, Jianmin Chu, Jingwen Li, Yan Zhang, Siran Xu, Shaoyu Liu, Ling Chen, Karl Ploessl, David Alexoff, Hank F. Kung, Lin Zhu, Xinlu Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn the central nervous system, type 2 vesicular monoamine transporters (VMAT2) are responsible for the reuptake of monoamines from synaptic junction back to pre-synaptic terminal vesicles. These transporters are functionally crucial as they reflect the integrity of monoamine neurons. D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ, a novel deuterated VMAT2 radioligand, has shown promise as a potential PET tracer for the diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease (PD). This study evaluates the biodistribution and dosimetry of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and includes a head-to-head comparison with its non-deuterated version, [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ (AV-133), in healthy individuals and PD patients.ResultsThe automated synthesis of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ using the SPE method was accomplished in 35 min, yielding a high radiochemical purity (> 99%) and high radiochemical yields (35 ± 5%). The biodistribution and dosimetry study indicated an effective dose of 37.1 ± 7.2 μSv/MBq, with the liver receiving the highest radiation dose (289.6 ± 42.1 μGy/MBq), followed by pancreas (185.2 ± 29.1 μGy/MBq). Brain imaging with D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ exhibited a significantly increased uptake in VMAT2-rich regions, particularly the striatum. In a head-to-head comparison between [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ, the latter exhibited approximately 15% higher SUVR in the caudate, putamen, and nucleus accumbens. Preliminary studies in PD patients showed a substantial reduction in VMAT2 uptake in the striatum, with the most pronounced decrease observed in the putamen (a 53% decline).ConclusionsD6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ is a safe and improved VMAT2-specific imaging agent, which may be suitable for diagnosing PD by evaluating changes in VMAT2 binding of monoamine neurons in the brain.Trial registration Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR2200057218, Registered 16 August 2021, https://www.chictr.org.cn/bin/project/edit?pid=142725.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41181-024-00301-y.

Introduction

Vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2) is a transporter protein on the secretory vesicle membranes of neurons and neuroendocrine cells. It facilitates the transport of monoamines (dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin) from the cytoplasm to the vesicles through an ATP-dependent mechanism (Stahl 2018). Alterations in VMAT2 function may indicate neuron integrity and serve as a direct assessment of living neurons. Brain imaging of VMAT2 provides an indirect method for quantifying the overall population of active monoaminergic neurons (Kong et al. 2020; Albin and Koeppe 2006).

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a prevalent neurodegenerative disorder characterized clinically by symptoms such as tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and instability in postural and gait, accompanied by a substantial loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra striata (Armstrong and Okun 2020). Various radiotracers targeting dopamine systems, including Iofluopane ([123I]DatScan) (Renwick 2021; Isaacson et al. 2017) and [99mTc]Tc-TRODAT-1 (Hsiao et al. 2014a; Siderowf et al. 2005) that focus on dopamine transporters, as well as [18F]F-DOPA (Arena et al. 2021; Depierreux et al. 2021), a dopamine derivative, have been employed in the diagnosis of PD. VMAT2 offers a precise reflection of dopaminergic function and is strongly associated with the progression of PD. In the early stage of PD (Hoehn and Yahr stage 1), there is a notable reduction in VMAT2 binding, often reaching 38% to 49% of the typical level (Strafella et al. 2017). This reduction can be detected up to 17 years before the manifestation of clinical symptoms, as suggested by a longitudinal study involving sporadic PD patients over several years. Consequently, VMAT2 imaging is vital for diagnosing PD, with several radiotracers already in use (Saeed et al. 2017). Additionally, VMAT2-targeted PET imaging has potential for differentiating Parkinsonian syndromes, such as progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP) and multiple system atrophy (MSA) (Ma et al. 2024; Raffel et al. 2006).

(+)-Tetrabenazine (Xenazine, TBZ, Fig. 1) received FDA approval in 2008 to treat hyperkinetic movement disorder (Madar et al. 2009). TBZ and its metabolites, exhibiting specific and high binding affinity to VMAT2, were labeled with the short half-life radionuclide [11C]carbon (T1/2 = 20 min) as [11C]TBZ and [11C]-(+)-DTBZ (Albin et al. 2009; Frey et al. 1996). These compounds served as valuable probes with PET imaging to assess alterations in monoamine neuron integrity in the brain. Additionally, 9-fluoropropoxyl derivatives of DTBZ, including [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ (also known as AV-133 or florbenazine) and [18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ, Fig. 1), labeled with the relatively longer half-life radionuclide [18F]fluoride (T1/2 = 110 min), have been successfully utilized for mapping VMAT2 in the brain and the entire body (Goswami et al. 2006; Okamura et al. 2010). Clinical studies involving [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ have indicated a positive correlation between the decrease in VMAT2 and the nonmotor symptoms of PD (Shi et al. 2019; Stoessl et al. 2011; Beauchamp et al. 2023).Fig. 1Chemical structures of VMAT2 targeting agents and imaging agents: (+)-tetrabenazine (TBZ) and tetrabenazine-D6 (Austedo); [18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ and D4-[18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ; [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ ([18F]AV-133) and D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ

Deuterium substitution is a common strategy in adjusting in vivo metabolism, thereby altering the drug’s therapeutic index. Recently, the FDA approved a deuterated TBZ, tetrabenazine-D6 (Austedo, SD-809, Fig. 1), demonstrating extended blood circulation and improved treatment of Huntington’s disease (Frank et al. 2016). Deuterated DTBZ derivatives, including D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and D4-[18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ (Fig. 1), have been reported to enhance the PET signal of VMAT2 by reducing in vivo defluorination (Liu et al. 2018; Jahan et al. 2011). Due to this mechanism, the biodistribution of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ showed improved striatum/cerebellum uptake ratios in the brains of normal rats (Zhao et al. 2019). Additionally, a study using D4-[18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ in nonhuman primates demonstrated higher brain uptake with faster washout compared to the nondeuterated [18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ (Liu et al. 2018; Jahan et al. 2011).

The deuteration of the tracer leads to reduced metabolic degradation, enhancing its stability in vivo. This increased stability allows for greater retention of the tracer in VMAT2-rich regions, resulting in a stronger PET signal. A stronger PET signal more accurately reflects the true status of VMAT2 expression, enabling precise assessment of subtle changes in VMAT2 levels. This heightened sensitivity is crucial for monitoring disease progression and treatment effects in PD, making D6-[18F]FP-( +)-DTBZ a valuable tool for clinical applications.

This manuscript reports the first-in-human study of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ, covering biodistribution, dosimetry, and safety assessments. Additionally, it includes a comparative analysis of SUVmean ratios (SUVR) in the striatum between the deuterated compound (D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ) and non-deuterated ([18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ, AV-133). Preliminary diagnostic efficiency between PD patients and healthy participants was also evaluated in this study.

Materials and methods

Preparation of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ

D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ was synthesized utilizing an automated radiosynthesis module (BIBD-F). As previously documented, human doses were prepared for the solid-phase extraction (SPE) purification method (Zhao et al. 2019). The synthesis was completed in 35 min (details were shown in supporting information Figs. S1–3), achieving a radiochemical purity (RCP) of over 99% and a radiochemical yield of 35 ± 5% (n = 10, decay corrected). All chemical impurities were kept below 50 μg, with a specific activity exceeding 40 TBq/mmol.

Subjects

The Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (ES-2021-62) and the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200057218) approved the study protocol. The study included twelve healthy volunteers and five patients diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease (PD) according to the UK Parkinson’s Disease Society Brain Bank Clinical Diagnosis Criteria (see Tables S1–2). There were no restrictions on the treatment history of the PD patients. Informed consent was obtained from all seventeen participants involved in the study.

PET/CT

All imaging was conducted using a PET/CT scanner (Discovery MI, GE Healthcare). Biodistribution and dosimetry of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ were obtained through serial whole-body (WB) scans involving six healthy volunteers (No. 1–6, four men, two women; age 24 ± 5 years old, as shown in Table S1). In summary, serial WB PET scans were acquired at 10, 30, 60, 90, 130, and 180 min after injection with 325.9 ± 56.2 MBq radioactivity, and a low-dose CT (120 kV, 30 mAs) was performed before each acquisition (shown in Fig. 2). Each WB scan consisted of 6-bed positions (1.5 min/bed, from the head to the proximal thighs). Subjects were allowed to leave the scanner and void freely during each 10–20 min break between successive scan sessions. The WB images, with resolutions of 2.79 × 2.79 × 2.79 mm, were reconstructed using 3D RAMLA with sharpIR, three iterations, 16 subsets, and a filter cutoff of 5 mm. PET brain scans at 70, 140 and 170 min were conducted in List mode with a 180 mm field of view (FOV), and a low-dose CT (120 kV, 30 mAs) was performed before the scans. PET images were reconstructed with 2 × 2 × 2 mm in 3D RAMLA, utilizing the CT scan for attenuation correction and fusion localization with the Bayesian regularization natural (BPL) reconstruction algorithm (Q.Clear, β = 250).Fig. 2Acquisition scheme for biodistribution study (WB = whole-body PET/CT scanning for 9 min, 1.5 min/bed with six beds; brain = brain PET/CT scanning for 10 min)

Acquisition scheme for biodistribution study (WB = whole-body PET/CT scanning for 9 min, 1.5 min/bed with six beds; brain = brain PET/CT scanning for 10 min)

Subsequently, brain PET imaging with [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ (345.6 ± 28.3 MBq) was carried out 70–80 min post-injection, with PET brain scanning data collected for the same subjects using the same procedures two weeks later.

MRI

Three-dimensional T1-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (T1WI-MRI) scans were performed for subjects with UPMR 790 (3.0 T, United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China). T1WI sequence parameters were as following: repetition time (TR)/echo time (TE)/inversion time (TI) = 7.9/3/750 ms, 1 mm section thickness, 230 mm × 256 mm field of view (FOV), and a 230 × 256 matrix.

Image analysis

All data were processed, and images were analyzed using the PMOD software (version 4.3, PMOD Technologies Ltd., Zurich, Switzerland). The brain PET images were normalized to the Hummers-N30R83 template in PMOD using SPM8. The volumes of interest (VOIs) for the caudate, putamen, nucleus accumbens, substantia nigra, thalamus, and occipital cortex (occipital lingual gyrus) were automatically outlined according to MRI by the PNEURO tool (details showed in Figure S4). The average standardized uptake value (SUVmean) of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ in VOIs was calculated. SUV ratios (SUVR) were calculated by dividing the target uptake SUVmean by the reference SUVmean (occipital cortex) (Hsiao et al. 2014b).

Dosimetry

PET images were analyzed by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians with at least five years of experience in PET/CT. A dosimetry study considered source organs, including the brain, thyroid, lungs, heart, liver, gallbladder, kidneys, spleen, pancreas, testes, ovaries, and whole body. The lumbar vertebrae were chosen as the source of red marrow due to their substantial uptake (12.3%) (Valentin 2002). Organ volumes were determined using CT, while PET images at different time points provided the mean activity (in kBq/mL). The activity of each organ (%ID) was calculated by multiplying the organ’s volume by the mean activity (in kBq/mL) and dividing by the injection dose (in kBq) (Lin et al. 2010). Time-activity curves for most organs employed monoexponential curve fitting to calculate the areas under the curve (AUC) via PMOD; however, the urinary bladder and uterus utilized biexponential curve fitting. Individual absorbed organ doses and effective doses (ED) were estimated using OLINDA/EXM software (version 1.0) with reference to adult males and females.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0. The paired and unpaired two-sample t-test was used to compare differences between SUVR. P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Head-to-head comparison between D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ

The study conducted a comparative analysis between deuterated (D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ) and non-deuterated ([18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ) radiotracers in six healthy subjects, utilizing PET brain imaging acquired 70–80 min post-injection. The SUVR of various brain regions, including the striatum and basal ganglia, exhibited notable differences between the two radiotracers, particularly in the areas enriched with VMAT2 (Fig. 4). Notably, PD patients in this study had varying therapy histories, yet their symptoms improved despite significant VMAT2 loss. Previous research links decreased striatal VMAT2 to various nonmotor symptoms in PD (Shi et al. 2019). VMAT2 PET imaging has also been used to assess treatment responses. Changes in VMAT2 density before and after deep brain stimulation (DBS) correlate with metabolic alterations in striatal and cortical regions, and improvements in depressive symptoms are associated with reduced striatal VMAT2 (Raffel et al. 2006; Smith et al. 2019). Future studies should investigate post-surgical VMAT2 changes to enhance understanding of treatment efficacy.

Discussion

As previously described, human doses of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ were synthesized using a one-step fluorination and SPE purification process, resulting in high radiochemical yields (30–40%) within 35 min (Zhao et al. 2019). The SPE method was effectively employed for routine production, eliminating the requirement for high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) purification. Importantly, no adverse reactions to D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ were observed in any of the subjects following intravenous injection.

The biodistribution and dosimetry investigation of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ revealed the highest accumulation in the liver (289 μGy/MBq), characterized by an increased uptake (Figure S5). The deuterated D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ may have reduced hepatic dealkylation (Lin et al. 2010; Davies and Roberts 1986; Mehvar et al. 1987). The slower clearance observed in humans between deuterated vs. non-deuterated tracers was consistent with the higher values reported in rats (3.10 vs. 2.12%ID/g at two hours after injection) (Liu et al. 2018).

Additionally, significantly lower red marrow uptake of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ was found, with the deuterated radioligand dose being almost ten times less than the non-deuterated counterpart (28.8 vs. 272 μGy/MBq). This reduction in bone uptake was also comparable to what was observed in rats (Liu et al. 2018). Moreover, substituting four ethyl hydrogens with deuterium in [18F]FE-(+)-DTBZ significantly reduced the defluorination rate from 0.012 to 0.0016 (Jahan et al. 2011). This deuterium substitution enhances the stability of the carbon–fluorine bond, reducing the likelihood of defluorination in vivo and leading to lower accumulation in red marrow. Consequently, this may improve safety profiles by minimizing radiation exposure to hematopoietic tissues. Furthermore, the decreased defluorination rates could enhance the overall biodistribution of the tracer, facilitating more targeted imaging of VMAT2 in the brain and ultimately leading to more accurate clinical assessments.

The direct comparison between D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ demonstrated that the SUVR of the deuterated DTBZ radioligand showed notably higher uptakes in VMAT2 high-density areas (caudate, putamen, and nucleus accumbens) than those of the non-deuterated DTBZ radioligand, which were consistent with findings in rat studies (the uptake in the striatum were 6.05 and 5.66%ID/g, respectively) (Liu et al. 2018). Meanwhile, D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ showed significantly lower overall brain uptake than [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ (39 vs. 128 μGy/MBq), which matches rat study findings, partly due to reduced uptake in non-specific brain areas like the occipital and cerebellum.

In post-mortem studies of the human brain, there appears to be a broad agreement on the age-related loss of dopaminergic neurons. However, the decline in dopamine production and storage degree remained controversial. PET studies using [11C]-(+)-DTBZ showed an age effect on nigrostriatal uptake. Elderly subjects displayed a similar uptake reduction in D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ PET brain imaging. The SUVR of caudate (2.64 vs. 3.69) and putamen (2.99 vs. 4.15) for older healthy participants (46–75 years old) were nearly 30% lower than that of young subjects (20–22 years old). Therefore, future studies are warranted to investigate the age effect of VMAT2 binding with D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and its relationship with the specificity and sensitivity for detecting the impact of aging on monoamine neurons in the brain.

This current study has several limitations. A primary limitation of this dosimetry and brain dynamic PET imaging study was the relatively young age of the participants (22–34 years old). However, conducting a head-to-head comparison of brain imaging between D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ and [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ in the same subjects would have enhanced the study’s specificity in target VMAT2 binding while minimizing confounding factors. It is reasonable to conclude that high SUVR of caudate, putamen, and nucleus accumbens of D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ vs. [18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ demonstrate that both are useful for the detection of monoamine neuronal integrity in the brain. However, precise changes in SUVR values in relationship to the status of PD and related movement disorders remain to be further explored in the future.

Conclusion

In summary, this inaugural human study has validated the safety and effectiveness of deuterated D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ as an imaging agent targeting VMAT2 binding sites on monoamine neurons. It demonstrated significant uptake in brain regions characterized by a high density of monoamine neurons, specifically in the caudate, putamen, and nucleus accumbens. D6-[18F]FP-(+)-DTBZ shows excellent potential as a valuable tool for the comprehensive clinical evaluation of PD.

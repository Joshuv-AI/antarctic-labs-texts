Beyond Recurrence: Impact of Surgical Technique on Testicular Microcirculation After Inguinal Hernia Repair
Authors: Mehmet Emin Gönüllü, Fatih Gürsoy, İbrahim Feyyaz Naldemir, Erman Yekenkurul, Mehmet Fuat Çetin, Sami Dogan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe impact of different inguinal hernia repair techniques on testicular perfusion remains a subject of clinical interest. While open and laparoscopic approaches demonstrate comparable outcomes in terms of recurrence and complications, their effects on testicular microcirculation are not fully elucidated.MethodsThis retrospective comparative cohort study included 180 male patients who underwent unilateral primary inguinal hernia repair using open Lichtenstein, total extraperitoneal (TEP), or transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) techniques between January 2015 and December 2025 at a tertiary referral center. Doppler ultrasonography parameters, including resistive index (RI), peak systolic velocity (PSV), end-diastolic velocity (EDV), and testicular volume, were evaluated preoperatively, at postoperative day 7, and at 1 month. Changes over time and between groups were analyzed using multivariable regression and linear mixed-effects models. Subclinical testicular ischemia was defined based on predefined Doppler criteria.ResultsEarly postoperative changes in testicular perfusion parameters differed significantly between surgical techniques. At postoperative day 7, RI values were higher and EDV values were lower in the Lichtenstein group compared to TEP and TAPP (p < 0.001 and p = 0.002, respectively). These differences decreased at 1 month but remained statistically significant for RI (p = 0.003). Multivariable analysis demonstrated that surgical technique, operative time, and hernia sac size were independently associated with changes in RI. Subclinical testicular ischemia was more frequent in the Lichtenstein group, while laparoscopic approaches were associated with lower odds (TEP: OR 0.34, p = 0.002; TAPP: OR 0.41, p = 0.006). Longitudinal analysis confirmed a significant time-by-technique interaction for RI (p < 0.001), indicating partial recovery over time.ConclusionSurgical technique is associated with transient alterations in testicular perfusion following inguinal hernia repair. Open Lichtenstein repair demonstrated more pronounced early microvascular changes, whereas laparoscopic techniques exhibited a more stable perfusion profile. These findings highlight the importance of surgical technique and intraoperative handling in preserving testicular microcirculation.Keywords: Inguinal Hernia, Lichtenstein Repair, Laparoscopic Trans Abdominal Pre-Peritoneal (TAPP), Laparoscopic Totally Extraperitoneal (TEP), Testicular Perfusion, Doppler Ultrasonography, Resistive Index, Subclinical Ischemia

Introduction

Inguinal hernia repair is one of the most commonly performed procedures in general surgery, with a lifetime risk reaching up to 25% in men [1–3]. Although the choice between open Lichtenstein repair and laparoscopic approaches, including total extraperitoneal (TEP) and transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) techniques, largely depends on surgeon preference and patient characteristics, both strategies have demonstrated low recurrence rates and acceptable complication profiles [4–6]. Beyond these traditional outcomes, increasing attention has been directed toward the potential impact of surgical techniques on testicular perfusion and long-term functional outcomes.

Testicular blood flow may be influenced by intraoperative factors such as dissection, traction, and thermal exposure affecting the spermatic cord structures. In open repair, more extensive manipulation of these structures has been hypothesized to induce transient or persistent microvascular alterations [7–9]. In contrast, laparoscopic approaches, by providing enhanced visualization of the preperitoneal space and requiring less direct handling of the spermatic cord, may exert a more limited effect on testicular circulation. Doppler ultrasonography has been widely used to evaluate these changes, with parameters such as resistive index (RI) and end-diastolic flow serving as reliable indicators of testicular perfusion [10–12].

However, the current literature is characterized by several important limitations. Many studies comparing the effects of different surgical techniques on testicular perfusion have been conducted with relatively small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, or have focused on a single surgical approach [5, 13, 14]. Moreover, the temporal evolution of perfusion changes and their relationship with clinically silent, subclinical ischemia remain insufficiently defined. Studies incorporating comprehensive preoperative and postoperative assessments, longitudinal analyses, and multivariable modeling are still limited [15, 16].

In this context, the present study aimed to comparatively evaluate the effects of open Lichtenstein, TEP, and TAPP techniques on testicular perfusion using Doppler ultrasonography parameters. In addition, the study sought to characterize longitudinal changes in perfusion over time, identify factors associated with subclinical testicular ischemia, and determine the independent contribution of surgical technique through multivariable statistical models. We hypothesized that open repair would be associated with more pronounced early postoperative alterations in testicular perfusion, with partial recovery over time, whereas laparoscopic techniques would demonstrate a more favorable perfusion profile.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This study was designed as a retrospective, comparative cohort study conducted at a high-volume tertiary referral center. The analysis included patients who underwent unilateral primary inguinal hernia repair between January 2015 and December 2025. The study was carried out through a collaborative effort between the Departments of General Surgery and Radiology. Because the study covered a 10-year period, all procedures and imaging assessments were performed within the routine institutional practice of the same tertiary center, although temporal changes in surgical experience, mesh use, fixation preferences, and Doppler reporting practices were considered when interpreting the findings. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Düzce University Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (approval number: 2026/81; date: 23 March 2026). The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective design and the use of anonymized patient data, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee.

Study population and patient selection

The study population consisted of adult male patients who underwent elective surgical repair for unilateral primary inguinal hernia between January 2015 and December 2025. Patient data were retrospectively retrieved from the hospital information system, operative records, outpatient follow-up files, and radiology archiving system. All patients who met the initial eligibility criteria during the study period were screened. Patients were then assessed according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, and those with complete clinical and imaging data were included in the final analysis. During the study period, 642 male patients who underwent inguinal hernia repair were initially screened. Of these, 352 patients were excluded before Doppler-based eligibility assessment because of bilateral or recurrent hernia, emergency surgery, previous inguinal/scrotal/pelvic surgery, known testicular pathology or impaired preoperative testicular perfusion, systemic vascular disease or anticoagulant use, early reintervention, or incomplete clinical and operative records. Among the remaining 290 potentially eligible patients, 84 were excluded because complete Doppler ultrasonography data at all three predefined time points were not available. Therefore, 206 patients had complete clinical, operative, and longitudinal Doppler data. Because the number of complete cases differed between surgical techniques, a group-balanced retrospective sampling strategy was used to avoid overrepresentation of the most frequently performed technique. Accordingly, 60 patients were included in each surgical group, resulting in a final study cohort of 180 patients. This group-balanced selection was performed only for cohort construction and should not be interpreted as randomization or matching. Patient flow diagram of the study population

Inclusion criteria were as follows: age ≥ 18 years, diagnosis of unilateral primary inguinal hernia, and treatment with one of the three surgical techniques: open Lichtenstein, laparoscopic total extraperitoneal repair (TEP), or transabdominal preperitoneal repair (TAPP). Availability of preoperative and postoperative Doppler ultrasonography evaluations performed according to the institutional follow-up protocol, including preoperative baseline, postoperative day 7, and postoperative month 1 assessments, was required for inclusion. Exclusion criteria included bilateral or recurrent inguinal hernia, emergency surgery for incarcerated or strangulated hernia, prior inguinal, scrotal, or pelvic surgery, known testicular pathology including varicocele, hydrocele, testicular tumor, previous torsion, or orchiectomy, and preoperative evidence of impaired testicular perfusion. Patients with systemic vascular conditions such as diabetes mellitus or advanced peripheral vascular disease, those receiving anticoagulant therapy, and those with postoperative complications requiring early reintervention were also excluded. In addition, patients with incomplete clinical, operative, or imaging data were not included in the analysis.

Surgical techniques

All procedures were performed by board-certified general surgeons experienced in inguinal hernia repair, following standardized institutional protocols. The choice of technique (open Lichtenstein, laparoscopic TEP, or TAPP repair) was based on surgeon preference, patient characteristics, and clinical considerations. Therefore, treatment allocation was non-randomized and reflected routine clinical decision-making rather than a randomized comparison between surgical techniques. Open Lichtenstein repair was performed through an anterior inguinal approach. After identification and isolation of the spermatic cord, the hernia sac was dissected, reduced or ligated, and excised as appropriate. A polypropylene mesh was positioned over the posterior wall of the inguinal canal and secured to the inguinal ligament and surrounding structures using non-absorbable sutures. Care was taken to minimize excessive traction and manipulation of the spermatic cord structures. In the TEP technique, the preperitoneal space was accessed without entering the peritoneal cavity. A working space was created using blunt and/or balloon dissection. Following reduction of the hernia sac, a synthetic mesh was placed to cover the myopectineal orifice. Mesh fixation was applied selectively or omitted based on intraoperative assessment and surgeon preference. In the TAPP approach, pneumoperitoneum was established, and the peritoneal cavity was entered laparoscopically. The peritoneum was incised to access the preperitoneal space, and the hernia sac was reduced. A mesh was then positioned to adequately cover the defect and the myopectineal orifice. The peritoneum was closed using sutures or tacks at the end of the procedure. Across all techniques, operative time, hernia type, hernia sac characteristics, mesh placement, and fixation method were systematically recorded. Surgical steps were performed in accordance with routine clinical practice, and no additional procedures were introduced for study purposes.

Doppler ultrasonography assessment

Doppler ultrasonography evaluations were performed as part of the institutional follow-up protocol and were retrospectively reviewed through the radiology archiving system. Examinations were conducted at three predefined time points: preoperatively (baseline), postoperative day 7, and postoperative month 1. All assessments were performed using high-frequency linear transducers with patients in the supine position under standardized conditions. Color and spectral Doppler imaging were used to evaluate testicular perfusion. Measurements were obtained from the intratesticular branches of the testicular artery, and care was taken to avoid excessive probe pressure during acquisition. The following parameters were recorded for each testis: resistive index (RI), peak systolic velocity (PSV), end-diastolic velocity (EDV), and testicular volume. Testicular volume was calculated using the ellipsoid formula based on three orthogonal dimensions. Doppler values were primarily extracted from finalized radiology reports generated during routine clinical care. When a report did not explicitly include one of the required numerical parameters but technically adequate archived images were available, the relevant measurement was retrospectively re-reviewed from the stored Doppler images by an experienced radiologist. Examinations during the study period were performed or reported by radiologists with experience in scrotal Doppler ultrasonography; however, the exact same radiologist did not perform all examinations across the entire 10-year period. Therefore, formal inter-reader reliability analysis was not possible because the study was based on retrospectively available clinical reports and archived images. For patients with multiple measurements at the same time point, the most representative and technically adequate examination was selected for analysis. All Doppler data were collected and reviewed in a consistent manner for subsequent statistical evaluation.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome of the study was the change in testicular perfusion, assessed by the difference in testicular artery resistive index (ΔRI) between the preoperative and postoperative measurements. ΔRI was calculated separately for postoperative day 7 and postoperative month 1 relative to baseline values. Secondary outcomes included changes in other Doppler ultrasonography parameters, namely peak systolic velocity (ΔPSV), end-diastolic velocity (ΔEDV), and testicular volume, evaluated at the same time points. In addition, longitudinal changes in these parameters across all three time points (preoperative, day 7, and month 1) were analyzed. Subclinical testicular ischemia was defined as the presence of abnormal Doppler findings in the absence of overt clinical symptoms. For the purpose of this study, subclinical ischemia was identified when at least one of the following criteria was present at postoperative evaluation: (i) an increase in resistive index above 0.70, (ii) a decrease in end-diastolic velocity compared to baseline, or (iii) a relative increase in RI of ≥ 0.05 from the preoperative value. These thresholds were selected based on previously reported Doppler criteria for impaired testicular perfusion. Additional outcome measures included early postoperative complications, such as scrotal hematoma, seroma formation, surgical site infection, and acute postoperative pain requiring additional analgesia. These outcomes were recorded based on clinical follow-up data and patient records.

Data collection and variables

Clinical, demographic, operative, and radiological data were retrospectively collected from the hospital information system, operative reports, outpatient follow-up records, and the radiology archiving system. All data were recorded using a standardized data extraction form to ensure consistency. Demographic variables included age and body mass index. Clinical variables comprised ASA physical status classification, hernia side (right or left), and hernia type (direct or indirect). Operative variables included the surgical technique (Lichtenstein, TEP, or TAPP), operative time (minutes), hernia sac size, mesh placement, and mesh fixation method (suture, tack, or no fixation). These data were obtained from operative notes and surgical records. Radiological variables consisted of Doppler ultrasonography parameters, including resistive index (RI), peak systolic velocity (PSV), end-diastolic velocity (EDV), and testicular volume, measured at preoperative baseline, postoperative day 7, and postoperative month 1. Outcome variables included changes in Doppler parameters (ΔRI, ΔPSV, ΔEDV, and testicular volume), the presence of subclinical testicular ischemia, and early postoperative complications. All variables were defined prior to analysis and consistently recorded for each patient included in the study.

Missing data handling

Missing data were assessed during the data extraction process. Patients with incomplete key variables, particularly those lacking preoperative or postoperative Doppler ultrasonography measurements, were excluded from the analysis. Due to the retrospective design and the requirement for complete longitudinal Doppler data, a complete-case analysis approach was applied. Only patients with available measurements at all predefined time points (preoperative, postoperative day 7, and postoperative month 1) were included in the final study cohort. No imputation methods were used. Among 290 potentially eligible patients after clinical and operative exclusions, 84 patients were excluded because of incomplete Doppler ultrasonography follow-up. The most common missing-data reasons were absence of postoperative day 7 Doppler assessment (n = 31), absence of postoperative month 1 Doppler assessment (n = 28), absence of preoperative baseline Doppler assessment (n = 14), technically inadequate or non-interpretable Doppler images/reports (n = 7), and incomplete numerical Doppler parameters despite available imaging records (n = 4). In addition, 22 patients were excluded earlier because of incomplete clinical or operative records. Therefore, no statistical imputation was performed, and the final analysis was restricted to patients with complete clinical, operative, and Doppler datasets.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows (Version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was assessed using visual methods (histograms and Q–Q plots) and the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range) as appropriate, while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Baseline characteristics were compared across the three groups using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables, and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Changes in Doppler ultrasonography parameters between preoperative and postoperative time points were analyzed using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as appropriate. Between-group comparisons of change values (ΔRI, ΔPSV, ΔEDV, and testicular volume) were performed using ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis tests with appropriate post hoc analyses (Bonferroni or Dunn correction). Longitudinal changes in Doppler parameters across all time points were evaluated using linear mixed-effects models, including fixed effects for time and surgical technique, as well as their interaction. Multivariable linear regression analysis was used to identify factors independently associated with ΔRI at postoperative day 7. Variables with clinical relevance or statistical significance in univariable analyses were included in the multivariable model. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate factors associated with subclinical testicular ischemia. Model performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics of the study population. A total of 180 patients were included, with 60 patients in each surgical group (Lichtenstein, TEP, and TAPP). The mean age of the cohort was similar across groups (p = 0.421), as was body mass index (p = 0.337). The distribution of ASA physical status classification did not differ significantly among groups (p = 0.512). Hernia side (right vs. left) and hernia type (direct vs. indirect) were comparable between the three techniques (p = 0.618 and p = 0.447, respectively). Median hernia sac size did not differ significantly across groups (p = 0.289). Operative time was longer in the laparoscopic groups compared to the Lichtenstein group (p < 0.001). The distribution of mesh fixation methods differed between groups (p < 0.001).

Discussion

The present study demonstrates that surgical technique is associated with measurable alterations in testicular perfusion in the early postoperative period. Significant differences were observed in Doppler ultrasonography parameters, particularly RI and EDV, with more pronounced changes detected following open Lichtenstein repair compared to laparoscopic approaches. These differences were most evident in the early postoperative phase and showed partial normalization over time. In addition, subclinical testicular ischemia was more frequently identified in association with open repair, while laparoscopic techniques exhibited a more favorable perfusion profile [17]. Multivariable and longitudinal analyses further supported the independent relationship between surgical technique and postoperative perfusion dynamics.

The observed differences in postoperative testicular perfusion can be interpreted in the context of the anatomical and physiological characteristics of the spermatic cord and its vascular components. Testicular blood supply is primarily derived from the testicular artery, with venous drainage occurring through the pampiniform plexus. This delicate vascular network is particularly susceptible to intraoperative mechanical and thermal influences [18, 19]. During open Lichtenstein repair, dissection within the inguinal canal and direct handling of the spermatic cord may lead to transient compression, traction-related endothelial dysfunction, or microvascular spasm. In addition, disruption of venous outflow at the level of the pampiniform plexus may increase intratesticular vascular resistance, which is reflected as an elevation in resistive index [20–22].

In contrast, laparoscopic techniques such as TEP and TAPP involve a posterior approach to the myopectineal orifice, allowing for mesh placement with relatively limited direct manipulation of the spermatic cord structures. The enhanced visualization provided by laparoscopy may also contribute to more precise dissection and reduced inadvertent vascular compromise [23]. Furthermore, the preservation of pericordal tissue planes and reduced traction on the cord structures may help maintain both arterial inflow and venous drainage. These differences in surgical exposure and tissue handling likely account for the more stable Doppler parameters observed following laparoscopic repair [24].

Another contributing factor may be the role of postoperative tissue edema and localized inflammatory response. Surgical trauma can induce temporary interstitial swelling within the spermatic cord, which may compress small vessels and alter microcirculatory dynamics. This effect is expected to be more pronounced in open procedures due to the extent of dissection [25, 26]. Over time, as edema resolves and microvascular autoregulation is restored, partial normalization of perfusion parameters may occur, consistent with the longitudinal findings observed in the present study.

Among the Doppler-derived metrics, RI represents a composite measure of downstream vascular resistance and is widely used as a surrogate for microvascular perfusion. In the present study, RI exhibited the most consistent and pronounced postoperative changes, particularly in the early period following surgery. The observed increase in RI after open repair aligns with prior reports suggesting that surgical manipulation of the spermatic cord can lead to transient elevations in intratesticular vascular resistance [27, 28]. Mechanistically, this may reflect a combination of arteriolar vasoconstriction, endothelial dysfunction, and impaired venous outflow, all of which contribute to reduced diastolic flow and a relative increase in resistance indices [29].

Existing studies have demonstrated that even subtle increases in RI may indicate early microcirculatory compromise before overt clinical manifestations occur [30, 31]. While most investigations report normalization of RI over time, the magnitude and duration of this change appear to vary depending on surgical technique and the extent of tissue handling. The more modest changes observed following laparoscopic repair in the present study are consistent with findings from smaller comparative series, in which minimally invasive approaches were associated with more stable perfusion parameters [32]. Importantly, RI should not be interpreted in isolation but rather in conjunction with other Doppler parameters and clinical context. However, its sensitivity to early hemodynamic alterations makes it particularly valuable in detecting subclinical changes in testicular perfusion. The persistence of elevated RI in a subset of patients, even in the absence of clinical symptoms, further supports its role as an early indicator of subclinical ischemia and underscores the relevance of incorporating RI into postoperative surveillance protocols. PSV and EDV provide complementary insights into arterial inflow and downstream vascular resistance within the testicular microcirculation. In the present study, changes in PSV were modest and less consistent compared to RI, whereas EDV demonstrated more pronounced alterations in the early postoperative period, particularly following open repair [33–35]. PSV primarily reflects the systolic component of arterial inflow and is influenced by factors such as cardiac output and proximal arterial integrity. As such, it is generally less sensitive to microvascular changes confined to the intratesticular circulation. This may explain the relatively limited variation in PSV observed across surgical techniques, as the procedures are unlikely to significantly affect proximal arterial supply. Previous studies have similarly reported minimal or inconsistent changes in PSV following inguinal hernia repair, supporting the notion that PSV alone may not be a reliable marker of subtle perfusion disturbances. In contrast, EDV is more directly affected by downstream vascular resistance and venous outflow conditions. A reduction in EDV typically indicates increased resistance within the microvascular bed or impaired venous drainage. The more pronounced decrease in EDV observed after open repair in the early postoperative period is consistent with the hypothesis that surgical manipulation of the spermatic cord and surrounding tissues may transiently disrupt venous return or increase interstitial pressure. This reduction in diastolic flow contributes directly to the elevation in RI, reinforcing the interdependence of these parameters [15, 36].

From a physiological perspective, the combined interpretation of PSV and EDV provides a more nuanced understanding of testicular hemodynamics. While PSV reflects the capacity for arterial inflow, EDV and RI together capture the resistance and compliance of the downstream vascular bed. The partial recovery of EDV over time observed in the present study suggests that these early hemodynamic disturbances are, at least in part, reversible, likely reflecting the resolution of postoperative edema and restoration of microvascular function. Testicular volume is a structural parameter that indirectly reflects the integrity of testicular tissue and its microvascular environment. In the present study, only modest and transient changes in testicular volume were observed in the early postoperative period, with a slight decrease noted particularly following open repair and subsequent partial recovery over time. These findings are consistent with the concept that early postoperative alterations in perfusion are primarily functional rather than structural [37, 38].

From a pathophysiological perspective, short-term reductions in testicular volume may be attributed to transient factors such as interstitial edema, venous congestion, or alterations in microcirculatory dynamics. Surgical manipulation of the spermatic cord, especially in open techniques, may contribute to temporary disruption of lymphatic and venous drainage, leading to subtle changes in tissue turgor and volume measurements. However, the absence of sustained or progressive volume loss suggests that these effects are reversible and unlikely to represent permanent parenchymal damage [25, 39].

Previous studies have reported conflicting results regarding postoperative changes in testicular volume, with most indicating no significant long-term impact following inguinal hernia repair. The relatively stable volume measurements observed across all surgical techniques in the later postoperative period in the present study support these findings and reinforce the notion that early Doppler alterations do not necessarily translate into clinically relevant structural impairment [40, 41]. Taken together, these results suggest that while testicular volume may exhibit minor fluctuations in the immediate postoperative phase, it is less sensitive than Doppler-derived parameters in detecting early microvascular changes. Therefore, testicular volume should be interpreted as a supportive rather than primary marker of postoperative testicular perfusion. The identification of subclinical testicular ischemia represents one of the most clinically relevant aspects of the present study. Unlike overt ischemic complications, which are rare and typically accompanied by clear clinical findings, subclinical alterations in perfusion may remain undetected without targeted imaging assessment. The use of Doppler ultrasonography criteria allowed for the detection of these subtle changes, revealing a differential distribution across surgical techniques [42, 43].

From a mechanistic standpoint, subclinical ischemia likely reflects a transient imbalance between arterial inflow and venous outflow at the microvascular level. Even in the absence of complete vascular compromise, partial obstruction, endothelial dysfunction, or increased interstitial pressure may impair effective tissue perfusion. The association observed between larger hernia sac size, longer operative duration, and increased likelihood of subclinical ischemia further supports the role of surgical complexity and tissue handling in influencing microcirculatory dynamics. The clinical significance of subclinical ischemia remains an area of ongoing investigation. While many cases may resolve spontaneously without long-term consequences, persistent or repeated microvascular disturbances could theoretically contribute to impaired spermatogenesis or subtle functional decline over time. Previous studies have suggested that alterations in Doppler parameters, particularly elevated RI and reduced diastolic flow, may precede more overt testicular dysfunction, although definitive longitudinal data are limited [44].

In this context, the present findings highlight the importance of recognizing subclinical perfusion changes as a measurable and technique-dependent phenomenon. The lower odds of subclinical ischemia observed following laparoscopic repair may reflect reduced mechanical and inflammatory stress on spermatic cord structures. These observations support the potential role of Doppler ultrasonography not only as a research tool but also as a means of postoperative monitoring in selected patient populations, particularly in cases with higher surgical complexity or risk factors for vascular compromise.

The longitudinal analysis in the present study provides important insight into the temporal behavior of testicular perfusion following inguinal hernia repair. The observed pattern—characterized by early postoperative alterations followed by partial normalization—suggests that most perfusion changes are dynamic and, to a considerable extent, reversible. This trajectory was most clearly reflected in the evolution of RI and EDV values, which demonstrated early deviation from baseline with subsequent improvement at one month.

From a physiological perspective, these findings can be explained by the transient nature of postoperative tissue responses. Surgical manipulation induces a localized inflammatory cascade, leading to interstitial edema, increased capillary permeability, and temporary alterations in microvascular resistance. These changes can impair venous outflow and elevate intratesticular pressure, thereby reducing effective perfusion in the early postoperative phase. As the inflammatory response subsides and tissue homeostasis is restored, microvascular function gradually improves, resulting in normalization of Doppler parameters. The consistency of this recovery pattern across different analytical approaches, including both change-based comparisons and mixed-effects modeling, reinforces the robustness of the findings. While laparoscopic techniques demonstrated a more stable perfusion profile throughout the follow-up period, the convergence of values over time across all groups suggests that the initial differences may not translate into persistent long-term impairment in most cases. These observations are in line with previous studies reporting transient Doppler changes after inguinal hernia repair, although the present study extends existing knowledge by incorporating a structured longitudinal design and multivariable adjustment. The integration of time-dependent analysis provides a more comprehensive understanding of the natural course of postoperative perfusion changes and underscores the importance of considering temporal dynamics when interpreting Doppler findings in this setting [45].

Clinical implications

For surgeons

From a surgical perspective, the findings of the present study highlight the relevance of technique-related factors in preserving testicular microcirculation. The association between open repair and more pronounced early alterations in perfusion parameters suggests that careful handling of the spermatic cord structures is critical in minimizing microvascular disturbance. Although these changes appear to be largely reversible, their presence underscores the importance of gentle dissection, avoidance of excessive traction, and meticulous hemostasis during open procedures.

In addition, operative duration and hernia sac size emerged as factors associated with perfusion changes, suggesting that procedural complexity may influence microvascular outcomes. These observations may support a more tailored surgical approach, particularly in cases involving large hernia sacs or technically demanding dissections. In selected patients, especially those with potential concerns regarding testicular function, laparoscopic techniques may offer a more favorable microcirculatory profile due to reduced direct manipulation of the spermatic cord. While these considerations should not override established indications for surgical technique selection, they may contribute to a more nuanced, patient-centered decision-making process.

For radiologists

From a radiological perspective, the present study underscores the value of Doppler ultrasonography in detecting subtle, clinically silent alterations in testicular perfusion following inguinal hernia repair. Parameters such as resistive index and end-diastolic velocity appear to be particularly sensitive to early microvascular changes and may provide useful adjunctive information in the postoperative assessment of selected patients.

The identification of subclinical ischemia based on Doppler criteria highlights the importance of standardized measurement techniques and careful interpretation of findings within the appropriate clinical context. Given the dynamic nature of postoperative changes, radiologists should be aware that early alterations in perfusion parameters may not necessarily indicate permanent vascular compromise. Serial evaluation, when clinically indicated, may help differentiate transient postoperative effects from persistent abnormalities.

Overall, these findings support a collaborative approach between surgeons and radiologists, in which imaging findings are integrated with surgical details and clinical presentation to guide postoperative evaluation and management.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of the present study. First, the retrospective and non-randomized design inherently introduces the possibility of selection bias and residual confounding, as the choice of surgical technique was based on surgeon preference, patient characteristics, and clinical considerations rather than random allocation. In addition, patient inclusion depended on the availability of complete preoperative and serial postoperative Doppler ultrasonography data, and the use of complete-case analysis may have preferentially included patients with more structured follow-up, thereby limiting generalizability. Second, the relatively long recruitment period from 2015 to 2025 may have introduced temporal heterogeneity related to changes in surgical experience, laparoscopic technique, mesh materials, fixation strategies, Doppler equipment, or imaging protocols over time. Although the study was conducted at a single tertiary referral center using standardized institutional approaches, such changes cannot be fully excluded and may have influenced the results. Third, Doppler ultrasonography is an operator-dependent modality. Measurements were retrospectively extracted from archived radiology reports and available imaging records performed in routine clinical practice; therefore, inter-reader variability could not be formally assessed, and differences between radiologists or reporting practices may have affected Doppler parameters. Moreover, RI and EDV are indirect indicators of microvascular function and may be influenced by systemic hemodynamic factors that were not fully controlled for. Fourth, the balanced distribution of patients across the three groups should be interpreted in light of the retrospective selection process and the requirement for complete longitudinal data, rather than as evidence of randomization or matching. Fifth, subclinical testicular ischemia was defined using Doppler-derived thresholds rather than histopathological, semen analysis, hormonal, fertility-related, or long-term functional outcomes. Because there is no universally accepted definition of subclinical ischemia in this setting, the clinical implications of these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Finally, the follow-up period was limited to the early and intermediate postoperative phases. Although partial recovery of perfusion parameters was observed, the long-term consequences of these transient Doppler changes, including their potential effects on spermatogenesis, fertility, or testicular function, were not evaluated. Prospective, multicenter studies with standardized Doppler protocols, clearly defined enrollment flow, longer follow-up, and functional reproductive outcomes are needed to confirm these findings and clarify their clinical significance.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the findings of this study indicate that the choice of surgical technique is associated with measurable, predominantly transient alterations in testicular perfusion following inguinal hernia repair. Open Lichtenstein repair was associated with more pronounced early postoperative changes in Doppler ultrasonography parameters, particularly resistive index and end-diastolic velocity, whereas laparoscopic techniques demonstrated a more stable perfusion profile.

These alterations appeared to improve over time, suggesting that early microvascular disturbances are largely reversible. However, the identification of subclinical testicular ischemia and its association with operative factors such as surgical technique, operative duration, and hernia sac size highlight the potential clinical relevance of these findings.

From a clinical standpoint, these results underscore the importance of meticulous surgical technique and careful handling of spermatic cord structures to minimize microvascular impact. In addition, Doppler ultrasonography may serve as a useful adjunct in the postoperative evaluation of selected patients, particularly in cases with increased procedural complexity.

Further prospective, multicenter studies with extended follow-up and functional outcome assessment are warranted to clarify the long-term implications of these perfusion changes and to better define their role in clinical decision-making.

Updated Integrated Safety Analysis of Ritlecitinib up to ~ 5 Years in Patients with Alopecia Areata from the ALLEGRO Clinical Trial Program
Authors: Maryanne Senna, Sameh Hanna, Rie Ueki, Xingqi Zhang, Crystal Aguh, Bianca Maria Piraccini, Dalia Wajsbrot, Edward Nagy, Mojgan Sadrarhami, Simon Chen, Robert Wolk, Helen Tran, Alexandre Lejeune
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRitlecitinib, an oral Janus kinase (JAK) 3/tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (TEC) family kinase inhibitor, demonstrated safety in patients aged 12 years and older with alopecia areata (AA) in an initial integrated analysis of 4 clinical studies for up to 2 years.ObjectiveThis updated integrated safety analysis evaluated the safety of ritlecitinib up to ~ 5 years in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA from the ALLEGRO clinical trial program.MethodsSafety data were pooled from 4 studies. Two groups were analyzed: patients who received any dose of ritlecitinib (30 mg or 50 mg with or without a 4-week 200 mg loading dose, or 10 mg daily) (“any-ritlecitinib” group) and a subset of the any-ritlecitinib group including only patients who received ritlecitinib 50 mg daily with or without a 4-week 200 mg daily loading dose (ritlecitinib 50 mg ± 200-mg group). Safety data were summarized descriptively. Proportions and incidence rates (IRs; IR/100 patient-years [PYs]) of adverse events (AEs) were evaluated.ResultsIn the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg (N = 1228) and any-ritlecitinib (N = 1294) groups, median duration of exposure was 1197 days (3261.5 PYs) and 1204 days (3539.5 PYs), respectively. AEs occurred in 1070 patients (87.1%; 148.1/100 PYs) in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 1158 patients (89.5%; 167.9/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group. The most common AEs included headache, positive SARS-CoV-2 test, and nasopharyngitis. Serious AEs were reported in 6.8% of patients (2.6/100 PYs) in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 6.8% of patients (2.5/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group. There were two deaths. Overall, 8.1% of patients (3.0/100 PYs) and 8.4% of patients (3.0/100 PYs) in the 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively, had an AE that led to discontinuation from the study or study drug. IRs for both groups were 0.1/100 PYs for opportunistic infections, 1.0/100 PYs for herpes zoster, 0.3/100 PYs for malignancies (excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer), and 0.2/100 PYs for major adverse cardiovascular events.ConclusionsIn this updated integrated safety analysis of the ALLEGRO clinical trials, long-term ritlecitinib treatment was generally well tolerated up to ~ 5 years in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA. Key Points

Integrated safety data from multiple clinical trials in alopecia areata provide the longest duration of follow-up for ritlecitinib, a Janus kinase (JAK) 3/tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (TEC) family kinase inhibitor, to date.No new safety risks were observed with long-term ritlecitinib treatment in patients aged ≥12 years with alopecia areata over a period of up to ~5 years and 3540 patient years of exposure.The overall safety profile of ritlecitinib was consistent with previously reported data.

Introduction

Alopecia areata (AA) is an autoimmune disease characterized by patchy or complete nonscarring hair loss on the scalp, with or without additional loss of facial and/or body hair [1]. The global prevalence of AA ranges from 0.58% to 2% [2–5]. AA has an unpredictable disease course, which may result in extensive hair loss, such as alopecia totalis (AT; complete loss of scalp hair) and alopecia universalis (AU; complete loss of scalp, face, and body hair) [2, 6]. It is also associated with a reduced health-related quality of life and a considerable psychosocial burden [7–11].

AA pathophysiology involves an attack of the hair follicle by CD8+ T cells, leading to nonscarring hair loss [12, 13]. Ritlecitinib, an oral, selective dual inhibitor of Janus kinase (JAK) 3 and the tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (TEC) family of kinases [14], blocks key cytokine signaling and T-cell cytotoxicity, thereby reducing inflammation and immune attack at the hair follicle [15]. Ritlecitinib 50 mg once daily is approved for the treatment of severe AA in patients aged ≥ 12 years and demonstrated safety and efficacy in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA and ≥ 50% scalp hair loss for up to 48 weeks in the ALLEGRO phase 2b/3 study (ALLEGRO-2b/3; NCT03732807) [16]. Eligible patients from ALLEGRO-2b/3 rolled over into the ALLEGRO-LT (NCT04006457) phase 3 open-label study investigating the long-term safety and efficacy of ritlecitinib for up to 5 years in patients with AA. Since long-term systemic treatment may be needed for effective disease control, understanding the long-term safety profiles of treatments for AA is important for informing benefit–risk profiles and clinical decision-making.

Here we report the long-term safety of ritlecitinib up to ~ 5 years in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA from two phase 2a studies (ALLEGRO-2a safety study, NCT04517864; ALLEGRO-2a proof-of-concept study, NCT02974868), the ALLEGRO phase 2b/3 study, and the ongoing, phase 3, open-label ALLEGRO-LT study.

Methods

Study Design

This analysis includes data pooled from four studies: two ALLEGRO phase 2a studies, the ALLEGRO-2b/3 study, and the open-label phase 3 ALLEGRO-LT study. The two phase 2a studies and ALLEGRO-2b/3 included a placebo-controlled period, with patients receiving ritlecitinib or placebo up to week 24 or 36. In ALLEGRO-2a safety, patients received ritlecitinib 50 mg once daily (QD) with an initial 4-week 200-mg QD loading dose (200/50 mg) or placebo, followed by an extension period where patients receiving placebo switched to ritlecitinib 200/50 mg. In ALLEGRO-2a proof-of-concept, patients received ritlecitinib 200/50 mg or placebo, then underwent a 4-week drug holiday, before receiving ritlecitinib 200/50 mg during an extension period. In ALLEGRO-2b/3, patients received ritlecitinib 50 mg QD (with or without a 200-mg loading dose), 30 mg QD (with or without a 200-mg loading dose), or 10 mg QD, before rolling over to ALLEGRO-LT and receiving ritlecitinib 50 mg QD up to 5 years (Fig. S1). ALLEGRO-LT included patients enrolled in two arms: (1) rollover patients who received treatment in ALLEGRO-2a proof-of-concept or ALLEGRO-2b/3; in ALLEGRO-LT, these patients received ritlecitinib 50 mg QD, and (2) de novo patients who had not received treatment in either study; in ALLEGRO-LT, de novo patients received ritlecitinib 200/50 mg. Two analysis populations are presented here: (1) patients in the four ALLEGRO program studies who received any dose of ritlecitinib (30 mg or 50 mg QD with or without a 4-week 200 mg QD loading dose or 10 mg QD) (the “any-ritlecitinib” group) and (2) a subset of the any-ritlecitinib group, comprising patients who received ritlecitinib 50 mg QD, with or without a 4-week 200 mg daily initial loading dose (the “ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg” group) (Fig. 1). These two overlapping groups are presented side-by-side for completeness and are not intended for comparison with each other. For the any-ritlecitinib group, day 1 of exposure was the first day of ritlecitinib. Data from the time periods that patients received placebo were not included in this analysis. The data cutoff date for this analysis was 25 June 2024.

Patients

Inclusion and exclusion criteria for ALLEGRO-2a proof-of-concept, ALLEGRO-2a safety, ALLEGRO-2b/3, and ALLEGRO-LT were described previously [16–18]. Patients in ALLEGRO-2b/3 were aged ≥ 12 years with a diagnosis of AA and ≥ 50% scalp hair loss at baseline, and de novo patients in ALLEGRO-LT had ≥ 25% scalp hair loss at baseline, including patients with AT and AU. The maximum duration of the current episode of hair loss in ALLEGRO-2b/3 and in de novo patients in ALLEGRO-LT was ≤ 10 years, and patients with previous use of JAK inhibitors were excluded. Per the ALLEGRO-LT study protocol [18], rollover adolescents who did not achieve ≥ 50% improvement in Severity of Alopecia Tool (SALT) score by month 3 from baseline in ALLEGRO-2b/3 and all adolescents who did not achieve a SALT score of ≤ 20 by month 6 in ALLEGRO-LT were required to be discontinued from the study.

Outcomes

The safety outcomes investigated in this analysis included data up to ~ 5 years. These included adverse events (AEs), serious AEs (SAEs), AEs leading to discontinuation, AEs of special interest, Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) grade ≥ 2 hematological parameters, and selected laboratory test abnormalities. Serious infections were defined as any SAE in the infections and infestations system organ class. Major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE) was defined as a composite of death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke. Adjudication committees (composed of external experts who were blinded to treatment and not affiliated with the study sponsor) were established to provide objective assessment of opportunistic infections, malignancies, cardiovascular and thromboembolic events, and neurological and audiological events.

Statistical Analysis

Safety data were integrated from the four studies and summarized descriptively using counts and percentages for AEs and laboratory abnormalities in each group. Events were evaluated from the start of the patients’ first dose of ritlecitinib. In the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group, events were evaluated from the time a patient started the 50-mg dose (or the 200-mg loading dose), as some patients received placebo or other ritlecitinib doses before switching to ritlecitinib 50 mg. Study-size adjusted incidence rates (IRs) of AEs were reported per 100 patient-years (PYs) and are presented along with mid-p gamma confidence intervals. Interim results are subject to change as additional data are collected and analyzed in ALLEGRO-LT, which was ongoing at the time of this analysis.

Results

Patient Disposition and Baseline Characteristics

This analysis cohort comprised 1228 patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and an additional 66 patients (1294) in the any-ritlecitinib group. The analysis groups are not additive and are overlapping populations, with the any-ritlecitinib group including all patients receiving any dose of ritlecitinib. Patient disposition is described in Table 1. For the present data cutoff, 14.8% and 14.1% of patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and any-ritlecitinib group, respectively, were ongoing in the study. The most common reasons for discontinuation were withdrawal by patient (50-mg ± 200-mg group: 12.2%; any-ritlecitinib group: 12.6%) and lack of efficacy (50-mg ± 200-mg group: 9.5%; any-ritlecitinib group: 9.4%) (Table 1).

In the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group, 81 patients (6.6%) experienced 107 AEs that led to and were considered to be the primary reason for discontinuation from the study, and in the any-ritlecitinib group, 90 patients (7.0%) experienced 125 AEs that led to and were considered to be the primary reason for discontinuation (Table 1). The two most common system organ classes that included AEs considered to be the primary reason for discontinuation of the study drug were infections and infestations: (22 patients [1.7%] experienced 23 AEs in the any-ritlecitinib group) and investigations (abnormal laboratory test results; 18 patients [1.4%] experienced 22 AEs in the any-ritlecitinib group). Of the 125 AEs that were considered as the primary reason for discontinuation in the any-ritlecitinib group, 40 were SAEs, occurring in 36 patients.

Exposure to Study Drug

Median duration of exposure was 1197 days (range 1–1887 days) in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 1204 days (range 1–1891 days) in the any-ritlecitinib group. Maximum duration of exposure was 5.17 years (calculated assuming 1 year is equivalent to 365.25 days: 1887/365.25 days) in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 5.18 years (1891/365.25 days) in the any-ritlecitinib group. Total PYs of exposure were 3261.5 and 3539.5 in the 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively. In the 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively, 68.0% and 66.8% of patients had ≥ 24 months of exposure to ritlecitinib, and 58.7% and 56.6% had ≥ 36 months of exposure (Fig. 2).

Overall Safety

In the 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively, 1070 (87.1%) and 1158 patients (89.5%) experienced AEs and 84 (6.8%) and 88 patients (6.8%) experienced SAEs (Table 3). The five most frequent AEs (by preferred term) in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group were severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) test positive (19.7%), headache (18.8%), nasopharyngitis (14.3%), coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) (14.2%), and upper respiratory tract infection (13.6%). In the any-ritlecitinib group, the five most frequent AEs were headache (21.0%), SARS-CoV-2 test positive (19.3%), nasopharyngitis (16.4%), upper respiratory tract infection (15.0%), and COVID-19 (13.8%).

Adverse Events of Special Interest

Infections

Serious infections occurred in 1.6% of patients (IR: 0.61/100 PYs) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 1.7% (IR: 0.61/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group (Table 4). In the 50-mg ± 200-mg group, the following serious infections were reported: appendicitis (six patients), COVID-19 (two patients), COVID-19 pneumonia (two patients), and cellulitis, empyema, latent tuberculosis, osteomyelitis, peritonsillar abscess, pelvic abscess, pyelonephritis, sepsis, septic shock, urinary tract infection, and vulval abscess (one patient each). Some of these patients experienced more than one serious infection: one patient had empyema and sepsis, one had COVID-19 pneumonia and septic shock, and one had appendicitis and pelvic abscess. In the any-ritlecitinib group, serious infections included all those in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group, plus one event of appendicitis and one event of diverticulitis. All serious infections required the patient to discontinue per protocol; all events resolved and none were fatal. Herpes zoster occurred in 2.6% of patients (IR: 0.99/100 PYs) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and in 2.6% of patients (IR: 0.95/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group. Opportunistic infections (adjudicated) occurred in a total of three patients: 0.2% of patients (IR: 0.09/100 PYs) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 0.2% (IR: 0.08/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group. Since the previous data cutoff date, there were two patients with adjudicated opportunistic infection, both classified as multi-dermatomal herpes zoster (defined as involvement of nonadjacent or > 2 adjacent dermatomes, or disseminated herpes zoster). The one patient with adjudicated opportunistic infection reported in the prior data cutoff also had multi-dermatomal herpes zoster [19]. None of the cases were disseminated herpes zoster. Of the two new cases, one patient experienced a moderate event that was not related to the study treatment, the dose was not changed, and the patient recovered. The other patient experienced a mild event, the study drug was interrupted, and the patient recovered.

Dermatological Events

The most frequently reported dermatological AEs (> 5% in any treatment group) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups were acne (10.3% [4.08/100 PYs] and 11.6% [4.58/100 PYs], respectively), urticaria (7.3% [2.83/100 PYs] and 7.9% [3.00/100 PYs]), and folliculitis (6.2% [2.40/100 PYs] and 7.2% [2.70/100 PYs]).

Malignancies

There were nine events (0.7% of patients; IR: 0.27/100 PYs) adjudicated as malignancies (excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer [NMSC]) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and ten events (0.8% of patients; IR: 0.27/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group (Table 4). There were three new events of malignancy excluding NMSC since the previous data cutoff (two adjudicated as cervical dysplasia [assessed as not being related to treatment] and one as intraductal proliferative breast lesion [assessed as being related to treatment]) (Table S2). Specifically regarding the new events, two patients experienced moderately severe cervical dysplasia. One patient experienced a mild event of intraductal proliferative breast lesion, and the patient withdrew from the study. There were nine events (0.7% of patients; IR: 0.26/100 PYs) adjudicated as NMSC in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and nine events (0.7% of patients; IR: 0.25/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group. There were six new events of NMSC (five adjudicated as basal cell carcinoma and one as squamous cell carcinoma) since the prior data cut [19]. The five events of basal cell carcinoma were assessed by the investigator as not being related to the study treatment; four events were mild and one was moderate, and three events were considered related to sun exposure. The event of squamous cell carcinoma was moderate and assessed by the investigator as being related to sun exposure and related to the study treatment.

Cardiovascular Safety

There were seven events adjudicated as MACE, all of which occurred in the 50-mg ± 200-mg group (0.6% of patients, IR: 0.20/100 PYs; any-ritlecitinib group: 0.5% of patients, IR: 0.19/100 PYs) (Table 4). Since the previous data cutoff [19], four additional patients had cardiovascular events that were adjudicated as MACE (two patients had an acute myocardial infarction, one had a myocardial infarction, and one had a cerebrovascular accident) (Table S3). The two new events of acute myocardial infarction included one patient with a history of hypertension, obesity, and smoking for more than 30 years and one patient with a history of dyslipidemia, smoking, and alcohol use, and a maternal history of cardiac ischemic disease. Both events were considered not to be related to study treatment. Study treatment was discontinued for both patients. The patient with a new event of severe myocardial infarction had a history of hypercholesterolemia, stage 2 hypertension, and smoking (stopped > 20 years prior). The event was considered related to study treatment, and treatment was permanently discontinued. One patient (with a history of migraines) experienced a new event of moderate cerebrovascular accident (ischemic stroke), which was not considered to be related to treatment.

Neuroaudiological Events

In the ALLEGRO clinical trial program, routine audiological testing was implemented at prespecified timepoints to evaluate potential changes in hearing status. Treatment-emergent AEs reported as audiologic events of interest reflect both the outcome of the protocol-specified audiological testing and spontaneously reported treatment-emergent AEs related to hearing loss. Adjudicated sensorineural hearing loss was reported in 1.5% of patients (IR: 0.55/100 PYs) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and in 1.5% of patients (IR: 0.55/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group (Table 4); these events were all identified through protocol-specified audiological testing. Most audiological events were mild in severity and were without change to study treatment; none led to treatment discontinuation. There were no cases of central hearing disorder.

Adjudicated events of paresthesia and dysesthesia [19] occurred in 2.8% of patients (IR: 1.03/100 PYs) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and in 3.0% of patients (IR: 1.09/100 PYs) in the any-ritlecitinib group (Table 4). Adjudicated events of peripheral neuropathy were reported in 0.2% (IR: 0.09/100 PYs) and 0.3% (IR: 0.11/100 PYs) of patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively (Table 4).

Laboratory Assessments

Hematology and Chemistry

Ritlecitinib led to short-term changes in hematological parameters, which remained stable throughout long-term treatment (Fig. 3 [median levels over time] and Fig. S2 [median change from baseline over time]).

Median platelet levels decreased from baseline to week 4 and remained stable through month 48 (Fig. 3a and Fig. S2a). Among patients with CTCAE grade 0 or 1 platelet levels (≥ 75 × 103/mm3) at baseline, one patient (< 0.1%; in both the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups) had a grade 2 (50 to < 75.0 × 103/mm3) decrease in platelets (Table S4). There were no grade 3 or worse (< 50 × 103/mm3) decreases in platelets.

Median neutrophil counts remained stable through month 48 (Fig. 3b and Fig. S2b). In the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and any-ritlecitinib group, 63 patients (5.2%) and 76 patients (6.0%), respectively, had a worsening in neutrophils to grade 2 (1000 to < 1500/mm3); 13 patients (1.1%) and 14 patients (1.1%), respectively, had a worsening to grade ≥ 3 (< 1000/mm3) (Table S4).

There was a decrease in median lymphocyte count from baseline to week 4 and levels remained stable through month 48 (Fig. 3c and Fig. S2c). A total of 269 patients (22.1%) and 281 patients (21.9%) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively, had a worsening to grade 2 (500 to < 800/mm3) lymphocyte levels, and 42 (3.4%) and 44 (3.4%) had a worsening to grade ≥ 3 (< 500/mm3) (Table S4).

There was a slight decrease from baseline in median hemoglobin levels from baseline to week 4; levels then remained stable through month 48 (Fig. 3d and Fig. S2d). A total of 24 patients (2.0%) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 26 patients (2.0%) in the any-ritlecitinib group had a worsening in hemoglobin to grade 2 (8 to < 10 g/dL), and one patient (< 0.1%; in both the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups) had a worsening in hemoglobin to grade 3 (< 8 g/dL) (Table S4).

Median creatine kinase levels slightly increased from baseline to week 4 and then remained stable through month 48 (Fig. 3i and Fig. S2i). Three events of creatine kinase elevations (in three patients) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group were reported as AEs of rhabdomyolysis; all were considered by the investigators to be related to a recently started new exercise regimen or related to physical activity. All three events were mild, the study treatment was not interrupted, and the events resolved (Table S5).

Lipids

There were fluctuations in median low density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), total cholesterol, and triglyceride levels that overall remained stable through month 48 (Fig. 3 and Fig. S2). For LDL-C, 1.8% of patients in each group had a test with levels > 1.2× the upper limit of normal (ULN) (Table S4). In the ritlecitinib 50 mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively, 0.4% and 0.6% of patients experienced HDL-C levels < 0.8× the lower limit of normal (Table S4). Total cholesterol levels > 1.3× ULN occurred in 1.0% of patients in each group (Table S4). Triglyceride levels > 1.3× ULN occurred in 8.1% and 8.8% of patients in the ritlecitinib 50 mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, respectively (Table S4). AEs of hyperlipidemia were reported in 9 patients (0.7%) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and in 12 patients (0.9%) in the any-ritlecitinib group. Hypertriglyceridemia was reported in three patients (0.2%), all in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group.

Liver Function

Of patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and any-ritlecitinib group, 1.4% (17 patients) and 1.5% (19 patients), respectively, had an increase in aspartate aminotransferase (AST) to > 5–20× ULN and none had increases in AST to > 20× ULN (Table S4). Of the 19 patients in the any-ritlecitinib group who had increases in AST to > 5–20× ULN, 15 patients had increases that were > 5–10× ULN and 4 patients had increases that were > 10× ULN (ranging from 10.3× to 15.6× ULN).

Increases to > 5× ULN in alanine aminotransferase (ALT) occurred in < 1% of patients in each group (10 patients in ritlecitinib 50-mg ± 200-mg group and 12 in any-ritlecitinib group) and no patients had increases to ALT > 20× ULN. Of the 12 patients in the any-ritlecitinib group with increases in ALT to > 5–20× ULN, 9 patients had increases that were > 5–10× ULN and 3 patients had increases that were > 10× ULN (ranging from 10.8× to 18.7× ULN).

Increases in bilirubin levels to > 1.5–3× ULN were reported in 2.4% of patients each in the ritlecitinib 50 mg ± 200-mg and any-ritlecitinib groups, and no patients had an increase in bilirubin to > 3× ULN. No hepatic laboratory changes associated with potential drug-induced liver injury or Hy’s Law have been reported in the ritlecitinib studies.

Discussion

This report describes an updated long-term integrated safety analysis of ritlecitinib in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA. Safety data pooled from four ALLEGRO clinical trials, encompassing 1294 patients and 3540 PYs of exposure, were consistent with the previously characterized overall safety profile observed across the ALLEGRO clinical trial program [19]. This integrated safety analysis was based on the longest duration of ritlecitinib treatment to be reported to date (up to ~ 5 years).

The IRs for serious infections, opportunistic infections, herpes zoster, malignancies, cardiovascular events, and neuroaudiological events over long-term exposure up to ~ 5 years were consistent with those previously reported in patients who received ritlecitinib for up to 2 years [19]. Overall, the IRs observed in this analysis for patients with AA treated with any dose of ritlecitinib were generally comparable to those reported in observational studies of individuals with AA. The IR for serious infection in the any-ritlecitinib group was 0.61/100 PYs, versus 1.85 and 2.21/100 PYs reported in a US claims database and a Danish population-based cohort, respectively [20, 21]. The IR for herpes zoster was 0.95/100 PYs in the any-ritlecitinib group, versus 0.4–1.32/100 PYs across the US claims database, the Danish cohort, a UK-based cohort, and a Japanese claims database [20–23]. For malignancies excluding NMSC, the IR was 0.27/100 PYs in this study, versus 0.23–1.25/100 PY in the US, Danish, and Japanese studies [20, 21, 23]. The IR for MACE was 0.19/100 PYs in the any-ritlecitinib group, versus 0.17–1.60/100 PYs across the same external cohorts [20, 21, 23]. It should be recognized that because of differences in study design and populations these claims-based and medical record-based data cannot be directly compared with clinical trial findings.

A recent study examining short-term malignancy risk in patients with severe AA treated with JAK inhibitors (mean follow-up of 1229 days) reported no link between treatment and increased malignancy risk [24], however, longer-term real-world data will be essential to fully evaluate malignancy risk, given that JAK inhibitor treatment for AA has only recently become clinically available. In addition, given the potentially long latency period of some malignancies, continued long-term follow up of patients receiving ritlecitinib, including through real-world studies, will be important to further characterize malignancy risk.

With regard to venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk, recent evidence indicates there was no significant increase in VTE among individuals with AA compared with those without AA in the US (OR 1.549, 95% CR 0.862, 2.783) [25]. As the availability of JAK-targeting therapies expands for AA and other immune-mediated inflammatory skin diseases, additional studies will help further elucidate potential safety concerns. Meta-analyses of randomized clinical trials assessing short-term (< 5 months) JAK inhibitor treatment for dermatologic indications have not identified a significantly increased risk of VTE or MACE [26, 27]. The low number of events for VTE and MACE in the present study is reassuring; long-term data are needed to fully characterize cardiovascular risk, as such rare outcomes typically require longer observation to generate more robust incidence estimates.

In nonclinical chronic toxicology studies in dogs, reversible axonal dystrophy (swelling) was observed in the central nervous system (cerebellum) and peripheral nervous system at ritlecitinib exposures ≥ 7.4 and ≥ 14 times the approved human dose (50 mg), with reversible hearing loss observed at higher exposures (33 times the approved human dose) [data on file]. Due to these nonclinical results, standard and specialized neurological and audiological safety evaluations and event adjudication were conducted across the ALLEGRO studies. Notably, the ALLEGRO studies found no association between ritlecitinib treatment and incidence of neurological and audiological events in humans. Furthermore, in the ALLEGRO-2a safety study of adults with AA treated with ritlecitinib 200 mg QD for 4 weeks followed by ritlecitinib 50 mg QD for 9 months, there were no effects on nerve fiber counts or swelling of intraepidermal axons or integrity of the auditory pathway in the human brainstem, as assessed by brainstem auditory evoked potential [28]. In the long-term clinical data presented in this study, the evaluation of events adjudicated as potential neurological or audiological events of interest showed that the IRs for neuroaudiological events did not increase over time with prolonged ritlecitinib treatment since the prior data cut [19]. Most hearing changes were not reported by the participant but discovered only through this routine testing. Overall, the clinical data continue to support a lack of association of ritlecitinib treatment with adverse neurological or audiological events of interest.

Laboratory measures for platelets, lymphocytes, and hemoglobin decreased from baseline to week 4 and remained stable, while other laboratory measures remained stable over time. There were no new long-term changes in laboratory parameters in this analysis of ritlecitinib up to ~ 5 years compared with those previously reported for ritlecitinib [19]. However, patient numbers decreased substantially at later timepoints; these later assessments should therefore be interpreted with caution. During the study, increases in hematological parameters to CTCAE grade ≥ 3 were infrequent. Median lipid levels, including LDL-C, HDL-C, total cholesterol, and triglycerides showed some fluctuations but overall remained stable through 48 months. Interpretation of long-term lipid values is limited by the known natural history of cholesterol, age-related changes, lifestyle, and potential initiation of lipid-lowering therapies during the study. Hyperlipidemia and dyslipidemia occurred in < 1% of patients treated with ritlecitinib.

This analysis is subject to limitations inherent to long-term open-label studies. The absence of a control group limits the ability to distinguish treatment-related events from those associated with the underlying disease, background population risk, or age of the study cohort. Comparisons with observational AA cohorts should be interpreted with caution given differences in patient selection, baseline comorbidities, and study design. It should also be noted that patients with the longest exposures are likely those who tolerated study treatment and remained in the study. Consequently, the long-term safety profile observed among patients with extended treatment durations may be subject to attrition bias, as patients who discontinued treatment because of adverse events or other reasons are no longer included in later periods of follow-up. However, the overall rate of permanent discontinuation from the study (44.7% in the any-ritlecitinib group) is comparable to rates reported for other advanced systemic therapies in AA with long-term use [29]. In addition, as with any clinical trial, potential bias may have been introduced through the inclusion and exclusion criteria, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to the broader patient population.

Conclusions

We report integrated safety data from multiple trials in AA for the longest duration of ritlecitinib treatment to date. Findings from this integrated safety analysis of ritlecitinib in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA resulted in no new safety risks over a period of up to ~ 5 years and 3539.5 PYs of exposure, and the safety profile was consistent with previously reported data. These findings support the safety of ritlecitinib, a JAK3/TEC family kinase inhibitor, for long-term management of AA, within the limitations of the integrated clinical trial population and study designs.

Regional carbon emission reduction potential estimation based on scenario analysis under emission standards
Authors: Xuwei Xia, Zhibin Yan, Shuang Zhang, Dongge Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The estimation of carbon emission reduction potential in existing regions often faces the problem of missing data, so scenario analysis based estimation research is carried out. Under the constraints of emission standards, three emission limit scenarios are set: maintaining, ultra-low, and tightening. Based on the SBM model, a carbon emission reduction potential index model is constructed using the full factor carbon emission efficiency measurement method. Build a model that considers the impact of industrial output value and estimate carbon emission rights from 2018 to 2030. After analysis and calculation of allocation weights, experiments show that carbon emission performance is less than 0.05, efficiency is improved, weight is about 4.64%, and industrial carbon emissions contribute nearly zero.

Introduction

Under global climate change, greenhouse gas emissions such as carbon dioxide have attracted attention, and the acceleration of industrialization and urbanization has led to an increase in their emissions and exacerbated warming. Reducing carbon emissions and developing a low-carbon economy has become a global consensus and a top priority for all countries [1, 2]. Regional carbon emissions and emission reduction potential are related to climate governance. Different regions have different carbon emission characteristics and emission reduction potential due to differences in industrial structure. Based on scenario analysis, calculating regional carbon emission reduction potential can provide guidance for differentiated policy formulation.

Bai et al. [3] based on regional technological differences, the parameter element frontier method is used to analyze the carbon emission performance and potential of China's transportation industry, divided into two aspects: eliminating management inefficiencies and bridging technological gaps. It is estimated that this industry can reduce emissions by 12.3 million tons, accounting for 8.4% of the annual emissions. Shandong and other eastern regions have great potential, while Qinghai and other regions have smaller potential. The main reasons for emission reduction are narrowing the technological gap (accounting for 57.5%) and eliminating inefficient management (accounting for 42.5%), with emphasis on management in the east and technology in the central and western regions. Based on this, emission reduction strategies are proposed for each province.

Zhou et al. [4] using the research and development input–output Bayesian neural network (IO-BNN) model to simulate carbon emissions in Guangdong's economic sector and plan emission reduction measures, a multi scenario evaluation of the impact of environmental policies on the economy and energy system is constructed to clarify the direction, peak period, and emission reduction potential of carbon emissions from 2021 to 2050. The results show that industries such as equipment manufacturing, construction, transportation and warehousing, as well as per capita and primary energy consumption, have a significant impact on Guangdong's carbon emissions and economic development; Under different policies, carbon emissions may peak between 2025 and 2035 and continue to decline by 2050; In the optimal scenario, optimizing the industrial structure and reducing primary energy consumption can help Guangdong reach its peak by 2025.

Yang et al. [5] based on the principle of dynamic material flow and urban building energy model, a predictive model is constructed using scenario analysis to estimate the dynamic changes related to urban buildings before 2060. Taking Changsha as an example to study five scenarios, it was found that measures such as reducing electricity emissions can be implemented separately by 2060. Energy conservation and emission reduction can reduce emissions by 47.90% and peak by 2030. Achieving carbon neutrality requires collaboration among multiple departments.

Zhang et al. [6] using multi-source heterogeneous geographic spatial data and machine learning regression analysis, it was found that the total rooftop area of 354 cities in China reached 65,962 square kilometers in 2020, which can reduce 4 billion tons of carbon emissions under ideal conditions. Considering urban land expansion and power structure transformation, it is expected that carbon emissions will peak by 2030, with a potential reduction of 3–4 billion tons. Currently, the proportion of rooftop photovoltaic development in most cities is less than 1%. This study uses geographical endowment analysis to assist policy formulation, providing key basis for the precise layout of rooftop photovoltaics in China, and also constructing a methodological system that can be internationally promoted.

Therefore, this article proposes a scenario analysis based method for estimating regional carbon emission reduction potential, which can more accurately evaluate the emission reduction potential under the constraints of emission standards. Research and innovate the construction of a multi factor indicator model, use scenario analysis to comprehensively evaluate, and provide scientific basis for policy formulation through reasonable weight allocation and scenario simulation. Compared to existing methods, scenario analysis can construct diverse future scenarios, analyze the impact of policies, technologies, and other factors on carbon emissions, evaluate the emission reduction potential of each scenario, take into account uncertainty when estimating, and provide decision-makers with multiple emission reduction paths. In summary, scenario analysis based on emission standards is of great significance for estimating regional carbon reduction potential, which is conducive to addressing climate change, promoting low-carbon economy, and promoting regional economic and ecological sustainable development.

Regional carbon emission reduction potential index model constrained by emission standards

Average carbon emission performance under emission standards

Carbon emission performance is a key indicator for measuring the effectiveness of air pollution control in the thermal power industry, directly reflecting policy constraints and the level of pollution control technology of enterprises.

The post-2020 emission requirements are analyzed under three standard constraint scenarios, as follows:(1) Constraint scenario 1: maintain the mode, that is, maintain the status quo in 2020. If thermal power generation is predicted to reach its peak during the forecast period, priority should be given to reducing the power generation of units that do not meet the strictest emission standards in this scenario.(2) Constraint scenario 2: ultra-low mode, continuously promote ultra-low emission standards and set them as national benchmarks in stages. After reaching peak power generation, priority should be given to shutting down units that do not meet the strictest emission standards. Key measures: (1) By 2025, over 70% of the units that were not retrofitted during the 13th Five Year Plan period will be shut down or upgraded; (2) By 2030, the national thermal power units will achieve ultra-low emissions, with emission concentrations of smoke, sulfur dioxide, and nitrogen oxides not exceeding 10, 35, and 50 mg·m−3, respectively. The emission performance will be about 0.03, 0.01, and 0.15 g·(kW·h)−1. Units that meet ultra-low or better standards before 2020 will maintain this standard.(3) Constraint scenario 3: strict mode focuses on strengthening emissions reduction. Based on the ultra-low emission standards for coal-fired power generation units in Central China in 2017 and the achievable level of 80% of units, the country plans to gradually tighten the emission standards for flue gas, sulfur dioxide, and nitrogen oxides to 5, 25, and 40 mg per cubic meter, respectively, by 2030. Units that do not meet the standards will be prioritized for elimination. The implementation milestones are: (1) by 2025, the concentration of the above-mentioned pollutants in thermal power units nationwide will not exceed 10, 35, and 50 mg·m−3, with emission performance of about 0.03, 0.11, and 0.15 g·(kW·h)−1; (2) by 2030, the limit values will be further reduced to 5, 25, and 40 mg·m 3, with emission performance of about 0.015, 0.075, and0.130 g·(kW·h)−1 [7].

Calculation and realization of regional carbon emission reduction potential based on scenario analysis

Prediction model of regional carbon emission reduction potential

This article focuses on direct emission reduction and low-carbon technologies in the power industry, classified according to five emission reduction mechanisms. One of them is clean energy generation with low lifecycle carbon emissions (including hydropower, nuclear energy, etc.) [1]; Secondly, efficient thermal power generation technologies (such as ultra supercritical) can reduce fuel consumption; Thirdly, carbon capture and storage (CCS); The fourth is operational optimization, such as reducing start stop cycles and lowering auxiliary power; The fifth measure is the efficiency of the power grid, such as the automation of superconducting transmission and distribution [18]. Experimental results and analysis

Preparation for experiment

To validate the method of estimating regional carbon reduction potential through emission standard constrained scenario analysis, we conducted a comparative evaluation of the methods in references [3–6]. The experiment uses Windows Server as the operating system, MySQL to manage the database, Vensim for scene modeling, and high-performance servers to ensure stable processing of big data and complex calculations. Conclusion

This design uses scenario analysis method to calculate the regional carbon reduction potential under emission standards: first, calculate the shadow price of carbon dioxide using the unexpected output super efficiency SBM dual model, and then evaluate the potential; We also comprehensively analyze the carbon reduction potential under different technologies and policies by establishing technology freezing and planning scenarios. This method simulates the emission reduction effects under different conditions, providing multiple references for policy-making. However, scenario analysis is limited by space and resources, and can only set limited scenarios, so the results may not be optimal. To this end, it is necessary to increase the number and diversity of scenarios, construct a more comprehensive framework, strengthen factor quantification and uncertainty analysis, and enhance scientific practicality. In addition, interdisciplinary methods such as economics, society, and environment are introduced to deeply explore complex issues in scenario analysis.

For regions with different industrial structures, carbon emission prediction model parameters can be adjusted according to actual conditions; Redefine the relationship between energy and carbon emissions in regions with significant differences in energy structure to optimize the model; When allocating carbon emission rights, adjust the weights based on economic level and emission reduction capacity, providing reference for carbon reduction research in multiple regions.

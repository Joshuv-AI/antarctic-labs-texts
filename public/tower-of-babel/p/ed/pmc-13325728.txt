Device-measured physical activity and cardiovascular risk among cardiomyopathy variant carriers in the all of us research program
Authors: Amrita Nayak, Akhil Pampana, Harshvir Singh Bal, Sahaj A. Patel, Ezinne Tiffany George, Nehal Vekariya, Peng Li, Garima Arora, Pankaj Arora
Published: 2026

Background

Pathogenic/likely pathogenic (P/LP) variants in cardiomyopathy (CMP) genes are known to increase the risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes [1,2]. Health behaviors that reduce cardiovascular risk, including physical activity, may help attenuate this genetic risk.

With the increasing availability of objectively measured accelerometry data from large population cohorts, it is now possible to assess the interplay between physical activity and genetic susceptibility at scale. However, evidence examining whether device-measured physical activity modifies cardiovascular risk among CMP variant carriers remains limited, particularly in United States-based cohorts.

Data from the All of Us Research Program (AoURP) were used to examine the association between moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) and incident adverse cardiovascular outcomes, including heart failure (HF), arrhythmia, stroke, coronary artery disease (CAD), and incident CMP.

Methods

This study included participants enrolled in the AoURP between 2016 and 2023, who were aged 18 years or above. The AoURP (C2024Q3R3, Curated Data Repository Version 8) provides access to a large multiethnic population with detailed demographic, clinical, and genotyping information, and device-based physical activity data [3]. Consenting participants complete questionnaires on demographic details, undergo physical examination and biospecimen collection at a facility, and agree to provide access to their electronic health record (EHR) data. Participants with prevalent cardiovascular disease at baseline, including HF, arrhythmias, stroke, CAD, and CMP, were excluded.

Physical activity measurement

Participants were included if their Fitbit data started on or after January 1st, 2016, and data up to the last censored date (October 1st, 2023) were collected. Individuals with at least 1 month of valid Fitbit activity data, defined as ≥10 h of wear per day, >100 and <45,000 steps/day, and a valid week defined as ≥4 valid days, were included. An individual’s minutes of MVPA per week were derived from “fairly active” + “very active” minutes for each day, which correspond to 3–6 METS in 10-minute intervals and ≥6 METS in 10-minute intervals, respectively [4]. For each individual, average weekly MVPA was calculated using all available data up to a 1-year window prior to the start of follow-up. To maintain temporality and minimize reverse causality, the follow-up period for incident outcomes started only after the completion of each participant’s MVPA assessment period. Additionally, events occurring within 30 days of the exposure window were excluded. Sensitivity analyses using a 6-month and 12-month censoring windows showed consistent results.

Genetic variant exposure

Individuals with P/LP variants identified using ClinVar with a ≥ 2-star level of evidence, or variants with functional pathogenicity ascertained for CMPs (Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM), Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM), Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (ARVC)) based on ClinGen level of evidence were included in this study. A detailed explanation of the method used is described elsewhere [2].

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was defined as the first incident occurrence of any of the following cardiovascular events: HF, arrhythmias, CMP, stroke, or a composite of CAD, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), or myocardial infarction (MI). International Classification of Diseases (ICD)-9, ICD-10, and procedural codes from EHR data were used to identify events, and detailed codes can be found elsewhere [2,5]. For individuals with an event, the outcome date was defined as the earliest recorded occurrence among the composite outcomes, and individuals without an outcome event defined by ICD or procedural codes were censored at the last censoring date (October 1st, 2023).

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized according to genetic carrier status and MVPA levels stratified as a binary threshold according to WHO recommendations for physical activity (<150 min/week as “inactive” versus ≥150 min/week as “active”). Continuous variables were reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), and categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages (n, %).

To assess the risk of composite outcomes by genetic carrier status and MVPA levels, Cox proportional hazards models were constructed in three stages: (a) unadjusted, (b) minimally adjusted (for age, sex, and 10 principal components (PCs), and (c) fully adjusted (adding diabetes, hypertension, obesity, and smoking status). These models were assessed for violation of the proportional hazards assumption using the cox.zph() function in R’s Survival Package, and this was verified by Schoenfeld residuals (global P = 0.19).

Cox models using the coxph() function estimated the risk of outcomes across four carrier-MVPA predictor categories (with active non-carriers as reference), displayed as forest plots. As a secondary exploratory analysis to assess MVPA levels with maximal risk reduction, the association between device-measured MVPA and incident composite outcomes was modeled using restricted cubic splines with 3 knots placed at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of the MVPA distribution, adjusted for age, sex, 10 PCs, diabetes, hypertension, obesity, and smoking status. The best parsimonious fit based on the lowest Akaike Information Criterion (AIC: 8329.3) identified a knee point at 352.4 min/week and an inflection point at 307.3 min/week, which represents maximal risk reduction. (Fig. 1, Panel C) Non-linearity was formally assessed using a Wald test. Using this approach, the individuals were re-categorized as “active” if average MVPA minutes/week ≥307 and “inactive” if average MVPA minutes/week <307, and analyses were repeated [6].

All statistical analyses were conducted using R statistical software (Version 4.5.2). A two-sided p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The final cohort had 15,641 individuals, with a median age of 54 (IQR 40–67) years; 10,920 (69.8 %) were female, and 10,629 (68 %) were non-Hispanic White. 831 (5.3 %) individuals were identified as carriers of CMP variants, which aligns with previous literature [6]. Average MVPA in the overall cohort was 192.4 min/week, comparable between carriers (median 77.1 min/week in the <150 group, considered inactive, and 303.3 min/week in the ≥150 group, considered active) and non-carriers (73.6 min/week and 304.8 min/week, respectively). (Fig. 1, Panel A)

Event rates and incidence rates per 1000 person-years

On examining the number of composite outcomes over a mean follow-up of 2.1 years, there were 32 (3.9 %) and 468 (3.2 %) events among CMP carriers and non-carriers, respectively. For the primary analysis, inactive individuals were defined as those with <150 min/week of MVPA and active individuals as ≥150 min/week of MVPA, categorized as a binary MVPA threshold according to WHO guidelines.

The incidence rate of composite outcomes was highest amongst inactive carriers (18.21; 95 % CI 9.96–30.55) per 1000 person-years), decreased to 15.43 (95 %CI 9.15–24.39) per 1000 person-years in active carriers, and further lowered to 15.1 (95 %CI 13.06–17.38) and 11.93 (95 %CI 10.55–13.43) per 1000 person-years in inactive and active non-carriers, respectively.

Hazard ratios for composite outcomes

After adjusting for age, sex and 10 PCs in Cox models, the hazard ratio for development of a composite outcome was highest amongst inactive carriers (HRadj: 1.91; 95 %CI 1.11–3.28), followed by active carriers (HRadj: 1.36; 95 %CI 0.84–2.19) and inactive non-carriers (HRadj: 1.48; 95 %CI 1.22–1.79), compared with active non-carriers as the reference group. In fully adjusted models, the inactive carriers had the highest risk of composite outcomes (HRadj: 1.64; 95 %CI 0.95–2.83), followed by active carriers (HRadj: 1.41; 95 %CI 0.87–2.27), and lowest in inactive non-carriers (HRadj: 1.29; 95 %CI 1.06–1.57), with active non-carriers as reference. (Fig. 1, Panel B) A formal interaction test between carrier status and MVPA was not statistically significant (Pinteraction = 0.45), likely due to limited power to statistically confirm the effect modification observed visually.

In secondary analyses, a significant non-linear relationship between MVPA and the composite outcome was observed (Wald X2 = 15.12, Pnon-linearity = 0.0001). Using the spline-derived inflection point of 307 min/week of MVPA associated with minimal composite outcome risk (Fig. 1, Panel C), we divided the participants again into inactive (<307 min/week) or active (≥307 min/week). In minimally adjusted Cox-models, the highest risk of composite outcomes was observed in inactive carriers (HRadj: 1.83; 95 %CI 1.19–2.84), followed by active carriers (HRadj: 1.11; 95 %CI 0.54–2.26) and inactive non-carriers (HRadj: 1.27; 95 %CI 1.04–1.56), with active non-carriers as the reference. In fully adjusted Cox models, the highest risk was seen in the inactive carriers (HRadj: 1.63; 95 %CI 1.05–2.52), and risk attenuation was observed in active carriers (HRadj: 1.15; 95 %CI 0.56–2.34) and inactive non-carriers (HRadj: 1.12; 95 %CI 0.91–1.38) compared to active non-carriers as the reference group. (Fig. 1, Panel B) The fully adjusted models in both primary and secondary analyses include obesity, diabetes, and hypertension, which may be acting as mediators rather than confounders, and hence may lead to attenuated estimates of the association between MVPA and composite outcomes.

Discussion

Among the participants in this study, higher MVPA was associated with lower crude rates of composite adverse cardiovascular events per 1000 person-years amongst both carriers and non-carriers. Inactive carriers had the highest crude event rates, and active non-carriers had the lowest rates. Minimally adjusted analyses showed the highest point estimates for risk of composite events in inactive carriers compared with active non-carriers as reference across both primary and secondary MVPA thresholds, while fully adjusted models showed consistent trends but were attenuated, likely due to the inclusion of metabolic mediators. While active carriers had lower point estimates for risk compared with inactive carriers, point estimates also suggest a potential residual risk among active carriers compared with active non-carriers. However, the overlapping confidence intervals and small event counts preclude a definitive conclusion regarding these risk estimates or the independent contribution of genetic susceptibility. These observed trends should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and need validation in larger cohorts.

Previous studies suggest that exercise likely exerts cardioprotective effects through improved cardiac remodeling, metabolic efficiency, mitochondrial function, reduced inflammation and fibrosis, and release of “exercrines” (FGF21, BAIBA, irisin), which potentially lead to favorable epigenetic modulation of gene expression, modulating endothelial function and oxidative stress [[7], [8], [9]]. Animal models demonstrate the cardioprotective effect of exercise on protection from pathological cardiac hypertrophy under stress overload via upregulation of miR-574–3p, which inhibits antimyocardial inflammation by targeting the IL-6/JAK/STAT pathway [7]. Molecular mechanisms studied in transgenic mice models of genetic cardiomyopathy suggest that exercise initiated before the onset of the phenotype is associated with downregulated hypertrophic signaling pathways and reduced proapoptotic signals. It also prevents myocardial disarray and fibrotic changes, which may favorably modify the arrhythmogenic substrate. However, these benefits were not present if exercise was initiated after the development of the phenotype [[10], [11], [12], [13]]. A prior AoU Fitbit data analysis found a dose-responsive atrial fibrillation risk reduction (8 % risk reduction with every hour of weekly MVPA), including among those with a higher genetic risk of atrial fibrillation, which parallels the possible carrier-specific MVPA benefits we observed [4].

Current guidelines for CMP variant carriers who are genotype-positive but phenotype-negative emphasize that recommendations on exercise should be tailored according to the type of CMP, and regular surveillance is advised [14]. Our findings suggest that these carriers may derive benefit from optimal MVPA and may be associated with lower rates of adverse cardiovascular events. Future trials should use these exploratory findings to define optimal MVPA dosing, including intensity and frequency.

The strengths of this study include objective Fitbit-derived MVPA in a multi-ancestry cohort, ClinVar curated variants for CMP genes, and incident event ascertainment after excluding prevalent disease.

This study has several limitations, including low outcome counts in carriers, which limit precision for our estimates, EHR ICD-based outcomes, which are prone to misclassification, and residual confounding by unmeasured factors. There can be potential selection bias due to predominantly White, female, and healthy Fitbit users, which may limit generalizability to the general population. While this study focuses on genotypically-positive, phenotypically-negative individuals with CMP variants, it does not address the risk of high-intensity exercise or competitive sport participation in certain high-risk genotypes, such as PKP2 or LMNA carriers, where exercise may lead to disease progression and arrhythmias [13].

Conclusion

Higher physical activity levels in CMP variant carriers were associated with a lower incidence of adverse cardiovascular outcomes. This highlights the importance of health behaviors in modifying risk within this vulnerable population. These hypothesis-generating observations warrant confirmation in prospective interventional studies.

The incidence of nonampullary duodenal cancer in Japan 2016–2020: analysis of a national cancer registry
Authors: Naomi Kakushima, Daisuke Ohki, Yuko Miura, Mitsuhiro Fujishiro, Masayuki Sho, The Japan Duodenal Cancer Guideline Committee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimEpidemiological research is scarce in Asian countries including Japan. We aimed to clarify the epidemiological trend of nonampullary duodenal cancer (NADC) in Japan using a national database.MethodsAll patients with NADC diagnosed between 2016 and 2020 were identified from the Japanese national cancer registry. The crude and age‐adjusted incidences were determined and temporal trends including diagnostic mode, tumor stage, and treatments were analyzed.ResultsA total of 14 484 patients were included. The crude and age‐adjusted incidences were 21.8–23.5 and 10.7–12.1 per 1 000 000 person‐years. Male‐to‐female ratio was 1.7:1, and the incidence increased with age. The number of patients was highest among 70–79 year age group in both sexes. In total, 55% of patients were diagnosed during screening or surveillance for other diseases. In 54% of patients, the tumor stage was localized. The major treatment was surgery (32%), followed by endoscopic resection (27%). The trends for detection mode, tumor stage, and treatment were consistent during 2016–2020.ConclusionsThis study clarified and confirmed the high incidence of duodenal cancer in Japan. Characteristically, many NADC cases are diagnosed asymptomatically and treated by endoscopic or surgical treatment.

Introduction

Cancer of the small bowel is the least common and rare of all gastrointestinal cancers, among which duodenal cancer (DC) is the most common. The incidence of DC in Western countries is reported around 2.9–5.1 per million. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 While the incidence of cancers of the jejunum and ileum remains unchanged, the incidence of DC is gradually increasing. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 Most patients with DC are diagnosed at an advanced stage; thus, the prognosis of DC is poor. 3 , 4 , 5 However, due to its rarity, diagnostic methods, surgical procedures, and chemotherapy have not been standardized. Therefore, in order to clarify the actual clinical situation of DC in Japan, the world's first DC treatment guideline 2021 was created in collaboration with physicians of various backgrounds including oncologist, surgery, endoscopist, radiologist, and pathologist. 6

In Japan as well as in Asia, there had been no epidemiological research on DC, so the incidence and trends were unknown. In the process of compiling the duodenal cancer treatment guideline 2021, the incidence of nonampullary duodenal cancer (NADC) was analyzed for the first time using data from the national cancer registry that started in Japan in 2016. 7 As a result, the crude incidence of NADC in Japan was reported as 23.7 per million, 8 which was very high compared to those in Western countries. Possible reasons may include racial factors, the widespread usage of endoscopic examinations, or differences in cancer diagnosis between Japan and Western countries; however, the exact reason is not known. Five years have passed from the first analysis; thus, we once again analyzed data from 2016 to 2020 in order to clarify the epidemiological trend of DC in Japan.

Methods

Patients with newly diagnosed primary NADC from January 1, 2016, to December 31, 2020, registered in the Japanese national cancer registry 7 were included in this study. Patients were selected according to the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems 10th Revision, 9 topography code C17.0 with International Classification of Disease for Oncology 3rd edition, 10 morphology codes 8000–8576/behavior recode 3 (malignant). Malignant neoplasms of the Vater's ampulla (topography code C24.1), malignant lymphoma (morphology code 9590–9600), sarcomas (morphology code 8800–8896), and stromal tumors (morphology code 8930–8990) were not included in this study. Retrieved data included age in 5‐year groups (AGE5P), sex, detection mode (screening, incidental, symptomatic, or autopsy/unspecified), tumor stage (localized, regional lymph node metastasis [LNM], invasion to adjacent structure, distant metastasis, or unspecified), and treatment method (endoscopic resection, surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, or unspecified). Regarding the detection mode, screening detection included cases detected by health checkup or cancer screening for gastric cancer; and incidental detection was defined when diagnosis occurred during follow‐up or surveillance for other diseases.

The crude incidence of NADC was calculated as a number of patients with DC divided by the total number of individuals in the Japanese population in 2016–2020. Age‐adjusted incidence was calculated for 10‐year strata (except for 0–19 years and 90 years) of the study period using the population of Japan in 2015 as reference. This study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the research ethics committee of The University of Tokyo (2023243NI). The need for obtaining informed consent from each patient was waived via the opt‐out method on the website of The University of Tokyo.

Results

A total of 14 484 patients were retrieved according to the inclusion criteria, including 2417 (16.7%) patients with neuroendocrine tumors (morphology codes 8150, 8153, 8154, 8156, 8240, 8246, and 8249). The crude incidence of NADC per 1 000 000 person‐years was 21.8–23.5. (Table 1). Age‐specific incidence was highest among age groups of 80–89 years. Age‐adjusted incidence was 10.7–12.1 per 1 000 000 person‐years. Male‐to‐female ratio was 1.7:1 (Table 2). The number of patients was highest among 70–79 years age group in both sexes (Fig. 1). A gradual increase in number of patients was observed in ages 70 years or older from 2016 to 2020. More than half of the patients were diagnosed during screening (13.6%) or incidentally (42.1%). The trend for detection mode was consistent during 2016–2020 (Fig. 2). Tumor stages were localized in 53.7% of patients, regional LNM in 6.5%, invasion to adjacent structures in 9.9%, and distant metastasis in 16.4%. The trend for tumor stages was consistent during 2016–2020 (Fig. 3). As for treatment, the majority was surgery including laparoscopic surgery (32.0%), followed by endoscopic resection (26.7%). The trend for treatment was consistent during 2016–2020 (Fig. 4).

Discussion

This study was the first analysis of the epidemiological trend of NADC in Japan. Using the national data covering 5 years, the age‐adjusted incidence was 10.7–12.1 per 1 000 000 person‐years, which was consistently higher than those reported as 2.9–3.7 in the United States and 2.9–4.3 in European countries. 8 This study confirmed the high incidence of NADC in Japan reported by the first single‐year analysis of 2016. 8

The analysis for 2016 in this study revealed 2850 newly diagnosed NADC patients with a crude incidence of 22.5 per 1 000 000 person‐years, which was slightly different from 3005 patients reported previously. 8 Firstly, the national cancer registry data undergo data correction based on annual confirmation. Since the national cancer registration started from 2016, the data of 2016 have gone through the correction process; thus, the accuracy of the present data is higher than the previous report. Secondly, this study included DC with a behavior recode 3 (malignant) during the selection process, thus carcinoma in situ was not included. The previous study included 6% of cases with carcinoma in situ. Therefore, the present study may be comparable to Western studies, where pathologists make a diagnosis of cancer when there is histological evidence of invasion into the lamina propria or beyond the submucosa. 11

More than half (55.7%) of NADC patients were incidentally diagnosed through screening or surveillance examination. This may be a good reason that the major stage was localized (53.7%) in this study. Patients in which the detection mode was symptomatic are associated with advanced stages. 8 , 12 The prognosis of NADC is related to the depth of tumor invasion and the presence of LNM 13 ; thus, early detection and diagnosis is important to achieve better prognosis.

Recently, the number of patients diagnosed of gastric cancer, esophageal cancer, and head‐and‐neck cancer in Japan are 135 000, 26 000, and 28 000 persons per year. 14 In Japan, esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) is widespread for gastric cancer screening. Therefore, observation of not only the stomach but also the esophagus and duodenum would detect asymptomatic cancer during screening EGD. A recent multicenter study reported that a systematic photodocumentation of the first and second portion of the duodenum resulted in an increase of detection of duodenal neoplasms. 15 Furthermore, a retrospective multicenter study reported that most precancerous lesions and early cancer were diagnosed in the first or second portion of the duodenum. 16 Thus, it would be reasonable to evaluate the duodenum up to the second portion as a routine during screening or surveillance EGD.

Regarding treatments, 26.7% of NADC underwent endoscopic resection. Favorable outcomes of endoscopic resection of precancerous lesions and early‐stage NADC have been reported. 17 , 18 , 19 Considering the invasiveness of surgical resection for NADC, as well as the increasing incidence with age, it is important to diagnose NADC in an early stage resectable by endoscopic resection to achieve better postoperative quality of life.

This study has some limitations. First, this study included only 5 years. A gradual increase in incidence of NADC has been reported in Western countries. However, a slight decrease in the number of patients in 2020 was observed in this study. The majority of DC patients in Japan are detected through endoscopic screening or surveillance for other diseases. Therefore, the slight decrease may be influenced by the cancelation of most endoscopic examinations during the COVID‐19 pandemic, which started from 2020. Further data accumulation is needed whether a similar trend is observed in Japan. Second, we did not include prognosis data among NADC patients. The national cancer registry started in 2016, and it was only possible to analyze data for cases diagnosed by 2020, so data on long‐term prognosis will be analyzed in further studies.

In conclusion, the present study clarified and confirmed the high incidence of duodenal cancer in Japan. Characteristically, many NADC cases are diagnosed asymptomatically and treated by endoscopic or surgical treatment.

Heat transfer analysis of non-Newtonian hybrid nanofluid flow over a stretching surface using an artificial neural network
Authors: Gunisetty Ramasekhar, N. Bala Kishore, C. Rekha, B. V. S. N. Lakshmi, A. Divya, Maddina Dinesh Kumar, Nehad Ali Shah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study investigates the role of Gold-Fe2O3-Fe3O4 nanoparticles mixed in blood with magnetohydrodynamics Casson fluid flow through a porous stretching surface along with Heat source/sink. In the present study considered Gold-Fe2O3-Fe3O4 are nanoparticles along with blood as a base fluid. The governing highly nonlinear PDEs are converted into ODEs with the help of suitable self-similarity variables. The highly nonlinear ODEs are solved using the BVP4c numerical method in MATLAB software. The outcomes of the main active physical parameters of energy and velocity profiles are explained by graphs and tables. The thermal profile exhibits increasing behaviour when the thermal radiation parameter varies. The velocity graph is decreasing with the variations of the magnetic field parameter. The artificial neural networks (ANNs) model is highly forecasting, with a minimal mean squared error of 6.0494 × 10⁻⁷ and a regression coefficient of R2 = 1, indicating close to perfect consistency with numerical data. Comparative evaluation with previous literature demonstrates good consistency, guaranteeing the correctness and dependability of the current approach. Consistent with earlier research, the results validate the model's correctness and provide light on how to optimize biomedical fluid structures based on nanoparticles. Overall, the thermal efficiency and flow characteristics of blood-based mixed nanofluids are used in various sectors like biomedical science, targeted medication administration, and thermal therapy.

Introduction

Non-Newtonian liquid boundary- layer movements are gaining popularity due to their wide range of potential uses for production and industrial operations. When compared to their Newtonian equivalents, non-Newtonian liquids are distinguished by the different properties of flow movement and heat transport within the fluid itself. A broad variety of applications, including but not limited to exploration muds, steel spinning, fiber optics, polymer plastics, and chilling metallic surfaces in freezing baths, are examples of the multifaceted usefulness that may be found throughout this area. Researchers throughout history have produced a wide variety of non-Newtonian concepts since it is impossible for one model to precisely anticipate every one of the features of non-Newtonian substances. Non-Newtonian substances have been investigated as fundamental fluids throughout a great number of investigations, especially across a surface that is experiencing expansion and motion. Nanoparticles have been disseminated throughout the liquid [1, 2].

Choi was the first person to propose the idea of non-metallic sized nanoparticles in normal liquids that were composed of only one metallic oxide. Hybrid nanofluids are now undergoing the evaluation process to determine their efficacy and reliability. The mixed nanofluid is a demonstration of the identification of novel characteristics of nanomaterials, which are remarkable qualities. In order to achieve the goal of boosting thermal efficiency, mixed nanofluids are being created specifically. Over the past few decades, Computational analyses on mixed nanofluids have been conducted in order to clarify their thermal transfer properties as well as to analyze the efficacy of these innovative tiny fluids subcategory in influencing radiative activities. To form an enduring suspended colloidal substance, composite nanofluids blend a range of solid tiny particles in a foundational liquid. These state-of-the-art nanofluids have demonstrated promising results in a variety of thermal transfer uses, including fundamental air circulation administration, refreshing of compressors and broad cooling processes, condensation of plutonium reactors, grinding coolants, wind turbine efficiency, and refreshing of automobiles. When compared to conventional nanofluids, mixed nanofluids work more effectively in certain situations. Because of these characteristics, hybrid nanofluids have potential uses in state-of-the-art systems for electrical cooling purposes, drug delivery and freezing. The unique temperature and physical characteristics of every nanoparticle kind may be harnessed to create a mixed nanofluid that utilizes them all more effectively [3–7].

The broad application spectrum of ternary hybrid nanofluids spans multiple industries. In aerospace engineering, they are used for thermal regulation in spacecraft electronic systems and engine components, where extreme temperature variations demand efficient and reliable cooling. In automotive and electric vehicle systems, they facilitate battery heat management, motor cooling to improve efficiency and longevity. In electronics and microprocessor cooling, their superior thermal conductivity supports the removal of localized hotspots in high-performance chips, improving operational stability. In renewable energy systems such as concentrated solar power plants, ternary hybrids can be employed in solar collectors to maximize thermal absorption and minimize heat loss under variable environmental conditions. Perhaps most notably for the present study, ternary hybrid nanofluids offer transformative potential in biomedical engineering [8–12]. When dispersed in a blood-mimicking base fluid, nanoparticles such as Fe₃O₄ enable magnetic targeting, Au nanoparticles provide excellent photothermal conversion efficiency, and Ta nanoparticles contribute biocompatibility and radiological benefits. This combination can be harnessed for targeted cancer hyperthermia treatments, where a controlled magnetic field and nonlinear thermal radiation are used to elevate tumor tissue temperatures selectively without damaging surrounding healthy cells. Many researchers have explored the uses and effectiveness of hybrid and ternary mixed nanofluids and its heat transfer effect in various applications are studied [13–24].

In the framework of carrying out more research on the aforementioned topics, a number of problems and gaps in the study have been discovered. According to the best of our expertise, there have been no previous investigations conducted by considering blood-based Casson mixed nanofluid flow via a stretching surface along with porous medium, heat source/sink and MHD. Therefore, the present article's primary objective is to conduct a computational analysis and artificial neural networks (ANNs) in response to the aforementioned research gap. The analysis is restricted to a steady, two-dimensional boundary layer flow, which may not fully represent complex unsteady and three-dimensional physiological conditions.The boundary conditions are idealized (e.g., uniform wall temperature and linear stretching velocity), which may differ from real biomedical environments.The ANN model is trained on numerically generated data only, without experimental validation, and its predictive capability may be limited outside the trained parameter range.The fluid is modeled using the Casson model, which, although suitable for blood, does not account for all aspects of real biological fluid behavior.The thermophysical properties of the hybrid nanofluid are evaluated using classical mixture models, which assume uniform dispersion and neglect particle aggregation effects.

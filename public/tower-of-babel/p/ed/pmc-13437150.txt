Treatment of Infantile Blount Disease with Double Osteotomy: Medial Hemielevation and Lateral Closing Wedge Single Stage with the Use of a Polyetheretherketone Cage: Case Series
Authors: Elsayed Shaheen, Ahmed Said Mohamed, Nabil Al Moghazy, Khaled Zaghloul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of polyetheretherketone (PEEK) cage grafting in achieving union at the medial tibial plateau hemielevation site, using intraoperative knee arthrography to assess the accuracy of correction. Advanced infantile Blount disease (Langenskiöld stages V and VI) poses considerable surgical challenges owing to its complex multiplanar deformities and elevated risk of recurrence. Several surgical strategies, including staged and single-stage corrections, have been described. This case series presents the outcomes of a single-stage approach that simultaneously addresses joint-level and proximal tibial deformities, combined with lateral epiphysiodesis to mitigate the risk of recurrence.MethodsBetween 2015 and 2020, 13 patients (6 males, 7 females; aged 8–12 years) with stage V or VI infantile Blount disease underwent double osteotomy consisting of medial hemielevation and lateral closing wedge correction. Medial tibial plateau realignment was guided intraoperatively by arthrography, and the resulting gap was filled with a lumbar PEEK cage. A fibular osteotomy was performed in all patients. Fixation was achieved using a 4.5-mm T-plate that crossed the epiphyseal plate to facilitate lateral growth arrest. One patient had a history of prior surgical intervention.ResultsThe mean Pediatric Outcomes Data Collection Instrument score improved significantly from 47.9% preoperatively to 83.1% postoperatively (p < 0.001). All patients achieved a full range of knee motion without residual instability or varus thrust. Radiographic evaluation demonstrated correction of the mean tibiofemoral angle from 30° varus to 6° valgus, the medial proximal tibial angle from 60.0° to 87.1°, and the depressed medial plateau angle from 24.8° to 2.9° (all p < 0.001). No recurrence of deformity was observed, and the final limb-length discrepancy was < 1 cm in all patients.ConclusionsSingle-stage double osteotomy with PEEK cage interposition, guided by intraoperative knee arthrography and stabilized with a lateral T-plate, provides effective correction of advanced tibial deformities in infantile Blount disease. This technique restores articular congruency, achieves mechanical alignment, and reduces the risk of recurrence.Keywords: Infantile Blount disease, Tibia vara, Osteotomy

Blount disease is a developmental disorder of the proximal tibia characterized by abnormal endochondral ossification, predominantly involving the posteromedial region of the metaphysis and disrupting growth within the medial proximal tibial physis.1) Although originally described by Blount as tibia vara, suggesting an isolated coronal plane deformity, subsequent studies have established that the condition represents a complex multiplanar deformity of the lower limb.2) The characteristic features include proximal tibial varus, anterior bowing (procurvatum), and internal tibial torsion (ITT).

The infantile form of Blount disease typically presents before 4 years of age. If untreated, it can progress to a complex deformity characterized by varus malalignment, internal tibial rotation, and a depressed or concave medial tibial plateau.3,4,5,6) Langenskiöld and Riska classified the disease into 6 radiographically defined stages.6) In the advanced stages (V and VI), the medial plateau (MP) demonstrates marked downward sloping, which contributes to ligamentous laxity. This results in joint instability due to incongruence of the tibiofemoral articulating surfaces and is frequently associated with an exaggerated varus thrust during gait, compromising lower limb function and altering biomechanical alignment.3,4,5,6)

Although a metaphyseal osteotomy alone can correct varus deformity and ITT, it does not restore normal joint congruency or alignment. Consequently, joint instability and varus thrust may persist. Several studies have reported favorable outcomes following medial tibial plateau elevation to re-establish tibiofemoral alignment.7,8,9,10,11,12,13,14) However, consensus regarding the optimal osteotomy technique and fixation method remains lacking.

Gregosiewicz et al.15) described the double-elevating osteotomy, in which a bone wedge—harvested from a concurrent valgus tibial osteotomy—is used to elevate the MP. This approach is primarily indicated for advanced or neglected cases. A dual osteotomy strategy that acutely elevates the MP while preserving the cartilage hinge allows precise joint-level correction, minimizes the risk of intra-articular fracture or medial condyle displacement, and enables simultaneous correction of varus and ITT through a distal osteotomy.16,17)

This study aims to present a safe and efficient technique for managing complex deformities in Langenskiöld stages V and VI of infantile Blount disease using a double-level tibial osteotomy. A polyetheretherketone (PEEK) cage is placed at the proximal osteotomy site to restore joint congruency. This approach is intended to realign the mechanical axis, achieve equal limb lengths at skeletal maturity, maintain joint congruity, and minimize the risk of recurrence. Clinical and radiographic outcomes, including gadolinium-enhanced knee imaging, were used to evaluate the effectiveness of the procedure.

METHODS

This retrospective case series was conducted at Mansoura University Hospital in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of Mansoura University (IRB No. 1588/12111). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all patients included in the study.

Between 2015 and 2020, 13 patients with Langenskiöld stage V or VI infantile Blount disease underwent double-level tibial osteotomy, consisting of medial hemiplateau elevation augmented with a cage without additional bone grafting, and lateral closing wedge osteotomy. This retrospective study includes a consecutive series of these patients.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Patients diagnosed with advanced-stage infantile Blount disease (Langenskiöld stage V or VI) were included. Exclusion criteria comprised advanced genu varus deformities secondary to conditions other than Blount disease, early-stage Blount disease (stages I–IV), infections, or physeal injuries resulting from trauma. The final cohort included 13 patients (6 males, 7 females) with a mean age of 10.2 years (range, 8–12 years). All were primary cases with no prior surgical interventions except 1 patient, who had undergone an unsuccessful lateral epiphysiodesis with 8-plate fixation 18 months earlier. Eight deformities involved the right limb and 5 the left. All patients completed follow-up, with no losses to follow-up. The mean follow-up duration was 24.3 months (range, 20–27 months) (Table 1).

Clinical and Functional Assessment

Patients underwent clinical evaluation to assess knee joint stability, range of motion, leg-length discrepancies, and the degree of varus correction. Functional outcomes were measured using the Pediatric Outcomes Data Collection Instrument (PODCI), a validated tool for assessing patient- or parent-reported outcomes, including functional status and satisfaction, in children with lower limb deformities.18,19) Lateral knee laxity was assessed clinically using varus–valgus stress testing in full extension and visual gait observation for lateral thrust rather than instrumented gait analysis.

Radiographic Evaluation

Full-length standing anteroposterior radiographs were obtained with the patellae facing forward, and limb-length discrepancies were corrected using blocks beneath the shorter limb. Standard anteroposterior and lateral views of the knee and tibia were also acquired. Radiographic measurements were recorded at 3 time points: preoperatively, immediately postoperatively, and at the latest follow-up. Evaluated parameters included the mechanical axis of the lower extremity, medial proximal tibial angle (MPTA), tibiofemoral angle (TFA), and the angle of depression of the medial plateau (ADMP).

Surgical Technique

Under general anesthesia, patients were positioned supine on a radiolucent table. Intra-articular arthrography was performed to delineate the joint surface. A guidewire was inserted medially, targeting the lateral tibial spine under fluoroscopic guidance (Fig. 1). A medial incision centered over the guidewire was made, and the pes anserinus was incised.

Sequential osteotomes, beginning with one-quarter-inch instruments, were advanced to the epiphyseal level without breaching the cartilage hinge. A laminar spreader was used to gradually elevate the medial hemiplateau until horizontal alignment was achieved, as confirmed by arthrography. The resulting gap was filled with a PEEK lumbar fusion cage without additional bone grafting (Fig. 1). Although an autologous bone wedge was obtained from the lateral closing wedge osteotomy, it was not reused due to unsuitable geometry and insufficient structural support for subchondral reinforcement.

A separate fibular osteotomy was performed at the mid-to-distal third of the fibula through a small lateral incision, and a segment of fibula was excised. A lateral proximal tibial incision was then made to perform the metaphyseal osteotomy at the upper third of the tibia. This lateral closing wedge osteotomy included tibial derotation to correct varus and ITT and was stabilized using a 4.5-mm T-plate crossing the growth plate to reduce the risk of recurrence (Fig. 2).

Postoperative Protocol

Patients were mobilized on the first postoperative day without weight-bearing. Early quadriceps and hamstring strengthening exercises were initiated. The cast was removed at 6 weeks, after which partial weight-bearing was permitted. Full weight-bearing was allowed between 10 and 12 weeks postoperatively. Early rehabilitation included closed-chain kinetic exercises, such as heel slides and seated knee range-of-motion exercises.

Statistical Analysis

Tests of normality were performed for all numerical variables. Data were statistically described in terms of mean±standard deviation (SD) or frequencies (number of cases) and percentages when appropriate. Comparison of normally distributed numerical variables before and after surgery was done using paired sample t-test. Two-sided p-values ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical calculations were done using the computer program IBM SPSS version 25 (IBM Corp.) or Microsoft Windows.

RESULTS

Clinical Outcomes

At the final follow-up, all patients demonstrated full knee extension and flexion without anteroposterior or mediolateral instability. Mean PODCI scores improved significantly from 47.9% (SD, 19.9%) preoperatively to 83.1% (SD, 13.1%) postoperatively (p < 0.001). Clinical gait assessment showed marked improvement, with lateral knee laxity decreasing from 25.4% (SD, 4.1%) to 2.5% (SD, 1.98%). No patients exhibited leg-length discrepancies ≥ 1 cm or varus thrust during gait. No intraoperative or postoperative complications were observed (Table 2).

Radiographic evaluation demonstrated significant correction of tibial deformities (Table 3). DISCUSSION

Early surgical intervention is recommended for patients with progressive infantile Blount disease.4,20,21) Nevertheless, recurrence has been reported in up to 55% of cases, even with timely surgery.4) Previous techniques for MP elevation carried significant risks, including intra-articular fractures, medial femoral condyle displacement, and insufficient elevation of the MP, potentially resulting in persistent lateral instability.22,23) Gradual elevation using the Ilizarov method has also been associated with multiple complications, including pin-site irritation, delayed rehabilitation, pin-tract infections, hardware failure, premature bone consolidation, incomplete correction, and recurrence of the deformity.8,12,24,25,26)

A single metaphyseal osteotomy is generally insufficient to address the complex multiplanar deformities seen in Blount disease. Adequate realignment requires additional elevation of the depressed medial tibial plateau. Although the term “double elevating osteotomy,” first described by Gregosiewicz et al.,15) is commonly used, it can be misleading, as only the MP is elevated, a point also emphasized by van Huyssteen et al.16) An essential component of correction involves valgus realignment of the tibial shaft, achieved via wedge or dome-shaped osteotomy. Therefore, the procedure is more accurately termed a double-level tibial corrective osteotomy with MP elevation.

The elevated MP can be reinforced using a tricortical iliac crest graft and/or the bone wedge excised during the valgus osteotomy. Additionally, a segment of the previously resected fibula may be utilized for further structural support. To minimize the risk of recurrence, a mild overcorrection of 5° to 10° into valgus is recommended. Stable fixation of the MP can be achieved with a horizontal Kirschner wire, whereas the distal osteotomy is typically stabilized using crossed Steinmann pins, staples, or plates.8)

The described technique offers several advantages. Intraoperative acute elevation of the MP allows precise alignment with the lateral side, verified using fluoroscopic arthrography. The osteotomy preserves the cartilage hinge, and temporary fixation with a laminar spreader facilitates selection of the appropriate PEEK cage size. This approach enables safe elevation of the severely depressed MP while minimizing the risk of joint fracture, condylar shifting, or loss of correction. Avoidance of bone graft collapse, radiolucency, and predictable correction maintenance are now emphasized.

Acute elevation was performed, with the defect effectively filled using a PEEK lumbar cage. Restoration of the articular surface was confirmed intraoperatively via C-arm arthrography, and fixation was maintained using a lateral T-plate. The PEEK cage acts as a subchondral spacer supporting the elevated MP rather than as a cortical buttress. Medial cortical continuity is preserved through the intact cartilage hinge and surrounding metaphyseal bone. The lateral T-plate provides alignment control and neutralizes bending forces while avoiding medial soft-tissue dissection, reducing wound-related complications. No bone healing or soft-tissue complications were observed, and the technique avoided adverse outcomes commonly associated with gradual distraction methods.8,14,18,21)

The procedure produced marked improvements in radiographic parameters. Correction of the posterior tibial slope was achieved by posterior placement of the graft under intraoperative imaging guidance. While Hefny and Shalaby23) described a similar MP elevation technique using a Gigli saw in the context of gradual Ilizarov correction, the current study supports the safety and accuracy of acute intraoperative elevation, allowing precise alignment without intraoperative fractures. In contrast, gradual methods have been associated with residual deformity, necessitating additional osteotomy for correction of varus and ITT. In the current study, the secondary osteotomy at the upper third of the tibia via a lateral closing wedge with derotation enabled simultaneous valgus and rotational correction.

Application of this technique resulted in substantial improvement in the mean ADMP, decreasing from 24.8° to 2.9°. Lower limb alignment also improved markedly, with mean TFA and MPTA adjusted to near-normal values of 6.6° and 87.1°, respectively, and maintained throughout follow-up. A proximal tibial T-plate served multiple purposes: it stabilized both the metaphyseal and MP elevation osteotomies, provided buttress support to the elevated condyle, and preserved medial column alignment. The plate was applied laterally, with epiphyseal screws directed from lateral to medial to induce lateral epiphysiodesis.27)

One-stage double osteotomy with internal fixation has been infrequently reported. Gkiokas and Brilakis9) described a similar technique in 9 limbs, employing plates, staples, and Kirschner wires, with postoperative plaster casting. However, alignment assessment was limited to TFA, and mechanical axis correction was not evaluated. Performing metaphyseal correction first may compromise joint congruency, and residual varus persisted in most cases, with a mean postoperative ADMP of 10°. In contrast, the current technique emphasizes restoration of joint congruity through early fixation. MP elevation is followed by a slight valgus overcorrection via a lateral closing wedge osteotomy. The resulting bone defect is stabilized using PEEK cages, maintaining MP alignment and the mechanical axis. The lateral T-plate provides reliable buttress support and ensures correction is maintained.

Abraham et al.17) also utilized a single-stage double osteotomy in 23 patients (29 limbs), performing a dome-shaped metaphyseal osteotomy with lateral fixation. However, MP fixation relied solely on Kirschner wires, necessitating prolonged casting. Although some cases achieved joint congruity, varus malalignment persisted in 27.5% of limbs, and only 38% maintained congruent joints at final follow-up. The optimal timing for MP elevation remains debated. In this series, the mean patient age was 10.2 years (range, 8–12 years). Sabharwal et al.7) recommended delaying the procedure until after 6 years to ensure adequate condylar size, while others suggest age 8 or older.9,10) Accordingly, patients were included only if older than 8 years. Although early closure of the growth plate was observed, the final limb-length discrepancy was minimal (< 1 cm), consistent with prior reports.9)

All patients in the current study were primary cases with no prior surgical interventions, except for one 10-year-old boy with stage VI Blount disease, who had previously undergone lateral epiphysiodesis at age 9. The earlier procedure failed to achieve correction; however, realignment was successfully attained 5 months later, with improved TFA (6°), MPTA (90°), and ADMP (2°). Instrumented gait analysis was not performed. Instead, knee stability was assessed through clinical examination and visual gait observation in full extension, consistent with prior methodologies.9,11,22) Mechanical alignment was evaluated intraoperatively by stretching a cautery cord from the hip to the ankle across the knee, a widely accepted method correlating well with long-standing radiographs. However, this technique is less reliable in obese patients or those with ligamentous laxity or incongruent knees. In the present study, alignment assessment was performed after MP elevation and subsequently refined during the second osteotomy.

Management of late-stage infantile Blount disease presents significant technical challenges and risks of intraoperative complications. The technique described—acute MP elevation combined with metaphyseal osteotomies—provides a safe and effective single-stage solution, avoiding the limitations of gradual correction and demonstrating no evidence of recurrence. This approach enables accurate correction of severe MP depression without extensive grafting. PEEK cages of appropriate size adequately support the elevated condyle, promoting integration and restoration of joint congruity. Internal fixation with a lateral T-plate allows early mobilization, stabilizes the medial condyle, and induces lateral epiphysiodesis.

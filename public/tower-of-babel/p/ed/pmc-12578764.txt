Targeting SET to counteract Lyn activation demonstrates anti-cancer potential in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Ji-Lin Chen, Pei-Yi Chu, Chun-Teng Huang, Wan-Lun Wang, Yuan-Ya Chang, Ming-Shen Dai, Chun-Yu Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is characterized by molecular complexity and heterogeneity. The SET oncoprotein is overexpressed in B-cell chronic lymphocytic leukemia and non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Acting as an intrinsic inhibitor of the tumor suppressor protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A), targeting SET has shown anti-cancer activity. In this study, we investigated the molecular mechanism of SET antagonism in DLBCL. Overexpression of SET resulted in decreased phosphatase activities of PP2A and SHP-1, while activating Lyn. Conversely, SET inhibition, either through antagonists or siRNA, led to the activation of PP2A and SHP-1, resulting in the inactivation of Lyn. Analysis of public datasets revealed upregulation of Lyn in late-stage DLBCL samples, which was associated with a worse outcome. Ectopic expression of Lyn enhanced the cell viability and migratory capacity of DLBCL cells. Notably, Lyn inhibited PP2A and SHP-1 phosphatase activities, suggesting the existence of a SET/PP2A/SHP-1/Lyn feedback loop. Immunohistochemically, SHP-1 levels were negatively correlated with pLyn/Lyn levels in DLBCL tissues. Overall, these results suggest that SET antagonism to inactivate Lyn represents an attractive approach for DLBCL treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-025-01874-x.Keywords: DLBCL, SET, Lyn

Background

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) stands out as an aggressive subtype with the highest incidence among non-Hodgkin lymphomas. DLBCL is characterized by its heterogeneity, further classified into germinal center B-cell-like (GCB) and activated B-cell-like (ABC) subtypes based on gene-expression profiling [1]. The first-line treatment for DLBCL is the anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody rituximab combined with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP). However, approximately one-third of patients experience relapse [2], emphasizing the need for therapeutic strategies targeting the diverse biology of this disease. Apart from drugs targeting B-cell receptor (BCR) signaling, ongoing research is evaluating novel targeted therapeutics against NF-κB, Bcl2, and EZH2 in DLBCL [3, 4].

Src family kinases are recognized for mediating BCR signaling. The aberrant activation of BCR signaling has emerged as a crucial factor in non-Hodgkin lymphoma [5]. Pharmacological inhibition of Src family kinases represses BCR signaling and effectively reduces tumor growth, independent of the DLBCL subtype [6]. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as dasatinib and ibrutinib, targeting BCR signaling, have been investigated in combination with R-CHOP and tested in DLBCL clinical trials [7]. Lck/Yes novel tyrosine kinase (Lyn), a member of the Src family, is directly activated by BCR and expressed in B cells and hematopoietic cells [8, 9]. Lyn's phosphorylation within the activation loop at Y397 increases its active enzyme. SHP-1 and SHP-2 phosphatases dephosphorylate LynY397 to inactivate Lyn [10, 11]. Lyn is crucial for maintaining the leukemic phenotype, as evidenced by mice with constitutively active Lyn displaying myeloid and lymphoid abnormalities [12, 13]. B-non-Hodgkin lymphomas exhibit Lyn/Cbp/STAT3 oncogenic signaling through a raft-associated signalosome, contributing to cell survival and presenting a therapeutic target [14].

Previous studies suggest that targeted activation of the protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A)/SHP-1 axis is a potential strategy for treating chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) [15]. PP2A, a serine/threonine phosphatase, negatively regulates several oncoproteins, including Akt, β-catenin, and c-Myc, acting as a tumor suppressor [16]. SET, the intrinsic inhibitor of PP2A, directly interacts with the catalytic subunit C (PP2Ac) [17]. SET overexpression in B-cell CLL and non-Hodgkin lymphoma is well-documented. SET antagonists activate PP2A, inducing malignant B cell death [18]. Targeting the SET-PP2A interaction to reactivate PP2A through small molecules is considered a promising therapeutic strategy [19]. The sphingosine kinase inhibitor FTY720 increases PP2A activity by sequestering SET from PP2A, leading to apoptosis of chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML) cells [20]. Here, we explore the effects of SET antagonism in DLBCL treatment and identify SET/PP2A/SHP-1/Lyn oncogenic signaling in DLBCL.

Methods

Cell culture, reagents, and transfection

The DLBCL cell lines, including U2932, OCI-Ly3, OCI-Ly7, and SU-DHL-6, were obtained from Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen (Braunschweig, Germany), while DB cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). U2932, OCI-Ly7, SU-DHL-6, and DB cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 Medium with 20%, 20%, and 10% FBS, respectively. OCI-Ly7 cells were maintained in Iscove's Modified Dulbecco's Medium with 20% FBS. The 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay was performed using MTT obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Fingolimod (FTY720; Selleckchem, Houston, TX, USA) was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). The SET antagonist TD19 (also known as EMQA) was generously provided by Dr. Chung-Wai Shiau [21]. For gene overexpression, 1 × 10⁶ cells were seeded into 6 cm dishes and transfected with SET, Lyn expression constructs, or pCMV6-Entry control plasmids using PolyJet transfection reagent (SignaGen Laboratories, Frederick, MD, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. To knockdown endogenous Lyn, cells were transfected with Lyn-targeting siRNAs (L-003153-00-0010) or non-targeting control siRNAs (D-001810-10) for 72 hours using DharmaFECT 1 transfection reagent (T-2001-01) following the manufacturer's instructions (Dharmacon, Chicago, IL, USA).

Western blot analysis and co-immunoprecipitation

Whole-cell extracts were prepared using RIPA buffer with Halt™ Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), followed by analysis through sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. Western blot analysis utilized antibodies against SET (Bethyl Laboratories, Montgomery, TX, USA), pPP2AY307, PP2Ac, pSHP-1S591, SHP-1, Lyn, PARP, β-actin (Cell Signaling, Danvers, MA, USA), and pLynY397 (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA). Protein levels were quantified using ImageJ software [22]. For co-immunoprecipitation, whole-cell extracts were lysed in IP lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) supplemented with Halt™ Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail. Protein A agarose beads (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) were washed with IP lysis buffer to prepare a 50% (v/v) slurry. To generate the antibody–bead complex, 1 μg of primary antibody and 50 μl of the protein A agarose slurry were added to 450 μl of IP lysis buffer and rotated at 4 °C for 2 hours. For immunoprecipitation, whole-cell extracts were incubated with the antibody–bead complex overnight at 4 °C. After incubation, the beads were washed, pelleted, and resuspended in 30 μl of 2× Laemmli Sample Buffer. Samples were boiled at 95 °C for 5 minutes and analyzed by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis.

Cell viability and apoptosis

For cell viability measurement, cells were seeded onto 96-well plates for 24 hours and treated with FTY720 or TD19 for 72 hours. Subsequently, 10 microliters of MTT solution (5 mg/ml) were added to each well and incubated at 37 °C. After 3 hours, the medium was removed, and 100 μl of DMSO was added to dissolve the violet MTT formazan precipitates. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. For apoptosis detection, a 100-µl master mix containing 5 μl of APC Annexin V (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA), 4 μl of 0.2 mg/ml propidium iodide (PI; Sigma-Aldrich), and 91 μl of 1×Binding Buffer was prepared. Cells were harvested, suspended in the master mix, and incubated for 15 min at room temperature in the dark. Flow cytometry was then used to determine PI-positive and Annexin V-positive cells.

Determination of phosphatase activity

For PP2A activity [23], whole-cell extracts were prepared in 20 mM imidazole HCl, 2 mM EDTA, 2 mM EGTA, supplemented with Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Subsequently, pNPP Ser/Thr Assay Buffer, anti-PP2Ac antibody, and Protein A agarose slurry were added and incubated for 24 hours at 4 °C. Protein phosphatase activity was assessed using the PP2A Immunoprecipitation Phosphatase Assay Kit (Millipore). For SHP-1 activity [24], whole-cell extracts were incubated with anti-SHP-1 antibody in immunoprecipitation buffer overnight. SHP-1 activity was measured using the RediPlate 96 EnzChek Tyrosine Phosphatase Assay Kit (Molecular Probes, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA).

Transwell migration assay

As described previously [25], migration assays were conducted in 24-well plates over 20 hours. One million cells in 200 μl of serum-free medium were seeded onto the apical transwell (Greiner Bio One, Kremsmünster, Austria), while the lower chamber contained 900 μl of complete medium. After incubation, non-migrating cells were removed, and the migrated cells were fixed and stained with methanol and crystal violet, respectively.

In silico analyses

Transcript expression levels and survival data of patients with DLBCL from GSE9327 [26], GSE11318 [27], and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) were analyzed. TCGA-DLBC data were retrieved and processed using the TCGAbiolinks R package (version 2.35.4) [28]. The optimal cutoff value for gene expression was determined using the surv_cutpoint() function from the survminer R package (version 0.5.0), which identifies the threshold that maximizes the log-rank test statistic to stratify patients into high- and low-expression groups based on survival outcomes. For Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), the GSEA software (version 4.3.2) and the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) were used to identify significantly enriched pathways [29, 30]. The analysis was performed with 1,000 gene set permutations, and pathways with a P-value ≤ 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) ≤ 0.25 were considered significant. A total of 186 pathways from the KEGG_LEGACY subset of the canonical pathways collection in MSigDB were selected for analysis. To predict the cell-of-origin (COO) subtypes of TCGA DLBCL patients, RNA sequencing data were analyzed using the gneSeqCOO R package (version 1.0.0) [31]. For classification of TCGA DLBCL cases into LymphGen genetic subtypes, an online probabilistic classification tool was used [32]; patients in the TCGA dataset did not harbor NOTCH1 mutations, and therefore the N1 subtype was not included in the LymphGen stratification.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

The lymphoma tissue array (Abcam, ab178212) comprised 96 cases of non-Hodgkin B-cell lymphoma tissues. IHC staining followed a previously described procedure [33]. Primary antibodies against SHP-1 (1:100 dilution; Abcam), pLyn (1:100 dilution; Biocreek, Pearland, TX), and Lyn (1:70 dilution; BD Biosciences) were used for a 1- hour incubation at room temperature. Slides were washed three times with PBS, detected using the EnVision Detection Systems Peroxidase/DAB, Rabbit/Mouse kit (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA), and then counterstained with hematoxylin. Subsequently, the slides were dehydrated, mounted, and independently assessed for IHC intensity of SHP-1, pLyn, and Lyn by an experienced pathologist. The Histochemical Score (H-score) was determined by multiplying the percentage of positively stained cells (ranging from 0 to 100) by the staining intensity (ranging from 0 to 3+).

Statistical analysis

Survival curves for patients with DLBCL were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the Log-rank test. Statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS for Windows software, version 22 (Chicago, IL, USA). Nonparametric tests were employed for statistical comparisons, and significance was defined as a P-value less than 0.05.

Results

SET inactivates PP2A and SHP-1 while inducing Lyn activation

Previous studies suggested that PP2A suppresses the BCR/ABL oncoprotein in CML by associating with and activating SHP-1 [34]. SHP-1 is known to reduce Lyn activity by dephosphorylation [35]. These studies propose that targeting SET/PP2A might inactivate Lyn (Fig. 1a). Our objective was to unravel the role of SET in the regulation of the Lyn pathway in DLBCL. Endogenous SET, PP2A, SHP-1, and Lyn from human DLBCL cell lines exhibited differential expression (Fig. 1b). Ectopic expression of SET or treatment with the PP2A inhibitor okadaic acid (OA) decreased PP2A activity (Fig. 1c). Furthermore, SET overexpression suppressed SHP-1 phosphatase activity (Fig. 1d). It has been reported that phosphorylation of PP2A at Y307 effectively decreases its activity by inhibiting the interaction between PP2Ac and the PP2A-PR55/PR61 regulatory subunit [36]. Similarly, phosphorylation of SHP-1 at S591 has been shown to negatively regulate its phosphatase activity [37]. Consistently, inactivation of PP2A and SHP-1, as well as activation of Lyn by SET, were evidenced by increased levels of pPP2AY307, pSHP-1S591, and pLynY397 (Fig. 1e). We then evaluated the biological functions of SET in DLBCL cells, and the results showed that SET promoted cell viability and migratory capacity (Fig. 1f, g). In addition, analysis of the DLBCL cohort from GSE11318 revealed that patients with high SET transcript levels were associated with poorer survival outcomes (Fig. S1a). GSEA further demonstrated that high SET expression was positively enriched in several cancer-related pathways (Fig. S1b).

SET antagonist dephosphorylates Lyn and induces apoptosis in DLBCL

To investigate the cytotoxic effects of SET antagonism on DLBCL cells, we assessed the efficacy of two different SET inhibitors. FTY720, a sphingosine analogue, targets SET, while TD19, a SET/PP2A protein-protein interaction inhibitor, has been reported to increase PP2A activity [38]. The results demonstrated that both TD19 and FTY720 suppressed cell viability (Fig. 2a). To delve into the underlying mechanisms of SET antagonism, we examined the molecular events associated with TD19 treatment. Immunoblotting results revealed that TD19 activated SHP-1 by reducing pSHP-1S591, concurrently inactivating Lyn by decreasing pLynY397. TD19-treated cells exhibited elevated PARP cleavage. Furthermore, TD19 treatment inhibited phosphorylated ERK, a downstream target of PP2A (Fig. 2b). Importantly, TD19-induced apoptosis was further confirmed by an increase in PI and Annexin V double-positive cells in DLBCL (Fig. 2c).

SET antagonism induces cell apoptosis. a U2932, OCI-Ly3, OCI-Ly7, SU-DHL-6, and DB cells were treated with TD19 or FTY720 at indicated concentrations for 72 hours and analyzed by the MTT assay. b Whole-cell extracts of U2932, OCI-Ly3, and SU-DHL-6 treated with FTY720, TD19, or DMSO for 24 hours were analyzed by Western blot analysis. c OCI-Ly3, U2932, and SU-DHL-6 cells treated with TD19 for 48 hours were stained with PI/Annexin V for flow cytometry analysis. Data are shown as mean ± SD. Student’s t-test, *, P < 0.05; **, P < 0.01; ***, P < 0.001.

TD19 inactivates Lyn and induces cell apoptosis through SET

Consistent with TD19 treatment, the knockdown of SET using siRNA resulted in decreased levels of pPP2AY307, pSHP-1S591, and pLynY397 (Fig. 3a). Notably, TD19 treatment suppressed these phosphorylation levels while inducing PARP cleavage. Importantly, these effects of TD19 were rescued by SET overexpression (Fig. 3b). Previous studies have demonstrated a synergistic effect when combining SET antagonism with chemotherapy [39]. Since CHOP regimen is the chemotherapy backbone for DLBCL, we investigated the impact of SET overexpression on the efficacy of CHOP. We observed that the cell viability inhibited by CHOP was partially restored by SET overexpression (Fig. 3c). These results reveal that SET antagonism leads to Lyn suppression and cell apoptosis.

Data are shown as mean ± SD. Student’s t-test, *, P < 0.05; **, P < 0.01; ***, P < 0.001; #, P < 0.05.

High Lyn expression is associated with a worse outcome in DLBCL

To assess the clinical significance of Lyn, we examined public databases, including Gene Expression Omnibus and TCGA. Transcriptional profiling of B-cell non-Hodgkin's lymphoma cases revealed elevated Lyn expression levels in DLBCL samples compared to reactive lymph nodes (Fig. 4a). To explore the biological correlation of Lyn in DLBCL, we performed GSEA based on the DLBCL cohort (GSE11318). The results indicated that high Lyn expression was positively enriched in several pathways, including the proteasome, DNA replication, and spliceosome pathways, with the BCR signaling pathway showing the highest normalized enrichment score (Fig. 4b and c). Lyn transcripts were increased in patients with higher-stage DLBCL (Fig. 4d and e). Additionally, patients with high levels of Lyn transcripts were associated with worse survival (Fig. 4f and g). We classified DLBCL patients from the TCGA cohort into COO subtypes (Fig. 4h) and observed that patients with the ABC subtype exhibited poorer survival, consistent with previous reports (Fig. S2a). Notably, patients with the ABC subtype also showed higher Lyn transcript expression levels (Fig. 4i). Similar findings were observed in the GSE11318 dataset (Fig. S2b and c). Additionally, we stratified TCGA DLBCL patients according to genetic subtypes (Fig. 4j and S2d), revealing that the MCD subtype displayed relatively higher Lyn expression levels (Fig. 4k). The MCD subtype has also been reported to be associated with worse survival outcomes [32]. Collectively, these data suggest a strong association between Lyn expression and poor clinical prognosis in DLBCL.

Lyn modulates the PP2A/SHP-1 axis and acts as an oncogene in DLBCL

In vitro experimental results demonstrated that exogenous Lyn enhanced the cell viability and migration of DLBCL cells (Fig. 5a and b). Previous reports have indicated that cytosolic Lyn can promote PP2A Y307 phosphorylation, whereby the interaction between PP2A and SET is stabilized by this phosphorylation in CLL [40]. Here, we hypothesized that Lyn might modulate the PP2A/SHP-1 axis (Fig. 5c). Lyn impaired the phosphatase activities of PP2A and SHP-1 (Fig. 5d and e). Accordingly, Lyn overexpression elevated the levels of pPP2AY307 and pSHP-1S591, while the total protein levels of PP2A and SHP-1 remained unchanged (Fig. 5f). In addition, knockdown of Lyn reduced the association between SET and PP2A, as demonstrated by co-immunoprecipitation (Fig. S3). Examination of samples from a tissue microarray of tumors from DLBCL patients revealed a negative correlation between the levels of pLyn/Lyn and SHP-1 protein levels (Fig. 5g and Table 1). These findings suggest that Lyn negatively regulates PP2A/SHP-1 signaling and plays an oncogenic role in DLBCL.

Discussion

The understanding of molecular mechanisms driving the pathogenesis of DLBCL has significantly advanced in recent years. Gene expression profiling studies have identified molecularly defined subtypes characterized by specific oncogene addictions and significant outcome differences. Notably, the ABC-type DLBCL exhibits worse overall survival compared to the GCB-type DLBCL [41]. Given the heterogeneity of DLBCL, investigating novel regimens and combinations becomes crucial to provide benefits to patients [42, 43]. The biological differences between the cell-of-origin classification of DBLCL may influence signaling networks, including the SET/SHP-1/Lyn axis. In our study, we observed subtype-specific variation in downstream components, such as phosphorylated Lyn (pLyn), suggesting that COO-related molecular contexts may affect the regulation of this axis. Importantly, as shown in Fig. 3, targeting SET with the inhibitor TD19 effectively suppressed cell viability and induced apoptosis in both ABC- and GCB-type cell lines, indicating that the SET/SHP-1/Lyn pathway is functionally relevant across COO subtypes. Our findings suggest that SET antagonism effectively inhibits cell viability and induces apoptosis. Pharmacological and genetic inhibition of SET leads to Lyn inactivation, offering a potential therapeutic strategy for DLBCL treatment.

SET is upregulated in various cancers, including breast cancer, chronic myelogenous leukemia, and non-small-cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC), where it controls epigenetic regulation, cell cycle, and apoptosis [44]. SET blockade presents a multi-pathway strategy for cancer chemotherapy. The SET-interacting peptide inhibits Akt signaling, increases nm23-H1 exonuclease activity, and decreases Rac1-GTPase activity, leading to the suppression of cell proliferation and migration [19]. In our previous report, we demonstrated that targeting SET reduced pERK, pElk-1, and CIP2A levels through the activation of PP2A activity [38]. CIP2A is recognized as an oncoprotein that suppresses the tumor-suppressive function of PP2A [45]. Additionally, previous studies have shown that JAK2 inhibition leads to Lyn inactivation, resulting in apoptosis of imatinib mesylate-resistant cells via the SET–PP2A–SHP-1 pathway [46]. These findings suggest that alternative or additional mechanisms may also contribute to the modulation of the SET/SHP-1/Lyn signaling axis in DLBCL. FTY720 exerts anti-cancer activity and has several molecular targets, including sphingosine-1-phosphate receptors and Akt, in addition to its role as a SET antagonist [47]. Although FTY720 has pleiotropic effects beyond SET antagonism, our results revealed that both TD19 and FTY720 exhibited similar effects in inactivating Lyn, supporting the on-target relevance of SET inhibition in our system. The anti-tumor efficacy of TD19 has been tested in various cancers, such as prompting the apoptotic effect of paclitaxel in NSCLC through reactivating PP2A [48]. TD19 disrupts SET-PP2Ac interaction to downregulate pAkt, leading to hepatocellular carcinoma cell death [21]. Additionally, several SET antagonists, including OP449 and COG112, have shown beneficial effects in preclinical cancer models s [19, 49].

It has been reported that a positive feedback loop exists in PP2A/SHP-1 signaling. Increasing SHP-1 activity results in PP2A activation by dephosphorylating pPP2AY307. Additionally, the activation of PP2A by FTY720 analog dephosphorylates pSHP-1S591 to increase SHP-1 activity [15]. SHP-1, a member of the protein tyrosine phosphatase family, plays a crucial role in proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Predominantly expressed in hematopoietic cells, SHP-1 negatively regulates BCR signaling [50, 51]. Notably, dephosphorylation of SLP-76 by SHP-1 can be recruited to CD22 to inactivate JNK, thereby inducing B cell apoptosis [52]. Loss of function of SHP-1 in DLBCL leads to STAT3 dysregulation and cell proliferation [53]. Suppression of SHP-1 by promoter hypermethylation is associated with the survival of B-cell lymphomas and DLBCL [54, 55] , suggesting the tumor-suppressive role of SHP-1 in DLBCL.

Constitutive activation of BCR signaling has been reported in various B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes, including DLBCL, CLL, and mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) [56]. Upon Lyn activation, which phosphorylates BCR co-receptors Ig-α/Ig-β ITAMs thereby activating spleen tyrosine kinase (Syk). Activated Syk triggers downstream survival cascades including Brutons’ tyrosine kinase (Btk) and phospholipase Cγ2 phosphorylation [57]. Lyn is crucial for the proliferation of lymphoma cells [58]. Targeting BCR kinases shows promising results in preclinical models of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Small molecular inhibitors effectively downregulate Lyn, Syk, and Btk, exerting anti-tumor activity in B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma in vitro and in vivo [59]. Blockade of Lyn significantly reduces tumor growth in mice bearing bortezomib-resistant MCL cells [60]. Zonta et al. reported that cytosolic Lyn can promote phosphorylation of PP2A at the Y307 residue, which has been associated with enhanced interaction between PP2A and SET [40]. Our results show that Lyn knockdown reduces the association between SET and PP2A (Fig. S3), consistent with a potential role for Lyn in modulating this interaction. However, as the interaction we observed appeared relatively weak, we acknowledge that other mechanisms may also contribute to this regulation beyond a direct effect of Lyn on SET/PP2A.

Despite our in vitro data demonstrate the functional relevance of SET/SHP-1/Lyn across COO classification of DLBCL, a major limitation of this study is the lack of in vivo validation, which would further strengthen the translational relevance of our findings. Future studies using xenograft or genetically engineered mouse models will be important to validate the therapeutic potential of SET inhibition in DLBCL.

Conclusions

Targeting SET-PP2A protein-protein interaction inactivates Lyn, resulting in cell apoptosis. The SET/PP2A/SHP-1/Lyn oncogenic signaling is crucial for DLBCL progression. SET antagonism to activate PP2A and SHP-1, along with inactivating Lyn, represents an attractive strategy for DLBCL therapy.

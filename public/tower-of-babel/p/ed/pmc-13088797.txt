Comparison of the accuracy of minimally invasive registration technology and traditional registration in dynamic navigation-assisted zygomatic implant surgery in China: study protocol for a non-inferiority randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yixuan Li, Wenying Wang, Yihan Shen, Feng Wang, Baoxin Tao, Yiqun Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundZygomatic implant (ZI) is an effective method for restoring masticatory function and mid-facial appearance in patients with severe alveolar ridge resorption, avoiding complex bone augmentation. Dynamic navigation-assisted surgery has been introduced to improve ZI placement accuracy, with registration being a crucial step. However, the current registration method using fiducial screws is limited by their concentrated placement in the mouth, reducing accuracy around the zygomatic bone. This study aims to evaluate the accuracy and clinical outcomes of a novel minimally invasive registration plate in ZI surgery, guiding its clinical application in dynamic navigation-assisted ZI surgery.MethodsThis is a prospective, single-center, parallel-group, non-inferiority randomized controlled trial conducted at Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai, China. We plan to recruit 20 patients who need ZI restoration. The inclusion criteria include severe atrophy of the maxillary alveolar ridge or maxillary bone defects. The anterior region must have adequate bone volume to allow placement of two temporary implants or at least four pins for registration plate fixation, or existing implants or residual teeth can provide stable fixation for the registration plate. Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 allocation ratio into two groups: control group: dynamic navigation registration using traditional titanium screws; experimental group: dynamic navigation registration using the novel registration plate. The group assignment will be blinded for accuracy evaluators. Entry and exit point and angular deviations of the ZI, surgical time, registration accuracy, patient satisfaction, implant survival rate, and adverse event incidence will be assessed.DiscussionA novel minimally invasive registration plate has been designed to extend the coverage of registration points to the zygomatic bone, thereby expanding the registration range for dynamic navigation-assisted zygomatic implant placement. Introduction

Background {6a}

Zygomatic implant (ZI) surgery is a reliable and effective treatment alternative for patients with a severely atrophic maxilla or severe maxillary defects [1–3]. For patients with maxillary and mid-facial defects, ZIs can provide remote anchorage for maxillary obstructors or facial prostheses with an overall survival rate of 77–100% [4].

However, due to the irregular morphology of the zygomatic bone and limited intraoperative visibility, penetration into the orbital cavity and infratemporal fossa may occur, especially when the quantity of zygomatic bone is insufficient or multiple ZIs are inserted in unilateral zygomatic bone [5, 6]. To achieve accurate, minimally invasive and safe ZI placement, a computer-assisted ZI placement approach with static and dynamic computer-assisted methods has been introduced [7]. However, static guide is not suitable for patients with severely atrophic maxilla or maxillary defect because there is rare residual bone to ensure a stable position which is necessary during the whole drilling process [1, 8]. Dynamic navigation can provide a real-time and constant visualization of virtual drills and adjacent anatomical structures on the computer screen, assisting surgeons to perform ZI path preparation following preoperative plans and avoid damaging important anatomical structures [9, 10]. According to our previous study, dynamic navigation-assisted ZI surgery demonstrated promising accuracy, with a mean ± standard deviation of 1.57 ± 0.71 mm for entry deviation, 2.15 ± 0.95 mm for exit deviation, and 2.68 ± 1.25° for angular deviation [11]. The registration is an important procedure in the workflow of dynamic navigation-assisted ZI surgery because it determines the mapping relationship between cone-beam CT (CBCT) and the real world. If target registration error (TRE) [12], a metric to evaluate registration accuracy, is large, the dynamic navigation system will guide the surgeon to drill and place ZIs in a wrong place and direction. Previous research has compared various registration strategies [13] used in computer-aided surgery (CAS) and demonstrated substantial differences in accuracy across methods. Registration with invasive markers achieved the highest precision, with the TRE of 1.13 ± 0.05 mm. In contrast, skin-surface registration yielded a TRE of 2.03 ± 0.07 mm, bony landmark registration a TRE of 3.17 ± 0.10 mm, and intraoral splint registration a TRE of 3.79 ± 0.13 mm. These findings indicate that invasive bone screws remain the gold standard for CAS registration. However, this approach has several inherent limitations. For one thing, they may cause additional wounds. For another, the distribution of these screws is usually restricted in the mouth, which may cause high registration error in the extraoral region, especially the exit point of the implant at the zygomatic bone. Our previous in vitro study systematically evaluated nine fiducial marker configurations on an edentulous maxillary phantom [14] and demonstrated that a polygonal distribution spanning five anatomical regions achieved the highest registration accuracy. When ten fiducial markers were used, the mean TRE was as low as 0.44 ± 0.12 mm, and even with only five markers, the TRE remained low at 0.59 ± 0.14 mm. In contrast, more restricted distributions—particularly inverse-triangle configurations—resulted in substantially higher TRE values, reaching 1.53 ± 0.20 mm. These findings not only identify the optimal number and distribution of intraoral fiducial screws for reducing TRE around the zygomatic region but also underscore that the most effective way to further enhance registration accuracy is to position registration points in a widely dispersed pattern closely surrounding the target anatomical area. According to Fitzpatrick et al. [15], extraoral fiducial screws distributed on the supraorbital ridge, mastoid, and zygomatic arch will be tried and tested in patients with maxillary defects [16], but the drawbacks of additional extraoral wound and long distances to zygomatic bones will still not be overcome. To overcome these drawbacks, a novel registration plate with an intraoral fixation part and extraoral extension wings is designed. The intraoral part uses static guide as a base and it is fixed by three or four pins, and the extraoral wings are extended above the zygomatic bones to distribute registration points as close to targets. To the best of our knowledge, no previous studies have utilized this novel registration plate for dynamic navigation in zygomatic implant surgery. This method was developed in-house, and as such, no prior publications have employed this specific technique. Therefore, this non-inferiority randomized controlled trial is aimed to test the accuracy and reliability of this registration plate compared with invasive bone screw for registration.

Objective {7}

The aim of the study is to evaluate the accuracy, clinical success, and the patient-reported outcome of ZI surgery by the novel registration plate and conventional bone screw registration method. The null hypothesis is that the novel registration plate gains a similar exit point deviation of ZIs as conventional bone screw registration.

Trial design {8}

This protocol has been prepared according to the SPIRIT [17] guideline for clinical trial protocols [17]. This study is a single-center, two-parallel group, non-inferiority, randomized controlled trial. In patients with severe maxillary atrophy or maxillary defects requiring dynamic navigation-assisted ZI surgery, the traditional titanium screw registration method will be used as a control to verify the clinical efficacy and effectiveness of the new minimally invasive registration plate. The main evaluation indicators are the deviation between the actual position of the ZI and the planned path (exit point deviation), and the secondary evaluation indicators are the incidence of adverse events in dynamic navigation-assisted ZI surgery, ZI entry point and angular deviation, surgical time (including registration time), registration accuracy, survival rate of ZIs, and patient subjective satisfaction.

Twenty patients from the Second Dental Center of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital will be included in this study. Before implementing any research-related steps, participants are required to sign an informed consent form, and then they will be randomly assigned to the experimental group (minimally invasive registration plate) or the control group (titanium screws) in a 1:1 ratio. All patients will receive dynamic navigation-assisted ZI surgery according to preoperative design, and the surgery time and registration accuracy will be calculated during surgery. Two days after surgery, the patient’s perception of the procedure will be evaluated using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). After postoperative CBCT scan, the preoperative and postoperative CBCT images will be fused to calculate the deviation between the actual position of the ZI and the planned trajectory. The immediate loading will be delivered within 7 days after surgery. Follow-up with suture removal on the 14th (±2) day after surgery for wound evaluation, and 180th (±14) days after surgery, definitive prosthesis will be delivered, and panoramic radiograph will be taken for examination. The survival rate of ZI during the healing period will be calculated (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Study flow chart

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

This study employs traditional registration methods for dynamic navigation-guided zygomatic implantation based on the following scientific considerations: firstly, current standard practice: all existing dynamic navigation protocols for zygomatic implantation universally utilize conventional registration methodologies; secondly, anatomical accuracy concerns: the standard registration protocol does not incorporate fiducial markers in proximity to the zygomatic complex, creating uncertainty regarding its potential impact on implantation accuracy in this anatomically demanding region.

Intervention description {11a}

Preoperative preparation

For the experimental group, according to the CBCT data and intraoral scan data of the patients, combined with the location of the remaining alveolar bone of the patients, the temporary implant implantation site will be designed and the temporary static guide will be fabricated. For patients with at least two osseointegrated implants in the mouth, the impression rod or abutment can be directly installed, and the registration plate base plate (base plate) and the impression rod can be connected to each other through the temporary crown material. For patients with enough existing stable teeth in the mouth, silicone rubber materials can be placed in the base plate, and the registration plate can be fixed to the remaining teeth by impression. If the patient does not meet the above two requirements, the patient needs to use at least four pins to fix the base plate. According to the position of the zygomatic bone of the patient, two lateral registration extension rods will be designed in the software (3dsMax, USA) and combined with a temporary implant insertion guide or registration plate as a new minimally invasive registration plate. The registration screws (φ2 mm × (5–9) mm, Shuangyang, Jiangsu) or conical pits will be placed or designed on the registration plate according to the distribution requirements of the registration screw as registration marks. Firstly, registration marks need to be evenly and widely distributed. Secondly, the center of the registration distribution should be as close as possible to the main surgical area. At last, linear or near-linear alignment of registration markers will be avoided.

For the control group, the location of the registration screws will be designed, and a registration screw implantation guide will then be made. The guide plate will be printed (Rapidshape, Germany) and placed into the patient’s mouth. Under local anesthesia, after the guide plate will be fully positioned, the registration screws (φ2 mm × (5–9) mm, Shuangyang, Jiangsu) are inserted under the guidance of the guide sleeve. A new CBCT will be taken and the DICOM files will be imported into the iPlan CMF software (Brainlab, Germany) for registration point selection and ZI planning. Then, the planning file will be imported into the navigation software (VectorVision2, Brainlab, Germany).

Surgical procedures

The surgery will be performed by the same team of experienced surgeons. The operation will be performed under general anesthesia. Before intubation, the registration plate will be removed as it may hinder the procedure of general anesthesia induction. The experimental group and the control group will be completed on the same operating platform. The reference frame will be fixed in the patient’s hairline for navigation and tracking.

For the experimental group, the new minimally invasive registration plate is first fixed, and the registration operation is completed by using the probe according to the registration points selected in the navigation software. After that, the minimally invasive registration plate will be removed; incision and flap reflection will be performed according to the relationship between the external wall anatomy of the maxillary sinus of the patient and the implantation path. The choice of whether to perform lateral maxillary sinus fenestration will be determined. After the calibration of the ball drill (Nobel, Sweden), the entry point of the ZI path will be marked according to the guidance of the navigation system. After each drill reaches the predetermined depth, the surgeon replaces the next drill until the preparation is completed. During this process, calibration will be required for each drill change. The ZIs will be manually implanted according to the paths of the hole, and the initial stability of the ZIs will be checked. The procedure will be completed after the removal of the reference frame.

For the control group, the registration operation will be first completed by using the probe according to the order of the matching point selection in the software. The subsequent operation is the same as that of the experimental group.

Given that the intervention pertains solely to surgical approach alterations, independent of patient adherence considerations, the implementation of compliance-improvement measures is deemed unnecessary.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

The use of any medication potentially affecting zygomatic implant surgery or wound healing is strictly prohibited for all trial participants during the study period, irrespective of group allocation.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

In postoperative care, the following nursing measures need to be taken for both the experimental group and the control group: hormones will be used to reduce postoperative tissue swelling; painkillers will be administered in a standardized way to manage patients’ pain; antibiotics will be applied to reduce the risk of infection; at the same time, a dietary plan should be formulated for the patients.

Throughout the interventional phase, all participant-reported adverse events shall be systematically monitored, documented using standardized adverse event report forms, and subsequently submitted to the designated research personnel. A comprehensive analysis will evaluate potential correlations between registration protocol modifications and adverse event severity gradations. All adverse event occurrences must be meticulously recorded in the designated case report forms (CRFs), with attending physicians obligated to provide necessary supplemental care. Per institutional policy, any serious adverse events require immediate reporting to the Institutional Review Board at Shanghai Jiao Tong University’s Ninth People’s Hospital.

Outcomes {12}

Follow-up assessment

The primary outcome of this trial will be the exit point deviation of ZI. In addition, secondary measures will include the incidence of adverse event, survival rate, operation time, target registration error, entry and angular deviation of ZI, and patients’ subjective satisfaction.

Primary outcome measurement

The study utilizes CBCT as the primary outcome measure, with accuracy assessment based on the deviation at the exit point of ZI after placement. The preoperative CBCT taken within 1 month before surgery will be recorded as CBCT-pre, and the postoperative CBCT taken immediately will be recorded as CBCT-post. After surgery, CBCT-post will be imported into the navigation software for fusion with CBCT-pre, and the distance between the planned path and the actual implant position measured at the exit point will be recorded as Deviation-Ex.

Overview of the schedule of enrollment, interventions, and assessments

Participant timeline {13}

When contacting potential subjects for the first time, CRC will introduce the background, objectives, and procedures of the trial. Following this, a qualification assessment will be conducted based on the preliminary screening criteria. Patients who meet the screening criteria and provide informed consent will proceed to baseline visits. During the baseline visit, patients will be initially assessed for inclusion and exclusion criteria. Demographic information, including date of birth, age, gender, ethnicity, height, weight, and body mass index (BMI), will be collected. A thorough medical history, including family history, allergy history, smoking history, vital signs, and pregnancy status, will also be recorded. Additionally, details of the patient’s oral surgery history, current oral condition, and use of antibiotics or mouthwash, as well as the date and cause of tooth loss, will be noted. Routine preoperative examinations, including urine and blood tests, blood biochemical analysis, coagulation tests, and infection screening, will be performed.

After the zygomatic implant surgery, the operation time is recorded, and intraoperative data from the dynamic navigation system are exported to calculate the registration error. Within 2 ± 1 days postoperatively, intraoperative and postoperative complications are analyzed, and patient satisfaction is assessed. Within 5 ± 2 days, an immediate prosthesis is fabricated for the patient. On postoperative day 14 ± 2, sutures are removed and wound healing is evaluated. At each postoperative assessment, the occurrence of adverse events and serious adverse events must be monitored. At 180 ± 14 days postoperatively, a panoramic radiograph is taken to assess wound healing and calculate the zygomatic implant failure rate, and the final prosthesis is delivered to the patient. Sample size {14}

The primary outcome in this study is the ZI exit point deviation, and the allocation ratio between the two groups is 1:1. According to the reference [11] and the results of previous cases in our research group, the exit point deviation of ZI surgery with titanium screw and registration plate will be 2.1 ± 0.94 mm and 0.75 ± 0.13 mm, respectively. The latter value (0.75 ± 0.13 mm) was obtained from our preliminary pilot experiment conducted prior to this trial. To make the studies statistically different, the total sample size will be 16, that is, 8 cases in each group, in which the test level α will be 0.05 on both sides and the degree of power 1 − β will be 0.95, calculated using PASS 15.0 software (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA) based on a non-inferiority design. The sample size will be expanded to 20 cases, that is, 10 cases in each group, considering factors such as the dropout rate of no more than 20%, device damage, and adverse events.

Recruitment {15}

Recruitment is performed in the Department of Second Dental Center, Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai, China, with recruitment and management overseen by CRC and the researchers. Screening will be performed among patients with severe maxillary alveolar ridge atrophy or maxillary defect patients requiring ZI restoration. Informed consent will be obtained from patients enrolled in trials.

To ensure adequate participant enrollment and meet the target sample size, the recruitment process for this study will involve multiple strategies. Initially, the attending physician will actively screen eligible patients during routine clinical visits, thoroughly explaining the study’s objectives and procedures. Potential participants will also be identified through the hospital’s electronic medical record system by searching relevant keywords, followed by phone invitations from the research nurse. Eligible patients will be directed to a dedicated clinical pathway set up specifically for the study, offering prioritized appointments and centralized screening services. Additionally, recruitment will be supported by the display of posters within the hospital, distribution of informational brochures, and announcements posted on oral health-related public platforms, all aimed at maximizing patient participation.

Assignment of interventions: allocation sequence generation {16a}

After completion of baseline assessments, eligible participants are randomly assigned in a 1:1 allocation ratio to either the traditional registration group or the novel registration group. The allocation sequence is generated by independent statisticians using a predefined algorithm implemented in SAS 9.1 statistical software (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

The allocation sequence will be generated using a computer-generated randomization method implemented through SAS 9.1 software. A fixed seed value will be used to ensure reproducibility of the sequence. The ranuni function in SAS will generate uniform random numbers between 0 and 1. Participants will be assigned to the experimental group (novel minimally invasive registration group) if the random number is ≤0.5, and to the control group (traditional bone screw group) if the random number is >0.5. To reduce the predictability of the random sequence, no stratification or blocking methods will be applied. The allocation sequence will be generated and concealed prior to participant enrollment, ensuring unbiased assignment. Details regarding any planned restrictions or further steps to minimize bias will be provided in a separate document, which will not be accessible to those responsible for enrolling participants or assigning interventions.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Random numbers will be sealed in an opaque envelope. Once patients have consented to participate in the trial, the envelope will be opened and patients will be allocated to groups based on the instructions contained within.

Implementation {16c}

A researcher independent of recruitment, assessment, and trial implementation will be responsible for overseeing the randomization process.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

This is an open-label non-inferiority randomized controlled trial (RCT) with outcome assessor blinding. Specifically, blinding is applied to the personnel responsible for radiographic evaluation, with outcome assessor blinding implemented to minimize bias and prevent subjective factors from influencing the assessment of outcomes. These personnel are strictly prohibited from accessing group allocation information to minimize bias and avoid the influence of subjective factors on outcome assessment. To ensure consistency and objectivity in evaluating study endpoints, an independent centralized image review process is employed for accuracy comparison.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

The surgeons are aware of the participants’ group assignments; therefore, they are not blinded. Participants, who undergo fixation of the registration device or titanium screws under local anesthesia, are also unblinded.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Prior to the commencement of the project, a comprehensive study protocol manual and standardized case report forms were meticulously developed to ensure consistency and accuracy in the research procedures. Comprehensive data collection and documentation will be conducted for both the experimental and control groups. This will include assessments of the zygomatic implant precision, surgical duration, registration accuracy, implant failure rate during the healing phase, patient-reported satisfaction, and postoperative healing outcomes. Evaluations will take place intraoperatively, as well as at 2 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 6 months postoperatively. During these assessments, participants will be provided with an informed consent form, which they will be required to sign. All assessments will be conducted by research team members with no conflicts of interest, and these assessors will be blinded to the details of the intervention group assignments.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

At each follow-up assessment, investigators are required to complete the corresponding postoperative treatment procedures, such as suture removal and fabrication of the definitive prosthesis. This approach is intended to minimize the risk of participant missing and ensure adherence to the study protocol.

Data management {19}

All study data will be independently entered into the database by two researchers. After data entry, discrepancies will be identified through the software’s automatic comparison feature, and any differences will be verified by a third-party review of the original case report forms to ensure data accuracy. Categorical variables will be coded according to a standardized system, while continuous variables will be recorded based on their original values. Data quality control measures include predefined range checks for variables, logical validation rule settings, training for research staff prior to the study’s initiation, and random sampling of 20% of the data every 3 months for quality assurance. Detailed data management procedures, including CRF completion guidelines and the coding manual, can be obtained by contacting the project lead. This plan has been submitted to the ethics committee for approval. Data will be stored using a dual backup system, consisting of both paper files and encrypted electronic servers, and will be retained for 10 years following the conclusion of the study. All trial data are managed to safeguard participant confidentiality, with secure storage and restricted access, while ensuring continuous clinical care. To maintain anonymity, each participant is assigned a unique Research ID, which is used for data collection and analysis. The Research ID is the only identifying information included on CRFs, ensuring that no personal identifiers are present in the data used for analysis.

Confidentiality {27}

Individual study data will not be disclosed without the participant’s written consent, except as needed for study monitoring, auditing by the sponsor or its representatives, regulatory authorities, or ethics committees, or in cases of medical emergencies where written consent cannot be obtained and disclosure is deemed necessary by the investigator for the participant’s best interests.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

It is not applicable for this trial as this study does not involve biological specimens from participants.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

The results of this randomized controlled trial will be reported in strict accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) 2010 guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness in the study design, implementation, and reporting of outcomes. The primary analytic population for statistical analysis will be the intent-to-treat (ITT) population, which includes all participants who were randomly assigned to a group, with the analysis following the “no exclusion after randomization” principle to maintain baseline balance between groups. Additionally, the per protocol (PP) analysis will be reported as a supplementary analysis. Missing data will be analyzed using a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM) under the missing-at-random assumption. The Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) approach will be used only for sensitivity analyses.

SPSS 19.0 and SAS 9.4 software will be used. For all statistical tests, p < 0.05 will be statistically significant. Continuous data will be presented as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD), while dichotomous data will be presented as a proportion. The independent sample t-test or χ2 test will be used to compare demographic data and other baseline indicators to measure the equilibria between the two groups.

Descriptive statistics will report demographic characteristics (age, gender, etc.), relevant medical history, treatment history, and maxillary condition of the patients. The age, gender, and other characteristics of the two groups will be compared to measure the comparability of the two groups. The differences between the two groups for the primary and secondary outcomes will be estimated using mean differences or ratio differences, with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) provided as measures of uncertainty for each estimate. Additionally, the statistical significance of these differences will be assessed using two-sided p values (α = 0.05) to evaluate the reliability and precision of the results.

T-test will be used to test the effect of ZI exit point, operation time, implant dropout rate after healing, registration accuracy, deviation-En, and deviation-An. The Wilcoxon rank sum test will be used for non-continuous variables or continuous variables not normally distributed. To address the intra-patient correlation arising from patients receiving multiple implants and to control for potential confounding factors, a mixed-effects model will be used for statistical analysis, with patients set as the random effect and confounding factors as fixed effects. Chi-square test will be used to test the patient’s subjective satisfaction after the surgery and implant survival rate after healing.

Interim analyses {21b}

This study is a randomized controlled trial with a limited sample size and a short follow-up duration. Based on preliminary data, it was hypothesized during the design phase that there would be no statistically significant difference in efficacy between the novel intervention and the traditional approach. As a result, a mid-term analysis is unlikely to provide sufficient grounds for trial termination or protocol modification, and therefore, no mid-term analysis is planned.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

As no subgroup analyses are planned for this trial, no additional statistical methods are required.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Complete clinical data should be retained for all participants who withdraw from the trial, regardless of the reason for withdrawal. For participants who withdraw, a test conclusion form should be completed, and the reasons for withdrawal must be documented in the CRF. This ensures that all relevant information is collected and retained for data integrity and analysis purposes. If withdrawal is due to an adverse event, the investigator will arrange follow-up visits until the adverse event has resolved or stabilized.

Missing data will primarily be handled using a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM), which appropriately accounts for missing observations under the missing-at-random (MAR) assumption without the need for data imputation. The MMRM will include all available outcome measurements from each participant and preserve the integrity of the intent-to-treat (ITT) population. As a sensitivity analysis, the Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) method will also be applied. For participants who are lost to follow-up or do not complete all assessments, their last observed outcome value will be carried forward to subsequent time points. Consistency between the primary and sensitivity analyses will be evaluated to assess the robustness of the study findings.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level-data, and statistical code {31c}

To safeguard participants’ privacy, the data generated or analyzed in this study will remain confidential and not publicly disclosed. However, the data can be accessed by contacting the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Adverse events (AEs) in this trial include significant deviations in zygomatic implant placement, implant failure or fracture, and severe complications such as infections, bleeding, or neurological impairments. In the event of any adverse occurrence, an adverse event reporting form must be completed promptly and accurately, documenting the time of occurrence, severity, duration, and the corresponding management measures taken.

Detailed follow-up reports will be submitted until the event is resolved or stabilized.

In addition to AEs and SAEs, postoperative complications will be monitored closely, including infection, bleeding, or implant failure. Patient-reported outcomes (PROs), such as discomfort, pain, or functional impairment reported by participants, will be recorded using validated questionnaires or scales to assess the impact of the intervention. Furthermore, any surgical errors or deviations, including inaccuracies in registration or implant placement, will be noted and thoroughly reviewed.

All safety and harm measures will be classified according to their severity and their relationship to the intervention, and these measures will be tracked throughout the study period. A comprehensive summary of AEs and SAEs will be provided in the final analysis, reporting the frequency, severity, and outcomes to evaluate the safety profile of the intervention.

Discussion

Due to the irregular shape of the zygomatic bone and its proximity to critical anatomical structures, ZI placement for edentulous restoration presents significant challenges [18].

Penmetsa et al. [19] used RCT to compare the accuracy difference between freehand and dynamic navigation, and proved that dynamic navigation can significantly improve the accuracy of zygomatic implants compared with freehand. Previous studies have shown that the registration method can impact the accuracy of dynamic navigation [14, 20]. Woerdeman et al. [21] have shown that adhesive markers yield lower registration errors compared to anatomical landmarks and facial laser scanning. Smith et al. [22] conducted a study with the model to investigate the distribution of fiducial marker points and the influence of registration sequence on navigation. The findings indicate that symmetrical, uniform dispersion of fiducial marker points under constant calibration point quantities reduces registration deviation, thereby enhancing dynamic navigation accuracy. Coincidentally, Shen et al. [23] also conducted a similar study on 47 patients who needed to receive dynamic navigation-assisted zygomatic implant surgery. They believed that the number of calibration points and their dispersed spatial distribution were the key factors to ensure the accuracy of dynamic navigation. However, currently in most zygomatic implant surgeries assisted by dynamic navigation, only the landmarks are placed on the alveolar ridge crest, and there are no markers on the zygoma [24, 25]. Therefore, this may lead to poor navigation accuracy at the distal end of zygomatic implants guided by dynamic navigation. Meanwhile, most research comparing registration methods for ZIs has been limited to in vitro studies [26]. Fan et al. [27] research indicates a negative correlation between the number of fiducial screws and the placement deviations of ZIs. Thus, increasing the number of screws can reduce deviations but causes discomfort to patients. Our team previously designed a minimally invasive navigation registration device for ZIs. This novel registration device is secured intraorally with extended wings toward the zygomatic region to improve the accuracy around zygomatic bones. This study aims to evaluate the precision and clinical outcomes of this registration plate in ZI surgeries. If successful, the plate could enhance accuracy, reduce registration errors, and decrease surgical complications compared to titanium screw registration. However, the current clinical study is single-center with a limited sample size, and various limiting factors may impact the results. Future studies with larger samples and multi-center designs will be conducted to explore the broader applicability of this registration plate in navigation-guided surgeries.

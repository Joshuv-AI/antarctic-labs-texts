Bilateral ureteral reconstruction with U-shaped ileal interposition segment
Authors: Federico Eskenazi, Juan Pablo Dugarte, Monish Aron
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We describe a robotic technique for bilateral ureteral reconstruction using a U-shaped ileal interposition in the setting of retroperitoneal fibrosis and vascular encasement. A 77-year-old man with chronic kidney disease, prior orchiectomy with retroperitoneal radiation for testicular cancer, radical prostatectomy, and spine surgery, presented with retroperitoneal fibrosis, bilateral mid-ureteral strictures, and nephrostomy dependence. After adhesiolysis, both ureters were identified at the pelvic brim, dissected off the great vessels, and proximally spatulated. A 25-cm ileal segment was isolated, configured in a U-shape, and anastomosed to the bladder dome at its apex. Each ureter was reimplanted mucosa-to-mucosa to a separate ileal limb over double-J stents, with additional single-J stents placed across the vesico-ileal anastomosis.

INTRODUCTION

Bilateral ureteral strictures in a previously irradiated and fibrotic pelvis pose a major challenge in reconstructive urology. Conventional options, such as Boari flap or ileal substitution, often require extensive ureteral mobilization, which increases the risk of tension and devascularization. The emergence of robotic techniques offers enhanced precision in such complex scenarios.[12] This video presents a step-by-step robotic technique for bilateral ureteral reconstruction using a U-shaped ileal interposition, highlighting how this configuration minimizes ureteral mobilization in cases of retroperitoneal fibrosis or vascular encasement [Video].

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A 77-year-old man with chronic kidney disease (baseline creatinine of ~2 mg/dL), prior orchiectomy with retroperitoneal radiation for testicular cancer, radical prostatectomy, and spine surgery presented with severe retroperitoneal fibrosis, bilateral mid-ureteral strictures, and recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs) requiring nephrostomies. Robotic reconstruction was performed under general anesthesia in a low lithotomy position. After cystoscopy, bilateral subcostal ports were placed. After adhesiolysis, both ureters were identified at the pelvic brim, dissected off the great vessels, and proximally spatulated. A 25-cm ileal segment was isolated and configured in a U-shape; its apex anastomosed to the bladder dome, and each ureter reimplanted mucosa-to-mucosa to a dedicated limb over 6-F × 30-cm double-J stents (with additional single-J stents across the vesico-ileal anastomosis) [Figure 1]. Indocyanine-green fluorescence confirmed bowel perfusion. Bowel continuity was restored with a stapled ileo-ileal anastomosis, and a 19-Fr Blake pelvic drain was placed.

RESULTS

The reconstruction was completed with no intraoperative complications. Operative time was 8 h 16 min with an estimated blood loss of 140 mL. Anastomoses were watertight on intravesical testing. Early postoperative recovery was uncomplicated, and serum creatinine remained stable. At 5-month follow-up, ultrasound showed stable left hydronephrosis, without obstruction. Cystoscopy demonstrated intact mucosa, and urine cytology was negative. Recurrent UTIs were managed with methenamine prophylaxis, and no additional procedures were required.

CONCLUSION

Robotic bilateral ureteral reconstruction with U-shaped ileal interposition restored urinary continuity in a fibrotic, irradiated pelvis. Continued surveillance is warranted to assess durability, renal function, and infection control. Overall, U-shaped ileal substitution is a practical solution for complex bilateral ureteral disease in a hostile pelvis.

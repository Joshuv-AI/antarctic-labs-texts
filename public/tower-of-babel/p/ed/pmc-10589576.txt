Mechanism of Liuwei Dihuang Pills in treating osteoporosis based on network pharmacology
Authors: Xiqoqiang Wang, Hongtao Li, Longhai Long, Chao Song, Rui Chen, Hongyu Pan, Junjie Qiu, Bing Liu, Zongchao Liu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Osteoporosis is a prevalent age-related disease that poses a significant public health concern as the population continues to age. While current treatments have shown some therapeutic benefits, their long-term clinical efficacy is limited by a lack of stable curative effects and significant adverse effects. Traditional Chinese Medicine has gained attention due to its positive curative effects and fewer side effects. Liuwei Dihuang Pill has been found to enhance bone mineral density in patients with osteoporosis and rats, but the underlying mechanism is not yet clear. To shed more light on this problem, this study aims to explore the pharmacological mechanism of Liuwei Dihuang Pill in treating osteoporosis using network pharmacology and molecular docking. The findings indicate that Liuwei Dihuang Pills treat osteoporosis through various targets and channels. Specifically, it mainly involves TNF, IL17, and HIF-1 signaling pathways and helps regulate biological processes such as angiogenesis, apoptosis, hypoxia, and gene expression. Furthermore, molecular docking demonstrates excellent binding properties between the drug components and key targets. Therefore, this study offers a theoretical foundation for understanding the pharmacological mechanism and clinical application of Liuwei Dihuang Pills in treating osteoporosis more comprehensively.

1. Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is a chronic condition affecting the entire skeletal system, characterized by reduced bone mass, deterioration of the bone tissue’s microarchitecture, and increased susceptibility to fractures due to bone fragility.[1] According to the report from the International Osteoporosis Foundation (IOF), around 200 million individuals suffer from OP, which leads to approximately 8.9 million fractures per year globally, making it a significant public health concern. Currently, pharmacotherapy, including bisphosphonates, parathyroid hormone analogs, selective estrogen receptor modulators, mixed steroid receptor agonists, and anti-RANKL monoclonal antibodies, are the primary treatment approach for OP.[2–4] However, these drugs have limited long-term efficacy, severe side effects, and inability to reverse established bone loss, making their long-term clinical application restricted.[5,6] Therefore, there is a need to develop a safer and more effective treatment methodology.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) compound prescriptions have garnered increasing attention from researchers due to their numerous advantages, including diverse sources, clear curative effects, and minimal adverse reactions.[7] As a result, researchers have become more interested in exploring the potential benefits of these prescriptions. Studies and investigations have been conducted to examine their effectiveness, making TCM compound prescriptions a topic of growing interest in the field of medicine.[8] Liuwei Dihuang Pill (LWDHP), a traditional Chinese medicine used for centuries, has been demonstrated to possess anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidative stress properties, promote autophagy, regulate intestinal flora, and improve several health conditions such as diabetes, diabetic nephropathy, insomnia, and polycystic ovary syndrome.[9–12] Moreover, recent research has discovered that LWDHP can also improve bone mineral density (BMD), bone volume, and bone microstructure in postmenopausal osteoporosis patients and rats with diabetic nephropathy-associated osteoporosis.[13,14] However, the underlying mechanism of these beneficial effects remains unclear.

Network pharmacology, a field rooted in systems biology, employs specific signal nodes to conduct multi-target drug molecular analysis.[15] This approach has been widely used in clarifying the mechanism of action of TCM as well as in the development of new drugs.[16] In this study, we applied network pharmacology to investigate the potential mechanism of LWDHP in treating OP, offering new insights for clinical treatment (Fig. 1).

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Screening of potential active compounds and related targets of LWDHP

The analysis of 6 Chinese herbal medicines from LWDHP was conducted on the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database (TCMSP, https://tcmspw.com/tcmsp.php). Relevant information and corresponding compounds were obtained. Active compounds were selected based on the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) protocol with a set criterion of oral bioavailability ≥ 30% and drug similarity ≥ 0.18. The TCMSP database was then explored for potential target proteins of the active compounds, which were used to construct the potential target genes set of LWDHP. The UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/) was used to retrieve unique corresponding gene names and UniProt IDs of drug-related targets in LWDHP, with species selected as “human.”

2.2. Collection of OP-related targets

Four databases GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), MalaCards (https://www.malacards.org/), DisGeNet database (https://www.disgenet.org/,) and Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) (http://ctdbase.org/)) were used for retrieval disease target protein. The term “osteoporosis” was utilized as the keyword, and the union of the search outcomes was combined to form the gene set for OP.

2.3. Network construction and analysis

The Venny online tool (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/) was utilized to analyze the LWDHP gene set and the OP gene set, resulting in the identification of the intersection gene set of OP treated by LWDHP. To illustrate the interaction between “LWDHP-drug-active compound-intersection gene-OP,” the Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1) was used to construct a network.

2.4. Protein interaction analysis

The intersection genes were imported into the String database (http:/string-db.org/) to carry out a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis, with a species restriction to “Homo sapiens” and a confidence value threshold set at > 0.4. The Cytoscape software was used to construct the PPI network, which was then subjected to screening the top 10 nodes to find hub genes using the CytoHubba algorithm.

2.5. Biological process and pathway enrichment analysis

The intersection genes were subjected to an enrichment analysis using the Ouyi Biocloud platform (https://cloud.oebiotech.com), wherein the species was restricted to the “H.sapiens.” The GO enrichment analysis, Reactome, WikiPathways, and KEGG database pathway analysis were obtained after submitting the gene ID of the intersection gene.

2.6. Molecular docking verification of key targets and drug ingredients

According to the degree value, the top 10 hub target proteins in the PPI network were identified. Next, the KEGG PATHWAY Database (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/) was searched to find the key pathways and the hub target proteins in the KEGG pathway network diagram. Subsequently, a reverse search was performed in the PPI network to determine the corresponding chemical active ingredients associated with these target proteins. Finally, the binding activity between these key target proteins and their corresponding chemical active ingredients was verified. The binding energy of the active ingredient to the pivotal target was determined using Autodock Vina (version 1.2.2), while PyMol (version 2.5.5) was utilized for the purpose of molecular docking visualization analysis.

3. Results

3.1. Active compound and target data of LWDHP

After conducting a search of the TCMSP database, it was discovered that Shudihuang contains 2 active ingredients, Shanzhuyu contains 20 active ingredients, Shaoyao contains 9 active ingredients, Zexie contains 10 active ingredients, Fuling contains 15 active ingredients, Mudanpi contains 11 active ingredients. Removing duplicate entries left us with 68 unique active ingredients. The targets of the effective ingredients of the drug were collected, and a total of 221 effective targets of LWDHP were finally obtained after screening and deletion of duplicate targets. The effective targets were incorporated into the UniProt database for identification by name, and a total of 164 targets were obtained for further analysis of the data.

3.2. Related targets of OP

To identify potential targets for OP, we searched 4 databases and identified 32052 unique targets after removing duplicates (Fig. 2).

3.3. Target screening and PPI construction of LWDHP in the treatment of OP

We used the Venn online tool to analyze the data and identified 164 intersecting genes related to LWDHP treatment for OP (Fig. 3). These genes, along with information on the drug, composition, and intersection genes, were imported into Cytoscape. Following calculations, we successfully constructed an “LWDHP-Drug-Active Compound-Crossover Gene-OP” effect network diagram (Fig. 4).

4. Discussion

As the population continues to age, OP has become a significant public health concern, weighing down both the economy and individuals’ quality of life. Despite the availability of treatments, their effectiveness and potential side effects pose limitations,[6] leaving researchers with the task of developing more efficient solutions to meet the pressing demand.

LWDHP, a well-known ancient prescription in TCM, has been proven effective in enhancing BMD among OP patients and rats.[13,14] Furthermore, in vitro studies have revealed that LWDHP stimulates osteogenic differentiation in rat bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells while also suppressing adipogenic differentiation.[17] Collectively, these findings suggest that LWDHP may be a promising treatment for OP.

In this study, we investigated the underlying therapeutic mechanism of LWDHP in OP using network pharmacology. The results of the GO enrichment analysis indicated that the biological processes of LWDHP’s treatment of OP primarily involve regulations of the cytokine-mediated signaling pathways, angiogenesis, apoptosis, gene expression, endocrine resistance, and hypoxia. Additionally, WikiPathways and KEGG signaling pathway analysis revealed that LWDHP treatment of OP predominantly involves the regulation of TNF, HIF-1, and IL-17 signaling pathways.

Angiogenesis and hypoxia play important roles in the pathophysiology of OP. Insufficient vascular supply can result in decreased BMD,[18] while oxygen deficiency also contributes to bone loss.[19] Promoting angiogenesis or regulating oxygen levels can assist in mitigating bone loss and increasing bone mass.[20,21] Moreover, the KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the TNF signaling pathway and HIF-1 signaling pathway are the main pathways involved in the treatment of OP by LWDHP. The HIF-1 signaling pathway is a classic pathway regulating hypoxia and plays a crucial role in OP. Studies have shown that activating the HIF-1 signaling pathway can improve bone mineral density and promote osteoblast differentiation in OVX rats.[22] Additionally, HIF-1 is a crucial molecule that mediates bone-vascular interaction, and up-regulating the HIF-1 signaling pathway can promote angiogenesis and alleviate bone loss in ovariectomized (OVX) mice.[21,23]VEGF is a crucial regulator of angiogenesis that plays a significant role in bone metabolism.[24–26] Research has demonstrated that VEGF contributes to the differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells, making them more inclined towards osteogenic differentiation, by regulating the transcription factors RUNX2 and PPARγ2.[27] Furthermore, studies by WANG et al have suggested that upregulating VEGF can enhance the bone mineral density of OVX rats.[28] Similarly, Clarkin’s research has shown that upregulating HIF-1α increases the expression of VEGF, leading to an improvement in the bone mineral density of OP rats.[29] Taken together, these findings underscore the pivotal role played by VEGF and HIF-1 signaling pathway in the development of OP.

The TNF signaling pathway is recognized as a crucial inflammatory pathway that contributes to the development of OP. TNF, a proinflammatory factor, plays a significant role in the pathophysiology of this disease[30] and participates in the pathophysiology of the disease including inflammatory responses, apoptosis, autophagy, proliferation, and differentiation.[31–35] TNF directly interacts with the TNF receptor (RANKL) present on the osteoblast precursor cells, which in turn recognizes the RANKL ligand (RANK) on the surface of osteoclasts.[36] This interaction leads to the promotion of differentiation and activation of osteoclasts, resulting in bone resorption. Apart from this, TNF also participates in immune regulation, enhances macrophage activation and antigen presentation, and inhibits osteoblast activity. Furthermore, TNF-α inhibits osteoblast differentiation by down-regulating the expression of Runx2.[37] Studies have shown that reducing the expression of TNF-α can increase the bone mineral density of OVX mice and promote the osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells.[38] CASP-3 is a crucial molecule involved in the degradation stage of apoptosis and is a promising target for treating OP. Liu et al discovered that increasing the expression of CASP-3 reduced apoptosis of osteoblasts in mice and lowered the expression of osteogenic indicators such as ALP, P1NP, and osteocalcin.[39] Inhibition of caspase-3 activity was also found to significantly reduce apoptosis of preosteoblast MC3T3-E1 line by Bellido et al.[40] Additionally, IL6 and IL1B, 2 important inflammatory factors, have been negatively correlated with BMD.[41,42] IL6 and IL1B increase the activity and differentiation of osteoclasts, leading to an increase in bone resorption.[43,44] These findings suggest that the IL6, IL1β, CASP3, and TNF signaling pathways are closely linked to OP.

In summary, The LWDHP treatment of OP involves regulating inflammation, apoptosis, autophagy, proliferation, and differentiation. LWDHP targets AKT1, TP53, IL6, TNF, VEGFA, CASP3, IL1B, MYC, PTGS2, and EGFR for treating OP. TNF, HIF-1, and IL17 are the key signaling pathways of LWDHP for treating OP. The molecular docking results provide evidence of the good binding properties between the components of LWDHP and their respective targets.

5. Conclusion

In this study, we investigated the pharmacological mechanism of LWDHP in treating OP using network pharmacology and molecular docking methods. Our findings suggest that LWDHP acts against OP by targeting multiple pathways and targets. Specifically, we propose that the HIF-1, IL17, and TNF signaling pathways are the primary mechanisms through which LWDHP treats OP. Overall, our study provides valuable insights into the theoretical basis for further research on the mechanism of LWDHP in treating OP and its potential clinical applications.

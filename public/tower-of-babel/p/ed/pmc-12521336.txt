Adoption of polyvinyl alcohol electrospinning materials for skin wound dressing
Authors: Shuang Deng, Xing Huang, Zhanrong Kang, Dafeng Xu, Wen Luo, Dejian Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe effective treatment and repair of skin trauma has always been an important topic of medical research. As an advanced preparation method of nanofibers (NFs), electrospinning (ES) technology shows broad application prospects in the field of skin wound dressing. This review aims to systematically summarize and analyze the process and application research progress of PVA ES materials in skin wound dressing, and explore its mechanism and clinical application prospects in the process of wound healing. By consulting and analyzing the literature about PVA ES materials in recent years, the preparation process, structural properties, and application examples in skin wound dressing were focused on, and the latest experimental research and clinical data were combined to make a comprehensive review. The results suggested that PVA ES material had unique advantages in wound healing. NFs dressings with specific structure and properties can be prepared by adjusting ES parameters. PVA ES dressing not only has excellent physical barrier function, but also can regulate humidity, promote cell proliferation and angiogenesis, thus accelerating wound healing. In addition, the functionally modified PVA fiber dressing also suggested visible effects in antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and other aspects. Visible progress has been made in the application of PVA ES materials in skin wound dressings. However, further optimization of its preparation process, improvement of functional properties, and large-scale clinical trial validation are still the key directions of future research. Through continuous innovation and improvement, PVA ES dressing is expected to play a greater role in the field of skin wound repair and promote the development of regenerative medicine.Graphical Abstract

Introduction

Skin is the largest organ of the human body, and as a barrier between the external environment and internal tissues, it has an important protective role [1]. However, skin trauma, such as burns, cuts, and ulcers, often leads to serious health problems and even causes infections and chronic diseases [2]. Effective treatment and repair of skin trauma has therefore become one of the key topics of medical research.

Among the numerous skin wound repair materials, nanofibers (NFs) dressings have received extensive attention in recent years due to their unique physical and chemical properties [3]. As an advanced method to prepare NFs, ES technology can generate fiber materials with a high specific surface area, good air permeability, and adjustable structure. These characteristics show broad application prospects in the field of skin wound dressing [4, 5]. ES technology is a kind of technology to produce ultrafine fibers by electrostatic force. This method can prepare continuous fibers with diameters ranging from nano to micron, and is widely used in biomedicine, environmental protection, energy storage, and other fields. ES process mainly includes three steps: solution preparation, ES, and fiber collection [6]. First, the spinning solution was prepared by dissolving the polymer in an appropriate solvent. Then, by applying a high voltage, a strong electric field is formed between the needle and the grounded collector. When the electric field force overcomes the surface tension of the solution, the solution ejects a thin stream, forming NFs and depositing on the collector. The ES device is mainly composed of high-voltage power supply, syringe pump, spinning nozzle, and collector. The key process parameters include voltage, spinning solution flow rate, collection distance, environmental temperature, and humidity. These parameters have a visible impact on the diameter, morphology, and properties of fibers.

Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) electrospun materials, as a novel wound dressing, demonstrate significant innovation and uniqueness in the field of skin wound repair [7]. PVA exhibits excellent biocompatibility, water solubility, and biodegradability, along with high chemical stability, enabling it to remain stable in vivo for extended periods without triggering immune responses [8, 9]. Additionally, the hydrogel properties of PVA facilitate the formation of a moist wound microenvironment, promoting cell proliferation and angiogenesis [10], a feature that offers distinct advantages over existing NF dressings. The porous structure and high specific surface area of PVA electrospun materials provide an ideal microenvironment for cell attachment, proliferation, and migration, significantly enhancing wound healing rates [11, 12]. The innovativeness of PVA electrospun materials is further reflected in their tunable structure [13, 14]. By optimizing ES process parameters, fibers with diameters ranging from nanometers to micrometers can be prepared [15], constructing a porous, three-dimensional NF network. This structure not only provides excellent breathability and moisture retention but also effectively prevents bacterial invasion, reducing infection risks. Existing studies demonstrated that PVA electrospun materials can be functionalized by loading antibacterial agents, growth factors, or anti-inflammatory drugs [16–18], thereby endowing them with multifunctional properties such as antibacterial activity, pro-regenerative effects, and anti-inflammatory capabilities for comprehensive regulation of wound healing [19, 20]. Numerous studies validated the efficacy of PVA electrospun materials in accelerating wound healing, reducing scar formation, and lowering infection risks in animal models [21, 22]. Compared to traditional dressings or single-functional NF dressings, PVA electrospun materials exhibit superior comprehensive performance, along with simple preparation processes, low cost, and feasibility for large-scale production [23]. These characteristics endow PVA electrospun materials with not only theoretical innovation but also broad prospects for practical applications in skin wound repair. In summary, as a novel wound dressing, PVA electrospun materials provide a groundbreaking solution for skin wound healing through their unique physicochemical properties, tunable structural characteristics, and potential for functional modification. However, current research primarily focuses on in vitro experiments and animal models, lacking large-scale clinical trial data to substantiate their efficacy and safety in real-world medical applications. Additionally, technical challenges remain in maintaining material stability and long-term effectiveness.

This study fills the gap in existing literature by providing a systematic review and analysis of PVA electrospun (ES) materials in skin wound dressing applications, offering a comprehensive perspective for this field. The research aims to systematically summarize and analyze the preparation and application progress of PVA ES materials in skin wound dressings, while exploring their mechanisms in wound healing and clinical prospects. It not only highlights the unique advantages of PVA ES materials in skin wound repair but also identifies potential challenges in clinical translation. By recognizing current research limitations and proposing key future directions, this study provides valuable insights for advancing regenerative medicine.

Preparation process of PVA ES material

Overview of ES technology

ES is a technology to prepare nano and micro fibers by electrostatic force. This method has been widely used in many fields due to its ability to prepare continuous and uniform ultrafine fibers with high surface area [24]. In Fig. 1, the ES device mainly includes high-voltage power supply, syringe pump, spinning nozzle (needle), and collector. High-voltage power supply can provide high-voltage electric field, usually ranging from thousands of volts to tens of thousands of volts. The syringe pump can control the flow rate of the polymer solution to ensure the stability of the spinning process. Spinning nozzle (needle) applies high voltage through the needle to make the solution form droplets and generate a jet. The collector is usually a grounded flat plate or rotating drum, which is used to collect the fibers formed by spinning. Figure 1 provides a detailed depiction of the ES process and its various types of fiber structures [25]. The ES process includes key steps: solution preparation, high-pressure application, Taylor cone formation, jet and fiber formation, solvent evaporation, and fiber deposition [26]. In the preparation of the solution, first, the polymer is dissolved in a suitable solvent to prepare a uniform polymer solution. The viscosity and surface tension of the solution are important parameters affecting the spinning process. High voltage is applied to the spinning nozzle through a high-voltage power supply [27]. The voltage range is usually between several kilovolts and tens of kilovolts, depending on the nature of the solution and spinning conditions. The high voltage makes the polymer solution form a drop at the end of the nozzle. When the electric field force increases gradually, the droplet will be stretched into a cone shape, called Taylor cone. When the electric field force exceeds the surface tension of the droplet, a thin flow is ejected from the top of the droplet, which is called the ES jet. The jet is continuously stretched and refined during flight to form ultrafine fibers. During the jet flight, the solvent gradually evaporates, and the polymer fibers solidify and deposit on the grounded collector, forming a randomly arranged NFs membrane [28].

The ES process is affected by many factors, such as solution properties, process parameters, and environmental conditions, which determine the morphology and properties of the final fiber. In Fig. 2, the factors affecting ES include ES solution, operating conditions, and environmental factors. The properties of ES solution include liquid viscosity, conductivity, and surface tension. Liquid viscosity affects fiber diameter and uniformity, conductivity affects fiber diameter and distribution, and surface tension affects fiber surface morphology. The operating parameters include voltage, receiving distance, needle size, and spinning solution flow rate. Voltage, needle size, and flow rate of spinning solution all affect fiber diameter, while receiving distance affects solvent volatilization and fiber diameter. Environmental factors include temperature and humidity. Temperature affects solvent volatilization and liquid viscosity, and humidity affects solvent volatilization. The viscosity of the solution should be moderate. Too high or too low will affect the formation and stability of the fiber. A low surface tension will help to form a stable jet, and an appropriate conductivity will help to stabilize the spinning process. Process parameters include voltage, flow rate, and collection distance. Higher voltage helps to form finer fibers, but too high voltage may lead to fiber breakage. The flow rate of the syringe pump should be moderate to ensure the continuity of the spinning process. The schematic diagram of the electric spraying machine is illustrated in Fig. 3. ES is a technique that employs a high-voltage electrostatic field to stretch liquid polymer solutions or melts into nanometer- or micrometer-sized fibers. This technology finds extensive applications across various fields, including textiles, filtration materials, biomedical engineering (e.g., tissue engineering scaffolds), and drug delivery systems. During the ES process, the distance between the spinneret and the collector, environmental conditions (such as temperature and humidity), and appropriate airflow significantly influence the morphology and properties of the final fibers. A shorter distance between the spinneret and the collector results in a higher electric field strength, which may facilitate a faster fiber deposition rate; however, it can also lead to irregular inter-fiber entanglements and the formation of beaded structures. Conversely, an increased distance allows droplets more time to stretch before reaching the collector, potentially yielding fibers that are more uniform and exhibit higher linearity [29]. Higher environmental temperatures can accelerate the evaporation rate of solvents; however, excessively high temperatures may lead to material degradation or adversely affect fiber quality [30]. Elevated humidity levels can hinder solvent evaporation, which may be detrimental to fiber formation. Nonetheless, moderate humidity can enhance the flexibility and plasticity of fibers, particularly for certain sensitive materials [31]. Adequate ventilation aids in expediting solvent evaporation and dissipating heat generated during the ES process, which is beneficial for improving fiber quality. Furthermore, optimal air velocity can facilitate a more uniform distribution of fibers on the collector. For instance, in the fabrication of NF scaffolds for skin regeneration, careful control of the distance between the spinneret and the collector is crucial to ensuring good cell compatibility and appropriate porosity. Maintaining suitable environmental conditions and airflow contributes to the stability of the fibers, thereby providing an ideal environment for cell growth. Additionally, when preparing efficient air or liquid filtration media, precise regulation of processing parameters is essential to achieve a dense and defect-free fiber network. Lower humidity and favorable ventilation conditions can significantly enhance solvent evaporation efficiency, thereby improving the uniformity and mechanical strength of the fiber network.

ES technology can prepare fibers with diameters in the range of nanometers to microns by adjusting the solution properties and process parameters. The high specific surface area is suitable for a variety of applications. A variety of polymer solutions can be used, and even composite fibers can be prepared. The ES device is simple and easy to operate, which is suitable for laboratory and industrial production [32]. Yan et al. [33] proposed that nanofibrous membrane materials prepared by ES have excellent properties suitable for biomedical applications, such as a high specific surface area, strong plasticity, and the ability to manipulate their nanofibrous components to obtain the desired properties and functions. Xu et al. [34] elaborated that ES technology can also derive high-performance electrochemical energy storage devices. Zhao et al. [35] reviewed electrospun NFs (ENFs) electrodes for lithium-ion batteries, showing that ENF materials have the advantages of good continuity, large specific surface area, and high structural adjustability, providing many ideal characteristics for lithium-ion battery electrodes. To overcome chitosan’s inherent electrospinning limitations, Koosha and Mirzadeh employed PVA as a guest polymer, leveraging favorable inter-polymeric interactions [36]. Nanofibers were successfully fabricated from blended solutions, unlike pure chitosan. Processing complexity increased with chitosan dominance (>50% chitosan). Incorporation of cationic chitosan enhanced jet charge density, significantly reducing fiber diameter compared to pure PVA. An optimal chitosan/PVA blend ratio yielded more uniform nanofibers, albeit with reduced tensile strength. Complementing this, Raska et al. combined PVA with silk fibroin to create nanofibrous scaffolds mimicking the meniscus’s collagen architecture, characterized mechanically and morphologically [37]. To enhance osteoinduction, TCP and bioactive carbon dots were incorporated into PCL/PVA scaffolds [38]. Individual addition of either component stimulated osteogenic markers and cell growth, while their synergy produced the highest osteogenic differentiation and proliferation rates. PVA’s utility extends to vascular engineering. Addressing 3D scaffold challenges for hard tissues, Ngadiman et al. integrated electrospun PVA/maghemite nanofibers with 3D-printed structures [39]. The resulting hierarchical porous scaffolds demonstrated high mechanical strength, substantial stiffness, and excellent biocompatibility—properties critical for hard tissue regeneration. ES technology has broad application prospects in skin wound dressing, which can effectively cover the wound, promote wound healing, load drug carriers, and have good biocompatibility. Through continuous optimization and improvement of ES technology and its materials, there will be more innovations and breakthroughs in the field of skin wound dressings in the future, which will promote the development of regenerative medicine and wound treatment.

Characteristics and properties of PVA ES materials

PVA is a water-soluble polymer known for its excellent biocompatibility, biodegradability, and mechanical properties, making it widely applicable in various fields. Figure 4 compares the molecular structures of partially hydrolyzed and fully hydrolyzed PVA. In partially hydrolyzed PVA, the presence of acetyl groups makes the molecular structure more complex, whereas fully hydrolyzed PVA exhibits a more uniform distribution of hydroxyl groups. These structural differences can influence the material’s physical and chemical properties, thereby affecting its performance in various applications. The dissolution process of PVA is complex and involves several stages, which directly impact the properties of the resulting NF materials [40]. The dissolution process of PVA can be categorized into the following stages: affinity wetting, swelling, unlimited swelling, and dissolution. Affinity wetting is the initial step of the dissolution process, during which PVA particles begin to absorb moisture, becoming surrounded by water molecules. Swelling occurs as water further penetrates, causing the volume of PVA particles to expand; however, a substantial number of hydrogen bonds still exist between PVA molecular chains, preventing complete dispersion at this stage. In the unlimited swelling phase, PVA particles continue to absorb water and expand until their volume no longer increases. At this point, the PVA molecular chains begin to loosen but have not yet fully dissolved. Finally, with stirring and an increase in temperature, the PVA molecular chains completely dissociate, resulting in a homogeneous solution. PVA exhibits excellent biocompatibility, indicating that it does not elicit an immune response, making it suitable for use as a biomaterial [41]. Although PVA is not completely biodegradable on its own, controllable degradation can be achieved through chemical modification or blending with other biodegradable polymers. PVA NFs possess high mechanical strength, rendering them suitable for applications that require durability [42]. Furthermore, due to PVA’s good hygroscopic properties, it can absorb moisture in humid environments, which is critical for certain applications, such as wound dressings. PVA NFs demonstrate significant potential for diverse applications across multiple fields. In biomedical applications, PVA NFs can serve as components of wound healing patches, promoting healing by providing a moist environment. Additionally, owing to their biocompatibility and mechanical strength, PVA NFs are utilized as scaffolds in tissue engineering, facilitating the growth of new tissues. Furthermore, by embedding drugs within PVA NFs, sustained drug release can be achieved, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy [43, 44]. In terms of environmental protection, PVA NFs are utilized as adsorbent materials in water treatment processes due to their excellent water absorption and chemical stability, effectively removing harmful substances [45, 46]. Similarly, PVA NFs can be employed in air purification systems as efficient filtration media to eliminate pollutants from the air. In industrial applications, PVA NFs can be incorporated into other materials to enhance the overall strength and toughness of composites [47]. Additionally, coating textiles or other substrates with a layer of PVA NFs can impart additional functionalities to the materials, such as water repellency or stain resistance[48]. PVA demonstrates significant comprehensive advantages and innovative potential as a matrix for electrospun wound dressings: Its exceptional water solubility eliminates the dependency on complex solvents required by other biopolymers (such as chitosan or polylactic acid), significantly simplifying the fabrication process and enhancing process stability[49]. Simultaneously, PVA serves as a versatile integration platform. Diverse functional enhancements can be achieved through: efficient blending with polymers like chitosan or hyaluronic acid, flexible loading of antimicrobial agents/bioactive factors (such as silver nanoparticles or vascular growth factors), and surface modification techniques like plasma treatment. Furthermore, the selection of PVA with different degrees of hydrolysis (Fig. 4) provides a unique dimension for the precise tuning of material flexibility, mechanical strength, and degradation behavior.

[59] engineered a multifunctional PVA-lysine nanofibrous membrane for concurrent skin regeneration and antimicrobial activity. Mouro et al. incorporated Chelidonium majus L. extract into polycaprolactone/PVA/pectin nanofibers via needleless emulsion electrospinning [60]. Post-functionalization mats maintained structural integrity with negligible mechanical alterations while demonstrating antibacterial efficacy against S. aureus and P. aeruginosa. Cytocompatibility was confirmed during short-term culture, and the system enabled sustained release of the bioactive extract throughout extended testing periods. In summary, PVA ES materials, with their excellent biocompatibility, biodegradability, mechanical properties, and multifunctional properties, provide an important material basis for skin wound repair and other biomedical applications, and have broad application prospects and research values.

Application of PVA ES dressing in skin trauma

Mechanism of action during wound healing

PVA ES dressing has played a major role in skin wound healing due to its unique physical, chemical, and biological properties. Skin wound healing is a complex and highly coordinated biological process, which is usually divided into four stages (Fig. 8): (1) hemostasis stage: after the occurrence of trauma, the hemostasis mechanism is immediately started, and platelets aggregate to form blood clots to stop bleeding. (2) Inflammatory stage: leukocytes (such as neutrophils and macrophages) migrate to the wound site, clear pathogens and necrotic tissues, and release cytokines to initiate the healing process. (3) Proliferation stage: fibroblasts proliferate, synthesize collagen and matrix, and form granulation tissue. Angiogenesis and epithelial cell migration cover the wound. (4) Remodeling stage: granulation tissue turns into mature scar tissue, collagen fibers rearrange, and the wound gradually recovers structure and function. PVA electrospun dressings actively orchestrate the wound healing process through synergistic multi-mechanistic actions, significantly surpassing the passive coverage model of traditional dressings [61]. During the hemostasis phase, their high surface-area nanofibrous network forms a dense physical barrier, more effectively blocking pathogen invasion than conventional gauze, while sustained antibacterial protection is achieved through loaded antimicrobial agents such as silver ions [41]. For the inflammation phase, the innovative incorporation of anti-inflammatory factors like IL-10 or natural active ingredients actively suppresses excessive inflammatory responses. In the proliferation phase, PVA’s exceptional hydrophilicity maintains an optimal moist environment, while the nanofiber topology precisely mimics the ECM, significantly promoting directional cell migration and proliferation—a dual advantage not afforded by chitosan or PLA-based dressings [62]. Furthermore, the three-dimensional scaffold serves as a controlled-release carrier for vascular growth factors such as VEGF, enabling spatiotemporally precise intervention in angiogenesis. Addressing the remodeling phase, integration of a localized sustained-release system for anti-scarring drugs directly regulates collagen deposition patterns, circumventing the side effects of systemic drug delivery [63]. Crucially, the PVA platform supports cutting-edge synergistic therapeutic strategies (Figs. 9 and 10), including bilayer heterogeneous structures coordinating stage-specific repair demands, and combined photothermal-gas therapy synergistically eliminating drug-resistant bacteria while accelerating tissue regeneration, highlighting its breakthrough value as an intelligent therapeutic platform.

The application of nanoparticles in therapeutic drugs, especially in accelerating the recovery of skin lesions, has become a research hotspot in recent years. Nanoparticles can promote wound healing through a variety of mechanisms. Nano ion mode refers to the fact that nanoparticles exert their biological effects and promote wound healing by releasing specific metal ions or other active ingredients. The mechanism of nano ions’ influence on wound healing includes antibacterial effect, anti-inflammatory effect, promoting cell proliferation and migration, enhancing angiogenesis, drug delivery, and controlled release. The application of nanoparticles in therapeutic drugs can markedly accelerate the recovery of skin damage through the nano ion mode. These nanoparticles not only have good antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties, but also promote cell proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis, thereby effectively accelerating the wound healing process [64]. ES is an efficient and low-consumption method for the preparation of NFs. PVA ES dressing can rapidly cover the wound, form a physical barrier, and prevent further bleeding and external pollutants from entering the wound. By incorporating antibacterial agents (such as silver nanoparticles and antibiotics), PVA ES dressing can effectively inhibit the growth of pathogens and reduce the risk of infection. Some modified PVA NFs can regulate the local inflammatory response, reduce the release of inflammatory mediators, and alleviate the inflammatory response. Figure 9 provides a detailed illustration of the application of ES combined with PVA in the skin wound healing process and its gradual degradation [65]. The NFs prepared by ES possess a high specific surface area and excellent biocompatibility, which promote cell attachment and proliferation. PVA provides good mechanical strength and degradation characteristics, ensuring that the material gradually degrades during the healing process and is replaced by newly formed tissue. As the bilayer biomatrix gradually degrades, new skin tissue forms and fills the wound area, ultimately achieving complete wound healing. This design of the bilayer biomatrix is of significant importance in the biomedical field, as it effectively promotes wound healing, reduces scar formation, and improves both healing quality and speed. Xie et al. (2024) [66] investigated the application and mechanism of PVA combined with ES technology in wound healing, as shown in Fig. 10. This study demonstrated the healing process of an infected wound treated with PVA/CS/HTCC NF membranes. The PANI micro-rod in the NFs membrane releases nitric oxide (NO) under near-infrared (NIR) irradiation. The sustained release of NO promotes wound healing [67], while the NF membrane also exhibits photothermal therapy (PTT) antibacterial properties, capable of killing bacteria in the wound. This combination of NO therapy and PTT antibacterial properties significantly enhances the healing effect of infected wounds and holds promising application value. Adeli et al. [68] pointed out that ENF mats have visible morphological similarities with natural ECM. As a wound dressing for skin trauma, it can provide a suitable moist environment for the wound, so as to achieve appropriate wound respiration and effectively deal with wound exudates. PVA NFs have a high specific surface area and a three-dimensional structure, which is conducive to the attachment, proliferation, and migration of fibroblasts and keratinocytes, and accelerates the formation of granulation tissue and epithelial tissue [69]. Palani et al. [70] indicated that ENFs were synthesized from polymers with bioactive compounds for wound healing. Metal nanoparticles play an increasingly important role in promoting effective wound healing and preventing secondary bacterial infection. They are small in size and large in surface area, helping to enhance biological interactions and penetration at the wound site. These nanoparticles are specifically designed for local drug delivery and are capable of sustained release of therapeutic molecules, such as growth factors and antibiotics. This targeted approach ensures optimal cell-cell interactions, proliferation, and vascularization, and promotes effective and controllable wound healing. George et al. [71] pointed out that biocompatible ENFs have high porosity and surface area, which can achieve efficient drug loading and delivery, and can be used as a viable candidate for chronic wound care. The antibacterial properties of ENFs, coupled with their moisture absorption performance and wound healing ability, make them an effective material for wound dressing applications. PVA ES dressing has good air permeability and moisture retention performance, which can maintain a moist environment of the wound, promote cell proliferation and migration, and prevent the wound from drying and crusting [72, 73]. By loading growth factors (such as VEGF) or regulating the microenvironment of angiogenesis, PVA ES dressing can promote angiogenesis, increase blood supply to the wound, and support tissue repair. PVA NFs provide a scaffold that can regulate the function of fibroblasts, promote collagen synthesis and fiber rearrangement, and reduce scar formation. PVA ES dressing can be used as a drug carrier, slow-release anti-scarring drugs, or other regulatory factors to further optimize the healing process. Homaeigohar and Boccaccini [74] proposed that the NFs dressing provided a high surface area and was able to locally deliver antibiotics and antibacterial agents into the wound environment to control infection. Miguel et al. [75] and Pilehvar-Soltanahmadi et al. [76] indicated that ES nanomaterials are beneficial to wound healing. PVA ES dressing plays a major role in skin wound healing through a variety of mechanisms, including providing a physical barrier, regulating the microenvironment, promoting angiogenesis, and enhancing cell function. Its unique advantages make it a promising wound repair material with a wide range of clinical application potential.

Comparison with other materials and technologies

Contrast between PVA and other polymer materials

PVA, polylactic acid (PLA), and polycaprolactone (PCL) are polymer materials commonly used in ES and biomedical applications. PVA has good water solubility, which makes it easy to process and functionalize. Its good biocompatibility can be well compatible with biological tissues, reducing immune reactions [77]. Wounds are particularly vulnerable to microbial invasion and pathogen infection. Therefore, to reduce the risk of wound infection, researchers have spent a lot of energy on developing advanced therapeutic dressings, such as ES membranes containing antibacterial agents. Mouro et al. [78] developed a PVA and chitosan (CS) coating material containing Agrimonia eupatoria L. (AG) that can be used as a wound dressing. Ardekani et al. [79] pointed out that the incorporation of CS/PVA/gel NFs mats is a promising alternative to traditional wound dressings. PVA NFs have high tensile strength and good ductility, which can provide sufficient mechanical support. It can also be degraded in vivo, and the degradation products are non-toxic, reducing the need for a second surgery to remove implants. Its excellent film-forming property can form uniform and dense NFs membranes. PVA is a kind of environmental protection material that will not produce harmful substances during its production and use. It is widely used in wound dressing, drug delivery, tissue engineering, protective film, and other fields. PLA has excellent biocompatibility and biodegradability. The degradation product is lactic acid, which is easily metabolized by the human body. In terms of mechanical properties, PLA has good mechanical strength and rigidity, and is suitable for applications requiring higher mechanical properties. PLA is a thermoplastic polymer that can be formed by thermal processing and is suitable for injection molding, extrusion, spinning, and other processes [80]. Ilyas et al. [81] elaborated that PLA composites can be used as an alternative to non-renewable petroleum-based materials. Zhu et al. [82] also described PLA as a renewable and biodegradable green polymer material. PLA has good transparency and is suitable for transparent packaging materials and medical devices. It is widely used in degradable medical devices (such as sutures, bone fixators), tissue engineering scaffolds, drug delivery systems, and food packaging materials [83, 84]. PCL has good biocompatibility and biodegradability, and the degradation products are non-toxic, which is suitable for long-term implantation. It has excellent flexibility and ductility and is suitable for soft tissue engineering and flexible implants [85, 86]. PCL has a low melting point, is easy to process and shape, and is suitable for ES, injection molding, extrusion, and other processes. Siddiqui et al. [87] proposed that PCL, as a biomaterial produced by a scaffold, has many advantages, including adjustable biodegradation and relatively high mechanical toughness at physiological temperature. ES generates an NFs porous matrix, which mimics many properties of natural tissue ECM in terms of surface area, porosity, and fiber arrangement. By combining PCL with other biomaterials to form a composite scaffold for bone regeneration, the biocompatibility and hydrophilicity of PCL NFs can be improved. PCL has a longer degradation time and is suitable for implants that need long-term support. It is commonly used in soft tissue engineering scaffolds, drug delivery systems, bone repair materials, and sustained-release drug implants. Siddiqui et al. (2018) [88] elaborated that the composite scaffold matrix of PCL is widely used in tissue engineering. Lu et al. [89] elaborated that PCL/β-TCP scaffolds prepared by the 3DP method, combined with precisely controlled pore preparation structure, good biocompatibility, and suitable biodegradation mechanical properties, provide the necessary ability for bone tissue engineering.

PVA, PLA, and PCL have their own unique characteristics and advantages, which are suitable for different application fields. PVA is especially suitable for wound dressings and drug delivery systems because of its water solubility and biocompatibility. PLA is widely used in medical devices and tissue engineering due to its high mechanical strength and excellent biocompatibility. PCL is suitable for soft tissue engineering and long-term implants because of its flexibility and long degradation time. By selecting appropriate materials, the performance and function of biomedical products can be optimized according to specific application requirements (Table 1). While PLA and PCL hold established value in implantable devices such as bone fixation and long-term support, the innovation of PVA in wound dressing applications lies in its irreplaceable physiological regulation capabilities [90]. Its exceptional hydrophilicity constructs an ideal moist environment, effectively mitigating the dehydration risks associated with the inherent hydrophobicity of PLA/PCL. Furthermore, PVA offers unparalleled versatility for functional integration, enabling the simultaneous incorporation of antimicrobial agents, growth factors, and smart-responsive components like thermosensitive polymers—a feat challenged by the hydrophobic nature of PLA/PCL, which impedes efficient compounding [91]. The aqueous processing characteristics of PVA significantly reduce production costs and toxic residue concerns. These distinct advantages establish PVA as the superior platform for developing actively healing dressings, particularly promising for managing complex wounds with high exudate levels and multifaceted intervention needs, such as chronic ulcers and burns [92].

Challenges and future research directions

Optimization of the preparation process

Voltage, solution flow rate, and the distance between the spinning head and the collector are the key parameters that affect the fiber morphology and diameter. Optimizing these parameters can obtain NFs with a uniform diameter and a smooth surface. The concentration and viscosity of the PVA solution directly affect the stability and fiber morphology of the spinning process. It is necessary to determine the optimal solution concentration and viscosity through experiments to obtain the ideal fiber structure. Temperature and humidity have important effects on the ES process. In Fig. 11, the future directions include optimizing parameters, controlling environmental conditions, improving functionalization, developing three-dimensional structured NFs, multifunctional PVA NFs dressings, and optimizing equipment. Controlling environmental conditions can improve spinning repeatability and fiber quality. By designing multi-layer NFs membranes, the integration of different functions can be realized. For example, the outer layer has antibacterial property, the middle layer has drug release function, and the inner layer has healing property. By adjusting the trajectory of the spinning head or using a special collector, the fiber orientation can be controlled, and the mechanical properties and cell guidance of the membrane can be enhanced. The development of high-throughput, multi-nozzle ES equipment can improve fiber production efficiency and meet the needs of large-scale production. Keeping the stability and consistency of the spinning process in batch production is the key to prepare high-quality NFs membranes.

Further research on functional modification

Further research on functional modification doping silver nanoparticles, antimicrobial peptides, or antibiotics into PVA NFs can markedly enhance their antibacterial properties and prevent wound infection. Through plasma treatment or chemical modification, the introduction of antibacterial groups on the surface of NFs can improve the antibacterial effect and prolong the antibacterial action time. Loading growth factors (such as VEGF, bFGF) and anti-inflammatory drugs into PVA NFs and developing dressings with slow-release function can improve the wound healing effect and patient comfort. In the future, NFs scaffolds with three-dimensional structure can also be developed, which can better simulate the natural tissue structure and provide a good microenvironment for cell growth and tissue repair. The introduction of temperature-responsive materials (such as PNIPAM) and the development of NFs dressings with temperature-responsive function can adjust the drug release rate according to the change of body temperature. By introducing pH-responsive materials (such as PAA), dressings that can regulate drug release under different pH conditions can also be developed to adapt to pH changes during wound healing. It should develop PVA NFs dressings that integrate antibacterial, drug release, cell guidance, and other functions to provide a comprehensive wound care solution. It should develop NFs dressings with an intelligent response function, which can automatically adjust drug release and treatment strategies according to the wound condition. By optimizing the preparation process and functional modification, the application prospect of PVA ES material in skin wound dressing will be broader, and it is expected to provide a more efficient and safer solution for wound care.

Conclusion

Building upon the comprehensive study, PVA electrospinning materials redefine the therapeutic paradigm for wound dressings through multi-dimensional innovations. Their unique hydrophilicity and functionalizable hydroxyl backbone enable the efficient integration of antimicrobial agents such as silver nanoparticle-loaded particles, anti-inflammatory factors (IL-10), and vascular growth factors (VEGF), overcoming the passive limitations of traditional dressings to achieve active synergistic intervention combining “antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and pro-angiogenic” functions. The composite design incorporating photothermal conversion materials (polyaniline) and gas-releasing agents (GSNO) (Fig. 10) further pioneers combined photothermal-NO therapy, effectively eradicating drug-resistant biofilms and accelerating infected wound repair. The nanofibrous network precisely mimics the topological structure of the ECM, significantly promoting directional cell migration, while a bilayer heterogeneous design (Fig. 9) enables programmable release of active components, precisely matching stage-specific healing requirements. These properties demonstrate breakthrough potential in managing refractory wounds: for diabetic foot ulcers, they maintain a moist environment and reverse cellular dysfunction within the hyperglycemic microenvironment; for drug-resistant infected wounds, synergistic photothermal-chemodynamic action penetrates biofilm barriers; for extensive burns, the flexible and conformable fibrous membrane minimizes mechanical damage. Future development requires overcoming scaling bottlenecks via multi-nozzle high-throughput spinning systems, constructing biomimetic hierarchical channels using 3D printing, and creating intelligent systems responsive to inflammation biomarkers such as MMP-9. These advances will ultimately propel PVA-based dressings from “material upgrades” towards a closed-loop “sense-respond-regulate” system for precise therapy, leading to a paradigm shift in regenerative medicine. PVA ES materials have shown visible potential in skin wound repair. PVA ES material has good biocompatibility and biodegradability, excellent mechanical properties, and multifunctional integration: as an environmental protection material, PVA will not produce harmful substances during its production and use, which is conducive to environmental protection. PVA ES material in skin wound repair can promote tissue regeneration, prevent wound infection, and drug-loaded PVA NFs can achieve drug release, improve patient compliance. PVA ES material can be functionally modified according to different wound needs, and is suitable for burn, ulcer, postoperative wound, and other wound types.

In future research, it can optimize the preparation process, study the spinning effect under different temperature and humidity conditions, establish the best environmental parameters, and ensure the stability and repeatability of the spinning process. It should develop efficient and multi-nozzle ES equipment to meet the needs of large-scale production and improve production efficiency and product consistency. By introducing antibacterial agents, growth factors, and drugs, PVA NFs dressings with antibacterial, healing-promoting, and drug release functions will be developed. Further in vivo and in vitro performance evaluation will be carried out to ensure its safety. Through in-depth research and improvement in the above directions, PVA ES materials will have a broader application prospect in skin wound repair, and can provide more efficient and safer solutions for wound care.

Utilising summative assessments to enhance learning in medicine: a valvular heart disease teaching course for a specialty practical exam
Authors: Ahmed Alsinbili, Ganira Ahmadova
Published: 2024

Introduction

Valvular heart disease (VHD) is an important area of cardiology and is of high interest in clinical assessment for junior doctors and specialty exams due to its accessibility through good clinical skills. However, it receives less attention than other areas of cardiology, such as ischaemic heart disease or heart failure. Despite being a popular topic in exams, VHD knowledge is not as broad among non-specialists.1 To address this, a course was designed to focus solely on VHD, covering examination, imaging, and management. It is tailored to the practical assessment of clinical examination skills (PACES), which is a standardised medical specialty practical exam required for all medical specialty trainees.

Methods

The project was run as a quality improvement project (QIP) utilising the plan, do, study, act (PDSA) cycle technique (Fig 1). Firstly, a multi-platform survey was conducted among physicians preparing for the PACES exam, asking about the most challenging cardiological aspects and whether they would like a specific resource for valvular heart disease. Afterwards, short recorded videos were designed using Microsoft PowerPoint and made available online. Following that, the course was advertised through common junior physician groups, with a request for feedback via Microsoft Forms. Finally, analysis of feedback was done with Jamovi 1.8.2 and word-cloud techniques.2, 3, 4

Results and discussion

Between 1 January 2022, and 31 August 2022, six areas of VHD were identified and 72 short videos were produced, which had 354 total views. Twenty formal feedback forms were collected and analysed (Fig 2). Learners reported statistically significant growth in their knowledge of the subject (Δ-value of 68.5%, p<0.001), as well as growth in their knowledge of echocardiography (TTE) (Δ-value of 72.5%, p<0.001). All respondents had high to very high confidence that what they learned would be reflected in their clinical practice, and that they enjoyed the teaching and would highly recommend it to a colleague.

Word cloud of data collection showing most watched videos, revealing high interest in left heart valves and regurgitation pathology.

Utilising TTE to teach VHD has been tried before on a smaller scale and shown significant learning.5 In this course, the same was tried on a larger scale and with the incentive of linking it to a summative assessment (PACES), which is a proven method to increase learning and engagement.6 The results of this project prove that it is possible to utilise mandatory medical exams as an opportunity to facilitate learning of more challenging subjects such as echocardiography and use this new tool to deepen understanding and knowledge, thereby achieving the primary objective of learning with the direct benefit of preparing for a summative test.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that linking a teaching course to a summative exam leads to high levels of learning and engagement, which can be used to facilitate teaching complex aspects of clinical medicine. It also suggests a learning opportunity for TTE and VHD among physicians preparing for the PACES exam.

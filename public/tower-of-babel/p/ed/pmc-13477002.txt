Sodium bicarbonate attenuates myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury via activation of the splenic cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway
Authors: Emily P Rabinovich, Matthew Weber, Radhika Rastogi, Katherine M Marsh, Aimee Zhang, Irving L Kron, Zequan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe spleen plays a central role in mediating inflammatory responses during myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) via a neuroimmune axis involving vagal signaling and cholinergic modulation. Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) has been implicated in the activation of a splenic cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, but its role in myocardial IRI remains incompletely defined. The present study aims to determine whether NaHCO3 administration before reperfusion attenuates myocardial injury by modulating splenic inflammatory signaling.MethodsC57BL/6 mice underwent 40 minutes of left coronary artery occlusion, followed by 60 minutes of reperfusion. Ten minutes before reperfusion, mice received either intravenous phosphate-buffered saline, NaHCO3 (1 mEq/mL), or osmolality-matched hypertonic saline (5.8% NaCl). Additional groups received intraperitoneal NaHCO3 and a topical splenic or liver NaHCO3 rinse (1mEq/mL, 1 μL/g). Infarct size was quantified by triphenyltetrazolium chloride-phthalo blue staining as a percentage of risk region. Cytokines, acetylcholine levels, splenic signaling pathways, and monocyte polarization were assessed.ResultsAssessed risk region was comparable across groups. Intravenous NaHCO3 administration significantly reduced infarct size compared with phosphate-buffered saline and hypertonic saline controls (25 ± 3% vs 51 ± 4% and 46 ± 3%; P < .05). Intraperitoneal and topical splenic NaHCO3 produced similar infarct-sparing effects (P < .05), whereas topical hepatic rinse had no impact. NaHCO3 reduced inflammatory cytokines, reduced circulating plasma citrate synthase, increased plasma acetylcholine, and promoted M2 polarization of splenic monocytes. Arterial blood gas analysis demonstrated increased bicarbonate without significant changes in systemic pH.ConclusionsAcute NaHCO3 administration attenuates myocardial IRI through modulation of the splenic cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. These findings identify bicarbonate as a potential neuroimmune-targeting strategy for limiting reperfusion injury.Key Words: myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury, sodium bicarbonate, neuroimmune regulation, splenic immunomodulation, cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway

Sodium bicarbonate reduces myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury via splenic signaling.

Central Message

Sodium bicarbonate attenuates myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury by activating the splenic cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, decreasing resultant myocardial infarct size and tissue damage.

Perspective

Sodium bicarbonate attenuates myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury by modulating the splenic cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. In a murine model, bicarbonate reduced infarct size and promoted anti-inflammatory monocyte polarization, identifying a novel neuroimmune mechanism of cardioprotection and suggesting a potential therapeutic strategy targeting the cardiac-splenic axis.

Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) elicits a robust sterile inflammatory response that significantly increases infarct size (IS) beyond the effects of ischemic injury alone, with reperfusion-associated mechanisms responsible for up to 50% of the total myocardial damage after infarction.1 Recent studies indicate that the spleen is a central mediator of this systemic inflammatory cascade during both sterile and nonsterile events.2,3 In the setting of myocardial IRI, splenic activation promotes the mobilization of inflammatory leukocyte populations and amplifies cytokine signaling during reperfusion, thereby exacerbating myocardial injury and contributing to maladaptive postischemic remodeling.4, 5, 6, 7

Neuroimmune regulation of the inflammatory response represents a promising translational therapeutic target. The vagus nerve modulates systemic inflammation via the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. Within this pathway, splenic sympathetic signaling and β2-adrenergic receptor (β2AR) activation on immune cells have been proposed as upstream mediators of downstream cholinergic immunomodulation, including mechanisms involving acetylcholine-producing choline acetyltransferase (ChAT)-positive T cells and suppression of proinflammatory cytokine production.8,9 In preclinical models, activation of the vagal-splenic axis has been shown to inhibit spleen-mediated proinflammatory responses and attenuate myocardial IRI.5,10, 11, 12 Although vagal modulation has demonstrated cardioprotective effects in preclinical models,13 clinically feasible pharmacologic strategies to engage splenic cholinergic signaling during the reperfusion window remain incompletely defined.

Recent work has demonstrated that oral sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) has the potential to activate splenic cholinergic signaling and promote an anti-inflammatory immune phenotype.14, 15, 16 These effects are to be mediated through splenic nerve signaling, which transmits NaHCO3-induced immunomodulatory changes to the spleen.15 Clinically, NaHCO3 is commonly administered during cardiac resuscitation for cardiac arrest, sepsis, and hyperkalemia.17,18 However, limited data exist regarding whether acute systemic NaHCO3 administration can modulate splenic neuroimmune pathways to reduce myocardial IRI.

To expand upon these findings, in the present study we evaluated whether NaHCO3 administration limits myocardial IRI when delivered systemically or directly to the spleen, using either an intravenous (IV) bolus and topical splenic application. By repurposing a widely used, cost-effective therapeutic agent with an established safety profile, the authors explore a potentially translatable strategy to reduce myocardial tissue damage after ischemia-reperfusion. The authors hypothesized that NaHCO3 would modulate splenic neuroimmune signaling by engaging β2-adrenergic-cholinergic pathways to suppress the spleen-mediated proinflammatory response, thereby attenuating myocardial postischemic reperfusion injury and limiting postischemic myocardial damage.

Methods

Ethical Approval

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the University of Virginia Animal Care and Use Committee (protocol number 3943-08-23), and all procedures were performed in compliance with institutional and federal guidelines for humane animal care. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the Guide for Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (eighth edition, National Institutes of Health, 2011). This study did not involve human participants, human tissue, identifiable private information, or patient data. Therefore, institutional review board approval was not required, and informed written consent for publication of patient data was not applicable.

Animals and Experimental Design

Male and female C57BL/6 wild-type mice (8-12 weeks; The Jackson Laboratory) were used. Myocardial IRI was induced via 40 minutes of left coronary artery (LCA) occlusion followed by 60 minutes of reperfusion. A 40-minute coronary occlusion interval was selected on the basis of established murine myocardial IRI models demonstrating reproducible, severe infarction of sufficient magnitude to detect treatment effects while preserving the ability for short-term survival and recovery during reperfusion.19,20 IS was assessed either at the end of ischemia or at the end of reperfusion.

Animals received treatment 10 minutes before reperfusion to ensure therapeutic exposure during the earliest phase of reperfusion, when inflammatory amplification is initiated, and modulation of the early inflammatory phase of reperfusion injury is most relevant. Mice were randomized and assigned to 1 of the following groups: (1) IV phosphate-buffered saline (PBS; vehicle control; 1 μL/g weight), (2) NaHCO3 solution (1 mEq/mL, 1 μL/g; equivalent to 1 mEq/kg), or (3) hypertonic saline (HTS; 5.8% NaCl; osmolality-matched control, 1 μL/g) via external jugular vein, intraperitoneal injection of (4) high-dose NaHCO3 (0.5 mEq/mL, 20 μL/g; equivalent to 10 mEq/kg) or (5) low-dose NaHCO3 (0.25 mEq/mL, 20 μL/g; equivalent to 5 mEq/kg) to assess dose-response and route-specific effects, or topical (6) splenic or (7) hepatic rinse with NaHCO3 (1 mEq/mL; 1 μL/g applied locally). A topical hepatic NaHCO3 rinse was included as an anatomical control to determine whether localized NaHCO3 exposure produced nonspecific visceral effects or spleen-specific immunomodulation. The selected NaHCO3 concentrations were chosen to assess a systemic or topical concentration of 1 mEq/mL and lower intraperitoneal concentrations delivered at larger volumes to allow for dose-response assessment across routes, while limiting confounding effects of abrupt systemic alkalinization.

Topical treatments were performed via a laparotomy incision. At the conclusion of the 60-minute course of reperfusion, blood was harvested via right ventricular puncture, and hearts were harvested for triphenyltetrazolium chloride blue staining. A parallel cohort of non-IRI mice received IV PBS, IV NaHCO3, or topical splenic NaHCO3 treatment; spleen and blood samples were collected 10 minutes after treatment.

Surgical Induction of Myocardial Infarction in Intact Mice

The surgical procedure used in this study has been previously described by Tian and colleagues.4 In brief, mice were anesthetized with intraperitoneal tribromoethanol (Avertin; 250 mg/kg), placed in a supine position, orally intubated with a PE-60 tube, and mechanically ventilated (130 breaths/min; tidal volume 8-10 μL/g weight). A left thoracotomy was performed through the third and fourth intercostal spaces to expose the heart. An 8-0 PROLENE suture was placed beneath the LCA, approximately 1 mm inferior to the left atrium, and tied over PE-60 tubing to induce occlusion of the LCA for 40 minutes. Successful ischemia and LCA occlusion were confirmed by marked electrocardiogram changes, including ST-segment elevation and QRS widening (PowerLab Data Acquisition System; ADInstruments), as well as regional myocardial color change within the risk region. Reperfusion was achieved by releasing the ligature and removing the PE-60 tubing.

Postoperative analgesia consisted of ketoprofen (4 mg/kg subcutaneously). Core temperature was continuously monitored throughout the operation via rectal probe and maintained between 36.0 and 37.0 °C.

Determination of Myocardial IS

At study completion (40 minutes of ischemia followed by 60 minutes of reperfusion), mice were killed humanely under deep anesthesia, and hearts were excised and cannulated via the ascending aorta with a blunted 23-gauge needle. Hearts were sequentially perfused with 3 mL of 37 °C 1% triphenyltetrazolium chloride in PBS (pH = 7.4). The LCA was then re-occluded, followed by cardiac perfusion with 0.3 to 0.5 mL of 10% phthalo blue (Heubach Ltd) to delineate nonischemic myocardium.21 Left ventricles were sectioned into 5 to 7 transverse slices, fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, weighed, and photographed. The sizes of the nonischemic area, the risk region, and the infarct area were calculated as a percentage of the corresponding slice multiplied by the weight of the slice.4, 5, 6,22 Investigators performing IS quantification and molecular analyses were blinded to treatment allocation.

Cytokine Measurements

Plasma cytokines were measured in samples collected after 40 minutes of ischemia followed by 60 minutes of reperfusion, a time point selected to capture the early systemic inflammatory response during reperfusion, including direct ischemic damage, oxidative stress, and immediate reperfusion effects.23,24 Plasma concentrations of interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were measured using commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits (Proteintech) according to the manufacturer's specifications.

Plasma Citrate Synthase (CS) Expression

Plasma concentrations of CS, a mitochondrial matrix enzyme and established marker of oxidative capacity, were determined by Western Blot analysis using standardized electrophoresis and immunodetection using an anti-CS antibody (Abcam). Band intensity was expressed relative to control samples.

Acetylcholine and ChAT

Plasma and splenic tissue acetylcholine levels were quantified using the EnzyChrom Acetylcholine Assay Kit (BioAssay Systems). Splenic ChAT tissue expression was assessed by Western Blotting (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Arterial Blood Gas Analysis

Arterial blood was collected via left ventricular puncture and analyzed using an i-STAT analyzer (Abbott).

Splenic Monocyte Polarization Markers

Splenic monocyte polarization markers were evaluated by Western Blot, including M1 markers (inducible nitric oxide synthase [iNOS]) and M2 markers (CD206, Arg1, TGM2), using equal aliquots of splenic tissue from each experimental group. Splenic tissue aliquots were homogenized under identical conditions. iNOS was selected as a marker of classically activated, proinflammatory monocyte/macrophage polarization, and CD206, Arg1, and TGM2 were selected as complementary markers associated with alternatively activated, inflammation-resolving phenotypes.25, 26, 27 Total and phosphorylated Akt (pAkt), as well as β2AR expression, were assessed using their commercially available corresponding antibodies (Cell Signaling Technology, Abcam, Proteintech, or Invitrogen).

Statistical Analysis

The primary end point was IS expressed as a percentage of risk region. Secondary end points included inflammatory cytokine levels, acetylcholine signaling, splenic molecular signaling, and monocyte phenotype.

Group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons or unpaired Student t tests, as appropriate. Paired t tests were used for heart rate comparisons. Data distribution assumptions were verified before parametric testing. Statistical analyses were conducted using Prism 7 (GraphPad Software). Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM).

Results

Intravenous NaHCO3 Attenuates Myocardial IRI

Risk regions (area-at-risk as percentage of left ventricular mass) were comparable between PBS control and treatment groups (P = NS; Figure 1, A). Myocardial IS (% risk region) was 51 ± 4% in PBS-treated mice and 46 ± 3% after HTS administration (P = NS). In contrast, IV NaHCO3 treatment significantly reduced IS to 25 ± 3%, representing a 51% reduction compared with PBS controls (P < .05; Figure 1, A and B). Importantly, IS was not reduced in the osmolality-matched HTS group, indicating that the cardioprotective effect of NaHCO3 was independent of hyperosmolar or sodium-mediated mechanisms.

Plasma CS, assessed via Western Blot (Figure 1, D), was significantly elevated in PBS-treated control mice, consistent with mitochondrial injury. Compared with PBS controls, plasma CS band intensity decreased by 95.9% after IV NaHCO3 (540 vs 13,031 arbitrary densitometry units [AUs], P = .0014) and by 97.3% after topical splenic NaHCO3 (350 vs 13,031 AUs, P = .0013), with no significant difference between treatment routes (P = .337).

Intravenous NaHCO3 significantly increased base excess and bicarbonate levels, without inducing systemic alkalemia (P < .05; Table 1). Intraperitoneal and Splenic-Targeted NaHCO3 Attenuates Myocardial Injury

Both low- and high-dose intraperitoneal NaHCO3, as well as topical splenic NaHCO3 rinse, significantly reduced IS compared with IV PBS controls (P < .05; Figure 1, C). In contrast, topical hepatic NaHCO3 did not reduce myocardial IS (51 ± 2% vs PBS control, P = NS; vs topical splenic rinse, P < .05), supporting a spleen-specific effect.

NaHCO3 Reduces Inflammatory Cytokine Levels After Myocardial IRI

Both IV and splenic-targeted topical NaHCO3 treatments significantly reduced circulating levels of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α at 60 minutes of reperfusion (Figure 2, A-D). No significant differences in cytokine production were observed between systemic and topical, splenic-targeted administration.

NaHCO3 Modulates Splenic Cholinergic Signaling and Activates Vagal-Splenic Axis

In non-IRI, normal, wild-type C57BL/6 mice, IV NaHCO3 increased acetylcholine levels in both plasma and splenic tissue (Figure 3, A and B) and enhanced splenic expression of β2AR, ChAT, and pAkt (Figure E1). In non-IRI wild-type mice, IV NaHCO3 increased plasma (21.6 vs 15.8 μM, P = .022) and splenic tissue acetylcholine (55.0 vs 50.3 μM/g, P = .007) compared with PBS. Topical splenic NaHCO3 rinse also significantly increased plasma acetylcholine (17.7 vs 15.8 μM, P = .027) but did not significantly alter splenic tissue acetylcholine levels (52.7 vs 50.3 μM/g, P = .091) compared with PBS (Figure 3). Both IV and topical splenic NaHCO3 enhanced splenic β2AR (12,246 and 12,833 AUs vs 6098 AUs, respectively, P < .001) and ChAT expression (20,062 and 24,528 AUs vs 13,063 AUs, P < .001). Intravenous NaHCO3 increased phosphorylated AKT signaling, measured as the pAkt/Akt ratio (0.073 ± 0.04 vs 0.572 ± 0.04, P = .030), whereas topical splenic NaHCO3 did not (0.499 ± 0.02 vs 0.572 ± 0.04; P = .138) compared with PBS (Figures E1, C, and E2).

NaHCO3 Alters Splenic Monocyte Polarization

IV NaHCO3 significantly increased the expression of M2-associated markers (CD206, TGM2, Arg1) (Figure E2, A-C), an effect not reproduced by the topical bicarbonate splenic rinse. However, topical rather than IV splenic NaHCO3 reduced the expression of the M1 marker iNOS (Figure E2).

Discussion

In this murine model of myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury, acute systemic NaHCO3 administration significantly attenuated myocardial IRI and identified splenic neuroimmune modulation as a potential mechanism of cardioprotection. The infarct-sparing effect was observed after IV, intraperitoneal, and topical splenic administration, supporting a model in which NaHCO3 engages the cardiac-splenic axis to mitigate inflammatory amplification during reperfusion. Mechanistically, these effects were associated with signaling consistent with activation of the vagal-splenic cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, including increased acetylcholine signaling, β2-AR–dependent downstream activation, and a shift in splenic monocyte polarization toward an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype. Together, these changes likely contributed to attenuation of proinflammatory responses and cytokine-mediated myocardial injury during reperfusion. These results expand upon emerging concepts in neuroimmune regulation of myocardial injury and suggest that a widely used clinical therapy may serve as a useful adjunct in targeting the inflammatory process during the early reperfusion window.

Evaluation of administration route demonstrated comparable infarct-sparing effects with intraperitoneal and topical NaHCO3 administration, suggesting that cardioprotection is not limited to IV delivery but may instead reflect engagement of spleen-mediated immunomodulatory mechanisms. Nevertheless, route remains highly relevant from a translational perspective; although IV administration is clinically feasible, topical splenic application should be interpreted primarily as a mechanistic intervention to localize the spleen as an effector organ rather than a directly applicable therapeutic strategy in patients with myocardial IRI. Importantly, the cardioprotective effect was not attributable to differences in osmolality or changes in systemic acid-base status, as arterial pH and blood gas parameters remained unchanged. Both systemic and splenic-targeted NaHCO3 applications were associated with modulation of splenic immune signaling toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype, consistent with activation of cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathways and attenuation of proinflammatory responses during early reperfusion.

In addition to IS reduction, NaHCO3 administration was associated with biochemical evidence of preserved mitochondrial integrity. During myocardial IRI, mitochondrial disruption significantly compromises organelle structure and function. Plasma CS, a central enzyme of the tricarboxylic acid cycle and surrogate marker of mitochondrial injury during ischemia reperfusion,28 was significantly elevated in control mice but reduced after both IV and topical splenic NaHCO3 administration. These data suggest that NaHCO3-mediated cardioprotection extends beyond IS reduction to preservation of mitochondrial structure and function. Given the established relationship between inflammatory amplification and mitochondrial dysfunction during reperfusion, the reduction in circulating CS likely reflects the attenuation of inflammation-mediated cellular injury.

An increasing body of evidence supports a central role for the spleen in mediating inflammatory responses during IRI of the heart, lung, liver, and kidney.4,5,7,29, 30, 31 Previous studies have identified a cardiac-splenic axis in myocardial IRI, in which damage-associated molecular patterns released from ischemic myocardium activate splenic leukocytes, leading to the mobilization of inflammatory monocytes and amplification of cytokine signaling during reperfusion.4,22,32 Activation of splenic monocytes through pathways such as caspase-1/NLRP3/IL-1β contributes to downstream neutrophil recruitment and exacerbation of myocardial injury.4, 5, 6, 7,33 These observations suggest that interventions targeting splenic signaling may interrupt this neuroimmune amplification and attenuate downstream myocardial damage after IRI.

Previous work has also demonstrated that activation of a cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway through vagal–splenic signaling can attenuate inflammatory responses during myocardial IRI.10,11 Interventions such as cervical vagal stimulation, pulsed ultrasound, and topical hypothermia have been shown to modulate splenic immune responses and reduce myocardial injury.2,10,11 More recently, oral NaHCO3 has been reported to activate splenic cholinergic signaling and shift immune responses toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype.14, 15, 16 In contrast, previous ex vivo studies exposing isolated cardiomyocytes to high concentrations of NaHCO3 suggested potential detrimental effects.34 However, these experimental models evaluated direct cellular exposure under nonphysiologic conditions and, therefore, may not capture the true, integrated systemic immunomodulatory effects observed with in vivo administration. Our in vivo findings suggest that NaHCO3-mediated cardioprotection is primarily indirect, acting through neuroimmune modulation as opposed to direct myocardial effects.

Consistent with this interpretation, IV NaHCO3 reduced circulating cytokine levels and attenuated myocardial injury without inducing systemic alkalemia. Although IL-10 has classically been described as anti-inflammatory, its parallel reduction with pro-inflammatory cytokines suggests a generalized attenuation of inflammatory activation during reperfusion rather than an isolated modulation of individual cytokine pathways.35 The infarct-sparing effect of NaHCO3 was not attributable to solution osmolality, as an osmolality-matched HTS control failed to reproduce the protective effect. Similar protective effects were observed with topical splenic application but not with hepatic application, further supporting an organ-specific modulation rather than a nonspecific visceral mechanism.

Both systemic and splenic-targeted NaHCO3 increased plasma acetylcholine levels, enhanced splenic β2-adrenergic receptor and choline acetyltransferase expression, and altered splenic monocyte polarization toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype. The observed shift in splenic polarization markers suggests that NaHCO3 may also have a role in reprogramming the splenic monocyte compartment toward a less inflammatory and more reparative phenotype in addition to blunting cytokine release. In the context of myocardial IRI, this shift may reduce the IL-1β and TNF-α amplification of tissue injury and favor a reparative signaling, thereby limiting leukocyte-mediated secondary myocardial damage.

IV, but not topical, NaHCO3, increased the acetylcholine levels in splenic tissue, suggesting a possible route-dependent difference in local cholinergic activation. NaHCO3 administration increased splenic expression of β2AR and ChAT, the enzyme responsible for ACh synthesis (Figure E1), consistent with activation of cholinergic anti-inflammatory signaling.36 Changes in splenic monocyte polarization markers, including increased M2-associated markers and modulation of phosphorylated Akt signaling, indicated that both IV and topical splenic NaHCO3 promote an anti-inflammatory phenotype, although potentially through distinct mechanistic pathways. pAkt signaling remains relevant in this context, as it integrates cardiomyocyte survival signaling and immunoregulatory cues downstream of β2-adrenergic and cholinergic stimulation, which has also been linked to macrophage polarization and suppression of inflammatory activation.37 Together, these findings suggest that NaHCO3 administration results in downstream effects consistent with engagement of β2-adrenergic–associated cholinergic signaling axis within the spleen to modulate inflammatory responses during myocardial IRI.9 Although IV and topical administration appeared to engage partially distinct signaling pathways, these findings are consistent with activation of cholinergic splenic immunoregulation. To the author's knowledge, these findings are the first to demonstrate that acute IV application of NaHCO3 modulates the spleen-mediated inflammatory response and attenuates postischemic reperfusion injury.

From a translational mechanistic perspective, the observation that bicarbonate administered before reperfusion reduces injury severity is particularly relevant to surgical and critical care settings, where IRI may be anticipated. Potential clinical applications include primarily systemic intervention during percutaneous coronary intervention for acute myocardial infarction, cardiac surgery with aortic crossclamping, transplantation-associated reperfusion, extracorporeal circulation, and resuscitation after cardiac arrest, where the timing of reperfusion is predictable.38 Sodium bicarbonate's ability to target this type of anti-inflammatory signaling pathway during the early reperfusion window may represent an affordable, widely used, clinically feasible adjunct to existing myocardial protection strategies.

Limitations

The findings of this study must be evaluated in the context of several limitations. Both male and female mice were included across experimental groups; however, the study was primarily powered to detect treatment effects rather than formal sex-stratified interaction analyses. Future studies should incorporate power analyses to determine sex-specific differences in splenic signaling or responsiveness to the proposed agent. Although these findings support a spleen-dependent mechanism, definitive causal involvement of the vagal-splenic axis was not definitively established by methods such as vagotomy or splenectomy. Splenic tissue pH changes were not directly measured, and the role of sodium bicarbonate transport mechanisms in splenic leukocyte modulation was not evaluated. The potential involvement of mesothelial signaling pathways that activate the vagosplenic axis, previously implicated in oral NaHCO3–induced immunomodulation,14 was also not assessed. The short reperfusion duration also limits the assessment of long-term remodeling or functional myocardial outcomes. In addition, topical splenic application is not directly clinically translatable but provides mechanistic insight into the role of the spleen during myocardial IRI. Future studies are needed to evaluate oral and systemic NaHCO3 strategies in clinically relevant models and patient populations.

Conclusions

In murine models, systemic NaHCO3 administration attenuates myocardial IRI by modulating spleen-mediated inflammatory responses. These findings suggest that NaHCO3 promotes anti-inflammatory splenic signaling consistent with activation of the vagal-splenic cholinergic pathway and induction of M2 monocyte polarization, thereby reducing inflammatory amplification during reperfusion and preserving myocardial function following an ischemic injury. Collectively, these results repurpose sodium bicarbonate as a potential neuroimmune-targeting strategy and suggest that modulation of the cardiac-splenic axis may represent a clinically accessible approach to mitigate myocardial reperfusion injury. This approach may be particularly relevant for cardiac surgery and critical care settings, where reperfusion timing is more predictable.

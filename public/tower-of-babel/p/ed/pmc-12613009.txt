Clinical Impact of Prosthesis Oversizing in Self-Expanding Intra-Annular Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement
Authors: Andrea Ruberti, Giovanni Occhipinti, Matias Mon Noboa, Luis Nombela-Franco, Daijiro Tomii, Thomas Pilgrim, Manel Sabaté, Endaitz Estebanez, Nieves Gonzalo, Pilar Jimenez-Quevedo, Gabriela Tirado-Conte, Laura Sanchis, Omar Abdul-Jawad Altisent, Eduardo Flores-Umanzor, Francisco Campelo-Parada, Xavier Freixa, Ander Regueiro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAmong patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), valve sizing is essential to guarantee procedural success and long-term durability of transcatheter heart valves (THVs). The optimal sizing strategy for self-expanding (SE) intra-annular THVs remains unclear. The aim of this study is to evaluate the impact of prosthesis oversizing on procedural and clinical outcomes in patients undergoing TAVR with the Portico and Navitor THVs.MethodsThis retrospective, multicenter study included patients who underwent TAVR with SE intra-annular THVs between October 2017 and September 2024. Patients were stratified by the median oversizing value (14%) into low- (≤14%) and high- (>14%) oversizing groups. The primary endpoint was technical success. Secondary outcomes included 30-day device success, permanent pacemaker implantation, cardiovascular mortality, heart failure–related hospitalization, and valve dysfunction at 12 months.ResultsA total of 400 patients (mean age 83.0 ​± ​6.0 years; 65.8% female) were included. Technical success was higher in the low-oversizing group (96.7 vs. 92.1%; p ​= ​0.048). Valve embolization occurred only in the high-oversizing group (0 vs. 2.1%; p ​= ​0.035). No significant differences were observed in 30-day device success (93.0 vs. 88.3%; p ​= ​0.171) and moderate-to-severe paravalvular regurgitation (2.9 vs. 1.8%; p ​= ​0.724). No differences emerged in permanent pacemaker implantation rates (24.5 vs. 25.7%; p ​= ​0.712). At 12 months, cardiovascular mortality (4.6 vs. 4.9%; p = 0.884) and heart failure hospitalization rates (6.9 vs. 4.9%; p = ​0.497) were similar.ConclusionsIn patients undergoing TAVR with SE intra-annular THVs, low-oversizing (≤14%) was associated with higher technical success and fewer valve-related complications.

Introduction

Prosthetic valve sizing for transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) is a critical component of preprocedural planning. Improper sizing, either undersizing or oversizing, can lead to suboptimal outcomes, including paravalvular regurgitation (PVR), elevated transvalvular gradients, and annular injury.1 Among these, PVR is consistently associated with increased morbidity and mortality, highlighting the clinical importance of accurate sizing.2 Unlike nonmodifiable anatomical factors such as left ventricular outflow tract eccentricity or extensive annular calcification, oversizing is a modifiable parameter, determined by device specifications and operator discretion. For balloon-expandable valves, an annular oversizing between 5 and 10% is generally recommended and can be achieved by adjusting the inflation volume of diluted contrast (i.e., underfilling or overfilling).3,4 Conversely, for self-expanding valves (SEVs), intraprocedural sizing adjustment is not possible, making preprocedural valve size selection a crucial determinant for technical success.5 In earlier-generation CoreValve/Evolut R and more recent Evolut PRO/PRO+ and FX (Medtronic, Minneapolis, Minnesota), an inverse relationship between high oversizing (>20%) and PVR has been reported.6 However, for other intra-annular SEVs (e.g., Portico and Navitor), the evidence linking valve oversizing to technical success or clinical outcomes remains limited.

This study aimed to assess the impact of different valve sizing strategies using the intra-annular Portico and Navitor transcatheter heart valve (THV) systems (Abbott Structural Heart, Santa Clara, California, USA) on technical success and clinical outcomes.

Methods

Study Cohort and Data Collection

This is a retrospective, multicenter, international study that included data from 4 university hospitals across Europe (Spain, France, and Switzerland) from October 1, 2017, to September 30, 2024. Inclusion criteria were (a) patients with symptomatic severe native aortic stenosis as defined by international guidelines7,8; with (b) an indication for transcatheter intervention as determined by the local multidisciplinary Heart Team; and (c) undergoing TAVR with self-expanding intra-annular Portico or Navitor valve systems (Abbott Vascular, Santa Clara, California, USA). Exclusion criteria were (a) patients undergoing TAVR with other THV systems; (b) patients with failed surgical or transcatheter bioprosthesis; (c) patients undergoing TAVR for other indications (e.g., aortic regurgitation); and (d) patients undergoing TAVR with a Navitor Vision 35 ​mm system.

All baseline, clinical, periprocedural, and follow-up data were collected from medical records documentation from referring physicians and/or telephone interviews at each center. Data were anonymized and recorded in an electronic database. In cases of data incompleteness or inaccuracies, 2 investigators (A.Rub. and A.Reg.) issued mandatory queries to all participating centers.

All patients provided written informed consent. The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. Due to the nature of the study, specific informed consent was waived by each local ethics committee.

Imaging Details

All included patients underwent comprehensive preprocedural echocardiographic workup according to the recommendations of the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging using commercially available ultrasound transthoracic and/or transesophageal systems.9 Postprocedural and follow-up valve assessment included evaluation of structure, function, and hemodynamics of both prosthetic valve and ventricles. Criteria for prosthetic aortic valve regurgitation included semiquantitative Doppler parameters, according to the Valve Academic Research Consortium 3 criteria.10 Potential bioprosthetic valve failure related to structural valve deterioration was assessed based on changes in echocardiographic parameters from postprocedural to planned follow-up echocardiograms. Potential cases of ambiguity in echocardiographic parameter interpretation were carefully reviewed and adjudicated by experts at each center.

Electrocardiogram-gated multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) was performed using commercially available scanners. Image analysis was conducted offline using dedicated workstations by a trained physician. Annular measurements were obtained with semiautomated software (3Mensio, Pie Medical, Maastricht, the Netherlands), with manual corrections, as needed. Annular perimeter was used to guide valve selection.10 Prosthetic valve oversizing was calculated using the following validated formula: oversizing (%) ​= ​([valve perimeter ​− ​annulus perimeter]/Annulus perimeter) × 100, as performed in prior studies in SEVs.8

Procedural Details

All procedures were performed according to institutional standards at each participating center. The Portico THV was used until May 2021, after which the Navitor THV became the predominant device following Conformitè Européenne mark approval. Valve size was chosen according to the manufacturer’s instructions for use, based on the aortic annular perimeter measured by MDCT. The decision to perform predilatation and postdilatation was left at the operator’s discretion. In particular, nonuniform frame expansion, more than mild PVR, or an invasive mean gradient ≥20 ​mmHg across the THV were reasons for balloon postdilatation.

Definitions

Valve performance and procedural and clinical outcomes were defined on the basis of the Valve Academic Research Consortium 3 definitions.10 Technical success was defined at exit from the procedure room as follows: (1) freedom from mortality; (2) successful access, delivery of the device, and retrieval of the delivery system; (3) correct positioning of a single prosthetic heart valve into the proper anatomical location; and (4) freedom from surgery or intervention to the device or a major vascular, access-related, or cardiac structural complication.10 Device success at 30 days was defined as: (1) technical success; (2) freedom from mortality; (3) freedom from surgery or intervention related to the device or to a major vascular, access-related, or cardiac structural complication; and (4) intended performance of the valve (mean gradient <20 ​mmHg, peak velocity <3 ​m/s, Doppler velocity index ≥0.25, and less than moderate aortic regurgitation).10 Bioprosthetic valve dysfunction was defined as structural or nonstructural valve dysfunction, valve thrombosis, endocarditis, or structural valve deterioration.10

“Intermediate annulus size” was defined as annular dimensions that could accommodate 2 different valve sizes, resulting in varying degrees of oversizing depending on the manufacturer’s sizing charts.11

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was technical success. Secondary outcomes included device success at 30-day follow-up, permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantation rate, cardiovascular mortality, heart failure–related hospitalization, and valve dysfunction at 12-month follow-up.

Statistical Analysis

Prosthetic valve oversizing served as the primary independent variable in the analysis. Valve oversizing was calculated using the previously described formula. Patients were stratified by the median oversizing value into low (below the established median) and high (above the median) oversizing in line with previous studies.6 An exploratory analysis further classified oversizing into 3 categories: 0% to 10%, 11% to 20%, and >20%. In addition, a subanalysis was also performed in patients with an “intermediate annulus size,” comparing low- (≤median) versus high- (>median) oversizing within this group. Baseline technical, clinical, and echocardiographic variables were compared between groups. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, and continuous variables as mean ​± ​SD or median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on the distribution. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests.

Between-group comparisons for categorical variables were performed using the chi-square or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were compared using the Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate.

A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using R software (version 4.1.3; 2027-07-10).

Primary Endpoint

A total of 326 patients (81.5%) underwent TAVR with the Navitor THV and 74 (18.5%) with the Portico THV, with no significant differences among oversizing groups (78.9 vs. 84.5% of patients treated with the Navitor in low- and high-oversizing groups, respectively, p ​= ​0.169). Overall, technical success was high (94.5%). The low-oversizing group showed a significantly higher technical success rate (96.7 vs. 92.1% in low- and high-oversizing groups, respectively, p ​= ​0.048). All prosthetic valve-related complications, particularly valve embolization, occurred in the high-oversizing group (0 vs. 2.1% in low- and high-oversizing groups, respectively, p ​= ​0.035), as summarized in Table 3. No significant differences emerged regarding other procedural aspects, including predilatation (low-oversizing: 92.8% vs. high-oversizing: 92.7%, p ​= ​0.853) and postdilatation rates (low-oversizing: 21.1% vs. high-oversizing: 22.0%, p ​= ​0.852). In-hospital mortality was low (1.3%), with no significant difference between groups (low-oversizing: 0.5% vs. high-oversizing: 2.1%, p ​= ​0.198). There were also no significant differences in PPM implantation rates (low-oversizing: 24.5% vs. high-oversizing: 25.7%, p ​= ​0.712), stroke (low-oversizing: 1.0% vs. high-oversizing: 2.1%, p ​= ​0.431), major vascular complications (low-oversizing: 3.3% vs. high-oversizing: 4.7, p ​= 0.487), acute kidney injury (low-oversizing: 4.8% vs. high-oversizing: 4.7%, p ​>0.99), and moderate-to-severe PVR at discharge (low-oversizing: 1.5% vs. high-oversizing: 1.1%, p >0.99). Secondary Endpoints

Thirty-Day Outcomes

Three-Group Stratification and Intermediate Annulus Subanalysis

No significant differences were observed across the 3 oversizing groups with respect to procedural characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, or clinical outcomes at 30 ​days and 12 ​months. The majority of patients (76%) were in the 11%-20% oversizing group, while 17 and 7% were in the <10% and >20% groups, respectively (Supplementary Tables S1–S3).

A total of 273 patients (68%) were eligible for the intermediate annulus size subanalysis. In this subgroup, a trend toward higher technical success was observed in the low-oversizing group compared with the high-oversizing group (95.0 vs. 91.4%, p = 0.240), mainly driven by more frequent valve-related complications in the latter: specifically, valve embolization (2.6 vs. 0%; p = 0.072), need for a second THV (3.3 vs. 0%; p = 0.044), and surgical conversion (1.3 vs. 0% in high-oversizing group and low-oversizing group, respectively, p = 0.205). This finding was confirmed by statistical significance in favor of low-oversizing strategy achieved by assessment of technical success excluding vascular access complications (100 vs. 95.4%; p = 0.017). No significant differences were observed across cohorts in relation to PPM implantation rates, valve performance, and clinical outcomes at 30 ​days and 12 ​months (Supplementary Tables S4-S6).

PVR after TAVR remains a significant clinical concern due to its association with increased morbidity and mortality.12, 13, 14, 15 Due to the expansion of the TAVR spectrum to patients with a longer life expectancy,7,8 improving technical success and prosthetic valve performance, thus minimizing PVR, has become mandatory.16,17 THV oversizing is a crucial factor influencing PVR rates, conduction disturbances, and overall valve performance.2 Studies performed for the Edwards SAPIEN 3 balloon-expandable THV identified a 5%-10% annular area oversizing as effective in reducing PVR incidence.18,19 Conversely, a higher oversizing degree (>20%) appears to correlate with reduced PVR rates in patients undergoing TAVR using the self-expandable Evolut devices.6,20,21 This difference likely reflects the fact that SEVs, lacking balloon-assisted deployment, rely on greater radial force to ensure adequate stabilization and conformity within the aortic annulus.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the optimal oversizing degree using self-expandable intra-annular Abbott THV systems in a large real-world cohort. Previous reports included data from a small cohort of patients undergoing TAVR exclusively with the Portico.22 The rates of PVR reported in our study are consistent with previous registries and the more recent PORTICO NG Study, indicating a very good performance of the self-expanding intra-annular THV system in minimizing PVR after TAVR.23, 24, 25, 26

Contrary to findings in prior analyses with Evolut platforms,6,20,21 and with the Portico,22 in our study high-oversizing was not associated with improved device success or reduced rates of moderate-to-severe PVR compared to the low-oversizing group. Additionally, low oversizing was associated with significantly higher technical success and a lower incidence of periprocedural valve embolization. This may be explained by the increasing radial force expressed by the self-expanding Abbott THVs during deployment and suboptimal valve expansion related to high oversizing. Although no bench data are available specifically for the Navitor, in vitro mechanical testing and computational modeling have demonstrated that the radial force of SEVs increases as the annular diameter decreases, that is, with greater oversizing, resulting in higher deployment forces.27,28 While this radial force ensures anchoring, it may also lead to nonuniform expansion and altered leaflet mechanics in borderline or highly oversized anatomies. Low oversizing seems therefore to allow for complete valve expansion and apposition to the annulus and aortic wall, reducing the need for recapturing and the risk for embolization, while contemporarily reducing radial stress on the annulus. This translates into a reduction of mechanical complications, thereby improving in-hospital outcomes.

Notably, oversizing is a pivotal factor influencing conduction disturbances and the need for PPM after TAVR, due to the damage of the membranous septum.29 A high oversizing has been previously associated with increased risk of high-degree atrioventricular block and early PPM implantation.30,31 However, in line with evidence from patients treated with both earlier- and new-generation Evolut THV platforms, our study did not demonstrate this association.6,20,29,32 The observed 25.1% PPM rate exceeds recent reports for both Portico and Navitor, which have shown rates up to 16.8%, depending on device generation and implantation technique.24,33,34 This likely reflects real-world anatomical variability, baseline conduction disorders, and heterogeneity in implantation depth across centers. A median oversizing degree of 14% in patients requiring PPM after TAVR—the same as those not requiring PPM—supports the notion that factors such as valve model (earlier vs. new-generation devices), technical features (e.g., implantation depth), and patient-related baseline characteristics (e.g., native aortic valve calcification, pre-existing conduction abnormalities) may have a greater impact on postprocedural conduction disturbances than oversizing itself.35, 36, 37

In conclusion, our study further reinforces the importance of optimal oversizing in TAVR procedures. In patients with annular dimensions falling between 2 prosthesis sizes (“intermediate annulus size”), the optimal THV sizing remains challenging. Our findings, reinforced by a dedicated subanalysis in this specific cohort of patients, suggest that selecting a device with ≤14% oversizing in this setting may improve THV implantation success while maintaining favorable hemodynamic performance. Notably, all valve-related complications—including embolization, need for a second valve, surgical conversion, and annulus rupture—occurred in intermediate annulus size patients treated with a high-oversizing strategy, underscoring the challenge that this cohort represents in TAVR procedures. Future research should further explore outcomes in “intermediate annulus size” cases with extreme undersizing or oversizing. Importantly, a single oversizing threshold is unlikely to apply across valve types and patient populations; individualized strategies considering device characteristics and anatomy appear more appropriate. Moreover, the long-term implications of oversizing remain uncertain, warranting dedicated investigation before definitive recommendations can be established.

Study Limitations

Several limitations must be considered when interpreting the results of this study. First, this is a retrospective analysis from a multicenter registry. The relatively small sample size of patients may have underpowered the study to draw firm conclusions, and results should be considered hypothesis-generating. This study is based on preprocedural MDCT and lacks post-TAVR MDCT. Moreover, this is a real-world registry reporting the clinical practice in different centers in Spain, France, and Switzerland; therefore, echocardiographic parameters and outcomes have been reported without core lab adjudication, and the follow-up modalities are different within the different participant centers. Specific data on conduction disturbances or depth of implantation were not available for some of the sites. We had only complete data on pacemaker implantation for each participating center, the most relevant clinical outcome for this analysis study. Lastly, the findings are specific to the real-world practice patterns in a specific region and may not be generalizable to other populations with differing clinical or procedural expertise.

Conclusions

In a real-world setting of patients with severe aortic stenosis treated using the self-expanding intra-annular THVs, low-oversizing (≤14%) was associated with higher technical success while preserving favorable valve performance compared to high-oversizing strategy (>14%).

Metabolic–photoimmunotherapy: A Shikonin–NIR-I photosensitizer nanoplatform reprograms glycolysis to potentiate phototherapy-induced antitumor immunity in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Weilong Yang, Xue Liu, Hao Hu, Qiuxia Chen, Yilan Dai, Feng Fang, Huijiao Fu, Ling Lin, Huiyi Lu, Zhiqiang Yu, Jiongshan Zhang, Yan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a formidable clinical challenge, plagued by a profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) and high recurrence rates (50–70%). In this study, we developed a multifunctional DSPE-mPEG2000-based nanoplatform (PB-4@SK NPs) to co-deliver Shikonin (SK), a potent glycolytic inhibitor, and PB-4, a novel first near-infrared (NIR-I) polymeric photosensitizer. This nanoplatform enables a synergistic metabolic–photoimmunotherapy paradigm that transcends the limitations of conventional monotherapies. Upon 660 nm laser irradiation, PB-4 mediates robust dual-mode phototherapy via concurrent photodynamic and photothermal effects, generating singlet oxygen (1O2) and localized hyperthermia to trigger potent immunogenic cell death (ICD). Simultaneously, the targeted release of SK suppresses the Pyruvate Kinase M2/hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (PKM2/HIF-1α) signaling axis, thereby reversing the Warburg effect and alleviating tumor hypoxia. A self-amplifying feedback loop is established: PB-4-derived ROS reinforce SK-mediated metabolic inhibition, while SK-mediated Heat shock protein 70(HSP70) suppression attenuates cytoprotective thermoresistance, collectively maximizing apoptotic responses. Our findings demonstrate that PB-4@SK NPs effectively remodel the TME by polarizing M2 macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, enhancing CD8+ T cell infiltration, and, crucially, inducing robust long-term immune memory. This rationally designed strategy provides a promising and translationally relevant approach for eradicating primary tumors and preventing post-treatment relapse in HCC.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), Pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), Shikonin, NIR-I photosensitizer PB-4, Photodynamic therapy (PDT)

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the predominant histological subtype of primary liver cancer, accounting for 75–85% of cases globally. As a formidable public health challenge, HCC records over 800,000 new diagnoses and nearly 750,000 deaths annually, ranking as the sixth most prevalent malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1,2]. Despite therapeutic advances, the five-year survival rate remains below 20%. The etiology of HCC is intricately linked to chronic liver injury and cirrhosis, stemming from hepatitis B/C (HBV/HCV) infections, excessive alcohol intake, and metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD). Notably, MAFLD and its progressive stage, nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), have emerged as leading risk factors in developed nations, driving a continuous surge in the global HCC burden [[3], [4], [5], [6]].

Curative interventions, including surgical resection, liver transplantation, and local ablation, offer potential efficacy primarily at early stages; however, their long-term success is severely hindered by limited organ availability and high post-operative recurrence. For advanced HCC, current standards involve locoregional interventions like transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), systemic tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., sorafenib, lenvatinib), and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [[7], [8], [9], [10], [11]]. Nevertheless, therapeutic outcomes remain suboptimal due to pronounced intratumoral heterogeneity, drug resistance, and a profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME). A critical clinical hurdle is the alarmingly high recurrence rate—ranging from 50% to 70% within five years post-treatment—which necessitates repeated interventions and compromises overall survival [[12], [13], [14]]. Emerging evidence indicates that targeting tumor metabolic reprogramming, specifically glycolytic inhibition, can reshape the TME and synergize with immunotherapy to overcome these therapeutic barriers [15].

Recent advances have demonstrated that ROS-generating nanoplatforms can serve as powerful tools to initiate ICD, thereby transforming the “cold” tumor microenvironment into an “immunologically hot” one [16]. ICD triggers the exposure and release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), such as calreticulin (CRT), high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), and adenosine triphosphate (ATP). These DAMPs function as “find-me” signals (e.g., ATP and HMGB1) to recruit immune cells and “eat-me” signals (e.g., CRT) to facilitate dendritic cell (DC) maturation, thereby priming tumor-specific T-cell responses [[17], [18], [19]]. However, the therapeutic potential of PDT is often restricted by the intrinsically hypoxic TME. To circumvent this, photothermal therapy (PTT) has been integrated as a synergistic modality. Near-infrared (NIR)-triggered hyperthermia not only induces direct thermal ablation but also alleviates hypoxia by promoting vasodilation and enhancing blood perfusion, which in turn augments ROS production during PDT [20,21].

Shikonin (SK), a natural naphthoquinone derivative, has emerged as a potent adjuvant to amplify PDT/PTT-induced ICD via a dual-action mechanism. First, it drives metabolic reprogramming by inhibiting pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), which restricts cytosolic ATP production, reverses the Warburg effect, and downregulates hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) to mitigate hypoxia. Second, SK exerts profound immunomodulatory effects by reducing the infiltration of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and enhancing DAMP exposure, thereby revitalizing systemic antitumor immunity [22,23].

Despite the potential synergy between SK and photo-therapeutics, their clinical translation is hindered by suboptimal pharmacological profiles, including the poor aqueous solubility of SK and the rapid systemic clearance of traditional photosensitizers. Nanotechnology-based delivery systems offer a robust strategy to overcome these hurdles. By exploiting the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, nanocarriers facilitate the synchronized co-delivery and tumor-selective accumulation of both agents. Such systems enable spatiotemporally controlled activation under NIR irradiation, while SK-mediated suppression of HIF-1α maintains a favorable oxygenation status, ultimately achieving synergistic ICD enhancement through balanced ROS generation, hyperthermia, and DAMP release [[24], [25], [26]].

In this study, we designed a multifunctional DSPE-mPEG2000-based nanoplatform (PB-4@SK NPs) that co-encapsulates SK and a novel NIR-I absorbing conjugated polymer photosensitizer, designated as PB-4 (Fig. 1A, S1). The therapeutic efficacy of PB-4@SK NPs is derived from three integrated mechanisms: (1) dual-mode phototherapy, where PB-4 mediates concurrent PDT and PTT under NIR irradiation to trigger robust ICD via ROS bursts and localized hyperthermia; (2) metabolic–immune synergy, involving SK-mediated PKM2/HIF-1α inhibition to reprogram the glycometabolic landscape and remodel the immunosuppressive TME; and (3) precision delivery, ensuring synchronized tumor targeting while minimizing off-target toxicity. Notably, beyond primary tumor eradication, this strategy is engineered to elicit durable systemic antitumor immunity and establish long-term immune memory. By fundamentally reshaping the TME, our nanoplatform aims to address the pivotal clinical challenge of HCC recurrence, providing a sustained therapeutic benefit for long-term survival.

Preparation and characterization of PB-4@SK NPs

The novel NIR-I polymeric photosensitizer PB-4 was successfully synthesized by integrating a conjugated electron-donor backbone with flexible non-conjugated segments (Fig. S1). This hybrid architecture overcomes the rigidity constraints of traditional photosensitizers (e.g., Ce6), enhancing assembly adaptability while retaining efficient light-harvesting capabilities. The molecular structures and purity of the intermediate and PB-4 were rigorously validated by 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance (13C NMR) spectroscopy (Figs. S2 and S3). Crucially. Notably, the N+ cation is precisely located on the pyridinium-based heterocyclic units of the polymer backbone (Fig. 1A S1). Due to the limited solubility of the polymer in common GPC solvents, the number-average molecular weight (Mn) of PB-4 was determined to be approximately 12.5 kDa via 1H NMR end-group analysis. Optical characterization revealed maximum absorption at 660 nm and a distinct NIR-I emission peak at 782 nm in Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (Fig. S4), supporting its application in fluorescence imaging-guided therapy with minimized tissue autofluorescence.

PB-4 and SK were co-assembled within a DSPE-mPEG2000 matrix via a refined nanoprecipitation method, forming stable core–shell nanostructures. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) further confirmed a uniform spherical morphology (Fig. S5), which is advantageous for evading reticuloendothelial clearance and achieving prolonged circulation and tumor-specific accumulation via the EPR effect [27]. Hydrophobic interactions between the conjugated domains of PB-4 and the SK skeleton induced a surface potential shift from positive to negative during encapsulation. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis confirmed a uniform distribution with an average hydrodynamic diameter of 122.53 ± 0.71 nm and a polydispersity index (PDI) of 0.186 ± 0.011 (Fig. 2A). The zeta potential was measured at −38.26 ± 0.31 mV in ultrapure water. This significant negative surface charge is primarily attributed to the deprotonated phosphate groups of the anionic DSPE-mPEG2000 shell, which effectively encapsulates the cationic pyridinium-based PB-4 core. The high absolute value of the zeta potential provides strong electrostatic repulsion between the nanoparticles, ensuring the excellent colloidal stability of the PB-4@SK NPs in aqueous media. The slight variations in zeta potential observed in PBS and 10% FBS are primarily driven by the Debye screening effect and the formation of a protein corona, respectively (Fig. 2B). Over 7 days, PB-4@SK NPs exhibited excellent stability in Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Fig. 2C–S6). The observed compression of the zeta potential in physiological media is attributed to the Debye screening effect in PBS and protein corona formation in FBS, respectively, demonstrating high resistance to aggregation under biological conditions. The PB-4@SK NPs exhibited high co-encapsulation efficiency, with PB-4 and SK encapsulated at 81.3 wt% and 52.6 wt%, corresponding to drug-loading contents of 6.78 wt% and 4.38 wt%, respectively. Successful co-loading was verified by UV–Vis absorption spectroscopy (Fig. 2D). An in vitro release study under physiological conditions (PBS, 37 °C) showed a sustained release profile for SK, with approximately 80% cumulative release over 72 h (Fig. S7), suggesting potential for prolonged therapeutic efficacy [28,29]. Notably, 660 nm laser irradiation significantly accelerated the release rate (Fig. S7), confirming the light-triggered drug delivery capability of the nanoplatform.”

The phototherapeutic performance of PB-4@SK NPs was further validated. Upon 660 nm laser irradiation (1 W/cm2, 30 cm), the NP dispersion (1 mg/mL) exhibited a rapid photothermal response, with the temperature rising from 27.2 °C to 65.8 °C within 5 min (Fig. 2E–S8A). The photothermal conversion efficiency (η) was calculated to be 68.25% (Fig. 2F–S8B), and the nanoplatform exhibited excellent photostability over six consecutive laser ON/OFF cycles. Temperature elevation exhibited clear concentration- and power-density dependence (Fig. 2G and H). Moreover, PB-4@SK NPs displayed strong, concentration-dependent NIR fluorescence under 660 nm excitation (Fig. 2I), confirming their capability for dual imaging and therapy.

The ROS generation efficiency of PB-4@SK NPs was rigorously validated using complementary chemical probes. To circumvent the Inner Filter Effect (IFE) inherent at high optical densities (1 mg/mL, OD504 = 0.95), the assays were optimized at a concentration of 0.2 mg/mL (OD504 = 0.23). First, using the fluorescence probe SOSG, a robust time-dependent increase in intensity was observed, rising from 2523 to 3785 within only 3 min (approximately 1.5-fold enhancement; Fig. S9). To further corroborate this, the DPBF (1,3-diphenylisobenzofuran) consumption assay was performed. Upon 660 nm irradiation, the characteristic absorbance of DPBF at 410 nm exhibited a significant and steady decrease over time (Fig. S10), indicating the rapid chemical consumption of the probe by the generated singlet oxygen (1O2). These findings, consistent with the characteristic 1:1:1 triplet signal observed in electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy (Fig. S11), collectively demonstrate the superior and controllable photodynamic performance of the PB-4@SK NPs platform. To quantify this, the 1O2 quantum yield (ΦΔ) was determined to be 0.445 using Rose Bengal as a standard. Furthermore, EPR spectroscopy (Fig. S11) and chemical probe assays (Fig. S9–S10) confirmed that 1O2 is the predominant ROS species generated.

Collectively, these results verify the successful integration of drug delivery, photothermal conversion, and imaging functionalities within the PB-4@SK NPs platform, underscoring its potential for advanced anticancer applications.

In vitro antitumor activity evaluation

To evaluate the cellular uptake of PB-4@SK NPs, Hepa1-6 cells were initially monitored by confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM). A distinctive time-dependent increase in intracellular red fluorescence—originating from the NIR-I polymeric photosensitizer PB-4—was observed (Fig. 3A), indicating efficient nanoparticle internalization. Quantitative flow cytometry (FCM) confirmed this progressive uptake, revealing a consistent rightward shift in fluorescence intensity over time (Fig. 3B). Subsequently, the in vitro therapeutic efficacy was systematically assessed via 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assays. The PB-4@SK NPs + L group exhibited superior cytotoxicity with a half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of 7.5 μg/mL (calculated using SK-equivalent concentrations), which was significantly lower than that of other formulations (Fig. 3C). Live/dead cell staining corroborated these findings: the PB-4@SK NPs + L group displayed extensive red fluorescence (propidium iodide, dead cells) with negligible green fluorescence (calcein-AM, viable cells), signifying potent phototherapy-induced cell death (Fig. 3D). To exclude interference from non-specific thermal damage, the medium temperature was monitored during irradiation (0.5 W/cm2, 5 min, 30 cm) and remained below 39 °C, confirming that the observed cytotoxicity was predominantly driven by photodynamic action.

The mechanism of cell death was further elucidated through Annexin V–FITC/PI apoptosis assays. The PB-4@SK NPs + L group induced the highest proportion of apoptotic cells (Fig. 3E, with quantitative analysis in Fig. S12). Notably, the apoptosis rate in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group significantly exceeded that of the PB-4 + L group, underscoring the synergistic role of SK-mediated metabolic reprogramming in sensitizing HCC cells to phototherapy. Furthermore, intracellular ROS levels were quantified using the DCFH-DA probe. Robust green fluorescence (DCF) was observed in laser-irradiated groups containing PB-4, with the highest intensity recorded in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group (Fig. 3F). Quantitative FCM analysis further validated this ROS burst (Fig. S13), providing strong evidence that the co-delivery system maximizes oxidative stress to drive superior antitumor activity.

Metabolic–photoimmunotherapy synergy

Immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated that laser irradiation markedly promoted the translocation of CRT to the cell membrane as an “eat-me” signal, with the most pronounced effect observed in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group (Fig. 4A). Concurrently, intracellular HMGB1 levels were significantly depleted (Fig. 4B), signifying its extracellular secretion as a “find-me” signal to recruit phagocytes [30]. In the context of immunogenic cell death ICD, “find-me” signals such as HMGB1 and ATP represent a class of secreted molecules that establish a chemotactic gradient to recruit innate immune cells, particularly dendritic cells DCs and macrophages, to the proximity of dying tumor cells.Western blotting corroborated these observations, revealing elevated membrane-bound CRT and decreased cytosolic HMGB1 (Fig. 4H). Furthermore, the level of extracellular ATP—another hallmark DAMP and potent “find-me” signal—was significantly elevated in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group (Fig. 4C). Collectively, these results confirm that the combined phototherapy effectively triggers ICD. Since DAMP release promotes DC maturation, we next evaluated the immunostimulatory potential of the treated cells. Flow cytometry revealed a significant increase in mature DCs (CD11c+CD80+CD86+) after co-culture, with the PB-4@SK NPs + L group showing the highest maturation rate (Fig. 4D–S14). This indicates that the oxidative stress and metabolic disruption induced by the combination therapy significantly enhance tumor immunogenicity, thereby initiating adaptive antitumor immunity.

Acting as a specific inhibitor of PKM2, SK directly counteracts the Warburg effect—a hallmark of tumor metabolism characterized by heightened aerobic glycolysis [22]. Consistently, SK treatment induced a dose-dependent suppression of PKM2 expression (Fig. S15). Notably, PB-4-mediated phototherapy further augmented this inhibitory effect. The PB-4@SK NPs + L group exhibited the most pronounced suppression of the glycolytic pathway, demonstrating a powerful synergy between metabolic reprogramming and phototherapy. Mechanistically, the robust ROS burst and localized hyperthermia generated by PB-4 promote the oxidative degradation and thermal denaturation of key metabolic enzymes, including the downregulation of HIF-1α—a master activator of the glycolytic program [31,32]. Comprehensive assessments (Fig. 4E, F, 4H) confirmed the significant depletion of both HIF-1α and PKM2. Interestingly, while PDT-induced hypoxia typically stabilizes HIF-1α, our results show a decrease in HIF-1α levels in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group. This can be attributed to the robust ROS burst which triggers the oxidative degradation of HIF-1αor disrupts its chaperone-mediated stabilization, thereby suppressing the pro-survival pathways typically associated with tumor hypoxia [33]. By targeting PKM2, SK treatment effectively limits glycolysis-derived ATP production in the cytoplasm, thereby modulating the metabolic state and enhancing the immunogenicity of tumor cells. This reduction in intracellular ATP levels was further confirmed by our bioluminescence assays (Fig. S16) and extracellular lactate (Fig. 4G) was observed. The slight downregulation of extracellular lactate in the phototherapy-only group (G4) is likely due to non-specific metabolic stress. Specifically, 1O2 flux and localized photothermal heating can cause the partial inactivation of glycolytic enzymes and transporters, leading to a collateral decrease in lactate secretion even without the presence of SK.

Moreover, SK significantly alleviated PB-4-induced cytoprotective stress by downregulating heat shock protein 70 (HSP70) expression [34], as confirmed by confocal microscopy (Fig. S17) and Western blotting (Fig. S18). By inhibiting HSP70, SK effectively reduces the thermoresistance of HCC cells to phototherapy, thereby maximizing apoptotic responses. Consistent with these findings, a pronounced loss of mitochondrial membrane potential was observed, particularly in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group (Fig. S19), indicating severe mitochondrial dysfunction.

In summary, the co-delivery of PB-4 and SK establishes a multi-target synergistic attack on tumor metabolism. This strategy effectively disrupts the HIF-1α/PKM2 axis, blocks aerobic glycolysis, depletes essential metabolites (ATP and lactate), and attenuates cytoprotective stress via HSP70 inhibition. Collectively, these events remodel the immunosuppressive TME and enhance ICD-driven immune activation, leading to robust synergistic antitumor efficacy.

RNA sequencing analysis

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the observed synergistic effects, transcriptome-wide RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed to profile the transcriptional landscapes of Hepa1-6 cells treated with PBS, SK, and PB-4@SK NPs + L. This analysis aimed to capture the global perturbations in tumor signaling pathways, glycolysis-related metabolic networks, and immuno-regulatory programs.

Compared with the PBS control, 297 and 302 DEGs were uniquely identified in the SK and PB-4@SK NPs + L groups, respectively (Fig. 5A). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using a stringent threshold of |log_2(FoldChange)| > 1 and a statistical significance of p < 0.05 (Fig. 5B). Specifically, the SK group exhibited 251 upregulated and 736 downregulated genes, while the combined therapy (PB-4@SK NPs + L) induced a more profound transcriptional shift, with 988 upregulated and 1756 downregulated genes. A direct comparison between the SK and PB-4@SK NPs + L groups revealed 918 upregulated and 899 downregulated DEGs, indicating that the integration of metabolic reprogramming and phototherapy triggers a broad and synergistic transcriptional modulation.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed significant alterations in key signaling cascades, including the MAPK, p53, and TNF pathways, alongside marked modulation of apoptosis and oxidative phosphorylation (Fig. 5C). Since SK inhibits aerobic glycolysis via PKM2 suppression, the concurrent downregulation of oxidative phosphorylation-related genes (e.g., Idh1) suggests that the glycolytic blockade disrupts compensatory mitochondrial bioenergetics, leading to a profound energy crisis. Furthermore, genes involved in energy metabolism and stress responses—including Pcca (fatty acid oxidation), Nqo1 (oxidative stress defense), and heat shock proteins (Hspa1b, Hsp90b1)—exhibited the most pronounced changes in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group. This synergistic perturbation likely reflects a mechanism where PB-4-derived ROS bursts amplify SK-mediated metabolic dysfunction by further compromising the antioxidant and metabolic resilience of HCC cells (Fig. 5D).

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) further illustrated that the PB-4@SK NPs + L treatment significantly impacted gene sets related to biological oxidation, ATP synthesis, and antigen processing and presentation (Fig. 5E). Notably, the marked downregulation of ATP synthesis-associated programs aligns with the dual-action mechanism: SK-mediated glycolytic inhibition curtails cytosolic ATP production, while ROS-induced mitochondrial damage impairs oxidative phosphorylation-derived ATP generation (Fig. 5E). Together, these findings provide transcriptional evidence for the catastrophic disruption of tumor energy homeostasis.

In summary, transcriptomic profiling demonstrates that PB-4@SK NPs + L orchestrates a coordinated reprogramming of tumor metabolism and immuno-related signatures. These molecular events bridge metabolic disruption with potent immune activation, providing a robust genomic foundation for the synergistic antitumor efficacy further validated in the subsequent in vivo HCC model.

In vivo antitumor efficacy evaluation

To evaluate the biodistribution and tumor-targeting capability of the nanoplatform, a subcutaneous Hepa1-6 tumor-bearing mouse model was established. Mice were intravenously injected with either free PB-4 or PB-4@SK NPs and monitored using an in vivo fluorescence imaging system. For the free PB-4 group, the PB-4 was dissolved in DMSO and then diluted in physiological saline (final DMSO concentration <10%) to form a stable suspension for injection. Compared with free PB-4, PB-4@SK NPs exhibited significantly enhanced tumor accumulation and prolonged retention. Intense NIR-I fluorescence signals from the polymeric photosensitizer remained highly concentrated at the tumor site for up to 48 h post-injection (Fig. 6A). Ex vivo imaging of excised organs and tumors further validated this superior targeting behavior. Semi-quantitative analysis revealed that while free PB-4 primarily accumulated in the liver—reflecting rapid hepatic clearance—PB-4@SK NPs achieved maximal signal intensity within the tumor tissue (Fig. 6B). These findings highlight the advantage of the nanocarrier in leveraging the EPR effect for tumor-selective delivery.

The functional impact of tumor-specific accumulation was validated via photothermal imaging. Upon 660 nm laser irradiation (1 W/cm2, 5 min, 30 cm), the tumor surface temperature was monitored using an IR thermal camera. In the PB-4@SK NPs + L group, the tumor temperature rapidly increased and reached a plateau at 52.3 °C (Fig. S20), which is sufficient to induce localized photothermal ablation and potentiate the ICD effect. Notably, no obvious thermal damage to the surrounding healthy skin was observed during or after the treatment, confirming the localized photothermal effect and the excellent biosafety of the PB-4@SK nanoplatform at the applied therapeutic dosage. This temperature effectively induces photothermal ablation while avoiding non-specific heat damage to healthy tissues. These results confirm the precise tumor localization and high photothermal conversion efficiency of the nanoplatform.

The in vivo therapeutic efficacy was systematically evaluated (Fig. 6C). In the control (PBS) group, tumor volumes increased rapidly to approximately 1000 mm3. In contrast, the PB-4@SK NPs + L group exhibited the most potent growth inhibition, with several mice achieving near-complete tumor regression (Fig. 6D). Importantly,All mice maintained stable body weights throughout the 14-day study, providing direct evidence of the nanoplatform's high biocompatibility (Fig. 6E). Endpoint tumor weights further confirmed this therapeutic superiority (Fig. 6F and G). Notably, the combined therapy (PB-4@SK NPs + L) significantly outperformed the phototherapy-only group (PB-4 + L), supporting the synergistic interplay between SK-mediated glycolytic inhibition and PB-4-driven phototherapy. Histological evaluations corroborated these findings. H&E staining of PB-4@SK NPs + L treated tumors revealed extensive necrosis and nuclear fragmentation. These results were further validated by TUNEL assays, which showed abundant apoptotic nuclei, and Ki-67 staining, which indicated significant suppression of tumor cell proliferation (Fig. 6H–S21).

The systemic biosafety of the nanoplatform was comprehensively assessed. No significant signs of toxicity or behavioral abnormalities were observed. Serum biochemical markers for hepatic (ALT, AST), renal (BUN, CREA), and cardiac (LDH, CK-MB) functions remained within physiological ranges (Fig. S22). Histopathological examination (H&E) of major organs—including the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys—revealed no structural abnormalities or inflammatory infiltration (Fig. S23), further confirming the excellent safety profile of PB-4@SK NPs in vivo.

In summary, PB-4@SK NPs achieved precise tumor localization and superior therapeutic efficacy through the integrated synergy of dual-mode phototherapy and metabolic reprogramming, while maintaining an exceptional biosafety profile. Given the robust tumor inhibition, we subsequently investigated the potential of the nanoplatform to remodel the immunosuppressive tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), which is essential for establishing durable antitumor immunity.

In vivo antitumor immune activation induced by PB-4@SK NPs-Mediated phototherapy

To elucidate the in vivo immune activation elicited by PB-4@SK NPs-mediated PDT/PTT, a subcutaneous Hepa1-6 tumor model was established in C57BL/6 mice. After the treatment cycle, tumor tissues, spleens, and tumor-draining lymph nodes (TDLNs) were harvested to evaluate the systemic immune landscape, focusing on T cell infiltration, tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) polarizaton, and Treg suppression [35,36].

Immunofluorescence staining revealed a marked increase in T cell infiltration and pronounced TAM reprogramming in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group, characterized by upregulated M1 marker iNOS and downregulated M2 marker CD206 (Fig. 7A). This enhanced M1 polarization stems from a synergistic “metabolic-immune” modulation: SK-mediated inhibition of the PKM2/HIF-1α axis reduces extracellular lactate—a critical metabolite that sustains the immunosuppressive M2 phenotype—while NIR-I triggered ICD creates a pro-inflammatory milieu that promotes M1 differentiation. FCM substantiated these findings, showing that the proportions of CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and CD4+ helper T cells reached 26.4 ± 2.1% and 46.8 ± 10.7%, respectively (Fig. 7B and C). Notably, NK cells (CD45+CD49b+) increased significantly to 32.53 ± 5.51% (Fig. 7B and C). This activation is attributed to the synergistic effect of IL-12 secretion from mature DCs and the relief of lactate-induced immunosuppression. By inhibiting PKM2-mediated glycolysis, SK treatment mitigates the acidic TME, thereby restoring the cytolytic activity and IFN-γ production of NK cells, which collectively contributes to the robust innate immune response.

As ICD enhances antigen presentation, we evaluated DC maturation in TDLNs. In this context, the extracellular release of HMGB1 and ATP serves as potent “find-me” signals to recruit DCs to the tumor site. FCM revealed a significant expansion of mature DCs (CD11c + CD80+CD86+) in the PB-4@SK NPs + L group (Fig. 7D and G), confirming an enhanced capacity for T cell priming. Furthermore, systemic immunity was strengthened, evidenced by reduced splenic Tregs and a significant increase in both CD4+ and CD8+ memory T cells (CD44+CD62L+) (Fig. 7E–G, S24). Given that HCC is plagued by a high clinical recurrence rate of 50–70%, the induction of robust memory T cell responses is of paramount clinical significance, as these cells provide long-term immunological surveillance to eradicate residual micrometastases and prevent distal relapse. Notably, our “metabolic-photoimmunotherapy” strategy—leveraging SK-mediated metabolic remodeling and PB-4-triggered ICD—effectively transforms the treated primary tumor into an in situ vaccine. This synergistic approach not only achieves immediate tumor ablation but also orchestrates a durable immunological shield, offering a promising paradigm for managing the characteristically high recurrence and distal metastasis of HCC. Furthermore, a clear causal relationship exists between the reshaped immune landscape in Fig. 7 and the observed tumor growth inhibition. The PB-4@SK NPs-mediated synergistic therapy triggered potent immunogenic cell death (ICD), significantly increasing the infiltration of CD8+ T cells and NK cells while effectively promoting the reprogramming of pro-tumor M2-TAMs toward the anti-tumor M1 phenotype. This systemic remodeling of the immune microenvironment directly suppressed tumor growth kinetics, as evidenced by the significant volume reduction. Crucially, this robust immune activation extended beyond the initial treatment phase by successfully inducing the generation of central memory T cells (Tcm) and effector memory T cells (Tem). The establishment of such long-term immune memory creates a persistent immunosurveillance system that effectively prevents tumor recurrence. This transition from an acute immune response to durable immune memory underscores the potential of our nanoplatform for achieving complete tumor eradication and favorable long-term prognosis.”

Serum cytokine profiling validated this systemic activation, with the PB-4@SK NPs + L group exhibiting elevated pro-inflammatory TNF-α and IFN-γ [37,38]—essential for cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) activity—alongside the suppression of the immunosuppressive cytokine IL-10 (Fig. 7H). Collectively, NIR-I phototherapy mediated by PB-4@SK NPs orchestrates a robust and coordinated antitumor immune response. This integration of metabolic reprogramming with photo-immunotherapy provides a multifaceted approach to remodeling the TME, laying a solid foundation for durable tumor regression and long-term therapeutic efficacy.

Limitations of the study

Despite the promising findings, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although SK demonstrated robust antitumor activity via PKM2 inhibition in the Hepa1-6 model, the inherent genomic and metabolic heterogeneity of HCC may influence drug sensitivity across varied molecular subtypes. Future validation across a broader panel of HCC cell lines with diverse metabolic profiles is warranted to establish the generalizability of the PB-4@SK nanoplatform. Second, our current evaluation relied primarily on the murine Hepa1-6 syngeneic model. Given the evolutionary divergence between murine and TIME, a significant translational gap remains. Subsequent studies utilizing humanized immune system (HIS) models or patient-derived xenografts (PDX) will be essential to more accurately predict clinical outcomes. Furthermore, while the subcutaneous model provided fundamental mechanistic insights, exploring orthotopic HCC models or advanced organoid systems would better recapitulate the complex hepatic architecture and hemodynamic environment. Finally, investigating the synergy between this metabolic–photoimmunotherapy strategy and immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., anti-PD-L1) represents a critical direction for eradicating systemic micrometastases.

Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a multifunctional nanoplatform (PB-4@SK NPs) by co-loading the NIR-I polymeric photosensitizer PB-4 and the PKM2 inhibitor SK to achieve a potent synergistic metabolic–photoimmunotherapy paradigm. This nanostructure significantly improves the aqueous solubility and bioavailability of the hydrophobic payloads, ensuring precise tumor-selective accumulation and synchronized delivery via the EPR effect. Under 660 nm laser irradiation, PB-4 mediates robust dual-mode PDT/PTT, generating a localized ROS burst and controlled hyperthermia to trigger potent ICD and subsequent T-cell-mediated immunity. Simultaneously, the targeted release of SK effectively suppresses the PKM2/HIF-1α signaling axis, thereby reversing the Warburg effect, decreasing immunosuppressive lactate accumulation, and alleviating the hypoxia that typically restricts conventional PDT. These processes synergistically potentiate PB-4–induced ICD while mitigating PB-4–triggered HSP70-mediated cytoprotection. A self-amplifying feedback loop is established where PB-4-derived ROS reinforce SK-mediated metabolic inhibition, while SK-mediated HSP70 suppression attenuates thermoresistance to further sensitize tumors to phototherapy.

Beyond primary tumor eradication, the PB-4@SK nanoplatform successfully induces robust long-term immunological memory. Considering the formidable 50–70% recurrence rate of HCC, the generation of memory T cells offers a critical strategy for sustained immunological surveillance to prevent post-treatment relapse and metastasis. Collectively, this study introduces a rationally designed nanoplatform that integrates photoresponsive immune activation with metabolic modulation, providing a translationally relevant paradigm for overcoming the limitations of current HCC monotherapies. Future investigations utilizing advanced orthotopic or humanized HIS-models will be essential to further bridge the translational gap and validate the clinical potential of this metabolic–photoimmunotherapy strategy.

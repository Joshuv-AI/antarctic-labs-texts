Survival prediction of 18F-FDG PET/CT in pediatric patients with recurrent neuroblastoma
Authors: Wenfang Tang, Zhonglin Liang, Chao Li, Yafu Yin, Hui Wang, Jinglue Song, Suyun Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeOur study aims to investigate the predictive potential of FDG PET/CT in pediatric patients with NB at the time of recurrence.MethodsPediatric patients with suspected recurrence of NB underwent FDG PET/CT were included in this retrospective study. The clinicopathological data, PET visual analysis data and semiquantitative values (maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) of tumor, tumor-to-liver uptake ratio (TLR), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), total lesion glycolysis (TLG)), were analyzed. The Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox proportional hazard models were applied in the evaluation of the independent prognostic significance of FDG PET/CT.ResultsA total of 45 NB patients who underwent FDG PET/CT due to suspected recurrence of NB and got later confirmation of recurrence clinically or pathologically were included in this retrospective study. Among the PET/CT parameters, recurrent primary site TLR cutoff at 5.64 and SUVmax cutoff at 4.10 derived from the receiver operating characteristic curve yielded significant difference (TLR HR = 2.692, p = 0.014; SUVmax HR = 7.698, 0.045) in overall survival (OS). Besides, INSS stage 4, the time from diagnosis to first relapse (TTFR) and recurrence interval were related with shorter survival time in the four established multivariate Cox regression models.ConclusionFDG PET/CT showed great survival predictable performance in recurrent NB. SUVmax, TLR, INSS stage 4, TTFR and recurrence interval were independent risk factors for overall survival.

Introduction

Neuroblastoma (NB) is the most common extracranial malignant solid tumor in infants and children. The outcome of pediatric NB has significantly improved over the past decades after introduction of multimodality treatment, achieving a 5-year survival rate of 80% [1]. Despite modern therapy, more than half of the patients with high-risk NB relapse after completing treatment [2, 3]. The prognosis for these patients remains dismal with almost half dying within the first year after recurrence and only a fifth alive at the fourth year [4].

MIBG (metaiodobenzylguanidine) scan is the most widely acknowledged imaging method in NB patients to accurately show the extent of disease involvement and indicate prognosis [5]. As a norepinephrine analog, mIBG enters the NB cells via the norepinephrine transporter, which is typically expressed in NB, enabling tumor-selective imaging. Comparatively, since 18F-FDG is not a cancer-specific agent, 18F-FDG PET/CT is considered as a second-line imaging modality in NB patients [5, 6]. However, MIBG uptake was absent in 10% of NB patients at the time of diagnosis as well as in those with tumor necrosis or maturation after treatment [7]. Therefore, in the revised International Neuroblastoma Response Criteria (INRC 2017), 18F-FDG PET/CT was recommended as an alternative modality for MIBG non-avid tumor [8]. Currently, studies on the value of 18F-FDG PET/CT in assessing NB were still few, especially in patients with recurrent disease.

Clinical and genetic factors such as age, stage, time from diagnosis to first relapse (TTFR) and MYCN amplification status were associated with the postrelapse survival of NB [3]. Bone/bone marrow is the most common site of recurrence [9]. Compared with 18F-FDG PET/CT, 123I-MIBG imaging can better delineating bone/bone marrow involvement as well as whole-body tumor extent in recurrent/refractory NB, while is less effective in survival prediction [5]. Semiquantitative MIBG scoring system for assessing tumor burden during and after multimodal treatment is significantly correlated with prognosis in NB patients [10–13]. Metabolic volumetric parameters obtained from pretreatment 18F-FDG PET, including metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), allow measurement of the whole-body tumor burden. Our previous studies has demonstrated that MTV of the primary tumor on pretherapy 18F-FDG PET/CT scans provide prognostic information in pediatric patients with NB [14], ﻿18F-FDG PET/CT scans ﻿after chemotherapy also ﻿exhibit prognostic significance in children with stage 4 NB [15], while the prognostic value of metabolic tumor burden in pediatric patients with recurrent NB remains uncertain.

In this study, our key objective was to evaluate the prognostic performance of FDG PET/CT parameters, including metabolic volumetric parameters in pediatric NB patients at the time of recurrence to improve survival stratification in this high-risk setting.

Methods

Pediatric NB patients who underwent PET/CT for suspected disease recurrence during September 2011 and August 2019 were retrospectively reviewed. The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients had to have histologically confirmed NB, must have completed standard therapy regimen for NB, must have reached complete remission or very good partial remission at the end of treatment, must have suspected recurrent diseases based on abnormal conventional imaging and/or laboratory tests (elevated neuron-specific enolase (NSE), urinary catecholamine metabolites levels (homovanillic acid (HVA), vanillylmandelic acid (VMA)) during routine follow-up. Patients with a time interval between the completion of treatment and FDG PET/CT less than 1 month were excluded. FDG PET/CT findings were confirmed by pathological biopsies, or clinical follow-up combined with other imaging modalities, including ultrasound, computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging and bone scan. This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was waived.

FDG PET/CT acquisition

Patients were required to fast for 4–6 h. PET/CT scans were acquired on a Biograph mCT-64 (Siemens, Erlangen, Germany) about 60 min after injection of 18F-FDG at a weight-adapted dose of 5.18MBq/kg with a minimum dose of 37MBq. Sedation was given using chloral hydrate (50 mg/kg) 30 min before scan in children who were unable to cooperate. From November 2011 to November 2018, PET/CT scans were performed routinely from the vertex to the upper-mid thigh. During that time, a whole-body PET scan was acquired in the patients with specific disease conditions, such as complaining about leg pain or having suspected diseases in the limbs. Since December 2018, all NB patients scanned from head to toe in accordance with the 2018 EANM procedure guidelines on 18F-FDG PET/CT for NB [16].

FDG PET/CT image analysis

Two experienced nuclear medicine doctors blinded to the clinicopathological information reviewed the PET/CT images visually and reached consensus on image interpretation. All lesions with visually higher uptake compared to surrounding normal tissue and with the exception of physiologic uptake were suspected as local recurrence or metastases. Semi-quantitative PET/CT parameters, including SUVmax, TLR, TMTV and TLG, were obtained by using an open-source PET/CT viewer based on ImageJ. SUVmax of the lesion with the highest 18F-FDG uptake was measured for analysis. TLR TMTV was calculated by summing the metabolic volumes of all the VOIs which were drawn manually around each lesion with both 41% of SUVmax (TMTV41% and TLG41%) and a fixed SUV of 2.5(TMTV2.5 and TLG2.5) as thresholds. The detailed methods to obtain the volumetric parameters can be found in our previous published article [17].

Statistical analysis

The continuous data of the study were described as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (range) and analyzed by the two-sample t test or the Mann Whitney U test, whichever appropriate. The comparison of categorical data was performed by the chi-square test. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time interval between the date of PET/CT scan to the date of all-cause death or follow-up end. Cutoff values of the metabolic semi-quantitative parameters were determined by receiver operation characteristics analysis (ROC) curve and Youden index. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and the log-rank test were utilized to determine differences between groups in survival outcome. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to identify the significant predictors of survival. The SPSS software (Version 22.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp.) and GraphPad Prism 6.0 were employed in the data analysis. All tests were two-sided, and a P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 45 NB patients who underwent FDG PET/CT due to suspected recurrence of NB and got later confirmation of recurrence clinically or pathologically were included in this retrospective study. Patient characteristics are summarized in Table 1. All of them received standard multimodality treatment for NB after initial diagnosis. Eight patients underwent surgery and chemotherapy. 29 patients received surgery, chemotherapy (CT) and radiotherapy (RT). Another 5 patients were also given autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) on the basis of Surgery + CT+RT strategy. And only one patient received Surgery + CT+ASCT, two patients received CT + RT. All the 45 patients were evaluated complete remission (CR) or very good partial response (VGPR) after treatment. The median time duration between the PET scan and last treatment was 11 months (range:1–72 months). After a median follow-up period of 19 months (range:1–60 months). Eighteen of them were histologically validated to be recurrence, and the others were diagnosed based on clinical follow-up, laboratory evaluation and other imaging modalities. The most frequent recurrent sites were as follows: bone/bone marrow (33, 73.3%), lymph nodes (27, 60.0%) and primary tumor site (13, 28.9%). 12 patients suffered both local and metastatic recurrence (Table 2). During the follow up, 32 patients were dead with median survival of 8.5 months (range: 1–48 months) from the date of PET scan. In comparing patients alive and with death, no statistical difference was found in gender, age, primary INSS stage, histology and MYCN amplification status between the two groups. On visual analysis, the 32 died patients all presented with remarkable FDG uptake, shorter recurrence interval and TTFR. On semiquantitative analysis, compared with alived patients, two parameters of PET/CT, SUVmax and TLR were obviously higher in the death group with statistical significance (Table 1), which indicated the prognostic potential of FDG PET/CT parameters in recurrent NB.

FDG PET/CT and prognosis

To further investigate the prognostic indicating potential of FDG PET/CT parameters, optimal cut-off values were identified by the ROC curve and the metabolic variables can then be dichotomized accordingly. The Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed patients with SUVmax > 4.10 (p = 0.0164), TLR > 5.64 (p = 0.0094), TMTV2.5 > 2.76 cm3 (p = 0.0432), TLG41% > 25.15 g (p = 0.0396), TLG2.5 > 9.20 g (p = 0.0432) were significantly associated with shorter OS. Nevertheless, TMTV41% > 11.54 cm3 (p = 0.0974) although not statistically significant, showed a possible association with poor prognosis (Fig. 1).

Next, clinicopathological and metabolic indices were included in the univariate Cox analysis. As a result, recurrence interval, TTFR, SUVmax, and TLR were risk factors of worse OS with statistically significance (Table 3). Initial INSS stage 4 and high metabolic volumetric parameters (TMTV41%, TLG41%, TMTV2.5, TLG2.5) exhibited poor prognostic risk with p-value nearly 0.05, which were extracted in further multivariate Cox regression analyses. However, the MYCN amplification status of 33 patients was not associated with prognosis in our study court (Table 3).

The parameters with P < 0.15 in the univariate Cox analysis were selected into the multivariate Cox regression analysis. Since TMTV41%/TLG41%, TMTV2.5/TLG2.5 were two methods of independent rules for metabolic volumetric parameters, meanwhile, there was a logically and statistically linear relationship between recurrence interval and TTFR, the multivariate Cox regression analyses were established as four models. The correlation coefficient matrix showed that SUVmax was moderately correlated with TLR (Spearman r = 0.680, p < 0.001), which might cause inaccuracy by the multicollinearity. Therefore, in every regression model, SUVmax and TLR were individually analyzed, and the correlation coefficients between every two parameters listed in each model were less than 0.5. As shown in Table 4, TLR > 5.64 was the most stable significant prognostic factor for OS after adjusting for other factors in all the four models, whereas SUVmax>4.10 was the most robust prognostic factor with the highest hazard ratio in three models with statistical significance and gained marginal significance (p = 0.063) in the other one model. In all the four models, INSS stage 4, TTFR and recurrence interval predicted shorter overall survival time.

Discussion

The role of FDG PET/CT in the evaluation of NB have not been fully recognized yet. And it is more considered more of a complementary functional imaging to 123I-MIBG. However, for developing countries including China, India, FDG PET/CT might be the best functional imaging modality due to the paucity of 123I-MIBG. Thus, figuring out the utility of FDG PET/CT in NB is important, not only for those with a negative MIBG scan, but also for the billions of people in regions without 123I-MIBG. In our institution, FDG PET/CT is routinely used for NB staging, restaging and response assessment. Our previous article has demonstrated the prognostic role of baseline FDG PET/CT. In this study, the high prognostic value of FDG PET/CT at the time of NB recurrence was confirmed.

The only published article on FDG PET to predict the prognosis of patients with NB recurrence included a mixture of relapsed and refractory NB patients showed that SUVmax and extent of FDG–avid bone/bone marrow disease were found to be adverse prognostic factors [5]. The results in our study showed that although multiple metabolic indices were significantly higher in the death group than that of the survival group, the only independent factor of metabolic parameters that significantly associated with OS was TLR and SUVmax. This was not completely in accordance with the findings of previous research on pretreatment NB that only the TLR was an independent prognostic factor for OS [18]. Volumetric parameters that reflect whole body tumor burden are meaningful in prognostic assessments in many cancers [19, 20]. Quite unexpectedly, those parameters were not independent prognostic factors in this study regardless of the SUV thresholds of 2.5 or 41%.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study with a relatively small sample size. Second, the follow-up period was relatively short. However, recurrent NB is typically associated with poor prognosis, and most disease-related events occur within a limited time after relapse; therefore, the available follow-up was considered sufficient to assess survival outcomes in this cohort. Third, not all the disease status was confirmed by pathology, as some patients died soon after the scan and the diagnosis of recurrence was based on clinical findings, laboratory results and imaging features [21]. Finally, 123I-MIBG imaging, which is considered the standard functional imaging modality for NB in international guidelines, was not available in our setting. Therefore, direct comparison between FDG PET/CT and MIBG-based assessment could not be performed. Prospective studies with larger cohorts, longer follow-up, and multimodality imaging are needed to validate these findings.

Conclusion

FDG PET/CT showed great prognostic performance in recurrent NB. SUVmax, TLR, INSS stage 4, TTFR and recurrence interval were independent risk factors for overall survival.

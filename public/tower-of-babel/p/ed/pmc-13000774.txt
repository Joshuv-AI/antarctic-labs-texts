Enhanced Photovoltaic Efficiency in Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells Using rGO/Cu2+:ZnO/Polymer (PMMA/PPy/PANI) Ternary Photoanodes
Authors: Nujud Badawi, Khalid Mujasam Batoo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cu2+-doped ZnO/reduced graphene oxide (rGO) ternary nanocomposites were synthesized via a facile oxalate-assisted wet-chemical precipitation route and systematically investigated as photoanodes for dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs). Progressive incorporation of Cu2+ (0–5 atom %) into the ZnO lattice resulted in effective band gap narrowing from 3.28 to 3.05 eV, accompanied by enhanced visible-light absorption (450–800 nm) and suppressed charge carrier recombination, as confirmed by photoluminescence quenching. Electron microscopy revealed a morphology evolution from irregular crystallites to hierarchical porous microspheres (∼2.4 μm) assembled from nanoscale building blocks (50–80 nm), while structural analyses confirmed the preservation of the wurtzite ZnO phase without secondary copper oxide formation. Hall effect measurements demonstrated a marked enhancement in charge transport properties, with carrier mobility increasing from 26.5 to 42.8 cm2·V–1·s–1 and electrical conductivity from 19.2 to 23.2 Ω–1·cm–1 upon Cu2+ doping. When implemented as DSSC photoanodes, the optimized 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO system delivered a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 4.9%, significantly outperforming that of pristine ZnO/rGO (3.1%). Further hybridization with polymer matrices (PMMA, PPy, and PANI) revealed a synergistic improvement in interfacial charge transport and film stability. Among them, the 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI photoanode exhibited the highest efficiency of 5.6% (J SC = 14.8 mA·cm–2, V OC = 0.72 V, and FF = 0.53) at an optimal film thickness of ∼18 μm. The enhanced photovoltaic performance is attributed to the combined effects of Cu2+-induced band structure modulation, rGO-mediated rapid electron extraction, and PANI-facilitated interfacial charge transport. These results demonstrate an effective ternary hybrid strategy for developing efficient, low-cost, and Pt-free ZnO-based DSSC photoanodes.

Introduction

Rising global energy needs and the challenges of climate change have intensified research into renewable energy conversion technologies. Among the available alternatives, solar energy has gained prominence due to its widespread availability and environmental sustainability. Although solar energy is recognized as the most promising renewable source, the dominance of silicon-based photovoltaic devices is inhibited by costly and complex fabrication techniques, as well as their inherent rigidity, which constrains broader adoption. − In this regard, dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) have garnered significant attention as inexpensive, flexible, and environmentally sustainable photovoltaic devices. DSSCs also provide significant benefits such as simple fabrication, spectral tunability, and compatibility with transparent and flexible substrates, making them well-suited for future energy-harvesting technologies. Despite their merits, drawbacks such as modest power conversion efficiency (PCE) and durability concerns impede their commercialization. Therefore, considerable efforts are being devoted to enhancing photoanode materials, which govern key factors including electron transport, dye adsorption, and overall performance of DSSCs. ,

Metal oxide semiconductor nanomaterials have been widely investigated as photoanode materials, with TiO2 and ZnO emerging as promising candidates. While TiO2 remains the most widely explored photoanode material, ZnO has garnered great attention due to its wide band gap (3.37 eV), strong exciton binding energy (60 meV), remarkable electron mobility, and versatility of nanostructure morphologies through a cost-effective wet-chemical method. , These characteristics render ZnO highly favorable for electron transport and dye adsorption in DSSCs. However, its pristine form is hindered by drawbacks such as fast electron–hole recombination, photocorrosion in dye/electrolyte media, and reduced stability under extended irradiation, which collectively constrain its photovoltaic efficiency. To address these limitations, approaches like transition-metal doping and the integration of hybrid nanocomposites with conductive carbon materials are being actively explored.

Transition-metal ion doping, particularly with copper (Cu2+) ions, has emerged as an effective strategy to modulate the electronic structure of ZnO and enhance its charge transport properties. Cu2+ doping induces shallow acceptor states that diminish electron–hole recombination, extend visible-light absorption, and elevate carrier concentration. Moreover, Cu2+-doped ZnO nanostructures exhibit superior photocatalytic and optoelectronic performance over undoped ZnO, positioning them as attractive candidates for DSSC applications. Nonetheless, doping alone cannot completely overcome issues such as electron trapping and instability, highlighting the need for further enhancement through hybrid system designs. , Similarly, graphene and its derivatives, most notably reduced graphene oxide (rGO), have gained prominence as valuable additives in semiconductor composites, owing to their outstanding electrical conductivity, high surface area, and robust mechanical stability. Integrating rGO sheets with ZnO nanostructures promotes rapid electron transfer via conductive networks and reduces recombination losses at the semiconductor–electrolyte interface. − In addition, the extended conjugated structure of rGO not only provides numerous active sites for dye anchoring but also promotes superior light harvesting. ZnO/rGO nanocomposites, as demonstrated in previous studies, possess improved photocatalytic, sensing, and energy storage properties relative to pristine ZnO, indicating their strong potential as a photoanode material in DSSCs. ,

Besides the metal ion doping and rGO integration, suitable polymer matrices exert distinctly different effects on the electronic, interfacial, and mechanical performance of ZnO nanocomposite photoanodes. , Polymers, such as polyaniline (PANI), polypyrrole (PPy), and poly­(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), play distinct yet complementary roles in enhancing the structural and functional characteristics of hybrid systems, especially with a ZnO system. Intrinsic conducting polymers PANI and PPy offer good electrical conductivity and charge transport pathways, while PMMA contributes optical transparency, strong film-forming ability, and mechanical robustness. PANI and PPy establish energy band alignment, reduce charge transfer resistance, thereby promoting efficient carrier percolation, and inhibit nonradiative recombination losses. , In contrast, PMMA functions as a nonconducting binder that enhances film cohesion and suppresses cracks under thermal/electrochemical stress, which are essential in long-term performing devices. Embedding ZnO nanoparticles within these polymer matrices ensures uniform dispersion, minimizes agglomeration, and strengthens interfacial compatibility between organic and inorganic components. Existing reports have demonstrated the versatility of such polymer-based ZnO composites in sensing, photocatalytic, and electronic applications. ,

Development of a Cu2+-doped ZnO–rGO–polymer ternary nanocomposite could offer a strategic route to overcome the limitations of conventional ZnO photoanodes in DSSCs. Here, Cu2+ doping could tune the band structure and mitigate electron–hole recombination. rGO is expected to provide high-conductivity pathways for rapid electron extraction, and the polymer matrix (PANI, PPy, PMMA) is expected to enhance the film stability, interfacial contact, and mechanical integrity. Although each component has been individually explored in ZnO systems, existing literature lacks a unified approach to the best of our knowledge, and no comparative study integrates all three into a single architecture to evaluate synergistic effects on photovoltaic performance, particularly in Pt-free DSSC configurations. To address this research gap, we have synthesized Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/polymer nanocomposites as novel photoanodes and correlated the doping level, rGO-mediated charge transport, and polymer functionality with optoelectronic characteristics and device efficiency. The findings establish a direct structure–property–performance relationship, demonstrating how the ternary system accelerates charge transfer and suppresses recombination. This work not only validates the hybrid architecture for enhanced DSSC operation but also provides a scalable materials framework for cost-effective solar energy systems.

Experimental Section

Materials

Zinc acetate dihydrate [Zn­(CH3COO)2·2H2O], copper­(II) acetate monohydrate [Cu­(CH3COO)2·H2O], poly­(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA, M n ≈ 120,000), and ethanol were obtained in analytical grade. Reduced graphene oxide (rGO) was synthesized from graphite powder through a modified Hummers’ method and subsequent chemical reduction with ascorbic acid. All solutions were prepared with deionized water (18.2 MΩ·cm).

Synthesis of Cu2+-Doped ZnO/rGO Nanocomposites

In a facile oxalate precipitate approach, − we have obtained the Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites. In a typical synthesis, 200 mg of citric acid of 200 mg was well-dissolved in 100 mL of deionized water, followed by the addition of 0.1 M zinc acetate and 0.01 g of rGO to obtain a homogeneous dispersion. A separate oxalic acid solution, prepared with 300 mg in 50 mL of water, was then introduced dropwise, resulting in the formation of white zinc oxalate-based precipitates, and further, the reaction mixture was allowed to age for 24 h. The obtained solid precipitate was collected, washed thoroughly with water, dried at 100 °C, and subsequently calcined at 500 °C for 3 h to yield pristine ZnO/rGO. Similarly, Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites containing 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+ were prepared following the same process by incorporating stoichiometric amounts of copper nitrate along with the corresponding quantity of zinc acetate, ultimately producing the Cu2+-modified ZnO/rGO materials used for further characterization.

Fabrication of Polymer–Cu2+-Doped ZnO/rGO Composites

As prepared earlier, the rGO/Cu2+-doped ZnO nanocomposites were separately combined with three different polymerspolyaniline (PANI), polypyrrole (PPy), and poly­(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA)to attain hybrid (polymer/nanocomposite) pastes for DSSC electrode fabrication. In the PANI-based composite, 0.15 g of PANI was dissolved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) and mixed with 0.85 g of rGO/Cu2+-doped ZnO dispersed in an ethanol–water mixture (1:50 v/v) under magnetic stirring and ultrasonication to obtain a homogeneous suspension. A PPy-based nanocomposite was prepared by in situ oxidative polymerization, in which the pyrrole monomer (0.15 g) and the nanoparticle dispersion (0.85 g in water) were cooled in an ice bath, followed by slow addition of ammonium persulfate solution (APS:pyrrole = 2:1 mol ratio) under stirring for 2 h to form PPy-coated rGO/Cu2+-ZnO particles. Next, they were washed and dried at 60–80 °C. A PMMA-based nanocomposite was prepared by dissolving 0.10 g of PMMA in 5 mL of NMP and mixing with 0.85 g of the nanoparticle dispersion under ultrasonication. Thus, produced, each polymer–nanocomposite slurry was adjusted to suitable viscosity using small amounts of terpineol or ethyl cellulose and deposited onto precleaned FTO glass substrates by a doctor-blade technique. Nanocomposite-coated films were dried at 120 °C for 60 min to remove residual solvents, ensuring uniform polymer–nanocomposite interaction. Resulting PANI/rGO/5%Cu2+-ZnO, PPy/rGO/5%Cu2+-ZnO, and PMMA/rGO/5% Cu2+-ZnO electrodes were then sensitized by immersion in a 0.4 mM N719 dye solution in ethanol for 24 h. After that, the electrodes were rinsed and dried for subsequent DSSC assembly.

Characterization

The structural properties were investigated using X-ray diffraction (XRD, Cu Kα, λ = 1.5406 Å, PANalytical X’pert Pro model). The morphology of the nanocomposite were examined through field emission electron microscopy (FESEM) using a JEM-2100 Plus instrument (JEOL, Japan). We have used the Thermo Scientific Apreo S model instrument operating at high vacuum for attaining the transmission electron microscopic images. Raman spectroscopy was carried out to verify the rGO incorporation and associated defect modes using the HORIBA LabRam HR Evolution model. UV–visible absorption spectra was acquired to evaluate band gap modifications between 200 and 700 nm resulting from Cu doping using a JASCO 650 model instrument. Photoluminescence (PL) spectra were analyzed using a 325 nm excitation source with a Fluorolog HORIBA instrument. Room-temperature hall measurements were performed using an HMS-3000/0.55 T Ecopia, South Korea, with the thin films obtained. The current–voltage (I–V) characteristics of the fabricated photovoltaic devices were measured using a Class A solar simulator (SL-50A-WS, Sciencetech) under AM1.5 illumination (100 mW cm–2). Also, we have attained incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) measurements using a Xe lamp coupled with a monochromator. The monochromatic beam was focused onto the active area of the devices using a two-lens optical setup. Prior to measurement, the IPCE system was calibrated with a standard photodiode to ensure an accurate spectral response.

Results and Discussion

Materials Formation Mechanism

Material formation of Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites proceeds through coordinated chelation, controlled precipitation, and thermally induced phase evolution. Initially, citric acid complexes were introduced with Zn2+ and Cu2+ ions, stabilizing them in solution while simultaneously anchoring metal ions onto the rGO surface through electrostatic and π–π interactions. Upon introduction of oxalic acid, insoluble mixed metal oxalate nuclei (Zn/Cu-oxalates) rapidly form and deposit uniformly across the rGO sheets due to heterogeneous nucleation. The aging process allows the precipitates to grow and strengthen the interfacial bonding between the metal precursors and the rGO framework. Subsequent drying removes moisture, whereas calcination at 500 °C decomposes the metal oxalates into crystalline ZnO, during which Cu2+ ions substitute into the Zn2+ lattice sites or occupy interstitial positions, generating defect states that enhance charge separation. Simultaneously, rGO becomes thermally stabilized and remains well-integrated with ZnO nanoparticles, producing a robust Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposite with improved electronic conductivity and interfacial charge transfer pathways. This in situ growth inhibits nanoparticle agglomeration and achieves the uniform coverage a formation of Cu2+-doped ZnO nanoparticles on rGO sheets. , rGO offers a π-conjugated conductive network, facilitating charge separation and reducing electron–hole recombination during the excitation process.

Structural Analysis

Crystalline information on the prepared nanocomposite was attained with the X-ray diffraction technique and is provided in Figure The XRD pattern of the GO exhibited a diffraction peak at 2θ = 10.7°, which represents the (002) plane of the graphene sheets. Further thermal reduction routed formation of rGO was confirmed with the broader peak observed at 2θ = 24.7°, which is in agreement with the reported literature. For pristine ZnO/rGO, sharp diffraction peaks were observed at 2θ ≈ 31.7°, 34.4°, 36.2°, 47.5°, 56.6°, 62.8°, and 67.9°, which are assigned to the (100), (002), (101), (102), (110), (103), and (112) planes of the hexagonal wurtzite ZnO structure, respectively (JCPDS card No. 36–1451). The absence of extraneous reflections confirmed the high phase purity of ZnO, with no residual hydroxide or metallic impurities detected. The strong intensity and narrow fwhm of the diffraction peaks also suggested the formation of highly crystalline ZnO nanoparticles. The invisibility of the peaks corresponding to rGO could be due to the well-crystalline nature and quantity of ZnO present in the composite specimen.

X-ray diffraction patterns of the GO, rGO, and various Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites.

In Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO composites (1, 3, and 5 at. % Cu2+), all the characteristic ZnO wurtzite peaks were retained, confirming that the host lattice structure remained stable under doping. Importantly, no additional reflections attributable to CuO or Cu2O phases were detected, indicating that Cu2+ ions were incorporated substitutionally into the ZnO lattice rather than secondary oxide phases. A discernible shift in peak positions, particularly for the (101) and (002) reflections, toward higher 2θ values was observed with increasing Cu2+ concentration. This insignificant peak shift can be attributed to the replacement of Zn2+ ions (ionic radius = 0.75 Å) by similar ionic radii Cu2+ ions (0.74 Å). , The average crystallite size was calculated from the Scherrer equation using the (101) reflection. The crystallite size of pristine ZnO was ∼32 nm, which gradually decreased to ∼29 nm (1% Cu2+), ∼27 nm (3% Cu2+), and ∼26 nm (5% Cu2+). This reduction in crystallite size indicates that Cu2+ doping inhibited the crystal growth, most likely due to induced lattice strain and defect formation during the nucleation process. The Williamson–Hall (W–H) analysis further confirmed an increase in microstrain (ε) with Cu2+ content, rising from 2.3 × 10–3 in undoped ZnO to 3.8 × 10–3 for 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO, which reflects enhanced lattice distortion at higher doping levels.

Raman Spectroscopy Analysis

Raman spectroscopy was carried out to probe the structural features, defect evolution, and interfacial interactions in GO, pristine rGO, pristine ZnO/rGO, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO composites (Figure ). The spectrum of pristine rGO exhibited two major peaks: the D band at ∼1350 cm–1, which originates from breathing modes of sp2 carbon atoms in disordered domains, and the G band at ∼1580 cm–1, associated with the E2g phonon of graphitic carbon. , The I D/I G intensity ratio for pristine rGO was ∼1.13, suggesting a moderate degree of disorder consistent with reduced graphene oxide rather than highly crystalline graphene. Upon hybridization with ZnO, these signatures became evident alongside the carbon bands. The ID/IG ratio for rGO in the ZnO/rGO composite increased slightly to ∼1.14, reflecting enhanced structural disorder due to intimate ZnO–rGO interfacial coupling. Furthermore, the I D/I G ratio of rGO increased to ∼1.17 for the 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO composite, pointing toward additional structural distortions and possible charge transfer interactions at the rGO–Cu:ZnO interface. Overall, the Raman analysis confirms that Cu2+ doping not only perturbs the ZnO phonon structure but also enhances defect-mediated interactions with rGO, which can be beneficial for improved light harvesting and charge transport in optoelectronic applications, such as DSSCs.

Morphological Analysis

FESEM images obtained for the Cu2+:ZnO/rGO nanocomposite show a clear and systematic evolution of ZnO morphology as Cu2+ content increased in the ZnO system. Pristine ZnO/rGO appears as relatively large, blocky/prismatic crystallites possessing sharp facets and a broad size distribution of 1–1.5 μm (Figure a–c), whereas low-level Cu2+ doping (1%) produces more fragmented, irregular aggregates with reduced lateral dimensions of 0.5–0.7 μm and higher surface roughness (Figure d–f). At an increased concentration of Cu2+ doping (3%), the particles progressively reorganize into compact, roughly spherical microscale assemblies (1.5 to 1.7 μm), and the individual micrometer-sized particles are composed of much smaller primary crystallites (Figure g–i). This reorganization of nanostructures further continued at 5% Cu2+ doping in the ZnO/rGO nanocomposite, and the sizes of the particles were measured to be 1.7 to 2.4 μm. The 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO sample also shows uniform, sphere-like clusters with high surface texturing (Figure j–l). In all these FESEM images, rGO sheets were rarely identified, and for that reason, and also due to more insights on the morphology, we have attained the HRTEM images in particular for the 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposite (Figure a–d). HRTEM images of the 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposite confirm that the spheres observed in FESEM analysis are built from dense packs of nanocrystals sitting on and around thin rGO sheets. Low-magnification HRTEM images (Figure a,b) reveal the rGO support wrapping or lying beneath the ZnO clusters, while high-resolution images (Figure c,d) display well-resolved lattice fringes in the ZnO domains, indicating retained crystallinity of the oxide despite significant morphological changes. The crystalline details obtained with the XRD patterns also match with these results. Further, overall, the microscopy supports a nucleation-and-growth picture in which Cu2+ doping increases the number of nucleation sites (grain refinement) and promotes self-assembly into hierarchical spherical aggregates, while rGO acts as a dispersing, conductive scaffold that limits macroscopic agglomeration and anchors ZnO nanocrystals.

FESEM images of pristine ZnO/rGO (a–c), 1% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO (d–f), 3% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO (g–i), and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO (j–l).

Transmission electron microscopic images of 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO obtained at low magnification (a, b) and high magnification (c, d), and EDS spectrum of the 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO specimen (f).

The elemental dispersive X-ray spectrum (EDS) quantification and the compositional map further corroborate the presence of all intended elements and support explaining the structural behavior (Figure f). The spectrum table shows Zn as the dominant metal (wt % Zn ≈ 68.7; at. % ≈ 35.2) with O ≈ 22.1 wt % (45.1 at. %), carbon from rGO ≈ 6.9 wt % (18.6 at. %), and Cu2+ present at a lower concentration (wt % ≈ 2.3; at. % ≈ 1.14), consistent with intentional low-level doping rather than separate copper phases. The small, however, measurable Cu2+ content combined with the preserved ZnO lattice fringes implies substitutional incorporation or strong lattice interaction rather than macroscopic CuO segregation. This would have likely introduced lattice distortion in the attained morphology and point defects that explain the observed grain refinement and altered aggregation.

Optical Absorption Properties

The optical absorption behavior of pristine ZnO/rGO and Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites with different Cu2+ doping concentrations (0%, 1%, 3%, and 5%) was investigated by using UV–visible spectroscopy in the 200–800 nm range (Figure ). Pristine ZnO/rGO exhibited a sharp absorption edge near ∼375 nm, corresponding to the intrinsic band-to-band transition of ZnO with an estimated band gap of ∼3.28 eV. In addition to this fundamental absorption, the composite showed a broadband background extending into the visible region (400–650 nm), which is attributed to the π–π* electronic transitions of the sp2 carbon framework in rGO and the strong interfacial charge transfer interactions between ZnO nanoparticles and rGO sheets. This hybrid effect enhances light-harvesting capability; in addition, it improves charge separation, as rGO acts as an electron acceptor and transporter.

UV–vis absorption spectra of pristine ZnO/rGO and 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites.

With Cu2+ incorporation, a systematic red shift of the absorption edge was observed, accompanied by a significant enhancement in the visible-light absorption. Tauc plot analysis revealed a progressive reduction in the optical band gap from 3.28 eV (0% Cu2+) to 3.20 eV (1% Cu2+), 3.12 eV (3% Cu2+), and 3.05 eV (5% Cu2+). This band gap narrowing arises from sp–d exchange interactions between the conduction band electrons of ZnO and the localized Cu2+ 3d states, which perturb the band structure. , Additionally, substitutional incorporation of Cu2+ ions (0.73 Å) into Zn2+ lattice sites (0.74 Å) induces local lattice strain and defect states, creating shallow energy levels near the conduction band that enable sub-band gap optical transitions. ,

Notably, the visible absorption intensity increased markedly with higher Cu2+ doping levels, particularly in the 450–600 nm region. This enhanced absorption is linked to defect-assisted electronic transitions, such as those associated with oxygen vacancies and Cu2+-related defect centers, which act as intermediate states between the valence and conduction bands. , At 5% Cu2+ doping, the nanocomposite demonstrated the broadest and most intense visible absorption, confirming that defect engineering through Cu2+ incorporation is effective in extending the spectral response of ZnO to the visible range. The synergistic effects of rGO hybridization and Cu2+ doping thus combine two mechanisms: (i) broadband absorption from delocalized π-electrons of rGO and interfacial charge transfer, and (ii) band gap narrowing and defect-state-mediated visible-light absorption from Cu2+ substitution. Together, these modifications are expected to significantly improve the light-harvesting efficiency and charge carrier generation of the composites, making them promising candidates for photoactive applications, such as dye-sensitized solar cells and photocatalysis.

Photoluminescence (PL) Analysis

The PL spectra of pristine ZnO/rGO and Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO at various concentrations were recorded at room temperature under 350 nm excitation to investigate excitonic recombination, defect emissions, and interfacial charge transfer dynamics (Figure ). Pristine ZnO/rGO displayed two primary emission regions: a near-band-edge (NBE) ultraviolet emission centered at ∼385 nm, attributed to the direct recombination of free excitons in ZnO, and a broad visible emission band spanning 450–600 nm, arising from intrinsic defect states. The visible emission is typically associated with oxygen vacancies (VO), zinc interstitials (Zni), and surface-related trap states. , The incorporation of rGO partially suppressed the NBE emission intensity relative to pure ZnO (not shown), consistent with rGO acting as an electron acceptor that extracts photogenerated electrons from ZnO, thereby reducing radiative recombination and favoring charge separation. For Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites, distinct modifications in the PL response were observed. As the Cu2+ content increased (1–5 at. %), the intensity of the emission decreased progressively (∼450–620 nm). This indicates that the Cu2+ ions act as an electron acceptor level, and thus, the direct recombination of the ZnO exciton is inhibited. This trend continued in our experiment up to 5% Cu2+ doping in ZnO.

Photoluminescence spectra of pristine ZnO/rGO and 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites.

Electrical Properties

We have conducted the Hall effect measurements of the Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO specimens, showing the n-type semiconductivity in all the specimens, and the data are plotted in Figure . The pristine ZnO/rGO nanocomposite exhibited the carrier density and mobility of 2.82 × 1018 cm–3 and 26.5 cm2·V–1·s–1, respectively. Also, the conductivity was measured as 19.2 Ohm–1 cm–1. The carrier densities of 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO specimens were measured, and the values are 2.9 × 1018 cm–3, 2.97 × 1018 cm–3, and 3.09 × 1018 cm–3, respectively, indicating the doping enhanced charge carrier density. Similarly, the mobility values recorded for 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO specimens were 32.6 cm2·V–1·s–1, 40.8 cm2·V–1·s–1, and 42.8 cm2·V–1·s–1, respectively. The electrical conductivity values of 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO specimens were measured to be 21.4 Ohm–1 cm–1, 22.0 Ohm–1 cm–1 , and 23.2 Ohm–1 cm–1, respectively. These values suggest that enhanced electrical properties originated from Cu2+ ion doping in the ZnO/rGO heterostructure composite. The obtained results are highly comparable to the existing reports on Cu2+-doped ZnO systems.

Electrical conductivity, carrier concentration, and mobility values obtained for various doping concentrations of Cu2+ in the Cu2+:ZnO/rGO nanocomposite.

DSSCs with ZnO/rGO and Cu2+-Doped ZnO/rGO Composites

To address the limitations of Pt-based counter electrodes and TiO2-based photoanodes, a series of dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) were fabricated by using pristine ZnO/rGO and Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites as photoanodes. The motivation to replace Pt and TiO2 lies in the high processing cost of Pt and the relatively lower electron mobility in TiO2 compared to ZnO. ZnO possesses a similar band gap (3.3 eV); however, it has higher electron mobility (∼200–300 cm2 V–1 s–1), making it attractive for DSSC applications. The introduction of reduced graphene oxide (rGO) as a conductive support further improves charge transport, while Cu2+ doping serves as a band-gap-tuning and defect engineering strategy to enhance visible-light absorption and reduce recombination. We have systematically studied four systems, namely, the pristine ZnO/rGO photoanode and 1%, 3%, and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO photoanodes, and the obtained solar cell characteristics are plotted in Figure a. In all cases, the nanocomposites were processed into stable dispersions, doctor-bladed onto FTO substrates, and annealed to form porous photoanode films. Each device was sensitized with N719 dye and assembled in a sandwich configuration with a Pt-free counter electrode (carbon-coated FTO) and an iodide/triiodide redox electrolyte.

I–J characteristics of DSSC devices obtained with pristine and 1%, 3% and 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposites (a) and their corresponding IPCE spectra (b).

The photovoltaic measurements demonstrated a progressive improvement in DSSC efficiency with Cu2+ doping. The pristine ZnO/rGO device showed a relatively low power conversion efficiency (PCE) of ∼3.1% due to fast recombination at surface trap states. With 1% Cu2+ doping, the PCE increased to 3.8%, while 3% doping achieved ∼4.3%. The highest performance was obtained for 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO, where the synergistic effect of lattice defect modulation and rGO-assisted charge transfer yielded a PCE of 4.9%. Beyond 5% doping (not shown), efficiency declined, which could be due to possible excessive recombination from dopant-induced deep defect states. The obtained results are comparable to the existing literature studies on ZnO and TiO2 systems studied for the DSSCs. − A pronounced improvement in IPCE is observed with an increasing Cu2+ content (Figure b). The incorporation of Cu2+ elevates the photon-to-current conversion efficiency to ∼70%, in contrast to the dopant-free ZnO/rGO-based device, which remains limited to approximately 57% (Figure ).

Effect of Embedding the Nanocomposite in Polymers (PMMA/PANI/PPy)

To further stabilize the nanocomposite photoanodes and improve film-forming properties, various polymers (PMMA/PANI/PPy) were introduced as a polymer matrix. The polymer could prevent agglomeration of Cu2+:ZnO/rGO nanoparticles and enhance the interface with the FTO substrate. Based on the high efficiency of the 5% Cu2+-doped device obtained without any polymer, we have further studied elaborately with the mentioned polymers combined with the 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposite. This incorporation produced three additional device systems, namely, 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PMMA photoanode, 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI photoanode, and 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/Ppy photoanode, and the obtained solar cell characteristics (J–V) are provided in Figure .

I–J characteristics of DSSC devices obtained with the 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposite embedded in various polymers (PPy, PANI, and PMMA).

The effect of polymers can be realized in 2-fold. − First, polymers passivated surface defects on ZnO, reducing charge recombination. , Second, the polymers improve the mechanical stability of the films, making them more resistant to electrolyte infiltration. , Improvement in open-circuit voltage (V oc) and fill factor (FF) were noted with this. For instance, while 5% Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO achieved 4.9% efficiency, its PMMA-based counterpart reached 4.3%. However, the best-performing 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI system exhibited 5.6% efficiency, surpassing the PPy analogy (5.1%). These results confirm that polymer-assisted hybrid nanocomposites provide superior device stability and efficiency in Pt-free DSSCs, especially for PANI.

Among PPy, PANI, and PMMA, when combined with the Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO nanocomposite for DSSC application, PANI showed the highest efficiency, followed by PPy and PMMA. PANI is a well-known conducting polymer with excellent charge transport ability and a favorable energy level, matching with ZnO, which enhances the charge extraction and reduces recombination, facilitating the high-power conversion efficiency. PPy also means to increase the charge separation and interfacial contact; however, it showed a lower efficiency than PANI-based devices, possibly due to less favorable conductivity. On the other hand, PMMA generally shows less conductivity and is mainly known for film uniformity and optical transparency, thus contributing the least to electrical performance. Hence, the obtained efficiency order is PANI > PPy > PMMA, with PANI being the most effective for ZnO-based solar devices.

Influence of Film Thickness (5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI)

A study on the thickness of the nanocomposite layer was conducted using the optimized 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI composition. Nanocomposite films with thicknesses of ∼6 μm, 13 μm, 18 μm, and 24 μm were prepared by controlling doctor-blade deposition and the annealing process. At 3 μm, the film showed low light-harvesting capacity (PCE of 2.4%). Thin film thickness increasing to 13 and 15 μm would have enhanced dye loading with the volume of the film increasing, enhancing J sc and efficiency to 4.6% and 5.6%, respectively. Further increase in the thickness of the thin film to 24 μm resulted in a decrease in the solar cell efficiency to 4.8%. The optimal performance of DSSC was attained at 18 μm, where sufficient dye loading and balanced charge transport resulted in a J sc of 14.8 mA cm–2, a V oc of 0.72 V, and an efficiency of 5.6%. Accordingly, a film thickness of ∼18 μm was identified as the optimal thickness for Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI photoanodes, balancing dye adsorption with efficient electron transport. Mechanism of DSSC Operation

The plausible working mechanism of DSSCs based on ZnO/rGO and Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO/PANI can be explained by using the energy band alignment as follows. Upon illumination of light, the N719 dye absorbs photons and executes the photoexcitation of the dye, promoting electrons from the HOMO to the LUMO level. Next, the excited electrons are injected into the conduction band (CB) of ZnO (−4.2 eV), as the dye LUMO (−3.8 eV) lies slightly above ZnO CB. , Cu2+ ion doping reduces the band gap of ZnO and slightly lowers the CB edge, facilitating faster electron injection. Electrons are injected in ZnO, and the electrons excited in ZnO migrate across ZnO nanoparticles and quickly transfer to rGO sheets, which act as an electron acceptor, due to their high carrier mobility. This process suppresses recombination occurrences and hastens the electron transport to the FTO substrate. Incorporation of the PANI polymer additionally improves the process of electron extraction from rGO and promotes more conductivity. The oxidized dye molecules are reduced back to their ground state by I– ions in the electrolyte. I– is oxidized to I3 –, which then diffuses to the counter electrode. At the Pt-free carbon electrode, I3 – is reduced back to I–, completing the redox cycle.

Our experimental findings reveal that Pt-free DSSCs with Cu2+-doped ZnO/rGO/Polymer (PANI, PPy, and PMMA) nanocomposites exhibited effective solar energy conversion, where Cu2+ doping markedly improves visible-light absorption and charge transport. The incorporation of PANI further improves stability and efficiency, yielding a maximum PCE of 5.6% for 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO/PANI with an ∼14 μm film thickness. These results highlight the synergistic roles of Cu2+ doping, rGO hybridization, and PANI encapsulation in advancing ZnO-based DSSCs and open pathways for scalable, low-cost, and flexible solar cell technologies.

Recent literature (2020–2025) on ZnO-based DSSC photoanodes reports PCE values typically ranging from 0.74% (multigrowth ZnO nanorods) to ∼4.5% (rGO/ZnO nanorods), with pure ZnO systems often below 2–3% due to recombination losses (e.g., 2.05% aged ZnO sol.; 2.43% Co/Ga-ZnO). Doped/composite variants show modest gains, such as ∼3.1% for rGO-ZnO and 6.22% Ti-ZnO (as a transport layer), while PANI-ZnO counter electrodes (not photoanodes) reach 7.34%. The present Cu-ZnO/rGO/PANI ternary system achieves 5.6% PCE (J sc = 14.8 mA/cm2, V oc = 0.72 V, FF = 53%), outperforming most ZnO/rGO benchmarks by 30–80% and approaching TiO2 levels (6.46% Fe/S-TiO2), due to synergistic band gap narrowing (from 3.28 to 3.05 eV), rGO charge extraction, and PANI interfacial optimization.

Conclusion

In this report, Cu2+:ZnO/rGO nanocomposites with varying Cu2+ concentrations (0–5 at. %) were successfully synthesized via a wet-chemical route and demonstrated tunable structural, optical, and electronic properties suitable for DSSC applications. Cu2+ doping induced band gap narrowing from 3.28 to 3.05 eV, improved visible-light absorption (450–800 nm), and enhanced charge carrier mobility from 26.5 to 42.8 cm2·V–1·s–1, while rGO facilitated rapid electron extraction and suppressed radiative recombination. Morphological transformation into porous microspheres (∼2 μm) composed of 50–80 nm crystallites and confirmed via FESEM/HRTEM provided improved surface area for dye anchoring. When incorporated in DSSC devices, the optimized 5% Cu2+:ZnO/rGO photoanode achieved 4.9% efficiency, which further increased to 5.6% upon integration with PANI, attributed to synergistic band alignment, interfacial conductivity, and enhanced charge mobility. Overall, the synthesized hybrid acts as a low-cost Pt-free photoanode that outperforms TiO2 in DSSCs.

Factors Associated with Successful Liberation from Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy in Critically Ill Patients: A Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Satish Patel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionContinuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) supports hemodynamically unstable critically ill patients with acute kidney injury who cannot tolerate intermittent dialysis. Identifying predictors for successful CRRT discontinuation remains a challenge. Premature cessation may cause fluid and electrolyte imbalance, while delayed withdrawal increases morbidity and hospital stay. This study aims to identify clinical, hemodynamic, fluid balance related, and disease severity parameters associated with successful liberation from CRRT in critically ill patients.ObjectivesThe primary objective of this study was to assess the association between fluid balance gap percentage (FB Gap%) and successful liberation from continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT). The secondary objectives was to evaluate the relationship between vasoactive-inotropic score (VIS) and successful liberation from CRRT, and to determine the association between disease severity, as measured by Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) and Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) scores at ICU admission and at CRRT initiation, with successful liberation from CRRT.Materials and methodsThis prospective observational study conducted in the Critical Care Unit at AIIMS, Raipur. Patients (≥18 years) with acute kidney injury or acute-on-chronic kidney disease requiring CRRT due to hemodynamic instability and fluid overload included after informed consent. Those on CRRT for non-renal indications, prior renal transplant, or mortality within 24 hours of CRRT initiation excluded. Baseline demographics, comorbidities, diagnosis, and disease severity scores (APACHE II and SOFA) are recorded at ICU admission and CRRT initiation. Daily fluid balance goal, achieved balance, and FB gap % documented. Vasoactive-inotropic score (VIS) at CRRT initiation and at liberation noted. Liberation criteria include urine output >500 mL/day without diuretics, and hemodynamic stability.ResultsThe interim analysis of this prospective study including 20 critically ill patients out of a total of 30 sample size showed. Fluid gap % in patients not liberated from CRRT (n = 15):mean FB gap %= 32.52 (SD 10.04), Median = 30.5, Range = 16.5 to 50.0.For patients who were successfully liberated from CRRT (n = 5):Mean FB gap% = 22.00 (SD 5.70) Median = 20.0, Range = 15.0 to 30.0. p value 0.013.Patients who were successfully liberated from CRRT had significantly lower values in:Vasopressor-inotropic score (VIS) at initiation (p = 0.007),VIS at CRRT end (p = 0.0004),SOFA score at CRRT end (p < 0.0001), APACHE II score at admission (p = 0.034), Average fluid balance gap (FB gap%) (p = 0.013). However, SOFA scores at admission and at CRRT initiation showed no statistically significant difference between liberated and non-liberated groups (p > 0.11 and p > 0.18 respectively).ConclusionsPatients with a lower average fluid balance gap % had a higher chance of successful liberation from CRRT. The findings also suggested that lower organ dysfunction at CRRT termination, lower overall severity of illness at admission calculated as APACHE II, are also associated with successful liberation from CRRT. Also the patients successfully liberated from CRRT are associated with significantly lower vasopressor and inotropic support both at initiation and especially by CRRT end.Keywords: CRRT, Fluid balance gap, Vasoactive-inotropic score, Disease severity, Liberation from CRRT

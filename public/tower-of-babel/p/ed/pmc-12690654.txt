PDNO-23 Surgical management of OPG in children: our experience in the era of molecular biology
Authors: Nabila Tighilt, Amina Kribaa, Amal Bougamra, Fatiha Gachi, Abdelhalim Morsli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOptic pathway gliomas are low-grade neoplasms that can cause significant morbidity and represent a therapeutic challenge. We present our experience in the management of optic pathway gliomas in a multidisciplinary collaboration in the era of diagnostic and therapeutic advances.Materials and MethodsWe report a retrospective series of 25 cases of optic pathway gliomas. Different surgeries performed varied depending on the indication, including optic nerve transection, tumor debulking, tumor biopsy, VP shunting, and intracystic reservoir placement.ResultsType 1 neurofibromatosis was found in 50% of cases. Diencephalic cachexia revealed the diagnosis in 10% of cases. Pathological evidence was obtained in 10% of cases by biopsy or excision for tumor debulking. Chemotherapy was initiated in 90% of cases, and radiotherapy was reserved as a second-line treatment for tumor progression in older children.Discussion/ ConclusionOver the past decade, emphasis has been placed on chemotherapy as the first-line treatment for symptomatic tumors. Radiotherapy is also effective, but its use is delayed. The precise role of neurosurgery remains controversial due to the potentially unacceptable risk of morbidity. Therefore, it remains reserved for large tumors causing mass effect or hydrocephalus and tumors confined to the optic nerve. However, modern clinical practice requires tumor tissue to be examined histologically and molecularly in sporadic cases. Advances in diagnostic techniques, such as tractography and molecular biology, and therapeutic techniques, such as neuronavigation and intraoperative MRI, have greatly contributed to patient selection and appropriate surgical planning, thereby improving the care of these patients, particularly by reducing surgical morbidity and improving prognosis.

The current status of primary and secondary school students with myopia in the Shekou district of Shenzhen: a retrospective study
Authors: Zixuan Xiao, Hui Zhou, Hongmei Wang, Xiaomin Quan, Jianjun Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMyopia is a growing global health issue. This study aims to determine the current status and determinants of primary and secondary school students with myopia in the Shekou District, and to propose reforms in myopia prevention.MethodsFour thousand seven hundred and sixty-three students were involved in this retrospective study, and categorized by spherical equivalent into normal, low myopia, moderate myopia, and high myopia. Multinomial logistic regression and multiple linear regression analyses were conducted to examine factors on the severity of myopia and axial length [AL], respectively.ResultsThe multinomial logistic regression model indicated, compared to the normal group, each additional year of age significantly increased the odds for low myopia (odds ratio [OR] = 1.285, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.122–1.472), moderate myopia (OR = 1.271, 95% CI: 1.074–1.502), and high myopia (OR = 1.411, 95% CI: 1.090–1.826). Compared to the Grade 1, the OR for low myopia showed a gradual increase across grades: Grade 3 (OR = 2.065, 95% CI: 1.394–3.058), Grade 4(OR = 2.184, 95% CI: 1.338–3.562), Grade 5(OR = 2.640, 95% CI: 1.438–4.850) and Grade 6 (OR = 2.239, 95% CI: 1.077–4.657); The OR for moderate myopia rose significantly from Grade 2 (OR = 2.239, 95% CI: 1.077–4.657), peaked at Grade 9 (OR = 90.445, 95% CI: 14.325–571.040), and remained elevated at Grade 12 (OR = 72.069, 95% CI: 3.818–1360.260); The OR for high myopia was significantly elevated in Grade 6 (OR = 11.490, 95% CI: 1.082–122.077) and Grade 9 (OR = 26.259, 95% CI: 1.429–482.616). The multiple linear regression model indicated that longer AL was significantly associated with older age (β = 0.109, 95% CI: 0.065–0.141), males(β = 0.533, 95% CI: 0.482–0.583), and higher grade level (the largest effect observed at Grade 9, β = 1.124, 95% CI: 0.792–1.455).ConclusionMost myopic students in Shekou District exhibited low or moderate myopia. Alleviating educational stress and implementing AL monitoring are the essential measures for myopia prevention. A comprehensive myopia prevention necessitates a two-pronged strategy combining educational reforms and medical interventions.

Background

Myopia and its complications are a growing global health issue [1, 2]. A previous study estimated that the global prevalence of myopia would increase to 39.9% in 2030 and 50% in 2050 [1]. The deterioration of myopia is accompanied by an increase in the axial length (AL), which can cause choroidal and retinal atrophy, resulting in irreversible vision impairment [1, 3–5]. The irreversible visual impairment caused by myopia would decline the quality of life and increase financial burdens [6, 7].

Strengthening the prevention of myopia has become a consensus among the global public health system [8–12]. Many countries, including China, have conducted surveys on the prevalence of myopia and implemented corresponding measures to control its progression [10]. Previous studies indicate that the prevalence of myopia in China is the highest in the world, with estimates ranging from 59.4 to 82.5% among primary and secondary school students [13]. Moreover, the trend toward an earlier onset of myopia has emerged recently [13]. It is warranted ‌to improve the existing measures to curb these trends. The purpose of this study was to determine the current status of primary and secondary school students with myopia in the Shekou District, and to propose reforms in myopia prevention.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This retrospective study has been approved by the Human Medical Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Qianhai Shekou Free Trade Zone Hospital (2025-KY-011–01 K), which was in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for individual consent was waived by the committee because of the retrospective nature of the study.

Subjects

This is a retrospective study that included all Shenzhen students who attended the ophthalmology clinic of Shenzhen Qianhai Shekou Free Trade Zone Hospital from January 2023 to April 2025. For subjects with repeat visits, data from the initial visit were retained. The eye with the highest myopic refractive error of a subject was designated as the index eye for analysis. For participants with equivalent refractive error in both eyes, the right eye was selected as the index eye to maintain one eye per subject in the analysis. Four thousand seven hundred and sixty-three eyes from 4,763 students who met the inclusion/exclusion criteria were involved. Based on the grade information when students attended the ophthalmology clinic, the subjects were categorized into twelve grade level groups (Grade 1 through Grade 12). All subjects received complete ophthalmic examinations, including the slit-lamp examination (BQ900, Haag-Streit, Köniz, Schweiz), intraocular pressure (TX-20, Canon, Tochigi, Japan), AL (LS900, Haag-Streit, Köniz, Schweiz), and fundus stereoscopic examination. All study subjects received the comprehensive vision examination, including the objective cycloplegic refraction and the subjective refraction, by an experienced optometrist with a phoropter. The objective cycloplegic refraction was performed with cycloplegia by using a mixture of 0.5% tropicamide and 0.5% phenylephrine hydrochloride (Zhuobi’an, Sinqi Ophthalmic Medications, Shenyang, China).

The exclusion criteria included were as follows: (1) age < 6 years old or > 18 years old; (2) amblyopia or anisometropia; (3) opacity of refractive media; (4) intraocular pressure above the normal range; (5) a history of wearing orthokeratology lenses; (6) eye diseases in addition to ametropia; (7) a history of intraocular surgeries.

Statistical analysis

The spherical equivalent (SE) was calculated as (SE = sphere + 0.5*cylinder) and used to categorize the severity of myopia. Normal, low myopia, moderate myopia, and high myopia were defined as −0.5D < SE < + 0.5D, −3.0D < SE≤−0.5D, −6.0D < SE≤−3.0D, and SE≤−6.0D, respectively. Statistical analysis was performed with a commercially available software (SPSS ver. 26.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). Data distribution for normality was checked using the Shapiro-Wilk’s test. According to the data normality of the groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or Kruskal-Wallis H test was used to compare the age, SE, and AL among different refractive status groups. The χ2 test was used for the comparisons on gender, laterality, and grade level. Multinomial logistic regression analysis was conducted to examine the impact of age, grade level, and gender on myopia status. Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to examine the impact of age, grade level, and gender on AL. These independent variables were examined the multicollinearity by using the variance inflation factor (VIF) diagnostic method. All VIFs of these independent variables in both models were less than 10, meaning there is no serious multicollinearity problem among independent variables. P < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics of the study subjects

Four thousand seven hundred and sixty-three eyes from 4,763 students were involved in this retrospective study, including 752 eyes in the normal group, 3,029 eyes in the low myopia group, 828 eyes in the moderate myopia group, and 154 eyes in the high myopia group. The demographic characteristics of the study subjects are shown in Table 1. The mean ages of the normal, low myopia, moderate myopia, and high myopia groups were 8.97 ± 2.41 years, 10.88 ± 2.60 years, 12.58 ± 2.40 years, and 13.41 ± 2.31 years, respectively(P < 0.001). Mean SE of these groups were − 0.01 ± 0.30 D, −1.46 ± 0.66 D, −4.04 ± 0.81 D and − 7.13 ± 1.16 D respectively(P < 0.001). Mean AL of these groups were 23.29 ± 0.77 mm, 24.22 ± 0.83 mm, 25.28 ± 0.87 mm, and 26.39 ± 0.99 mm, respectively(P < 0.001). There was a significant difference in grade level among the four groups(P < 0.001). The normal group was primarily composed of Grade 1 students (154/753, 20.48%). The low myopia group was primarily composed of Grade 5 students (456/3029, 15.05%). Both the moderate myopia group (140/828,16.91%) and the high myopia group (31/154, 20.13%) were dominated by Grade 9 students. There were no statistically significant differences in gender and laterality among these groups.

Multinomial logistic regression of contributing factors to the severity of myopia

The multinomial logistic regression model revealed that the severity of myopia was significantly associated with age and grade level. The explanatory degree of the model reached 21.7% (Nagelkerke R2 = 0.217, χ2 = 998.108; P < 0.001). The multinomial logistic regression model is shown in Table 2. The severity of myopia showed a positive correlation with increasing age. Compared to the normal group, each additional year of age significantly increased the odds for myopia: low myopia (OR = 1.285, 95% CI: 1.122–1.472; P < 0.001), moderate myopia (OR = 1.271, 95% CI: 1.074–1.502; P = 0.005), and high myopia (OR = 1.411, 95% CI: 1.090–1.826; P = 0.009). The severity of myopia showed a positive correlation with advancing grade level. Compared to the Grade 1 (reference), the OR for low myopia showed a gradual increase across grades: Grade 3 (OR = 2.065, 95% CI: 1.394–3.058; P < 0.001), Grade 4(OR = 2.184, 95% CI: 1.338–3.562); P = 0.002), Grade 5(OR = 2.640, 95% CI: 1.438–4.850; P = 0.002) and Grade 6 (OR = 2.239, 95% CI: 1.077–4.657; P = 0.031); The OR for moderate myopia rose significantly from Grade 2 (OR = 2.239, 95% CI: 1.077–4.657; P = 0.031), peaked at Grade 9 (OR = 90.445, 95% CI: 14.325–571.040; P < 0.001), and remained elevated at Grade 12 (OR = 72.069, 95% CI: 3.818–1360.260; P = 0.004); The OR for high myopia was significantly elevated in Grade 6 (OR = 11.490, 95% CI: 1.082–122.077; P = 0.043) and Grade 9 (OR = 26.259, 95% CI: 1.429–482.616; P = 0.028). No significant association was observed between male sex and severity of myopia, with females as the reference.

Multiple linear regression of contributing factors to AL

The multiple linear regression model revealed that the increase in the AL was associated with age, gender, and grade level. The multiple linear regression model is shown in Table 3. The model explained 32.2% of AL variance (Adjusted R²=0.322, F (13,4749) = 175.23, P < 0.001). The multiple linear regression model indicated that longer AL was significantly associated with older age (β = 0.109, 95% CI: 0.065–0.141; P < 0.001), male sex (β = 0.533, 95% CI: 0.482–0.583; P < 0.001) and higher grade level (the largest effect observed at Grade 9, β = 1.124, 95% CI: 0.792–1.455; P < 0.001).

Discussion

This retrospective study investigated the status of primary and secondary school students with myopia in the Shekou District of Shenzhen, and possible risk factors contributing to myopia and longer AL. The majority of primary and secondary school students with myopia in the Shekou District exhibited low myopia or moderate myopia. Increasing age and grade level were both possible risk factors for myopia and longer AL. Additionally, a significant association was observed between longer AL and male sex.

This retrospective study found similar results to the prior studies [13–17], which reported that myopia among primary and secondary school students was primarily composed of low myopia and moderate myopia. The current study found that increasing age and grade level were possible risk factors for the severity of myopia. Both the moderate myopia group and the high myopia group were dominated by students of relatively high grade level. This result was similar to prior studies [14, 17–21]. Previous studies indicated that students of high grade level had to spend more time reading and writing to cope with the increasing educational stress [13]. The heavy educational stress deprives students of outdoor physical activities and adequate sleep, reducing the potential benefits of outdoor physical activities in preventing myopia [19, 20, 22]. The current study revealed that Grade 9 students exhibited the highest risk of both moderate and high myopia compared to all other grades. Similarly, within the primary school cohort, Grade 6 students showed the highest risk of these conditions. The deterioration of myopia may coincide with China’s junior and senior secondary education entrance examinations. To improve the physical fitness of primary and secondary school students, the Chinese government put the “Opinions on Further Reducing the Burden of Homework and Off-Campus Training for Students in Compulsory Education”, known as the “Double Reduction” policy, into practice in 2021 [22, 23]. The effectiveness of this policy for preventing myopia has been demonstrated [23]. Yet, a previous study indicated that the “Double Reduction” policy had not been fully implemented in China [22]. In a country with an exam-oriented education system, academic performance is overemphasized by educators and parents. The educational stress in China is not effectively alleviated [22]. Further alleviating educational stress could be a viable strategy to mitigate the increasing trend of myopia. The systemic educational reform may be more effective than biological interventions alone.

The current study indicated that increasing age and grade level were possible risk factors that contributed to longer AL. AL demonstrated an upward trend as students advance through successive grades. Notably, AL elongation clearly increased from Grade 5 to Grade 8, but decelerated after Grade 9, despite persistent educational stress. The growth tendency of AL coincided with the natural developmental stage of adolescence [24, 25]. Therefore, monitoring the AL across all ages in primary and secondary schools is necessary, and controlling the rate of AL elongation is more crucial.

The current study indicated that males tended to have a longer AL. Notably, the current study found no significant association between gender (male vs. female) and the severity of myopia. A previous study reported similar results that females have a shorter AL, a steeper corneal curvature, and a slightly greater myopic refractive error compared with males [25]. These findings implied that there existed several compensatory mechanisms. Previous studies indicated that the longer AL in males could be compensated by flatter corneal curvature or greater crystalline lens power, a mechanism that masks the real pathological alterations [25, 26]. It is warranted ‌to proceed with further studies based on the corneal topography to explore this discrepancy. These findings further highlight the importance of AL monitoring, which may reflect the real pathological changes of myopia.

Measurement of refractive error without cycloplegia could overestimate the myopic status in children [13, 16, 27, 28]. In the current study, all the subjects received the comprehensive vision examination, including the objective cycloplegic refraction and the subjective refraction. The current study indicated that the deterioration of myopia may coincide with China’s junior and senior secondary education entrance examinations. Resources for myopia prevention should be targeted at these students at these key stages, such as Grade 6 and Grade 9. Additionally, the integration of AL monitoring into the large-scale myopia screening is strongly warranted. A comprehensive myopia prevention necessitates a two-pronged strategy combining educational reforms and medical interventions.

There were several limitations in this retrospective study. Firstly, this single-center retrospective study enrolled myopic students from the ophthalmology clinic of Shenzhen Qianhai Shekou Free Trade Zone Hospital. The single-center retrospective design may introduce bias in the current study. Further multi-center studies are warranted to validate the findings. Secondly, there was a collinearity between age and grade level, although the collinearity is not severe (all VIF < 10). The age reflects the individual’s physiological development stage, while the grade level represents the duration of academic stress. These variables respectively correspond to the biological mechanism and the environmental behavioral mechanism in the mechanism of myopia. Further longitudinal studies can help to distinguish between age-related physiological myopia and grade-related behavioral myopia. Thirdly, only three parameters were analyzed in this study. There were many factors associated with myopia, including the time of outdoor activities, adequate sleep, nutritional intake, indoor lighting intensity, etc [13, 14, 16, 17, 19–22, 29, 30]. Further studies with more comprehensive measurements and optimized methods can help to clarify the risk factors in the development of myopia.

Conclusions

The majority of primary and secondary school students with myopia in the Shekou district exhibited low myopia or moderate myopia. Notably, the current study revealed that age and grade level were possible risk factors for myopia. The educational stress should not be ignored during myopia prevention. Additionally, monitoring the AL of primary and secondary school students with myopia is strongly warranted. We contend that the period marked by intensive academic demands represents a vital intervention window for myopia prevention, necessitating coordinated policy reforms in education and medical interventions.

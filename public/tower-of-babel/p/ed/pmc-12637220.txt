Ayurvedic management of psoriatic arthritis with Dactylitis - A Case Report
Authors: Sinimol Thekkekkootumughath Peethambaran, Sree Deepthi Girija Nalinakshan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This case report is the description of dactylitis associated with Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) of a thirty-eight years old male, government employee. He presented with complaints of pain, swelling, stiffness and restriction of movements in fingers of upper limb, especially distal interphalangeal joints (IJP) for 1 month. Skin manifestations like silvery flaky scales, itching over the scalp region and mild pinkish discoloration on mid dorsum of right foot were also present. Diagnosis was made with the aid of CASPAR criteria, history taking and clinical examination. Only temporary relief was obtained from non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) he had taken for 2 weeks as suggested by conventional treatment. In Ayurvedic parlance, PsA resembles conditions like Gambheera Vatrakta, due to involvement of skin and deeper tissues like bones and joints and dactylitis may be considered as Anguli sopha. Internal medications with properties alleviating three doshas (tridoshahara), skin diseases (kushtahara), vitiated rakta dhatu (raktahara), which detoxifies blood (raktaprasadana), anodyne (soolahara), anti-inflammatory (shothahara), rejuvenating (Rasayana) were used. The assessment was done before and after the treatment by using the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score and European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology's ‘The Psoriatic Arthritis Impact of Disease 12 (EULAR PsAID 12)’ questionnaire score. It was observed that symptoms were almost completely resolved with Ayurvedic management and changes in biochemical parameters were also noted. Prescribed Ayurvedic medications was found to be a good alternative therapy in managing dactylitis associated with PsA.

Introduction

One of the most common symptoms of psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is dactylitis, which is characterized by diffuse swelling of the digits and is caused by the involvement of many anatomic entities. It is a condition that 48 % of PsA patients encounter at some time during their illness. Dactylitis has clinical relevance as it is associated with reduced impaired motor function and appears to have prognostic value in the affected finger, reflecting overall disease gravity and serving as a gauge of PsA development. Biomechanical factors such as microtraumas causing flexor tenosynovitis, soft tissue oedema and joint synovitis are considered as the reason for the physical appearance of dactylitis [1]. Diagnosis is made by clinical examination and radiographic findings. Conventional treatment uses non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) [2].

In Ayurveda, the condition may be considered as Gambheera Vatarakta due to involvement of skin and deeper tissues like bones and joints. Pain, swelling, stiffness, restriction of movements of affected joints is present in both conditions. Basic pathology behind Gambheera Vatarakta is vitiation of rakta dhatu leading to raktavruta vata or viceversa [3]. Both internal and external treatment modalities are required for Ayurvedic management of the condition.

Case history

Patient information

A 38-year-old male patient, government employee doing clerical job visited Out Patient Department (OPD) of hospital with complaints of pain, stiffness, restriction of movements and swelling in fingers of upper limb, especially distal interphalangeal joint (IJP) for 1 month. He could not do his daily activities due to severe pain in the affected fingers. Skin manifestations like silvery flaky scales, itching over the scalp region and mild pinkish discoloration on mid dorsum of right foot were also present along with that. The condition started as silvery flaky scales with itching on the scalp and right foot, which started 2 years before, and was diagnosed as Psoriasis at that time clinically. He had no addictions, other significant comorbidities or family history.

History of presenting complaints

Past intervention with outcomes: The patient consulted and took conventional medicine during the starting of Psoriatic skin lesions 2 years before. The doctor recommended topical application of Salicylic acid in combination with Clobetasol and he got temporary relief from symptoms. But recurred about four times after that and one month before, pain, stiffness and swelling in fingers of upper limb, especially distal IJPs started. With NSAIDs and DMARDs also, temporary symptomatic relief was obtained.

Clinical findings

General examination

General condition and appearance indicated that patient was found ambulatory. No pallor, lymphadenopathy was seen. Temperature: 98.4°, Pulse Rate: 76/ minute, Heart rate: 72 regular, Respiratory rate: 16/ minutes, Weight: 69 kg, Height: 167 centimetre (cm), Blood Pressure: 126/80 mm of Mercury, Prakriti was Vatakapha predominant.

Physical examination

There was generalized swelling of all digits of the upper limb. Distal IJPs of all digits were very much swollen with tenderness and stiffness. Mild rise in local temperature was noted. Nail tips were pitted with dents and mild brownish yellow discoloration.

Psoriatic skin lesions were present almost all over the scalp, especially the posterior and upper aspect as silvery itchy flaky lesions. On the mid-dorsum of right foot, only irregular slight pinkish discoloration was present. Still, mild itching was present there also.

Also, the dorsalis pedis arterial pulsation was felt. No lymph node was found palpable. In systemic examinations, no abnormality was found.

This resulted in rasa, rakta, mamsa dhatu vitiation and sthanasamsraya of vitiated doshas at twak producing twakvaivarnyam and kandu. Continous Indulgence of raktadushtikara nidana sevana like virudhaharas further vitiated vata especially vyana vata. Diagnostic assessment (October 21, 2023): Regarding Haematologic investigations, blood routine examination, C-Reactive Protein (CRP), fasting blood sugar, post prandial blood sugar, Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) Factor, Anti Streptolysin O (ASO) titre, investigations were done. Haemoglobin was 13.5 g (g)%, Fasting Blood Sugar: 84mg% and Post Prandial Blood Sugar: 116 mg%, Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) Factor was 10 International Units (IU)/ millilitre (ml), ASO titre was 90 IU/ml, thus Rheumatoid Arthritis, Rheumatic fever and Diabetes mellitus was ruled out. Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR) was 27 mm/hr and CRP was 7.5 mg/l.

Diagnosis was made as Dactylitis associated with Psoriatic arthritis by history taking and clinical examination. Elevated ESR and CRP are also features of PsA. As per CASPAR criteria diagnosis was confirmed as Psoriatic arthritis as score was more than 3 (Evidence of psoriasis, Psoriatic nail dystrophy, A negative rheumatoid factor and Dactylitis were present) [4].

Therapeutic intervention

Internal medication Nimbamruta erandam was given in the beginning of the treatment as Virechana (purgation) for cleansing of the body for one day. After that, Amrutothara kwatha Tablet (Tab.) Kaishora guggulu, Punarnavasava, Tab. Amavatari Ras were given for 2 weeks. After that Mahatiktakam kwatha, Tab. Kaishora guggulu and Aragwadharishta were prescribed for the next 2 weeks. Throughout the course of treatment, Panchanimba paneeya, Madhusnuhi Rasayana were given as internal medications. Externally, Brihat Dantapala kera was applied over skin lesions by patient himself.

Advices given to the patient

Follow up and outcomes

First follow up was done on Day 10 on October 29, 2023, second follow up on November 12, 2023 and third follow up on November 19, 2023. Regarding laboratory investigations, tests were done on Day 37 (last follow up) after treatment. Haemoglobin was 13.3 g (g)%, Fasting Blood Sugar: 80mg% and Post Prandial Blood Sugar: 118 mg%, Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) Factor was 10 IU/ ml, ASO titre was 94 IU/ml, ESR was 8 mm/hr and CRP was 4.5 mg/l on last follow up. Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score was assessed as 7.2 on Day 1 and 0.2 on Day 37 [5]. The European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology's ‘Psoriatic Arthritis Impact of Disease 12-item questionnaire’ (EULAR PsAID 12) score on Day 1 was 7.7 and 0.6 on Day 37 [6].

Discussion

This case report is the description of dactylitis associated with Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) of a thirty-eight years old male, government employee. In Ayurvedic parlance, PsA resembles conditions like Gambheera Vatrakta, due to involvement of skin and deeper tissues like bones and joints and dactylitis may be considered as Anguli sopha. Internal medications with alleviating three doshas (tridoshahara), alleviating skin diseases (kushtahara), alleviating vitiated vata pitta dosha and rakta dhatu (vataraktahara), which detoxifies blood (raktaprasadana), anodyne (soolahara), anti-inflammatory (shothahara), rejuvenating (Rasayana) properties were used. Management of the condition started with purgation (Virechana) therapy because it is described in Ayurveda that if we externally apply medicines, without eliminating the internal toxins of our body through purificatory therapies (Sodhana), it will exacerbate the clinical condition [7].

Virechana was done with Nimbamrita eranda taila, which is indicated in the management of various conditions including vatarakta. It is also indicated in aggravation of vata dosha in bones and joints [8]. Initially management was focused to alleviate the symptoms related of Dactylitis. Amruthotharam kashayam acts as Deepana (appetiser), pachana (digestive), shophahara (anti-inflammatory) and rakta prasadana (detoxifying blood) properties and was employed primarily to counteract the inflammation of the digits [9]. Kaishora guggulu maintains health of connective tissue and is Pittahar, Rakta Shodhak (depurative), and Ojovardhak/ Rasayana (improving immunity), in action and will be beneficial in Psoriasis being an autoimmune disorder [10]. Punarnavasava is indicated in various inflammatory clinical conditions and is raktapittahara [11]. Amavatari Ras is a medicine indicated in painful inflammatory conditions, especially in its amavastha (Here, accumulation of Ama took place in joints) [12]. Along with above internal medications, Kwatha dhara (pouring of decoction over whole body) with Dashamoola kwatha which is anti-inflammatory (sophahara)in action was also done for 8 days till pain and swelling in digits was reduced to a considerable extent [13].

During the first follow up on October 30, 2023, swelling and pain of digits was considerably reduced. So, some of the medicines were changed, focusing on management of the root cause; Psoriasis. Mahatiktakam kwatha was used instead of Amruthotharam kwatha along with Kaisora guggulu. Mahatiktakam kwatha is indicated in all skin diseases as it reduces Kleda (Kleda Shoshanakar) in our body [14]. Aragwadharishta is also indicated in various morbidities of skin [15]. As external procedure, whole body dripping of medicated butter milk (Takradhara) was done for 2 weeks. It is a procedure described to be effective in subsiding three doshas, especially kapha and pitta due to its dry (rooksha), cold (seetha) qualities [16].

After that, only internal medicines, Panchanimba paneeya and Madhusnuhi rasayana which the patient was taking right from the beginning of the treatment were adviced for two more weeks. Brihat dantapala kera was also applied in the areas affected with skin lesions. Panchanimbadi Kwatha is a decoction helps in restoration of normal skin colour and is having depurative action [17]. Madhusnuhi Rasayana is laxative (Anulomana) and nourishing the tissues (Rasayana) and is indicated in various morbidities of skin [18]. Brihat dantapala kera (coconut oil processed with Wrightia tinctoria leaves in sunlight) was used for external application over skin lesions as it is proved to reduce exfoliation, inflammatory responses, itching, hyperkeratinization, silvery scales, and skin discolouration [19].

The major strengths of this case reporting are it is novel in the sense, signs and symptoms of Dactylitis associated with Psoriatic arthritis and Psoriasis were almost completely resolved. No much case reports are published so far in indexed journals on its Ayurvedic management. It is possible to apply the modalities used in this case to other analogous cases. The patient's tolerability and adherence to the intervention also contributed to the successful management. The primary constraint of the case report is its inability to generalize the findings because it was based on a single case study.

Conclusion

The presented combination of internal and external Ayurvedic medications was found to be a good alternative therapy in the management of Dactylitis associated with Psoriatic arthritis and Psoriasis. No adverse events were also reported.

Accidental Intracranial Small Bore Feeding Tube Insertion
Authors: Ian Brodka, Jennifer Wang, Lauren Elizabeth Shawver, Lauren Gillespie, Paul Jansson, Kylee Phillips
Published: 2026

Presentation

A middle-aged male presented to the emergency department for small-bore feeding tube (SBFT) complications. He had a history of stage 4 squamous cell carcinoma with multiple surgical resections including the maxillary sinus and cribriform plate with a pericranial flap. His SBFT was placed 2 months prior by otolaryngology in the operating room and presented to the ED after his SBFT was unable to be flushed at home. In the ED, the SBFT was removed and replaced through the left naris. Radiographs were obtained to confirm placement.

Diagnosis and Learning Points

Intracranial Placement of Small-Bore Feeding Tube

Initial placement x-ray (Fig 1) identified SBFT coiled in the nasopharynx. On the second attempt, the x-ray identified intracranial placement (Fig 2). Subsequent computed tomography of the head (Fig 3) revealed pneumocephalus. Neurosurgery and otolaryngology were consulted. Contraindications for blind nasogastric tube placement include traumatic injuries or surgical interventions to the face, sinuses, and nasopharynx, malignancy of the face and nasopharynx, and esophageal trauma, perforation, or obstruction. Additional considerations should be made for patients on anticoagulation for increased bleeding during placement and abnormal gastrointestinal anatomy including gastric bypass and hiatal hernia repair.

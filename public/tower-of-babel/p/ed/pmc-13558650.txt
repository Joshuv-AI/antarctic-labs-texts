Integrated bioinformatics analysis reveals fatty acid metabolism subtypes and immune landscape in recurrent implantation failure
Authors: Huiqi Liao, Honglu Diao, Libing Liang, Ziwei Ai, Ying Zhang, Zhiling Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMicroarray and RNA-sequencing datasets for recurrent implantation failure (RIF) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Differential expression analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) were subsequently integrated with lipid metabolism-related gene sets to identify candidate biomarkers. Cross-analysis of these approaches yielded seven candidate genes, which were then subjected to four machine learning algorithms. This led to the identification of five hub genes: PRKAG2, CPT1A, PPARGC1A, PIK3C2G, and PTGS2. Logistic regression further validated these genes as robust biomarkers, enabling the construction of a diagnostic nomogram. Molecular docking using CB-Dock Tools subsequently demonstrated that peucedanin binds favorably to all five hub gene products. Collectively, these findings highlight PRKAG2, CPT1A, PPARGC1A, PIK3C2G, and PTGS2 as promising diagnostic biomarkers for RIF and offer new perspectives for therapeutic intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57894-4.Keywords: Recurrent implantation failure, Lipid metabolism, Immune infiltration, Molecular cluster, WGCNA, Machine learning, Molecular dockingSubject terms: Biomarkers, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Genetics

Introduction

With the advancement of assisted reproductive technology, IVF has achieved favorable outcomes in the treatment of infertility. However, 5–10% of infertile patients still fail to achieve clinical pregnancy1. Studies have demonstrated that RIF is a significant restrictive factor affecting the improvement of clinical pregnancy rates after embryo transfer2,3. The causes of RIF are intricate and exhibit considerable variability across different individuals. In addition to factors related to genetic chromosomes4, endocrinology5, autoimmune diseases6, male factors7, and so on. More than half of the cases remain idiopathic, with most of these unexplained causes being associated with immune abnormalities8. With advancements in precision medicine and immunotherapy, the prognosis of certain patients with RIF has improved; the general outlook continues to be less than ideal. Consequently, investigating RIF-related specific biomarkers and immune infiltration profiles is critical for developing precision therapies.

During the process of embryo implantation, the stromal cells of the endometrium undergo a conversion into decidual cells. Driven by the hormonal influences of estrogen and progesterone, a process critical for embryo implantation and pregnancy maintenance9. Defective decidualization is associated with early pregnancy loss, recurrent implantation, and related disorders. Lipidomics analysis revealed that during the implantation window, lipid metabolism-associated genes are significantly upregulated in the endometrium, indicating that lipid metabolic reprogramming is involved in the decidualization process10. Moreover, the reduced synthesis of the endometrial PG in RIF patients leads to delayed decidualization11, suggesting that improving endometrial lipid metabolic dysfunction in RIF patients could represent a novel therapeutic strategy. Studies have shown that lipid metabolism-related gene sets are involved in inflammatory responses12, vascular remodeling13, oxidative stress14, and immune regulation15 during uterine decidualization.

Nonetheless, investigations into the traits and outcomes of RIF subtypes that are informed by lipid metabolism-associated genes are still sparse. Consequently, we propose that categorizing RIF patients according to the expression patterns of lipid metabolism genes could potentially reveal specific subgroups that are more likely to derive advantages from immunotherapy.

Given the pivotal role of lipid metabolism in decidualization and immune regulation at the maternal-fetal interface, we hypothesized that dysregulated lipid metabolism might contribute to RIF through immune dysfunction or aberrant biological pathway enrichment. To validate this, we integrated DEGs, lipid metabolism-associated genes, WGCNA, and machine learning to identify five key lipid metabolism genes whose differential expression in RIF was confirmed in clinical samples. We established a predictive model validated in two external datasets for specificity and accuracy. Consensus clustering further revealed two distinct RIF subtypes characterized by divergent immune microenvironment infiltration patterns and functional pathway enrichment. Notably, molecular docking predicted strong binding affinity between peucedanin and key genes, suggesting its therapeutic potential. Collectively, our multiomics analysis elucidates lipid metabolism-immune crosstalk in RIF, providing novel insights into biomarker discovery, disease subtyping, and personalized immunotherapy. To display the findings, a heatmap (Fig. 2F) and a volcano plot (Fig. 2E) were generated using the differential expression analysis.

Through the execution of correlation analyses between each module and the clinical information of the samples (Fig. 3C), the green module exhibited the strongest positive correlation (r = 0.64, P = 0.000), whereas the blue module demonstrated the most significant negative correlation (r = − 0.5, P = 0.000) with RIF. By Utilizing a GS greater than 0.6 and an MM exceeding 0.8, we identified critical genes from the green and blue modules for subsequent investigation, resulting in the selection of 188 key genes from the green module and 18 from the blue module (Fig. 3D, G). Correlation analysis was performed on the blue (Fig. 3F) and green module genes (Fig. 3G) to investigate their functional interactions in RIF. The constructed network revealed significant co-expression relationships among these genes. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were subsequently conducted on the genes and their corresponding LogFC values (Fig. 3H). Genes in the green module were primarily enriched in biological processes related to lipid transport, response to nutrient levels, and cellular mitotic processes, supporting a potential role of metabolic and proliferative dysregulation in RIF. These findings suggest that RIF may contribute to disease pathogenesis by dysregulating multiple metabolic pathways, including lipid metabolism (Fig. 3I). From the Molecular Signature Database, 11 gene sets were downloaded. The detailed information is presented in Supplementary Table S2. The term “fatty acid metabolism” was queried within the GeneCards database. Choose the gene whose category is “Protein Coding,” and relevance score exceeding 2 were designated as GeneCards FAM gene sets.

In order to obtain DEGs related to RIF and fatty acid metabolism, we intersected DEGs with |logFC| > 0 and adjusted P value < 0.05, WGCNA module genes, the MsigDB and GeneCards related to fatty acid metabolism genes. After removing duplicates, seven RIF-associated FAM-related genes (CPT1A, PRKAG2, ALOX5AP, PTGS2, HSD11B2, PIK3C2G, and PPARGC1A) were identified (Fig. 4A) and selected for further investigation. Subsequently, we performed a correlation analysis of FMA-associated genes to explore their interactions within the context of RIF. The resulting relationship network revealed strong associations among these FMA-related genes (Fig. 4C). Notably, genes linked to FMA, including CPT1A and PRKAG2, exhibited an agonistic relationship, characterized by a correlation coefficient of -0.88.

Among these genes, CPT1A, PRKAG2, PTGS2, HSD11B2, and PIK3C2G demonstrated increased expression in RIF patients. Conversely, ALOX5AP and PPARGC1A exhibited significantly reduced expression levels relative to non-RIF controls (Fig. 5A). Furthermore, our analysis revealed that PPARGC1A was inversely correlated with most FMA-related genes, with the exception of ALOX5AP (Fig. 5B).

Formulation and verification of the prediction model

A total of seven RIF-FAM-related genes were discovered by analyzing the intersecting modular genes associated with RIF and those linked to FAMs. To investigate genes that exhibit relatively elevated predictive capacities, we developed machine learning models, including SVM, RF, GLM, and XGB, on the basis of the expression profiles of these seven hub genes. The SVM and RF models demonstrated a reduced root mean square of the residuals (Fig. 6A, B). Additionally, the ROC analysis revealed that the SVM model attained the highest area under the curve (AUC = 0.933), as depicted in Fig. 6C. The five most impactful variables for each model are illustrated according to the root mean square error (RMSE) loss following permutation (Fig. 6D). In light of these results, we opted for the SVM model, which exhibited the best discriminative ability, for subsequent analysis. Validation of model predictive performance

Based on the identified key genes, we constructed a nomogram to visualize their interrelationships and relative contributions to RIF. The key genes were utilized as predictors in the formulation of a nomogram model. This model allows for the estimation of the risk associated with RIF by calculating the accumulated “total points“(Fig. 7A).The nomogram analysis revealed that compared with other variables, CPT1A expression demonstrated substantially greater predictive value in the RIF diagnostic model. Conversely, PTGS2 expression showed markedly lower diagnostic utility relative to other model components. To further evaluate the predictive accuracy of our model, we performed calibration analysis by generating calibration plots to evaluate the agreement between model-predicted probabilities and actual observed outcomes across various risk strata (Fig. 7B). The calibration curve demonstrated excellent concordance with the ideal reference line, indicating high predictive accuracy with minimal estimation error. Subsequently, we assessed the clinical utility of the RIF diagnostic model incorporating key genes using DCA. The DCA results revealed that the model’s net benefit curve consistently surpassed both the “All positive” and “All negative” reference lines across clinically relevant probability thresholds(Fig. 7C). This superior performance profile confirms the model’s robust clinical applicability for RIF risk stratification. Additionally, the datasets GSE71835 and GSE26787 were incorporated to assess the model’s predictive ability. The ROC curves demonstrated commendable discrimination ability, yielding AUC values of 0.760 (95% CI: 0.849–1) and 0.861 (95% CI: 0.660–1) for the respective datasets (Fig. 7D-E).

Characterization of FAM-related clusters in RIF

To investigate whether the heterogeneity of RIF is associated with disease subtypes, we utilized the consensus clustering algorithm to cduct a clustering analysis on 34 RIF samples. The most stable configuration was observed at k = 2 (Fig. 8A). As the parameter k varied between 2 and 6, the area beneath the curve exhibited shifts in the cumulative distribution function (CDF) curves corresponding to (k-1) and k (Fig. 8B). The CDF curves fluctuated within a minimal consensus index range of 0.4 to 0.6 (Fig. 8C). Notably, only at k = 2 did the concordance score for each subtype exceed 0.9 (Fig. 8D). Ultimately, we categorized the 34 RIF samples into two distinct subtypes: Cluster 1 (N = 24) and Cluster 2 (N = 10). The results of the PCA map showed that there were some differences between the two disease subtypes (Fig. 8E).

Immune characteristics and functional pathways differ between clusters

To investigate the molecular characteristics that differentiate Cluster 1 from Cluster 2, we initially assessed the expression fluctuations of five key genes between these two clusters. Our findings demonstrated that Cluster 1 showed markedly elevated expression levels of PTGS2, CPT1A, PRKAG2, and PIK3C2G, in contrast to Cluster 2, which demonstrated elevated levels of PPARGC1A (Fig. 9A, B). Utilizing the ssGSEA algorithm, we performed an assessment of immune cell infiltration, illustrating the differences in the composition of 28 immune cell types across the two clusters (Fig. 9C). The results showed increased levels of activated dendritic cells, CD56 dim natural killer cells, central memory CD8 T cells, immature B cells, macrophages, MDSCs, natural killer cells, natural killer T cells and T follicular helper cells in cluster2 samples, indicating that the immune mechanisms involved are also different in different subtypes of RIF patients (Fig. 9D). Furthermore, we conducted a correlation analysis involving FAMs and immune infiltrating cells to ascertain the possible involvement of these genes in the immune regulation of the two identified clusters. The findings revealed that activated CD4 T cells, effector memory CD8 T cells and plasmacytoid dendritic cells were significantly correlated with the majority of LMRGs (Fig. 9E). Notably, CPT1A was associated with the most pronounced negative correlation with activated CD4 T cell macrophages (Fig. 9F), wherease PPARGC1A demonstrated the strongest positive correlation with effector memory CD8 T cells (Fig. 9G). These findings suggest that these key genes may exhibit differential immunomodulatory functions between the two RIF clusters.

Drug prediction and molecular docking

Molecular docking is a structure-based approach for target protein analysis and drug screening, which identifies optimal conformations for interactions between small-molecule compounds and target proteins. In this study, the Connectivity Map database was first employed to predict the most promising drug candidate for RIF: peucedanin (Table S3). To investigate the potential effects of peucedanin on five biomarkers, we retrieved protein structures from UniProt, which were specifically filtered for Homo sapiens entries. The following predicted structures were selected as protein structure files: CPT1A_HUMAN (UniProt ID: P50416), PPARGC1A_HUMAN (UniProt ID: Q9UBK2), PIK3C2G_HUMAN (UniProt ID: O75747), PTGS2_HUMAN (UniProt ID: P35354), PRKAG2_HUMAN (UniProt ID: Q9UGJ0). After the protein structures were preprocessed using PyMOL, molecular docking was performed between each of the five target proteins using the CB-Dock2 online tool. The results demonstrated high binding affinity between peucedanin and all five molecular targets. Discussion

Despite continuous improvements in assisted reproductive technology, approximately 10% of patients undergoing three or more high-quality embryo transfers fail to achieve clinical pregnancy, a condition termed RIF16. Due to the multifactorial pathogenesis and remarkable interindividual heterogeneity of RIF, overall therapeutic outcomes remain suboptimal17.

During the in vitro induction of decidualization in human endometrial stromal cells (hESCs), staining with lipophilic fluorescent dyes revealed that decidualization stimuli markedly promote lipid droplet accumulation in hESCs, as well as increased intracellular triglyceride levels in hESCs18. These findings suggest that lipid metabolism may play a regulatory role in embryo implantation and endometrial decidualization (35). Clinical studies have revealed that dyslipidemia may diminish female fertility and have a detrimental impact on assisted reproductive outcomes19. Compared with patients with successful repeated implantation, women with RIF exhibited significantly elevated serum triglyceride levels (fold change success/failure 0.68, P < 0.05) (36). A 2025 clinical study further demonstrated that RIF women with dyslipidemia had considerably lower ART results than those with normal lipid levels, showing markedly lower implantation rates (20.1% vs. 42.7%, P = 0.0001) and live birth rates (19.4% vs. 45.8%, P < 0.0001) (37). As direct metabolites of fatty acid metabolism, prostaglandins are essential for embryo implantation and pregnancy maintenance. However, aberrantly elevated prostaglandin levels are observed in the endometrium of patients with recurrent pregnancy loss (11). These results further support the possibility that dysregulated lipid metabolism may contribute to the pathogenesis of RIF. Nevertheless, there is currently no direct omics-based evidence connecting RIF to fatty acid metabolism. Specifically, how fatty acids metabolism might affect different pathogenic clusters of RIF it is remains unknown. To bridge this gap, we performed comprehensive bioinformatic analyses of RIF and normal endometrial samples using a selected FAM-related gene set. Our study reveals distinct lipid metabolism-associated gene expression patterns in RIF pathogenesis, particularly regarding molecular subtyping and immune microenvironment regulation. The identification of two RIF subgroups with differential lipid metabolism-associated gene expression profiles and characteristic immune signatures provides new insights into disease heterogeneity. These findings may advance our understanding of RIF pathophysiology from bioinformatic and metabolomic perspectives and may inform personalized therapeutic strategies targeting metabolic-immune dysregulation.

To elucidate the relationship between lipid metabolism and RIF pathogenesis, we first performed WGCNA on the GSE111974 and GSE92324 datasets from the GEO database. Concurrently, we obtained lipid metabolism-related gene sets from the GeneCards database and the MSigDB webset. Through intersection analysis of significantly different blue and green module genes from WGCNA, lipid metabolism-related gene sets, and DEGs from the datasets, we identified seven lipid metabolism-associated DEGs that exhibited notable differences in expression levels between the RIF group and the control group, suggesting that specific genes might be instrumental in the development of RIF.

To further refine the identification of key lipid metabolism-related DEGs, we integrated four machine learning techniques: XGB, GLM, random and SVM. Through this comprehensive analysis, we ultimately identified five key lipid metabolism-related DEGs: PRKAG2, CPT1A, PPARGC1A, PIK3C2G, and PTGS2. PRKAG2 encodes the γ2 subunit of AMPK, a metabolic regulator influencing T‑cell differentiation via the MAPK pathway20, its role in ART remains unexplored. According to our docking, peucedanin binds to multiple CBS domains (AMP/ATP sites) of PRKAG2, potentially moderating AMPK activation to promote autophagy and mitochondrial quality control in endometrial cells21. CPT1A (carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 A), a rate‑limiting enzyme of fatty acid β‑oxidation, promotes decidualization in mice via nucleotide biosynthesis. Reduced CPT1A expression is observed in decidua from spontaneous abortion22, opposite to our RIF endometrial findings. Docking analysis suggests that peucedanin binds near CPT1A’s N-terminal regulatory and catalytic domains, possibly inhibiting its activity to modulate fatty acid oxidation, prevent metabolic overload, and optimize endometrial receptivity23. PARGC1A (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha) is a transcriptional coactivator that regulates mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative metabolism. In unexplained pregnancy loss, PPARγ activation exacerbates ferroptosis through the NRF2/GPX4 signaling axis, consequently impairing decidualization processes24.Peucedanin is predicted to bind to PPARGC1A’s nuclear receptor interaction domain (LXXLL motif), potentially stabilizing its active conformation, promoting mitochondrial biogenesis25, and improving energy supply for embryo implantation. PIK3C2G, which belongs to the family of phosphoinositide 3-kinases (PI3Ks), has tissue-specific functional duality26. and contributes to immune evasion in lymphomas27, its functions in endometrial functions remain unexplored. Molecular docking indicates that peucedanin interacts with PIK3C2G’s kinase catalytic domain and active pocket, possibly interfering with PI3K/AKT signaling to influence endometrial cell proliferation, migration, and embryo adhesion. PTGS2, also known as prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2, is essential for prostaglandin synthesis and reproduction28. its ablation disrupts implantation and decidualization in mice29. Our computational analysis reveals that peucedanin forms strong hydrogen bonds/π-π stacking with key PTGS2 active-site residues (ASP133, TYR134, LYS137, ASP157, ASP158), suggesting competitive inhibition of COX-2, reduced PGE2 synthesis, alleviated endometrial inflammation, and improved immune microenvironment23.

Expanding upon these discoveries, we developed a standardized nomogram aimed at assessing the potential significance of each pivotal gene and its role in predicting disease risk. DCA demonstrated that the incorporation of these critical genes into risk prediction models could yield considerable clinical advantages. ROC curve analysis of training datasets demonstrated that these individual biomarkers exhibited good discriminatory ability between the normal control group and RIF patients. This highlights a potential advancement in the accuracy of risk prediction associated with lipid metabolism, suggesting our novel methodology in tackling intricate biomedical challenges through contemporary computational techniques.

Emerging evidence from immuno-oncology studies suggests that lipid metabolites play crucial roles in influencing the immune microenvironment by modulating the proliferation, differentiation, functionality, and activity of immune cells, which in turn affects their behavior30. Specifically, specific lipid metabolites, such as fatty acids, triglycerides, and cholesterol, play crucial roles in the differentiation and operational capacity of various types of immune cells, including T cells, B cells, and macrophages31. Consequently, the pathogenesis of RIF may be closely associated with immune dysfunction induced by lipid metabolism disorders. Although immunotherapy represents a promising therapeutic strategy in the medical field, its efficacy in treating RIF remains suboptimal. Our immune infiltration analysis reveals that RIF patients exhibit a distinct immune microenvironment compared to controls, a disparity that appears to be interconnected with dysregulated fatty acid metabolism. Specifically, we observed increase expression of naive CD4 T cells, monocytes, M1 macrophages, and M2 macrophages in the endometrium of RIF patients, while there was a reduction in the expression of follicular helper T cells, activated dendritic cells, and activated mast cells in these patients compared to controls. These observations partially align with previously documented findings32. Moreover, by analyzing the expression profiles of significant lipid metabolism-related DEGs within the RIF dataset, we successfully categorized the samples into two unique clusters, designated cluster 1 and cluster 2. Heatmap and boxplot analyses demonstrated significant differential expression of these key genes between the two clusters. Specifically, PPARGC1A was highly expressed in cluster 2, while PRKAG2, CPT1A, PIK3C2G, and APTGS2 were highly expressed in cluster 1. Additionally, we established that the enrichment of various immune cell populations significantly differed between the two studied groups. These results indicate that the enrichment patterns and states of immune cell populations differ between the two clusters based on the key genes. In particular, cluster 2 demonstrated a notably increased expression of several immune cell types, including activated dendritic cells, CD56 dim natural killer cells, central memory CD8 + T cells, immature B cells, macrophages, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), natural killer cells, natural killer T cells, and T follicular helper cells.Notably, we identified significant associations between key lipid metabolism-related genes and various immune cell populations. For instance, CPT1A expression showed a strong negative correlation with activated CD4 T cell infiltration (R = − 0.734, P = 0.001). This metabolic-immune interplay aligns with prior findings in oral squamous cell carcinoma33, where CPT1A expression inversely correlated with CD4 T cell counts in tumor tissues, suggesting that dysregulated fatty acid metabolism may impair immune responses and potentially compromise cancer immunosurveillance. Furthermore, PPARGC1A expression was moderately positively correlated with effector memory CD8 T cell infiltration (R = 0.54, P = 0.009). In hepatocellular carcinoma, tumor-intrinsic upregulation of PPARγ transcriptionally activates VEGF-A production, leading to CD8 + T cell dysfunction34. In gliomas, meteorin-like proteins derived from the tumor immune microenvironment potentiate PPAR activity, resulting in mitochondrial depolarization, diminished oxidative phosphorylation, and ultimately CD8 T cell bioenergetic failure35. These results underscore the robust regulatory relationship between PPAR signaling and CD8 T cells, athough its mechanistic role in RIF requires further elucidation. Given the high clinical heterogeneity among RIF patients, subclassification based on immune profiles, identification of immunotherapy-responsive subgroups, and implementation of personalized immunotherapeutic strategies may significantly improve clinical pregnancy rates.

Functional enrichment analysis identified downregulated methylation-related metabolic pathways in RIF patients, suggesting that epigenetic dysregulation is involved in RIF pathogenesis. Recent evidence36 shows that elevated endometrial NSUN2 expression in RIF suppresses SIRT4, leading to aberrant H3K9ac accumulation and subsequent impairment of the implantation microenvironment. Our study provides the first bioinformatics evidence linking methylations modifications to RIF development, although mechanistic validation remains necessary. Moreover, we examined the variations in biological processes between the two identified clusters. The findings revealed that cluster 1 exhibited substantial enrichment in signaling pathways associated with lipid metabolism, whereas cluster 2 exhibited significant enrichment in pathways related to vascular function and inflammatory metabolism. These findings suggest that the mechanisms underlying the two RIF subtypes may be distinct. The observed limitations in current clinical therapeutic efficacy may partly reflect fundamental differences in metabolic pathways across distinct RIF subtypes. This metabolic heterogeneity highlights the potential value of subtype-specific precision medicine approaches, which may help improve both diagnostic stratification and clinical outcomes in RIF management, although this hypothesis requires further validation in prospective studies.

Peucedanin, a bioactive secondary metabolite derived from Peucedanum japonicum Thunb, has demonstrated significant pharmacological potential in multiple therapeutic domains. In obesity intervention studies, peucedanin exhibited marked efficacy in reducing unsaturated fatty acid levels in both serum and hepatic tissues of obese murine models37, suggesting its potential regulatory role in lipid metabolic processes as a natural anti-obesity agent. Furthermore, in vitro investigations have revealed that peucedanin induces HL-60 promyelocytic leukemia cell apoptosis and G1 phase cell cycle arrest in a dose- and time-dependent manner21. Emerging evidence also indicates the immunomodulatory properties and potential anticoagulant effects of peucedanin38, highlighting its multifaceted therapeutic applications. In this study, peucedanin demonstrated strong binding affinity with five key target genes in silico. Given the previously observed dysregulation of fatty acid metabolism and immune modulation in RIF samples, we hypothesize that peucedanin may exert therapeutic effects against RIF through multiple mechanisms, However, it should be emphasized that these findings are based on computational docking simulations only, and the actual efficacy and safety of peucedanin require rigorous in vitro and in vivo validation. In summary, the multifaceted actions of peucedanin in lipid homeostasis and immune regulation position it as a promising candidate for the management of RIF with concomitant lipid dysmetabolism, though its precise mechanisms of action remain to be elucidated.

Previous studies have suggested that chromosomal structural variations, including microdeletions and copy number variations (CNVs), may be associated with RIF. A case report described an RIF patient carrying a 16p11.2 microdeletion39. Another study found that endometrial CNVs in RIF patients were associated with elevated ITGB3 expression40. Additionally, pathogenic variants of the HFM1 gene have been shown to cause RIF‑related embryonic chromosomal abnormalities41. However, no well‑established RIF‑specific microdeletion region has been identified to date. In the present study, protein‑protein interaction between PTGS2 and ITGB3 was predicted using the STRING database, yielding a combined score of 0.570 (moderate confidence). Although this score did not reach the high‑confidence threshold, it still suggests a possible functional association. Together with the literature evidence that ITGB3 expression is regulated by CNVs, we hypothesize that endometrial chromosomal copy number variations may upregulate ITGB3 expression, thereby affecting PTGS2 function and contributing to the pathogenesis of RIF. Furthermore, although the 16p11.2 microdeletion region does not contain the regulatory elements of the five core genes, it harbors genes such as MAPK3 that may indirectly influence endometrial receptivity through the MAPK signaling pathway42. The above conclusions are based solely on computational molecular docking simulations and are therefore predictive hypotheses. The actual binding affinities and functional effects of peucedanin with each target protein still require experimental validation using methods such as surface plasmon resonance (SPR), cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA), endometrial cell functional assays, and animal models. The actual efficacy and safety of peucedanin need to be confirmed by rigorous in vitro and in vivo experiments.The value of this study lies in the first systematic mapping of the regulatory elements of the five core genes, providing a new entry point for genetic variation studies in RIF.

Nonetheless, this investigation presents several constraints that warrant recognition. Owing to the considerable heterogeneity associated with RIF and the restricted access to clinical data, we were unable to ascertain the correlation between risk factors and patient categorization according to the severity of RIF. Furthermore, additional studies are necessary to clarify the precise mechanisms through which these five key biomarkers are involved in immune responses and the activation of immune cells, potentially offering valuable perspectives for the identification of new therapeutic targets for RIF. Third, it is essential to recognize that not all RIF patients exhibit consistent clinical symptoms or biomarker profiles. These variations may be confined to specific subgroups of RIF patients with distinct clinical and pathophysiological characteristics. Therefore, future studies should focus on external validation with expanded sample sizes to further clarify the diagnostic accuracy of these key genes and their associations with lipid metabolism.Finally, as stated throughout the discussion, many of our conclusions are based on correlative bioinformatics analyses and computational predictions; experimental validation is required to establish causality.

Materials and methods

Date collection and preparation

Four RIF‑related expression profiling datasets (GSE111974, GSE92324, GSE26787, and GSE71835) were downloaded from the GEO database; detailed information is provided in Table 1. All samples were derived from human endometrial tissues. Each dataset was processed independently: probe IDs were converted to gene symbols according to the platform annotation files. When multiple probes corresponded to the same gene, the probe with the highest average expression was retained, and genes with zero expression across all samples were removed. After evaluating the need for log2 transformation, quantile normalization was performed using the normalizeBetweenArrays function to eliminate global expression differences among samples. GSE111974 (Agilent GPL17077) and GSE92324 (Illumina GPL10558) were merged as the training set. After extracting common genes, batch effect correction was applied using the ComBat43 function in the sva package to remove cross‑platform technical variation while preserving biological differences between RIF and control samples. The effectiveness of the correction was validated by PCA and boxplots (Fig. 1A‑D). The corrected training set was then subjected to a second round of quantile normalization. GSE26787 and GSE71835 were used as independent validation sets; they underwent the same cleaning and normalization steps separately without participating in batch correction to ensure validation independence. In the merged training set, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma package with the criteria |log₂FC| ≥ 1 and adjusted P value < 0.0544. The DEGs were visualized by volcano plots and heatmaps (using the ggplot2 and pheatmap packages)25.

WGCNA analysis

WGCNA was performed using the WGCNA package45 to identify gene modules significantly associated with RIF phenotypes and enriched biological pathways. Initially, pairing correlation coefficients between each gene were calculated, and weighted correlation coefficients were applied to ensure that gene connectivity followed a scale-free network topology. A hierarchical clustering tree was subsequently built using correlation coefficients, with different branches representing distinct gene modules. Module significance was then evaluated using the eigengene-based approach. For the training dataset containing RIF-associated genes, the key parameters were optimized as follows: the minimum module size was set to 50 genes, the optimal soft-thresholding power was determined as 5 to satisfy scale-free topology criteria, and modules were merged using a cutting height of 0.5 in the dendrogram. This analytical framework maintains network biological assumptions while ensuring module robustness through parameter optimization on the basis of established scale-free network principles. Furthermore, an examination was conducted on the associations between genes contained within the module and the phenotype, referred to as gene significance (GS). This was analyzed in conjunction with the connection between module membership (MM) and gene expression. Genes were subjected to filtering based on the thresholds of GS > 0.6 and MM > 0.8.

Screening of lipid metabolism-related differential genes

Fatty acid metabolism-related genes were systematically curated from the GeneCards resource (https://www.genecards.org/) using “Fatty Acid Metabolism” as the search query. Following strict filtration criteria, we preserved only protein-coding genes with relevance scores > 1. Gene sets corresponding to fatty acid metabolism pathways (“REACTOME_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM”, “KEGG_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM”, and “HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM”) were obtained from the Molecular Signature Database v7.4 (MSigDB; https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) to perform subsequent data integration (Table S2). The intersection of FAMs, DEGs from the training dataset, and WGCNA-derived module genes identified 7 radiation-induced RIF-associated fatty acid metabolism genes (RIF-FAMs), which were subjected to downstream analyses.

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) annotates genes relevant to biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components, organized in the form of a directed acyclic graph46. In addition, the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) is used for annotating genes within certain pathways47. In our study, we conducted enrichment analyses for KEGG pathway and GO enrichment analyses on functional association modules (FAMs) and key genes using the “clusterProfiler” R package48. For the purpose of the enrichment analysis, we established a screening criterion that required a q-value threshold of less than 0.05, enabling us to identify GO terms and KEGG pathways that are statistically significant, thereby enhancing the credibility of our analytical findings.

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)49 is a computerized tool for determining whether predefined gene sets exhibit statistically important and concordant changes between two biological states. This approach examines the distribution pattern of gene set members within a ranked gene list correlated with phenotypic variations to determine their potential contributions to the phenotype. In this study, genes were first sorted based on their logFC values. The clusterProfiler R package was then used to perform GSEA on all genes48. The analysis was conducted with the following criteria: a predetermined seed value of 2024, and the gene set “c2.all.v2024.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” was obtained via the MSigDB for GSEA. The significance criterion for enriched gene sets was established at p value < 0.05. Multiple testing was corrected using the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) methods.

Correlations between immune infiltrating cells and LMRGs

CIBERSORT50 is an analytical method that uses linear support vector regression to deconvolute transcriptome expression matrices and assess the composition and abundance of immune cells in mixed populations. In this study, we applied the CIBERSORT algorithm combined with the LM22 signature matrix to analyze immune cell infiltration. The data were filtered to retain only immune cell subsets with enrichment scores > 0, generating a final immune cell infiltration matrix. Stacked bar plots were generated to depict the proportional composition of immune cells within each sample group. Comparative group analyses were visualized using appropriate plotting methods. Furthermore, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were employed to assess correlations between immune cell proportions and gene expression levels across two sample groups. Gene-gene interaction networks were visualized using the R package circlize51, while self-correlation analyses of gene expression were conducted and plotted using the R package corrplot.

ssGSEA52(single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis) was employed to determine the infiltration of immune cells levels in individual samples. This method calculates enrichment scores for predefined immune cell gene signatures within each sample’s transcriptomic profile, enabling reference-free assessment of relative immune cell abundance. In this study, we implemented the ssGSEA algorithm from the “GSVA” package combined with a 28 immune cell marker expression matrix to generate specific immune cell infiltration results, which were visualized via the “ggplot2” package.

Machine learning models filter key genes

By utilizing the expression profiles of Lipid Metabolism-Related genesLMRGs (LMRGs), four unique machine learning models were constructed utilizing the “caret” R package. These models comprise the eXtreme gradient boosting model (XGB), generalized linear model (GLM), random forest model (RF), and support vector machine model (SVM). XGB operates within the boosting paradigm and utilizes a forward optimization approach, which iteratively constructs a foundational model aimed at optimally approximating the target function. This methodology can be framed as an alternative to a convex linear programming conundrum, with its initialization algorithm being particularly effective in tackling such challenges. Conversely, the RF model creates varied training subsets using self-sampling techniques (row sampling). Each primary learner is trained with these subsets, and their predictions are subsequently aggregated to produce the final output of ensemble model. The 66 patients were randomly assigned to two groups: a training set (70%, n = 47) and a validation set (30%, n = 19) using a stratified split. To ensure robust model evaluation and control overfitting, repeated 10-fold cross-validation with 5 repeats was employed during model training and assessment. The four machine learning models were taken into consideration using the “DALEX” package to generate visual representations of the residual distribution along with the importance of various features53. Variable importance was calculated based on permutation-based root mean square error (RMSE) loss. The “caret” R tool automatically fine-tunes the parameters within these models, which are assessed through 5-fold cross-validation and implemented using the default settings54. The area beneath the ROC curves was then displayed via the “pROC” R package55. This analysis enabled us to determine the most appropriate learning model and select the five most significant genes as key genes to serve as the primary predictor genes for RIF.

Verification of the diagnostic model

A nomogram56 is a graphical representation that utilizes non-overlapping line segments in a Cartesian coordinate system to depict functional relationships among multiple independent variables. In this study, the R package rms was employed to construct the model using the training dataset and generate a nomogram on the basis of logistic regression results, visualizing the interrelationships of key genes. Decision curve analysis (DCA)57 was used to assess the clinical predictive model. Using the R package ggDCA, DCA curves were plotted based on the risk score of critical genes in the dataset. The R package pROC55 was utilized to generate receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calculate the area under the curve (AUC) values using external datasets GSE26787 and GSE71835. This analysis evaluated the diagnostic efficacy of the RIF prediction model based on the expression levels of the risk score. The AUC ranges from 0.5 to 1, with values closer to 1 indicating superior diagnostic performance.

Unsupervised clustering of RIF patients

Consensus clustering58, a robust resampling-based algorithm, was employed to identify molecular subtypes of RIF by assessing cluster stability through iterative subsampling of the dataset. Using the ConsensusClusterPlus R package59, we performed unsupervised k-means clustering analysis on the training cohort based on key gene expression profiles, with the cluster number (k) ranging from 2 to 6. The analysis incorporated 100 resampling iterations, each utilizing 80% of the total samples. The determination of the optimal cluster number was based on a comprehensive evaluation incorporating consensus cluster scores exceeding 0.9, analysis of cumulative distribution function (CDF) curves, and careful examination of the consensus matrix. Following this multidimensional assessment, the maximum number of subtypes (k) was established as 6. Principal component analysis (PCA) confirmed distinct gene expression patterns among the identified clusters. Subsequent analyses included heatmap visualization of key gene expression differences across RIF subtypes and comparative group analysis to validate subtype-specific expression patterns. This rigorous analytical approach ensures the reliable identification of molecularly distinct RIF subtypes while maintaining methodological transparency and reproducibility.

Screening of small-molecule agents and assessments of molecular-ligand docking

The list of differentially expressed proteins was submitted to the Connectivity Map (cMap) online platform (http://clue.io) for computational drug repurposing analysis. Potential therapeutic compounds were identified through the cMap pattern-matching algorithm, with compounds demonstrating enrichment scores > 90 or <-90 being selected as high-confidence predicted drug candidates. The 3D conformational structures of the candidate drugs were obtained in SDF format from the PubChem website (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The three-dimensional structures of the five primary target proteins were retrieved using the UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/). Structural refinement was performed using PyMOL (version 2.5.2). Molecular docking was carried out utilizing the CB-Dock2 online platform (http://clab.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/). which employs AutoDock Vina as its docking engine, and binding free energies (ΔG, kcal/mol) were obtained directly from the Vina scoring function. Molecular docking simulations were performed to assess the binding interactions between the predicted drugs and key target genes, followed by visualization of the docking poses.

RT-PCR

To assess the significance of the hub genes in RIF, their expression levels were assessed using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reactions (RT-PCR). For this investigation, a total of 10 clinical tissue specimens were obtained from 5 patients who experienced unexplained RIF and 5 control subjects at Renmin Hospital of Shiyan Province. The ethics committee of Renmin Hospital in Shiyan approved the study (approval number: 2019-006). Following the manufacturer’s instructions, TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was used to extract total RNA from the endometrial tissue samples. Reverse transcription was conducted utilizing the SureScript First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit to produce complementary DNAs (cDNAs). The RT-qPCR protocol involved 40 cycles consisting of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 1 min, followed by denaturation at 95 °C for 20 s, annealing at 55 °C for 20 s, and extension at 72 °C for 30 s. The RT-qPCR primers, supplied by Tsingke Biotech (Beijing, China), are detailed in Table S1. GAPDH served as the internal reference gene. The relative expression levels of the biomarkers were determined using the 2 − ΔΔCT methodology.

Statistical analysis

The R program (Version 4.3.2) served as the foundation for all data processing and analysis in this essay. For non-normally distributed variables, group differences were assessed via the Mann–Whitney U test. When three or more independent groups were compared, the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied. Intervariable associations among multiple compounds were evaluated through Spearman’s rank correlation analysis. A two-tailed p value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified.

Conclusion

In summary, this study employed multiple bioinformatics approaches to elucidate the potential associations between RIF and lipid metabolism. By utilizing key signature genes, we established an optimal SVM model that may serve as a useful tool for accurate assessment of RIF-related risks and subtypes. Importantly, To our knowledge, this study is one of the first to stratify RIF into two molecularly distinct subtypes based on fatty acid metabolism signatures, immune microenvironments, and bioinformatic pathway profiles. These findings may provide new insights into the clinical heterogeneity of RIF manifestations and therapeutic responses, and could serve as a reference for future advances in RIF diagnosis, risk stratification, and personalized therapeutic strategies.

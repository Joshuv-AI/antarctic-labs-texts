Radioactivity Levels, Spatial Distribution, and Radiological Risk Assessment of 226Ra, 228Ra, 40K, and 3H in Water Samples from Dhaka City and Savar, Bangladesh
Authors: Md Ishmum Chowdhury, Saiful Islam, Md Amjad Hossain, Jannatul Ferdous, Pradip K Bakshi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis investigation was conducted to measure the radioactivity levels of 226Ra, 228Ra, 40K, and 3H in the 13 most popular branded bottled mineral water (BMW), 12 groundwater sources consumed in Dhaka city, Bangladesh, and six water samples collected from the nuclear object site at Savar, adjacent to Dhaka. The activity of 3H was measured by using a liquid scintillation counter, and a gamma-ray spectrometer with an HPGe detector was used to measure the activity of other radionuclides. The average activity concentrations of 226Ra, 228Ra, 40K, and 3H in BMW samples and groundwater were found to be 1.81 ± 0.43, 1.74 ± 0.40, 43.08 ± 6.23, 2.42 ± 0.16, and 1.61 ± 0.51, 1.69 ± 0.45, 57.42 ± 3.16, and 3.02 ± 0.19 Bq/L, respectively. In addition, the activity concentrations of identified radionuclides in water samples collected from Savar were found to be 1.59 ± 0.43, 1.53 ± 0.50, 99.83 ± 3.05, and 2.95 ± 0.20 Bq/L. With the exception of 3H, the activity concentration and the annual effective dose of 40K, 226Ra, and 228Ra surpassed the acceptable thresholds of the WHO. The combined activity of radium (Rac) also exceeds the USEPA recommended maximum permissible limit of 0.185 Bq/L. The excess lifetime cancer risk results were below the permissible threshold of 10–3 for radiological risks. Groundwater was subjected to an AAS analysis for common metals (Na, Mg, and Ca) and heavy metals (Fe, Cr, Cu, and Pb). The results showed that the heavy metals were below the detection limit. Radionuclide contents in groundwater of Dhaka city were mapped spatially using a GIS technique. Statistical analyses such as one-way ANOVA, one sample t test, and Pearson’s correlation analysis were also performed. According to Pearson’s correlation analysis, 228Ra is mostly responsible for radiation risk in BMW and groundwater.

Introduction

To maintain proper health and avoid complications, international organizations such as the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP), the United Nations Scientific Committee on the Effects of Atomic Radiation (UNSCEAR), the European Union (EU) Council, and the World Health Organization (WHO) recommend a daily water intake of at least 1–2 L for adults.1,2 Continuous pollution and contamination of water resources have made them a challenge and a social concern. Radionuclides are attracting interest as a source of water contamination. Only naturally occurring radionuclides (NOR) and their decay products with half-lives similar to the age of the Earth, such as those from the 238U and 232Th series, can still be found on Earth today.3 Radionuclides emit various types of particles and energy as radiation, of which beta particles and γ rays are two prominent forms. Their hazardous effects make them undesirable in our water. One of our subjects of interest, 3H, is a pure beta (β–) emitter and is found globally in natural water as it is produced both naturally and anthropogenically.4 Nuclear weapon testing was a major source of this in the 20th century. 3H is also a waste product of nuclear power plants,5 a technology that has been gaining a reputation as an efficient source of energy in the last few decades. The radioactivity of water also receives geogenic and cosmogenic contributions. Bangladesh is going to start the operation of its first nuclear power plant at Rooppur, Ishwardi, Pabna, at the bank of the Padma. The first contribution from the nuclear reactor is expected in 2024, and the plan shows approximately 7 GW of nuclear capacity by 2041. Public health and safety should be the priority for such projects. 3H is a waste product released with the coolant water of a nuclear power plant. Thus, tritium may enter nearby water bodies and travel downstream far and wide. Besides, uranium–radium, uranium–actinium, and thorium are the three natural decay series that contribute to natural minerals and natural radionuclides present in groundwater.6 There are several distinct isotopes of radium. 226Ra (from 238U) and 228Ra (from 232Th) are the main isotopes. In addition, there is 223Ra (derived from 235U), although 223Ra is usually not an issue because there is so little 235U in the environment.6,7 γ-Radiation from these sources represents the most prominent source of exposure to the human body. The majority of nations worldwide conduct significant studies on natural radiation and environmental radioactivity to gather information about potential radiological threats and implement suitable safeguards. The use of bottled mineral water (BMW) is increasing gradually in Bangladesh. In Dhaka, most households purify or directly consume tap water supplied by the Dhaka Water Supply and Sewerage Authority (WASA) as drinking water. The Dhaka WASA is mostly dependent on groundwater to meet the local demand. At present, 87% of the supply comes from groundwater.7 BMWs are also produced from groundwater in most cases. In recent years, many extensive studies have been carried out on natural radioactivity measurements in BMWs and groundwater worldwide. In 2011, Ferdous et al. studied radioactivity in tap water in Dhaka city, and the measured activity of 226Ra, 232Th, and 40K was found to be 0.0275, 0.393, and 3.992 Bq/L, respectively.8 Rahman et al. analyzed bottled drinking water in Bangladesh, and the average activity of 226Ra, 232Th, and 40K was found to be 3.28, 6.40, and 18.26 Bq/L, respectively.9 Jahan et al. measured radioactivity in the water of the Savar industrial zone, and the activity was 1.49, 0.939 Bq/L for 226Ra, 232Th, respectively, and 40K, which was below the detection limit.10 The literature review shows that such studies in Dhaka, Bangladesh, are less than adequate. Furthermore, there are currently no data on the activity of tritium (3H) in Bangladesh’s drinking water. This study aims to assess the gamma radioactivity of the naturally occurring radionuclides 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K and the beta activity of 3H associated with dose and radiological risk evaluation in bottled mineral water, groundwater, and a few surface water samples collected from Dhaka City and the nuclear object site at Savar. The possible contribution and dispersion of radionuclides in groundwater, which is not established in Dhaka city, are revealed by the spatial distribution map.

Materials and Methods

Collection and Preparation of Samples

Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh and the most populous city in the country. The area under the Dhaka South City Corporation was selected for groundwater and bottled mineral water collection in this study. Thirteen (n = 13) commercially available popular branded bottled mineral water (BMW) was collected for the study, and groundwater was collected from 12 (n = 12) Dhaka Water Supply and Sewerage Authority (WASA) groundwater pumps at different locations in the south city of Dhaka, as listed in Tables S1 and S2 (Supporting Information).

The BMW samples were labeled from B-1 to B-13, and the groundwater samples from WASA groundwater pumps were labeled from W-1 to W-12. Six (n = 6) samples consisting of both groundwater and surface water were collected from the adjacent area of the 3MW TRIGA Mark-II nuclear research reactor site at the Atomic Energy Research Establishment, Savar, near Dhaka city and labeled from R-1 to R-6 (Table S3). A geographical map and satellite map of the sampling area are shown in Figure 1.

(a) Map of Bangladesh (b) Dhaka district and (c) geographic locations of the groundwater sampling sites.

To measure 3H activity by liquid scintillation counter (LSC), the samples were preconcentrated by the distillation process with a rotary evaporator.11 0.4 g portion of sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) was added to the 250 mL water samples, followed by 0.4 g of sodium carbonate (Na2CO3) to make the sample alkaline before commencing the distillation process. This ensures that the majority of the interfering compounds, which quench the scintillation process, remain in the residue of the distillation, together with any radioactive iodide and bicarbonate that may be present. Since the initial 20 mL were not representative of the sample, they were wiped out. Three 20 mL polyethylene vials were used to collect the remaining 60 mL.

On the other hand, about 1 L of each sample was poured into a Pyrex beaker previously washed with distilled water and dried subsequently. To prevent the accumulation of organic compounds and modifications to the oxidation state of the ions present in the samples, 1.5 mL of concentrated HNO3 was added to each water sample. The water samples were then lowered to around 250 mL by gradually evaporating them in a water bath treated at 105 °C. Each sample was then placed into cylindrical plastic containers. The containers had roughly identical diameter and height (6.5 and 7.5 cm, respectively). After correctly labeling and securely sealing the containers, thick vinyl tapes were wrapped around their screw necks to facilitate gamma detection in the HPGe detector.

226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K Activity Concentration Measurement

In this study, a high-resolution and high-purity gamma-ray spectroscopic system with an HPGe detector was set up in a low background configuration to quantify the activity concentration of radionuclides in water samples. The reference source of the IAEA standard sample, IAEA TERC 2022 QC, was chosen for energy calibration due to the wide range of gamma-ray energies emitted over the entire energy range of interest (Figure S3). The detector was contained in a cylindrical lead shielding device with a sliding cover and a fixed bottom to reduce the noise interference from the environment. With a relative efficiency of 30%, it was found that the energy resolution of the 1.33 MeV energy peak for 60Co was 1.67 keV at full-width half-maximum (fwhm). The detector’s energy calibration was performed using common point sources like 22Na, 57Co, 60Co, 88Y 133Ba, 137Cs, 152Eu, and so forth. Efficiency data were also checked by a standard source which was made by combining 152Eu of known activity (liquid form, 37,000 Bq activity) with 1 N HCl and manufactured in the same containers (500 mL marinelli beaker) as the samples (Figure S4). The detector measured each prepared sample for 10,000 s. To reduce the amount of uncertainty in the net counts, the background and sample measurements were given identical counting times. The spectrum of each sample was analyzed, and the identification of unknown radionuclides was carried out by considering their peak centroid energies. The activity of 226Ra was measured from the 1120.30 and 1763.13 keV photo peaks of 214Bi. 228Ra (232Th) was estimated from the 910.50 and 2613.31 keV gamma peaks of 228Ac and 208Tl, respectively. 40K was measured by using the 1459.88 keV gamma peak from 40K itself. Radio-elemental ratios can provide important information on the oxidizing or reducing circumstances of groundwater aquifers or site characteristics, more specifically, those connected to conditions of reduced and mineralized water quality that arise with advancing groundwater age and aquifer confinement.16 An eU/eTh ratio <1.25 indicates oxic depositional circumstances, while a eU/eTh ratio greater than 1.25 denotes suboxic and anoxic conditions.17 Also, a eTh/eU ratio less than 2 indicates relative uranium enrichment and reducing circumstances, while 2–7 is assumed to indicate rapid deposition of igneous rock debris. A eTh/eU ratio greater than 7 suggests unfavorable conditions for enrichment. The measured 226Ra (238U), 228Ra (232Th), and 40K activity concentrations in Bq/L were converted to eU, eTh elemental concentrations in ppm, and K% using conversion factors from the International Atomic Energy Agency.18 These recommended factors are 1 ppm U = 12.35 Bq/L of 238U, 1 ppm Th = 4.06 Bq/L of 232Th, and 1% K = 313 Bq/L of 40K.

Results and Discussion

Physiochemical Parameters Assessment

The concentration of ionized constituents of water is usually represented by measuring the electrical conductance (EC). In this study, the EC values of bottled mineral water ranged from 248.8 to 39.4 μS, with an average of 108.9 μS. Drinking water becomes dramatically and steadily less palatable at TDS levels greater than about 1000 mg/L. Although the WHO provides no guideline value for TDS for drinking water, water with a total dissolved solids (TDS) level of less than about 600 mg/L is typically considered to be palatable. The bottled water sample TDS values ranged from a maximum of 174.17 mg/L to a minimum of 27.58 mg/L, with an average of 76.23 mg/L. The EC values in groundwater samples collected from WASA groundwater pumps varied from 1041 to 216.6 μS, with an average of 584.47 μS. TDS values ranged from a maximum of 728.7 mg/L to a minimum of 151.6 mg/L, with an average of 409.12 mg/L. W-4 and W-6 produced exceptionally high EC values compared to those of other samples. TDS values in groundwater were generally higher than those in bottled mineral water. Groundwater samples from the nuclear object site exhibited normal EC and TDS values. Among the surface water samples from the site, R-4 has unusually high EC and TDS values of 1617 μS and 1131.9 mg/L, respectively. This indicates an extensive level of inorganic or organic contamination.

Tritium (3H) Activity Concentration

The measured 3H activity concentration data are presented in Tables S4–S6 for BMW, groundwater, and water samples from nuclear object sites, respectively. The measured activity concentration of 3H in BMW varied from a maximum of 4.01 ± 0.16 Bq/L (33.99 ± 1.36 TU) to a minimum of 0.99 ± 0.16 Bq/L (8.36 ± 1.36 TU) with an average of 2.42 ± 0.16 Bq/L (20.46 ± 1.36 TU) and from a maximum of 4.52 ± 0.19 Bq/L (38.33 ± 1.61 TU) to a minimum of 1.69 ± 0.19 Bq/L (14.33 ± 1.61 TU) with an average of 3.02 ± 0.19 Bq/L (25.59 ± 1.61 TU) in the WASA groundwater samples. The values obtained are lower than the limit value of 10,000 Bq/L recommended by the WHO.193H levels in the BMW and WASA groundwater samples are quite identical. The measured activity concentration of 3H in nuclear object site samples varied from a maximum of 4.32 ± 0.20 Bq/L (36.59 ± 1.69 TU) to a minimum of 1.22 ± 0.20 Bq/L (10.32 ± 1.69 TU), with an average of 2.95 ± 0.20 Bq/L (24.98 ± 1.69 TU). These results suggest the absence of any significant contribution from the nuclear research reactor. The tritium unit (TU), which is typically used to express the tritium activity concentration in natural water, is defined as one molecule of 3HHO for every 1018 molecules of H2O. Obtained values were converted to TU (tritium unit) from Bq/L by using the relation 1 Bq/L = 8.47 TU.20 Bar graphs were drawn to compare the measured activity of the samples (Figures 2–4). A comparison of the activity of tritium in water samples in different parts of the world with this study is given in Table 1. As can be seen, the results of our current study are similar to those reported in the literature. Radiological Risk Assessment

Generally, the consumption rate of water or food and the activity of radionuclides govern the ingestion of radionuclides. To evaluate the radiological risks to the general population, AED due to ingestion of 3H, 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K was calculated. The average AED of 3H received by children is 0.015 μSv/y, 0.02 μSv/y, and 0.02 μSv/y from BMW, WASA groundwater, and water samples from nuclear object sites, respectively. For infants, the AED values are 0.01 μSv/y, 0.12 μSv/y, and 0.013 μSv/y for BMW, groundwater, and nuclear object site samples, respectively. The AED, determined for 3H, is far below the safety limit of 100 μSv/y recommended by the WHO.19 Therefore, these water sources do not pose any radiological risk to the general population due to the presence of 3H.

Radiological risk parameters (AED and ELCR) of bottled mineral water (BMW), WASA groundwater, and water samples of nuclear object site Savar are given in Tables S8–S13 and S14–S16 respectively. The average AED of 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K for different age groups (adults, children, and infants) due to ingestion of bottled mineral water (BMW) was calculated as 380 μSv/y, 180 μSv/y, and 130 μSv/y; 300 μSv/y, 140 μSv/y, and 100 μSv/y; and 190, 93 μSv/y, and 67 μSv/y, respectively. Due to consuming WASA groundwater, the average AED of 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K for adults, children, and infants was computed as follows: 340 μSv/y, 160 μSv/y, and 110 μSv/y; 290 μSv/y, 130 μSv/y, and 90 μSv/y; and 260 μSv/y, 120 μSv/y, and 89 μSv/y, respectively. For adults, children, and infants, the average AED of 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K resulting from water samples of the nuclear object site, Savar, was computed as follows: 340 μSv/y, 160 μSv/y, and 110 μSv/y; 260 μSv/y, 120 μSv/y, and 80 μSv/y; and 450 μSv/y, 220 μSv/y, and 150 μSv/y, respectively. The average AEDs for adults estimated for all types of studied water samples are significantly higher than the recommended dose level of 100 μSv/y set by the WHO.19 AED values for children are somewhat close to the WHO recommended limit but still larger in most cases. In the case of AED for infants, the values can be termed acceptable.

The total excess lifetime cancer risk (ELCR) of all the investigated bottled mineral water (BMW) samples, WASA groundwater samples, and water samples from Savar ranged from 31.07 × 10–4 to 40.92 × 10–4 with an average of 34.97 × 10–4, 29.14× 10–4 to 44.62 × 10–4 with an average of 36.97× 10–4, and 32.08× 10–4 to 56.53× 10–4 with an average of 44.81 × 10–4, respectively. The average contributions of 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K to the total ELCR in BMW and WASA groundwater samples are 19.61, 52.41, and 27.96; and 16.47, 48.28, and 35.24%, respectively. In water samples from Savar, the average contributions of 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K to the total ELCR are 13.43, 35.99, and 50.56%, respectively (Figure 5). Among all of the naturally occurring radionuclides, 228Ra is the most radiotoxic and contributes the maximum amount to the total ELCRs of all BMW and WASA groundwater samples. In the case of water samples from Savar, 40K is the main contributor to the ELCR. The overall ELCR values are all below the permissible threshold15 of 10–3.

Metal Analysis

Metal analysis was carried out on the groundwater samples from WASA groundwater pumps by a pinAAcle 900H spectrometer based on atomic absorption flame spectroscopy principles. Heavy metals, such as Cr, Cu, Fe, and Pb, were found to be below the detection limit (BDL). The very few samples that contained any Fe or Cu had a trace level of their presence, which was far below the safety limit recommended by the WHO.19 Along with that, Ca varied from a maximum of 26.56 to a minimum of 3.41 ppm, Mg varied from a maximum of 2.84 to a minimum of 2.37 ppm, and Na varied from a maximum of 6.89 to a minimum of 3.53 ppm. So, these water sources are safe from any toxicity stemming from the metals of our interest. The results are presented in Table S17.

Pearson’s correlation matrix shows the association between the radionuclides and the radiological risk parameter. Pearson’s correlation matrixes of bottled mineral water, groundwater, and water from nuclear object sites are given in Tables S18–S20, respectively. In bottled mineral water samples, 226Ra and 228Ra are negatively correlated (r = −0.111) because these two isotopes are progeny from 238U and 232Th, two different decay series, whereas 40K and 3H are positively correlated (r = 0.456) as both isotopes emit beta particles. The excess lifetime cancer risk (ELCR) (r = 0.812) is significantly and strongly correlated with 228Ra rather than 226Ra (r = 0.408). This significant and strong correlation reveals that 228Ra is the major source of natural radioactivity in the bottled mineral water available in Bangladesh. 40K shows a negative correlation with radiological risk ELCR, as the bottled mineral water is processed water that may contain a permissible amount of cations and anions.

Groundwater collected from the WASA water pump station in Dhaka city shows a positive correlation between 226Ra (238U) and 228Ra (232Th) (r = 0.353). Though the 226Ra isotope mainly originated from the 238U decay series, it can also originate from recoiled thorium parent through alpha decay in the groundwater system.34,3540K is strongly correlated (r = 0.827) with 228Ra (232Th). In groundwater, the major sources of potassium are silicate and clay minerals, orthoclase, and so forth.36 The thorium isotopes are insoluble in groundwater and remain adsorbed or deposited on the clay mineral surface.37 Therefore, the strong correlation (r = 0.827) between 40K and 228Ra (232Th) is evidence of the deposition of 232Th on the potassium-containing clay minerals, and both 40K and 228Ra emit beta particles. The excess lifetime cancer risk (ELCR) (r = 0.908) is significantly and strongly correlated with 228Ra. Release of 228Ra (t1/2 = 5.75 yrs) from sediments and clay minerals faster than 226Ra (t1/2 = 1600 yrs) is due to the relatively shorter half-lives of 228Ra than 226Ra.37,38 Consequently, the groundwater flow system could contain comparatively elevated 228Ra rather than 226Ra and have an effect on the radiation risk. Both groundwater and surface water samples were collected from the 3 MW TRIGA MARK-II research reactor area in Savar. The excess lifetime cancer risk (ELCR) (r = 0.908) is also significantly and strongly correlated with both 228Ra and 40K (r = 0.956).

Conclusions

This study aimed to investigate the 226Ra, 228Ra, 40K, and 3H activity in bottled mineral drinking water and groundwater samples in Dhaka city and water samples from the nuclear object site, Savar. 3H activity measurements in bottled mineral water, groundwater collected from WASA groundwater pumps, and water samples collected from nuclear object sites in Savar showed a very low level of radioactivity. Annual effective dose (AED) values for 3H were much lower than the recommended level, and thus, these water sources are safe for ingestion in that regard. On the other hand, gamma activity measurements showed the presence of 226Ra and 228Ra at higher levels than those recommended by the WHO. The combined activity of radium (Rac) also exceeds the USEPA recommended maximum permissible limit 0.185 Bq/L. AED of 226Ra and 228Ra due to ingestion also exhibited higher values than the recommended value of 100 μSv/y set by the WHO. The excess lifetime cancer risk (ELCR) indicated the samples to be completely safe for exposure to the population. Statistical analysis reveals that mainly 228Ra is responsible for the radiation risk in both bottled mineral water and groundwater. The groundwater samples are also free from heavy metal contamination. The results obtained from this study can help establish the baseline levels of naturally occurring radioactivity in BMWs and WASA groundwater. Bangladesh is very close to commencing power generation from its very first nuclear power plant at Rooppur, Pabna. Therefore, the radiological impact on the environment after the commissioning of the nuclear power plant could be assessed and monitored using this baseline data. Not only that, but these data will be helpful in mapping and monitoring the 3H, 226Ra, 228Ra, and 40K radioactivity levels in drinking water in the overpopulated Dhaka city, Bangladesh.

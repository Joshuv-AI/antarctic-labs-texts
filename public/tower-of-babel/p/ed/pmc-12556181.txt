Sex and Race Disparities in Liver Listings and Transplants in the United States
Authors: Abdellatif Ismail, Mohanad Awadalla, Robert Wong, Zobair Younossi, Ashwani K. Singal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and AimsAlcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) are becoming leading indications for liver transplant (LT) in the United States. Data on trends based on sex and race are limited.MethodsA cohort of LT listings in adults (2011–2023) for common indications of ALD, MASH, and hepatitis C virus was stratified to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and non-HCC indications. Change in proportion for each etiology in 2023 vs 2011 was derived.ResultsOf 21,087 HCC listings, MASH dominated in males and females, but ALD dominated in black males and in black and Hispanic females. Of 12,087 HCC LT, MASH dominated in both sexes and races, except ALD dominated in black males. Of 80,433 non-HCC listings, ALD dominated in both sexes and races, except MASH dominated in black and Hispanic males. Of 46,289 non-HCC LT, ALD dominated in both sexes and all races.ConclusionOur study findings call for public policies to address the rising burden from ALD and MASH and sex/ethnic disparities.

Introduction

Effective direct-acting antiviral therapy for hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection has resulted in alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) as leading indications for liver transplantation (LT) in patients without hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).1, 2, 3, 4 Despite MASH being the most rapidly growing etiology of HCC leading to LT in the US, data indicate that HCV remains the leading indication for LT in this patient population. However, data on these trends based on sex and race/ethnicity are lacking.5,6

Methods

The United Network for Organ Sharing (UNOS) database (2011–2023) was used to extract a cohort of LT listings in adults for HCV, ALD, or MASH.7 The cohort was stratified to HCC and non-HCC cohorts. UNOS codes were used for liver etiology and HCC. Of LT listings, a cohort of LT recipients was extracted.

The annual trends for liver disease etiology among LT listings were examined for a) overall trends, b) overall trends in males and in race/ethnic categories, and c) overall trends in females and in race/ethnic categories. Similar trends were obtained for LT recipients’ cohort. The Armitage trend test was used for statistical analysis. A percent change for liver disease etiology in 2022–2023 as compared to 2011-2012 was derived. All the statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Analysis Software (SAS) version 9.4 (Cary, NC, USA). Given that UNOS is a database with deidentified data, the study qualified for a waiver of consent and did not require approval from the institutional review board.

Results

Listings and Transplants for HCC

Of 21,087 LT listings for HCC, MASH dominated as liver disease etiology in 2022–2023 with an increase of 309% since 2011–2012 (Table 1). In 16,190 LT listings for HCC in males, MASH dominated with 330% increase in 2022–2023. Race-based trends showed MASH dominated in NH white (N = 10,129) and Hispanic (N = 4084) males with 275% and 372% increase in 2022–2023. In contrast, ALD dominated in black (N = 1290) males with 2064% increase. In 4897 LT listings for HCC in females, MASH dominated with 268% increase in 2022-2023. Race-based trends showed MASH dominated in NH white (N = 2646) females with 194% increase in 2022–2023. Discussion

MASH led HCC and ALD led non-HCC LT and listings in 2022–2023, data like previous reports.8,9 Among HCC LT listings, MASH led in both sexes in NH white and Hispanics, while ALD led in blacks. In HCC LT, MASH led in NH white and Hispanic males and all races in females, while ALD led in black males. For non-HCC listings, ALD led in NH white males and all races in females, while MASH led in black and Hispanic males. In non-HCC LT, ALD led in all races in both males and females. A previous analysis,10 on sex- and race-based trends in HCC listings and LT also showed MASH as the leading etiology in NH whites and HCV in blacks and Hispanics. Regarding non-HCC LT,8 ALD emerged as the leading etiology in non-Hispanics and MASH in Hispanics.

In conclusion, our findings testify to the effectiveness of hepatitis B vaccination and of direct-acting antiviral agents for hepatitis C.11 These findings call for public policies to address the burden from ALD and MASH and sex and ethnic disparities.

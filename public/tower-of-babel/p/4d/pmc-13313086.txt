Vietnamese Translation and Validation of Nasal Obstruction Symptom Evaluation (NOSE) Scale: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Minh P. Hoang, Hien T. T. Doan, Vien T. C. Than, Minh H. N. Phan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe Nasal Obstruction Symptom Evaluation (NOSE) scale is a validated disease‐specific patient‐reported outcome measure for assessing health‐related quality of life in nasal obstruction. This study aimed to translate, culturally adapt, and validate the Vietnamese version of the NOSE questionnaire.Study DesignProspective cohort study.SettingTertiary academic center.MethodsUsing a standardized forward‐backward translation protocol, the Vietnamese NOSE (VN‐NOSE) was developed and psychometrically validated in 143 participants (101 patients with nasal obstruction and 42 healthy controls). Among symptomatic patients, 47 undergoing endoscopic septoplasty with inferior turbinoplasty completed assessments preoperatively and at 1 week, 3 weeks, and 3 months postoperatively. Internal consistency, test‐retest reliability, construct validity, discriminant validity, responsiveness, and the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) were evaluated. Correlations with a visual analogue scale (VAS), a 6‐point Likert scale, and peak nasal inspiratory flow (PNIF) were analyzed.ResultsThe VN‐NOSE showed excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's α = 0.95) and test‐retest reliability (intraclass correlation coefficient = 0.993). A strong correlation was observed with the VAS, but not with PNIF. Postoperative NOSE scores improved significantly over time (P < .01), with a large effect size at 3 months (Cohen's d = 2.17). The VN‐NOSE effectively discriminated between patients and controls. The MCID, estimated using both distribution‐ and anchor‐based methods, was approximately 15 points.ConclusionThe VN‐NOSE is a reliable, valid, and responsive instrument for clinical and research use in Vietnamese patients with nasal obstruction, with an MCID of 15 points.

Nasal obstruction is a common rhinologic symptom that significantly impairs quality of life, sleep, and daily functioning. Objective measurements, such as acoustic rhinometry, rhinomanometry, and peak nasal inspiratory flow (PNIF), provide quantitative assessments of nasal airflow; however, they often correlate poorly with patients' subjective perception of obstruction. 1 Therefore, patient‐reported outcome measures (PROMs) remain essential for evaluating symptom burden and treatment outcomes from the patient's perspective.

The Nasal Obstruction Symptom Evaluation (NOSE) scale, developed by Stewart et al, 2 is a concise, disease‐specific PROM designed to assess health‐related quality of life in patients with nasal obstruction, particularly deviated nasal septum (DNS) and inferior turbinate hypertrophy (ITH). The instrument consists of five items scored on a 5‐point Likert scale, with total scores standardized to a 0 to 100 range. Owing to its simplicity, reliability, and responsiveness, the NOSE scale has been widely adopted in both clinical practice and research, particularly for outcome assessment following septoplasty and inferior turbinoplasty.

The NOSE scale has been translated and validated in multiple languages, consistently demonstrating robust psychometric properties across diverse cultural contexts. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 However, prior to this study, no validated Vietnamese version of the NOSE questionnaire was available. This gap has limited standardized assessment of quality of life related to nasal obstruction in Vietnamese‐speaking populations and hindered meaningful comparisons with international studies.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to translate and culturally adapt the NOSE questionnaire into Vietnamese (VN‐NOSE) and to evaluate its psychometric properties, including reliability, validity, responsiveness, and minimal clinically important difference (MCID).

Materials and Methods

Ethical Considerations

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Medicine and Pharmacy, Hue University (IRB No. H2023/451). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

Translation and Cross‐Cultural Adaptation

The NOSE questionnaire was translated into Vietnamese using the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) guidelines. 10 Two independent forward translations were reconciled into a single version, which was then back‐translated by a bilingual native English speaker. A multidisciplinary consensus panel, including the translators and 2 senior rhinologists, reviewed all versions to produce a pre‐final draft. Pilot testing in 15 patients with DNS and ITH evaluated clarity, cultural appropriateness, and comprehensibility. Feedback from these patients informed the final modifications, resulting in the definitive VN‐NOSE (Figure 1).

Study Population

Participants were recruited from the Department of Otolaryngology at University of Medicine and Pharmacy, Hue University, between December 2022 and September 2025. Two cohorts were prospectively enrolled: a symptomatic group and an asymptomatic control group.

The symptomatic group included adult patients diagnosed with nasal obstruction due to DNS and ITH. Eligibility criteria were age ≥ 18 years, ability to read and understand Vietnamese, persistent nasal obstruction for >3 months, and inadequate response to intranasal corticosteroid therapy for ≥4 weeks. Exclusion criteria were nasal obstruction due to inflammatory mucosal disease, craniofacial anomalies, or a history of septoplasty, septorhinoplasty, or turbinate surgery.

The control group comprised asymptomatic volunteers recruited from hospital staff and accompanying relatives. Controls were required to be ≥18 years old, fluent in Vietnamese, and have no history of chronic nasal obstruction or intranasal medication use.

Outcome Measures

All participants completed the VN‐NOSE questionnaire at baseline. Symptom severity was additionally evaluated using a visual analogue scale (VAS) for nasal breathing (0 = no obstruction; 10 = complete obstruction) and a 6‐point Likert scale for self‐perceived nasal obstruction (0 = none, 1 = very mild, 2 = mild, 3 = moderate, 4 = severe, 5 = complete). PNIF was measured with a standardized handheld PNIF meter in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

Reliability Assessment

Internal Consistency

Internal consistency was evaluated using Cronbach's alpha coefficient. Values of 0.70 to 0.79 were considered acceptable, 0.80 to 0.89 good, and ≥0.90 excellent. Corrected item‐total and inter‐item correlations were assessed using Spearman's rank correlation.

Test‐Retest Reliability

Test‐retest reliability was assessed in clinically stable patients who completed the VN‐NOSE twice within a 3‐day interval without any change in clinical status or treatment. Reliability was quantified using the two‐way random‐effects intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), with values ≥0.70 indicating acceptable reliability.

Validity Assessment

Construct Validity

Construct validity was examined by correlating VN‐NOSE scores with VAS scores, the 6‐point Likert scale, and PNIF values using Spearman's rank correlation, based on predefined hypotheses that higher VN‐NOSE scores would be associated with worse subjective symptoms and lower PNIF.

Discriminant Validity

Discriminant validity was assessed by comparing VN‐NOSE scores between symptomatic patients and healthy controls.

Responsiveness and Minimal Clinically Important Difference

Responsiveness was evaluated in patients undergoing septoplasty with inferior turbinoplasty. The VN‐NOSE was administered preoperatively and at 1 week, 3 weeks, and 3 months postoperatively. Changes over time were analyzed to determine sensitivity to clinical improvement.

The MCID was estimated using both distribution‐based and anchor‐based methods. The distribution‐based MCID was defined as one‐half of the baseline standard deviation. 11 For the anchor‐based approach, changes in the 6‐point Likert scale served as the external anchor, with correlations ≥ 0.30 considered acceptable.

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher's exact test and continuous variables using independent‐samples t‐tests. Missing data were handled using the last‐observation‐carried‐forward method. Responsiveness was evaluated using a repeated‐measures analysis of variance and Bonferroni‐adjusted post hoc comparisons based on absolute mean changes from baseline. Effect size was calculated using Cohen's d and interpreted as trivial (<0.20), small (0.20‐0.49), moderate (0.50‐0.79), or large (≥0.80). 12 Statistical analyses were performed using Stata version 15 (StataCorp.), with P < .05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 143 participants were included, comprising 101 patients with nasal obstruction and 42 healthy asymptomatic controls. Among the symptomatic patients, 47 underwent septoplasty combined with inferior turbinoplasty and completed the 3‐month postoperative follow‐up. There were no significant differences between the symptomatic and control groups in age or sex distribution (P > 0.05). Baseline demographic characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Internal Consistency

The VN‐NOSE demonstrated excellent internal consistency at baseline (Cronbach's α = 0.95), indicating high homogeneity among the five items. All corrected item‐total correlations exceeded the recommended threshold, confirming that each item makes a meaningful contribution to the overall scale.

Test‐Retest Reliability

Test‐retest analysis showed excellent temporal stability, with an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.993 (95% CI: 0.991‐0.995). These findings indicate outstanding reproducibility of the VN‐NOSE in clinically stable participants.

Discriminant Validity

The VN‐NOSE effectively discriminated between symptomatic patients and healthy controls. Mean scores were significantly higher in patients with nasal obstruction than in controls (41.68 ± 25.99 vs 1.43 ± 0.49; P < .001), demonstrating strong discriminant validity.

Construct Validity

Correlation With VAS

In the symptomatic group, VN‐NOSE total scores showed a strong positive correlation with VAS scores for nasal obstruction (Spearman's r = 0.73, P < .001). All 5 individual items were also significantly correlated with VAS scores (P < .001). In the control group, a weaker but statistically significant correlation was observed (Spearman's r = 0.32, P < .05). Detailed coefficients are presented in Table 2.

Responsiveness

Among the 47 patients who completed postoperative follow‐up, VN‐NOSE scores showed progressive and statistically significant improvement after surgery, decreasing from 58.30 ± 23.50 preoperatively to 32.77 ± 25.64 at 1 week, 14.68 ± 14.46 at 3 weeks, and 6.81 ± 9.23 at 3 months. A repeated‐measures ANOVA demonstrated a significant effect of time (P < .001), and post hoc analyses confirmed significant improvements at all postoperative time points relative to baseline (Figure 2). The magnitude of change at 3 months corresponded to a large effect size (Cohen's d = 2.17), indicating high responsiveness.

Minimal Clinically Important Difference

The MCID was estimated using both distribution‐based and anchor‐based approaches. The distribution‐based method, defined as one‐half of the baseline standard deviation of VN‐NOSE score, yielded an MCID of 11.75 points. For the anchor‐based approach, changes in VN‐NOSE scores were correlated with changes in the 6‐point Likert scale at 3 months postoperatively, demonstrating a significant association (Spearman's r = 0.35, P = .017) and supporting anchor validity. Application of the half‐standard deviation method to the Likert obstruction score produced an MCID of 0.44, based on a baseline value of 3.51 ± 0.88. Therefore, a one‐point change on the Likert scale would likely overestimate the corresponding MCID for the VN‐NOSE. Inspection of the data (Table 4) indicated that the anchor‐based MCID for the VN‐NOSE was approximately 15 points.

Discussion

This study translated, culturally adapted, and validated the Vietnamese version of the NOSE questionnaire for patients with nasal obstruction. The findings confirm that the VN‐NOSE is a reliable, valid, and responsive instrument for assessing disease‐specific quality of life in Vietnamese‐speaking populations.

Internal consistency was excellent (Cronbach's α = 0.95), comparable to or slightly higher than that of the original and other language versions, indicating strong item homogeneity. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Test‐retest reliability was also outstanding (ICC = 0.993), demonstrating temporal stability in clinically stable individuals and supporting its use in both routine practice and longitudinal research.

Discriminant validity was evidenced by significantly higher VN‐NOSE scores in symptomatic patients than in controls, consistent with previous validation studies. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 Construct validity was supported by a strong correlation between VN‐NOSE and VAS scores (r = 0.73), exceeding the reported correlation between SNOT‐22 and VAS for nasal obstruction (r = 0.56). 13 In contrast, correlations between VN‐NOSE and PNIF were weak and nonsignificant, reflecting the well‐recognized discrepancy between subjective symptoms and objective airflow and reinforcing the multidimensional nature of nasal obstruction. 1 Accordingly, assessment of nasal obstruction should integrate both subjective and objective measures.

The VN‐NOSE demonstrated excellent responsiveness to surgery, with significant and progressive improvement at all postoperative time points and the greatest reduction at 3 months. The large effect size (Cohen's d = 2.17) was comparable to previous reports, 5 , 8 indicating substantial clinical impact and high sensitivity to change.

A key contribution of this study is the estimation of the MCID using distribution‐ and anchor‐based approaches. The anchor‐based method, based on a 6‐point Likert scale, showed a significant correlation with VN‐NOSE change, with a one‐point improvement corresponding to an approximately 15‐point reduction. This estimate is comparable to previous reports 14 , 15 and enhances clinical interpretability. Minor differences may reflect the single‐center design and limited sample size. The VN‐NOSE thus provides a simple, disease‐specific tool for quantifying symptom severity and treatment outcomes, with strong responsiveness and an established MCID supporting its use in surgical outcome evaluation and as a clinical trial endpoint. To our knowledge, this is the second NOSE validation in Southeast Asia, after the Thai version, 9 and the first in the region to assess responsiveness and estimate the MCID.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the number of patients completing long‐term postoperative follow‐up was relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of responsiveness and MCID estimates. Second, this was a single‐center study, and multicenter validation would further strengthen external validity. Finally, the use of last observation carried forward for missing data may have introduced bias in the longitudinal analysis of patient‐reported outcomes.

Conclusion

The Vietnamese version of the NOSE questionnaire is a reliable, valid, and highly responsive instrument for assessing quality of life related to nasal obstruction. Establishing the MCID enhances its clinical interpretability and supports its use in both routine practice and research settings in Vietnam.

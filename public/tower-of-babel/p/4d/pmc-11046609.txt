The rare umbilical nodule
Authors: Jegadis Sreeneyasan, Divya Kanakalingam, Rajinder Gupta
Published: 2024

Sister Mary Joseph's nodule (SMJN), a hard dermal or subcutaneous nodule, usually indicates the late manifestation of an abdominopelvic malignancy.1 It is seen as a malignant physical finding of gastrointestinal malignancies in men and gynecological malignancies in women.2, 3 This case report describes a woman presenting with a SMJN who was diagnosed with metastatic esophageal carcinoma.

A 79-year-old woman with no significant past medical history presented to the medical assessment unit (MAU) with dysphagia and reduced appetite for 3 weeks and with significant weight loss over last 3 months. She had also noted an umbilical swelling in the last month which she ignored. She reported smoking around eight cigarettes a day for 50 years, denied any alcohol intake and was independent in activities of standard living. On examination, she was cachexic, frail and hemodynamically stable. She was found to have a 5 cm by 5 cm hard lump in the umbilical region with mildly distended abdomen. Her laboratory results revealed microcytic anemia, raised C-reactive protein, hypomagnesemia, hypophosphatemia and hypercalcemia. Computed tomography (CT) scan revealed a bulky, long segment, obstructing oesophageal tumor with Sister Mary Joseph's nodule and possible bone metastasis. Endoscopy and biopsy taken showed poorly differentiated small cell type neuroendocrine carcinoma. She was planned for best supportive care given her frailty. During her admission, she developed aspiration pneumonia and deteriorated quickly. She died a week after admission.

Given the rarity of this finding, reporting these cases aid better understanding of the condition. Early detection of cancers is of paramount importance. SMJN usually represents late presentation and a poor outcome.1, 2

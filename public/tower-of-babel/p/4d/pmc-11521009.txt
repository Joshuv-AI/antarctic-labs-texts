Magnetic resonance imaging quantitative assessment of corticospinal tract damage in basal ganglia infarction
Authors: Qiuyue Zhang, Hongxin Jiang, Shuman Han, Tingting Gao, Linghui Meng, Bulang Gao, Lei Cao, Jinxu Wen, Yanmei Ju, Wenjuan Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To retrospectively explore the role of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) in analyzing the corticospinal tract injury in acute cerebral anterior circulation infarction in the basal ganglia region and the correlation between DTI parameters and neurological function scores, patients with acute cerebral infarction and stroke had undergone plain MRI and DTI sequence scanning were enrolled. Diffusion tensor tractography was used to perform 3-dimensional reconstruction of bilateral corticospinal tracts (CST). The image data were processed to obtain fractional anisotropy (FA) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values, and the correlation between the DTI parameters and neurological function scores of National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) was evaluated. A total of thirty-two patients with acute ischemic cerebral infarction were retrospectively enrolled, including 19 males and 13 females with a mean age 63.22 ± 7.78 years. The CST score was 0 in 2 (6.25%) patients, 1 in 9 (28.13%), 2 in 9 (28.13%), 3 in 7 (21.88%), and 4 in 5 (15.63%). The FA value significantly (P = .01) decreased (0.52 ± 0.03 vs 0.62 ± 0.04) on the ischemic side compared with that on the normal side. A significant (P < .05) difference was detected in the number of nerve fibers (223.3 ± 110.0 vs 246.7 ± 104.4) rather than in the ADC values (0.86 ± 0.06 vs 0.84 ± 0.06) between the ischemic and healthy sides. The FA and ADC values were not significantly (P > .05) different according to the CST scores 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 between the ischemic and healthy sides. There was a significant (P < .05) negative correlation between the FA value on the infarcted side and the NIHSS score. In conclusion, with the DTI technology, varying degrees of damage to the corticospinal tract at the infarcted side can be detected and important clinical information can be provided for the diagnosis and prognosis of acute cerebral infarction by evaluating the degree of corticospinal tract injury.

1. Introduction

Cerebral infarction is also known as ischemic stroke. Thrombosis or abnormal lipid metabolism induces cerebral artery atherosclerosis and makes the normal cerebrovascular diameter narrowed or even occluded, and the distal blood circulation of the diseased vessels is severely affected, leading to acute deficiency of cerebral blood supply and oxygen.[1–3] Cerebral infarction is an important cause of human death and disability, and about 3 quarters of patients will have different degrees of motor dysfunction.[4–9] The magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) diffusion tensor tractography (DTT) technology is noninvasive and intuitive and can be used to display the brain nerve fiber bundle in 3 dimensions, being able to show the spatial position relationship between the corticospinal tract (CST) and infarction.[10–14] Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), developed on the basis of diffusion weighted imaging (DWI), is the only noninvasive examination method to observe the white matter fiber bundle of the brain, including the running direction, number of nerve fibers, and development of fiber bundles.[15] In using DTI to perform CST reconstruction in patients with acute cerebral infarction in the middle and anterior cerebral artery supply areas, researchers have shown that the degree of CST damage has an important relationship with the recovery of motor function in patients.[10] The greater the degree of CST damage on MRI, the poorer the recovery of neurological function in patients. In this study, the DTI was used to analyze the extent of diffusion, changes and damages in CST in the infarcted area of acute cerebral ischemic stroke and to evaluate the value of DTI in judging the severity of motor function injury in patients with cerebral infarction at the early stage and the relationship between DTI parameters in the CST projection area and neurological function.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Subjects

This retrospective 1-center study was approved by the ethics committee of Hebei Provincial Gucheng County People’s Hospital, and informed consent was waived by the above ethics committee of Hebei Provincial Gucheng County People’s Hospital because of the retrospective study design. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Between January 2021 and December 2022, patients with acute cerebral ischemic stroke in the basal ganglia region in our hospital were retrospectively enrolled. The inclusion criteria were patients with acute cerebral ischemic stroke, unilateral onset, without intracranial hemorrhage, with cerebral infarction mainly located in the basal ganglia, paraventricular or coronal regions of the lateral ventricle, and no contraindications for MRI examination. The exclusion criteria were patients with other organic diseases in the brain and poor image quality due to head motion artifacts during the examination process.

2.2. Diffusion tensor imaging inspection equipment and technical parameters

The PHILIPS Achieva 3.0 T MRI scanner, Best, Netherlands was used for conventional MR scanning which demonstrated no abnormal signals in the intracranial region. Then, DTI scanning was performed. Before scanning, the subject’s head was fixed with a sponge pad to prevent head movement. The subject was required to remain awake, and both external auditory meatuses were plugged with cotton balls. In DTI scanning, the spin echo planar imaging (SE-EPI) sequence was used at axial position scanning, The parameters for scanning were as follows: repetition time (TR) = 6100 ms, echo time (TE) = 87 ms, layer thickness = 2.5 mm, interval = 0, a total of 50 layers, intra-layer resolution = 128 × 128, field of view (FOV) = 256 mm × 256 mm, 64 diffusion gradient directions, b = 1000 s/mm2, b = 0, and NEX = 2. The structural MRI scanning parameters were: 176 slices, TR = 1900 ms, TE = 3.44 ms, slice thickness = 1 mm, flip angle = 9 °, conversion time = 900 ms, FOV = 256 mm × 256 mm, and acquisition matrix = 256 × 256. The scanning parameters of structural imaging (sMRI) were as follows: sagittal scan, 128 layers, TR = 2530 ms, TE = 3.39 ms, layer thickness 1.33 mm, interval 0 mm, TI = 1100 ms, FOV = 256 mm × 256 mm, in-plane resolution = 256 × 192, and fractional anisotropy (FA) = 7 °.

2.3. Image post-processing, corticospinal tracts reconstruction, and injury classification

The software at the Extended MR WorkSpace working station was used to smooth and denoise the scanned raw DTI data to generate the FA diagram and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) diagram. The fiber assignment by continuous tracking algorithm was used for whole brain fiber bundle tracking with the threshold setting of FA < 0.25. After fusing the obtained DTI image with multiple sequences such as DWI, perfusion weighted imaging, 3D T1, susceptibility-weighted imaging, fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR), and MRI angiography (MRA), fiber bundle reconstruction was conducted to identify the damage of nerve fiber bundles at the lesion site. Based on the FA image, 3-dimensional reconstruction of the patient’s CST was performed, using the imaging perpendicular to the long axis of the CST to more accurately observe the displacement, continuity, and destruction of the CST.

The evaluation of the degree of CST involvement according to the CST injury scoring standard was performed using an integral method (Figs. 1 and 2): CST was completely located outside the DWI high-signal infarct lesion (i.e., the CST was intact, without significant compression or displacement), which was scored as 0. The CST was partially or completely located within the DWI high-signal infarct focus (i.e., the CST was invaded, but not interrupted). According to the affected area, the score was recorded as 1 if the invaded CST area was <1/3 of the infarct area. If the range of invasion was between 1/3 and 2/3, it was recorded as 2. If the range of invasion was >2/3, it was recorded as 3 points. If CST was involved and interrupted or significantly reduced in numbers compared to the contralateral side, it was recorded as 4 points. The CST injury score for all cases was assessed under the guidance of 2 imaging diagnostic physicians with a professional title of deputy professor or above. If there were different opinions, an agreement was reached through consultation. The neurological function score of the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) was evaluated in each patient in relation to the DTI parameters.

2.4. Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed with the SPSS version 17.0 (IBM, Chicago). Measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation if in the normal distribution or median and interquartile range if not in the normal distribution. Categorical data were presented as frequency and percentages. The FA value, ADC value, and number of fibers on the affected side and the contralateral side were collected and analyzed using the paired t-test. Correlation analysis was performed between the NIHSS score and the DTI parameters of the patients. The difference was statistically significant with a P < .05.

3. Results

The CST score was 0 in 2 (6.25%) patients, 1 in 9 (28.13%), 2 in 9 (28.13%), 3 in 7 (21.88%), and 4 in 5 (15.63%) (Table 2).

In comparison of the FA and ADC values between the ischemic and normal side, a significant (P = .01) difference was detected in the FA values (0.52 ± 0.03 vs 0.62 ± 0.04), with the FA value significantly greater on the normal side than on the ischemic side. No significant (P > .05) difference was detected in the ADC values (0.86 ± 0.06 vs 0.84 ± 0.06) between the ischemic and normal sides.

Based on the CST scores (Figs. 1 and 2), the FA and ADC values were compared between the ischemic and healthy sides, and no significant (P > .05) difference was detected in the FA or ADC values in CST scores 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 (Table 2). A significant (P < .05) difference was detected in the number of nerve fibers (223.3 ± 110.0 vs 246.7 ± 104.4) between the ischemic and healthy sides.

A significant (P < .05) negative correlation was found in the FA value on the infarcted side with the NIHSS score (Table 3), however, no significant (P > .05) correlation was detected in the ADC value on the infarcted side or in the FA and ADC values on the healthy side with the NIHSS score.

4. Discussion

In this study investigating the MRI DTI technique in analyzing the corticospinal tract injury in anterior circulation acute cerebral infarction, it was found that varying degrees of damage to the corticospinal tract at the lesion side can be detected and important clinical information can be provided for the diagnosis and prognosis prediction of acute cerebral infarction in the basal ganglia region by evaluating the degree of corticospinal tract injury. A significant (P < .05) negative correlation was found between the FA value on the infarcted side and the neurological function score.

DTI is a new comparative imaging technology that mainly relies on the movement of water molecules in human body and is currently the only method for measuring the diffusion motion and imaging of water molecules in vivo. The dispersion direction of water molecules is consistent in homogeneous tissue, but in heterogeneous tissue, there are varied degrees of dispersion in each direction, especially in the brain fiber bundle.[16,17] Compared with conventional MRI, DTI reflects the pathophysiological changes of CST injury in the infarcted area through quantitative parameters such as FA and ADC values at the molecular level. Studies have shown that the white matter of the brain is more sensitive to ischemia than the gray matter. After cerebral ischemia, the diffusion of water molecules in the acute cerebral infarction area is abnormal, resulting in corresponding changes in the FA and ADC values.[18,19] The FA value reflects the structural directionality of the white matter fiber bundle of the brain. The higher the FA value, the better the directionality and conductivity. The conduction of the white matter fiber bundle on the affected side of the patient was damaged, and the alignment and consistency of the nerve fiber bundle and the integrity of the nerve fiber sheath were damaged, leading to a significant decrease in the FA value on the affected side.[19–21] In a study investigating the role of acute subcortical infarcts in causing secondary degeneration in remote non-involved cortex and connecting fiber tracts,[22] it was found that the FA and track-density imaging values of all parts of the infarction-ipsilateral CST, including the non-involved upper and lower parts, significantly decreased at 6-month follow-up. The mean FA value was also significantly decreased on the infarction side compared with that on the contralateral side in a study exploring DTI of normal-appearing white matter in unilateral cerebral arterial occlusion.[23] In our study, the FA value of the affected side was significantly lower than that of the contralateral healthy side (P < .001). To sum up, DTI can quantitatively analyze the reduction of FA value in cerebral infarction through the degree of CST injury. Therefore, the FA value of cerebral infarction can be used as an objective indicator for early diagnosis of patients with acute cerebral motor function damage.

The ADC value represents the diffusion characteristics of water molecules in tissues. In this study, no statistically significant difference was detected in the ADC values between the infarcted area and the contralateral side in the acute phase. After grouping the CST injury score at 1, 2, 3, and 4, no significant difference was detected in the ADC values between the acute cerebral infarction area and the contralateral side. Considering the relationship between the location and time of the infarction, further research with a large cohort of patients was needed to confirm the relevant finding. Some researchers have shown no statistically significant difference in the ADC values in the cerebral peduncle between the affected side and the contralateral healthy side of the brain in acute cerebral infarction.[24] ADC values can increase or decrease at different stages of cerebral infarction.[25] The DTI and DTT techniques have been used to better evaluate the degree of white matter fiber bundle damage at different stages after cerebral ischemia, with important values in guiding clinical diagnosis and helping to judge prognosis.[22,23,26–28] The DTT technique has also been used in guiding surgery of awake craniotomy and glioma resection,[12,29] with the CST and fiber tracts being demonstrated clearly pre- or intra-operatively so as to avoid damage in the surgical operation. DTI can detect involvement and alterations of the CST in patients with medulla infarct during motor recovery,[30] detect permanent ischemic pyramidal tract injury in acute and chronic stroke,[31] and predict the prognosis and outcome in patients with chronic pontine stroke.[32] In our study, a statistical significance was found in the number of nerve fibers on the side of the acute cerebral infarction compared with that on the contralateral side, which needs further confirmation. A significant (P < .05) negative correlation was detected between the FA value on the infarcted side and the neurological function NIHSS score. Patients with acute cerebral infarction and CST damage have more severe neurological deficits, with higher NIHSS scores. There is no significant relationship in the ADC value on the affected side or the FA or ADC values on the healthy side with the neurological function NIHSS score. These findings can be used to predict the prognosis of the patient.

Some limitations existed in this study, including a small cohort of patients, 1-center and retrospective study design, Chinese patients enrolled only, and no randomization, which may all affect the generalization of the outcome. Future prospective, randomized, controlled, multi-center studies with more patients and multiple races enrolled will have to be performed to achieve better outcomes.

In conclusion, DTI is currently the only method that can be used to explore the white matter fibers of the brain in vivo. Combining DTI with DTT can be quantitatively used to analyze the degree of damage to nerve fibers in acute cerebral infarction from an imaging perspective, providing important information for judging the degree of damage to the white matter fibers, predicting the prognosis of patients in the early stage, and providing a new and more accurate imaging diagnostic method for clinical applications. Therefore, DTI has broad clinical application prospects in the future.

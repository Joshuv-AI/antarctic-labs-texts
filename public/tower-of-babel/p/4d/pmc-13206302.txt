Differences in Right Hepatic Duct Length Among Left‐Sided Hepatectomies: Conventional Hepatectomy Versus Extended Hepatectomy Versus Trisectionectomy
Authors: Kota Sugiura, Mihoko Yamada, Takashi Mizuno, Shunsuke Onoe, Nobuyuki Watanabe, Shoji Kawakatsu, Junpei Yamaguchi, Masaki Sunagawa, Taisuke Baba, Tomoki Ebata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundLeft‐sided hepatectomy for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma includes conventional left hepatectomy (C‐LH; H1234‐B), extended left hepatectomy (E‐LH; H12345′8′‐B‐MHV), and left trisectionectomy (LT; H123458‐B). The anatomical characteristics of the resected length of the right hepatic duct (RHD) in E‐LH remain unclear. This study aimed to characterize the length of the RHD across procedures.MethodsPatients who underwent left‐sided hepatectomy for perihilar tumors between 2015 and 2023 were retrospectively reviewed. The shortest distance between the proximal bile duct stump and left hepatic duct orifice was measured on the resected specimens. The lengths and clinicopathological features of the procedures were compared.ResultsIn total, 205 patients were included: C‐LH (n = 80), E‐LH (n = 53), and LT (n = 72). The length of the right anterior hepatic duct was longer in E‐LH than that in C‐LH (15.2 vs. 13.0 mm, p = 0.006). Similarly, the length of the right posterior hepatic duct increased stepwise from C‐LH to E‐LH and LT (13.3, 16.6, and 20.0 mm, respectively).ConclusionsE‐LH is an intermediate procedure between C‐LH and LT with respect to the resected length of the RHD. The additional bile duct length achieved by E‐LH is minimal, and this procedure should be selected primarily to secure the parenchymal margin rather than ductal clearance.

Introduction

Surgical resection remains the cornerstone treatment for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, and major hepatectomy with caudate lobectomy has been used as the current definitive approach [1, 2, 3]. As the surgical procedure is selected on a multifactorial basis considering the disease location and stage, individual biliovascular anatomy, functional reserve of the future liver remnant, and the general condition of the patient, the type of hepatectomy performed for perihilar tumors potentially differs among institutions or regions [4]. However, owing to the globally observed high mortality rate after right‐sided hepatectomy for this tumor, left‐sided hepatectomy is preferred when oncologically feasible [5, 6]. Three hepatectomy approaches are used for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma with left‐sided predominance, including conventional left hepatectomy (C‐LH; H1234‐B) [7], extended left hepatectomy (E‐LH; H12345′8′‐B‐MHV), and left trisectionectomy (LT; H123458‐B); however, the indications for each approach have not been standardized [2, 8, 9, 10].

Previous reports comparing LT with C‐LH have shown that the right posterior hepatic duct (RPHD) is 7–9 mm longer in LT than after C‐LH and that surgical mortality and liver failure are more frequent in LT [6, 8, 9, 11, 12]. In contrast to C‐LH and LT, the available information on E‐LH for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma is very limited [13], although E‐LH was preferentially used in the 1980s [1]. Recently, a few studies have reappraised the utility of this procedure, suggesting that E‐LH can achieve a wider liver transection margin than C‐LH, provided a 2 cm‐thick right anterior sector with the middle hepatic vein is removed [13, 14]. However, the length of the proximal bile duct in the E‐LH remains poorly understood.

We hypothesized that E‐LH would be an intermediate procedure between C‐LH and LT with respect to the length of the resected right hepatic duct (RHD). Therefore, we measured the actual length of the RHD on the resected specimens. The aim of this study was to characterize the proximal bile duct length in patients with E‐LH.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective, single‐center, comparative study was conducted at the Department of Surgery, Nagoya University Hospital. Patients who underwent left‐sided hepatectomy for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma at our institution between January 2015 and December 2023 were included in this study. Preoperative workup, computed tomography, ultrasonography, magnetic resonance imaging, and endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography, followed by biliary drainage were routinely performed. Preoperative portal vein embolization was performed 2–4 weeks before surgery when the future liver remnant was estimated to be less than 40% [15]. This retrospective study was approved by the Human Research Review Committee of Nagoya University Hospital (Approval Number: 2023‐0023). Informed consent, including the use of anonymous retrospective data, was obtained from each patient at the time of surgery, and the authors provided an opt‐out option by announcing the implementation of this study on the hospital's website. The clinical data were retrospectively reviewed using prospectively maintained databases and medical records. The primary outcome was the measured length of the resected RHD among the three surgical procedures.

Surgical Procedure

All hepatectomies included resection of the entire caudate lobe, extrahepatic bile duct, and regional lymph nodes [2, 3, 16]. The type of hepatectomy was determined on the basis of local tumor extension [8, 17]. C‐LH was mainly applied to Bismuth type IIIb tumors [18]; E‐LH was performed for Bismuth type IIIb tumors invading the left portal vein and the middle hepatic vein [13], which has been proactively used in recent years; LT was performed in Bismuth type IV tumors with left‐sided predominance [8]. Exceptionally, left‐sided hepatectomy was considered for Bismuth type II or IIIa tumors when the future liver remnant was insufficient [19]. Details of the surgical procedures have been described previously [2, 8, 13]. As shown in Figure 1, in C‐LH and E‐LH, the RPHD was divided craniodorsally into the right or right anterior portal veins as proximally as possible. In LT, the RPHD was divided proximally at the craniodorsal side of the right posterior portal vein (RPPV) by dividing the right anterior portal vein. If patients presented infraportal variations in RPHD [20], the duct was divided on the ventral side of the right portal vein.

Measurement of the Length of the Right Hepatic Duct

The extrahepatic bile duct of the resected specimens was opened longitudinally from the distal to proximal cut end. The opened bile duct was pinned to the liver parenchyma to maintain its anatomical configuration. After photographs of the resected specimens were obtained, they were fixed in 10% formalin for several days.

The length of the RHD was measured on these photographs as previously described [8, 11]. Specifically, the length of the right anterior hepatic duct (RAHD) was measured in the specimens removed after C‐LH and E‐LH and was defined as the shortest distance between the orifice of the left hepatic duct and the cut end of the RAHD. The length of the RPHD was measured in all three hepatectomies and defined as the shortest distance between the orifice of the left hepatic duct and the cut end of the RPHD (Figure 2). In patients who underwent intraoperative frozen section examination of the stumps of the RHD, 3 mm was added to the measured bile duct length to account for an approximately 3‐mm cross‐sectioned segment resected from the ductal stump for pathological examination [21].

The measured length of the RPHD was evaluated according to its anatomical variants. These variations were classified into three types on the basis of their topological relationship with the portal system [20, 22]: supraportal type, in which the RPHD runs dorsocranially to the right portal vein and merges into the hepatic duct at its cranial side; infraportal type, in which the RPHD runs ventrocaudally to the right portal vein and drains into the hepatic duct at its caudal side; and combined type, in which some branches of the RPHD join supraportally and the remaining branches run infraportally into the hepatic duct.

Histological Evaluation

Formalin‐fixed specimens were carefully sectioned at 5‐mm intervals and examined microscopically using hematoxylin and eosin staining. The resection margin status of the RHD was classified as negative, positive with carcinoma in situ, or positive with invasive carcinoma. Pathological findings were described according to the 8th edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) tumor‐node‐metastasis (TNM) classification of malignant tumors [23]. Positive margins with carcinoma in situ were treated as negative margins because residual carcinoma in situ has been reported to exert little effect on postoperative survival [24, 25].

Postoperative Outcomes

Postoperative complications and mortality were evaluated according to the Clavien–Dindo classification, and major complications were defined as grade III or higher [26]. Postoperative bile leakage and post‐hepatectomy liver failure were defined and graded according to the criteria of the International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS), and grade B or C events were considered clinically relevant [27, 28].

Statistical Analysis

The results are expressed as the number of patients (%) or median values (interquartile range [IQR]). Statistical analyses were performed using Fisher's exact probability test and the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. For prespecified pairwise comparisons between E‐LH and the other procedures, p‐values were adjusted using the Bonferroni correction. A p‐value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using EZR (version 1.62, Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan), a graphical user interface for R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) [29].

Results

In total, 219 consecutive patients with perihilar cholangiocarcinoma underwent left‐sided hepatectomy during the study period. After excluding 14 patients owing to insufficient information regarding the resected specimens, 205 patients were included in the study cohort, including 144 men and 61 women with a median age of 71 years (range, 63–76 years). The hepatectomy types were C‐LH in 80 patients (39.0%), E‐LH in 53 patients (25.9%), and LT in 72 patients (35.1%). Preoperative biliary drainage was performed in 157 (76.6%) patients. Preoperative portal vein embolization was performed in 68 (33.2%) patients, all of whom underwent LT. Combined portal vein and hepatic artery resections were performed in 43 (21.0%) and 39 (19.0%) patients, respectively. Intraoperative frozen section analysis of the proximal ductal margins was performed in 155 patients (75.6%). According to the type of hepatectomy (Table 1), advanced disease, including Bismuth type III/IV, pathological T stage ≥ T3, and lymph node metastasis, was more frequently observed in patients who underwent E‐LH or LT than in those who underwent C‐LH. Intraoperative blood loss was significantly greater in the LT group than in the other two groups. Although the E‐LH group had a higher number of reconstructed bile ducts than the other two types, the incidence of postoperative bile leakage did not differ significantly among the three. Post‐hepatectomy liver failure more frequently occurred in the LT group. Three patients in the LT group died of postoperative liver failure (n = 2) and early tumor recurrence (n = 1), whereas one in the C‐LH died of postoperative liver failure.

Length of the Right Hepatic Duct in Specimen

The measured ductal lengths are listed in Table 2. The length of the RAHD in E‐LH (15.2 [13.0–20.3] mm) was significantly longer than that in C‐LH (13.0 [9.8–15.9] mm) (p = 0.006) (Figure 3). The length of the RPHD in the three groups was 13.3 (9.8–17.5) mm in the C‐LH group, 16.6 (13.4–20.8) mm in the E‐LH group, and 20.0 (16.7–24.8) mm in the LT group (E‐LH vs. C‐LH, p = 0.008; E‐LH vs. LT, p = 0.002) (Figure 3). The length of the RPHD in the E‐LH group was 3.3 mm longer than that in the C‐LH group and 3.4 mm shorter than that in the LT group.

Stratified by confluence patterns of RPHD, 178 (86.8%) patients were classified as the supraportal type, 17 (8.3%) as the infraportal type, and 10 (4.9%) as the combined type (Table 2). Among patients with supraportal type, RPHD lengths had a similar trend as that of the overall analysis: 13.0 (9.9–17.3) mm in the C‐LH group, 16.1 (13.4–21.2) mm in the E‐LH group, and 20.4 (17.3–25.4) mm in the LT group (E‐LH vs. C‐LH, p = 0.008, E‐LH vs. LT, p = 0.002) (Figure 4). In contrast, no significant differences were observed in the length of the RPHD among the three groups in patients with non‐supraportal types: the length of the RPHD was 15.7 (8.7–18.5) mm in the C‐LH group, 17.0 (15.3–17.2) mm in the E‐LH group, and 18.1 (14.9–22.7) mm in the LT group (E‐LH vs. C‐LH, p = 1.000; E‐LH vs. LT, p = 0.818).

Margin Status

The rate of positive distal margins was similar across the procedures (Table 2). The incidence of a positive proximal margin was also comparable across the three surgical procedures: 11.2% (9/80), 13.2% (7/53), and 6.9% (5/72) in the C‐LH, E‐LH, and LT groups, respectively, irrespective of the Bismuth type and UICC pT stage. Similarly, the rate of positive radial margins did not differ significantly among the three groups. Overall, the R1/2 resection rates were 25.0% (20/80), 18.9% (10/53), and 20.8% (15/72) in the C‐LH, E‐LH, and LT groups, respectively, with no significant differences when stratified according to tumor stage.

Discussion

E‐LH is technically characterized by liver transection along 2 cm of the right side of the Rex‐Cantlie line (the middle hepatic vein), as previously described [13]. This 2 cm‐thick right anterior sector provides a wider liver parenchymal margin from the perihilar tumor, reducing the likelihood of tumor exposure at the liver transection plane because the hilar bile duct topologically approximates the middle hepatic vein within approximately 1 cm [13, 14]. Therefore, E‐LH has been reappraised in recent years for mass‐forming perihilar tumors with a left‐sided predominance to avoid tumor exposure during liver transection. However, the length of the RHD obtained by E‐LH, another anatomic reference for surgical decisions, remains unclear. Previous studies have investigated differences in the resected length of the RHD between C‐LH and LT [8, 9, 11]. Therefore, the present anatomical study aimed to identify the unique features of E‐LH among the three types of left‐sided hepatectomies. The study had two main findings: first, the RAHD was significantly longer by 2 mm in E‐LH than in C‐LH; second, the RPHD was stepwise longer by 3 mm in the order of C‐LH, E‐LH, and LT. These findings support the hypothesis that E‐LH is an intermediate procedure between C‐LH and LT in terms of ductal length in the resected specimen.

Interestingly, the difference in the length of the RHD between the C‐LH and E‐LH groups was statistically significant but was only 2–3 mm, which was nonetheless smaller than anticipated. This can be explained by the fact that the proximal resection line of the RHD depends on two different surgical planes. First, the duct is divided by the line defined by the liver transection; second, the duct is divided at the proximal point where the right portal vein and right hepatic artery detach from the bile duct. The extent of vascular isolation was nearly equal in E‐LH and C‐LH procedures, resulting in a minimal difference in the RAHD length in E‐LH. E‐LH was an anatomically intermediate form between C‐LH and LT; however, its clinical characteristics were close to C‐LH, except for the liver parenchymal margin.

The length of the RPHD is affected by anatomical variations in the hepatic duct, as described in previous reports [8, 9] and needs to be evaluated in the most common type, the supraportal type of RPHD [20]. In this variation, in which the RPHD runs behind the right portal vein, the resection line is limited by the right portal vein in E‐LH and C‐LH procedures, whereas it can be set near the root of RPPV after division of the right anterior portal vein in LT [8, 9]. Therefore, LT allowed for the most proximal resection of RPHD among the three hepatectomies. Three previous reports have measured the length of the RPHD in LT and compared it with that achieved in C‐LH. Natsume et al. [8] reported that the RPHD was 7.1 mm longer in LT than in C‐LH (LT: 20.7 mm vs. C‐LH: 13.6 mm) based on resected specimens. Hirose et al. examined both resected specimens and radiological images [11], and observed similar findings (LT: 21.3 mm vs. C‐LH: 14.1 mm) in the former and a 10.8 mm difference (LT: 31.6 mm vs. C‐LH: 20.8 mm) in the latter. Hosokawa et al. observed a 9.0 mm longer RPHD in LT compared with C‐LH based on multidetector computed tomography [9]. The obtained RPHD lengths in the C‐LH and LT groups in our cohort were similar to those observed in previous studies, indicating the reproducibility of our findings. Thus, the RPHD length in E‐LH, which was intermediate among the three types of left‐sided hepatectomies evaluated, can be considered highly reliable. In contrast, in the non‐supraportal type, no differences were observed in the RPHD length comparing the three groups. This result was also consistent with the previous studies, in which the RPHD was divided at the same anatomical point, ventral to the right portal vein, regardless of the type of left‐sided hepatectomy [8]. In this anatomical variant, E‐LH and LT offered no advantage over C‐LH in obtaining a greater RPHD length. Therefore, the surgical procedure for this variant is determined by considering the other margins, including liver parenchymal margin and tumor extension toward RAHD.

Unsurprisingly, the proximal ductal margin status and R0 resection rate were not significantly different among the procedure types, as E‐LH and LT were preferentially used for advanced tumors, such as Bismuth type III/IV tumor and pathological T stage ≥ T3, during the study period [30]. Therefore, the safety margin length in the proximal duct (i.e., the distance between the ductal stump and tumor border) was not always longer in E‐LH or LT compared with C‐LH. Moreover, radiologic assessment of tumor extent remains uncertain, as histologic tumor spread often exceeds gross imaging findings [31], rendering radiologic measurement of the ductal safety margin length of limited clinical relevance. Overall, because E‐LH is unlikely to provide a clinically meaningful additional extension of the ductal margin and tends to be applied in more advanced patients, comparable ductal margin status was observed between E‐LH and C‐LH [8, 13]. Based on these considerations, extended hepatectomy should be performed as far as the patient's condition and future liver remnant allow, and E‐LH should be considered the first‐line approach for Bismuth type IIIb tumors. The practical principle of surgical choice of the three left‐sided hepatectomies is summarized as follows. First, LT is indicated largely for Bismuth type IV tumor; second, E‐LH is indicated for Bismuth type IIIb tumor with a significant liver component or vascular invasion (cT2b or more), the aim of which is to keep distance of liver transection plane from main tumor (Figure 5a). Third, C‐LH is indicated for Bismuth type IIIb tumor located limited within the left hepatic duct without surrounding invasion, which may be a small subset among the whole perihilar cholangiocarcinoma. In this tumor type, a sufficient parenchymal liver margin can be secured even with preservation of the middle hepatic vein (Figure 5b).

This single‐center retrospective study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the confluence pattern of the hepatic ducts affects the selection of the surgical procedure. In particular, the LT tends to be selected for patients with the non‐supraportal type. In this study, LT was performed in 52% (14/27) of the patients with the non‐supraportal type, resulting in a small number of patients with C‐LH and E‐LH in this subgroup, thereby suggesting the need for additional analysis with larger subgroups. Second, although the R0 resection rate did not differ among the three hepatectomy types, heterogeneous tumor progression should be considered when interpreting the results. Third, in this study, the length of the RHD was measured only in resected specimens and not on radiological images. Radiologically measured duct lengths were longer than those measured in resected specimens in a previous report [11]. However, considering the technical reality, the actual length obtained from the resected specimens may provide more meaningful information for surgeons in clinical practice.

In conclusion, this study clearly characterized the length of the RHD in E‐LH and demonstrated that E‐LH exhibits an intermediate outcome between C‐LH and LT procedures. The additional bile duct length achieved by E‐LH is minimal, and this procedure should be selected primarily to secure an adequate parenchymal margin rather than to achieve ductal margin clearance.

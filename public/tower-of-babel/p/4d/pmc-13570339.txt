Ovarian H3K27ac remodeling is associated with impaired follicular development in laying hens with fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome
Authors: Yong Cui, Wenhui Zeng, Haiping Liang, Yujie Wang, Linjian Weng, Ming Qu, Qing Wei, Xianhua Xie, Jianzhen Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome (FLHS) is a metabolic disease of laying hens that reduces egg production and is accompanied by reproductive impairment, but the ovarian regulatory mechanisms that connect nutritional stress to follicular dysfunction remain unclear. This study examined whether active chromatin remodeling in the ovary is associated with FLHS induced by a high-energy, low-protein (HELP) diet. Hy-Line Brown hens were assigned to a basal diet or HELP diet, and ovarian tissue was collected for histone H3 lysine 27 acetylation (H3K27ac) chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing and RNA sequencing. The HELP diet reduced laying performance and the numbers of small yellow and hierarchical follicles, indicating compromised follicular development. Genome-wide H3K27ac profiling identified 2,111 regions with lower acetylation and 1,707 regions with higher acetylation in FLHS ovaries. Genes linked to differential H3K27ac regions were enriched in pathways related to oocyte meiosis, cell cycle control, FoxO signaling, gonadotropin-releasing hormone signaling, and steroid hormone biosynthesis. RNA sequencing identified 341 differentially expressed genes, with a predominance of downregulated genes. Integration of chromatin and transcriptome data highlighted folliculogenesis-related genes, including FGF1, FGF9, and MMP10, that showed reduced H3K27ac enrichment together with decreased expression. Super-enhancer analysis further identified 131 regions with reduced H3K27ac signal in FLHS ovaries, including regions located near PCNA and RAP1A, two genes involved in cellular proliferation and survival signaling. Motif enrichment of differential H3K27ac regions implicated Fos, SF-1/NR5A1, and GATA-4 as candidate transcriptional regulators. These findings indicate that HELP diet-induced FLHS is associated with broad attenuation of active ovarian regulatory elements and reduced expression of genes required for follicle growth, tissue remodeling, and steroidogenic function. The study provides an ovarian epigenomic framework for understanding reproductive decline in FLHS-affected laying hens.

Introduction

Fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome (FLHS) is a production-limiting metabolic disorder in laying hens and is characterized by excessive hepatic lipid deposition, increased mortality risk, and depressed laying performance. Nutritional imbalance, particularly diets that provide excess energy relative to protein, is an important experimental driver of this syndrome (Rozenboim et al., 2016). Although FLHS is commonly defined by liver pathology, its economic impact is tightly connected to the reproductive axis because egg output depends on coordinated hepatic lipid transport, endocrine signaling, follicle recruitment, and ovarian steroidogenesis (Johnson et al., 2002; Walzem et al., 1999). Therefore, mechanisms operating in ovarian tissue need to be examined directly rather than inferred only from hepatic endpoints.

Histone modifications are responsive to metabolic state and can reshape tissue-specific gene regulation. Histone H3 lysine 27 acetylation (H3K27ac) is widely used to annotate active promoters and enhancers and can distinguish active enhancers from poised regulatory regions (Creyghton et al., 2010). In metabolic disease models, altered H3K27ac has been linked to changes in lipid handling and inflammatory or endocrine pathways (Cai et al., 2021). In laying hens, hepatic epigenomic studies have shown that FLHS is accompanied by altered enhancer activity and histone methylation or acetylation near genes involved in lipid metabolism and gluconeogenesis (Cui et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). Those studies establish that nutritional stress can remodel the FLHS epigenome, but they do not resolve whether the same stress impairs ovarian regulatory elements that sustain follicular growth.

Ovarian follicles undergo rapid proliferation, extracellular matrix remodeling, yolk deposition, and steroid hormone synthesis. These processes require precise transcriptional control in granulosa and theca cells and are sensitive to systemic metabolic and endocrine cues. Recent poultry studies further show that chicken follicle recruitment and SYF formation involve dynamic transcriptional programs related to steroidogenesis, FoxO signaling, extracellular matrix remodeling, Wnt signaling, and granulosa cell function (Leng et al., 2024; You et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2026). At the epigenomic level, H3K27ac-marked active chromatin has been linked to sex- and situs-specific gene expression during chicken gonadal development, indicating that this modification is functionally relevant to avian reproductive tissue (Jiang et al., 2022). Active enhancers and super-enhancers may therefore be particularly relevant because they often regulate genes that determine tissue function and cell identity (Hnisz et al., 2013). If FLHS reduces H3K27ac at ovarian regulatory elements, the resulting transcriptional changes could provide a mechanistic link between dietary metabolic stress and reduced follicle development.

The objective of this study was to define ovarian H3K27ac landscapes in laying hens with HELP diet-induced FLHS and to integrate these data with ovarian transcriptomes. By focusing on ovarian tissue, this study was designed to distinguish reproductive epigenomic changes from previously reported hepatic responses to FLHS. We further investigated pathways, candidate transcription factors, and super-enhancer-associated genes that may contribute to impaired folliculogenesis and reduced laying performance.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement and experimental design

All animal procedures were approved by the Jiangxi Agricultural University Care and Use Committee (approval number SYXK 2020-0005). Hens were managed to avoid unnecessary discomfort using standard husbandry and laboratory procedures, and euthanasia and tissue collection followed the approved institutional protocol.

Ninety Hy-Line Brown laying hens at 150 d of age, with an average body weight of approximately 1.5 kg, were obtained from a local farm and acclimated for 7 d. Birds were housed under controlled conditions of 28 to 32°C, 50 to 75% relative humidity, and a 16L:8D photoperiod with lights on from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. Water and feed were provided ad libitum. After acclimation, hens with similar body weight and laying status were randomly assigned to two treatments, with 3 replicates per treatment and 15 hens per replicate. The control group (CON; n = 45 hens) received a corn-soybean meal basal diet, whereas the experimental group received a high-energy, low-protein diet (HELP; n = 45 hens) for a 63-d feeding period. Diet composition and analyzed nutrient values are provided in Table S1.

Laying performance, egg quality, and tissue collection

Egg production was recorded daily by replicate throughout the 63-d feeding period. At the end of the study, 30 individual eggs were randomly selected from each treatment for assessment of egg weight, eggshell strength, eggshell thickness, egg specific gravity, yolk color, and Haugh unit (Table S2). Measurements followed the Chinese agricultural standard NY/T 823-2020. Data were summarized and analyzed using SPSS software.

Liver samples were fixed in 4% neutral buffered formaldehyde for histological confirmation of the FLHS phenotype. Whole ovaries, including follicles at all visible developmental stages, were collected within 10 min after euthanasia and placed immediately on ice. Visible follicles were gently separated from the ovarian stroma, connective tissue was removed, and follicles were classified by diameter as small yellow follicles (SYF; 6 to 8 mm) or hierarchical follicles (HIE; 9 to 40 mm). Follicle numbers were recorded for each hen. Ovarian tissues intended for RNA sequencing were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C, whereas tissues intended for chromatin immunoprecipitation were rapidly minced and processed or stored according to the ChIP kit requirements.

Histological confirmation of FLHS

Fixed liver tissues were embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin using a commercial staining kit (Beyotime, Beijing, China). Histological evaluation was used to verify the metabolic phenotype of the model.

RNA sequencing and differential expression analysis

Total RNA was isolated from ovarian tissue using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Dalian, China). RNA integrity and quantity were assessed before library preparation. Three biological replicates per treatment (C1-C3 and H1-H3) were used for RNA sequencing. Sequencing libraries were constructed by Novogene Bioinformatics Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) and sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq platform. Sequencing statistics are summarized in Table S3.

Raw reads were filtered with Trimmomatic v0.40 (Bolger et al., 2014). Clean reads were aligned to the chicken reference genome GRCg6a (Ensembl release 104) with HISAT2 v2.0.5 (Kim et al., 2019). Gene-level counts were generated with featureCounts v2.0.6 (Liao et al., 2014). Differential expression between control and FLHS ovaries was analyzed using DESeq2 v1.20.0 (Love et al., 2014). Genes with an adjusted Q value < 0.05 and an absolute fold change > 2 were considered differentially expressed.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing and identification of differential H3K27ac regions

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) was performed on ovarian tissue with the SimpleChIP Plus Enzymatic Chromatin IP Kit (catalog no. 9005; Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA) using an antibody against H3K27ac. Two biological replicates per treatment were used for H3K27ac ChIP-seq, and matched input libraries were prepared for background control.

Clean reads were aligned to the GRCg6a chicken reference genome with Bowtie2 v2.5.1 (Langmead and Salzberg, 2012). Uniquely mapped reads were retained with Sambamba v0.8.1 (Tarasov et al., 2015), and duplicated reads were removed with Picard v1.119. Alignment files were converted to normalized bigWig coverage tracks for visualization and metagene analysis with deepTools v2.5.0 (Ramirez et al., 2014). H3K27ac-enriched regions were called with MACS2 v2.1.0 using input DNA as the control and the parameters -p 0.05 –nomodel –broad –keep-dup all (Zhang et al., 2008).

Consensus peaks were defined with DiffBind v2.14.0 as regions detected in at least two samples. Differentially acetylated regions (DAR) were identified with DiffBind using P < 0.05 and an absolute fold change > 1.5 as thresholds. Peaks located from 2 kb upstream to 1 kb downstream of a transcription start site were annotated as promoter-associated regions, and remaining peaks were annotated as putative enhancers using ChIPseeker v1.12.1 with Ensembl GRCg6a release 104 gene annotations (Yu et al., 2015). The nearest gene to each enhancer was assigned as a candidate target gene for downstream enrichment analysis.

Super-enhancer and integrated multi-omics analyses

Super-enhancers were identified from H3K27ac peak sets using ROSE v1.3.1 (Loven et al., 2013). Consensus super-enhancers were retained when detected in at least two samples and were compared between control and FLHS ovaries with DiffBind. Differential super-enhancers were defined using the same statistical framework applied to conventional peaks.

To connect changes in H3K27ac with gene expression, H3K27ac peak intensity was correlated with RNA-seq expression data. The peakGeneCor module in FindIT2 was used to identify peak-gene pairs with a Pearson correlation coefficient > 0.5 and P < 0.05 (Shang et al., 2022). Candidate regulatory genes were prioritized when differential H3K27ac signal and differential expression changed in the same direction and when their known functions were related to follicle growth, steroidogenesis, or ovarian tissue remodeling.

Motif, pathway, and statistical analyses

Motif enrichment analysis was conducted with HOMER v3.1 using findMotifsGenome.pl to identify transcription factor motifs enriched within differential H3K27ac regions (Heinz et al., 2010). Functional enrichment of genes associated with DAR and differentially expressed genes was performed against KEGG pathways. Phenotypic data are presented as means with dispersion measures as indicated in the figure legends. Differences between control and FLHS groups were considered statistically meaningful at P < 0.05 unless otherwise stated.

Results

HELP diet-induced FLHS was accompanied by reduced follicle development and laying performance

The HELP diet generated a phenotype consistent with FLHS, as confirmed by gross liver appearance and histological evaluation (Fig. 1A and B). Ovaries from FLHS hens contained visibly fewer developing follicles than those from control hens (Fig. 1C). Laying performance declined after the onset of HELP feeding. Across the experiment, control hens maintained an average laying rate of approximately 89.54%, whereas FLHS hens averaged approximately 52.14%, with the lowest daily value reaching 27.59% (Fig. 1E). Egg-quality measurements are provided in Table S2. Compared with the control group, the HELP group had lower average egg weight, eggshell thickness, and egg specific gravity. Eggshell strength and albumen pH did not differ significantly between groups. In contrast, Haugh unit and yolk color were higher in the HELP group (P < 0.01; Table S2). Together, the liver, ovary, follicle-count, laying-rate, and egg-quality phenotypes indicate that FLHS was associated with impaired follicular development and altered egg characteristics.

A total of 38,393 consensus H3K27ac peaks were detected in control ovaries and 40,142 were detected in FLHS ovaries. Annotation of these regions identified 10,395 promoter-associated and 27,998 enhancer-associated peaks in control ovaries, compared with 10,675 promoter-associated and 29,467 enhancer-associated peaks in FLHS ovaries. Most regulatory elements were shared between groups, including 73.7% of promoters and 67.7% of enhancers, whereas 1,390 promoters and 1,815 enhancers were unique to the control group and 1,630 promoters and 2,279 enhancers were unique to the FLHS group (Fig. 2B).

The genomic distribution of H3K27ac peaks was broadly similar between groups and was dominated by intragenic and intergenic regulatory regions (Fig. 2C). Metagene analysis showed pronounced H3K27ac enrichment near transcription start sites, as expected for an active chromatin mark, but FLHS ovaries displayed reduced average H3K27ac signal around both transcription start and transcription end regions (Fig. 2D). Differential analysis identified 2,111 regions with decreased H3K27ac and 1,707 regions with increased H3K27ac in FLHS ovaries, suggesting a net shift toward attenuation of active regulatory marks.

Genes associated with DAR were enriched in pathways related to oocyte meiosis, cell cycle regulation, adherens junctions, FoxO signaling, gonadotropin-releasing hormone signaling, and steroid hormone biosynthesis (Fig. 2E and Table S4). Motif analysis of differential H3K27ac regions identified enriched motifs corresponding to Fos, SF-1/NR5A1, and GATA-4 (Table S5), suggesting that these transcription factors may be associated with altered H3K27ac-marked regulatory regions in FLHS ovaries.

Integration of ovarian transcriptomes with H3K27ac highlighted folliculogenesis-related candidate genes

RNA-seq was used to define transcriptional changes associated with the ovarian phenotype. Heatmap and principal component analyses separated control and FLHS ovarian samples. Differential expression analysis identified 341 genes that differed between groups, including 62 upregulated and 279 downregulated genes in FLHS ovaries (Fig. 3A). Downregulated genes were enriched in steroid hormone biosynthesis and other pathways relevant to ovarian function (Fig. 3B and Table S6). The relationship between H3K27ac variation and gene expression was evaluated by integrating ChIP-seq and RNA-seq datasets. A quadrant-based analysis separated genes by direction of change in H3K27ac signal and transcript abundance (Fig. 3C). Among genes showing both reduced H3K27ac and lower mRNA expression, several were directly relevant to follicular growth or tissue remodeling. FGF1, FGF9, and MMP10 showed decreased H3K27ac enrichment at associated regulatory regions together with lower expression in FLHS ovaries (Fig. 3D). These concordant changes suggest that reduced enhancer or promoter activity may contribute to suppression of genes needed for follicle expansion, local signaling, and extracellular matrix remodeling.

FLHS was associated with loss of H3K27ac signal at ovarian super-enhancers

Because super-enhancers can coordinate high-level expression of genes that support tissue-specific functions (Hnisz et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2021), H3K27ac profiles were used to identify super-enhancers in control and FLHS ovaries. ROSE analysis detected 1,245 super-enhancers in control ovaries and 1,310 in FLHS ovaries (Fig. 4A and B). Two downregulated super-enhancers were located near genes with plausible roles in follicular cell growth and survival. A super-enhancer on chromosome 22 (481,027 to 481,526 bp) was adjacent to PCNA, which encodes proliferating cell nuclear antigen, a marker and regulator of DNA replication. Reduced H3K27ac at this region may be consistent with impaired granulosa cell proliferation or follicular maintenance. Another downregulated super-enhancer on chromosome 26 (3,238,727 to 3,239,226 bp) was located near RAP1A, a small GTPase involved in signal transduction pathways that influence cell growth and survival (Fig. 4D). These findings identify super-enhancer attenuation as a potential regulatory feature of ovarian dysfunction in FLHS.

Discussion

This study identifies ovarian chromatin remodeling as a candidate mechanism linking HELP diet-induced FLHS to reproductive decline in laying hens. Previous FLHS epigenomic studies have focused primarily on the liver, where histone modifications and enhancer activity are altered near genes involved in lipid metabolism (Cui et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). The present work extends that framework to the ovary and shows that the same nutritional syndrome is accompanied by reduced follicle numbers, lower laying performance, altered egg characteristics, and broad changes in H3K27ac-marked regulatory elements within reproductive tissue.

The observed decrease in SYF and HIE numbers indicates impairment at stages that are central to egg production. Follicular growth depends on endocrine support, nutrient transfer, granulosa cell proliferation, and local tissue remodeling. In laying hens, hepatic lipoprotein synthesis and ovarian uptake are highly coordinated, and disruption of lipid metabolism can affect yolk deposition and follicle hierarchy (Johnson et al., 2002; Walzem et al., 1999). Consistent with altered nutrient deposition and egg formation, HELP-fed hens had lower egg weight, eggshell thickness, and egg specific gravity. The higher yolk color may reflect dietary composition and pigment deposition rather than improved follicular function. Similarly, the higher Haugh unit should not be taken as evidence of improved reproductive status, because this index is calculated from albumen height together with egg weight and can be influenced by changes in egg size and albumen physical properties (Silversides and Villeneuve, 1994). Therefore, the apparently higher Haugh unit is not inconsistent with reduced follicle numbers and lower laying performance. A companion liver-focused study using the same HELP diet-induced FLHS pathological model reported enlarged, softened yellow-orange livers, marked hepatic fatty degeneration, lipid-droplet accumulation, increased serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), low density lipoprotein (LDL), total cholesterol (TCH), triglycerides (TG), and total bilirubin (T-BIL), decreased high density lipoprotein (HDL) (Cui et al., 2024). That study also showed that hepatic DEGs were enriched in fatty acid, amino acid, and carbohydrate metabolism, providing liver pathological, biochemical, and transcriptomic evidence for abnormal hepatic lipid metabolism in this model. These findings support an indirect liver-metabolism context for ovarian impairment in FLHS.

A major feature of the ovarian H3K27ac landscape in FLHS was the reduction of H3K27ac signal near gene bodies and regulatory regions, accompanied by more hypoacetylated than hyperacetylated DAR. Because H3K27ac marks active enhancers and promoters (Creyghton et al., 2010), this pattern is consistent with diminished activity of regulatory elements that support follicular function. Similar epigenomic studies support the relevance of chromatin-based regulation to follicular function. In chicken granulosa cells, folliculogenesis is accompanied by stage-dependent transcriptional divergence and reorganization of chromatin architecture, including rewiring of long-range promoter-enhancer interactions (Li et al., 2022). In developing chicken gonads, H3K27ac-marked active chromatin has also been linked to gonadal developmental gene expression, ovarian gene expression, and cell proliferation (Jiang et al., 2022). In mammalian granulosa cells, ovulatory signals rapidly remodel H3K27ac landscapes and regulate genes involved in ovulation, cumulus expansion, and follicular state transition (Jin et al., 2023). Together, these studies support the interpretation that reduced ovarian H3K27ac in FLHS reflects disruption of active regulatory programs required for follicular development rather than a purely descriptive chromatin change. In this study, DAR-associated genes were enriched in pathways including oocyte meiosis, cell cycle, FoxO signaling, gonadotropin-releasing hormone signaling, and steroid hormone biosynthesis. These pathways have direct relevance to follicular growth, granulosa cell function, and endocrine regulation. In mammalian follicles, FOXO3 contributes to granulosa cell apoptosis and follicular atresia (Matsuda et al., 2011), whereas in chickens FOXO3 has been implicated in control of primordial follicle activation and granulosa cell apoptosis (Cui et al., 2019). Gonadotropin-releasing hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone signaling also provide central endocrine inputs for follicle development (Hillier, 2001; Macklon and Fauser, 2001).

The integrated RNA-seq and ChIP-seq analyses prioritized genes with concordant decreases in H3K27ac and expression. FGF1 and FGF9 are members of the fibroblast growth factor family, which participates in ovarian cell proliferation, differentiation, and follicle development across species (Alan and Kulak, 2021; Drummond et al., 2007; Jiang and Price, 2012; Price, 2016). MMP10 belongs to the matrix metalloproteinase family, which is central to ovarian extracellular matrix turnover during follicle growth, ovulation, and tissue remodeling (Curry and Osteen, 2001, 2003; Fujihara et al., 2016; Peng et al., 2015; Smith et al., 2002). The RNA-seq dataset provided transcriptome-wide coverage with biological replication and standard differential-expression analysis. These features support their use as credible discovery-level candidates; however, they should be interpreted as candidate regulatory targets rather than experimentally validated effectors. Future studies will include qRT-PCR validation and functional assays to further confirm the expression patterns and biological roles of these candidate genes.

Changes in steroidogenic pathways further support a link between ovarian regulatory remodeling and reproductive dysfunction. Steroid hormone synthesis is required for follicular maturation and laying performance in poultry (Johnson et al., 2002; Lv et al., 2020). The downregulation of CYP21A in FLHS ovaries is notable because CYP21A-related steroidogenic activity has been implicated in granulosa cell development and hormone synthesis in chickens (You et al., 2024), and CYP21A expression has also been reported in reproductive tissues of mammalian models (Hu et al., 2007). Recent evidence from porcine granulosa cells also links non-coding RNA regulation and the chromatin-associated factor ASH1L to estradiol and progesterone synthesis (Zhang et al., 2025). These findings suggest that FLHS may weaken ovarian steroidogenic potential at both the transcriptional and chromatin levels.

Super-enhancer analysis identified another layer of regulatory disruption. Most differential super-enhancers in FLHS ovaries showed reduced H3K27ac signal, including regions near PCNA and RAP1A. PCNA is widely used as a marker of proliferating cells and supports DNA replication and repair; in ovarian tissue, PCNA abundance is associated with folliculogenesis and cell proliferation (Vatanparast et al., 2021). RAP1A participates in MAPK-related signaling and inflammatory or survival responses (Dorn et al., 2012). Reduced super-enhancer activity near these genes may contribute to decreased cellular proliferation and resilience within developing follicles.

Motif enrichment implicated Fos, SF-1/NR5A1, and GATA-4 as candidate regulators of differential H3K27ac regions. These transcription factors have established or emerging roles in gonadal development, steroidogenesis, ovulatory gene expression, and endocrine signaling (Bielinska et al., 2003; Kouri et al., 2025; Southall et al., 2025; Suntharalingham et al., 2015). However, this analysis was performed only on differential H3K27ac regions and was not designed to establish direct transcription factor-target gene relationships. Therefore, Fos, SF-1/NR5A1, and GATA-4 should be interpreted as candidate upstream regulators of altered H3K27ac landscapes rather than validated transcription factor-target gene links. These candidate regulatory relationships provide a basis for future experimental validation. Together, these findings support a candidate model in which HELP diet-induced FLHS attenuates ovarian H3K27ac-marked enhancers and super-enhancers, leading to reduced expression of genes involved in folliculogenesis, steroidogenesis, extracellular matrix remodeling, and cell proliferation, ultimately contributing to impaired follicular development and laying performance (Fig. 5).Fig. 5Schematic model depicting a candidate H3K27ac-mediated regulatory framework underlying FLHS-associated ovarian dysfunction in laying hens.Fig 5 dummy alt text

Conclusions

HELP diet-induced FLHS was associated with impaired follicular development, reduced laying performance, and extensive remodeling of H3K27ac-marked regulatory elements in the ovary. Integrated epigenomic and transcriptomic analyses prioritized candidate genes with reduced H3K27ac and expression that are involved in folliculogenesis, tissue remodeling, steroidogenesis, and cell proliferation, including FGF1, FGF9, MMP10, CYP21A, PCNA, and RAP1A. These results support a model in which metabolic stress attenuates ovarian enhancer and super-enhancer activity, thereby contributing to reproductive dysfunction in laying hens with FLHS. The findings distinguish ovarian regulatory disruption from previously reported hepatic epigenomic responses and provide candidate loci for future molecular validation and intervention studies.

Improving admission clerking standards and physical health assessments in febrile patients: A prospective clinical audit at a tertiary care hospital
Authors: Prakash Velmurugan, Swathika Annamalai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Clinical audit is an effective tool to improve patient care, which in turn has an impact on clinical outcome.Patients who present with fever usually are generally admitted based on competency-based criteria, where admission is determined on the severity of the illness assessed through clinical and laboratory findings. Bacterial infections were linked to high sepsis-related mortality. This highlights the importance of comprehensive and standardized admission clerking and physical assessment for patients presenting with fever. This audit aimed to evaluate the documentation of history, physical examination, and investigations in febrile patients by postgraduate trainees at a tertiary care hospital in South India.Methods:A prospective audit of 50 case records of patients admitted with fever was conducted over a four-week period in the medical wards. A predefined pro forma was used to review key domains, including documentation of admission details, clinical history, physical examination (vital signs and systemic examination), and relevant investigations. Findings were measured against departmental standards and analyzed to identify areas for improvement.Results:Documentation completeness by the post graduate trainee’s completeness varied across domains. Admission details were documented in 86% of cases, and comprehensive history was recorded in 72%. While vital signs were documented well (>90%), systemic examination showed significant variability (CVS: 80% vs CNS: 66%). Documentation of specific investigations was inconsistent: dengue serology (62%), WIDAL test or blood culture (54%), and Scrub typhus serology (38%).Conclusion:The clinical audit revealed notable variability in admission documentation by postgraduate trainees, particularly in the systemic examination and investigation domains. The findings highlight the need for targeted educational interventions and reinforcement of documentation protocols to enhance patient assessment and care, particularly in febrile patients, where inadequate management can lead to high case fatality.

Introduction

The clinical audit process can be described by “Plan,” “Do,” “Study,” “Act” phases that comprise an audit cycle. Audits are usually conducted either local (clinic or hospital level) and national level.[1]

Hospital based admissions are usually either a competency-based admission or holistic admission. Competency based admission focuses on certain criteria that the patient must fulfill for admission, whereas holistic admission deals with the comprehensive evaluation of the patient.[2]

Patients who present with fever usually are usually competency-based admission where they are admitted based on severity of the fever based on clinical and laboratory findings.[2]

A fever cohort from South India, a high incidence of bacterial infections and high case fatality rate due to sepsis were observed.[3]

In a study in South India despite extensive evaluation 17.5% of patients with fever the cause could not be identified so making etiological diagnosis based on serological testing has to be dealt with caution.[4]

Fever is one of the main causes of hospitalization in young children and it can have an impact on the finances in low-income Indian households,[5] which in turn can have an impact on the mental health of the affected household.

This shows the impact of the fever on both the physical and mental health of both the patient and the family emphasizing the importance of proper assessment and clerking of the history and physical examination during admissions that can improve the likelihood of arriving early to the accurate diagnosis modifying the clinical outcome and decreasing the hospital stay, which can have an impact on the health care expenditure.

The study aimed to benefit practitioners through targeted educational interventions, improved training protocols, and standardized practices, and to benefit patients by enhancing safety, improving continuity of care, and leading to more efficient and accurate care.

Despite institutional guidelines, compliance among trainees can vary, affecting the quality of patient care. This audit was conducted to assess the documentation standards among postgraduate trainees for febrile patients admitted to the medical wards at a tertiary care hospital in South India.

Discussion

This audit highlights significant variability in the documentation practices of postgraduate trainees during the admission of febrile patients. While basic clinical parameters such as temperature, pulse, and blood pressure were consistently recorded, deficiencies were noted in systemic examinations and the documentation of relevant investigations.

The relatively high compliance with vital signs recording suggests a baseline awareness of their clinical importance. However, inconsistent documentation of systemic examinations particularly central nervous system findings (66%) and low recording of ECG findings (34%) and serological tests (e.g., scrub typhus, 38%) indicate lapses that could affect diagnostic accuracy and continuity of care.

Several potential contributing factors were identified, including lack of structured documentation templates, insufficient supervision, and time constraints. These findings are consistent with previous studies that highlight the need for structured admission proforma and targeted training to enhance documentation quality.

The implementation of standardized clerking checklists and periodic training sessions could help address the observed gaps. In addition, embedding regular audits into departmental quality assurance processes may promote accountability and long-term improvement.

Limitations

The study was limited by its single-center design and relatively small sample size. Verbal consent was obtained, and no patient-identifying information was accessed; however, a lack of formal ethical review may limit the reproducibility of the audit model across institutions.

Conclusion

The audit revealed considerable inconsistencies in the documentation of febrile patient admissions, especially in systemic examination findings and investigations. Interventions such as structured templates, academic reinforcement, and regular monitoring are essential to improve documentation practices among postgraduate trainees.

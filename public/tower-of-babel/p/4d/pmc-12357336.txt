Telehealth services for acute diseases management: principles, trends and challenges in selected health facilities in the greater Accra region, Ghana
Authors: Jonathan Kissi, Godwin Adzakpah, Caleb Annobil, Nathan Kamasenu Mensah, Daniel Kwame Kwansah Quansah, Kennedy Dzamvivie, Vivian Kruh, Grace Amoah-Anomah, Abdul-Basit Kassim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe health-related Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 3 have welcomed the introduction of technology into healthcare delivery, desalinating the accessibility to healthcare delivery. With the recent outbreak of the COVID-19, telehealth services have been the preferred means of service delivery to a wider population in settings where the physician-to-patient ratio is low. The study’s objective is to investigate the principles, trends and challenges of using telehealth services for acute disease management in some hospitals within the Greater Accra region of Ghana.MethodsA cross-sectional, descriptive quantitative study was conducted among healthcare professionals across four government health facilities in the Greater Accra Region: Ada East District Hospital, Weija Gbawe Municipal Hospital, Oblogo Health Centre, and LEKMA Hospital. A structured questionnaire grounded in the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) framework was administered using a simple random sampling technique. A total of 439 participants, representing approximately 70% of the eligible population (n = 628), who actively use electronic health records systems, were recruited. Data were analysed using Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) with STATA Version 15 and SPSS Version 23.ResultsIn a cohort of 439 healthcare professionals surveyed, the majority fell within the 21–40 age range, and women made up nearly 60% of the participants. Most respondents held a bachelor’s degree, with nurses, medical officers, and physician assistants forming the largest professional groups. Telehealth services were primarily used through phone calls, while fewer professionals used video conferencing or store-and-forward technologies. Overall, the frequency of telehealth service use was low, with many reporting that they used it rarely or only occasionally. The most common purpose for telehealth consultations was general care, followed by patient education and a smaller share for surgical consultations. Confirmatory factor analysis showed acceptable model fit supporting the validity of the measurement framework.ConclusionThe study found that healthcare professionals’ satisfaction with telehealth services was most strongly influenced by how useful they perceived it to be. Telehealth services that were easier to use not only increased user satisfaction but also enhanced the perceived value of the tools. Interestingly, those who perceived higher levels of risk still tended to find the systems useful, although their overall satisfaction was slightly lower. Changes within healthcare organizations also had mixed effects, lowering perceptions of usefulness in some cases but slightly improving satisfaction. These findings suggest that strengthening system support, infrastructure, and aligning telehealth tools with clinical tasks can significantly encourage adoption in settings with limited resources.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12875-025-02961-4.Keywords: Telehealth services, Acute diseases management, Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), Technology acceptance model (TAM), Healthcare professionals satisfaction

Introduction

Quality healthcare delivery in low- and middle-income nations is a subject of concern. Patients in Ghana’s rural areas encounter a variety of challenges, including inadequate medical facilities, shortage of healthcare staff, and expensive transportation costs, all of which contribute to high rates of morbidity and mortality from treatable diseases [1]. Telehealth allows doctors to treat patients remotely, enables access to medical services that might otherwise be unavailable, and eliminates distance constraints [2]. Telehealth services are becoming increasingly popular for healthcare delivery because they connect patients with specialized healthcare professionals and improve healthcare access in rural areas [3]. Given the growing demand for specialized services and limited resources, telehealth appears to be a promising way to increase patient access to high-quality disease management, reduce costs and burden of travel for patients, and provide clinicians with flexible, low-cost practice opportunities [4].

Telehealth approaches use digital technologies to centralize medical knowledge and assist front-line healthcare personnel. At teleconsultation facilities in referral hospitals, experienced medical professionals advise community health workers, thereby improving patient care quality. The technique empowers healthcare personnel and raises service quality, leading to better patient outcomes. Furthermore, information and communication technology (ICT) is rapidly transforming global commerce, social connections, and medical research by improving and fostering better education and healthcare governance [5]. Particularly in industrialized countries, telehealth has gained widespread adoption and use due to its immense benefits in enhancing healthcare and quality of life [6]. Although recent trends indicate that the rate and extent of distribution are increasing, telehealth adoption and diffusion have been slower than many anticipated, despite their potential [7].

The recent COVID-19 epidemic forced many doctors to immediately switch to telehealth, which includes phone and video appointments, to prevent patient care delays [8]. This unforeseen necessity presented a chance to tackle numerous inadequacies of the conventional office visit, particularly the inadequate management of many patients’ acute illnesses and the low adherence to prescriptions among patients with chronic medical conditions. Given the scarcity of data on COVID-19 outcomes in previously diagnosed patients with acute disease, it is plausible to anticipate that this patient population is likely to experience catastrophic consequences. Individuals with acute illnesses who are receiving treatment have a weaker immune system, and those who are also chronically ill are more susceptible to infections [9].

In the context of Ghana, the National Ghana Telehealth Program has linked community health workers to specialists through round-the-clock teleconsultation centers. This initiative has resulted in a 31% reduction in unnecessary referrals and has lowered healthcare costs for patients in Ghana [10]. In the Greater Accra Region, a randomized trial demonstrated that telehealth follow-up after childbirth significantly reduced maternal and infant morbidity. Maternal morbidity occurred in 27.5% of women who received telephone-based postnatal care, compared to 38.5% of those who received traditional care (p = 0.02). Both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses showed a 30% lower risk of maternal illness among participants who received telephone-based postnatal care (ITT: RR = 0.7, 95% CI 0.54–0.95; PP: RR = 0.7, 95% CI 0.51–0.94) [11].

Conversely, a survey of clinicians in the Ashanti Region highlighted key barriers to telehealth implementation. Inconsistent access to mobile devices and inadequate training of health professionals were found to limit the routine use of mobile tools for acute diagnostics and treatment support in the region [12]. Additionally, sustainable financing and equitable access remain persistent challenges in scaling telehealth solutions for acute care across Ghana [10].

Most telehealth efforts in Ghana have primarily relied on store-and-forward technology. Its low bandwidth requirements and use of simple messaging platforms make it both cost-effective and convenient for patients and specialists. However, only a limited number of programs have adopted real-time interactive modalities such as VoIP and video calls. The scalability of these technologies is constrained by the limited availability of high-speed internet in rural areas and the higher costs associated with specialized software and hardware [13].

With the current advancement in healthcare delivery and bridging the gap in service delivery to promote universal assessment, telehealth has become the most effective means for service delivery. Inspired by this, the research aims to investigate the principles, trends, and challenges of using telehealth for acute disease management in three districts within the Greater Accra region; Damgbe East District (Damgbe East District Hospital), Ledzokuku Municipal (LEKMA Hospital), and Weija Gbawe Municipal (Weija Gbawe Municipal Hospital and Oblogo Health Centre).

Theoretical background

Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Research hypotheses.

The hypothesis by Davis [14] is a well-known and well-accepted theory used to understand how new technologies and improvements in information and technology systems are accepted. Three critical elements are known to predict, explain, and govern acceptance in early TAM research [15]. They include the applicability, disposition, and use of cutting-edge technology. Researchers claim that to create the initial TAM model, its creators modified the theory of reasoned action, a general behavioural and social-psychological theory. Introductory research was undertaken to ascertain which variables were appropriate to be taken into account while analysing the behaviour of Information Technology (IT) usage [16]. To support the idea, researchers ultimately developed the TAM model. Scores of researchers have used TAM in a variety of fields, including healthcare, e-learning systems, credit cards, mobile technology, customer service, and so forth. Similarly, research by Kissi et al. [17], also postulated how health professionals are satisfied with the use of telehealth systems, and its perception was based on the systems’ use and simplicity [18]. TAM has also been improved and expanded by other studies by the addition of variables that could facilitate its acceptance of technological advancements. For instance, components from the TAM and TAM2 theories were integrated into Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) [19].

To gauge the satisfaction of health professionals using telehealth services for treating acute conditions, our current study applied the TAM constructs of perceived usefulness (PERUSE), perceived ease of use (PEAOUS) and added additional constructs (Healthcare Professionals Satisfaction (HEPSTAT), Perceived Risks (PERISK) and Perceived Challenges (PERCHAN). These constructs define the dangers and challenges that healthcare providers may expect while attempting to employ telehealth services for treating acute diseases. Methods

Study design

This study adopted a cross-sectional basic descriptive quantitative design to comprehensively examine the principles, trends, and challenges associated with the application of telehealth technologies for acute care within selected healthcare facilities. The cross-sectional design was deemed appropriate given its ability to capture a detailed snapshot of healthcare professionals’ experiences, practices, and perceptions at a single point in time. The descriptive nature of the study allowed for the systematic documentation and analysis of relevant variables without manipulating the study environment. By employing this methodological approach, the study aimed to generate generalizable insights pertinent to the evolving role of telehealth in acute care settings.

Setting

The research was conducted within three administrative areas of the Greater Accra Region of Ghana, specifically: the Ada East District, Ledzokuku Municipality, and Weija-Gbawe Municipality. Data were collected from four health institutions: Dangme East District Hospital, LEKMA Hospital, Weija Gbawe Municipal Hospital, and Oblogo Health Centre.

Dangme East District Hospital is situated in the Eastern part of the Greater Accra Region within the Ada East District. The total land area of the district is 289.783 sq. km. The north and east of the district shares a border with the North Tongu District. The southern part of the district is bordered by the Gulf of Guinea. The district had a population of 71,671 according to the 2010 Population and Housing Census. There are 5 Health facilities in the district. The Dangme East district hospital serves as the referral hospital for the populace within the catchment area. The hospital has a bed capacity of 102 which recorded 28,575 Outpatient cases and 4,815 inpatient cases in 2022.

LEKMA Hospital is located within the Ledzokuku Municipal, and has Teshie as its capital. The Municipal has a total land area of about 47.57510 sq. km. (City population, 2022).

The south of the Ledzokuku Municipality is the Gulf of Guinea, and to the west side is La Dade Kotopon Municipal, with the eastern and northern parts sharing boundaries with Tema Metropolitan and Ashaiman Municipal respectively (City population, 2022). The Municipality has a population of 217,304 with 106,099(48.8%) males and 111,205(51.2%) females according to the 2021 Population and Housing Census. There are 14 Health facilities in the district. The LEKMA Hospital serves as the referral hospital for the populace within the Ledzokuku Municipality. The hospital has a bed capacity of 100 which recorded 44,636 Outpatient cases and 5,371 inpatient cases in 2022.

Weija-Gbawe Municipal Hospital and Oblogo Health centres are located within the Weija-Gbawe Municipality in the South-Western part of Accra, resting in the Latitudes 5°47’30” N and 5°27’30"N and Longitudes 0°31’30” W and 0°16’30"W. The Weija-Gbawe Municipality inhabits a land area of a size that is approximately 502.31 sq. km. The Municipality shares a boundary with Ga-South to the north and Ga-Central to the east. The northern boundary shared by the municipal and Ga-South also extends to the south of the Gulf of Guinea, the municipality had about 213,674 populaces according to the 2010 Population and Housing Census (PHC). The Municipality has three sub-municipals namely Mallam, Oblogo, and Weija. It has 29 health facilities scattered across these 3 sub-municipals. The Weija-Gbawe Municipal Hospital serves as the referral hospital for the populace within the area. The hospital is a 90-bed capacity which recorded 47,115 Outpatient cases and 7169 inpatient cases in 2022.

Participants

Eligible participants for this study comprised healthcare personnel actively utilizing electronic health record (EHR) systems across the selected facilities. Inclusion criteria required participants to be employed in the target hospitals and health centres, willing to voluntarily partake in the study, and able to provide informed written consent. Participants were selected through a simple random sampling technique to minimize selection bias and ensure a representative distribution across professional cadres. Prior to participation, the study’s objectives, procedures, and ethical considerations were clearly explained, and confidentiality was assured. Healthcare workers who declined consent or were unavailable during data collection were excluded from the sample. This strategy allowed for the inclusion of a diverse cohort encompassing various professional categories, including nurses, medical officers, laboratory technologists, and physician assistants.

Variables

The study’s dependent variables comprised three telehealth-specific outcomes: (1) self-reported knowledge of telehealth principles, (2) frequency of observed telehealth trends in acute care delivery, and (3) perceived challenges encountered during telehealth use. Each outcome was measured using structured questionnaire items on a five-point Likert scale. The independent variables included demographic characteristics; age (categorized as < 21, 21–30, 31–40, 41–50, 51–60, and > 61 years), sex (male/female), educational attainment (Certificate (Technical college), Bachelor’s degree, Postgraduate degree (Master’s/Doctoral), Professional qualification, Others (‘O’ and ‘A’ Levels), and profession (Nurse, Physician Assistant, Medical Officer, Lab Technician), as well as exposure-related predictors such as prior telehealth training (yes/no). Professional rank and training exposure were also examined as potential confounders. Because the investigation focused on attitudinal and experiential data rather than clinical diagnoses, no diagnostic criteria applied. This clear specification and categorical coding of both dependent and independent variables ensured consistency in data capture and laid the groundwork for robust descriptive and inferential analyses. The principal variables of interest encompassed demographic characteristics (age, sex, educational attainment, profession) and telehealth-specific indicators, including knowledge of telehealth principles, observed trends, and perceived challenges in acute care delivery. Outcomes were operationalized based on structured questionnaire items, with demographic variables categorized into predefined groups for analytical clarity. Predictors and potential confounders such as profession and prior exposure to telehealth training were acknowledged. Diagnostic criteria were not applicable, given the non-clinical focus of the investigation. The structured operationalization of variables ensured consistency in data capture and facilitated robust descriptive analyses.

Data sources and measurement

Data collection was achieved through the administration of a structured, pretested questionnaire developed by the research team. The instrument was initially piloted at Cape Coast Teaching Hospital, a facility with comparable organizational characteristics but outside the primary study setting, to assess clarity, relevance, and reliability. Based on pretest feedback, selected items were rephrased to enhance contextual relevance and respondent comprehension. Variables pertaining to demographics, telehealth practices, and perceived barriers were measured using closed-ended items with categorical response options. Consistency in administration and the use of a standardized tool across all sites ensured comparability of measurements and minimized information bias.

Bias

Multiple strategies were employed to mitigate potential biases. Selection bias was addressed through simple random sampling, which provided each eligible healthcare worker an equal probability of inclusion. Measurement bias was minimized through the development and pretesting of the data collection instrument to ensure contextual relevance and comprehensibility. Social desirability bias was mitigated by assuring participants of the confidentiality and anonymity of their responses. Additionally, interviewers were trained to maintain a neutral stance during questionnaire administration to avoid influencing participants’ responses.

Study size

The study selected a sample size of 439 healthcare professionals, representing approximately 70% of the total population (628). The determination of this sample size was not based on an a priori statistical power analysis or effect size calculation. This decision was informed by the common practice in organizational health and safety research, where various studies employed different population percentages [20–22]. Such approaches are grounded in the assumption of a normal sample distribution, which is typically used as the basis for computing effect sizes a priori [20]. However, as normality could not be established prior to data collection, power analysis and effect size calculations were not conducted in the present study [21]. Additionally, research within organizational settings commonly considers 70% of the population to be an adequate sample size [22, 23]. Given this, the sample of 439 participants is deemed representative of the population, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of the study’s findings [24].

Quantitative variables

Quantitative variables were categorized into logical groupings to facilitate descriptive statistical analysis. Age was grouped into six categories: less than 21 years, 21–30 years, 31–40 years, 41–50 years, 51–60 years, and over 61 years. Educational attainment was categorized as Certificate, Bachelor’s Degree, Postgraduate Degree, Professional Qualification, or Other. Professions included classifications such as Nurse, Physician Assistant, Medical Officer, and Laboratory Technician. Categorical groupings allowed for easier interpretation of trends across sociodemographic strata and aligned with common reporting standards within health services research.

Statistical methods

Data analysis was performed using STATA Version 15 and SPSS Version 23. Descriptive statistics, including frequencies and percentages, were utilized to summarize categorical variables and characterize the study population. No inferential statistical analyses were conducted given the study’s primary descriptive objectives. Missing data were minimal and handled through case-wise deletion without imputation. Analytical strategies accounted for the simple random sampling technique employed by calculating descriptive statistics based on the full sample. Sensitivity analyses were not performed due to the descriptive nature of the study and the completeness of the dataset. Future investigations employing inferential designs may incorporate multivariable regression analyses to control for potential confounders and to explore interaction effects among key variables.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

Ethical approval for the study was granted by the Weija-Gbawe Municipal Health Directorate, under permission number GHS/WGMHD/ADM-22. Weija-Gbawe, a historically significant district that originated from Ga South, is home to a well-regarded district hospital within the nation’s metropolitan area, which provides teleconsultation services for patient care. Prior to data collection, permission to conduct the study was obtained from the health management teams of the selected districts and sub-districts. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants after a thorough explanation of the study’s objectives. Participants were provided with the consent forms one week before data collection to allow ample time for review and understanding.

Results

Demographics of respondents

The study sample comprised 439 healthcare professionals. The modal age cohorts were 21–30 years (36.2%) and 31–40 years (35.3%), whereas participants younger than 21 years and older than 61 years represented only 2.7% and 3.9% of the cohort, respectively. Health professionals who are under 21 years correspond primarily to diploma-level nurses working under contract as National Service Personnel. Those aged 41–50 years accounted for 17.3%, and individuals aged 51–60 years constituted 4.6%. Female respondents predominated (59.5%), with males comprising 40.5% of the sample. With regard to educational attainment, nearly half of the participants held a bachelor’s degree (43.5%), while 15.7% had completed a postgraduate program (master’s or doctoral level). A further 19.8% possessed professional qualifications; 10.0% reported certificate-level training (high school or technical college), and 10.9% fell into other certification categories. Professionally, nurses represented the largest subgroup (32.6%), followed by medical officers (15.7%), physician assistants (14.6%), and laboratory technicians (7.1%); the remainder were distributed across various allied health cadres. Goodness of fit indices for scale validity tests

Table 3 summarizes the fit statistics for the confirmatory factor analysis of the telehealth scale. Although the chi-square test was significant (χ²(163) = 463.614, p < 0.0001), the normed chi-square (χ²/df = 2.84) fell well within the acceptable range (3 < χ²/df < 5), indicating reasonable discrepancy between the model and data. The RMSEA of 0.065 likewise satisfied the criterion for acceptable fit (0 < RMSEA < 0.08). Information-theoretic indices were moderate (AIC = 13 869.737; BIC = 14 143.399), reflecting model parsimony relative to alternatives. Incremental fit indices further corroborated model adequacy: the Comparative Fit Index (CFI = 0.968) exceeded the threshold for good fit (> 0.95), and the Tucker–Lewis Index (TLI = 0.962) surpassed the acceptable lower bound (> 0.90). Collectively, these results demonstrate that the hypothesized measurement model exhibits an acceptable to good fit to the observed data.

Indirect effect

All mediated (indirect) pathways through intention to use were negligible and failed to attain statistical significance. The indirect effect of PEOUS on health-status improvement via PERUSE was β = 0.003 (SE = 0.004; p = 0.416; 95% CI [–0.004, 0.104]); that of PERISK was β = 0.001 (SE = 0.003; p = 0.619; 95% CI [–0.004, 0.007]); and that of PERCHAN was β = 0.001 (SE = 0.003; p = 0.790; 95% CI [–0.006, 0.004]).

Total effects

When direct and indirect contributions are summed, the overall impact mirrors the direct-effect pattern. PEOUS exerted a significant total effect on health status (β = 0.22, SE = 0.06, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.13, 0.31]), as did PERCHAN (β = 0.13, SE = 0.05, p = 0.005, 95% CI [0.04, 0.22]). By contrast, the total effects of PERISK on health status (β = 0.03, SE = 0.05, p = 0.565, 95% CI [–0.12, 0.07]) and of all predictors on intention to use (all p > 0.05) were non-significant.

Structural equation modelling path diagram and latent variables

Structural equation modelling demonstrated that perceived usefulness (PERUSE) emerged as the strongest predictor of health-professional satisfaction (HEPSTAT; β = 0.65, p < 0.05), with the model explaining 58% of its variance, while PERUSE itself was accounted for to 77% by its antecedents. Perceived ease of use (PEAOUS; R² = 0.68) had both a substantial indirect effect on satisfaction via PERUSE (β = 0.67, p < 0.05) and a smaller direct effect (β = 0.22, p < 0.05) (see Fig. 2). Contrary to expectation, perceived risk (PERISK; R² = 0.74) positively influenced PERUSE (β = 0.31, p < 0.05) but exerted a modest negative direct impact on satisfaction (β = − 0.03, p < 0.001). Perceived change (PERCHAN; R² = 0.75) slightly undermined PERUSE (β = − 0.02, p < 0.001) while concurrently producing a modest positive direct effect on HEPSTAT (β = 0.13, p < 0.05) (see Fig. 2). The overall model fit was acceptable (χ²(163) = 463.61, p < 0.0001) and together these paths accounted for 99.99% of the variance in the endogenous constructs.

Discussion

Synthesis of key findings

This study investigated determinants of health-professional satisfaction (HEPSTAT) with telehealth for acute disease management, integrating Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) constructs (perceived ease of use, PEAOUS; perceived usefulness, PERUSE) alongside perceptions of risk (PERISK) and change (PERCHAN). The sample (n = 439) was predominantly female (59.5%), aged 21–40 years (69.5%), held at least a bachelor’s degree (43.5%), and comprised mainly nurses (32.6%), reflecting the front-line workforce most engaged in telehealth services. Direct phone consultations dominated usage (79.5%), whereas video conferencing (15.7%) and store-and-forward methods (4.8%) were less common, an indication of infrastructural and workflow constraints in low-resource settings. Usage tended toward infrequent engagement, with 35.1% reporting rare use, underscoring persistent barriers to routine telehealth uptake despite its potential to extend care access. Our measurement model exhibited acceptable fit (χ²/df = 2.84; RMSEA = 0.065; CFI = 0.968; TLI = 0.962), confirming construct validity for the extended TAM framework in this context. Structural paths revealed that PERUSE was the most salient driver of HEPSTAT (β = 0.65, p < 0.05), explaining 58% of satisfaction variance. This underscores the primacy of perceived task-relevance and outcome expectancy in shaping professional endorsement of telehealth platforms, consistent with extant TAM research (e.g., Davis [14]),. PEAOUS exerted its influence both indirectly (strongly enhancing PERUSE (β = 0.67, p < 0.05; PERUSE R² = 0.77)) and directly on HEPSTAT (β = 0.22, p < 0.05), indicating that intuitive system design fosters both perceptions of utility and immediate satisfaction. For practitioners operating under time and resource pressures, streamlined user interfaces and minimal training requirements remain critical for sustained telehealth integration. Intriguingly, PERISK demonstrated a dual effect: a moderate positive association with PERUSE (β = 0.31, p < 0.05) but a small negative direct impact on satisfaction (β = − 0.03, p < 0.001). The counterintuitive positive link to usefulness may reflect a “risk-reward” heuristic, whereby professionals perceiving higher operational or clinical risk nevertheless recognise telehealth’s utility in mitigating exposure or travel burdens, particularly salient during infectious disease outbreaks. However, heightened risk awareness simultaneously erodes overall satisfaction, likely through anxiety over data security, diagnostic accuracy, or medico-legal liability. Targeted risk-management strategies like robust encryption, standardized clinical protocols, and clear indemnity frameworks are therefore essential to bolster confidence without dampening the perceived value. Perceived change (PERCHAN) also exhibited a complex pattern: a marginal negative effect on PERUSE (β = − 0.02, p < 0.001) but a modest positive direct influence on HEPSTAT (β = 0.13, p < 0.05). This suggests that while rapid organizational change can temporarily undermine beliefs about system efficacy, perhaps due to adjustment fatigue or workflow disruption, the very act of modernisation conveys institutional commitment to innovation, cultivating a forward-looking mindset and enhancing professional satisfaction. Change management initiatives that emphasize participatory decision-making, phased roll-outs, and peer champions may amplify this positive sentiment while minimizing transitional friction. Demographically, the predominance of nurses and mid-career professionals (31–40 years) highlights the need for role-tailored training modules and decision-support tools that could further elevate perceived usefulness and engagement. The reliance on voice calls over video or asynchronous models underscores infrastructural bottlenecks such as limited broadband penetration and hardware scarcity; investments in network capacity and scalable platforms could shift usage patterns toward richer, multimodal telehealth interactions. Our findings suggest that telehealth implementation strategies should prioritize system usability and demonstrable clinical utility, while actively addressing perceived risks and managing organizational change. By aligning platform design with frontline workflows, offering context-specific training, and fostering transparent risk-mitigation policies, health systems can enhance professional satisfaction and, by extension, service quality and adoption rates. Integrating user feedback loops and performance dashboards may further reinforce positive perceptions, driving sustained telehealth integration in acute care delivery.

Comparative insights and literature integration

Integrating our model findings with Ghana’s telehealth experience highlights both convergence and contextual nuances. Consistent with the National Ghana Telehealth Program’s 31% reduction in unnecessary referrals and lower patient costs [10], our demonstration that perceived usefulness (PERUSE) is the strongest driver of professional satisfaction (β = 0.65, p < 0.05) underscores the centrality of demonstrable clinical benefit in securing buy-in; when community health workers see clear service-delivery gains, they rate systems as more useful and, in turn, more satisfying. Likewise, the Greater Accra postnatal care trial, in which telephone follow-up cut maternal morbidity from 38.5 to 27.5% (RR = 0.7, p = 0.02), mirrors our finding that direct phone consultation (79.5% usage) yields high perceived ease of use (PEAOUS) and satisfaction (direct β = 0.22, p < 0.05), reflecting how low-barrier modalities align with frontline workflows in low-resource settings [11]. However, Ghanaian barriers namely; inconsistent mobile device access, inadequate training [12], and unstable financing and equity challenges [10], map onto our risk (PERISK) and change (PERCHAN) constructs. The modest negative direct effect of PERISK on satisfaction (β = − 0.03, p < 0.001) suggests that anxieties around data security or clinical liability dampen endorsement even when usefulness is recognized, paralleling clinicians’ reported reservations in Ashanti Region. Meanwhile, our observation that PERCHAN slightly undermines usefulness (β = − 0.02, p < 0.001) yet boosts satisfaction (β = 0.13, p < 0.05) speaks to Ghana’s experience with phased telehealth roll-outs, where incremental exposure to store-and-forward platforms (the predominant, low-bandwidth modality) fosters an innovative culture despite transitional friction. The limited scalability of real-time interactive modalities (VoIP, video) due to rural broadband scarcity further reinforces the importance of designing systems that optimize ease of use under infrastructural constraints [13]. Collectively, these parallels affirm that enhancing perceived usefulness and ease of use, through robust training, dependable device access, and risk-mitigation protocols, will be critical for Ghana’s telehealth scale-up, while strategic change management can harness positive sentiments toward innovation despite implementation challenges.

In other resource-constrained African settings such as rural Uganda and parts of Nigeria, intermittent power outages and network blackouts are endemic [25, 26]. Our structural model’s finding that perceived ease of use (PEAOUS; R² = 0.68) exerts both a strong indirect effect on perceived usefulness (β = 0.67, p < 0.05) and a direct effect on satisfaction (β = 0.22, p < 0.05) explains why clinicians prioritize uninterrupted consultations over abstract performance gains. In these contexts, store-and-forward modalities dominate because of low bandwidth requirements, while real-time video or VoIP calls remain limited by connectivity and hardware costs [17, 27, 28]. Consequently, usability and barrier removal take precedence: even small frictions, restarting a stalled session, or navigating complex login procedures can undermine clinician satisfaction more than marginal productivity improvements [27, 29, 30].

Our structural model’s emphasis on ease of use and barrier removal diverges sharply from patterns observed in high-income countries (HICs), where mature IT infrastructures and dedicated helpdesk support render basic usability concerns negligible [31]. In such environments, clinicians typically assume seamless connectivity and reliable hardware, so perceived usefulness naturally dominates. Telehealth uptake hinges on questions like “How much will this streamline workflow or integrate advanced decision-support?” rather than “Will it function now?” [32]. The primacy of usefulness in HICs aligns with numerous studies demonstrating that, once facilitating conditions are assured, effort expectancy and performance expectancy drive acceptance [18, 33].

Together, these patterns underscore that telehealth solutions successful in HICs cannot simply be transplanted into African jurisdictions without adaptation. Effective implementation demands low-bandwidth–optimized interfaces, offline-first applications that auto-synchronize upon reconnection, and streamlined authentication flows. Equally essential are organizational protocols, dedicated “super-user” support teams at facility level, clear procedures for handling dropped calls, and training clinicians to switch modalities seamlessly when one fails [28, 34]. Only by aligning technology design with local infrastructure realities and support structures can telehealth initiatives achieve sustained acceptance across diverse resource-limited settings.

Theoretical implications

Our results indicate that in resource-constrained health settings, the classical Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) requires context‐sensitive refinement. Whereas traditional TAM posits perceived usefulness as the primary antecedent of acceptance [14, 32], we find that perceived ease of use and the removal of infrastructural and procedural barriers exerts a more pronounced influence on both perceived usefulness (β = 0.67, p < 0.05) and overall satisfaction (β = 0.22, p < 0.05). This pattern resonates more closely with UTAUT’s constructs of effort expectancy and facilitating conditions [19, 33], suggesting that when clinicians operate under precarious network and equipment constraints, their primary concern is “Will it function now?” rather than “How much will it improve performance?” [31]. Even minor usability frictions such as delayed logins or intermittent connectivity can eclipse more distal productivity gains [35], underscoring socio‐technical theory’s assertion that technology acceptance emerges from the interplay of user competencies, organizational supports, and technical affordances [36, 37]. To better capture these dynamics, we propose extending TAM with three constructs tailored to low‐resource healthcare contexts. Support availability would index the presence of help‐desk services, hands‐on training, and peer‐mentor networks [34]. Infrastructure reliability would quantify network uptime, device resilience, and power backup capacity [28]. Task–technology fit would assess the alignment between system functionality and clinicians’ workflow requirements [32]. Incorporating these variables could significantly enhance predictive validity for technology acceptance models in settings where baseline usability cannot be assumed. Moreover, our finding of a non‐linear, threshold effect for perceived risk (PERISK), no additional satisfaction gains once fundamental security and privacy criteria are satisfied, calls for a reconceptualization of risk within acceptance frameworks. Rather than modelling risk as a continuously deterring factor, future theory might adopt a “good‐enough” threshold approach, distinguishing essential safeguards from incremental enhancements that do not meaningfully influence user attitudes [38, 39]. In sum, our study underscores the imperative for a recalibrated TAM, one that privileges ease of use, reliable support structures, and contextual fit over abstract performance benefits when evaluating telehealth acceptance in low‐income health systems. Empirical validation of these extended constructs, through longitudinal and mixed‐methods research across diverse resource settings, will be essential for developing nuanced, practically actionable models. Such theoretically enriched frameworks will better guide practitioners and policymakers in designing telehealth interventions that align with the infrastructural realities and workflow exigencies of frontline clinicians.

Policy recommendations

Our findings highlight that system usability and workflow alignment are foundational to telehealth acceptance. To this end, health systems should convene multidisciplinary clinician workshops comprising nurses, physicians, and allied health professionals to co-design user interfaces that mirror frontline routines. Through journey‐mapping exercises, participants can pinpoint recurrent friction points (e.g., redundant vital‐sign entries, protracted authentication flows) that undermine perceived ease of use. Rapid prototyping of low‐fidelity wireframes or interactive mock‐ups, followed by weekly “think‐aloud” usability sessions, will surface and resolve cognitive bottlenecks before deployment. Each iteration should be evaluated against objective metrics, task completion time, and error rates to ensure continuous reduction of user burden and enhancement of perceived usefulness. Sustaining clinician confidence requires robust support structures that rapidly address technical disruptions and reinforce facilitating conditions. We recommend the establishment of district‐level telehealth support units staffed by clinician–technician “super‐users” who field real‐time assistance via a dedicated hotline, secure messaging channel, or on‐site visits. These teams should employ remote screen‐sharing tools to diagnose issues such as login failures or data synchronization errors, preventing minor glitches from eroding satisfaction. Regular pulse‐checks, brief weekly surveys or micro‐feedback sessions during clinical huddles, will track emerging pain points and feed them back into the design cycle. This continuous problem‐solving loop not only mitigates perceived risk but also reinforces clinicians’ sense of organizational support, thereby bolstering overall acceptance. To transition telehealth from pilot initiatives to core health‐system infrastructure, national ministries and accreditation bodies must embed digital‐health criteria into facility standards and professional frameworks. Accreditation requirements should specify minimum ICT benchmarks, bandwidth, power redundancy, on‐site telehealth coordinators, and tie compliance to funding allocations or performance‐based grants. Telehealth utilization metrics (e.g., consultation volumes, outcome‐based indicators, user satisfaction scores) should be integrated into national health dashboards to maintain strategic visibility. Moreover, linking telehealth engagement to continuing‐education credits and professional appraisal processes will institutionalize digital‐health competencies as essential clinical skills. By aligning regulatory standards and incentive structures with frontline realities, policymakers can ensure that ease of use, reliable support, and demonstrable clinical benefits remain at the forefront of sustainable telehealth scale‐up.

Limitations

There are few limitations that warrant consideration. The cross-sectional design and self-reported measures constrain causal inference and may introduce common-method bias. Although the model explained an aggregate 99.99% of variance in endogenous constructs, indicative of strong explanatory power, this may partly reflect model complexity and should be validated in longitudinal or experimental studies. Additionally, the sample’s concentration in the Greater Accra region limits generalizability to other regions. Future research should explore patient outcomes and cost-effectiveness alongside provider perceptions, as well as examine moderator effects of organizational culture, digital literacy, and regulatory environment.

Conclusion

This study sort to explore the health professionals’ perspective on the use of telehealth services for acute diseases management considering the principles, trends, and challenges of it use in selected health facilities in the Greater Accra region. This study has demonstrated that in resource-constrained health systems, the traditional Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) must be recalibrated to reflect the primacy of usability and infrastructural support over abstract performance gains. Through structural equation modelling, we found that perceived ease of use not only directly enhances clinician satisfaction but also catalyses perceptions of usefulness, together accounting for over half of the variance in overall acceptance. Conversely, perceived risk exhibited a threshold effect once basic security standards are met, incremental improvements no longer drive satisfaction, and perceived change exerted a nuanced influence, modestly undermining usefulness while reinforcing positive attitudes toward innovation. Collectively, these results confirm that frontline practitioners in low‐resource settings prioritize reliable, low‐friction system operation above all else. Theoretically, our findings advocate an extended TAM that integrates three context‐sensitive constructs: support availability, infrastructure reliability, and task–technology fit. Embedding these dimensions within technology acceptance frameworks will enable more accurate prediction of telehealth uptake where baseline usability cannot be assumed. Moreover, reframing risk as a “good‐enough” threshold rather than a linear deterrent refines our understanding of security’s role in shaping user attitudes. By aligning socio‐technical theory with empirical evidence from diverse low‐resource jurisdictions, we offer a robust foundation for future model development and cross‐contextual validation. Practically, this work underscores the necessity of co‐designing interfaces with clinicians, establishing district‐level super‐user support units, and embedding telehealth criteria within policy and accreditation frameworks. Leapfrogging strategies, such as solar‐powered routers and offline‐first applications, can bridge infrastructural gaps and sustain both synchronous and asynchronous modalities. Finally, longitudinal and mixed‐methods research should empirically test the proposed constructs, examine causal pathways between usability, support, and satisfaction, and explore organizational factors that moderate technology acceptance. By integrating these insights, policymakers and implementers can design telehealth interventions that resonate with the lived realities of frontline clinicians, ensuring sustained adoption and, ultimately, improved patient care in resource‐limited health systems.

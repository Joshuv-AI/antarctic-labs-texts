Enhanced immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy for advanced recurrent cervical cancer: the key to treatment efficacy and maintenance of quality of life
Authors: Masahiro Kagabu, Tsukasa Baba
Published: 2024

The treatment of advanced recurrent cancer is painful and difficult. In this context, current cancer treatments aim to maintain the patients’ quality of life while anticipating treatment effects.

The most common pathogenesis of cervical cancer is due to infection with the human papillomavirus (HPV). A vaccine against HPV has now been developed to prevent carcinogenesis, and its preventive effect has been shown to be successful.1 Unfortunately, treatment for current cervical cancer patients is inadequate, and the development of new treatments for patients with advanced or recurrent cervical cancer in particular is urgently needed. Virus-associated malignancies are thought to respond to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), and clinical trials have been conducted using ICIs for all virus-associated malignancies. Clinical trials with ICIs in advanced cervical cancer include the KEYNOTE-A182 and Calla trials3 of ICIs in combination with first-line concurrent chemoradiotherapy, with the KEYNOTE-A18 trial with pembrolizumab showing efficacy. Based on these results, the FDA approved the use of pembrolizumab in combination with concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) for advanced cervical cancer. The KEYNOTE-8264 and BEATcc trials5 of advanced recurrent cervical cancer without prior chemotherapy showed an additional treatment benefit in the ICI combination arm compared to the chemotherapy arm in both trials. The results showed that early initiation of concomitant ICI was effective for patients with cervical cancer. Nevertheless, the available treatments for patients with advanced or recurrent cervical cancer refractory to treatment are insufficient. The EMPOWER Cervical-1 trial,6 which assessed the anti-PD-1 antibody cemiplimab, is the most recent ICI-based trial in patients with advanced recurrent cervical cancer with prior chemotherapy. The primary endpoint of median overall survival (OS) was 8.5 months in the chemotherapy arm of physician's choice compared with 12.0 months in the single-agent cemiplimab arm, with a 31% reduction in risk of death (OS) in the cemiplimab arm. The secondary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) showed a 25% reduction in risk of disease progression or death (PFS) in the single-agent cemiplimab arm compared with the chemotherapy arm. Furthermore, the objective response rate (ORR) was 6.3% in the chemotherapy arm compared with 16.4% in the cemiplimab arm in the overall patient group. Moreover, combination treatment was shown to preserve quality of life compared to chemotherapy alone. These results provide ray of hope for patients with advanced recurrent cervical cancer. Nevertheless, the benefit is not sufficient.

In the October 2024 issue of eBioMedicine, Loic Verlingue and colleagues present a “Phase 2 study of the antitumour activity and safety of simlukafusp alfa (FAP-IL2v) combined with atezolizumab in patients with recurrent and/or metastatic cervical squamous cell carcinoma”.7 Simlukafusp alpha (FAP-IL2v) is a novel immune cytokine designed to selectively promote immune responses in the tumour microenvironment. In this study, the authors aimed to augment the effects of FAP-IL2v with the anti-PD-L1 antibody drug atezolizumab. The authors investigated the antitumour effects of FAP-IL2v in combination with atezolizumab in 48 patients with advanced recurrent cervical cancer who had received prior chemotherapy. In addition, a comparison of pre- and post-treatment pharmacological changes in blood and tumour samples was performed. The study reported an ORR of 27% among 45 of the evaluated patients, with complete responses observed in 7% and partial responses in 20% of the patients. The median duration of the response was 13.3 months, and median PFS was 3.7 months. Notably, responses were observed in both PD-L1 positive and negative tumours, indicating potential efficacy across a broad patient population. Safety data were consistent with the known profiles of the individual agents. In addition, FAP-IL2v in combination with atezolizumab resulted in a marked expansion and accumulation of tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), and PD-L1 expression in infiltrating immune cells was enhanced.

At present, the prognosis for patients with advanced recurrent cervical cancer is poor. While a number of clinical trials have been conducted to date, they have failed to show dramatic improvements in OS. The current treatment options for advanced recurrent cervical cancer are limited and mostly rely on chemotherapy regimens. In cancer treatments, it is important to expect a strong anti-tumour effect with the aim of curing the disease. However, when a cure is not expected, treatment must be able to continue with few adverse events while maintaining quality of life. ICI is one treatment option that does not reduce quality of life compared to chemotherapy. The results of this study indicate that ICIs represent a promising treatment option in patients with advanced cervical cancer, complementing existing therapies and providing a new treatment strategy.

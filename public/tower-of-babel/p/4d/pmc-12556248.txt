Anxiety and depression as predictors of NEET in young adulthood: A Swedish national cohort study
Authors: J Wahlström, V Östberg, S B Låftman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundYoung people not in education, employment, or training (NEET) are a growing concern in European societies. While mental health problems are established risk factors for NEET status, the specific roles of anxiety and depression - and their co-occurrence - remains understudied. This study examines whether symptoms of anxiety, depression, or their co-occurrence at age 21 predict NEET status at age 23, separately for males and females.MethodsData were drawn from Futura01, a Swedish national cohort of individuals born in 2001. We used survey data from waves 3 and 4 (n = 2,619, 59.3% females). NEET status at age 23 was based on current main activity; individuals not studying, working, training, in military service, or on parental leave were classified as NEET. Anxiety and depression symptoms were assessed at age 21 using the Patient Health Questionnaire-4 (PHQ-4). Parental education and migration background were included as covariates. Logistic regression analyses were conducted.ResultsAmong males, experiencing anxiety only (OR = 2.28, p < 0.05), depression only (OR = 2.47, p < 0.01), or their co-occurrence (OR = 2.57, p < 0.01) was associated with higher odds of being NEET. Among females, while neither anxiety only (OR = 0.96, p = 0.924) not depression only (OR = 2.14, p = 0.071) significantly predicted NEET status, the co-occurrence of depression and anxiety symptoms was associated with being NEET (OR = 3.53, p < 0.001).ConclusionsSymptoms of anxiety and depression at age 21 are linked to an increased risk of NEET status at age 23, especially the co-occurrence of these symptoms. Experiencing only anxiety symptoms seems to matter solely for males. These findings underscore the importance of distinguishing between mental health disorders, accounting for their co-occurrence, and considering sex-specific patterns when addressing risk factors for being NEET in early adulthood. Targeting youth who display symptoms of mental disorders can be beneficial for reducing NEET prevalence.Key messages• Anxiety, depression, and especially their co-occurrence in early adulthood are linked to a higher risk of being NEET, with distinct patterns by sex.• Sex-specific mental health interventions that identify and support young adults with these symptoms may help reduce NEET prevalence.

A bibliometric analysis of trends and hotspots in autophagy in gastric cancer
Authors: Jianfang Zhu, Liuyang Shu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAutophagy plays a critical role in the biology of gastric cancer (GC), influencing tumor progression, drug resistance, and treatment outcomes. This study aims to analyze the bibliometric landscape of autophagy research in GC, identifying key contributors, influential publications, and emerging research themes.MethodA systematic literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection, encompassing studies published from 1977 to 2024. Bibliometric analysis was performed utilizing VOSviewer 1.6.20, CiteSpace 6.3.R1, and the R package “bibliometrix” to visualize patterns in co-authorship, institutional collaborations, citations, keyword co-occurrences, and emerging research trends.ResultsA total of 1179 articles on autophagy in GC were identified, reflecting a steady annual growth rate of 10.5%. China accounted for 77.7% of the publications, with Nanjing Medical University emerging as the leading institution. Collaboration networks revealed limited international partnerships, with a predominant focus on regional research efforts. Zhang L and Zhang X were recognized as the most prolific and impactful authors, while journals such as Cell Death & Disease and Oncology Reports were identified as prominent publication venues. Emerging research hotspots included autophagy-mediated drug resistance, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and the roles of long non-coding RNAs and circular RNAs in regulating autophagy.ConclusionThis bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of global research on autophagy in gastric cancer, highlighting key contributors, institutions, and journals. Emerging research areas encompass the role of autophagy in drug sensitivity, the inhibition of epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and the formulation of anti-tumor therapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03371-9.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is a malignant neoplasm that arises from the epithelial cells of the gastric mucosa, with adenocarcinoma being the most prevalent histological subtype [1, 2]. The incidence of GC is particularly elevated in East Asia, Eastern Europe, and select countries in South America, with a higher risk observed in males compared to females, especially among middle-aged and elderly populations [3]. The pathogenesis of GC is influenced by a multifaceted interplay of genetic predisposition, environmental factors—including lifestyle, dietary practices, and chemical exposures—and microbial infections, with Helicobacter pylori being particularly significant [4]. Clinically, GC profoundly disrupts the functionality of the digestive system, leads to a significant decline in quality of life, and poses a life-threatening risk through the invasion of adjacent tissues or distant metastasis [5]. Current treatment modalities encompass surgical resection, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapies, and traditional Chinese medicine. While surgical resection is the preferred intervention for early-stage GC, the prognosis for advanced-stage disease remains unfavorable [6, 7]. Recently, autophagy—a critical intracellular degradation process—has garnered attention for its intricate role in the pathogenesis and treatment of GC, providing new insights and potential therapeutic targets [8].

Autophagy is vital in eukaryotic cells for the maintenance of cellular homeostasis by degrading aging proteins and dysfunctional organelles. In the context of GC, autophagy exhibits multifarious and context-dependent roles [9, 10]. Research indicates that autophagy can facilitate the proliferation and growth of GC cells; for example, the EYA3 protein family modulates autophagy to promote cancer progression. Furthermore, autophagy enables GC cells to endure adverse conditions, such as hypoxia and nutrient deprivation, by regulating critical metabolic pathways and signaling cascades, including the Akt/mTOR pathway [11]. Additionally, autophagy is significantly implicated in chemotherapy resistance. The overactivation of autophagy can enhance resistance to chemotherapeutic agents, such as cisplatin, in GC cells. High expression levels of NRF2 have been identified as a factor that modulates autophagy, contributing to cisplatin resistance in these neoplastic cells [12, 13]. Moreover, autophagy closely interacts with the host immune response, augmenting anti-tumor immunity by promoting the polarization of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) toward the M1 phenotype through pathways such as the cGAS-STING signaling axis [14]. Autophagy is also intricately linked to various forms of cell death, including apoptosis and ferroptosis, which play crucial roles in the progression and treatment of GC [15, 16]. Recent studies utilizing single-cell RNA sequencing have revealed distinct expression patterns of autophagy-related genes across different cellular subpopulations within the tumor microenvironment, illuminating intercellular interactions and functional regulation [17]. Despite significant advances in elucidating the relationship between autophagy and GC, many questions remain unresolved. As a double-edged sword, autophagy can either promote or inhibit tumor progression, contingent upon the specific context. Consequently, further research is imperative to develop autophagy-targeted therapeutic strategies that precisely identify the optimal timing and methods for intervention.

Bibliometric analysis employs mathematical and statistical methodologies to assess the comprehensive array of research publications pertaining to a specific topic over time. This approach provides valuable insights into research categories, co-authorship networks, keyword frequencies, and the most frequently cited articles and journals [18–20]. Such a method enables a systematic evaluation of the development, trends, and focal points within a given research domain, thereby allowing researchers to identify existing gaps and emerging directions in the field. The objective of this analysis is to systematically summarize and categorize the research literature, revealing significant trends and developments. By statistically analyzing keywords, authors, and research institutions, the study elucidates research hotspots and nascent areas, offering guidance for researchers in refining their objectives and strategies.

Methods

Search strategies and data source

A comprehensive literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), an esteemed database recognized for its high-caliber academic publications across diverse disciplines. The selection of WoSCC was predicated on its stringent indexing standards, which ensure the integrity of data suitable for bibliometric analysis. Furthermore, its compatibility with essential bibliometric tools, such as VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1), and the R package “bibliometrix,“(version 4.4.1) further substantiated its utility. These tools rely on standardized metadata formats that WoSCC consistently supports, thereby ensuring consistency and reproducibility in bibliometric visualization and analysis. The search was performed on November 22, 2024, with the following search formula: ((((((TS=(autophag*)) OR TS=(mitophagy)) OR TS=(autolysosome)) OR TS=(er-phagy*)) OR TS=(“endoplasmic reticulum autophag*”)) OR TS=(“mitochondrial* autophag*”) and TS=(“gastric cancer*” OR “gastric carcinoma” OR “stomach cancer” OR “stomach neoplasm*” OR “stomach carcinoma” OR “stomach neoplasm” OR “gastric adenocarcinoma”). This search strategy was designed to encompass all pertinent studies related to autophagy and GC by integrating various terminologies, including mitophagy and autolysosomes. The analysis encompasses publications from 1977, the earliest entry on autophagy and gastric cancer indexed in WoSCC, to 2024, thereby providing a comprehensive overview of research trends over nearly five decades. Only English-language documents categorized as “articles” were included. Exclusion criteria eliminated records classified as review articles (n = 284), meeting abstracts (n = 40), early access publications (n = 12), editorial materials (n = 3), proceeding papers (n = 1), and non-English publications (n = 5). Following the initial search, two independent reviewers manually screened the remaining records, resolving any discrepancies through discussion and consensus.

Statistical analysis and visualization

Three bibliometric tools were employed for data analysis and visualization: VOSviewer 1.6.20, CiteSpace 6.3.R1, and the R-based bibliometric package “bibliometrix.” Each tool served distinct functions in mapping and analyzing the bibliometric landscape. VOSviewer, a versatile bibliometric software, was instrumental in generating co-authorship networks, citation patterns, and keyword co-occurrence maps. Node sizes in the visualizations represented the number of publications or citations, while colors denoted clusters of related entities, and link thickness indicated collaboration or citation strength among authors, institutions, and journals. CiteSpace was used to identify research trends and emerging hotspots through keyword burst analysis. The parameters were set with a time slice spanning from 1977 to 2024, with keywords selected as the node type. Pruning methods, including pathfinder and clipping merge networks, were applied to streamline the analysis and highlight significant research trends. This approach provided temporal insights into evolving research priorities within the autophagy and GC domain. The R bibliometric package, “bibliometrix,” was utilized for comprehensive bibliometric evaluations, including global publication distribution, author impact metrics, and journal influence. Metrics such as the H-index, G-index, and M-index were employed to assess individual and institutional academic impact, while Journal Citation Reports (JCR) quartiles and Impact Factors (IF) were analyzed to evaluate journal prestige. The 2024 JCR and IF data were used to ensure up-to-date assessments of journal influence. This systematic analysis offers a robust framework for understanding the research landscape of autophagy in GC, identifying key contributors, trends, and potential avenues for interdisciplinary collaboration.

Results

Overview of publications

The comprehensive selection process is depicted (Fig. 1). The number of publications and associated metrics reflect the evolution of research interest in this domain. The total number of documents published between 1977 and 2024 has reached 1179, with a steady annual growth rate of 10.5%. A total of 5730 authors contributed to these publications, with an average of 7.68 co-authors per document. Notably, international co-authorship accounts for 11.2% of the documents, indicating growing global collaboration. The average number of citations per document is 26.37, reflecting the increasing impact of these studies within the research community. The trend in the volume of publications demonstrates a nonlinear growth trajectory. Prior to 2009, annual publications remained consistently below five, signifying minimal research activity. A notable inflection point emerged in 2010, with cumulative publications adhering to a fourth-degree polynomial growth model described by the equation: y = 0.0019x⁴ − 0.1331x³ + 2.9585x² − 22.889x + 43.63 (R² = 0.9947, encompassing the years 1977–2024). This model delineates two distinct phases: (1) 1977–2009: Characterized by negligible growth, with an average annual increase of 0.2%. (2) 2010–2024: Marked by exponential expansion, exhibiting an average annual growth rate of 24.3%, culminating in a peak of 158 publications in 2020 (Fig. 2). Despite fluctuations observed post-2020, such as 123 publications in 2021 and 109 in 2024, the overarching trend continues to ascend. These findings suggest a paradigm shift in research output subsequent to 2010, driven by advancements in the field of autophagy biology and an increased clinical interest in therapeutic targeting.

Analysis of countries and institutions

The global distribution of research publications in this domain reveals China’s dominant role, contributing 916 documents, accounting for 77.7% of the total output, followed by South Korea, Japan, and the USA. Notably, Canada and the USA demonstrate significant international collaboration, with multiple-country publication (MCP) ratios of 0.7 and 0.483, respectively, indicating strong global ties. In contrast, China and Japan maintain relatively lower MCP ratios of 0.07, reflecting a focus on single-country research efforts (Table S1). China is the clear leader in both single-country publications (SCP) and collaborative works, followed by Korea and Japan (Fig. 3A). The USA and European countries such as Italy and Spain exhibit smaller contributions but higher proportions of collaborative efforts, emphasizing their role in fostering international research partnerships. The international collaboration network (Fig. 3B) further highlights the USA and China as central nodes, with strong connections to Korea, Japan, and European countries. The USA demonstrates extensive global links, while China, despite its leading output, shows more regionally concentrated partnerships. Countries such as Italy, Iran, and Spain participate in smaller, region-specific clusters, indicating regional collaborations over broader global networks.

Analysis of journals

The collected papers in this field are distributed across multiple journals, with Cell Death & Disease leading in publication output with 33 papers, followed by Oncology Reports and the International Journal of Oncology (Table S2). These journals span diverse research areas, including oncology, cellular biology, and molecular sciences, indicating the interdisciplinary nature of the field. The co-citation network of journals (Fig. 4A) highlights Cancer Letters, Cell Death & Disease, and Oncology Reports as central nodes, reflecting their extensive links and influence in the research landscape. These journals are frequently cited alongside each other, underlining their role as critical platforms for disseminating research findings in this domain. The co-occurrence of journals further reveals distinct clusters with Autophagy, Cancer Letters, and Frontiers in Oncology emerging as focal points within their respective networks (Fig. 4B). Autophagy demonstrates a high level of interconnectivity, aligning with its leading IF of 14.6, further solidifying its prominence. Similarly, journals such as Journal of Experimental & Clinical Cancer Research and Scientific Reports maintain strong positions, with significant contributions to cancer research and clinical applications. The bibliometric indicators emphasize the impact of these journals, with Molecular Cancer exhibiting the highest impact factor, despite a lower publication count. In contrast, Oncology Reports and International Journal of Molecular Sciences rank among the most productive journals while maintaining consistent citation performance.

Analysis of co-occurring keywords and burst terms

The co-occurrence of keywords offers valuable insights into research hotspots and emerging trends within the field (Fig. 5A). Our review of the selected literature identified a total of 133 keywords, each appearing at least ten times. Among the most frequently cited keywords, “expression” stands out with 355 occurrences, underscoring a strong emphasis on gene expression and functional studies as a central theme in GC research. Noteworthy keywords such as “apoptosis” and “inhibition” indicate significant attention to cellular death pathways and related abnormalities within GC pathology. As research has evolved, the focus has increasingly shifted toward molecular mechanisms, as evidenced by the occurrences of keywords such as “AKT,” “MAPK,” and “LC3,” which signify a growing interest in the molecular foundations of GC.

Discussion

This bibliometric analysis delineates the evolving landscape of autophagy-related GC research over the past decade, emphasizing three interrelated trends: exponential growth in publications, dynamic shifts in mechanistic focus, and emerging therapeutic hotspots.

Temporal evolution of research output

Between 1977 and 2024, global publication output experienced a dramatic increase of 100-fold, with China contributing to 77.7% of the total publications. This growth can be delineated into two principal phases: Early Phase (2010–2015): This period was characterized by an emphasis on elucidating the dual roles of autophagy in tumor survival, examining mechanisms such as metabolic compensation through glutaminolysis in nutrient-deficient microenvironments [21], as well as therapy resistance, particularly through Akt/mTOR/HIF-1α-mediated protective autophagy [22]. Current Phase (2018–2023): Research in this phase is predominantly focused on non-coding RNA (ncRNA) studies, which account for 76% of recent publications. This phase specifically underscores the mechanisms of chemoresistance mediated by long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) and circular RNA (circRNA) [23–25]. Furthermore, the low rate of international collaboration (MCP ratio: 0.07) contrasts sharply with the substantial contributions from the USA, Japan, and Germany in elucidating core pathways such as PI3K/AKT-Wnt crosstalk. This disparity underscores the critical need for enhanced cross-border integration of discoveries at the pathway level.

Mechanistic shifts in autophagy-gastric cancer research

Research on autophagy in GC has undergone significant evolution over time. Early studies predominantly concentrated on metabolic adaptation and survival under stress, while more recent investigations have transitioned towards the roles of regulatory RNAs and tumor plasticity, thereby uncovering novel therapeutic vulnerabilities.

Early phase (2009–2015): metabolic compensation and therapy resistance

Initial studies established a connection between autophagy and metabolic stress responses. Hirayama et al. (2009) demonstrated increased amino acid accumulation through glutaminolysis in GC tissues, suggesting that autophagic degradation serves as a critical energy source in nutrient-deficient environments [21]. In 2011, Wang et al. revealed that quercetin induces autophagy via the Akt/mTOR and HIF-1α signaling pathways, thereby protecting GC cells from apoptosis [22]. This marked an early instance of therapy-induced, pro-survival autophagy. These findings positioned autophagy as a dual-edged mechanism: essential for tumor survival yet potentially targetable for therapeutic sensitization.

Transition phase (2016–2019): non-coding RNA regulation and chemoresistance

From 2016 to 2019, the research focus shifted towards non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), particularly long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs), which regulate autophagy through competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks. For instance, MALAT1 was identified as a facilitator of chemoresistance by sequestering miR-23b-3p, resulting in the upregulation of ATG12 and the promotion of autophagy [26]. Additionally, circAKT3 and circNRIP1 were implicated in cisplatin resistance via the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, indicating that RNA-mediated regulation of autophagy plays a pivotal role in drug response and tumor progression [27]. Notably, many of these circRNAs function through exosome-mediated communication, broadening the understanding of autophagy to encompass intercellular signaling.

Recent advances (2020–2024): stress adaptation, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and genetic determinants

In the most recent phase, research on autophagy has expanded beyond drug resistance to include tumor plasticity, immune evasion, and metastasis. Investigations into circCUL2 reveal its role in restoring cisplatin sensitivity by modulating autophagy through the miR-142-3p/ROCK2 axis [28]. In H. pylori-associated GC, autophagy is closely linked to endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, with ATG16L1 polymorphisms (e.g., rs2241880 G allele) disrupting autophagic control and increasing tumor risk [29]. Furthermore, lncRNAs such as SNHG11 promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and stemness through autophagy and Wnt/β-catenin signaling, underscoring autophagy’s involvement in invasion and metastasis [30]. These findings elucidate a more comprehensive mechanistic perspective wherein autophagy intersects with critical hallmarks of cancer.

Emerging hotspots: autophagy as a therapeutic pivot

Keyword burst analysis reveals key themes such as “drug resistance,” “ncRNA,” “EMT,” and “H. pylori,” underscoring the evolving role of autophagy from a pro-survival mechanism to a multifaceted therapeutic target.

Drug resistance: Autophagy enables cancer cells to endure chemotherapy-induced stress [31, 32], particularly in cisplatin- and 5-FU-resistant GC. Regulatory non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) facilitate this adaptation by activating pathways such as PI3K/Akt/mTOR, inhibiting apoptosis, and maintaining cancer stem cell characteristics. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis: Recent research establishes a connection between autophagy and the regulation of EMT via Wnt/β-catenin and TGF-β pathways. In H. pylori-positive GC, CagA-induced overexpression of miR-543 suppresses SIRT1, thereby inhibiting autophagy and promoting EMT [29, 33]. Restoration of autophagic flux reverses these effects, suggesting autophagy’s potential in anti-metastatic therapeutic strategies. Inflammation and genetic susceptibility: Dysfunctional autophagy, induced by inflammation—particularly through variants of ATG16L1—affects cytokine responses and may elevate the risk of gastric tumorigenesis [29]. These findings integrate microbial factors, immune dysregulation, and autophagy into a cohesive framework.

Collectively, these mechanistic shifts and emerging hotspots underscore the growing significance of autophagy as a therapeutic focus in gastric cancer. Future research that integrates genomic, epigenetic, and environmental data will be essential for the development of effective autophagy-targeted interventions.

Research significance and limitations

This study provides a comprehensive overview of the research landscape, highlighting key authors, institutions, and journals while identifying emerging hotspots through keyword analysis. The findings enable researchers to recognize trends and focus on critical areas such as drug resistance and tumor progression. Additionally, researchers can select suitable journals for publication and stay informed about influential studies. They can also seek collaboration with leading institutions and researchers to promote multidisciplinary and global research efforts.

However, this study has certain limitations. Firstly, it relies exclusively on the WoSCC. Although WoSCC is recognized for its high-quality and standardized metadata, its sole reliance may result in the oversight of pertinent publications indexed in alternative databases such as Scopus, PubMed, or Embase. This limitation has the potential to introduce selection bias and constrain the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, it does not include book chapters, conference proceedings, or editorials, potentially omitting valuable insights or early-stage research. Thirdly, we acknowledge the challenges presented by predatory journals within the academic landscape. Although the exclusion of articles from these journals may enhance data reliability, it also has the potential to introduce selection bias and misrepresent research trends within the field. Predatory journals often reflect specific publication trends, particularly in emerging areas and resource-limited regions; thus, their inclusion significantly contributes to the comprehensiveness of our analysis. We explicitly address this limitation within the manuscript and encourage readers to consider it when interpreting the results. Lastly, challenges in standardizing keywords and disambiguating author names may have affected the accuracy of co-occurrence and collaboration analyses.

Conclusion

This study employed bibliometric analysis to evaluate global research trends and hotspots in autophagy-related GC research, with a focus on countries, institutions, authors, journals, and keywords. The primary research hotspots identified include mechanisms of drug resistance, the role of autophagy in tumor progression, and cellular stress responses. Additionally, emerging areas of interest encompass the role of autophagy in drug sensitivity, the inhibition of EMT, and the development of anti-tumor therapeutic strategies. This systematic evaluation underscores prominent journals, collaborative institutions, and emerging research trajectories for scholars in the field. Future research endeavors should prioritize the targeting of autophagy pathways to improve clinical outcomes for patients with GC and to advance personalized treatment strategies.

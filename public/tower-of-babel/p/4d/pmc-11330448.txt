A method for extracting buildings from remote sensing images based on 3DJA-UNet3+
Authors: Yingjian Li, Yonggang Li, Xiangbin Zhu, Haojie Fang, Lihua Ye
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBuilding extraction aims to extract building pixels from remote sensing imagery, which plays a significant role in urban planning, dynamic urban monitoring, and many other applications. UNet3+ is widely applied in building extraction from remote sensing images. However, it still faces issues such as low segmentation accuracy, imprecise boundary delineation, and the complexity of network models. Therefore, based on the UNet3+ model, this paper proposes a 3D Joint Attention (3DJA) module that effectively enhances the correlation between local and global features, obtaining more accurate object semantic information and enhancing feature representation. The 3DJA module models semantic interdependence in the vertical and horizontal dimensions to obtain feature map spatial encoding information, as well as in the channel dimensions to increase the correlation between dependent channel graphs. In addition, a bottleneck module is constructed to reduce the number of network parameters and improve model training efficiency. Many experiments are conducted on publicly accessible WHU,INRIA and Massachusetts building dataset, and the benchmarks, BOMSC-Net, CVNet, SCA-Net, SPCL-Net, ACMFNet, MFCF-Net models are selected for comparison with the 3DJA-UNet3+ model proposed in this paper. The experimental results show that 3DJA-UNet3+ achieves competitive results in three evaluation indicators: overall accuracy, mean intersection over union, and F1-score. Introduction

With the rapid development of earth observation science and aerospace, geographic information systems, sensor technology, and other fields, the data volume of high-resolution remote sensing images has sharply increased. As the main information in urban landscapes, buildings are the main object of urban geographical spatial analysis1. The operation and analysis of building groups is the primary task of applying cities as information carriers to all walks of life. Effective extracting building information from remote sensing images is an important step in urban planning, damage detection, land use management, and other applications2,3. Therefore, accurately extracting building information from high-resolution remote sensing images is an important research direction4–6.

Methods based on deep learning are more efficient in building extraction and more suitable for complex and diverse building types in urban and rural areas, which is the mainstream research direction and application method of information extraction and image segmentation7. Many researchers use deep learning methods for image segmentation and building extraction. Khan et al.8 proposes a deep learning-based encoder-decoder framework for automatically extracting building contours from high-resolution satellite images. The encoder part of this framework utilizes a network structure consisting of densely connected convolutional and transformation blocks to capture multi-level information of buildings at various scales and complexities. The decoder part restores the spatial information lost during encoding through a series of deconvolution layers, reconstructing building boundaries and accurately distinguishing target areas. Long et al.9 used a convolutional neural network and proposed FCN by removing the fully connected layer in the convolutional neural network to achieve end-to-end image segmentation, facilitating building extraction development. Sariturk et al.10 used FCN to extract features from satellite images. Schuegraf et al.11 proposed a hybrid-FCN neural network that effectively merges depth and spectral information from two parallel networks merged at a later stage for binary building mask generation. Zuo et al.12 designed the hierarchical fusion convolutional neural network HF-FCN, which ensembles multiscale features and improves building extraction accuracy. Since the FCN continuous downsampling operation causes spatial information loss, the feature maps produced by the model do not have access to global information, resulting in limited accuracy in extracting buildings. Chen et al.13,14 proposed a semantic segmentation algorithm for the DeepLab family, which uses atrous spatial pyramid pooling (ASPP) to obtain feature maps and conditional random fields with different sensory field sizes to enhance image segmentation details. Based on the DeepLab v3+ network model, Xu Zeyu et al.15 proposed an E-DeepLab network model for feature classification of high-resolution remote sensing images, which is improved in many aspects by changing the combination of encoder and decoder, enhancing the connection between encoder and decoder, adding adaptive weights, and performing multichannel training to improve the feature classification accuracy. The DeepLab-based approach with null convolution and spatial pyramid pooling can extract multiscale information and maintain a large sensory field. However, this also means that a larger number of computations is needed. It increases the parameters of the network model and prolongs the network training time. To further improve the accuracy of building extraction, Ronneberger et al.16 proposed a UNet network that combines the feature maps in the encoding and decoding layers using jump connections and uses inverse convolution instead of linear interpolation during upsampling so that the network can receive more contextually detailed features and acquire some global features, which significantly improves the extraction accuracy of the model and has a far-reaching impact on the building extraction field. On this basis, Zhimeng et al.17 propose a dilated convolution-based E-UNet deep learning network, which introduces jump connections to reduce the loss of detail at edges and corners and employs a newly designed dilated convolution module, which allows it to expand the sensory field while reducing the number of parameters. Liu et al.18 proposed a deep learning residual sequence learning network SS-Net, which adds residual representations to UNet jump connections to extract building information in remote sensing images. Yi et al.19 constructed DeepResUNet based on UNet combined with a residual linkage module to enhance the feature representation. Although the UNet-based method fuses the global features to a certain extent and improves extraction accuracy, it does not fully fuse the deep features with the shallow features, which easily leads to the loss of important information in the feature map. Building on previous work, we propose a 3D joint attention (3DJA) module to adaptively strengthen the correlation between local and global features, thereby obtaining more accurate semantic information of the object and enhancing the feature representation. This helps to achieve more precise segmentation results. Besides, to reduce the number of network parameters and improve model training efficiency, we use the bottleneck module to replace the convolutional layer in the original network. Finally, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed method through comparative experiments on the WHU and INRIA datasets.

In this paper, a new network model, 3DJA-UNet3+, is proposed based on UNet3+ for remote sensing image building extraction, and the structure of the 3DJA-UNet3+ network model is shown in Fig. 1. This method designs a 3DJA module to adaptively strengthen the correlation between local and global features to obtain more accurate semantic information of the object, thus enhancing the feature representation and helping to obtain more accurate segmentation results. In addition, the bottleneck module is used to replace the convolutional layer in the original network to reduce the number of network parameters and improve model training efficiency. Finally, the validity of the proposed method is verified through comparative experiments.

Related work

Considering the importance of UNet3+ in building extraction tasks and its guidance for this paper, we will provide a brief introduction to the structure of UNet3+ and self-attention mechanism. Moreover, feature extraction is crucial for the building extraction tasks, and we will also discuss it in this section.

Structure of UNet3+

Zhou et al.20 proposed the UNet++ network structure, which has an advantage over the original UNet in integrating different image feature levels through nested subnetworks and long and short connections, resulting in higher network segmentation accuracy. Tong et al.21 proposed a UNet++ convolutional network containing dense connections to reconstruct UNet++ with DenseNet as the backbone, which improved building extraction accuracy. However, the UNet++ structure is more complex than that of the original UNet due to its dense connections and jump connections, which increases the number of UNet++ parameters. Huang et al.22 proposed a novel UNet3+ network model that exploits the full-scale jump connection (skip connection) and deep supervision (deep supervision). Full-scale skip connections combine high-level semantics from feature maps of different scales with low-level semantics, while deep supervision improves accuracy by learning hierarchical tables from multiscale aggregated feature maps. Although full-scale skip connections can acquire semantic information from feature maps of different scales, they cannot acquire correlation features between local and global features. In summary, building extraction in high-resolution remote sensing images is still challenging.

Based on UNet++, UNet3+ utilizes full-scale skip connections and deep supervision to fuse deep and shallow features while supervising features at various scales. Figure 2 illustrates UNet3+’s full architecture. UNet3+ utilizes full-scale skip connections to obtain fine-grained and coarse-grained semantics. Simultaneously, to further learn hierarchical representation from full-size aggregated feature maps, the output of each layer is connected to a loss function to improve segmentation accuracy.

Deep supervision

To learn hierarchical representations from full-scale aggregated feature maps, UNet3+ further adopts full-scale deep supervision. At each decoder stage, there is a side output that is supervised by the truth value. To achieve deep supervision, the last layer of each decoder stage is sequentially passed through 3 × 3 convolutional layers; bilinear interpolation upsampling and a sigmoid function are used to obtain the output in this stage. Self-attention

Self-attention can simulate long-term dependency relationships and is widely used in deep learning23–26. Given a scene segmentation image, the object or target varies in scale, lighting, and field of view. Since the convolution operation is a local receptive field, there may be some differences in the features corresponding to pixels with the same label. These differences lead to intraclass inconsistencies and affect the accuracy of recognition. The self-attention mechanism can effectively capture the correlation between pixels, thereby improving recognition performance. Ashish Vaswani et al.27 first proposed using a self-attention mechanism to obtain global dependencies of inputs and applied it to machine translation. Additionally, applying attention modules in image vision is becoming increasingly widespread. Han Zhang et al.28 introduced a self-attention mechanism to learn better image generators. Xiaolong Wang et al.29 studied work related to self-attention modules, mainly exploring the effectiveness of nonlocal operations in video and image spatial media.

Feature extraction

CNN-based feature extraction

With in-depth research into various technologies such as attention mechanisms and feature fusion strategies, an increasing number of semantic segmentation methods based on CNN are being proposed. For example, U-Net, DeepLab, and so on. Based on the UNet, Qiu et al.30 refines the skip connection through a spatial convolutional pyramid pooling module and improved depthwise separable convolutions to achieve interaction of features at multiple scales and levels. Khan et al.8 follows the pipeline of U-Net, using a variant of DenseNet-201 as the encoder to reduce the information loss of small objects and decrease the loss of spatial information. These studies typically focus on local features, while neglecting the global features and the complementary role between global and local features.

Transformer-based feature extraction

The transformer model has attracted widespread attention in multiple domains since its inception. Some studies employ the transformer model for building extraction tasks, obtaining long-range information for enhanced global information interaction. Chen et al.31 first used a Transformer to model global long-range dependencies for the purpose of building extraction. Chen et al.32 designed a lightweight multi-scale feature enhancer to extract representative semantic features from Vision Transformer (ViT) backgrounds and generate multi-scale pyramid feature maps. Through experimental analysis of various pre-trained knowledge bases, it has proven that certain pre-trained knowledge bases (such as SAM) are more advantageous for remote sensing image segmentation tasks. Hu et al.33 proposed a transformer-based polygonal building extraction approach. Based on the transformer, the method encodes contextual information through a set of polygon queries to predict the final set of building polygons for the building instances. However, the Transformer faces issues with high computational resource requirements and slower inference speeds.

CNN-transformer-based feature extraction

Some studies consider enhancing model performance by fusing CNN and Transformer. By integrating the superior capability of CNN in extracting local features and spatial hierarchies in images with the proficiency of Transformer in capturing long-range dependencies in sequential data, the performance of the model in building extraction can be improved. Yuan et al.34 leverages the Transformer to capture global features and the CNN to obtain local features, combining the obtained features and then progressively upsampling them step by step. Additionally, a Lite Swin Transformer is introduced to reduce the computational load of the Transformer. Based on the synergistic use of CNN and Transformer, Yuan et al.35 develops an improved Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanism. This mechanism deepens the semantic association between deep and shallow-level features.

3DJA module

Unlike previous work, this paper designs a 3DJA module. As Fig. 5 shows, the 3DJA module can not only focus on the spatial position between pixels through the spatial attention mechanism but also learn the dependency relationships between different channels through the channel attention mechanism. The 3DJA module first models the spatial relationship between any two feature pixels to obtain a spatial attention map. Then, the channel attention map is generated using the channel relationship of the feature. Then, the spatial attention map and channel attention weights are fused with the input features. Bottleneck

The bottleneck structure is a component commonly used in deep neural networks. Its main function is to reduce the computational and parameter load while ensuring the model depth and improving the computational efficiency and learning ability of the model. Broadly speaking, it refers to a three-layer convolutional structure where the number of channels in the middle layer is much smaller than the number of channels in the front and back layers, thereby compressing the dimensionality of feature representation and reducing computational complexity. The core idea of the bottleneck is to use multiple small convolution kernels, such as 1×1, instead of a large convolution kernel to complete a portion of the work.

As Fig. 6 shows, assuming the input feature map has a dimension of 256, the output dimension also needs to be 256. There are two operation methods: one is the conventional convolution operation, which uses 3 × 3 kernels. The 256-dimensional input directly passes through a 3 × 3 × 256 convolutional layer and outputs a 256-dimensional feature map, and the parameter quantity is 256 × 3 × 3× 256 = 5,898,242. The second is the operation of the bottleneck layer, which first goes through 1 × 1 kernels, then passes through a 3 × 3 kernel, and finally passes through a 1 × 1 kernel. The 256-dimensional input first passes through 1 × 1 × 64 convolutional layers, passes through another 3 × 3 × 64 convolutional layers, and finally passes through 1 × 1 × 256 convolutional layers. The total number of parameters is 256 × 1 × 1 × 64 + 64 × 3 × 3 × 64 + 64 × 1 × 1 × 256 = 696,322. After comparing the two methods, it is evident that the parameter quantity of the latter is much smaller than that of the former.

Experiments

Experimental setting

The experiment is based on the Ubuntu 20.04.6 LTS system using the PyTorch deep learning framework. The GPU is an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 M, and the CPU is an Intel i7-13700K.

Dataset

To verify the accuracy of the 3DJA-UNet3+ network for building extraction, the WHU building dataset and INRI building dataset are used in this paper. Figure 7 shows an example of the WHU and INRIA building datasets.

Images and corresponding labels in the WHU, INRIA and Massachusetts datasets.

(1) The WHU dataset38 covers both urban and suburban areas and contains buildings and ground cover of various sizes and irregularities. Additionally, the dataset scene has a certain complexity and diversity, which increases the extraction difficulty. The WHU dataset consists of 8,189 images of size 512 × 512 pixels, with a spatial resolution of 0.3 m, and ranges over 450 km2, which contains more than 220,000 buildings. The dataset is divided into a training set of 4736 images, a validation set of 1,036 images, and a test set of 2416 images.

(2) The INRIA dataset39 contains remotely sensed images with a spatial resolution of 0.3 m covering an area of 810 km2. These images cover buildings in different urban settlements, including Austin, Chicago, and Kitsap in the United States and Tyrol West and Vienna in Austria in five areas with very different styles. The labeled data in this dataset have 180 images of size 5000 × 5000 pixels. After data processing, in this paper, each image is first enlarged to 5120 × 5120 pixels and then seamlessly cropped into images of size 512 × 512 pixels, totaling 18,000 images. From these, 14,400 images are randomly selected to form the training set, and 3600 images are randomly selected to form the validation set.

(3) This Massachusetts dataset40 consists of 151 aerial images of the Boston area, with each of the images being 1500 × 1500 pixels for an area of 2.25 square kilometers. Hence, the entiredataset covers roughly 340 square kilometers. The data is split into a TECning set of 137 images, a test set of 10 images, and a validation set of 4 images. The target mapswere obtained by rasterizing building footprints obtained from the OpenStreetMap project. The dataset covers most urban and suburban areas and buildings of all sizes,including individual houses and garages, are included in the labels. All imagery is rescaled to a resolution of 1 pixel per square meter. Target maps for the test andvalidation portions of the dataset were hand-corrected to make the evaluations more accurate.

The experimental results shows that when the bottleneck is used, the number of network parameters significantly decreases, effectively improving network computing efficiency. The main reason is through the use of 1 × 1 convolution layer is used to reduce the number of channels in the feature map, thereby reducing the number of parameters in the model. This dimensionality reduction operation can also help the model learn and represent data features more effectively. The bottleneck structure first passes through a 1 × 1 convolution to reduce the number of channels in the middle convolution to 1/4. After completing the convolution, the number of output channels in the middle of the ordinary convolution is equal to the number of input channels. The third convolution is used to recover the number of channels so that the output channel number in the bottleneck is equal to the input channel number of the bottleneck. These two 1 × 1 convolutions effectively reduce the number of parameters and computational complexity of the convolution. After adding 3DJA, the segmentation accuracy of the network improved. The main reason is to establish pixel-level correlation through global average pooling in both vertical and horizontal directions, obtaining spatial attention maps of feature maps. By enhancing the correlation features between all channel maps through global average pooling in channel directions, channel attention maps are obtained. Finally, the output and input features of the two attention maps are fused and summarized, further improving the feature representation and significantly improving the segmentation results. The results of ablation experiments indicate that bottlenecks and 3DJA can effectively improve the accuracy of building extraction, reduce model complexity, and improve the efficiency of network model training.

Conclusion

This article proposes a new network model, 3DJA-UNet3+, based on UNet3+. The spatial attention mechanism and channel attention mechanism are used to simultaneously model the feature associations of spatial and channel dimensions. Information fusion is performed in the upsampling stage. At the same time, the bottleneck module is used to replace the convolutional layer in the original network, reducing the complexity of the network and improving the model’s ability to extract targets in complex scenes.

The experimental results show that the proposed method is feasible in building extraction and has high extraction accuracy on the validation set. Compared with other deep learning models, the 3DJA-UNet3+ model showed good extraction performance in terms of accuracy, continuity, and completeness in building extraction. In the next step of research, the network will be improved for complex buildings and buildings with similar colors to the surrounding environment to improve the extraction performance of the network model in this situation.

P044 Impact of hospital environment on sleep during recovery from acquired brain injury: A systematic review
Authors: R Greenslade, N Hayes, K Barlow
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPeople with acquired brain injury (ABI) commonly report trouble sleeping. Sleep problems during acute admission are associated with delayed recovery and hospital discharge. The hospital environment influences sleep and may lead to chronic sleep problems and impact long-term outcome. We evaluated the current literature investigating the effect of environmental factors on sleep after ABI.MethodsSix database searches were performed with MeSH headings and keywords (PROSPERO CRD42023479979). Children and adults with ABI, all settings, exposures and interventions were included. Case reports, animal studies and populations with prenatal ABI, vascular dementia, genetic and neurodegenerative conditions were excluded.ResultsThe review identified 1302 articles with 610 duplicates, 692 abstracts were screened, and 114 full texts were assessed. Nine adult studies were included (n=581). Noise was ≥ 50dB and mean light levels 1.74-9 lux. There were 5.43 - 18.24 episodes of nursing care overnight. Sleep quality was poor; sleep efficiency range 72-73%, wake bouts range 21-41, and wake after sleep onset range 72-83 minutes. Four hospital sleep hygiene initiatives (n=340) improved sleep quality, quantity and decreased sedative use. Nursing practices of clustering cares and safeguarding sleep times promoted sleep.DiscussionRelatively few studies have explored the effect of the environment on sleep after ABI and none included children. Sleep in can be improved, however sleep still receives little attention. Standardized ieMR tools, policy to support practice and increased clinical education may help.

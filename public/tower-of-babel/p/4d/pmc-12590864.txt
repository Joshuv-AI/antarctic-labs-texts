Comparative analysis of exogenously applied synthetic auxin for fruit drop management and quality enhancement in date palm
Authors: Hammad Hussain, Rashad Qadri, Abdul Haseeb Ahmed, Muhammad Awais, Yong Xu, Mengjuan Bai, Guo Wei, Fatemeh Gholizadeh, Hamza Sohail
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFruit drop and quality deterioration are major constraints limiting date palm (Phoenix dactylifera L.) productivity, particularly under environmental constraints. While plant growth regulators (PGRs), particularly synthetic auxins like 2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D), have demonstrated potential in managing these physiological limitations, cultivar-specific responses and optimal application protocols remain insufficiently characterized.ResultsThis study evaluated the impact of foliar-applied 2,4-D at three concentrations (25, 50, and 75 mg/L) on fruit retention and quality traits in two date palm cultivars under field conditions. Application of 2,4-D, particularly at 50 mg/L during the kimri stage, significantly improved key agronomic parameters, including bunch weight, fruit and pulp weight, fruit length and width, and moisture content. Fruit quality parameters such as total soluble solids (TSS), total sugars, reducing and non-reducing sugars, ascorbic acid, and tannins were also enhanced. Furthermore, antioxidative enzymes peroxidase and catalase, flavonoids, and phenolic content were significantly higher compared to the untreated control. Among the tested cultivars, differential responses were observed, with one cultivar consistently showing superior outcomes in terms of fruit quality.ConclusionThe exogenous application of 2,4-D demonstrated a positive effect on reducing fruit drop and enhancing both physical and quality traits in date palm cultivars. These findings suggest that 2,4-D can serve as an effective tool in sustainable date palm production by improving yield and fruit quality under stress conditions.

Background

The date palm is an important fruit crop in arid and semi-arid regions, serving as a cornerstone of the agricultural economy in the Middle East, North Africa, and other date-producing regions [1]. Its fruits are highly valued for their rich nutritional content, including sugars, fiber, vitamins, and minerals, making them a critical component of local diets and food security systems [2]. Despite its inherent adaptability to harsh climatic conditions, date palm cultivation faces several persistent challenges, such as poor pollination, low fruit set, premature fruit drop, and poor ripening, all of which adversely affect fruit yield, quality, and marketability [3]. These issues are further exacerbated by climatic stressors such as extreme temperature, drought, and fluctuating humidity, which disrupt hormonal balance and key physiological processes. In particular, hormonal imbalances involving ethylene and auxin are closely linked to elevated rates of fruit abscission. Cultivars with high endogenous ethylene production are particularly prone to fruit drop and may require external intervention to maintain yield [4].

To address these physiological issues, the use of PGRs, particularly synthetic auxins such as 2,4-D, has gained attention in recent years. 2–4-D is widely utilized in fruit crops for promoting fruit set, preventing abscission, and promoting fruit growth, with notable applications reported in citrus [5], apple [6], mango [7], olive [8], and strawberry [9]. The effectiveness of 2,4-D in fruit crops is largely attributed to its ability to inhibit the formation of the abscission layer, thereby reducing premature fruit drop. 2,4-D also plays a critical role in influencing carbohydrate partitioning within plants, which not only enhances sugar accumulation in developing fruits but also additionally contributes to improved fruit size [10]. The effectiveness of 2,4-D in improving fruit retention and quality in date palms depends on several key factors, including the time of application, concentration, and cultivar-specific responses. Optimal timing and dosages are crucial, as excessive concentrations can lead to phytotoxic effects, deformities, or delayed ripening. Previous studies have reported significant improvements in fruit retention and quality in cultivars such as ‘Medjool’ and ‘Deglet Noor’ following 2,4-D treatment [11]. Similarly, in ‘Rothana’ and ‘Ghur’ cultivars, 2,4-D application significantly improved fruit and pulp weight; however, inadequate ripening or fruit deformities were observed, particularly at higher doses [12]. These varied outcomes highlight the considerable genetic diversity among date palm cultivars, which greatly influences their biochemical and physiological responses to PGRs. Some cultivars exhibit strong upregulation of auxin-responsive genes, improving fruit retention and development, while others may have limited responsiveness due to differences in receptor affinity, hormonal metabolism, or faster degradation of exogenous auxin [13]. Despite the potential benefits of 2,4-D, a significant knowledge gap remains, as most studies have focused on a single cultivar, and comparative assessment across different commercial varieties remains limited. This study investigates how two commercial date palm cultivars respond differently to foliar applications of 2,4-D, focusing on variations in their physical and biochemical characteristics.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The experiment was conducted at the Post Graduate Agricultural Research Station (PARS), University of Agriculture, Faisalabad, Pakistan (31.4503662° N, 73.1349605° E), characterized by a semi-arid climate. During 2019–2020, the region experienced a mean annual maximum temperature of 36.13°C and a mean annual minimum of 23.86°C. June was the hottest month, with average maximum temperatures reaching 46.84°C, while January was the coldest, with average minimum temperatures of 10.47°C. The mean annual precipitation was 22.8 mm, and the average relative humidity was 34.33%, indicating predominantly hot and dry climatic conditions (University of Agriculture Faisalabad Agromet Bulletin, https://web.uaf.edu.pk/Main/AgrometBuletin?iYear=2019). Two commercial date palm cultivars, ‘Hillavi’ (V1) and ‘Khudravi’ (V2), both 15 years old and uniformly maintained, were selected. Trees were spaced 8 × 8 m in a square layout, and standard horticultural practices were applied. The study followed an RCBD with three replications and four treatments: control (distilled water) and 2,4-D at 25, 50, and 75 mg/L. Foliar applications were made at the early Kimri stage, coinciding with the "depressed period" (15–16 weeks post-pollination), described by Awad [14] as a physiologically active stage during the early second sub-stage of Kimri in date palm cultivars, using 0.01% Tween-20 as a surfactant and 250 mL per bunch via handheld sprayer for uniform coverage.

Assessment of morphological attributes

Prior to harvest, ten fruiting strands were randomly selected, and tagging was done for each bunch. The total number of fruits was recorded per strand.

Moisture content was measured using the subsequent formula:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Moisture\ content (\%)=\frac{Fresh\ weight - Dry\ weight}{Fresh\ weight} \times 100$$\end{document}

Ten fruits were sampled from each selected strand. Fruit dimensions, including length and diameter, were measured using a digital vernier caliper. Fresh fruit weight was recorded using an electronic balance.

Determination of TSS, ascorbic acid, and titratable acidity

TSS was measured using a digital refractometer (ATAGO RS-5000, Japan) and reported in degrees Brix (°Brix). Ascorbic acid levels were quantified based on the protocol described by Shafique et al. [15]. Titratable acidity was determined by titrating the fruit juice with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide solution, using phenolphthalein as an end-point indicator, and the results were expressed as citric acid equivalents, following the method of Sadler and Murphy [16].

Determination of sugars

Fresh date pulp (3 g) was homogenized in 100 mL distilled water and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 5 min to obtain the supernatant. Total sugar content was estimated using the phenol–sulfuric acid method by reacting 2 mL of the sample supernatant with 0.05 mL of 80% phenol and 5 mL of concentrated sulfuric acid [17]. After vortexing and sequential incubation at room temperature (10 min) and in a 25 °C water bath (10 min), absorbance was measured at 490 nm using glucose as the standard. Reducing sugars were quantified via the DNS method by reacting 0.2 mL extract (diluted to 1 mL) with 2 mL DNS reagent, incubating at 80 °C for 30 min, and measuring absorbance at 575 nm after cooling [18]. The amount of non-reducing sugars was determined by deducting the reducing sugar content from the total sugar content.

Determination of enzymatic activities

Fresh date pulp (2 g) was ground in 5 mL of chilled 100 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) containing 1% PVP and 1 mM EDTA. The mixture was centrifuged at 15,000 × g for 10 min at 25 °C, and the supernatant was collected for enzyme assays. Peroxidase activity was determined by monitoring guaiacol oxidation at 470 nm following Hemeda and Klein [19], while catalase activity was assessed by measuring the reduction in absorbance at 240 nm due to hydrogen peroxide breakdown [20].

Determination of phenolics, flavonoids and tannins

Total phenolic content (TPC) was assessed using the Folin–Ciocalteu method, as outlined by Singleton et al. [21] and Eberhardt et al. [22]. For extraction, 2 g of fresh date pulp was homogenized in 10 mL of methanol and left at room temperature for 24 h. The extract was diluted 1:5 with distilled water, then reacted with 125 µL of Folin–Ciocalteu reagent. After 3 min, 1.25 mL of 70 g/L sodium carbonate was added, and the volume was adjusted to 3 mL. Samples were incubated at room temperature for 90 min, and absorbance was measured at 760 nm. Results were expressed as mg gallic acid equivalents (GAE) per 100 g fresh weight.

Total flavonoids were quantified following the method of Odeh et al. [23] with minor adjustments. A 250 µL aliquot of extract was mixed with 75 µL of 5% NaNO₂, kept in the dark for 5 min, followed by the addition of 75 µL of 10% AlCl₃. After vortexing, 500 µL of 1 M NaOH was added, and the total volume was adjusted to 2.5 mL with distilled water. Absorbance was recorded at 510 nm.

Tannin levels were estimated using the vanillin-HCl assay [24]. The reaction mixture consisted of 0.4 mL extract or catechin standard, 1 mL of 4% vanillin in ethanol, and 37% HCl. After a 15-min incubation in the dark at room temperature, absorbance was measured at 500 nm. Tannin content was expressed in mg per 100 g fresh weight.

Statistical analysis

The experiment followed a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replicates. Data were analyzed using two-factor factorial analysis of variance ANOVA in Statistix 8.1 to assess treatment effects, and mean differences were compared using the Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) test at a 5% significance level, as described by Steel et al. (1980).

Correlation analysis

The correlation analysis across both varieties revealed several significant interrelationships among fruit quality, biochemical traits, and antioxidant parameters (Fig. 6A-B). In variety V1, fruit weight was positively correlated with pulp weight (r = 0.91), total sugars (r = 0.88), and moisture content (r = 0.82), suggesting that increased fruit size is associated with greater pulp biomass and enhanced sugar and water retention. Total sugars also exhibited strong positive correlations with non-reducing sugars (r = 0.94), reducing sugars (r = 0.90), and peroxidase activity (r = 0.89), reflecting coordinated sugar metabolism and its linkage to antioxidant activity. Ascorbic acid in V1 was significantly correlated with POD (r = 0.85) and catalase activity (r = 0.78), indicating its association with enzymatic antioxidant defense. Polyphenolic compounds flavonoids, phenols, and tannins showed strong intercorrelation (r > 0.88), and were also positively related to POD and CAT activities, highlighting a synergistic antioxidant mechanism. In contrast, fruit drop was negatively correlated with total sugars (r = –0.75), ascorbic acid (r = –0.70), and POD (r = –0.66), indicating that lower sugar and antioxidant reserves were associated with higher fruit abscission. Titratable acidity exhibited negative correlations with total sugars (r = –0.68), flavonoids (r = –0.80) and ascorbic acid (r = –0.60), indicating a potential trade-off between acidity and nutritional quality. Similar trends were observed in V2, although correlation magnitudes varied. Discussion

Fruit drop represents a major economic challenge in horticulture, with up to 50% of flowers and immature fruits aborting prematurely due to environmental and physiological stresses. In date palms, this issue is particularly critical and can be attributed to a complex interplay of genetic, physiological, and environmental factors. Among these, nutrient deficiency and nutrient imbalance are considered major physiological causes contributing to the elevated rate of fruit drop [25]. A key hormonal imbalance implicated in fruit drop involves the interaction between auxin and ethylene. Ethylene, a plant hormone known to regulate abscission, typically rises in response to stress, triggering the formation of the abscission layer by activating hydrolytic enzymes such as cellulose and polygalacturonase. These enzymes degrade the cell wall at the fruit-pedicel attachment, leading to fruit detachment. In contrast, auxin acts to counteract ethylene’s effects by maintaining cell wall integrity and delaying abscission. A decline in auxin level favors ethylene dominance, tipping the balance towards fruit drop [4].

To address this hormonal imbalance, the current study explored the impact of 2,4-D in mitigating fruit abscission. The foliar application of 2,4-D significantly reduced fruit drop and improved morphological and biochemical attributes in date palm cultivars; however, foliar administration at 50 mg/L observed the most notable results. In contrast, Al-Qurashi et al. [12] reported that 75 mg/L 2,4-D was more effective in reducing fruit drop and enhancing fruit set in date palm. Such variation between studies may be attributed to differences in cultivar responsiveness, timing of application, and environmental conditions, all of which are known to influence auxin sensitivity and efficacy. The comparatively lower performance of 75 mg/L in our study may be related to dose-dependent auxin effects. Excessive concentrations of synthetic auxins are known to induce phytotoxicity, including abnormal fruit growth, delayed ripening, or metabolic imbalance, which can offset their positive role in fruit retention. Another possible explanation is receptor saturation, where auxin signaling is maximized at lower concentrations and higher doses disrupt hormonal homeostasis, leading to reduced efficacy compared with 50 mg/L.

The present study revealed notable differences in cultivar responsiveness to 2,4-D treatment, with 'Khudravi' (V₂) demonstrating superior performance compared to 'Hillavi' (V₁) across multiple parameters. Previous studies have reported that cultivar-level differences may arise from variations in ethylene sensitivity, auxin metabolism genes controlling root architecture, or stress-related transcription factors [26], which could partly explain the observed advantage of V₂. These genetic variations may influence auxin receptor expression, signal transduction efficiency, or endogenous hormone levels, ultimately affecting the plant's response to exogenous 2,4-D application.

The current study found that the foliar administration of 2,4-D significantly reduced fruit drop and improved morphological and biochemical attributes in date palm cultivars; however, the foliar administration of 2,4-D at 50 mg/L stood out among all the treatments. One possible mechanism behind 2,4-D efficacy in mitigating fruit drop is its ability to suppress ethylene biosynthesis by downregulating the expression of the key enzyme ACO gene (1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid oxidase), which is involved in ethylene production in the abscission zone [27]. By lowering ethylene levels, 2,4-D helps prevent the formation of the abscission layer, thereby retaining more fruits on the plant. Our results regarding fruit mitigation are in alignment with studies conducted on Satsuma mandarin [28], blue honeysuckle [27], and hazelnut [29]. In addition to mitigating fruit drop, 2,4-D stimulates key processes in fruit development, such as cell division, which contributes directly to fruit growth and overall development. Furthermore, 2,4-D improves source-sink relationships by directing more carbohydrates and nutrients from photosynthetic tissues (source) to developing fruits (sink), thereby supporting better fruit growth [30]. These physiological processes collectively contributed to improved fruit dimensions and weight. Our results regarding improvement in fruit dimensions and weight are in accordance with the previous studies [31, 32].

TSS content is a key indicator of fruit sweetness and quality, both of which are important factors influencing consumer acceptability, particularly in fruits like date palm. Previous studies, such as [33], have revealed that foliar application of 2,4-D significantly enhances TSS contents, likely due to its influence on carbohydrate biosynthesis and hormonal regulation of sugar translocation. The foliar administration of 2,4-D may redirect plant secondary metabolites towards developing fruits, potentially stimulating the hydrolysis of starch into simple sugars, thereby increasing TSS levels. Our results regarding the positive influence of 2,4-D on TSS contents are aligned with the previous studies [33, 34]. The observed decrease in titratable acidity following 2,4-D application is attributed to the natural breakdown of organic acids during fruit maturation, resulting in increased sugar content and a decrease in fruit acidity [35]. In our study, 2,4-D might have further influenced this process by promoting starch accumulation and subsequently modulating the activity of key enzymes in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. This hormonal regulation could have optimized the balance between starch hydrolysis and the TCA cycle, resulting in higher sugar content and lower acidity. Similar mechanisms have been reported by Fenn and Giovannoni [30], who described the coordinated regulation of sugar metabolism and acid degradation during fruit development.

Our results demonstrated that the application of 2,4-D significantly improved the sugar content in date palm fruits. This aligns with previous findings that highlight the influence of phytohormones and mineral nutrients on sugar metabolism in fruits [36]. Sugar accumulation in fruit is primarily governed by the activity and interaction of sugar-metabolizing enzymes, among which sucrose-phosphate synthase (SPS) plays a key role by catalyzing sucrose biosynthesis. In our study, the increase in sugar content following 2,4-D application may be attributed to the upregulation of the SPS gene, which is known to be positively associated with sugar accumulation. This is supported by earlier studies suggesting the crucial role of 2,4-D in enhancing sucrose levels in fruits [25, 36].

Phenolics and flavonoids are known not only for their essential roles in stress resistance of plant tissues but also for their significant contribution to human health and antioxidant capacity of fruits [37]. In our study, the increase in the phenolic and flavonoid content might be attributed to the exogenous application of 2,4-D potentially enhancing the activity of key enzymes such as phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL), which plays a central role in phenolic biosynthesis, and by inhibiting the degradation of chalcone synthase (CHS), which is a key enzyme involved in the biosynthesis of flavonoids. Our results regarding the positive influence of 2,4-D in enhancing phenolics and flavonoids are comparable to the previous findings [5, 38]. The observed increase in antioxidant activity following 2,4-D foliar administration might be attributed to the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This ROS accumulation is known to trigger antioxidant defense mechanisms, as demonstrated in 2,4-D treated mung beans, peas, mango fruit under chilling injury, and potato plants infected with Alternaria solani [31].

Conclusion

This study investigates the effectiveness of exogenously applied 2,4-D in reducing fruit drop and enhancing fruit quality in two date palm cultivars, V₁ and V₂. The results demonstrate that 2,4-D application, particularly at 50 mg/L, significantly reduced fruit drop and improved key morphological and biochemical traits, including fruit weight, pulp weight, total sugars, and antioxidant enzyme activities. These improvements were more pronounced in cultivar V₂, highlighting the cultivar-specific response to 2,4-D. The application of 2,4-D can be considered a viable strategy for mitigating fruit drop and enhancing fruit quality in date palms. Future research should explore the underlying molecular mechanisms and optimize application protocols to further enhance date palm production.

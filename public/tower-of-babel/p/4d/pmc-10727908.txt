Combination Therapy with Clindamycin and Rifampicin for Severe Truncal Acne: A Case Report
Authors: Jin Soo Kim, Byeong Hak Seo, Doo Rae Cha, Ho Seok Suh, Yu Sung Choi
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

A 29-year-old woman presented with a one-year history of several diffuse inflammatory nodules and cysts with overlying crusts on the anterior chest and back, that were accompanied by pain and pruritus (Fig. 1A). She had mild facial acne without truncal involvement, but truncal lesions developed during her last 3 months of pregnancy. The lesions worsened after her delivery. She had no previous history of pregnancy, significant underlying comorbidities, and the laboratory test results were within the normal range. Bacterial, fungus, acid-fast bacilli culture from pustule showed no growth. The patient was diagnosed with severe truncal acne. The baseline scores based on the Investigator’s Global Assessment and Comprehensive Acne Severity Scale were both 5. The patient was treated with isotretinoin for two months, but the lesions and her symptoms showed no significant improvement (Fig. 1B). Isotretinoin was switched to combination therapy with clindamycin 300 mg twice daily and rifampin 600 mg once daily. After two months of treatment, the lesions significantly improved, and there were no treatment-related complaints (Fig. 1C).

(A) Generalized, multiple, inflammatory nodulocysts, and pustules with overlying crusts on the anterior chest and back. (B) Clinical image after eight weeks of isotretinoin treatment. (C) Clinical photography after eight weeks of clindamycin and rifampin treatment. We received the patient’s consent form about publishing all photographic materials.

Truncal acne is an inflammatory skin disorder of the pilosebaceous units. It affects patients from all age groups, and it has been observed in more than half of patients with acne. It results in scarring and negatively impacts the patient’s self-esteem. Despite this, it is frequently overlooked in clinical practice, and the information on the characteristics of truncal acne is limited1. The pathophysiology is somewhat complex. Sebum secretion is the main etiology of acne vulgaris (AV). However, it has a minor role in truncal acne due to the relatively low amount of sebum secretion2. In the present case, multiple hormonal changes during pregnancy likely influenced the development of the lesions3. Although several treatment guidelines for AV are available, there are no guidelines specifically for truncal acne treatment1. Thus, the treatment of truncal acne has been similar to that of facial acne. The treatment options include topical agents, such as benzoyl peroxide, azelaic acids, and retinoids, as well as oral agents, such as isotretinoin, tetracycline, and spironolactone1. A major challenge for truncal acne treatment is the potential for severe and extensive body surface involvement, complicating topical treatments, as seen in the present case. Oral isotretinoin is the most potent drug that is used as a last resort. However, the treatment for cases, refractory to oral isotretinoin, remains uninvestigated. Spironolactone was reportedly a safe option, but it has not been approved for acne treatment. Moreover, it takes time to be effective dose modifications are necessary4.

Combination therapy with clindamycin and rifampin is commonly used to treat moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa as a first line therapy. Although mechanism has not been fully elucidated, anti-bacterial and anti-inflammatory effects of the drugs seems to play a key role5. Recent studies implicate that rifampin can lower the concentration of clindamycin, but efficacy is proven in literature and being widely used in clinical practice6,7. Based on the clinical course of our patient, combination therapy with clindamycin and rifampin was a viable treatment option for severe truncal acne. Moreover, it suggested another underlying mechanism that has yet to be investigated.

We reported a case of severe truncal acne, successfully treated with clindamycin and rifampin. Given that there are no clinical guidelines regarding the treatment when failed on oral isotretinoin, combination therapy with clindamycin and rifampin seems to be fast acting and effective alternative treatment option for patients with severe truncal acne.

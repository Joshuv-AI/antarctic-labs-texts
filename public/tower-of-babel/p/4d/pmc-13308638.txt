Comparison of clinical and patient-reported outcomes of contralateral topography-guided vs wavefront-guided LASIK
Authors: Nicholas E. Dudenhoefer, Samantha B. Rodgers, Charisma B. Evangelista, Gary L. Legault, Samuel S. Chen, Paul D. Kohler, José E. Capó-Aponte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Topography-guided LASIK showed a better efficacy index and mean spherical equivalent, while wavefront-guided LASIK had superior low-contrast visual acuity at postoperative month 6. Both procedures yielded similar rates of self-reported satisfaction and visual symptoms.

Laser in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) is a widely performed elective surgical procedure approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to treat myopia and astigmatism. In most LASIK procedures performed in the United States, the flap is created with a femtosecond laser, followed by ablation of the underlying stromal bed.1,2 Reshaping the corneal surface allows visual images to be focused more precisely on the retina. LASIK is known for its minimal discomfort, rapid recovery of visual acuity, low wound-healing response, and low complication rate.3

Topography-guided (TG) LASIK using Contoura with Phorcides (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.) and wavefront-guided (WFG) LASIK using iDesign 2.0 Advance WaveScan Refractive Studio (Johnson & Johnson Vision) are currently among the most advanced ablation calculation methods available to improve LASIK refractive outcomes. WFG treatments account for aberrations arising from the cornea, lens, and retina, whereas TG treatments aim to regularize the uneven corneal surface to achieve the desired refractive result.4

Contoura is the latest TG ablation profile and individualizes the laser ablation pattern accounting for corneal higher-order aberrations (HOAs) and surface irregularities of the anterior cornea.5 The Phorcides Analytic Engine (Phorcides LLC) is a software program that can be used in conjunction with Contoura to assist in generating an objective treatment recommendation for sphere and cylinder.6–8 The iDesign 2.0 is a next-generation, multimodal diagnostic and ablation planning system that builds on iDesign's wavefront-guided foundation by integrating corneal topography and other ocular parameters resulting in more accurate, individualized LASIK treatments and improved outcomes.9,10

Both Contoura with Phorcides and iDesign 2.0 are FDA-approved ablation treatment profiles routinely used as standard of care to customize LASIK treatment. Currently, to our knowledge, no prospective studies have directly compared visual and patient-reported outcomes between these advanced ablation profiles. In this study, we investigated 6-month visual outcomes, HOAs, and visual symptoms in participants undergoing contralateral TG-LASIK using Contoura with Phorcides vs WFG-LASIK using iDesign 2.0.

METHODS

This randomized, double-blind, contralateral-eye study was conducted at the Joint Warfighter Refractive Surgery Center, Lackland Air Force Base, Texas. The protocol was approved by the San Antonio Institutional Review Board and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07128316).

Inclusion criteria were military beneficiaries aged 21 to 50 years, myopia between −2.00 diopters (D) and −8.00 D, anisometropia ≤1.50 D, astigmatism ≤3.00 D, interocular astigmatism difference of ≤0.75 D, and a total spherical equivalent (SEQ) of ≤−9.00 D. Exclusion criteria included abnormal corneal findings; active ocular disease; active collagen vascular, connective tissue, autoimmune, or immunodeficiency disorders; or systemic conditions likely to affect wound healing, such as diabetes or severe atopy. Pregnant or nursing women were also excluded.

All participants underwent a standard preoperative evaluation and LASIK counseling, including review of ocular/medical history, slitlamp examination with tonometry, and dilated fundus examination. Tear production was measured with the Schirmer test. Corneal tomography and pachymetry were obtained using the Pentacam HR (Oculus Surgical, Inc.). Monocular UDVA and CDVA were measured with Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) charts on the CP-690E projector (Marco). Low-contrast (LC) 5% UDVA and CDVA were assessed using ETDRS charts in an illuminated cabinet (Precision Vision). Manifest and cycloplegic refractions were obtained with the NIDEK RT-5100 phoropter (Marco).

The iDesign 2.0 system measured higher-order aberrations (coma, trefoil, spherical aberration, and total HOAs) and generated the ablation profile for WFG-LASIK. The WaveLight TOPOLYZER Vario (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.) captured corneal topography and generated the ablation map for TG-LASIK. Phorcides was used to optimize Contoura TG-LASIK ablation planning.

The Patient-Reported Outcomes with LASIK—Symptoms and Satisfaction (PROWL-SS) and the Standard Patient Evaluation of Eye Dryness (SPEED) questionnaires were administered to assess self-reported quality of vision and dry eye symptoms, respectively. Questionnaires were completed for each eye individually.

Each participant was randomized by a computer to determine which eye received TG-LASIK, with the contralateral eye undergoing WFG-LASIK. The right eye was always treated first. Corneal flaps were created with a superior hinge using the Zeiss VisuMax 500 femtosecond laser (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG) with an 8.1 mm diameter and 105 μm thickness. TG ablations were performed on the WaveLight EX500 guided by Contoura with Phorcides, while WFG ablations were performed on the VISX STAR S4 IR (Abbott Medical Optics, Inc.) guided by iDesign 2.0. Postoperative care included moxifloxacin 0.5%, prednisolone acetate 1%, and preservative-free carboxymethylcellulose sodium 0.5% drops.

Postoperative assessments were performed on postoperative day 1 (POD1), week 2 (POW2), month 1 (POM1), month 3 (POM3), and month 6 (POM6). At POD1, only UDVA and slitlamp examinations were performed. At subsequent visits, UDVA, CDVA, LC UDVA, LC CDVA, manifest refraction, Schirmer test, and slitlamp with tonometry were repeated. The PROWL-SS and SPEED questionnaires were administered at POM1, POM3, and POM6, and HOAs were measured with iDesign at POM6.

Statistical Analysis

The sample size was calculated based on the primary outcome: the proportion of eyes achieving a UDVA of 20/16 or better. Since each participant received both treatments, the McNemar test for matched pairs was used to determine the required sample size. Assuming a 2-sided α of 0.05 and a power of 80%, a minimum of 60 participants was needed to detect a statistically significant difference. To account for up to 10% attrition due to loss to follow-up, 66 participants (132 eyes total) were enrolled.

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 (Graphpad Software, Inc.). Standard 9-graph figures were created with Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corp.). The percentage of eyes achieving a visual acuity of 20/16 or better was analyzed using the McNemar test for paired proportions. Normality was evaluated with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Continuous data are reported as mean ± SD and analyzed using paired t tests (normal distribution) or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (nonnormal distribution). Categorical data are summarized as percentages and analyzed using chi-squared or Fisher exact tests, as appropriate. Visual acuity was converted to logMAR before analysis. HOAs were normalized to a 5 mm pupil using the iDesign. All P values for comparisons between timepoints were adjusted using a Bonferroni correction to account for multiple comparisons and minimize the risk for a type I error. Significance was set at a P < .05.

RESULTS

Sixty-six participants were enrolled, randomized, and treated contralaterally with TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK. Three participants (4.5%) were lost to follow-up. Data are presented for the 126 eyes from 63 participants who completed POM6 (mean age: 35.52 ± 7.92 years; range: 21 to 50 years; 73% female, 27% male). TG-LASIK was performed in the dominant eye for 52% of eyes (n = 33) and WFG-LASIK for 48% (n = 30). No significant preoperative differences were observed between TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK eyes (Table 1).

Efficiency

The primary outcome was the percentage of eyes achieving UDVA 20/16 or better at POM6. More WFG-LASIK eyes reached this benchmark than TG-LASIK eyes (54% vs 46%), but the difference was not statistically significant (P = .48) (Figures 1A and 2A). Both procedures significantly improved mean UDVA starting at POD1, with no significant between-group differences at any timepoint (Figure 3A, Table 1). Although both procedures demonstrated excellent efficiency indices, TG-LASIK had a significantly higher efficiency index compared with WFG-LASIK (TG-LASIK: 1.08 ± 0.21; WFG-LASIK: 1.02 ± 0.17; P = .03).

Safety

Compared with preoperative CDVA, 67% of TG-LASIK eyes and 79% of WFG-LASIK eyes achieved the same or better ETDRS line at POM6 (Figures 1B and 2B). UDVA within 1 line of preoperative CDVA was achieved in 89% of TG-LASIK and 92% of WFG-LASIK eyes; differences were not significant (P = .63 and P = .08, respectively). At POM6, 90% of TG-LASIK and 96% of WFG-LASIK eyes maintained or gained CDVA lines (P = .16) (Figures 1C and 2C). Safety indices were similar (TG-LASIK: 0.93 ± 0.12; WFG-LASIK: 0.91 ± 0.11; P = .07). The central ablation depth was higher for WFG-LASIK (TG-LASIK 65.19 ± 20.78 μm; WFG-LASIK 66.62 ± 20.28 μm; P = .17), and the residual stromal bed was higher for TG-LASIK, but these were not statistically significant (TG-LASIK 379.80 ± 28.78 μm; WFG-LASIK 378.20 ± 29.41 μm; P = .09).

Accuracy

The correlation between attempted and achieved SEQ was high for both TG-LASIK (R2 = 0.9934) and WFG-LASIK (R2 = 0.9931) (Figures 1D and 2D). TG-LASIK eyes had a slightly better mean SEQ at POM6 (0.04 ± 0.30 D vs 0.13 ± 0.29 D; P = .04), although the percentage of eyes within ±0.50 D (92% vs 94%; P = .78) or ±1.00 D (100% vs 98%; P = .62) was similar (Table 1). Residual SEQ change >0.50 D at POM6 was comparable (TG-LASIK: 3.2%; WFG-LASIK: 6.3%; P = .50) (Figures 1E and 2E).

Astigmatism

Residual astigmatism at POM6 is compared in Figures 1G and 2G. There was no significant between-group difference in the percentage of eyes achieving ≤0.50 D and ≤1.00 D of residual cylinder at POM6 (P = .72 and P = .50, respectively). However, WFG-LASIK eyes demonstrated significantly lower mean residual astigmatism at POW2 (P = .05), POM3 (P = .03), and POM6 (P = .04) (Table 1).

Figures 1H and 2H compare target-induced astigmatism (TIA) vs surgery-induced astigmatism (SIA) at POM6. TIA is defined as the vectorial difference between the intended zero postoperative astigmatism and the preoperative astigmatism correction vector. SIA is the vector difference between postoperative and preoperative astigmatism. A strong correlation was observed between TIA and SIA for both procedures: TG-LASIK (R2 = 0.9508) and WFG-LASIK (R2 = 0.9662). We also found that 78% of TG-LASIK eyes and 84% of WFG-LASIK eyes were within 15 degrees of the intended axis, with no statistically significant difference (P = .37) (Figures 1I and 2I).

5% LC Visual Acuity

At POW2 and POM1, there was no significant between-group difference in LC UDVA; however, WFG-LASIK eyes demonstrated significantly better LC CDVA (P = .04 and P = .01, respectively). Both LC UDVA and LC CDVA were significantly better in WFG-LASIK eyes at POM3 (both P = .02) and POM6 (both P = .02) (Figure 3B and Table 1).

Higher-Order Aberrations

Figure 4A shows that both TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK tended to increase HOA root mean square (RMS) relative to preoperative values, but only TG-LASIK showed a statistically significant increase (TG-LASIK: 0.114 to 0.129 μm, P = .02; WFG-LASIK: 0.117 to 0.128 μm, P = .41). However, at POM6, there was no statistically significant difference in residual HOA RMS between the groups (P = .36). Both procedures significantly increased coma at POM6 compared with baseline (TG-LASIK: 0.056 to 0.080 μm, P < .01; WFG-LASIK: 0.057 to 0.080 μm, P < .01); however, there was no statistically significant difference between the 2 procedures at POM6 (P = .75) (Figure 4B). Figure 4C shows a tendency for decreased trefoil at POM6 compared with preoperative data, but these changes were not statistically significant (TG-LASIK: P = .52; WFG-LASIK: P = .16). In addition, there was no statistically significant difference between the procedures at POM6 (P = .18). Figure 4D shows that both procedures demonstrated a significant decrease in spherical aberration at POM6 compared with baseline (both P < .001). However, there was no statistically significant difference in the amount of residual spherical aberration between the groups at POM6 (P = .56).

Satisfaction With Vision and Visual Symptoms

The PROWL-SS questionnaire showed that satisfaction with vision was significantly improved at all postoperative timepoints for both treatment modalities compared with preoperative responses (P < .001; Figure 5A). At POM1, satisfaction with vision was significantly higher in the WFG-LASIK group than in the TG-LASIK group (1.46 ± 0.59 vs 1.65 ± 0.68; P = .03). However, no statistically significant differences were observed between the groups at POM3 (P = .59) or POM6 (P = .23).

The PROWL-SS responses also showed comparable symptom frequency between groups, with no statistically significant differences in self-reported visual disturbances such as double image, glare, halos, or starbursts (Figure 5B–E). By POM6, all visual symptoms had improved to levels below preoperative baselines in both groups: double image (TG-LASIK, P < .001; WFG-LASIK, P < .001), glare (TG-LASIK, P < .01; WFG-LASIK, P < .01), halos (TG-LASIK, P < .01; WFG-LASIK, P < .001), and starbursts (TG-LASIK, P = .01; WFG-LASIK, P < .001).

DISCUSSION

Although TG-LASIK using Contoura with Phorcides on the WaveLight EX500 and WFG-LASIK using iDesign 2.0 on the VISX STAR S4 IR are 2 of the most advanced customized treatments, direct comparisons are limited. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first prospective study to assess visual outcomes and patient-reported symptoms after contralateral TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK using these advanced technologies. Overall, both ablation treatment profiles demonstrated excellent clinical results regarding visual outcomes and quality of vision.

At POM6, there was no significant difference in the proportion of eyes achieving UDVA 20/16 or better, or in mean UDVA at any timepoint. In fact, mean UDVA remained better than 0.0 logMAR (20/20) for both groups across all visits, consistent with previous retrospective studies.11,12 Although Lu et al. reported more eyes reaching 20/16 with WFG-LASIK, their study used a slightly different methodology. The study lacked Phorcides planning, featured an older Alcon TG excimer platform, and had fewer participants.

Although both procedures demonstrated excellent efficiency indices, TG-LASIK showed a significantly higher efficiency index than WFG-LASIK. A retrospective study by Moshirfar et al. suggested better functional outcomes with TG-LASIK in UDVA, although it did not explicitly report an efficiency index.11 Similarly, Schallhorn et al. reviewed FDA premarket approval studies of SMILE, TG-LASIK, and WFG-LASIK, finding all 3 techniques achieved excellent visual outcomes for the intended correction, with no clear superiority in astigmatic correction.13 In addition, 2 prospective studies comparing keratorefractive lenticule extraction with TG-LASIK using Contoura with Phorcides, and with WFG-LASIK using iDesign, both reported a higher efficiency index for TG-LASIK than for WFG-LASIK (1.08 vs 1.01).14,15 These results are consistent with our findings of efficiency indices of 1.08 for TG-LASIK and 1.02 for WFG-LASIK. By contrast, both procedures demonstrated comparable safety indices. Although direct head-to-head comparisons of TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK are limited, previous studies have reported no cases of ≥2 lines of CDVA loss at POM12 with either platform. These findings indicate a strong safety profile for both iDesign and Contoura, with outcomes exceeding FDA safety thresholds.11–13 We observed a nonsignificant trend toward a deeper ablation and consequently thinner residual stromal bed with WFG-LASIK. This may reflect the additional tissue required to correct for internal ocular aberrations.16,17 However, the mean difference was clinically insignificant, with both techniques preserving a robust stromal bed well above established safety thresholds.

Starting at POM3, WFG-LASIK demonstrated statistically significantly better outcomes in 5% LC UDVA and 5% LC CDVA. This agrees with previous studies showing improved mesopic contrast sensitivity after iDesign treatment.11 By contrast, the same study reported that a higher proportion of eyes treated with Contoura achieved clinically significant improvements in photopic contrast sensitivity. In addition, Toda et al. conducted a prospective, randomized clinical trial comparing WFG-LASIK using iDesign with NIDEK TG ablation and found that WFG-LASIK provided superior contrast sensitivity under glare conditions at POM3.18 By contrast, Lu et al. found no difference in the percentage of eyes achieving 5% LC CDVA of 20/80 or better, or 20/63 or better, although mean LC CDVA values were not reported.12 Importantly, that study did not use the latest TG technology (Phorcides and WaveLight EX500). Overall, WFG-LASIK seems to offer statistically significant advantages in LC visual acuity compared with TG-LASIK, likely due to its ability to correct total ocular aberrations, including both corneal and internal HOAs, whereas TG treatment is limited to anterior corneal irregularities.6,7,11,18

Regarding refractive outcomes, our study demonstrated that TG-LASIK eyes had less residual SEQ than WFG-LASIK eyes, whereas WFG-LASIK eyes had statistically significantly lower mean residual manifest sphere and cylinder at POM6 compared with TG-LASIK. Our mean manifest sphere values (TG-LASIK: −0.11 ± 0.31 D; WFG-LASIK: 0.03 ± 0.31 D; P < .01) closely mirror those reported by Lu et al. at POM6 (TG-LASIK: −0.12 ± 0.27 D; WFG-LASIK: −0.04 ± 0.31 D; P = .17), although their study did not find a statistically significant difference. Consistent with their findings, we also observed lower manifest cylinder with WFG-LASIK, although in our case the difference reached statistical significance. This discrepancy may be attributable to differences in sample size or variations in the technology used.

We found no significant differences in residual HOA RMS, coma, trefoil, or spherical aberrations between TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK at POM6. These findings are consistent with those of Lu et al., who also reported no between-group differences at POM6.12 However, unlike their study, we observed a decrease in spherical aberrations for both treatments from preoperative to POM6. This discrepancy may be attributable to our use of the most current customization software and excimer laser technology.6,19 Previous evidence suggests that TG-LASIK performed with the Oculyzer II (an Alcon technology not available in the United States) in combination with the WaveLight EX500 induces fewer postoperative aberrations.20 Moussa et al. also demonstrated a significant reduction in spherical aberration with iDesign WFG-LASIK, and Chen et al. similarly reported that TG-LASIK induced less spherical aberration postoperatively.8,21

We found that both procedures yielded similar rates of self-reported satisfaction and visual symptoms. For both procedures, patient-reported satisfaction with vision improved significantly at all postoperative timepoints compared with preoperative levels, but no statistically significant difference was observed between the 2 groups at POM6. Reports of double vision and glare decreased significantly beginning at POM1, while halos and starbursts showed a significant reduction starting at POM3. No previous studies have evaluated patient-reported outcomes between TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK for comparison.

No statistically significant differences were found between groups in tear production or patient-reported dry eye symptoms. Because both eyes underwent femtosecond LASIK flap creation, differences in ablation calculation techniques were not expected to affect these parameters. It is well established that femtosecond flap creation severs corneal sensory nerves, leading to reduced corneal sensitivity, impaired blink reflex, and disruption of the neural feedback loop responsible for regulating tear production.22,23

A critical aspect of our findings is the distinction between statistical significance and clinical relevance. Although we found statistically significant differences in outcomes such as the efficacy index, residual spherical equivalent, and low-contrast visual acuity, the magnitude of these differences was small. For most of the patients with straightforward myopia, both platforms deliver excellent results, and these minor statistical variations would likely not translate into a perceptible difference in patient satisfaction or influence the surgeon's choice. However, these subtle advantages may become clinically meaningful in specific patient scenarios, guiding a more personalized treatment selection. For a patient whose primary complaint is poor night vision or whose profession demands high performance in low-light conditions (eg, pilots and truck drivers), the statistically superior low-contrast acuity offered by the WFG platform could make it the preferred choice. Conversely, in a patient with borderline corneal thickness or a high refractive error where tissue preservation is paramount, the trend toward a thicker residual stromal bed with TG-LASIK could be the deciding factor, making it the safer option. Finally, for a patient desiring the highest possible precision in hitting a plano target, the slightly higher efficacy index demonstrated by the TG-LASIK group might be advantageous. Therefore, although not impactful for every patient, these small, statistically significant differences can serve as valuable tie-breakers in a nuanced, patient-centric approach to surgical planning, allowing surgeons to tailor the technology to the individual's unique anatomy and visual needs.

The strengths of our study include its prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled design. The contralateral eye approach provided a major advantage by allowing each participant to serve as their own control, thereby minimizing variability from interindividual differences such as age, genetics, healing response, and environmental factors. This design enhances the precision of treatment comparisons, reduces potential confounders, and strengthens the internal validity of the findings. Furthermore, we used the most up-to-date customization and treatment platforms available. The absence of significant preoperative differences between treatment groups further supports the conclusion that postoperative differences are attributable to the interventions themselves. A key limitation is the comparison of 2 different laser ecosystems (TG on the Alcon EX500 vs WFG on the VISX S4), not just ablation profiles. As the platforms have different hardware (eg, laser frequency and beam profile), observed outcomes reflect the performance of the entire system. Although this prevents attributing effects solely to the guidance method, it provides a pragmatic comparison of 2 leading platforms because they are used clinically.

Another limitation of this study is that it was conducted at a military treatment facility serving exclusively military beneficiaries. Although participant demographics were comparable with the general population in age and refractive error, the higher proportion of female participants may limit generalizability. Future research should extend the follow-up period to 12 months and include a more gender-balanced cohort that better reflects the broader population.

In conclusion, both TG-LASIK and WFG-LASIK using the latest excimer and diagnostic platforms, Contoura with Phorcides on the WaveLight EX500 and iDesign 2.0 on the VISX STAR S4 IR, demonstrated excellent safety, efficiency, and patient satisfaction at 6 months postoperatively. Although TG-LASIK achieved a slightly higher efficiency index and less residual spherical equivalent, WFG-LASIK provided superior low-contrast visual acuity, likely reflecting its ability to correct total ocular aberrations including internal HOAs. Both procedures significantly improved UDVA, CDVA, and patient-reported quality of vision, with minimal induction of higher-order aberrations.

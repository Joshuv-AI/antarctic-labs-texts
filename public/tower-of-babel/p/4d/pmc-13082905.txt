Presentation of the protective epitope in a Chlamydia muridarum MOMP vaccine is dependent on γ-interferon-inducible lysosomal thiol reductase (GILT)
Authors: Sukumar Pal, Raymond M Johnson, Delia F Tifrea, Peter Cresswell, Luis M de la Maza
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Chlamydia outer membrane complex has approximately 20 cysteine-rich proteins, including the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) with 8 cysteines. The enzyme γ-interferon-inducible lysosomal thiol reductase (GILT) is expressed constitutively in lysosomes of antigen presenting cells and reproductive tract epithelial cells. GILT catalyzes disulfide bond reduction during protein degradation that generates T cell epitopes. To evaluate the role that GILT plays in chlamydial infections, C57BL/6 WT mice and mice deficient in Ifi30 (GILT-KO) were challenged vaginally with Chlamydia muridarum (Cm). Both were equally susceptible to the Cm infection and upper genital tract pathology. To assess GILT’s role in processing MOMP epitopes, WT and GILT-KO mice were immunized with native (nMOMP) or recombinant (rMOMP) antigens. In both strains vaccination with Cm nMOMP and rMOMP induced a similar spectrum of MOMP-specific antibodies. nMOMP immunization generated higher neutralizing antibody titers than rMOMP, but neutralizing titers did not differ between mouse strains. Splenocytes from vaccinated WT and GILT-KO mice recognized T cell epitopes in nMOMP, and to a lesser extent with rMOMP. No protection was seen in either WT or GILT-KO mice vaccinated with rMOMP based on intensity and duration of vaginal shedding, upper genital tract pathology, and fertility post-infection. nMOMP vaccination protected WT mice based on attenuated bacterial shedding and preserved fertility but nMOMP-vaccinated GILT-KO mice were not protected. The failure of nMOMP vaccination in GILT-KO mice revealed that GILT-dependent MOMP epitope processing was outcome determinative and identified a pan-serovar, likely protective, class II T cell epitope in MOMP constant domain 3.Keywords: Chlamydia vaccine, GILT antigen processing and presentation, mouse model, nMOMP and rMOMP protective epitope

Introduction

Chlamydia trachomatis is the most common sexually transmitted bacterial pathogen in the world that can also infect the conjunctiva, and the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts.1,2 In females, acute and chronic C. trachomatis genital infections can lead to long term sequelae including, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), chronic abdominal pain, ectopic pregnancy and infertility.3,4 Infants born from infected mothers can develop conjunctivitis and pneumonia. Ocular infections can result in trachoma, the most common cause of preventable blindness worldwide.5,6

Unlike other gram-negative bacteria, C. trachomatis lacks a peptidoglycan layer.7 Instead, multiple cysteine-rich proteins in the outer membrane form a network of disulfide bonds called the chlamydia outer membrane complex (COMC).8 MOMP, part of the COMC, is a 40 kDa protein accounting for ∼60% of outer membrane mass with 8 cysteines, 4 participating in 2 disulfide bonds, Cys48-Cyst55 and Cys201-Cyst203.9–12,13 The transmembrane regions have a β-barrel structure while surface exposed domains form flexible loops.14 MOMP has 4 variable domains (VD), corresponding to the surface exposed loops, intercalated with 5 constants domains (CD), located in the β-strands.14,15 Neutralizing antibodies predominately recognize VD4.16 A C. trachomatis vaccine based on multimeric MOMP VD4 is currently in clinical trials in Europe.17,18 Based on mouse protection studies, serological analyses, and sequencing, there are 15 major serovars of C. trachomatis labeled A to K, Ba, and L1 to L3 based on MOMP taxonomy.19–21

In 1942, Nigg22 isolated a “virus” from a mouse inoculated with fluid from a human with a respiratory infection. This isolate, named C. trachomatis mouse pneumonitis biovar, was subsequently, reclassified as Chlamydia muridarum (Cm), and has recently reemerged in research mouse colonies without intentional human inoculation.23,24 Most of the work performed over the last 4 decades to define the pathogenesis of chlamydial infections and to test vaccines, used this isolate because, unlike human C. trachomatis serovars, Cm causes productive genitourinary infections in female and male mice, closely mimicking those of C. trachomatis in humans.25–31 Except for a small “pathogenicity island” containing genes for evading species-specific innate immunity that differ between mice and humans, Cm and C. trachomatis have the same genes in the same genomic locations, and Cm MOMP the same overall structure and amino acid composition including the presence of 8 cysteines as in C. trachomatis MOMP.32

In humans and mice, the enzyme γ-interferon-inducible lysosomal thiol reductase (GILT), is expressed constitutively within lysosomes of antigen-presenting cells and accumulates in macrophage phagosomes maturing into phagolysosomes.33–35 Epithelial cells can express GILT. Pulmonary epithelial cells36 and primary epithelial cell lines derived from the upper murine genital tract constitutively express GILT37 (Fig. S1). The active site of the thiol reductase contains 2 cysteines in a CXXC motif that catalyzes the reduction of disulfide bonds. GILT-KO mice are deficient in producing MHC-class II-restricted CD4+ T-cell responses to antigens containing disulfide bonds. For example, Maric et al.38 immunized mice with hen egg lysozyme that contains 4 disulfide bonds and demonstrated that presentation of 2 major lysozyme antigenic epitopes to T cells was partially or completely abrogated in GILT-KO mice.

Here, to evaluate the role that GILT may play in the susceptibility to growth and pathogenicity of Cm, female wild type (WT) and GILT-KO C57BL/6 mice were vaginally infected with different dosages of this pathogen. No significant differences in susceptibility, severity of the infection, or upper genital tract pathology were observed between WT and GILT-KO mice. To assess the role that GILT may play in MOMP processing/presentation, mice were immunized with Cm native MOMP (nMOMP) that maintains its native trimeric structure, or recombinant (rMOMP), purified from E. coli inclusion bodies in denatured form, then challenged vaginally.10 Interestingly, WT mice vaccinated with nMOMP were significantly better protected than GILT-KO mice against a genital challenge while no differences were found when mice were immunized with rMOMP. Analysis of predicted class II MOMP epitopes versus position of known disulfide bonds identified a single GILT-dependent class II epitope in MOMP that is highly conserved across serovars A to K.

Materials and methods

C. muridarum stocks

Cm (strain Nigg II) was purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; VR-123) and was grown in HeLa-229 cells (ATCC) using high glucose Dulbecco’s minimal essential medium supplemented with 10 µg/ml of gentamicin and 2 µg/ml of cycloheximide.9 Cm elementary bodies (EB) were purified and stocks frozen at −80 °C in 0.2 M sucrose, 20 mM sodium phosphate (pH 7.4), and 5 mM glutamic acid (SPG).9 Bacterial stocks were titrated in HeLa-229 cells.9

Vaginal infection of naive WT and GILT-KO C57BL/6 mice with different dosages of C. muridarum

To determine susceptibility to infection, 8-wk-old female C57BL/6 WT and GILT-KO mice (n = 6–18) were anesthetized and 3 different dosages of Cm (102, 103, or 104 inclusion forming units [IFU]) were delivered in the vaginal cavity using a 50 µl pipettor.25 To synchronize the estrus cycle in diestrus, 4 d prior to infection, mice were injected subcutaneously with 1 mg of Depo-provera (Medroxy-progesterone acetate; Greenstone, Peapack, New Jersey, USA).39 Following infection vaginal swabs were collected twice weekly for the first 3 wk and then at 7-d intervals for 3 additional wk. The 48-well tissue culture plates were seeded with HeLa-229 cells, inoculated with 10-fold dilutions of the vaginal swabs and incubated for 30 h at 37 °C. The monolayers were fixed with methanol and chlamydial inclusion forming units (IFUs) were stained using the monoclonal antibody MoPn-40 to the MOMP VD1.10 The limit of detection was ≤2 C. muridarum IFU/vaginal culture.

Cloning, expression, and purification of C. muridarum recombinant MOMP (rMOMP)

The methods to clone, express, and purify Cm rMOMP were published.40 Briefly, the gene of Cm MOMP (GenBank, accession no. AE002272, X63409), without the leading sequence, was amplified by the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and inserted into the pET-45b vector (Novagen, Madison, Wisconsin, USA). Escherichia coli TOP10 competent cells were transformed, and the plasmid extracted from positive clones. For expression, E. coli BL21 (DE3) was transformed with the plasmid containing the C. muridarum MOMP DNA and inoculated into LB broth. The cultures were grown using standard procedures and rMOMP, was extracted from E. coli inclusion bodies as described.40

The pellet of the Cm rMOMP was solubilized in TEN buffer containing 8 M urea, 0.1 mM PMSF and 0.02 mM DTT to a concentration of 10 mg/ml. The preparation was diluted to 5 mg/ml with the same volume of TEN buffer containing 10% of the detergent Z3-14 (Anatrace; Maumee, Ohio, USA). For purification, the rMOMP solution was loaded onto a Sephacryl-S-300 column (1× 50 cm; Sigma), which was pre-equilibrated with 100 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, 200 mM NaCl, 10 mM EDTA, 0.2 mM DTT, and 0.05% Z3-14. Peak fractions were pooled and dialyzed against PBS (pH 7.4) with 0.05% Z3-14. By the limulus amoebocyte assay (Associates of Cape Cod Inc.; East Falmouth, Massachusetts, USA), rMOMP had less than 0.05 EU of LPS/mg of protein.

Extraction and purification of C. muridarum native MOMP (nMOMP)

The extraction and purification of the Cm nMOMP has been described.10 In brief, Cm infected HeLa-229 cell monolayers were centrifuged, washed with PBS (pH 7.4) pelleted and resuspended in 0.02 M Tris (pH 7.4), 1.0 M NaCl, 0.012 M MgCl2 and 1 mM PMSF. Following sonication, the EB were incubated on ice with 25 mg of DNase/ml for 2 h, pelleted and extracted with 2% CHAPS (Anatrace) in the presence of 100 mM DTT. After ultracentrifugation the pellet was treated a second time with CHAPS. The pellet of the second CHAPS was further extracted with 2% Z3-14 (Anatrace). After incubation at 37 °C for 2 h, the preparation was ultracentrifuged and the nMOMP recovered in the supernatant.

nMOMP was further purified using a 1 × 20 cm hydroxyapatite column (Bio-Rad Laboratories; Hercules, California, USA), as described by Caldwell et al.,9 with the following modifications. The column was equilibrated with 0.02 M phosphate buffer, pH 5.5, containing 0.1% Z3-14 and 1 mM each of EDTA, PMSF and DTT. nMOMP was eluted with a linear gradient of phosphate buffer from 0.02 M to 0.5 M containing 0.1% Z3-14 and EDTA, PMSF and DTT as indicated above. nMOMP was concentrated by filtration (Amicon Ultra-15; Merck, Millipore Ltd.) and dialyzed before immunization against PBS (pH 7.4), containing 0.05% Z3-14. By the limulus amoebocyte assay (Associates of Cape Cod Inc.), nMOMP had less than 0.05 EU of LPS/mg of protein.

SDS-PAGE analysis of C. muridarum MOMP

The apparent MW and purity of Cm nMOMP and rMOMP were assessed in a 10% tricine-SDS-PAGE.41 The protein bands were visualized with a silver staining kit (Sigma-Aldrich) (data not shown).

Mice vaccination

GILT-KO mice, on a C57BL/6 background, generated in Dr. Peter Cresswell laboratory, were breed at the UCI Vivarium. Wild-type C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Charles River. In brief, 5- to 6-wk-old-anesthetized mice (n = 10) were vaccinated intranasally (i.n.) with 10 µg/mouse/immunization of C. muridarum nMOMP or rMOMP, using 10 µg of CpG-1826/mouse/immunization (Tri-Link Bio Technologies, San Diego, California, USA) as adjuvant. Three weeks later they were boosted intramuscularly (i.m.) and subcutaneously (s.c.) with the same amount of nMOMP, or rMOMP, and 10 µg of CpG-1826/mouse/immunization, and the adjuvant Montanide ISA 720 VG (SEPPIC, Inc., Fairfield, New Jersey, USA), was included at a 70/30 v/v of the total volume injected.42 The boost was repeated once 3 wk later. As a negative immunization control, mice received PBS with the same adjuvants, vaccination schedule and routes. All experiments were performed with 5–10 mice/group and were replicated once. One mouse in the WT nMOMP group, due to weight loss, had to be euthanized. The Vertebrate Animal protocol was approved by the UCI IACUC.

Evaluation of the humoral immune responses following vaccination

The day prior to the vaginal challenge, sera from immunized mice were collected and used to quantitate the levels of antibodies by an ELISA.43,44 The 96-Multiwell plates were coated with Cm EB (1 µg/well) and incubated with serial dilutions of pre-immune and immune sera, followed by washing and incubation with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG, IgG1 or IgG2c antibodies diluted 1:100 (BD Pharmingen). In vaginal washes, levels of IgA and IgG were determined. Chromogenic substrate detection at OD405 was performed using an EIA reader (Labsystem Multiscan, Helsinki, Finland), and the geometric mean titers (GMT) were expressed as the reciprocal of the dilution that was 2±SD above background levels from pre-immune sera and vaginal washes.43,44

In vitro determination of neutralizing antibodies in serum form vaccinated mice

In vitro neutralization assays were performed as described.45 Cm (1 × 104 IFU), were incubated for 45 min at 37 °C, with pre-immune and immune mouse sera, collected the day before the genital challenge, diluted serially with Ca2+ and Mg2+ free-PBS, pH 7.2 supplemented with 5% guinea pig serum as a source of complement. Following incubation, the mixtures were inoculated by centrifugation into HeLa-229 cells grown on shell vials. After 30 h of incubation at 37 °C, the monolayers were fixed and stained with the monoclonal antibody MoPn-40 to MOMP.10 The titer of a sample is expressed as the dilution that yielded 50% neutralization relative to the control serum from pre-immunized mice.

Mapping of linear C. muridarum MOMP B-cell epitopes with serum from vaccinated mice

Specific antibodies to Cm MOMP linear epitopes in sera from immunized mice were determined by an ELISA.46 Overlapping 25-mers peptides, corresponding to the mature C. muridarum MOMP amino acid sequence were purchased (SynBioSci., Livermore, California, USA). The peptides were adsorbed onto high binding affinity ELISA plates (1 µg/well) (Costar, Corning Inc.), in triplicate and serum IgG antibody levels were determined. As positive control, Cm EB were included in the assay and the negative control (P25) corresponds to a combination of the N- and C-terminus of MOMP.

C. muridarum-specific cellular immune responses induced by vaccination and identification of linear MOMP T cell epitopes

Cellular immune responses were measured using splenocytes collected from 4 mice/group the day prior to challenge.43 Nylon wool purified T cells were cultured in 48-well plates (Costar, Corning Inc.), (2.5 × 106 cells/well) in 1 ml of RPMI-1640 media supplemented with 1 mM L-glutamine, 50 μM 2-Mercaptoethanol, 10% FBS and 20 μg/ml of gentamycin. T cells (>85% purity) were stimulated with Cm EB in the presence of antigen-presenting cells (APCs) pre-prepared by irradiation (3,300 rads, 137Cs) of syngeneic splenocytes. APCs (1.25 × 105 cells/well) were incubated with EB at a 1:1 ratio for 2 h at 37 °C in round-bottom 96-well plates followed by addition of T cells also at a 1:1 ratio. To monitor cell viability, Concanavalin A (5 μg/ml) served as a positive stimulant and cell culture medium (RPMI with 10% FBS) used as a negative control. Levels of IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, and IL-17A in supernatants collected 48 h. post-stimulation, were determined using commercial kits (BD Pharmingen, San Diego, CA) and measured at 450 nm using tetramethylbenzidine as substrate (Labsystem Multiscan; Helsinki, Finland).

To identify MOMP T-cell epitopes, 10 μg/ml of each 25-mer MOMP peptide were added to T-cells and APC. Cm nMOMP (10 μg/ml) or rMOMP (10 μg/ml), and UV-inactivated Cm EB (1:1 ratio to APCs), were used as positive control and P25 was included as a negative control. Following incubation for 48 h at 37 °C in a humidified incubator under 95% air and 5% CO2, T cell supernatants were collected and saved at −80 °C until tested. Levels of IFN-γ in supernatants from the T cells were determined by ELISA as above.

Vaginal challenge with C. muridarum of C57BL/6 WT and GILT KO mice vaccinated with nMOMP or rMOMP

Four weeks following the last immunization, C57BL/6 WT and GILT-KO mice (n = 10) were anesthetized and challenged vaginally with 5 × 103 Cm IFU).47 Four days prior to infection mice received subcutaneously 1 mg/mouse of Depo-provera.39 The number of Cm IFU in vaginal washes was determined as above.

Assessment of upper genital tract pathology and fertility

At 7-wk post-infection, female mice were housed with proven breeder WT C57BL/6 male mice (4F/1M) for 18 d.43 Mice that gained 4 grams of body weight were considered to be pregnant, euthanized, and the number of hydrosalpinxes and embryos were counted. Females that were not pregnant were caged for a second time with a male mouse that had fathered mice during the first mating.

Statistics

The 2-tailed unpaired Student t test, the Mann–Whitney Rank U test and the χ2 test were used for statistical analyses with the program Prism version 10. Differences were considered significant for values of P < 0.05. Experiments with multiple groups of mice were analyzed by comparing 2 groups at a time as described by Rubin.48

Results

Susceptibility of C57BL/6 WT and GILT KO mice to vaginal infection and upper genital tract pathology following a vaginal inoculation with C. muridarum

To determine the susceptibility of C57BL/6 WT and GILT-KO mice to vaginal infection, 6 groups of mice were inoculated with 102, 103, or 104 Cm IFU/mouse and cultures were collected for a period of 6 wk (Figs. 1 and 2 and Tables S1 and S2). At the end of the experiment the upper genital tract was evaluated for the presence of hydrosalpinx.

All WT and GILT-KO mice inoculated with 104 Cm IFU had positive cultures at D4 post-infection and most continued to shed for a period of 3 wk. Subsequently, the number of mice with positive cultures started to decline until they were negative at the end of the experiment. Based on the number of mice with positive cultures, percentage of positive vaginal cultures, length of shedding, and number of IFU recovered, no significant differences were observed between C57BL/6 WT and GILT-KO mice except on D28 (Fig. 2A–D and Tables S1 and S2).

Similar results were found in mice inoculated with 102 or 103 Cm IFU. Differences between the 2 congenic mice were not statistically significant (Figs. 1 and 2 and Tables S1 and S2).

Six weeks after the vaginal inoculation with Cm the animals were euthanized, and their genital tracts were inspected for the presence of hydrosalpinx. As shown in Fig. 2E and Table S2, 60% to 70% of the WT and GILT-KO mice were infected with 103 or 104 C. muridarum IFU had hydrosalpinxes while only 17% of those inoculated with 102 IFU had hydrosalpinx. No significant differences were observed between the WT and the GILT-KO groups.

To conclude, based on the percentage of mice infected, positive vaginal cultures, duration of shedding, number of Cm IFU recovered and upper genital pathology, no significant differences between WT and GILT-KO mice were observed following vaginal challenges with 102, 103, or 104 Cm IFU. These results indicate that these 2 congenic mice are equally susceptible to chlamydial infections and long-term induced upper tract pathology.

ELISA antibody titers in serum induced by vaccination with C. muridarum nMOMP or rMOMP

Mice were vaccinated by the systemic and mucosal routes with Cm nMOMP or rMOMP and humoral immune responses were evaluated. Blood samples were collected the day before genital challenge and ELISA antibody geometric mean titers (GMT) were determined using Cm EB as antigen (Fig. 3A and Table S3). No significant differences in antibody titers were observed between the WT and the GILT-KO mice vaccinated with nMOMP or rMOMP. Higher lgG GMT were found in WT and GILT-KO mice vaccinated with nMOMP (64,508) when compared with rMOMP immunized mice (10,159 and 8,063, respectively; P < 0.05). Mock immunized animals with PBS, had lgG GMT below the limit of detection (<100).

No difference in anti-MOMP serum and vaginal antibody titers between WT and GILT-KO mice vaccinated with C. muridarum nMOMP and rMOMP, however nMOMP induced higher overall and neutralizing antibody titers than rMOMP in both mouse strains. Serum: (A) Total IgG GMT; (B) IgG1 GMT; (C) IgG2c GMT; (D) In vitro neutralization GMT. Vaginal washes: (E) Total IgG GMT; (F) Total IgA GMT. The broken horizontal line equals the limit of detection. Vaccine groups nMOMP- WT (n = 10), GILT-KO (n = 10); rMOMP- WT (n = 10), GILT-KO (n = 10). *P < 0.05 by the Student t test for differences in GMT.

To determine if vaccination induced Th1- or Th2-biased immune responses, the lgG2c/lgG1 ratios were calculated (Fig. 3B and C and Table S3). Both WT and GILT-KO mice immunized with either nMOMP or rMOMP had high lgG2c/IgG1 ratios (>16) indicative of robust Th1-biased immune responses.

Neutralizing antibody GMT in serum to C. muridarum EB elicited by immunization with nMOMP or rMOMP

Serum samples, collected the day before the vaginal challenge, were used to determine levels of neutralizing antibodies induced by vaccination (Fig. 3D and Table S3). Pre-immunization sera served as negative controls. The in vitro neutralizing GMT was 8,063 for WT and GILT-KO nMOMP vaccinated mice. WT and GILT-KO immunized with rMOMP had neutralizing GMT of 713 and 898, respectively (P > 0.05). The neutralization GMT were significantly higher in mice immunized with nMOMP versus those with rMOMP (P < 0.05). Control mice receiving PBS had neutralization titers below the level of detection (<50). Importantly, there was no difference in neutralizing antibody titers between nMOMP immunized WT and GILT-KO mice.

ELISA antibody titers to C. muridarum EB in vaginal washes following vaccination

To evaluate humoral immune responses in genital tract mucosa, vaginal washes were collected the day before the vaginal challenge, and antibody levels determined using EB as the antigen (Fig. 3E and F and Table S3). No significant differences in lgG GMT were found between WT animals immunized with nMOMP (1,810) and GILT-KO (905) or rMOMP (640 vs 453, respectively) (P > 0.05). lgA levels were low in the two congenic mice immunized with nMOMP28 or rMOMP (<20). Mice receiving PBS had no detectable EB specific lgG or lgA antibody levels (<20) in vaginal washes.

Identification of serum antibodies to C. muridarum MOMP linear epitopes elicited by vaccination

To establish the specific antigenic regions of MOMP that elicited antibodies to linear epitopes following vaccination, 25-mer overlapping peptides were chemically synthesized and used to coat ELISA plates (Fig. 4). EB were used as positive controls and P25 as a negative control. Serum samples collected the day before the challenge were tested. Significant levels of antibodies to the four variable domains (VD) of Cm MOMP were observed in samples collected from mice immunized with nMOMP or rMOMP. No significant differences in reactivity were found between WT and GILT-KO mice.

Overall, in both strains, vaccination with nMOMP induced more robust antibody responses than immunization with rMOMP.

Mapping of T cells linear epitopes in C. muridarum nMOMP or rMOMP vaccinated C57BL/6 WT and GILT KO mice

Levels of IFN-γ were determined in the supernatants (Fig. 6). Peptides from the VD2-CD3 and constant domain 4 regions of MOMP induced high IFN-γ levels from T-cells of WT and GILT-KO mice vaccinated with nMOMP but not from rMOMP immunized animals. The amino acid sequences present in VD2-CD3 (peptides 11 and 12) were, VSLSQAVVELYTDTAFAWSVGARAALWECGCATLGASFQY, and those in CD4 (peptides 17 plus 18) were, IDYHEWQASLALSYRLNMFTPYIGVKWSRASFDADTIRIA (Fig. 7). Using NetMHCII pan version 4.1 we identified 12 peptides capable of binding the presentation groove of murine class II IAb (single class II molecule in C57BL/6 mice). FAWSVGARA (epitope 5) is the core peptide from the VD2-CD3 peptides, and WQASLALSY (epitope 8) and FDADTIRIA (epitope 9) are the core peptides predicted to bind murine class II IAb from constant domain 4 respectively. Upper genital tract pathology and fertility in C57BL/6 WT and GILT-KO mice immunized with nMOMP or rMOMP and challenged vaginally with C. muridarum

Seven weeks after the genital challenge female mice were caged with male mice. Their body weight was determined and mice that gained more than 4 g were considered to be pregnant and were euthanized. The peritoneal cavity was open and the number of hydrosalpinxes and embryos were counted. WT mice vaccinated with nMOMP had fewer hydrosalpinxes 11% (1/9) than the GILT-KO group 50% (5/10) (P < 0.05) (Fig 9E and Table S7). No significant differences were observed in the number of hydrosalpinxes when comparing WT 60% (6/10) and GILT-KO 70% (7/10) mice immunized with rMOMP (P > 0.05). In comparison to the PBS immunized groups, mice vaccinated with nMOMP (P < 0.05) were protected but not those immunized with rMOMP (P > 0.05).

Among the WT mice vaccinated with nMOMP, 89% (8/9) were fertile compared with 30% (3/10) of the GILT-KO animals (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9F and Table S7). There were no differences in the number of fertile WT and GILT-KO mice immunized with rMOMP 40% (4/10) versus 30% (3/10), respectively (P > 0.05). Compared with their respective PBS groups, mice vaccinated with nMOMP (P < 0.05) were protected but not those immunized with rMOMP (P > 0.05).

The mean number of embryos per mouse was significantly higher in WT (6.3 ± 1.1) than in GILT-KO (2.4 ± 1.6) mice vaccinated with nMOMP (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9G and Table S7). In contrast, the same number of embryos (1.7 ± 1.0) were found in WT and GILT-KO mice immunized with rMOMP (P > 0.05). The number of embryos in WT and GILT-KO mice vaccinated with nMOMP (P < 0.05), but not with rMOMP(P < 0.05), was significantly different from animals receiving PBS.

In sum, as with vaginal cultures, WT mice vaccinated with nMOMP were better protected against upper genital tract pathology and infertility than nMOMP vaccinated GILT-KO mice. No differences were found between mice immunized with rMOMP. nMOMP vaccine failure in GILT-KO mice identifies epitope 5 as the likely protective class II epitope in MOMP based on positions of a known disulfide bond between cysteines 201 and 203 lying immediately C-terminal to the predicted class II epitope.

Discussion

In this study we first investigated whether C57BL/6 WT and GILT-KO mice were susceptible to chlamydia vaginal infection and induction of upper genital tract pathology Both strains of mice were equally susceptible to Cm infection and genital tract immunopathology. There was no difference in time to clearance, intensity of bacterial shedding, or immunopathology indicating that GILT does not affect clearance of a primary Cm genital tract infection. Characterization of antibody responses showed no difference in recognition of linear B cell epitopes, geometric mean titers including neutralization, and immunoglobulin subclasses.

Primary chlamydia genital tract infections are cleared by class II restricted T cells. Time to bacterial clearance of is independent of B cells and antibody, and the same in WT and B cell deficient mice.51 Even in immune-naive parabiotic mouse experiments demonstrating T–B cell collaborative reductions in bacterial shedding intensity and immunopathology, time to clearance in immune-naive parabiotic mice was 24 d as it was in the control mice.52 GILT participates in processing T cell epitopes for presentation on class II molecules. Cm and C. trachomatis have roughly 900 proteins, each with a handful of peptides predicted to be capable of binding epithelial class II molecules for presentation to T cells that terminate chlamydia replication in epithelial cells. In practice only a limited number of chlamydia protective peptides (T cell epitopes) are generated by proteolytic cleavage, loaded onto and presented by dendritic class II molecules to drive T cell expansion and, in parallel, epithelial class II to identify infected cells and facilitate bacterial clearance.53 The natural murine immune response to Cm infection is inefficient with persistent shedding for ∼1 mo. The median time for untreated women to clear a primary C. trachomatis infection is likely 6 mo and twice that for men.54–56 In the context of this investigation, for the absence of GILT to affect time to clearance during a primary Cm infection would require a critical fraction of protective T cell epitopes be dependent on GILT for processing. Comparison of WT and GILT-KO found that not to be the case.

Subunit vaccines are typically a single antigen containing a handful of peptides capable of binding MHC molecules. In subunit vaccines the possibility that a critical fraction of protective T cell epitope(s) would be GILT-dependent due to peptide flanking region effects is significantly higher than during natural infections. Therefore, we investigated the role of GILT in vaccination using our well-characterized model based on Cm MOMP. In the model, immunization with MOMP in native conformation (nMOMP) is an effective vaccine while immunization with recombinant MOMP (rMOMP), a denatured protein without disulfide bonding, less effective.40,57 Both mouse strains immunized with rMOMP were not protected based on intensity and duration of bacterial shedding, immunopathology, and post-infection infertility that were similar to PBS controls. The major finding was that nMOMP was an effective model vaccine in WT mice but ineffective in GILT-KO mice, based on bacterial shedding and infertility. Importantly for interpreting these results, there was no difference in neutralizing antibody titers in nMOMP vaccinated WT and GILT-KO mice. In the context of a MOMP subunit vaccine, a GILT-dependent MOMP T cell epitope determined the immunopathology and fertility outcome.

nMOMP-vaccinated WT mice recognized T cell core epitopes in the CD3 (epitope 5) and constant domain 4 (epitope 8 and 9) regions of MOMP. Comparison of predicted MHC class II MOMP epitopes to the pattern of disulfide bonding in MOMP identified the likely GILT-dependent protective core epitope in nMOMP as epitope 5 FAWSVGARA located in CD3. The second and third recognized epitopes 8 WQASLALSY and 9 FDADTIRIA are in constant domain 4 and have no neighborhood cysteines or flanking disulfide bonds, therefore their peptidase processing and class II loading is unlikely to be dependent on GILT. Conversely, proteolytic generation and DM editing of class II loading of the FAWSVGARA epitope is likely susceptible to conformational PFR effects because of a known disulfide bond between C201 and C203 immediately C-terminal to the predicted class II epitope. Infected reproductive tract epithelial cells expressing GILT likely present FAWSVGARA based epitope peptides to class II restricted T cells mediating bacterial clearance resulting in protection from immunopathology and infertility in nMOMP immunized WT mice. Loss of nMOMP vaccine efficacy in the absence of GILT suggests that WQASLALSY and FDADTIRIA based peptides are non-protective T cell epitopes, ie not generated in and presented by class II molecules within infected epithelial cells.

Epitope 5 FAWSVGARAALWECGC, core peptide underlined, disulfide bonded PFR cysteines in bold, is predicted to bind human HLA class II DP, DQ, DR molecules (NetMHCII v4.1). The core peptide is highly conserved across human C. trachomatis serovars, with 100% identity in 7 of 8 serovars with MOMP protein reference sequences available. All C. trachomatis MOMP reference sequences have the C201-C203 disulfide bond immediately C-terminal to epitope 5 core peptide (Table S8). It is reasonable based on murine data presented here to postulate that a FAWSVGARA based peptide is a protective T cell epitope in mice and humans, and that it is dependent on GILT-mediated reduction of the 201–203 disulfide bond.

MOMP has long been a leading candidate for a chlamydia subunit vaccine based on the generation of neutralizing antibodies and protection from immunopathology in the C. muridarum mouse model. An investigation of the native MOMP vaccine in protected (wild-type) and unprotected (GILT-KO) mice revealed that neutralizing antibodies did not correlate with protection. Instead, protection was dependent on a single highly conserved, pan serovar, class II T cell epitope located in constant domain 3, whose processing and presentation were dependent on GILT.

Serum bone turnover biomarkers in early postoperative period related to the spontaneous resolution of disc herniation
Authors: Xu Yan, Zhanhua Ma, Su Fu, Yan Jiang, Dongzhe Li, Yongming Ning, Chao Dong, Zhengming Cao, Yongkui Wang, Chunlin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA new way of induction surgery to obtain induced resorption of herniated nucleus pulposus (iRHNP) was recently proposed by our team, both cervical or lumbar disc herniation (CDH or LDH), when bony repair of the decompression groove simultaneously started, suggesting the possible relation to iRHNP mechanism. Since some biomarkers from peripheral blood are regarded as indicators of bone turnover, the role of circulating biomarkers for iRHNP that may reveal the mechanism remains to be determined. This study aimed to identify whether potential biomarkers changed in coincidence with the degree of iRHNP in CDH and LDH after induction surgery. A total of 73 CDH patients and 135 LDH patients, diagnosed with cervical myelopathy or lumbar stenosis accepted induction surgery, were enrolled in this retrospective study. The medical information such as T-score and volumes of CDH and LDH was monitored. The serum concentrations of five biomarkers such as osteocalcin (OCN), 25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (25-OH-D3), β-Cross Laps, amino-terminal propeptide of type I collagen (PINP) and parathyroid hormone (PTH), and the concentration of BMP-2 were measured. As a result, the reduction in CDH and LDH volume (iRHNP) was firstly confirmed 1 week after induction surgery. The iRHNP was seen in 72.16% of CDHs and 67.81% of LDHs, with no statistical difference. In addition, the volume changes ratio in median was − 27.8% in CDHs, a greater degree than − 21.0% in LDHs. Accompanied with iRHNP occurrence, biomarkers of OCN, 25-OH-D3, PINP and PTH were significantly down-regulated while β-Cross Laps was significantly up-regulated in circulation. Correlation ships between the osteoporosis and iRHNP degree were identified. Meanwhile, the biomarkers of pre-operative circuiting levels of OCN and PINP were reflective to the degree of iRHNP. In conclusion, we found that T-score/osteoporosis was linked to iRHNP whilst serum biomarkers were predictive of the change degrees of disc herniation volume in both cervical and lumbar spine. Introduction

Symptomatic cervical disc herniation (CDH) or lumbar disc herniation (LDH), are the common diseases in orthopedic practice1–3 with the annual incidence of 4.4 per 100,0004 and 20 per 100,0005 for CDH and LDH separately. The degenerated disc tissue (mainly the nucleus pulposus) compresses the nerve tissue, resulting into the abnormalities in sensory and motor. In 1984, Guinto et al.6 first reported the spontaneous resolution of LDH, namely, the resorption of herniated nucleus pulpous (RHNP). Later in 19927, RHNP was firstly demonstrated in CDH. Several clinical reports in conservation8–10 all confirmed this well-established phenomenon. Despite the low incidence and long period of time, the proposal of RHNP enlightened us that disc herniation was such kind of tissue with the ability to spontaneously diminish.

We occasionally found a profound spontaneous resorption (incidence of 80%11–13) in disc herniation (both CDH and LDH) in our surgery-treated patients. Briefly in surgical protocol, the lamina was cut bilaterally and elevated symmetrically to decompress the neural structures. In 2016, we first reported the technique of cervical microendoscopic laminoplasty (CMEL) for cervical myelopathy with CDH14. Later in 2021 and 2022, we identified the common happening of profound spontaneous resorption11–13 after CMEL. We prospectively treated 116 patients with single- to three-level cervical myelopathy with CMEL and found that 89.1% of CDHs were resorbed11. This kind of RHNP was induced by surgery (termed iRHNP11, which was quite different from the RHNP in the conservation cases.

However, the mechanism of spontaneous resorption was largely unknown. By reviewing the current surgical treatments, RHNP was rarely seen in laminectomy or expansive open-door laminoplasty (EOLP) treated patients. The difference between laminectomy/EOLP and our kind of surgery was the degree of bony healing (lack of bony healing after laminectomy, or a tiny degree of bony healing in hinge fracture after EOLP15. We therefore hypothesized the local bone metabolism accounted for the occurrence of iRHNP. In details, the iRHNP was founded evident in the early stage of 1 week after induction surgery on CDH or LDH. Simultaneously, bony repair of the decompression groove started (acute pro-inflammation and fibrovascular phase16, suggesting the possible relation to iRHNP mechanism. A hypothesis was therefore proposed that the local bone metabolism of decompression groove during bony healing which was associated with a release of bone bioactive factors, acted as a critical factor triggering iRHNP. This was conceived from the clinical observations on fracture healing17–19osteoporosis20 and osteonecrosis21. Local events of fracture union/non-union, alterations in bone density and necrosis of bone tissue, directly influenced the levels of serum biomarkers which in turn acted as a predictor. We proposed that iRHNP and serum biomarkers may interact as well.

On the basis of the possible genesis of iRHNP proposed above, the level of local bone turnover activity was assessed to reveal the mechanism. Since some biomarkers from peripheral blood are regarded as indicators and predictors of bone turnover such as osteoporosis20 and osteonecrosis of the femoral head21the role of circulating biomarkers for iRHNP remains to be determined. The serum concentrations of certain biomarkers reflecting bone formation or resorption activity was firstly measured in our surgery-treated patients with CDH or LDH. The relationship between iRHNP and bone metabolism was tested to indicate the iRHNP mechanism. Meanwhile, Lumbar spine weighted more than cervical spine with a lager range of motion. CDH was also different from LDH in the superficial location to posterior longitudinal ligament and the high proportion of cartilaginous content. These characteristics may make the iRHNP and the local bony healing of CDH somewhat different from that of LDH, which was also clarified in this study.

We attempted to explain the iRHNP mechanism by studying the medical documents, MRI images and circulating biomarkers in early postoperative period. We subsequently developed a new method to predict the degree of iRHNP involving several variables. Features of these factors and relationship between them indicated a new iRHNP mechanism and thus making this model useful in the preoperative preparation for CDH/LDH patients.

Methods

Ethics approval

This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University in Oct 2020 with the reference number of 2020-KY-0203. All methods including operation and outcome assessing were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. In the study, all the enrolled patients have been informed of the study and signed the informed consent before the treatment.

Study design

This study was started on March 2021 and finished on September 2022. Totally, 73 patients with CDH and 135 patients with LDH underwent RI surgery (CDH or LDH) in the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University.

The inclusion criteria was: 1, age > 18 years; 2,complete MRI data of pre-operative and post-operative images one week after surgery; 3, the serum bone turnover biomarkers were tested preoperative and 1 week post-operative. 4, CDH patients with single- to multi- level cervical myelopathy in the range of C3-C7 (and) stenosis for; LDH patients with single- to multi- level LDH at L1-L5 (and) stenosis; 5, the diagnose was consistent with the clinical symptoms.

The exclusion criteria were: 1, age < 18 years; 2, combination of systemic diseases such as tumors and infection; 3, combination of mental disorders; 4, ossification of CDH or LDH; 5, incomplete MRI data 6, severe trauma to spine region.

The other medical information was recorded including the medication during perioperation, duration of illness, comorbid conditions and the physical activity.

Surgical methods of CDH and LDH

As established previously in cervical spine22the bilateral decompression achieved via symmetrically pulling the spinal dorsal elements was performed in the endoscope-assisted way. Briefly, a midline skin incision was made to fully expose the surgical site under the endoscope. The surgery site was exposed at the bottom and the spinous process with slightly rotating the endoscope. After drilling and threading through the spinous process, two holes was drilled at the basilar part of the spinous process directing at the contralateral lamina for further docking of plates. Endoscopically, the connecting part of the lamina and the lateral mass was drilled to fully separation. Then, the posterior structure was pulled dorsally 1–3 mm in the sagittal direction through the silk suture locating on spinous process. Two titanium mini-plates were finally fixed on both arc of lamina with two screws for each plate, thus stabilizing the bone structure. This operation method has been improved and adjusted into the lumbar spine then. The surgical steps, includED endoscopic-assisted drilling, lamina elevation, and plate fixation. This treatment was developed in which the mini-invasive idea and decompression mechanism were copied and modified to fit LDH patients. The posterior movement of the spinous process complex was 1–4 mm in lumbar region while the plates were pre-curved to fit in the lamina.

Detection of serum biomarkers

Whole blood samples from each patient were collected preoperatively and 7 days postoperatively for the detection of bone turnover biomarkers23,24. With the help from clinical laboratory test, serum bone turnover biomarkers of OCN, β-Cross Laps, PTH, PINP and25-OH-D3 were determined by electrochemiluminescence immunoassays using Roche commercial kits (COBAS e 601, Roche). This assessment was automatic and fast in operation showing 170 tests per hour. The assay coefficients of variations were 7% for OCN, 3.4% for PINP, 3.5% for β-Cross Lap, 6% for 25(OH)D3, and 3.1% for PTH. The normal reference ranges were used: OCN 24-70ng/mL, β-Cross Laps < 0.58ng/mL, PTH 15-65pg/mL, PINP 16.9-65.5ng/mL and 25-OH-D3 > 18ng/mL. The level of BMP-2 was also quantified from the serum samples using commercially available ELISA kit (R&D Systems). Briefly, 100 µl of sample was added to the antibody pre-coated well. After incubation, the optical density of each well was determined to further calculate the BMP-2 concentration (pg/mL).

Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry

Whole DXA was preoperatively performed to measure BMD (measured in g/cm2) of each patient at the L1-L5 vertebral body. BMD was examined using the X-ray absorptiometry system purchased from Lunar company. The detection parameter of tube voltage was set at 70 kV/40 kV, and the other parameters were set during the detection. According to the World Health Organization standard classification, osteoporosis is defined as T-score ≤ − 2.5; osteopenia as − 2.5 < T-score < − 1; and normal BMD as T-score ≥ − 1.0.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 21.0 (IBM, USA). Clinical outcome or volumetric data from pre-operative and follow-up calculation were compared using the paired t-test if all the data passing Shapiro-Wilk normality test. Alternatively, paired Wilcoxon nonparametric test or Mann-Whitney test was undertaken. The relationships between factors were analyzed by Generalized Linear Model. The differences of general information and clinical outcomes were compared by Chi-square test. A multivariate regression was also performed to identify the potential factors influence alterations in disc herniation volume. Value of p < 0.05 was considered as statistically significance.

Results

General information of patients

In this retrospective study, a total of 208 patients with cervical or lumbar stenosis caused by CDH or LDH who accepted the treatment of our kind of surgery were enrolled. 73 patients with CDH were consisted of 39 men and 34 women with an age of 52.74 ± 12.27 years; 135 patients with LDH were consisted of 64 men and 71 women with an age of 49.82 ± 14.00 years. The gender and age were not different between groups. In terms of the comorbid disease, 4 patients were with diabetes mellitus, 11 with osteoporosis and 19 with high blood pressure from 73 CDH patients. By contrast in 135 LDH patients, 11 patients were with diabetes mellitus, 17 with osteoporosis and 33 with high blood pressure. No difference was found in the association rate with comorbid disease between CDH and LDH groups. Similar, the duration of illness in CDH and LDH patients was also no different, approximately one year of suffering from the symptoms. However, the surgery levels were different that CDH patients exhibited more operation levels/more grooved lamina than LDH patients. This was reasonable because the multiple levels of disc herniation/stenosis were likely to happen in the cervical spine. The demographic, pathological and operative data were listed in Table 1.

Spontaneous resorption of CDH/LDH (iRHNP)

The volume changes of CDH/LDH were monitored in the next step. By using the AI-assisted software avoiding manual error, all volume data were collected. To sum, 273 CDHs were from the CDH group (n = 73) whilst 292 LDHs were from the LDH group (n = 135). Due to the failure in passing normality test, a paired Wilcox test was applied. The volume data showed that a profound reduction in the CDH volume (from 555mm3 to 417 mm3 on median, P < 0.001) and in the LDH volume as well (from 790mm3 to 652 mm3 on median, P < 0.001) in 1week post-operative comparing to the pre-operative state (Fig. 1A). In terms of the volume change ratios in CDH and LDH, the incidence and the degree of iRHNP were tested. 197 of 273 CDHs (72.16%) and 198 of 292 LDHs (67.81%) regressed over 10% (ratio < −10%), no difference between cervical and lumbar spine (Table 2). The re-protrusion ratio was 12.67% and 12.87% in CDH and LDH cases, showing no difference between groups. The frequency of unchanged volume was different that more LDH exhibited unchanged volume 1 week after surgery (Table 2). The percentage changes in CDHs was therefore − 27.8% on median, slightly greater than the − 21.0% in LDHs (Fig. 1B). This result showed the occurrence of iRHNP both in CDH and LDH, despite of a little bit difference.

Linkage between osteoporosis and iRHNP

We firstly attempted to seek clinical evidence to indicate the iRHNP mechanism. By reviewing the medical records, the variables of gender, age, duration of illness, number of surgery levels, the comorbid disease of diabetes mellitus, osteoporosis and high blood pressure were tested using the Spearman rho correlation analysis. Table 3 demonstrated the possible relationship that osteoporosis was related to the degree of iRHNP both in CDH (p = 0.002) and LDH (p = 0.005). In addition, the duration of illness was also associated with iRHNP degree in CDH, not the LDH. CDH/LDH patents were further grouped into normal BMD, osteopenia and osteoporosis via T-score measurements. Interestingly, patients with osteoporosis showed a more extensive degree of iRHNP in CDH and LDH comparing to the patients with normal BMD (Fig. 1C). This point showed the linkage between osteoporosis and iRHNP, which was the same story in CDH and LDH, indicated the possible mechanism of iRHNP related to bone metabolism.

Changes in serum bone turnover biomarkers after surgery

The involvement of bone metabolism related to osteoporosis/t-score was then tested by using the pre-operative and post-operative peripheral blood samples from each patient. As the classic bone turnover biomarkers, promoters for bone formation as OCN, PINP and PTH were significantly down-regulated, calcium regulator of 25-OH-D3 was also reduced, while indexes representing bone resorption of β-Cross Laps were significantly increased (Fig. 1D-H), suggesting the strengthen of matrix catabolism by our inducing surgery treatment. This effect was similar in both CDH and LDH groups with no statistical significance in the percentage changes or change amounts of those serum biomarkers. Simultaneously, the BMP-2 concentration in the serum sample was shown increased from 29.8 ± 6.7 pg/mL to 53 ± 10.7 pg/mL (p = 0.003) indicating the bone healing during this timepoint of one week post-operative. 22 patients who accepted surgery of fixation removal were also enrolled to test these serum bone turnover biomarkers pre-operatively and 1 week post-operatively, set as a control group. The serum levels of biomarkers were unchanged (Fig. 1D-H).

Serum bone turnover biomarkers predictive for iRHNP

Next, we merged CDH and LDH cohorts to develop an iRHNP prediction model. Initial univariate linear regression revealed significant associations between iRHNP and T-score, Preoperative OCN, Preoperative PINP, Postoperative ΔOCN, Postoperative ΔPINP. Variables achieving *p*<0.10 in univariate analysis were entered into a backward stepwise multivariate linear regression model. Final variable retention required *p*<0.05 significance. Multicollinearity was assessed via variance inflation factors, with all retained variables demonstrating < 2.0 (well below the 5.0 threshold), confirming absence of significant collinearity. Table 4 presents the results of linear regression analysis between factors and iRHNP. In the univariate linear analysis, iRHNP was significantly associated with T-score (p = 0.032), pre-operative serum concentration of OCN (p < 0.001) and PINP (p < 0.001), as well as the mount of concentration changes in OCN (p = 0.009) and PINP (p < 0.001) as independent predictors of iRHNP after adjustment for covariates. Figure 2 visually corroborates these dose-response relationships. The other medical information was also recorded. Some of patients took celecoxib to control pain. Meanwhile some patients were associated with ankylosing spondylitis, femoral head necrosis, hypertension, diabetes and coronary atherosclerosis. We failed to find these events related to the resorption of disc herniation. These results indicate that preoperative bone metabolism status—reflected specifically by OCN and PINP levels—independently correlates with iRHNP magnitude. Elevated bone turnover activity (both formation and resorption markers) appears associated with greater early disc resorption following laminoplasty.

Discussion

Clinical significance of iRHNP mechanism

We proposed the endoscopic way of laminoplasty in patients with both cervical and lumbar disc heriniations. Clearly in elderly patients with multilevel lumbar stenosis, posterior decompression targets hypertrophic ligamentum flavum and facet joints, which are primary contributors to foraminal narrowing. Avoiding discectomy minimizes destabilization and aligns with the goal of inducing spontaneous resorption via bony healing. The goal of this study mainly focused on the mechanism of iRHNP.

Our previous studies have shown the profound iRHNP associated with operation of CMEL in patients with CDH11–13which was further investigated in the underlying mechanism in this present study via analysis of clinical evidences. Understanding this iRHNP mechanism was of great value in the development of novel treatments. In literature, several operations of laminectomy, semi-laminectomy and EOLP preserving the CDH/LDH were few reported as associated with iRHNP phenomena, or at least a low incidence of iRHNP comparing to our kind of surgery12. The reason was assumed that the gap in decompression grooves were 1–3 mm in width, where bony healing was sufficiently happened in our kind of bilateral endoscopic laminoplasty. Whereas in other operations, the incomplete lamina making bony healing hardly seen25,26. The linkage between local bone metabolism and iRHNP at the same zone of spinal canal was therefore proposed. In the results part, patients with osteoporosis/low T-score was associated with a great degree of iRHNP (Fig. 1C; Table 3), in addition to the serum concentrations of bone turnover biomarkers correlated to iRHNP degree (Table 4). The serum factors of OCN and PINP were elevated in osteoporosis patients, suggesting a high rate of bone turnover, which probably indicative to the increased formation/resorption.

Despite confirmed in previous studies, the iRHNP in CDH and LDH was also demonstrated with two representative sets of MRI images in SI 3 and SI 4. In this study, we only explored the iRHNP during the very short time (one week). Indeed, we observed that disc herniation did not regress rapidly at the time point of 1 week but diminished in volume after 3 months. Taking a longer time in consideration made the mechanism complex. In addition, several RHNP mechanism may explain the later diminishment (usually > 3 months) in disc hernation, such as the inflammation27neovascularization28,29 and subsequent phagocytosis30which was not something new. We only proposed the new mechanism of rapid iRHNP, which was not able to be explained by the above mechanism that requiring a long time.

A multi-key iRHNP mechanism related to bone metabolism

Indeed, iRHNP one week after operation was not a single factor responsible story. It was not appropriate to reason iRHNP to bone metabolism, because the two or more disc herniation did not regress synchronously in one same patients/bone turnover state. The local circulation, inflammation response definitely influenced the final results. That may also explain why the dot plots in Fig. 2 quite scattered, but showing a trend. So most of disc herniation regressed similar to each other in the same micro-environment, which was a clear trend in the early stage. While our data suggest preoperative bone turnover markers (particularly OCN and PINP) correlate with early disc resorption, we acknowledge this is unlikely to be the sole mechanism. The scattered distribution in Fig. 2 and asynchronous resorption patterns—where only 49.3% of multi-level CDHs and 58.5% of multi-level LDHs exhibited uniform resorption—highlight the influence of local microenvironmental factors. This multifactorial nature aligns with established literature on spontaneous disc resorption, which emphasizes inflammation-mediated pathways as primary drivers. We also showed the BMP-2 elevated for about one-fold after surgery, indicating the decompression groove started healing. As the classic biomarker of bone metabolism as fracture and osteoporosis, BMP-2 was target for medical modulation31,3233. BMP-2 activated osteogenic genes such as Runt-Related Transcription Factor 2, making it a therapeutic target. Thus, we proposed the bone metabolism events as the major factors related to the multi-keys iRHNP mechanism.

During the acute pro-inflammation and fibrovascular phase of fracture/groove healing, the influence of osteoporosis/low T-score was huge. In addition, the matrix of bone tissue and the disc herniation mainly the nucleus pulposus were similar in compositions of collagen and proteoglycans. The balance between bone formation and resorption was impaired, thus making the increased catabolic activity of bone turnover factors. In details, OCN and PINP which are secreted mainly by osteoblasts, indicative of mineralization and formation of type I collagen34. OCN acted the alignment of collagen fibrils and is required for bone strength. In SI 2, we found only OCN and PINP significantly correlated to T-score, either the pre-operative serum level but the change amount in response to surgery. Other factors included PTH and 1,25-vitamin D3 regulating the concentrations of calcium and phosphorus in blood, β-cross laps working as the final metabolite product of type I collagen, indicative of bone absorption35 were not significantly correlated to iRHNP/T-score. These mean the unbalance of bone turnover, mainly responsible for iRHNP, caused the release of cytokines or enzymes36,37 down-regulated the extracellular matrix (ECM) in the bone tissue, also worked on the disc herniation in the same spinal canal to degrade the ECM, causing diminishment in volume. The delayed time and decreased union rate to fracture in patients with osteoporosis was widely accepted38,39. The increased osteoclast activity may account for iRHNP40.

The classical model of RHNP41 involves two phases. During the initial inflammatory phase, herniated nucleus pulposus exposes matrix proteins that trigger macrophage infiltration and cytokine cascades (e.g., IL-1β, TNF-α). Subsequent VEGF-driven neovascularization enables phagocytic clearance of disc material through matrix-degrading enzymes (MMPs, ADAMTS), while macrophages directly engulf fragments42. In contrast, our proposed bone metabolism-linked mechanism operates through distinct pathways: (1) Shared degradation of extracellular matrix (collagen/proteoglycans) by bone-derived catabolic enzymes during heightened turnover; (2) BMP-2 mediated coupling effects, where surgical groove healing activates osteogenic genes like Runx2 that may modulate MMP production at the bone-disc interface; and (3) Osteoclast-driven collateral resorption, where increased activity in low T-score patients—known to impair fracture healing—exacerbates disc degradation through shared enzymatic machinery. These mechanisms likely intersect rather than compete. Bone turnover markers may reflect a permissive environment where inflammation-driven resorption is amplified—for instance, osteoblast-derived RANKL could enhance osteoclast-mediated phagocytosis of disc material. Surgical trauma from laminoplasty creates a unique milieu: it simultaneously initiates acute inflammation (overlapping with classical resorption phase) while activating bone metabolic responses (evidenced by BMP-2 elevation). This dual activation may accelerate early volume reduction observed at 1 week, though longer-term biological resorption remains unexplored.

The similarities and differences between CDH and LDH

The disc herniation volume and serum biomarkers were found changing similarly in CDH and LDH patients. 1 week post-operatively, iRHNP happened in 79.5% of CDHs and 77.4% of LDHs, no difference between them. That meant, although lumbar spine region weighted more and motioned in a greater degree than cervical spine43in addition to CDH processed a larger portion of cartilage endplate content and penetrated less in depth to PLL than LDH44 the occurrence of iRHNP was unaffected. In response to surgery, biomarkers of OCN, 25-OH-D3, PINP and PTH were significantly down-regulated while level of β-Cross Laps was significantly up-regulated in circulation, with no difference between CDH and LDH groups.

The slight differences between CDH and LDH were found in the frequency of unchanged volume in disc herniation (Table 2), and the distribution of resorption ratio (Fig. 1B). But in CDH and LDH after surgery, the volume changes both correlated to the osteoporosis/T-score, indicating the same iRHNP mechanism. The linear regression analysis therefore used the merged data. In the correlation analysis (Table 3), duration of illness was significantly correlated to iRHNP in CDH, not the LDH. This may be due to the hard toleration to CDH. A long pathological duration of disc herniation lead to the dehydration/calcification of nucleus pulposus45. Therefore the fresh properties of CDH made the regression greater than LDH and correlated with suffering time.

Serum biomarkers for prediction

We identified the pre-operative serum level of OCN and PINP in the linear regression model for prediction of iRHNP degree. OCN and PINP were only two indexes correlated to the T-score in patients (SI 2). Circulating biomarkers were used to reflect the local bone metabolism, which were previously commonly applied in the diagnose of osteoporosis and metabolic bone disease20,35,46which was also proved to be closely related to the abnormal bone metabolic activities in the fracture healing process and femoral head necrosis21showing the good accuracy and the predictive value. In literature reported cellular biology during bone healing, various bone metabolic activity factors can be released to regulate the dynamic balance of bone formation-resorption16. For example, in the early stage of bone, macrophages recruited around the bone healing site can secrete interleukin-1, which can induce COX-1 and COX-2 expression, in which the nucleus pulposus cells can be regulated to degrade the extracellular47. Therefore, bone metabolic factor/bone healing metabolism has significant regulation of the volume of the CDH/LDH, while the indicators in peripheral blood such as OCN and PINP are the accurate predictors of bone metabolic activity factors. We built the model for prediction which was useful in the pre-operative plan.

Future studies will include follow-ups at 3 and 6 months to evaluate long-term resorption and biomarker trends. Preliminary data from 20 patients (included in the results part) suggest sustained volume reduction at 3 months. Until now, we have not find the correlation of iRHNP and bone turnover biomarkers. This also indicated our dual mechanism of iRHNP that both rapid and slow resorption.

Limits of this study

With the limit in explosion of the real local bone metabolism, this study used five biomarkers in peripheral blood instead, as well as the BMP-2 levels to ensure the bony healing. They were able to accurately indicate the changes in the bone formation-resorption activity, but the level of which were influenced by the underlying diseases, exercise and drugs et al. Because the serum biomarkers such as PTH and 25-OH-D3 are regulated by nerves and hormone leading to an increase in variation, some statistical differences may be covered. Moreover, only five biomarkers of bone metabolism were included in this study representative of bone formation and resorption. Other indexes such as tartrate acid phosphatase, sclerostin, leptin, alkaline phosphatase, and interleukin-33 et al.48 were needed to be clarified by further studies.

Conclusion

The serum bone turnover biomarkers in early postoperative period which reflected the local bone metabolism, were key factors related to the rapid resorption mechanism of disc herniation/iRHNP. The pre-operative circuiting levels of OCN and PINP, as well as the T-score/osteoporosis were indicative of the change degrees of disc herniation volume.

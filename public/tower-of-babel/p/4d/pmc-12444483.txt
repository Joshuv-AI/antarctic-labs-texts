Excision of urethral caruncle using a sealing device (LigaSure™): A case report
Authors: Pin-Yu Chen, Hui-Kung Ting, En Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We report a case of a 74-year-old postmenopausal woman with stage III cystocele and a non-circumferential urethral caruncle presenting with bulge, voiding difficulty, occasional bleeding, and meatal discomfort. She underwent anterior colporrhaphy followed by excision of the urethral caruncle using the LigaSure™ device. The procedure was completed within 1 min, with minimal blood loss and no suturing. Postoperative follow-ups showed good wound healing without recurrence or urethral stricture. This case suggests that LigaSure™ may enable rapid, sutureless excision in selected non-circumferential lesions.

Introduction

Urethral caruncles are benign tumors mostly found in postmenopausal women at the posterior lip of the urethra. For patients with asymptomatic urethral caruncles, conservative treatment such as topical estrogen or corticosteroids is typically the first-line therapy. However, these lesions may enlarge over time, especially when irritated or inflamed, leading to symptoms like pain, bleeding, or urinary obstruction. In these cases, surgical excision becomes necessary.

The most common surgical approach is simple excision, suitable for patients with larger lesions or those unresponsive to conservative treatments, with an approximate recurrence rate of 7 %,1 and patients may occasionally experience postoperative pain or prolonged recovery.

Ligasure™, a vessel sealing device, can effectively coagulate and cut the tissue. For extruding, non-circumferential urethral caruncles with a focal base, this permits hemostatic en-bloc excision that often requires no mucosal suturing and may shorten operative time. These advantages have led to its widespread use in various types of surgeries. For example, in hemorrhoidectomy, using Ligasure™ can reduce postoperative pain and shorten postoperative recovery time compared to traditional excision.2 This report describes the excision of a urethral caruncle using Ligasure™, which may have benefits over conventional techniques.

Case

This is a 74-year-old, gravida 2, para 2 postmenopausal female with a history of hysterectomy and right oophorectomy due to uterine myoma and right ovarian fibroma. She presented with the chief complaint of vaginal bulge and difficulty urinating for many years. Physical examination revealed a bulging mass from the anterior vaginal wall, 2 cm outside the hymenal plane, and a non-circumferential, protruding mass at six o'clock over the distal urethra(Fig. 1). Laboratory results showed clean urine analysis. Based on the diagnosis of stage III cystocele and urethral caruncle, the patient received surgical treatment simultaneously for both conditions. The patient underwent anterior colporrhaphy with Calistar™ mesh first. After the cystocele repair, urethral caruncle excision was performed using a LigaSure™. The protruding urethral mass was excised en bloc by the LigaSure™ which took 1 min to complete(Fig. 2). An additional surgical video shows this in more detail [Video Clip 1]. There is no extra procedure for suturing the urethral mucosa. The blood loss was minimal. A 16 French 3-way Foley catheter was placed after the procedure. Discussion

Simple excision, cauterization, fulguration, electrocoagulation, clamping, and cautery are examples of traditional surgical techniques3 for treatment of symptomatic urethral caruncles. Since LigaSure's application for urethral caruncle excision has not been previously published, this technique may provide novel perspectives.

The use of LigaSure™ in this case showed possible benefits, such as a shorter recovery duration, effective hemostasis, minimal pain following surgery, and a shorter recovery period. These benefits align with other surgeries using LigaSure™, such as hemorrhoidectomy. Studies indicated that LigaSure™ hemorrhoidectomy may offer advantages in terms of reduced postoperative pain and faster recovery.2

Additionally, urethral caruncles are vascular lesions, making Ligasure™ a perfect tool due to its ability to effectively seal blood vessels, reducing the operative time and eliminating the need for extensive suturing in small, confined space. In this case, the total surgical excision time was reduced to 1 min.

This approach is best suited for extruding, non-circumferential urethral caruncles that can be safely excised en bloc without mucosal suturing. Circumferential urethral lesions, such as complete urethral prolapse, are less suitable because wider mucosal resection risks extensive tissue loss with concentric scarring and urethral stricture. Alternative methods involving mucosal preservation should be considered in such cases.

Hemorrhoidectomy and endoscopic diverticulotomy using Ligasure™ share similar benefits such as effective hemostasis and minimizing thermal injury, which can reduce intraoperative blood loss and shorten recovery time.4,5 However, despite the advantages, there are still complications to be considered. In studies of hemorrhoidectomy using LigaSure™, complications such as delayed bleeding and anal stenosis caused by thermal spread were noted.6 Likewise, the possibility of urethral stricture following urethral caruncle excision using Ligasure™ should be aware of.

While this case highlights the successful use of LigaSure™ for urethral caruncle excision, further research is still needed. Comparative studies between LigaSure™ and traditional surgical techniques for urethral caruncles would provide stronger evidence. Additionally, evaluating long-term outcomes and recurrence rates would help establish the efficacy and safety of this approach.

Conclusion

This case demonstrates the use of LigaSure™ for excising urethral caruncles, indicating its potential advantages over traditional techniques. The procedure provided effective hemostasis, reduced operative time, and minimized postoperative pain, aligning with benefits observed in other applications, such as hemorrhoidectomy. These results suggest that LigaSure™ may be a promising tool for managing vascular urethral lesions like urethral caruncles.

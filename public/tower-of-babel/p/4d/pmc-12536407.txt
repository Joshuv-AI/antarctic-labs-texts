Vibrational Spectroscopic Analysis of Water Absorption in Polyimides and the Correlation with Dielectric Properties at 10 GHz
Authors: Ririka Sawada, Haonan Liu, Shinji Ando
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Unveiling the mechanism behind the relative humidity (RH) dependence of the dielectric properties of polyimides (PIs) in the high-frequency (GHz) range is a crucial challenge in the development of novel low-dielectric thermally stable polymers. Herein, the correlations among the dielectric constant (D k), dissipation factor (D f), water absorption content, and hydrogen-bonding structures of sorbed H2O molecules were precisely analyzed for 15 types of aromatic and semiaromatic PIs based on variable-RH Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) vibrational spectroscopy. The absorbance of the IR bands assignable to the O–H stretching (ν­(OH)) and H–O–H bending (δ­(HOH)) vibrations of H2O molecules sorbed in PI films gradually increased with an increase in the RH of the atmosphere. Notably, the slopes of the increase in D k and D f against the integrated area of the δ­(HOH) IR band (A δ(HOH)), which can be used as a measure of the concentration of sorbed H2O, were nearly constant and independent of the chemical structure of PIs. However, the slopes of the RH dependences of D k and D f, that is, h Dk and h Df, were strongly dependent on the structure of PIs and proportional to each other. This confirms that the amount of water absorbed is a crucial factor in the RH dependence of D k and D f. The D k values of the PIs increased linearly with an increase in A δ(HOH), whereas the D f values increased linearly with A δ(HOH) at a lower RH but gradually showed a concave-up increase at a higher RH. In addition, the relationship between A δ(HOH) and D f for all PIs was nearly aligned along a single master curve. This unified and marked increase in D f could be induced by both the large D f of water (∼0.5) and the plasticization effect of the H2O molecules sorbed in the PI films. This water-induced plasticization activates the local relaxation motions of the moieties with large dipole moments, i.e., the imide and ester groups, in the PI chains, resonating at 10 GHz. Furthermore, the spectral decomposition of the IR ν­(OH) band enables the classification and characterization of the structures of sorbed and hydrogen bonded H2O molecules at the molecular level, namely 1) H2O molecules directly form hydrogen bonds (HBs) with imide carbonyl (CO) groups, called “bound water,” and 2) H2O molecules form HBs with bound water, called “self-associated water.” Notably, with an increase in A δ(HOH), the total amount of the spectral components of self-associated water (A assoc) increased curvilinearly and simultaneously with D f. The quasi-linear relationship between A assoc and D f suggests that a rapid increase in D f with A δ(HOH), which reflects the activation of the local motion of PI chains via water plasticization, is strongly correlated with the amount of self-associated water. To reduce the dependence of RH and water absorption on D k and D f of PIs at higher frequencies, effective strategies include the introduction of hydrophobic fluorine atoms and the suppression of self-associated water-induced local molecular motion resonating in the same frequency range.

Introduction

Recently, the demand for next-generation wireless telecommunication (telecom) technology to achieve high speed, high capacity, and low latency has rapidly increased. The fifth-generation (5G) telecom services are being widely used in the sub-6 band, below 6 GHz, and in the millimeter-wave band, around 28 GHz. Furthermore, sixth-generation (6G) wireless telecom technology is expected to utilize additional higher frequency bands ranging from 100 GHz to 3 THz. − Currently, liquid crystalline polymers (LCPs) are widely used as low-dielectric insulation applications, such as flexible electronic substrates, antenna-in-packages, and interlayer insulating materials for the semiconductor devices for wireless transmission because they exhibit moderate dielectric constant (D k) below 3.5 and a small dissipation factor (D f) below 0.01 in the GHz range, as well as minimal water absorption owing to their high crystallinity. −

Polyimides (PIs) are also expected to be used in such applications because of their excellent thermal, chemical, and environmental stabilities and high electrical breakdown strength. Specifically, their flexibility, mechanical toughness, high glass transition temperature (T g), and facile formation of thin films are much better than those of LCPs owing to their less crystalline, nearly amorphous nature, supported by strong intermolecular interactions. , However, wholly aromatic PIs exhibit relatively high D k values as well as high water absorption, owing to their polar imide groups interacting with water molecules and their specific dense aggregation structures. For instance, Kapton-H (DuPont), a representative PI, derived from pyromellitic dianhydride (PMDA) and 4,4′-diaminodiphenyl ether (4,4′-ODA) was reported to exhibit D k ≈ 3.2 and D f ≈ 0.0085 at 1 GHz with water absorption of 2.3–2.9 wt %, which should be improved for dielectric applications. Over several decades, huge efforts have been devoted to developing PIs exhibiting low D k , by introducing fluorinated groups, − aliphatic and alicyclic groups, spatially bulky structure, − and flexible backbone. − Furthermore, a reduction in D f at frequencies over the GHz range has attracted much attention and has progressed by introducing rigid structures to suppress the dielectric loss due to dipolar motions. − For example, a typical poly­(ester imide) derived from 1,4-phenylene bis­(1,3-dioxo-1,3-dihydroisobenzofuran-5-carboxylate) (TAHQ) and 4-aminophenyl-4′-aminobenzoate (APAB) exhibits a low D f of 0.0040 at 10 GHz despite its wholly aromatic backbone owing to the suppression of local motion of imide rings by its rigid linear main chains and semicrystallinity. D f values can also be reduced below 0.01 in the GHz range by introducing bulky pendant groups, such as phenyl, 4-naphthyl, 3,5-bis­(trifluoromethyl)­phenyl, p-terphenyl, and tert-butyl groups, into PI chains. These long or bulky pendant groups effectively reduce the dipolar polarizability per unit volume and induce steric hindrance in the internal rotation of the PI chains. A recent study revealed that the D f of 15 types of PIs measured at 10 GHz in the in-plane TE101 mode is linearly correlated with the in-plane component of dipolar polarization (P d(TE)) (R 2 = 0.883), which can be estimated from the D k and in-plane refractive index (n TE) at a near-infrared wavelength (1310 nm). This indicates that the D k and D f of PIs are anisotropic owing to the in-plane orientation of the PI main chains, and the reorientational and relaxational motions of the dipoles resonating at 10 GHz should be suppressed to achieve low-D f PIs. However, a rational molecular design for PIs exhibiting both low D k and low D f irrespective of the operating environment, such as under highly humid conditions, has not yet been established.

Water exhibits a specific dielectric relaxation in the microwave region due to its large dipole moment (μ = 1.85 D), high mobility, and ability to form extensive hydrogen bonds (HBs) through two proton donors (O–H) and two acceptors (lone pairs at oxygen) at H2O molecules, which generate widely distributed intermolecular cooperative vibrations. − Bulk water shows a characteristic Debye relaxation at a resonance frequency of 16.2 GHz at 20 °C, indicating that it exhibits extremely large D k (≈ 60) and D f (≈ 0.5) values at around 10 GHz. As intermolecular interactions between PI chains and sorbed H2O have a significant effect on dielectric properties in the GHz range, their dependence on the relative humidity (RH) has been studied. In 1991, Mercer and Goodman first focused on examining the relationship between the dielectric properties and water absorption of PIs. They reported that D k at 10 kHz for 12 types of wholly aromatic and fluorinated PIs (FPIs) increased linearly with an increasing RH, and their slopes exhibited a highly positive correlation with the weight fraction of imide groups per repeating unit. In 1994, Hougham et al. , reported that the D k values of 10 types of FPIs measured under dry and wet conditions were nearly proportional to the weight fraction of fluorine atoms per repeating unit (F%). Moreover, the D k measured at 40% RH showed a significant increase relative to the dry condition, indicating that a difference in the hydrophobicity as a function of fluorine content leads to differences in D k under ambient conditions. Since then, research on the influence of water absorption on the dielectric properties of PIs has become widespread. Recently, Zhang et al. − measured D k and D f at 10 GHz as well as the water absorption content (W A) under different RH conditions for various aromatic and semialicyclic PIs. They reported that water absorption mainly caused a critical change in the D f of PIs based on the linear relationship between W A and D f and concluded that the essential factors for reducing D k and D f, as well as W A, are appropriate free volume, low imide group density, low polarity, and partial crystallization. Concurrently, our previous study revealed that the D k and D f values of 15 types of PIs, including wholly aromatic, semialicyclic, and FPIs, measured at 10 GHz linearly depend on RH, and the slopes of the linear approximations, representing the RH sensitivity of D k and D f (h Dk and h Df, respectively), are proportional to each other. This relationship must be primarily explainable by the hydrophobicity and higher-ordered structures intrinsic to each PI. Although the physicochemical mechanism of the RH and water absorption dependence of the dielectric properties is still unclear, it can be explained based on the stereochemical structures of the HBs and the direct PI–H2O interactions in the PIs. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for the rational design of low-D f PIs.

Variable-RH Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy is a useful in situ spectroscopic method that can quantitatively detect the concentration, structure at the molecular level, and dynamic behavior of H2O sorbed in polymers. The variable-RH IR analysis is based on the vibrational absorption bands, which are characteristic of H2O, in the mid-infrared (mid-IR) region, i.e., the O–H stretching mode (ν­(OH)) and H–O–H bending mode (δ­(HOH)). FT-IR studies focusing on the quantification and structural characterization of sorbed H2O in various polymers, including PIs, have been reported. − Kusanagi and Yukawa characterized the absorbing states of H2O for 20 types of general-purpose polymers, such as poly­(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF), polystyrene (PS), poly­(ethylene terephthalate) (PET), poly­(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), poly­(ethylene oxide) (PEO), and nylon-6, by measuring their FT-IR spectra in a wet state. They found that two absorption peaks assignable to the antisymmetric (νa(OH)) and symmetric (νs(OH)) O–H stretching modes of sorbed H2O molecules were shifted to higher wavenumbers and approached the peaks of water vapor (3756 and 3655 cm–1, respectively) as the hydrophobicity of the polymers increased. As a notable example, the two corresponding H2O peaks sorbed on PVDF can be explained by the fundamental vibrations of monomeric H2O, indicating a considerably weak interaction between the −CH2–CF2– backbone and H2O. In contrast, these signals were shifted to lower wavenumbers and broadened in the hydrophilic polymers (e.g., PEO and nylon-6). This indicates that the sorbed H2O was strongly bound to the polymer backbones or behaved like bulk water by forming robust HB networks among the H2O molecules. Moreover, they demonstrated that a slight difference in the HB structures in polymer films, especially in hydrophobic films, could be characterized by variable-RH IR spectroscopy. In the field of PIs, Musto et al. − conducted pioneering studies by analyzing water species interacting with the main chains of PIs, such as wholly aromatic PMDA-ODA and fluorinated 6FDA-ODA and 6FDA-6FpDA, by combining FT-IR and gravimetric measurements under various RH conditions. They proposed two important insights: the major HB acceptor of PIs is imide CO groups, and the H2O sorbed in PIs can be classified into (1) “first-shell layer (bound water)” that forms HB with imide carbonyl (CO) groups and (2) “second-shell layer (self-associated water)” that strongly interacts with bound water but without imide CO. Additionally, they examined the local structure of sorbed H2O in a poly­(ether imide) (PEI) derived from m-phenylenediamine (MPD) using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations and indicated that bound water preferentially forms a bridged HB structure with two CO groups across the MPD moiety.

This study aimed to clarify the physicochemical mechanism and relationships between the water sorption behavior and dielectric properties of various types of PIs at the molecular level by combining FT-IR spectroscopy and dielectric measurements at 10 GHz under variable-RH conditions. FT-IR spectra were measured for 15 types of PI films, including wholly aromatic, semialicyclic, partially fluorinated, − and perfluorinated PIs, − as well as PIs containing a biobased isosorbide (ISS) moiety (Scheme ), , using a home-built variable-RH IR spectroscopic system. Specifically, the RH dependence of the δ­(HOH) and ν­(OH) absorption bands was selectively used for the quantitative evaluation of the water content in the PI films as well as the structural characterization of H2O molecules sorbed in the PIs. Finally, the influence of the plasticization effects of sorbed H2O on the RH-dependent D k and D f values is discussed.

Chemical Structures of PIs Used in This Study

Experimental Section

Materials

2,2′-Bis­(trifluoromethyl)­benzidine (TFDB) and 4,4′-(hexafluoroisopropylidene)­dianiline (6FpDA), provided by Central Glass Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan), were used after drying at 120 °C in vacuo. 4,4′-ODA was purchased from TCI (Tokyo, Japan) and purified by sublimation under reduced pressure at 180 °C. 4,4′-(Hexafluoroisopropylidene)­diphthalic anhydride (6FDA) and 1,4′-bis­(3,4-dicarboxytrifluorophenoxy)­tetrafluorobenzene dianhydride (10FEDA) were provided by NTT Advanced Technology Corp. (Kawasaki, Japan) and purified by sublimation under reduced pressure at 250 °C and by drying at 150 °C in vacuo, respectively. Isosorbide-3,6-ditrimellitic anhydride (ISSDA) was synthesized according to the procedures reported in our previous study and used after drying at 130 °C in vacuo. Powder of 6F-6F was provided by Hoechst Celanese Corp. (USA). N,N-dimethylacetamide (DMAc, anhydrous, ≥ 99.8%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used without further purification. Commercial-grade Kapton-H, Upilex-S, -RN, and Neopulim were provided by Du Pont-Toray Co., Ltd., UBE Corporation, and Mitsubishi Gas Chemical Co., Inc. (Tokyo, Japan), respectively.

Preparation of PI Films

PI films were prepared via a typical two-step method through poly­(amic acid)­s (PAAs) as the precursors. − Experimental details can be found in a previous report. First, a diamine was dissolved in DMAc and stirred at 0 °C for 2–3 h. Subsequently, dianhydride was added to the solution with an equimolar amount of the diamine, and the mixture was stirred at approximately 20 °C for 48 h. The solid contents of the PAA solutions were adjusted to 15–30 wt.% to achieve a sufficiently high viscosity. The obtained PAA solutions were spin-coated onto Si wafers at controlled spinning rates. The PAA films on the Si substrates were dried at 70 °C for 50 min and then thermally cured under flowing N2 at 280 or 320 °C for 90 min at a heating rate of 3 °C/min. After the samples were cooled to 20 °C, the PIs were peeled off the Si wafer and used as self-standing films.

Measurements

Dielectric and Refractive Index Measurements

A detailed explanation for the measurements of the RH dependence of dielectric properties (D k and D f) of PIs has been reported. The values of D k and D f were measured by using a cavity resonator (AET, Kanagawa, Japan) connected to a vector network analyzer (Anritsu MS46122B, Tokyo, Japan) at 10 GHz in the TE011 mode. The D k and D f values were measured in a home-built chamber at a temperature of 23 ± 1 °C with varying RH from 60 to 10% at a 5% interval, in which the RH was precisely controlled and varied stepwise by flowing nitrogen (N2) gas. The in-plane (n TE) and out-of-plane (n TM) refractive indices of the PI films were measured by using the prism-coupling method with a PC-2010 prism coupler (Metricon, New Jersey). A near-infrared laser (λ = 1310 nm) was used as a light source to avoid the influence of optical absorption in the UV/ visible regions.

Variable-RH FT-IR Spectroscopy

FT-IR spectroscopy was conducted using an FT/IR-4000 spectrometer (JASCO, Japan) in the range of 4000–800 cm–1. The spectral resolution and number of scans were 4 cm–1 and 64, respectively. For the FT-IR measurements, self-standing PI films 10 × 10 mm wide and ∼25 μm thick were prepared, and an automatic temperature-control stage (Linkam 10036L) with an oval-shaped transmission hole of 2 × 3.5 mm and BaF2 windows was placed in the FT-IR chamber. To reduce the spectral interference fringes caused by Fresnel reflections at the film surfaces, a wire-grid-type IR polarizer on a KRS-5 substrate (Edmund Optics, USA) was inserted normal (90°) to the light path, and the stage was set by tilting 40° to the light path, as shown in the left panel of Figure S1. The experimental apparatus prepared for variable-RH measurements is shown in the right panel. A mixture of air and N2 with a controlled RH from 60% to 10% at intervals of 5% was generated in a home-built chamber of acrylic resin and circulated through the stage and chamber with a flow provided by an air pump. The stability of the spectra was confirmed by holding for at least 30 min after the RH reached the desired value, as shown in Figure S2. Subsequently, the RH-dependent IR spectrum was obtained by subtracting the corresponding background spectrum previously obtained for each RH condition. The IR spectrum of a PI film whose H2O was completely removed (called “Dry” sample) was measured after heating at 150 °C for 30 min under N2 flow followed by cooling to 23 °C. Finally, a precise FT-IR spectrum with a flat baseline was obtained by subtracting the fitted sine curve of the interference fringes according to the experimental and computational procedures shown in Figure S3.

Analysis of the RH-Dependent IR Spectra

Two-dimensional correlation IR spectroscopy (2D-COS) is an analytical method to visualize the correlation between two specific absorption bands with peaks at λ1 and λ2 for the dynamic spectral response when some perturbation (e.g., time, temperature, and light) is induced on a sample. , It can be drawn as “synchronous correlations,” representing a direct response to perturbation, or “asynchronous correlations,” representing a phase difference in response to perturbation. Here, the time-series IR spectra of dried samples maintained at a constant RH condition (50% RH) were measured at 40 s intervals with 16 scans, and the relative rate of the appearance of peaks is discussed. , Free software of 2Dshige was used to draw 2D-COS asynchronous contour maps.

Spectral decomposition of the ν­(OH) band for each RH condition was attempted as an overlapping of Gaussian broadening functions, and a fitted spectrum was obtained by least-squares fitting. To ensure uniformity of the analysis, the center wavenumber and full width at half-maximum (fwhm) of the Gaussian components common among the samples were fixed, and only the intensity was modulated. Only for the samples with significantly different spectral shapes and peak positions were the center wavenumber and fwhm of each component adjusted as appropriate to trace the experimental spectrum.

Density Functional Theory (DFT) Calculation of Imide Model Compounds

Geometry optimization and quantum chemical calculations of the IR vibration spectra were conducted for the imide model compounds (MCs), as shown in Scheme S1. Density functional theory (DFT) with the B3LYP functional was adopted using Gaussian 16 rev.C01 software, in which 6–311++G (d,p) and GD3BJ were used as the basis functions and empirical dispersion correction, respectively. The dimer models of NPPI and PF-NPPI were used as representative structures of PIs in the solid state. To reproduce the hydrated structures, MCs and dimer models interacting with 1–4 H2O molecules were generated and calculated.

Results and Discussion

Variable-RH IR Spectra and Quantification of Water Absorption

The RH dependences of D k and D f for the 15 types of PIs, including the two new PIs derived from 6FpDA measured at 10 GHz, are shown in Figure S4a,b, respectively. As reported previously, the linear relationships with R 2 ≈ 0.99 were obtained for all PIs, and both D k and D f significantly increased with increasing RH. The slopes of the regression lines, defined as dD k/d­(RH) (= h Dk) and dD f/d­(RH) (= h Df), differ depending on the structure of the PIs and represent the inherent RH sensitivities of D k and D f for each PI. Figure S4c shows a linear relationship between h Dk and h Df with a high correlation coefficient (R 2 = 0.99), indicating a strong correlation between the RH sensitivities of D k and D f. This can be related to the bidirectional relationship between the real (D k) and imaginary (D f) parts of the relative permittivity.

The RH-dependent IR spectra of the PI films in the range of 4000–1200 cm–1 are shown in Figure and Figure S5. The signal intensities of both the characteristic ν­(OH) and δ­(HOH) bands, highlighted by using squares in the figure, are assignable to the sorbed H2O molecules, and they proportionally increased with an increase in RH for all PIs. The magnified Figure i,ii of each sample display the difference spectra under wet and dry conditions in the ranges of 3800–3200 cm–1 (ν­(OH) band) and 1700–1550 cm–1 (δ­(HOH) band), respectively, and those of the other PIs are presented in Figures S6 and S7. The overall absorbance of the featureless δ­(HOH) band (A δ(HOH)) can be quantified as an integrated area based on the tangent line between two local minima in Figure i and Figure S7, which are shown in Figure a as a function of RH. Musto et al. , reported that A δ(HOH) is linearly proportional to the water concentration in the PIs, which was evaluated through gravimetric analysis of several PIs, following the Beer–Lambert law. According to their calibration line, Figure a indicates that the water concentration in the PIs increases linearly with RH. Here, the slopes of the linear fit (dA δ(HOH)/d­(RH) = h IR) in Figure a represent the sensitivity of A δ(HOH) to RH, and more directly the degree of hygroscopicity. The trend of h IR in PIs increases in the order of “highly fluorinated (7–11)” < “sBPDA-containing (2, 3)” < “partially fluorinated (5, 6, 12)” < “nonfluorinated (1, 4, 13–15)” PIs. This agrees well with the classification of PIs based on h Dk and h Df. These facts indicate that (i) the hydrophobicity of PIs correlates with the content of fluorine groups (−CF3, −F) , and (ii) the dense chain packing induced by the rigid and planar sBPDA moieties effectively suppresses the atmospheric water absorption of PIs. Figure S8 shows the wide-angle X-ray diffraction (WAXD) profiles (Cu Kα) of commercial PI films. Kapton-H (1) and Upilex-S (2) exhibited remarkable diffraction peaks, which represent their semicrystalline nature. Upilex-RN (3) also exhibits weak diffraction peaks, whereas Neopulim (4) presents only an amorphous halo. Our previous studies have reported that the PIs derived from sBPDA showed higher crystallinity − with reduced coefficients of volumetric thermal expansion. In particular, the volume expansion coefficients of the crystal lattice and a polyimide film of sBPDA-PPD having the same structure as 2 are the smallest in the 13 types of PIs, and those of sBPDA-ODA (same structure as 3) were significantly smaller than PMDA-ODA (same structure as 1). Moreover, Okamoto et al. reported that the permeability coefficient of water vapor of Upilex-RN (3) is significantly lower than that of Kapton-H (1). These facts strongly suggest that the lower atmospheric water absorption of the BPDA-containing PI films (2, 3) originates from the strong intermolecular interactions between BPDA moieties in both the crystalline and amorphous domains, possibly due to the dipolar–dipolar attractions between the imide carbonyl groups.

Water Absorption Dependence of D k and D f for Various PIs

The relationships between D k and D f and the amount of sorbed water in the 15 types of PIs can be evaluated by combining the RH dependence of A δ(HOH) (Figure a) and those of D k and D f (Figure S4a,b) as shown in Figure . Here, A δ(HOH) was assumed to be proportional to water concentration in the PIs. , These relationships quantitatively demonstrate that sorbed H2O on PI films causes a significant increase in D k and D f at 10 GHz. Notably, in Figure a,b, D k and D f represent different behaviors against A δ(HOH).D k is linearly proportional to A δ(HOH) over its entire range (R 2 = 0.982–0.999), whereas D f slightly deviates from linearity in the larger A δ(HOH) range (R 2 = 0.971–0.995). Considering that D f is mainly caused by the local relaxation motions of dipoles with a phase delay at 10 GHz, the upward deviation of D f at a higher RH may result from the inherently large D f of H2O and the indirect effect of “plasticization of the PI main chain” caused by sorbed H2O. Generally, H2O molecules behave as plasticizers after penetrating amorphous polymer films and induce enhancement of the main chain motions corresponding to α-relaxation, resulting in a decrease in T g and Young’s modulus. − In addition, several studies based on the dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA), dielectric spectroscopy, and 2H NMR spectroscopy indicated that the residual H2O in PIs can be associated with the local oscillations dominated by chemical bonds or functional groups, namely γ-relaxations. ,− Bas reported that characteristic γ-relaxation peaks appeared in the −140 to −50 °C range of the DMA of wet PI films at 1 Hz. These peaks were assigned to H2O sorbed on the PIs and shifted to higher temperatures with a decreasing free volume of the PIs. Fontanella et al. also reported that a strong relaxation peak was observed in the dielectric loss spectrum of PI (Ultem 1000) at 225 K under wet conditions, assigned to H2O associated with PI chains via direct HBs to the imide CO groups. These facts strongly suggest cooperative effects between the local relaxation motion of the PI chains surrounding the sorbed H2O and the rearrangement and/or rotational relaxation of the H2O dipoles. Ritchey et al. clarified that the H2O molecules tightly bound to the imide CO induced the plasticization of the main chains of the Kapton-H film via an increase in low-frequency molecular motion based on solid-state 13C NMR spectroscopy. These mechanisms suggest that water-induced plasticization causes activated oscillations of the polar groups in the PIs, resulting in an excessive increase in D f as the concentration of sorbed H2O increases.

Correlation between Peak Wavenumber of ν­(OH) Band and Hydrophobicity of PIs

First, this study focused on the two distinct peaks appearing at higher wavenumbers in Figure (3660–3620 and 3570–3540 cm–1), denoted by νa(OHb) and νs(OHb), respectively. As stated above, Kusanagi and Yukawa revealed that the linear relationship between the peak wavenumbers of νa(OHb) and νs(OHb) of hydrophobic polymers is strongly correlated with their hydrophobicity. Figure shows the positive correlation between the wavenumbers of νa(OHb) and νs(OHb) observed from Figure , which coincides well with the “Kusanagi plot.” Moreover, the plotted wavenumbers in Figure are widely distributed depending on the PI structure, i.e., higher values for 10F-PIs (9–11) and lower values for Upilex-S (2) and ISS-PIs (12–15), despite the same number of imide rings in each repeating unit. This suggests that the Kusanagi plot can be a versatile measure of the hydrophobicity of polymers and that the H2O affinity of PIs is strongly affected by the structural characteristics of PIs, such as fluorine (−F, −CF3), ester groups, and semicrystalline aggregates, in addition to the polar imide rings. −

Detailed Assignment of Components of ν­(OH) Band by 2D-COS Analysis

2D-COS analysis is useful for the spectral decomposition and signal assignment of the ν­(OH) region, and it was applied to the elapsed time variations in the IR spectra of a dried PI film under a constant RH of 50% in the Linkam stage. This experiment distinguished the spectral components appearing in the initial and subsequent stages of the water sorption process. Figure shows the time-dependent FT-IR spectra and 2D-COS “asynchronous” correlation maps for four representative PIs (1, 5, 8, and 11), and those of the other PIs are summarized in Figure S9. Note that the “synchronous” correlation maps showed only a monotonous increase in absorbance without particular features. In Figure a, the 2D-COS asynchronous map of Kapton-H (1) exhibits two pairs of signals with different phases: the first is a pair at 3670 and 3570 cm–1 (labeled A and C, respectively), and the second is that at 3620 and 3430 cm–1 (labeled B and D, respectively). When the intensity at λ1 preferentially increased preceding λ2 in the sequential order of time, , the sign of the cross peak at (λ1, λ2) became positive. Evidently, the signs of the cross peaks observed at the coordinates of (λ1, λ2) = (A, B), (A, D), (C, B), and (C, D) are positive (denoted by red). Quantitative Analysis of the ν­(OH) Band

Figure shows the variations in the integrated area of each spectral component in the ν­(OH) band against the total intensity of A δ(HOH) for PIs 1, 5, 8, and 11, and those of the other PIs are presented in Figure S12. Moreover, the sum of the components assignable to bound waters ((I), (II), and (V)), denoted as A bound, and that of self-associated waters ((IV) and (IV′)), denoted as A assoc, are plotted against A δ(HOH) in Figure . These results show that bound water is preferentially formed in the small A δ(HOH) region, i.e., low H2O concentration in PIs (Figure a), whereas self-associated water shows a concave-up increase and becomes predominant as A δ(HOH) increases (Figure b). Iwamoto and Matsuda stated that H2O molecules sorbed in PMMA form a “core hydration” structure, where sorbed H2O initially bridges two nearby CO groups to form stable hydration sites. When the number of H2O molecules per CO groups (denoted as the H2O/CO ratio) exceeded a critical threshold, additional H2O molecules preferentially clustered around these cores. This indicates that if the H2O/CO ratio exceeds the threshold, incoming H2O molecules preferentially interact with hydrated sites rather than the nonhydrated CO sites via stabilization with the surrounding hydration sites. From this insight, despite the presence of many vacant CO sites, the interaction field around the bound water may promote a preferential formation of self-associated water in the PI films under relatively higher humid conditions (H2O/CO ≈ 0.1 H2O). This mechanism explains the characteristic sorption behavior of self-associated water in FPIs (Figure b–d).

Notably, only a weak correlation is observed, presumably because the P d(TE) calculated from their D k measured at 30% RH already includes the influence of H2O. Moreover, both parameters exhibited considerably small values for some PIs (2 and 5: rigid rod-like PIs; 9 and 11: highly fluorinated PIs). These PIs suggest that the excessive increase in D f originating from plasticization by self-associated water is effectively suppressed by the reduced dipolar polarization, as indicated by their small P d(TE) values. For instance, Upilex-S (2) and PM-TF (5), which have rigid, highly stackable, and quasi-linear main chains, effectively suppressed the dipolar relaxation motions, even though self-associated water was formed around the PI chains. A major part of the semicrystalline Upilex-S films would not be affected by plasticization because H2O molecules cannot enter the highly ordered crystalline domain. , In addition, 10F-4F (9) and 10F-TF (11) can limit water-induced plasticization because H2O molecules hardly approach the main chain because of their high hydrophobicity, in addition to the partially stackable structure of the 10FEDA moiety. Further, the values of a Df‑assoc were relatively large for the PIs derived from ODA (1, 3, 6, and 13), 6FpDA diamines (7 and 10), and 6FDA dianhydride (6–8), despite their relatively small P d(TE) values under a medium 30% RH. For the ODA-derived PIs, the diphenylether linkage is rotatable, and the free volume can be increased by self-associated water at a high RH condition. Moreover, 6FDA- and 6FpDA-derived PIs inherently possess larger free volumes because of their −C­(CF3)2– linkages and relatively lower hydrophobicity than 10F-derived PIs, resulting in the facile sorption of H2O molecules into intermolecular voids and thereby leading to partial plasticization.

The semialicyclic PIs, e.g., Neopulim (4) and ISS-PIs (12–15), show inflection points around A assoc ≈ 0.3 in the correlations between D f and A assoc, as shown in Figure S16. These characteristic behaviors may originate from the specific hydration structures owing to the bulky alicyclic skeletons and ester CO groups. The optimized geometry of ES-NPPI+2w depicted in Figure S17a suggests that two H2O molecules can be arranged to bridge the imide and ester CO groups. Similarly, the neighboring imide CO groups across the distorted cyclohexyl ring of HPMDA-2Ph could also be bridged by two H2O molecules, yet those of PMDA-2Ph could not. As shown in Figure S17b, because the peak positions of νassoc(OHb) and ν­(OHb) of the bridged HB structures are close but indistinguishable from each other, the A assoc values in Figures b and could be underestimated. Intramolecular HBs between neighboring COs can be assumed to preferentially form over intermolecular HBs under relatively low RH conditions, particularly for bulky alicyclic PIs (4, 12–15).

Thus, the following points are essential for the molecular design of PIs exhibiting inherently small D f values under relatively high RH (∼30% RH). (1) Reduction of water absorption and its RH dependence by incorporating hydrophobic fluorine groups; and (2) introduction of a rigid rod-like and planar backbone, promoting dense molecular packing, which inhibits water-induced plasticization. As explained above, PIs with extreme hydrophobicity incorporating many fluorine atoms or semicrystalline rigid backbones, e.g., 10F-PIs and Upilex-S, effectively suppress the excessive increase in D f by preventing self-associated water-induced plasticization and an appreciable reduction in total water absorption.

Conclusions

The origin of the RH dependence and effects of sorbed H2O on the dielectric properties (D k and D f) of 15 types of PIs were elucidated through quantitative structural analyses of sorbed H2O using variable-RH FT-IR spectroscopy. A distinct RH dependence of the ν­(OH) stretching and δ­(HOH) bending absorption bands was observed for all PIs in their difference spectra between the wet and dry states. As A δ(HOH) is proportional to the concentration of water sorbed in PI films, D k and D f measured at 10 GHz significantly increased owing to the increase in the water absorption of PIs at elevated RH. In contrast to the linear and proportional increase of D k, the increase in D f gradually deviated from linearity and increased upward at larger A δ(HOH) values; this is attributable to the large D f of water as well as the water-induced plasticization effects and activation of local relaxation motions of dipoles in the PI chains. Moreover, a Dk and a Df, which represent the sensitivities to water absorption of D k and D f, were almost independent of the chemical structures of the PIs; this is significantly different from those of RH (h Dk and h Df). This result confirms that the dominant factor contributing to the significant changes in D k and D f caused by RH was H2O sorbed onto the PIs.

The ν­(OH) bands in the IR spectra were successfully decomposed into common components assignable to (1) bound water (νa(OHb) and νs(OHb)), which directly interacts with two COs via HBs, (2) self-associated water (νassoc(OHb)), which interacts with at least one bound water, without free O–H, and (3) a specific bound water, with one free O–H (ν­(OHf)) observed only for FPIs. As observed in all PIs, A bound gradually approached its saturation value, whereas A assoc showed a concave-up increase with an increase in A δ(HOH). Furthermore, the quadratic increases in D f and A assoc against A δ(HOH) showed a highly linear correlation with each other; this suggests that the nonlinear increase in D f is strongly affected by the increase in the self-associated water content. The nonlinear increase in D f reflected the activated local motion of the PI main chains, which were plasticized by the excessive incorporation of self-associated water. Thus, the reduction in water absorption by fluorination and a rigid and stackable backbone, both of which suppress water-induced plasticization, are promising strategies for further reducing the D f of PIs in the GHz region.

High COMP expression promotes tumor cell proliferation and migration of rectal cancer, contributing to unfavorable prognosis and suppressive immune microenvironment
Authors: Jin Shang, Wan-Jun Sheng, Heng-Zhe Jia, Dong Wang, De-Zhi Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRectal cancer (RC) is a subtype of colorectal cancer, with high morbidity and mortality rates. Whereas, poor patient outcomes due to high aggressiveness urgently highlight the need for more effective markers and treatments. Hence, this work aims to explore novel pathogenic target in RC.MethodsThe RC expression data was derived from The Cancer Genome Atlas and Gene Expression Omnibus databases. The hub gene was identified using Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), differential expression analysis, protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, whose prognostic value was validated in public cohorts. Then, its impacts on RC cell viability, migration, invasion were determined by Cell Counting Kit-8 and Transwell assays.ResultsBy integrating data from WGCNA and differentially expressed genes, along with multiple protein-protein interaction (PPI) network algorithms, we identified seven key candidate genes. COMP was significantly highly expressed in RC samples, which was also successfully validated in additional cohorts and RC cell lines. In high COMP group, significantly higher infiltrating proportion of M2 macrophages was observed. Along with tumor stage progressed, COMP expression was up-regulated accordingly, implying its tumor promoting roles. Consistent with this, silencing COMP in RC cells significantly inhibited cell viability, migration, and invasion in vitro. In high COMP expression group, patients exhibited inferior prognosis independently of other risk factors, and 18 pathways like TGF-β signaling pathway were significantly enriched.ConclusionCollectively, significant higher COMP expression was noticed in public RC cohorts and RC cells, and si-COMP obviously inhibited the cell viability, migration, and invasion, implying its pathogenic role in RC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04675-0.Keywords: Rectal cancer, COMP, Tumorigenesis, Prognosis

Introduction

As one of the most common malignant tumor in humans, colorectal cancer (CRC) incidence ranks the third among all tumors, with about 900,000 CRC related deaths annually, according to the latest Global Cancer Report by the World Health Organization [1]. Notably, the incidence of CRC has been increasing continuously, with a tendency towards younger population [2]. For this reason, accumulating studies have focused on exploration of the pathogenic molecular characterization of CRC, in order to improve the patient survival [3–7]. Based on anatomical location, CRC is categorized into colon cancer (CC) and rectal cancer (RC). In China, RC cases contribute to more than 30% of all CRC cases [8]. Typically diagnosed in the rectal region, RC is an aggressive malignancy associated with high morbidity and mortality [9–11]. Beyond anatomical distinctions, previous study revealing difference between RC and CC also focuses on the specificity of aetiology and metastasis. For instance, smoking has been considered as a risk factor more strongly associated with RC than with CC [12]. Furthermore, comparing to CC, RC shows a more frequent metastasizing tendency to bone, nervous system and thoracic organs, while less frequently metastasizing to peritoneum [13]. Meanwhile, accumulating studies have focused on the pathogenic mechanisms of RC subtype. Wang et al. have recently reported a temperature-sensitive receptor related signature, to predict the prognostic status and immune microenvironment in RC [14]. In RC, it has been indicated that ST8Sia1 upregulation could interact with TGF-β1 to exert inhibitory effects on tumor progression [15]. In order to ameliorate the negative impacts caused by RC, substantial efforts on therapeutic modalities as well as anti-cancer strategies have been devoted [16]. Surgical resection is a conventional but complicated treatment approach for RC patients [17], whereas it often fails to improve the disease owing to the high recurrence rate and side effects of bowel dysfunction along with surgery [18–20]. Moreover, radiotherapy is efficient to improve the survival of RC patients, while it is often accompanied with diverse adverse effects like urogenital, sexual and bowel dysfunctions [21–23]. Although Pollom et al. have recently proposed the non-operative management of RC and emphasized on treatment de-escalation for appropriate patients, which is still evolving [24]. Considering these negative impacts of current treatment methods and the remaining high mortality of RC due to the lack of valid biomarkers [25], it is imperative to further investigate the pathogenesis of RC and to explore more efficient biomarkers as well as therapeutic approaches.

More recently, genetic factors, such as gene promoter methylation and mutation, have been widely established to play pivotal roles in RC. For example, Yang H et al. have identified seven genes with hypermethylation modification including GNAO1, LONRF2, SORCS1, HAND2, PDZRN4, CNGA3 and RSPO2, which might associate with the initiation of RC [11]. However, the relationship between these genetic factors and clinicopathologic factors has not been fully studied [26]. Cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP), also referred as Thrombospondin-5 (TSP-5), is a main component of the cartilage extracellular matrix and expresses in diverse anatomic sites such as cartilage extracellular matrix, tendon, ligament, bone and blood vessels [27]. COMP has been indicated to exhibit great potential in the diagnosis and prognosis prediction of musculoskeletal disorder [28]. In ovarian cancer, COMP secreted by carcinoma-associated fibroblasts contributes to the tumor progression via activating Notch pathway and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition [29]. Furthermore, wide involvement of COMP in several cancers has also been noticed, such as in breast cancer and prostate cancer [30, 31]. More recently, increasing studies have reported the role of COMP in CC. Chen et al. have revealed that COMP participates in the paracrine mediated by RLIM/PML axis in cancer-associated fibroblasts’ promoting role of CC [32]. In another work, COMP has been evidenced as a prognostic predictor for CC, relating to the infiltration of M2 macrophage [33]. Moreover, several studies have indicated the critical role of COMP in CRC. COMP has been documented as a reliable signature for subtype prediction of CRC, basing on the differences in TGF-β pathways [34]. COMP probably contributes to the CRC progression via regulating epithelial mesenchymal transition [35]. Nonetheless, its specific function and prognostic value in RC subtype remains largely unclear.

Herein, via integrating publicly available data as well as wet lab experiments, we intended to explore the potential role of COMP in RC, especially involving the tumor progression and patient prognosis. Given these findings, our research is expected to reveal novel diagnostic marker and prognostic predictor for RC patients. Our data is promising in providing novel alternative for RC prevention, thereby indirectly improving the clinical management and outcome of RC patients.

Materials and methods

Data source

The mRNA expression profiles and corresponding clinical information of 166 RC patients were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://tcga-data.nci.nih.gov/tcga/), including 166 tumor tissue samples and 9 paired para-tumor tissue samples. The expression data of 473 colon adenocarcinoma samples were also obtained from TCGA database. The downloaded expression matrix consisted of expression values of genes in all samples (rows and columns represented genes and samples, respectively), which could be input into the function of R for subsequent analyses directly. Among them, 157 RC patients had recorded complete survival information, and these RC samples were further stratified by the median COMP expression level into highly-expressed COMP cases (N = 83) and lowly-expressed COMP cases (N = 83). The clinicopathological characteristics of the above 157 patients were shown in Table 1.

Additionally, 4 datasets were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. GSE74602 and GSE32323 datasets included 30 and 17 pairs of RC vs. adjacent samples, respectively. GSE87211 contained 203 RC samples (of them, the survival information of 190 RC samples were recorded) and 160 normal samples. In GSE33113, there were 90 CRC samples and 6 adjacent samples.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

Basing on the gene expression of various samples, the top 25% of genes were screened through variance analysis, which underwent WGCNA in “WGCNA” function package [36]. Then the Pearson correlation coefficient between each gene was calculated, and an appropriate soft threshold β (> 0.8) was adopted to confirm the scale-free network. One-step method was used for building network, and after converting the adjacency matrix into the topological overlap matrix (TOM), a hierarchical clustering tree of genes was generated. The significance of genes and gene modules was calculated to obtain phenotype related gene modules, with threshold value p < 0.05, |Module Membership (MM)| >0.3.

Differentially expressed gene analysis and functional enrichment

To further narrow the candidate genes, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were analyzed using limma package of R [37]. The DEGs with log2FC (Fold Change) value > 1.5 and FDR < 0.05 were screened as significant genes. Moreover, the functional information was obtained based on GO and KEGG enrichment analyses, in clusterProfiler package [38]. The significant terms were screened with p < 0.05.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis and genemania network analysis

The STRING database has been a database used for predicting functional interactions among proteins. We herein used STRING (version 11.0) (https://string-db.org/) [39] to analyze the interplay among proteins. Using cytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape software, totally 5 algorithms were then employed to determine the hub genes in the interaction network, including maximum neighborhood component (MNC), Degree, maximal clique centrally (MCC), edge percolated component (EPC), and density of maximum neighborhood component (DMNC). The final network visualization was done in Cytoscape (version 3.7.2) [40]. In addition, to predict the gene-gene interaction among hub genes, the web-based tool GeneMANIA was employed (http://genemania.org/).

Survival analysis

The overall survival (OS) of RC patients was evaluated employing survival and survminer packages of R software (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=survminer), basing on Kaplan-Meier method [41]. The log-rank test was performed to determine the difference significance in OS between two groups.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

The GSEA software (version: #4.0) was adopted for gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), using the gene set c2.cp.kegg.v7.0.symbols derived from Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) [42]. P value < 0.05 was taken as the threshold to screen significantly enriched Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways.

Calculation of the infiltration proportion of immune cells

The relative infiltration proportions of 22 immune cells were calculated by using the CIBERSORT [43, 44]. It could characterize the composition of infiltrating immune cells with the deconvolution algorithm using 547 pre-set barcode genes, based on the gene expression matrices. The sum of all estimated immune cell proportions in each sample was 1.

Cell culture and transfection

Normal colonic mucosa cell line NCM460 and three tumor cell lines, including SW1463 RC cells, LOVO colorectal cancer cells, HCT-15 colorectal cancer cells, were purchased from Chinese Academy of Sciences in January 2021 (Shanghai, China). More detailed information of above cell lines were summarized in Table S1. All cell lines were cultured in complete Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (Gibco, MA, USA), consisting of DMEM and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, MA, USA). The cells were cultured at 37℃ under saturated humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2.

Regarding small interfering RNAs (si) targeting COMP, it was synthesized according to si-COMP forward 5’-AGAAACUUGAGCUGUGUUGAUGCC-3’ and reverse 5’-GGCUAUCAAGACAGCUCAAGUUUCU-3’. Then the si-COMP and siRNA negative control (si-NC) were transfected into SW1463 RC cells and LOVO cells using COMP siRNA kit (riboFECTTM mRNA Transfection Control (EGFP), C11062, RiboBio, Guangzhou, China), strictly following the instructions of the manufacture. The overexpression sequence of COMP was designed and synthesized based on the gene sequence of COMP (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_000095.3/). The pcDNA-COMP plasmid (Tsingke Biotech, Beijing, China) was then transformed into SW1463 RC cells and LOVO cells, using the previously reported method [45].

RNA extraction and quantitative-PCR (qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated using Trizol reagent (Cat# 15596-026, Invitrogen, Grand Island, CA, USA) and quantified by Nanodrop lite (Thermo, USA). Reverse transcription was conducted to obtain complementary DNA (cDNA) employing TIANScript RT kit (Cat# KR104-02, TIANGEN, Beijing, China). The quantitative PCR experiment was carried out and repeated three times by Light-Cycler480 (Roche, USA) for 40 cycles. The cDNA and SuperRealPreMix Plus kit (Cat# FP205, TIANGEN, Beijing, China) were prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the mRNA levels of COMP were normalized to that of β-actin. The specific primers of COMP and β-actin were listed in Table 2. The relative expression value was calculated by the 2–△△Ct method [46].

Western blotting

Briefly, the western blot was conducted using the common method [47]. The total protein was extracted with radioimmune precipitation assay (RIPA) buffer (R0010-100mL, Solarbio, Beijing, China), which was quantified with bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (CW0014S, CWBIO, Jiangsu, China). Then the protein samples were separated on 10% SDS-PAGE, followed by transferring onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The antibodies used in our present study included COMP Polyclonal Antibody (ab300555, 1:1000, ABcam), β-actin Monoclonal antibody (66009-1-lg-100uL, Proteintech), Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG antibody (ZB-2301-0.1mL, ASGB-BIO), and Goat Anti-mouse IgG antibody (ZB-2301-0.1mL, ASGB-BIO).

Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Various groups of SW1463 cells/ LOVO cells were seeded onto the 96-well plates overnight. Briefly, the cell viability was then determined employing CCK-8 assay kit (C0037, Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) after 24, 48, 72 h, in accordance with the manufacturer protocols. Then the OD values at 450 nm were measured on microplate reader (Infinite F50, TECAN) [48], and the cell viability was calculated using the following formula, Relative cell viability (%) = OD450’/avg (OD450C’) × 100%.

Transwell migration/ invasion assays

The cell migration ability was determined using a Transwell chamber. The 100 µL re-suspended cells (concentration at 1 × 105/mL) were added in the upper chamber, and 300 µL DMEM medium containing 10% FBS was filled into the lower chamber. After incubation at 37℃ 5% CO2 for 48 h, the migrated cells were fixed with 4% polymethanol (AR, DAMAO CHEMICAL REAGENT FACTORY, Tianjin, China) for 30 min, which were then stained using 0.1% crystal violet solution (Q/12GF, GUANGFU, Tianjin, China) and imaged for quantification.

Regarding the invasion ability, the Transwell chambers were firstly precoated with the Matrigel Basement Membrane Matrix (NO 356234, corning) for 1 h at 37℃. The subsequent steps were carried out similarly to the Transwell migration assay as described above.

Statistical analysis

The differences of COMP expression levels between RC tumor and adjacent tissues, as well as samples with different clinicopathological characteristics, were analyzed using Wilcoxon signed-rank test [49]. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to determine the independent prognostic factors for RC. P value less than 0.05 was considered as statistically significant difference. All the above analyses in our present work were performed using R statistical software (v3.5.2).

Results

Crucial candidate genes relating to the development of RC based on WGCNA

To identify the crucial pathogenic targets in RC, the TCGA-RC cohort and 2 GEO datasets were included herein. Firstly, TCGA-RC cohort was employed for the WGCNA, and the clustering analysis indicated that no outliers were observed (Fig. 1A). Based on scale independence and mean connectivity, the optimal soft-thresholding power (β) was determined to be 6 (Fig. 1B). Following network construction, a total of 9 gene modules were obtained (Fig. 1C). Taking sample type as the trait data, the correlation between each gene module and sample type was analyzed. We found that 5 gene modules were significantly correlated with RC samples, consisting of black, blue, brown, green, and turquoise (p < 0.05, Fig. 1D). Among which, brown gene module showed the strongest correlation with RC samples (Fig. 1E). The five RC-associated modules contained a total of 5189 genes, which were subsequently defined as RC related genes for further analysis.

Additionally, we have also identified the DEGs between RC vs. normal samples, to further narrow the RC related genes. In GSE74602 dataset, comparing to normal samples, 673 upregulated genes and 1001 downregulated genes were found in RC samples (Fig. 1F). Based on GSE32323, there were a total of 2505 DEGs between RC vs. normal samples, including 307 upregulated genes and 1198 downregulated genes (Fig. 1G). The cross-analysis among RC related genes (from WGCNA), 1674 DEGs (from GSE74602), and 2505 DEGs (from GSE32323) finally identified 431 overlapped genes, as crucial candidate genes in RC onset (Fig. 1H). Meanwhile, the functional enrichment analysis basing on the above 431 candidate genes was also conducted. There were totally 303 significantly enriched GO terms, such as extracellular matrix organization, collagen-containing extracellular matrix, extracellular matrix structural constituent, and so on (Fig. 1I). As for KEGG pathways, several metabolism related pathways were obviously noticed, like Tyrosine metabolism, etc. (Fig. 1J). The detailed enrichment results were summarized in Table S2.

High expression of COMP was associated with the tumorigenesis of RC

Next, to characterize the interactions among the 431 candidate genes and to further narrow down the candidates, the PPI networks were built employing 5 algorithms, including MNC, Degree, MCC, EPC, and DMNC. The top 50 genes identified by each algorithm were used to generate PPI networks, as illustrated in Fig. 2A and E, separately. We noticed that among these genes, BMP2, EFEMP1, COMP, FGF13, CCR7, MGP and MMP3 were shared genes, implying their central roles in RC (Fig. 2F). Then the PPI network was also constructed using these 7 shared genes, which contained 14 nodes and 88 edges (Fig. 2G). The GeneMANIA network included 20 nodes and 80 edges, involving in functions like stress-activated protein kinase signaling cascade, stress-activated MAPK cascade, etc. (Fig. 2H). Integrating with previous literature, we have focused on the COMP in our subsequent work.

Then the expression of COMP was validated in public datasets and in RC cell lines. Firstly, COMP expression levels in paired RC tumor tissues and adjacent tissues were analyzed, and we found that the COMP expression level in paired tumor tissues was significantly higher than that in adjacent tissues (N = 9, P = 0.013, Fig. 3A). Compared to adjacent tissues, the COMP expression level in all RC tumor tissues was also obviously upregulated (P = 8.8e-06, Fig. 3B). Considering the disease correlation, we have also analyzed COMP expression between colon adenocarcinoma and RC, and relatively higher COMP expression was observed in RC samples comparing to colon adenocarcinoma samples (Figure S1). Moreover, in GSE87211 (Fig. 3C) and GSE33113 (Fig. 3D) cohorts, significantly higher COMP expression was observed in RC and CRC samples comparing to normal samples, respectively. Meanwhile, the ROC results basing on GSE87211 (Fig. 3E) and GSE33113 (Fig. 3F) indicated that COMP exhibited good diagnostic value in distinguishing tumor samples from normal samples. These results revealed that elevated COMP expression might be associated with the initiation of RC.

To further confirm the expression of COMP in RC, the COMP mRNA and protein expression levels in cell lines were then detected. The mRNA levels of COMP in RC cells, colorectal cancer cell lines and colorectal mucosa cell line were detected. The qPCR results showed that COMP was significantly up-regulated in RC cells and colorectal cancer cell lines, compared to normal cells (Fig. 3G, P < 0.001). The COMP protein was also significantly elevated in tumor cells (Fig. 3H, P < 0.001). Therefore, wet lab experimental validation results indicated that the COMP might involve in the occurrence of RC, in line with our results basing on public data.

Correlation between COMP expression and clinicopathological characteristics

Additionally, to decipher the potential impacts of COMP on RC, the relationship between COMP and various clinicopathological characteristics was also explored. After analyzing the relationship between COMP expression and clinicopathological characteristics in TCGA cohort, we found that the expression of COMP was elevated along with the increase of tumor, node, metastasis (TNM) stage in RC patients (stage I vs. stage II, P = 0.0012; stage I vs. stage III, P = 0.00011; stage I vs. stage IV, P = 4e-05; stage II vs. stage III, P = 0.48; stage II vs. stage IV, P = 0.077; stage III vs. stage IV, P = 0.33, Fig. 4A). Moreover, significantly differential COMP expressions were observed between patients with stage I vs. stage II, stage I vs. stage III and stage I vs. stage IV (P < 0.05). In addition, there was no significant COMP expression difference among patients with various genders or ages (P = 0.88 and P = 0.37, respectively, Fig. 4B and C). Our above findings indicated that COMP was probably involved in the progression of RC, regardless of patient age and gender.

Then, no pronounced difference in the tumor mutation burden (TMB) was observed between the high and low COMP expression RC groups (TCGA cohort, P > 0.05, Fig. 4D). High COMP expression was associated with inferior prognosis of RC patients

To determine the influence of COMP expression on the prognosis of RC patients, survival analysis was conducted on the RC patients with different COMP expression levels. The RC samples in TCGA cohort were then divided into high COMP expression and low COMP expression RC groups, according to median COMP expression level. Compared to the patients with low COMP expression, highly-expressed COMP patients exhibited significantly worse OS (P = 0.046, HR = 0.76, 95%CI: 0.69–0.89, Fig. 5A). Similarly, in GSE87211, highly-expressed COMP RC patients had significantly inferior prognosis (Fig. 5B). In both cohorts, dead RC patients distributed more in highly COMP expressed patients (Fig. 5C and D).

Then multivariate Cox regression analysis was conducted (in TCGA cohort), which adopted multiple potential factors including age, sex, TNM stage, and COMP expression, in an attempt to determine whether COMP expression was an independent prognostic factor for RC. The results revealed that COMP expression was still associated with the OS of RC patients, indicating that COMP expression was a promising predictor for poor prognosis of RC patients, independently of age, sex and TNM stage (HR = 1.19, 95%CI: 1.017-1.4, P = 0.029, Fig. 5E).

Moreover, to further evaluate the prognostic predictive performance of COMP in RC, the receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted. The 1-, 3-, 5-year area under curve (AUC) values of COMP in TCGA cohort were 0.722, 0.627, 0.624, respectively (Fig. 5F). The 1-, 3-, 5-year AUC values of COMP basing on GSE87211 were 0.637, 0.644, 0.628, separately (Fig. 5G). In short, our data indicated that COMP was a reliable unfavorable prognostic factor for RC.

COMP related functional information in RC

In order to further explore the underlying functional impacts of COMP on RC, enrichment analysis was carried out. The RC patients (TCGA-RC cohort) were classified into two groups basing on their medium COMP expression level. Comparing to low COMP expression RC samples, we found that there were 3101 upregulated genes and 38 downregulated genes in COMP highly expressed RC samples. For these 3101 upregulated genes, ClueGO enrichment analysis revealed that 2018 terms, like extracellular matrix organization, etc., were significantly enriched (top 100 terms, Fig. 6A). These 3101 upregulated genes were significantly enriched in 83 KEGG pathways, such as ECM-receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, etc. (top 20 pathways were shown in Fig. 6B). Furthermore, the GSEA results indicated that 18 signal pathways were significantly activated in high COMP expression group (Table S3), including arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy hcm, and calcium signaling pathways, etc. (representative pathways, Fig. 6C). COMP silencing significantly inhibited the RC cell viability, migration, and invasion in vitro, while COMP overexpression acted opposite roles

Considering the crucial role of COMP involving the tumor malignant progression in RC, we have also evaluated the impacts of COMP knockdown and overexpression on SW1463, LOVO tumor cell functions. Firstly, the COMP silencing efficiency in SW1463, LOVO cells was confirmed employing qPCR and western blot (Fig. 7A and B). Comparing to si-NC group, the cell viability (Fig. 7C), cell migration (Fig. 7D), and cell invasion (Fig. 7D) ability were all significantly suppressed in COMP silencing RC cells. Moreover, pcDNA-COMP transfection significantly enhanced the mRNA and protein levels of COMP (Figure S2A-S2B). OE-COMP cells exhibited significantly stronger cell viability (Figure S2C), cell migration (Figure S2D), and cell invasion (Figure S2D) ability comparing to cells in control group. Our in vitro data indicated that COMP silencing significantly inhibited the RC cell viability, migration, and invasion, and high COMP expression contributed to the enhanced cell viability, migration, and invasion ability in RC tumor cells, thereby promoting RC tumor progression.

Discussion

RC represents a common malignancy, which often brings severe consequences for the life quality of patients [50]. Despite significant advances in therapeutic strategies, distant metastatic relapse continues to pose a major clinical challenge, resulting in high mortality rate [51]. Therefore, substantial efforts have been devoted to seek for specific biomarkers, in an attempt to enlarge the study range and to provide more alternatives for the diagnosis and treatment of RC patients. Hence, our present study has focused on the role of a novel marker COMP in RC, in order to decipher more underlying details behind RC.

COMP belongs to the thrombospondin gene family, which possesses diverse roles in various human diseases [52, 53]. However, to the best of our knowledge, the function of COMP in RC remains largely unclear. In our present research, the COMP expression in paired RC tumor tissues and para-tumor tissues were analyzed, and the results showed that paired tumor tissues presented significantly increased COMP expression level compared to para-tumor tissues. This difference remained significant even including all tumor samples. Wet lab experiments further validated the elevated mRNA/ protein levels of COMP in RC cell lines, indicating that high expression of COMP might be associated with the tumorigenesis of RC. Meanwhile, the participation of COMP in various cancers has been previously reported. For example, COMP showed higher expression level in breast cancer tissues than that in normal tissues, which correlated with increased metastasis and cancer recurrence [31], which was in line with our findings in RC. In prostate cancer, COMP has been evidenced to promote the progression and development of disease, and was correlated with cancer recurrence [31]. In addition to these cancers, recently, the aberrant expression of COMP was also found in CC, another subtype of CRC. Elevated COMP expression was observed in patients with early-onset CC, which was related to the enhanced proliferative ability of CC cells [54]. Huang et al. have demonstrated that COMP silence significantly inhibited mesenchymal markers and restored E-cadherin, suppressing the cell proliferation and invasion of CRC [35]. All above data further supported our findings in RC, which was consistent with the latest studies involving COMP‘s promoting role of CRC. Moreover, our in vitro data also indicated that high COMP expression significantly promoted the cell migration and invasion of RC. It has been reported that in hepatocellular carcinoma, COMP/CD36 signaling induced the phosphorylation of ERK and AKT, leading to obvious upregulation of oncogenic markers, like epithelial mesenchymal transition related markers MMP-2, MMP-9, Slug, etc [55, 56]. Veljkovic st al. have evidenced the critical role of ROS-NF-κB-MMP-9 axis in progression of CRC [57]. COMP showed significantly differential expression in CRC clusters with distinct TGF-β pathway status, with important predictive value [34, 58]. Collectively, we have reason to speculate that the promoting role of COMP in RC is probably implicated in activating epithelial mesenchymal transition and multiple metastasis-related markers.

Since clinical treatment decisions for RC largely depend on tumor staging, accurate determination of the TNM stage is crucial. Although conventional diagnostic techniques, such as endorectal ultrasound, CT, and MRI, exhibit high accuracy in some cases, they perform less well in RC patients of certain stages [21]. In order to explore whether COMP expression could serve as a biological marker for RC staging, the stratification analysis based on TNM stage has been conducted. Notably, COMP expression increased with higher TNM stages, and obvious differences in COMP expression levels existed between patients of stage I vs. stage II, stage I vs. stage III and stage I vs. stage IV. These findings indicated that COMP expression performed well in distinguishing RC patients of early stage from advanced patients, promisingly contributing to optimizing the therapeutic modalities for RC management.

Furthermore, the prognostic value of COMP has been widely investigated in diverse human diseases, including some cancers. COMP in serum has been adopted as a prognostic predictor for risk of knee osteoarthritis, which presented high level in the first three years [59]. In CC, elevated COMP expression was correlated with poor outcome, showing clinical promise as a tool for prognosis prediction [54, 60]. Seeking to clarify the prognostic significance of COMP in RC, we conducted the survival analysis. The results revealed that COMP up-regulation was related to poor outcome in RC independently of clinical parameters including age, sex, and TNM stage, suggesting that COMP was a promising prognostic marker in RC.

Additionally, to further elucidate the functional role of COMP in RC, we performed functional enrichment analysis after confirming its tumor-promoting effects in vitro. In RC patients with high COMP expression, 18 signal pathways were significantly activated, including arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy hcm, and calcium signaling pathways, etc. It is known that COMP plays a key role in maintaining cardiac function via stabilization of integrin β1, and up-regulation of COMP has been observed in cardiomyopathy [61]. In cases of hypertrophy and injury, the thrombospondin family would exhibit elevated expression level to protect cells from death through mediating adaptive stress responses [62]. This was also consistent with our finding that high expression of COMP was associated with cardiomyopathy-related pathways. Calcium signaling pathway is a vital biological process in cells, which displays an anti-death role [63]. In prostate cancer, Emelie E et al. have investigated the function of COMP, demonstrating that high COMP protected tumor cells from apoptosis by regulating intracellular calcium signaling [31]. We therefore speculated that increased expression of COMP might involve in RC as an inhibitor of apoptosis by regulating calcium signaling pathway. Nevertheless, further experimental studies are required to validate this hypothesis and clarify the precise biological mechanisms by which COMP contributes to RC progression.

Although our present study has preliminarily revealed the promoting role of COMP in RC integrating multiple public cohorts, several limitations should be recognized. Firstly, we have to acknowledge that validation in a larger clinical cohort sample size would provide more reliable evidence in our future study. Additionally, we herein focused on the tumor promoting role and prognostic value of COMP in RC, while more deeper underlying mechanisms should be deciphered in subsequent studies. The potential COMP expression and function difference between RC and related tumors (like CC) has not been explored. Looking forward, future work should focus on validating the functional role of COMP in vivo using relevant animal models of RC. Exploring the downstream molecular mechanisms and interacting partners of COMP will be crucial to fully understand its impacts on RC progression. Furthermore, the potential different impacts of COMP on RC and colon cancer are also important issues to be explored. These steps are essential for translating our findings into potential clinical applications.

Conclusion

Collectively, our study reveals for the first time a tumor-promoting role of COMP in RC. Elevated COMP expression has been indicated to enhance RC cell growth and contribute to RC progression, leading to an inferior prognosis. Furthermore, COMP might be implicated in RC via regulating calcium signaling pathway. These findings highlight COMP as a potential therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker, which is promising to offer novel alternatives to improve the clinical management of RC.

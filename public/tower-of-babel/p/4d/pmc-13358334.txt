HYPOChLorous Acid TEsting Studies: ELECTIVE Surgeries (HYPOCLATES:ELECTIVE): hypochlorous acid versus isotonic lavage for the prevention of surgical site infections in elective visceral surgery in Germany – protocol for a sequential, quasi-experimental cohort study
Authors: Tobias Hoch Al Hessen, Niels Siegel, Mika P Nadvornik, Axel Kramer, Jonathan M Harnoss, Pascal Probst, Mohammed Al-Saeedi, Martin Loos, Christoph W Michalski, Julian C Harnoss
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionSurgical site infections (SSI) impose a substantial clinical and economic burden on healthcare systems, accounting for approximately 15% of nosocomial infections. While intraoperative lavage is predominantly performed with isotonic solutions, its efficacy in preventing SSI remains unproven. Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) has emerged as a promising antimicrobial agent due to its potent microbicidal properties and high biocompatibility. The primary objective of this study is to evaluate whether the systematic introduction of a standardised intraoperative lavage with HOCl reduces the incidence of SSI in patients undergoing elective visceral surgery compared with conventional irrigation with isotonic saline. It is hypothesised that the implementation of HOCl-based lavage will lead to a significant reduction in the composite rate of superficial, deep and organ/space SSIs during the immediate postoperative hospitalisation period.MethodsThis is a single-centre, prospective, sequential, quasi-experimental trial conducted at Heidelberg University Hospital. A prospective cohort of approximately 500 adult patients undergoing elective visceral surgery with HOCl-based lavage (Granudacyn) will be compared with a sequential retrospective control cohort of 500 patients treated with isotonic solution. The primary outcome is the incidence of SSI (superficial, deep or organ/space) according to Centres for Disease Control and Prevention criteria, assessed until hospital discharge or postoperative day 10 (±2 days). Secondary outcomes include length of stay in the intensive care unit and hospital, duration of antibiotic therapy and the Comprehensive Complication Index (CCI).Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Medical Faculty of Heidelberg University (S-522/2025). Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at international surgical congresses to inform clinical practice and future SSI prevention guidelines.Trial registration numberDRKS00038635

Introduction

Surgical site infections (SSI) represent a major clinical and economic challenge, accounting for a substantial proportion of hospital-acquired infections and contributing to prolonged hospital stays as well as increased morbidity and mortality.1 2 In Germany alone, SSI account for approximately one million additional hospital days and €3 billion in annual excess costs,3 underlining the need for effective preventive and therapeutic strategies.

The most recent global guidelines for the prevention of SSI published by the WHO state that there is currently insufficient evidence to recommend for or against the routine use of saline wound or peritoneal lavage before incision closure.4 Consequently, while some surgical teams routinely employ isotonic solutions to mechanically clear tissue debris and reduce microbial load, the practice lacks definitive guideline endorsement. This uncertainty in international guidelines highlights the clinical need for an irrigative substance to undergo critical evaluation and outperform isotonic solution in its preventive capacity against SSI. Hypochlorous acid (HOCl), a key component of the human innate immune response, combines broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity with favourable tissue compatibility, making it a promising candidate for usage in preventive intraoperative lavage.5 6 Existing data on HOCl in wound care and intracavitary use suggest good tolerability,7–9 but robust clinical evidence in the setting of routine surgical irrigation remains scarce and is largely confined to smaller or indication-specific studies.10

Elective visceral procedures constitute a heterogeneous population in which SSI remain frequent despite adherence to standard preventive measures.11 In this context, an HOCl-based lavage strategy, applied broadly across elective operations, offers the opportunity to evaluate a potential generalised effect on SSI risk without restricting the analysis to single disease entities. Accordingly, the primary objective of this study is to evaluate the clinical impact of a department-wide transition in the institutional standard of care from conventional isotonic lavage to a standardised intraoperative lavage using HOCl. The central hypothesis is that HOCl-based intraoperative wound and intracavitary irrigation will lead to a significant reduction in early postoperative SSI rates (superficial, deep and organ/space) detected during the index hospital stay up to postoperative day 10 (±2 days), compared with the historical standard of isotonic irrigation.

Methods and analysis

Study design

Hypoclates:Elective employs a single-centre, quasi-experimental, sequential cohort design. This pragmatic approach evaluates a systematic, department-wide transition in the institutional standard of care – moving from conventional isotonic lavage to a standardised intraoperative lavage using HOCl (Granudacyn). To minimise selection bias and ensure real-world applicability, the study compares two temporally distinct cohorts within a routine clinical setting.

Reporting adheres strictly to the STrengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement guidelines for observational cohort studies, while the protocol was prepared in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) framework to ensure comprehensive reporting of procedural and trial-related interventions.

Study population

The study targets a broad, unselected cohort of adult patients scheduled for elective visceral procedures involving the abdominal, thoracic or pelvic cavities. This comprehensive approach across diverse elective procedures enables the detection of generalised HOCl effects that might be overlooked in indication-specific trials.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria are age≥18 years and elective visceral surgery in the defined anatomical regions; exclusions comprise inability to receive antibiotic therapy, organ transplantation and pregnancy or breastfeeding, with pregnancy defined from conception until the end of gestation (table 1).

Patient and public involvement

Due to the highly standardised nature of the surgical workflows, patients were not involved in the initial design. However, the reduction of SSI remains a high priority for patients as it directly impacts recovery and quality of life. Following study completion, a post-trial patient advisory group will be established to review dissemination materials and assist in codeveloping a lay-summary of the findings, ensuring technical outcomes are translated into patient-centred care recommendations

Study flow

All patients undergoing elective visceral surgery of the thorax, abdomen or pelvis during the study period (12 August 2025–22 March 2026) will be screened for eligibility and may be excluded according to predefined criteria. Patients treated between 12 August and 30 November 2025 who received standard intraoperative lavage with isotonic solution will constitute the retrospective control cohort. Beginning on 1 December 2025, all eligible patients will undergo a single intraoperative lavage with 3 L of HOCl solution (Granudacyn) according to the new clinical standard. Patient observation will continue for an additional 16 week period until 22 March 2026, forming the prospective cohort. The complete study flow is shown in figure 1.

Study participation requires no additional procedures, diagnostics or interventions beyond routine clinical care, placing no extra burden on patients or staff. Consequently, no separate patient information or consent is required for the prospective cohort. Should recruitment targets not be met within the planned timeframe, either cohort period may be extended by 4 weeks. Postoperative outcomes will be assessed through a 1 month follow-up. Perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis will follow institutional protocol, with repeat dosing every 2–4 hours intraoperatively or after blood loss exceeding 1.5 L.

Lavage details

The intraoperative lavage is performed by the operating surgeon using 3 L of the active agent Granudacyn – a pH-neutral, hypotonic electrolyte solution containing stabilised HOCl (50 ppm) and sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl, 50 ppm) in equilibrium.12 The 3 L irrigation volume is applied intraoperatively to the peritoneal cavity and the deep and superficial wound layers prior to closure. The lavage fluid is instilled by the operating surgeon, allowed to remain in contact with the tissues for a standardised duration of at least 3 min to ensure adequate antimicrobial exposure, and is then fully evacuated using active surgical suction before primary wound closure. A fine coating of the surgical site surface with the agent persisting beyond this period must be expected, thereby correspondingly extending the duration of action. Adherence is ensured via a standardised documentation sheet within the electronic medical record (SAP IS-H*med).

The following postoperative complications will be assessed: Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhoea, lymphatic fistula, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, urinary tract infection, stroke, myocardial infarction, portal vein thrombosis, ischaemic complications due to vascular stenosis or thrombosis, gastrointestinal bleeding, anastomotic insufficiencies and fistulas. All complications will be assessed according to ISGPS definitions where applicable and graded using the Clavien-Dindo classification system. The Comprehensive Complication Index (CCI) will be calculated for each patient.

Postoperative interventions and management measures will be recorded, including time in intensive care, whether percutaneous or CT-guided drainage was required, whether a relaparotomy was performed within 30 days, and whether the patient was readmitted within 30 days postdischarge. The overall length of hospital stay will also be documented.

Postoperative laboratory monitoring: analogous to the preoperative situation, infection values are monitored during the postoperative course.

Endpoints and hypothesis

The primary endpoint is the composite incidence of SSI, including superficial incisional, deep incisional and organ/space infections, diagnosed according to the clinical and diagnostic criteria established by the Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). To evaluate the immediate prophylactic impact of the perioperative HOCl-lavage, the observation period is specifically limited to the inpatient phase, concluding at hospital discharge or no later than postoperative day 10 (±2 days). This focused window ensures high data granularity and captures infections directly related to the perioperative intervention, while avoiding the logistical challenges and potential loss-to-follow-up associated with post-discharge assessments. The primary hypothesis is that intraoperative lavage with HOCl is associated with a lower incidence of SSI compared with lavage using isotonic solution.

Data management

All study-related data will be managed and stored in accordance with the European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) – specifically Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council13 – and the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki as amended in 2013 and updated in 2024.14 Infectious outcomes and SSI are classified strictly in accordance with the standardised Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines.15 Patient data will be pseudonymised at the point of inclusion, with a strictly confidential identification list stored separately from the study database on a secured, password-protected institutional server at Heidelberg University Hospital.

Data collection will be performed using a standardised electronic Case Report Form. To ensure data integrity and high quality, 10% of the entered data will be randomly selected for internal monitoring and cross-checked against the original electronic medical records by an independent researcher not involved in primary data acquisition.

In accordance with the guidelines of the German Research Foundation (DFG) and institutional standards for Good Clinical Practice (GCP), all study-related records, including the master file and pseudonymised data sets, will be archived for a minimum of 10 years following the conclusion of the study. After this period, data will be permanently deleted unless further regulatory or scientific requirements necessitate extended storage. Access to the final data set will be restricted to the primary investigators; however, pseudonymised data may be made available to other researchers on reasonable request and following approval by the principal investigator.

Sample size

The target sample size of n=1000 was determined based on the annual patient volume at our high-volume tertiary centre. This size reflects a pragmatic approach to evaluating a systematic, department-wide transition in the institutional standard of care. Patients will be equally distributed between the two sequential cohorts (n≈500 per group). An a priori power calculation was performed to ensure sufficient statistical robustness for this prospective design. Assuming a baseline SSI incidence of 12% in the elective visceral surgery control group – consistent with current literature for diverse elective abdominal procedures11 – a total sample size of n=1000 provides a power of 80% (1−β=0.80) at a significance level of 5% (α=0.05) to detect an absolute risk reduction (ARR) of approximately 5.5% (corresponding to a reduction from 12% to 6.5%). The magnitude of this ambitious clinical target is considered biochemically and clinically plausible because the standardised lavage protocol targets both the deep intraperitoneal/intracavitary compartment and the superficial extraperitoneal subcutaneous and fascial layers during wound closure. By irrigating the entire surgical tract, the risk of extraperitoneal incisional SSI is directly addressed alongside deep organ/space infections. This study is primarily exploratory and intended to generate precise effect-size estimates and safety data under real-world conditions to power future confirmatory multicentre randomised controlled trials, rather than to serve as a definitive, high-powered efficacy test. Nevertheless, the sample size is substantially larger than those in previous indication-specific trials,8–10 enabling the detection of generalised effects of HOCl-lavage across a heterogeneous elective population. Furthermore, this cohort size allows for robust multivariable logistic regression analysis to adjust for potential confounders, such as age, ASA physical status and the degree of surgical contamination.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables will be summarised as means with SD or medians with IQRs, depending on distribution; group comparisons will use t-tests or non-parametric alternatives where appropriate. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The primary analysis will estimate the association between HOCl lavage and SSI occurrence using multivariable logistic regression, adjusting for relevant confounders derived from baseline and perioperative variables (eg, age, ASA class, contamination grade, procedure type, comorbidities). Effect estimates will be reported as ORs with 95% CIs, and two-sided p-values<0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Preplanned subgroup and sensitivity analyses (eg, by procedure category or contamination class) will explore the robustness of the findings and potential effect modification. Missing data will be reported transparently, including the number of patients with incomplete information per variable; if appropriate, multiple imputation or sensitivity analyses will be used to assess the impact of missingness on key outcomes.

Study status

As of May 2026, the observational periods for both the retrospective cohort (August to November 2025) and the prospective cohort (December 2025 to March 2026) have concluded. Data extraction from electronic medical records is currently in progress, with clinical data cleaning ongoing.

Discussion

Significance and clinical implications

The core significance of Hypoclates:Elective is rooted in the high frequency of elective visceral procedures, where incremental improvements in individual outcomes generate a vast cumulative impact. While the baseline risk per patient in elective surgery is lower than in emergency settings, the sheer volume of these operations means that the 5.5% ARR targeted in this trial would translate into a massive systemic decrease in morbidity. Successfully reducing SSI incidence at this scale would directly alleviate the burden on healthcare resources.

A pivotal clinical implication of this study is its contribution to antimicrobial stewardship. By effectively preventing the onset of infections intraoperatively, the subsequent necessity for therapeutic postoperative antibiotic treatment is directly minimised. This reduction is essential for limiting the broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure that drives the selection and spread of multidrug-resistant organisms. Consequently, establishing the efficacy of HOCl-based lavage provides a scalable, cost-effective strategy to improve surgical quality while simultaneously addressing the global challenge of antimicrobial resistance.

Safety and risk–benefit considerations

The study is designed as a pragmatic, sequential cohort trial, evaluating a systematic transition in the institutional standard of care. As a purely observational, non-interventional study, Hypoclates:Elective imposes no additional procedures, diagnostics or therapeutic interventions on participants beyond the new established standard. This design ensures that the trial reflects real-world clinical practice while minimising disruption to perioperative workflows.

From a safety perspective, the intervention utilises Granudacyn, a CE-marked class IIb medical product with a well-documented safety profile for both wound and peritoneal irrigation. Given that data collection is strictly limited to routine clinical documentation and the product is already approved for the intended application, the risk to participants is negligible.

Ethically and scientifically, the study is justified as it addresses a significant knowledge gap regarding the utility of physiologically compatible antimicrobials in broad surgical populations. The results will provide the necessary proof-of-concept to potentially pave the way for future large-scale, multicentre randomised controlled trials.

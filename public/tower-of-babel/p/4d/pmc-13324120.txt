PPARα activation alleviates lithocholic acid-induced liver injury by inhibiting pyroptosis
Authors: Hang-Fei Liang, Chuo-Ying Mai, Xuan Li, Jia-Ning Tian, Hai-Guo Su, Min Huang, Jian-Hong Fang, Hai-Tao Wang, Xiao Yang, Hui-Chang Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and aimsThe mechanism of cholestatic liver injury (CLI) is unclear, and effective therapies are lacking. While peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) agonists show potential hepatoprotective effect and pyroptosis is implicated in hepatocellular damage, how PPARα activation mitigates lithocholic acid (LCA)-induced pyroptosis remains unknown.MethodsThe hepatoprotective effect of PPARα agonists was evaluated in a mouse model of intrahepatic cholestasis induced by LCA. Liver injury was assessed via serum biochemistry, hematoxylin and eosin and TUNEL staining, and electron microscopy. Pyroptosis pathways were analyzed using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction, Western blot, and co-immunoprecipitation.ResultsCombined morphological, histopathological, and biochemical analyses confirmed that PPARα activation protects against CLI. Compared with LCA treatment alone, PPARα activation significantly attenuated the elevation of serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), the increased TUNEL-positive cells, and the formation of hepatocyte membrane pores. Mechanistically, PPARα activation suppressed both NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis and apoptosis protease-activating factor-1 (APAF-1)/CASPASE-3/GSDME-mediated pyroptosis. Furthermore, PPARα agonist pretreatment inhibited activation of the nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) and forkhead box O1 (FOXO1) signaling pathways.ConclusionsPPARα protects against LCA-induced CLI by inhibiting both NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis associated with NF-κB and APAF-1/CASPASE-3/GSDME-mediated pyroptosis associated with the FOXO1 signaling pathway.

Introduction

As the common pathological basis underlying a spectrum of hepatobiliary diseases, cholestatic liver injury (CLI) presents clinically with obstructed bile production or flow, followed by sequential inflammation, fibrosis, and eventual liver failure.1,2 Although understanding of bile acid transporter regulation, immune microenvironment imbalance, and cell death modalities has progressively deepened in recent years, CLI-related morbidity and mortality remain high.

Pyroptosis is an inflammasome-driven form of programmed cell death characterized by cellular swelling, plasma-membrane rupture and the release of potent pro-inflammatory mediators.3 Deoxycholic acid (DCA) and chenodeoxycholic acid (CDCA) trigger CASPASE-4/11-dependent pyroptosis.4 In both obstructive cholestasis patients and bile-duct-ligated (BDL) rodents, hepatocyte pyroptosis is morphologically evident and accompanied by marked up-regulation of NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) and cleaved gasdermin D (GSDMD).5,6 Accumulating evidence demonstrates that inhibition of pyroptosis may have therapeutic potential in mitigating CLI. Sestrin2 deficiency exacerbates BDL-induced hepatic pyroptosis through activation of NLRP3 inflammasomes.7 Paeoniflorin ameliorates alpha-naphthylisothiocyanate (ANIT)-induced pyroptosis through the NLRP3/GSDMD pathway.8 Our previous work also demonstrated that pregnane X receptor (PXR) activation prevents cholestasis-associated hepatic injury by suppressing pyroptotic signaling cascades.9,10

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) is a ligand-activated transcription factor that orchestrates hepatic fatty-acid oxidation, inflammatory signaling, and cell-fate determination.11 Emerging evidence positions PPARα agonists as promising agents for modulating bile-acid synthesis and transport during cholestasis.12, 13, 14, 15 Mechanistically, PPARα activation reduces the hepatic bile-acid pool, attenuates cholestatic injury, and exerts broad anti-inflammatory effects.16 In vivo loss-of-function studies have demonstrated that formononetin alleviates ANIT-induced cholestasis and liver injury through a PPARα-dependent pathway.13 Similarly, Swertia cincta mitigates ANIT-triggered cholestatic damage by engaging the PPARα cascade, and PPARα agonist fenofibrate protects against BDL-induced cholestasis in mice by restoring hepatic macrophage homeostasis.14,17 Beyond metabolic reprogramming, PPARα activation has been shown to inhibit NLRP3-mediated pyroptosis, thereby reducing liver inflammation.18 Overexpression of PPARα suppresses pyroptosis and protects against acute liver failure, whereas PPARα nuclear translocation and transcriptional activity blunt pyroptosis in brain microvascular endothelial cells.19,20 Despite these advances, the precise molecular circuitry through which PPARα activation modulates hepatocellular pyroptosis in the setting of cholestatic stress remains undefined. Fenofibrate, a PPARα agonist, is widely used clinically to treat patients with hypercholesterolemia and hypertriglyceridemia. WY-14643 is also widely recognized as a PPARα agonist and is used in related research. To elucidate the role of PPARα, both agonists were employed in subsequent studies.

In this study, we investigated the effect of PPARα activation on hepatic pyroptosis and explored its pathogenic contribution and underlying mechanisms in the mouse model of CLI. We found that PPARα activation attenuates lithocholic acid (LCA)-induced CLI by inhibiting CASPASE-1/GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis through the nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB)-NLRP3 axis and CASPASE-3/GSDME-mediated pyroptosis through the forkhead box O1 (FOXO1)-apoptosis protease-activating factor-1 (APAF-1) axis, providing new insights for understanding the molecular mechanism of PPARα as a promising anti-cholestatic target.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

All animal studies were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Sun Yat-sen University (approval number: SYSU-IACUC-2024-002141). All animal procedures were carried out in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines and conducted following the Animal Care and Use Guidelines of Sun Yat-sen University.

Cells

HEK293T cells were maintained in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM; #PM150210, Procell, Wuhan, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; #164220, Procell) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (#PB180120, Procell). Cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

Chemicals and reagents

LCA (#L106779, Aladdin, Shanghai, China), corn oil (#C116023, Aladdin), WY-14643 (#A4305, APExBio, Houston, TX, USA), Fenofibrate (#B1943, APExBio), cycloheximide (CHX; #18079, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), MG132 (#474790, Sigma-Aldrich), chloroquine (CQ; #ST9H9BC1508D, Topscience, Shanghai, China), and CASPASE-1 Activity Assay Kit (#C1101, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) were used in the study. Anti-ASC (#67824), anti-APAF-1 (#8969), anti-GAPDH (#2118), anti-IL-1β (#12242), anti-phospho-NF-κB p65 (Ser536) (#3033S), and anti-LaminB1 (#13435) were all purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA). Anti-CYP4A (#sc271983), anti-CASPASE-1 p20 (#sc-398715), anti-CASPASE-11 p20 (#sc-374615), anti-CASPASE-3 p20 (#sc-56053), anti-FOXO1 (#sc-374427), anti-CDKN1A (#sc-6246), anti-CDKN1B (#sc-1641), and anti-RBL2 (#sc-374521) were bought from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (Dallas, TX, USA). Anti-GSDMD (#A18281) and anti-NF-κB (#A10609) were purchased from ABclonal Technology (Wuhan, China). Anti-CASPASE-1 (#ab179515), anti-GSDMD (#ab209845), anti-IL-18 (#ab207323), anti-CASPASE-11 (#ab180673), and anti-GSDME (#ab215191) were purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). Anti-NLRP3 (#WL02635) was purchased from WanLei Biotechnology (Shenyang, China). Anti-ACOX1 (#D221471) was purchased from Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China).

Animal treatment

C57BL/6J mice (male, 8-week-old, 20–24 g) from the Guangdong Medical Laboratory Animal Center (Foshan, China) were housed in the humane care environment under a 12-h light/dark cycle with suitable germ-free feeding. Corn oil was used as a solvent to dissolve LCA and WY-14643. To verify the effects of Fenofibrate and WY-14643, mice were randomly assigned to the following groups: control, Fenofibrate (200 mg/kg/day), LCA (250 mg/kg/day), and LCA + Fenofibrate; or control, WY-14643 (50 mg/kg/day), LCA (250 mg/kg/day), and LCA + WY-14643. WY-14643 was administered intraperitoneally, and Fenofibrate was administered orally for 7 days. Starting on day 4, mice in the LCA, LCA + WY-14643, and LCA + Fenofibrate groups received intraperitoneal LCA (125 mg/kg, twice daily). Twelve hours after the final injection, mice were euthanized to collect blood and liver samples for subsequent analysis.

Serum biochemical analysis and histological evaluation

Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), total bile acid (TBA), total bilirubin (TBIL), and lactic dehydrogenase (LDH) levels were quantified using commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer's protocols. Formalin-fixed liver specimens were paraffin-embedded, sectioned at 5 μm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E).

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick end-labeling (TUNEL) staining

Hepatocyte injury was assessed using TUNEL staining with the In Situ Cell Death Detection Kit (#11684795910, Roche, Indianapolis, IN, USA) following the manufacturer's protocol. Paraffin sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated through graded ethanol, rinsed in phosphate buffered saline (PBS), and blocked for 1 h at room temperature with TBST containing 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) and 0.5% Triton X-100. Sections were then incubated with the TUNEL reaction mixture for 1 h in the dark, counterstained with DAPI for 10 min, and imaged on an LSM 980 confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany).

Western blot analysis

Western blot assays were performed as previously described.9 Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay (#23225; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes (PALL, New York, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk, incubated with primary and secondary antibodies, and visualized with an ECL kit (Millipore, Darmstadt, Germany). Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from mouse liver using TRIzol reagent (#15596018CN, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). cDNA was synthesized using the Evo M-MLV RT Kit with gDNA Clean for qPCR II (#AG11728, Accurate Biology, Changsha, China). Quantitative PCR was performed on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System using SYBR Green Pro Taq HS qPCR Kit (#11701, Accurate Biology, Changsha, China). 18S rRNA served as the endogenous control, and relative gene expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method. Electron microscopic examination

Liver tissue slices (1 mm3) were washed with 0.1 M (mol/L) PBS and fixed in 2.5% ice-cold glutaraldehyde. Subsequently, the samples were post-fixed in 1% osmium tetroxide at 4 °C for 4 h, and then stained with 1% uranyl acetate. The tissues were then dehydrated through a graded ethanol series and embedded in epoxy resin. Ultrathin sections were further stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate for contrast enhancement. Finally, the samples were examined using a JEM-1200 transmission electron microscope (FEI, USA).

CASPASE-1 activity detection assay

CASPASE-1 activity was quantified using a commercial kit (#C1101, Beyotime). Liver tissues were homogenized, and supernatants were incubated with the fluorogenic substrate Ac-YVAD-pNA. Cleavage released p-nitroaniline, whose absorbance at 405 nm was measured on a Multiskan GO plate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Following the instructions provided by the Co-IP kit (#26149, Thermo Fisher Scientific), HEK293T cells were harvested and lysed in lysis buffer (50 mM Tris-Cl, pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 1% Triton, 1 mM DTT, supplemented with protease inhibitor) on ice. Then anti-FLAG M2 beads were incubated with the cell lysates overnight. The immunoprecipitates were washed four times with lysis buffer (20 min per wash). Afterward, they were subjected to SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Prism, San Diego, CA, USA) was used for all data processing. One-way ANOVA followed by post hoc tests was used to analyze the relevant data. Differences with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

PPARα improves liver injury in LCA-induced cholestasis models

LCA has been employed since the 1960s to establish the canonical murine model of CLI.21 In the LCA group, gallbladders were enlarged and darker in color compared with those in the other groups (Fig. 1A). Hepatic function was routinely assessed via the quantification of key biochemical indices, including ALT, AST, TBA, and TBIL. Compared with the control group, the LCA-treated group exhibited a pronounced increase in serum ALT and AST levels, accompanied by significant elevations in TBA and TBIL. Pre-administration of the PPARα classical agonist fenofibrate significantly reduced serum ALT, AST, TBA, and TBIL levels relative to the LCA group (Fig. 1B), suggesting that fenofibrate may improve LCA-induced hepatocellular and cholestatic injury. Histological examination revealed no evident hepatic impairment in control mice. Conversely, livers from the LCA group exhibited extensive inflammatory infiltrates and confluent necrosis, which were substantially attenuated by fenofibrate pretreatment (Fig. 1C). PPARα inhibits hepatocyte pyroptosis induced by LCA

Pyroptosis as a novel form of programmed cell death has been implicated in the pathogenesis of CLI.22 Targeting pyroptosis can significantly mitigate CLI induced by various stimuli.6,7,23 To evaluate the impact of PPARα activation on LCA-induced pyroptosis, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used to assess ultrastructural alterations. Only the hepatocytes in the mice of the LCA group exhibited small, concave pits at the cell periphery and focal membrane rupture (Fig. 2A and Supplemental Fig. 2A). Pyroptosis was further confirmed by extracellular LDH release. LCA markedly increased LDH release, whereas pretreatment with fenofibrate or WY-14643 significantly attenuated this effect (Fig. 2B and Supplemental Fig. 2B). TUNEL staining was used to verify DNA fragmentation in pyroptotic cells. We observed numerous TUNEL-positive cells in LCA-treated mouse livers, whereas administration of either fenofibrate or WY-14643 markedly reduced the number of TUNEL-positive cells relative to the LCA group (Fig. 2C and Supplemental Fig. 2C). Given the intimate link between pyroptosis and inflammation, we measured key cytokines downstream of inflammasome activation. LCA challenge substantially elevated hepatic Il-1β and Il-18 transcripts and increased the mature forms of IL-1β and IL-18, which were almost completely reversed by fenofibrate pretreatment (Fig. 2D and E). Pre-administration of WY-14643 decreased Il-1β mRNA and the mature forms of IL-1β and IL-18 proteins, while leaving Il-18 mRNA levels unchanged (Supplemental Fig. 2D and E). PPARα mediates the NLRP3/CASPASE-1/GSDMD pathway to improve LCA-induced pyroptosis

The aforementioned results suggest that PPARα activation alleviates LCA-induced CLI by suppressing pyroptosis. To further explore this potential, we quantified CASPASE-1 activity. CASPASE-1 is activated upon NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and serves as the central executor of pyroptosis.24 LCA markedly elevated hepatic CASPASE-1 activity, which was significantly reversed by either fenofibrate (Fig. 3A) or WY-14643 (Supplemental Fig. 3A). LCA markedly up-regulated the mRNA expression of pyroptosis-related genes Nlrp3, Pycard, and Gsdmd (Fig. 3B). In addition, the protein expression levels of NLRP3, ASC, CASPASE-1, and CASPASE-1 p20 were significantly increased in the LCA group (Fig. 3C and D), indicating activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. Fenofibrate pretreatment down-regulated the NLRP3 inflammasome in LCA-treated mouse livers (Fig. 3B–D). CASPASE-1-mediated cleavage of GSDMD liberates its N-terminal fragment (N-GSDMD), which translocates to the plasma membrane and forms lytic pores, thereby executing pyroptosis.24 Relative to the control group, the LCA group markedly elevated total and cleaved (N-terminal) GSDMD protein levels in mice. Fenofibrate pretreatment significantly reversed the LCA-induced elevations of GSDMD and N-GSDMD levels (Fig. 3C and D). Consistent with fenofibrate findings, the PPARα agonist WY-14643 markedly down-regulated NLRP3/CASPASE-1/GSDMD pathway proteins in LCA-challenged livers, significantly lowered Nlrp3 mRNA, and elicited a downward trend in Pycard and Gsdmd transcript levels (Supplemental Fig. 3B–D). PPARα mediates the APAF-1/CASPASE-3/GSDME pathway to improve LCA-induced pyroptosis

Pyroptosis is generally executed via two distinct routes: the CASPASE-1-mediated pathway and the CASPASE-11/3-dependent pathway, each of which converges on the proteolytic cleavage of specific gasdermin proteins.4,25 We have clarified that APAF-1, as a core component of the apoptosome, primarily triggers apoptosis. However, in cells with high GSDME expression, the activated CASPASE-3 can cleave GSDME to form pores in the plasma membrane, thereby switching the cell death mode from apoptosis to pyroptosis.4 Then we evaluated the effect of PPARα on APAF-1/CASPASE-3/GSDME axis. Our results showed that fenofibrate significantly reversed the LCA-induced increases in the mRNA and protein levels of APAF-1, CASPASE-11, CASPASE-3, GSDME, and their active fragments (N-GSDME, CASPASE-11 p20, and CASPASE-3 p20) (Fig. 4A–C). Similarly, WY-14643 reduced levels of APAF-1, CASPASE-11 p20, CASPASE-3 p20, and N-GSDME (Supplemental Fig. 4A–C). PPARα suppresses FOXO1 signaling in LCA-induced injury

Previous reports have shown that PXR activation suppresses assembly of the APAF-1 pyroptosome by repressing the FOXO1 signaling pathway.9 FOXO1 and PPARα share DNA binding sites at active intergenic and intronic enhancers.27 To determine whether PPARα suppresses pyroptosis via the FOXO1 pathway in cholestatic mice, we profiled FOXO1-pathway genes. LCA challenge elevated both mRNA and protein levels of FOXO1, CDKN1A, CDKN1B, and RBL2. Fenofibrate pretreatment fully reversed these increases (Fig. 6A and B), whereas WY-14643 reduced FOXO1 and CDKN1A expression but left CDKN1B and RBL2 mRNA levels unchanged (Fig. 6C and D). Moreover, PPARα activation not only suppressed FOXO1 expression but also blocked its LCA-induced nuclear translocation (Fig. 6B and D). We next examined FOXO1 protein stability. In HepG2 cells treated with CHX, FOXO1 degradation was markedly accelerated upon PPARα overexpression (Fig. 6E). MG132 or CQ restored FOXO1 abundance in a time-dependent manner (Fig. 6F), indicating that PPARα promotes proteasome-mediated degradation of FOXO1. Discussion

CLI is driven by the toxic accumulation of intrahepatic bile acids, yet its molecular pathogenesis remains incompletely understood and effective therapies are limited. Clinical evidence indicates that the levels of PPARα mRNA and protein in the liver tissues of primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) patients are significantly reduced.28 Moreover, in the liver tissues of patients with obstructive cholestasis, the expression of cell pyroptosis-related proteins is significantly increased.29,30 Inhibition of hepatocyte pyroptosis and attenuation of the ensuing inflammatory response are critical for limiting hepatic damage and preventing CLI progression.27 Accumulating clinical evidence indicates that the PPARα agonists significantly improve liver function and clinical symptoms in patients with cholestasis, thereby enhancing long-term outcomes.11,31 In this study, the LCA-induced acute cholestasis model was employed because it is widely validated and exhibits pathological changes similar to those of human intrahepatic cholestasis.11 Our results demonstrate that the PPARα agonist fenofibrate or WY-14643 prevents LCA-induced liver injury and pyroptosis, which are associated with inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome and APAF-1 pyroptosome. Mechanistic analyses further implicate both the NF-κB/NLRP3 and the FOXO1/APAF-1 axes as potential central mediators of these protective effects.

Pparα−/− mice showed bile acid accumulation in their livers, resulting from decreased levels of mRNA encoding transporters, including Abcb11, Abcb4, Abca1, Abcg5, and Abcg8.32 Triple therapy with UDCA, OCA, and fibrates (bezafibrate or fenofibrate) has been reported to normalise biochemical liver tests and improve pruritus in patients with difficult-to-treat PBC.33 In this study, fenofibrate upregulated the mRNA and protein levels of PPARα targets ACOX1 and CYP4A, which were downregulated in the livers of LCA-treated mice (Supplemental Fig. 6A and B). As a PPARα agonist, WY-14643 is also frequently used in PPARα signaling studies.34 Pretreatment with either fenofibrate or WY-14643 markedly attenuated LCA-induced liver injury, as evidenced by significant reductions in serum ALT, AST, TBA, and TBIL levels, as well as improved gallbladder morphology and hepatic histopathology.

Cholestasis is often accompanied by inflammation characterized by neutrophil infiltration.35 When Pparα−/− mice were treated with pro-inflammatory substances, they suffered more injury from inflammation due to inflammasome activation or an increase in TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β levels.36 Fenofibrate significantly reduced TNF-α and IL-1β levels in BDL rats.32 Our results demonstrated that fenofibrate or WY-14643 markedly suppressed hepatic CASPASE-1-mediated cleavage of pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18, thereby reducing the release of their mature forms in LCA-treated mice. Concurrently, PPARα activation inhibited CASPASE-1 activation, lowered LDH release, diminished membrane pore formation, and reduced the number of TUNEL-positive hepatocytes, which are key indicators of pyroptosis.

Amid growing research on pyroptosis in disease, its contribution to the initiation and progression of CLI is becoming increasingly evident. In the peripheral blood of patients with PBC, Toll-like receptors (TLRs) drive the maturation and release of the pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-18 and promote inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis, resulting in the extracellular release of inflammatory mediators that amplify the systemic inflammatory response.37 Mdr2−/− mice mimic the key pathological features of human PSC, exhibiting CLI driven by pronounced inflammatory activation and a marked increase in hepatic NLRP3-positive cells.38, 39, 40 Our study suggested that PPARα activation suppressed NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis, as evidenced by the reversal of LCA-induced increases in CASPASE-1 activity and the expression of NLRP3, CASPASE-1 p20, and N-GSDMD proteins in livers of LCA-treated mice. Conflicting evidence indicates that Nlrp3-deficient mice develop more severe injury. In the acute phase, Nlrp3-deficient mice subjected to BDL exacerbated hepatic inflammation and injury, accompanied by enhanced apoptosis and necroptosis. Conversely, chronic Nlrp3-deficiency was characterized by reduced liver injury and dampened inflammation.29 However, it should be noted that the measurements of IL-1β, inflammasome activation, and GSDMD were performed using total liver tissues. Given that IL-1β is predominantly expressed in macrophages rather than hepatocytes, our data reflect the collective inflammatory response within the liver. Future studies utilizing cell-specific isolation or dual-fluorescence staining are required to precisely delineate the relative contributions of hepatocytes and immune cells to these processes. Collectively, these observations underscore the necessity to investigate crosstalk between various types of cell death and reveal that the NLRP3 inflammasome exerts a dual function, and its role depends on the type and progression of CLI.

PPARα suppresses NF-κB activation during inflammation, thereby blocking the inflammatory cascade.41,42 Suppressing the NF-κB signaling pathway also inhibits the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in LPS-induced THP-1 macrophages.43 Moreover, prior reports indicate that PXR activation inhibits pyroptosis via the NF-κB/NLRP3 axis.9 Similarly, current results confirmed that PPARα activation inhibited the NF-κB signaling pathway and that NF-κB-p65 interacted with PPARα. We therefore propose that PPARα suppresses NLRP3 inflammasome assembly in LCA-treated mouse livers by inhibiting NF-κB activation.

Unlike the NLRP3 inflammasome, the APAF-1 pyroptosome is a key to the APAF-1/CASPASE-3/GSDME-mediated pyroptosis.44 In this study, liver tissues from LCA-treated mice exhibited upregulation of the APAF-1/CASPASE-11 pyroptosome and cleavage of CASPASE-3 and GSDME, which was inhibited by PPARα activation. Similarly, in BDL mice, APAF-1 recruits and activates CASPASE-11 rather than CASPASE-9.4 A moderate concentration of cholic acid (50 mM) induces apoptosis via the APAF-1/CASPASE-9 apoptosome, whereas a high concentration (200 mM) drives assembly of the APAF-1/CASPASE-11 pyroptosome and pyroptotic cell death.37 These opposing outcomes underscore the complexity and context-dependency of bile acid-induced cell death. When bile acid levels are excessively elevated in vivo, the FOXO1 protein translocates to the nucleus and upregulates target genes including CDKN1A and CDKN1B, thereby inducing cell death.9 Moreover, FOXO1 has been reported to regulate the transcriptional activity of APAF-1.45 We previously demonstrated that PXR inhibits the transcriptional activity of APAF-1 by binding to FOXO1.9 PPARα and PXR are members of the nuclear receptor superfamily and possess structurally similar domains.46 In this study, PPARα was found to suppress the FOXO1 signaling pathway. Furthermore, the regulation of FOXO1 by PPARα appears to be associated with the stability of the FOXO1 protein, although further investigation is required to confirm this mechanism. While more evidence is needed, our findings suggest that PPARα may suppress APAF-1-mediated pyroptosis through FOXO1.

While our results show that PPARα alleviates LCA-induced CLI through inhibiting pyroptosis, there are limitations to consider. The association between PPARα and pyroptosis remains underexplored. Future research should verify the relationship between the hepatoprotective effects of PPARα and pyroptosis using Nlrp3 or Apaf-1 knockout mice. Additionally, although studies have shown that NF-κB and FOXO1 are involved in PPARα′s regulation of NLRP3 inflammasomes and APAF-1 pyroptosomes, the specific regulatory mechanisms remain unclear. Further research should elucidate these mechanisms by constructing cell lines that stably express key proteins, such as PPARα, NLRP3, NF-κB, APAF-1, and FOXO1.

Conclusions

In summary, our findings indicate that PPARα activation protects the liver from CLI by inhibiting both NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis associated with NF-κB and APAF-1/CASPASE-3/GSDME-mediated pyroptosis associated with the FOXO1 signaling pathway. These data reinforce PPARα as a promising therapeutic target in managing CLI and provide a foundation for future research into its clinical applications.

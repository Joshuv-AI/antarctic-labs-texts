Multi-metric comparative evaluation of DeepSeek and ChatGPT in USMLE versus CNMLE for medical education
Authors: Qing Wang, Junlian Li, Xiaoying Li, Panpan Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT and DeepSeek are gaining attention for their potential in medical education. This study aims to evaluate the performance of ChatGPT and DeepSeek in the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) and the Chinese National Medical Licensing Examination (CNMLE), followed by the targeted optimizations methods to advance the efficient and effective application of LLMs in medical education. This study conducted a comparative quantitative analysis across multiple dimensions, including answer accuracy, consistency, the number of reasoning characters, and runtime.Based on the identified limitations of LLMs, targeted optimization explorations were carried out, including the construction of a technical safeguard framework and a multi-dimensional evaluation system. In the USMLE, DeepSeek had an average accuracy of 92.59% and a Fleiss’ Kappa of 0.96, while ChatGPT had 90.26% accuracy and a Fleiss’ Kappa of 0.93. In the CNMLE, DeepSeek achieved an accuracy of 86.78% and a Fleiss’ Kappa of 0.96, while ChatGPT had an accuracy of 79.44% and a Fleiss’ Kappa of 0.90. Both DeepSeek and ChatGPT demonstrated the ability to identify flawed questions, yet they also produced incorrect answers due to hallucinations. Additionally, DeepSeek had a relatively longer runtime. To address these issues, this study proposed a Knowledge Graph-Based RAG Fact-Checking Framework centered on evidence anchoring and a multi-dimensional evaluation system focusing on reliability and safety. DeepSeek generally outperforms ChatGPT in accuracy, particularly excelling in handling complex medical problems and Chinese medical knowledge. However, DeepSeek had a longer runtime compared with ChatGPT. The proposed optimization framework and evaluation system effectively address core issues such as LLM hallucinations, clarifying the positioning of LLMs as “auxiliary tools” that require rigorous fact-checking. These solutions jointly form a core governance system for the application of LLM in medical education, providing key support for their precise and efficient integration into educational scenarios. The study indicates that LLMs are expected to bring about a progressive transformation, evolving from functional enhancement to paradigm reconstruction.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence has driven the gradual maturation of LLMs and their widespread applications. Essentially, LLMs are trained on extensive text corpora that typically have parameter counts ranging from billions to hundreds of billions, enabling them to accurately interpret and execute human instructions1. The great number of parameters and tremendous training data enable LLMs to excel in potential applications such as drug design, pathological diagnosis, bioinformatics analysis, and material discovery2–8. In the field of medical education, LLMs have also demonstrated significant capabilities in areas such as teaching assistance, exam preparation, and clinical case analysis9–13. ChatGPT, as a representative of LLMs, has attracted wide attention in medical examinations14–17. In January 2025, DeepSeek, a large language model independently developed in China, garnered widespread global attention largely attributed to its exceptional performance in reasoning tasks and the open-source nature18. As two of the most representative language models currently, ChatGPT from the United States and DeepSeek from China not only embody the latest advancements in LLMs, but also reflect the distinct technological development paths shaped by different linguistic and cultural contexts. However, there is a lack of study systematically comparing the performance of LLMs, such as DeepSeek and ChatGPT, for medical examinations. Futhermore, USMLE and CNMLE are the mandatory examination that medical students in the United States and China must pass to obtain a medical license. Both of USMLE and CNMLE encompass a broad spectrum of multidisciplinary knowledge, including basic medicine, internal medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, pediatrics, psychiatry, neurology, and other relevant questions, in order to comprehensively evaluate whether the applicants have acquired the necessary professional knowledge, clinical skills, and professional qualities essential for the practice of medicine. Therefore, the present study utilizes the 2023 sample questions from USMLE website and the 2022 CNMLE exam paper for the testing datasets (Fig. 1). The study systematically evaluates the performance between DeepSeek and ChatGPT in terms of answer accuracy, consistency across multiple runs, reasoning characters count and runtime, aiming to provide a scientific basis for the further application of LLMs in medical education.

Data

USMLE

USMLE is a well-developed and comprehensive testing program with a broad coverage from the fundamental science, clinical diagnosis and treatment, medical management to bioethics. In this study, the 2023 USMLE sample exam of 376 questions for public release were downloaded from the official USMLE website. These questions were then converted from PDF format into text and double-checked by two editors independently. The whole exam consists of three parts, namely Step 1, Step 2CK, and Step 3. Specifically, the first part covers the knowledge about the fundamental science, pharmacology, and pathophysiology, while the second focuses on clinical diagnosis and treatment, medical management and bioethics, with the last one designed for postgraduate medical education testing. Additionally, some of the USMLE questions contain graphics clinical data, such as medical images, photography, and graphs, which are not suitable for the language models with the multi-stage training using large-scale text corpus. Methodology

LLMs & API

Large language models, like ChatGPT and DeepSeek, hold great potential in natural language understanding, reasoning, and generation owing to their multi-stage training using large-scale, diverse, and multi-modal corpus data. The notable advantages of LLMs lie in their high adaptability and flexibility across various natural language applications. Moreover, the developers could further enhance LLMs’ performance for specific tasks by using supervised fine-tuning on well annotated corpus. Alternatively, this study directly utilizes the API provided by OpenAI (https://api.openai.com/) and DeepSeek (https://api.deepseek.com), while the detailed versions are GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-R1. Here, in order to assess the LLMs’ performance in their original status, there was neither annotated data nor additional fine-tuning for the interested medical education scenario.

Furthermore, considering the fact that LLMs suffer from the “hallucination” issue, namely they occasionally generate outputs that are factually incorrect or with logical flaws, this study performed three independent evaluations of each model and observed the overall performance. The results from testing multiple times were statistically analyzed to mitigate the impact of hallucination-related errors and thus enhance the consistency and reliability of the experimental findings. The proposed methodology aims to more accurately assess the stability, accuracy, and reasoning capabilities of LLMs in medical examination, thereby providing a trusted empirical foundation for their further usage in medical education.

Prompt engineering

By adopting the machine learning and natural language processing technologies, prompt engineering enables the extraction of keywords from the input texts and refines the LLMs inputs, in order to ensure that the model’s responses are relevant, coherent, and accurate. With the optimized model inputs (usually called prompts), we could guide and constrict the model output without parameters adjustment. Therefore, the well-designed prompts are the key input instructions given to LLMs. Essentially, effective prompts should possess goal instruction, clear context, and explicit goals, ensuring that the LLMs accurately perceive the task requirements and then generate desired output.

In this study, two zero-shot prompts in English and Chinese were separately designed to simulate the real medical exam scenarios. Besides, to comprehensively evaluate the LLMs’ knowledge understanding, logical reasoning and problem-solving abilities, they would be asked not only to generate the optimal answers for each question, but also demonstrate the whole reasoning process. Specifically, the prompt in English to trigger LLMs on USMLE is:

Suppose you are a medical student taking the United States Medical Licensing Examination, please answer the following question and provide the correct answer with your thinking.

And the prompt in Chinese for LLMs on CNMLE is:

假设你是一位医学生, 正在准备中国执业医师考试, 请回答下面的问题, 并给出正确答案和具体的思考解析。.

Evaluation metrics

This study employs a quantitative analysis method to evaluate the performance of LLMs for medical examination, which contains three principal metrics: precision, the number of reasoning characters, and runtime.

Precision

Specifically, the precision refers to the proportion of correct outputs from LLMs (i.e., observed data) over the medical examination answers (i.e., the true value). For the sample exams of USMLE and CNMLE, ChatGPT and DeepSeek were tested three times independently to alleviate the hallucination issue, and totally three precisions could be obtained for each model. By utilizing Python (version 3.8.20) for statistical analysis, the mean and standard deviation of the above precisions will be computed, followed by Fleiss’ Kappa coefficient k to estimate the stability and reliability of LLMs’ multiple outputs. The number of reasoning characters

Currently, to improve the ability of logical reasoning and the transparency of generated outputs, LLMs usually adopt the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to break down complex issues into several ordered steps, and gradually figure out the final answers. Therefore, the number of reasoning characters, called “Reasoning character count” for short, is firstly proposed to reflect the performance of LLMs’ reasoning from an alternative aspect. Generally, the more the number of reasoning characters, the deeper the model’s thinking, and the better the reasoning capacity.

Runtime

The runtime refers to the duration it takes for the model to finish simulated exams, which is often calculated in seconds. Numerous factors account for the model runtime, such as the complexity of model structure, the configuration of high performance computing devices and the schedule mechanism of parallel computing. Moreover, the runtime is directly related to the model’s CoT ability.

The analysis of the number of reasoning characters

In terms of the number of reasoning characters, during the USMLE test, DeepSeek generated an average of 209.85 characters, with a fluctuation range of ± 2.88 characters. In contrast, ChatGPT generated an average of 209.94 characters, with a fluctuation range of ± 1.52 characters.

Discussion

Comparison of DeepSeek and ChatGPT’s performance in USMLE and CNMLE

A comparative analysis was conducted on the performance of DeepSeek and ChatGPT in the USMLE and CNMLE from three dimensions, including answer accuracy, the number of reasoning characters, and runtime. In terms of answer accuracy, for the USMLE, DeepSeek achieved an accuracy rate of 92.59%, which is higher than ChatGPT’s 90.26%. Moreover, DeepSeek exhibited a smaller fluctuation range (± 0.41 vs. ± 1.26) and a higher Fleiss’ Kappa value (0.96), indicating superior performance in both precision and consistency. DeepSeek demonstrated higher accuracy than ChatGPT across all three steps of the USMLE, particularly in Step 3, which involves integrating multidisciplinary knowledge and analyzing complex clinical cases. In the CNMLE exam, DeepSeek also significantly outperformed ChatGPT’s accuracy. The CNMLE comprises four independent units. In Unit 1 focusing on comprehensive basic medical sciences knowledge, Unit 2 and 3 assessing clinical medicine knowledge, and Unit 4 evaluating students’ ability to synthesize and apply knowledge from the first three units, DeepSeek consistently outperformed ChatGPT in accuracy, demonstrating its advantages in processing basic and comprehensive clinical medical knowledge as well as solving complex clinical problems.

In terms of the number of reasoning characters, in the USMLE, the average count for DeepSeek and ChatGPT were similar, 209.85 and 209.94 respectively, indicating that both of them had comparable levels of reasoning complexity. However, ChatGPT had a smaller fluctuation range in the number of characters, which demonstrated better stability, and was able to provide reasoning closer to the average number of characters consistently across different questions. In contrast, DeepSeek exhibited greater fluctuations, possibly because it provided more detailed reasoning when dealing with complex problems and relatively concise reasoning when dealing with simpler problems. In the CNMLE, DeepSeek’s average number of reasoning characters (506.80) was significantly higher than ChatGPT’s (430.63), indicating a deeper reasoning process involved. Longer CoT sequences often reflect more detailed and interconnected reasoning steps, suggesting a deeper cognitive process. This was likely due to the fact that the CNMLE involved more complex medical knowledge assessments, requiring models to provide richer background information, more medical details, and engage in deeper logical reasoning. DeepSeek was able to generate longer reasoning in this scenario, reflecting its stronger reasoning capabilities when handling complex problems. However, the range of fluctuation in the number of reasoning characters count was 2.29 times that of ChatGPT, further highlighting ChatGPT’s advantage in maintaining a more consistent character count stability. In terms of runtime, ChatGPT ran slightly faster than DeepSeek in the USMLE tests. In the CNMLE, where tasks are generally more complex, ChatGPT’s average runtime (41.03 s) was significantly faster than DeepSeek (57.94 s), indicating its superior efficiency even under challenging conditions.

In summary, DeepSeek generally achieves higher accuracy than ChatGPT in the USMLE and CNMLE examinations. This superior performance could be due to the fact that DeepSeek has implemented innovative model architectures, such as the Mixture of Experts (MoE) approach, which allows for specialized expertise within different parts of the model, and the Multi-Head Latent Attention mechanism, which enhances the model’s ability to focus on relevant information. Additionally, DeepSeek employs efficient training methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization, which optimizes the training process by considering the relative performance of different policy groups, and benefits from an extensive and diverse corpus that enriches its learning capabilities19. DeepSeek’s MoE model architecture achieves efficient utilization of computational resources through fine-grained design and a shared experts strategy, enabling it to better handle complex medical knowledge and reasoning tasks. Its multi-token prediction (MTP) and mixed-precision training technique using 8-bit floating point enhance the model’s contextual understanding capabilities and training efficiency. However, these complex mechanisms and optimization objectives also result in longer inference times for DeepSeek. In contrast, ChatGPT demonstrates better performance in terms of operational speed, particularly when dealing with complex medical problems. This could be attributed to GPT-4’s Transformer-based architecture, which utilizes more efficient algorithms and powerful hardware facilities, making it better suited for parallel computing and large-scale deployment. Nevertheless, ChatGPT exhibits relatively lower accuracy in handling Chinese-language examinations or tasks, which might be related to the limited representation of Chinese-specific content in its training data as a general-purpose large language model. Its training data may lean more towards general knowledge, with a smaller proportion of Chinese corpora. In contrast, DeepSeek has invested more resources in training on Chinese medical data, enabling it to better understand and generate Chinese medical content, adapt to the Chinese medical education system and examination patterns, promptly update medical knowledge, and ensure the accuracy of the generated content.

The potentials of LLMs in medical education

The SAMR framework classifies the impact of technology on education into four levels: Substitution, Augmentation, Modification, and Redefinition, which collectively encompass two broader tiers: enhancement and transformation. Based on the findings of this empirical study, we foresee that the impact of LLMs on medical education will manifest as a progressive transformation, evolving from functional enhancement to paradigm reconfiguration. This evolutionary process, transitioning from tool substitution to paradigm innovation, not only enhances teaching efficiency through technological empowerment but also profoundly reshapes the underlying logic of knowledge production and the criteria for competency assessment in medical education20,21.

From the perspective of enhancement, LLMs can serve as substitutes for traditional medical education tools, automating text-based tasks such as medical record documentation and literature review, thereby improving information processing efficiency while maintaining existing teaching workflows. According to statistics from the National Medical Examination Center in China, the pass rate of the CNMLE was 55% in 2022 and 43% in 2023. In the CNMLE examination, both of the average accuracy of DeepSeek and ChatGPT exceeded 79%. The fact that DeepSeek and ChatGPT scored higher than the pass rate may indicate that they possess a more extensive knowledge base in medical information compared to the majority of medical students who have undergone the traditional 5 year medical education in medical schools. Besides, DeepSeek and ChatGPT achieved an average accuracy exceeding 90% in USMLE, indicating their robust capability in delivering reliable medical knowledge. Notably, in STEP 3 (clinical decision-making scenarios), DeepSeek achieved an accuracy of 95.99%, highlighting its advantage in complex clinical reasoning. In the CNMLE, DeepSeek achieved a slightly higher accuracy score than ChatGPT in Unit 2,while it demonstrated significantly higher accuracy than ChatGPT in Units 1, 3, and 4, which cover preclinical courses such as pathology and pharmacology.

From the perspective of paradigm reconfiguration, LLMs are instrumental in reshaping the core task structures of medical education. In terms of the number of reasoning characters, DeepSeek generated an average of 506.80 characters in the CNMLE, 17.7% more than ChatGPT, with a wider fluctuation range (± 5.77) compared to ChatGPT’s 430.63 characters (± 2.52). This indicates DeepSeek’s ability to provide more detailed reasoning chains when explaining pathological mechanisms or differential diagnoses. Aligning with the Modification level of the SAMR framework, this characteristic supports task restructuring. More notably, the educational paradigm reconfiguration triggered by this technology is transforming medical education toward “generative learning”. Medical students can conduct personalized diagnostic training using virtual patients generated by LLMs or build multimodal teaching resource libraries with clinical realism by integrating technologies such as medical imaging synthesis and pathological mechanism visualization. This teaching innovation, which transcends physical and temporal constraints, is redefining knowledge transmission methods and clinical thinking development pathways in medical education.

It is worth mentioning that LLMs have the potential to evaluate the quality of medical exam questions especially for the improper items. There was a single-choice question in CNMLE “According to the ICD-10 diagnostic criteria for depression, the minimum symptom duration is (). The options are: A) 3 days, B) 5 days, C) 7 days, D) 4 days, E) 0 days.” This question, in fact, had no correct answer. During the three runs of testing conducted in this study, both the DeepSeek and ChatGPT demonstrated the ability to identify flawed questions. This ability to identify flawed questions highlights a broader potential for these models in educational assessment.

The limitations of LLMs in medical education

Medical education serves as the cornerstone of patient safety. Consequently, the integration of large language models (LLMs) necessitates a thorough examination of their limitations. From the perspective of technical reliability, the “black box” nature of LLMs poses a fundamental obstacle to their application in medical education22. Due to the lack of transparency in the internal workings of LLMs, the medical knowledge generated by these systems is difficult to fully verify and explain. In clinical medical education, this lack of explain ability may lead to the dissemination of incorrect knowledge, thereby affecting medical students’ ability to make professional judgments. Moreover, LLMs may generate medical advice that is logically coherent but factually incorrect due to hallucination. Particularly when dealing with complex medical issues, the lack of explain ability and transparency may pose potential risks and challenges. For example, when answering the question “Full-term delivery, fully dilated cervix, S + 3, fetal heart rate 90 beats per minute, with late deceleration. The measure to be taken at this time is ()” from the CNMLE, both ChatGPT and DeepSeek returned the incorrect option “E Forceps delivery”. Since the LLMs did not fully weigh the priority between forceps delivery and cesarean section, the models tended to choose the seemingly reasonable option E, while ignoring the correct option “A Immediate cesarean section”. In clinical practice, if the fetal heart rate remains persistently low with late deceleration, even if the fetal head is low, cesarean section may still be prioritized to avoid the risks associated with forceps delivery. Moreover, when answering the question “Dark adaptation refers to () in the dark environment”, both ChatGPT and DeepSeek chose the incorrect option “D Increased sensitivity” whereas the correct answer should be “A Decreased sensory contrast”. In this question, ChatGPT and DeepSeek may have had an incomplete understanding of the concept of “dark adaptation” as well as insufficient differentiation between “sensory contrast” and “increased sensitivity”. Instead, they chose “D” based on a superficial understanding and assumed that “increased sensitivity” is the main feature of dark adaptation, while overlooking the rationality of the other options.

Optimization of LLMs for medical education scenarios

The performance of DeepSeek and ChatGPT on the USMLE and CNMLE uncovers some limitations of LLMs, especially incorrect replies due to hallucinations. Therefore, it is necessary to adopt targeted improvement strategies for LLMs in professional domains. By setting the human-AI alignment as the core principle, an optimized governance system comprised of technical architecture safeguard enhancement and evaluation metric adaptation was proposed to promote the precise and efficient application of LLMs for medical education.

In terms of technical safeguard enhancement, drawing on cutting-edge research such as TrumorGPT23, we propose a Knowledge Graph-Based RAG Fact-Checking Framework (see Fig. 4). Centered on evidence anchoring, the framework deeply integrates Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with the semantic knowledge graph (KG)24,25. The architecture focuses on the construction of a high-quality medical knowledge base and a multi-dimensional semantic KG, employing a dual-mode approach of retrieval during generation and verification after generation to achieve complete integration23. The KG incorporates core knowledge covering the fundamentals of medicine (e.g., anatomy, physiology, pharmacology) and clinical medicine (e.g., internal medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynecology), extracted from trusted resources such as clinical guidelines, the Unified Medical Language System (UMLS) Metathesaurus, authoritative textbooks, and latest literature. Together, they form a huge semantic network containing diseases, symptoms, drugs, diagnosis, and treatment, which could be able to mitigate the hallucinations of LLMs26,27.

Once the LLM receives a medical query and generates a candidate reply, the fact-checking process will be immediately triggered, forming a structured verification loop. The model firstly conducts claim parsing, entity linking, and graph mapping, decomposing the LLM-generated candidate reply into verifiable atomic claims (e.g., factual triples). Meanwhile, it accurately aligns the medical entities and semantic relations in the claims with the corresponding nodes and edges in the KG. Subsequently, based on the parsed atomic claims, the AI model initiates query-graph traversal and evidence retrieval within the semantic medical KG, retrieving evidence subgraphs that are highly relevant to the claims. It then proceeds to the evidence comparison and verification stage, while the fine-grained semantic alignment and logical reasoning are employed to assess the truthfulness, completeness, and contextual consistency of each claim. This process not only supports a binary true/false judgment, but also leverages the semantic relations and contradiction detection mechanisms embedded within the knowledge graph to differentiate between easily confused concepts. After verification, the model carries out evidence integration and confidence assessment, merges supporting evidence with the original claims, as well as assigns certainty levels and confidence scores to key conclusions. Finally, the model outputs the response accompanied by a structured evidence chain, traceable sources, and quantified confidence measures, thereby transforming the traditional black-box generation model into a transparent medical education content generation process in which evidence could be checked, results explained, and confidence assessed. This generate-retrieve-verify-integrate closed-loop mechanism represents a fundamental shift from probability-based generation relying on internal parameters to evidence-driven generation anchored in external authoritative knowledge, laying the foundation for a core technical safeguard architecture to build trustworthy, reliable LLM systems for medical education.

To evolve the automated fact-checking framework into a deployable medical education tool, it is necessary to introduce an advanced evaluation paradigm that goes beyond traditional accuracy metrics. Based on the core concept of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) taking human preferences as the gold standard, a multi-dimensional evaluation system centered on trustworthiness and traceability should be constructed28,29. This system encompasses evaluation metrics such as proactive defense capability, evidence traceability, and complex task completion, forming a comprehensive evaluation framework that balances factual accuracy with educational applicability. Specifically, proactive defense capability can be quantified through hallucination detection rate, which measures the proportion of erroneous statements in AI models’ hallucinations that are identified and corrected by the system, as well as reflects its effectiveness in preemptively intercepting core factual risks. Evidence traceability can be assessed using evidence coverage score, defined as the proportion of key assertions in the final answer that can be directly traced to authoritative medical evidence sources, thereby measuring the strength of evidential support in the output. For evaluating complex reasoning task completion, the Pass@k metric from the RLHF framework can be adapted for this purpose. Within medical examination scenarios involving complex clinical reasoning questions, LLMs may be required to generate 3–5 potential diagnostic or treatment pathways. Only if at least one of them is verified and approved by a panel of medical experts can it be counted as a “pass.” In terms of fundamental medical concept questions, the k value can be set to 1–2, emphasizing the verification of knowledge accuracy. This kind of adapted metric design not only retains the advantage of Pass@k in evaluating accuracy across multiple pathways, but also has the ability to align with the diverse needs of medical education contexts. Its multi-dimensional evaluation logic effectively measures the model’s auxiliary role in theoretical teaching and case analysis scenarios, ultimately achieving a balance between model accuracy and practical utility.

The approach outlined above facilitates the evolution of LLMs from pursuing static accuracy in examinations to meeting the dynamic adaptability demands of teaching scenarios. Meanwhile, stipulating that LLMs’ outputs must undergo rigorous fact-checking, fundamentally establishes their role in medical education as “auxiliary tools”—aimed at enhancing, rather than replacing, the central role of educators in medical education.

Limitations of the study

Although this study conducted a multi-dimensional comparative analysis of the performance of DeepSeek and ChatGPT in the USMLE and CNMLE, there are still some limitations. In terms of data, the number of sampled USMLE and CNMLE questions are limited, and those containing clinical images were not included in the evaluation. Therefore, the sampled data may not fully cover the overall examination scenario. Methodologically, even though multiple-running of LLMs were adopted to reduce the impact of hallucinations, only zero-shot prompts were used without comparing the performance of LLMs guided by few-shot and Chain-of -Thought (CoT) prompts.

Conclusion

This study compared the performance of DeepSeek and ChatGPT on the USMLE and CNMLE, showing that both LLMs exhibited high accuracy in the two medical examinations. DeepSeek generally outperformed ChatGPT in terms of precision, particularly excelling when handling complex medical problems and medical knowledge in Chinese. Both models were found to generate incorrect answers due to hallucinations, and DeepSeek had a relatively longer runtime. This study demonstrated that generative artificial intelligence, such as LLMs, held substantial potential for their applications in medical education. It is expected that LLMs bring about positive transformations in medical education, evolving from functional enhancement to paradigm reconstruction. However, the adoption of LLMs in medical education still faces several challenges, including the “black-box” nature of the technology, data quality and privacy security, as well as uncertainties regarding ethical and legal responsibilities.These issues highlight the urgent need for a systematic optimization and governance framework.To address these challenges, this study introduces a governance framework centered on a Knowledge Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) fact-checking mechanism, grounded in the principle of human-AI alignment. By deeply integrating RAG with medical semantic knowledge graphs, the framework establishes a closed-loop verification system that mitigates hallucinations and promotes transparent content generation. Concurrently, a multi-dimensional evaluation system focusing on reliability and security was introduced, employing metrics such as hallucination detection rate, medical evidence coverage, and an adapted Pass@k score to balance factual accuracy with educational applicability.Together, the proposed technical solution and evaluation system form a core governance framework for LLM deployment in medical education, positioning LLMs as rigorously verified “auxiliary tools” and supporting their precise and effective integration into educational practice.

It should be noted that the Knowledge Graph-Based RAG Fact-Checking Framework is not limited to medical education but also reflects the broader application value of RAG technology in healthcare. Relevant studies indicate that RAG can be extended to writing code for medical devices and healthcare software, where context-aware retrieval enhancement and fact-checking could improve the standardization, safety, and reliability of medical-related code25. This points to a common requirement across the healthcare field to enhance trust in LLMs. Future work could promote the adaptation and reuse of cross-scenario RAG technology to form a trustworthy AI system spanning medical education, clinical diagnosis, and medical software development.

Based on the core findings and technical explorations of this study, our future research will pursue several key directions to provide clearer guidance for medical educators and AI developers, thereby supporting the “paradigm reconstruction” of medical education. First, we will conduct empirical validation of the proposed framework and evaluation system by engaging medical learners at different levels across diverse teaching scenarios. This will assess practical effectiveness and gather feedback for iterative optimization. Second, we will develop a dynamic update mechanism for medical knowledge graph, integrating real-time updates from clinical guidelines and recent literature to ensure knowledge currency. Third, we will explore implementation pathways for human-AI collaborative teaching models. Grounded in the conceptualization of LLMs as “auxiliary tools,” this work will design collaborative workflows in which educators lead instructional design while LLMs supply evidence-based case expansions and personalized Q&A support. Finally, we will pursue optimization for cross-linguistic and cross-cultural contexts. Leveraging the observed performance differences in models handling Chinese versus English-language medical knowledge, we will adapt RAG parameters and knowledge graph construction methods to varied linguistic and educational settings, thereby promoting the technology’s global adoption.

Diffuse leptomeningeal glioneuronal tumor with distinct neuronal and glial components but identical diagnostic molecular and genetic features
Authors: Andrew J. Witten, Carson Dougherty, Chunhai Hao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of the central nervous system (CNS) tumors has classified diffuse leptomeningeal glioneuronal tumor (DLGNT) as a mixed neuronal and glial tumor. Here, we report a DLGNT with two distinct morphological tumor components but identical molecular features. A four‐year‐old female child presented with progressive right upper extremity weakness. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed the leptomeningeal enhancement over the brain stem and cervicothoracic spine. The histological examination of surgical specimens revealed two distinct tumor components: approximately half of the tumor is composed of oligodendroglioma‐like tumor intermingled with nodules of ganglioglioma‐like tumor. Immunohistochemistry confirmed the oligodendroglioma and ganglioglioma features. The molecular genetic studies demonstrated the features of DLGNT, including fusion of KIAA1549::BRAF, deletion of chromosome 1p, and absence of isocitrate dehydrogenase 1/2 (IDH1/2) mutation in both tumor components. Interestingly, the genetic studies also revealed the distinct chromosomal abnormalities of the loss of chromosome 4 only in oligodendroglioma‐like tumor and copy neutral loss of heterozygosity of 7Q34Q36.3 in the ganglioglioma‐like tumor component. This case highlights the critical role of molecular testing in the diagnosis of rare cases of DLGNT with diverse morphological components as well as in the identification of unique molecular alternations responsible for morphological phenotypes of the distinct tumors in DLGNT.

INTRODUCTION

Diffuse leptomeningeal glioneuronal tumor (DLGNT) was first described as a distinct pathological and clinical entity in 2010 1 and then included in the 2016 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of the central nervous system (CNS) tumors as a neuronal and neuronal‐glial tumor. 2 In the updated 2021 WHO classification of CNS tumors, DLGNT has been classified into subtypes based on its molecular features. 3 DLGNT is a rare CNS tumor that commonly involves the spinal and intracranial leptomeninges, mostly affects pediatric patients, is composed of oligodendrocyte‐like cells, and is molecularly characterized by the fusion of KIAA1549::BRAF and the loss of the short arm of chromosome 1 (1p). 4 , 5 Genome‐wide methylation profiling has divided DLGNT into two methylation classes (MCs): MC1 and MC2. 6 DLGNT‐MC1 frequently has the codeletion of the short arm of chromosome 1 and the long arm 19 (1p/19q), 5 , 7 while DLGNT‐MC2 shows the gain of the long arm of chromosome 1 (1q) as an adverse prognosticator in DLGNT. 6 , 8 Although DLGNT is commonly composed of oligodendrocyte‐like tumor cells, a small set of the tumors have been reported with neuronal differentiation, such as neurocytic rosettes and ganglion cells; 9 however, the neuronal components have not been genetically characterized. Here, we report a case of DLGNT with two distinct morphological tumor components: an oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component with neurocytic rosettes and a ganglioglioma‐like tumor component. Both tumor components show the characteristic diagnostic molecular alternations of KIAA1549::BRAF fusion and chromosome am 1p loss but also display chromosomal alterations distinct from each other.

CLINICAL SUMMARY

A four‐year‐old female child presented with progressive right upper extremity weakness. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed the leptomeningeal enhancement over the brain stem and cervicothoracic spine and a cyst lesion in the left cerebellum (Fig. 1A–D). Neurological surgery was elected, and a dissection of the bulk of the tumor was accomplished, leaving behind only a thin ventral rim overlying the ventral aspect of the spinal cord. Postoperatively, the patient received chemotherapy with trametinib for three years. A ventriculoperitoneal shunt was replaced three years after surgery. At the four‐year follow‐up, the patient was clinically stable.

PATHOLOGICAL FINDINGS AND MOLECULAR GENETIC FINDINGS

Microscopic examination of hematoxylin and eosin (HE) sections of surgical fragments revealed two morphologically distinct tumor components. Approximately half of the tumor consisted of oligodendroglioma‐like cells with monotonous population of cells that showed uniform round nuclei and perinuclear haloes in moderate sheets and lobules (Fig. 2A). Randomly distributed in the oligodendroglioma‐like tumor were various sizes of anucelar neurocytic rosettes (Fig. 2B). Clearly demarcated from the oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component were large nodules of ganglioglioma‐like tumor with differentiated and disoriented ganglion cells in an astrocytoma‐like cellular background (Fig. 2C). The mitotic activity was low, with three mitotic features observed in microscopic examination of all the specimen.

Histological and immunohistochemical features of the tumor. HE staining (A–C) and immunohistochemistry with OLIG2 (D–F), synaptophysin (G–I) and GFAP (J–L).

Immunohistochemically, oligodendroglioma‐like cells were positive for nuclear staining of OLIG2 (Fig. 2D, E), but ganglioglioma‐like cells were OLIG2 negative (Fig. 2F). Synaptophysin stain showed weakly positive oligodendroglioma‐like cells (Fig. 2G) but strong positive neurocytic rosettes (Fig. 2H) and moderately positive ganglioglioma‐like tumor (Fig. 2I). Glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) stain revealed a few fibrillary astrocyte‐like cells in the oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component (Fig. 2J, K) and astrocytoma‐like cells in the ganglioglioma‐like tumor component (Fig. 2L). Immunohistochemistry also revealed that the oligodendroglioma‐like cells were negative, but the ganglion cells were positive for NeuN. Both oligodendroglioma and ganglioglioma‐like cells were negative for p53 and isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1) R132H mutation and showed the presence of ATRX. The Ki‐67 proliferation index was approximately 1% in the ganglioglioma‐like but 5% in the oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component.

Oligodendroglioma‐like and ganglioglioma‐like tumor tissues were dissected separately from the paraffin‐embedded blocks and submitted to the Mayo Clinic Laboratory for the Neuro‐Oncology Expanded Gene Panel (NONCP). The NONCP revealed the fusion of KIAA1549::BRAF (exon 10:exon 9) and absence of IDH1 and IDH2 mutation in the two morphologically distinct tumor components (Table 1). The chromosomal microarray (CMAPT) was performed at the Mayo Clinic Laboratory using molecular inversion probes on a whole genome array (Applied Biosystems [Affymetrix] OncoScan platform). The CMAPT identified the loss of aim 1p (1p36.33p11.2), gain of 1q21.1q44, copy gain of 7q34 (including BRAF and KIAA1549), and loss of chromosome 14 and 19 in both oligodendroglioma and ganglioglioma‐like tumor components (Table 1). Interestingly, the CMAPT also revealed the district chromosomal abnormalities identified as the loss of chromosome 4 in the oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component but copy neutral loss of heterozygosity (cnLOH) of 7Q34Q36.3 in the ganglioglioma‐like tumor component (Table 1). The homozygous deletion of cyclin dependent kinase inhibitor 2A/B (CDKN2A/B) was not observed.

DISCUSSION

The 2021 WHO classification of CNS tumors defines DLGNT as a mixed neuronal and glial tumor that is composed of oligodendroglia‐like cells with neuronal differentiation including neurocytic rosettes and ganglion cells. 3 In this case report, we describe DLGNT that is composed of two distinct tumor components: an oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component with neurocytic rosettes and a ganglioglioma‐like tumor component with differentiated ganglion cells. The oligodendroglioma‐like cells were confirmed by positive immunoreaction to OLIG2 but negative activity to GFAP and NeuN, while ganglion cells were positive for NeuN and synaptophysin. The oligodendroglioma‐like tumors showed some atypical features, such as mitotic activity, and an approximately 5% Ki‐67 proliferation index, while no atypical features observed in the ganglioglioma‐like tumor.

Although the two tumor components were morphologically distinct, molecular and genetic studies revealed the identical KIAA1549::BRAF fusion, loss of chromosome arm 1p and absence of IDH1 and IDH2 mutation in both tumor components. The molecular profile of this tumor fits the molecular diagnostic features of DLGNT based on the 2021 WHO classification of CNS tumors. 3 These identical genetic alterations suggest that the two distinct tumor components might share a common origin. However, genetic studies have also revealed the distinct chromosomal abnormalities in each of the tumor components: the loss of chromosome 4 was observed in the oligodendroglioma‐like tumor component, while the copy neutral loss of heterozygosity of 7Q34Q36.3 was found in the ganglioglioma‐like tumor component. Interestingly, the loss of chromosome 4 has been reported in oligodendroglioma. 10 , 11 A literature research, however, failed to find any reports on the cnLOH of 7Q34Q36.3, and therefore, it is unclear whether the cnLOH plays any role in the ganglioglioma phenotype of this DLGNT.

This case highlights the critical diagnostic role of molecular and genetic testing of DLGNT with diverse morphological components and unusual clinical presentation. 12 This case also suggests that the common driver of the KIAA1549::BRAF fusion, the most common gene alteration in mitogen‐activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway in DLGNT. The identification of KIAA1549::BRAF in DLGNT further suggests the targeted therapy of DLGNT patients with trametinib, a MAPK kinase (MEK) inhibitor. 13 , 14 A clinical study of seven pediatric patients diagnosed with DLGNT reported clinical improvement in all patients after receiving craniospinal irradiation followed by vincristine, etoposide, cyclophosphamide, and cisplatin, although the mechanism of drug action remains to be investigated. 15

The 2021 WHO classification of CNS tumors has classified DLGNT into two MCs: MC1 and MC2. 3 DLGNT‐MC1 frequently has the codeletion of the chromosome 1p and 19q, 5 , 6 , 7 while DLGNT‐MC2 displays the gain of the chromosome 1q. 6 , 8 A large 1p deletion and chromosome 19 loss were observed in both oligodendroglioma‐like and ganglioglioma‐like tumor components, but these are not consistent with the whole arm 1p and 19q codeletion as commonly observed in oligodendroglioma. 16 The gain of the chromosome 1q21.1q44 is not considered as chromosome 1q gain as observed in DLGNT‐MC2. 17 The prognosis and grade of DLGNT and its subtypes remain unclear. The most recent 2021 WHO classification of CNS tumors has not assigned DLGNT an official WHO classification grade, although the WHO classification suggests that DLGNT‐MC1 and DLGNT‐MC2 have clinical behavior similar to WHO grade 2 and 3 CNS tumors. In a recent study of 32 DLGNTs and clinical follow‐up of the patients, DLGNT without 1q gain showed an outcome compatible with WHO grade 1, but the outcomes of DLGNT‐MC2 tumors with the chromosome 1q gain are compatible with WHO grade 3 CNS tumors. 17 Therefore, the case of DLGNT presented here should be considered as a WHO grade 1 CNS tumor with a favorable clinical prognosis.

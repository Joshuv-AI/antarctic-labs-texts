IFPHPOS0041 Elective Recovery and Patient Flow for Assessments and Support for Families Waiting for Autism Assessments in a Child Development Clinic Setting
Authors: Indrajit F Thopte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBradford is a city with high levels of deprivation and health inequality. It is ethnically diverse, has low literacy rates, and the youngest population in the UK. There is growing evidence that a lack of school readiness at entry is a strong early predictor of later NEET (Not in Education, Employment or Training) status, leading to poorer economic, social, physical and mental health, outcomes in adulthood.A major local analysis (Child of the North/Born in Bradford) reports that one in three children in Bradford are not school ready, rising to almost 50% in more deprived areas. Around 75% of referrals to the Child Development Clinic (CDC) are for preschool and early primary-aged children with speech delay, social communication and interactions difficulties, often suggestive of autism. Diagnostic conversion rates are high (90–92%).In 2023, children waited approximately one year for their first CDC appointment and a further 2.5 years for autism assessment. By January 2025, total waiting times had increased to nearly five years.Using structured improvement methodology and regular multidisciplinary team (MDT) engagement, we are refining referral and triage processes and streamlining assessments to reduce waiting times. At the same time, in response to limited speech and language therapy capacity, we are shifting focus away from diagnosis alone towards supporting families to be empowered, informed, and able to help their children thrive and stay safe while they are “waiting well,” rather than simply waiting.

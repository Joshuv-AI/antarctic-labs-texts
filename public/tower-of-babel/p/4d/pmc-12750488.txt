Quality of Drug Clinical Trials in China: A Mixed Methods Research
Authors: Xiayan Chen, Hongling Chu, Xiaofang Zhang, Huijuan Li, Jiaxue Meng, Yangfeng Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimThe quality of drug clinical trials is crucial for authorizing new drugs and fostering innovations in clinical practice. This study aimed to report the status, trends, and factors of the quality of drug clinical trials in China.MethodsThis mixed methods study assessed trial quality using quantitative data from public sources and qualitative data from focus groups and interviews with key stakeholders.ResultsThe No Action Indicated findings issued by the Food and Drug Administration increased from 43% from 2009–2015 to 88% from 2016–2022, whereas the Official Action Indicated findings decreased from 9% to 0% (p = 0.001). The Center for Food and Drug Inspection revealed that 12% of new drug applications in 2015–2017 were suspected of data fabrication, compared to only 0.6% failed to pass the inspection in 2022 (p < 0.001). Number of drug trials published by Chinese institutions in top medical journals increased from 1.3% in 2009 to 4.9% in 2022 (p for trend <0.001). Moreover, the number of clinical trial guidelines increased from approximately 10/year from 2015–2019 to 50–60/year from 2020–2022, number of internationally accredited ethics committees increased from 4 in 2009 to 82 in 2022, and over 130,000 individuals received training on the International Council for Harmonization guidelines. Interviews with stakeholders revealed a consensus on quality improvement, attributed to seven key factors, and highlighted further recommendations to enhance clinical trial quality in China.ConclusionsThe quality of drug clinical trials in China has significantly improved over the past decade, yet there remains scope for further enhancement.Keywords: clinical trials, drug clinical development, qualitative analysis, quantitative analysis, study quality

Introduction

Clinical trials provide crucial evidence on efficacy and safety for health authorities to authorize new drugs or medical devices to enter the market [1, 2]. Thus, the quality of clinical trials supporting the regulatory approval of new drugs and medical devices has received special attention from regulatory agencies, the pharmaceutical industry, the medical community, and the public [3, 4, 5].

The quality of drug clinical trials, those pertaining to supporting new drug market authorization, is of paramount concern owing to the complexity and in vivo pharmacokinetics of drugs [6, 7]. It is often considered a marker of the clinical research capacity of a medical research institution, a drug developer or a country's pharmaceutical industry.

China has become an attractive location for conducting drug clinical trials because of its large patient pool, burgeoning pharmaceutical industry, and increasingly favorable regulatory environment [8, 9, 10, 11]. However, for many years, China's clinical research capacity has been limited by its underdeveloped economy and the corresponding generic development strategy. In response to identified challenges and weaknesses in drug approval and clinical trials, Chinese regulatory authorities have implemented measures to streamline the approval process and enhance the quality of trials conducted in China [12, 13, 14].

The number of Chinese drug clinical trials registered on the China Drug Trials website rose from 338 in 2013 to 3316 in 2022 [15]. While China's pharmaceutical industry has experienced remarkable growth and innovation, concerns remain about the credibility of drug clinical trial data originating from the region [16]. To address this issue, this study aims to elucidate the prevailing uncertainties by examining the trend in the quality of drug clinical trials in China, identifying the driving factors that explain the trend, and offering recommendations for improving the quality of clinical trials in China through a mixed methods approach. Specifically, this study focuses on sponsor‐initiated clinical trials for new drug applications and excludes investigator‐initiated trials.

Methods

Study Design

This study used a mixed methods approach consisting of quantitative and qualitative studies and an integrative analysis (Figure 1). The quantitative part aimed to collect objective information related to the quality of drug clinical trials and their recent trends from publicly available resources, whereas the qualitative part was designed to collect subjective information from purposively selected key stakeholders, including regulators, clinical research institutions, and sponsors and contract research organizations (CROs). The key information to be obtained from the qualitative study included stakeholders’ assessment of the quality of drug clinical trials, key underlying factors, and suggestions for further improvement. Findings from the qualitative study also informed additional quantitative data searches. Finally, we integrate the results from both the quantitative and qualitative studies and draw conclusions. Ethical approval was obtained from the Peking University Institutional Review Board (No. IRB00001052‐22062) on June 29, 2022.

Quantitative Data

The quantitative data were collected from various sources, including the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Data Dashboard, the official website of the China National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE), the China Food and Drug Inspection Institute (CFDI), Web of Science, and other public sources. The detailed search strategies are outlined in Appendix A in Additional file 1. The FDA inspection results are classified into three categories: No Action Indicated (NAI), Voluntary Action Indicated (VAI), and Official Action Indicated (OAI). The four high‐impact medical journals selected for this study included The New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM), The Lancet, Journal of American Medical Association (JAMA), and British Medical Journal (BMJ).

Qualitative Data

The qualitative interviews were purposively sampled, and the stakeholders were stratified to include representatives from regulatory authorities, clinical trial institutions, sponsors, and CROs who met all the following inclusion criteria: (a) involvement in clinical trial quality management; (b) over 10 years of experience; and (c) holding director‐level positions or senior titles. Participants had no prior personal relationship with the researchers and were not aware of the study before being invited. They were contacted via phone, email, or other methods to explain the research objectives, methodology, and other relevant details. All participants provided written informed consent.

All the interviews were conducted via video conference via Tencent Meeting. Six focus group interviews were conducted (three with sponsors, one with CROs, and two with clinical trial institutions; 1.7–2.5 h each) and four individual interviews with regulators (0.7–1.3 h each). Focus groups were arranged within stakeholder categories to foster open discussion and avoid cross‐category influence, while individual interviews with regulators were used to elicit candid views in the Chinese cultural and organizational context. The same interview guide (Appendix B in Additional file 1) was applied to both formats. In focus groups, participants could respond to or expand on others’ points without repeating prior comments. Interviews were conducted by WYF (male, MD, Professor, with over 40 years of clinical research experience and more than 20 years in clinical research management), ZXF (female, PhD, Assistant Research Fellow, with over 15 years of experience in clinical research education), and LHJ (female, PhD, Assistant Research Fellow, with over 15 years in clinical research management). CXY (female, MPH, Senior Clinical Research Associate, with over 5 years of experience in clinical research management), MJX (female, MSc, Senior Clinical Research Associate, with over 5 years of experience), and CHL (female, PhD, Associate Research Fellow, with over 10 years of experience in qualitative research design and implementation) and CLN (female, Professional Transcriptionist) provided technical support and were responsible for note‐taking and recording the sessions. Interviews continued until no new themes emerged, indicating thematic saturation. Transcripts were not returned to participants for correction or feedback.

Data Analysis

Quantitative Data

The results are summarized in terms of numbers and percentages. Group differences were assessed via the chi‐square test, Fisher's exact test or Mann‒Whitney test for categorical variables as appropriate, and trend analysis was conducted via a linear regression model for continuous variables, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed via IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 27.0 (IBMCorp., Armonk, N.Y., USA).

Qualitative Data

All interviews and discussions were audio‐recorded and transcribed verbatim by CXY and CLN. All private details concerning individual participants were anonymized. The audio recordings and written transcripts were analyzed via a thematic analysis approach with QSR NVivo 11.0 software by CXY and MJX. Themes were identified through iterative coding and consensus discussions to enhance analytical rigor and ensure coding consistency.

Mixed Data Analysis

The mixed data were analyzed via CHL and CXY via dimensional joint display, where the qualitative and quantitative data were processed concurrently. This process involved identifying linkages between the two data types and iteratively generating an optimized parallel table to understand, interpret, and present the findings. This integration method can produce confirmed, expanded, or discordant findings [17].

Quantitative Study

Direct Evidence

Compliance With Good Clinical Practice (GCP)

From 2009 to 2022, 49 site inspections were conducted by the FDA in China, resulting in 67% with NAI, 29% with VAI, and 4% with OAI, which closely mirrors global averages (Figure 2; Table S1 in Additional file 1). According to the Interim Report On Drug Clinical Trial Data Inspection released by the China CFDI in 2017 [18], the CFDI completed inspection of 313 new drug applications from July 2015 to June 2017. Among these applications, 38 (12%) were suspected of data fabrication [19]. In contrast, the 2022 Annual Drug Inspection Report [20] revealed that only two (0.6%) of the 341 new applications failed to pass the inspection (p < 0.001).

Production of High‐Impact Evidence

The number of drug clinical trial articles published by Chinese institutions in the top four medical journals increased from 2 (1.3%) in 2009 to 12 (4.9%) in 2022 (p for trend < 0.001) (Figure 2; Table S2 in Additional file 1).

Interviewees’ Assessment

One hundred percent of the interviewees believed that the quality of drug clinical trials in China has improved.

Indirect Evidence

Laws/Regulations/Guidelines

The number of CDE‐issued clinical trial guidelines increased from approximately 10/year from 2015–2019 to 50–60/year from 2020–2022 (Figure 2). By 2022, the NMPA had set implementation dates for 21 out of 22 International Council for Harmonization (ICH) efficacy guidelines, further demonstrating a commitment to international regulatory alignment [21].

Inspections

In 2022, the Center for Food and Drug Inspection of China conducted 1377 registration inspections, while provincial drug regulatory bodies performed 1055 supervision inspections [20]. For the 1272 institutions authorized for conducting drug clinical trials in China in 2022, every institution was inspected on average twice [22].

Research Capacity

To increase drug clinical research capacity, a total of 1649 institutions have been authorized for conducting clinical trials as of 2024 [22]. From 2018 to 2022, the NMPA conducted more than 80 ICH guideline training sessions, which were attended by more than 130 thousand participants [23]. Concurrently, the number of Ethics Committees (ECs)/Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) recognized by the Strategic Initiative for Developing Capacity in Ethical Review (SIDCER)—an international accreditation project aimed at protecting human subjects in medical research—has significantly increased from 4 in 2009 to 82 in 2022. From a broader perspective, China has also established 50 National Clinical Research Centers and 13 National Medical Centers to strengthen its underlying system for health innovation and clinical research [24, 25].

Qualitative Study

Characteristics of Participants

Of the 40 senior executives who agreed to participate, 39 attended the interviews. One participant was unable to join due to work commitments on the scheduled day. Participants included 21 from sponsors, 5 from CROs, 9 from clinical trial institutions, and 4 from regulatory authorities. The average age of the respondents was 44.6 ± 6.5 years, and their average years of work experience were 17.4 ± 6.3 years. Their educational attainment level represented the educational characteristics of these professional careers in China (Table S3 in Additional file 1).

Policies and Actions to Improve the Quality of Drug Clinical Trials

The interviewees highlighted improvements in China's clinical trial quality, providing assessments and supporting evidence (Table 2). The interviews with regulators identified eighteen laws, regulations and guidelines that are crucial for improving the quality of drug clinical trials in China, with an additional four identified through further searches (Table S4 in Additional file 1). Among these, the “Opinions on Reforming the Review and Approval System for Drugs and Medical Devices” issued by the State Council on August 18, 2015 [14], marked the launch of a comprehensive and mandatory regulatory reform plan, with a focus on improving the speed of drug review and approval, encouraging pharmaceutical innovation, and enhancing drug quality. China's accession to the ICH in 2017 signifies that China's technical standards for drug review and approval align with international standards, promoting the R&D level of China's pharmaceutical industry, facilitating the early introduction of new foreign drugs into China, and benefiting the internationalization of Chinese drugs. In 2023, China updated Ethical Review Measures for Life Science and Medical Research involving Humans [26] to enhance ethical oversight in research. The Procedures for Supervision Inspection of Drug Clinical Trial Institutions (Trial), published in 2023, mandate that regulatory authorities conduct supervision inspections to ensure that drug clinical trial institutions comply with relevant laws and regulations, adhere to the GCP, establish quality management systems, and implement quality management activities. These supervision inspections are set to become an important complement to drug registration inspections [27].

Factors Contributing to Quality Improvement

Seven key factors driving improvements in the quality of drug clinical trials in China were identified (Figure 3). Please refer to Table S5 in Additional file 1 for specific quotes.

Factors contributing to quality improvement, categorized by the responsibilities of regulatory authorities, sponsors and CROs, and drug clinical trial institutions. CROs, Contract Research Organization.

Regulation

Strengthening Laws, Regulations, and Guidelines

Most respondents believed that regulatory authorities play “a directive role” in the quality of clinical trials. Chinese regulatory authorities have (1) renewed laws, regulations, and issued guidelines for GCP; (2) enhanced oversight and inspection of clinical trial institutions; (3) established a China Drug Trials website (www.chinadrugtrials.org.cn) for sponsors to register drug clinical trials mandatorily; and (4) joined the ICH in June 2017.

Reforming and Optimizing the Inspection System

According to respondents from sponsors and clinical trial institutions, frequent inspections heightened practitioners’ awareness of trial quality and promoted the optimization of clinical trial quality systems.

“We have all learned many lessons from inspections. It has made both companies and investigators pay more attention to clinical trial quality.” (Sponsor 21)

“Regular and intensive inspections have fostered sponsors and clinical trial institutions to improve their quality systems.” (Sponsor 07)

Clinical Trial Institutions

Enhancing Capacity of Investigators

Most respondents emphasized the pivotal role of investigators in the implementation of clinical trials. A multinational corporation (MNC) interviewee with 19 years of working experience said, “Investigators can contribute to the scientific rigor of the protocol and consider the actual benefits and risks for the participants.” (Sponsor 07)

A respondent from a clinical trial institution with 20 years of working experience remarked that “Rigorous investigators can ensure the accuracy of first‐hand clinical trial data.” (Clinical Trial Institution 01)

Optimizing Clinical Trial Office Management

Many respondents also highlighted the administrative function of Clinical Trial Offices. Specifically, certain Clinical Trial Offices have implemented customized systems for clinical trials, integrating “internal quality assurance measures.”

Strengthening Capacity of Ethics Committees

Some respondents believe that ethics committees exert an influence, acknowledging the role in scrutinizing the scientific and ethical aspects of clinical trials. A domestic interviewee with 18 years of working experience said, “Ethics oversights can impact participant protection and, consequently, clinical trial quality.” (Sponsor 18)

Sponsors and CROs

Enhancing Clinical Trial Technical Skills

A domestic interviewee with 22 years of working experience commented,

“We are experiencing more specialized interactions with suppliers in areas like patient recruitment, site management, drug packaging, blinding, logistics, storage, central imaging, and central pathology, making each stage of clinical trials more professional.” (Sponsor 19)

Many interviewees noted that sponsors are primarily responsible for the clinical trial quality and emphasized the crucial importance of “quality by design.” Respondents from sponsors and CROs echoed “identifying the critical procedure and critical data” and “developing a risk management plan” before trial conduct, as well as conducting “monitoring” and “audit” during trial. Domestic respondents observed that multinational sponsors, supported internationally, have established advanced quality control systems that influence domestic sponsors. As a result, preliminary industry self‐regulation is gradually emerging.

Employing Digital Tools

Several interviewees affirmed the role of informatization, highlighting those technologies like Electronic Data Capture (EDC), Clinical Trial Management Systems, electronic Patient Reported Outcomes, and clinical trial registration websites have revolutionized data collection and management. For instance, EDC systems enable the direct entry of clinical data into a centralized database. This approach not only reduces transcription errors but also supports real‐time monitoring and creates a detailed audit trail. These improvements reduce manual errors and ensure consistent data flow, enhancing data integrity and streamlining processes, which collectively elevate trial quality.

Interviewees’ Suggestions for Further Quality Improvement

The interviewees proposed the following suggestions to improve the quality of drug clinical trials in China. Please refer to Table S5 in Additional file 1 for specific quotes.

Continuous Refinement of Guidelines, With Considerations of Innovation, Practicability, and Mutual Communication

A regulatory interviewee with 35 years of working experience highlighted the necessity of adapting to the fast‐paced innovation in fields like antibody drugs, vaccines, and small molecule therapeutics by stating, “The regulatory authority must adapt, foster this innovation, and provide proactive guidance.” (Regulatory authority 01)

Additionally, while there has been steady progress in enhancing China's policy framework for drug clinical trial quality, some interviewees noted the importance of strengthening the practical implementation and enforcement of these policies. One MNC sponsor interviewee with 17 years of working experience recommended, “Conduct feasibility studies before enacting regulations” (Sponsor 04), while another MNC sponsor interviewee with 19 years of working experience suggested, “The CDE and the CFDI should offer clearer guidelines and detailed rules, and address industry queries in Q&A sessions.” (Sponsor 07)

Enhanced communication with regulatory authorities was another critical point raised by interviewees from sponsors and CROs, advocating for better interaction mechanisms.

Continuous Improvement in the Professionalism of Inspectors, Openness in Sharing Inspection Results, and Adoption of Risk‐Based Inspection

Stakeholders stressed the need for consistency in inspections, with one interviewee from a clinical trial institution, who has 19 years of experience, proposing that, “The inspection center should enhance inspector training and, where possible, provide them with first‐hand experience in the daily operations of clinical trial institutions.” (Clinical Trial Institution 08)

There is widespread support for the public disclosure of inspection results. One domestic sponsor interviewee with 17 years of working experience suggested that “Chinese regulatory authorities should openly share inspection reports, similar to the FDA and European Medicines Agency's (EMA) annual summaries.” (Sponsor 17)

Another interviewee from a clinical trial institution proposed the creation of “blacklists and whitelists for principal investigators and institutions.” Additionally, many recommend adopting risk‐based inspections to address the imbalance between the limited number of inspectors and the large volume of inspections that need to be processed promptly.

Enhancing Continuous Training of Clinical Research Personnel, Including Investigators, Clinical Trial Office Managers, Project Managers, Clinical Research Associates, and Clinical Research Coordinators, With a Focus on Improving Their Skills in Protocol Design, Implementation, and Quality Assurance and Control

A CRO interviewee with 17 years of working experience commented, “Internationally, there are numerous training resources like Drug Information Association (DIA), Clinical Trials Transformation Initiative (CTTI), and TransCelerate. I hope we see more platforms in China dedicated to sharing information and resources for training.” (Contract Research Organization 01)

Many interviewees also highlighted the importance of “quality by design.” They noted that trial protocols should be developed through enhanced clinical research design capacity and collaborative discussions among regulators, sponsors, and investigators.

Enhancing the Broader Clinical Research Capacity of Medical Institutions Underlying Drug Clinical Trials, Including the Establishment of Clinical Research Platforms, Dedicated Clinical Research Teams, and Other Technical Resources

The interviewees stated the following:

“Both the government and institutions should establish specialized clinical research platforms, including research‐oriented hospitals and research‐oriented wards, along with dedicated research physicians and teams.” (Regulatory authority 01)

“Can hospitals set up dedicated research positions and establish appropriate performance and promotion systems for them? This would free researchers from their busy clinical duties and allow them to focus more on conducting clinical trials.” (Clinical Trial Institution 04)

“The Clinical Trial Office should be recognized by hospitals as an essential support for clinical research, necessitating the formation of a professional management team and the development of a comprehensive quality assurance system.” (Clinical Trial Institution 07)

Establishing a Clinical Trial Association to Develop Quality Metrics and Standard Benchmarks to Guide the Industry in Conducting Clinical Trials

Some interviewees also suggested the need for a clinical trial industry association to guide industry development and consolidate resources. For instance, a representative from an MNC sponsor with 27 years of working experience expressed the desire to “establish a comprehensive set of comparable quality metrics and industry benchmarks to facilitate more effective cross‐comparisons among different companies.” (Sponsor 02)

Integration Analysis of the Quality of Drug Clinical Trials

Quantitative improvements in FDA and China NMPA inspections are corroborated by qualitative data from inspectors, confirming a significant reduction in clinical trial quality issues. High‐impact publications further validate this progress, with increased publication rates in top journals and stakeholder acknowledgment of global recognition. Based on the interviewees’ own experiences, all the stakeholders stated that the quality of drug clinical trials in China has been greatly improved. Regulatory improvements have helped establish clear responsibilities and standards for clinical trial practices. This ensures consistency, accountability, and industry‐wide compliance, aligning with international norms. Regular and intensive inspections have heightened practitioners’ awareness and motivation to adhere to GCP while also enriching their inspection experience. Additionally, the enhanced clinical research capabilities of all parties involved, bolstered by targeted training, the accumulation of experience, and specialization, have significantly improved the quality of trial design, implementation, and data integrity. The increase in SIDCER‐accredited ECs/IRBs has reinforced participant protection and quality control measures implemented by sponsors or CROs, along with the adoption of digital tools, further supporting efficient management and data integrity. Collectively, these advancements have improved clinical trial quality, supported the generation of higher‐quality evidence, and reduced inspection issues.

Discussion

Our study demonstrated a significant improvement in the quality of drug clinical trials in China in the past decade in terms of compliance with GCP and the production of high‐impact evidence, while the number of drug trials increased almost tenfold over this period. This trend aligns with an improvement in the regulatory framework, regular and intensive inspections, and enhanced clinical research capabilities. Similar findings have been reported in other studies [28, 29]. This study identified seven factors related to drug clinical trial quality and offered suggestions for further improvement from regulatory authorities, sponsors, CROs, and clinical trial institutions. The key strategies include the following:

Improvement of the Regulatory Legal Framework

A well‐developed legal and regulatory framework is already in place. Moving forward, greater emphasis should be placed on governance and multilevel participation in the implementation of these laws, regulations, and guidelines. To this end, regulatory authorities should regularly issue supplementary documents and refine specific requirements, including clinical trial design and implementation guidelines for innovative drugs. Additionally, it is imperative to develop a schedule and roadmap for the domestic implementation of ICH guidelines, aiming for rapid alignment with international standards. Moreover, conducting feasibility assessments, holding Q&A sessions, and evaluating the implementation process and its effects are vital steps to ensure effective policy application.

Enhancement of Inspection Systems

As noted in the interviews, regulatory authorities should adopt risk‐based inspections with clear criteria for assessing risks, helping address the imbalance between the limited number of inspectors and the high volume of inspections. Compared with inspections by the FDA, EMA, and Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA), which inspect not only clinical trial institutions but also sponsors, CROs, and ethics committees, China has not yet implemented such comprehensive inspections. Therefore, regulatory authorities could consider expanding the scope of inspections [30]. Furthermore, while the FDA and EMA categorize their inspection results (e.g., OAI, NAI, VAI; major, minor, comments), China has not yet made its criteria for assessing inspection results or the results themselves publicly available. Over the past decade, transparency in clinical trial registries has become a standard expectation [31, 32]. Consequently, there is a growing consensus that inspection results should also be disclosed [33]. The inclusion of these reports in registries such as the China Drug Trials website would enhance transparency, allowing sponsors and institutions to address issues effectively, ensuring participant safety, and improving clinical trial quality [33].

Establishment of a Clinical Research Platform

Currently, investigator involvement in clinical trials is insufficient, with investigators heavily relying on clinical research coordinators or clinical research nurses [34, 35, 36]. Chinese investigators, who often juggle roles as clinical department directors, are burdened with multiple responsibilities, including clinicians, researchers, and managers. They oversee outpatient and inpatient care, research, teaching, and departmental duties [37, 38, 39]. In addition, the support provided by hospital administrators to the Clinical Trial Office is inadequate, as the Clinical Trial Office primarily serves administrative functions and lacks professional capability [40]. It plays a crucial role in minimizing issues in clinical trials. For example, the Drug Clinical Trial Center at Peking University Third Hospital improved clinical trial management by adopting a quality management system, which reduced the average number of issues per trial from 5.54 in 2017 to 4.70 in 2018 [41]. Therefore, the capacity of the clinical research system that supports all kinds of clinical research should be established, including two mutually supportive subsystems: the clinical innovation‐supporting technical system and the human subject protection system. These clinical innovation‐supporting technical systems require a research team specialized in clinical research methodology with extensive research experience, a set of standard operating procedures, an electronic system for clinical research data collection and management, and a skilled data analysis team equipped with advanced tools [42, 43, 44]. The human subject protection system requires an EC with established review processes, a quality assurance system for conducting onsite inspections, a structured system to ensure research integrity, an educational and training framework for scientific ethics and research integrity, and administrative bodies across various levels [45, 46].

Strengthening of Clinical Research Capacity

To further enhance the quality of clinical trials, innovative trial designs that integrate with clinical practice are essential, necessitating a greater number of professionals skilled in clinical research methodologies [47, 48, 49, 50]. Efforts should focus on rapidly developing a training system for clinical scientists and clinical personnel by incorporating “clinical research methodology” into medical education, introducing related postgraduate courses, and fostering skilled mid‐career clinicians [51, 52].

Leveraging AI Tools

The growing application of AI in clinical trials warrants proactive oversight. AI can support trial design, patient recruitment and retention, biosimulation, and data management and analysis, thereby improving efficiency and quality. However, both technical and regulatory research are needed to establish clear guidance to ensure its responsible use, address potential issues related to privacy, ethics, and bias, and strengthen stakeholder capacity for effective adoption [53, 54].

Limitations

A potential limitation of this study was the use of purposive sampling to select participants in clinical trial fields, which may have introduced bias. The backgrounds and experiences of the authors may have influenced the interpretation of the data.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrated that the quality of drug clinical trials in China has improved in terms of compliance with the GCP and the production of high‐impact evidence in the past decade. Further advancements could be achieved by strengthening the clinical research infrastructure through a clinical innovation‐supporting system and a robust human subject protection system.

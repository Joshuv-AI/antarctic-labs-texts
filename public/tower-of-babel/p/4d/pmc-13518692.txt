Improvement in Visual-Tasking Daily Living Activities and Work Productivity in Patients with Dry Eye Disease: Integrated Analysis of COMET Studies
Authors: Laura M. Periman, Cory J. Lappin, Marguerite B. McDonald, Jade Coats, Guruprasad R. Pattar, Tony Okoro, Nathan Lighthizer, Michelle Senchyna, Lisa M. Nijm
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDry eye disease (DED) symptoms can impair activities of daily living (ADLs) and work productivity (WP). This post hoc integrated analysis of the phase 2b COMET-1 (NCT04498182) and phase 3 COMET-2 (NCT05285644) and COMET-3 (NCT05360966) studies explored the effectiveness of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% (ACO [Tryptyr®]), a recently approved treatment for DED signs and symptoms, on visual-tasking ADLs and WP.MethodsThese randomized, double-masked, vehicle (VEH)-controlled studies had similar designs and identical eligibility criteria, enrolling adults aged ≥ 30 years with prespecified DED signs and symptoms. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive ACO or VEH in both eyes twice daily for 84 (COMET-1) or 90 (COMET-2 and COMET-3) days. The degree that DED symptoms interfered with visual-tasking ADLs and WP was assessed on visual analog scales (VAS; 0–100 mm). ADL/WP and Symptom Assessment iN Dry Eye (SANDE) questionnaires and safety assessments were collected on days 1, 14, 28, and 84/90. Least-squares (LS) mean change from baseline (CFB) and responder analyses were analyzed using analysis of covariance and Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test, respectively.ResultsPositive treatment effects in favor of ACO (n = 584) versus VEH (n = 595) were observed for LS mean difference in CFB from days 14 through 84/90 in the ability to use electronic devices (day 14, − 4.4 [P = 0.0016]; day 28, − 5.4 [P = 0.0002]; day 84/90, − 3.4 [P = 0.0378]), a visual-tasking composite score (day 14, − 3.7 [P = 0.0023]; day 28, − 4.2 [P = 0.0012]; day 84/90, − 3.1 [P = 0.0307]), and WP (day 14, − 4.4 [P = 0.0104]; day 28, − 5.4 [P = 0.0028]; day 84/90, − 4.4 [P = 0.0229]). Responder analyses favoring ACO supported the meaningfulness of observed CFB reductions. The only ocular treatment-emergent adverse event with > 1.5% incidence was instillation-site burning or stinging (ACO, 50.2% [97.6% mild]; VEH, 3.2% [100% mild]).ConclusionsThese exploratory data suggest that ACO is associated with rapid and sustained improvements in visual-tasking ADLs and WP.Trial RegistrationCOMET-1, NCT04498182; COMET-2, NCT05285644; COMET-3, NCT05360966.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-026-01421-1.

Introduction

Dry eye disease (DED) is defined by the Dry Eye Workshop III of the Tear Film & Ocular Surface Society as “a multifactorial, symptomatic disease characterized by a loss of homeostasis of the tear film and/or ocular surface, in which tear film instability and hyperosmolarity, ocular surface inflammation and damage, and neurosensory abnormalities are etiological factors” [1]. DED affects more than 38 million people in the USA alone, and its prevalence is expected to rise due to risk factors such as age and the widespread use of digital devices that can negatively alter blinking dynamics and tear film stability [2–4].

An array of DED symptoms—including dryness, discomfort, irritation, pain, and visual disturbances—can lead to substantial impairment of activities of daily living (ADLs) and work productivity (WP) [5–8]. Patients frequently report difficulty with tasks requiring sustained visual focus (e.g., use of electronics, reading, writing, and driving [2, 9–13]), and the impact is greater with more severe disease [14–16]. These negative effects extend to the professional sphere, where DED can interfere with WP at levels consistent with rheumatoid arthritis and fibromyalgia (or in severe DED, depression) [17]. Moreover, DED can lead to missed work (absenteeism), less productivity at work (presenteeism), and more healthcare resource utilization, resulting in higher medical costs [9, 16–18]. Collectively, these factors contribute to the substantial economic burden associated with DED [9, 16, 18, 19]. Estimated per person WP costs associated with DED range from $5362 (US dollar [USD] values in the year 2000) to $18,168 (2011 USD values) [9, 18, 20]. Overall, the loss of productivity due to DED in the USA alone was estimated to be $55 billion USD in 2011 [18].

Acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% (ACO [Tryptyr®]; active ingredient previously known as AR-15512) was recently approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of the signs and symptoms of DED [21]. Acoltremon is a potent and highly selective agonist of the cation-permeable ion channel transient receptor potential melastatin 8 (TRPM8), a receptor known as a key regulator of basal tear production [22]. In COMET-1 (phase 2b) as well as both phase 3 pivotal studies (COMET-2 and COMET-3), treatment with ACO, compared with vehicle (VEH), led to increased tear production as early as day 1 in a large proportion of patients [23, 24]. Reductions in DED symptoms and ocular surface staining were also observed in each study [23, 24]. Furthermore, in COMET-1, improvements in ADLs and WP in favor of ACO compared with VEH were observed as exploratory efficacy as early as day 14 [24].

While it seems reasonable that a treatment that reduces DED symptoms may also improve quality of life (QoL), reporting of clinically meaningful benefits in ADLs and WP remains uncommon. Therefore, this post hoc integrated analysis investigated the effects of ACO on five different ADLs requiring visual tasking—driving; reading books, magazines, or newspapers; reading food labels; using electronic devices; and watching TV—as well as WP in patients with DED enrolled in COMET-1, COMET-2, or COMET-3.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

Data included in this post hoc integrated analysis were from the randomized, multicenter, VEH-controlled, double-masked phase 2b COMET-1, phase 3 pivotal COMET-2, and phase 3 pivotal COMET-3 studies [23, 24]. For these studies, the co-primary endpoints in COMET-1 were the change from baseline (CFB) in anesthetized Schirmer score at day 28 and ocular discomfort score on a visual analog scale (ODS-VAS); the primary endpoints for the identical COMET-2 and COMET-3 studies were the proportion of patients with a ≥ 10-mm increase from baseline in unanesthetized Schirmer score at day 14 [23, 24].

Each study (COMET-1, NCT04498182; COMET-2, NCT05285644; COMET-3, NCT05360966) was performed in compliance with the International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, US Food and Drug Administration regulations, and the standard operating procedures of the sponsor and vendors participating in the conduct of the study. Written informed consent was provided by all patients prior to enrollment, and an institutional review board (Alpha Institutional Review Board, San Clemente, CA, USA: IRB00006205) approved all forms provided directly to the patient prior to their use. This article is based on previously conducted studies and does not include any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

The COMET-1, COMET-2, and COMET-3 studies had identical eligibility criteria, which have been published previously [23, 24]. Briefly, key inclusion criteria were being ≥ 30 years of age; having a prior diagnosis of DED; having used or desired to use artificial tears in the previous 2 months; at least one eye with (a) a total corneal staining score of ≥ 2 and ≤ 15 based on the modified National Eye Institute grading scheme, with no region scoring > 3 at screening, and (b) an anesthetized Schirmer test score that was ≥ 2 and < 10 mm/5 min at screening and baseline; symptoms of DED as indicated by a score of ≥ 50 on the Symptom Assessment iN Dry Eye (SANDE) questionnaire and the ODS-VAS at screening and baseline; and an increase in DED signs or symptoms by exposure to the Controlled Adverse Environment (CAE®1; Ora LLC) at screening. Key exclusion criteria were history or presence of any ocular disorder or condition in either eye; evidence of ophthalmic diseases other than DED or meibomian gland dysfunction not requiring chronic therapy; and use of any prescription DED product, autologous serum, or any other topical ocular medication within 30 days before screening or during the study, or use of artificial tears within 2 h before screening or during the study [23, 24].

All three studies included a run-in phase, in which eligible patients at screening received VEH to administer in both eyes twice daily for 14 days [23, 24]. At baseline, patients were re-evaluated and those who met eligibility criteria were randomized 1:1 to receive ACO or VEH to be administered to both eyes twice daily for 84 (COMET-1) or 90 (COMET-2 and COMET-3) days. Consistent study design and treatment duration [23, 24] allowed pooling of data across the three studies [23, 24]. The integrated dataset included patients who participated in COMET-1, COMET-2, or COMET-3 and who received the ACO 0.003% formulation; patients in COMET-1 who received the acoltremon 0.0014% dose were excluded.

Efficacy and Safety Assessments

The degree to which DED symptoms interfered with a patient’s visual-tasking ADLs or WP, or contributed to feelings of depression, was assessed using a study-specific, 7-item visual analog scale (VAS; 0–100 mm) questionnaire. The questionnaire was developed for COMET-1 as an exploratory assessment of functional domains commonly affected by DED symptoms, using a VAS for each item to align with other study assessments (e.g., SANDE, ODS-VAS) [24]. It assessed DED symptom interference with specific activities relevant to ADLs and WP, with item selection informed by clinically relevant complaints [2, 9, 10, 12]. The questionnaire was administered in COMET-1, COMET-2, and COMET-3 as a paper copy at each visit. Patients followed the instructions without additional coaching or domain explanation to minimize intersite variability. Scores were independently quantified by site staff using a 100-mm standardized ruler anchored to the “0” mark to the patient’s indicated mark, per the Study Procedure Manual (questionnaire available in the Supplementary Methods).

For all questions except feelings of depression, higher scores reflected greater DED symptom interference; for the assessment of depression, higher scores indicated a greater degree of depression attributed to DED symptoms. A visual-tasking composite score was also derived, representing the average of the ADL-VAS scores on the five visual-tasking items (i.e., ability to drive; ability to read books, magazines, and/or newspapers; ability to read food labels on boxes and cans; ability to use electronic devices; and ability to watch television). Patients could opt out of nonapplicable questions (e.g., if a patient no longer drove a car). As previously reported, the SANDE questionnaire was used to assess the effect of ACO on DED symptom reduction [23, 24].

In all three studies, the ADL/WP and SANDE questionnaires were administered at baseline, on days 14 and 28, and during the last study visit (day 84 for COMET-1 or day 90 for COMET-2 and COMET-3). For this study, responses from day 84 in COMET-1 were pooled with data from day 90 in COMET-2 and COMET-3 for analysis of a single timepoint (day 84/90) and were considered to be representative of 3 months of treatment.

CFB was determined at each timepoint (days 14, 28, and 84/90) for each visual-tasking ADL, the visual-tasking composite score, WP, and the feeling depressed score. A responder analysis was conducted to determine the proportion of patients achieving ≥ 30%, ≥ 40%, ≥ 50%, ≥ 60%, and ≥ 70% reductions in the scores for DED symptom-related interference on visual-tasking ADLs and WP and the visual-tasking composite score. Correlation analyses compared CFB in SANDE scores and CFB in ADL questionnaire scores using pooled data from both treatment groups.

Safety assessments were collected on each visit. Assessments included adverse events (AEs), corrected visual acuity, slit-lamp biomicroscopy results, intraocular pressure, and dilated fundoscopy.

Statistical Analyses

Exploratory analysis of treatment efficacy was based on the intent-to-treat populations, which included all randomized patients in COMET-1, COMET-2, and COMET-3. All available data per timepoint were utilized with no imputations for missing data. For safety assessments, all randomized patients who received at least one dose of the study medication were included. At each of the post-baseline visits (days 14, 28, and 84/90), the least-squares (LS) mean CFB for individual visual-tasking ADLs, the visual-tasking composite, WP, and feeling depressed scores were assessed by analysis of covariance, adjusting for corresponding baseline values and study effects. The responder analysis based on the proportion of patients who achieved reductions of ≥ 30%, ≥ 40%, ≥ 50%, ≥ 60%, and ≥ 70% in DED symptom-related interference across visual-tasking ADLs, the visual-tasking composite score, and WP was conducted using the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test, stratified by study. The relationship between symptom improvement and functional outcomes was explored independent of treatment assignment by correlating CFB in SANDE scores with CFB in ADL questionnaire scores on days 14, 28, and 84/90 using Pearson correlation analysis. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05, unadjusted for multiplicity, and should be interpreted as descriptive rather than confirmatory.

Results

Patient Disposition, Demographics, and Baseline Characteristics

The integrated dataset included 1179 patients drawn from the phase 2b COMET-1 study and the phase 3 pivotal COMET-2 and COMET-3 studies. Of these, 584 patients had been randomized to receive ACO and 595 to receive VEH (Supplementary Fig. S1).

The baseline demographic characteristics were well balanced across the ACO and VEH treatment groups (Table 1). The majority of patients were female (74.6% [n/N = 880/1179]), and the mean (SD) age was approximately 61.5 years. At baseline, measures of DED symptoms were similar in both treatment groups: mean (SD) global SANDE score was 73.77 (11.83) in the ACO group and 73.52 (12.37) in the VEH group, consistent with moderate-to-severe DED symptoms. Efficacy

At baseline, ADL-VAS responses related to visual tasking reflected a generally moderate burden of DED symptoms (Supplementary Table S1). The range of mean (SD) values for visual-tasking ADLs for the ACO and VEH groups was 42.9 (27.8) mm (ACO group; item: ability to drive) to 59.1 (25.1) mm (VEH group; item: ability to read books, magazines, and/or newspapers); the baseline values of WP were mean (SD) 46.1 (26.8) mm and 47.7 (27.2) mm for the ACO and VEH groups, respectively. The mean (SD) baseline values for the item “My dry eye symptoms have made me feel depressed” were considerably lower at 27.5 (27.3) mm in the ACO group and 29.8 (28.2) mm in the VEH group, suggesting patients in this study did not have a substantive baseline emotional burden attributable to DED.

Across all visual-tasking ADL-VAS and WP-VAS response measures, ACO was associated with greater LS mean reductions in CFB scores compared with VEH for all timepoints (Fig. 1; Supplementary Table S1). For four of the five visual tasking-related measures (i.e., driving, reading food labels, use of electronic devices, and television viewing), the visual-tasking composite score, and WP, the LS mean CFB differences were significantly greater with ACO compared with VEH on day 14 (all P ≤ 0.0211). Improvements in the five visual tasking-related measures, the visual-tasking composite score, and WP were all significantly greater in the ACO group compared with the VEH group on day 28 (all P ≤ 0.0232). These effects remained in favor of ACO at day 84/90, with scores for reading food labels, use of electronic devices, the visual-tasking composite score, and WP (all P ≤ 0.0378) continuing to show significant improvements. Given the breadth of data, two outcomes that are among those most frequently impaired by DED symptoms (i.e., use of electronic devices and WP [9, 10]) as well as the visual-tasking composite score were selected for additional focus (Fig. 1). Treatment with ACO compared with VEH led to significantly greater reductions in DED symptom interference with electronic device use as early as day 14 (LS mean CFB, − 9.4 vs − 5.0; P = 0.0016), with continued significant reductions on day 28 (LS mean CFB, − 12.2 vs − 6.9; P = 0.0002) and day 84/90 (LS mean CFB, − 17.6 vs − 14.2; P = 0.0378). Similarly, a significantly greater LS mean CFB in favor of ACO compared with VEH was observed for the visual-tasking composite score (− 8.9 vs − 5.2; P = 0.0023) on day 14 and was sustained through day 28 (− 11.2 vs − 7.0; P = 0.0012) and day 84/90 (− 16.9 vs − 13.8; P = 0.0307). For WP, ACO was again associated with significantly greater LS mean reductions in DED symptom interference versus VEH, evident as early as day 14 (− 7.7 vs − 3.2; P = 0.0104) and sustained through day 28 (− 9.6 vs − 4.2; P = 0.0028) and day 84/90 (− 16.0 vs − 11.6; P = 0.0229).

In the responder analyses, greater proportions of patients treated with ACO versus VEH achieved a ≥ 30% reduction in DED symptom interference on four of five visual-tasking measures (i.e., driving, reading food labels, use of electronic devices, and television viewing), the visual-tasking composite score, and WP as early as day 14 and on all measures on day 28 (Fig. 2; Supplementary Table S2). Similar to CFB data, while some variability was noted in the degree of separation from VEH, numerical treatment differences in favor of ACO continued across all responder thresholds (≥ 30% to ≥ 70%) on days 28 and 84/90 for all items. Safety

ACO was well tolerated, with the vast majority of treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) classified as mild in severity (Table 3). Instillation-site pain (burning or stinging) was reported by 293 of 584 (50.2%) patients in the ACO group and 19 of 595 (3.2%) patients in the VEH group, with the majority occurring in the ACO group graded as mild in severity (97.6% [n/N = 286/293]). Two severe TEAEs were reported in the ACO group: one case of corneal vital dye staining and one case of instillation-site pain. No severe TEAEs were reported in the VEH group. AEs led to treatment discontinuation in 10 of 584 (1.7%) ACO-treated patients and 10 of 595 (1.7%) VEH-treated patients. Four ACO-treated patients discontinued because of instillation-site burning or stinging sensation. Overall, discontinuation rates were similar between the ACO (7.5% [n/N = 44/584]) and VEH (7.9% [n/N = 47/595]) groups. Other than AEs, the other most common reason for discontinuation was consent withdrawal (ACO, 1.7% [n/N = 10/584]; and VEH, 1.8% [n/N = 11/595]). Discussion

Patients with DED experience an array of symptoms that can lead to substantial impairment of QoL, including the reduced ability to undertake common everyday visual-tasking activities (e.g., looking at screens or reading for extended periods), which may also impair WP [6, 27, 28]. The impact of moderate or severe DED on QoL has been reported to be comparable to the impact of other disabling conditions such as angina or hip fracture, and even mild DED has a negative influence on QoL [29].

In each COMET study, as well as in the integrated population evaluated in this analysis, ACO led to consistent reductions in DED symptoms based on SANDE, eye dryness-VAS, and ODS-VAS scores as early as day 7 (COMET-2 and COMET-3) or day 14 (COMET-1) of treatment [23, 24]. As such, this post hoc integrated analysis was conducted to explore the potential treatment benefit of ACO on critical elements of a patient’s QoL, specifically visual-tasking ADLs and WP.

Treatment with ACO compared with VEH led to progressive and clinically meaningful improvements in visual-tasking ADLs, the visual-tasking composite score, and WP. Detected as early as day 14, improvements in these measures were robust and consistent, with significant treatment differences in all scores favoring ACO by day 28 and significant or numerical differences maintained through 3 months. The study population had low scores on the VAS for depression attributed to DED symptoms, suggesting that patients did not have a substantial emotional burden associated with DED during the study. However, the interpretation of “feeling depressed” may vary significantly between patients, and the VAS question and site staff did not guide patients on how to reference their emotional state. Thus, while minimal changes in feelings of depression were observed over time in our analysis, additional, more detailed studies are suggested to gain specific insights into this important domain.

With the wide range of data, our analysis focused on ADLs that generally represent the most common and burdensome complaints for patients, including electronic device use; general visual tasking as represented through a composite of five representative activities; and WP [2, 9–13]. In all cases, significantly greater LS mean CFB reductions with ACO compared with VEH were shown across these three outcomes as early as day 14, with effects sustained through day 84/90. For WP, we observed that approximately one-third of the integrated analysis population chose “not applicable” for the WP-VAS item, likely reflecting the mean age of the population (approx. 61.5 years) and potential retirement from employment. Notably, however, the number of respondents remained robust for this item (n ≥ 321 patients/arm at each timepoint), and the magnitude of change and observed treatment differences at each timepoint for WP were comparable to those observed for use of electronic devices and the visual-tasking composite score.

As there are no validated metrics that we are aware of to quantify the clinical meaningfulness of an absolute change in a VAS score from a DED symptom measure, responder analyses were performed to provide context. To address this, we evaluated thresholds between ≥ 30% and ≥ 70% and found that notable and increasing proportions of patients achieved not only ≥ 30% reductions but also ≥ 40% to ≥ 70% reductions in DED interference across use of electronic devices, the visual-tasking composite score, and WP from day 14 through day 84/90. The greatest treatment differences were observed on day 28; while less separation from vehicle was observed on day 84/90, this finding may reflect the highly subjective nature of these questions. Overall, results of the responder analysis highlight the substantial reductions in DED symptom interference on everyday activities, including deeper levels of reductions across three outcomes, that patients may realize as early as 1 month after treatment initiation. Importantly, the rapid and profound improvements in ADLs and WP may potentially facilitate increased long-term treatment compliance.

While many factors may affect ADLs, the functional improvements observed here align with the symptomatic reductions reported in the COMET studies [23, 24]. Correlation analyses conducted in this work pooled all available data from both treatment arms of COMET-1, COMET-2, and COMET-3. The rationale for this approach was to examine the relationship between symptom improvement and reductions in ADL burden, independent of treatment assignment. This treatment-agnostic approach was intended to provide broad insight into the association between symptom relief and functional outcomes, reflecting real-world settings. Using this approach, we observed moderate correlations between changes in global SANDE score and changes in ADL-VAS and WP-VAS scores at all timepoints for electronic device use; reading books, magazines and/or newspapers; watching television; and for the visual-tasking composite score and WP-VAS scores. The correlation coefficients strengthened over time for the remaining ADL-VAS items, such that most on day 28 and all on day 84/90 were classified as moderate [26]. Together, these observations suggest that greater symptom improvement was associated with meaningful QoL gains through reductions in the day-to-day impact of DED on patients’ lives.

While there are multiple prescription products available for treatment of signs and symptoms of DED, effectiveness on reducing the burden of DED on ADLs and WP has not been well characterized in the peer-reviewed literature. Several recent studies of varying scope and rigor evaluating various DED treatment strategies have reported improvements in reading speed, various visual tasking ADLs, or performing manual work or favorite activities [30–33].

While exploratory in nature, the findings from more than 1100 patients with moderate-to-severe DED pooled from three VEH-controlled, randomized multicenter studies demonstrate the positive benefits ACO may provide to a patient’s QoL. The significant improvements observed in this analysis, including in electronic device use, overall visual tasking, and WP, are noteworthy given how important functional vision is to maintain a modern lifestyle and support optimal individual performance and QoL.

This study is not without limitations. It is a post hoc analysis because the three COMET studies were not designed to statistically test the impact of ACO on ADLs and WP; thus, data should be considered exploratory and interpreted descriptively. Because data were limited to 3 months, it remains unclear whether continued benefit would be observed with extended treatment. Additionally, the 7-item questionnaire was designed to align with VAS administered during the study, but its brevity and focus limited capture of details of ADLs and WP, preventing additional analyses. We acknowledge that numerous variables can affect WP (i.e., age, employment status, work schedule, screen use, environment) and depression (i.e., multifactorial nature, medications). These variables were not controlled for and may have increased response heterogeneity, limiting generalizability in these domains. Thus, visual-tasking ADL and WP findings should be considered hypothesis-generating as functional outcomes and interpreted with caution. While the ADL questionnaire used in this study was designed to specifically and directly assess the impact of DED symptoms on functional domains frequently cited by patients and relevant to payers concerned with real-world burden, it has not been formally validated. Validated questionnaires measuring visual function and QoL changes in patients with DED such as the National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire-25 [34], Ocular Surface Disease Index [35], or Standard Patient Evaluation of Eye Dryness Questionnaire [36] could be explored in future studies to build on the insights gained through this work.

Conclusions

Adding to the established efficacy profile of ACO, post hoc integrated analyses of data collected in the three COMET studies demonstrate rapid, consistent, and clinically meaningful improvements on ADLs associated with visual tasking and WP. These findings provide unique insight and further support for ACO as an effective treatment option for the signs and symptoms of DED.

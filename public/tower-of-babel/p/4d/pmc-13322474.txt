The Effect of Bearing Surface On the Survival of Cementless and Hybrid Total Hip Arthroplasty: An Analysis of Linked National Joint Registry and Hospital Episode Statistics Data
Authors: Edward T. Davis, Maria Raikou, Alistair J. McGuire, Joseph Pagkalos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Crosslinked polyethylene-based bearings have led to marked improvements in total hip arthroplasty (THA) survival. Antioxidant highly crosslinked polyethylene bearings are the latest addition to the bearing options. Previous reports were limited by low numbers and short follow-up in this category therefore limiting comparisons.Methods:We combined the UK National Joint Registry data set with polyethylene manufacturing characteristics to classify polyethylene as crosslinked (XLPE) or conventional polyethylene. The Hospital Episode Statistics data set was used to link ipsilateral surgical procedures for the treatment of periprosthetic hip fractures. The effect of bearing surface on THA survival was analyzed through estimating cause-specific cumulative incidence functions to allow for competing risks. This was followed by regression analysis that fitted a Fine-Gray proportional subdistribution hazards model for reoperation.Results:A total of 696,013 THAs and 17,468 reoperations were included in this analysis. Average follow-up time was estimated to be between 4.83 and 9.64 years depending on the bearing combination while the maximum follow-up was 19.1 years across all bearings. The estimated cause-specific cumulative incidence functions allowing for competing risks revealed that Ceramicised Metal on XLPE (CMoXLPE) and Ceramic on Antioxidant XLPE (CoAoXLPE) had the lowest probability of reoperation by the end of the follow-up period. The regression analysis (all ages) with Metal on Polythelene (MoP) as the reference bearing adjusted for age, sex, and stem fixation showed CMoXLPE to be associated with the lowest risk of revision for any reason (HR = 0.64, 95% CI = 0.58-0.72) followed by CoAoXLPE XLPE (HR 0.65, 95% CI = 0.57-0.74).Conclusion:This analysis, which includes fixations as well as revisions in defining reoperation for periprosthetic fracture, covers a longer follow-up period compared with earlier reports. Regression analysis in a model including all ages, all bearings, with MoP as the reference revealed the lowest risk of revision for any reason in THAs using CMoXLPE and CoAoXLPE.Level of Evidence:Therapeutic Level III. Introduction

The introduction of highly crosslinked polyethylene (polyethylene as crosslinked [XLPE]) in total hip arthroplasty (THA) has markedly improved the rate of aseptic loosening at midterm follow-up1. Long-term reports from radiostereometric analysis studies, randomized controlled trials, and registry data have been very encouraging2-4. In the 2024 Australian Orthopaedic Association National Joint Replacement Registry (AOANJRR) report, reason for revision varied with time. Analysis of revisions performed at a follow-up of 11 years or more post implantation revealed that periprosthetic fracture and aseptic loosening were the primary indications for revision5.

Since the introduction of XLPE, further modifications of polyethylene for THA have been introduced. Antioxidant stabilized crosslinked polyethylene (antioxidant highly crosslinked polyethylene [AoXLPE]) was introduced with a view to improving wear and fatigue resistance6. A previous analysis of polyethylene manufacturing characteristics by our group was limited by the small number of AoXLPE bearing THAs7. Ceramic on Ceramic (CoC) use has declined in recent years but remains a common bearing choice for some surgeons5.

This study aims to provide a more comprehensive capture of patients undergoing reoperation after THA. Linkage with the Hospital Episode Statistics (HES) data set allows capture of patients undergoing fixation procedures where the implant was not revised8. Those events were not captured by previous registry studies using the National Joint Registry (NJR) data set9. In addition, previous reports on AoXLPE bearings were limited by short follow-up and low numbers. This report includes a larger sample size of AoXLPE THAs with longer follow-up making this a distinct bearing category in the analysis.

Materials and Methods

Primary THAs from the NJR data set were combined with polyethylene manufacturing characteristics as previously reported to define the various bearing combinations9. Polyethylene irradiated with ≥5 MRad during the manufacturing process was classified as XLPE, in keeping with other registry reports5,10. The data set was restricted to THAs using cementless acetabular components with either cementless or cemented stems (fully cementless and hybrid). Patients with bilateral THAs were included, and each side was analyzed separately. A total of 696,013 primary THAs implanted using a cementless acetabular component between 2 April 2003 and 31 December 2020 were identified. The most frequent indication for implantation was osteoarthritis, and this was recorded in 92.32% of the cases.

HES Linkage

The HES data set includes coding data for all procedures undertaken during an inpatient hospital stay if this was funded by the National Health Service. The Office of Population Censuses and Surveys codes are used to record procedures undertaken during the inpatient episode. These were used to identify ipsilateral internal fixation procedures in the proximal femur recorded after the primary THA implantation date. A detailed description of the process is included as supplementary material with this article.

Statistical Analysis

The effect of bearing surface on THA survival was analyzed through estimating cumulative incidence functions to allow for competing risks. The method uses a weighting procedure where individuals with a competing event remain at risk, with a weight that depends on the censoring distribution for the study population as a whole11.

Analysis of reoperation for all reasons, aseptic loosening, and periprosthetic fracture were undertaken by bearing combination. Death was treated as a competing risk. Revision for a different indication was also treated as a competing risk during indication-specific analyses.

This analytical approach is in keeping with our previous analysis7. Regression analysis based on fitting a semiparametric proportional hazards model for subdistribution allowing for competing risks was used to obtain the subhazard ratios for the different bearing combinations adjusting for age, sex, stem fixation, head size (included in the analysis of the contemporary bearings), and a yearly cohort effect11,12. The latter was used to control for potential underreporting of revision outcomes in the early years of the NJR data set. More specifically, the variables included in the model were age (<55 years, 55-<65 years, 65-<75 years and ≥75 years), gender, bearing combination (Ceramicised Metal on XLPE [CMoXLPE], Ceramic on Antioxidant XLPE [CoAoXLPE], CoC, Ceramic on XLPE [CoXLPE], Metal on Anioxidant XLPE [MoAoXLPE] and Metal on XLPE [MoXLPE]), stem fixation (cemented, cementless) and head size (≤28 mm, 32 mm, ≥36 mm), and the yearly cohort effect.

Analyses were performed using STATA software, version 18. The research was approved by the NJR research committee.

Multivariate analysis of the contemporary bearings also included head size. This was restricted to contemporary bearings alone as large head sizes (≥36 mm) were infrequently used in THAs with conventional polyethylene. This analysis was undertaken for the endpoint of reoperation for all reasons, revision for aseptic loosening, and reoperation for periprosthetic fracture (Supplementary Tables 2-4).

Reoperation For Any Reason

Based on the contemporary bearings analysis, all ages, with MoXLPE as the reference bearing, the lowest risk of revision was in CMoXLPE (HR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.69-0.86) followed by CoAoXLPE (HR 0.78, 95% CI = 0.68-0.88), CoXLPE (HR = 0.86, 95% CI = 0.82-0.91), CoC (HR 0.95, 95% CI = 0.90-1.0), and MoAoXLPE (HR 1.02, 95% CI = 0.90-1.15). Age <55, male sex, cementless stem fixation, and head size ≤28 mm were associated with a higher risk of revision (Supplementary Table 2).

Revision For Aseptic Loosening

Based on the contemporary bearings analysis, all ages, with MoXLPE as the reference bearing, the lowest risk of revision for aseptic loosening was in CMoXLPE (HR = 0.41, 95% CI = 0.29-0.58) followed by CoAoXLPE (HR 0.54, 95% CI = 0.38-0.76), CoXLPE (HR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.67-0.88), MoAoXLPE (HR 0.85, 95% CI = 0.63-1.16), and CoC (HR 1.01, 95% CI = 0.90-1.14). Age <55, male sex, cementless stem fixation, and head size ≥36 mm were associated with a higher risk of revision (Supplementary Table 3).

Reoperation For Periprosthetic Fracture

Based on the contemporary bearings analysis, all ages, with MoXLPE as the reference bearing, the lowest risk of reoperation for periprosthetic fracture was associated with CoAoXLPE (HR 0.81, 95% CI = 0.63-1.05), CoC (HR 0.85, 95% CI = 0.77-0.95), CoXLPE (HR = 0.86, 95% CI = 0.77-0.95), MoAoXLPE (HR 0.94, 95% CI = 0.76-1.17), and CMoXLPE (HR = 1.00, 95% CI = 0.82-1.23). Age ≥75 years, head size ≥36 mm, and cemented stem fixation were associated with a higher risk of reoperation for periprosthetic fracture (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to combine polyethylene manufacturing characteristics, the NJR data set, and reoperation data from the HES data set to provide mid to long-term analysis of modern bearing THA survival. The capture of patients undergoing fixation procedures and the inclusion of AoXLPE bearings significantly expands on the existing registry literature on THA bearings.

A recent analysis of the NJR data set focused on bearing combinations in both cemented and cementless acetabular component THAs13. The authors concluded that the lowest risk of revision was in THAs with ceramic on highly crosslinked XLPE and oxidized zirconium (ceramicised metal) on XLPE bearings. The best performing bearings are in keeping with the results of our analysis which was limited to THA using a cementless acetabular component (hybrid and cementless). In addition, our analysis used a competing risks analytical approach. The inclusion of reoperations for fracture captured by the HES data set achieved a more comprehensive capture of reoperation events.

Our analysis is in keeping with the 2023 report of the AOANJRR that reported cumulative percent revision per bearing surface. The lowest risk of revision at 15 years was 4.9% (95% CI: 4.5-5.3) in THAs with ceramicised metal/XLPE. This was followed by CoXLPE (5.5%, 95% CI: 5.2-5.8), MoXLPE (6.1%, 95% CI: 5.9-6.3), and CoC (6.5%, 95% CI: 6.5-7.0)5. The AOAJRRR urge caution when interpreting the CMoXLPE results as this is a single company product. This may have a confounding effect “making it unclear if the lower rate of revision is an effect of the bearing surface or reflects the limited combinations of femoral and acetabular prostheses”. We performed additional adjustments for confounding factors to further minimize bias. However, the same limitations continue to apply to our data set and analysis.

Our previous analysis of XLPE modifications showed promising results in vitamin E stabilized polyethylene but was limited by shorter follow-up in this group7. The results of our current analysis are in keeping with the 2023 American Joint Replacement Registry report which identified no significant difference in survival between XLPE and AoXLPE THAs10. Antioxidant polyethylene in this current analysis is restricted by a small number of products and manufacturers. The most frequently used AoXLPE liner product in our data set consisted of a vitamin E infused product which is no longer commercially available. The AoXLPE-based bearing results should be interpreted with caution as these are associated with a smaller number of femoral stem and cup combinations.

Recent reports using registry and institutional database data have reported an association of some cemented polished tapered stem THAs with an increased risk of periprosthetic fracture14–16. In our observational study, regression analysis of reoperation for periprosthetic fracture revealed that cementless stem fixation was associated with a lower risk of reoperation for periprosthetic fracture (HR 0.90, 95% CI: 0.83-0.97). Analysis for reoperation for aseptic loosening showed the reverse association. Cementless stem fixation was associated with a higher risk of revision for aseptic loosening (HR 2.16, 95% CI: 1.93-2.42). Aseptic loosening is known to have improved with the use of XLPE in THA but remains a known failure mechanism for some cementless stems4,17.

Femoral head size is often considered in association with bearing wear, taper corrosion, and the risk of dislocation18. In our analysis, all cause revision was lowest in THAs with 32-mm heads when compared with the reference size of ≥36 mm, HR 0.92 (95% CI: 0.89-0.97). Reoperation for periprosthetic fracture risk was lower in THAs with 32 mm (HR 0.90, 95% CI: 0.83-0.97) and 28-mm heads (HR 0.84, 95% CI: 0.75-0.94) when compared with the reference head size of ≥36 mm. This is keeping with the previous work by Lamb et al. who reported an increased risk of periprosthetic fracture with increasing head size, analyzed as a continuous variable (HR 1.04 per millimeter)16. Although this is plausible, with large head sizes leading to increasing torque on the stem, there is potential for unaccounted confounding as large heads may be used in frail patients to mitigate instability.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. Registry data are observational in nature and statistical modelling cannot account for all confounding factors. The periprosthetic fracture risk is associated with patient factors such as bone density and frailty. In addition, many surgeons in the UK use both cemented and cementless stems and are more likely to use cemented implants for frail patients at higher risk of fracture19. Therefore, the observed association of stem fixation and periprosthetic fractures has to be interpreted with caution as it is dependent on the clinical threshold to use cemented implants. Misclassification during coding is possible and could lead to inaccurate capture of fixation episodes on the HES data set. In addition, despite linkage with the HES data set, capture of reoperations that were not recorded on the NJR data set is incomplete as HES data only capture NHS-funded procedures in England. Nonoperatively treated fractures could not be captured. Further characteristics of the THA construct that were not included in the analytical approach of this study may confound the bearing associations observed. These include stem design and ceramic stratification. Patient activity levels may have an association with revision for aseptic loosening and this is not possible to account for using registry data. As is the case with CMoXLPE, AoXLPE-based bearings are restricted to specific manufacturers and therefore limited stem/cup combinations. This should be taken into consideration when interpreting these results.

In this analysis of the NJR and HES data sets, CMoXLPE and CoAoXLPE were associated with the lowest risk of reoperation for any reason and the lowest risk of revision for aseptic loosening. Linkage of the HES dataset improved capture of the patients undergoing reoperation for fracture. Small absolute risk differences seen in observational registry data should be weighed against other factors including cost, availability, and surgeon familiarity. Further analysis is required to further clarify the association between femoral stem design, fixation, and the risk of periprosthetic fracture and aseptic loosening.

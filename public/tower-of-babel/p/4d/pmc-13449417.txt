α‑Fluoronitroalkenes as Fluorinated Building Blocks: Solvent-Controlled Functionalization with Hydrazones
Authors: Matthew A. Saucier, Hamdan Alrefaei, Peggy A. McCluggage, Mohammed K. Abd El-Gaber, Tzu-Yu Huang, Sampad Jana, Sudeshna Roy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
While α-fluoronitroalkenes have been widely explored in annulation and cycloaddition chemistry, controlled intermolecular C–N bond formation through conjugate addition remains largely undeveloped. Moreover, solvent-controlled divergence between conjugate addition and cyclization from identical starting materials has not been systematically demonstrated for α-fluoronitroalkenes. Herein, we report a base-mediated 1,4-conjugate addition of hydrazones to α-fluoronitroalkenes under aprotic conditions, affording N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones. In contrast, protic acidic conditions divert reactivity toward 4-fluoropyrazole formation, highlighting a solvent-controlled divergence in reaction pathways. This one-pot protocol tolerates a wide range of aromatic, heteroaromatic, and aliphatic substrates, providing isolable N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones in moderate to high yields. This study expands the synthetic utility of both hydrazones and α-fluoronitroalkenes as building blocks of interest for applications in organic synthesis and medicinal chemistry.

The demand for fluorinated molecules has increased across pharmaceutical, agrochemical, and materials research. , As interest expands from simple aromatic fluorination to more diverse fluorinated chemotypes, including aliphatic and heterocyclic motifs, , there is a growing need for modular intermediates that enable predictable fluorine incorporation. In this context, fluorinated synthons that offer new reactivity modes are valuable tools for modern organic synthesis.

Among these, α-fluoronitroalkenes have emerged as versatile electrophilic building blocks capable of delivering fluorinated scaffolds that are otherwise challenging to obtain, such as regioselective cycloaddition manifolds and functionalized aliphatic motifs. Despite this potential, the broader functionalization chemistry of α-fluoronitroalkenes remains comparatively underexplored compared to the cycloaddition reactions. Recent studies, including our own contributions, have demonstrated their utility in cyclization chemistry. − In particular, reactions of α-fluoronitroalkenes with hydrazones under acidic, protic conditions have been reported to furnish 4-fluoropyrazoles (Scheme ). However, in contrast to cyclization pathways, the conjugate addition reactivity of α-fluoronitroalkenes with N-nucleophile equivalents such as hydrazones remains underexplored.

Reactivity of Hydrazones with α-Fluoronitroalkenes

To address this gap, we investigated the reactivity of α-fluoronitroalkenes with hydrazones and identified a mild base-mediated 1,4-conjugate addition that proceeds efficiently under aprotic conditions. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first example of base-mediated conjugate addition of hydrazones to α-fluoronitroalkenes. Notably, we found that solvent choice governs a divergence between conjugate addition and cyclization pathways, enabling selective access to either N-fluoroalkyl-substituted hydrazones or pyrazoles from the same coupling partners. Moreover, we report the formation of 3-alkenyl/alkylpyrazole derivatives, which have not been described in previous reports.

Given the limited understanding of hydrazone reactivity with α-fluoronitroalkenes beyond cyclization pathways, and prior evidence for solvent effects in cycloaddition chemistry, we hypothesized that solvent choice could influence hydrazone reactivity with α-fluoronitroalkenes. −

To evaluate this hypothesis, benzaldehyde (1a), methylhydrazine (2a), and (2-fluoro-2-nitrovinyl)­benzene (4a) were selected as model substrates to explore the reactivity trends and optimize the reaction conditions. We envisioned this transformation as a one-pot process, wherein the corresponding aldehydes 1 and hydrazines 2 undergo in situ formation of hydrazones 3, which subsequently react with α-fluoronitroalkenes 4 to afford the desired adducts. , The key challenge in developing this transformation lay in identifying a solvent system that not only promotes the complete conversion of the aldehyde to the corresponding hydrazone 3 but also selectively drives the reaction toward the 1,4-conjugate addition pathway or the competing 1,3-cycloaddition. Such solvent control is crucial, as it directly influences both the reactivity of the in situ-formed hydrazone intermediate and that of α-fluoronitroalkenes.

Reaction progress was monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) during the Schiff base condensation step and by 19F NMR spectroscopy for the subsequent 1,4-conjugate addition/cycloaddition product formation. In the 19F NMR spectra, the relative conversions to the desired 1,4-addition product 5a, the [3 + 2]-cycloaddition product 6a, and the unreacted α-fluoronitroalkene starting material 4a were quantified. The two diastereomers of 5a were identified by characteristic doublets of doublets at δ −152.1 (J = 49.2, 17.7 Hz) and δ −154.6 (J = 49.7, 14.5 Hz). The cyclized pyrazole product 6a appeared as a singlet at δ −174.8, while the unreacted α-fluoronitroalkene 4a was detected as a doublet at δ −111.9 (J = 26.4 Hz). No attempt was made to optimize the diastereoselectivity.

Solvents such as 1,2-dichloroethane (DCE) and diethyl ether (Et2O) resulted in incomplete aldehyde consumption, as indicated by TLC, and displayed poor reactivity in the subsequent addition step with α-fluoronitroalkenes, as evidenced by 19F NMR analysis (Table , entries 1 and 2). In contrast, polar protic solvents such as methanol (MeOH) and ethanol (EtOH) promoted complete conversion of aldehydes to the corresponding hydrazones within 3–5 h, as confirmed by TLC. However, under these conditions, the reaction predominantly yielded the [3 + 2] cycloaddition pyrazole product, indicating that protic media favor the cyclization pathway (Table , entries 3 and 4). Notably, when the reaction was performed in EtOH under acetic acid, the pyrazole product was obtained exclusively in 97% yield (Table , entry 5).

Conducting the reaction in THF without any additives led to small amounts of both the 1,4-conjugate addition product 5a (10%) and the pyrazole cycloadduct 6a (6%) (Table , entry 6), with nearly 80% of the starting material 4a unreacted. Incorporating a mild base (1.1 equiv. K2CO3) together with a slight excess of hydrazone (1.2 equiv) markedly improved the efficiency, affording 87% of 5a and leaving 13% of unreacted 4a (Table , entry 7). Further increasing the hydrazone to 2.0 equiv ensured complete substrate conversion and delivered 93% of 5a (in 37:63 diastereomeric ratio by 19F NMR), with no detectable formation of 6a (Table , entry 8). In conclusion, we developed a synthetic protocol for the synthesis of N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones under mild basic conditions in THF. Notably, mechanistic studies of reaction outcomes under acidic conditions (AcOH) in THF also afforded product 5a in a 90% yield (Scheme ). This demonstrates that the reaction can proceed efficiently under either mild acidic or basic catalysis under aprotic solvent conditions.

Comparison of the reactivities of aliphatic and aromatic hydrazones revealed that benzaldehyde-derived substrates (5a–5d) generally afforded lower yields than their aliphatic counterparts (5g–5i). Within the aromatic series, electron-donating substituents, such as p-methoxy (5b) and 1,3-benzodioxole (5d), delivered moderate yields (36% and 61%, respectively), whereas electron-withdrawing substituents, such as m-cyano (5c), substantially diminished the reactivity, providing the corresponding product in only 14% yield.

The methodology was also compatible with heteroaromatic substrates, including indole-derived aldehydes, furnishing products 5e and 5f in 68% and 89% yields, respectively. Notably, for these indole-based systems, the nature of the N substituent on the hydrazine had a pronounced effect on the diastereoselectivity. Methylhydrazine afforded 5e in 68% yield with a 24:76 dr, whereas phenylhydrazine delivered 5f in higher yield (89%) with markedly improved and reversed diastereoselectivity (95:5 dr). This trend is consistent with the increased steric bias imparted by the bulkier phenyl substituent.

Beyond aromatic systems, the reaction exhibited broad tolerance toward aliphatic and α,β-unsaturated N-methylhydrazones, including pentanyl, cyclohexyl, cyclohexenyl, and myrtenyl derivatives, which furnished the corresponding N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones 5g–5j in 40–80% yield.

To demonstrate the applicability of the synthetic protocol, we conducted a larger-scale reaction (1.0 mmol) using a cyclohexyl-substituted hydrazone, affording the corresponding 1,4-addition N-fluoroalkyl hydrazone 5k in 86% yield with 33:67 dr (Scheme ).

Overall, the scope highlights the generality and robustness of the method across aromatic and aliphatic hydrazones, with electron-donating aryl substrates outperforming electron-withdrawing analogs, and the examined aliphatic hydrazones generally affording the desired products in high yields, while the N-phenyl-substituted example displayed opposite diastereoselectivity compared to the corresponding N-methyl analogue.

Next, we investigated the stability of N-fluoroalkyl hydrazone 5a under various conditions (Table ). We noticed that 5a is stable in CDCl3 solution and in the solid state for over 2 months, as confirmed by periodic 1H and 19F NMR analyses. To further assess its chemical stability, we examined its behavior under acidic and basic conditions (Table ). The compound was found to be stable under neutral and acidic conditions in polar protic solvents, such as ethanol (EtOH). Likewise, treatment with a strong base (t-BuOK) in THF resulted in no detectable degradation. In contrast, when basic conditions were applied in a protic solvent (t-BuOK) in EtOH, 5a underwent complete cyclization to the corresponding pyrazole derivative 6a, as confirmed by 19F NMR spectroscopy. This behavior is consistent with the facilitating role of protic solvents, which can potentially stabilize the transition state and facilitate the cyclization pathway (Scheme ). Notably, the N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones accessed in this study, as exemplified in Scheme , are bench stable and structurally distinct from previously reported hydrazone products, representing an interesting class of fluorinated architectures.

Having established the conjugate addition scope, we next evaluated the cycloaddition scope (Scheme ). α-Fluoronitroalkenes were previously reported in a study to undergo 1,3-dipolar cycloadditions to generate 4-fluoropyrazoles under refluxing conditions in trifluoroethanol (TFE) with trifluoroacetic acid (TFA). The examples reported therein featured exclusively aromatic substituents at the 3- and 5-positions of the pyrazole core, with no aliphatic substrates represented. Recognizing this as an opportunity, we sought to broaden the synthetic utility of α-fluoronitroalkenes in 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition chemistry by accessing substituted 4-fluoropyrazoles bearing aliphatic substituents under our previously optimized conditions (Table , entry 5).

We employed hydrazones derived from aliphatic and α,β-unsaturated aldehydes, including myrtenal, retinal, and cyclohexenyl- and cyclohexyl-based derivatives. These substrates successfully underwent 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition with α-fluoronitroalkene 4, affording the corresponding 4-fluoropyrazoles 6b–6e in 29–80% yield, highlighting the method’s broad tolerance toward aliphatic and unsaturated N-methylhydrazones.

We conducted benchtop experiments to validate the competing pathways of 1,4-conjugate addition versus 1,3-cycloaddition (Scheme ). In THF, the addition of 1 equiv of AcOH strongly favored conjugate addition, leading to 90% conversion to 5a. In contrast, treatment with 1 equiv of K2CO3 in EtOH produced a mixture of products, including cyclized pyrazole 6a (31%), pyrazolidine intermediate 7a (25%), and the 1,4-conjugate addition product 5a (23%), as determined by 19F NMR analysis.

Based on experimental observations and relevant literature precedents, a plausible mechanistic pathway is outlined in Scheme . Under aprotic conditions, hydrazone 3 engages in a 1,4-conjugate addition to the β-position of α-fluoronitroalkene 4a through nucleophilic attack by the N­(sp3) center, affording intermediate INT-I. Subsequent proton transfer delivers the conjugate addition product 5a. This pathway likely involves proton transfer steps facilitated by either acidic (THF/AcOH) or basic (THF/K2CO3) additives.

Proposed Mechanism for 1,4-Conjugate Addition and 1,3-Cycloaddition

In contrast, under protic and acidic (EtOH and AcOH) conditions, the reaction preferentially follows a cyclization pathway, leading to the formation of the pyrazolidine intermediate 7, which subsequently undergoes oxidation and elimination of HNO2 to furnish the 4-fluoropyrazole product 6. Time-resolved mechanistic 19F NMR studies (see the Supporting Information) support rapid formation of pyrazolidine 7 as the primary intermediate under these conditions, with subsequent slower aromatization to pyrazole 6. The aromatization step was significantly accelerated in the presence of oxygen, supporting the involvement of oxidative processes in the conversion of 7 to 6. In contrast, little to no accumulation of N-fluoroalkyl hydrazone 5 was observed under fully protic conditions. The aromatization step is presumed to occur via elimination of HNO2 and oxidation mediated by adventitious oxygen or nitro-assisted redox processes, as reported in the related nitroalkene cyclization literature. This divergence highlights the critical role of solvent and proton availability in governing the reaction outcome.

Additional solvent-dependent behavior was observed during stability studies of N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones 5. Cyclization to pyrazolidine intermediate 7 occurs only when a strong base (t-BuOK) and protic solvent (EtOH) are simultaneously present, suggesting that hydrogen-bonding interactions and enhanced proton shuttling may facilitate deprotonation and intramolecular nucleophilic attack. Although solvent participation in the transition state is proposed (INT-II), direct experimental evidence is currently lacking. Subsequent HNO2 elimination and oxidation led to the formation of pyrazole 6.

In summary, hydrazones undergo base-mediated 1,4-conjugate addition to α-fluoronitroalkenes under mild aprotic conditions, affording bench-stable N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones across aromatic, heteroaromatic, and aliphatic substrates in a one-pot process. Reaction outcome is dictated by solvent environment, with aprotic media favoring conjugate addition and protic solvents promoting cyclization to pyrazoles. This work provides a solvent-controlled platform for accessing N-fluoroalkyl hydrazones and pyrazoles from a common set of precursors and establishes α-fluoronitroalkenes as practical fluorinated building blocks for controlled C–N bond formation.

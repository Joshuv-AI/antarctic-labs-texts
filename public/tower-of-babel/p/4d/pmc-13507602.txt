Clinical Images: Skeletal fluorosis presenting with diffuse periostitis and polyarticular synovitis
Authors: Mitali Sen
Published: 2026

The patient, a 33‐year‐old White male with hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and mild hepatomegaly, presented with abrupt‐onset, disabling polyarticular pain, swelling, and stiffness (hands, wrists, elbows, shoulders, hips, knees, and ankles); progressive loss of mobility; and wheelchair dependence. Examination showed marked polyarticular synovitis and palpable periarticular bony nodularity at the wrists and shins. Radiographs demonstrated bilaterally symmetrical, diffuse, solid periosteal reaction in small and large tubular bones without effusions or erosions. Lumbar spine dual x‐ray absorptiometry Z score was +10.2, far exceeding biologic expectations and likely spuriously elevated by generalized osteosclerosis and degenerative/calcific change in fluorosis rather than true trabecular hypertrophy. Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (CT) showed diffusely increased skeletal radiotracer uptake concordant with periosteal proliferation and no malignancy. Chest CT and CT enterography excluded pulmonary or gastrointestinal drivers of hypertrophic osteoarthropathy. Laboratory testing showed an elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate/C‐reactive protein level, low vitamin D level, elevated parathyroid hormone level, and elevated bone‐specific alkaline phosphatase level; rheumatoid factor, cyclic citrullinated peptide, antinuclear antibody, extractable nuclear antigen, and HLA‐B27 test results were negative. His serum fluoride level was 1.9 mg/L (reference <0.13 mg/L), supporting fluoride excess as the unifying etiology, although cause of toxicity is unknown at this time. Fluoride toxicity integrates the clinicoradiologic picture: fluoride incorporates into hydroxyapatite (fluorapatite), stimulates osteoblasts, and drives osteosclerosis and periosteal apposition; later, disordered mineralization contributes to elevated alkaline phosphatase and secondary hyperparathyroidism. 1 Radiographic hallmarks include generalized osteosclerosis, diffuse periostitis, entheso/ligamentous ossification, and sacroiliac changes without erosions. 1 Typical exposures include high fluoride groundwater, heavy tea consumption, industrial/inhalational sources (eg, aluminum smelting), and fluorinated medications (voriconazole or difluoroethane inhalant abuse). 2 , 3 Diagnosis rests on an elevated serum/urinary fluoride level with compatible imaging and exclusion of mimics. 1 This case underscores skeletal fluorosis as a treatable mimic of inflammatory arthropathy; management centers on identifying and removing the fluoride source, vitamin D repletion, and symptomatic control. 1

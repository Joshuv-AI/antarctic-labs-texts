Research on intelligent assembly method of aero-engine deep-cavity nuts based on torque-angle control
Authors: Zhenyu Liu, Xiaodong Huang, Jianrong Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The assembly quality of high-torque nuts in the deep cavity (95mm-diameter, 800mm-deep) of aero-engine low-pressure rotors directly determines engine operational reliability. Introduction

As the core power unit of aircraft, the connection reliability of the aero-engine rotor system directly determines the operational safety and service life of the entire engine1,2. Threaded connections, serving as key force-transmitting components in the rotor system, play a crucial role in torque transmission, coaxiality assurance, and sealing performance maintenance. According to reliability investigation data in the aerospace field, a significant proportion of engine mechanical failures are associated with defects in fasteners and threaded connections3. Among these, high-torque nuts in deep-cavity structures are particularly prone to assembly-related issues due to their complex assembly environment and extreme service conditions, making them a critical focus for reliability improvement.

From the perspective of service conditions, threaded connections in aero-engines endure combined loads including thermal, centrifugal, and vibrational effects. Under full-load operation, the turbine region temperature can reach 700–\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$800^\circ \textrm{C}$$\end{document}, which significantly degrades the elastic modulus and yield strength of Ni-based superalloys such as Inconel 718 (Chinese designation: GH4169)4. Simultaneously, centrifugal forces generated by rotor rotation induce radial expansion of the shaft, which attenuates axial preload through the Poisson effect5. Repeated start-stop cycles further introduce thermal shock and vibrational loads, accelerating stress relaxation in threaded connections and posing long-term reliability risks. Extensive research has been devoted to modeling and improving the behavior of threaded connections6. Fractal theory provides an effective means to describe microscopic surface contact; the W-M (Weierstrass-Mandelbrot) function proposed by Mandelbrot has been widely applied to characterize thread surface topography and establish more accurate contact models7.

The effects of material properties and loading conditions also play a prominent role: simulation and experimental studies have shown that elevated temperatures promote plastic deformation of surface asperities and modify contact stiffness8,9. In terms of connection stiffness design, the guidelines of VDI 2230 and subsequent finite-element-based optimizations have significantly improved the prediction accuracy of joint stiffness10,11. Preload control techniques for threaded connections have evolved sequentially from torque-only control to torque-angle composite control and yield-point control. Conventional torque control methods exhibit high sensitivity to variations in the friction coefficient, typically resulting in 20%–30% uncertainty in preload12.

Torque-angle control improves accuracy by incorporating rotational angle feedback but faces practical limitations in deep-cavity environments where angle sensing is constrained by spatial restrictions. Yield-point control can further reduce preload error but requires high-precision measurement systems that are not always compatible with the mechanical properties of Ni-based superalloys13.

From an engineering application perspective, the challenge of deep-cavity nut tightening has driven the development of specialized assembly technologies, including hydraulic torsional tightening systems, bolt elongation monitoring devices, and anti-vibration fastening mechanisms14,15. However, adapting these technologies to cavities with depths of several hundred millimeters remains problematic, as preload accuracy, visual positioning, and assembly efficiency continue to be unresolved technical bottlenecks16. In summary, previous studies have significantly advanced the understanding of threaded joint mechanics, preload relaxation mechanisms, and deep-cavity assembly technologies17. Nevertheless, four major research gaps can be identified:Limited quantification of the coupled thermo-mechanical effects (temperature, centrifugal force, and vibration) on preload evolution;Insufficient incorporation of micro-scale surface topography and friction coefficient variability in predictive models;Lack of adaptive, high-precision preload control strategies applicable to deep cavities in aero-engine rotors;Absence of identification methods for the angular relationship between nut anti-loosening grooves and turbine shaft grooves, as well as high-precision alignment cooperative measurement methods under the constraint of large tightening torque range parameters.To address these gaps, this study establishes a multi-physics coupling model for aero-engine rotor threaded connections, integrating high-temperature material behavior, centrifugal effects, and surface contact mechanisms (Fig. 1). Furthermore, an advanced control strategy for deep-cavity nut tightening is proposed to improve preload accuracy and assembly efficiency. Mechanical modeling and multiphysics analysis of threaded connections

The mechanical properties of threaded connections are crucial for determining the assembly stability and service reliability of aero-engine low-pressure rotors. In the complex service environment of aero-engines, threaded connections are subjected to combined loads such as thermal, centrifugal, and vibrational forces, which can easily lead to preload attenuation, contact performance degradation, and other issues, thereby threatening the operational safety of the engine19 . Starting from the microscopic contact behavior of threaded surfaces, this study constructs a fractal contact model to quantify the influence of surface topography on preload. Based on the stiffness series principle, a macroscopic connection stiffness model is established to clarify the influence law of key process parameters. Combined with the actual operating conditions of the engine, thermal-mechanical-centrifugal multi-physics coupling analysis is conducted, providing theoretical support for tightening process optimization and visualization device design.

Working process

The nut tightening shaft can access the rotor deep cavity for high-torque nut tightening, with its workflow integrating mechanical positioning, servo control, and visual feedback. First, the tightening tool connects to the pneumatic boom arm via a quick-change flange for auxiliary lifting and posture adjustment. Meanwhile, the rear anti-rotation tooling is fixed to form reliable torque reaction support, ensuring effective torque transmission. Front-end guiding tooling is also installed to prevent collisions between the tooling and rotor components during deep cavity entry.

After pre-guidance, the workflow moves to angular calibration: the nut is loaded, the tooling advances to the target position, and the angle measuring shaft connects to the turbine shaft. Initial angular values are collected by the inclinometer, then the measuring shaft retracts and locks. Next, the servo motor activates to drive the reducer for tightening, with the torque sensor feeding real-time data to the industrial PC for closed-loop control.

A key torque-angle switching step follows: when torque reaches 1900 N m, the visual module captures locking groove images to calculate angular deviation. The system then switches to angle control mode, compensating for the deviation (0–\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4.29^\circ$$\end{document}) to align the groove, while ensuring torque \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\le 2100$$\end{document} N m. Qualified data is recorded for traceability. Finally, the tooling withdraws from the deep cavity, completing the nut tightening process, after which the lock washer and axial retaining ring are assembled sequentially.

To achieve high-precision coordinated control of the deep-cavity visual tightening system, a hierarchical control architecture integrating Industrial PC (IPC), Programmable Logic Controller (PLC), and Servo Drive is established. Software module design

The core functional modules of the software system include torque-angle dual closed-loop control, visual feature matching, preload prediction, and Manufacturing Execution System (MES) integration. These modules collaborate to achieve precise control of the assembly process and full-process data traceability. Developed in the Visual Studio 2019 environment using C#, the system adopts the Model-View-Controller (MVC) design pattern. By decoupling the data layer, interface layer, and control logic, the maintainability and scalability of the system are significantly improved. The design of each core module is detailed as follows.

The torque-angle dual closed-loop control module adopts a PID algorithm with feedforward compensation to realize smooth switching between torque control and angle control modes. In the torque control phase, the PID parameters are set as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_p = 0.8$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_i = 0.05$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_d = 0.1$$\end{document}. The feedforward term is calculated based on the friction coefficient (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu = 0.25$$\end{document}) and thread parameters, which can suppress the torque overshoot within 5%. When the torque reaches the threshold of 1900 N\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\cdot$$\end{document}m, the system automatically switches to the angle control mode. The PID parameters are adaptively adjusted to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_p = 1.2$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_i = 0.02$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_d = 0.3$$\end{document} through the fuzzy control algorithm, effectively improving the positioning accuracy.

Based on the OpenCV 4.5 computer vision library, the visual feature matching module achieves high-precision recognition of the locking groove. The entire image processing process takes no more than 0.5 s, meeting the real-time requirements of assembly. The processing flow consists of three parts: in the preprocessing stage, Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) is used to enhance the contrast of deep-cavity images, and Gaussian filtering (kernel size 5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}5) is applied to eliminate noise; in the feature extraction stage, the Canny edge detection algorithm (threshold 50/150) is adopted to extract the edge contour of the locking groove, and Hough line transform is used to fit the central axis of the groove; in the deviation calculation stage, camera calibration (error \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\le$$\end{document} 0.02\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^\circ$$\end{document}) is performed to convert the pixel coordinate system to the mechanical angle system, and the least square method is used to calculate the angular deviation between the nut and the turbine shaft locking groove.

The preload prediction and compensation module realizes indirect prediction of preload based on torque, angle, and temperature parameters by establishing a Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) model. Taking the tightening torque (T), rotation angle (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta$$\end{document}), and ambient temperature (t) as input variables, and the preload (F) as the output variable, the model selects the squared exponential function as the kernel function, and optimizes the hyperparameters through the maximum likelihood estimation method. The prediction error of the model is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\le$$\end{document} 3%, which provides data support for the preload redundancy compensation strategy. When the predicted preload is lower than the design threshold of 200 kN, the system automatically increases the tightening angle according to the stiffness model (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5.06 \times 10^5$$\end{document} N/mm) to ensure the preload meets the assembly requirements.

Complying with the OPC UA communication standard, the MES integration and data traceability module realizes efficient data interaction with the enterprise MES system. The uploaded information includes assembly timestamp, part serial number, torque-angle curve, preload value, and operator information. The system automatically generates a PDF quality report for each assembly task, which contains complete process data and qualification judgment results, and supports quick retrieval through the part serial number, meeting the full-process traceability requirements specified in the aerospace industry standard AS9100D.

Three core indicators were selected to comprehensively evaluate the performance of the visual tightening system. The selection is based on aero-engine assembly requirements, covering preload error, assembly efficiency, and collision rate. This system ensures the comprehensiveness and scientificity of the performance assessment.

Result analysis

The visual tightening system’s excellent performance stems from three key factors: (1) the fractal contact model and multi-physics coupling analysis provide an accurate theoretical basis for preload compensation, avoiding the empiricism of traditional adjustment methods; (2) integration of visual monitoring and servo control enables closed-loop control of positioning-tightening-alignment, solving the poor visibility and low positioning accuracy issues in deep cavities; (3) selection of high-precision components (servo motors, torque sensors) ensures torque and angle control accuracy.

Key factors affecting preload error include friction coefficient variability and torque sensor temperature drift. Friction coefficient can be controlled within 0.2–0.3 via lubrication process optimization, while temperature compensation algorithms reduce temperature drift error to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$<0.5\%$$\end{document}, further improving system stability.

Discussion

Technical advantages

Compared with the existing deep-cavity nut assembly technologies, the innovation of this study lies in the organic combination of theoretical modeling and engineering application: (1) the fractal contact model quantifies the influence of micro-topography on preload for the first time, and the prediction error of real contact area is less than 5%, which provides a new idea for the microscopic mechanism analysis of threaded connection preload error; (2) the multi-physics coupling simulation clarifies the superposition attenuation law of preload under thermal-force-centrifugal coupling, and the compensation strategy based on this reduces the preload attenuation rate from 24 to 2%; (3) the integrated design of visual positioning and torque-angle control solves the technical bottleneck of high-torque and high-precision assembly in deep cavities, and the angular alignment accuracy reaches \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm 0.18^\circ$$\end{document}.

In terms of engineering application, the system has strong compatibility and can be adapted to the assembly of low-pressure rotors of multiple types of aero-engines by replacing the quick-change interface. The data acquisition system can realize the whole process traceability of assembly parameters (torque, angle, preload, time), which meets the quality management requirements of the aerospace industry30. The collision rate is reduced to 0%, which effectively reduces the maintenance cost caused by part damage.

Limitations and improvement directions

The study still has certain limitations in terms of model adaptability, simulation comprehensiveness, and technical efficiency, as detailed below: in terms of model adaptability, the fractal contact model is established based on milled surfaces. Although n tests demonstrate its applicability to ground (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R_a=0.8\ \upmu \text {m}$$\end{document}) and turned (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R_a=3.2\ \upmu \text {m}$$\end{document}) surfaces by adjusting fractal parameters (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D=2.5/2.1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma =1.6/1.4$$\end{document}), a slight increase in prediction error (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\le 7\%$$\end{document}) is observed. Regarding simulation comprehensiveness, the multi-physics coupling simulation fails to account for the creep deformation of GH4169 under long-term high-temperature service. Thus, the preload evolution law under long-term cyclic service conditions requires further supplementation by integrating fatigue damage and creep models31. Regarding technical efficiency, the visual positioning speed is constrained by the traditional Canny edge detection and Hough line transform algorithm, resulting in a positioning time of approximately \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5\ \text {s}$$\end{document}.

Future improvement directions: (1) establish a multi-process surface fractal database, and realize adaptive adjustment of model parameters according to the surface processing method; (2) introduce fatigue damage theory to improve the multi-physics coupling model, and predict the service life of threaded connections; (3) adopt deep learning algorithms (such as YOLOv5) to optimize the visual feature matching speed, and further improve assembly efficiency.

Industrial application prospects

The visual tightening system developed in this study has been successfully applied to the assembly line of a certain type of aero-engine, with a cumulative assembly volume of over 300 engines (realizing the expansion of test volume to actual production scale). The assembly pass rate has been increased from 82% (246 qualified among 300 engines with traditional methods) to 99.5% (298 qualified among 300 engines with the new system). The economic benefits are significant: for each engine, the assembly time is reduced by 1.4 h, and the part loss cost is reduced by about 200,000 CNY. Based on the annual output of 100 engines, the annual economic benefit is about 20 million CNY. With the popularization of the system, it is expected to be applied to the assembly of deep-cavity fasteners in other fields such as gas turbines and large generators, and has broad industrial application prospects27.

Conclusions

This study addresses the core technical challenges of high-torque nut assembly in aero-engine deep cavities, covering mechanical modeling, system design, and experimental verification. A W-M function-based fractal contact model is established, which reveals that the real contact area of threaded surfaces (Ra=1.6 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\upmu$$\end{document}m) only accounts for 12–18% of the nominal area; with a preload fluctuation prediction error of ±18%, this model significantly outperforms the traditional smooth surface model (±35%) and provides a micro-theoretical basis for preload control. Meanwhile, a series stiffness model of threaded connections is constructed, yielding an overall connection stiffness of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5.06\times 10^5$$\end{document} N/mm (error < 1%); the study clarifies that a friction coefficient of 0.2–0.3 and milling surfaces can effectively enhance connection stiffness, and proposes optimal process parameters accordingly. Multi-physics coupling simulations further quantify the preload attenuation law, showing 18% attenuation at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$800^\circ \textrm{C}$$\end{document}, 7% reduction at 2000 rpm, and 24% superimposed attenuation under coupled working conditions, and a targeted angle compensation and preload redundancy strategy is put forward to control the preload error within ±8%. Additionally, a visual tightening system integrating servo drive and micro-vision is developed, which achieves ±0.18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^\circ$$\end{document} angular positioning in deep cavities; experimental verification confirms that this system reduces the single assembly time by 35% and lowers the collision rate to 0%, fully meeting the high-reliability assembly requirements of aero-engines. Overall, this work provides important theoretical support and technical means for improving the assembly quality and efficiency of aero-engine deep-cavity nuts, and holds significant engineering value for promoting the intelligent development of aero-engine assembly technology.

Comparison of the Serial Humoral Immune Response according to the Immunosuppressive Treatment after SARS-CoV-2 mRNA Vaccination
Authors: Hiroya Menjo, Midori Hasegawa, Hidetsugu Fujigaki, Takuma Ishihara, Shun Minatoguchi, Shigehisa Koide, Hiroki Hayashi, Midori Saito, Kazuo Takahashi, Hiroyasu Ito, Yukio Yuzawa, Kuniaki Saito, Naotake Tsuboi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe objective of this study was to estimate the humoral immune response evaluated by immunoglobulin G (IgG) against the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) spike receptor-binding domain (RBD-IgG) following the third mRNA coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) vaccination in patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment.MethodsThe primary outcome was RBD-IgG levels after the third SARS-CoV-2 vaccination. The primary comparison was the RBD-IgG levels between patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment (n=124) and those who did not (n=33).ResultsThe RBD-IgG levels were significantly lower in the patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. The RBD-IgG levels were lower in patients treated with glucocorticoid monotherapy than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. Even in patients who received ≤5 mg prednisolone, the RBD-IgG levels were significantly lower. Nine of the 10 patients who received rituximab within one year before the first vaccination did not experience seroconversion after the third vaccination. Meanwhile, all nine patients who received rituximab only after the second vaccination experienced seroconversion, even if B cell recovery was insufficient. Patients treated with mycophenolate mofetil plus glucocorticoid plus belimumab had significantly lower RBD-IgG levels than those treated with mycophenolate mofetil plus glucocorticoid.ConclusionThe RBD-IgG levels were lower in patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. Low-dose glucocorticoid monotherapy affected the humoral immune response following the third mRNA COVID-19 vaccination.Keywords: COVID-19, glucocorticoid, humoral immune response, immunosuppressive treatment, kidney disease, vaccination

Introduction

Immunosuppressive treatment increases susceptibility to infections. There have been many reports of reduced immunity against severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) after mRNA vaccination in patients with kidney transplantation (1-3) and in those on dialysis (4). Immunosuppressive treatment of rheumatic diseases is associated with reduced immunity to mRNA vaccines (5,6) and poor outcomes (7). Although there are several reports on seroconversion against SARS-CoV-2 vaccines in patients with kidney disease receiving immunosuppressive treatment (8,9), few studies have compared the degree of serial humoral immune responses among different treatment regimens. The objective of this study was to estimate the humoral immune response, as measured by the immunoglobulin G (IgG) levels, against the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain (RBD-IgG) following the third mRNA coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) vaccination in patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment.

Materials and Methods

Study design and population

This prospective observational study included patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment and attended the outpatient clinic of Fujita Health University Hospital, Department of Nephrology, between December 2021 and March 2022. Patients with kidney disease who did not receive immunosuppressive therapy were included in the control group. Kidney disease was confirmed by kidney biopsy, kidney dysfunction [estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <60 mL/min/1.73 m2], or urinary findings. Moreover, the participants either completed or planned to receive a third BNT162b2 or mRNA-1273 vaccination. In total, 240 patients were enrolled in this study. Antibodies against the nucleocapsid protein (N-antibody) were measured to screen for patients with the SARS-CoV-2 infection for all samples [Elecsys Anti-SARS-CoV2(200)RUOⓇ; (Roche Diagnostics)]. The following patients were excluded: patients who did not receive the third vaccination (n=27), had experienced a COVID-19 infection (n=22), had only one blood sample after the third vaccination (n=9), had undergone kidney transplantation (n=5), had received immunosuppressive treatment for the comorbidity (n=9), had received antitumor drugs (n=2), had participated in another clinical trial (n=3), had poor adherence (n=2), had an unknown vaccination date (n=2), underwent non-standard treatment (n=1), and withdrawal of consent (n=1). The analysis was performed using the data from the remaining 157 patients (Fig. 1). Clinical information was obtained from the medical records. Blood samples were collected at least 14 days after the third dose of SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccine was administered as the first sample. Sampling was repeated on the day of the outpatient visit closest to the third and sixth month after the third vaccination. Humoral immunogenicity was measured as the IgG level against RBD-IgG, using an AccuraseedⓇ COVID-19 antibody (FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical). An RBD-IgG level >2 U/mL was considered positive.

Study flow chart.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujita Health University School of Medicine (HM21-342). Informed consent was obtained from all the participants. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

The primary outcome was the RBD-IgG levels after the third SARS-CoV-2 vaccination. The primary comparison was the RBD-IgG levels between patients with kidney disease who did and did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. The secondary comparison was the RBD-IgG level between patients with kidney disease who received glucocorticoid monotherapy [≤5 or ≥6 mg/day of prednisolone (PSL)] and those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. For individual regimen evaluation, the RBD-IgG levels were analyzed in the following regimens: rituximab administration within 1 year before the first vaccination, first rituximab administration after the second vaccination, glucocorticoid plus mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), glucocorticoid plus MMF plus belimumab, and other immunosuppressive regimens.

Statistical analyses

The patient characteristics were summarized using medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) for continuous variables, and counts and percentages for categorical variables. The association between the number of days since the third SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination and RBD-IgG levels was compared between patients who did and did not receive immunosuppressive treatment using a nonlinear regression model. Covariates in the nonlinear regression model included the presence or absence of immunosuppressive treatments, number of days since the third SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination and their interaction, age, vaccine type, eGFR, hypertension, and diabetes mellitus. An analysis of variance was conducted on the nonlinear term and found p<0.001, thus indicating a significant result for nonlinearity. A restricted cubic spline curve was used to represent the nonlinear relationship between the number of days since the third SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination and the RBD-IgG levels. As multiple data points were included for each patient, the Huber-White robust sandwich estimator was used to estimate the variance of the regression coefficients. The seven groups were divided for immunosuppressive treatment, and a nonlinear regression model was used to evaluate the association with RBD-IgG over time. Seven groups (PSL ≤5 mg/day, PSL ≥6 mg/day, rituximab administration within 1 year before the first vaccination, rituximab administration after the second vaccination, glucocorticoid plus MMF, glucocorticoid plus MMF plus belimumab, and other immunosuppressive regimens) were administered according to the immunosuppressive treatment. The other covariates were the same as those used in the model described above. In the Figure 2～4, the log-transformed values are back-transformed to the original scale. Differences were considered statistically significant at a two-sided p value of <0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using the R statistical software version 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

The RBD-IgG levels were significantly lower in patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment (p<0.001) (Fig. 2). The RBD-IgG level in a patient who received immunosuppressive treatment was 0.137-fold that in a patient who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. According to a multivariable analysis, RBD-IgG levels were significantly associated with immunosuppressive treatment, the number of days since vaccination, and age (Table 2). However, the interaction between the number of days since vaccination and immunosuppressive treatment was not statistically significant (p=0.615), thus indicating that the slope over time did not differ according to immunosuppressive treatment.

Rituximab was administered 24 months before the first vaccination to 15 patients. In nine patients, rituximab was administered within 6 months before the first vaccination, and none of the patients experienced seroconversion. In these patients, the proportion of CD19-positive B cells was 0% in seven patients and 0.1% in two patients. The patient who received rituximab 312 days before the first vaccination exhibited an RBD-IgG level of 15 U/mL 21 days after the third vaccination. One patient who received rituximab between 13 and 18 months before the first vaccination and after the second vaccination did not experience seroconversion. Two of the three patients who received rituximab between 13 and 18 months before the first vaccination but not after the second vaccination experienced seroconversion; RBD-IgG levels were 107 U/mL at 37 days and 157 U/mL at 35 days after the third vaccination, respectively. All nine patients who received the first rituximab dose after the second vaccination experienced seroconversion. The proportion of CD19-positive B cells was 0% in five patients, 0.1% in two, and 0.2% in two. The median RBD-IgG level was 47 (IQR 43-114) U/mL at a median of 28 (IQR 24,35) days after the third vaccination (Table 4).

Discussion

In this cohort, the RBD-IgG levels were lower in patients who received immunosuppressive treatment than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. These results show that immunosuppressive treatment affects post-vaccination humoral immunity in patients with reduced eGFR, similar to those with rheumatic diseases. Sex (10,11), age (10,11), hypertension (10,12), diabetes (13), and vaccine type (14) are reportedly associated with the post-vaccination antibody titers. In the present cohort, comprising patients with a median eGFR of 47.6 (31.0, 72.4) mL/min/1.73 m2, age, but not sex, hypertension, and diabetes mellitus, was associated with post-vaccination humoral immunity. Regarding the vaccine type, the RBD-IgG levels were significantly higher following vaccination with mRNA-1273 than after BNT162b2 vaccination, after adjusting for different immunosuppressive treatments, age, sex, eGFR, hypertension, and diabetes mellitus. There have been no reports on the relationship between eGFR and antibody titer; however, eGFR was not significantly associated with antibody titer in the present cohort.

Generally, the maintenance dose of glucocorticoids for kidney disease that requires immunosuppressive treatment is ≤5 mg/day (prednisolone). In the present cohort, the RBD-IgG levels were lower in patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment with ≤5 mg/day prednisolone than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. Of the 46 patients and 8 patients who received ≤5 mg/day and 6-9 mg/day of prednisolone, respectively, 1 patient in each group did not experience seroconversion. Glucocorticoids affect human dendritic cell differentiation and maturation, resulting in decreased peptide presentation, reduced stimulation of T cells, and a decreased antigen-specific antibody production by B cells (15). The B-cell response is also directly inhibited by glucocorticoids (16). With prolonged glucocorticoid administration, the number of circulating B-lymphocytes may be reduced (17). Chronic glucocorticoid therapy can become hypogammaglobulinemia (18). Fischer et al. reported that prednisolone >10 mg daily was associated with impaired humoral immunity in a longitudinal study (19). An increased risk of infection with ≤5 mg prednisolone has also been reported (20). The risk associated with 5 mg of prednisolone taken for the last 3 years is similar to that associated with 30 mg taken in the last month (21). Longer periods of glucocorticoid administration have been suggested to be associated with immunocompetence. In the present cohort, the median duration of glucocorticoid administration among patients who received ≤5 mg was 10.0 (IQR 4.3, 19.0, max 36.0) years, and that of patients who received ≥6 mg was 6.5 (IQR 2.8, 17.0, max 33.0) years. A longer duration of glucocorticoid administration may be one of the reasons for the lower humoral immune response observed in the present cohort.

Rituximab is indicated for the treatment of relapsing/steroid-dependent minimal change disease (22) and ANCA-associated vasculitis (23). Patients who receive rituximab have been reported to have poor seroconversion rates after SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination (3). Rituximab depletes peripheral B cells for six months (24). CD19-positive B-cell counts and the time between the last rituximab infusion were independently associated with seropositivity (3,13,14). In the present study, all nine patients who received rituximab within 6 months before the first vaccination did not experience seroconversion, whereas all nine patients who received the first rituximab after the second vaccination experienced seroconversion, despite an insufficient B cell recovery. We infer that the following mechanism played a role in this result: in patients who had not received vaccination prior to rituximab administration, memory B cells for the SARS-CoV-2 antigen did not exist, and depletion of B cells following rituximab infusion decreased humoral immune responses to primary antigens, including SARS-CoV-2. Therefore, the nine patients who had been administered rituximab within 6 months of the first vaccination experienced seroconversion. Meanwhile, the patients who had undergone two vaccinations had formed memory B cells for the SARS-CoV-2 antigen. Memory B cells are central to long-term immunity, preserve epitope memory, and give rise to new antibody-secreting cells upon repeated antigen exposure (25). Therefore, all nine patients who received the first rituximab only after the second vaccination experienced seroconversion.

Belimumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds to the circulating B-cell activating factor. The results of previous studies on the humoral immune response to SARDS-CoV-2 vaccination in belimumab-treated patients are inconsistent. The seropositivity rate 30 days after the third Sinovac-CoronaVac dose was 78.6% (22/28) for anti-SARS-CoV-2 S1/S2 IgG and 53.6% (15/28) for N-antibody in a phase 4 prospective longitudinal study in Brazil (26). In a French single-center study, belimumab did not affect the vaccine response evaluated by anti-SARS CoV-2 RBD-IgG after two doses of the BNT162b2 vaccine in 15 patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (27). However, concomitant medication was not mentioned in this study. In a New York University Lupus Cohort study, 4 of 10 patients who received belimumab had a lower SARS-CoV-2 spike RBD antibody response (28). A comparison of the antibody levels between the glucocorticoid plus MMF and glucocorticoid plus MMF plus belimumab regimens has not yet been conducted. Our results suggest that the addition of belimumab to the glucocorticoid plus MMF regimen may further reduce the humoral immune response.

None of the patients initiated belimumab after the second vaccination. In four patients who did not experience seroconversion, the timing of the first vaccination and belimumab initiation varied, and the association between initiation and antibody response was unclear in the current cohort.

This study is associated with some limitations. This was a single-center study with a small sample size. Larger multicenter studies are required to verify our results.

In conclusion, the RBD-IgG levels were lower in patients with kidney disease who received immunosuppressive treatment than in those who did not receive immunosuppressive treatment. Low-dose glucocorticoid monotherapy can affect the humoral immune response.

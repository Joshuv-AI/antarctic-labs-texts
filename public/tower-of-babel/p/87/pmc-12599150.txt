Complete chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of Iris confusa (Iridaceae)
Authors: Ya-nan Li, Jing Yan, Yan-xia Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIris confusa Sealy is highly valued for its ornamental and medicinal applications. Here, we report the complete chloroplast genome sequence of I. confusa obtained using Illumina pair-end sequencing. The complete circular genome of I. confusa is 152,470 bp in length and contains a large single-copy region (83,251 bp), a small single-copy region (18,503 bp), and two short inverted repeat regions (25,358 bp). The genome sequence encodes 133 genes including 87 protein-coding genes, 8 ribosomal RNA genes, and 38 transfer RNA genes. This study will facilitate the understanding of chloroplast genome features of genus Iris and contribute to further studies on phylogenetic analyses.Keywords: Iris confusa, chloroplast genome, illumina sequencing, phylogenetic relationship

Introduction

The Iris genus is one of the largest genera in the Iridaceae family, comprising approximately 300 species worldwide. Classifying the Iris genus has always been difficult due to its wide variety of species, significant morphological variation, and natural hybridization. The composition and taxonomic status of the species within this genus remain controversial. Iris confusa Sealy 1937 is a perennial herb, which grows in moist forests, valleys, slopes, and mountain grasslands. It is mainly distributed in the Guangxi, Sichuan, and Yunnan provinces of China (Zhao et al. 2000). The flower stem of I. confusa is 20–30 cm in height, with raceme branches, and light blue or white flowers with a diameter of 5-5.5 cm, making it highly valuable in landscapes and ornaments (Figure 1). Its thick rhizomes can be used to treat acute tonsillitis and bronchitis (Chen et al. 2018). In terms of morphology, the nutrients organs of I. confusa are very similar to I. wattii, which is easily confused (Zhao et al. 2000).

Molecular data, including genome sequences, offer a more reliable theoretical basis for resolving controversial classification problems than morphological features, as they are less prone to distortion by environmental factors. Therefore, it is necessary to use sequencing methods to identify the species and study the phylogeny of I. confusa at the molecular level. The chloroplast is a semi-autonomous organelle for photosynthesis in green plants and plays a vital role in maintaining plant life. All genetic information residing within it comprises the chloroplast genome. (Jansen and Ruhlman 2012). The chloroplast genome of angiosperms is mostly matrilineal and is conserved with few recombination events and intraspecific variation, making it suitable for phylogenetic studies at different taxonomic levels (Li et al. 2013; Fan and Guo 2022). With the development of high-throughput sequencing technology, the cost of obtaining a complete chloroplast genome has been greatly reduced, and an increasing number of chloroplast genomes of Iris species have been reported (Wilson 2014; Lee et al. 2017; Ai et al. 2019; Kang et al. 2020).

In the current study, the complete chloroplast genome sequence of I. confusa was obtained based on Illumina pair-end sequencing, and its sequence characteristics, genome structure, and phylogenetic relationships with related species were clarified. This study facilitates our understanding of the chloroplast genome features of genus Iris and contributes to further studies on the species identification and phylogenetic analyses.

Materials and methods

Healthy leaves were collected from a single individual of I. confusa grown in the garden of Xi’an Medical University (Xi’an, Shaanxi, China; 34°12′ N, 108°58′ E), and voucher herbarium specimen was identified by Ya-nan Li (Lyn890120@163.com) and deposited at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Medical University under the voucher number FAH0320240068. Total genomic DNA was extracted from the silica-dried leaves using an improved CTAB method (Doyle 1987). High-throughput sequencing was performed using the Illumina HiSeq platform (Biomarker Technologies Inc. Beijing, China) to acquire raw reads. Quality trimming was performed using CLC Genomics Workbench v7.5 (CLC bio, Aarhus, Denmark). The generated raw reads were assembled into a complete chloroplast sequence using GetOrganelle v.1.7.7.0 with the embplant_pt option for de novo assembly (Jin et al. 2020). The Burrow-Wheeler Aligner (BWA) (Li and Durbin 2009) program was used to generate the coverage depth by aligning the sequencing data onto the chloroplast genome. To annotate the complete chloroplast genome, programs PGA (Qu et al. 2019) and CPGAVAS2 (Shi et al. 2019) were used by comparing the chloroplast genome with Iris dichotoma (NC_056172) as reference. The circular chloroplast genome map of I. confusa and cis/trans-splicing genes maps were drawn by CPGView software (Liu et al. 2023). A total of 25 chloroplast genome sequences were selected to infer the phylogenetic position of I. confusa in the Iridaceae family, with Crocus sativus as the outgroup. A maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using RAxML v7.2.8 (Stamatakis 2006) with 1000 replicates.

Results

The annotated chloroplast genome of I. confusa was 152,470 bp in length, with a mean coverage depth of 321.83× (Figure S1). It had a typical quadripartite structure with a large single-copy region (LSC, 83,251 bp), a small single-copy region (SSC, 18,503 bp) and two short inverted repeat regions (IRa and IRb, 25,358 bp) (Figure 2). A total of 133 genes were annotated, including 87 protein-coding genes, 8 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes, and 38 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes. Among them, 16 genes contained one intron, and 3 genes contained two introns. Most genes occurred in a single copy, but seven protein-coding genes, eight tRNA genes and four rRNA genes occurred in twice located in two IR regions. Thirteen cis-splicing genes and the trans-splicing gene rps12 were detected using CPGView (Figures S2 and S3). The GC content of the genome was 37.8%, whereas the corresponding values for the LSC, SSC, IR regions were 36.1%, 31.4% and 43.0%, respectively. The topology of the ML phylogenetic tree strongly supported I. confusa was closely related to I. wattii, which was similar in morphology, with 100% bootstrap value (Figure 3). The chloroplast genome sequence of I. confusa had been submitted to GenBank under the accession number PP916053.1.

Discussion and conclusion

The close phylogenetic relationships between I. confusa and I. wattii not only complements traditional taxonomic frameworks but also highlights critical insights into understanding hybridization events within the genus Iris. Their interactions may drive adaptive radiation or lead to the loss of genetic diversity. However, to validate these hypotheses, further integration of nuclear genomic data and multidisciplinary approaches including ecology and population genetics are essential to provide empirical evidence for biodiversity conservation and evolutionary theory. Additionally, I. confusa is endemic to southwestern China, inhabiting forest margins and humid slopes. Habitat fragmentation due to deforestation and agricultural expansion poses a critical threat. Thus, ex-situ conservation (like seed banking and cultivation in botanical gardens) and habitat protection of its native range are necessary to preserve genetic diversity of I. confusa.

Moreover, secondary metabolites found in Iris species, such as isoflavones, triterpenoids, and phenolic acids, exhibit anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial activities (Yoshizawa et al. 2025). Previous studies proved that I. confusa can be used to treat acute tonsillitis and bronchitis (Chen et al. 2018), suggesting potential applications for I. confusa. In conclusion, medicinal potential, coupling with threats from habitat loss and hybridization of I. confusa calls for more interdisciplinary research to inform its conservation and sustainable use. Therefore, resolving its phylogenetic relationships will refine classifications across Iridaceae, emphasizing the need to integrate genomic data, especially chloroplast genome data, in biodiversity studies.

In this study, the complete chloroplast genome of I. confusa is assembled and annotated. It is 152,470 bp in length and contains 133 genes. Phylogenetic relationships indicates that I. confusa is closely related to I. wattii among the species in Iridaceae family. This study provides basic information on the complete chloroplast genome features of I. confusa and paves the way for future studies on species identification, chloroplast genome evolution, and phylogenetic relationships related to I. confusa. Furthermore, it will make contribution to the formulation of potential conservation and management strategies for Iris species.

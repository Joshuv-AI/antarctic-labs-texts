Relationship of Carotid‐Femoral Pulse Wave Velocity and Brachial‐Ankle Pulse Wave Velocity With the Risk of New‐Onset Cardiovascular Events: A Cohort Study in the Beijing Community‐Based Population
Authors: Lan Gao, Xiangning Zhang, Tianhui Dong, Fangfang Fan, Jia Jia, Yang Yu, Yong Huo, Jianping Li, Yan Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCarotid‐femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) and brachial‐ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV) are widely used indicators of arterial stiffness, with cfPWV considered the gold standard and baPWV increasingly applied in Asian populations. In this community‐based cohort from Beijing, we compared the associations of cfPWV and baPWV with the risk of new‐onset cardiovascular events. A total of 5723 residents from the Shougang community with available cfPWV and baPWV measurements from the 2018 follow‐up survey were included. CfPWV was measured using Pulsepen and baPWV using the Omron BP‐203RPE III device. Cardiovascular events, defined as a composite outcome of cardiovascular death, first‐ever myocardial infarction, or stroke, were identified from national and municipal registries through December 31, 2021. Over a median follow‐up of 3.15 years, 173 cardiovascular events occurred. Both cfPWV and baPWV were associated with incident cardiovascular events in Kaplan‐Meier analyses. However, in multivariable Cox regression models adjusting for traditional cardiovascular risk factors, cfPWV showed a nonsignificant trend (p = 0.072), while baPWV remained significantly associated with new‐onset events (p < 0.001). These findings indicate that in this community‐based population, baPWV, but not cfPWV, demonstrates a robust association with cardiovascular risk, suggesting that baPWV may serve as a more practical and effective tool for routine cardiovascular risk assessment in Asian populations.

Introduction

Arterial stiffness is a well‐established risk factor of cardiovascular diseases [1]. Pulse wave velocity (PWV), defined as the velocity of blood pressure pulse wave propagate through the arteries, is generally considered a standard indicator of arterial stiffness [2, 3, 4]. The prediction value of PWV for cardiovascular events has been consistently demonstrated [5, 6, 7, 8]. CfPWV and baPWV are two different methods to measure PWV. CfPWV, which reflects aortic stiffness, is recognized as the gold standard for the noninvasive assessment of arterial stiffness and is acknowledged in clinical practice guidelines, particularly those issued in Europe [9, 10, 11, 12]. Several cohort studies have demonstrated the association between cfPWV and cardiovascular outcomes. In a large cohort study of the Framingham population, elevated cfPWV was significantly associated with higher risk first cardiovascular events (hazard ratio [HR], 1.48; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.16–1.91) [1]. Similar findings—linking cfPWV to cardiovascular risk and all‐cause mortality—have been reported in prospective cohort studies of patients undergoing hemodialysis and those with type 1 diabetes mellitus [13, 14]. Furthermore, Zhong et al. conducted a meta‐analysis confirming the positive relationship between cfPWV and total cardiovascular events and cardiovascular mortality [15]. However, contradictory evidence exists; Kozakova et al. reported no significant association between cfPWV and cardiovascular events in the cross‐sectional analysis [16].

On the other hand, baPWV reflects stiffness in both large and medium‐sized arteries [9, 17]. Due to its simplicity and ease of measurement, baPWV has been widely applied, particularly in East Asia, and incorporated into clinical guidelines [18]. Evidence from several cohort studies indicated that elevated baPWV predicts the development of cardiovascular diseases and mortality in the general population as well as patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus or hypertension [8, 19, 20, 21]. Nevertheless, findings are not entirely consistent. A prospective cohort study found that baPWV exhibited a saturation effect on the first stroke in hypertensive adults, where increased baPWV was positively associated with first stroke only among the population with a baPWV < 17.5 m/s [22]. Another cohort study in hypertensive adults suggested an increased risk of stroke in the highest baPWV quartile, yet this association was attenuated as baPWV was analyzed as a continuous variable [20]. These discrepancies implies that the association between baPWV and cardiovascular or cerebrovascular outcomes may differ across specific populations. To address this, in addition to exploring both cfPWV and baPWV in relation to adverse outcomes, our study further performs subgroup analyses to assess population‐specific effects.

Increasing evidence suggests that baPWV is not merely a surrogate for cfPWV. BaPWV covers a broader vascular territory, and some studies have indicated baPWV is more strongly associated with basic indicator of cardiovascular structure and function [17, 23, 24]. Several studies have directly compared the associations of cfPWV and baPWV with cardiovascular outcomes. Takana et al. reported in a cross‐sectional study that baPWV demonstrated associations with cardiovascular risk factors, stroke, and coronary artery disease comparable to those of cfPWV [9]. In our previous work, vascular age derived from baPWV was independently associated with cardiovascular risk in community‐based cohort, whereas no significant associations were observed for vascular age derived from cfPWV [25]. Moreover, a meta‐analysis by Ohkuma et al. confirmed a positive association between baPWV and incident of cardiovascular disease (CVD), although significant interactions were observed with diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and Framingham risk score group categories [26]. However, there is a paucity of large‐scale studies simultaneously directly comparing cfPWV and baPWV in relation to cardiovascular risk. Therefore, our present study was designed to investigate the relationship between cfPWV and baPWV with the new‐onset cardiovascular events in a Beijing community‐based cohort. In addition, we performed stratified analyses according to demographic characteristics, comorbidities and medication use to further clarify which index may be more suitable for cardiovascular risk assessment in large populations.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

Participants in this study were obtained from an atherosclerosis cohort survey conducted in the Gucheng and Pingguoyuan communities of the Shijingshan District in Beijing, China. The cohort was established from December 2011 to April 2012. The details were described in a previous study [27]. A total of 6568 participants were interviewed on‐site in 2018, and the data obtained served as the baseline for the present study. We excluded those with missing values of the key variables and those with stroke and/or myocardial infarction at baseline. Finally, 5723 subjects were enrolled in our analysis.

Ethics committee approval was obtained from Peking University First Hospital for all study procedures. We obtained written informed consent from all participants before data collection.

Data Collection

Data on demographic characteristics, lifestyle behaviors, comorbid conditions, and medication use were obtained using standardized paper questionnaires by trained investigator in the community health service center. Current smoking was defined as smoking ≥1 cigarette/day for ≥6 months, and current alcohol consumption was defined as drinking ≥1 time/week for ≥6 months. Hypertension was defined as a self‐reported history of hypertension and/or being on antihypertensive medications and/or elevated blood pressure (systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or a mean diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg) on the survey day. Diabetes was defined as a self‐reported history of diabetes and/or being treated with hypoglycemic drugs (oral hypoglycemic agents or insulin) and/or fasting blood glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L on the day of examination and/or oral glucose tolerance test ≥11.1 mmol/L 2 h after meals. Hyperlipidemia was defined as having self‐reported hyperlipidemia and/or being on lipid‐lowering therapy and/or having at least one abnormal lipid marker on the survey day, total cholesterol (TC) ≥5.18 mmol/L, triglyceride (TG) ≥1.70 mmol/L, low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‐C) ≥3.37 mmol/L, high density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‐C) < 1.04 mmol/L [27].

Physical Examination

Physical measurements included body mass index (BMI), calculated as weight (kg) divided by height squared (m2). Blood pressure was measured in the right brachial artery after 5 min of rest using a calibrated Omron HEM‐7130 sphygmomanometer (Omron Healthcare, Lake Forest, IL, USA) with an appropriately sized cuff. Three consecutive blood pressure readings, taken with at least 1‐minute intervals, were averaged to obtain systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure [27].

PWV Measurement

The cfPWV was measured using the PulsePen device (DiaTecne, Milan, Italy) by trained operators following standardized operating procedures with the gold‐standard tonometric method [28]. Patients were asked to rest in supine position for at least 5 min before the collection of pulse waveform of that strongest pulsation point from the carotid artery and femoral artery, respectively. Then researchers took measurement of the distance from the carotid artery to femoral artery, from the carotid artery to sternal angle, and from the sternal angle to femoral artery. The cfPWV used in analyses were calculated as the mean of at least twice measurement. A third measurement was taken if the first two measurements differed by >0.5m/s.

The baPWV measurement was taken using BP‐203 RPE III arteriosclerosis detection device (Omron Healthcare), also according to a standard operating procedure by trained technicians. Patients first need to take a rest in supine for at least 5 min before measurement. During the procedure, four cuffs with pressure sensors were tied to both arms and ankles. Then technicians attached electrocardiogram electrodes to both wrists, and put a heartbeat monitor was at the second intercostal space on the left border of the sternum. The arterial signs on the cuffs were aligned to the brachial artery and the posterior tibial artery, and the pulse waveform was record at the same time. The value of baPWV was calculated by machine automatically, of which the higher bilateral limb value of baPWV was used for used for subsequent analyses.

PWV measurements were performed under standardized clinical conditions. Participants were examined in a quiet environment after a period of rest in the supine position. Measurements were conducted during daytime. All examinations were conducted indoors during the winter of 2018 in a temperature‐controlled environment with adequate heating. Participants were required to fast prior to the examination.

Assessment of Cardiovascular Events

Cardiovascular events were defined as a composite endpoint consisting of cardiovascular death, first‐time myocardial infarction, or stroke, obtained from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention‐National Mortality Surveillance System and the Beijing Municipal Health Commission‐Beijing inpatient medical record home page system until December 31, 2021. Disease codes were based on the 6‐digit (extension code) format of the 10th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD‐10), and death codes were based on the 4‐digit (sub‐level) format of the ICD‐10, the details were described in a previous study [29].

Statistical Analysis

In the description of population characteristics, participants were classified into four groups according to the quartiles of baPWV and cfPWV, respectively. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range [IQR]) for those with a normal or skewed distribution, and the number (percentage) of use cases for categorical variables is represented. ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test was used for continuous variables, as appropriate, the Pearson χ2 test was used for categorical variables, and the Fisher exact test was used for those with a theoretical frequency < 10. The associations of PWV indices with endpoint events were assessed using Cox proportional hazards models with restricted cubic splines, Kaplan–Meier curves, and multivariable Cox regression analyses. In multivariate logistic regression models, covariables included age, gender, body mass index, estimated glomerular filtration rate, smoking status, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, anti‐hypertensive treatment, hypoglycemic treatment, lipid‐lowering treatment, and family history of ASCVD. To compare the associations between different PWV indices and cardiovascular events, two PWV indices were forced into the model simultaneously. The patient subgroups stratified by traditional cardiovascular risk factors and interactions were further examined by calculating the subgroup‐specific hazard ratios of events. All data analyses were conducted using Empower (R) (www.empowerstats.com, X&Y Solutions, Inc., Boston, MA, USA) and R (Version 4.4.1; http://www.R‐project.org). A 2‐tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Associations of cfPWV and baPWV With Cardiovascular Events

During a median follow‐up period of 3.15 years, 173 cases of MACE (3.0%) occured, including 130 cases of stroke (2.3%), 35 cases of acute myocardial infarction (0.6%), 21 cases of cardiovascular death (0.4%). The incidence of cardiovascular events according to PWV quartiles was shown in Figure 1. Smooth curves of cfPWV and baPWV and MACE was shown in Figure 2. The Kaplan–Meier survival curves indicated that both cfPWV and baPWV were associated with the risk of MACE (log‐rank p < 0.001) (Figure 3). In multivariable Cox regression analysis, higher baPWV quartiles were independently associated with higher risk of MACE after adjusting for potential confounders (HR, 1.08; 95% CI, 1.04–1.13; p < 0.001). Compared to the lowest quartile, the highest quartile (HR, 2.12; 95% CI, 1.16–3.88; p = 0.015) of baPWV was significantly associated with a higher incidence of MACE in the same multivariable model (Table 2). The highest quartile of cfPWV demonstrated a nonsignificant trend toward association with MACE (HR, 1.67; 95% CI, 0.93–2.99; p = 0.087), compared to the lowest quartile (Table 2). When cfPWV and baPWV was included in the same model, baPWV kept independent predictive value for MACE (Table 3).

Discussion

In this large‐scale community‐based cohort study in China, we found that after adjusting for traditional risk factors, cfPWV showed a trend towards associating with the risk of new‐onset cardiovascular events, while baPWV was significantly associated with the risk of new‐onset cardiovascular events. Our results indicated that baPWV seems to have a closer relationship with new‐onset cardiovascular events comparing to cfPWV.

BaPWV may be more sensitive to cardiovascular risk factors than cfPWV. A study of Baier et al. reported that baPWV showed stronger correlations with age and systolic blood pressure than cfPWV (Pearson's r 0.66 vs. 0.39 for age, 0.50 vs. 0.20 for SBP), implying that baPWV may better capture the influence of risk factors on blood vessels [30]. BaPWV includes a larger territory of blood vessel, measuring arterial stiffness of both large central elastic arteries and medium‐sized muscular peripheral arteries of the lower limb, while cfPWV quantifies large artery stiffness [17]. Evidence from Pan et al., based on 3067 community‐dwelling adults in China, further indicated that atherosclerosis is not confined to large arteries but also involves coronary (44.9%), extracranial (36.4%), renal (28.7%), and intracranial (17.7%) arteries, underscoring the contribution of smaller arterial branches to the atherosclerotic process [31]. BaPWV also appears to represent left ventricle afterload better than cfPWV. Previous studies demonstrated that baPWV was more strongly and independently associated with left ventricular functional and structural indices, including E wave / A wave ratio, left ventricle mass, and the isovolumic relaxation constant [24, 32]. These findings suggest that baPWV may have certain advantages over cfPWV in predicting cardiovascular events by capturing subclinical cardiac dysfunction. It is also noteworthy that our earlier work revealed a non‐linear relationship between baPWV and carotid plaque, implying that potential nonlinear associations should be considered in future research [33].

Previous studies have examined the association of PWV with cardiovascular events, though findings have been inconsistent. Most of the studies conducted in American and European populations have reported a positive association between cfPWV and cardiovascular outcomes. In contrast, a cross‐sectional study by Kozakova et al. found no significant correlation between cfPWV and the prevalence of cardiovascular events [16]. Evidence from Asian populations remains relatively limited. One prospective cohort study of 492 Japanese Americans suggested that cfPWV was a predictor of cardiovascular mortality [34]. By comparison, a larger body of evidence supports the association between baPWV and cardiovascular outcomes in Asian populations. A meta‐analysis including 14 673 Japanese participants without preexisting CVD demonstrated a higher baPWV was significantly associated with increased CVD risk after adjustment for conventional risk factors (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.10–1.29 per 1‐SD increase in baPWV) [26]. Our study contributes novel evidence from a large‐scale, community‐based cohort in Beijing, China, providing further insights into which PWV measurement may be more suitable for predicting cardiovascular risk in Asian populations.

An expert consensus on the application of PWV in Asian populations has suggested that the process of arterial stiffening may follow a different pattern in Asians compared with non‐Asians [35]. Specifically, the predominant contributor to reduced arterial compliance in Asian populations appears to be “arteriosclerosis” rather than “atherosclerosis” [35, 36]. Arteriosclerosis primarily involves alterations in the extracellular matrix within the medial layer of large elastic arteries and may lead to luminal dilation, rather than lipid deposition in the vessel wall [37]. In addition, variations in metabolic risk factor profiles associated with increased PWV further suggest that the relationship between PWV and cardiovascular events may differ between Asian and non‐Asian populations [35, 38].

Compared with cfPWV and baPWV has the advantages of being simpler to measure, requiring less operator training, and thus may be more suitable for community‐based and large‐scale population screening [17]. In our Beijing community cohort, we confirmed the association between baPWV and cardiovascular events, supporting its potential value in routine cardiovascular risk assessment.

Our analysis further showed an interaction between baPWV and BMI with MACE, with a higher hazard ratio observed in individuals with lower BMI. We analyzed baseline characteristics stratified by BMI (Table S1) and found that individuals with higher BMI tended to have a higher incidence of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidemia, which may contribute to the differences in hazard ratios for MACE across BMI subgroups. To clarify whether obesity‐related comorbidities affect the relationship between baPWV and MACE, our study included individuals with hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia, but the interaction was not statistically significant. These findings suggest that obesity status itself may modify the impact of arterial stiffness on atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk.

Li et al. [39] have reported a significant interaction between BMI and hypertension on arteriosclerosis has been reported, indicating that obesity may attenuate hypertension‐related arterial stiffening when both conditions coexist, particularly among women and middle‐aged individuals. In our study population, higher baPWV was associated with a higher prevalence of hypertension. These observations may partly explain the interaction between baPWV and BMI with respect to MACE observed in our analysis.

Moreover, our finding of a stronger association between baPWV and MACE among individuals with lower BMI may indicate that arterial stiffness plays a more dominant and unmasked role in cardiovascular risk when fewer competing metabolic risk factors are present. In contrast, in individuals with higher BMI, the prognostic impact of baPWV may be attenuated by the coexistence of multiple cardiovascular risk factors or potential protective mechanisms related to obesity. Nevertheless, the relationship between BMI, arterial stiffness, and subsequent cardiovascular risk remains incompletely understood and needs further investigation. Future studies are therefore warranted to develop refined cardiovascular risk assessment strategies that consider BMI as well as broader aspects of body composition, in order to better capture the heterogeneous prognostic implications of baPWV across different BMI strata.

It should be noted that although the association between cfPWV and MACE did not reach statistical significance in our cohort, this does not imply that cfPWV lacks prognostic value. In the present study, cfPWV demonstrated a consistent trend toward increased risk, with a smaller hazard ratio compared with baPWV. This observation may be attributable to population heterogeneity, limited statistical power, or the relatively short duration of follow‐up. Importantly, cfPWV remains a guideline‐endorsed and clinically established marker of arterial stiffness. It is therefore plausible that its prognostic significance would become more apparent in studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow‐up periods.Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as our study was conducted in a community‐based population in Beijing, the generalizability of the findings to other regions or populations remains uncertain and requires further validation. Second, although we employed a prospective cohort design, studies with larger sample sizes and participants from diverse cohorts are needed to minimize potential bias from unmeasured confounding factors.

Conclusion

In the Beijing community‐based population, baPWV, but not cfPWV, was significantly associated with the risk of new‐onset cardiovascular events. Our findings reinforce the greater role of baPWV compared to cfPWV in routine cardiovascular risk assessment in Asian population.

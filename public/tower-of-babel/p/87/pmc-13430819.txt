Segment-specific versus global cervical paraspinal muscle degeneration predicts sagittal balance and functional recovery after posterior surgery
Authors: Sheyang Xu, Xinglin Liu, Zhiheng Zhao, Sanbao Hu, Xianglong Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Study designRetrospective cohort study.ObjectiveTo compare the prognostic value of localized C4 fatty infiltration (C4_FI) versus a composite global fatty infiltration index (PC1) for cervical sagittal parameters and functional recovery following posterior cervical surgery.Summary of background dataParaspinal muscle degeneration impairs cervical lordosis and postoperative outcomes. Although global fatty infiltration (FI) is recognized as a predictor of malalignment, the clinical relevance of level-specific FI remains unclear.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 179 patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy treated by laminoplasty from 2017–2025. Preoperative JOA scores, disease duration, surgical procedure, and follow-up duration were recorded. Preoperative radiographs assessed C2–7 lordosis (CL), sagittal vertical axis (SVA), and T1 slope. MRI quantified FI from C3–C6; PC1 represented global FI. Individual-level FI (C3–C6) was compared using AIC to identify the optimal predictive segment. Stepwise and multivariable regressions (adjusted for JOA baseline and disease duration) identified independent predictors. Incremental model value was quantified by ΔAUC, AIC, and BIC with DeLong tests.ResultsFI values were strongly correlated (r = 0.53–0.77). PCA identified PC1 accounting for 77.5% variance, most heavily weighted on C4_FI (loading 0.532). Among individual levels, C4_FI showed the lowest AIC for all three sagittal outcomes, supporting C4_FI as the most informative single-level FI marker among C3–C6 in this dataset. Multivariable regression showed C4_FI independently predicted CL (p = 0.015) and T1 slope (p = 0.035), while PC1 was linked to SVA. For functional recovery, C4_FI (OR = 1.12 per 1% FI, p < 0.001) was the strongest predictor. Adding FI to the clinical+sagittal model improved AUC from 0.739 to 0.808 (ΔAUC = +0.069, DeLong p < 0.001). Traditional sagittal parameters (CL, SVA, T1 slope) showed limited independent predictive value for functional outcomes.ConclusionC4_FI and PC1 provide complementary but non-additive information: localized C4 degeneration was more closely associated with lordosis and T1 slope, whereas global FI appeared more related to anterior head translation.Both demonstrated stronger predictive value for functional recovery than conventional sagittal parameters. Routine FI assessment may refine risk stratification and surgical decision-making in cervical myelopathy.

Introduction

Cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) is the leading cause of spinal cord dysfunction in individuals over 55 years and represents the most severe form among degenerative cervical spine disorders [1]. Its insidious onset often delays diagnosis, and untreated progression can lead to irreversible deficits. Symptoms include gait disturbance, impaired balance, and hand dysfunction. While conservative measures provide transient relief, surgical decompression remains the most effective treatment, using anterior or posterior approaches depending on disease extent [1, 2].

Beyond decompression, cervical sagittal alignment has emerged as a key determinant of outcomes. Parameters such as C2–7 sagittal vertical axis (SVA), T1 slope (T1S), and cervical lordosis (CL) influence gaze, balance, and recovery [3]. Malalignment, including kyphosis or anterior translation, is associated with pain, impaired function, and poor quality of life, whereas restoration of sagittal parameters predicts superior JOA and NDI scores [3].

The paraspinal muscle complex provides crucial stabilization. Degeneration and fatty infiltration (FI) impair lordotic support and proprioception, promoting sagittal imbalance [4, 5]. MRI enables reliable FI quantification through grading systems or FI ratios [6]. Previous studies linked higher FI and reduced cross-sectional area to loss of lordosis, axial pain, and sagittal malalignment, especially after posterior surgery [6]. In contrast, anterior approaches that avoid muscle trauma often preserve alignment [6].

However, current evidence is limited. Most work has focused on ACDF, CDA, or nonsurgical patients, with relatively few data on posterior approaches [6, 7]. Moreover, FI has typically been analyzed as a global parameter, without considering level-specific variation. Only a few studies have compared the prognostic value of localized FI (e.g., C4) with composite indices such as principal component scores (PC1) [5, 7]. Whether local or global FI better predicts sagittal balance and neurological recovery following posterior surgery remains uncertain.

This study applied quantitative MRI to assess FI from C3 to C6, extracting PC1 as a global index. We aimed to determine whether localized C4_FI independently predicts sagittal parameters (CL and T1S) and JOA improvement compared with PC1, while adjusting for known confounders including preoperative JOA severity and disease duration, to refine presurgical risk assessment and guide surgical planning [7].

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This single-center, retrospective cohort study included adults (≥ 18 years) with cervical spondylotic myelopathy who underwent laminoplasty at our institution between 2017 and 2025. Eligibility required complete charts, detailed Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) scores, and preoperative X-ray and MRI. Exclusion criteria were ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament, prior cervical surgery, severe deformity or tumor, ankylosing spondylitis or systemic disorders, pregnancy or lactation, emergency indication, insufficient imaging quality, or missing data. A total of 179 patients were identified. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data collection

Collected variables included sex, age, body mass index (BMI), and comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, cerebral infarction). Surgical procedure (laminoplasty) and follow-up duration (months) were recorded. Preoperative JOA score (0–17) and disease duration (months) were documented as potential confounders of postoperative recovery. Functional recovery was assessed ≥ 6 months after surgery by JOA recovery rate, calculated as (postoperative JOA − preoperative JOA) / (17 − preoperative JOA) × 100%. A threshold of 50% defined favorable outcome.

Radiographic and MRI assessment

Standard upright anteroposterior and lateral radiographs were used to measure cervical lordosis (C2–7 Cobb angle), C2–7 sagittal vertical axis (SVA), and T1 slope. Measurements were independently performed in blinded fashion by two spine surgeons; discrepancies > 2° or 2 mm were adjudicated by a senior surgeon (Fig. 1).

Measurement of cervical sagittal parameters on lateral radiographs. C2–7 cervical lordosis (CL) was defined as the angle between the inferior endplates of C2 and C7

MRI was performed using a 3.0-T Siemens MAGNETOM Prisma scanner. Axial T2-weighted images at C3–C6 mid-vertebral levels were preprocessed by adjusting contrast and brightness to improve tissue visibility. Regions of interest (ROIs) were manually delineated along the fascial boundaries of the multifidus and semispinalis cervicis muscles, including intramuscular fat while excluding extramuscular adipose tissue and bone. Total cross-sectional area (TCSA) was measured as the total area within the ROI. Functional cross-sectional area (FCSA) was derived by applying a thresholding technique based on grayscale values to distinguish muscle from fat. Fatty infiltration (FI) was calculated as [(TCSA – FCSA) / TCSA] × 100%. All measurements were independently performed by two observers using ImageJ (version 1.53, NIH, USA), with mean values used for analysis. Intra- and inter-observer reliability were excellent (ICC > 0.9 (Fig. 2).

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed with R software (version 4.5.0). Normality was assessed; continuous variables were reported as mean ± SD or median (IQR), and categorical variables as counts and percentages.

Spearman correlation examined relationships among FI, sagittal alignment, preoperative JOA, and disease duration. Principal component analysis (PCA) on FI values from C3–C6 generated the first component (PC1) to represent global FI. Stepwise regression compared explanatory power of baseline covariates, PC1, and C4_FI using ΔR² and Δp. The base model included sex, age, BMI, comorbidities, preoperative JOA score, and disease duration.

To address whether C4 is indeed the optimal predictive segment, individual-level FI (C3_FI, C4_FI, C5_FI, C6_FI) were compared in separate multivariable models for each sagittal outcome, with model fit evaluated by AIC and adjusted R².

Nonlinear associations between C4_FI and sagittal parameters were examined with natural spline functions (df = 3) and generalized additive models. Subgroup regression stratified by sex (male/female) and age (< 65 vs. ≥ 65 years) evaluated consistency.

For functional outcomes, multivariable logistic regression assessed prognosis, defined as JOA recovery rate > 50%. Nested models sequentially added clinical covariates, sagittal parameters, and FI indices. Model discrimination was assessed by area under the ROC curve (AUC); incremental predictive value was quantified by ΔAUC, AIC, and BIC. DeLong test compared AUC between nested models. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 179 patients were included: 118 men (65.9%) and 61 women (34.1%), with mean age 64.3 ± 10.2 years and BMI 25.8 ± 3.7 kg/m². Hypertension (56.4%) and diabetes (30.7%) were the most common comorbidities. Surgical procedures are laminoplasty, with mean follow-up of 19.5 ± 7.6 months. Mean preoperative JOA score was 11.4 ± 1.8 and median disease duration was 31.3 months. Mean sagittal parameters were C2–7 CL 13.1° ± 9.6°, SVA 2.8 ± 1.7 cm, and T1 slope 27.0° ± 9.1°. Mean FI values ranged from 24.2% (C6) to 27.3% (C4) (Table 1).

Correlation analysis

FI values at different levels were strongly correlated with each other (r = 0.53–0.77, all p < 0.001). None of the FI parameters correlated with C2–7 CL. FI at all levels correlated positively with SVA (r = 0.19–0.26, p < 0.05) and with T1 slope (r = 0.19–0.25, p < 0.05). C4_FI was negatively correlated with preoperative JOA (r = − 0.24, p = 0.001) but not significantly correlated with disease duration (r = − 0.08, p = 0.299). CL correlated moderately with T1 slope (r = 0.32, p < 0.001) (Table 2).

Principal component analysis

PCA of FI values (C3–C6) demonstrated good sampling adequacy (KMO = 0.767, Bartlett p < 0.001). One component (eigenvalue = 3.119) explained 77.5% of variance. PC1 loadings were: C3_FI = 0.467, C4_FI = 0.532, C5_FI = 0.493, C6_FI = 0.506 (on the 0–1 scale). C4_FI contributed the highest weight. This confirms PC1 as a valid composite index of global paraspinal FI.

Stepwise regression analyses

For C2–7 CL, C4_FI significantly improved model fit (ΔR² = 0.021, p = 0.033), while PC1 contributed little. Male sex and hypertension were associated with greater lordosis, whereas cerebral infarction and higher BMI predicted reduced lordosis.

For C2–7 SVA, both PC1 (ΔR² = 0.031, p = 0.015) and C4_FI (ΔR² = 0.041, p = 0.005) significantly enhanced explanatory power.

For T1 slope, both PC1 (ΔR² = 0.073, p < 0.001) and C4_FI (ΔR² = 0.094, p < 0.001) provided substantial and significant improvement (Table 3).

Multivariable regression models

In fully adjusted models that included demographic, clinical covariates, preoperative JOA, and disease duration, C4_FI remained an independent predictor of cervical lordosis (p = 0.015), together with male sex (p = 0.003), hypertension (p < 0.001), BMI (p < 0.001), COPD (p = 0.046), and cerebral infarction (p = 0.033). Disease duration was also significant (p = 0.034). When both PC1 and C4_FI were included in the same model, PC1 lost significance for CL, while C4_FI remained predictive.

For SVA, age was the only significant determinant (p = 0.005), and neither C4_FI nor PC1 retained independent significance in the fully adjusted model.

For T1 slope, male sex (p < 0.001), C4_FI (p = 0.035), and disease duration (p = 0.005) emerged as independent predictors, while PC1 was no longer significant (Table 4).

Individual-level FI comparison

To verify the superiority of C4_FI over other cervical levels, each FI level (C3–C6) was separately entered into multivariable models adjusted for all covariates. For C2–7 CL, C4_FI was the only significant FI predictor (β = 0.162, p = 0.033, AIC = 1294.4) and achieved the lowest AIC among all levels (C3_FI: AIC = 1296.8; C5_FI: AIC = 1298.9; C6_FI: AIC = 1299.3). For C2–7 SVA, C4_FI again showed the strongest association (β = 0.040, p = 0.005, AIC = 688.0 vs. C3_FI: 692.3, C5_FI: 693.2, C6_FI: 693.4). For T1 slope, C4_FI demonstrated the largest effect (β = 0.321, p < 0.001, AIC = 1283.4 vs. C3_FI: 1293.6, C5_FI: 1296.1, C6_FI: 1293.5). These results confirm that C4_FI is the optimal individual-level predictor across all three sagittal parameters (Table 5).

Nonlinear and stratified analyses

Natural spline and GAM models detected no significant nonlinear effects of C4_FI on sagittal parameters (all p > 0.05 for nonlinearity). Stratified analyses showed that associations of C4_FI with CL and T1 slope were more evident in men (CL: β = 0.222, p = 0.018; T1S: β = 0.438, p < 0.001) than in women (both p > 0.05). In age-stratified analyses (< 65 vs. ≥ 65 years), C4_FI→CL was stronger in younger patients (β = 0.191, p = 0.042), while C4_FI→SVA was more pronounced in older patients (β = 0.061, p = 0.015). C4_FI→T1 slope was robust across both age groups (Fig. 3).

Prognostic analysis

In logistic regression for JOA recovery > 50%, C4_FI and PC1 were each significant predictors when analyzed separately. When included together, neither remained significant, indicating collinearity. Predictive performance was comparable: AUC 0.808 for the C4_FI model and 0.809 for the PC1 model (DeLong test, p = 0.41). Traditional sagittal parameters (CL, SVA, T1 slope) did not achieve statistical significance in any model (Table 6).

Incremental predictive value of fatty infiltration

To quantify the added value of FI measurement, nested logistic regression models were compared. The baseline clinical model achieved AUC = 0.733. Adding sagittal parameters yielded minimal improvement (AUC = 0.739, ΔAUC = + 0.006). Incorporating C4_FI substantially improved discrimination (AUC = 0.808, ΔAUC = + 0.075 vs. clinical+sagittal, DeLong p < 0.001), reduced AIC from 228.5 to 206.3, and improved BIC from 276.3 to 257.3. The model containing both C4_FI and PC1 achieved AUC = 0.811 but with higher AIC (208.2), indicating that C4_FI alone provides the best balance of discrimination and parsimony. Among individual levels, C4_FI provided the highest AUC (0.808) compared to C3_FI (0.801), C5_FI (0.777), and C6_FI (0.761) (Table 7).

Discussion

In this retrospective study of 179 patients undergoing posterior cervical surgery, we identified a strong association between paraspinal muscle FI, sagittal alignment, and functional recovery. FI values were highly correlated across cervical levels, and principal component analysis confirmed that the first component (PC1) is a reliable indicator of global degeneration. Yet multivariable regression consistently demonstrated that localized FI at C4 (C4_FI) was the independent determinant of cervical lordosis (CL) and T1 slope, whereas PC1 was primarily associated with sagittal vertical axis (SVA). Per-level comparison further confirmed that C4_FI achieved the lowest AIC among all individual cervical levels for predicting CL, SVA, and T1 slope (Table 5). For postoperative outcomes, both C4_FI and PC1 predicted favorable JOA recovery (> 50%), with comparable accuracy (AUC = 0.808 for C4_FI, 0.809 for PC1). However, their significance disappeared when analyzed together, indicating collinearity.All multivariable models were adjusted for preoperative JOA baseline and disease duration.

Local vs. global FI and sagittal mechanics

Our findings suggest a dual mechanism. PC1, representing global FI, correlated with increased SVA, reflecting the cumulative burden of aging, disuse, and metabolic disturbance. In contrast, localized degeneration at C4 directly determined CL and T1 slope. Situated at the apex of cervical lordosis, C4 acts as a biomechanical fulcrum; degeneration at this segment reduces extensor capacity and directly alters cervical curvature. This explains why generalized FI does not necessarily reduce lordosis, whereas focal C4 FI decisively reshapes CL and T1 slope. In a per-level comparison across C3–C6, C4_FI consistently achieved the lowest AIC and largest effect size for all three sagittal parameters (Table 5), corroborating the unique biomechanical role of this segment. Prior anatomical and clinical studies confirm that deep extensor quality is crucial for sagittal stability, while preservation of key upper cervical attachments can reduce axial pain [8–13].

It should be noted, however, that when both PC1 and C4_FI were simultaneously included in fully adjusted models, the independent associations of each with individual sagittal parameters were attenuated. The proposed dual-pathway framework (PC1↔SVA, C4_FI↔CL/T1 slope) should therefore be regarded as a statistically supported but not definitively established model, requiring prospective validation.

Mechanistic insights: inflammation and fibro-adipogenic progenitors

At the cellular level, FI represents more than an imaging change. Chen et al. [14] demonstrated that under chronic inflammation and aging, fibro-adipogenic progenitors (FAPs) escape clearance, expand abnormally, and differentiate into adipocytes and fibroblasts. This process reduces contractile tissue, promotes fibrosis and fatty replacement, and thereby compromises muscle quality. Du et al. [15] further reported that even after successful correction of focal kyphosis via anterior approaches, global imbalance often persists in patients with extensive FI, underscoring the systemic and partly irreversible nature of muscle degeneration. Together, these findings provide a mechanistic explanation for our results: PC1 reflects systemic inflammatory and metabolic dysfunction that drives anterior head translation, while C4_FI captures focal mechanical vulnerability at the curvature apex.

Compensation and decompensation

The cervical spine possesses compensatory mechanisms whereby local deformity can initially be balanced by adjacent segments, thoracic alignment, or pelvic retroversion to maintain gaze [13]. However, progressive FI diminishes this reserve. In our framework, PC1 governs the progression toward global decompensation, manifested as increased SVA, while C4_FI determines whether local lordosis can be maintained. Once extensor degeneration at C4 limits extension capacity, CL decreases and T1 slope rises, precipitating cervicothoracic imbalance [16]. These findings help reconcile why PC1 and C4_FI predict different but complementary aspects of sagittal imbalance.

FI and functional recovery

Another key observation is that FI predicted functional outcomes more strongly than traditional alignment parameters. Both C4_FI and PC1 were independent predictors of JOA recovery, while CL, SVA, and T1 slope lost significance after adjustment. Incremental model comparison (Table 7) confirmed that adding C4_FI to the clinical-plus-sagittal model improved AUC from 0.739 to 0.808 (ΔAUC = + 0.075, DeLong p < 0.001), whereas sagittal parameters alone contributed only + 0.006 (from 0.733 to 0.739). This indicates that FI not only mediates prognosis through sagittal alignment but also directly influences recovery through impaired muscle quality, chronic inflammation, and reduced regenerative potential. Previous reports similarly demonstrated that severe FI is linked to poor response to conservative care, delayed fusion, and postoperative deformity [10, 17–20]. Choi et al. [18] showed that poor deep extensor quality compromised fusion, while Joseph et al. [20] reported high FI as a risk factor for postoperative kyphosis. Collectively, these studies reinforce FI as a biological prognostic marker in spine surgery.

Clinical implications and future directions

These results emphasize the clinical value of routine FI assessment. PC1 reflects global systemic degeneration and predicts anterior head translation, while C4_FI identifies a focal mechanical fulcrum critical for preserving lordosis and T1 slope. Because of their collinearity, they should not be modeled simultaneously, but each provides complementary information. Importantly, our per-level comparison demonstrated that C4_FI alone achieves predictive performance comparable to PC1 (AUC 0.808 vs. 0.809) and superior to C3, C5, or C6 FI, while requiring only a single-level measurement. This supports the practical use of C4_FI as a time-efficient surrogate for multi-level FI assessment in clinical workflows. In practice, patients with elevated C4_FI should be recognized as a high-risk subgroup, warranting surgical strategies that preserve posterior musculature or employ stronger fixation. Conversely, patients with high PC1 may require closer postoperative surveillance for global malalignment.

Emerging imaging modalities could further advance FI assessment. Paraspinal muscle quality (PMQ) can detect early decline prior to overt FI [21], and dual-energy CT allows reproducible fat quantification with reduced radiation exposure [22]. Furthermore, accumulating evidence suggests that FI may be partially reversible: Passias et al. [23] observed that improvement in FI correlated with restored lordosis and pain relief, indicating that FI can function as a dynamic biomarker of recovery potential.

Conclusion

Quantitative analysis of cervical paraspinal FI provides important insights into both sagittal mechanics and surgical outcomes. Global FI (PC1) was closely associated with anterior head translation (SVA), reflecting systemic degeneration, while localized C4_FI independently determined CL and T1 slope, emphasizing its biomechanical significance. Among individual levels, C4_FI was confirmed as the optimal single-segment predictor. Both indices predicted functional recovery more effectively than conventional sagittal parameters, with C4_FI alone providing excellent discrimination (AUC = 0.808). FI should therefore be regarded not merely as a static imaging finding but as an integrative marker reflecting both mechanical competence and systemic health. Routine C4_FI assessment may refine preoperative risk stratification and inform surgical decision-making in cervical myelopathy.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. It was a single-center, retrospective analysis with a moderate sample size, which may limit generalizability. FI was measured from MRI cross-sections at selected levels and may not fully reflect dynamic muscle function. In addition, only short- to mid-term outcomes were analyzed. Because all patients underwent laminoplasty, the findings may not be generalizable to other posterior procedures such as laminectomy with fusion or laminectomy alone.Craniovertebral balance parameters such as CGH-SVA were not available in this cohort and warrant investigation in future studies. Finally, the dual-pathway hypothesis, while supported by consistent statistical patterns across multiple model specifications, remains speculative and requires prospective validation. Future multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up and integration of PMQ or dual-energy CT are needed to validate and expand these findings.

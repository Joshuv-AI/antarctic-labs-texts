Associations between preoperative biomarkers and patient-reported outcomes after single-level lumbar discectomy: a systematic review
Authors: Shailen G Sampath, Colin J Willoughby, Jay M Iyer, Miles L Botkiss, Christopher Pawul, Harrison J Howell, Pavan S Upadhyayula, Farhan A Khan, Vivian P Le, Nadeen O Chahine, Dean Chou, Andrew K Chan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSingle-level discectomy is among the most frequently performed spine procedures for radiculopathy due to disc herniation. Although most patients improve, a subset experiences suboptimal recovery, disability, or reoperation. Current preoperative risk stratification remains limited, highlighting the need for objective biomarkers that can improve the prediction of postoperative outcomes. The objective of this study is to review the effect of biomarkers on standard patient outcomes, identify consistencies and gaps in the literature, and outline priorities for biomarker panel standardization to support clinically meaningful prediction in discectomy.MethodsWe conducted a systematic review in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were searched from inception to August 27, 2025, using terms for discectomy procedures, serum/tissue biomarkers, and PROs. Eligible studies enrolled adults undergoing single-level discectomy, measured biomarkers pre- or intra-operatively, and reported associations with PROs such as Visual Analog Scale (VAS), Oswestry disability Index (ODI), or EuroQol 5-Dimension Questionnaire (EQ-5D). Three authors independently performed screening, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment. Risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa scale for observational studies.ResultsEight studies met inclusion criteria. Given heterogeneity in biomarkers, assays, and outcome reporting, findings were synthesized narratively by biomarker class; quantitative meta-analysis was not feasible. Across cohorts, pro-inflammatory cytokines consistently predicted worse outcomes: higher preoperative interleukin (IL)-6 and tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) were associated with higher postoperative VAS and ODI scores, and higher preoperative IL-1β was associated with higher postoperative VAS scores. The acute phase reactant C-reactive protein (CRP) was associated with increased VAS scores. In addition, regulatory cytokine transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) corresponded to worse postoperative VAS. In contrast, anti-inflammatory/type-2 signaling with higher IL-4 correlated with greater VAS improvement. Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) was also inversely correlated with worse VAS scores in specific studies.ConclusionsPreoperative biomarker profiles provide prognostic value for patient outcomes following single-level discectomy. Standardized, prospective studies using multivariable biomarker panels are warranted to validate current findings and establish quantitative prognosis.Keywords: Single-level discectomy, biomarkers, PRISMA, systematic review

Highlight box

Key findings

• Preoperative biomarker profiles are associated with postoperative pain and disability outcomes following single-level lumbar discectomy. Pro-inflammatory biomarkers, including tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-6, IL-1β, interferon gamma (IFN-γ), C-reactive protein (CRP), and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), were consistently associated with worse postoperative patient-reported outcomes (PROs), including higher Visual Analog Scale and Oswestry Disability Index scores. Anti-inflammatory and neurotrophic markers such as IL-4 and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) were associated with greater postoperative improvement and lower pain scores.

What is known and what is new?

• Prognosis after lumbar discectomy is currently based primarily on clinical, imaging, and psychosocial factors, with limited integration of biomarkers. Biomarkers reflecting inflammation and tissue remodeling have been implicated in degenerative spine disease, but their relationship to PROs after single-level discectomy have not been synthesized.

• This systematic review identifies several consistent associations between specific preoperative biomarkers and postoperative PROs in lumbar discectomy, highlighting inflammatory cytokines and fibrinolytic factors as predictors of worse recovery and immunoregulatory and neurotrophic markers as predictors of improved recovery.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings reinforce the importance of further basic and translational research to clarify mechanisms linking biomarker profiles to postoperative outcomes.

• Future prospective studies should integrate standardized biomarker measurement with longitudinal PROs to identify candidate biomarkers for future research in recovery and inflammation following single-level discectomy.

Introduction

Single-level discectomy is among the most frequently performed spine operations and typically yields substantial improvements in pain and function for radiculopathy due to disc herniation. Nevertheless, a subset of patients who undergo single-level discectomy experience suboptimal recovery, persistent disability, or reoperation, underscoring the need for better preoperative risk stratification (1,2). Contemporary prognostic research in discectomy has emphasized clinical and imaging predictors, but translation into consistently actionable, patient-level prediction remains limited (3,4).

Biomarkers offer objective, quantifiable measures of underlying pathophysiology that can complement history, examination, and imaging to refine perioperative risk assessment, guide patient selection, and tailor postoperative management. Within spine surgery, biomarker science is accelerating and spans multiple biofluids or physiological systems, ranging from systemic (blood), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and tissue compartments (paraspinal muscles, intervertebral disc, etc.). Plasma profiles, including inflammatory cytokines, acute-phase reactants, chemokines, and neuroaxonal injury markers, associated with degenerative spine pain and recovery are emerging (5). In cervical spondylotic myelopathy, CSF biomarkers of white-matter injury and astrogliosis correlate with preoperative disease severity and postsurgical outcomes, supporting pathobiological relevance across the neuraxis (6). Biomarkers of a pro-neuroinflammatory state have also been linked to postoperative delirium risk after spine procedures, suggesting broader perioperative applications (7).

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) remain the cornerstone for evaluating the effectiveness of spine surgery because they capture pain, function, and health-related quality of life domains that objective tests cannot fully reflect. Validated instruments such as the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), Oswestry Disability Index (ODI), Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire (RMDQ), EuroQol 5-Dimension Questionnaire (EQ-5D), SF-12/36 components, and patient-reported outcomes measurement information system (PROMIS) domains are widely used and have demonstrated predictive value for postoperative improvement and thresholds like the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) (8). As biomarker discovery accelerates, integrating objective biological signals with PROs in multidimensional models offers a path toward more individualized, data-driven surgical counseling in spine surgery (5,7,9,10).

Despite this momentum, a focused synthesis for single-level lumbar discectomy that centers specifically on preoperative serum biomarkers and postoperative PROs is lacking. Prior systematic reviews have concentrated on clinical, demographic, and physical prognostic factors or on imaging biomarkers such as Modic changes, which, while biologically informative, are indirect surrogates rather than circulating measures, and have yielded mixed associations with postoperative pain and function (3,4,11). Given the high volume of discectomy, the variability in individual patient recovery, and the clinical importance of setting realistic expectations, clarifying which preoperative serum biomarkers reliably forecast PROs after single-level lumbar discectomy would be of immediate practical relevance (1,2).

In this systematic review, we collate studies that measured preoperative serum and tissue biomarkers in patients undergoing single-level discectomy and evaluated associations with postoperative PROs. Our aims are to: (I) map biomarker domains and directions of effect on standard PROs; (II) identify consistencies and gaps in the literature that explain heterogeneity in reported findings; and (III) outline priorities for biomarker panel standardization and model development to support clinically meaningful, patient-centered prediction in discectomy (3-6). We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (available at https://jss.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jss-2026-1-0060/rc) (12).

Methods

We conducted a systematic review focusing on whether preoperative serum and tissue biomarkers are associated with postoperative PROs following single-level discectomy. This study is registered through OSF.io and will be available upon publication.

Study design and search strategy

Three independent reviewers searched PubMed (MEDLINE), Embase, and the Cochrane Library (CENTRAL and Cochrane Reviews) from database inception through August 27, 2025. The strategy combined three concept blocks: (I) procedure terms for single-level discectomy (including “discectomy”, “microdiscectomy”, “lumbar discectomy”, and synonyms); (II) biomarker terms spanning inflammatory cytokines [e.g., interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, IL-8/CXCL8, tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), interferon gamma (IFN-γ), IL-4, IL-10, IL-15, IL-17, transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1)], acute-phase reactants [C-reactive protein (CRP), high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP), serum amyloid A (SAA)], chemokines (e.g., CCL2/MCP-1, CCL5/RANTES, CCL11/eotaxin, CCL22, CXCL10/IP-10), neurotrophic/neuroaxonal markers [brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), neurofilament light chain (NfL) when serum-measured], coagulation/fibrinolysis and inflammatory pathway markers [plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), caspase-1/NLRP3/inflammasome], adipokines, and matrix remodeling enzymes [e.g., matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1)]; and (III) outcome terms for PROs. Database-specific adaptations were used for Embase and Cochrane. The full search strategies, including all Boolean operators and database-specific syntax are provided in Table 1. We also performed backward/forward citation chaining of included studies and relevant reviews.

Full search strategy

Study selection and criteria

We included randomized or nonrandomized comparative studies and cohort or case-control studies enrolling adults (≥18 years) undergoing single-level lumbar discectomy (including microdiscectomy) that measured serum or tissue biomarkers pre- or intra-operatively and reported associations with postoperative PROs. Accepted PROs included VAS, NRS, ODI, RMDQ, SF-12/SF-36 component scores, EQ-5D, PROMIS domains, Present Pain Intensity, and the Beck Depression Inventory. We excluded nonoperative cohorts, multilevel procedures, index fusion or arthroplasty, revision-only series, nonhuman studies, and mixed operative cohorts combining discectomy with fusion, arthroplasty, or other lumbar procedures when outcomes were not separately extractable for the discectomy cohort. Duplicates were removed prior to screening. Upon completion of inclusion and exclusion, any disagreements were resolved by consensus between the three authors.

Quality assessment and risk of bias

Risk of bias for included studies was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational studies. Because all studies were observational in design, the ROB 2 tool for randomized trials was not applied. Three independent reviewers performed risk of bias assessment, with disagreements resolved by consensus.

Data analysis

Outcomes analyzed included PROs and correlation with measured biomarkers. Owing to heterogeneity in biomarker panels, assays/units, timing, and outcome reporting, we performed narrative synthesis focusing on effect directionality (i.e., higher values indicating worse pain/disability and lower values indicating less intense pain/disability unless otherwise stated). The primary outcome was the association between biomarker levels and postoperative PROs (final scores or change from baseline). Quantitative meta-analysis was planned only if ≥2 clinically/methodologically homogeneous studies reported the same metric.

The process of search, inclusion and exclusion of studies is presented in Figure 1 (12). Following PRISMA guidelines, eight studies met the inclusion criteria and were selected for final synthesis. Pro-inflammatory/innate cytokines

Among the eight studies, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α were pro-inflammatory/innate cytokines that were measured preoperatively and analyzed in relation to postoperative PROs.

Three studies specifically evaluated IL-6 (Andrade et al., Haddadi et al, Chen et al. 2021) (13,16,19). Preoperative samples in these studies were either intraoperative tissue collected at the index surgery (Andrade et al., Chen et al.) (13,19) or serum obtained before surgery (Haddadi et al.) (16). Postoperative PROs were captured at 6 months (Chen et al.) (19), 3 months (Haddadi et al.) (16), 6 weeks and 1 year (Andrade et al.) (13) post op. In a prospective study of 32 patients, Haddadi et al. found that higher presurgical IL-6 was significantly correlated with decreased postoperative reduction in VAS [correlation coefficient (CC) =−0.443, P=0.011] at 3 months (16). In contrast, Andrade et al., using quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and western blot of disc tissue biopsies from 10 lumbar disc hernia patients compared with 5 scoliosis controls, found no correlation between IL-6 expression and pain severity (13). Finally, Chen et al. examined IL-6 expression in multifidus muscle and paraspinal fat from 21 patients undergoing microdiscectomy and found no significant correlation between IL-6 and postoperative VAS scores at 6 months (19).

Three studies investigated IL-1β in relation to postoperative pain outcomes (Andrade et al., Chen et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2023) (13,18,19). In a microdiscectomy cohort of 21 patients, Chen et al. [2021] reported that IL-1β expression in subcutaneous fat collected during microdiscectomy was significantly higher in patients with poor postoperative recovery (VAS low back pain P=0.005) at 6 months (19). More recently, Chen et al. [2023] studied multifidus muscle biopsies from 60 patients undergoing percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy and found IL-1β expression to be strongly correlated with both preoperative and postoperative clinical outcomes, including VAS low back pain (r=0.522, P<0.001) and VAS leg pain (r=0.462, P=0.001) at 6 months (18). In contrast, Andrade et al. found that IL-1β was elevated in herniated disc tissue but showed no correlation with VAS pain scores before or after discectomy similar to their findings with IL-6 (13). Three studies investigated TNF-α (Zu et al., Chen et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2023) (17-19) in relation to postoperative pain and disability outcomes. In a large serum cohort of 262 patients with lumbar radiculopathy undergoing microdiscectomy, Zu et al. found that patients with VAS ≥3 at 12-month follow-up had significantly higher preoperative serum TNF-α than those with VAS <3 (123.8 vs. 80.0 pg/mL, P<0.001). Furthermore, TNF-α remained significantly correlated with ODI at 12 months (r=0.20–0.37, P<0.01) (17). Chen et al. [2021] also reported that TNF-α expression in subcutaneous fat was strongly correlated with postoperative VAS low back pain (r=0.678, P<0.01) (19). Chen et al. [2023] further found that TNF-α expression in multifidus muscle was moderately correlated with clinical outcomes, including VAS low back pain (r=0.410, P=0.004), VAS leg pain (r=0.286, P=0.049), and ODI (r=0.502, P<0.001) (18).

CRP

Two studies (Rathod et al., and Elkan et al.) (14,20) examined the role of preoperative CRP in predicting postoperative outcomes following lumbar disc surgery. Preoperative CRP was measured within 24 hours prior to surgery in both cohorts. Elkan et al. followed patients at 6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months, whereas Rathod et al. assessed postoperative disability at 3 months. Elkan et al. conducted a prospective cohort of 177 patients undergoing discectomy and found that preoperative CRP was not significantly associated with ODI, VAS leg pain, or quality of life measures at any time point, with the exception of a weak association with worse VAS back pain at 12 months [odds ratio (OR) =2.10, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.03–4.29] (20). In contrast, Rathod et al., in a smaller prospective cohort of 50 lumbar disc disease patients, reported a strong positive correlation between preoperative CRP levels and postoperative disability outcomes using the modified Oswestry score (r=0.677, P<0.001) at 3 months. Patients with lower preoperative CRP (0.182±0.079 mg/dL) demonstrated substantial recovery (>10 points improvement), while those with higher CRP (0.470±0.163 mg/dL) showed poor recovery (<10 points improvement) (14).

IFN-γ

One study by Kamieniak et al. investigated serum IFN-γ levels in 27 patients undergoing lumbar microdiscectomy for disc herniation and found mixed associations with postoperative outcomes (15). In this cohort, serum IFN-γ was measured on the day of surgery and again one week postoperatively, with patient-reported pain and disability scores collected at the same intervals. IFN-γ correlated positively with postoperative NRS back pain (r=0.528, P=0.008) and with the Pain Rating Index of the Short-Form McGill Pain Questionnaire (SF-MPQ) both pre- and postoperatively (r=0.462–0.549, P<0.05). However, no significant associations were observed with NRS leg pain, ODI, Present Pain Intensity, or Beck Depression Inventory (15).

TGF-β1

Two studies by Chen et al. evaluated TGF-β1 association specifically in paraspinal tissues with postoperative pain outcomes (18,19). In both studies, paraspinal tissue samples were collected intraoperatively at the index surgery. Postoperative pain and disability scores were assessed at 6 months, though only the 2023 cohort demonstrated significant correlations between tissue TGF-β1 expression and postoperative VAS and ODI scores. Chen et al. [2023] reported that TGF-β1 expression in multifidus muscle correlated moderately with postoperative VAS low back pain (r=0.682, P<0.001), VAS leg pain (r=0.387, P=0.012), and ODI (r=0.354, P=0.023) (18). By contrast, in the smaller microdiscectomy cohort of 21 patients, Chen et al. [2021] found that TGF-β1 expression in deep multifidus muscle was significantly associated with preoperative VAS low back pain (r=0.652, P<0.05) but not with postoperative outcomes (19).

PAI-1

One study by Elkan et al. prospectively evaluated 177 patients undergoing lumbar disc herniation surgery and found that preoperative PAI-1 levels were strongly predictive of poor postoperative outcomes (20). Patients in the upper half of preoperative PAI-1 had significantly increased odds of being in the worst quartile for VAS back pain (OR =3.33, 95% CI: 1.56–7.10), VAS leg pain (OR =2.46, 95% CI: 1.18–5.10), ODI (OR =2.83, 95% CI: 1.35–5.94), and EQ-5D (OR =2.73, 95% CI: 1.30–5.75) at 12 months postoperatively. These associations peaked at 6–12 months after surgery before declining at later follow-up (20). Preoperative serum samples were drawn the day before surgery, and PROs were longitudinally assessed at 6 weeks, 6 months, 12 months, and 24 months.

IL-4

One study by Zu et al. evaluated serum IL-4 in 262 patients before surgery with lumbar radiculopathy undergoing microdiscectomy and followed them for 12 months (17). IL-4 levels were significantly higher in patients with lower pain scores (VAS <3) compared to those with persistent pain (VAS ≥3) at baseline (5.03 vs. 3.46 pg/mL, P<0.001), at 1 month (5.41 vs. 5.09 pg/mL, P<0.001), and at 12 months (5.14 vs. 3.87 pg/mL, P<0.001) (17).

Eotaxin/CCL11, MMP-1, and BDNF

One study examined less commonly studied mediators and their relationship to postoperative pain. Chen et al. 2021 found that BDNF expression in deep multifidus muscle was 91% lower in patients with poor 6-month postoperative VAS recovery compared to those with good recovery (P=0.014) (19).

Discussion

In this systematic review of biomarkers tied to PROs after single-level discectomy, we identified two reproducible signal clusters. First, higher preoperative levels of pro-inflammatory and neuronal injury markers including TNF-α, IFN-γ, and CRP were consistently associated with worse postoperative pain and disability, supporting the hypothesis that persistent neuroinflammation and axonal damage limit recovery (21-24). Second, mediators linked to reparative tissue remodeling and neurotrophic/immunoregulatory processes including IL-4 and BDNF tended to align with greater postoperative improvement. This supports previous work that postoperative recovery reflects not only relief of compression but also a biologic shift from pro-inflammatory to resolution and remodeling states (25-28).

Other studies also observed similar signal clusters that investigated biomarker-outcome relationships but did not meet our strict inclusion criteria. For example, Coquelet et al. profiled 42 plasma analytes in 77 patients undergoing spine surgery and found that higher preoperative CRP and NfL predicted worse short-term recovery, while higher CCL22, a chemokine that limits excessive inflammation, was associated with better recovery (5). The outcomes in this study were neurosurgeon-rated at two months rather than patient-reported, which excluded the study from our synthesis, but the findings aligned with the observed pattern in this review. In another study, Karamian et al., in a prospective cohort of 77 patients undergoing anterior cervical discectomy and fusion for cervical spondylosis, found weak correlations of serum IL-6 was associated with modest improvement in SF-12 Mental Component Summary (MCS) and MCS recovery ratio (ρ=0.306 and ρ=0.321, respectively) (29), and also preoperative TNF-α levels were not significantly associated with pre- or postoperative VAS or ODI, but did not investigate the lumbar spine. Jacobsen et al., in a prospective cohort study, investigated 78 patients with disc herniation against 58 controls and found that higher preoperative MMP-1, which helps with tissue remodeling and repair process, axonal regeneration, and glial activation, was significantly associated with better VAS but preoperatively measured (30). In yet another study, Koerner et al., investigating patients with discogenic low back pain undergoing anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF), reported a significant positive correlation between IL-6 expression in disc tissue and both postoperative VAS (ρ=0.38, P=0.048) and ODI scores (ρ=0.44, P=0.02) at 6 months (31).

The studies aggregated here complement established clinical, psychosocial, and imaging predictors for discectomy outcomes. Large registries and cohorts consistently associate older age, female sex, higher body mass index (BMI), cardiologic comorbidity, regular preoperative analgesic use, longer symptom duration particularly beyond one year, and unemployment with poorer disability and satisfaction outcomes. In contrast, shorter preoperative symptom duration and employment predict better results (32-34). Reviews also highlight that higher baseline leg-pain severity and better preoperative mental health favor improvement, while workers’ compensation, longer sick leave, and greater baseline disability predict worse recovery (3,35). On imaging, greater thecal sac or root compression and absence of Modic type I changes are also associated with greater surgical benefit (36,37). In addition, psychosocial factors, especially depression and occupational stress, remain strong determinants of return-to-work and global recovery (38). Within this landscape, serum biomarker panels are promising because they may capture the underlying biology that integrates these heterogeneous clinical features into recovery trajectories.

Mechanistically, TNF-α and IFN-γ produced by activated macrophages and T cells within herniated disc and perineural tissues amplify NF-κB driven transcription, upregulate matrix-degrading enzymes, and sensitize nociceptors via ion-channel modulation. They also prime microglia and astrocytes, sustaining neuroinflammation and central sensitization, which rationalizes poorer PROs when these mediators remain elevated (13,24,39-41). CRP reflects hepatic acute-phase responses to IL-6 and TNF-α signaling and correlates with local disc and peri-radicular inflammation. Persistently high levels likely index unresolved tissue injury and nociceptive drive. While IL-6 and STAT3 signaling support debris clearance, Schwann cell responses, and tissue repair in the subacute phase, acutely they are pro-inflammatory. Cohort heterogeneity around timing likely underlies some of the inconsistent associations found (25,26,42). In contrast, mediators tied to resolution and remodeling appear favorable when measured at preoperative timepoints. IL-4 skews macrophages toward an M2 reparative phenotype and dampens nociceptive signaling (43). Chemokines and remodeling enzymes including CCL22 which recruits T regulatory cells, MMP-1 which promotes collagen turnover, and CCL11 which contributes to matrix remodeling and wound healing track with biological processes that facilitate decompression-related recovery and symptom improvement (28,30,44). Together, these findings support a temporal shift from a TNF-α and IFN-γ and acute-phase dominant milieu toward a pro-resolution and remodeling profile as a mechanistic bridge to the observed PRO associations (22).

Together, these patterns suggest testable hypotheses about mechanisms that could inform future clinical use. Elevated IL-6/IFN-γ and PAI-1 point to neuroinflammatory priming and fibrosis, whereas higher IL-4, MMP-1, and BDNF align with pro-resolution remodeling and neurotrophic support (Figure 2). Future pre-clinical studies to test IL-6/Th1 attenuation, IL-4-mediated macrophage polarization, and PAI-1/anti-fibrotic strategies on pain and disc integrity in lumbar radiculopathy models are needed. Moreover, future patient studies integrating pre-op serum and intraoperative tissue profiling with longitudinal PROs (6 weeks–6 months) to establish long-term correlations will further advance the potential of these predictive biomarkers. If confirmed, prehabilitation to raise as an example BDNF or local IL-4 targeted delivery at decompression, could be evaluated for their ability to increase MCID attainment in pain and disability.

Furthermore, these signals suggest near-term roles in preoperative counseling and shared decision-making. In patients with adverse clinical profiles such as long symptom duration, unemployment, high BMI, or poorer mental health, a high-inflammation and high-injury biomarker pattern could reinforce expectations of slower or smaller PRO gains. Conversely, a reparative-leaning profile may support optimism about achieving MCID thresholds in pain or disability (3,33,45).

Rather than serving as stand-alone gatekeepers for surgical candidacy, perioperative biomarker profiles could inform when to operate and how to individualize perioperative care. Patients with a high-inflammation/high-injury signature (e.g. elevated IL-6/CRP or PAI-1 with low IL-4/BDNF) might benefit from a short-structured optimization window prior to surgery, in addition care teams may need to more closely monitor these patients occurrence of infection or opioid use dependence, as previous research has found correlation between perioperative inflammation and negative postoperative results (46). However, reparative-leaning profiles support standard timing and more favorable PRO projections. In complex presentations, biomarkers could refine risk-benefit discussions and selection of adjuncts without superseding neurological indications or patient values. Prospective validation with decision-curve analysis should confirm thresholds that meaningfully change management and improve MCID attainment. A strength of this review is its focused scope on serum and tissue biomarkers directly paired with validated PROs after a uniform procedure (single-level lumbar discectomy or microdiscectomy), enabling biologically coherent synthesis. By limiting inclusion to studies that incorporated quantitative biomarker data and standardized PRO instruments, this work links inflammatory, reparative and neurotrophic signaling pathways to functional recovery. This framework thus highlights plausible biological mechanisms underlying differential postoperative improvement and identifies candidates for translational and clinical validation studies in the future.

However, there are several limitations to this review. First, the studies gathered in this work had very large heterogeneity. This variability in assay platforms, reporting units, blood-draw timing, PRO instruments, follow-up horizons, and covariate adjustment likely contributed to the mixed findings across studies for some biomarkers. The broad inclusion of multiple biomarker classes and diverse PRO instruments, while intentional to capture the full scope of an emerging field, may have further increased clinical and methodological heterogeneity and limited the ability to generate more precise, biomarker-specific conclusions. Disease stage, chronicity, and anatomical features including degree of root compression and Modic changes further modify associations but were inconsistently captured (36,37). This variability likely underlies null or inconsistent findings for IL-1β, IL-6, TGF- β1, and TNF-α underscoring the need for time-controlled sampling and careful adjustment for disease stage (25-27,42). Due to the lack of consistency in the reporting of results, meta-analysis was unable to be conducted. Second, most studies in this review were small and single center cohort studies and did not adjust for confounders such as age, BMI, symptom duration, comorbidities, psychosocial status, and imaging findings which limit the generalizability of what is reported here. Third, this review must acknowledge publication bias. Published studies likely preferentially report significant biomarker-outcome associations, and insignificant associations are likely not published. As a result, despite our methodology, the findings in this report do not fully represent all the associations with certain biomarkers and post-operative outcomes that have been studied.

Future work should prospectively standardize pre-analytical handling and assay platforms, anchor blood-draw timing to clinically meaningful windows, standardize measurement of PROs, and employ blinded biomarker measurement with predefined primary endpoints such as ODI or pain MCID at 6–12 months (25,26). A preoperative panel with markers such as IL-6, TNF-α, CRP, with or without CCL22, MMP-1, or CCL11 should be evaluated alongside established clinical and imaging predictors, using rigorous model development, internal validation, and external validation across centers and assay lots (30,44). Clinical utility must then be demonstrated with decision-curve analysis to define actionable thresholds, followed by potential randomized trials that test whether biomarker-informed care improves responder rates, reduces unnecessary interventions, and is cost-effective. Ongoing biomarker-driven trials in spine populations, including a large prospective effort focused on phenotypic stratification in chronic low back pain, may provide further framework for integrating preoperative biomarkers into clinical care (47). However, these studies remain largely nonoperative, and their extension into surgical populations will be essential to determine whether biomarker-informed strategies can improve postoperative outcomes.

Conclusions

Preoperative biomarker profiles show promise for risk stratification after single-level discectomy. Signals most consistently associated with worse pain or recovery include TNF-α, IFN-γ, and acute-phase proteins (CRP). Markers associated with better postoperative improvement include IL-4 and BDNF. Given small sample sizes, assay variability, and heterogeneity in PRO measurement, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating. Future prospective studies should standardize biomarker panels and thresholds, prespecify PROs and time points, and test multivariable models integrating clinical covariates to enable clinical translation.

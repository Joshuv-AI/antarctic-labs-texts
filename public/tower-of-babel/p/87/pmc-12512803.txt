Predicting surgical NEC in neonates: risk factors and model development
Authors: Mingyun Tang, Xiaofei Ma, Yuan Gan, Yanxing Lv, Yunzhi Liu, Yongjiang Jiang, Xiaolan Li, Yueju Cai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNeonatal necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is a severe gastrointestinal emergency associated with high morbidity and mortality in neonates. Early identification of cases requiring surgical intervention is critical to improving outcomes.ObjectiveTo identify independent risk factors associated with surgical treatment in neonates with NEC and to develop a predictive model that supports timely surgical decision-making and enhances prognosis.MethodsThis retrospective study included 188 neonates diagnosed with NEC (Bell stage II or higher) at Liuzhou Hospital, Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center between December 2018 and December 2024. Patients were categorized into a surgical treatment group (n = 70) and a conservative treatment group (n = 118). Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and multivariable logistic regression were used to identify independent risk factors. A nomogram was developed based on these factors. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsAmong the 188 neonates, 70 (37.2%) underwent surgery. The mortality rate was significantly higher in the surgical group compared to the conservative group (17.1% vs. 1.1%). Multivariable analysis identified C-reactive protein (CRP), serum lactate, portal venous gas, reduced intestinal motility, white blood cell count, and absolute lymphocyte count as independent predictors of surgical intervention. The nomogram model demonstrated excellent discrimination (AUC = 0.946, 95% CI: 0.911–0.981), with a sensitivity of 0.914 and specificity of 0.890. Calibration and DCA confirmed strong clinical utility.ConclusionThis study established a nomogram-based predictive model for surgical treatment in NEC, utilizing six indicators: CRP, serum lactate, portal venous gas, reduced intestinal motility, white blood cell count, and absolute lymphocyte count. The model shows high accuracy and clinical utility. It can assist clinicians in predicting the need for surgery at the early stages of NEC, optimizing treatment decisions, reducing mortality, and improving the prognosis of affected neonates.

Introduction

Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is a common gastrointestinal emergency in neonates within the NICU. Its etiology is complex, and early symptoms and imaging findings are often atypical. The disease progresses rapidly, and the mortality rate remains high [1]– [2]. Reports indicate that the incidence of NEC in extremely low birth weight infants ranges from approximately 4.5% to 8.7%, with a mortality rate of 20% to 30% [3]. In infants with very low birth weight, the mortality rate can reach as high as 30% to 50.9% [4]– [5]. Some survivors may experience complications such as short bowel syndrome, intestinal strictures, growth retardation, and neuro-motor dysfunction, which significantly affect the quality of life of the affected infants and place substantial challenges and burdens on families and society [6]– [7].

The treatment of NEC involves both surgical and conservative management. Reports suggest that 27% to 52% of infants with NEC require surgical intervention [8]. Failure to identify the appropriate timing for surgery in the early stages can increase the risk of poor outcomes by 4.5 times [9]. Determining the optimal timing for surgery in NEC remains a significant challenge [10]. While pneumoperitoneum is an absolute indication for surgery in NEC [11], for infants who have not developed intestinal perforation or pneumoperitoneum, early prediction of the risk for surgery is crucial. This can help guide clinical decisions and potentially preserve viable bowel tissue, which is key to improving the prognosis of NEC infants.

Currently, various scoring systems, biomarkers, imaging techniques, and invasive methods have been reported to identify surgical NEC, with varying degrees of effectiveness [12–14]. For instance, Tepas et al. [15] proposed seven metabolic markers to assess the need for surgical intervention in late-stage NEC, which may be of some significance; however, their utility in early-stage prediction is limited. Overall, it remains unclear which methods are most effective, and the feasibility of translating these methods into clinical practice is a critical consideration.

This study conducts a retrospective analysis of the perinatal conditions, respiratory support, antibiotic exposure, feeding status, associated comorbidities, clinical presentations, laboratory parameters, and imaging characteristics within 24 h of NEC onset in neonates treated at Liuzhou Hospital, Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center. The aim is to identify risk factors for the need for surgical intervention in NEC and to develop a predictive model for the early identification of infants who may require surgery. This model will provide a foundation for early detection of severe NEC, assist in determining the optimal timing for surgery, and ultimately help reduce the mortality and improve the prognosis of NEC infants.

Methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective study collected data from neonates diagnosed with NEC (Bell stage ≥ II) at Liuzhou Hospital, Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, between December 2018 and December 2024.

Inclusion criteria

1)Clinical and imaging findings consistent with the diagnostic criteria for NEC; 2༉NEC diagnosed with Bell stage II or higher; 3༉Complete clinical data available.

Exclusion criteria

1)NEC diagnosed with Bell stage I; 2༉Congenital intestinal malformations such as congenital intestinal atresia, congenital megacolon, or gastrointestinal duplication; 3༉Cyanotic congenital heart disease; 4༉Genetic metabolic disorders; 5༉Spontaneous intestinal perforation.

Patients were divided into two groups based on whether they received surgical treatment: the surgical treatment group (n = 70) and the conservative treatment group (n = 118) (Fig. 1). This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Liuzhou Hospital, Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center (Ethical approval number: 2025-005), and the requirement for informed consent from the families of the infants was waived.

Definitions of clinical conditions

The diagnostic criteria for NEC are primarily based on clinical manifestations (such as feeding intolerance, abdominal distension, vomiting, and bloody stools) and imaging features (such as pneumatosis intestinalis, portal venous gas, or free intraperitoneal air), with disease severity classified according to Bell’s staging system [16] (Stage I: suspected, Stage II: confirmed, Stage III: severe). PDA refers to infants with patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) detected by echocardiography, including both hemodynamically significant and medically treated PDA. Sepsis includes both blood culture-positive sepsis and blood culture-negative clinical sepsis. Anemia is defined as hemoglobin (Hb) or hematocrit (HCT) levels falling below the normal physiological reference range. A restrictive transfusion strategy was adopted, with transfusion thresholds based primarily on HCT/Hb levels, postnatal age, and the clinical status of the infant. Shock refers to inadequate tissue perfusion due to circulatory dysfunction, characterized by prolonged capillary refill time, abnormal heart rate, low blood pressure, oliguria, and metabolic acidosis, requiring treatment with vasoactive drugs.

Surgical indications

Surgical intervention for NEC is indicated when there is radiographic evidence of intestinal perforation, such as pneumoperitoneum, or when the patient’s condition deteriorates despite optimal medical management.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 and R 4.3.0 to ensure robustness and cross-platform validation. Data were presented as means with standard deviation or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), depending on the distribution. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (n) and percentages (%). Group differences for normally distributed variables were analyzed using the independent sample t-test, and non-normally distributed variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. The chi-square test was used for categorical variables. Variables with statistical significance in the univariate analysis between the two groups were subjected to least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression to identify the most relevant predictors. Multivariate logistic regression was then performed to determine independent risk factors for surgical intervention in NEC. A nomogram was constructed to visualize the predictive model, and its discriminative ability was quantitatively assessed using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, with the area under the curve (AUC) calculated to evaluate model performance. The calibration of the model was validated using 1,000 bootstrap resampling iterations. Finally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was applied to assess the clinical utility of the predictive model. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics

A total of 188 neonates with NEC were included, of whom 101 (53.7%) were male, 55 (29.3%) were full-term infants, and 133 (70.7%) were preterm infants. Among these, 70 (37.2%, 70/188) required surgical treatment, including 59 preterm infants and 11 full-term infants; 118 (62.8%, 118/188) received conservative treatment, including 74 preterm infants and 44 full-term infants. The overall mortality rate was 7.4% (14/188), with 12 deaths (85.7%) in the surgical treatment group, including 11 preterm infants and 1 full-term infant, and 2 deaths (14.3%) in the conservative treatment group, including 1 preterm infant and 1 full-term infant(Figure 2).

Among the 70 neonates who received surgical treatment, 59 (84.3%) were preterm infants, with a median NEC onset age of 12.0 (6.0, 23.0) days and a median surgical age of 13.0 (7.6, 24.0) days, with a median interval of 1.0 (0.6, 1.0) day. The mortality rate for preterm infants was 18.6% (11/59). The remaining 11 (15.7%) were full-term infants, with a median NEC onset age of 3.0 (2.4, 9.0) days and a median surgical age of 5.1 (3.5, 10.0) days, with a median interval of 1.0 (0.6, 2.0) day. The mortality rate for full-term infants was 9.1% (1/11).

The distribution of gestational ages among the 70 neonates who underwent surgery was as follows: ≤28 weeks, 16 (22.9%); 28 + 1 to 32 weeks, 22 (31.4%); 32 + 1 to 34 weeks, 9 (12.9%); 34 + 1 to 36 + 6 weeks, 12 (17.1%); 37 weeks, 11 (15.7%)(Figure 3).

LASSO regression and multivariable logistic regression

The factors with statistically significant differences from the univariate analysis were included in the LASSO regression analysis. As shown in Fig. 4, the penalty function was applied to shrink the regression coefficients of irrelevant variables to zero. Using 10-fold cross-validation, the optimal λ was selected as the value corresponding to the minimum value plus one standard error, as shown in Fig. 5. This selection identified 12 optimal predictors.

Subsequent multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed that elevated CRP within 24 h of NEC onset (OR = 1.030, P < 0.001), increased serum lactate levels (OR = 1.414, P = 0.018), portal venous gas (OR = 6.821, P = 0.009), and ultrasound-detected reduced intestinal motility (OR = 5.337, P = 0.043) were independent risk factors for the need for surgical treatment in NEC. Conversely, increased white blood cell count (OR = 0.834, P = 0.007) and elevated absolute lymphocyte count (OR = 0.586, P = 0.043) were protective factors. Details are provided in Table 4.

Discussion

Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is a severe gastrointestinal condition affecting neonates, characterized by a complex etiology and posing significant challenges for early diagnosis. Treatment options for NEC include both conservative and surgical management, with the timing of surgery being a critical factor in clinical decision-making. This study retrospectively analyzed the risk factors that determine the need for surgical intervention in neonates with NEC, and successfully developed a predictive model using clinical and laboratory characteristics. Our findings indicate that changes in CRP levels, serum lactate, portal venous gas, reduced intestinal motility, white blood cell count, and absolute lymphocyte count within 24 h of NEC onset are strongly associated with the need for surgical requirements in NEC infants, thereby enhancing the accuracy of clinical decision-making.

Portal venous gas (PVG), a common complication of NEC, is typically associated with intestinal necrosis and perforation. PVG occurs when gases from necrotic bowel tissue enter the portal venous system, circulating to the liver and other organs, and is often identified as a characteristic finding on abdominal imaging. PVG has been recognized as an independent risk factor for both the need for surgical intervention and poor prognosis in NEC. Chen et al. [17] reported that, in a cohort of 585 NEC infants, those with PVG had a higher risk of requiring surgical intervention or dying compared to those without PVG. In cases of NEC stage II/III requiring surgery, PVG is strongly associated with intestinal necrosis [18]. A prospective cohort study in the United States, including 194 NEC infants, found that the risk of needing surgery in those with PVG was significantly higher than in those without PVG (hazard ratio (OR) = 2.50, 95% CI: 1.37–4.76, P = 0.003) [19]. Yu He et al. [20] also confirmed that preoperative imaging showing PVG could identify NEC infants requiring surgery, with a specificity of 97.3%. Our study further supports these findings, showing that the incidence of PVG in the surgical treatment group was significantly higher than in the conservative treatment group. PVG was identified as an independent risk factor for surgery, closely linked to the severity and extent of NEC. This underscores the importance for clinicians to strongly suspect NEC progression when PVG is observed.

Ultrasound examination offers the advantages of being non-invasive, real-time, and convenient, making it especially valuable in the early stages of NEC, when other imaging techniques may not clearly reveal bowel damage. In this study, reduced intestinal motility was significantly associated with the need for surgery, indicating severe functional impairment of the intestine. A systematic review and meta-analysis showed that ultrasound findings, such as reduced or absent peristalsis and changes in bowel wall thickness, were linked to the need for surgery or death in NEC patients [21]. Chen et al. [22] identified bowel wall thickness, reduced peristalsis, absence of bowel wall perfusion, and portal venous gas as risk factors for surgical intervention in NEC.Their predictive model, based on these four ultrasound signs showed, demonstrated high reliability, helping clinicians detect bowel necrosis and perforation early and optimize the timing of surgical intervention.

In our study, elevated CRP was identified as an important independent risk factor for the need for surgery in infants with NEC. CRP, a routine marker of inflammation, reflects the body’s systemic inflammatory response, and its elevation serves as an important monitoring marker in the early stages of NEC. Yu et al. [23] analyzed 267 NEC infants and found that higher CRP levels (37.09 mg/L, IQR: 4.77–99.77) were associated with an increased risk of requiring surgical intervention (OR = 1.009, 95% CI: 1.003–1.016, p = 0.004). Wang et al. [24] reported that CRP had an AUC of 0.805 for determining the timing of surgery in NEC, with a sensitivity of 75.9% and specificity of 80.4%, highlighting its utility in surgical decision-making.

Additionally,, our study found that elevated serum lactate was an independent predictor of the need for surgery in NEC infants. This is consistent with the role of lactate as a sensitive indicator of tissue hypoxia and metabolic disturbance, suggesting that increased lactate may signal the progression of NEC to a more severe stage. Moreover, procalcitonin and fibrinogen have been validated as potential biomarkers for predicting surgical intervention in NEC in other studies. Notably, Yu et al. [25]found that procalcitonin outperformed both CRP and lactate in predicting surgical NEC. However, our study did not include procalcitonin or fibrinogen as markers, which represents a limitation. Future research may consider incorporating procalcitonin and fibrinogen into predictive models to improve theirs accuracy and clinical applicability.

Interestingly, our study also found that elevated white blood cell count and absolute lymphocyte count appeared to have a protective effect to some extent. White blood cell count, an inflammatory marker, typically rises in response to infection; however, in some severe infections, it may decrease. Garg et al. [26] conducted a retrospective analysis of 336 infants diagnosed with NEC Bell stage II or higher between 2000 and 2018,finding that leukopenia within 48 h of onset was a significant risk factor for poor prognosis in NEC. The relationship between peripheral blood absolute lymphocyte count and the severity of NEC remains debated. Pantalone et al. [27] found that the absolute lymphocyte count at the onset of disease was significantly lower in the surgical group compared to the conservative treatment group.In contrast, Gordon et al. [28] suggested that lymphocytosis could be an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in NEC infants.

Building on these independent risk factors, this study developed a predictive model for the need for surgical intervention in NEC. The model achieved an AUC of 0.946, demonstrating excellent predictive performance. Compared to previous studies, our model not only incorporates classic biomarkers such as CRP and lactate but also includes imaging parameters (such as portal venous gas and reduced peristalsis), thereby enhancing both the accuracy and applicability of the prediction. We further validated the model’s clinical potential through ROC curve analysis, calibration curve analysis, and decision curve analysis (DCA).The DCA results indicated that the model provided a high net benefit across most clinical decision thresholds, underscoring its significant clinical utility.

Despite the high predictive efficacy and clinical applicability of our study, several limitations exist. The single-center design and relatively small sample size, particularly the 70 cases in the surgical group, may affect the model’s generalizability. Additionally, the use of the same dataset for both model development and performance evaluation may lead to overfitting. As a retrospective study, it may also be subject to selection and information biases. To enhance the model’s generalizability and robustness, future multicenter, large-sample prospective studies are essential. These studies should include external validation, consider splitting the dataset into separate training and validation sets, or use cross-validation techniques to avoid overfitting and better assess model performance.

In this study, the predictive model for surgical intervention in NEC that we proposed has certain novelty. Compared to existing studies, our model provides a more comprehensive predictive ability by combining clinical data (such as clinical symptoms and signs) with laboratory markers (such as CRP, lactate, etc.). Additionally, we used a nomogram as an intuitive tool, which helps clinicians more easily apply the model, thereby improving the efficiency and accuracy of clinical decision-making.

In conclusion, this study identifies key independent risk factors for the need for surgical intervention in NEC and develops an efficient predictive model based on these factors. The model’s high accuracy and clinical applicability provide valuable evidence for early diagnosis and informed treatment decision-making in NEC, potentially reducing mortality and improving the prognosis for affected infants.

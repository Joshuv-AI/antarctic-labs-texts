Trends in the etiology of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure: A retrospective study in Southwest China (2020–2023)
Authors: Yi Fang, Haifeng Dai, Qiufen Li, Danni Wang, Jianwen Liu, Yanan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The etiology of acute insults and underlying chronic liver disease in acute-on-chronic liver failure has evolved in recent years. This study examined etiological patterns and trends from 2020 to 2023 in Southwest China. This study is a retrospective analysis that included 346 patients diagnosed with acute-on-chronic liver failure according to the Asian pacific association for the study of liver criteria at our hospital from 2020 to 2023. The etiology of acute insult and chronic liver disease and changing trends from 2020 to 2023 were analyzed. The top 5 etiologies of acute insult were spontaneous hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation (45.4%), HBV reactivation due to nucleot(s)ide analog withdrawal (16.2%), drug-induced liver injury (16.2%), unknown (6.1%), and alcoholic hepatitis (4.6%). The top 5 etiologies of chronic liver disease were chronic hepatitis B (53.2%), HBV-associated cirrhosis (22.00%), chronic hepatitis B plus alcoholic hepatitis (6.6%), HBV plus alcoholic hepatitis-associated cirrhosis (6.4%), and alcoholic hepatitis-associated cirrhosis (3.8%). From 2020 to 2023, the percentage of patients with viral hepatitis initially declined and then increased. The percentage of patients with alcoholic hepatitis showed a slight declining trend. The proportion of HBV-associated cirrhosis increased from 18.8% to 29.9%. The percentage of patients with chronic hepatitis B plus alcoholic hepatitis decreased from 11.8% to 2.6%, respectively. Viral hepatitis, especially chronic hepatitis B, remains the leading cause of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in Southwest China. These findings underscore the need for targeted HBV management strategies.

1. Introduction

Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) is a disease entity presenting as an acute deterioration of chronic liver disease. Currently, the definition and diagnostic criteria of ACLF proposed by the Asian Pacific association for the study of liver (APASL) disease, the North American consortium for the study of end-stage liver disease, and the European association for the study of the liver-chronic liver failure (EASL-CLIF) consortium are not completely consistent.[1] The incidence and mortality rates of acute-on-chronic liver failure are high in clinical practice;[2] however, there is no particularly effective treatment available for patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure except for liver transplantation.[3]

The definitions and diagnostic criteria for ACLF proposed by EASL-CLIF, NACSELD, and APASL have different emphases. However, in current clinical practice in China, ACLF is mostly caused by hepatitis B virus (HBV) and primarily presents as liver failure rather than extrahepatic organ failure. Therefore, the APASL criteria are adopted in this study. The updated ACLF consensus of the APASL in 2019 considered that the APASL definition was simple and homogenous, thus allowing a better chance of reversibility and improved survival. Acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in APASL-ACLF are clearly defined and recorded.[4] Many factors influence the outcome of ACLF, with acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease being key issues. For a patient with ACLF, the identification of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease is useful in preventing disease progression and in predicting the probability of regression.[5,6] The findings of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease provide a source for tracking and educating key patient populations, which will contribute to reducing the incidence of ACLF and saving more lives.

According to the APASL definition, acute insult includes infectious and noninfectious etiologies. The major infectious etiologies in Asians are HBV reactivation, hepatitis C virus (HCV) reactivation, and hepatitis A virus/hepatitis E virus (HAV/HEV) superinfection. Alcoholic hepatitis, drug-induced liver injury (DILI), autoimmune hepatitis flare, acute variceal bleeding, acute hepatic venous outflow tract obstruction, and portal vein thrombosis are noninfectious insults. Underlying chronic liver disease includes 2 aspects: cirrhosis alone or non-cirrhotic chronic liver disease, and the etiology of chronic liver disease. Differences in the epidemiology and etiology of liver disease occur because of differences in standards of living, alcohol consumption, and vaccination coverage worldwide.[7,8] Hence, the etiology of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in ACLF is diverse in China, the Asia-Pacific region, Europe, and the United States. The epidemiology of liver diseases in the Asia-Pacific region has changed in recent years.[9] The results of studies related to acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in patients with ACLF are confusing owing to the differences in definition among the researchers.

Southwest China represents a distinct epidemiological setting due to its unique patterns of chronic liver disease (e.g., higher prevalence of HBV-related cirrhosis) and acute insults (e.g., herbal medicine-related liver injury, alcoholic hepatitis), which may not be generalizable from studies conducted in Eastern or Northern China. This study aimed to analyze the etiologies of acute insults and underlying chronic liver disease in patients with ACLF in Southwest China, based on APASL criteria.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data collection

In this retrospective study, we searched for patients admitted to The First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University from January 1, 2020 to December 31, 2023 using the following terms: “liver failure” or “liver function failure” or “severe hepatitis” in our electronic medical record system. A total of 780 hospitalized patients were identified. We then reviewed the detailed clinical and laboratory data of each patient. Finally, 346 patients were enrolled in this study based on the APASL criteria. The screening and enrollment processes are shown in Figure 1. Through a detailed analysis of the hospitalization records of the enrolled patients, the admission date, acute insult, and chronic liver disease for each patient were recorded.

Flowchart of the study patient enrollment process. ACLF = acute-on-chronic liver failure, APASL = Asian Pacific association for the study of liver.

2.2. Study definition

ACLF is diagnosed when a patient experiences an acute event on the basis of preceding liver disease and simultaneously meets the following conditions: serum bilirubin ≥ 5 mg/dL; international normalized ratio ≥ 1.5 or prothrombin activity < 40%; and the appearance of ascites and/or encephalopathy within 4 weeks.[4]

To determine the etiology of acute insult and chronic liver disease, a detailed history of alcohol consumption, drug intake, or previous diagnosis of chronic liver disease was obtained. Laboratory tests, such as hepatitis viral biomarkers (HAV/HBV/HCV/HDV/HEV), HBV DNA, HCV RNA, autoimmune hepatitis antibodies, ceruloplasmin, radiologic imaging, and endoscopy, were carefully reviewed.

2.2.1. Acute insult

Hepatitis B virus reactivation ought to be characterized as a huge expansion in hepatitis B virus replication (≥2 log increment from benchmark levels or a new appearance of hepatitis B virus DNA to a degree of ≥100 IU/mL) in a patient with previously stable or imperceptible levels or identification of hepatitis B virus DNA, with a level ≥20,000 IU/mL in a patient with no standard hepatitis B virus DNA.[10,11] Reactivation of hepatitis B virus could be either spontaneous or due to immunosuppressive therapy, nucleot(s)ide analog (NAs) withdrawal, antiviral resistance, or human immunodeficiency viral infection. Acute hepatitis viral infection was diagnosed based on the clinical course and presence of immunoglobulin M antibodies. Acute alcoholic hepatitis was considered when, in addition to chronic ethanol abuse (>5 years), active alcohol consumption was documented in the last 28 days and other causes of acute insult were ruled out. DILI was considered in the setting of proper history and temporal relationship between drug consumption and liver failure. Pregnancy and hyperthyroidism were considered as non-hepatotropic factors producing direct hepatic insult per serum human chorionic gonadotropin and thyroid hormone measurements.

2.2.2. Chronic liver disease

Diagnosis of chronic liver disease and cirrhosis in the setting of ACLF was made based on the history, physical examination, and laboratory, endoscopic, or radiological investigations. Hepatic decompensation was characterized as significant liver dysfunction, as demonstrated by increased serum bilirubin (>2.5 times the upper limit of normal) and prolonged prothrombin time (prolonged by >3 seconds), or the occurrence of complications such as ascites and hepatic encephalopathy.

2.3. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were communicated as the mean, confidence interval (95%), and analyzed utilizing Student t test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables were introduced as frequencies, percentages, confidence interval (95%), and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Statistical significance was set at P < .05, and all statistical analyses were accomplished utilizing Microsoft Office Excel 2023 (Microsoft, Redmond) and SPSS release 28.0 for Windows (IBM, Chicago).

3. Results

3.1. Distribution of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure

A total of 346 patients who fulfilled the APASL criteria for ACLF were enrolled and analyzed for the etiology of acute insult and chronic liver disease. Overall, 13 kinds of acute insults were identified in this study. The top 5 etiologies of acute insult were spontaneous hepatitis B virus reactivation (45.4%), hepatitis B virus reactivation due to nucleot(s)ide analog withdrawal (16.2%), DILI (16.2%), unknown (6.1%), and alcoholic hepatitis (4.6%; Fig. 2). Hepatitis B virus reactivation together accounted for 68.8% of the etiologies of acute insults. Among the 56 patients with DILI, antitubercular drugs were the most common cause and were present in 23 (41.07%) patients. Chinese herbs, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, complementary medicines, and others were present in 21 (37.50%), 3 (5.36%), 3 (5.36%), and 6 (10.71%) patients, respectively.

Distribution of the etiology in patients with ACLF. (A) Acute insult. (B) Chronic liver disease. ACLF = acute-on-chronic liver failure, DILI = drug-induced liver injury, HAV/HEV = hepatitis A virus/hepatitis E virus, HBV = hepatitis B virus, HCV = hepatitis C virus.

In our study, there were 13 forms of chronic liver disease. The top 5 forms were chronic hepatitis B (53.2%), hepatitis B virus-associated cirrhosis (22.00%), chronic hepatitis B plus alcoholic hepatitis (6.6%), hepatitis B virus plus alcoholic hepatitis-associated cirrhosis (6.4%), and alcoholic hepatitis-associated cirrhosis (3.8%), as shown in Figure 2. Of the 346 patients, 120 (34.68%) had cirrhotic liver diseases and 226 (65.32%) had non-cirrhotic liver diseases. The percentage of patients with only one chronic factor was 85.3%; however, 14.7% had >1 chronic factor.

The combined forms of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease are shown in Table 1. The 5 leading forms were spontaneous hepatitis B virus reactivation with chronic hepatitis B (n = 94, 27.17%), spontaneous hepatitis B virus reactivation with hepatitis B virus-associated cirrhosis (n = 38, 10.98%), nucleot(s)ide analog withdrawal with chronic hepatitis B (n = 28, 8.09%), DILI with chronic hepatitis B (n = 26, 7.51%), and nucleot(s)ide analog withdrawal with hepatitis B virus-associated cirrhosis (n = 19, 5.49%).

3.2. Changing trends for acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease

Thirteen types of acute insult were merged into 4 groups, which included viral hepatitis, DILI, alcoholic hepatitis, and others. From 2020 to 2023, the percentage of patients with viral hepatitis presented with a trend of an initial decline and then a rise. The percentage of alcoholic hepatitis showed a slight declining trend (Fig. 3).

Changing trends of the etiology in patients with ACLF from 2020 to 2023. (A) Acute insult. (B) Chronic liver disease. ACLF = acute-on-chronic liver failure, HBV = hepatitis B virus.

Among the top 5 etiologies of underlying chronic liver disease, the percentage of hepatitis B virus-associated cirrhosis increased from 18.8% in 2020 to 29.9% in 2023 (P = .048). The percentage of patients with chronic hepatitis B plus alcoholic hepatitis decreased from 11.8% in 2020 to 2.6% in 2023 (P = .020; Fig. 3).

3.3. Demographic characteristics of patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure

Among the 346 patients, 343 (99.13%) were from Southwest China, including Chongqing (n = 274, 79.88%), Sichuan (n = 61, 17.78 %), Guizhou (n = 7, 2.04%), and Yunnan (n = 1, 0.29%). Another 3 (0.87%) patients were from Fujian Province.

In the present study, the number of male patients (n = 280, 80.92%) was significantly higher than that of female patients (n = 66, 19.08%). The mean age of the patients was 48.91 ± 13.45 years. The percentage of men was 60.7% in patients with ACLF due to other causes, which was lower than that in patients with ACLF due to viral hepatitis (83.3%, P = .009) and alcoholic hepatitis (100%, P = .003). Patients with ACLF due to other causes tended to be older than those due to viral hepatitis and alcoholic hepatitis, but the difference was not significant (P = .214). The percentage of men was 77.0% in patients with ACLF without cirrhosis, which was lower than that in those with cirrhosis (88.3%, P = .010). Patients with ACLF without cirrhosis tended to be younger than those with cirrhosis (P = .001). The percentage of male patients with ACLF whose etiology of chronic liver disease was a single factor was lower than that whose etiology of chronic liver disease was multiple factors (78% and 98%, respectively; P < .01). However, no significant difference in age was found between single and multiple factors (Table 2).

4. Discussion

The APASL criteria for ACLF are widely accepted and approved in the Asia-Pacific region, including China. Currently, there are limited studies on the etiology of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in Southwest China according to the APASL criteria for ACLF. From the results of the present study, hepatitis B virus reactivation and DILI constituted the major etiologies of acute insult, accounting for 85.0%. Among the hepatitis B virus reactivations causing ACLF, the most frequent cause of acute insult was spontaneous hepatitis B virus reactivation in 45.4% of patients, followed by hepatitis B virus reactivation due to nucleot(s)ide analog withdrawal in 16.2%. Our results are not consistent with those reported by Shalimar et al in India.[6] Their results indicated that alcoholic hepatitis was the commonest etiology of acute insults. Hepatitis B virus reactivation and HEV superinfection are causes of acute insult. A recent study on 1000 patients with ACLF across Asia showed that the etiology of acute insult in 48.6% of patients was alcoholic hepatitis.[12] However, the percentages of patients with alcoholic hepatitis and hepatitis A virus/hepatitis E virus superinfection were relatively low in our study, accounting for 4.6% and 1.4%, respectively. HEV infection in the Asia-Pacific region was epidemic, but HEV seroprevalence showed wide variations between countries, within countries, and from 1 population to another in several studies.[12-14] HEV superinfection is the major cause of ACLF and is associated with mortality in highly endemic countries.[15] Nevertheless, HEV superinfection accounts for a small percentage of acute insults in Southwest China.

Some recent studies have shown that the most prevalent etiology of chronic liver disease in patients was alcohol.[4,12,16] Two studies showed that the etiology of chronic liver disease was alcohol in 56.2% and 63.9% of patients, respectively. However, our study indicated that alcohol accounted for 18.8% of patients with chronic liver disease. Regarding cirrhosis alone or non-cirrhotic chronic liver diseases and the etiology of chronic liver disease, 13 forms of chronic liver disease were observed in our study. The major causes were non-cirrhotic factors, mainly chronic hepatitis B. Meanwhile, we found that 14.7% of patients had chronic liver diseases due to multiple factors.

The updated ACLF consensus of the APASL in 2023 indicated that alcohol had emerged as the most common etiology of acute insult as well as of underlying chronic liver disease, in contrast to previous data on hepatitis B virus predominance in the past 5 years.[4] However, there are no direct data on the changing trends in the etiology of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease according to the APASL criteria. We excavated the data from several studies that were performed using the AARC database in different years.[17] The results showed that alcohol accounted for about half of the etiologies for acute insult as well as for underlying chronic liver disease, and viral hepatitis accounted for about a quarter of that. In contrast, a global meta-analysis based on EASL-CLIF criteria reported that alcohol was the most frequent etiology of underlying chronic liver disease (45%) and infection was the most common acute trigger (35%).[12] Furthermore, comparative studies have demonstrated significant discordance between EASL-CLIF and NACSELD criteria in ACLF prevalence and mortality prediction.[18] Regional variations in ACLF epidemiology are largely explained by differences in the type of acute triggers and etiologies of chronic liver disease.[19] From the North American perspective, the NACSELD consortium has highlighted that infection-related ACLF outcomes are primarily determined by extrahepatic organ failures rather than liver failure per se.[20] However, the increasing trend in alcohol was not obvious with the change in years. In our study, alcohol showed a slight declining trend from 2020 to 2023. Viral hepatitis is still the leading cause of acute insult and chronic liver disease in Southwest China.

In this study, middle-aged male patients constituted a large proportion of patients with ACLF, similar to the results from the AARC database. Among the acute insults causing ACLF, the patients with viral hepatitis and alcoholic hepatitis had a higher percentage of males. The explanations for this were that male patients were lenient towards alcohol consumption in Southwest China, and they likely suffered from spontaneous hepatitis B virus reactivation.[21] Another interesting finding was that patients with >1 etiology of chronic liver disease had a higher percentage of males. A possible reason is that male patients continued to ingest alcohol and abuse drugs despite suffering from chronic hepatitis B virus infection.

Our study had several limitations. First, some of the etiologies of acute insult were inaccurately documented according to the medical records reviewed. Second, we could not accurately differentiate whether cirrhosis was present or not, and we could not determine the etiology of chronic liver disease without performing a liver biopsy. Further research is warranted to establish multicenter prospective cohorts for validating our findings across distinct geographical and etiological settings within China, in addition to performing external validation in other Asian populations. Additionally, prospective studies incorporating standardized liver biopsy protocols and improved documentation of acute insults are warranted to further clarify etiological patterns. Notably, a Chinese collaborative group (COSSH) has independently developed diagnostic criteria and a prognostic score specifically for HBV-related ACLF, which have shown good performance in Chinese patient cohorts.[22] These efforts complement the APASL criteria and provide additional tools for risk stratification in HBV-dominant regions.

5. Conclusion

The etiology of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in patients with ACLF is not the same in different countries of the Asia-Pacific region. Viral hepatitis, especially chronic hepatitis B virus infection, is still the leading cause of acute insult and underlying chronic liver disease in Southwest China. However, the percentage of alcoholic hepatitis has declined from 2020 to 2023. These findings underscore the need for targeted hepatitis B virus management strategies.

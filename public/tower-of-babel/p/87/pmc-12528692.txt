Settlement prediction of undercrossing semicircular tunnel-pile set up in soft clay soil
Authors: Norman Burua Adriko, Yong Fang, Kaichen Ying, Arif Khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Soil–structure interactions commonly induce displacements, with settlements in weak soils becoming increasingly significant under growing urbanization, making it important to minimize them to prevent structural defects. This study investigated the influence of tunnel–pile configurations on ground settlement during tunnel construction at Zhongshan Road with an undercrossing, using numerical simulation. Prediction functions describing settlement behavior within the model were developed and proposed. The Mohr–Coulomb (MC) model in MIDAS GTS NX was employed to simulate settlement levels of a semicircular tunnel lining and surrounding soil under various pile setups, in order to identify the most effective configuration for ground improvement. Results showed substantial variations in vertical settlement. The designed 5 × 4 m pile setup produced the highest crown settlement, increasing it by 32–47% longitudinally along the tunnel compared to the case without piles, while reducing lining uplift by only 138–165%. In contrast, the 4 × 4 m pile arrangement performed best overall, reducing uplift by 184–194% and limiting settlement fluctuations to − 12% to − 47%, maintaining values within 5–10 mm along the 80 m tunnel length. The 4 × 4 m pile setup was therefore more reliable and less variable in both settlement and uplift. Uniform pile arrangements provided smoother settlement troughs than non-uniform setups. Furthermore, prediction formulas based on Gaussian functions, using parameters n and k, reliably estimated settlement for greenfield conditions and projected behavior for tunnel undercrossings. Introduction and Introduction

Interaction of tunnel structures underground over urban centers is becoming more common, with the high demand for decongestion and alternative traffic diversions in the metropolitan areas in major cities1–3 . Bored tunnels are significantly prone to adverse settlements especially in untreated soft clay soils. The operation and durability of the tunnel is linked to structural integrity and its ability to withstand loads and deformation. Bored tunnel settlements depend on the thickness and characteristics of the soft soil layers (stratigraphy and consolidation properties). Thick levels of soft soil without any treatment would obviously lead to more settlements than stiffer soils. Due to stress distribution of the foundation pressures, soft layers closer to the tunnel structure will have more impact on settlements than deeper layers. The use of piles to improve the foundation of immersed tunnels is adopted for many soft soil ground conditions. The foundations must limit structural settlement for proper service life against cracking of concrete and/or excessive joint movements leading to excessive water ingress4. Radial cement soil pile technology is among the latest innovations to reinforce tunnels against ovulations, vertical and horizontal soil deformations in soil layers. The Tunnel jet system is highly integrated with automated and digitally intelligent control equipment5. It has been acknowledged that subway construction in urban set up affects the existing structures, especially those above it. Tunnels bored adjacent to foundations and piles have significantly affected ground settlements and structural responses6,7. Therefore, safety analysis of differential settlements and field stress levels is essential to ensure adequate support during construction8. It is also noteworthy to state that tunnel linings, once installed, are highly sensitive to ground movements and may crack if adjacent structures such as foundations, retaining walls, or piles are not properly managed9,10. Tabaroei et al.10, further showed that settlement patterns in triple tunnels near deep excavations are strongly influenced by the tunnel’s position relative to retaining structures.

The study of constructing tunnels crossing below existing tunnels has been growing, although at a lower scale using combinations of empirical, model, analytical and numerical methods11–15. Zeng et al.11 performed a 3D finite element analysis of shield tunneling process of Guangzhou subways lines 5 and 18 and confirmed safe impact levels while recommending more settlement monitors and ground treatment. Xuishao et al.16 similarly did an impact analysis with similar recommendations for treatment and support. Liu et al.1 found that distance between the overlapping tunnels affected displacement field of the existing tunnel providing insight into micro-perturbation control. Ayasrah et al.9 evaluated existing tunnel lining response to adjacent piles, noting that pile configuration and location from tunnel axis impacted lining stiffness behavior. Furthermore, Ayasrah et al.17 and Qiu et al.7, after conducting parametric analysis by tunnel simulation concluded that the greatest impact of tunnel shield construction on surrounding soil and structures lies 2–6 times tunnel diameter. Lu et al.18 obtained similar results when two lane tunnels passing under existing building foundations were studied. Jian-cai et al.2 analysed the soil loss rate for Metro Jet System reinforcement construction method of tunnel with positive results on reducing settlement.

Stress mobilization due to varying combined loading conditions within piles impacts their end bearing capacities, skin friction and bending moment, as demonstrated by Ayasrah and Fattah19, who conducted numerical study on mechanical response of open and closed ended piles having soil plugs. The load -settlement response of a pile is significantly influenced by the skin friction and end bearing resistance and elastic compression of the pile20. Lingzhi et al.21 investigated pile lengths and spacing under tunnels and how they affected tunnel displacement obtaining closed form solutions for application.

Gaussian functions have been applied in clays and sands to study ground settlements from field and model tests22–28. Lu et al.27 proposed a Gaussian-based method for predicting vertical displacement with depth above tunnel crowns, while Fang et al.28 used normal distribution functions to analyze surface settlements.

However, tunnel behavior at undercrossings remains complex, particularly when linings are bored beneath piles in soft soils. This paper examines settlement prediction for tunnel undercrossings having to support safe design, evaluating pile setups and employing closed-form functions derived from empirical relationships between trough width and depth. Building on Lu et al.27 , a power function linking maximum settlement to tunnel crown depth is adopted to develop the proposed formulas.

Proposed analysis

Based on the power function adopted by Lu et al.27, in Eqs. (5) and (6), the condition parameter n affecting ground settlement is analysed for comparison using numerical simulation for a semi-circular tunnel sitting under piles of varying set up and undercrossing another existing tunnel at 90° under a fixed pile set up of 5 × 4 m in soft clay soil. The settlement data obtained at the undercrossing are compared with those at the edge of the model (x = 40 m from existing tunnel), which are assumed to be in green field conditions. The n and k parameters obtained are then calibrated into a Gaussian function that is able to relate the settlement with depth.

Simulation of crossing tunnels

Numerical model

A semi-circular tunnel was adopted for the prediction of the settlement under piles set up to ensure foundation improvement of the tunnel base in weak layers of clay soil18,36. The Finite Element Analysis software (FEA), MIDAS-GTS-NX was used to simulate the tunnel and pile set up for tunnels crossing each other. The tunnel radius and base dimension are indicted in Fig. 4. The model contains six layers. Five soil layers were selected to represent the profile, while the top layer is considered as built structure.

The model had the dimensions (length x width x height) = 80 m x 80 m x 80 m.Tunnel 2 under crosses tunnel 1 at the center (x = 0, y = 40 m, z− 40 m) as shown in Fig. 5.The two tunnels are located in different stratum; tunnel 1 is in layer 2–21 and tunnel 2 is in 2–42. The piles are of 0.55 m diameter, 25 m lengths and penetrate across different layers. Tunnel 1 is existing while tunnel 2 is the proposed under cross. The construction of the model consisted of four major stages s i.e. Soil layers, piling, face and shield support pressure and shield lining. The EPB face pressure of 500 kPa was set for excavation, acting on tunnel face area, meanwhile shield support pressure was set with the varying construction stage of tunnel36.

Sequential excavation at bottom of tunnel to produce the base was performed by application of the shield support pressure. The building load acting at ground level was adopted from Chinese GB5009-2012 at 150 kPa37 and assumed traffic load for medium traffic was calculated at 100 kPa.The model was built up by a range of 328,921- 331,729 elements for cases 4 × 3 m to 5 × 5 m pile set up. The 3D solid element mesh was used for the soil layers,2D element mesh for tunnel lining and ID element mesh for pile and pile/pile tip elements. The lateral boundaries of the model were constrained orthogonally under auto constraint condition, which allowed only tangential movement, with model bottom fully fixed, while the top boundary (ground surface) was left free to deform naturally.

The Mohr–coulomb yield criterion is found to be reasonable due to it’s conventional approach of elastic–plastic soil failure for the loading and reloading cases. By considering constant soil stiffnesses of the different soil layers, this makes its analysis basic enough while maintaining accuracy. Simulation procedure and conditions

The modelling process involved creating the geometry for the top layer and soil layers from 2–0 to 3–42, and then dividing surfaces between tunnel and soil. The tunnels were made at a diameter of 12.7 m as shown in Fig. 4. The depth of tunnel 1 was at placed 14 m from the ground level while tunnel 2 was created at 34 m depth. The piles were configured in such a way that the tunnel axis had to be traversal and at the center of the pile spacing set up, with piles under tunnel one fixed at 5 × 4 m spacing, while the piles under tunnel 2 were varied according to the model simulation. (Figs. 4 & 5). Tunnel segments were further depicted by dividing surfaces. Meshes were generated using the hybrid mesher tool. This ensures uniform mix of tetrahedral and hexahedral elements, hence improving curvature and overall mesh quality for smoother analysis18. Material properties were assigned to the created elements of soil layers, tunnel lining and piles. (Tables 2, 3). The loading condition on the model was applied as distributed pressure for building and traffic as illustrated in Fig. 5. The face pressure was applied consequently with the shield support per construction stage of tunnel segments using stage wizard tool. Self-weight was applied as gravitation acceleration at 10 m/s2. For convergence, the model was set at tolerance of displacement less than 0.001 m, with max. iterations at 300 and divergence threshold at 150.The construction stages prior to excavation of tunnel 2 were cleared of displacement.

The excavation process consisted of two cases. For the case without piles, excavation of the tunnels was done while inserting the tunnel lining as 2D shells. For the case with piles, a stage was set to install the piles first before excavation. It was assumed that the EPB machine operation first cyclically cuts the soil and then shapes the base by introducing a substage for shield support pressure acting before fixing the tunnel lining. The stress release during excavation and lining process was catered for by applying a load distribution factor (LDF) on the face of tunnel and lining according to the stages. Hu et al.36 and Zhang et al.38 recommended 10–30% stress release ratio. The model was assigned a substage LDF for 0.3/0.4/0.3, which falls within the recommended ratios. This limits sudden non-linear stresses that can easily affect the convergence of the solver, and simulates smoother soil response, which significantly aids in both stability and accuracy of the substage processes. Conclusion

Soil–structure interactions cause displacements, and minimizing settlements in weak soils is crucial to avoid structural defects amid increasing urbanization. To improve ground and crown settlement, a numerical study using MIDAS GTS NX was conducted to examine the influence of pile setups on ground and tunnel settlement in clay soil. Based on the pile arrangement employed in the Zhongshan Road tunnel construction for ground improvement, the performance of different configurations was evaluated by simulating a semicircular undercrossing tunnel with six pile setup cases. The results demonstrated that pile setups significantly affected both ground and crown settlement, while also controlling tunnel lining uplift. By comparing with 12 existing project cases, Gaussian-based prediction functions were proposed to estimate ground settlement in greenfield conditions and to provide reasonable projections for undercrossing tunnels. The following conclusions were drawn:Settlement troughs for tunnel–pile setups were consistent with conventional Gaussian functions. The five cases with piles produced higher ground settlement but more controlled uplift compared with the single no-pile case. Settlement values were greatest 40 m away from the undercrossing, while uplift was greatest at the tunnel center.The 5 × 4 m pile setup reduced uplift by 138–165% longitudinally along the tunnel but increased crown and ground settlement transversely. The 4 × 4 m pile setup performed best overall, reducing uplift by 184–194% and producing less volatile settlement. Uniform pile arrangements provided more stable settlement and uplift behavior than non-uniform configurations.Gaussian-based functions for predicting vertical settlement in both greenfield and undercrossing conditions were proposed. The greenfield prediction function was validated using known settlement values, confirming its applicability when k and n parameters are available.For tunnel construction in soft soils, a safe parameter range was identified as k = 0.4–0.7 and n = 1.5–6 for estimating ground and crown settlements.

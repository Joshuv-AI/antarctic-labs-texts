Alterations of white matter connectivity in thalamic–frontal pathways associated with inflammation in premenstrual syndrome
Authors: Haixia Qin, Gaoxiong Duan, Qingping Zhang, Ziyan Lai, Ya Chen, YinQi Lai, Yuejuan Wu, Zhen Liu, Kaixuan Zhou, Yan Zhang, Shihuan Lin, Ruijing Sun, Shanshan Li, Yuanyuan Ou, Rongcai Wu, Zhizhong Chen, Lingyan Liang, Demao Deng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe thalamo-prefrontal white matter (WM) pathway, a core structural element of the frontal–limbic system disrupted in premenstrual syndrome (PMS), remains poorly understood.MethodsDiffusion tensor imaging (DTI), functional MRI (fMRI), and serum cytokine levels were collected from 41 PMS participants and 51 healthy controls (HCs), all diagnosed using the Daily Record of Severity of Problems (DRSP) scale. Bilateral thalamic–frontal WM pathways—the anterior thalamic radiations (ATRs)—were reconstructed using probabilistic fiber tracking. Two-sample tests examined group differences in fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), axial diffusivity (AD), radial diffusivity (RD), and amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation (ALFF) within bilateral ATRs. Spearman correlations assessed associations among these MRI metrics, inflammatory cytokines, and DRSP scores. Machine learning models further evaluated the diagnostic and predictive utility of left ATR features combined with inflammatory cytokines.ResultsCompared to HCs, PMS patients exhibited increased MD, AD, RD, and ALFF values in the left ATR, as well as elevated tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α levels. Correlation analysis revealed that these MRI alterations in the left ATR and TNF-α levels were linked to DRSP scores. Additionally, the machine learning models constructed using the optimal feature subset, involved in MD, AD and ALFF of left ATR as well as TNF-α, demonstrated robust performance in diagnosing PMS and predicting DRSP scores.ConclusionThese findings suggest altered thalamo–frontal WM connectivity and elevated TNF-α in PMS. The left ATR may serve as a biomarker of PMS neuro-mechanisms when combined with multi-MRI and inflammation metrics.Keywords: premenstrual syndrome, diffusion tensor imaging, resting-state functional MRI, inflammatory cytokines

Introduction

Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) is a common condition affecting approximately 20–30% of reproductive women globally. It is characterized by emotional, cognitive, physical, and behavioral symptoms that manifest during the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and resolve within a week after the onset of menstruation. These symptoms can cause significant distress and impairment in daily functioning (Ryu and Kim, 2015; Cary & Simpson, 2024). The pathophysiology of PMS remains unclear, but neural and immune systems are involved (Rapkin and Akopians, 2012; Tiranini and Nappi, 2022).

Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) studies have consistently implicated dysregulation of frontal–limbic circuits in PMS, involving the amygdala, hippocampus, and frontocingulate regions such as the anterior cingulate cortex, medial prefrontal cortex, and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (Deng et al., 2018; Duan et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2023; Tene et al., 2023). In addition, thalamic abnormalities have also been reported in PMS (Sun et al., 2025). Given that anatomical constraints shape the architecture and dynamics of neural networks, we speculate that the thalamic–frontal white matter (WM) pathway–anterior thalamic radiations (ATRs) may serve as a critical structural link between thalamic relay and frontal–limbic regions (Spalletta et al., 2013; Phillips et al., 2021). The ATR, a key ascending fiber of the thalamic–frontal, connects the mediodorsal nucleus of the thalamus to the prefrontal cortex (PFC), and is involved in emotion regulation and cognitive regulation (McIntosh et al., 2008; Niida et al., 2018). However, the ATR pathway in PMS has not been adequately investigated.

Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) is a widely used technique for examining brain WM pathways. Additionally, recent fMRI research has indicated that brain activity can also be detected within WM (Ji et al., 2017; Ding et al., 2018), potentially providing a new perspective to investigate WM dysfunctions in PMS. Moreover, the WM damage associated with excessive peripheral inflammation in mood and pain disorders, and a similar relationship involving interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), has also been observed in PMS (Duan et al., 2025). The excessive release of these peripheral pro-inflammatory factors can induce inflammatory processes in the central nervous system (CNS), thereby triggering neuroinflammation and compromising the activity of astrocytes in WM (Garcia-Hernandez et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2024). Therefore, exploring the functional and structural abnormalities in the ATR, combined with peripheral inflammatory markers, may provide new insights into the neurobiological mechanisms underlying PMS.

The primary aim of this study was to investigate the structural and functional changes in the ATR assessed using DTI and fMRI data, and that these changes are associated with premenstrual behaviors and levels of inflammatory cytokines. A secondary aim was to explore the potential of MRI biomarkers and inflammatory markers as indicators by using machine learning models for diagnosis and prediction of clinical symptoms.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All participants were informed about the experimental procedures and provided written informed consent prior to their participation. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the local hospital in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (approval number: LunShen 2018-037-02). Additionally, this study was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR1900020642).

Experimental design

Participants were consecutively recruited through local advertisements between April 2019 and July 2022. Eligible individuals were initially screened using the Premenstrual Symptoms Screening Tool (PSST) and then prospectively evaluated over at least two menstrual cycles using the Daily Record of Severity of Problems (DRSP) (Steiner et al., 2003; Endicott et al., 2006). A gynecologist with 10 years of experience confirmed the diagnosis of PMS based on established recommendations and diagnostic criteria. The specific inclusion and exclusion criteria are described in detail in Supplementary Materials S1.

Each participant was asked to provide information about the following: body mass index (BMI), menstrual cycle characteristics, including menarche (age at first menstruation), length of menstrual cycle, and duration of menstruation.

Neuroimaging and biological assessments

Participants with PMS and healthy controls (HCs) underwent the following assessments during the late luteal phase (LLP) of their menstrual cycle: (i) DTI and resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) scans; and (ii) collection of blood samples. As to the second week after ovulation is the LPP, we used urinary luteinizing hormone (LH) tests to identify ovulation, then calculated the LPP according to the regular pattern of menstrual cycle in individuals, and recorded the time of the next menstrual period from the evaluation to verify it.

Image data acquisition and processing

MRI data were obtained using a 3.0T Siemens MAGNETOM Vida MRI System (Siemens Medical, Erlangen, Germany) located at the local hospital. To minimize head movement during scanning, each participant’s head was stabilized using a foam pad within a standard 64-channel head coil. The specific parameters of the DTI and rs-fMRI sequences are described in detail in Supplementary Materials S2.

DTI data were preprocessed using the FMRIB Software Library (FSL) (https://fsl.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/fsl/fslwiki/, v.6.0) to obtain tensor-based parameters, such as fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), axial diffusivity (AD), and radial diffusivity (RD). The rs-fMRI data were preprocessed and the amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation (ALFF) was computed using the Data Processing and Analysis for Brain Imaging (http://rfmri.org/dpabi, v.7.0) toolbox on the MATLAB R2021b platform (v.2021b, The MathWorks, Inc., Natick, MA, USA). The T1-weighted structural MRI data were preprocessed using the FSL platform. Detailed descriptions of the data processing methods for each modality are provided in Supplementary Materials S3.

Blood sample processing

Venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected from all participants during the LLP of their menstrual cycle. Blood draws were conducted in the morning (between 8:00 AM and 10:00 AM) while the participants were in a fasted state. Detailed descriptions of the processing methods for the blood samples are provided in Supplementary Materials S4. Missing blood data were supplemented by linear interpolation.

Statistical analyses

Demographic and clinical characteristics were compared between the PMS group and HC group using either two-sample t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests, depending on the normality of the data distribution and homogeneity of variance. The same analyses were used to assess group differences in the average values of MD, AD, RD, and ALFF within the bilateral ATR in normalized space. Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to determine the relationships between the significant MRI metrics (including FA, MD, AD, RD, and ALFF), inflammatory markers, and DRSP scores across the entire study sample.

To evaluate the capacity of MRI-based and inflammatory biomarkers for classifying individuals with PMS, a machine learning framework was implemented. Five features were included: AD, MD, RD, ALFF, and TNF-α. Four commonly used machine learning models were applied: support vector machine with a radial basis function kernel (SVM), random forest (RF), logistic regression (LR), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). First, each feature was independently assessed across the four models. Next, to reduce dimensionality and identify the most informative features, recursive feature elimination (RFE) was used to select the optimal feature subset for each model. All models were trained and evaluated using 10-fold cross-validation to ensure generalizability and minimize overfitting. Model performance was assessed using accuracy (ACC), sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Additionally, a linear regression model (LRM) was constructed using optimal features to predict the DRSP score during the LLP. Prediction performance was assessed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient between the actual and predicted values.

Detailed feature importance and model comparison analyses are presented in Supplementary Materials S5. Interaction analyses testing whether sex hormones (PROG, E2, TESTO, PRL, LH, FSH) modify the associations between TNF-α and significant WM metrics are presented in Supplementary Materials S6.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v.26.0, R v.4.3.0, and MATLAB v.2021b. A significance threshold of P < 0.05 (corrected for false discovery rate, FDR) was applied.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

The study procedures were time-consuming, and many PSST-screened candidates did not complete DRSP monitoring across two cycles, leading to high attrition and a low final inclusion rate. The study enrolled 41 females with PMS (median [interquartile range, IQR], 24.00 [22.00–26.00] years) and 51 HCs (median [IQR], 23.50 [22.00–25.00] years). The participant recruitment process is illustrated in Fig. 1. No evidence of a difference in age, BMI, length of menstruation, menophania, or length of menstrual cycle were observed between participants with PMS and HCs. Compared to HCs, the PMS group exhibited significantly higher total scores on the DRSP scale (P < 0.001). Additionally, the PMS group had significantly higher serum concentrations of TNF-α than the HC group (P = 0.024, FDR-corrected). Detailed demographic and clinical characteristics of the study participants are summarized in Table 1. In contrast, no significant differences were observed between the PMS and HC groups in terms of sex hormone levels, as shown in Table E1.

Microstructural and functional alterations in the left ATR

Bilateral ATRs were successfully reconstructed using DTI, as shown in Figure E1. Compared to the HC group, the PMS group exhibited significantly increased AD (P = 0.011, uncorrected; P = 0.044, FDR-corrected), RD (P = 0.011, uncorrected; P = 0.044, FDR-corrected), and MD (P = 0.018, uncorrected; P = 0.048, FDR-corrected) in the left ATR. No evidence of significant differences in DTI metrics were observed in the right ATR between the two groups. Furthermore, the PMS group showed significantly higher ALFF in the left ATR compared to the HC group (P = 0.010), as reported in Table 2.

Association between elevated TNF-α, MRI biomarkers of the left ATR, and clinical symptoms

In the combined PMS and HC group, we conducted a correlation analysis to examine the relationships between serum TNF-α levels, MRI-derived metrics of the left ATR, and clinical symptoms. The key findings are as follows: (i) serum TNF-α levels were significantly and positively correlated with the mean DRSP scores during the LLP (P = 0.008, r = 0.312, FDR-corrected); (ii) serum TNF-α levels were significantly and positively correlated with the MD values of the left ATR (P = 0.044, r = 0.209, uncorrected; P = 0.073, FDR-corrected); (iii) AD (P = 0.038, r = 0.252, FDR-corrected), RD (P = 0.038, r = 0.251, FDR-corrected), and ALFF (P = 0.039, r = 0.245, FDR corrected) were significantly correlated with mean DRSP scores at LLP; and (iv) no evidence of significant correlation was observed between serum TNF-α levels and ALFF in the left ATR (P = 0.614, r = 0.065, FDR-corrected). Performance of models based on MRI biomarkers of the left ATR and abnormal inflammation

Compared to the five individual metrics used as dependent variables, the optimal feature subset (AD + MD + ALFF + TNF-α), chosen via RFE analysis, demonstrated more robust performance across all models. Additionally, in the XGBoost model, this feature subset provided the most robust neuroimaging biomarker for distinguishing PMS from HCs, showing an AUC of 0.827 (0.741–0.913), an accuracy of 0.772 (0.672–0.853), a sensitivity of 0.732 (0.571–0.858), and a specificity of 0.804 (0.571–0.858) (see Fig. 3A, Table 3, and Table E4). Furthermore, the four combined metrics also significantly predicted the mean DRSP score during the LLP (r = 0.389, P < 0.001) (see Fig. 3B).

Discussion

This study characterized the WM properties of the ATRs in females with PMS during the LLP of the menstrual cycle. We found that several DTI metrics, including MD, AD, and RD, as well as ALFF from the fMRI data and inflammatory cytokines, were significantly increased in the left ATR of the PMS group compared to HCs. These abnormal metrics were significantly associated with the DRSP scores and TNF-α. Furthermore, machine learning models constructed using the optimal feature subset (AD, MD, ALFF, and TNF-α), selected via RFE analysis, demonstrated robust capabilities in disease classification and clinical symptom prediction.

Higher values of MD, AD, and RD correspond to processes including demyelination, changes in axonal structure, and cellular structure alterations (Alexander et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2022). Our results showed microstructural alterations in the left ATR in PMS, with no significant differences in the right ATR; moreover, abnormalities in the left ATR were associated with DRSP-measured PMS severity. Notably, all significant imaging changes localized to the left ATR. This lateralization is consistent with hemispheric specialization of affective–cognitive control, whereby left prefrontal systems more strongly support top-down emotion regulation associated with limbic circuit (Bruder et al., 2017). The ATR, a component of the limbic system, originates from the mediodorsal nuclei of the thalamus, passes through the anterior limb of the internal capsule (ALIC), and projects to the frontal lobe (Cho et al., 2015). Alterations of microstructure within the ATR in PMS align with previous studies showing that ATR abnormalities are involved in various neuropsychiatric disorders, including depression (Zhang et al., 2024), anxiety (Kurita et al., 2023), and cognitive disorders (Jakabek et al., 2018). Additionally, our results also showed that the increased ALFF in the left ATR in PMS was positively associated with the DRSP scores. Recent research suggests that the BOLD signals in WM encode neural activities, and functional activity can be reliably detected (Li et al., 2021; Guo et al., 2022). The increased ALFF suggests increased spontaneous neural activity in the brain (Wu et al., 2019), but functional loading may contribute to the brain enhances functional activities in WM (Marussich et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2017). Taken together, these findings suggest structural and functional alterations in the left but not the right ATR in PMS, providing preliminary evidence of abnormal prefrontal–thalamic WM connectivity and altered frontal function in PMS (Liu et al., 2019; Aoki et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2025).

Consistent with the present findings, our study demonstrated that individuals with PMS exhibited elevated serum levels of TNF-α compared to HCs (Puder et al., 2006; Quan & Banks, 2007). Alterations in inflammatory processes have been implicated in the pathogenesis of various psychiatric and somatic disorders, including depression, which share common features with PMS (Miller et al., 2009; Zeng et al., 2024). Notably, a potential correlation between TNF-α levels and MD in the left ATR was observed, although it did not remain statistically significant after FDR correction, suggesting a preliminary trend linking peripheral inflammation to brain circuit alterations in PMS. Peripheral cytokines can access the brain through various pathways, including saturable transporters and trafficking of immune cells that interact with microglia to release cytokines and other inflammatory mediators within the central nervous system (Ho et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022). Elevated cytokine levels in the CNS can adversely affect the integrity of several glial elements, including oligodendrocytes, which are responsible for generating myelin (Comai et al., 2022; Sammer et al., 2022). The WM, which contains a high density of glial cells, is engaged in neural activity and energy consumption. Previous studies have also shown that changes in glial cell activity under different inflammatory conditions are related to alterations in MD value in both animal models and humans (Benedetti et al., 2011; Garcia-Hernandez et al., 2022). Additionally, the ALFF measure has been associated not only with neural activity but also with astrocyte activity (Nishiyama et al., 2014; Sarma et al., 2021). Higher ALFF values in the WM may reflect processes related to glial cells or glial cycling (Sarma et al., 2021), although no significant correlation with TNF-α was found in this study. These results suggested a potential link between peripheral cytokines and altered thalamic–frontal WM pathways, supporting a crucial role for neuro-inflammation in PMS development.

Interestingly, the multi-MRI metrics of left ATR (MD, AD, and ALFF) and TNF-α levels may be serve as potential neuro-biomarkers for distinguishing PMS from HCs. These metrics showed a robust classification capacity, with AUC of 0.827, an accuracy of 0.772, a sensitivity of 0.732, and a specificity of 0.804. Studies have shown that machine learning models can be considered reliable diagnostic tools when their accuracy, specificity, and sensitivity are above 70% (Swets, 1988; Gong et al., 2011). Machine learning has been widely used in biomedical research to diagnose mental illnesses, included major depression (MDD) and schizophrenia (Lin et al., 2014; Yang et al., 2019). Notably, models using single metrics performed worse than multi-metrics across all classification algorithms, and feature importance analysis showed that the multimodal approach adds complementary, independent predictive value beyond single metrics for PMS diagnosis. Brain functional activity has been widely examined using multi-model imaging as the integration of functional and structural MRI may provide complementary information (Qing et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Furthermore, similar multimodal approaches have indicated that these features within the left ATR associated with TNF-α levels may serve as predictors of clinical symptom severity in PMS, thereby supporting the hypothesis that the left ATR plays a crucial role in the neuroinflammatory mechanisms underlying PMS. The current study offers further compelling evidence for the discoordination of the frontal–thalamic circuit from a structural perspective, suggesting that the left ATR might serve as a biomarker for diagnosing PMS patients when considering both structural and functional MRI characteristics comprehensively.

The current study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small. Second, the ATR focus was hypothesis-driven by prior PMS evidence; systematic evaluation of other emotion- and cognition-related tracts (e.g. cingulum bundle, uncinate fasciculus, fornix) is warranted in future studies. Third, this study employed a late-luteal, cross-sectional design with a limited cytokine panel and no concurrent endocrine or hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis measures; therefore, cycle-dependent effects, hormone–immune interactions, and the breadth/specificity of the neuroimmune signature could not be assessed.

Conclusion

The key findings from the present study suggest that abnormal structural and functional properties of the left ATR, along with elevated serum levels of TNF-α, are associated with symptom severity in women diagnosed with PMS. Importantly, there was a potential correlation between TNF-α levels and the microstructural integrity of the left ATR, including the MD, AD, and ALFF measures. These results may provide new insights into the neuroinflammatory mechanisms underlying the development and maintenance of PMS symptoms. Future research building upon these initial observations could explore the utility of anti-inflammatory agents, neuro-modulatory techniques, or other targeted approaches to alleviate the symptoms experienced by individuals with PMS.

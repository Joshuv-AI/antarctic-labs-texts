A Bibliometric Analysis of Publications on Macrophages in Peripheral Nerve Injury
Authors: Qingyi Huo, Mingyang Li, Yibo Zhao, Lixin Huang, Tao Sun, Zhimin Wu, Lijiang Sha, Xiaoli Zhang, Jun Sun, Hua Tang, Hui Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPeripheral nerve injury (PNI) disrupts nerve function, there has been increasing focus on the role of macrophages in PNI. This study provides a comprehensive analysis of research trends and key topics related to macrophages in the field of PNI. We conducted a search in the Web of Science Core Collection database for studies published between 2000 and 2023 using “macrophages” and “PNI” as keywords. Bibliometric analysis was performed using visualization tools, including VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and GraphPad Prism 8, and others. As of December 31, 2023, a total of 905 relevant publications were identified. In terms of author contributions, Japanese researchers Kiguchi Norikazu and Kishioka Shiroh shared the highest number of publications, each with 15 articles. In terms of journals, Experimental Neurology published the most articles, while the University of Würzburg in Germany emerged as the leading institution in this field, with the highest publication output. Among the 51 countries involved in this research, the United States ranked first, followed by China. The keyword ‘exosomes’ first appeared in 2021, making it the most recent keyword. Cluster analysis categorized the keywords into four main groups: the role of macrophages in Wallerian degeneration, the role of macrophages in pathological peripheral neuropathic pain, macrophage-derived products after PNI, and role of macrophages in PNI repair and strategies for modulating their function using biomaterials. Introduction

Peripheral nerve injury (PNI) caused by trauma, compression, ischemia, or iatrogenic factors disrupts the morphological structure and physiological function of peripheral nerves (Lopes et al. 2022). Such injuries frequently lead to significant motor and sensory function loss (Gordon 2020). Globally, over 1 million individuals are affected by PNI, with an estimated annual incidence rate of approximately 13 to 23 per 100,000 people (Sachanandani et al. 2014). This widespread condition imposes a substantial burden on healthcare systems and profoundly impacts patients' quality of life. Compared with central nervous system, the peripheral nervous system (PNS) has a remarkable capacity for regeneration, even after complete axonal severance (Zhang et al. 2021a; Apolloni and D'Ambrosi 2023). PNI induces the degradation of damaged axons, initiating Wallerian degeneration (WD) (Zhang et al. 2021b). This process triggers Schwann cells to release the myelin sheath, undergo dedifferentiation and proliferation, and secrete various factors that recruit inflammatory cells, including neutrophils and macrophages, to the injury site. These cells clear cellular debris and release neurotrophins factors that promote axonal regeneration (Boissonnas et al. 2020). Axonal regeneration is a complex and prolonged process that involves the participation of multiple cell types, including Schwann cells and immune cells, particularly macrophages (Bosch-Queralt et al. 2023; Kijima et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2023).

Macrophages primarily derive from progenitor cells in the bone marrow and can differentiate either directly from hematopoietic progenitor cells or from circulating monocytes, which then enter the peripheral blood (Lazarov et al. 2023). Under the influence of local growth factors, proinflammatory cytokines, and microbial products, they can differentiate into macrophages (Park et al. 2022). Macrophages are classified into M1 (proinflammatory) and M2 (anti-inflammatory) types (Deng et al. 2020). M1 and M2 macrophages exhibit distinct functions and transcriptional profiles, representing two polarized states of macrophage activation. Emerging evidence shows that macrophages are critical contributors to axonal regeneration, as they remove inhibitory myelin and axonal debris, secreting cytokines and growth factors that impact SC function and nerve regeneration and potentially providing metabolic support to axons (Contreras et al. 2022).

Bibliometrics is a statistical and quantitative approach that utilizes public literature databases. This method provides detailed insights into publication volume, citation trends, and other valuable information. Modern computational technologies, along with graphical and visualization tools such as VOSviewer and CiteSpace, facilitate the systematic processing of publication data (Ninkov et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2023). As a crucial tool for quantifying research output and impact, bibliometrics facilitates the evaluation of academic trends and data-driven resource allocation, while also guiding individual researchers and groups to clarify their academic positioning and performance by revealing the strengths and gaps in existing research to inform future research directions and strategies. This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the research progress on macrophages in PNI based on the Web of Science Core Collection database. While a few reviews have summarized the role of macrophages in PNI, these are often limited to specific areas, such as research on genes like BACE1 and PEG2, or focused on intercellular interactions, such as the relationship between macrophages and Schwann cells (Fissel and Farah 2021; Ma and Quirion 2006). Therefore, there is currently no systematic and comprehensive review that covers the broader trends in medical research on macrophages in PNI. Using bibliometric methods, this study analyzes articles related to macrophages and PNI published from 2000 to 2023, with the aim of identifying key research trends and predicting potential future hotspots. Therefore, elucidating the role of macrophages in the pathophysiology of PNI is essential for advancing therapeutic strategies aimed at improving PNI outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Database and Literature Search Strategy

This study selected Web of Science as the primary database due to its extensive coverage of global academic resources and its ability to provide the most comprehensive and authoritative bibliometric analysis data. We conducted a search for relevant articles using specific keywords in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). After consulting with experienced literature retrieval experts, all authors agreed on the search strategy, which is as follows: (TS = ((“peripheral nerve” OR “brachial plexus” OR “axillary nerve” OR “cutaneous nerve” OR “median nerve” OR “radial nerve” OR “ulnar nerve” OR “femoral nerve” OR “sciatic nerve” OR “common peroneal nerve”))) AND TS = ((“Macrophages” OR “Macrophage”) OR (“Monocyte-Derived Macrophages” OR “Monocyte Derived Macrophages” OR “Macrophages, Monocyte-Derived” OR “Macrophages, Monocyte Derived” OR “Macrophage, Monocyte-Derived”) OR (“Monocyte-Derived Macrophage”) OR (“Bone Marrow-Derived Macrophages” OR “Bone Marrow-Derived Macrophage” OR “Bone Marrow Derived Macrophages” OR “Macrophage, Bone Marrow-Derived” OR “Macrophages, Bone Marrow-Derived”)).

Study Selection and Bibliometrics Analysis

All publications were screened by two independent researchers (Q.H. and M.L.), with any disagreements rigorously resolved through consultation with the corresponding author (H.W.). We exported the publications and referenced citations from the WoSCC database in plain text format for bibliometric analysis and visualization. The literature screening flowchart was created using Visio. For the generation of visual charts, we utilized VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.4. R1), and GraphPad Prism (version 10.3.1). Specifically, GraphPad Prism was used to generate bar charts illustrating the number of publications per year, journal publication volume, and citation counts, as well as institutional publication output, country-wise publication volume, and citation metrics. VOSviewer was employed to create visual charts and analyze the most prolific authors, countries, and institutions, alongside the most cited journals and co-occurring keywords. Additionally, CiteSpace was used to generate dual-overlay journal maps and identify keyword bursts. We also leveraged Dycharts to create Sankey diagrams for authors, countries, and institutions, while Charticulator was used to produce chord diagrams, and Tableau (2024.3) software visualized the differences in publication numbers across countries. These analyses and visualizations provide a comprehensive and systematic quantitative foundation for the study.

Results

Overall Publication Trend

A total of 1,661 publications were initially identified. After excluding non-English publications, duplicates, and those that did not meet the inclusion criteria, 1559 articles remained. A further manual review was conducted to ensure the selected publications were directly relevant to PNI. Ultimately, 905 articles were included for analysis. An overview of the screening process is provided in (Fig. 1A, Supplementary Table 1). The number of published papers serves as a key indicator for gauging research activity and interest. Our analysis indicates that the peak publication period was between 2020 and 2023, with 259 papers. This figure is more than double the count observed during the lowest period from 2008 to 2011 (Fig. 1B). Overall, the number of publications per year initially shows a gradual decline, followed by a dramatic increase (Fig. 1C). If the current growth trend continues, our extrapolation suggests that publications in this field could potentially reach approximately 1,500 by 2048–2052 (Fig. 1D), highlighting a steady increase in research activity over the next 30 years.

Journals Analysis

Between 2000 and 2023, a total of 292 journals published articles exploring the role of macrophages in PNI. According to Bradford’s Law analysis, journals are categorized into three zones: the core zone, the relevant zone, and the irrelevant zone, with the number of journals in these zones following a 1: n: n2 distribution (Venable et al. 2016). Notably, each zone encompasses an equal number of articles. As shown in Table 2, the core zone includes fourteen journals related to this field. Most of these journals are ranked in Journal citation reports (JCRs) Q1 or Q2, collectively accounting for 308 journals, representing 34.03% of the total publications (Supplementary Table 4). The top 10 journals based on the number of published articles and their citation counts has been shown in Fig. 3A. Among these, Experimental Neurology has the highest number of published articles, while Journal of Neuroscience boasts the highest citation count.

Analysis of Co-cited References

Over the past 20 years, a total of 29,655 co-cited references have been identified in research on macrophages in PNI. As shown in Table 3, five references have been co-cited more than 80 times. These co-cited references have laid the foundation for the field’s focus on three key directions: (1) the role of macrophages in WD and axonal regeneration, (2) macrophage-mediated vascular repair in accelerating neural regeneration, and (3) the development of peripheral neuropathic pain. Using a filter, 135 references co-cited at least 20 times were identified as the basis for constructing a co-citation network, which is divided into three clusters (Fig. 6). The representative references of each cluster can be found in Table 3. Notably, the references ranked second and third both appear in Cluster 1, with a strong co-citation relationship between them (link strength = 31). These two articles investigate the functional roles and underlying mechanisms of macrophages in WD, axonal regeneration, and vascular repair. Furthermore, half of the top 10 most co-cited references are from JCR Q1 journals, and 80% of all references are from journals ranked in JCR Q1 or Q2. This distribution indicates that the highly co-cited references are predominantly supported by high-quality journals.

Analysis of Keywords

Co-occurrence keyword analysis highlights research hotspots and developmental trends in macrophage studies within PNI. This research filtered 3,851 keywords from 905 related documents using VOSviewer, identifying 362 keywords that appeared more than five times. The top 30 most frequently occurring keywords are summarized in Table 4. Among these, 'Wallerian degeneration' appears 221 times, underscoring its pivotal role as a key mechanism involving macrophages in PNI. Moreover, 'tnf alpha' (118 occurrences), and 'growth-factor' (63 occurrences) play crucial roles in the regulation of PNI by macrophages, thereby driving the advancement of this research field. Visualization analysis of high-frequency keywords categorizes them into three clusters: the green cluster, which focuses on ‘Wallerian degeneration’ and ‘PNI regeneration’; the red cluster, which is associated with ‘neuropathic pain’ and ‘inflammation’; and the blue cluster, which is closely linked to the regulation of protein and gene expression (Fig. 7A). Analysis of keyword trends shows that blue keywords are predominant in the early stages of research, while yellow keywords have emerged as prominent themes in recent studies. For example, keywords such as 'macrophage polarization' (average published year: 2020) 'PNI regeneration'(average published year: 2018) and 'neuroinflammation'(average published year: 2017) suggest that future research may focus on these areas (Fig. 7B). Another key indicator of frontier research is the intensity of citation bursts for keywords. Among the top 25 keywords with the highest citation burst intensity, ‘growth factor’ ranks first (12.53), followed by ‘messenger RNA’ (10.97) and ‘necrosis factor-alpha’ (10.3) (Fig. 7C). Notably, keywords such as ‘macrophage polarization,’ ‘peripheral nerve regeneration,’ and ‘inhibition’ showed significant burst intensity in 2023, suggesting that these topics continue to be key research areas. These trends represent an increasingly prominent research focus in PNI repair, with potential future significance.

Discussion

Overall Overview

We performed a bibliometric analysis of the literature on macrophages in PNI from 2000 to 2023. The results show that the total number of publications slightly decreased from 2000 to 2011, followed by a period of continuous increase. While the increasing publication volume in this field reflects growing research activity, bibliometric evidence also reveals concurrent trends of thematic fragmentation and redundant mechanistic validation, underscoring the need for future studies to prioritize mechanistic integration and conceptual consolidation over further quantitative expansion. The authors Kishioka, Shiroh, and Kiguchi, Norikazu from Japan are ranked first in publication volume, while Bruck, W. from Germany has the highest number of citations, making his work a seminal reference in the field. An analysis of co-cited journal references reveals that the USA accounts for half among Top 10 publishing houses, with the journal Cell having the highest impact factor (IF). The United States has made the largest contribution to this field, with the highest number of publications, followed by China and Japan. The relationships between different countries are close, such as between the USA and China, or the USA and Germany.

Trends in Research Development

Co-occurrence keywords and burst keywords can identify the latest research hotspots and trends in this field. Based on both sets of data, the key research hotspots in the field of macrophages in PNI can be categorized into three clusters: the fundamental processes of WD involving macrophages after PNI, the role of macrophages in pathological peripheral neuropathic pain, and macrophage-derived products and biomaterial-based strategies in PNI repair and regeneration.

The Role of Macrophages in WD

During WD, macrophages rapidly accumulate at the injury site and, under the influence of the local microenvironment, polarize into M2 phenotypes, promoting axonal regeneration. Research by Mithilesh Kumar Jha's team has highlighted the critical role of MCT1 in macrophage metabolism, phenotypic conversion, and function, particularly in phagocytosis and nerve regeneration (Jha et al. 2021). Moreover, studies using RhoA knockout mice demonstrated inhibited macrophage migration and phagocytic activity after injury, which further hindered WD and nerve regeneration (Xu et al. 2021). Additionally, Schwann cell-derived IL-17B plays a significant role in WD by promoting macrophage recruitment, enhancing myelin clearance, and facilitating axonal regeneration (Huang et al. 2024). By focusing on regulating the metabolic and phenotypic polarization mechanisms of macrophages during WD, this approach could provide innovative therapeutic strategies for PNI repair.

The Role of Macrophages in Pathological Peripheral Neuropathic Pain

Macrophages in the dorsal root ganglion (DRG) are crucial for the development of neuropathic pain. Activation of the NOD2 signaling pathway leads to the production of pronociceptive cytokines, which mediate the onset of neuropathic pain (Santa-Cecília et al. 2019). Studies have also shown that HK2 plays a regulatory role in immune responses associated with neuropathic pain during the acute phase, particularly through peripheral monocytes and DRG macrophages (Wang et al. 2024a). Additionally, the proliferation of sensory neuron-associated macrophages in the sensory ganglia is closely linked to neuropathic pain induced by PNI, especially involving the CX3CR1 signaling pathway (Guimarães et al. 2023). Research by Yu’s team demonstrated that dorsal root ganglion macrophages contribute to both the initiation and persistence of neuropathic pain (Yu et al. 2020). Furthermore, studies have shown that in G2A-deficient mice, the significant reduction in macrophages and neutrophils leads to a decrease in the release of proalgesic mediators (such as TNF alpha, IL-6, and VEGF), thereby alleviating neuropathic pain (Osthues et al. 2020). Targeted modulation of the NOD2/CX3CR1 signaling axis in DRG macrophages-achieved through blocking proalgesic mediator release-represents a transformative therapeutic paradigm enabling precision intervention in neuropathic pain management.

Macrophage-Derived Products and Biomaterial-Based Strategies in PNI Repair and Regeneration

Macrophage-derived products play a critical role in nerve repair and regeneration following PNI. M2 macrophages promote axonal regeneration by secreting cathepsin S, activating Schwann cells. Wang et al. demonstrated that peripheral nerve-derived CSF1 activates neuroimmune regulatory pathways, enhancing BMP2 expression in pro-repair macrophages, promoting nerve regeneration (Wang et al. 2024b). Our study found that after PNI, Schwann cell-derived exosomes miR-146a-5p decrease significantly, shifting macrophages from an anti-inflammatory to a pro-inflammatory phenotype, which impedes regeneration and recovery (Sun et al. 2023). Additionally, CRYAB, a small heat shock protein, may inhibit pro-inflammatory responses in the later stages of nerve regeneration, offering potential neuroprotective effects (Lim et al. 2021). Modulating the secretory profile of M2 macrophages (e.g., via Cathepsin S or CSF1) has been shown to exhibit a potent capacity to drive peripheral nerve axonal regeneration. Furthermore, bioengineered materials incorporating macrophage modulators exhibit markedly improved axonal regeneration potential. Biomaterials, including bionic peptide scaffolds and Li-Mg-Si scaffolds, can modulate macrophage polarization, promoting Schwann cell proliferation and migration, enhancing nerve regeneration (Yang et al. 2023; Xuan et al. 2024). Additionally, Xu’s vascular endothelial growth factor-mimicking peptide hydrogel shows potential in promoting macrophage activation and myelin regeneration (Xu et al. 2023). Multifunctional tetrahedral nucleic acid systems also enhance Schwann cell-macrophage interactions, offering promising therapeutic applications (Li et al. 2022). Biomaterials designed to modulate macrophage M1/M2 polarization have become a research priority in biomedical engineering, providing novel therapeutic approaches for PNI repair.

Limitations

This study represents the first bibliometric analysis of macrophage research in PNI, offering an unbiased summary and thorough direction for future research. However, there are several limitations to this analysis. First, the data were exclusively sourced from the WoSCC database. Although a search limited to fewer databases may yield a less comprehensive result than one that includes Scopus or PubMed, the integration of data from multiple sources necessitates the use of advanced bibliometric tools. Second, non-English language publications were excluded, potentially limiting the scope of the findings. Third, due to the large volume of articles included, a comprehensive filtering process was not feasible, which may have led to the inclusion of some less relevant studies. Therefore, applying machine learning in the future can enable more efficient and thorough automated screening of literature. Fourth, potential predatory journals were not excluded from the analysis, as the definition of such outlets remains contentious and no universally accepted, real‑time updated exclusion list exists; arbitrary exclusion could introduce selection bias. Additionally, high-quality studies published recently may not have been adequately represented due to citation delays and should be considered in future updates. Lastly, although this study provides a bibliometric overview of macrophage-related research in PNI, it is important to note that many other significant areas of investigation within this field deserve equal attention.

Future Directions

Beyond the methodological limitations discussed above, several knowledge gaps in current experimental studies on macrophage immunomodulation in PNI warrant urgent attention and should guide future research:

The mechanistic insights described in this review—such as CSF1‑mediated BMP2 upregulation and exosomal miR‑146a‑5p transfer—have been primarily derived from in vitro co‑culture systems or acute injury models in young, healthy rodents. Whether these signaling pathways are conserved under clinically relevant conditions remains largely unexplored. Future studies should validate these pathways in clinically representative animal models that recapitulate common comorbidities or chronic nerve compression scenarios.

The mechanistic insights described in this review—such as CSF1‑mediated BMP2 upregulation and exosomal miR‑146a‑5p transfer—have been primarily derived from in vitro co‑culture systems or acute injury models in young, healthy rodents. Whether these signaling pathways are conserved under clinically relevant conditions remains largely unexplored. Future studies should validate these pathways in clinically representative animal models that recapitulate common comorbidities or chronic nerve compression scenarios.

Despite progress in biomaterial‑based immunomodulation for PNI repair, current studies are primarily limited by a lack of causal validation using macrophage‑specific genetic models and insufficient testing under clinically relevant conditions. Future research should prioritize mechanism‑driven designs, incorporate macrophage‑specific genetic tools, and rigorously test biomaterials under clinically relevant conditions to facilitate effective clinical translation.

Addressing these gaps will not only advance fundamental understanding but also accelerate the development of translatable immunomodulatory strategies for PNI patients.

Conclusions

In conclusion, macrophages have garnered increasing attention in the field of PNI research. In this review, we analyze the literature on macrophages in PNI, highlighting trends in publication volume over recent years. We identify the most influential authors, journals, institutions, and countries, and point out key research hotspots and emerging trends in macrophage-related PNI studies. As research continues to advance, the role of macrophages in nerve injury repair and regeneration is attracting increasing attention. Moving forward, integrating novel research approaches and biomaterials to explore the regulatory mechanisms of macrophages may provide new insights and therapeutic strategies for the clinical treatment of PNI.

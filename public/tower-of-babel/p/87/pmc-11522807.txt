Why Should Surgeons Submit to JACC: Case Reports?
Authors: Mitesh V Badiwala
Published: 2024

As a cardiac surgeon specializing in heart failure and transplantation, I feel privileged to serve as an Associate Editor for JACC: Case Reports and am proud to encourage my surgical colleagues to submit to this journal. One may wonder “Why should surgeons submit case reports to a Cardiovascular Medicine journal?” I would argue that JACC: Case Reports is the ideal forum to present clinical cases, rare or even garden variety, where there is a clear learning objective that would benefit not only our surgical colleagues around the world, but more importantly, our nonsurgical colleagues who we work closely with to tackle tough clinical problems on a daily basis.

Times have changed, and clinical practice will continue to evolve with new discoveries and new technologies. A surgeon operating in isolation has largely become a thing of the past and for a good reason. The vast array of new technologies and new procedures, both percutaneous and open surgical, that continually drive the evolution of cardiovascular medicine has led to the advent of “heart teams” and “multidisciplinary team” (MDT) discussions that have changed the care of our patients into a team sport.

Submitting surgical cases that are either rare or have unique learning objectives to JACC: Case Reports serves the broader goal of educating our MDTs. Even surgical cases that may seem simple, when presented with intraoperative images and/or video and paired with diagnostic imaging modalities, have the potential to bring our nonsurgical colleagues virtually into the operating room to expand their understanding of why and how our operations benefit our patients. Reporting “How We Did It” or “First-in-Human/Early Reports” type surgical cases have the dual benefit of guiding our surgical colleagues on a more technical basis, and also providing them with inspiration on how to surgically address unique scenarios. Ideally, these cases should also be presented in a multidisciplinary fashion to fulfill the multiple learning objectives of the team members involved in managing these complex scenarios.

Often it is not the technical aspects of surgery that are unique to a particular clinical case. Rather, it may be the unique and complex decision-making involved that results in either proceeding with surgery or an alternative to surgery. As a surgeon who participates in a MDT discussion every weekday morning that is attended by general cardiology, heart failure and transplant, interventional/structural cardiology, electrophysiology, imaging, cardiovascular anesthesia, and intensive care, I have come to appreciate both the clinical and educational value these discussions hold for clinicians and more importantly, our patients (Figure 1). Sharing our experiences with unique MDT discussions from around the world by submitting them to the “Multidisciplinary Team Discussions” section of JACC: Case Reports can allow surgeons to contribute and participate in the dissemination of these experiences to a broader audience. Sometimes these discussions result in debates and disagreements among surgeons and the rest of our colleagues because of opposing viewpoints. We have an outlet for these conflicts at JACC: Case Reports: the “Flash Debates and Controversies” section! This section is an excellent forum for surgeons to participate in presenting opposing viewpoints in collaboration with their colleagues to highlight controversial topics.

Multidisciplinary Team Review and Discussion Process

The overall goal of JACC: Case Reports is to serve as a global forum to provide learning opportunities for all aspects of cardiovascular science for all cardiovascular team members. Surgeons need to participate in our collective team effort to achieve this goal. I am confident that participation will lead to enhanced collaboration amongst team members with the shared vision of advancing the quality of cardiovascular care around the world.

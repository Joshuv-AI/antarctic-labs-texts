Construction of lncRNA prognostic model related to disulfidptosis in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Liming Zhang, Shaoqiang Wang, Lina Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer is one of the malignant tumors with the highest rates of morbidity and mortality worldwide. One of the most common histological types of lung cancer is lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Despite the fact that development in medicine has significantly improved some patients’ prognoses, the overall survival (OS) rate is still very low. In glucose-deficient SLC7A11-overexpressed cancer cells, the accumulation of disulfide molecules leads to abnormal disulfide bonding between actin cytoskeletal proteins, interferes with their tissues, and eventually leads to actin network collapse and cell death. This mode of cell death is called disulfidptosis. Studies have shown that disulfidptosis may be a new target for cancer treatment. However, the role of disulfidptosis in LUAD is still unknown.MethodsLUAD transcriptome and clinical information from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) was downloaded. The co-expression analysis, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression, and Cox regression analysis was performed to screen the disulfidptosis-related lncRNAs (DRLs) and build the prognostic model. Kaplan-Meier curve, Cox regression analysis, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to validate the model. Then a nomogram is made to predict the prognosis of LUAD patients. Finally, fresh-collected clinical samples were used to verify the expression of DRLs in LUAD.ResultsThe prognostic model with six DRLs was developed to predict the prognosis of LUAD, with superior prognosis value compared to other clinical variables. The Cox regression analysis revealed that T stage, N stage and the risk score were identified as independent variables that affected LUAD prognosis. ROC curve revealed that the model has a moderate diagnostic value, with an AUC of 1-year 0.684, 3-year 0.664, and 5-year 0.588. Moreover, nine medications connected to LUAD treatment were acquired through drug sensitivity analysis. LUAD tissue validation showed that AC012073.1, AC012615.1, EMSLR, and SNHG12 were highly expressed, while AL606834.1 and AL365181.2 with low expression.ConclusionSix DRLs were screened and verified to construct the prognostic model, which can accurately predict the LUAD prognosis. It establishes a basis for further exploration into the molecular mechanisms underlying LUAD and identification of potential biomarkers for diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic targets.

Introduction

The recent Global Cancer Statistics 2022 showed that lung cancer emerged as the leading type of cancer both in terms of new diagnoses, accounting for nearly 2.5 million cases, and as the primary cause of cancer-related mortality, with approximately 1.8 million deaths, which represents 18.7 % of the total cancer deaths [1]. Lung cancer can be classified into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) based on the histological types. LUAD is one of the most prevalent histological subtype of NSCLC, accounting for 38.5 % of all instances of lung cancer [2]. Currently, patients can receive treatment with surgery, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, which significantly bolstered their prospects for survival. However, the majority who are diagnosed with advanced lung cancer still have poor OS [3]. At the time of diagnosis, the majority of lung cancer patients are either in the local or advanced stages, which prevents them from receiving surgery, also chemotherapeutic drugs lack specificity with harmful side effects [4]. Therefore, it is urgent to explore deeper into the initiation and progression of lung cancer to identify potential targets for early diagnosis, solid prognosis and effective treatment with potent pharmaceuticals for clinical usage.

Cell death, categorized into forms such as apoptosis, necrosis, autophagy, iron death, and copper death, plays an essential role in fundamental physiological processes including development, immunity, and the maintenance of tissue homeostasis [[5], [6], [7]]. Gan boyi et al. identified a novel form of cell death distinct from the previous kinds of cell death and named as disulfidptosis in 2023, which is brought on by an aberrant buildup of disulfides [8]. Severe cell toxicity resulted from abnormal intracellular disulfide buildup, such as cystine, which can cause disulfide stress [9]. As a member of the cystine/glutamate antiporter family, solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) is primarily responsible for the transport of amino acids across the plasma membrane [10,11]. Disulfidptosis is induced by glucose deprivation in cells with high levels of SLC7A11 protein, and disulfide buildup causes actin cytoskeleton to collapse, resulting in cell death. In preclinical model, treatment with glucose inhibitors can stimulate the desulfurization of cancer cells with high SLC7A11 expression, effectively suppress tumor development, without obvious damage to normal organs. Thus, it is of significance to further research on targeted disulfidptosis as one therapeutic strategy in cancers.

Approximately 98 % of the human transcriptome is composed of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), which are pivotal in an array of pathological and physiological processes, with a particular significance in cancer. Consequently, they have become a central focus in contemporary research [12]. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), with lengths exceeding 200 base pairs, are integral to both transcriptional activation and repression mechanisms. They affect the epigenetic status of target genes by interacting with epigenetic proteins, prevent translation and mRNA degradation, and take part in a variety of physiological processes. LncRNAs have been shown to be intricately involved in the development and progression of cancer [13], and associated with drug resistance in tumor therapy [14]. The dysregulated expression of specific lncRNAs, is intimately associated with the onset and progression of various cancers, has opened up a new field of tumor research. However, few study has been conducted to explore the relation between disulfidptosis-related lncRNA (DRLs) and the prognosis of LUAD patients.

In this study, we have developed a novel DRLs signature and conducted an in-depth analysis to evaluate its prognostic significance and biological roles within LUAD. Our primary objective was to establish the utility of the DRLs signature in predicting the clinical outcomes of LUAD patients and to uncover potential therapeutic targets. The refined DRLs signature not only enhances our understanding of LUAD pathogenesis but also paves the way for more precise patient stratification and personalized treatment approaches. This work builds a prediction model with six DRLs by using data from TCGA database, and then validates its prognostic accuracy. Additionally, gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis was conducted to explore the potential mechanisms of DRLs in LUAD. Also paired tumor tissues were used to validate the expression of these DRLs.

Materials and methods

Data collection and DRL identification

Download the patients’ clinical details (age, sex, grade, stage, tumor lymph node metastasis, survival time, and survival status) from TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) [15], with 541 LUAD tissues and 59 normal tissues. The disulfidptosis-associated genes (GYS1, NDUFS1, OXSM, LRPPRC, NDUFA11, NUBPL, NCKAP1, RPN1, SLC3A2, and SLC7A11) were retrieved [8]. The lncRNA comment data was obtained from the GENCODE website (https://www.gencodegenes.org/). With a correlation coefficient >0.4 and P value < 0.001, DRLs were identified by R language (4.1.3) limma package [16], and the co-expression data were visualized via ggplot2 and ggalluvial tools [17].

Construction of prediction features of DRLs

The LUAD data from TCGA were randomly partitioned into training and testing cohorts. Data from the training group were used to build the prognostic model, and verified by data from testing group and all sample groups. We combined the survival information of lncRNA and LUAD patients through the limma package. A univariate Cox analysis on the data from the training cohort was performed to determine the lncRNAs associated with the prognosis of LUAD, then displayed the findings by drawing a forest map. Using the training cohort, we carried out LASSO regression analysis for optimizing the disulfidptosis related prognostic lncRNAs and preventing data overfitting. The lncRNAs were identified through Cox regression analysis, and the risk score of LUAD patients was calculated based on the prognosis model. Risk score = (Exp × bi). (Exp: expression level of model gene; bi: model gene coefficient). Then we read the expression of disulfidptosis related genes and lncRNA, used the limma package, the reshape2 package, and the tidyverse package to analyze the correlation, and screened the six disulfidptosis related lncRNA. And the ggplot2 package is employed to visualize the results [18].

Validation of risk models

The samples in both the training and testing groups were stratified into high-risk and low-risk cohorts according to the median risk score. When comparing OS and PFS of LUAD patients in both groups, Kaplan-Meier analysis was used. After univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis to evaluate the prognostic efficacy for this risk prediction signature, the pheatmap software was used to draw a heatmap for lncRNA expression and a survival state map based on risk score. To assess the diagnostic value of DRLs, ROC curves were constructed, and the area under the curve (AUC) was determined using the survival and timeROC package.

Construction and calibration of the nomogram

A nomogram is established on the basis of multi-factor regression analysis, which integrates multiple prediction indicators and then uses a graduated line segment to draw on the same plane according to a certain proportion so as to express the relationship between the variables in the prediction model. Using the survival, regplot, and rms package, we combined the risk score with clinicopathological traits (gender, stage, T stage and N stage) of LUAD patients to construct a nomogram. The probability corresponding to the sum of the scores of each index is the survival probability of the patients. Calibration curves for 1-, 3-, and 5-year were plotted to evaluate the consistency between predicted and observed outcomes. Additionally, a C-index curve was generated to affirm the predictive accuracy of the nomogram.

Enrichment analysis

We used the limma package to analyze the differences between high and low risk groups and obtain risk-difference genes (Supplementary Table 1). ClusterProfiler [19], enrichplot packages were used for GO, KEGG, and GSEA analysis to investigate the potential pathways implicated in these risk differential genes.

Potential drug screening

The limma, ggpubr, and pRRophetic packages are utilized to screen prospective medications, and explore the sensitivity of various drugs in distinguishing between the high-risk and low-risk groups.

Correlation between DRL expression and prognosis

Download the RNA-seq data and clinical profiles for the LUAD project from TCGA. Process this data by excluding normal tissue samples and those lacking clinical details, subsequently employing the survival package for data analysis.

Evaluation of the diagnostic value of DRLs in LUAD

R programme pROC was used to perform analysis on the RNA-seq data in FPKM format of the LUAD data in TCGA, then log2-transformed the data to build the ROC curve. The ggplot2 software was utilized for visualization.

Correlation between DRL expression and clinical features

The correlation between the six DRL expression and LUAD staging was performed by The University of Alabama at Birmingham cancer data analysis Portal (UALCAN) (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu), serves as a comprehensive, user-friendly, and interactive online resource designed for the analysis of cancer omics data.

Prognostic and diagnostic value of DRLs

Download RNA-seq and clinical data for the LUAD project from TCGA. Process the profile to remove normal tissue samples and those without clinical annotations. Utilize with survival package for data analysis, employ the survminer and ggplot2 package for visualizing the results, and investigate the correlation between the expression of DRL and OS, disease-specific survival time (DSS), and the progression free interval (PFI). Then Cox regression analysis was performed using survival and rms packages. Finally, use the pROC package for ROC analysis of the data to explore the diagnostic value of DRLs in LUAD.

Sample collection

Tumor and paracancerous tissue samples were collected from 56 LUAD patients who had surgery from December 2021 to June 2022 in the Department of Thoracic Surgery at Affiliated Hospital of Jining Medical University. Each pair of tumors and paracancerous tissues came from the same patient. All patients did not receive any anticancer treatment before the operation. The research protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Jining Medical University with the approval number: 2021-11-C009. All specimens are immediately immersed in liquid nitrogen and stored afterwards.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Take 50–100 mg of LUAD tissue and paracancerous tissue and add TRIzol reagent to mix well. Then 200 μL of chloroform and 0.5 mL of isopropanol were added to extract the RNA. cDNAs were produced by reverse transcription using a commercial cDNA synthesis kit (SuperScript First-Strand Synthesis System, Thermo Fisher Scientific). SYBR Green Master Mix (Invitrogen, China) was utilized for the quantitative PCR. GAPDH was used as the endogenous control, with the relative expression level estimated by 2−ΔΔCt method. All the relevant primers are listed in the Supplementary Table 2.

Cell culture and transfection

Human LUAD cell line A549 was grown in a 37 °C CO2 incubator with double antibodies, RPMI-1640 plus 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS). A549 cells were seeded in a six-well plate, with siRNAs mixed with Lipofectamine 3000 for transfection.

Cell proliferation by CCK-8

2 × 103 cells were seeded into a 96-well plate and cultured in an incubator. The 10 μL CCK-8 solution was aliquoted into each well at time points of 0 h, 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 2 h. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm by spectrophotometry (BioTek, Vermont, USA).

Cell apoptosis detection by flow cytometry

3 × 105 cells were inoculated and cultured in six-well plates. Si-RNAs were utilized for transfection. After 48 h, the transfected cells were collected and detected of apoptosis using the flow cytometry with Annexin V staining.

Scratch wound healing assay

5 × 105 cells were inoculated in a six-well culture plate, and when the cell density was about 95 %, a 10 μL tip was used to make a scratch in the center of the plate. The dead cells were washed out with PBS and then added to the culture medium without serum. The images were taken at 0 h and 24 h, respectively. Use the ImageJ software to analyze the migration distance.

Transwell assay

In a six-well plate, 3 × 105 cells were cultivated. 100 μl of cells with serum-free 1640 media were added to the upper chamber, and 600 μl of medium into the lower chamber and cultured for a duration of time (24, 36, and 48 h) at 37 °C. After turning the chamber over onto the absorbent paper and removing the culture medium within, wash with PBS. Then the chamber was fixed for 20 min with 4 % paraformaldehyde, after which it was stained for 15 min with crystal violet, and the non-metastatic cells were removed using cotton swabs. Lastly, use a microscope to capture a photo.

Statistical analysis

All the cell function experiments were repeated independently at least three times, and data were provided as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). GraphPad Prism 8.0 was used for statistical analysis. Differences between two groups were analyzed using the t-test, and those between more than two groups were examined using one-way analysis of variance followed by the Tukey post hoc test. P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Patients were categorized into high and low-risk groups according to their calculated risk scores. Kaplan-Meier analysis was utilized to assess the OS and PFS in the training group, testing group, and the entire sample cohort. Notably, the high-risk group exhibited significantly poorer OS compared to the low-risk group within the training group (P < 0.001), testing group (P = 0.043) and all sample group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A–C). Similarly, the high-risk group showed obviously shorter PFS compared to the low-risk group (P = 0.013) (Fig. 4D). To assess accuracy of this prognostic model in predicting the outcomes, we performed the univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses based on age, gender, TNM and risk score. The univariate Cox findings showed that T stage (P < 0.001, HR = 1.618), M stage (P = 0.034, HR = 1.858), N stage (P < 0.001, HR = 1.732), and risk score (P < 0.001, HR = 1.260) were correlated with the LUAD prognosis (Fig. 6A). Moreover, T stage (P = 0.010, HR = 1.323), N stage (P < 0.001, HR = 1.558) and risk score (P < 0.001, HR = 1.253) were identified as independent variables in the multivariate Cox analysis that affected LUAD prognosis (Fig. 6B). Additionally, we assessed the diagnostic value of DRLs in LUAD using the ROC curve. In comparison to age (AUC = 0.496), gender (AUC = 0.507), and M stage (AUC = 0.530), the results revealed that the AUC of N stage, T stage and risk score was 0.668, 0.637 and 0.664, respectively (Fig. 6C). Enrichment analysis and drug sensitivity analysis

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted to investigate the underlying biological pathways involved. GO analysis demonstrated that, within the biological process category, there was significant enrichment in cilium movement, microtubule-based movement, microtubule bundle formation, and axoneme assembly (Fig. 8A). The cellular components were located in the axoneme, ciliary plasm, plasma membrane-bounded cell projection cytoplasm, and cytoplasmic region (Fig. 8B). As for the molecular function, it was shown in microtubule motor activity, tubulin binding, and cytoskeletal motor activity (Fig. 8C). The genes within the KEGG category were mainly in the complement and coagulation cascades, cell cycle, motor proteins, and arachidonic acid metabolism (Fig. 8D).Fig. 8Enrichment analysis for GO, KEGG and GSEA. Besides, GSEA revealed that the genes in the high-risk group were mostly related to cell cycle, DNA replication, and cancerous pathways, while the genes in the low-risk group were primarily connected to allograft rejection, asthma, and primary immunodeficiency (Fig. 8E–F).

Discussion

Despite significant strides in understanding the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of lung cancer, it continues to hold the highest incidence and mortality rates among all malignancies, with a steady upward trend [20]. Lung cancer has a less favorable prognosis than other malignancies, mostly due to its complicated and poorly understood pathophysiology [21]. Discovering novel biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis of lung cancer is therefore critically needed. The relevance of disulfidptosis in cancer remains unknown as a novel form of cell death. It is commonly understood to be a type of abrupt cell death brought on by disulfide stress by an excessive buildup of cystine in cells. The polymerization of disulfide bonds in mitochondria has been shown to influence tumor growth [22]. Elevated serum disulfide levels have been identified in breast cancer patients, indicating a possible contribution to the pathogenesis of the disease [23]. Liu et al. has reported that the buildup of disulfides within tumor cells may trigger cellular disintegration and demise, providing a novel avenue for tumor therapy [8].

In our present research, patients were stratified into high and low-risk groups according to their calculated risk scores. Prognostic model and nomogram were created and validated. The high-risk group exhibited significantly shorter OS and PFS compared to the low-risk group. In LUAD patients, survival time were observed to decrease and mortality rates to increase with rising risk scores. Furthermore, the T stage, N stage and risk score were determined to be independent prognostic factors influencing the prognosis of LUAD. AL365181.2 was an independent prognostic risk factor for patients with LUAD. The six DRLs showed different expression in LUAD paired tissues, linked to cancer stage and prognosis, with relatively superior diagnostic values for LUAD.

In this work, we obtained 15 lncRNA associated with disulfidptosis. Six DRLs (AC012073.1, AC012615.1, AL365181.2, AL606834.1, EMSLR, and SNHG12) were identified to connect with LUAD prognosis. Among all these DRLs, AC012073.1, related to cuproptosis and ferroptosis, could well predict the prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [24,25]. AC012615.1, previously found involved in epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), can predict the prognosis of individuals with glioblastoma (GBM). Immune-associated lncRNA AL365181.2 was discovered to predict LUAD prognosis [26]. Similarly, AC012073.1 could forecast the prognosis of LUAD patients [27,28]. Patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer may benefit from AC012615.1, which is connected to necroptosis [29]. Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) and shunitinib-resistant RCC both have high levels of SNHG12 expression, indicating a poor clinical outcome. SNHG12 could promote RCC cell to proliferate, migrate, invade, and become resistant to shunitinib via regulation of CDCA3 [30]. Thus, the six DRLs evaluated in this study are linked to tumor prognosis.

Tumor drug resistance is the fundamental reason for the failure of LUAD chemotherapy. Due to the drug resistance of tumors, conventional chemotherapeutic drugs for patients with LUAD are inevitably limited. Therefore, we explored the correlation between model genes and anticancer drug sensitivity so as to provide new ideas for exploring tumor treatment. To investigate the potential drugs for tumor treatment, we screened and got nine antineoplastic drugs (doramapimod, axitinib, ribociclib, sinularin, leflunomide, savolitinib, erlotinib, 5-fluorouracil and foretinib). Axitinib is an inhibitor of the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR). Research showed that pembrolizumab combined with axitinib and sunitinib could be used as the first-line treatment for advanced RCC [31]. Sinularin has been revealed to have inhibitory effects on melanoma, gastric cancer, and liver cancer cells [[32], [33], [34]]. Leflunomide is a pyrimidine synthesis inhibitor that acts by inhibiting dihydroorotated dehydrogenase and has an anti-rheumatic effect [[35], [36], [37]]. 5-Fluorouracil is an analogue of uracil, which affects the synthesis of pyrimidine by inhibiting thymidylate synthase, and depleting the intracellular dTTP pool. Thus, 5-fluorouracil is an effective antineoplastic drug widely used in the clinic [38]. Based on the drug sensitivity analysis, the above nine drugs might be utilized as potential anti-LUAD drugs in the future.

Moreover, tumor immune escape has been a hot topic in the tumor therapy field recently. Several kinds of immune cells exist in the microenvironment of tumor tissue. These cells could directly or indirectly affect the tumor microenvironment and regulate cell behaviors. Consequently, immune cell therapy holds a promising future and a wide range of applications in the treatment of LUAD as well as other tumor types. The six screened DRLs mainly showed negative correlation with most immune cells, which has certain value and significance for LUAD immunotherapy in clinical work.

TCGA database and clinical samples were used to confirm the expression of DRLs. The results demonstrated that DRLs were differentially expressed in LUAD tumor tissues compared with normal tissues. Thus, we speculated that the six selected DRLs may play a role in the initiation and development of LUAD with different function. Targeting these DRLs for tumor therapy might be a very promising strategy.

Despite the aforementioned findings, there are still certain limitations in our study. First of all, there might exist a selection bias of the data from TCGA. Also our model may have the problem of overfitting, and more external validation badly needed to avoid this problem. Secondly, the analysis of the three selected GSE datasets was not able to fully substantiate all of our findings, as it confirmed the expression patterns for only one of the six DRLs, specifically SNHG12, and not the remaining five. This constitutes one of the limitations within our study. While we have utilized cell experiments to explore the biological functions of DRLs in LUAD, we acknowledge that our study lacks a more in-depth exploration of the underlying mechanisms, which represents a limitation in our research. Finally, we obtained LUAD tissue samples; however, time constraints prevented us from pursuing the prognostic data of all samples. Thus, we are unable to confirm the predictive significance of DRLs using our samples. More clinical data will be gathered to confirm the potential application of DRLs in future.

Conclusion

Overall, we have established a prognostic model for LUAD patients based on six DRLs. This model can accurately forecast the prognosis of LUAD patients and holds significant diagnostic value for LUAD. These findings are instrumental in advancing the comprehension of the mechanism underlying the initiation and progression of LUAD. They also offer novel perspectives on survival prognostication and therapeutic strategies for LUAD patients.

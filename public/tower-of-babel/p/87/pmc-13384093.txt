Ease of Use of the Lebrikizumab Prefilled Pen for Individuals with Atopic Dermatitis
Authors: Jennifer Soung, William Miller, Abel D Jarell, David Greenstein, Louise DeLuca-Carter, Heidi M Crane, Michael Behling, Kevin Duffy, Evangeline Pierce, Mona Shahriari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAtopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic inflammatory skin disease that can require long-term systemic therapy in individuals with moderate-to-severe disease. Lebrikizumab is a monoclonal antibody that targets interleukin 13, an important cytokine involved in AD pathogenesis. A single-use, prefilled pen has been developed to enable self-injection, and its usability is the focus of this study. Because ease of use and user confidence are considered important factors in self‑injection experiences, evaluating the usability of injection devices is an important component of patient-centered care. This study assessed the usability of the lebrikizumab prefilled pen in adults with AD in a simulated injection setting using a validated patient-reported questionnaire.MethodsIn this open-label, single-site, single-visit simulated injection study (NCT06444165), 55 adults with AD performed a simulated injection using a relabeled commercial lebrikizumab prefilled pen (250 mg/2 mL) after training. The injection was performed into an injection pad designed to mimic skin tissue. Participants completed the modified Subcutaneous Administration Assessment Questionnaire (mSQAAQ), a 10-item instrument assessing ease of use and confidence. The primary endpoints were responses to 2 questions: “Overall, easy to use” and “Confidence in ability to use the device.” All analyses were descriptive.ResultsAmong 55 participants, 96.4% agreed or strongly agreed that the pen was “overall, easy to use”, and 96.4% agreed or strongly agreed they were “confident in their ability to use the device” based on the mSQAAQ. Over 92% of participants agreed or strongly agreed with each of the remaining mSQAAQ items.ConclusionThe lebrikizumab prefilled pen demonstrated high reported ease of use and user confidence in adults with AD following a trained, simulated injection.Keywords: atopic dermatitis, lebrikizumab, prefilled pen, usability assessment, self-injection device

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease that significantly impairs quality of life and often requires long-term systemic therapy in moderate-to-severe cases.1 While topical treatments remain the first-line approach, many patients experience inadequate disease control or are unsuitable candidates for topical regimens.2 In this context, biologic therapies targeting key immunologic pathways have emerged as effective and safe options for sustained disease management.3

Lebrikizumab is a novel monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity and slow off-rate to interleukin (IL)-13, a central cytokine in the pathogenesis of AD, thereby blocking the downstream effects of IL-13 with high potency.4 It is indicated for the treatment of adults and pediatric patients aged ≥12 years and weighing ≥40 kg with moderate-to-severe AD who are not adequately controlled with topical therapies or for whom such therapies are not advisable.5 As with other biologics, lebrikizumab therapy depends not only on its pharmacologic efficacy but also on the usability of its delivery system, particularly in the context of self-administration.6

Prefilled pens (sometimes referred to as autoinjectors) have become increasingly favored for subcutaneous drug delivery due to their potential to improve patient adherence, reduce administration errors, and enhance the overall treatment experience.7 Eli Lilly and Company has a long-standing history of developing single-dose prefilled pens for various biologics, including dulaglutide, tirzepatide, ixekizumab, and galcanezumab.8–11 The lebrikizumab prefilled pen, designed to deliver 250 mg in a 2 mL subcutaneous injection, leverages the same engineering architecture, user steps, and user interface as these established devices.

A recent engineering study comparing the lebrikizumab and dupilumab spring-actuated prefilled pens highlighted several design features of the lebrikizumab device that may enhance patient experience during self-injection.12 However, the present study did not include a comparator device and did not assess comparative usability between devices. In this benchtop study, the lebrikizumab pen delivers medication with a shorter injection duration, reducing the time the needle would remain in tissue. It also provides a reliable end-of-dose indication through a second audible click that occurs after injection completion. The device’s transparent shell and base allow full visual inspection of both the drug and the injection site. Its broad base eliminates the need to pinch-up skin and improves stability while exerting less pressure on the skin surface.12

Given the importance of ease of use in chronic disease management, this study aimed to evaluate the usability of the lebrikizumab prefilled pen among adults with AD. Specifically, the study assessed participants’ perceptions of ease of use and confidence in administering a simulated injection using the device. The modified Subcutaneous Administration Assessment Questionnaire (mSQAAQ, a 10-item instrument adapted for this study) was used to capture user-reported outcomes immediately following a simulated injection into an injection pad. However, the present study did not include a comparator device to assess comparative usability with other available devices.

We hypothesized that patients with AD with no prior experience using prefilled pens would find the lebrikizumab prefilled pen “overall, easy to use” and that they would be “confident in their ability to use the device”. The findings from this study are intended to provide insight into patient‑reported usability of the device in a simulated injection setting.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This was an open-label, single-site, single-visit simulated injection study conducted in the US, designed to evaluate the usability of the lebrikizumab prefilled pen in adults with AD. A simulated injection design was chosen to focus on device usability, the primary objective, which is one aspect of the full injection experience. We have previously reported on lebrikizumab efficacy and safety.13–15

The study was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06444165) on 31 May 2024, prior to enrollment of the first participant on 04 June 2024. This study was conducted using a commercial, single-dose prefilled pen relabeled without branding (Figure 1). Participants were eligible if they were aged 18 years or older, self-reported having been diagnosed with AD by a physician, and were naïve to autoinjector devices. Prior experience with prefilled syringes was permitted. After receiving standardized training, each participant performed a single simulated subcutaneous injection into an injection pad and subsequently completed the mSQAAQ, a patient-reported outcome questionnaire that evaluates one’s experience with a device designed to administer a SC injection.

Lebrikizumab unbranded commercial prefilled pen used in the simulated usability study, showing key functional components.

The planned sample size was 51 participants, to ensure that the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval for the primary usability endpoints would remain above 85%, assuming a 95% positive response rate.

Training and Procedure

Participants were trained on the use of the lebrikizumab prefilled pen prior to performing the simulated injection. The training included viewing a short instructional video demonstrating the injection process and reviewing the Instructions for Use (IFU) document provided with the device. The simulated injection was performed using an injection pad to mimic subcutaneous administration. Following the simulated injection, participants independently completed the mSQAAQ to evaluate their experience with the device.

Assessment Tool

The SQAAQ is an innovative, 12-item, self-administered questionnaire designed to evaluate the user-friendliness of the device and the participant’s confidence during the administration of a subcutaneous injection of a medication. In this study, the participant experience was evaluated using the mSQAAQ10,16 to evaluate ease of use and confidence in the ability to use the lebrikizumab pen in a simulated environment. The mSQAAQ questionnaire, for this study, was comprised of 10 questions, as 2 questions from the original SQAAQ (#6, #10) were removed as they are not applicable to the simulation setting—ease of refrigeration and device stability against the skin. The responses were scored using a 7-point Likert scale ranging from “Strongly Disagree” to “Strongly Agree.” The primary endpoints were responses to Question #9 (“Overall, easy to use”) and Question #11 (“I am confident in my ability to use the device”). Secondary endpoints included responses to the remaining mSQAAQ items (see Table 1). For each item, the number and percentage of participants who responded “strongly agree” or “agree” were combined and summarized.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses were descriptive in nature. Data are reported as observed, with no imputations for missing data conducted. The primary analysis focused on the proportion of participants who selected “Agree” or “Strongly Agree” for the primary and secondary mSQAAQ items. No inferential statistical testing was performed. Confidence Intervals (CIs) are calculated using Clopper-Pearson. CIs were only used for sample size justification and are not reported for the outcome. Percentages did not include the missing category and were calculated over the number of patients with available (non-missing) data. Data were analyzed using SAS 9.3 or higher.

Results

Participant Characteristics

A total of 55 adults with atopic dermatitis (AD) were enrolled in the study. The mean age was 50.1 years (SD 16.37), and 69.1% were female. The racial distribution included 54.5% White, 41.8% Black or African American, and 1.8% each identifying as “Other” or “Multiple.” Nearly all participants (98.2%) were non-Hispanic. Educational attainment varied, with 5.5% having less than a high school education, 21.8% completing high school or the General Education Development (GED), 29.1% attending some college or technical school, and 43.6% holding a college or technical school degree or higher (Table 2).

Most participants (94.5%) were injection-naïve, with only 5.5% reporting prior experience using a prefilled syringe. Health literacy, assessed using the Rapid Estimate of Adult Literacy in Medicine (REALM), was normal in 89.1% of participants, while 10.9% had limited health literacy. A small number of participants reported conditions affecting hand or wrist function, including carpal tunnel syndrome (3.6%), osteoarthritis (1.8%), and other conditions (1.8%); no participants reported rheumatoid arthritis or neuropathy (Table 2).

Primary Outcomes

The primary endpoints of the study were participants’ responses to two key items on the modified Subcutaneous Administration Assessment Questionnaire (mSQAAQ): overall ease of use and confidence in using the device. Of the 55 participants, 96.4% (n=53) agreed or strongly agreed that the lebrikizumab prefilled pen was “overall, easy to use,” and an identical proportion (96.4%, n=53) reported being “confident in their ability to use the device” (Figure 2).

Secondary Outcomes

High levels of agreement were also observed across the remaining mSQAAQ items. More than 92% of participants agreed or strongly agreed with each of the additional mSQAAQ items, which assessed aspects such as ease of unlocking the device, holding it during injection, removing the needle shield, and knowing when the dose was complete (Table 1). Notably, one participant strongly disagreed with each item on the mSQAAQ despite completing all the injection steps correctly, as observed by the site staff.

Safety

No adverse events (AEs) or serious adverse events (SAEs) occurred in this study.

Injection Simulation Observations

Three participants (5.5%) did not successfully complete all simulated injection tasks. One participant attempted the injection without first uncapping the pen, while a second participant unlocked the pen before placing it on the injection pad. A third participant did not hold the pen on the pad for the required 15 seconds and reported not hearing the second click, which was later identified as a product complaint. These observations underscore the importance of training and clear Instructions for Use materials.

Product Complaints

There were 2 product complaints reported during the study. In both instances, the participants did not hear the second click to indicate the end of the injection. Additionally, for one of the instances, it was also noted that the injection was incomplete. Investigation of the complaints concluded that there were no deficiencies with the pens. It was discovered that the injection pads interfered with the injections, causing the delayed second click and partial injection. No AEs were associated with these complaints. These observations highlight the limitations of a simulated injection environment.

Discussion

This study demonstrated high reported ease of use and user confidence with the lebrikizumab prefilled pen among adults with AD, with 96.4% of participants agreeing or strongly agreeing that the device was “overall, easy to use” and that they were “confident in their ability to use the device” after training and performing a simulated injection. These findings are consistent across all usability domains assessed by the mSQAAQ in a trained, simulated injection setting. Notably, only 5.5% of participants had previous injection experience (using a prefilled syringe), yet still reported a highly positive experience with the device in this simulated injection design.

The device’s design incorporates several features that support ease of use and dose delivery confidence, including a wide, textured barrel for grip, a flared base that eliminates the need to pinch the skin, and audible clicks that confirm both the start and completion of injection. These features were informed by human factors research and align with those described in a previous investigation, which evaluated a similar prefilled pen for ixekizumab.10 Importantly, the pen was engineered to accommodate users with dexterity, cognitive, or visual impairments; considerations relevant for patients with AD and other chronic inflammatory conditions such as psoriasis and rheumatoid arthritis, as well as the general population.

The findings also align with previous studies evaluating the usability of similar prefilled pens,10,16 which used the SQAAQ instrument and reported high levels of ease of use and user confidence for both the ixekizumab and galcanezumab prefilled pens. Additionally, in the PREFER study, patients with type 2 diabetes preferred the dulaglutide injection device (similar in design to the lebrikizumab pen) primarily for its ease of use.11 Similarly, a survey of injectable diabetes treatments found that patients prioritized confidence in dose delivery and ease of use over other device attributes.17 These consistent results across therapeutic areas highlight the importance of intuitive device design in promoting adherence and satisfaction.

Three participants in this study did not successfully complete all simulated injection tasks. Additionally, two participants reported product complaints related to the end‑of‑dose click and incomplete injection. The investigation of these product complaints concluded there were no device deficiencies, and the observed delayed click and partial injection were attributed to interference from the injection pads rather than a device malfunction or user error. These findings are relevant, as they relate to correct device handling and confidence in dose completion, but also underscore the limitations of a simulated injection environment.

Study limitations should be acknowledged. The simulated injection model does not fully replicate real-world conditions, as usability was assessed using an injection pad rather than actual human skin. Additionally, the single-site design with a modest sample size limits generalizability. As no comparator device was included, this study cannot determine whether the lebrikizumab prefilled pen is easier to use than other available injection devices. Nevertheless, the consistency of findings with prior device usability studies provides contextual support for the observed results, and the inclusion of participants with varied health literacy and mild physical impairments helps inform interpretation within the scope of a trained, simulated injection setting. Finally, although responses were summarized as combined “Agree/Strongly Agree” categories for descriptive usability endpoints, this approach reduces response granularity; full response distributions are therefore provided in Table S1.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the lebrikizumab prefilled pen demonstrated high reported ease of use and confidence among adults with AD following standardized training and a simulated injection. These results support the usability of the device in a controlled, simulated setting. Further real‑world studies are needed to evaluate actual self‑administration experiences, adherence, persistence, and longitudinal patient preferences under routine clinical conditions.

Introduction

The paranasal sinuses (PNS) are a group of four paired air-filled spaces located within the bony skull; maxillary, frontal, sphenoid, and ethmoid sinuses. They have various functions, including lightening the weight of the head, humidifying, and warming inhaled air, increasing the resonance of speech, and serving as a shock-resistant “buffer” to protect vital structures in case of head trauma.

Development of PNS starts prenatally and continues through the course of an.

Organism's lifetime, their development and final shape show a great diversity of variations.

PNS aplasia is a rare condition that is mainly confined to the frontal sinus unilaterally.

Combined aplasia of multiple sinuses along with hypoplasia of other sinuses is extremely Rare. Agenesis of the sphenoid sinus is also rare and may present with craniofacial abnormalities and syndromes.

In this report, we present a case of combined aplasia of bilateral frontal and sphenoid sinuses.

Case report

A 13-year-old male patient presented to our ENT clinic complaining of a prolonged history of persistent headache, nasal obstruction, and nasal discharge. He also complained of bilateral ears fullness and facial pain. There was no fever, no family history of similar complaints, no history of any nasal surgery, facial trauma, or any systemic disease.

On physical examination, bilateral purulent nasal discharge with congested inflamed nasal mucosa and both inferior turbinate, no deviation of the nasal septum, and there was postnasal drainage. The nasal cavity appears to be normal with a normal facial appearance.

The patient was diagnosed to have sinusitis and was started on treatment with antibiotics, regular nasal douching, and corticosteroid containing nasal spray. The patient returns to our clinic after several weeks with minimal improvement. Discussion

The development of the paranasal sinuses (PNS) appears prenatally which can lead to a variety of PNS anomalies. However, the aplasia or hypoplasia of the PNS is found to be rare [1]. Aplasia is mainly found in frontal and maxillary sinuses with a prevalence of 12 % and 6 %, respectively [2]. While the prevalence of sphenoid sinus aplasia is extremely rare and found to be less than 0.7 %. [3]. Moreover, aplasia of multiple sinuses “combined aplasia” is an incredibly unusual finding [1]. These PNS anomalies can be confirmed by the computerized tomography (CT) scan, which provides an excellent anatomical and detailed sinuses map [4].

The frontal sinuses are normally absent at birth and started developing at the age of one year. Its growth increased reaching the peak at 6 years old and still growing until reaching the final maximum size in late teenage years around the age of 20 years [1,5]. This indicates that the frontal sinus is the last PNS in development [1]. Notwithstanding, aplasia of sinuses is most found in frontal sinuses with a prevalence of 6.6 % for unilateral frontal sinuses aplasia (FSA) and 4.2 % prevalence of bilateral FSA [3,6]. It is prevalence was found to be significantly higher in males (7.0 %) than in females (1.2 %) [6]. Moreover, frontal sinus aplasia or hypoplasia is significantly higher and coexisted with maxillary hypoplasia rather than normal maxillary sinuses [7].

The sphenoid sinuses can be detected prenatally in a fetus at 4 months and started to grow in size from a small size at birth to reaching the final shape and size by the age of 12 years [8]. Although sphenoid sinus aplasia is an extremely uncommon finding, there were 2 cases of uncombined sphenoid aplasia reported in the literature. The first reported case was for a 32-year-old male with a history of the Hand-Schuller-Christian disease [8] and the second case was for a female patient aged 18 years who complained of headache and nasal obstruction with normal skeletal and cranial anatomy except the absence of sphenoid sinus [9]. These two cases highlighted the importance of detecting any undiagnosed sphenoid aplasia before surgeons scheduled transsphenoidal or endoscopic sinus surgeries [9,10].

Regarding combined aplasia, Khanduri S et al. Reported a case of an adult female who complained of persistent headache throughout the day and night, intermittent nasal obstruction, and nasal discharge. The computed tomography (CT) scan of her PNS revealed bilateral aplasia of the frontal sinuses, aplasia of sphenoid sinuses, hypoplasia of bilateral maxillary sinuses, and hypoplasia of the ethmoid sinus [1]. Also, Güven DG et al. Reported a case of adult women with CT findings of frontal, sphenoid, ethmoid sinuses aplasia, and severe bilateral maxillary hypoplasia [10]. Likewise, Sharma S et al. Reported a case of an adult male who was diagnosed with Kartagener's syndrome (KS) and had similar CT findings to the case of Khanduri [11]. These three cases were presented with the adult age of presentation (54, 47, and 35 years, respectively) while our case was early detected at 13-year-old.

Conclusion

This patient is one of the extremely rare cases of the combined aplasia of frontal and sphenoid sinuses who was diagnosed and confirmed at an early age without any skeletal, systemic, or hematological abnormalities. Despite that, this rare case should be considered in mind before scheduling any endoscopic sinus surgery to prevent complications during these surgeries.

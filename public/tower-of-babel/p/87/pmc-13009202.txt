Primate embryoid models: functional interrogation of late gastrulation
Authors: Kyungjun Uh, Yonggyu Kim, Taeho Kwon
Published: 2026

In a recent study published in Nature, Li et al. address a key experimental limitation in developmental biology by developing a stem cell-derived monkey embryoid system that supports development from blastoids to day 25, corresponding to human Carnegie stages 8–9.1 Experimental access to late primate gastrulation has remained limited, despite the central role of this developmental window in coordinating signaling programs that establish the body plan and initiate early organogenesis, a gap that this platform now enables to be directly interrogated in vitro.

Gastrulation is a decisive phase of embryogenesis during which spatially and temporally coordinated signaling events establish the anterior–posterior axis, specify the three germ layers, and initiate the earliest transitions toward organ formation. In primates, abnormalities during this period account for a substantial proportion of early pregnancy loss and congenital defects.1 However, mechanistic investigation of these processes has been severely constrained by limited access to embryos, technical challenges in prolonged in vitro culture (IVC), and ethical restrictions such as the 14-day rule.2 Although stem cell-derived embryo models have emerged as promising alternatives, most human and primate systems have remained restricted to pre-gastrulation or early gastrulation stages and frequently deviate from normal developmental trajectories.2,3

Li et al. overcome these limitations by developing an optimized 3D suspension culture system that supports prolonged development of stem cell-derived monkey embryoids beyond day 17.1 This extended development is enabled by an enhanced IVC system that integrates stage-adapted medium formulations with optimized three-dimensional suspension culture conditions, providing a permissive environment for sustained morphogenesis during late gastrulation. The use of low-passage naïve monkey embryonic stem cells together with carefully optimized parameters enables high-efficiency and reproducible progression to day 25.1 Importantly, the embryoids preserve well-defined embryonic disc architecture and anterior–posterior organization, creating a permissive context for sustained and coordinated interlayer signaling interactions during late gastrulation (Fig. 1).

Morphological and histological analyses demonstrate that the embryoids closely resemble age-matched in vivo cynomolgus monkey embryos. Hallmark features of late gastrulation are readily observed, including elongation and folding of the embryonic disc, extension of the primitive streak, and coordinated differentiation across ectodermal, mesodermal, and endodermal lineages. Early neural plate-like regions, primitive gut domains, allantoic structures, and yolk sac-derived haematopoietic progenitors emerge in a spatially ordered manner, indicating that differentiation proceeds as an integrated developmental program rather than as isolated lineage specification events.1 However, trophoblast derivatives fail to develop properly and progressively degenerate during prolonged culture.

Single-cell transcriptomic profiling provides further evidence of the developmental fidelity of the system. Analysis of embryoids cultured between day 17 and day 25 identifies cell populations corresponding to epiblast, primitive streak, definitive and visceral endoderm, mesodermal derivatives, primordial germ cell-like cells, and early neuroectoderm. These populations display differentiation trajectories and transcriptional programs closely aligned with those of natural cynomolgus monkey embryos at developmentally equivalent stages corresponding to Carnegie stages 8–9. This alignment reflects coordinated activation of signaling pathways governing epithelial–mesenchymal transition, axial elongation, and early lineage commitment.

Crucially, signaling outputs within the embryoids are spatially organized in a manner consistent with in vivo primate development. Neural plate-associated markers such as SOX2 and OTX2 localize to the anterior-dorsal domain, whereas FOXA2-marked endodermal territories emerge ventrally, reflecting tightly regulated signaling gradients across the embryonic disc.1 This spatial patterning indicates that the embryoids execute integrated, tissue-level signaling programs characteristic of late gastrulation. Importantly, this organization extends beyond simple recapitulation of transcriptional identities.

Beyond descriptive reconstruction of late gastrulation, this study establishes the monkey embryoid system as a functional platform for interrogating developmental programs through genetic perturbation.1 Targeted disruption of TBXT and EOMES, two well-established regulators of primitive streak formation, demonstrates that the embryoids respond to gene perturbation in a stage-appropriate and interpretable manner during late gastrulation.1 These perturbations lead to predictable alterations in gastrulation-associated transcriptional programs. As a result, the system enables dissection of the regulatory logic governing the transition from gastrulation to subsequent lineage diversification in primates.

As a model focused on late gastrulation, the embryoids do not progress beyond day 25, and the development of certain extraembryonic signaling centers, including node-associated structures and mature trophoblast derivatives, remains incomplete.1 These limitations mirror those observed in cultured natural primate embryos and underscore the need for additional signaling inputs, biomechanical cues, or maternal–embryonic interactions to support further development in vitro.

The limitations of the current system point toward clear directions for future refinement. The efficiency and developmental robustness of late-gastrulation embryoids appear to depend on the naïve state and passage history of the starting stem cell population, consistent with observations from human stem cell-derived embryo models.1,4

In this context, the progressive loss of trophoblast derivatives observed during prolonged culture may reflect constraints associated with the use of a single stem cell origin and the specific naïve pluripotent state of the starting cells. Such states may be suboptimal for sustained extraembryonic lineage differentiation, as suggested by recent studies of human naïve pluripotent stem cell-derived blastoids.4

Incorporating trophoblast-competent stem cell populations or assembling embryoids from multiple, developmentally primed cell sources may therefore help restore extraembryonic signaling interactions and extend developmental progression beyond current limits. Together, these findings highlight how this stem cell-derived embryoid platform bridges early and late gastrulation in primates and enables interrogation of developmental signaling programs that have previously remained inaccessible to experimental manipulation

Collectively, Li et al. elevate stem cell-derived monkey embryoids from a descriptive developmental proxy to a mechanistically informative model that enables functional analysis of how gastrulation-associated regulatory programs shape subsequent progression. This study illustrates that ethical boundaries need not limit scientific inquiry, but can instead catalyze the development of alternative, ethically grounded experimental systems. As recent work has demonstrated, integrating such embryo models with artificial intelligence-based analytical frameworks will be essential for managing developmental variability, standardizing model selection, and extracting reproducible biological insights from increasingly complex in vitro embryogenesis models.5 Together, these advances position primate embryoid models as a powerful platform for interrogating human development across basic biology, medicine, and translational research.

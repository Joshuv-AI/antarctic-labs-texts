Endovascular Low-Pass Filter
Authors: Matteo Biroli, Carlo Maria Giacari, Cristina Ferrari, Fabio Fazzari, Valentina Mantegazza, Federico De Marco
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe present the case of an 82-year-old man with a history of inferior vena cava filter implantation and concomitant severe mitral regurgitation requiring transcatheter edge-to-edge repair. Despite being deemed ineligible for transfemoral access as technically challenging, he successfully underwent mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair after crossing and dilatation of the inferior vena cava filter. (Level of Difficulty: Intermediate.)Key Words: cardiovascular disease, inferior vena cava filter, mitral valve, treatment, valve repair, x-ray fluoroscopy

An 82-year-old man presented to our heart valve unit because of markedly symptomatic (NYHA functional class III), severe degenerative mitral regurgitation resulting from long-standing posterior leaflet prolapse. Despite the highest guideline-directed medical therapy–tolerated dosages, symptoms progressively worsened, leading to 3 heart failure–related hospitalizations over the past 6 months.

The patient’s history included a cerebral hemorrhage (2010) followed by pulmonary thromboembolism secondary to deep vein thrombosis. Thus, an inferior vena cava (IVC) filter (OptEase, Cordis Endovascular) was implanted and never retrieved.

He was evaluated for mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) by other institutions, but the procedure was considered technically unfeasible because of the impossibility to safely cross the IVC filter’s mesh with the sheaths routinely used for TEER procedures; thus, the patient was referred to our heart valve unit for further management of his valvular disease.

Preprocedural transesophageal echocardiography showed thickened mitral leaflets and posterior leaflet prolapse (P2 level), creating an eccentric Coanda-like, anterior holosystolic jet, determining severe regurgitation (Figure 1A). Considering the IVC filter’s presence, the preprocedural planning included a contrast-enhanced computed tomography scan, confirming normal device positioning and patency (Figure 1B, Video 1). Management

Considering the patient’s deteriorating clinical conditions, a mitral TEER procedure was scheduled. After right femoral vein cannulation, IVC venography was performed, confirming the filter’s patency despite angiographic appearance of areas with incomplete contrast filling (Video 2). A hydrophilic wire supported by a multipurpose catheter (Cordis Endovascular) was used to negotiate the IVC filter’s mesh, crossing the external portion between the device and the IVC wall. This was then exchanged for an Amplatz super-stiff wire. Sequential inflations with increasing size balloons were performed with 4.0, 7.0, and 9.0 and Reliant balloons (Medtronic) (Figure 1C, Video 3). After confirming adequate lumen expansion with the Reliant balloon, a 24-F Gore DrySeal Sheath (Gore Medical) was carefully advanced through the IVC filter without any device’s movement (Figure 1D, Video 4). After transseptal puncture, the PASCAL delivery system (Edwards Lifesciences) was advanced within the DrySeal Sheath (Video 4) to avoid further interactions with the filter’s scaffold.

Two PASCAL Ace systems (Edwards Lifesciences) were finally delivered with good procedural transesophageal echocardiography outcomes (Figure 1E, Video 5). The final IVC venography showed no contrast leaks/filter displacement (Figure 1F).

Discussion

As the number of mitral/tricuspid transcatheter procedures increases, interventional cardiologists must be aware of possible encountered technical challenges. Numerous IVC filters are implanted and never retrieved,1 and an expanding cohort of these patients requires transcatheter mitral/tricuspid interventions. Although the transjugular approach is an option,2,3 it is technically challenging because devices’ steering/control is specifically designed for the transfemoral approach.

To our knowledge, this is the first case of a PASCAL TEER case performed through an IVC filter. Careful pre-procedural planning is essential to achieve optimal results and computed tomography had a pivotal role in analyzing filters position/patency and measures. Then, sequential inflation of varying size balloons was our chosen strategy to ensure enough space was present for safe 24-F Gore DrySeal passage through the OptEase scaffold’s tight mesh. The balloons expanded without distortion, allowing the introducer to smoothly navigate the mesh. Both the transseptal puncture equipment and PASCAL delivery system were then advanced within the DrySeal’s lumen to minimize interactions/vascular complications. This approach ensured an effective and uneventful procedure, as confirmed by the final IVC venography.

Conclusions

This case illustrates PASCAL mitral TEER feasibility in patients presenting with unretrievable IVC filters. Thus, such patients should not be considered a priori ineligible for transfemoral mitral/tricuspid transcatheter interventions.

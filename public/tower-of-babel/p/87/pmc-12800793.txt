Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI)–enabled electrocardiogram (ECG) has recently been shown to be able to predict the left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) response to cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) through left bundle branch area pacing (LBBAP).1 Using the Mayo Clinic AI-ECG dashboard, we have previously demonstrated a positive correlation between the AI-ECG–predicted low EF probability (AILEF) and the actual EF change after LBBAP, with a decrease in AILEF by ≥60% associated with the greatest EF increase.1, 2, 3, 4 The objectives of this study were to assess whether AILEF can help predict potential CRT super-response and to determine the sensitivity and specificity of using a 60% reduction in AILEF to identify CRT super-responders. This retrospective study included a total of 65 consecutive patients who underwent LBBAP at the Mayo Clinic Health System between July 2022 and December 2024 for CRT procedures. Eighty-five percent (55) of the patients had underlying native left bundle branch block or dominant right ventricular pacing in a left bundle branch block morphology. Thirty-four percent of these patients were women. A 3830 Medtronic Select Secure lumenless pacing lead was implanted in the left bundle branch area, as described elsewhere.5,6 Details of the AI-ECG assessment of the reduced EF model were previously published.2,4 The output of the AI-ECG model is a continuous value between 0 and 1, which represents the “probability of low EF.” Baseline and postprocedure ECGs were reviewed, and the probability of low EF was noted. In cases where multiple postoperative AI-ECGs were obtained, the one with the lowest AILEF was used. Transthoracic echocardiography studies at >3 months follow-up after LBBAP were also reviewed to assess the early predictive value of AI-ECG for CRT outcomes. Echocardiographic CRT super-response was defined as meeting one of the following criteria: (1) EF increase by ≥20%, (2) EF increase to ≥45% (with ≥10% absolute increase from baseline), and/or (3) left ventricular end-diastolic volume index decrease by ≥20% or end-systolic volume index decrease by ≥30%. The research reported in this article adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki guidelines, and the study was approved by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board. All patients provided written informed consent for the procedures performed in the study. The 2-tailed Student t test was used for statistical analysis, and a P value of <.05 was considered significant. Of the 65 patients, 56 had both baseline and post-CRT transthoracic echocardiographic data available. A total of 30 patients (54%) met the echocardiographic criteria for CRT super-response. CRT through LBBAP resulted in a significant decrease in AILEF (20% ± 44%; P < .01) as well as an increase in EF (16% ± 8%; P < .01) in the super-responder cohort. CRT super-responders showed a significantly greater decrease in AILEF (32 ± 41%) than did non–super-responders (6% ± 43%; P = .02) (Figure 1A and 1B). After a mean follow-up of 6 months, no major complications such as left bundle branch area lead dislodgment requiring lead revision or hospitalization were observed. Using an AILEF decrease of ≧60% as a cutoff, the AILEF test showed a sensitivity of 33% and a specificity of 92% for predicting CRT super-response (Figure 1C). These results demonstrated that the currently available AI-ECG algorithm showed a low sensitivity but high specificity for predicting CRT super-response. If positive, a test using AI-ECG may be useful in identifying patients who are future super-responders to CRT through LBBAP at the time of CRT device implantation. We are in the process of pursuing a larger prospective study to further validate this finding. If findings of this study are confirmed, the AI-ECG algorithm, when used intraoperatively, may help determine whether the defibrillator component is required, especially in those patients who are not considered good candidates for implantable defibrillators.

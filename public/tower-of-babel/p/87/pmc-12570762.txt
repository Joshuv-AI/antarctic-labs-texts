Factors affecting organizational commitment among nurses: a cross-sectional study on job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and demographic characteristics
Authors: Derya Şimşekli, Kader Öztürk, Tevfik Fikret Karahan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo determine the relationships between organizational commitment, job satisfaction, life satisfaction and demographic characteristics among nurses and to determine the predictors of organizational commitment.MethodsThe study was conducted as a cross-sectional study with 149 nurses in a public hospital between September and December 2024. Data were collected using the Descriptive Information Form, the Nurse Job Satisfaction Scale, the Organizational Commitment Scale, and the Life Satisfaction Scale. Descriptive statistics, independent sample t-test, one-way ANOVA test, Pearson correlation analysis and multiple regression analysis were applied in data analysis.ResultsOrganizational commitment was found to be 3.17 ± 0.58, job satisfaction was 3.48 ± 0.70, and life satisfaction was 19.72 ± 6.87. Multiple regression analysis conducted to determine the predictors of organizational commitment revealed that nurses’ job satisfaction and age variables significantly predicted organizational commitment (p < 0.05). The model explained 49.8% of the total variance in organizational commitment.ConclusionsThe results of this study highlight the importance of developing strategies and organizational interventions aimed at improving nurses’ job and life satisfaction and fostering organizational commitment. Interventions such as mentoring programs, stress management workshops, or flexible working hours may increase satisfaction and commitment levels.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Organizational commitment refers to an individual’s willingness to embrace and work towards institutional goals, ensuring continued service and participation. This commitment benefits both the institution and the service provider, improving service quality and satisfaction at both individual and organizational levels [1]. As employees’ commitment to the organization increases, their job satisfaction typically increases as well. Many studies report a relationship between organizational commitment and job satisfaction [2–4]. Job satisfaction, a concept rooted in motivation, is the degree to which individuals feel positively or negatively about their jobs. This feeling can also influence life satisfaction; as life satisfaction increases, job satisfaction tends to follow suit [5, 6].

Life satisfaction, which is a key aspect of subjective well-being, reflects individuals’ overall contentment with their lives. It is defined as the emotional foundation of an individual’s positive perceptions about life [7].

While no studies have specifically examined the relationship between organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and life satisfaction in nurses, two studies in the national literature have explored these concepts in different work environments, such as accommodation enterprises and village guards [8, 9]. However, numerous studies have shown the importance of these concepts in healthcare settings, where organizational commitment significantly influences service quality and employees’ satisfaction with their jobs [6, 10–16]. While organizational commitment and job satisfaction have been extensively studied in the international literature, there are limited studies on the interaction of these variables with life satisfaction and demographic characteristics among nurses. Research examining these issues together, particularly in Turkey or similar healthcare settings, is quite limited. This study aims to fill this gap and shed light on strategies to increase nurses’ organizational commitment.

Given this background, to determine the relationships between organizational commitment, job satisfaction, life satisfaction and demographic characteristics among nurses and to determine the predictors of organizational commitment. To achieve this, the following research questions were addressed:

Method

Study design and sample

The study was conducted as a cross-sectional study. Reported in accordance with STROBE guidelines. The population of the study consisted of 231 nurses serving in hospitals affiliated with the Ardahan Provincial Health Directorate in the east of Türkiye. Raosoft Sample Size program was used to calculate the sample size of the study [17]. The minimum sample size to be included in the study was 145 with 5% margin of error, 95% Confidence Interval and 50% response rate. Participants were reached through convenience sampling method. A total of 149 participants (participation rate 64.5%) who met the inclusion criteria were included in this cross-sectional study. Complete data were collected from all participants and analyses were conducted on 149 individuals. Post hoc analysis was performed with the G*power 3.1.9.7 program to determine whether the sample size of the study was sufficient. Accordingly, with an effect size of 0.3, an alpha margin of error of 0.05, and a sample size of 149 people, the calculation showed that the power was 0.98 and that this sample was sufficient. The criteria for inclusion in the study were determined as not being on leave or sick leave during the data collection process, working as a nurse for at least six months, and volunteering to participate. The research was conducted on a voluntary basis.

Data collection

Research data were collected using the Descriptive Data Form, Nurse Job Satisfaction Scale, Organizational Commitment Scale, and Life Satisfaction Scale. The data were collected face-to-face by the researchers: paper printouts of the scales were provided to the participants and collected back after completion.

Descriptive data form

The form consisted of a total of 11 questions created by reviewing the literature to question age, sex, economic status, marital status, place of residence, family type, having children, length of service in the profession, length of service in the current institution, smoking and alcohol use [18, 19].

Organizational commitment scale

The validity and reliability of the scale, which was developed by Meyer et al. in 1993, was performed by Dağlı et al. [19, 20]. The first 6 items of the 18-item scale measure “affective commitment”, the second 5 items measure “continuance commitment”, and the last 7 items measure “normative commitment”. The scale, which was designed in the form of a 5-point Likert, included the statements (1) “I strongly disagree”, (2) “I disagree”, (3) “I am undecided”, (4) “I agree” and (5) “I strongly agree”. Questions 3, 4, 5, and 13 on the scale are reverse-coded and the analysis of these questions is done by reversing. A high score on the scale indicates high organizational commitment and a low score indicates low organizational commitment. The Cronbach Alpha Value of the scale varies between 0.58 and 0.78 in subgroups [19]. Cronbach alpha values ​​in this study were found to be 0.71 for the affective commitment sub-dimension, 0.74 for continuance commitment, 0.74 for normative commitment, and 0.79 for the overall scale. While the minimum and maximum values ​​taken from the scale sub-dimensions vary between 1.00 and 5.00, the overall scale varies between 1.44 and 4.56.

Nurse job satisfaction scale

The Turkish validity and reliability of the scale, which was developed by Muya et al. in 2014, was performed by Türe Yılmaz and Yıldırım in 2016 [21, 22]. The scale was created to determine the job satisfaction of nurses and the affecting factors. The scale, which consists of a total of 27 items and 4 sub-dimensions, is scored in a 5-point Likert type. The 6th and 20th items of the scale are reverse-scored. The sub-dimensions are positive emotions associated with work, appropriate support from superiors, perceived importance in the workplace, and a pleasant work environment. The Cronbach Alpha Reliability Coefficient of the scale was detected to be 0.90 [21]. In this study, Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficients were found to be 0.78 for the work-related emotions subscale, 0.93 for appropriate support from superiors, 0.83 for perceived importance in the workplace, 0.77 for a pleasant work environment, and 0.93 for the overall scale. Minimum and maximum scores for the subscales were 1.00–5.00 and 1.26–4.85 for the overall scale.

Life satisfaction scale

The scale was developed by Diener, Emmons, Larsen, and Griffin in 1985 [23], was adapted to Turkish by Köker in 1991, and later repeated by Dağlı and Baysal in 2016 [18]. The scale consists of five positive statements, aims to measure general life satisfaction, and is suitable for all ages from adolescents to adults. The scale has a 7-point Likert-type assessment. The level of agreement with the scale items is scored as “1 = Not at all appropriate,” “2 = Not appropriate,” “3 = Somewhat inappropriate,” “4 = Neither appropriate nor inappropriate,” “5 = Somewhat appropriate,” “6 = Appropriate,” “7 = Very appropriate.” A minimum of 5 and a maximum of 35 points can be received from the scale and a low score on the scale is considered an indicator of low life satisfaction. The Cronbach Alpha Value in the validity and reliability study conducted by Dağlı and Baysal was reported as 0.88 [18]. The Cronbach Alpha Value was 0.904 in this study.

Ethical considerations

Prior to the study, ethics committee approval was obtained from the Ardahan University Scientific Publications and Ethics Board, dated September 20, 2024, and numbered E-67796128-819-2400031884. Institutional permission for the conduct of the study, dated September 26, 2024, and numbered E-26687954-799-255142885, was obtained from the Ardahan Provincial Health Directorate. Verbal and written informed consent was obtained from the participants. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data analysis

The SPSS 26 Package Program was used in the analysis of the data. For the first research question, numbers, percentages, means, and standard deviations were calculated. Skewness and Kurtosis values of ± 1.5 were taken as the basis [24]. Since the Skewness and Kurtosis values were − 0.287–0.298 for organizational commitment, -0.963-1.436 for job satisfaction and 0.009–0.700 for life satisfaction, parametric tests were used. To determine the difference in organizational commitment, job, and life satisfaction based on socio-demographic variables, an independent samples t-test was used for binary variables, and a one-way ANOVA was used for variables with three or more variables. In the advanced analysis, Levene’s test was conducted to assess homogeneity. Since p > 0.05, indicating equal variances, the Bonferroni post-hoc test was applied. Pearson correlation analysis was used to determine the relationship between the scales. Correlation analysis was used to determine the relationship between the scales. Correlation coefficients between 0.00 and 0.10 show negative correlation, 0.10–0.39 show weak correlation, 0.40–0.69 show moderate correlation, 0.70–0.89 show strong correlation, and 0.90-1.00 show very strong correlation [25]. Multiple regression analysis was used to determine the influence of job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and age on nurses’ organizational commitment levels. p < 0.05 was taken as the basis for statistical significance.

Results

The comparison of nurses’ organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and life satisfaction according to sociodemographic data

The mean age of the participants was 33.83 years (SD = 7.89), and most were female (80.5%). Job satisfaction differed according to smoking status (F(2, 146) = 5.643, p = 0.004). Bonferroni post-hoc tests showed that smokers reported higher job satisfaction compared to former smokers (Mean difference = 0.64, 95% CI [0.17, 1.10], p = 0.003), and nonsmokers reported higher satisfaction compared to former smokers (Mean difference = 0.43, 95% CI [0.01, 0.86], p = 0.018). No significant difference was observed between smokers and nonsmokers (p > 0.05). Life satisfaction showed significant differences based on economic status (F(2, 146) = 13.48, p < 0.001), educational level (F(2, 146) = 4.98, p = 0.008), and place of residence (F(2, 146) = 3.44, p = 0.035). Bonferroni post-hoc tests revealed that those with a good economic status were significantly higher than those with a moderate (Mean difference = 0.80, 95% CI [1.89, 7.71], p < 0.001) and poor economic status (Mean difference = 0.34, 95% CI [4.88, 15.80], p < 0.001). It was determined that high school graduates had significantly higher life satisfaction than undergraduate (Mean difference = 4.67, 95% CI [0.82, 8.52], p = 0.012). It was determined that those living in the province had significantly higher life satisfaction than those living in the district (Mean difference = 5.34, 95% CI [0.40, 10.27], p = 0.029) (Table 1).

The predictors of organizational commitment among nurses: nurse job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and age analysis

Multiple regression analysis was conducted to examine the predictors of nurses’ organizational commitment, including nurse job satisfaction, life satisfaction, and age (Table 3). The overall model was significant (F(3, 145) = 49.89, p < 0.001), with R = 0.713, R² = 0.508, and adjusted R² = 0.498, indicating that 49.8% of the variance in organizational commitment was explained by the predictors. Nurse job satisfaction was a strong positive predictor of organizational commitment (B = 0.601, SE = 0.053, β = 0.730, 95% CI [0.496, 0.707], p < 0.001). Age also showed a small but significant positive effect (B = 0.010, SE = 0.004, β = 0.113, 95% CI [0.001, 0.018], p = 0.024). Life satisfaction was not a significant predictor (B = -0.006, SE = 0.005, β = -0.071, 95% CI [− 0.017, 0.005], p = 0.272). Model diagnostics indicated that assumptions of linearity, normality of residuals, and homoscedasticity were met. No influential outliers were detected (Cook’s distance < 1). Multicollinearity was not a concern (all VIF values < 2; Tolerance > 0.5).

Discussion

The results of the present study, which was conducted to determine the relationship between nurses’ organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and life satisfaction, are discussed in light of the literature data as follows.

A strong relationship was detected in the present study between organizational commitment and nurse job satisfaction, and in the regression analysis, job satisfaction in nurses was a strongly associated with of organizational commitment. This can be expressed as job-related satisfaction is positively associated with commitment to the organization. The literature supports the present study in this sense [16, 26–29]. The intention to leave the job, which can be considered the opposite of job satisfaction, was associated negatively with organizational commitment in the literature. Nurses’ organizational commitment is negatively associated with their intention to leave [30]. In a study conducted in China, a negative relationship was reported between organizational commitment and job burnout, and commitment to the organization caused a decrease in job-related burnout. It was also stated that psychological capital is closely associated with organizational commitment and job-related burnout [31]. China is a country where group harmony, hierarchy, and workplace discipline are highly valued. In this context, employees’ organizational commitment was found to be negatively associated with job stress and burnout, and positively associated with in-group harmony, relationships with managers, and job satisfaction. Compared to findings in Turkey, considering how similar mechanisms may interact with cultural differences helps us gain a more comprehensive understanding of the relationship between organizational commitment and job satisfaction. In the relationship between organizational commitment and job satisfaction, the behaviors of managers and psychological empowerment positively affect the relationship by increasing cooperation with the team [32–34]. In the Turkish context, this relationship is shaped by tangible factors such as long working hours, staffing, managerial support, and team cohesion. The province where the study was conducted was a public hospital in a low-population city in eastern Turkey. Therefore, the small number of employees not only strengthened communication among employees but also allowed managers to better understand and support each employee, thus reinforcing the relationship between job satisfaction and organizational commitment. In light of this key finding, examining the relationship between organizational commitment and life satisfaction is crucial for understanding nurses’ overall life satisfaction.

A weak and positive relationship was detected in the present study between organizational commitment and life satisfaction. Commitment to the organization shows a positive relationship with life satisfaction. The emotional attachment of individuals to the workplace may increase job satisfaction and life satisfaction. The feeling of belonging to the workplace and increased organizational commitment can contribute to the individual feeling more valued and increasing life satisfaction [31]. The positive relationship between organizational commitment and life satisfaction can also contribute to psychological well-being [35]. Also, organizational commitment positively affects life satisfaction by increasing satisfaction in work life. In addition to these strong relationships, the research provides important information about organizational commitment, job satisfaction and life satisfaction variables and the influencing factors.

In the present study, the average organizational commitment was found to be 3.17 ± 0.58 above moderate level. Similarly, it was determined that the three sub-dimensions were above average. Studies in the literature report that organizational commitment in nurses is at a moderate level [30, 36, 37]. In the present study, organizational commitment did not differ according to sociodemographic variables. In this sense, the results of some studies in the literature overlap with the results of the present study [36, 37]. Unlike the present study, many studies in the literature show that organizational commitment is affected by age, sex, educational status, marital status, and length of experience [26, 27, 30, 38]. In a study conducted with Filipino nurses, it was reported that the organizational commitment of nurses varied according to sex, age, education level, and length of experience and that women, those over the age of 40, those with a higher level of education and those with more than 5 years of experience had higher organizational commitment scores [30]. This finding can be interpreted within the context of the cultural context: the Philippines is considered to have a more collectivist culture; employees in such cultures generally value group harmony, social relationships, and hierarchical structures. In this context, the higher organizational commitment of women and more experienced nurses may reflect their behaviors consistent with these cultural norms. Therefore, the lack of significant differences in sociodemographic variables in our study in Turkey may be explained by the influence of different cultural norms, management styles, and workforce structures. This suggests that the findings in our study are inconsistent with the literature in some respects. These findings suggest that organizational commitment is influenced not only by individual characteristics but also by factors such as the work environment, management style, and organizational culture. In conclusion, the findings of our study demonstrate that organizational commitment is a multidimensional construct and cannot be explained solely by sociodemographic variables. Therefore, in efforts to increase organizational commitment, it is important to focus on organizational practices and the work environment, in addition to individual characteristics.

In the present study, the average job satisfaction of nurses was found to be 3.48 ± 0.70, which is above the moderate level. This result suggests that nurses are generally satisfied with their jobs. A moderate level of job satisfaction may indicate positive perceptions of nurses’ working conditions, in-house support mechanisms, and professional motivation. In a study conducted during the COVID-19 period, it was reported that the job satisfaction of nurses was high and was associated with commitment to work [39]. It was reported that job satisfaction was effective in increasing the effectiveness of work, patient safety, and quality of care in nurses [40]. It was reported in another study conducted with Iranian nurses that job satisfaction was at a moderate level and that there was a relationship between job satisfaction and quality of work life [6]. Iran is a country with a communal and family-oriented culture, where traditional values ​​and social norms are strong. In this context, nurses’ job satisfaction may be shaped not only by individual work conditions but also by factors such as family expectations, social support, and society’s perception of professional status. Compared to findings in Turkey, the high job satisfaction among nurses suggests that working conditions and institutional support mechanisms may be effective regardless of cultural differences. While the subscales of the job satisfaction scale are mostly above average, parallel to the overall average, it is noteworthy that the “pleasant work environment” subscale is below average. This suggests that while nurses are generally satisfied with their jobs, they also experience dissatisfaction stemming from factors such as the physical conditions of the work environment, social relationships, or the psychological climate of the workplace. Therefore, initiatives aimed at improving job satisfaction should consider not only the work itself but also the quality of the work environment and employee satisfaction with it. In the present study, nurse job satisfaction differed according to smoking status. Smokers had higher job satisfaction levels than those who quit smoking. This difference may be related to the temporary stress-relieving effect of smoking and the difficulty of adapting to quitting [41]. Future studies are recommended to further explore this relationship to understand the reasons behind this interesting finding.

It was also found in the present study that the life satisfaction level of nurses was 19.72 ± 6.87 below the average level. This result suggests that nurses’ overall life satisfaction is below expected levels. Low life satisfaction may stem from factors such as occupational stress, workload, long working hours, and work-life balance issues. Furthermore, despite above-average job satisfaction, low life satisfaction suggests that nurses’ work and personal life circumstances are affected by different dynamics. Contrary to the present study, in a study conducted with nurses during the pandemic period, it was detected that life satisfaction had a lower value than the present study, and this situation could also affect general well-being [42]. This is thought to be related to the timing of the study. It was found that life satisfaction differed according to economic status, educational status, and place of residence. Nurses who had good economic status had a significantly higher life satisfaction average than those with moderate and poor economic status. This can be interpreted as an increase in quality of life and pleasure from life as the economic status increases [43]. The life satisfaction of nurses who graduated from high school and lived in the city was higher. An increase in the level of education can increase professional expectations, expectations regarding promotion, and demands for income. Failure to meet these can negatively affect life satisfaction [44]. The life satisfaction of nurses living in the city was higher than those living in the district. It is considered that this may be because of the transportation problems experienced by nurses living in the district in the rural city where the study was conducted.

Overall, these results highlight the importance of strategies that support job and life satisfaction in increasing nurses’ organizational commitment in Turkey. Improving management practices and the work environment play a critical role in increasing both organizational commitment and nurses’ life satisfaction.

Conclusion

The study findings indicate that nurses’ organizational commitment and job satisfaction levels are above average. This highlights the importance of management strategies and internal support mechanisms. Therefore, nurse managers should develop policies that enhance job satisfaction to increase employee commitment. Low life satisfaction suggests the need for social and psychological programs to support nurses’ work-life balance. Management can develop measures to improve employees’ quality of life (flexible working hours, stress management programs, social support mechanisms). The strong relationship between organizational commitment and job satisfaction highlights the importance of managers focusing on the work environment and working conditions to increase employee commitment. This study contributes to the literature on understanding cross-cultural nursing behavior by demonstrating the relationships between organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and life satisfaction in the Turkish context. This study can provide the basis for comparative studies on the effects of sociodemographic variables and cultural context on organizational commitment and work/life satisfaction. While international literature generally examines individual or professional factors in this area, data from Türkiye enriches the general knowledge by demonstrating the impact of cultural differences and local healthcare system practices. Based on these results, the following recommendations are offered:

Limitations of the study

The current study has several limitations. The study design limits the ability to establish causal relationships. It may prevent observation of time-varying effects across participants. This could affect the measurement results if a transient situation exists. Additionally, the sample was limited to nurses working in a state hospital in a small city, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. The reliance on self-reported data through scales may have introduced response biases, as participants might have provided socially desirable answers. The use of convenience sampling method in the study prevents the random selection of participants and provides ease of access, therefore the sample may not fully represent the entire population targeted by the study, which may limit generalizability to the universe. Other limitations of our study are that it was conducted in a single center and confounding factors were not evaluated.

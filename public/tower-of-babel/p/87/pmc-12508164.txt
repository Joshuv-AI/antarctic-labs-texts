TransBreastNet a CNN transformer hybrid deep learning framework for breast cancer subtype classification and temporal lesion progression analysis
Authors: Aluri Brahmareddy, Mercy Paul Selvan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer continues to be a global public health challenge. An early and precise diagnosis is crucial for improving prognosis and efficacy. While deep learning (DL) methods have shown promising advances in breast cancer classification from mammogram images, most existing DL models remain static, single-view image-based, and overlook the longitudinal progression of lesions and patient-specific clinical context. Moreover, the majority of models also limited their clinical usability by designing tests for subtype classification in isolation (i.e., not predicting disease stages simultaneously). This paper introduces BreastXploreAI, a simple yet powerful multimodal, multitask deep learning framework for breast cancer diagnosis to fill these gaps. TransBreastNet, a hybrid architecture that combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for spatial encoding of lesions, a Transformer-based modular approach for temporal encoding of lesions, and dense metadata encoders for fusion of patient-specific clinical information, forms the backbone of our system. The breast cancer subtype and disease stage are predicted simultaneously from a dual-head classifier. They are then used to construct temporal lesion sequences, either by employing genuine longitudinal data or by adding sequence augmentation to sample sequences, thereby strengthening the model’s ability to learn Progression Patterns. We conduct extensive experiments on a public mammogram dataset and demonstrate that our model outperforms several state-of-the-art baselines in both subtype classification, achieving a macro accuracy of 95.2%, and stage Prediction, with a macro accuracy of 93.8%. We also provide ablation studies, which confirm how every module contributes to the framework. Unlike prior static single-view models, our framework jointly models spatial, temporal, and clinical features using a CNN-Transformer hybrid design. It simultaneously predicts breast cancer subtypes and lesion progression stages, while generating synthetic temporal lesion sequences where longitudinal data is scarce. Built-in explainability modules enhance interpretability and clinical trust. BreastXploreAI offers a robust, scalable, and clinically relevant approach to diagnosing breast cancer from full-field digital mammogram (FFDM) images. Introduction

Breast cancer is among the leading malignant diseases of women worldwide, and early detection is crucial for enhancing survival and response to treatments. However, mammographic screening has been the standard method of early detection, which is subject to variations in image quality, lesion morphology, and interpretation bias that can complicate its effectiveness. Although artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning (DL) models have recently been used to automate accurate classification across breast cancer subtypes, existing models can only learn spatial features using static images. Usually, they cannot model temporal lesion evolution or provide clinical context information for breast cancer patients1,2.

Based on previous research, CNNs, attention mechanisms, and hybrid architectures have yielded promising results in the breast cancer classification task34,. Still, there are remaining gaps in the use of longitudinal image sequences, along with clinical metadata, in an integrated approach. Moreover, most models only consider subtype classification and ignore disease stage prediction, which is equally essential as subtype classification in clinical decision-making5,6. This necessitates a more holistic framework that can both model the progression of lesions and integrate heterogeneous data types to enable multi-task learning.

Even if CNN, Transformer, and the two methods have performed well in breast cancer classification1–6, some of their quality still lacks. The majority of previous studies are static, studying single-view images, but lesions may evolve dynamically over time. The temporal evolution of lesions is a key prognostic and treatable characteristic, but is not accounted for in most approaches that focus on static image data. Furthermore, imaging features are seldom fused with patient-specific clinical metadata, including but not limited to hormone receptor status, tumor size, and genetic markers, and thus render personalized predictions unattainable. Reassuringly, existing methods mainly target subtype classification and do not predict disease stages jointly, which is vital for clinical decisions.

Thus, in this study, we present a novel BreastXploreAI, powered by the recently proposed TransBreastNet architecture, dedicated to addressing these gaps. We combine CNN-oriented spatial feature extraction with Transformer-oriented temporal lesion modeling, both of which are fused with structured clinical metadata to provide context-aware diagnostics in a single framework. The main novelties are: (i) an integrated model harnessing spatial, temporal and clinical inputs jointly; (ii) dual-tasking the prediction of breast cancer subtypes and lesional progression deeper than the current gold standard; (iii) generating anatomically and biologically plausible synthetic temporal sequences where longitudinal data is sparse; and (iv) explainability via Grad-CAM and attention rollout to elucidate lesion regions and timepoints contributing to the predictions. All these contributions go beyond all the previous work by providing a robust, interpretable, and oncology-relevant diagnostic system.

At the heart of our proposed system is the TransBreastNet: A model that employs CNN-based lesion feature extraction to extract spatio-temporal features, followed by Transformer-based temporal encoding for robust feature learning and metadata-aware fusion. Key features include synthetic temporal sequence creation, dynamic multi-modal fusion, and a dual-head classifier optimized to avoid learning bias towards the primary class in imbalanced class distributions.

It enriches the existing body of knowledge in both theoretical and empirical contexts in several essential ways. First, it presents a temporally aware structure that learns the spatial features of lesions across synthetic or real longitudinal mammograms in a sequential way to improve the modeling of progression. Step two: Infusing a third modality with clinical metadata to enable personalized decision support. Thirdly, multi-task learning is accomplished through a joint classification pipeline for predicting subtype and stage. Fourth, the platform’s design is modular, enabling adaptation to a wide range of imaging scenarios and facilitating scalability for clinical deployment.

TransBreastNet is primarily distinct from prior static or single-modality models through its hybrid architecture and integrated design. We integrate spatial encoders based on CNNs and temporal modeling with Transformers to model the temporal evolution of lesions. Moreover, it combines patient clinical metadata with image features for context-aware predictions. Firstly, our approach incorporates a dual-task framework for subtype classification and lesion stage progression, along with built-in explainability, which can be easily provided and clearly distinguishes us from traditional attention-based or CNN-only methods.

Proposed framework

The BreastXploreAI framework was designed to perform multi-faceted breast cancer diagnosis through an integration of spatial, temporal, and clinical elements. Its system architecture, model configuration, workflow process, training approach, and evaluation protocol were each optimized to address key limitations of existing methods and to enhance diagnostic accuracy, general applicability, and clinical relevance.

Overview of the BreastXploreAI system

The BreastXploreAI system functions as a complete intelligent diagnostic platform that merges deep learning, temporal modeling, and clinical data fusion to enable precise breast cancer subtype classification and lesion development examination. It tackles significant challenges in current diagnostic workflows by unifying spatial and temporal feature extraction across diverse imaging modalities while incorporating individualized patient details for context-aware predictions. The system accepts multimodal imaging inputs, including mammograms, MRIs, ultrasounds, and histopathological images, alongside structured clinical information such as patient age, tumor stage, breast density, and receptor status. These heterogeneous data streams undergo a unified preprocessing module, which carries out standardization, augmentation, and sequence construction to generate lesion-specific temporal inputs.

At the core of BreastXploreAI lies the TransBreastNet model, which combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Transformer encoders to simultaneously extract spatial lesion features and capture lesion evolution patterns across time. The CNN module handles each frame independently to extract local tissue and boundary features, while the Transformer module models temporal dependencies between consecutive lesion images, representing disease progression. Clinical metadata is encoded through dense layers and fused with temporal features to enrich the diagnostic decision space with patient-specific context. The system supports dual-task predictions: One head classifies the breast cancer subtype into IDC, ILC, DCIS, or benign categories, while the other estimates lesion progression stages as early, intermediate, or advanced.

To promote transparency and clinician trust, BreastXploreAI incorporates a built-in explainability engine that provides spatial and temporal visualizations, highlighting the most influential features and lesion regions that contribute to the diagnosis. The complete system pipeline is modular and scalable, enabling deployment in both research and clinical environments. Functional workflow of BreastXploreAI

The functional workflow of BreastXploreAI outlines the step-by-step process that transforms raw multimodal patient data into clinically meaningful predictions and interpretable outputs. This ensures a coherent and modular pipeline from data acquisition to final diagnostic decision support, enhancing usability in both research settings and real-time clinical use. The process begins with the ingestion of imaging and clinical data. Multi-modal image series, including mammograms, MRIs, ultrasounds, and histopathological images, are either collected from longitudinal patient records or constructed as synthetic sequences. Alongside this, structured clinical metadata such as age, tumor stage, receptor status, and genetic markers are also gathered.

Following the acquisition, the data undergoes preprocessing and standardization. Imaging data undergoes resizing, intensity normalization, and contrast enhancement to ensure consistency across modalities. Then, sequence construction is performed by either temporally aligning authentic diagnostic images or generating lesion progression sequences through augmentation techniques. Clinical metadata is normalized using z-score scaling, and values are imputed where necessary to address missing data.

The preprocessed and structured inputs are then fed into the TransBreastNet model, which is the core diagnostic engine of the system. For each image in a lesion sequence, spatial features are extracted using a convolutional neural network (CNN) encoder. These features are then sequenced and passed into a Transformer encoder, which models temporal relationships across the lesion’s evolution. Simultaneously, clinical metadata is encoded and fused with the temporal lesion features using an attention-based mechanism to form a comprehensive diagnostic representation.

The joint feature representation is passed to two task-specific heads within the model: One performs multi-class classification of breast cancer subtypes, and the other estimates the stage of lesion progression. In parallel, interpretability outputs are generated to support clinical validation. Grad-CAM is applied to the CNN pathway to localize salient lesion regions. At the same time, attention rollout maps are extracted from the Transformer to highlight key temporal frames that contribute to the model’s prediction. These visual explanations are packaged alongside prediction results to assist clinicians in reviewing and interpreting the AI’s recommendations. Finally, BreastXploreAI outputs subtype and progression labels, confidence scores, and annotated image maps, facilitating integration into diagnostic reports or electronic health record systems. Algorithmic implementation

This section explores the intricate algorithms that comprise the BreastXploreAI framework. Firstly, a temporal lesion sequencing algorithm constructs chronological patient histories from imaging and biopsies. A convolutional neural network then extracts spatial features from each medical image. A Transformer network adeptly encodes the progression of findings over time. Finally, a fusion algorithm judiciously combines the temporal and spatial analyses with relevant clinical metadata. Syndicating these interwoven algorithms results in discerning breast cancer subtype and stage with reliability and reason.

Temporal lesion sequence construction.

Algorithm 1 constructs an ordered set of lesion snapshots for each patient, incorporating temporal information where available. If genuine temporal analytics are provided, the images are initially sequenced based on their timestamps and then structured into a progression that unfolds the genuine chronology.

In scenarios where temporal annotations are in short supply or partial, the algorithm engineers a synthetic lesion evolution by opting for an underlying lesion portrait and applying a collection of augmentation alterations to birth supplementary variant images. These enhanced images simulate lesion unfolding over time, enabling temporal modeling even without explicit time stamps. Every snapshot in the sequence is then downsized and standardized to a fixed spatial dimension to maintain uniformity across the information bank. The consequent sequence forms the temporal input to the model, allowing it to learn from both real and fabricated lesion progression designs.

Spatial feature extraction using CNN Encoder.

Algorithm 2 adeptly extracts salient spatial features from each lesion image in the temporal sequence using a convolutional neural network (CNN) encoder. The CNN encoder generates a feature map for every image, capturing textural specifics, borders, and morphological properties pertinent to identifying lesions.

A worldwide averaging pooling operation then reduces each feature map to a compact, multidimensional vector that encapsulates spatial data. These vectors are collated sequentially, forming a spatial attribute succession that portrays the temporal modification of lesion traits. This succession serves as an input to subsequent mechanisms, enabling the model to examine both spatial particulars and chronological fluctuations efficiently.

Temporal progression encoding using transformer.

Algorithm 3 encodes the temporal progression of lesion features using a Transformer encoder in a complex manner. First, positional encodings are added to the intricate spatial feature sequence to provide multifaceted information about the succession of images in the sequence, enabling the model to comprehend temporal relationships.

The enriched feature sequence is then passed through the intricate Transformer encoder, which captures far-reaching interdependencies and interactions across the elaborate temporal dimension. The resultant output is an intricate temporal feature representation that effectively models intricate lesion progression patterns over time. Downstream tasks utilize this representation, for example, subtype and stage classification, to significantly improve predictive accuracy, including sentences that are longer and more complex, alongside shorter, simpler ones.

Clinical Metadata Fusion and Multi-Task Prediction.

Algorithm 4 innovatively incorporates clinical metadata with temporal lesion attributes to perform multi-faceted prediction. The clinical metadata, including aspects such as patient age, tumor size, and receptor status, is initially encoded into a feature vector using a deep neural network encoder.

The encoded metadata is then combined with the temporal qualities extracted from the Transformer encoder to create a comprehensive portrayal that integrates imaging and clinical data. This fused portrayal is fed into two independent classifiers: One forecasts the breast cancer subclass, and the other anticipates the development stage. Both expectations are met, allowing the model to handle both subclass categorization and stage development tasks simultaneously. Occasionally, shorter sentences are used alongside more elaborate constructions to capture a diverse range of expressions.

Failure case analysis

Though TransBreastNet performed well overall, we did identify a handful of misclassifications. The majority of errors in borderline cases with unclear lesion boundaries or shared visual patterns of different subtypes. Morphological similarity led to the misclassification of some HER2—enriched cases with low contrast and irregular shapes as triple-negative. Conversely, some cases with subtle features of early-stage lesions were interpreted as benign. Such failures were predominantly associated with either poor images, breast density, or unclear lesions.

Results from the proposed TransBreastNet show promise, but some limitations exist. First, the number of patients within the dataset is limited, in the context of a potential large-scale clinical deployment, and independent validation on more varied multicentric cohorts is needed. Second, temporal follow-up intervals between patients are not homogeneous; differences between cohorts in the temporal ordering of short and long-term follow-up play into how lesions are tracked utilizing consecutive imaging pairs. Third, while Grad-CAM visualizations allow for improved interpretability, further studies with a focus on explainability are needed that include radiologist feedback. We will tackle these issues with larger datasets, improvements to temporal alignment methods, and clinical validation in future work.

Detailed exploration of the results unravels that the hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture and the metadata fusion in TransBreastNet together improve class separability and enhance spatial–temporal representation, leading to improved performance. When cases of temporal lesion evolution and clinical factors are clearly represented, correct predictions are associated with strong feature learning. On the other hand, misclassifications frequently arise in low-contrast settings, cases with uncertain lesion edges, or wherever temporal follow-up data are lacking. This is corroborated by the failure case visualizations in (Figure 18) and likely implies increased performance if the models were given more temporal data and higher resolution imaging.

Discussion

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of mortality among women worldwide, necessitating accurate and early diagnosis for improved prognosis. Conventional machine learning and CNN-based deep learning models have demonstrated reasonable accuracy in classifying breast cancer subtypes and stages. However, they often fail to capture the complex spatio-temporal lesion progression and lack integration of clinical metadata, leading to reduced generalizability and clinical relevance.

The present research addresses these gaps through BreastXploreAI. This novel multi-task framework integrates a CNN-Transformer hybrid architecture with temporal lesion sequence modeling and the fusion of clinical metadata. Unlike traditional single-stream models, our architecture decouples spatial and temporal learning while leveraging attention mechanisms to capture fine-grained lesion progression patterns. The inclusion of synthetic temporal sequences compensates for the lack of real longitudinal data, addressing a key limitation in existing datasets. Moreover, the fusion of clinical variables improves the contextual understanding of subtype classification and stage prediction.

Experimental results demonstrate the superior Performance of TransBreastNet, the core model of BreastXploreAI, over existing CNN and Transformer baselines. The framework achieved a macro accuracy of 94.6% in subtype classification and 93.2% in stage prediction, outperforming previous models by notable margins. The results confirm that combining temporal lesion patterns and patient metadata substantially improves the robustness and reliability of forecasts.

Our study closes the gap between image-only diagnostic systems and clinically relevant, multimodal decision systems. This translates into clinically operational, real-time usages with quantitative and interpretable diagnostic information for oncologists. Moreover, the architecture of BreastXploreAI has a modular form, particularly suitable for similar diseases or imaging-based disease domains where temporal progression is crucial.

In keeping with standard reporting guidelines (e.g., Equator Network STARD and CONSORT checklists), we provide a critical appraisal of our results to aid clarity and reproducibility. However, performance metrics are provided along with confidence intervals that address model robustness and generalizability. To describe comparative advantage and potential limitations, we also contextualized our findings against prior studies (see Section"Comparative evaluation with state-of-the-art models"). All aspects of the experimental design, including data sources, patient-level partitioning, and evaluation metrics, were reported transparently to enable reproducibility. We followed these steps to ensure that our study is consistent with optimal practice for reporting diagnostic research, so that readers obtain a broader understanding of the relative strengths and limitations of the system and its external validity.

BreastXploreAI demonstrated its ability to manage data limitations, augment heterogeneous input modalities, and accurately diagnose clinical outcomes, indicating the importance of adopting advanced architectures in cancer informatics.

Limitations of the study

Although the current study shows promising performance, it has some limitations. Second, the generation of synthetic temporal lesion sequences, although useful, may not account for real longitudinal progression, reducing biological realism. Second, we relied on a single primary data set, which could limit the model’s generalizability to different populations or imaging modalities. Third, the available clinical metadata was limited to a small subset of variables, and potentially, not all patient-specific factors were influential. The need for future work with real multi-temporal datasets, validation, and inclusion of data from multiple institutions, as well as expanded metadata, is apparent and represents constraints applicable to both this study and the wider literature. While synthetic sequences offer a practical workaround, they may not capture the full biological complexity of real lesion progression. Future work will incorporate actual longitudinal datasets to further validate temporal modeling under real clinical progression timelines.

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce BreastXploreAI, a novel multi-task deep learning framework designed to enhance breast cancer subtype classification and progression stage prediction by simultaneously leveraging spatial, temporal, and clinical modalities. In the proposed TransBreastNet model, we integrate CNN-based spatial feature extraction, Transformer-driven temporal encoding, and metadata fusion to convey the underlying complexities in lesion dynamics and patient-specific behaviors. BreastXploreAI overcomes fundamental limitations in current models, including a lack of temporal progression and limited incorporation of clinical context, resulting in macro accuracy scores (Internal validation) of 94.6% for subtype classification and 93.2% for stage prediction; the model achieved superior specificity and sensitivity compared to other published models. These empirical results support the premise that spatio-temporal learning, in conjunction with patient metadata, can produce clinically relevant and accurate diagnoses. The flexible, modular architecture of the framework, as well as the simulated lesion progression, renders it readily accommodating for other imaging-based disease domains. The BreastXploreAI framework proposed in this paper is novel in multiple ways: (i) modelling the spatial features of lesions on prior mammograms along with their temporal progression and metadata of the patients; (ii) bi-task learning for both subtype-classification and lesion stage-prediction; (iii) synthetic temporal lesion sequence generation to mitigate longitudinal sample scarcity; and (iv) integrated explainability to boost the clinical trust. These contributions build on existing methods and offer a clinically relevant and interpretable solution. Specifically, BreastXploreAI fills the void of literature that investigates both classification and progression analysis together through a model with clinical relevance. Conclusion: This study demonstrates the potential of an explainable, reliable, and context-aware software-based diagnostic support system, TransBreastNet, to facilitate clinical workflows. This unified approach thus advances the AI-assisted oncology space and lays the groundwork for future studies on explainability and temporally aware medical imaging systems.

Future directions

Although TransBreastNet shows promising results, there are still multiple potential avenues to improve its applicability and impact. First, we hope to further validate on a larger, multicenter cohort with broader demographic and pathological variation to increase clinical generalizability. Next, we will improve temporal alignment tactics and bring in other longitudinal imaging modalities, like ultrasound and MRI, to better follow lesions across time. Third, we will conduct more extensive clinical validation with radiologists to assess usability and diagnostic value in routine workflows. We will evaluate the interpretability of Grad-CAM and attention-based explanations and their potential for integration into clinical decision support systems. In the same way, multi-omics data (e.g., genomic, proteomic) in combination with imaging may provide even further insight into the molecular underpinnings of the tumor and how it responds to treatment. One potential extension is to adopt federated learning paradigms where models can be trained cross-institutionally in a privacy-preserving way without the need to centralise the data. Last but not least, automated reporting modules and visualization interfaces could also facilitate clinical uptake of the framework by integrating it into existing health care systems seamlessly. These future work directions seek to enhance both the technical performance of the framework and its translational relevance to enable the development of next-generation intelligent, interpretable, and clinically practical breast cancer diagnostic tools.

Long-term Outcomes of Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection Versus Surgery in Late Older Adult Patients Aged ≥75 Years With Early Gastric Cancer Meeting the Curative Resection Criteria
Authors: Seung Han Kim, Seon-Young Park, Young-Il Kim, Chung Hyun Tae, Hyeong Ho Jo, Jeong Hoon Lee, Ayoung Lee, Jae Myung Park, Hwoon-Yong Jung, Dae Hwan Kim, Hong Man Yoon, Hyunsoo Chung, The Korean Elderly Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection (ESD) Study Group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeWe compared the long-term outcomes of endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) with those of surgery in late older adult patients aged ≥75 years with early gastric cancer (EGC) who met the curative resection criteria.Materials and MethodsThis multicenter retrospective study included 4,241 older adults with EGC who were treated with either ESD (n=4,083) or surgery (n=158) at 22 hospitals between 2010 and 2020. Overall survival (OS) and gastric cancer (GC) recurrence were investigated in the overall cohort and a 4-to-1 propensity score (PS)-matched cohort.ResultsDuring a median follow-up of 6.6 years, the 5-year OS rates were 84.8% and 82.9% in the ESD and surgery groups, respectively, and OS did not differ between the 2 groups in the overall (P=0.396) and PS-matched cohorts (P=0.248). In the PS-matched cohort, the multivariate analyses showed that overall mortality after ESD (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.77; 95% confidence interval, 0.57–1.04) did not increase compared to that after surgery. The GC recurrence rate after ESD was higher than that after surgery (6.1% [248/4,083] vs. 0.6% [1/158]; 5-year rate: 9.3% vs. 0.7%, respectively; P<0.001 for the overall and PS-matched cohorts). However, approximately 77% (190/248) of the recurrences in the ESD group were metachronous, most of which were successfully managed with endoscopic treatment. The ESD group had fewer adverse events (AEs, 7.6% vs. 12.0%; P=0.044) than the surgery group, and no ESD treatment-related deaths were observed.ConclusionsIn late older adult patients (aged ≥75 years) with EGC, long-term outcomes after ESD were comparable to those after surgery for EGC meeting the curative resection criteria, with acceptable treatment-related AEs.

INTRODUCTION

Gastric cancer (GC) is an age-related disease that is detected in approximately two-thirds of patients aged ≥65 years [1]. The highest worldwide incidence is observed in patients aged ≥75 years in Korea, Japan, and China [23]. The treatment decisions and choice of treatment methods for GC are challenging in late older adult patients because of the potential for multiple comorbidities that can affect outcomes [45]. Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is a minimally invasive treatment for early gastric cancer (EGC) with negligible risk of lymph node (LN) metastasis [67]. Furthermore, it is a safe and feasible treatment with acceptable long-term outcomes in patients with EGC [8910]. Given the high incidence of GC, an increasing number of older adult patients with EGC are undergoing ESD.

A meta-analysis of 18 retrospective studies showed that the long-term overall survival (OS) after ESD was comparable to that after surgery for EGCs that met the curative resection criteria for ESD [11]. Most studies comparing the long-term outcomes of ESD with those of surgery were performed in younger patients (aged <70 years) [11]. Similar to previous results in studies involving younger patients, older adult patients with EGC aged ≥70 years who underwent ESD had comparable OS rates to those who underwent surgery [12131415]. However, previous studies included only a small number of patients who underwent ESD (<60 patients) [1213], had short follow-up periods (median follow-up <2 years) [14], and included a higher proportion of patients who underwent ESD with noncurative resection (>50%) [15]. Therefore, the outcomes of ESD and surgery in late older adult patients with EGC should be compared using the same curative resection criteria and long-term follow-up periods.

In this study, we compared the outcomes of ESD with those of surgery in late older adult patients aged ≥75 years with EGC whose tumors met the curative resection criteria for ESD.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients

This multicenter retrospective study included patients with EGC aged ≥75 years who underwent ESD in 22 hospitals or surgery in 2 hospitals between January 2011 and December 2020. The final histopathological findings of the EGC lesions that met the curative resection criteria were as follows [6]: in tumors resected with a negative resection margin, negative lymphatic invasion and venous invasion, 1) mucosal tumor sized ≤2 cm, differentiated histologic type without ulceration (absolute criteria), 2) mucosal tumor sized >2 cm, differentiated histologic type without ulceration (expanded criteria I), 3) mucosal tumor sized ≤3 cm, differentiated histologic type with ulceration (expanded criteria II), 4) mucosal tumor sized ≤2 cm, undifferentiated histologic type without ulceration (expanded criteria III), and 5) submucosal tumor (invasion ≤500 μm) sized ≤3 cm, differentiated histologic type (expanded criteria IV). Patients were excluded if their tumors did not meet the curative resection criteria, if they had a history of gastrectomy, or if they had incomplete information on their post-treatment follow-up status. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the National Cancer Center, Korea (IRB number: NCC2024-0061) and the Review Boards of each participating hospital. The requirement for informed signed consent from the patients was waived by the IRB because of the minimal risk associated with this study.

ESD procedure, surgical treatment, and histopathological evaluation

All ESD procedures were performed by an experienced endoscopist who was qualified by the Korean Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy. The procedure followed typical ESD sequences, including lesion marking, pre-cutting outside the markings, and submucosal dissection with simultaneous hemostasis. Finally, endoscopic hemostasis was performed for all visible non-bleeding vessels on the ESD ulcer bed. Surgical treatment consisted of radical gastrectomy and LN dissection according to the Korean Practice Guidelines for GC [6]. Total, pylorus-preserving, proximal, or distal gastrectomy was performed by experienced surgeons depending on the tumor location.

Resected EGC specimens were fixed and immersed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for paraffin embedding. The section intervals were 2 mm for the ESD specimen and 4–5 mm for the surgical specimens [16]. The sliced specimens were embedded in paraffin, and hematoxylin/eosin-stained 5-μm sections of each paraffin block were evaluated by specialized gastrointestinal pathologists.

Follow-up after EGC treatment

For patients who were curatively treated with ESD and surgery, endoscopy and abdominal computed tomography were performed every 6 months for the first 2–3 years and then annually for 5 years after treatment. In patients who underwent ESD, additional endoscopic surveillance was conducted annually after 5 years.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was OS, which included death from any cause. Secondary outcomes included disease-specific survival (DSS), GC recurrence, treatment-related adverse events (AEs), including death, and risk factors associated with overall mortality and GC recurrence. The DSS event was death due to GC. GC recurrence included local or distant recurrence, metachronous recurrence (GC occurring in the remnant stomach ≥1 year after treatment), and death with evidence of GC recurrence. Follow-up data on deaths and GC recurrence until February 2025 were collected.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive data were analyzed using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. A logistic regression model was used to estimate the propensity scores (PSs) by including the following variables that could affect the outcomes of ESD and surgery: age, sex, comorbidities (diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and cerebrovascular disease), American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status (ASA-PS) score, and tumor characteristics (tumor size, depth, ulceration, histological type, and curative resection criteria [absolute vs. expanded criteria]). The treatment group (ESD vs. surgery) was the dependent variable, and 4:1 nearest-neighbor PS matching was performed using a caliper of 0.2. In addition to the PS-matched analysis, a sensitivity analysis using inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was performed. Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and comparisons between groups were performed using the log-rank test for OS and Gray’s test for DSS and GC recurrence. In this study, a PS-matched analysis was performed to reduce selection bias in comparisons between the ESD and surgery groups. To investigate the risk factors for overall mortality and GC recurrence, we performed univariate and multivariate analyses using the Cox proportional hazards regression model for overall mortality and the Fine–Gray regression model for GC recurrence [17]. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata 18.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA) and R version 4.4.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Baseline clinical and tumor characteristics of the overall and PS-matched cohorts are presented in Table 1. The mean patient age in the overall cohort was 78.7 years. The ESD group had a significantly higher age (mean age: 78.7 years vs. 78.0 years; P=0.011) and a larger proportion of male patients (66.2% vs. 58.2%; P=0.037) than the surgery group. Comparison of long-term outcomes

During a median follow-up of 6.6 years (interquartile range, 4.6–8.9), the 5-year OS rates were 84.8% and 82.9% in the ESD and surgery groups, respectively. The OS did not differ between the ESD and surgery groups in the overall cohort (P=0.396 by the log-rank test; Fig. 2A) or in the PS-matched cohort (P=0.248 by the log-rank test; Fig. 2B). GC-related deaths occurred only in the ESD group. Of the 7 patients who died of GC, three received systemic chemotherapy for extragastric recurrence, and 4 (2 extragastric recurrences and 2 metachronous recurrences) were managed conservatively without treatment for recurrent GC. In the overall cohort, no significant difference was observed in DSS rates between the ESD and surgery groups (5-year DSS rates, 99.8% vs. 100%, respectively; P=0.554 by Gray’s test), whereas no GC-related deaths were observed in the PS-matched cohort.

In the overall cohort, GC recurrence occurred in 248 patients (6.1%, 248 of the 4,083 patients; 190 metachronous recurrences, 52 local recurrences, and 6 distant recurrences) in the ESD group and in one patient (0.6%, 1 of the 158 patients; 1 distant recurrence) in the surgery group (Supplementary Table 2). Most patients with local (88.5%) and metachronous recurrences (93.7%) were treated with endoscopic therapy or surgery. The 5-year GC recurrence rates in the ESD and surgery groups were 9.3% and 0.7%, respectively, and the ESD group had a significantly higher incidence of GC recurrence than the surgery group (P<0.001, Gray’s test; Fig. 3A). In the PS-matched cohort, the GC recurrence rate was higher in the ESD group than in the surgery group (P<0.001, Gray’s test; Fig. 3B).

Risk factors for overall mortality and GC recurrence

Risk factors for overall mortality and GC recurrence were evaluated in a PS-matched cohort. In the univariate analyses, the significant risk factors for overall mortality were age 80–84 and ≥85 years; male sex; presence of chronic liver disease, chronic kidney disease, and other malignancies; and a higher ASA-PS score (2 and ≥3) (Supplementary Table 3). In the multivariate analysis, the overall mortality rate after ESD (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 0.77; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.57–1.04) did not differ from that after surgery (Table 2). Age 80–84 years (aHR, 1.98; 95% CI, 1.48–2.63) and ≥85 years (aHR, 3.82; 95% CI, 2.39–6.10), male sex (aHR, 1.55; 95% CI, 1.18–2.04), presence of other malignancies (aHR, 1.63; 95% CI, 1.07–2.48), and an ASA-PS score of 2 (aHR, 1.81; 95% CI, 1.07–3.05) were significant risk factors for overall mortality. In a sensitivity analysis after IPTW, there was no difference in the overall mortality between the ESD and surgery groups (aHR in the ESD group, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.58–1.18) (Supplementary Table 4).

Hospital stays and treatment-related AEs

The hospitalization duration of EGC treatment was significantly shorter in the ESD group than in the surgery group (median, 4 vs. 10 days; P<0.001; Table 3). The ESD group had a lower proportion of patients with treatment-related AEs than the surgery group (7.6% vs. 12.0%, P=0.044). In addition, AEs requiring surgical treatment occurred less frequently in the ESD group than in the surgery group (0.1% vs. 1.3%, P=0.026). In the surgery group, 2 patients (1.3%) died of treatment-related AEs (one due to septic shock after developing anastomotic leakage and one due to septic shock caused by intestinal obstruction after postoperative adhesions). No deaths owing to treatment-related AEs were observed in the ESD group.

DISCUSSION

This multicenter retrospective cohort study showed that the long-term OS rates after ESD were comparable to those after surgery for EGC, that met the curative resection criteria for ESD in late older adult patients aged ≥75 years. Owing to the higher rate of local or metachronous recurrence, the ESD group had a higher incidence of GC recurrence than the surgery group. However, most of the local and metachronous recurrences in the ESD group were treated endoscopically. Additionally, the ESD group had a shorter hospital stay and fewer treatment-related AEs than the surgery group.

Compared to younger patients, older adult patients exhibit a higher proportion of intestinal-type cancers than diffuse-type cancers. In addition, because older adult patients in their mid-70s (especially ≥75 years) exhibit a marked decline in physical and cognitive function, they have a high risk of frailty and are categorized as a group different from younger-old patients (65–74 years) [181920]. Given that >70% of late older adult people aged ≥75 years have multiple comorbidities [21], minimally invasive treatments are preferred options to reduce treatment-related morbidity and mortality. In Korea and Japan, where nationwide GC screening programs have been implemented, >50% of newly diagnosed GCs are in the localized stage [2223]. Based on the 2023 Korean nationwide registry data regarding GC treatment, older patients aged ≥71 years accounted for approximately 32% of the patients who underwent surgery [24], and those aged ≥75 years comprised 25% of the patients who underwent ESD [25]. Nevertheless, a report analyzing a nationwide cohort database revealed that 37.6% of late older adult patients with GC did not receive any treatment [26]. Even among those with localized disease curable by ESD or gastrectomy, 23.2% of patients did not receive any treatment [26]. GC-related deaths accounted for 72% of the total mortality in these late older adult patients who did not receive any treatment [26]. Therefore, active treatments, including ESD or gastrectomy, should be considered for late older adult patients with an acceptable physical status to tolerate these treatments. While many studies have compared long-term outcomes between ESD and surgery for EGC that meets the ESD curative resection criteria, studies focusing on late older adult patients aged ≥75 years are scarce, and most existing studies have limitations [12131415]. Long-term survival outcomes were evaluated in a heterogeneous patient group that underwent both curative and noncurative resections [12131415]. Particularly, the OS rates of older adult patients with EGC with noncurative resections, in contrast to those with curative resections, were affected by whether or not they underwent additional surgery [27]. Although a randomized controlled trial (RCT) is needed to provide more robust evidence on the long-term outcomes after ESD in late older adult patients compared to those after surgery, it is difficult to conduct an interventional RCT involving late older adult patients due to patient safety concerns. Therefore, we conducted a multicenter cohort study that included a large number of patients with EGC who met the curative resection criteria for ESD.

In our study, long-term survival outcomes after ESD for late older adult patients with EGC who met the curative resection criteria were favorable, with a 5-year OS rate of 84.8% and a DSS rate of 99.8%. In addition, the long-term survival rates after ESD were comparable to those after surgery in the overall and PS-matched cohorts, which are consistent with the findings of previous studies involving younger patients with EGC that compared outcomes between ESD and surgery for tumors that met the curative resection criteria [8910]. In our study, although older age (80–84 and >85 years), the presence of other malignancies, and an ASA-PS score of 2 were risk factors for overall mortality in multivariate analysis, the outcomes were not affected by treatment modality (ESD vs. surgery) or curative resection criteria (absolute vs. expanded). These findings suggest that ESD is a primary treatment option in late older adult patients with EGC who have tumors that meet the ESD curative resection criteria.

Metachronous recurrence is the main pattern of GC recurrence after curative resection by ESD for EGC, whereas extragastric recurrence is rare [8910], with similar recurrence patterns in older adult patients with EGC [1428]. Because of metachronous recurrence after ESD, a higher GC recurrence rate was observed in patients who underwent ESD than in those who underwent surgery [89101428]. Moreover, our study showed a higher rate of GC recurrence in the ESD group than in the surgery group, mainly because of local or metachronous recurrence after ESD. Similar to previous studies involving younger and older patients with EGC, most local (71%) or metachronous (84%) cancers were successfully treated with endoscopic therapy. As in younger patients, older patients with EGC who undergo ESD should receive Helicobacter pylori therapy to prevent metachronous recurrence and undergo long-term endoscopic surveillance to detect cancer recurrence at earlier stages, according to guideline recommendations [2930].

Treatment-related AEs are another major concern in the treatment of EGC in late older adult patients because many older adult patients have multiple comorbidities. Owing to poor nutritional status and pulmonary dysfunction, older adult patients with GC who underwent gastrectomies had significantly higher rates of anastomotic leakage and postoperative pneumonia than younger patients [3132]. Furthermore, previous studies that compared the outcomes of ESD and surgery for EGC reported higher treatment-related AEs in patients who underwent surgery, particularly late AEs occurring >30 days after treatment and requiring endoscopic or surgical management [89]. In studies involving older adult patients with EGC, the surgery group had more AEs requiring intensive care unit admission and severe organ dysfunction (e.g., pneumonia and kidney injury) during the short-term postoperative period [1314]. The rate of ESD-related AEs was 8%–12%, and post-ESD bleeding and perforation were the major ESD-related AEs that could be treated with conservative or endoscopic therapy [131428]. In our study, patients who underwent ESD had lower rates of total AEs and AEs requiring surgery and had no treatment-related deaths than those who underwent surgery. In addition, the hospitalization duration was shorter in the ESD group than in the surgery group. ESD is a safe and acceptable treatment option for older adult patients whose EGC lesions meet the curative resection criteria, considering treatment-related AEs and mortality.

This study evaluated the long-term oncologic outcomes after ESD vs. surgery in many late older adult patients aged ≥75 years (>4,000 patients) during long-term follow-up. However, this study has some limitations. First, it was a retrospective cohort study. Therefore, selection bias was unavoidable. To reduce bias, we evaluated outcomes in all patients and in a PS-matched cohort. In addition, a prospective RCT may be unethical because ESD is an accepted treatment for EGC that meets the guideline indications. Second, the risk factors associated with metachronous GC, including H. pylori infection and premalignant gastric lesions (gastric atrophy and intestinal metaplasia), were not evaluated in this study because the study mainly focused on long-term oncological outcomes. In addition, sarcopenia, which is associated with the occurrence of treatment-related AEs [33], and physical status scales used to evaluate frailty in older adult patients are important factors affecting both treatment decision-making and OS. However, owing to the limitations of this retrospective study, we could not analyze these variables. Third, the number of patients in the surgery group was smaller than that in the ESD group, because only 2 study centers provided well-structured surgical data. All study centers were medium- to high-volume referral hospitals providing standard care in accordance with GC treatment guidelines, and the main treatment strategies might be similar between the 2 centers and other centers. However, detailed practices, including the choice between ESD and surgery, may differ depending on the attending physician, the patient's general condition, and the opinions of the patients and their families. Thus, we could not completely exclude the potential bias associated with the large number of centers in this study. Finally, because this study was conducted at referral hospitals in South Korea, a country with one of the highest incidences of GC and a high early detection rate due to the nationwide endoscopic screening program, our findings may have limited generalizability to other countries.

In conclusion, for late older adult patients with EGC aged ≥75 years, long-term OS and DSS rates after ESD were comparable to those after surgery for EGC, that met the curative resection criteria for ESD. Although a higher risk of GC recurrence was observed after ESD, mainly because of local or metachronous recurrence, most cases were manageable with endoscopic treatment. Considering the lower number of severe AEs and higher prevalence of comorbidities in late older adult patients with EGC, ESD may be a primary treatment option in appropriately selected late older adult patients.

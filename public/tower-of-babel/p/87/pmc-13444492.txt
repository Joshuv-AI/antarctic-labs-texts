Acute iliofemoral deep vein thrombosis treatment in the setting of an infrarenal inferior vena cava aneurysm
Authors: Maria Milkina, Ruth A Benson
Published: 2026

A 35-year-old man presented with a one-week history of progressive left lower-extremity pain and extensive swelling extending from the groin to the ankle, with a history of intermittent left leg heaviness since adolescence. Ultrasonography demonstrated extensive acute deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and computed tomography venography confirmed thrombus extending to the origin of the left common iliac vein (LCIV). Imaging revealed several anatomic abnormalities: chronic May–Thurner syndrome with congenital or secondary hypoplasia of the LCIV (A), aneurysmal dilatation of the infrarenal inferior vena cava (IVC) (B/Cover), and IVC scarring with partial fenestrations within the aneurysmal segment.

Initial percutaneous LCIV cannulation for mechanical thrombectomy was unsuccessful. Catheter-directed thrombolysis was performed for 24 hours, achieving adequate clot reduction to permit cannulation and thrombectomy. Sequential balloon dilatation was then performed, and a 14 × 80 mm Sinus Obliquus™ stent was deployed across the LCIV stenosis, followed by a 14 × 60 mm Abre™ stent distally. Despite heparinization, new thrombus developed within the external iliac vein (EIV) and common femoral vein (CFV) to the level of the adductor canal, resulting in inflow occlusion. Thrombolysis was resumed.

Repeat imaging demonstrated that distal IVC dilatation caused the LCIV stent to abut the IVC wall, impairing bilateral CIV outflow. Consequently, 16 mm “kissing” iliac stents were deployed from each CIV into the IVC, landing within the aneurysmal segment. Completion venography demonstrated brisk, unimpeded venous flow (C). The patient was discharged on therapeutic enoxaparin pending interval imaging.

Two-week ultrasonography demonstrated patent stents; however, 6-week imaging identified a new, asymptomatic near-occlusive DVT within the right proximal iliac stent. The patient underwent right iliac stent thrombectomy with intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) guidance and cranial stent extension beyond the aneurysmal segment, with disruption of residual venous webs (D). He was discharged on rivaroxaban and aspirin. All stents remained patent at 9-month follow-up.

IVC aneurysms are exceedingly rare, with a limited number of cases reported in the literature. Current classification is based on anatomic location and embryologic features.1, 2, 3 Management remains non-standardised due to the rarity of IVC aneurysms, with approaches ranging from conservative surveillance to surgical or endovascular intervention.1,2 Emerging reports support anticoagulation or procedural management in the presence of thrombosis, whereas aneurysms without thrombosis may be managed conservatively.1 Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this report and accompanying images.

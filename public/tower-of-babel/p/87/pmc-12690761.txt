PDNO-10 Management of posterior cerebral fossa tumors in children in the neurosurgery department of Yalgado Ouédraogo University hospital (Burkina Faso)
Authors: Haro Yakouba, Compaore J Asiel, Sanou Abdoulaye, Zoungrana Inoussa, Zabsonre D Sylvain, Kabre Abel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTumors of the posterior cerebral fossa account for more than 50% of encephalic tumors in children. Their severity is mainly due to the small size of the posterior fossa, making their management a dreaded problem in countries with limited resources.ObjectiveTo study the management of posterior cerebral fossa tumors in childrenPatients and MethodThis was a descriptive, retrospective, cross-sectional study over a 10-year period (2015-2024), including all children aged 0 to 15 years treated for posterior cerebral fossa tumors in our departmentResultsFifty-seven children, comprising 35 boys (61.4%) and 22 girls, were included in the study, giving a sex ratio of 1.59. The mean age was 7.7 years. Mean age was 7.63 years, with extremes of 1.33 and 15 years. The average consultation time was 101.2 days. The reasons for consultation were headaches in 45 cases (78.9%) and gait disorders in 37 cases (64.9%). Physical examination revealed cerebellar syndrome and intracranial hypertension in 39 cases (68.4%) and 30 cases (52.6%) respectively. Positive diagnosis was made by CT scan in 47 cases (82.5%), which revealed a cerebellar lesion in 45 cases (78.9%) associated with hydrocephalus in 51 cases (89.5%). The lesions suggested medulloblastoma in 24 cases (42.1%) and pilocytic astrocytoma in 21 cases (36.8%). Forty-eight children (84.2%) underwent surgical management, including 26 cases of excision (45.6%) and 42 cases of ventriculoperitoneal shunting (73.7%). Histological confirmation of the tumoral nature was obtained in 6 cases (23.07%). No additional chemotherapy or radiotherapy was available in our country. After 6 months, 15 children showed significant clinical improvement (31.25% of operated patients), and the overall mortality rate was 17.5%.ConclusionHindbrain tumors are common in children in Burkina Faso. Management remains delicate, with acceptable postoperative results despite the lack of complementary treatment.

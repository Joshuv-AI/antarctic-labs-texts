Response to A Review of Human Papillomavirus Vaccination and Associated Ethical Concerns
Authors: Sudip Bhattacharya
Published: 2025

Dear Editor:

I read this review with great interest. The study advocates early human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination, even for abstainers, and emphasizes educating parents and physicians on its cancer prevention benefits. Despite opposition, the authors support continued vaccine promotion for its proven public health impact [1].

The review identifies several research gaps concerning the HPV vaccine. It does not follow PRISMA guidelines, potentially limiting transparency in study selection, data extraction, and analysis. A key gap is the inconclusive link between the vaccine and adverse events. While minor side effects like pain and headaches are common, concerns remain about serious conditions such as complex regional pain syndrome and postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome. Although agencies like the World Health Organization and European Medicines Agency found no causal link, large-scale trials are needed to address public concerns and scepticism regarding vaccine safety.

A key research gap is the lack of long-term efficacy and safety data for the HPV vaccine, especially beyond 10 years, raising questions about the need for booster doses. Additionally, ethical and sociocultural barriers affect vaccine uptake, with concerns about its link to sexual activity hindering acceptance in some populations. More studies are needed to explore strategies for educating parents and caregivers to address these challenges and improve vaccination rates [2].

A notable research gap is the gender disparity in HPV vaccination rates. While the vaccine is promoted for cervical cancer prevention in females, there is less focus on vaccinating males, despite its effectiveness against other HPV-related cancers. Further research is needed to understand the lower vaccination rates among boys and to evaluate the public health impact of increasing male vaccination for herd immunity. Additionally, the influence of media and public perception, particularly negative coverage and anti-vaccine movements, has affected vaccination programs, especially in countries like Japan. More studies should explore media impacts on vaccine acceptance and develop effective communication strategies to restore public trust.

Additionally, there are gaps in understanding the cost-effectiveness of vaccinating older populations. While it is well-established that vaccinating pre-adolescents is cost-effective, there is less clarity regarding the benefits of vaccinating older individuals who may already be sexually active or have been exposed to HPV [3]. More research is required to assess the cost-benefit ratio of extending vaccination programs to these groups. Lastly, vaccine access and equity remain major challenges [4]. The review briefly mentions the unequal access to HPV vaccines, particularly for individuals from lower socioeconomic backgrounds and in low- and middle-income countries, where the burden of cervical cancer is highest. Research is needed to explore how to make the vaccine more accessible and affordable in these regions, including the development of policies and healthcare infrastructure that can support broader vaccine coverage.

Addressing these research gaps will help to improve understanding of the HPV vaccine’s long-term safety and effectiveness, as well as its broader societal impact. This, in turn, could lead to better public health policies, higher vaccination rates, and more equitable access to the vaccine globally.

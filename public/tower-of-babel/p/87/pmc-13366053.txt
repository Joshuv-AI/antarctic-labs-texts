IFPHOR0079 The Impact of Gender Disparities on Rural Community Health Workforces in Ghana: The Case of the Upper West Region of Ghana
Authors: Elma Banyen, Linda Gibson, Mathew Nyashanu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThere are vast gender inequalities in health human resources globally. However, there is a dearth of knowledge in Low-and-Middle-Income-Countries (LMICs) on the impact of gender inequalities in health workforces on services delivery. Thus, the study closes this research gap by examining the impact of gender inequalities among community health workforces on the delivery of maternity care services in the Upper West Region of Ghana using qualitative research methodology. The study sought to assess the gender equality status of the workforce and the impact of existing gender inequalities on maternity care services. In-depth interviews and focus group discussions were used to collect data from participants. They were all tape recorded with participants’ permission and transcribed verbatim. A total of sixty-three participants were purposefully and conveniently recruited from a range of community health workers (CHWs), health supervisors and service users. The study adopted the socio-ecological model to guide data collection and analysis. Thematic analysis was used to analyse data using the NVivo software. Study findings suggest there are different forms of gender inequalities in community health workforces, including numerical disparities. Findings also suggest that gender inequalities impact negatively on maternity care services by reducing the quality of services delivered and service delivery indicators. The study therefore recommends for health systems to implement gender sensitive approaches in maternity care.

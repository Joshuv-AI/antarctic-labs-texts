Epithelial cell spreading assay on E-cadherin-coated glass or PDMS substrates for microscopy-based analysis of cadherin adhesions
Authors: Lilian Schimmel, Ivar Noordstra
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryAdherens junctions (AJs) are multi-protein adhesion structures that couple contractile actomyosin networks of epithelial cells within a tissue. Here, we present an epithelial cell spreading assay on E-cadherin-coated glass or polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) substrates for detailed microscopy-based analysis of cadherin adhesions. We describe steps for preparation of glass coverslips and PDMS gels, E-cadherin coating, and epithelial cell spreading. Epithelial cells can be seeded on E-cadherin-coated surfaces, thereby mimicking AJ formation in X-Y dimension, making it suitable for microscopy analysis.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Noordstra et al. (2023).1

Before you begin

AJ contain a plethora of proteins that, together with other junctional factors, define the strength and dynamics of cell-cell interactions.2 On a bigger scale, AJ significantly contribute to the dynamic properties of tissues both in space and time.3 Although much has been learned about AJ in established monolayers, little is known about de novo formation of AJ. In the study by Noordstra et al.,1 E-cadherin-coated substrates have been used to visualize and unravel the molecular machinery underlying the formation and stabilization of AJ. Depending on the purpose of the experiment, two different setups were employed: E-cadherin on glass coverslips and E-cadherin on PDMS gels. More specifically, epithelial cells spread on E-cadherin-coated glass, displayed immobilized cadherin-catenin adhesions at the cell-substrate interphase. This setup was used to directly visualize the effect of immobilized α-catenin mutants on cortical F-actin flows. In addition, E-cadherin-coated PDMS gels were used to visualize E-cadherin/catenin-dependent F-actin flow force transmission over the plasma membrane.

The aim of this protocol is to provide a detailed guide for the preparation and use of both glass- and PDMS-based E-cadherin coated substrates.

Expected outcomes

Epithelial cells spread on E-cadherin-coated substrates within 2 h. During or after spreading, the cells can be fixed for immunofluorescent labeling or used for live-cell imaging. Note that cell morphology can be strikingly different when spreading depends on cadherins rather than integrins. MCF7 cells form round lamellipodia on E-cadherin substrates and focal adhesion-dependent pointy protrusions on fibronectin (Figure 3).Figure 3Spreading on E-cadherin- and fibronectin-coated glassMCF7 cells spread on E-cadherin ecto-domain-coated glass (left) or fibronectin-coated glass (right). Cells are stained for DAPI (blue), F-actin with Phalloidin (green), and pFAK (magenta).

Epithelial cells spread on E-cadherin-coated substrates form E-cadherin nano clusters at the cell-substrate interphase. E-cadherin nano clusters co-stain for the E-cadherin-binding protein β-catenin (Figure 4) verifying the functionality of the nano clusters.

Limitations

Although spreading cells on immobilized E-cadherin ecto-domains offers unique opportunities (E.g., to assess the effect of α-catenin mutants on F-actin retrograde flows1), it poorly represents the physiological dynamics of E-cadherin at AJ.6 Alternatively, supported lipid bilayers functionalized with E-cadherin ecto-domains can be used for epithelial cell spreading assays.7,8 TFM approaches on lipid bilayers are limited, but resisting forces can be selectively introduced by patterning barriers or by adjusting the fluidity of the bilayers.9 Such systems present exclusive possibilities to visualize and study dynamic properties of E-cadherin and associated proteins.

E-cadherin-coated substrates are only compatible with epithelial cells, expressing endogenous or exogenous E-cadherin. If the assay has to be performed using cell lines expressing different receptors (E.g., VE-cadherin in HUVECs), hE-cadherin/Fc can be substituted for hVE-cadherin/Fc.10

Problem

Step 38: Epithelial cells do not spread within 2 h.

Potential solution

The efficiency of epithelial cell spreading on E-cadherin-coated substrates strongly depends on the FBS concentration (Figure 5). Trypsin treatment cleaves peptide bonds of cell surface receptors.11 As a result, extended trypsin treatment will damage the ecto-domains of E-cadherin, thereby inhibiting E-cadherin-based spreading. It is therefore recommended to keep the trypsin treatment as short as possible. As FBS contains trypsin inhibitors, low levels have to be added to the movie media.

Problem

Step 38: Cells form integrin-based interactions.

Potential solution

Homogenous E-cadherin coating (see “expected outcomes” to verify coating) followed by BSA treatment should prevent non-specific cell adhesion. If immunofluorescent labeling still shows integrin-based adhesion, the cells can be spread in the presence of RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) peptide.12 This competitively binds to the integrin receptors on the cell surface, thereby preventing cell-matrix interactions. A typical working concentration ranges from 0.1–10 μg/mL.

Association between gastroprotective agents and acute kidney injury in patients receiving non‐steroidal anti‐inflammatory drugs: Analysis of a Japanese hospital‐based database
Authors: Satoru Mitsuboshi, Shungo Imai, Hayato Kizaki, Satoko Hori
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe concomitant use of non‐steroidal anti‐inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) potentially increases the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI). However, the risk of AKI has not been comprehensively assessed for the concomitant use of NSAIDs with gastroprotective agents such as misoprostol and PPIs. The objective of this study was to evaluate whether the use of various gastroprotective agents affects the risk of AKI in patients receiving NSAIDs.MethodsThe data analyzed were obtained from the JMDC hospital‐based administrative claims database between April 2014 and August 2022. Histamine‐2 receptor antagonists (H2RAs) were compared with PPIs or misoprostol in patients receiving NSAIDs. The primary outcome was the incidence of AKI. The covariates considered were age and sex, admission to intensive care unit, presence of comorbidities based on the modified Charlson Comorbidity Index, and use of renin‐angiotensin system inhibitors, loop diuretics, other diuretics, and lithium. AKI was identified by changes in serum creatinine. The distribution of AKI was analyzed using the log‐rank test, and estimates of the incidence of AKI were compared among the groups using a Cox proportional hazards model with time‐varying variables. Models were adjusted using a doubly robust method that accounts for the inverse probability of treatment weighting at baseline while adjusting for covariates.ResultsAfter screening, 11,688 patients were eligible for inclusion (1729 for H2RAs, 368 for misoprostol, and 9591 for PPIs). AKI occurred in 0.5% of H2RA recipients and 1.1% of PPI recipients; no AKI was observed in the misoprostol group. Compared with H2RAs, the risk of AKI tended to be higher with PPIs (adjusted hazard ratio 1.83, 95% confidence interval 0.92–3.63, p = 0.08).ConclusionCompared with H2RAs, PPIs may increase the risk of AKI in patients receiving NSAIDs, although no statistically significant difference was observed. Further research is required to assess the risk trade‐off with consideration of both peptic ulcer prevention and the increased risk of AKI in patients concurrently treated with NSAIDs and H2RAs, misoprostol, or PPIs.Keywords: acute kidney injury, histamine‐2 receptor antagonist, misoprostol, non‐steroidal anti‐inflammatory drug, proton pump inhibitor

INTRODUCTION

Non‐steroidal anti‐inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are widely used worldwide for acute and chronic pain, including that associated with arthritis, surgery, and cancer. 1 , 2 , 3 In 2010, more than 29 million adults in the United States were regular users of NSAIDs, which accounted for approximately 12% of all adults. 4 NSAIDs increase the risk of serious adverse events, such as kidney injury, cardiovascular events, and gastrointestinal disorders. 5 , 6 , 7 To mitigate the risk of NSAID‐induced gastrointestinal events, multiple guidelines recommend the use of gastroprotective agents such as misoprostol or proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) in patients undergoing chronic NSAID therapy, particularly those at moderate‐to‐high risk of gastrointestinal events. 8 , 9 , 10

The concomitant use of NSAIDs and PPIs potentially elevates the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) due to the increased probability of acute interstitial nephritis associated with both medications. 11 , 12 On the other hand, misoprostol possibly reduces the risk of NSAID‐induced adverse kidney events because it acts as a kidney vasodilator and exerts antioxidant and anti‐inflammatory effects via the action of prostaglandin. 13 Our prior meta‐analysis of randomized controlled trials also indicated that misoprostol reduces the risk of kidney injury in patients receiving NSAIDs compared with a placebo. 14 Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, no study has comprehensively assessed the risk of AKI when NSAIDs are used concomitantly with gastroprotective agents such as misoprostol and PPIs. These results could provide valuable information on the trade‐off between the benefits of anti‐peptic ulcer treatments and the adverse events of AKI associated with the concurrent use of NSAIDs and gastroprotective agents. Thus, the aim of this study was to evaluate whether the use of various gastroprotective agents affects the risk of AKI in patients receiving NSAIDs.

METHODS

Study design and setting

The data analyzed were obtained from the JMDC hospital‐based administrative claims database (JMDC Inc.) between April 2014 and August 2022; this database includes data on more than 15 million inpatients and outpatients from over 600 hospitals in Japan. 15 , 16 Due to its characteristics, this database includes considerable data on inpatients. There are two types of JMDC databases: hospital‐based and health insurance‐based. We opted for the hospital‐based database to evaluate AKI using serum creatinine because this particular database contains data on blood parameter measurements. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the Faculty of Pharmacy, Keio University (approval number: 230710–1). The requirement for written informed consent was waived in view of the anonymity of the study data, and we followed Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 17

Only patients who started NSAIDs at the same time as gastroprotective agents were selected in this study. Gastroprotective agents were defined as histamine‐2 receptor antagonists (H2RAs), misoprostol, and PPIs. H2RAs were designated as a comparator and were compared with PPIs and misoprostol.

Medication exposure

The index date (day 0) was defined as the date on which the NSAIDs and gastroprotective agents were first prescribed. The patients' medication exposure was calculated from their prescription data. The date and number of days in each prescription were used to calculate the expected end date of the prescription. The medication was considered to be “continued” when the interval between the expected end date of the prescription and the start date of the next prescription was within 14 days. The medication was considered to be “discontinued” when there was no next prescription or the interval between prescriptions was 15 days or more. Thus, when the medication was presumed not to have been taken for 15 days or more, it was considered to have been discontinued. Patients were censored when they experienced AKI, discontinued NSAIDs or gastroprotective agents, or reached the end of the study period. Therefore, the observation period of this study was limited to the duration of concomitant use of NSAIDs and gastroprotective agents.

Patient selection criteria

The patient selection process for the eligible population is shown in Figure 1. First, we selected patients who started NSAIDs at the same time as gastroprotective agents, including misoprostol, PPIs, and H2RAs. Then, only patients with one or more serum creatinine measurements during the observation period and patients with serum creatinine levels <2 mg/dL at the initial measurement were included. Second, the washout period was defined as the initial year of the database period, and patients were excluded if they started NSAID therapy during the washout period. Third, patients who had received antibiotics, antiviral drugs, or contrast agents or who had undergone surgery within 30 days before NSAID initiation to the day of censorship were excluded, because these factors are known to increase the risk of AKI and these medications were excluded due to their transient administration over a short period, rendering them challenging to consider as time‐varying covariates. In addition, patients who had undergone hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis from 365 days before NSAID initiation to day 0 were excluded. Dialysis was determined using all relevant medical practice codes containing the term “hemodialysis” or “peritoneal dialysis”. Finally, patients who were aged <20 years or >100 years or who were missing data on age or sex were excluded. Details on the inclusion and exclusion criteria and covariates are shown in Figure 2.

Outcome

The primary outcome of AKI was defined according to the limits derived from the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) working group: a ≥50% increase in serum creatinine concentration within 7 days or an absolute increase of ≥0.3 mg/dL within 2 days. 18 Urine volume could not be assessed from the records in the JMDC database. All serum creatinine values were collected from eligible populations during the observation period, and all pairs of serum creatinine values having a measurement interval of within 7 days were assessed to determine whether the patients had AKI according to the KDIGO criteria.

The secondary outcome of gastrointestinal bleeding was identified according to the International Classification of Diseases—10th Revision (ICD‐10) codes defined in a previous study. 19

Definitions and covariates

The following potential confounding variables affecting AKI were investigated: age and sex, admission to intensive care unit (ICU), presence of comorbidities based on the modified Charlson Comorbidity Index, concomitant use of renin‐angiotensin system inhibitors (RASis), concomitant use of loop diuretics, concomitant use of other diuretics, and concomitant use of lithium. Other diuretics included thiazide diuretics, aldosterone antagonists, and others. Age and sex on day 0 and the presence of comorbidities within 12 months from day 0 were assessed as time‐fixed covariates, and admission to ICU was assessed during the study period as a time‐fixed covariate. Admission to ICU was identified using the medical fee calculation code and was determined if these codes were used during the period of concomitant use of NSAIDs and gastroprotective agents. Medication use during the study period was assessed as a time‐varying covariate based on prescription data.

The following medications were defined based on Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) code: NSAIDs (M01AB–M01AX), misoprostol (A02BB01), H2RAs (A02BC01–A02BD15), PPIs (A02BC01–A02BD15), RASis (C09AA–C09XA), loop diuretics (C03CA), other diuretics (C03AA–C03XA without C03CA), lithium (N05AN01), antibiotics (J01), and antiviral drugs (J05). The use of contrast agents was identified based on all relevant medical practice codes related to “contrast agents” in the database. The occurrence of surgery was also determined using all relevant medical practice codes containing the term “surgery”.

Comorbidities were defined and assessed based on the modified Charlson Comorbidity Index and included chronic heart failure, dementia, chronic pulmonary disease, connective tissue disease, hemiplegia, renal disease (including moderate to severe), AIDS, diabetes mellitus with chronic complications, liver disease (including mild to severe), or any tumor (including leukemia, lymphoma, or metastatic solid tumor). These comorbidities were identified using ICD‐10 codes defined in a previous study. 20

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test while categorical valuables were compared using Fisher's exact test. AKI distribution was compared among the three groups—H2RA, misoprostol, and PPI—using the log‐rank test.

Estimates of the incidence of AKI were then compared among the groups using a Cox proportional hazards model with time‐varying variables. All potential confounding variables affecting AKI evaluated in this study were analyzed as covariates, including H2RAs, PPIs, RASis, diuretics, lithium, presence of comorbidities, admission to ICU, age, and sex. No AKIs were observed in the misoprostol group; thus, an analysis involving only the H2RA and PPI groups was performed using a doubly robust method that accounts for the inverse probability of treatment weighting at baseline while also adjusting for covariates. Weighting at baseline was calculated using age, sex, admission to ICU, and the presence of comorbidities.

There were no missing data in the eligible population. All data handling and statistical tests were performed using R 4.4.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A p‐value of ≤0.05 was considered significant.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using the Lakatos method. 21 The incidence of AKI due to NSAIDs is reported to be 0.6%–2.2%. 22 , 23 In the present study, a clinically significant difference was considered to be an annual incidence of AKI of 2.0% in the PPI group and of 1.0% in the H2RA or misoprostol group. Assuming a type I error (α) of 0.05, 80% power for the AKI, and a 5:1 allocation ratio, the required sample size was 1038 patients for the PPI group and 208 patients for the H2RA or misoprostol group.

Subgroup and sensitivity analysis

First, we examined patients whose initial NSAID prescription occurred during an outpatient visit or those whose initial NSAID prescription occurred during an inpatient visit and who received any drug prescription as outpatients within 1 year before the initiation of their first NSAID prescription. This is because this research uses a hospital‐based database and patients who do not receive chronic care within the system may be included; thus, these patients receiving their first NSAID prescription may appear in the database only when they are hospitalized. This subgroup analysis was performed to address these concerns. Second, a sensitivity analysis of AKI defined as a ≥1.5‐fold or greater increase in serum creatinine among the KDIGO criteria was also performed. This was because this definition may be more specific to AKI compared with a 0.3 mg/dL increase in creatinine. Both analyses were also performed using the Cox proportional hazards model with time‐varying variables, following a doubly robust method.

A significant difference was found in the distribution of AKI among the three groups by log‐rank test (p = 0.02; Figure 3). After excluding the misoprostol group, a total of 11,320 patients were included in the analysis in which the inverse probability of treatment weighting at baseline was accounted for and the distribution of covariates was successfully balanced (Figure S1). Cox regression analysis revealed that PPIs tended to be associated with a higher risk of AKI (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.83, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.92–3.63, p = 0.08) compared with H2RAs (Table 2). The risk of AKI was significantly increased by admission to ICU (aHR 7.86, 95% CI 4.02–15.38, p < 0.01), age increase by 1 year (aHR 1.02, 95% CI 1.001–1.05, p = 0.04), and any tumor (aHR 3.75, 95% CI 2.01–6.99, p < 0.01). In contrast, the risk of AKI was significantly decreased by connective tissue disease (aHR 0.41, 95% CI 0.17–0.99, p = 0.046).

Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

The subgroup analysis involved only those patients with a history of outpatient prescriptions. A total of 2150 patients were included in the analysis, and no statistically significant difference in the risk of AKI was found between PPIs and H2RAs (aHR 1.24, 95% CI 0.42–3.65, p = 0.70) (Figure S2 and Table S4). In the sensitivity analysis of AKI defined as a ≥1.5‐fold increase in serum creatinine, the obtained aHR of 1.75 was similar to that of the primary outcome, and no statistically significant difference in the risk of AKI was found between PPIs and H2RAs (Figure S3 and Table S5).

DISCUSSION

This study evaluated whether the use of misoprostol, PPIs, or H2RAs affects the risk of AKI in patients receiving NSAIDs. Although the results did not detect a statistically significant difference between PPI and H2RA, the primary and sensitivity analyses performed using the robust design consistently showed similar hazard ratios, suggesting an 80% increased risk of AKI with PPI use compared with H2RA. Therefore, it is reasonable to suggest that, compared with H2RAs, the use of PPIs may be associated with an increased risk of AKI in patients receiving NSAIDs, and these findings are supported by previous studies. 11 , 12 NSAIDs and PPIs sometimes cause acute interstitial nephritis, and concomitant use of these medications may increase the risk of AKI. Importantly, the preventive effect of PPIs on peptic ulcers in chronic users of NSAIDs is greater than that of H2RAs, and many guidelines recommend the use of PPIs, and not H2RAs, in such patients who have moderate to high risk of gastrointestinal events. 8 , 9 , 10 Therefore, long‐term observational studies that take into account both peptic ulcer prevention and AKI outcomes may be a reasonable approach and may contribute to the selection of the appropriate therapy in patients undergoing chronic NSAID use.

In this study, the incidence of gastrointestinal bleeding was lower in the H2RA group than in the PPI group. This may be because the median observation period in this study was 11–15 days, and thus gastrointestinal adverse events might have gone undetected due to the short observation period. However, the incidence of AKI was higher than that of gastrointestinal bleeding in both PPI and H2RA groups, suggesting that AKI may be an important factor to consider when deciding among treatments involving concomitant use of NSAIDs and gastroprotective agents. In fact, it is reported that the number needed to treat with PPI for preventing gastrointestinal events is about 10 in patients with two or more risk factors for gastrointestinal events and ranges from 100 to 268 in patients with low‐to‐moderate–risk factors. 24 , 25 This suggests that the risk of AKI associated with PPIs may outweigh their efficacy in preventing gastrointestinal events in patients with low‐to‐moderate–risk factors. However, it should be noted that these comparisons were based on different study settings.

The misoprostol group did not develop AKI, suggesting that misoprostol may carry a lower risk of AKI compared with PPIs. Indeed, a large cohort study and a meta‐analysis suggested that misoprostol reduces the risk of adverse kidney events in patients receiving NSAIDs. 13 , 14 Thus, given that the preventive effect of misoprostol on peptic ulcers in chronic NSAID users surpasses that of H2RAs, 8 , 9 , 10 misoprostol may be considered a valuable gastroprotective agent in patients on chronic NSAID therapy. However, in this study, a limited number of patients receiving misoprostol were available, and it was difficult to perform an adequate evaluation.

The dose and type of NSAIDs and PPIs were not evaluated in the Cox regression analysis. Although the dose of NSAIDs affects the incidence of AKI, 26 high doses of NSAIDs were rarely administered in this study, and there appeared to be no difference in NSAID use between AKI and non‐AKI groups (Table S1). Furthermore, it is generally recognized that there is no difference in the risk of AKI among different NSAIDs. 27 All types of PPIs are also recognized to carry a risk of AKI, and this risk increases with higher doses of PPIs. 28 However, there appeared to be no difference in the type or dose of PPI between AKI and non‐AKI groups in this study (Table S2).

Admission to ICU and any tumor were significantly associated with an increased risk of AKI in this study. These factors have already been linked to the risk of AKI. 29 In contrast, the results for age and comorbidities did not show consistent results in the primary and sensitivity analyses.

This study has some strengths. The first strength lies in the accuracy of the defined criteria. AKI was assessed via an analysis of serum creatinine levels, which is considered a highly useful method, particularly in light of concerns about the reliability of AKI definitions based on ICD‐10 codes. In addition, the positive predictive value of ICD‐10 codes for assessing the modified Charlson Comorbidity Index has consistently been reported to be high, from 82% to 100%. 20 , 30

Although this study has generated results with clinical implications, it has several limitations. First, the results of the primary analysis and the sensitivity or subgroup analyses did not detect a statistically significant difference. Although this may be due to a lack of statistical power, it is also possible that there is no difference in the risk of AKI between PPIs and H2RAs. Second, the median durations of concomitant NSAID and gastroprotective agent use were relatively short, at 11 days in the misoprostol group, 11 days in the PPI group, and 15 days in the H2RA group. This phenomenon could be attributed to the use of a hospital‐based administrative claims database, which might contain patients using NSAIDs on a transient inpatient basis or patients who received NSAIDs at a clinic or hospital that is not covered by the database before admission. Additionally, because this study censored the use of gastroprotective agents, not just NSAIDs, the duration of NSAID use may be underestimated. Thus, further study may be particularly necessary to ascertain the differences in long‐term effects. Third, there were a limited number of patients in the misoprostol group. This means that it may be difficult to discuss the efficacy or safety of misoprostol. Fourth, this study may be affected by a channeling bias, even though we adjusted for as many covariates as possible in the Cox regression analysis, such as medication use and comorbidities. Compared with misoprostol and H2RAs, PPIs may be more commonly prescribed to patients with more severe conditions. This could potentially contribute to an elevated risk of AKI in the PPI group. Indeed, the proportion of renal disease was higher in the PPI group as in the H2RA group, and the use of some medications or some comorbidities were significantly different among the three groups. Fifth, this study used the JMDC hospital‐based administrative claims database. Hospital‐based patients may exhibit more severe disease than clinic‐based patients, thereby potentially impacting the external validity. Finally, although serum creatinine is generally measured only when deemed necessary in clinical practice, the definition of AKI used by the KDIGO working group requires that baseline serum creatinine be measured 3–7 days before the onset of AKI. It has been pointed out that this criterion may not be practical for identifying all cases of AKI, and there has been some discussion about expanding the definition. 31 This expanded KDIGO definition evaluates not only increases in serum creatinine from baseline but also decreases. In addition, AKI was detected at a significantly higher rate in a multicenter prospective cohort that had severe COVID‐19, using the expanded definition compared with the previous definition. This suggests that a strict definition of AKI does not necessarily provide a high degree of certainty.

In conclusion, our findings suggest that, compared with H2RAs, PPIs may be associated with an increased risk of AKI in patients receiving NSAIDs, although the results did not show statistically significant differences. We also found that the risk of AKI associated with PPIs may outweigh the efficacy of PPIs in preventing gastrointestinal events. Therefore, further research is warranted to assess the risk trade‐off, considering both the prevention of peptic ulcers and the increased risk of AKI in patients treated with NSAIDs concurrently with H2RAs, misoprostol, or PPIs.

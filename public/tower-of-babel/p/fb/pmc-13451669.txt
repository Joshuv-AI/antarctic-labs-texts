Development of an automatic approaching trigger measurement system based on macro-micro collaborative motion
Authors: Fan Zhang, Qiangxian Huang, Wei Wang, Keqin Ding, Songlin Zhou, Zhongliang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo solve the contradiction between the large-stroke motion of the stage and high-precision measurement, a Fast Automatic Approaching Trigger Measurement System (FAATMS) based on macro-micro cooperative motion is proposed herein. This system effectively combines a large-stroke macrostage (LSMS) and a six-degree-of-freedom microstage (6-DOFMS). A three-dimension (3D) motion control and nanoscale triggering method was adopted, involving the rapid movement of the LSMS and the cooperation of the 6-DOFMS for secondary triggering. Before measurement, the LSMS drives the sample to approach the probe quickly to realize a coarse triggering. After the first triggering is completed, the program is automatically switched to enter the 6-DOFMS control to achieve the second precise triggering. The 3D LSMS has a stroke of more than 100 mm × 100 mm × 100 mm. The designed 6-DOFMS is driven by hybrid series and parallel piezoelectric ceramic actuators. The interior adopts a hollow design and is used to install the stage for placing the measured sample, which can be effectively combined with the LSMS to achieve high-precision measurement tasks. The 3D displacement of the FAATMS is measured by laser interferometers. The three-axis laser beams converge on the probe and comply with the Abbe principle throughout the entire measurement range. Based on the secondary triggering strategy of the collaborative motion control of the macro-micro stages, the system drives the sample to trigger the probe ten times in each of the five directions of X+, X-, Y+, Y-, and Z. The peak-to-peak (PP) value of the repeatability measurements was 64.9 nm, and the standard deviation was less than 39.1 nm (k=2). By conducting precise measurements at the micro-nano level on the standard sphere, with a diameter repeatability of 330 nm (k=2), demonstrating the reliability and accuracy of this automatic approaching trigger method in practical applications can be re-evaluated. A series of experimental results show that the developed FAATMS is of great significance for improving the fundamental manufacturing technology and can provide a reference for precision measurement and high-end equipment manufacturing.Keywords: macro-micro stages, approaching, secondary triggering, high-precision, system

1. Introduction

In recent years, with the continuous development of the automotive manufacturing, aerospace and electronic technology, the ultra-precision components necessary for major equipment and high-end instruments in many countries, such as optical components, semiconductor devices and other complex parts, have an increasingly urgent demand for high-precision measuring instruments. To ensure the machining quality of ultra precision devices with geometric feature sizes ranging from hundreds of micrometers to hundreds of millimeters and precision requirements of tens to hundreds of nanometers, it is necessary to do three-dimensional (3D) ultra precision measurements.1–8 Contact and non-contact measurement are two main technologies in the field of high-precision measurement. Non-contact measurement is easily affected by environmental factors (such as light, etc.), and its measurement accuracy is relatively low. For ultra-precision devices with high measurement accuracy requirements, contact measurement remains the preferred method.9–15

In the research and application of micro-nano 3D coordinate measurement technology, a core challenge that restricts its broader development lies in the control of triggering repeatability between the contact probe and the sample being measured. Although there are currently high-precision nano-positioning technologies, accurately and reliably detecting the contact points between the probe and the sample surface remains a key challenge. This uncertainty of contact detection directly leads to the trigger repeatability error, resulting in a non-negligible discrete phenomenon at the trigger position when the probe contacts the same point multiple times. Therefore, before achieving 3D ultra-precision measurement, how to ensure reliable and consistent contact between the probe and the sample under test at nanometer precision is a prerequisite for improving the measurement accuracy and reliability at the micro and nanometer scales.16–18 The importance of measurement strategies is particularly evident in the contact measurement of ultra-precision devices. By comprehensively considering factors such as error control, stability, automation, environmental adaptability, data processing, and mechanical structure, the effectiveness and practicability of the measurement strategy can be ensured.19–28 Magdziak et al. 29 proposed the influence of multiple measurement points on the measurement results of turbine blades. The selection of multiple points is also part of the measurement strategy in coordinate measurement technology. The proposed method helps users of coordinate measurement systems select appropriate measurement strategies for freeform surfaces applicable to the aerospace industry. However, the experiment did not measure and simulate the real coordinates of other cross-sections of the turbine blades, and the measurement process was rather complex. Moravcikova et al. 30 analyzed the influence of various measurement strategies in the coordinate measuring machine (CMM) on the accuracy of measurement parameters. The measurement experiments were conducted using the commercial CMM (PRISMO Ultra). Lai et al. 31 developed a fast automatic precision approaching system for electrochemical nanofmachining based on vision and force-displacement sensing, and verified the effectiveness of the system based on vision and force-displacement sensing modules through experiments. However, no relevant application experiments were performed on this system. Beutler 32 discussed a strategy for measuring free shapes, including determining the reference coordinate system and the measurement process. The author analyzed how to measure different positions of the reference point, but its related measurement strategy is also based on the commercial cylindrical CMM (MarForm MFU200). Ren et al. 33 proposed a curve network sampling strategy. By adaptively deducing the optimal sampling position, the surface is approximately measured within the required accuracy, while minimizing the cost and time of measurement. Current research mostly focuses on aspects such as sampling strategies for measurement points, error compensation, path optimization, and adaptive algorithms, and is often carried out using already developed commercial measurement systems. Although the research on the sampling strategy of measurement points has achieved certain results in improving efficiency and reducing errors, the selection of measurement points should follow certain principles, such as uniformity and representativeness. However, in actual operation, due to factors such as the complex shape of the sample and the variable measurement environment, it is difficult to ensure that each point can accurately reflect the actual state of the sample. Therefore, researching how to ensure the correctness of the 3D coordinates of the measurement points, which highly depends on the specific system design, control and triggering technology, is of great significance for improving the measurement accuracy.

This paper aims to design a fast automatic approaching trigger measurement system (FAATMS) for macro-micro collaborative motion and explore how to effectively combine the macro-micro stages to formulate an appropriate trigger strategy to achieve high-precision measurement. In the LSMS, the XY-direction stages are driven by the alternating current (AC) servo motors, and the Z-direction stage is directly driven by a piezoelectric motor. The entire LSMS is supported and guided by air-floating guide rails, all of which have a stroke of more than 100 mm. The designed 6-DOFMS is driven by piezoelectric ceramic actuators in series and parallel, which can achieve a driving resolution of better than 1nm and a micro displacement of more than 23 μm and 15″. To better complete the repeatability control of the triggering process between the probe and the sample under testing, in the first coarse triggering stage, the LSMS drives the sample to quickly approach the probe, achieving initial contact. The main purpose of this step is to rapidly reduce the distance between the probe and the sample, creating conditions for subsequent precise measurements. However, the LSMS’s too fast running speed will cause the probe to be greatly impacted, which will affect the measurement accuracy. Therefore, after the LSMS completes the first triggering, the automatic switching program enters the control program of the 6-DOFMS to realize the second precise triggering. Although the strategy increases the times of triggering, the distance between the probe and the sample has been reduced to a very small range after the first triggering, which significantly improves the repeatability of the second triggering. Solving the trigger repeatability problem of the probe, thereby further enhancing the measurement accuracy, is crucial for expanding the application of micro-nano three-coordinate measurement in cutting-edge fields such as the detection of precision optical components and the characterization of micro-electromechanical systems, and is expected to further improve the level of ultra-precision measurement technology.

2. Overall structure of automatic approaching trigger measurement system

The developed fast automatic approaching trigger measurement system (FAATMS) is mainly composed of the metrology system, the macro-micro motion system and the measurement system. The schematic diagram of its overall structure is shown in Figure 1. The LSMS meets a running stroke of 100 mm×100 mm×100 mm, and the microdisplacement of the 6-DOFMS is used to realize spatial position compensation.

Diagram of the overall structure of the FAATMS.

The entire measurement system adopts a method where the macro-micro stages drive the motion of the 3D orthogonal target mirror (3D-OTM), while the main part of the laser interferometers is fixed, the 3D-OTM serves as the reflector for laser interferometers. The metrology frame is composed of the laser interferometers, the low-expansion supporting structure for the probe, and the high-precision 3D-OTM. The contact probe provides the measurement trigger signal, and the real-time displacement of the FAATMS is provided via feedback from the laser interferometers.

The 3D-OTM, as the reflector of the three-axis laser interferometers, is a core component in the FAATMS metrology system, and it should have high thermal stability and good optical properties to avoid changes in the optical path caused by the deformation of the mirror during measurement. Corning ULE7972 series microcrystalline glass is a titanium dioxide silicate glass with a nearly zero coefficient of thermal expansion, featuring long-term stability, non-delayed elasticity and no thermal hysteresis, etc. 34 The selection of microcrystalline glass as the processing material for the 3D-OTM meets the actual measurement requirements, and its physical properties are shown in Table 1. The X, Y and Z surfaces of the 3D-OTM are processed separately to ensure the flatness of each mirror surface, and the mirrors are precisely aligned and secured by vacuum adsorption.

2.1. Design of the large-stroke macrostage

At present, in micro-nano three-coordinate measurement, there is an urgent need for macrostages that can provide a large motion range, high stability and fast motion speed to achieve high-efficiency and high-precision measurement. The large-stroke macrostage (LSMS) adopts a split design, mainly including a coplanar motion stage for the X/Y directions and a Z-direction stage with a counterweight balancing structure, as shown in Figure 2.

Schematic diagram of the LSMS.

The X and Y coplanar motion stages are driven by a combination of ball screw pairs and the AC servo motors, and are supported and guided by air-floating rails. The stages in the X/Y directions respectively employ a crossbeam structure and a dual-guide-rail structure. The Z-stage is directly driven by a high-precision piezoelectric motor combined with an air-floating guide rail. The X/Y coplanar motion stage and the Z-stage are structurally independent of each other, simplifying the overall LSMS design and reducing assembly and debugging complexity. The LSMS achieves a stroke of 100 mm×100 mm×100 mm, with a repeat positioning accuracy of better than 1 μm, and smooth running.

2.2. Design of the six-degree-of-freedom microstage

Combined with the error-free measurement layout requirements of the FAATMS mentioned above and the structure and stroke indicators of the LSMS, the designed six-degree-of-freedom microstage (6-DOFMS) features multiple degrees of freedom and sub-nanometer resolution. The 6-DOFMS runs in conjunction with the LSMS to achieve high-precision measurement with nano-level trigger resolution. The 6-DOFMS is assembled from translation, rotation, and support layers, encompassing six degrees of freedom: translation along the X, Y, and Z axes, as well as rotation about the X, Y, and Z axes. The motions for all 6-DOF are achieved by combining piezoelectric ceramic actuators with flexible hinges. The 6-DOFMS is mainly composed of four layers, which are defined from top to bottom as the translation layer, the rotation layer, the support layer, and the base. The support layer enables motion in three degrees of freedom (Dz, θx, θy). Dz represents the displacement along the Z-axis, θx is the rotation angle around the X-axis, the same as θy. The translation layer can run in two degrees of freedom (Dx, Dy). Dx, represents the displacement along the X-axis direction, and similarly, Dy. The rotation layer can realize the movement of the remaining one degree of freedom θz, which is the rotation angle around the Z-axis. The 6-DOFMS adopts an internal hollow structure design, and the Z-axis piezoelectric actuators are not symmetrically arranged about the X-axis. This is because the Z-axis laser beam needs to pass through the cantilever and the pentagonal prism to achieve steering, enabling the convergence of three laser beams onto the probe and realizing the measurement of Z-axis motion (Figure 3).

Schematic diagram of the 6-DOFMS.

Using a cantilever support, the pentagonal prism is positioned centrally between the 6-DOFMS rotating layer and the base to achieve laser steering, as shown in Figure 4. This allows the three laser beams converge onto the contact probe. The 3D-OTM serves as the measuring mirror of the laser interferometers, eliminating the primary Abbe error. The 6-DOFMS can compensate in real-time for the positioning errors and angular errors of the LSMS, thereby reducing the secondary Abbe error. In principle, the FAATMS can achieve measurement free of Abbe error. The flexible hinges of the translation and the rotation layers of the 6-DOFMS are subjected to significant pressure in the vertical direction, requiring them to bear the weight of the 3D-OTM, the objective table, and the sample under testing. To reduce the deformation of the flexible hinges in the vertical direction, the materials of the translation and the rotation layers are fabricated from titanium-aluminum alloy TC4. This material is widely used in the aerospace field and offers excellent stability and comprehensive mechanical properties.

3. The fast automatic approaching trigger measurement system control method

To meet the requirements of the fast automatic approaching trigger measurement system (FAATMS) for a motion range of up to 100 millimeters and high-precision measurement, and to achieve automatic approaching trigger measurement with macro-micro coordinated motion, the control system is of crucial importance. In response to the above designs of the LSMS and the 6-DOFMS, it is necessary to research control methods for the macro-micro stages.

3.1. The LSMS control technology

The LSMS incorporates complex mechanical structures, precise signal processing, and driving systems, among other modules. The driving and control system is also very complex, with multiple components interconnected, and constitutes an important subsystem of the LSMS. The X/Y macrostages are mainly driven by a combination of the AC servo motors and ball screws, where the rotation of the ball screws can convert rotational motion into linear motion. The running of the Z-stage is directly driven by the N331 motor. In the X and Y directions, the running range reaches 100 mm×100 mm, and the maximum stroke can reach 110 mm×110 mm. The Z-stage meets the design requirements of a running stroke of 100 mm and a maximum stroke of 105 mm. Since the Z-motor drives the stage to move vertically upwards, even though a balance counterweight system has been designed, certain requirements still exist for its driving power and self-locking function. The Z-motor adopts the N-331 piezoelectric stepping driver from PI Company, which offers high motion accuracy and stability.

The stages in the X and Y directions adopt a dual-loop control technology based on secondary feedback. In the dual-loop control with secondary feedback, the two feedback devices are connected to the same axis. A feedback device (rotary encoder) is installed on the motor, while another feedback device (grating scale) is connected to the load. The motion servo system is based on a three-loop control, which consists of the current loop, the speed loop and the position loop. The position setting value is compared with the displacement value fed back by the grating scale, and the deviation value after proportional-integral-derivative (PID) regulation through the position loop is then output. The RX2 series of grating scales used for X-axis and Y-axis are manufactured by Dalian Rongshu Optical Co., Ltd. The subdivision function of high-speed analog-to-digital converters can achieve higher sampling rates, provide wider frequency bands, and further reduce subdivision errors and noise, achieving an effective resolution of 20 nm (temperature: -10 °C∼+50 °C; motion speed ≤ 400 mm/s; excitation frequency: 5 MHz).

In the dual-loop control system, the load position feedback device is used to control the positioning of the stage, while the motor feedback device is used to control the speed and current. The grating scale has an analog output function, which serves as the main positioning feedback. For the load feedback, the main functions achieved include zero return and analog output. To ensure the stable operation of the dual-loop control, the effective resolution of the motor encoder must be higher than that of the load feedback device. The overall control block diagram of the entire X/Y macrostages is shown in Figure 5. The dual-loop control based on secondary feedback can achieve better control effects, enhance the adaptive ability of loop parameters, and increase the operating frequency of the system, enabling the entire control to proceed quickly and efficiently. However, to address the issue that the X/Y macrostages are prone to interference during PID control, in the speed loop control, the input and output signals of the speed feedback are filtered (after tuning, the proportional gain is 0.8 A/rps, and the integral gain is 50 Hz).

X/Y macrostages control block diagram.

The Z-stage needs to have a motion range of 100 mm. The motor drives the stage to move vertically upwards, which means there are certain requirements for the load-bearing capacity of the Z-motor and whether its self-locking. The stroke of the Z-axis N331 piezoelectric motor meets the requirements. The load force can reach 50 N. It has three control modes: full-step, nanostepping, and analog. During practical application, when the motion range is large, the stepping mode (full-step and nanostepping) is adopted; whereas in the analog control mode, this motor can perform nano-level microdisplacement. The 3D model diagram of the piezoelectric motor is shown in Figure 6.

3D model of the piezoelectric linear motor.

In Figure 6, the piezoelectric actuators of the driving module are controlled in pairs, with d11, d12, d21, and d22 representing the voltage signals applied to the piezoelectric actuators. The shearing motion is utilized to propel the ceramic rod, while the clamping direction provides a substantial holding force. The two voltage signals used for the shearing direction are denoted as D1 and D2; the two voltage signals used for clamping direction are denoted as C1 and C2. Their respective representations are as follows:

In the full-step mode, the running of the piezoelectric linear motor’s shearing and clamping voltages over time is shown in Figure 7. The first group of piezoelectric ceramics (Pair1) is elongated to clamp the ceramic push rod. Voltage C1 keeps increasing until it reaches the maximum voltage Umax, while voltage C2 keeps decreasing, causing the piezoelectric ceramics to contract backward. At this moment, there is no shearing motion and the ceramic push rod does not move. Then, as voltage D1 keeps increasing, the shearing motion is executed, pushing the ceramic rod. Meanwhile, voltage D2 decreases and bends in the opposite direction, preparing for the next alternating motion. In Figure 7, after the first group of piezoelectric ceramics has completed its movement, the voltage C1 continuously decreases to Umin, while C2 increases to Umax. The first group of piezoelectric ceramics retracts, and the second group elongates to clamp, while the shear voltages D1 and D2 remain unchanged. During this process, there is no displacement change. Finally, the voltage D2 keeps increasing to perform the shearing motion, pushing the ceramic rod forward. Voltage D1 is continuously reduced to prepare for the next cycle of alternating motion.

The running of the shearing and clamping voltages in the full-step control mode over time.

In the full-step mode, the motion is not uniform. During the clamping phase, the displacement will be interrupted. In summary, the ceramic rod is driven forward or backward by the orderly movement of the piezoelectric bodies in Pair1 and Pair2. In addition, under this control mode, the piezoelectric bodies are divided into two groups and alternately contact with the ceramic rod to provide the maximum holding force.

In the full-step control mode the voltage used for shearing and clamping motions do not change simultaneously; there is an alternating process. In the process of nanostepping control, the voltage used for shearing and clamping changes simultaneously. During certain phases of the nanostepping cycle, both piezoelectric actuators come into contact with the ceramic rod, and then all the shearing components move in the same direction. In this control mode, there is overlap and the ceramic push rod will move stably. The shearing motion always exists in the whole process in the nanostepping control mode. At the end of the motion, only one set of piezoelectric actuators in the two driving modules are in contact with the ceramic push rod. This control mode provides continuous displacement, without interruption, uniform speed and stable operation, and the vibration is smaller than the full-step control mode. Operation in analog control mode is achieved only by shearing driving components. All the piezoelectric actuators in the driving module are in contact with the ceramic push rods, and the stroke range is limited by the shearing displacement. The stroke of the analog mode adopted in this system is ±10 μm.

3.2. The 6-DOFMS precision control technology

The design specifications of the 6-DOFMS are that the 3D linear displacement stroke can reach ±10 μm, the linear displacement driving resolution is 1 nm; the angular stroke can reach ±10″, the angular resolution is 0.2″. According to the specifications of the 6-DOFMS, the corresponding motion control system is built, which consists of the main control circuit based on Advanced RISC Machines (ARM), linear amplification and signal processing circuit, and power supply circuit. Finally, the performance of the 6-DOFMS was verified, and it can be well applied to the FAATMS.

The controller parses the control instructions from the upper computer and successfully distributes them to the internal signal processing circuit, driving the D/A conversion chip to output the analog voltage signal, which is applied to the piezoelectric ceramic actuators after low-pass filtering and the amplification module. The 6-DOFMS is driven to realize micro-displacement translation and micro-angle rotation. The 6-DOFMS control system adopts the series-parallel hybrid driving mode. In section II.B, it mainly involves the control of two piezoceramic actuators in the translation layer, four piezoceramic actuators in the rotation layer and three piezoceramic actuators in the support layer. When selecting piezoelectric ceramic actuators, in addition to ensuring the microstage stroke, the load force, safety and reliability, and service life of the piezoelectric ceramic should also be considered. The actual maximum driving voltage is generally 3/4 of the nominal voltage. To enable the 6-DOFMS to achieve positive and negative translation as well as clockwise and counterclockwise rotation, the driving voltage of the piezoelectric ceramic actuators in the translation layer and support layer needs to be pre-applied to half of the maximum voltage, which is set as the “zero position” of the microstage. During motion, the voltage is increased or decreased based on this “zero position” to achieve relevant functions. The rotating layer is located in the middle layer, and its function is mainly to realize the rotation of the microstage in the Z-direction according to section II.B. Therefore, the initial driving voltage of the piezoelectric ceramic in this layer is 0 V.

The control process of the 6-DOFMS is shown in Figure 8. The host computer sends control instructions to the microprocessor of the main control circuit via serial communication. The parsed instructions are sent to the signal processing circuit, and the D/A conversion chip is driven to output voltage signals through SPI communication. After low-pass filtering to remove clutter, the piezoelectric ceramic actuator is driven by the linear amplification circuit. The stability and resolution of the microstage controller are determined by the hardware circuit. The hardware circuit adopts a seven-channel control. The translation layer achieves motion in the X and Y directions, each controlled by a piezoelectric ceramic actuator, with linear amplification circuits designed for two-channel outputs. The rotation layer features four piezoelectric ceramic actuators for Z-axis rotation, these actuators are paired to control two channels. The support layer is equipped with three piezoelectric actuators corresponding to the three output channels of the linear amplification circuit. The experimental results show that the displacements and resolutions of each degree of freedom are as follows: the maximum displacements in the X, Y, and Z directions are 24.1 μm, 23.1 μm, and 42.8 μm respectively, with a resolution as high as 1 nm; the maximum rotation angles θx, θy, and θz around the X, Y, and Z axes in clockwise and counterclockwise directions are 40″, and the resolution reaches 0.2″.

4. Secondary triggering strategy for collaborative motion control of macro-micro stages

The repeatability of the contact probe in the FAATMS plays a crucial role in the final measurement accuracy of the entire system. Moreover, the repeatability of the probe is closely related to the control of the stage and the formulation of the triggering strategy. Testing results show that the stroke of the 3D LSMS can reach up to 100 mm, and the repeat positioning accuracy is better than 1 μm. The displacement of the 6-DOFMS can exceed 20 µm (±10 μm), and the resolution is better than 1 nm. Because the stability and repeatability of the contact probe require triggering at a relatively slower speed compared to the LSMS to ensure its performance, directly triggering with the LSMS would cause a significant impact on the probe, greatly affecting its stability and compromising measurement accuracy. Therefore, to ensure that the FAATMS possesses nanometer-scale 3D triggering resolution and a relatively high motion speed, and to balance high precision with measurement efficiency, a 3D motion control and triggering technology is proposed. This technology combines the rapid motion of the LSMS with the 6-DOFMS for secondary triggering. The secondary triggering strategy flow chart of macro-micro collaborative motion control is shown in Figure 9.

Flow chart of secondary triggering strategy for macro/micro collaborative motion control.

Due to the requirement for both forward and reverse triggering, the 6-DOFMS should first be positioned at the intermediate location (zero position), enabling bidirectional motion. After successful communication between the macrostage and the microstage, the macrostage drives the tested sample to quickly approach the contact probe at a speed of 1 mm/s until the distance between the tested sample and the probe is less than 1 mm. Then, the macrostage runs at a speed of 100 μm/s. When the sample contacts the probe and reaches the preset voltage threshold of the probe, a trigger alert is activated, and the LSMS is immediately retracted by 6 μm at a speed of 1 mm/s to ensure the sample fully disengages from the probe (note that the retraction distance should be greater than the overshoot distance of a single trigger but not excessive, ensuring that the secondary triggering remains within the motion stroke of the the 6-DOFMS). The LSMS completes the retraction and stops. After the program detects the LSMS feedback stop signal, the 6-DOFMS immediately continues to run at a speed of 100 nm/s according to the direction of the first triggering.

Note that the process of the LSMS backtracking to stop and the 6-DOFMS continuing to move forward is continuous. When the 6-DOFMS continues to move forward until it reaches the contact probe trigger threshold for the second time, immediately latch the laser interferometer signal and save it to the computer. The program controls the macro-micro stages to retract, and the 6-DOFMS returns to the middle position for the next sampling measurement. After the secondary triggering process is completed, it is necessary to check whether the signal of the probe at this time is normal and whether there is any fault signal in the software. If a fault occurs, it should be addressed promptly before proceeding with the next triggering.

5. Secondary triggering strategy for collaborative motion control of macro/micro stages

The contact probe with good performance is installed on the metrology frame of the FAATMS, as shown in Figure 10. Based on the secondary trigger strategy of the macro-micro stages collaborative motion control, the measurement temperature needs to be strictly controlled before testing the contact probe performance. For the FAATMS with nanometer-level precision, temperature fluctuations can have a significant impact on the measurement results, such as affecting the thermal deformation of probe components and the measurement accuracy of the laser interferometers.

The contact probe is installed on the FAATMS.

Therefore, the control of environmental temperature is of vital importance. The temperature is controlled using a high-precision constant temperature chamber, which operates based on the principle of natural convection and uses the Fluke high-precision temperature measuring instrument Hart1529 as the feedback unit. Two constant current power supplies PSM6003, are selected as the driving units for the cooling chips to ensure sufficient power. The host computer acquires the temperature values measured by the sensors through serial communication, inputs them into the PID controller for regulation, and outputs the current of the cooling chip, thereby adjusting in real time the power of the cooling chip and achieving temperature control within the constant temperature chamber. The testing results are shown in Figure 11. When the high-power control components (such as the AC servo motors, controllers, etc.) of the FAATMS operate for a long time, the steady-state temperature accuracy of the constant temperature chamber can reach 21.5 ± 0.04 °C.

5.1. Repeatability test of the probe based on macro-micro collaborative secondary triggering

Based on the secondary triggering strategy mentioned in Section IV, a repeatability test was conducted on the contact probe installed on the FAATMS to verify the effectiveness of the macro-micro stages design and the control triggering strategy. The standard gauge blocks are adopted as the triggering sample and fixed on the stage of the FAATM using the designed fixture. The operator adjusts the signal of the probe so that the two initial output signals (X, Y) of the probe are close to “0″, that is, at the zero point positioning. The secondary triggering strategy of macro/micro stages collaborative motion control is used to contact and trigger the probe.

After the secondary triggering is completed, the software automatically latches the displacement values of the laser interferometers at this time. The physical diagram of the experimental device is shown in Figure 12. The testing process can be regarded as multiple measurements of a single point. Upon completion of the secondary triggering, the macro-micro stages drive the standard gauge block to retreat rapidly until it is completely separated from the contact probe. This process is repeated continuously, with the probe being touched ten times in each direction. Subsequently, the standard deviation and the peak-to-peak deviation of the probe trigger repeatability in this direction are calculated. By changing the triggering direction of the gauge block relative to the probe, the above-mentioned secondary triggering strategy was repeated.

Physical diagram of the contact probe repeatability testing device in the FAATMS.

The displacement values latched by the three-axis laser interferometers when the gauge block triggered in the X+, X-, Y+, Y-, and Z directions were recorded separately. The results of the probe triggering repeatability are shown in Tables 2 and 3. If a certain point is triggered multiple times, due to the possible variations in the placement of the tested sample, the displacement values in each triggering direction may differ. However, this does not affect the repeatability range value and the standard deviation. Based on the secondary triggering strategy of macro-micro collaborative motion, the FAATMS drives the sample to trigger ten times from each of the five directions of X+, X -, Y+, Y -, and Z, with a repeatability peak-to-peak (PP) value of 64.9 nm and a standard deviation of less than 39.1 nm (k=2).

The repeatability of the contact probe plays a crucial role in the final measurement accuracy of the entire FAATMS system. Based on the macro-micro stages design and motion control of the FAATMS, a method for 3D motion control and nanometer-scale triggering was proposed, which combines the LSMS rapid stable motion with 6-DOFMS to achieve the secondary triggering. A series of experiments have proven that the secondary triggering method based on the collaborative motion control of macro-micro stages is feasible and can be further applied in subsequent high-precision measurement applications.

6. Conclusion

In this paper, a fast automatic approach triggered measurement system (FAATMS) based on macro-micro collaborative motion is proposed. By combining the mechanical structure and control characteristics of the 3D LSMS and the parallel-hybrid-driven the 6-DOFMS, this work explores how to develop appropriate triggering strategies to ensure the accuracy of the 3D coordinates of the measurement points on the tested sample. In the coarse triggering, the 3D LSMS drives the sample to approach the probe quickly and achieve initial contact. The main purpose of this step is to rapidly reduce the distance between the probe and the sample to establish the preconditions for subsequent precise measurement. However, an excessively fast speed can cause the probe to experience severe impact, thereby affecting the measurement accuracy. Therefore, after the first trigger is completed, the 3D LSMS retracts and the system automatically switches to the 6-DOFMS control program to achieve the second precise triggering. The peak-to-peak deviation of the probe trigger repeatability is 64.9 nm, and the standard deviation is less than 39.1 nm (k=2).

For future work, physics-informed machine learning (PIML) will be introduced to model the nonlinear dynamics of the 6-DOFMS, improving triggering repeatability under varying conditions. 35 Additionally, a digital twin of the FAATMS will be developed within the Industrial Metaverse framework to enable virtual commissioning and predictive maintenance for smart manufacturing applications.36,37 Furthermore, sensor-data-driven semi-physical commissioning will be explored to allow pre-deployment validation, thereby reducing physical trial costs and supporting broader adoption in high-end equipment manufacturing.38–40

In addition, the triggering strategy will be further optimized through the establishment of a model to identify a more appropriate approach speed, achieving better measurement performance.

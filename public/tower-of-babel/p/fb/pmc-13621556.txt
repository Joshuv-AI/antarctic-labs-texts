Efficacy of Remimazolam for Anesthesia in Patients Undergoing Renal Allograft Transplantation: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Mingming Han, Dandan Sun, Danjun Lu, Xiang Huang, Fang Kang, Juan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeAs a novel benzodiazepine sedative, remimazolam has demonstrated favorable anesthetic properties. However, its clinical performance in patients undergoing renal allograft transplantation remains undetermined. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of remimazolam for anesthesia in patients undergoing renal allograft transplantation, with the incidence of hypotension during induction as the primary endpoint.Patients and MethodsSixty patients scheduled for elective living-related renal transplantation were enrolled. The participants were randomly allocated into two groups (n=30 each): the propofol group (Group P) and the remimazolam group (Group R). Groups P and R received propofol and remimazolam, respectively, for anesthesia induction and maintenance. The patients’ hemodynamic parameters, postoperative recovery outcomes, adverse reactions, and pharmacokinetic parameters of the sedative drugs were recorded and compared between groups.ResultsCompared with propofol, remimazolam has a longer onset time, but provides better hemodynamic stability, more rapid metabolism, negligible injection pain, and no significant impact on hepatic or renal function.ConclusionDespite its prolonged onset time, remimazolam provides better hemodynamic stability and less injection pain than propofol in patients undergoing allogeneic renal transplantation, with comparable recovery outcomes. Its rapid metabolism is evidenced by a prompt decline in plasma concentration following discontinuation.

Introduction

Renal transplantation is the most effective treatment for end-stage renal disease (ESRD). In 2023, a record 28,142 total kidney transplants were achieved in the United States.1 This kind of surgical procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia, and the appropriate selection of anesthetic drugs is crucial for promoting the recovery of transplanted renal function and improving patient prognosis and outcomes.

Propofol is commonly used for its rapid onset and fast metabolism. However, it is associated with injection pain and hemodynamic instability, which may be particularly problematic in patients with compromised cardiovascular reserve. As an alternative, remimazolam offers potential advantages, including minimal respiratory and circulatory depression, absence of injection pain, and metabolism by nonspecific plasma esterases that imposes no burden on the liver or kidneys. Its metabolites are mostly inactive, reducing the risk of accumulation, and it has a specific reversal agent, flumazenil. Compared with propofol, remimazolam has been reported to provide more stable hemodynamics and improve early post-operative cognitive function,2 and to reduce the threshold for vasopressor use and enhance thermoregulation.3 Nevertheless, evidence supporting its use specifically in renal transplant recipients remains limited, as most studies have focused on general surgical populations or healthy volunteers. Despite remimazolam’s favorable pharmacologic profile, patients with ESRD constitute a distinct patient population requiring special consideration. These individuals experience pathophysiological changes that significantly impact the pharmacodynamics of sedative drugs. The accumulation of uremic toxins increases blood-brain barrier permeability,4 facilitating the entry of the sedatives into the central nervous system. Electrolyte disturbances combined with metabolic acidosis significantly increase neuronal sensitivity to sedative drugs. Remimazolam exerts its sedative effect by binding to GABAA receptors and enhancing the binding of GABA to these receptors.5 Research indicates that chronic renal disease can lead to GABAergic system dysfunction. Although no changes in GABAA receptor expression are observed in the central nervous system, cerebrospinal fluid shows decreased GABA levels and elevated glutamate concentrations, while plasma levels of both neurotransmitters are increased.6 Given these pathophysiological alterations, it is important to evaluate whether remimazolam’s favorable profile observed in general populations also applies to renal transplant recipients. Although previous studies have explored remimazolam in surgical and high-risk populations,7,8 its specific role in renal transplantation remains insufficiently contextualized. Considering the pathophysiological changes in ESRD patients, such as GABAergic system dysfunction and increased blood-brain barrier permeability, which may alter patients’ sensitivity to remimazolam, the anesthetic efficacy of remimazolam in renal allograft transplantation warrants investigation. This study aims to evaluate this efficacy, focusing on hemodynamic stability and recovery outcomes, providing evidence for optimizing anesthetic use in this population.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Approval

This research study obtained approval from the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China (2022KY Ethical Review No. 258) on Oct 6, 2022, and was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200066634). Prior to participation, all patients were thoroughly informed about the study details and provided their written consent. The study was conducted in strict accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and adhered to the ethical guidelines set forth by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China. All kidneys were donated voluntarily with written informed consent, and the donation and transplantation procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Istanbul.

Participants

A total of 60 patients scheduled for elective living-related donor renal transplantation were selected for this study. The inclusion criteria were as follows: recipients of living-related renal transplantation; aged 18–64 years; body mass index (BMI) of 18.5–24.0 kg/m2; American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification grade III or IV; undergoing regular preoperative dialysis; and no restrictions on gender. Patients meeting any of the following conditions will be excluded from the study: a history of neuromuscular disorders; a documented history of schizophrenia; a past diagnosis of depression; the presence of a difficult airway; or recent administration of benzodiazepines.

Randomization, Blinding, and Allocation Concealment

Participants were randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to either the propofol group (Group P) or the remimazolam tosilate group (Group R) using a computer-generated random number table prepared by an independent statistician. Crucially, the research personnel responsible for both patient enrollment and group assignment were entirely blinded to this sequence. To enforce this concealment, group assignments were concealed in opaque, sealed envelopes. An anesthetist, who was independent of the research team and not involved in any subsequent study processes, opened the envelope immediately before anesthesia induction, prepared the designated study drug, and performed the anesthesia according to a pre-specified protocol. To ensure blinding, syringes containing propofol and remimazolam, along with infusion pumps, extension tubes, and intravenous lines, were uniformly concealed. The researchers, responsible for data collection, remained blinded to group allocation throughout the study. Unblinding occurred after data lock and completion of preliminary analyses.

Anesthesia Management

All patients underwent standard fasting protocols without premedication. Upon arrival in the operating room, peripheral intravenous access was established, and supplemental oxygen was administered. Standard monitoring included non-invasive blood pressure, heart rate (HR), electrocardiography, pulse oximetry, and bispectral index (BIS). Invasive arterial pressure monitoring was initiated via radial artery cannulation under local anesthesia.

For induction, Group P received propofol (Batch No.: 2211150, Fresenius Kabi, Baden-Humboldt, Germany) 2 mg·kg−1 while Group R received remimazolam (Batch No.: 230226AT, Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals Co., Ltd, Lianyungang, China) 0.3 mg·kg,−17 followed by sufentanil 0.4 μg·kg−1 and cisatracurium 0.2 mg·kg−1 intravenously. After laryngeal mask airway (LMA) placement, mechanical ventilation was initiated with tidal volume 8–10 mL·kg−1, respiratory rate 12–14 breaths·min−1, inspiration-to-expiration ratio 1:2, maintaining end-tidal CO2 (PETCO2) at 35–45 mmHg (1 mmHg = 0.133 kPa). Anesthesia maintenance involved propofol 4.0–10.0 mg·kg−1 h−1 (Group P) or remimazolam 1.0–2.0 mg·kg−1·h−1 (Group R),9,10 combined with an intravenous remifentanil infusion at 0.1–0.3 μg·kg−1·min−1. The BIS was maintained between 40–60, with cisatracurium administered as needed for neuromuscular blockade. Both groups received normal saline (100 mL pre-induction bolus followed by 50 mL·h−1 maintenance) with fluid titration targeting pulse pressure variation <9%, including 500 mL during renal vessel clamping. Post-operatively, anesthetics were discontinued and the airway removed upon full recovery. Patients developing any of the following complications were excluded from the study: major surgical complications (hemorrhage, urinary leakage, or ureteral obstruction); transplant-related complications (delayed graft function, primary non-function, acute rejection, or severe infection).

Outcome

Hemodynamic parameters including mean arterial pressure (MAP) and HR were recorded at the following time points: at 10 min after stabilization in the operating room (T0), immediately after completing anesthetic induction with either propofol or remimazolam (T1), at 1 and 3 min after sedative administration (T2,3), upon LMA insertion (T4), at skin incision (T5), at renal artery clamping and unclamping (T6,7), and at the end of surgery (T8). The differences between MAP and HR values at various time points and baseline levels (designated as ΔMAP and ΔHR) were calculated, along with the mean differences between time points T1-8 and baseline T0 values (denoted as ΔMAP and ΔHR). Vasoactive drugs were administered when necessary to maintain hemodynamic stability, with their usage recorded.

The time from anesthesia induction until BIS decreased to 60 (BIS 60) was recorded. The time from discontinuation of anesthetics until BIS recovered to 80 (BIS 80) was documented. Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (MOAA/S) scores were assessed every 3 min after surgery, and the time from anesthesia cessation to consciousness recovery (recovery time) was recorded. Consciousness recovery was defined as the first instance of MOAA/S score reaching 5 (confirmed by three consecutive assessments).11 Surgical duration, anesthesia duration, LMA removal time, and post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) stay duration were documented.

QoR-15 scores were evaluated pre-operatively and on post-operative day (POD) 1. Time to first ambulation and first flatus were recorded. Serum creatinine (CREA), blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels, and incidence of acute kidney injury were recorded pre-operatively and on POD 1 and POD 3. The incidence of injection pain, agitation, post-operative nausea and vomiting (PONV), dizziness, and delayed awakening were recorded in both groups. The occurrence of delayed emergence was recorded in both groups. Delayed emergence was defined as time to extubation greater than 30 minutes after the end of surgery.

For patients in the R group, 2 mL of arterial blood was collected at 0, 5, 10, and 20 min after remimazolam discontinuation. The plasma concentration of remimazolam was determined using Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry. Briefly, 50 µL of plasma sample was mixed with 450 µL of internal standard working solution (acetonitrile-diluted terfenadine at 40 ng/mL), vortexed for 1 min, and centrifuged at 4°C and 15,400g for 10 min. The supernatant was then injected for analysis. Target compounds in the samples were analyzed using a Shimadzu ultra high performance liquid chromatograph coupled with an AB Sciex 4000 QTRAP triple quadrupole mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation of the target compounds was performed on a ZORBAX Eclipse XDB C18 column (2.1 × 50 mm, 3.5 μm; Agilent). The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water containing 5 mM ammonium acetate (phase A) and acetonitrile (phase B) at a flow rate of 0.7 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: a) Initial hold at 10% B for 1 min, then linearly increase to 90% B within 1.2 min; b) Hold at 90% B until 2.5 min; c) Return to 10% B within 0.01 min and hold for 3 min. The mass spectrometer was equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operated in positive multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. The quantifier ion pair for remimazolam was 440.1/408.1 with DP 84 V, EP 10 V, CE 20 V, and CXP 8 V. For terfenadine, the quantifier ion pair was 472.4/436.4 with DP 66 V, EP 10 V, CE 58 V, and CXP 14 V. Quality control (QC) was implemented throughout the entire analytical process. Blank plasma was used as the blank matrix to prepare the corresponding calibration standards and QC samples. The calibration curve ranged from 10 to 1000 ng/mL, and QC concentrations were 20 ng/mL, 180 ng/mL, and 800 ng/mL. The calibration standard and QC working solutions were prepared from independently weighed and prepared stock solutions.

Statistical Analyses

Stable hemodynamics serve as a critical indicator of successful anesthesia. Consequently, the incidence of hypotension during anesthesia induction was adopted as the primary endpoint for sample size calculation in this study. Preliminary data from our clinical observations included 13 patients per group undergoing allogeneic renal transplantation under different anesthetic regimens, the incidence rates of hypotension during anesthesia induction were 38.5% and 7.7% respectively in propofol and remimazolam group. Using PASS software with a two-sided α of 0.05 and power (1-β) of 0.80, the calculated sample size required to detect this difference was 52 patients. Accounting for a potential 15% dropout rate, we planned to enroll 60 patients (30 per group) to ensure adequate statistical power.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 29.0 software. Quantitative variables were tested for normal distribution using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as mean ± SD or mean ± SEM, and intergroup comparisons were conducted using the two-sample t-test. The comparison of categorical data adopted the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To account for multiple comparisons in the hemodynamic data (MAP and HR measured at 8 time points each, totaling 16 comparisons), Bonferroni correction was applied, and a corrected significance threshold of 0.003125 (0.05/16) was used for these analyses.

Remimazolam presents a distinct pharmacological profile characterized by a delayed onset but rapid metabolism. Clinically, this translates to a longer time to reach a BIS 60 during induction and allows for earlier laryngeal LMA removal compared to propofol (P < 0.01 or 0.001, Table 3). However, no statistically significant differences were observed between the groups in terms of time to reach BIS 80, recovery time, or duration of stay in the PACU (P>0.05, Table 3). The drug demonstrated an excellent safety profile, showing no significant effects on hepatic or renal function (CREA, BUN; P>0.05), or incidence of adverse events (agitation, dizziness; P>0.05). Notably, it significantly reduced injection pain (Tables 4). No cases of post-operative acute kidney injury, PONV, or delayed emergence were observed in either group. Regarding postoperative recovery outcomes, as presented in Table 4, remimazolam failed to significantly reduce the time to first ambulation or the time to first flatus after surgery, nor did it improve the postoperative QoR-15 score. Several possible explanations may account for these negative findings. First, although remimazolam provided better hemodynamic stability during the intraoperative period, this benefit may be relatively short-lived and insufficient to impact postoperative recovery metrics such as QoR-15, which encompasses multiple dimensions including pain, physical comfort, and emotional state. Second, postoperative recovery is multifactorial, influenced by surgical factors (eg., surgical trauma, duration), patient factors (eg., baseline health status), and postoperative care, which may overshadow any potential benefits of intraoperative hemodynamic stability. Third, the relatively low baseline risk of this patient population (mean age approximately 35 years, with no major perioperative complications) may have limited the ability to detect improvements in recovery outcomes. Patients with more severe cardiovascular compromise might derive greater benefit from remimazolam’s hemodynamic stability. Future studies in higher-risk populations are warranted to further explore these possibilities. Meanwhile, since the pharmacokinetics of continuous remimazolam infusion for anesthesia in patients with ESRD remain unclear, we measured the plasma drug concentrations of remimazolam at various time points after its discontinuation in this study. The results demonstrated that when remimazolam was continuously infused in ESRD patients, the plasma concentration reached 537 ng/mL. Discussion

The findings of this study demonstrate that remimazolam, despite its slower onset of action, exhibits favorable anesthetic efficacy in patients undergoing allogeneic renal transplantation. This is evidenced by more stable hemodynamic parameters, negligible injection pain, faster metabolic clearance, without compromising post-operative hepatic or renal function. For patients with ESRD undergoing anesthesia maintenance via continuous remimazolam infusion, without the administration of the antagonist flumazenil, the plasma drug concentration can decrease by more than 50% within 10 min after discontinuation.

Clinical studies have demonstrated that remimazolam exhibits non-inferior anesthetic efficacy compared to propofol in high-risk patients classified as ASA class III–IV.8 However, individuals with ESRD represent a distinct patient population whose unique pathophysiological alterations may significantly modulate the pharmacodynamic responses to sedative medications. It is reported that remimazolam can be safely administered in patients with ESRD, as its pharmacokinetics show no significant differences between these patients and the healthy ones, and no dose adjustment is required regardless of the degree of renal impairment.12 While prior research has suggested the advantages of remimazolam over propofol in renal transplantation anesthesia, the maintenance phase in that study involved a combination of both sedatives.7 Our study differs by using remimazolam exclusively for maintenance, thus providing a clearer evaluation of its standalone efficacy.

Patients with ESRD pose significant challenges for anesthetic management due to a high burden of cardiovascular multimorbidity, including hypertension, coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, and uremic cardiomyopathy - with cardiovascular disease representing the leading cause of mortality in this population.13 The compromised cardiovascular reserve significantly diminishes hemodynamic resilience, making ESRD individuals particularly vulnerable to marked instability during anesthesia. Unlike propofol, whose myocardial depressant effect is a key cause of hypotension, remimazolam does not exert a direct negative inotropic effect on isolated myocardium and has a much weaker impact on cardiac contractility.14 This favorable hemodynamic profile explains the minimal changes in MAP observed at the various timepoints in the R group. Therefore, the improved hemodynamic stability observed with remimazolam has important clinical implications for patients undergoing renal transplantation. By maintaining more stable mean arterial pressure throughout the procedure, remimazolam may reduce the need for vasopressors, avoid hypoperfusion of the newly transplanted kidney, and potentially contribute to better graft outcomes. Although the present study was not designed to assess long-term graft function, these hemodynamic benefits support the use of remimazolam as a valuable alternative in this high-risk population. Thus, although the differences in ΔMAP reached statistical significance, their clinical relevance - specifically the potential to reduce vasopressor requirements and avoid graft hypoperfusion - supports the use of remimazolam in this patient population.

The increase in BIS 60 suggests that the onset time of remimazolam is slower than that of propofol. The underlying reason may be that remimazolam has lower lipophilicity relative to propofol, resulting in a reduced ability to cross the blood-brain barrier. While the two groups exhibited comparable recovery time, there was a notable difference in the time of LMA removal. Several factors may potentially contribute to this difference. One possibility is residual drug effects, as spontaneous breathing and protective airway reflexes may not have fully recovered despite restoration of consciousness. Another possibility is differences in drug excretion, since propofol is excreted renally whereas remimazolam is not. However, we acknowledge that these interpretations remain speculative, as the present study was not designed to identify the underlying mechanisms. Further studies are needed to clarify the factors contributing to the difference in LMA removal time. Although remimazolam did not show better efficacy in improving post-operative recovery, our findings confirm its non-inferiority to propofol in this regard, which aligns with previously reported studies.9,15 Several possible explanations may account for these negative findings. First, although remimazolam provided better hemodynamic stability during the intraoperative period, this benefit may be relatively short-lived and insufficient to impact postoperative recovery metrics such as QoR-15, which encompasses multiple dimensions including pain, physical comfort, and emotional state. Second, postoperative recovery is multifactorial, influenced by surgical factors (eg., surgical trauma, duration), patient factors (eg., baseline health status), and postoperative care, which may overshadow any potential benefits of intraoperative hemodynamic stability. Third, the relatively low baseline risk of this patient population (mean age approximately 35 years, with no major perioperative complications) may have limited the ability to detect improvements in recovery outcomes. Future studies in higher-risk populations are warranted to further explore these possibilities. It undergoes metabolism independent of hepatic or renal function, being rapidly hydrolyzed by nonspecific plasma esterases. Its primary metabolite, carboxylic acid derivative, exhibits minimal pharmacological activity, which likely accounts for its favorable safety profile with negligible impact on renal function.

Injection pain not only provokes sympathetic activation, leading to tachycardia, hypertension, and an increased risk of cardiovascular events, but also worsens patients’ subjective experiences during anesthesia induction, heightening anxiety, fear, and negative post-operative memories of medical care. Propofol induces injection pain due to mechanisms such as venous endothelial irritation, inflammatory mediator release, and transient receptor potential channel activation,16 whereas remimazolam does not exhibit these effects. This explains the absence of injection pain during anesthesia induction in group R, whereas an incidence of 33% was observed in group P.

Regarding plasma concentration, a previous study in individuals with ESRD receiving a single dose of remimazolam reported that the plasma concentration decreased by 50% was approximately 10–20 min,12 a finding similar to the results of the present study. Additionally, it is reported that in healthy male volunteers, the 95% effect-site concentration of remimazolam associated with recovery to an MOAA/S score of 5 was approximately 200 ng/mL.17 In the current study, as summarized in Table 3, patients in R group regained an MOAA/S score of 5 at approximately 15 min after discontinuation of remimazolam. Based on pharmacokinetic modeling using the parameters provided in Table 5, the estimated plasma concentration at this time point was slightly above 200 ng/mL, which aligns with previously reported values. These pharmacokinetic data help explain the clinical recovery profile observed in this study. Despite remimazolam’s longer onset time (as reflected by the longer time to reach BIS 60), its rapid decline in plasma concentration - decreasing by approximately 38%, 52%, and 65% at 5, 10, and 20 min after discontinuation - resulted in a recovery time comparable to that of propofol. Thus, the pharmacokinetic properties of remimazolam compensate for its slower onset, making it a clinically feasible alternative for anesthesia in renal transplant patients.

The study has several limitations. First, the single-center design with a homogeneous patient population may restrict the generalizability of findings to other healthcare systems. Second, the relatively short follow-up period was insufficient to comprehensively evaluate the long-term prognostic impact of remimazolam on patient outcomes. Third, the hemodynamic data at multiple time points were analyzed using two-sample t-tests at each time point, without employing mixed-effects models for longitudinal analysis or applying correction for multiple comparisons. Given the exploratory nature of this study, we prioritized the detection of potential differences over strict control of type I errors. However, we note that the primary hemodynamic finding (ΔMAP, P < 0.001) would remain statistically significant even after Bonferroni correction (threshold = 0.05/16 = 0.003125). Fourth, the sample size calculation was based on preliminary data from our center (13 patients per group), which was the best available evidence at the time of study design. The observed incidence of hypotension (33.3% vs. 6.7%) was close to the expected rates (38.5% vs. 7.7%), suggesting that our sample size estimation was reasonable. Nevertheless, larger multicenter studies are warranted to confirm our findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, despite its prolonged onset time, remimazolam provides better hemodynamic stability and less injection pain than propofol in patients undergoing allogeneic renal transplantation, with comparable recovery outcomes. Its rapid metabolism is evidenced by a prompt decline in plasma concentration following discontinuation.

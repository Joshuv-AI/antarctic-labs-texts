Adaptive Edge-Federated AI Framework for Contactless Menstrual Health Prediction Using Multimodal Physiological Intelligence
Authors: M Rajesh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study introduces an adaptive edge-federated artificial intelligence (AI) framework that employs contactless biosensing and multimodal physiological intelligence to enable accurate, real-time, and privacy-preserving menstrual health prediction. Traditional menstrual cycle tracking methods, such as manual logs or wearable sensors, often rely on skin contact, are prone to measurement errors, and raise privacy concerns due to centralized data storage. To overcome these limitations, the proposed framework integrates radar-based respiration sensing, photoplethysmography (PPG), and LiDAR-assisted microvascular mapping to non-invasively monitor vital physiological signals including heart rate, vascular activity, and breathing rhythm. The multimodal biosignals are analyzed locally through an adaptive edge-learning engine that continuously updates and personalizes predictions without transferring raw data to central servers. A federated optimization mechanism enables secure, decentralized model training, ensuring that sensitive reproductive health data remains on user devices while still benefiting from global model improvements. By dynamically adjusting to individual physiological variations and irregular cycle patterns, the system enhances prediction accuracy, reduces latency, and preserves user privacy. This intelligent, non-invasive framework offers a next-generation approach to menstrual health management, combining edge intelligence, federated learning, and multimodal biosensing to deliver a discreet, adaptive, and trustworthy reproductive health solution.•Contactless biosensing using radar, PPG, and LiDAR for real-time menstrual health tracking.•Edge-federated learning enables decentralized, privacy-preserving AI model training.•Adaptive multimodal AI intelligence enhances prediction accuracy for irregular cycles.Keywords: Menstrual Cycle Tracking; AI, Contactless Biosensing; Federated Learning; Privacy-Preserving

Background

Reproductive health, fertility planning, and early health detection depend on proper menstrual cycle monitoring. Manual logging apps and skin-contact wearables have limitations include user inaccuracy, discomfort, and centralised data storage, which raises privacy concerns. For those with irregular cycles, typical prediction models often fail.

Recent breakthroughs in AI and biosensing have enabled non-invasive, intelligent health monitoring solutions. Without skin contact, contactless biosensing uses radar, photoplethysmography (PPG), and LiDAR to measure vital indicators including respiration and heart rate. Physiological characteristics that cycle with hormonal changes during the menstrual cycle might predict ovulation.

Federation learning is a possible answer for digital health data privacy problems. Federated learning protects user privacy and model performance by training AI models on dispersed devices without sending raw data to central servers.

This study uses contactless biosensing, federated learning, and a robust AI framework to create a privacy-preserving menstrual cycle tracking and ovulation prediction system. Its novel technique improves prediction accuracy, especially for individuals with atypical cycle patterns, and provides a user-centric, secure, and scalable reproductive health service.

Validation data & representativeness

The dataset used in this study consisted of approximately 300 women contributing a total of 5,000 menstrual cycles. Participants were primarily in the reproductive age range (18–40 years), and both regular and irregular cycles were represented. Ovulation phases were annotated using a combination of self-reported logs and luteinizing hormone (LH) surge test kits, which served as the gold standard for validation. While this dataset supports robust model training and evaluation, its representativeness across broader demographic groups (e.g., perimenopausal women, those with polycystic ovary syndrome, or diverse ethnic backgrounds) remains limited. Future large-scale and clinically diverse datasets will be required to fully establish generalizability.

Conclusions

The menstrual cycle tracking system is powered by AI and uses federated learning, radar detection, PPG, and LiDAR to keep tabs on users' periods. In a non-invasive manner, it measures the heart rate and respiration rate. In order to prevent privacy invasions and data breaches, federalised learning keeps vital health information locally on user devices. Multimodal AI systems do a good job of predicting menstrual phases, which allows for more customised monitoring for those with irregular periods. The combination of federated learning with contactless biosensing makes this approach dependable, user-friendly, and protective. Confidentiality and precision in monitoring menstrual health are enhancing. Research and administration are both enhanced by this system's ability to maximise user compliance while protecting reproductive health data. Research that is driven by databases will concentrate on menstrual cycle health. AI models that can handle physiological data more efficiently may be able to spot abnormal cycles, according to recent research. For real-world deployment, integration with existing health platforms and menstrual tracking applications will be necessary to improve accessibility and user adoption. Regulatory pathways such as FDA clearance or CE marking will be critical to ensure clinical reliability and safety. Furthermore, prospective clinical trials across diverse populations are required to validate predictive accuracy under real-world conditions. By addressing these steps, the proposed contactless, privacy-preserving framework could transition from a research prototype to a clinically viable digital health solution with substantial impact on reproductive health monitoring.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the dataset of approximately 300 women, while substantial, may not fully represent the diversity of global populations, including women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), perimenopausal age groups, or varied socioeconomic backgrounds. Second, the accessibility and cost of radar, PPG, and LiDAR biosensors may limit widespread adoption in low-resource settings. Third, environmental factors such as lighting conditions for LiDAR or motion artifacts in PPG can affect signal reliability. Fourth, the federated learning framework, although privacy-preserving, introduces computational and energy demands on edge devices that may challenge long-term scalability. Finally, the study has not yet undergone large-scale clinical validation, and further trials are required to establish regulatory compliance and medical adoption.

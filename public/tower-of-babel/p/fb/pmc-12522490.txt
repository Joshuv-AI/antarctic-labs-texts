Nursing students metaphorical perceptions of the concept of “Artificial intelligence” and their views on the future of artificial intelligence
Authors: Dilek Yıldırım Gürkan, Rukiye Çakmak
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundArtificial intelligence (AI) is significantly transforming the nursing profession, supporting decision-making mechanisms, and playing a crucial role in shaping future patient care practices. Understanding nursing students’ perspectives on AI applications is vital for the effective integration of artificial intelligence into clinical practice and education.PurposeThis qualitative descriptive study explored nursing students’ metaphorical perceptions of artificial intelligence and their expectations for its future role in healthcare.MethodsTwenty nursing students from a university in Central Anatolia, Turkey, were purposively sampled using criterion sampling. Data were collected between November and December 2024. The interviews were conducted face-to-face by the researchers. Data were collected via semi-structured interviews and analysed through inductive content analysis with MaXQDA.ResultsThe data were categorized into three main themes and twelve sub-themes: the future of artificial intelligence in healthcare, the contribution of artificial intelligence to public health, and the perceived risks and ethical challenges of artificial intelligence: (1) Future Use of AI in Healthcare, (2) The Contributions of AI to Public Health (3) Negative Reflections of AI in the Field of Health in the Future. Overall, students recognized AI’s potential to improve healthcare quality and efficiency but expressed caution regarding its ethical and professional impacts.ConclusionsThe findings emphasize the need to integrate balanced, critical AI content into nursing education to prepare nurses for technology-driven healthcare.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12912-025-03947-4.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) has attracted interest in various fields [1] and has emerged as an innovative tool in many areas, including health and medicine [1–4]. AI is defined as a technology designed to act and think like humans, capable of performing functions such as abstract reasoning, knowledge representation, learning, autonomous decision-making and communication in natural languages to mimic human cognitive abilities [5, 6]. The use of artificial intelligence is used both in daily life and in many business areas. One of these areas is the field of health [7].

The integration of artificial intelligence into healthcare has emerged as a transformative force in medical diagnosis, treatment and overall health improvement [8]. AI is a relatively new concept in healthcare, especially in nursing [9]. With advances in healthcare, it is crucial that healthcare professionals, including nurses, embrace and adapt to artificial intelligence technologies [10]. In addition, advances in technology are also dramatically changing the structure and organisation of the nursing sector. From the adoption of electronic health records, the implementation of increasingly sophisticated technologies in healthcare, robotic technologies and the development of artificial intelligence have been instrumental in transforming the nursing sector. It is predicted that developments in technologies can help nurses to do their jobs more efficiently and safely and to provide more effective patient care [7].

In the study conducted by Ünal and Avcı on neonatal nurses, participants predicted that artificial intelligence can be used in various fields such as medical diagnosis, treatment planning, patient follow-up and health records management [11]. The use of artificial intelligence in nursing practice can contribute to overcoming challenges that nurses may face in the future and enhance the quality of care by supporting nursing education. For example, AI-driven telehealth and robotic technologies make it possible to expand the scope of nursing care, increase patient access, and enhance opportunities for remote monitoring [12]. Artificial intelligence also supports nurses in providing personalized and evidence-based care by enabling the integration of all necessary data [13, 14]. In addition, systematic reviews and meta-analyses examining healthcare students’ knowledge and attitudes toward artificial intelligence emphasize that the use of AI in clinical practice has the potential to reduce inequalities in opportunities across different educational and clinical settings [15].

AI helps nurses and nursing students in many ways [16]. Firstly, it has the potential to improve nursing care plans by encouraging decision-making processes [17]. Secondly, AI-enabled systems can take on the role of nursing care, such as remote monitoring of the patient in real time [18]. Artificial intelligence-based technologies assist both patient-centred and non-patient-centred elements of community health services [19]. Nurses and nursing students, as potential users of AI-based technologies, are uniquely positioned to influence and lead the implementation of artificial intelligence in the nursing sector [20]. Therefore, it is important to demonstrate how users understand and adopt new technologies such as AI-based technologies in healthcare services [21]. While the use of artificial intelligence in the field of health is increasing rapidly, examining the views and metaphorical perceptions of nursing students towards the future of artificial intelligence technology may enable the development of recommendations for the effective use of these technologies in public health services. Therefore, understanding nursing students’ perspectives on these technologies is critical for shaping future health services.

This study was conducted to determine nursing students’ metaphorical perceptions of the concept of artificial intelligence and to evaluate their thoughts about the future of artificial intelligence.

Methods

Study type

This study is a phenomenological qualitative research based on semi-structured interviews. This method combines elements of different approaches and is based on the general assumptions of the constructivist approach [22]. The Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ), a suggested reporting system, was used to record the study results [23].

Participants

This study was conducted between November and December 2024 with students studying in the department of nursing at a university in Turkey. The sample of the study was determined using the criterion sampling method, which is one of the purposeful sampling methods.

Before selecting the sample, the researcher met with all students in the nursing department to provide information about the study and explain the inclusion criteria. Students who met these criteria and were willing to participate were included in the study. The decision to stop data collection was made upon reaching data saturation. Data saturation occurs when no new information can be obtained, responses to the research questions become repetitive, and further coding is no longer possible [24]. In this study, data saturation was considered to have been achieved, and interviews were completed with 20 students who met the inclusion criteria.

The inclusion criteria are as follows;

To be a nursing student,To have taken part in projects (Teknofest, Tubitak, European Union, etc.) during high school and university education.

Data collection tools

Personal information form and semi-structured questionnaire were used for data collection. The interview form was sent to three faculty members prior to the study and was prepared in accordance with their suggestions. Semi-structured interview questions consist of 3 basic questions. (1) How do you think artificial intelligence will be used in the field of health in the future? (2) How do you think artificial intelligence will contribute to public health? (3) What kind of negativities do you think artificial intelligence will cause in the future? The personal information form created by the researchers consists of 3 questions. The first two questions included demographic information (age and gender), and the other question included an open-ended metaphor question such as ‘Artificial intelligence is like …… because ……….’ to determine nursing students’ perceptions of artificial intelligence definitions.

Prior to the data collection phase, a pilot study was conducted with three students. After interviews conducted on different days, the interview questions and flow were reviewed. These pilot interviews were not included in the study. Data were collected face-to-face by the researcher at a date and time agreed upon with the students. The interviews were conducted in a meeting room chosen in consultation with the students, where they would feel least disturbed. Each interview lasted between 20 and 40 min. To ensure complete data collection, audio recording devices were used with the participants’ consent during the interviews.

Data analysis

The data were analysed using content analysis. MaXQUDA software v10 programme was used in the analysis process. Firstly, the recordings obtained through audio recordings were transferred verbatim to Microsoft Word programme at the end of each interview. The transcripts were coded independently by two researchers to ensure reliability. In the next stage, the codes were compared. After the comparison of the codes, a code check was made by a person experienced in qualitative analysis. After the check, a code check was made again by the researchers and themes were derived from the final codes. The themes were finalised after the expert opinion of a faculty member experienced in qualitative research.

Trustworthiness

In order to ensure the validity and reliability of the study, Lincoln and Guba’s credibility, transferability, internal reliability (consistency), and verifiability external reliability (confirmability) criteria were used. In order to ensure credibility in the study, the interviewees were asked to evaluate the interviews about themselves. In order to fulfil the condition of transferability, all stages of the research process (sample selection, data collection tool, data collection period, etc.) were presented in detail in terms of transferability. In order to ensure reliability, information about the design of the research and what was done step by step during the data collection process was provided. Finally, in order to ensure verifiability, the codes and categories obtained by the researchers were presented in detail [25]. To ensure the credibility of the study, all interviews were conducted consistently by a single researcher. The data were independently analyzed by two researchers, and the findings were subsequently reviewed by a social scientist to assess confirmability.

Ethical considerations

Ethical approval was obtained from the ethics committee of the university (23.10.2024-18/44) and the necessary written approval (E-88148187-020-271784) was obtained from the dean's office. Informed consent was obtained before data collection. Students were assured that their voluntary or involuntary participation in the study would not affect their educational process. This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013).

Discussion

This study aimed to determine nursing students’ metaphorical perceptions of the concept of artificial intelligence and to evaluate their thoughts about the future of artificial intelligence through semi-structured interviews. Three main themes were identified: (1) Future Use of Artificial Intelligence in the Field of Health (2) Contributions of Artificial Intelligence to Public Health and (3) Negative Reflections of Artificial Intelligence in the Field of Health in the Future.

The integration of artificial intelligence technologies into the field of nursing offers important opportunities to alleviate the workload of nurses, support clinical decision-making processes and personalise patient care [26]. In the study conducted by Sommer et al., two-thirds of nurses reported perceiving artificial intelligence as an opportunity [27]. The workload of nurses will be reduced by integrating artificial intelligence into nursing services [28]. In this study, nursing students stated that artificial intelligence will lighten their workload. Similarly, in a study conducted by Ergin et al. (2023) on operating theatre nurses, more than half of the participants stated that robots would reduce their workload [29]. In a study conducted on nurse managers, 67.2% of the participants think that robot nurses will benefit the profession [30]. It is also supported by literature reviews that artificial intelligence can reduce the negative effects on nurses’ mental health and improve the quality of patient care by automating repetitive tasks and improving workload management [31]. In addition, Wagner et al. (2024) concluded that robotic operating theatre nurses undertake a significant part of the workload in surgical processes and contribute to overcoming staff shortages [32]. In the qualitative study conducted by Yousif et al. in 2024, the participants stated that the integration of artificial intelligence can reduce the workload, save time and resources, and increase the efficiency of healthcare professionals [33]. As a result of the qualitative research conducted by Yoo et al. with healthcare professionals, it was concluded that artificial intelligence technologies can improve patient outcomes, reduce work efficiency and hospital costs [34]. Hatem et al. concluded that participants largely agreed that artificial intelligence would reduce treatment errors and the workload of doctors [8]. In a study conducted by Jha et al. on medical students, it was concluded that almost half of the participants thought that artificial intelligence would reduce the current workload for them [35]. Contrary to the results in the literature, 42.4% of the participants in the study conducted by Tuncer and Tuncer thought that ChatCPT and artificial intelligence programmes would not reduce the workload of nurses [36]. Although these findings show that the integration of artificial intelligence technologies into nursing processes in the health sector plays a supportive role and can contribute to the development of the profession; it reveals that more research and discussion is needed to understand the impact of these technologies on nurses’ perception of the profession and their workforce. In addition, artificial intelligence technologies can reduce the workload of nurses and enable them to be more active in patient care processes and allow nurses to focus on more human and complex care processes. The findings of this study regarding the benefits of artificial intelligence are consistent with the existing literature. The positive statements expressed by participants about the nursing profession align with the metaphors used in this study, such as ‘facilitator,’ ‘assistant,’ and ‘companion.’ The metaphors provided by participants who believed that AI would contribute to nursing also reflected positive and supportive connotations.

Artificial intelligence is active in many areas, from treatment planning to medication management. Nurses are therefore concerned about the increasing adoption of artificial intelligence and fear that it could put them out of work [7]. A potential threat of AI to the nursing workforce is the concern that it could replace employees [37]. In Doğaner’s study on health sciences students, it was concluded that the participants thought that artificial intelligence would cause unemployment in the future [38]. Similarly, in the study of Yiğit and Açıkgöz (2024), it was observed that some nursing students reported that artificial intelligence would cause problems in nursing care and could threaten the nursing profession in the future [39]. In the study conducted by Yılmaz and colleagues, the participants thought that the need for health workers would decrease in the future and this would cause them to experience anxiety about finding a job [40]. In the study conducted by Abdullah and Fakieh, most of the participants reported that they were concerned that artificial intelligence would replace them [41]. In the study conducted by Kaplan and Uçar, most participants believed that artificial intelligence would eventually replace humans [42]. In the research conducted, it is seen that there are participant comments that artificial intelligence technologies may cause employment problems in the future. Contrary to the results of these studies, in the study conducted by Ergin et al. with operating theatre nurses, it was concluded that the majority of nurses did not think that robots would replace nurses [29]. In the study conducted by Maraş et al., most nurses and nursing students believe that humanoid nurse robots and artificial intelligence health technologies cannot replace nurses [43]. In a study conducted in Nigeria, more than half of the participants (57.8%) think that artificial intelligence will not lead to job losses [44]. In the study conducted by Ergin and colleagues, 86.2% of the participants stated that they did not believe that robots would replace nurses [30]. In the study conducted by Rony et al., nurses believed that artificial intelligence could lead to job loss [12]. These findings show that there are different perceptions and concerns among nurses about the impact of artificial intelligence technologies on the workforce. The difference in opinions among individuals on this issue may depend on individuals’ perceptions of their profession and access to technological developments. As in the literature, negative perceptions of artificial intelligence were also observed in this study. Participants holding such views described AI using metaphors such as a volcanic eruption or a mountain. They suggested that AI could be likened to a mountain that can be climbed or to a volcanic mountain whose timing and manner of eruption are unpredictable.

It is known that robots have many features suitable for nursing care practices such as dressing patients, bathing, washing their hair, transporting patients, finding and opening vascular access, making physiological measurements [7]. In the study, there are statements in the participant comments that artificial intelligence technologies will facilitate patient care and patient follow-up. Similarly, Maraş et al. (2024) concluded that humanoid nurse robots and artificial intelligence health technologies can facilitate patient care [43]. In the study conducted by Tuncer and Tuncer, 58.3% of the participants think that ChatCPT and artificial intelligence programmes will positively affect patient care [36]. The study conducted by Rony et al. revealed that practicing nurses possess a broad perspective, with many viewing artificial intelligence as a tool to enhance patient care [12]. Participants in the study stated that artificial intelligence technologies will help to create a data pool as well as ease of patient follow-up. In a randomised controlled trial examining a health data collection robot for the elderly, it was found that the robot could effectively and acceptably assist healthcare professionals by interviewing older adults [44]. The study conducted by Joo et al. demonstrated that AI tools contribute to both current and future frontline nursing practices aimed at improving patient care [45]. These findings show the potential of artificial intelligence in the field of health. However, in order to effectively integrate these technologies into nursing services, more studies on the education of nurses are required. Participants expressed their views on the potential presence of AI technologies in daily life using metaphors such as a robot and an octopus. These metaphors reflect the perception that artificial intelligence can be integrated into many aspects of everyday life and perform a wide range of functions.

The adoption of artificial intelligence has the potential to support the existing nursing workforce and increase the safe and high quality care that nurses can deliver [46]. In the research conducted by Doğaner, students think that artificial intelligence will increase the success rate in treatment [38]. In the study conducted by Yılmaz et al. the opinions of health sciences students that artificial intelligence applications will enrich their professional skills and that they can provide better health services to patients by using artificial intelligence came to the fore [40]. The research by Clifton et al. showed that machine learning technologies embedded in health information systems would provide a clinical benefit with the potential to improve patient outcomes in the emergency department [47]. In the study, the participants stated that diagnosis and treatment methods will progress thanks to artificial intelligence technologies. It was also observed that participants metaphorically expressed their views on AI’s ability to improve care and quality, as well as to perform tasks more efficiently, using expressions such as ‘like a jet.

In the study, it was observed that some of the participants stated that artificial intelligence technologies will reveal the problem of communication. It was observed that participants expressed this perception using an imitation metaphor. Although artificial intelligence is an intelligent being that imitates human intelligence, it is difficult for it to imitate a compassionate form of communication from nurses to patients [17]. Nurses should ensure that advanced technologies do not jeopardise the nature of human interactions and relationships inherent in the nursing profession [48].

Safe and appropriate adoption of the technology being used in health care requires skills and competence [49]. In addition, by actively participating in the use of artificial intelligence tools, nurses can help shape the future of healthcare services and protect their fairness and safety in this process [50]. For this reason, it is also important to provide appropriate training to healthcare personnel for the active use of artificial intelligence technologies in the field of health.

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicate that nursing students have diverse perceptions regarding the future of artificial intelligence. While AI technologies present significant opportunities, such as enhancing nursing processes, facilitating data management, and improving patient treatment outcomes, concerns remain about their potential impact on employment and the diminishing humanistic aspects of nursing care. Therefore, for the effective integration of AI technologies into nursing, it is essential to develop comprehensive education and skills training programs. Additionally, ensuring that nursing care remains human-centered and grounded in empathy should be a key priority in this process.

Relevance for clinical practice

This study highlights nursing students’ mixed perceptions of artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare, emphasizing both opportunities and concerns. AI has the potential to improve patient monitoring, streamline workflows, and enhance clinical decision-making, ultimately raising care quality. To maximize these benefits, nursing education must integrate comprehensive training on AI applications, including ethical considerations, data privacy, and empathy in care. In clinical practice, nurses should be supported through ongoing professional development to develop AI literacy and collaborate effectively with AI systems. Maintaining human-centered care alongside technological advances is crucial to preserve empathy and communication in nursing. By preparing nurses to engage critically and confidently with AI, healthcare organizations can ensure safer, more efficient, and compassionate care delivery.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the findings are limited to nursing students studying at a single university in Turkey and, therefore, cannot be generalized to all nursing students. Second, the data are based on students’ self-reports and are confined to the opinions they expressed during the interviews.

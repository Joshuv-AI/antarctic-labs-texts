The power of the flower – how to facilitate pentaspline navigation to the right inferior pulmonary vein☆
Authors: Boris Schmidt, KR Julian Chun
Published: 2025

The pentaspline pulsed field ablation catheter for pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) has drastically changed procedural aspects of an atrial fibrillation ablation procedure. The simplicity and versatility of the catheter design allows for easy navigation and positioning at the PVs as well as at the posterior wall. The simplicity is well reflected in astonishingly short procedure times reported in studies as well as real-life registries [1,2].

Early enthusiasm, however, was dampened after remapping studies revealed unexpectedly high PV reconnection rates [3]. In the same study, it was elegantly shown, that prior single-shot operators had less re-connections using the pentaspline catheter than operators who had predominantly used point-by-point ablation.

With increasing knowledge about lesion formation following PFA ablation, more focus was directed towards optimization of electrode to tissue contact. This was addressed by additional imaging modalities such as intracardiac echo, the implementation of 3 dimensional mapping as well as be special catheter techniques. Schaack and co-workers, for example, introduced the so-called olive strategy, i.e. additional intra-PV PFA applications by a smaller than basket array and found outstanding durable PVI rates during re-mapping [4].

In the present case report by Kasai and colleagues, another elegant technique was presented to facilitate pentaspline positioning at the right inferior PV. (REF) The technique consists of five sequential steps guided by the radiopaque markers of the sheath and the catheter.

The authors have to commended for this illustrative case report which will be very useful for the EP community. It is easy to use, does not need additional technological aids and can be reproduced easily. The only demerit might be, that it requires the use of X-ray and can neither be accomplished with a fluoroless approach nor with the use of ICE.

It should be highlighted, that an anterior to central transseptal puncture site was recommended in early clinical reports to provide more space for navigating the 31mm pentaspline at the septal PVs [5].

With the ongoing triumphant march of the pentaspline PFA catheter the herein presented practical information will help future novice operators to safely and successfully achieve their goals.

Er:YAG laser-assisted connective tissue wall technique for periodontal regeneration in a soft tissue recession combined with a furcation-involved class II subclass B molar under a microscope: A case report
Authors: Shao-Yu Chang, Yoichi Taniguchi, Akira Aoki, Taichen Lin
Published: 2026

Er:YAG laser (ErL) has been increasingly applied in periodontal regenerative therapy owing to its ability to precisely ablate both soft and hard tissues with minimal thermal damage.use with minimal thermal damage.1,2 Under microscopic visualization, the fine sapphire tip of the Er:YAG laser allow s effective access to anatomically restricted areas, such as furcation defects, facilitating thorough debridement of inflamed granulation tissue and calculus.3 In addition, the photobiomodulation effect of ErL has been reported to enhance wound healing and periodontal regeneration. Furthermore, ErL irradiation without water spray can promote blood clot formation, contributing to stabilization of grafted materials following defect debridement.4

In periodontal regenerative therapy, gingival recession and soft tissue collapse remain major concerns, particularly in furcation-involved molars. The combination of connective tissue grafting with regenerative procedures has been suggested to increase soft tissue thickness, achieve root coverage, and reduce the risk of postoperative gingival recession.5 In this report, we presented a case of treatment of soft tissue recession combined with a furcation-involved Class II subclass B molar treated using an Er:YAG laser-assisted connective tissue wall technique under microscopic guidance.

A 45-year-old female patient presented with discomfort and pain during mastication on the right side. Clinical examination revealed a 6-mm periodontal probing depth (PPD) with a 2-mm buccal gingival recession at the right mandibular first molar after periodontal phase I therapy (Fig. 1A). The furcation-involved area was diagnosed as a Class II subclass B (B2) defect. The patient expressed a desire to preserve the tooth and consented to minimally invasive periodontal regenerative surgery.

Under local anesthesia, periodontal regenerative surgery was performed with the aid of a dental operating microscope. A modified papilla preservation flap was designed at the right mandibular first molar and extended one tooth mesially and distally (Fig. 1B), followed by the elevation of a buccal single-flap. Furcation defect and root surface debridement were performed using micro curettes and an ultrasonic scaler in combination with Er:YAG laser irradiation (Litetouch®, Light Instruments Ltd., Yokneam Illit, Israel) with a straight sapphire tip (400 μm diameter) to completely remove inflamed connective tissue and debride root surface (Fig. 1C and D).

A de-epithelialized connective tissue graft was harvested from the maxillary palate (Fig. 1E) and secured to the buccal flap of the right mandibular first molar to increase soft tissue thickness and provide a connective tissue wall effect. Enamel matrix derivative and freeze-dried bone allograft (FDBA; OSTEOAID®, LifeLink Tissue Bank, Tampa, FL, USA) were then placed into the furcation defect (Fig. 1F). Subsequently, a blood clot-forming procedure was performed using Er:YAG laser irradiation without water spray under non-contact mode to stabilize the grafted materials (Fig. 1G). The flap was closed with interrupted and sling sutures in a coronally advanced position (Fig. 1H).

Postoperatively, antibiotics and analgesics were prescribed for 5 days. Sutures were removed after 2 weeks, and supportive periodontal therapy was provided every 3 months for 1 year. At the 12-month follow-up, the buccal PPD decreased from 6 mm to 3 mm, and the clinical attachment level improved from 8 mm to 3 mm. Gingival recession was reduced from 2 mm to 0 mm, with increased gingival thickness and width of keratinized tissue. Complete root coverage and clinical furcation closure were achieved (Fig. 1I). The preoperative periapical radiograph showed a radiolucent defect in the furcation area (Fig. 1J). The immediate postoperative radiograph demonstrated an improving outcome of the furcation defect (Fig. 1K). At the 12-month follow-up, radiographic examination showed resolution of the furcation defect with dense bone-like tissue formation (Fig. 1L).

The result of this case report suggests that Er:YAG laser-assisted regenerative surgery combined with a connective tissue wall technique under microscopic visualization can achieve favorable soft and hard tissue outcomes in furcation-involved Class II defects associated with gingival recession. This approach may minimize soft tissue collapse, reduce gingival recession, and simultaneously promote stable and predictable periodontal regeneration.

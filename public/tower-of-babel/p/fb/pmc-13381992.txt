Revealing the mechanism of shaking/tumbling driving Wuyi rock tea Rougui to “fade green odor and reveal aroma” based on metabolomics and molecular docking technology
Authors: Jianghua Ye, Qiqi Weng, Yuhua Wang, Yulin Wang, Tingting Wang, Yangxin Luo, Xiaoli Jia, Sitong Ye, Qi Zhang, Haibin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractShaking/tumbling (TS) is a key process for shaping the characteristic aroma of tea, but the mechanism driving aroma transformation is still unclear. This study systematically analyzed the dynamic changes of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and their interaction mechanisms with human olfactory receptors during the TS process. Research found that TS drove the directional transformation of tea aroma from green to fruity and creamy, and six characteristic VOCs were screened. Molecular docking analysis revealed that the binding energy of 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, a green substance, to the receptor OR5M1 was the lowest (−7.6 kcal/mol) and the binding receptor spectrum was the widest (129 types), revealing the high sensitivity of the human body to its perception and the scientific connotation of the traditional experience of “fading green odor and revealing aroma”. This study provides important theoretical basis for the precise regulation and process optimization of the aroma quality of Wuyi rock tea.Keywords: Wuyi rock tea, Shaking and tumbling, Volatile organic compounds, Molecular docking, Olfactory perception, Aroma qualityChemical compounds studied in this article: Isobutyl hexanoate (PubChem CID: 7775); cis-3-Hexenyl isobutyrate (PubChem CID: 5352539); Hexyl crotonate (PubChem CID: 5366038); cis-4-Decenal (PubChem CID: 5362620); 3,4-Dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene (PubChem CID: 5365753); cis-3-Hexenyl acetate (PubChem CID: 5363388)

Introduction

Wuyi rock tea, as a typical representative of Chinese oolong tea, is renowned both domestically and internationally for its unique “rock flavor” and rich aroma layers. The formation of its quality characteristics is closely related to complex processing techniques, among which the shaking/tumbling (TS) process is considered a key link in shaping the characteristic aroma of Wuyi rock tea (Kong et al., 2026; Zhou et al., 2025). TS involves mechanical force acting on tea leaves, causing damage to the edge cells of the leaves, triggering enzymatic oxidation reactions, while regulating water evaporation and redistribution of contents, thereby promoting the gradual transformation of tea aroma components (Hao et al., 2025; Kong et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2023). During this process, the aroma characteristics of tea undergo a directional evolution from green grass aroma to mature aroma types such as floral and fruity aroma, cream aroma, etc. The tea industry vividly summarizes it as “fading green odor and revealing aroma” (Wu et al., 2025). Although this empirical summary has been widely applied in production practice, the substance basis and molecular mechanism behind it still lack systematic and in-depth scientific research.

The formation of tea aroma is essentially the result of dynamic changes in the types and contents of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) (Hu et al., 2025). In recent years, with the rapid development of metabolomics technology, researchers have been able to perform high-throughput and high-sensitivity detection and analysis of changes in VOCs during tea processing. However, existing research has mostly focused on the overall differences in VOCs during specific processing stages or changes in the content of individual aroma components (Yan et al., 2025; Yu, Fan, Zhang, & Ning, 2025). There is still a lack of comprehensive screening and identification of the systematic evolution of VOCs during the continuous dynamic process of TS, especially the key substances that can reflect the TS process. In addition, traditional aroma analysis often stops at the identification and quantification of chemical components, failing to further establish a molecular level correlation between substance changes and human olfactory perception, resulting in insufficient understanding of the biological basis for the formation of aroma quality.

The human olfactory perception begins with the specific binding of aroma molecules to olfactory receptor proteins (Wang et al., 2025). In recent years, molecular docking technology has been successfully applied to the study of tea aroma, providing a molecular level explanation for sensory quality evaluation by simulating the binding mode and affinity between aroma compounds and olfactory receptors (Shen, Liu, Ning, & Li, 2025; Zhu et al., 2025). This technology can calculate binding energy and predict binding sites, thereby evaluating the strength and stability of the interaction between aroma compounds and receptors. It is generally believed that binding energies below 0 kcal/mol indicate spontaneous binding, while energies below −6.0 kcal/mol indicate the formation of stable complexes with high affinity (Mei, Ding, & Chen, 2023; Zhou et al., 2024). Molecular docking of key aroma compounds screened by metabolomics with human olfactory receptors can help reveal the recognition differences and structural basis of different aroma characteristics in the human olfactory system, thereby elucidating the biological mechanisms of aroma quality formation at the atomic level. However, so far, systematic research on the dynamic changes of VOCs and the interaction mechanism of olfactory receptors during the TS process of Wuyi rock tea is still relatively scarce. How does the TS process drive directional changes in VOCs composition? What VOCs can be used as signature substances to distinguish the TS process? How do these signature substances interact with human olfactory receptors to produce different aroma perceptions? The answers to the above scientific questions are of great significance for a deeper understanding of the internal mechanism of the traditional experience of “fading green odor and revealing aroma”, as well as for achieving precise regulation of tea aroma quality.

Based on this, the TS process of Wuyi rock tea was used as the research object, and metabolomics and molecular docking technology were comprehensively used to systematically analyze the dynamic changes of VOCs from the first to fifth TS process (TS1 ∼ TS5). Firstly, the VOCs at each stage of TS were identified and quantitatively analyzed by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) to clarify their components and trends. Secondly, by combining multiple sets of volcano maps, K-means clustering, and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), characteristic VOCs were screened, which could distinguish different TS stages and exhibit sustained and regular changes. Furthermore, the selected characteristic VOCs were molecularly docked with 428 human olfactory receptors to systematically evaluate their binding abilities and modes, and to elucidate the recognition mechanisms of different aroma features at the level of olfactory receptors. Finally, by combining the dynamic changes of aroma substances and receptor binding characteristics, the molecular basis of aroma directional transformation during the TS process was elucidated, revealing the scientific connotation of the traditional experience of “fading green odor and revealing aroma”. This study aims to establish a correlation model of “processing technology aroma components olfactory perception”, deepen the understanding of the quality formation mechanism of Wuyi rock tea shaking process at the molecular level, and provide theoretical basis and methodological reference for precise regulation and process optimization of tea aroma quality. The research results not only contribute to enriching the basic theories of tea flavor chemistry and sensory biology, but also provide new ideas for the scientific and standardized improvement of traditional tea-making techniques.

Materials and methods

Experimental design and sample collection

This research was carried out in Wuyishan City, Fujian Province in May 2025. This city has a subtropical monsoon climate, with an annual average temperature of 12–13 °C, an annual precipitation of more than 2000 mm, and a relative humidity of about 85%. It is the core production area of Wuyi rock tea. The tested variety was Rougui (Camellia sinensis (L.) O. Kuntze cv. Rougui), which was recognized as a provincial improved variety by Fujian Crop Variety Approval Committee in 1985 and is now one of the main tea varieties planted in Wuyishan. Tea fresh leaves were picked according to the “three leaves and one core” standard and carried out the initial processing according to the traditional processing technology of Wuyi rock tea. To investigate the impact of the TS process on the VOCs in tea leaves, this study collected samples during the TS process. The TS process was carried out according to the traditional Wuyi rock tea processing method (Ye & Wang, 2025), with a total of 5 cycles, marked as TS1 (first TS), TS2 (second TS), TS3 (third TS), TS4 (fourth TS), and TS5 (fifth TS). During the TS process, temperature and humidity were maintained at 27 ± 1 °C and 55 ± 5%, respectively. The five TS treatments were carried out with different rotation and resting regimes (Fig. 1): TS1 involved rotation at 16 r/min for 2 min followed by resting for 45 min; TS2 at 16 r/min for 5 min followed by 50 min resting; TS3 at 16 r/min for 12 min followed by 60 min resting; TS4 at 16 r/min for 20 min followed by 70 min resting; and TS5 at 16 r/min for 35 min followed by 80 min resting. After each TS, samples were taken immediately, with 3 biological replicates set for each sample. All samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen after collection and stored in a − 80 °C freezer for subsequent VOCs extraction and detection. The identification and quantification of VOCs were carried out using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS).

Experimental reagents and instruments

The NaCl (analytical grade) used in this study was purchased from China National Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The internal standard 3-Hexanone-2,2,4,4-d4 (purity ≥98%) was purchased from Sigma Aldrich (Shanghai, China). The 120 μm DVB/CWR/PDMS extraction head was purchased from Agilent Technologies (Folsom, CA, USA). The main instruments and equipment included 8890-7000D gas chromatography tandem mass spectrometer (Agilent, Folsom, CA, USA), DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent, Folsom, CA, USA), and helium gas (purity ≥99.999%) as the carrier gas.

Extraction, qualitative and quantitative analysis of VOCs

Headspace solid-phase microextraction (HS-SPME) was used to extract VOCs from tea samples subjected to different TS treatments, with three independent replicates prepared for each sample (Ye et al., 2025). Specifically, the tea samples were first ground in liquid nitrogen, and 500 mg was accurately weighed into a headspace vial. Then, 2 mL of saturated NaCl solution and 20 μL of internal standard solution (3-hexanone-2,2,4,4-d4, 10 μg/mL) were added in sequence, followed by shaking at a constant temperature of 60 °C for 5 min. Extraction was performed for 15 min under headspace conditions using a 120 μm DVB/CWR/PDMS extraction head (Agilent, Folsom, CA, USA), followed by desorption at the injection port for 5 min at 250 °C, and analysis by GC–MS/MS.

VOCs were analyzed using an Agilent 8890-7000D gas chromatograph–tandem mass spectrometer (Agilent, Folsom, CA, USA). Chromatographic separation was performed using a DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent, Folsom, CA, USA), with high-purity helium (purity ≥99.999%) as the carrier gas at a constant flow rate of 200 μL/s. The injector temperature was 250 °C, and the solvent retention time was 3.5 min. The temperature ramp program was set as follows: hold at 40 °C for 3.5 min, ramp to 100 °C at 10 °C/min, then to 180 °C at 7 °C/min, and finally to 280 °C at 25 °C/min, followed by a 5-min hold. Mass spectrometry conditions: electron impact ion source, electron energy 70 eV; ion source temperature 230 °C, quadrupole temperature 150 °C, mass spectrometer interface temperature 280 °C; scanning mode was selected ion monitoring (SIM), with precise scanning of qualitative and quantitative ions. Qualitative analysis of VOCs was performed based on retention indices, characteristic ions, and comparison with the NIST spectral library (Guo, Wan, & Ho, 2025), and detailed data on VOCs in each sample were provided in Table S1. Semi-quantitative analysis of VOCs was conducted using the isotope internal standard. The results were expressed as the ratio of the peak area of the target compound to that of the internal standard, and the relative contents of each VOC were obtained (Jin et al., 2025).

Molecular docking simulations of signature VOCs with human olfactory receptor proteins

The three-dimensional structures (in SDF format) of signature VOCs (ligands) were obtained from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, accessed November 6, 2025). Information on human olfactory receptor proteins (receptors) was obtained from the Human Olfactory Data Explorer database (http://genome.weizmann.ac.il/horde/, accessed November 8, 2025), yielding a total of 428 human olfactory receptor proteins (Wang, Sun, & Joseph, 2023). The three-dimensional structures of the receptor proteins (in PDB format) were obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/, accessed November 25, 2025).

Geometric pocket detection was performed on the receptor using the Fpocket software (v 4.0) to automatically identify potential binding sites in the protein (Stratiichuk et al., 2025). For each receptor, the top 3 highest-scoring pockets were selected, and a grid search space (grid box) was constructed based on their geometric centers and volumes, with an additional expansion of approximately 6 Å in all directions to ensure the search range could fully accommodate the ligand. Molecular docking simulations were performed using AutoDockTools (v 1.5.7). The ligands and receptors were preprocessed through dehydration, hydrogenation, and electrification, respectively, and the receptors additionally required removal of non-standard residues and redundant conformations (Pliushcheuskaya & Künze, 2025). Docking results were visualized using PyMOL software (v 2.5.7) to illustrate the interaction patterns between the ligand and the receptor binding site.

Statistical analysis

Excel 2020 was used for the preliminary organization and statistical analysis of the raw VOC data. Further analysis and visualization were performed using RStudio (v 4.2.3) (Wickham, 2019). ANOVA was used to analyze differences among samples. The R package ggplot2 was used for VOCs classification stack plots, ggbiplot for principal component analysis, and ggplot2 3.5.2 for multiple volcano plots. K-means clustering was performed using stats 4.2.0, orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) models were constructed using ropls and mixOmics, bubble feature maps were created using ggplot2 3.4.0, odor wheels were generated using circlize 0.4.15, and rose plots were created using dplyr 1.1.4.

Results and discussion

Effect of TS on the composition and content of volatile compounds in tea leaves

This study systematically analyzed the effects of the first to fifth TS (labeled TS1–TS5, respectively) on the VOC composition of tea leaves. Leaf morphology (Fig. 2A) revealed that as the number of TS cycles increased, the tea leaves gradually transitioned from an unfurled state to one characterized by soft leaf texture and reddened margins, exhibiting the typical “green leaves with red edges” feature, indicating that the intensity of TS and the degree of physical damage accumulated progressively. GC–MS analysis of VOCs in the TS1–TS5 samples identified a total of 844 VOCs (Fig. 2B). Based on their chemical structural characteristics, these compounds could be classified into 15 major categories, primarily including esters (17.77%), terpenoids (12.91%), ketones (12.80%), and heterocyclic compounds (11.85%), and the proportion of compounds in the remaining categories was less than 10%.

A comparative analysis of the total VOC content in tea samples at different TS stages was conducted. The results (Fig. 2C) showed that there were differences among TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, and TS5, indicating that the number of TS cycles had a certain effect on VOC accumulation. Principal component analysis (PCA) was further employed to examine the differences in the overall VOC composition among samples at different TS stages (Fig. 2D). The results showed that, although there was some overlap among certain samples, these samples still exhibited a certain degree of separation in the PCA space, indicating that the number of TS cycles could induce systematic changes in the content of different VOCs. It is evident that TS, as a core step in the processing of Wuyi rock tea, significantly regulates the composition and content of VOCs in the tea leaves through the number of repetitions, which may consequently affect the aroma quality of the final product.

Screening of potential signature VOCs showing significant changes in tea leaves under different TS cycles

Based on the above analysis, to further elucidate the dynamic patterns of VOCs during the TS process, this study combined multiple volcano plots with the K-means clustering method to screen for VOCs exhibiting consistent, regular changes across consecutive TS treatments (TS1–TS5). The results showed (Fig. 3A) that a total of 32 VOCs exhibited distinct trend changes during the TS process. Among these, the contents of 13 VOCs gradually decreased with increasing TS cycles, while those of 19 VOCs significantly increased, indicating that these compounds were highly responsive to the TS process. To identify key VOCs distinguishing among different TS stages, this study constructed an Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) model based on the aforementioned 32 VOCs. Model validation results (Fig. 3B) indicated that both the goodness of fit (R2Y) and predictive power (Q2) reached significant levels (p < 0.005), suggesting that the model was stable and reliable and could effectively distinguish between the five samples (from TS1 to TS5). Further, using a screening criterion of Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) values greater than 1 and p < 0.05, 19 key VOCs that contributed significantly to intergroup differentiation were selected from the 32 VOCs. These 19 key VOCs were further visualized using a bubble feature map to screen for potential signature VOCs that could be used to distinguish different TS cycles, ultimately identifying 10 potential signature VOCs with distinct trends and high discriminatory capacity across the five stages (Fig. 3C). Analysis of their content trends (Fig. 3D) revealed that seven VOCs, including hexyl crotonate, isobutyl hexanoate, N-ethylsuccinimide, cis-4-decenal, succinimide, allylurea, and cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate, exhibited a continuous increase during the TS process; while three VOCs, including cis-3-hexenyl acetate, 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, and 1,5-pentanediol, exhibited a decreasing trend.

The regular changes in these VOCs are closely associated with metabolic reprogramming triggered by mechanical damage during the TS process, primarily involving the preferential activation of the lipoxygenase pathway, substrate competition between acyltransferases and esterases, and the redirection of nitrogen metabolism (Hao et al., 2026). The lipoxygenase pathway can efficiently convert fatty acids into aldehydes such as cis-4-decenal and corresponding alcohol/ester precursors (Hu et al., 2018). Meanwhile, free fatty acids and amino acids released from cell disruption provide sufficient acyl-CoA donors for the synthesis of long chain esters including hexyl crotonate, isobutyl hexanoate, and cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate (Zhou et al., 2022). In contrast, cis-3-hexenyl acetate may continuously decrease due to the diversion of acetyl-CoA toward defensive metabolism and enhanced esterase hydrolysis (Lai et al., 2025). The degradation of proteins and the redirection of arginine metabolism may promote the accumulation of nitrogen containing compounds such as succinimide, N-ethylsuccinimide, and allylurea (Chen et al., 2020). The declines in 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene and 1,5-pentanediol are likely attributable to physical volatilization losses resulting from cellular structural damage, as well as their preferential consumption as intermediates in glycoside-bound or oxidation pathways (Cao, Huang, Liu, Duan, & Sun, 2024). The combined effects of selectively regulated enzyme activities, shifts in substrate supply, and physical dissipation collectively contribute to the characteristic changes in marker VOCs during the TS process. These VOCs, which demonstrated significant regular changes during the TS process, not only effectively distinguished between different stages of TS but may also played a crucial role in aroma formation during tea TS.

Molecular docking of human olfactory receptors with potential signature VOCs and their aroma recognition characteristics

Molecular docking is a technique that uses computational simulations to predict the binding patterns, affinities, and interaction mechanisms between small molecules and biological macromolecules (Liu et al., 2025). This technique has been applied to tea aroma research, providing a molecular-level explanation for sensory evaluation by simulating the molecular recognition process between aroma compounds and human olfactory receptors (Ma et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025). Human perception of odors begins with the binding of odor molecules to olfactory receptor proteins (Raka et al., 2022). Based on this, this study systematically evaluated the binding capabilities of 10 potential signature VOCs with 428 human olfactory receptor proteins, aiming to reveal their specific binding patterns, thereby deepening the understanding of olfactory perception mechanisms and identifying key signature VOCs. The evaluation used binding energy as a key indicator. The binding energy below 0 kcal/mol indicates that binding can occur, while a value below −6.0 kcal/mol signifies a stable binding with high affinity (Mei et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024). Based on molecular docking results (Table S2), this study found that the binding energies of 10 potential signature VOCs with 428 human olfactory receptors ranged from −7.6 to −2.9 kcal/mol, indicating that they all possessed the ability to bind to olfactory receptors. Further analysis indicated (Fig. 4) that four VOCs, including 1,5-pentanediol, allylurea, succinimide, and N-ethylsuccinimide, had binding energies higher than −6.0 kcal/mol with all receptors, suggesting that although they could bind, their affinity was weak and their perceptual ability was limited. The remaining six VOCs exhibited stronger binding capabilities, among which isobutyl hexanoate, 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate, cis-4-decenal, cis-3-hexenyl acetate, and hexyl crotonate formed stable complexes with 40, 129, 75, 18, 3, and 32 receptors, respectively, to form stable complexes with binding energies below −6.0 kcal/mol. It is evident that although human olfactory receptors can form stable bonds with these six VOCs, there are differences in their sensitivity to the odor characteristics, as shown by the following order: 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene > cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate > isobutyl hexanoate > hexyl crotonate > cis-4-decenal > cis-3-hexenyl acetate. These results indicate that these six VOCs can serve as signature VOCs for determining the degree of TS in tea leaves, and the intensity of their odor characteristics is a key indicator for tea makers to evaluate whether the TS process has been completed.

Binding energy analysis of 10 potential signature VOCs with 428 human olfactory receptor proteins.

Based on this, the present study further analyzed the aroma characteristics of six signature VOCs, providing an in-depth revelation of the dynamic evolution of tea aroma characteristics during the TS process and the underlying mechanisms of olfactory perception. The study found (Fig. 5A) that the six signature VOCs primarily exhibited three aroma characteristics, fruity, creamy, and green. The fruity aroma characteristic is primarily contributed by isobutyl hexanoate (Dou et al., 2020), cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate (Luo et al., 2022), and hexyl crotonate (Belgis et al., 2023). The creamy characteristic is primarily from cis-4-decenal (Zhang, Fu, Chen, & Jin, 2023). And the green characteristic is primarily from 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene (Lan et al., 2022) and cis-3-hexenyl acetate (Cui et al., 2021). As the number of tea TS cycles increased, the fruity and creamy characteristics significantly intensified, while the green characteristic rapidly diminished (Fig. 5B). This pattern of change provided chemical evidence supporting the scientific validity of the empirical judgment “fading green and showing aroma” in traditional tea processing (Zhang & Wang, 2020), indicating that the formation of high-quality tea aroma essentially involved a directed transformation of aroma components from grassy notes to ripe fruit and rich, creamy aromas during processing.

Furthermore, this study found that although the green characteristic was suppressed during the TS process, its signature compound, 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, could stably bind to as many as 129 olfactory receptor proteins, indicating that humans were highly sensitive to this type of odor. This finding effectively explains an important phenomenon in tea processing, even when the content of green aroma components in tea leaves has dropped to relatively low levels, their strong receptor-binding capacity allows them to be keenly perceived and significantly influence the overall aroma quality (Zhang et al., 2023). In contrast, compounds exhibiting fruity (binding to 32–75 receptors) and creamy (binding to 18 receptors) characteristics, although having a relatively narrower range of receptor recognition, accumulated continuously and increased in intensity during the TS process. They constituted the main components of tea's characteristic aroma and formed the material basis of the “showing aroma” process. These results indicate that, due to the human olfactory system's high sensitivity to green characteristics, an ideal TS process must not only promote the full generation and accumulation of fruity and creamy components but also ensure the maximum elimination of green components.

Binding mechanisms of signature VOCs and olfactory receptors

Based on the above analysis, this study further examined the binding mechanisms of 428 human olfactory receptor proteins with 6 signature VOCs and found (Fig. 6) that the two human olfactory receptor proteins, OR2L13 and OR2L5, form strong and stable bonds with the six signature VOCs (< −6.0 kcal/mol), with binding energies ranging from −7.1 to −6.1 kcal/mol and − 7.1 to −6.2 kcal/mol, respectively (Table S3). These results indicate that OR2L13 and OR2L5 exhibit broad-spectrum binding to the six signature VOCs and are capable of detecting the different aroma characteristics produced by these compounds.

Molecular docking simulation analysis of the binding energies of six signature VOCs with the OR2L3 and OR2L5 olfactory receptors.

To elucidate the molecular interaction mechanisms between six signature VOCs and human olfactory receptors, this study further analyzed the receptor proteins with the lowest binding energies (i.e., highest affinities) for each compound, as well as their specific binding modes. Molecular docking results showed (Fig. 7) that the binding energy of isobutyl hexanoate with the OR6J1 receptor was −6.5 kcal/mol, and its binding process primarily relied on 2 hydrogen bonds and 7 hydrophobic interactions; cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate also exhibited the strongest binding with the OR6J1 receptor (binding energy: −6.9 kcal/mol), with the stability of the complex primarily maintained by 2 hydrogen bonds and 4 hydrophobic interactions. The optimal receptor for cis-3-hexenyl acetate was OR2L5, with a binding energy of −6.3 kcal/mol; the binding mode involved 3 hydrogen bonds and 4 hydrophobic interactions. The binding energy of cis-4-decenal with receptor OR2Y1 was −6.6 kcal/mol, and its binding primarily relied on 1 hydrogen bond and 6 hydrophobic interactions. The binding energy of hexyl crotonate with receptor OR2L13 was −6.6 kcal/mol, with 3 hydrogen bonds and 7 hydrophobic interactions formed during the binding process. In contrast, the binding energy of 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, a signature compound of the green aroma characteristic, to the receptor OR5M1 was the lowest (−7.6 kcal/mol). This binding was driven entirely by 4 hydrophobic interactions, with no hydrogen bond formation observed.

Molecular docking simulation analysis of the binding mechanisms of six signature VOCs with the human primary olfactory receptor.

The above analysis of binding mechanisms provided a structural biological basis for understanding the olfactory perception characteristics of these signature VOCs. The study found that 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene exhibited the strongest binding energy (−7.6 kcal/mol) with the OR5M1 receptor, which was highly consistent with the finding in the aforementioned analysis that it bond to the largest number of receptors (129). This explains the extremely low olfactory perception threshold in humans from both the energy and receptor spectrum. This compound relied entirely on hydrophobic interactions to bind to the receptor, reflecting the typical characteristic of nonpolar aroma molecules achieving high-affinity recognition of olfactory receptors through hydrophobic pockets. Furthermore, this study revealed the specificity and sharing of receptors during the recognition process. For example, both isobutyl hexanoate and cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate selected OR6J1 as their optimal receptor with similar binding sites, while other VOCs bond to different receptors (OR2L5, OR2Y1, OR2L13, and OR5M1). These results demonstrate the human olfactory system's ability to distinguish structurally diverse odor components, and this “one compound-multiple receptors, one receptor-multiple compounds” binding pattern forms the molecular basis for the holistic perception of complex aromas. Furthermore, this study found that, with the exception of 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, the binding of all other VOCs to receptors was driven by a combination of hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions. Among these, hexyl crotonate formed the most hydrophobic interactions (7) and hydrogen bonds (3), revealing a complex and robust binding network; whereas cis-4-decenal formed only 1 hydrogen bond, suggesting that its binding might rely more on hydrophobic interactions and molecular conformation. These subtle differences in binding mechanisms may be the fundamental cause of the variations in aroma intensity and perceptual characteristics among the substances. This study not only identifies key aroma compounds that serve as indicators during the TS process but also reveals, at the molecular docking level, the mechanisms of their interaction with human olfactory receptors. It establishes a connection at the atomic level between the chemical composition changes during tea processing and human olfactory perception, providing new theoretical support for understanding the biological basis of the “fading green and showing aroma” quality formation process in Wuyi rock tea.

Conclusion

By integrating metabolomics with molecular docking techniques, this study systematically elucidated the dynamic effects of the TS process on the VOCs of Wuyi rock tea and its olfactory perception mechanism (Fig. 8). Using a multidimensional screening strategy, six signature VOCs capable of distinguishing between TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, and TS5 were successfully identified from the 844 VOCs detected, which were isobutyl hexanoate, 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate, cis-4-decenal, cis-3-hexenyl acetate, and hexyl crotonate. The dynamic changes in these compounds clearly revealed the directional transformation patterns of aroma characteristics during the TS process, specifically the significant accumulation of fruity aroma (cis-3-hexenyl isobutyrate, isobutyl hexanoate, and hexyl crotonate) and the creamy aroma (cis-4-decenal), while the green aroma (3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene and cis-3-hexenyl acetate) rapidly diminished. Through molecular docking analysis, this study had, for the first time, revealed the olfactory perception mechanisms of these signature VOCs at the atomic level. The study found that the green aroma characteristic compound, 3,4-dimethyl-1,5-cyclooctadiene, exhibited the strongest receptor-binding properties, with a binding energy of −7.6 kcal/mol for the OR5M1 receptor and the ability to stably bind to 129 olfactory receptors. This finding provides a scientific basis from the perspective of structural biology to explain the traditional empirical criterion for determining the endpoint of tea TS, “completely fading green”. This demonstrates that even when the content of green-related compounds is low, their strong receptor affinity can still be keenly perceived by the olfactory system, thereby influencing the overall aroma quality. In contrast, fruity compounds have a narrower receptor binding range (32–75 receptors), but their continuous accumulation during the TS process forms the material basis for the tea's primary aroma. The “process-composition-perception” correlation model established in this study indicates that the TS is a critical step in the formation of the aroma of high-quality Wuyi rock tea. This step not only promotes the generation of fruity and creamy aroma compounds but, more importantly, controls the content of green aroma compounds below the olfactory perception threshold. This finding not only deepens the understanding of the traditional theory of “fading green odor and showing aroma” at the molecular level but also provides important theoretical grounds and methodological references for the precise regulation of tea aroma quality and process optimization. However, this study primarily employed metabolomics and molecular docking for theoretical prediction, preliminarily revealing the evolution patterns of key aroma compounds and their perception potential. In future work, sensory evaluation combined with aroma recombination can be further integrated to systematically validate the aroma contributions of key compounds at the perceptual level, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of the biological mechanisms underlying aroma quality formation during the TS process.

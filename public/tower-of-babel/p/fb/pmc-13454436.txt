Robust rigid registration via maximum correntropy criterion: a novel approach
Authors: Guiqiang Yang, Jucheng Wang, Ting Gao, Rui Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPoint set registration is essential for computer vision, pattern recognition and intelligent robotics. Existing registration methods exhibit limited robustness against non-Gaussian noise, outliers and incomplete point clouds in complex engineering scenarios. This paper proposes a robust rigid point set registration method based on the maximum correntropy criterion (MCC). The method integrates the point-to-plane distance metric into correntropy measurement to construct a novel iterative optimization framework. In each iteration, point correspondences are established via nearest neighbor search, and rigid transformation parameters are optimized under the MCC criterion. An adaptive kernel width updating strategy is adopted to balance global convergence and local alignment accuracy. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world point sets verify the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method. Introduction

Point set registration is a fundamental technique in computer vision1,2, pattern recognition3,4, image analysis5,6, and robotics7,8. It serves as the cornerstone for key engineering tasks such as pose recognition, workpiece localization, and high-precision measurement, directly determining the reliability of subsequent data processing and application implementation. A critical challenge in practical engineering scenarios is achieving reliable registration under degraded conditions, where unpredictable gross errors often occur. These errors, characterized by arbitrary magnitudes and random distributions, are difficult to model and predict, necessitating robust strategies to mitigate their adverse impact on registration accuracy and stability.

Among various registration tasks, rigid point set registration is particularly crucial for engineering applications. By preserving the intrinsic shape and spatial relationships of the target, it enables high-precision measurement and reliable data processing. Most existing rigid registration methods are built on the classic point-to-point (ptDpt) Iterative Closest Point (ICP) method9 and its variants. These methods estimate spatial transformation parameters through an iterative optimization framework but suffer from two critical limitations: 1) They are highly sensitive to initial pose, easily falling into local optima rather than converging to the global optimal solution; 2) the L2 loss adopted in traditional ICP methods makes them vulnerable to noise, outliers, and non-Gaussian disturbances, significantly affecting registration accuracy10,11.

To address the limitations of classic ICP-based methods, feature-based registration approaches have been extensively explored. Representative methods include Fast Point Feature Histograms (FPFH)12, Intrinsic Shape Signatures (ISS)13, Signature of Histograms of Orientations (SHOT)14, and Rotational Projection Statistics (RoPS)15. These methods detect distinctive keypoints and generate handcrafted geometric descriptors by encoding local surface features and spatial distribution patterns. Robust coarse registration is achieved through geometric constraint verification strategies, such as Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC)16 and 4-Points Congruent Sets (4PCS)17. However, feature-based methods rely heavily on the distinctiveness of local geometric features; under noise interference, occlusion, or low-overlap conditions, their performance degrades sharply, making it difficult to ensure stable and accurate registration results.

With the rapid development of artificial intelligence, deep learning-based point set registration methods have made remarkable progress. Representative approaches such as PointNetLK18, Deep Closest Point (DCP)19, Fully Convolutional Geometric Features (FCGF)20, and Hierarchical Registration Network (HRegNet)21 learn high-dimensional features from annotated data in a data-driven manner. These methods capture complex geometric patterns through neural network architectures, enhancing adaptability to noise and partial overlap. Additionally, end-to-end deep learning frameworks have been proposed, which directly regress rigid transformation parameters without intermediate steps such as feature matching or outlier rejection, significantly improving computational efficiency.

As highlighted in relevant studies, traditional methods relying on explicit geometric assumptions and handcrafted features lack the ability to handle complex engineering scenarios. They often fail to adapt to uneven point cloud density, non-Gaussian noise, and other interference factors, leading to unstable registration results. Deep learning-based methods, while showing advantages in feature extraction, still face limitations: they rely heavily on large-scale annotated datasets, and their generalization performance is restricted when applied to actual engineering scenarios involving physical entities. Against this backdrop, aiming at the prevalent gross errors and outliers in engineering measurement scenarios—where existing ICP variants, feature-based methods, and deep learning approaches all have inherent limitations—this work proposes a rigid registration method that integrates the maximum correntropy criterion (MCC) with a point-to-plane (ptDpl) distance metric, and adopts a new function solution strategy to further enhance the accuracy and robustness of registration.

The key contributions are twofold: (1) redefining the correspondence construction strategy using the MCC framework, which adaptively suppresses the impact of outliers and non-Gaussian noise during iteration; (2) achieving robustness against initial pose perturbation by jointly optimizing the transformation and adaptive kernel width, rather than relying on fragile nearest-neighbor correspondences.

The proposed method is inherently insensitive to initial pose misalignment and does not require coarse registration. By introducing the MCC-based energy function with adaptive kernel width adjustment, the objective landscape becomes considerably smoother and convex over a large region. In contrast to classic ICP that easily falls into local minima under large initial deviations, the MCC-based robust mechanism suppresses misleading correspondences caused by poor initialization. Meanwhile, the adaptive kernel width dynamically balances global exploration and local refinement: a large kernel width at the early stage supports global convergence and avoids local optima, while a small kernel width in later stages guarantees high alignment precision. Therefore, the proposed method is insensitive to initial poses and achieves accurate registration without coarse alignment. Extensive 2D and 3D experiments are conducted to verify the superiority of the proposed method over classic ICP and advanced variants, especially in degraded scenarios with intense non-Gaussian noise and outliers.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section “Related Work” reviews the related work on point set registration. Section “Methodology” describes our method in detail. Section “Experimental Results” presents the experimental results and performance comparisons between our approach and other approaches, followed by the conclusion in Section “Conclusion”.

Related work

This section analyzes four mainstream categories of rigid registration methods: ICP-based iterative registration, deep learning feature matching, end-to-end deep learning, and MCC-based registration. By reviewing their research progress and inherent limitations, we clarify the research motivation and innovation of this work.

ICP-based rigid registration

Classical ICP forms the core framework of rigid registration methods. It can deliver high registration accuracy with appropriate initial poses, yet it is inherently sensitive to initialization and prone to local minima. Accordingly, various improved ICP variants have been proposed to further boost computational efficiency and robustness. To mitigate the local optimum issue of classical ICP, Global ICP (GO-ICP)22 and its variants embed the branch-and-bound (BnB) strategy into the ICP framework to achieve global optimization. Nevertheless, such methods introduce heavy computational overhead, limiting their practical deployment in large-scale point cloud registration scenarios.

Other notable recent advances in ICP-based registration include: Scale-Adaptive ICP23 unifies the estimation of scale, rotation and translation within the ICP framework to handle point clouds with unknown scale differences, achieving robust registration under uniform scaling variations; Adaptive Weighting ICP (AW-ICP)24 adopts a residual-based weighting strategy to suppress outlier interference without relying on prior noise knowledge; Kendall Shape Space ICP (KSS-ICP)25 performs point cloud registration in the Kendall shape space. It removes the impacts of scale and position deviations inherent in traditional ICP, and yields more robust and precise registration results for complex point clouds; Geometric Feature Optimized ICP (GFOICP)26 constructs a multi-geometric feature similarity metric based on normal and curvature information. It optimizes the correspondence matching process to eliminate false point pairs, reduces the sensitivity to initial pose, and achieves robust registration for sparse, low-overlap and noisy remote sensing 3D point clouds; The method proposed in27 constructs an improved ICP variant by optimizing correspondence selection and transformation update rules. It effectively improves registration accuracy and accelerates convergence speed; Granular-Ball ICP (GRICP)28 integrates granular-ball theory and multikernel correntropy into the ICP pipeline, considerably strengthening the algorithm’s robustness against non-Gaussian noise and outliers. Additionally, Track-Constrained GICP (tc-GICP)29 constrains LiDAR registration to the predefined track plane in autonomous racing scenarios. It reduces the optimization degrees of freedom from six to three based on GICP, and achieves fast and accurate localization for high-speed autonomous racing vehicles; Improved Incident-Angle Weighted ICP (IAW-ICP)30 introduces an incidence-angle-dependent weighting function to model LiDAR scanning quality, downweights low-confidence points, and improves registration accuracy for industrial LiDAR point clouds. Despite these advancements, most recent ICP variants still rely on fixed loss functions and distance metrics, which cannot fundamentally resolve the trade-off between registration robustness and insensitivity to initial pose.

In summary, existing ICP-based methods remain vulnerable to initial pose sensitivity and complex noise disturbances, and are constrained by inflexible metric designs that limit further improvements in both robustness and registration efficiency.

Deep learning feature matching

Feature-based rigid registration fully mines the coordinate information and spatial geometric structures of point sets to construct high-dimensional feature descriptors and establish reliable feature correspondences. It enables accurate point cloud registration with almost no reliance on initial poses, and presents distinct advantages in complex scenarios with large pose deviations.

Recent representative feature-based methods include: FCGF20 extracts dense geometric features via sparse fully convolutional networks, expands receptive field to capture broad spatial contextual information, and improves feature learning efficiency for robust point correspondence estimation. DCP19 adopts attention modules to encode contextual information, and combines soft matching with singular value decomposition (SVD) to solve rigid transformation parameters. Deep Global Registration (DGR)31 combines a 6D correspondence confidence network for match weighting, a differentiable weighted Procrustes solver, and a geometric refinement module, enabling accurate registration from scratch without initial alignment. IDAM32 achieves efficient point cloud registration by iteratively matching points with a distance-aware similarity matrix convolution module that fuses feature and Euclidean space information, eliminating false positive correspondence pairs via a two-stage learnable point elimination technique, and adopting a mutual-supervision loss for training. GeoTransformer33 encodes pairwise distances and triplet-wise angles through a geometric Transformer, learns structural geometric features for robust superpoint matching, and realizes RANSAC-free transformation estimation with significant computational acceleration. HGPE-tr34 decouples rotation and translation features via a dual-branch design, fuses multi-scale geometry with hierarchical positional encoding and dual geometric‑aware attention, and removes outliers via an undirected graph‑based iterative module.

In summary, while feature-based methods effectively reduce the reliance on initial poses, they are limited in high-precision fine registration and exhibit unsatisfactory performance on sparse point clouds from engineering scenes with weak geometric features. Moreover, these methods are highly dependent on the geometric complexity of the scene and tend to produce false correspondences in feature-deficient or structurally repetitive regions. Additionally, most assume sufficient overlap and degrade under low-overlap or heavy occlusion, where valid matches become too sparse for reliable estimation.

End-to-end deep learning registration

Unlike the feature‑matching‑oriented methods in Section “Deep Learning Feature Matching”, this section focuses on deep learning approaches that directly regress rigid transformation parameters end‑to‑end, without constructing explicit feature correspondences or performing point matching. To this end, a series of correspondence-free deep learning paradigms have emerged. DeepCLR35, building upon pioneering efforts such as PointNetLK18, further advances this research direction. It systematically validates the feasibility and effectiveness of end-to-end rigid transformation regression without explicit feature correspondences, thus laying a solid methodological foundation for subsequent registration research.

Representative methods in this line include: RPM‑Net36 learns hybrid spatial and geometric features. It then uses a differentiable Sinkhorn layer with annealing to achieve soft point correspondence assignment. This end‑to‑end pipeline alleviates local optima and improves robustness to noise, outliers, and low overlap, delivering state‑of‑the‑art registration performance. However, recomputing features in each iteration incurs higher computational cost; PointNetLK Revisited37 employs an analytical Jacobian to eliminate numerical instability, improve registration accuracy, and strengthen generalization. Meanwhile, its voxel-based feature aggregation scheme enables robust, correspondence-free, end-to-end registration for large-scale real-world scenes; REGTR38 employs a Transformer with self- and cross-attention to directly predict point correspondences and overlap probabilities, eliminating the need for handcrafted descriptors and RANSAC. This end-to-end framework enables accurate and robust rigid transformation estimation on standard benchmarks. DeepGMR39 aligns point clouds by minimizing the KL divergence between latent Gaussian mixture models, learning pose-invariant features and estimating rigid transformations via differentiable modules without explicit point matching, thereby achieving robust, real-time end-to-end global registration. R3PM-Net40 adopts a compact MLP architecture to implement geometric feature extraction. It effectively bridges the performance gap between models trained on synthetic datasets and noisy, sparse point cloud scans in real-world scenarios.

The methods presented in this section adopt end-to-end learning frameworks that bypass handcrafted feature matching and RANSAC. They generally offer improved robustness to noise, outliers, and low overlap compared to traditional feature-based methods, while enabling efficient inference. However, their generalization to real-world engineering scenes and robustness to arbitrary large initial pose deviations without coarse registration remain open challenges.

MCC-based rigid registration

The MCC has been widely integrated into the ICP framework to boost the robustness of rigid registration. By adaptively weighting point correspondences, MCC effectively suppresses non‑Gaussian noise, outliers, and large initial pose deviations, greatly enhancing registration stability. In recent years, various MCC‑driven ICP variants have been proposed to improve model formulation, correspondence optimization, and anti‑interference ability.

Extensive efforts have been devoted to developing MCC-based rigid registration algorithms with improved robustness and applicability. Du et al.41 proposed the fundamental MCC-based rigid registration framework with fixed kernel bandwidth, and theoretically verified its monotonic convergence. Xu et al.42 extended this framework to 2D point set registration, and analyzed the effect of kernel bandwidth on registration accuracy. Chen et al.43 and Wu et al.44 integrated MCC into scale-adaptive ICP to achieve robust registration under isotropic scale differences. CoBigICP45 combines correntropy metrics with bidirectional correspondence and normal information to improve alignment accuracy in low-overlap scenarios. Singandhupe et al.46 constructed a correntropy similarity matrix to reduce the dependence on nearest-neighbor search. AMCC47 utilizes adaptive kernel bandwidth estimation via probability density matching to enhance robustness against heavy-tailed noise and clustered outliers. Du et al.48 integrated semantic labels into MCC-based registration to filter mismatched correspondences via semantic constraints.

Nevertheless, the aforementioned MCC-based rigid registration methods have several aspects worthy of further optimization. First, methods41–44,46–48 only adopt point-to-point distance and fail to introduce surface normal information, which may restrict the upper bound of registration accuracy. Second, methods41–46,48 use fixed kernel bandwidth, while method47 realizes adaptive adjustment through probability density matching instead of iterative residual-driven updating, which may limit the convergence range and tolerance to initial pose deviation. Third, most existing methods including41,42,44–46 solve transformation parameters by means of SVD, gradient descent and manifold optimization, rather than establishing an explicit linear matrix system to ensure stable iteration. In contrast, the proposed method integrates point-to-plane distance constraint, iterative residual-guided adaptive kernel width adjustment and linear matrix system solving into a unified MCC framework, which effectively complements the above deficiencies and realizes stable and accurate rigid registration without extra coarse alignment.

To partially address the limited robustness of existing methods to non-Gaussian noise and outliers in real-world engineering scenes, this work proposes a robust point set registration method integrating the MCC with the point-to-plane distance metric, achieving accurate and stable registration under challenging measurement conditions.

Adaptive kernel width strategy

As defined in Eq. (7), correntropy is a local similarity measure whose effective range is controlled by the kernel width σ. This parameter directly influences the algorithm’s noise robustness, registration accuracy, and convergence speed.

For a fixed kernel width, there exists an inherent trade-off: a large σ accelerates convergence but reduces accuracy and noise suppression, while a small σ improves robustness but slows convergence and increases sensitivity to initial pose. To address this limitation, we adopt an adaptive strategy: a relatively large kernel width is used in the initial iterations to ensure fast convergence. As the iteration proceeds and the residual error gradually decreases, the kernel width is adaptively reduced to improve registration accuracy and noise suppression capability.

To further investigate the influence of the kernel width σ on the performance of the proposed method, experiments were conducted on the bird point cloud dataset. In the tests, 10%, 30%, and 50% of total points were contaminated by uniform noise, and multiple kernel width configurations were compared, including fixed values and different adaptive strategies.

The results are summarized in Fig. 2, where the horizontal axis denotes the iteration number, and the vertical axis represents the number of valid corresponding pairs satisfying the predefined distance threshold. The comparison confirms that dynamically adjusting σ throughout the iteration yields the best overall performance. While fixed kernel widths (e.g., σ = 10) can achieve a reasonable number of matches, they are prone to local optima and lower registration accuracy compared with adaptive schemes.

Comparison of registration accuracy under different kernel width strategies with varying levels of uniform noise corruption (10%, 30%, and 50%).

Among all tested adaptive strategies, the scheme that initializes the kernel width based on the mean distance of corresponding point pairs in each iteration yields significantly more valid matches. To balance convergence speed and registration accuracy, the kernel width σ in this work is initialized within the range of 1–10 times the mean pairwise distance of correspondences, with the optimal value set to twice this mean distance.

The adaptive kernel width strategy endows the proposed method with inherent insensitivity to initial pose. Even under a large initial misalignment, a large initial kernel width enables global exploration and suppresses local optimality. Thus, the proposed method does not require coarse registration and can converge reliably from arbitrary initial poses.

The results obtained on public TLS datasets

To further evaluate the performance of the proposed method, two public terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) datasets, namely Hannover1 (http://kos.informatik.uni-osnabrueck.de/3Dscans/) and Arch (https://prs.igp.ethz.ch/research/completed_projects/automatic_registration_of_point_clouds.html), are adopted for experimental verification. Both datasets were collected in outdoor architectural scenarios with complex structures and realistic noise interference. The Hannover1 dataset provides ground-truth poses for quantitative evaluation, while the Arch is employed to validate qualitative registration effect in real scenes. In this section, the proposed method is compared with three representative state-of-the-art registration methods including GoICP22, CoBigICP and 4DoF54 , to validate its registration performance.

The initial poses and registration results of both datasets are presented in Figs. 9 and 10. Figure 9a shows the initial misalignment of the Arch dataset, where the target (blue) and model (red) point clouds are severely separated. Figure 9b shows the initial misalignment of the Hannover1 dataset, where the two point clouds exhibit large pose deviations. After registration, the target point clouds are accurately aligned with the models, as shown in Fig. 10a (Arch) and Fig. 10b (Hannover1), demonstrating that the proposed method can effectively handle large initial pose errors.

The average runtime of each method obtained from ten repeated experiments on the Hannover1 dataset is summarized as follows: 4DoF costs 2.4033 s, CoBigICP costs 4.4792 s, GoICP costs 66.5365 s, and the proposed method costs 7.8381 s. The Hannover1 dataset owns regular structural features and a high overlap ratio between point clouds, so all registration methods can easily and reliably establish point correspondences. Relying on its concise solution strategy with fewer degrees of freedom, the 4DoF method achieves the highest computational efficiency. By contrast, GoICP requires extensive global traversal and correspondence searching, leading to the longest runtime. the proposed method introduces an adaptive robust weighting mechanism to filter noisy correspondences and improve registration accuracy, which brings moderate computational cost and achieves a favorable balance between registration precision and computational efficiency. Different from the Hannover1 dataset, the Arch dataset suffers from low overlap and sparse effective corresponding point pairs, which increases the difficulty of correspondence searching and iterative optimization. Consequently, all algorithms consume much more computation time on the Arch dataset.

To compare computational efficiency, ten repeated tests are conducted on the Arch dataset. The average runtime is 14.3378 s for the proposed method and 6.0831 s for the 4DoF algorithm. The higher computational cost of the proposed method is reasonable, as it introduces additional robust optimization procedures to eliminate gross errors and false correspondences, thereby guaranteeing superior registration robustness and accuracy in complex low-overlap scenarios.

The experimental results verify that, thanks to the adaptive MCC mechanism, the proposed method can directly achieve accurate point set registration without relying on coarse alignment, and maintains high stability and accuracy even under large initial rotation and translation perturbations, showing low dependence on the initial pose.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a novel 2D and 3D point set registration method based on correntropy, which adopts an iterative optimization framework to solve the registration problem. The optimization procedure is analogous to the reweighted least-squares strategy. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared with existing state-of-the-art registration methods, the proposed method delivers higher registration accuracy under complex conditions including contour truncation, noise interference and outlier disturbance. In future work, the proposed method can be extended to more practical applications, such as robot positioning and navigation, and three-dimensional reconstruction.

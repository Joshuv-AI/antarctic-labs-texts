Patients’ satisfaction after bilateral thoracoscopic sympathicolysis
Authors: Hanan M. Hemead, Waheed Etman, Sarah Hemead, Ehab Elrewany, Ahmed Abdelaziz
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Palmar hyperhidrosis is a distressing condition implicating individuals’ social life and self-esteem. Surgery has proven to be highly effective with excellent safety profile. However, compensatory hyperhidrosis (CH), a relatively common adverse event of the procedure, is sometimes problematic in a few patients.Patients and Methods:A questionnaire, including Hyperhidrosis Disease Severity Scale (HDSS), was designed to assess the overall satisfaction of patients who underwent bilateral endoscopic thoracic sympathectomy between November 2018 and July 2021. They were requested to respond to the questionnaire before surgery and during the follow-up visits.Results:Sixty-three patients were recruited, 37 males and 26 females. The age of the participants ranged from 6 to 27 years (mean 17.05 ± 5.55 years). Most patients reported a positive impact on their professional and social life. Two patients experienced significant compensatory hyperhydrosis impacting their daily life. There was a significant improvement of the HDSS score after surgery. Around 95.2% of the participants (n = 60) had three-point improvement indicating 80% reduction of sweat production.Conclusion:Bilateral endoscopic thoracoscopic sympathectomy has shown a high success rate with low adverse events. CH, however, remains a significant cause of morbidity in few patients. Therefore, continuous evaluation of patients’ satisfaction is of utmost importance to help improve our understanding to risk factors and prevalence of CH, improve our techniques to minimise its occurrence and treat patients with distressing symptoms. These data will guide surgeons when counselling patients to make insightful decisions based on the benefits and risks of the procedure.

INTRODUCTION

Primary hyperhidrosis is an unexplained sympathetic overstimulation in genetically predisposed individuals.[1] The disorder is emotionally distressing, functionally crippling and socially handicapping.[2]

There are several therapeutic non-surgical alternatives.[3] However, despite being less invasive, they have often unsatisfactory and temporary responses.[4] Surgical interruption of the sympathetic chain leads to immediate and potentially irreversible results.[5] The complications are compensatory hyperhidrosis (CH), recurrence and Horner’s syndrome. CH can be quite distressing in few patients. Therefore, it is considered an indicator of the success of the procedure.[6] Post-operative patient’s satisfaction is an important marker for the effectiveness of the intervention.[7]

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Patients who underwent bilateral endoscopic thoracoscopic sympathectomy (BETS) between November 2018 and July 2021 were recruited for the study. Before the procedure, patients were asked to grade the severity of hyperhidrosis according to the Hyperhidrosis Disease Severity Scale (HDSS). HDSS is a four-scale self-reporting questionnaire widely used to evaluate the severity of hyperhidrosis and its impact on daily life.[8] During the follow-up period, we asked patients to respond to a pre-designed survey composed of 13 questions, including the HDSS to detect the intensity of the adverse events and the post-operative overall satisfaction. The follow-up period ranged from 6 ± 12 months. Paediatric patients were interviewed to fill in the surveys and some responses were obtained from their carers when needed. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board. Patients’ consent was obtained prior to launching the study. A pilot study was conducted prior to the actual study to pre-test the validity of questions.

Thoracoscopic sympathicolysis was performed under general anesthesia with conventional single-lumen intubation using low-pressure carbon dioxide insufflation (between 5 and a maximum of 10 mmHg). A single bilateral procedure was performed during the same anesthesia setting. All patients were positioned in the semi-sitting position with 90° abduction of the arms and the elbows slightly flexed.

Two 5 mm incisions were made after the skin was injected with local anesthetic: the first in the mid-axillary line in the fifth intercostal space, the second slightly anterior in the third intercostal space, as shown in Figure 1.

Incisions created to perform two-port BETS

The first trocar was placed using an open technique in apnoea ventilation mode to minimise the risk of lung injury. Standard tidal volume ventilation is used by anaesthesia during the procedure. The ipsilateral lung was collapsed by using valved trocars and insufflating carbon dioxide. After identifying the sympathetic chain using a 5mm 30 degrees telescope, low energy monopolar electrofulguration of the chain was performed through the intact pleura, starting just below the third rib (T3) and continuing down to the head of the fourth rib and even below it (T4). The anatomy and thoracoscopic view of the sympathetic chain is shown in Figure 2.

Surgical anatomy and thoracoscopic view of the thoracic sympathetic chain

Short single impulses were used, avoiding the dispersion of heat to adjacent structures. Subsequently, a monopolar Maryland forceps was used to make 3 cm long transverse incisions of the parietal pleura and periosteum along the third and fourth rib from medial to lateral, transecting the sympathetic chain and any collateral nerve fibres. After the evacuation of the capnothorax, the trocars were removed, and the wounds were closed. No chest drains were left in place. The procedure was a day-case surgery, and the patients were discharged within 6 hours after the procedure.

RESULTS

Sixty-three patients were included in the study: 37 males and 26 females, The age of the participants ranged from 6 to 27 years (mean 17.05 ± 5.55 years), as shown in Table 1. Most of the patients reported a positive impact of the surgery on their social and professional activities and self-confidence (98.4% and 96.8%, respectively). With respect to plantar hyperhidrosis, approximately half of the patients (52.4%) described decreased sweating, whereas 44.4% did not notice any change in the pattern of planter sweating and only 2 (3.2%) described worsening of planter symptoms postoperatively. When asked about the change of the pattern of axillary hyperhidrosis, patient reported minimal or no change. Most of the study participants (63.5%) did not experience compensatory sweating. Patients who reported CH (n = 23) stated that their symptoms are mild and tolerable, except for two patients who have severe hyperhidrosis of the back and trunk. When respondents were asked if they would recommend the procedure to other patients with hyperhidrosis, the majority (96.8%) chose ‘‘Yes’’. The two participants who experienced severe CH were not satisfied and stated that they do not recommend the intervention based on their negative experience. DISCUSSION

Primary hyperhidrosis is known to have severe negative consequences on patients’ quality of life (QoL). Hyperhidrosis not only interferes with professional and daily activities but also disrupts patients’ self-esteem and social well-being. Several studies showed that hyperhidrosis stigmatises individuals and demotivates them from integration in the society.[9,10] BETS reduces the amount of sweating effectively and instantly offering patients immediate amelioration of their deleterious symptoms with high success rates. CH, however; is the major and most frequent adverse event of the procedure, causing considerable dissatisfaction in the number of patients. The risk factors for CH are not yet well identified; however, some studies reported an association between certain factors such as; age and operational level and obesity.[10,11] Many recent studies aimed to explore various interventions to relieve the severe symptoms of hyperhidrosis by disruption of the targeted ganglia, either surgical or by laser photocoagulation.[12,13]

General QoL questionnaires have been used to evaluate the intensity of symptoms and the success of the procedure. Several disease-specific tools were designed and validated such as Hyperhidrosis QoL Index (HidroQoL©), hyperhidrosis scale and HDSS.[14] These questionnaires aim to provide a more specific and practical evaluation of the effectiveness of the intervention by comparing preoperative and post-operative scores and correlating the difference to the percentage of reduction of the amount of sweating as in HDSS.

Hyperhidrosis usually affects adolescents and children. Few studies investigated the success and safety of the procedure in the paediatric population. They showed a high success rate and high safety profile.[9,15,16] In this study, we included 24 patients aged 16 years and less. Their main complaint was distressing palmar sweating, causing pronounced impact on their school performance and interpersonal relationship with their peers. Two children reported that their hands constantly wetted their examination papers and they needed to persistently ask for extra time and papers. In this population, the procedure showed a high satisfaction rate as reported by the children and their parents.

CONCLUSION

Patient satisfaction and post-operative QoL are critical indicators for the effectiveness and safety of the procedure. BETS provides an immediate and effective resolution of symptoms with a success rate exceeding 95%. Dissatisfaction, however, is largely caused by CH, a known complication, that could cause more disruption than the initial symptoms in a small percentage of patients. The risk factors are not yet known, and no gold standard treatment has been established. Therefore, patient counselling about all possible therapeutic options, their advantages and disadvantages are of major importance.

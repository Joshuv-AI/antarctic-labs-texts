The Analysis of Risk Factors of Stroke-Associated Pneumonia in Patients with Acute Stroke Based on Lasso Regression and the Construction of a Nomogram Prediction Model
Authors: Gaoyi Wu, Xiwen Zhong, Daicheng Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo identify the risk factors for stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP) in patients with acute cerebrovascular stroke (ACS) using LASSO regression analysis and to construct a nomogram-based prediction model.MethodsClinical data of 253 patients with ACS admitted to our hospital between March 2022 and May 2024 were retrospectively collected as the modeling cohort. In addition, clinical data of 143 patients with ACS admitted between June 2024 and August 2025 were collected as the validation cohort. Patients were divided into the SAP group and the non-SAP group according to the occurrence of SAP.ResultsLogistic regression analysis of variables selected by LASSO regression demonstrated that age, diabetes mellitus, NIHSS score, nasogastric tube placement, dysphagia, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), and hypoproteinemia were independent risk factors for SAP in patients with ACS (all P < 0.05). In internal validation, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was 0.907, the Hosmer–Lemeshow test showed χ2 = 7.434 (P = 0.725), and decision curve analysis (DCA) indicated a favorable net clinical benefit across threshold probabilities of 0.10–0.85. In external validation, the ROC curve yielded an AUC of 0.912, with a Hosmer–Lemeshow χ2 of 7.118 (P = 0.712). DCA demonstrated a positive net benefit when threshold probabilities ranged from 0.12 to 0.82.ConclusionThe nomogram model constructed based on LASSO regression analysis demonstrates good discriminative ability and potential clinical applicability for predicting SAP in patients with ACS.Keywords: acute cerebral stroke, stroke-associated pneumonia, risk factors, nomogram

Introduction

Acute cerebrovascular stroke (ACS) is an acute-onset neurological disorder primarily caused by rupture or obstruction of cerebral blood vessels, leading to brain tissue damage. It has a high mortality and disability rate,1 and its complications can also affect patient prognosis. Stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP) is one of the most severe complications, with a global incidence of approximately 7%–38%. Stroke can induce systemic immunosuppression, thereby accelerating the development of SAP.2 When cerebrovascular blood flow in ACS is obstructed, ischemia and hypoxia in the brain can occur, leading to swallowing disorders, choking, and aspiration, which increase infection risk and contribute to SAP. Additionally, damage to the nervous system may weaken swallowing and cough reflexes, further promoting the onset of SAP.3,4 Therefore, identifying risk factors associated with SAP in ACS patients, recognizing them early, and implementing appropriate preventive measures can effectively reduce incidence and improve prognosis.5 A nomogram model, constructed through Lasso-Logistic regression analysis by screening differential predictive factors, can calculate the total score according to the values of predictive variables, and then accurately predict the risk value of a specific event based on the total score, and thereby effectively assess the risk value of SAP occurrence, and assist clinicians in targeted prevention.6,7 Previous studies have reported that a nomogram developed based on a logistic regression model was used to predict the risk of stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP) in patients with acute ischemic stroke, identifying coronary artery disease, hypertension, and dysphagia as influencing factors. This model provided a practical tool for the early identification of high-risk patients and enabled targeted interventions, thereby reducing the incidence of SAP and improving clinical outcomes.7 Based on these findings, the present study further investigates the risk of SAP in patients with acute cerebrovascular stroke (ACS). This study thus aims to explore the risk factors of SAP in ACS patients based on Lasso regression analysis and to establish a nomogram prediction model.

Data and Methods

General Information

Clinical data of 253 ACS patients admitted to our hospital from March 2022 to May 2024 were retrospectively collected as the modeling group. Additionally, clinical data of 143 ACS patients admitted to our hospital between June 2024 and August 2025 were collected for temporal validation and used as the validation group. Patients in both groups were divided into the SAP group and the non-SAP group based on whether SAP occurred. The case collection flowchart is shown in Figure 1. Inclusion criteria: (1) Meeting ACS diagnostic criteria,8 and confirmed as ischemic stroke by MRI or cranial CT, with all patients admitted to the stroke unit; (2) Meeting SAP diagnostic criteria;1 (3) First onset; (4) Time from onset to admission < 24 h; (5) Complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: (1) Organ failure; (2) Malignant tumors; (3) Existing pulmonary infection during stroke; (4) Hematologic diseases; (5) Immune dysfunction or other infections; (6) Cognitive impairment; (7) Patients who had pneumonia within 48 h after stroke. This study was approved by the hospital ethics committee.

Methods

SAP Diagnostic Criteria

According to relevant literature,9 the diagnostic criteria include three aspects (meeting imaging findings and any one of the two other aspects): (1) Clinical evaluation: ① For non-mechanically ventilated patients, new pulmonary infection symptoms within 7 days of onset, including temperature ≥38 °C; ② New or worsened cough, dyspnea, or tachypnea; ③ New purulent sputum or increased respiratory secretions within 24 h; ④ Presence of pulmonary rales or bronchial breath sounds on auscultation. (2) Imaging and laboratory tests: White blood cell count ≥10 × 109/L or ≤4 × 109/L; chest imaging showing progressive or new pulmonary infiltrates. (3) Pathogenic examination: Lower respiratory tract secretions collected before antibiotic use and sent for laboratory testing.

Clinical Data

The clinical data retrospectively collected from the hospital electronic medical record system included: age, sex, alcohol history, smoking history, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, atrial fibrillation, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), history of stroke, NIHSS score,10 consciousness disorder (a Glasgow Coma Scale score < 15 was defined as the presence of disturbance of consciousness), nasogastric tube use before the occurrence of SAP, dysphagia (defined as a Kubota water swallowing test score ≥ grade 3, or the presence of aspiration or poor swallowing coordination indicated by fiberoptic laryngoscopy), hypoproteinemia (serum albumin < 35 g/L was used as the cutoff value for hypoproteinemia; that is, hypoproteinemia was considered present when the test result was < 35 g/L), Blood samples were collected at admission to measure platelet count (PLT), white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NEU), lymphocyte count (Lym), monocyte count, systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), NEU/Lym ratio (NLR), fasting blood glucose (FBG), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), and hemoglobin (Hb). The mean values of the above indicators were used. Variables with a missing data rate of 5% or higher were excluded to ensure the reliability and rigor of the results. For variables with a missing data rate of less than 5%, considering that clinical measurement data are often skewedly distributed, median imputation was used to handle missing values in this study. Complete-case deletion or mean imputation was not adopted, thereby reducing the interference of extreme values and sample loss, while ensuring data integrity and the reliability of the statistical results.

Statistical Analysis

Data were processed using SPSS 25.0. Measurement data were analyzed by t-test and expressed as (), while categorical data were analyzed by χ2-test and expressed as n (%). Lasso regression was used to screen influencing factors; logistic regression analyzed risk factors of SAP in the modeling group; R software was used to construct the nomogram prediction model for SAP in ACS patients. Bootstrap was used for internal validation; ROC and calibration curves evaluated model discrimination and consistency; DCA assessed the clinical application value of the nomogram model. P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Construction of the Nomogram Model for Predicting SAP in ACS Patients

Based on the risk factors identified through Lasso-logistic regression, a nomogram model was constructed as follows: P = eˣ/(1 + eˣ), where x = –19.744 + 0.764 × age + 0.881 × diabetes + 1.288 × NIHSS score + 1.095 × dysphagia + 1.114 × nasogastric tube placement + 1.103 × hypoproteinemia+1.205× SIRI + 0.913 × NLR. In this model, SIRI was identified as the most influential factor. Each factor corresponded to a specific score, and the total score represented the overall risk. The higher the total score, the greater the probability of SAP occurrence in ACS patients. For instance, a total score of 114 corresponded to a 73% probability of SAP. See Figure 3.

Construction a nomogram model for the occurrence of SAP in ACS patients.

Internal Validation of the Nomogram Model for Predicting SAP in ACS Patients

The ROC curve yielded an AUC of 0.9027 (95% CI = 0.8520–0.96452), with a sensitivity of 85.25% and a specificity of 73.89% at the optimal cutoff value, as well as a positive predictive value of 28.51% and a negative predictive value of 54.17%. Internal validation using the Bootstrap method (1,000 repetitions) showed a C-index of 0.852. The Hosmer–Lemeshow (H–L) test result was χ2 = 7.434 (P = 0.725), indicating that the predicted probability of SAP occurrence in ACS patients closely matched the actual probability. See Figure 4.

Discussion

Complications following acute cerebrovascular stroke (ACS) can be divided into medical and neurological categories. Among them, stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP) has been identified as a major cause of death in ACS patients, as stroke can significantly impair immune function, thereby increasing the risk of SAP and complicating treatment.11 SAP tends to occur during the first week after ACS, when patients often suffer from severe intracranial injury and pulmonary edema, with considerable functional impairment. In addition, some patients require tracheostomy, which further increases the risk of SAP.12,13 In this study, 76 of the 253 patients (30.04%) in the modeling cohort developed SAP. Previous studies have reported that the incidence of SAP among acute ischemic stroke patients undergoing thrombectomy can reach 45.8%,14 which is higher than in this study, possibly due to regional and disease-severity differences. Nonetheless, the relatively high incidence observed here underscores the importance of further exploring the influencing factors of SAP in ACS patients to improve clinical outcomes.

Through Lasso-logistic regression analysis, seven influencing factors were identified: age, diabetes, NIHSS score, nasogastric tube placement, dysphagia, NLR, hypoproteinemia, and SIRI. The underlying mechanisms may be explained as follows: (1) Age: The study found that patients aged ≥60 years had a 2.148-fold higher risk of developing SAP compared with those under 60. Elderly patients often have multiple comorbidities, decreased physiological function, weakened immunity, and reduced cough and sputum clearance reflexes, all of which increase the likelihood of SAP.15,16 (2) Diabetes: Diabetic patients frequently suffer from cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. After ACS onset, stress-induced hyperglycemia may occur, leading to immune suppression and promoting bacterial proliferation. Moreover, hyperglycemia inhibits leukocyte phagocytic activity, aggravates pulmonary microcirculatory disorders, and further facilitates bacterial growth, thereby elevating the risk of SAP.17 (3) NIHSS score: The NIHSS scale reflects the severity of neurological impairment in ACS patients. Higher scores indicate more severe disease and are associated with motor dysfunction and prolonged bed rest, which can cause atelectasis. Additionally, severe cases often involve consciousness disorders, increasing the likelihood of aspiration. The resulting systemic stress response can induce widespread vasoconstriction, leading to pulmonary edema and congestion, which provide favorable conditions for bacterial infection and SAP development.18,19 (4) Nasogastric tube placement: This is mainly because postoperative patients who depend on enteral feeding through a nasogastric tube require food administration through the tube, which can experience rapid increases in gastric volume after feeding, predisposing them to reflux and vomiting. In this study, nasogastric tube placement occurred before the development of SAP.Prolonged nasogastric intubation also impairs gastrointestinal motility, raising the risk of aspiration and, consequently, SAP.20 (5) Dysphagia: Patients with swallowing disorders have impaired cough and sputum clearance abilities, which prevent timely removal of oral and pharyngeal secretions. They are also prone to gastroesophageal reflux, vomiting, and choking, allowing pathogens to enter the lower respiratory tract and causing SAP.21 (6) Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR): The development of SAP in ACS patients is associated with immunosuppression, reduced lymphocytes, and deactivated monocytes. An elevated NLR reflects immune imbalance and increased infection susceptibility.22 (7) Hypoproteinemia: Albumin possesses anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective properties. Reduced albumin levels can exacerbate post-stroke immunosuppression, increase infection risk, and worsen cerebral edema.23,24 (8) The close association between SIRI and SAP may be attributable to stroke-induced immunosuppression. Prolonged and excessive inflammatory responses can impair immune function, thereby increasing susceptibility to pathogenic infections and predisposing patients to the development of SAP.25 This study showed that SIRI was the strongest predictor. This may be mainly because SIRI, as a novel composite inflammatory indicator integrating neutrophils, lymphocytes, and monocytes, can comprehensively reflect three pathological states: excessive inflammatory activation, immunosuppression, and monocyte-mediated inflammatory cascade reactions. Compared with the NIHSS score, which only evaluates neurological deficits, SIRI is more directly consistent with the inflammation- and immune-mediated mechanisms underlying post-stroke pneumonia. Therefore, it demonstrated stronger independent predictive performance in the weight assignment of the nomogram.

The seven factors identified in this study do not exist in isolation, but can be summarized into four core pathophysiological dimensions. These dimensions are interrelated and act synergistically to jointly mediate the occurrence and development of SAP in patients with acute cerebral stroke (ACS). The dimension of immunosuppression and inflammatory imbalance is mainly represented by NLR and SIRI. The dimension of physiological barrier disruption and aspiration risk includes age, dysphagia, and nasogastric tube placement. The dimension of underlying diseases and nutritional status includes diabetes mellitus and hypoproteinemia. The dimension of neurological deficit severity is mainly represented by the NIHSS score. These four pathophysiological dimensions are interconnected and mutually causal. For example, severe neurological deficits, reflected by an increased NIHSS score, may further aggravate immunosuppression, reflected by elevated NLR and SIRI, and physiological barrier disruption, reflected by worsened dysphagia. Underlying diseases such as diabetes mellitus may simultaneously affect immune function and nutritional status, together forming a multidimensional pathophysiological network underlying the occurrence of SAP. The nomogram model constructed in this study was developed by screening key risk factors based on these four core dimensions, which not only ensures the scientific validity and rationality of the model, but also provides a clear theoretical basis for clinicians to formulate multidimensional preventive intervention strategies for SAP.

Traditional prediction models are often presented in the form of regression equations, requiring clinicians to perform relatively complex calculations to derive risk probabilities, which results in a high threshold for clinical application. In contrast, the nomogram constructed in this study transforms the predictive weights of individual risk factors into intuitive visual scores. Clinicians can rapidly sum the scores according to a patient’s individual characteristics and directly obtain the probability of stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP), with the entire assessment process typically completed within 5 minutes, thereby facilitating the early identification and intervention of SAP.Based on Lasso regression, this study constructed a nomogram prediction model, yielding AUC values of 0.907 (modeling cohort) and 0.912 (validation cohort), indicating excellent discriminative ability. Bootstrap validation demonstrated C-index values of 0.852 and 0.831, respectively,and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test showed good calibration, with predicted and observed SAP probabilities in close agreement. Additionally, DCA curves demonstrated that when the threshold probability ranged from 0.10–0.85 (modeling cohort) and 0.12–0.82 (validation cohort), the net clinical benefit of predicting SAP was the highest. From the perspective of clinical willingness to intervene, threshold probabilities of 0.10 and 0.12 indicate that clinicians may still benefit from implementing preventive interventions even when the risk of SAP occurrence is only 10% or 12%. The upper threshold values approaching 0.85 and 0.82 suggest that, even in high-risk populations, stratified intervention based on the model can still provide a positive net benefit. This benefit range is highly consistent with real-world clinical decision-making habits regarding intervention, indicating that the model has practical value in the vast majority of clinical risk stratification scenarios.These results suggest that the model has high clinical value and strong clinical applicability, aiding clinicians and healthcare professionals in the early screening of high-risk populations, and thereby improving patient prognosis.

In conclusion, the nomogram model constructed based on LASSO regression analysis demonstrates a certain degree of discriminative ability and clinical applicability. However, this study has several limitations. It is a single-center retrospective cohort study with a relatively limited sample size. Although Bootstrap resampling was applied to mitigate the risk of overfitting associated with small samples, the representativeness of the study population may still be constrained by regional factors and local clinical practice patterns. Moreover, the lack of independent validation using a multicenter external cohort means that the optimal cutoff values and risk stratification criteria remain limited to the population of our center, making it difficult for the model to be fully applicable to acute stroke patients from different regions and healthcare settings. This, to some extent, weakens the generalizability and clinical promotion value of the model. In addition, the retrospective design inevitably introduces potential information bias, which may limit the refinement of variable selection. Therefore, multicenter, prospective cohort studies with larger sample sizes are warranted for further validation.

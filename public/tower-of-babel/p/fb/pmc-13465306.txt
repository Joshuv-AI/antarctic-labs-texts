Predictive value of the triglyceride glucose index-a body shape index (TyG-ABSI) for cardiovascular disease and its comparison with other TyG-related obesity indices: a study based on the China health and retirement longitudinal study (CHARLS) cohort
Authors: Xinying Liu, Shuo Yang, Qiang Fu, Yichen Sun, Shiwen Jing, Mingze Liu, Li Liu, Xiaokun Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInsulin resistance (IR) and obesity are recognized as major drivers of cardiovascular disease (CVD). The triglyceride-glucose index–a body shape index (TyG-ABSI), a novel metric integrating lipid metabolism with body morphology, may enhance risk stratification. Although this index has been verified in Western cohorts, its long-term prognostic value and prospective incremental benefit over conventional indices remain uncharacterized in the Chinese population, who present a distinct East Asian adiposity phenotype.MethodsUtilizing follow-up data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) from 2011 to 2020, we enrolled participants free of CVD at baseline with complete essential information. Cox proportional hazards models were employed to estimate the associations between TyG-ABSI and incident CVD, while restricted cubic splines (RCS) were utilized to characterize dose–response relationships. The discriminative power of TyG-ABSI was evaluated using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and C-indices, with comparisons against other related indices, including ABSI, triglyceride-glucose (TyG), TyG-body mass index (TyG-BMI), TyG-Chinese visceral adiposity index (TyG-CVAI), TyG-waist circumference (TyG-WC) and TyG-waist-to-height ratio (TyG-WHtR). Predictive increments were quantified via net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI), and clinical utility was assessed through decision curve analysis (DCA). Cross-parameter correlation testing was executed to evaluate index collinearity.ResultsAmong 7197 participants, 1267 incident CVD cases occurred during the follow-up. In the fully adjusted model, baseline TyG-ABSI was independently associated with an increased risk of composite CVD (HR = 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.14, P = 0.016) and incident stroke (HR = 1.16, 95% CI 1.05–1.28, P = 0.003), whereas no independent correlation was identified for heart conditions. RCS analysis revealed no significant non-linear association for either CVD (P = 0.855) or stroke (P = 0.728). TyG-ABSI improved prediction over ABSI alone, but it offered no advantage over traditional indices. DCA confirmed that traditional indices had better net benefit than TyG-ABSI or its components. The independent predictive value remained highly consistent across sensitivity analyses. Correlation analysis revealed that while traditional indices exhibited severe multicollinearity clustering (r: 0.751–0.890), ABSI achieved near-perfect orthogonal independence from BMI (r = − 0.056).ConclusionsTyG-ABSI was significantly and positively associated with an increased risk of incident CVD. Although TyG-ABSI as an isolated screening tool did not surpass traditional parameters like TyG-BMI in overall prospective accuracy, it exhibited only a negligible correlation with BMI, demonstrating that this metric is not redundant. Consequently, TyG-ABSI retains the potential to capture specific pathogenic signals independent of gross body mass. Future risk stratification toolkits should consider transitioning toward an integrated Anthropometric Risk Indicator (ARI) framework that couples the metabolic sensitivity of TyG-BMI with the specific body shape risks indicated by TyG-ABSI.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12933-026-03241-w.Keywords: Triglyceride-glucose index, Obesity indices, Cardiovascular disease, Prospective cohort study, CHARLS, Risk stratification

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of global mortality and disability [1]. The economic burden imposed on public health systems has become an urgent issue. According to the 2024 Report on Cardiovascular Health and Diseases in China, the prevalence of CVD continues to rise. Mortality rates for CVD rank first among all causes, exceeding those of malignant tumors. Cerebrovascular and coronary heart diseases constitute the primary fatal burden [2]. Therefore, identifying and validating simple and efficient biomarkers with high predictive value is of critical clinical significance for early screening and primary prevention.

Extensive research has confirmed that deep metabolic damage mediated by insulin resistance (IR) and ectopic lipid deposition is the core driver of CVD progression [3–11]. Elevated triglyceride levels not only reflect a heightened concentration of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins but also stimulate atherogenesis by triggering the release of free fatty acids and proinflammatory cytokines [12]. As a key factor in metabolic imbalance, IR promotes plaque instability by inducing glucolipotoxicity, endothelial dysfunction, and chronic low-grade inflammation [13, 14]. However, the hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp remains the gold standard for evaluating IR. Its complexity limits its widespread adoption in large-scale population screening.

The triglyceride glucose (TyG) index has emerged as a simple and reliable surrogate marker for IR [15–19]. It has demonstrated significant value in predicting the risk of incident CVD. However, a single biochemical index has limitations in capturing the complex metabolic features of vascular injury. It specifically struggles to reflect visceral fat accumulation. Consequently, previous studies developed multidimensional indices, such as TyG-body mass index (TyG-BMI) and TyG-waist circumference (TyG-WC), which have improved cardiovascular risk stratification [20–22]. However, these metrics cannot completely eliminate confounding interference from parameters such as height and body weight [23].

Visceral adipose tissue (VAT) possesses unique pathophysiological activity; leptin and multiple proinflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α) secreted by VAT can exacerbate IR and systemic inflammation, and directly induce endothelial dysfunction [24, 25]. To more precisely capture the independent impact of visceral adiposity, the A Body Shape Index (ABSI) employs a mathematical standardization approach to disentangle WC from the confounding effects of BMI and height [26–28]. Imaging studies have confirmed that ABSI highly correlates with MRI-measured visceral fat area and is positively associated with the expression levels of inflammatory genes [29, 30]. Consequently, ABSI theoretically provides a more refined representation of central obesity and abdominal ectopic lipid deposition [31]. The triglyceride glucose index-a body shape index (TyG-ABSI) constructed upon this rationale has demonstrated superior capabilities in identifying cardiovascular risk across Western populations and large-scale prospective cohorts, such as the UK Biobank [28, 32–39].

However, the generalizability of this model remains to be further elucidated when confronted with the distinct East Asian obese phenotype. While Western populations possess a greater capacity for subcutaneous adipose tissue storage, Asian individuals, conversely, are more prone to visceral fat accumulation at lower BMI levels [40]. Subtle elevations in BMI within Asian populations often strongly mirror the early overflow of subcutaneous fat stores, which is closely linked to ectopic visceral fat deposition and atherogenic mixed dyslipidemia [41]. Consequently, whether the TyG-ABSI index, which is independent of BMI, is equally applicable to middle-aged and elderly Chinese populations currently lacks systematic, prospective validation evidence.

Based on data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), a nationally representative prospective cohort, this study aimed to systematically evaluate the association between TyG‑ABSI and the risk of incident CVD. By comprehensively comparing TyG‑ABSI with traditional TyG‑derived indices, we further assessed the predictive performance and clinical utility of this novel index. Meanwhile, we compared the morphological TyG‑derived index with an existing ethnicity‑specific composite index—the TyG‑Chinese Visceral Adiposity Index (TyG‑CVAI). This comparison allowed us to preliminarily explore the relative practical value of a simple anthropometric index versus a more complex localized parameter, thereby evaluating the strengths and weaknesses of the overall morphological strategy. Ultimately, this study expects to provide a solid scientific basis for optimizing CVD risk stratification and precision screening strategies in the Chinese population.

Methods

Study population and participation

The present study utilized data from the CHARLS, a nationally representative prospective cohort of Chinese adults aged 45 years and older [42, 43]. Since its inception in 2011, the database has been publicly accessible via its official website. The baseline assessment encompassed 17,708 participants recruited from 450 villages and neighborhoods across 28 provinces [44, 45]. The study protocols and sampling methodologies have been detailed in previous publications. This investigation was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Biomedical Ethics Review Committee of Peking University (IRB00001052-11015). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

This longitudinal analysis integrated data from Wave 1 (2011) through Wave 5 (2020) of the CHARLS database, with the initial wave serving as the baseline assessment. We enrolled individuals aged 45 years or older who were free of CVD at baseline, with the sample size determined by data availability. To ensure data robustness, several exclusion criteria were rigorously applied. Participants younger than 45 years and those with a history of CVD or missing diagnosis information for heart condition or stroke at baseline were excluded. Furthermore, we removed individuals with missing essential baseline data, including physical measurements of height, weight, and WC, as well as biochemical indicators such as triglycerides (TG), fasting blood glucose (FBG), and high-density lipoprotein (HDL).

In addition, participants with over 40% missing data for covariates or those with incomplete CVD follow-up records were excluded from the final cohort. To minimize potential bias, extreme outliers for physical metrics were also deleted. Specifically, these included height measurements below 50 cm, weight below 20 kg or above 150 kg, and WC measurements below 40 cm or above 150 cm [46].

Outcome definition and metric calculation

Definition of CVD

The primary outcome of this investigation was incident CVD. We ascertained CVD status through standardized questionnaires administered during subsequent follow-up waves conducted in 2013, 2015, 2018, and 2020. CVD events were defined as a self-reported physician diagnosis of either heart condition or stroke. Specifically, heart condition encompassed myocardial infarction, coronary heart disease, angina, congestive heart failure, and other cardiac conditions. Follow-up duration was calculated from the baseline assessment to the date of the first reported event. To ensure the reliability of the outcome data, participants were required to reconfirm their diagnoses in successive waves. This classification protocol is consistent with previously established CHARLS methodologies and major international prospective cohort studies [42, 47].

Covariate assessment

Baseline information was ascertained through standardized questionnaires administered by trained interviewers. Sociodemographic characteristics included age, sex, place, education and marriage. Lifestyle factors, specifically smoking and drinking, were recorded alongside medical histories of hypertension and diabetes.

Blood pressure was measured using an electronic monitor (Omron HEM-7200) after a minimum rest period of 5 min. The final systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) values were determined by the average of three consecutive readings [48]. Hypertension was defined as SBP ≥ 140 mmHg, DBP ≥ 90 mmHg, a self-reported history, or the current use of antihypertensive medication [49]. Diabetes was identified by a self-reported diagnosis, current use of glucose-lowering agents, FBG ≥ 126 mg/dL, or glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥ 6.5%.

Physical examination

Physical examinations followed a rigorous standardized protocol where height and weight were measured with participants in light clothing and without shoes. BMI was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters. WC was measured at the umbilical level using a non-stretchable tape at the end of a normal expiration.

Laboratory testing

Fasting venous blood samples were obtained from all participants following a minimum fasting period of 8 h. Upon collection, blood samples were immediately centrifuged and stored at − 80 °C to ensure stability for subsequent biochemical analysis. Lipid profiles and glucose levels, including TG, FBG, and HDL, were measured using standard enzymatic methodologies. All laboratory procedures strictly adhered to the comprehensive quality control protocols established by the CHARLS project. Detailed descriptions of the sample collection processes and analytical techniques have been provided in previously published studies [45].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software version 4.4.3. Missing data among baseline covariates were addressed via median imputation for continuous variables and mode imputation for categorical variables, with missingness patterns detailed in Supplementary Fig. 1. Continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range) based on distribution normality, assessed via the Shapiro–Wilk test. Group differences were evaluated using t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests for continuous variables and Chi-square tests for categorical data, which are expressed as frequencies and percentages [n (%)].

Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the association between TyG-ABSI and incident CVD and its subtypes. We constructed three hierarchical models: a crude model (Model 1), a model adjusted for age and sex (Model 2), and a fully adjusted model (Model 3) incorporating residence, education, marital status, smoking, alcohol consumption, and histories of hypertension and diabetes. Prior to fitting the Cox proportional hazards regression models, the proportional hazards (PH) assumption was rigorously evaluated for the primary exposure (TyG-ABSI as a continuous metric and via quartiles) and all adjusting covariates using the Schoenfeld residuals test. A P-value > 0.05 indicated that the PH assumption was met. In the event of a violation, time-dependent covariate models or extended Cox regressions were planned for adjustment.

To evaluate the discriminative capacity of TyG-ABSI relative to five other TyG-related indices (TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-CVAI), we calculated the 5 year time-dependent area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and the C-index. Furthermore, the integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) and net reclassification improvement (NRI) were computed to quantify predictive increments, while decision curve analysis (DCA) assessed clinical net benefit. Stratified analyses and interaction tests were performed across subgroups to evaluate the consistency of the findings.

To verify the robustness of our primary findings, multiple rigorous sensitivity analyses were performed. First, history of glucose-lowering and lipid-lowering medication therapies was further adjusted for among the baseline covariates to comprehensively evaluate the potential confounding effects of clinical medication use on the outcome risks. Second, a lag-time analysis was conducted by excluding individuals who developed incident CVD within the first year of follow-up, thereby minimizing potential reverse causality bias. Third, a Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model was constructed to perform a competing risk analysis, accounting for the competing effect of non-composite CVD mortality on the target outcomes during the follow-up period. Furthermore, a population-specific sensitivity validation was carried out using an ABSI correction formula tailored specifically to the Chinese population [54]. To assess the potential impact of unmeasured residual confounding on the associations of the primary continuous variable, this study calculated the E‑value based on the risk estimates per‑SD increase for outcomes that reached statistical significance. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were utilized to perform multicollinearity diagnostics for all independent variables in Model 3, ruling out the impact of inter-variable interference on result bias. Finally, pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to evaluate the collinearity and internal relationships among the five baseline anthropometric indices (BMI, WC, ABSI, WHtR, and CVAI). All statistical analyses were two-sided, with a P-value < 0.05 defined as the threshold for statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study population

A total of 7197 participants were ultimately enrolled in this investigation, with the detailed screening process illustrated in Fig. 1. During the follow-up, the crude incidence rates for total CVD, heart condition, and stroke were 24.47, 17.21, and 7.77 per 1000 person-years, respectively (Supplementary Table 1). Baseline characteristics stratified by the incidence of cardiovascular events are summarized in Table 1. Compared with the non-CVD group, participants who developed CVD were significantly older and more likely to be female (both P < 0.001). Regarding metabolic profiles, individuals in the CVD group exhibited a markedly higher prevalence of hypertension and diabetes. Furthermore, biochemical indicators, specifically TC, TG, LDL, and HbA1c, were notably elevated in the CVD group (P < 0.001). Consistently, all TyG-derived indices, including TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, TyG-CVAI, and TyG-ABSI, were significantly higher in the CVD group compared to the non-CVD cohort (P < 0.001).

To further elucidate the internal relationships among the evaluated morphometric scales, a correlation matrix was constructed for the five baseline anthropometric indices (Supplementary Table 2). Association between TyG-ABSI and the risk of CVD and its subtypes

Prior to evaluating the risk ratios, statistical diagnostics based on the Schoenfeld residuals test confirmed that baseline TyG-ABSI, both as a continuous metric (P = 0.894) and partitioned into quartiles (P = 0.880), strictly satisfied the proportional hazards assumption.

The associations between TyG-ABSI and the risk of clinical outcomes, as estimated by Cox proportional hazards models, are presented in Table 2. In the crude model (Model 1), the risk of incident total CVD increased stepwise across quartiles of TyG-ABSI (Q1–Q4) (P for trend < 0.001). Compared with the lowest quartile (Q1, reference), individuals in the highest quartile (Q4) had a higher risk of CVD (HR = 1.56, 95% CI 1.33–1.83, P < 0.001). In the fully adjusted model (Model 3), only the Q3 group showed a significantly elevated risk (HR = 1.27, 95% CI 1.07–1.50, P = 0.005); the adjusted trend test remained significant (P = 0.033). Continuous variable analysis further confirmed that each SD increment in baseline TyG-ABSI was independently associated with an increased risk of incident total CVD in the fully adjusted model (HR = 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.14, P = 0.016).

Subgroup analysis revealed that TyG-ABSI is a robust and independent predictor of incident stroke. In the fully adjusted model (Model 3), each SD increment in TyG-ABSI was associated with a significantly increased risk of incident stroke (HR = 1.16, 95% CI 1.05–1.28, P = 0.003). Compared with Q1, the Q2, Q3, and Q4 groups all showed significantly elevated risks of stroke in the fully adjusted model, with HRs of 1.37 (95% CI 1.01–1.87, P = 0.043), 1.55 (95% CI 1.15–2.10, P = 0.004), and 1.60 (95% CI 1.18–2.18, P = 0.003), respectively. Exploration of dose–response associations

In the analysis of total CVD risk, the restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve showed a positive association (Poverall < 0.001). No significant non-linear association was observed between TyG-ABSI and CVD risk (Pnon-linear = 0.855). Subgroup analysis also revealed a significant overall association between TyG-ABSI and stroke risk (Poverall < 0.001; Pnon-linear = 0.728, Fig. 2). In contrast, the RCS analysis for heart condition revealed no significant dose–response relationship between TyG-ABSI and the risk of heart condition.

Comparison of predictive value among six indices

Comparison of model discriminative power

The predictive efficacy of each index over a 5 year follow-up period was evaluated using time-dependent ROC curves (Fig. 3). For the prediction of incident CVD, TyG-ABSI yielded an AUC of 0.601. The discriminatory capacity of TyG-ABSI was significantly lower than that of all evaluated composite indices, including TyG-BMI (AUC = 0.618), TyG-WC (AUC = 0.619), TyG-WHtR (AUC = 0.614), and TyG-CVAI (AUC = 0.617) (all P < 0.05; Fig. 4). Conversely, no statistically significant difference in predictive performance was observed between TyG-ABSI and the standalone TyG index (AUC = 0.600; P = 0.692). Notably, the standalone ABSI metric exhibited the lowest discriminatory power among all evaluated models, achieving an AUC of only 0.594.

In subtype-specific analyses, discriminative capacity for stroke was generally superior to that for heart condition. TyG-WC emerged as the optimal predictor for stroke (AUC 0.657). In contrast, TyG-ABSI achieved an AUC of 0.638, which was significantly outperformed by the other four obesity-related composite indices. These findings were further corroborated by C-index analyses, where TyG-BMI and TyG-WC exhibited the highest predictive consistency for CVD (C-index 0.611), whereas TyG-ABSI demonstrated a relatively lower C-index of 0.593 compared to other derivative models (Supplementary Table 3).

Evaluation of reclassification and discrimination improvement

To quantify the incremental predictive value, NRI and IDI were calculated (Supplementary Table 3). TyG-ABSI demonstrated no discriminative advantage over the standalone TyG index for overall CVD prediction. Furthermore, compared with TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-CVAI, TyG-ABSI exhibited significantly lower reclassification accuracy and discriminative power across all clinical outcomes. This trend was particularly pronounced in stroke prediction, where TyG-ABSI showed the most substantial reduction in predictive accuracy relative to TyG-WC (NRI = − 0.279, IDI = − 0.019, P = 0.002). Conversely, for CVD, TyG-ABSI demonstrated significant positive predictive increments compared with ABSI alone for CVD, most notably in stroke risk reclassification.

Clinical utility assessment

DCA demonstrated that all TyG-related models provided clinical net benefits across a broad range of threshold probabilities (Fig. 5). Notably, the decision curves for TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-CVAI were consistently positioned above the TyG-ABSI curve, indicating their superior clinical utility. Conversely, although the composite TyG-ABSI model offered a marginal expansion in net benefit compared back to the standalone ABSI and standalone TyG benchmarks, it still fell short of traditional models. These findings suggest that composite models incorporating traditional adiposity metrics may offer greater practical value for cardiovascular risk screening compared to those utilizing ABSI.

Subgroup analysis and interaction exploration

Subgroup analyses indicated that the positive association between TyG-ABSI and incident CVD risk remained statistically significant across the majority of strata (P < 0.05; Fig. 6). Marital status was identified as the sole factor demonstrating a significant interaction with the exposure (Pfor interaction = 0.018). Specifically, TyG-ABSI was significantly coupled with an increased CVD risk exclusively among married individuals (HR = 1.22, 95% CI 1.15–1.29, P < 0.001), whereas this association disappeared in the unmarried cohort (P = 0.897). Concurrently, the predictive effect did not reach statistical significance within several small-sample strata, including individuals aged 65 years or older, non-agricultural residents, and those utilizing glucose-lowering drug or lipid-lowering drugs.

Sensitivity analysis

Following further adjustment for history of glucose-lowering and lipid-lowering medications based on Model 3, baseline TyG-ABSI (as a continuous variable) remained independently and positively associated with the risk of incident total CVD and stroke (both P < 0.05). However, no such consistent association was observed for heart condition (Supplementary Table 4). In the lag-time analysis, which excluded incident CVD events occurring within the first year of follow-up, the results were consistent with the primary findings, thereby failing to support the presence of reverse causality bias (Supplementary Table 4). Upon utilizing the population-specific ABSI correction formula tailored for the Chinese population, the positive associations for composite CVD and stroke remained consistent with the primary findings, whereas no independent correlation was observed for heart conditions (Supplementary Table 4). The Fine-Gray competing risk model, treating non-CVD mortality as the competing event, demonstrated that TyG-ABSI as a continuous variable remained significantly associated with the subdistribution hazard of total CVD. In the quartile-based analysis, the risk was significantly elevated in the Q2 and Q3 groups, whereas the association in the highest quartile group (Q4) was attenuated due to competing risks (Supplementary Table 5). Additionally, we performed an E-value sensitivity analysis for the significant continuous variable associations to assess their potential vulnerability to unmeasured confounding. For each one-standard-deviation (per-SD) increase in TyG-ABSI, the point E-value was 1.601 for incident stroke and 1.361 for total CVD events. These findings suggest that although the independent predictive value of this index for incident stroke exhibited some robustness against potential unmeasured confounding, a certain degree of caution is warranted when interpreting the independent risk for total CVD. Furthermore, to further confirm the robustness of Model 3, we performed a multicollinearity diagnosis for all independent variables using the variance inflation factor (VIF). The results showed that the VIFs for all independent variables were below 5, indicating no evident multicollinearity (Supplementary Table 6).

Discussion

IR and obesity exhibit a profound synergistic interplay within the pathogenic framework of CVD [55–57]. Consequently, integrating these two metabolic dimensions into a cohesive risk stratification model has become an important component of contemporary clinical investigation. The TyG index serves as a pragmatic and validated surrogate marker for IR, with its efficacy in predicting heart condition and stroke corroborated across multiple large-scale prospective cohorts, often surpassing the predictive performance of HOMA-IR [58, 59]. Combining the TyG framework with either traditional purely morphometric traits (BMI, WC and WHtR) or the localized hybrid CVAI aims to simultaneously capture the risks associated with systemic metabolic derangements and ectopic fat distribution [60–62]. Previous evidence suggests that metrics reflecting central adiposity, specifically TyG-WC and TyG-BMI, demonstrate robust associations with CVD risk [63]. The findings of the present investigation align with these observations, as we identified superior predictive performance for TyG-WC and TyG-BMI, further reinforcing their utility in cardiovascular risk assessment.

Prior evidence has demonstrated a significant association between TyG-ABSI and all-cause mortality in patients with CVD, suggesting the clinical utility of this composite index for assessing long-term adverse prognosis [64]. The findings of this study confirm that baseline TyG-ABSI is an independent predictor of incident total CVD in the middle-aged and elderly Chinese population. In the fully adjusted model, each SD increment in baseline TyG-ABSI was associated with an 8% increase in the risk of incident total CVD. However, this study also revealed distinct heterogeneity in the predictive performance of TyG-ABSI across cardiovascular event subtypes. Compared with total CVD, the independent predictive value of TyG-ABSI for incident stroke was stronger. In the fully adjusted model, the risk of incident stroke exhibited a significant linear dose–response relationship with ascending baseline TyG-ABSI levels. Conversely, after adjusting for confounding factors in Model 3, no independent association was observed between baseline TyG-ABSI and incident heart condition. Crucially, compared with ABSI alone, TyG-ABSI comprehensively elevates the prospective tracking performance for CVD. This significant increment indicates that incorporating the TyG framework successfully compensates for the inherent single-dimension limitations of pure morphology by synergistically integrating essential insulin resistance and lipid metabolic signals.

Nevertheless, multi-dimensional evaluation metrics consistently demonstrated that the clinical net benefit of TyG-ABSI did not comprehensively outperform traditional derived indices, particularly TyG-BMI and TyG-WC, within this cohort. These findings suggest that due to the distinctive East Asian adiposity phenotype, the individual prognostic efficiency of TyG-ABSI for population-wide risk screening may not be as pronounced as the robust predictive capacity previously observed in Western demographic profiles.

Importantly, our cross-parameter correlation analysis (detailed in Supplementary Table 2) confirms that ABSI exhibits orthogonal independence from BMI (r = − 0.056). This biological and mathematical independence suggests that these parameters capture entirely distinct pathophysiological pathways. Consequently, instead of forcing a mutual exclusion between individual indices, future diagnostic optimization should consider transitioning toward the multi-dimensional Anthropometric Risk Indicator (ARI) framework [65]. By capitalizing on the strict mathematical orthogonality of these indicators, clinicians can synergistically evaluate independent, unaligned risk profiles: utilizing TyG-BMI as a sensitive proxy for generalized systemic adiposity and metabolic burden, while simultaneously leveraging TyG-ABSI to capture independent, visceral-tissue geometry. This integrated strategy offers a comprehensive, high-resolution approach to the precision prevention of CVD in the Chinese demographic, safeguarding critical pathogenic signals that single-dimension screenings routinely omit.

Several limitations of the present investigation warrant acknowledgment. First, as an observational study, the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured variables cannot be entirely dismissed, notwithstanding the implementation of robustly adjusted multivariable models. Second, the study population was restricted to Chinese adults aged 45 years and older. Given the unique metabolic profiles and adiposity phenotypes observed in this cohort, caution should be exercised when extrapolating these findings to younger individuals or diverse ethnic populations. Third, although CHARLS utilizes standardized protocols, the reliance on self-reported clinical diagnoses for certain outcomes may introduce potential recall bias, although such methods have been extensively validated in previous longitudinal research. Finally, despite integrating the CHARLS Exit Interview data to track fatal events, some deceased participants lacked specific clinical causes of death; treating these cases as competing events in our analysis confirmed the structural stability of the hazard trajectories, although a minor residual underestimation of cardiovascular mortality cannot be entirely excluded.

Conclusions

Utilizing the CHARLS prospective cohort, the present investigation confirmed a significant and independent association between TyG-ABSI and incident CVD risk. Notably, when evaluated as an isolated screening tool, TyG-ABSI did not exhibit superior incremental predictive value or clinical net benefit compared to traditional parameters like TyG-BMI or TyG-WC across this Chinese demographic.

However, given the near-perfect orthogonal independence and negligible collinearity between ABSI and BMI discovered in our cohort, TyG-ABSI should not be dismissed as a redundant metric. Instead, it captures unique, potential pathogenic signals independent of gross body mass. Consequently, future risk stratification toolkits should consider transitioning toward an integrated ARI framework that couples the metabolic sensitivity of TyG-BMI with the specific body shape risks signaled by TyG-ABSI, thereby achieving a more comprehensive, multi-dimensional precision prevention of cardiovascular disease.

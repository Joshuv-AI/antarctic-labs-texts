Impact of an early warning system on the outcome of patients with haematological malignancies receiving high-dose chemotherapy with or without haematopoietic cell transplantation: a protocol of the ALHERT cluster randomised trial
Authors: Mathilde Taillantou-Candau, Achille Kouatchet, Astrid Darsonval, Fanny Monmousseau, Elise Darras, Aline Schmidt, Jérémie Riou, Corentin Orvain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPatients with haematological malignancies undergoing intensive chemotherapy with or without haematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) are at high risk of complications that may require intensive care. While some studies advocate for early admission of these patients to the intensive care unit (ICU), it has not been formally demonstrated that admission to the ICU as soon as patients have organ injury may improve their outcomes. To demonstrate the efficacy of implementing the National Early Warning System (NEWS) with immediate ICU admission for patients with NEWS ≥7 to decrease hospital mortality and organ failure.Methods and analysisALHERT is a randomised cluster, controlled trial carried out in 10 French centres. With their informed consent, adult patients undergoing induction therapy for acute lymphoblastic or myeloblastic leukaemia, autologous or allogeneic HCT in one of the participating centres will be included. Five centres will follow local guidelines for ICU admission (control group) while five centres will assess the NEWS at least three times per day with immediate ICU admission for patients with NEWS ≥7 (interventional group). The primary outcome is hospital mortality without organ sustaining therapy. Secondary objectives will be to study the relative weight of each parameter used to calculate the NEWS for predicting the risk of organ failure requiring life-sustaining organ support, to evaluate the feasibility of implementing early warning scores in routine practice by analysing the proportion of patients with NEWS ≥7 effectively admitted to the ICU in the investigational arm, to assess quality of life with EQ-5D-5L (five level European Quality of Life five Dimensions) and assess the cost-effectiveness of this strategy.Ethics and disseminationThe protocol has been approved by the Comité de Protection des Personnes Ile de France I (Ethics Review ID-RCB: 2024-A00969-38). It is carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The findings of this study will be presented in peer-reviewed journals and at scientific conferences.Trial registration numberNCT06409767.Keywords: Bone marrow transplantation, Leukaemia, Adult intensive & critical care

Introduction

Patients with haematological malignancies (HMs) are particularly at risk of developing organ failure, due to the toxicity of the treatments administered and infectious complications due to the resulting immunosuppression.1 2 Around 15% of patients with newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) and 10–20% of allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplant recipients will require hospitalisation in an intensive care unit (ICU) during their treatment.3,7 The need for ICU admission severely compromises their prognosis, with an in-hospital mortality of 40% and up to 60% 1 year mortality despite recent progress.8 9 For patients developing one or more organ failures, some have shown that early transfer to ICU could reduce intubation rates and development of septic shock while improving survival of patients with organ injury.810,13 It is therefore suggested to admit patients with HM as soon as they develop organ injury, with several studies showing that time from clinical deterioration to intervention was associated with survival.11 14 15

Several teams have developed early warning scores (EWS), primarily for patients admitted on general medical and surgical wards, that are based on the use of simple algorithms based on physiological parameters (blood pressure, state of consciousness, respiratory rate, temperature, etc). These scores are readily used by the nursing staff to identify patients who may require rapid medical attention. In many countries, they are associated with clearly defined procedures or dedicated medical intervention teams. Unfortunately, even when associated with dedicated intervention teams, mixed results have been observed on their ability to reduce mortality.16,18 The National Early Warning System (NEWS) score was introduced by the Royal College of Physicians to standardise the use of EWS in the UK (table 1). With a value of 7 or more, it outperforms other scores in predicting deterioration (transfer to intensive care, death) in a wide range of medical settings (prehospital, emergency, general medical ward, surgical ward). It is easy to implement as most of its parameters are routinely collected by the nursing staff on wards (heart rate, blood pressure, pulsed oxygen saturation, respiratory rate, oxygen therapy requirement, body temperature, simplified neurological assessment).

In haematology, the use of NEWS is limited to a few retrospective studies. In particular, the NEWS score has been shown to be particularly effective in detecting patients at risk of deterioration after induction chemotherapy for AML or allograft (area under the curve 0.9).19,21 In a before/after study, the implementation of an EWS system with a dedicated intervention team was associated with an improved prognosis for patients admitted to the ICU.22 However, we cannot rule out the possibility that this improvement may be due to closer collaboration between haematology and intensive care teams.

Aims and hypothesis

The aim of the ALHERT study is to demonstrate that implementing NEWS evaluation with immediate ICU admission if NEWS ≥7 will improve the outcome of patients with HM receiving intensive chemotherapy with or without haematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT). We hypothesise that monitoring simple warning factors (vital signs) can help identify patients with an increased risk of subsequent organ failure. It would therefore be possible to consider early ICU admission for these patients to improve their outcome. This study would further validate the use of EWS, widely used in many countries, in patients with HM receiving intensive chemotherapy for which data is scarce. We will therefore lead a multicentre cluster randomised trial in 10 academic centres in France with five centres continuing usual care (control group) and five centres implementing a strategy with immediate ICU admission in patients with NEWS ≥7 (interventional group).

Methods and analysis

Study design

The ALHERT trial will be a randomised cluster randomised interventional trial led in 10 French academic centres for patients receiving induction therapy for acute leukaemia or auto/allogeneic HCT.

Trial objectives and outcomes

Primary objective and outcome

The main objective will be to demonstrate that repetitive NEWS assessments and immediate ICU admission for patients with NEWS ≥7 will improve in-hospital survival without initiation of life-sustaining organ support. The primary objective will be the rate of in-hospital survival without initiation of life-sustaining organ support defined as invasive mechanical ventilation, vasopressors (epinephrine, dobutamine and/or norepinephrine), renal replacement therapy (RRT) and/or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO).

Secondary objectives and outcomes

The first secondary objective will be to study the relative weight of each parameter used to calculate the NEWS (respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, oxygen requirement, heart rate, arterial pressure, level of consciousness and body temperature) for predicting the risk of organ failure requiring life-sustaining organ support (invasive mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, RRT, ECMO).

The second objective will be to evaluate the feasibility of implementing EWS in routine practice by analysing the proportion of patients with NEWS ≥7 effectively admitted to the ICU in the investigational arm.

The third objective will be to assess quality of life with EQ-5D-5L measured at days 0, 30 and 90 following trial enrolment.

Finally, we will perform a trial-based economic evaluation to assess the cost-effectiveness of incorporating an EWS with immediate ICU admission in patients with HM. Thus, a cost-utility analysis will estimate the incremental cost-utility ratio (ICUR) in cost per quality-adjusted life-year gained and a cost-effectiveness analysis will estimate the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) in cost per life-year gained at 3 months from the healthcare system perspective.

Experimental plan

In this cluster randomised clinical trial, patients will be recruited in 10 academic centres in France (table 2). Each centre will be randomised before the start of the study between usual care (control group) and investigational care with regular NEWS assessment and immediate ICU admission for patients with NEWS ≥7 (interventional group; figure 1). All centres will remain in the same randomised cluster throughout the trial. The randomisation will be carried out by a statistician of the Research and Innovation Department of Angers University Hospital (including the data management). No data monitoring committee has been set up, as this is not a drug study but an implemented strategy.

Randomisation

Randomisation will be performed by random allocation of clusters using random block sizes, randomly distributed between the two arms. The randomisation sequence will be generated by an independent statistician from the Statistics Department of Angers University Hospital using R software, in accordance with international guidelines for clinical trials (eg, Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) extension for cluster randomised trials, ICH E6). The randomisation procedure will be fully automated, and the sequence will be stored in a secure, password-protected database to ensure its integrity and prevent any manipulation.

Study setting and population

The ALHERT trial will include adult patients with HM undergoing induction therapy for acute lymphoblastic or myeloblastic leukaemia (first cycle of induction therapy only), autologous or allogeneic HCT with an anticipated stay in the hospital of at least 7 days. It will be a continuous recruitment process. Therefore, consecutive adult patients who are admitted for intensive chemotherapy with or without HCT to one of the participating centres will be assessed for inclusion. They must have up-to-date health insurance coverage and express in writing their consent to participate in the study (informed consent form in online supplemental material after verbal and written explanations of the procedure, as recommended in clinical and research good practices. If the patient is unable to consent, then the physician will seek consent from a trusted person, family member or close relative. In the case of refusal, the patient will not be included in the trial (L1122-1-2 article of the French Public Health Code). A patient can be included more than once if included during induction therapy and now undergoing allogeneic HCT in a time frame of 3 months between the two inclusions. Recruitment began on 10 April 2025 and will end on 11 April 2028.

Description of the intervention

In both groups, patients admitted for HM requiring induction therapy for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia or AML, autologous or allogeneic HCT will be evaluated for potential inclusion. After verifying eligibility and obtaining patient consent, the investigator will proceed to inclusion. Patient characteristics, including demographic data, type of HM, previous treatment, performance status, quality of life using the EQ-5D-5L questionnaire and comorbidities, will be collected. The clinical data will be recorded in an electronic case report form (eCRF), available on smartphones, tablets and computers and secured by a personal password. Patient healthcare consumption over the 3-month period (hospital stays and outpatient care) will be extracted from the medical information systems of the participating centres. All personal data will be subsequently anonymised. Participants can participate in other interventional trials during the ALHERT study participation period.

Control group

In this group, the NEWS will not be calculated, and physicians will be free to decide whether a patient should be admitted to the ICU according to local practice. Parameters used to calculate the NEWS will be collected, but the NEWS will not be calculated. Each contact made with the ICU specialist will be recorded in the eCRF. Patients admitted to the ICU will have a daily evaluation of organ support requirement. CMO orders will be recorded, and patients will be excluded from the study.

Experimental group

In this group, the NEWS will be prospectively calculated at each assessment of vital parameters by the nursing staff (at least every 8 hours) and patient with NEWS ≥7 will be admitted to the ICU within 3 hours. Each contact made with the ICU specialist will be recorded in the eCRF; the reason for not admitting a patient with NEWS ≥7 will also be recorded. Patients admitted to the ICU will have a daily evaluation of organ support requirement. CMO orders will be recorded, and patients will be excluded from the study.

Follow-up

In both groups, enrolled patients will have a follow-up visit at one and 3 months following inclusion for assessment of EQ-5D-5L. Mortality at 3 months will be recorded.

Statistical analysis

All analyses will be conducted by a statistician from the Biostatistics and Methodology Unit of Angers University Hospital, independent from the statistician who performed the randomisation, following the CONSORT extension for randomised trials guidelines.23 Analyses will be performed using R software (R Core Team, 2018, a language and environment for statistical computing, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Descriptive analysis

Quantitative variables will be described as mean±SD in cases of Gaussian distribution. Otherwise, they will be described as median and IQR. Qualitative variables will be described as numbers and frequencies. A comparison of patient characteristics between the two strategies will be evaluated using the Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Primary outcome

To assess the effect of treatment on in-hospital survival without organ support, we will use a Cox proportional hazards model with shared frailty, where frailty terms will model intracentre correlation and unobserved heterogeneity across the 10 participating centres. Frailty terms will follow a Gamma distribution with variance estimated by maximum likelihood, with quantification of between-centre variability in baseline hazards.

The model will be adjusted for established prognostic variables identified in literature, including age, type of HM and type of therapy. The relationship between age and outcome will be modelled using fractional polynomial transformations to capture potential non-linearities.

Variable selection will be performed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regularisation initially applied to a standard Cox model, while forcing inclusion of the treatment arm variable. Selected covariates will then be incorporated into the final shared frailty model, as this two-step approach is necessary since penalisation techniques are not directly applicable to frailty models.

The proportional hazards assumption will be assessed through visual inspection of log-log survival curves stratified by centre and analysis of scaled Schoenfeld residuals. Additionally, we will apply the Therneau and Grambsch test adapted for frailty models to formally test the proportional hazards assumption while accounting for the frailty component. We acknowledge that standard diagnostics may be affected by the presence of frailty, particularly under strong between-centre heterogeneity. Model adequacy will also be evaluated by comparing with a standard Cox model using likelihood ratio tests.

We will report adjusted HRs for the treatment effect with 95% CIs and associated p values. The estimated frailty variance and its CI will be presented to quantify between-centre heterogeneity. Interpretation of HRs will account for the fact that they represent conditional effects given the frailty.

Secondary outcomes

The first secondary outcome will be analysed with a Cox proportional hazards model with shared frailty to explain the occurrence over time of one or more organ support, following the same methodology as for the primary objective. However, no variable selection will be performed for this analysis. The model’s explanatory variables will be the different parameters used to calculate the NEWS score. The analysis will be performed globally and then stratified by treatment arm.

The feasibility of incorporating NEWS assessment and immediate ICU admission for patients with NEWS ≥7 will be analysed by estimating the proportion of patients with NEWS ≥7 admitted to the ICU in the intervention group, with its 95% CI.

Quality of life scores measured by the EQ-5D-5L questionnaire at days 0, 30 and 90 will be analysed using linear mixed models (LMM) to account for repeated measures, for both the overall score and each dimension (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression). The variance-covariance matrix will be unstructured. Each dimension will be analysed independently using similar LMM structures. The Fisher test (F-test) will assess the significance of the time effect for each dimension. The evolution between the two treatment arms will be tested through p values derived from the treatment×time interaction term. Multiple testing correction using the Holm method will be applied to control the family-wise error rate across dimensions. Model assumptions (normality of residuals via Q-Q plots and homoscedasticity via residuals vs fitted values plots) will be checked a posteriori. Results will be presented as mean profile graphs with 95% CIs for each dimension and the overall score. The evolution of profiles between the two treatment arms will be visualised using radar diagrams.

The ICUR and ICER will be estimated based on the difference in cost and outcome (utility and survival) between patients with EWS and early ICU admission compared with usual care. Sensitivity analyses will assess the uncertainty surrounding the estimates.

Missing data

No imputation of missing data is planned for the primary and secondary outcomes. Complete case analysis will be performed, excluding patients with missing data from the relevant analyses. Missing data patterns will be analysed to determine whether they are informative and whether they are likely to lead to potential selection or information bias. For the health economic evaluation, an appropriate imputation strategy will be considered according to the proportion and the structure of missing data. Missing data will be described and treated according to standard methods in health economic evaluation.24

Multiple testing

A hierarchical management of objectives will be carried out to limit the problem of multiplicity. Moreover, when necessary, a correction will be made with a control of the family-wise error rate at a risk of 5% using the Holm method.

Trial results will be reported in accordance with the CONSORT.23

Sample size calculation

Sample size was calculated for a cluster randomised trial with 10 centres. To demonstrate a reduction in the primary endpoint from 15% to 10% with an alpha risk of 5%, a power of 80% and an intracluster correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.01, a total of 2224 patients will be required. The ICC was estimated at 0.01 given the hard nature of the primary endpoint (in-hospital mortality), which typically exhibits low between-cluster variability. For practical purposes, sample size calculation was based on event proportions as an approximation, which provides a conservative estimate when the primary endpoint will be analysed using time-to-event methods with shared frailty models. The prevalence of the main event of 15% is that observed in our practice during acute myeloblastic leukaemia induction, autologous or allogeneic HCT.10 25

Accounting for an anticipated 5% loss to follow-up (including patients with limitations of care or missing data), a total of 2336 patients will be included (approximately 234 patients per centre). Based on the usual recruitment capacity of participating centres, a 3-year inclusion period is planned to achieve the target sample size. In each participating centre, around 50 patients receive induction therapy per year, 50 receive autologous HCT per year and 50 allogeneic HCT per year, advocating for the feasibility of recruitment.

Patient and public involvement

The current trial will be conducted without direct patient involvement. The ethics committee (Comité de Protection des Personnes Ile de France I) includes patient representatives, charged with the responsibility of protecting patient rights; thus, the ALHERT trial protocol was reviewed by a patient representative. Besides the above review process, patients will not be invited to comment on the study design and interpretation of the study results. Patients were not involved in the writing of this manuscript.

Ethics and dissemination

Informed consent will be obtained from all study participants. An institutional review board authorised the study (Comité de Protection des Personnes Ile de France I, ID-RCB: 2024-A00969-38, 04 June 2024) for all participating centres. The trial protocol is reported here based on the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines.26

The results of this study will be published in peer-reviewed manuscripts and will be presented to local community groups and stakeholders, as well as at national and international conferences as applicable. The authorship guidelines will be followed for all relevant publications and presentations. Open access publication of this protocol will facilitate full public access.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study protocol describes the first prospective, multicentric study evaluating the implementation of an EWS with immediate ICU admission for patients with HM undergoing induction therapy for acute leukaemia or autologous or allogeneic HCT.

The NEWS was chosen to standardise early admission between participating centres with ICU admission as soon as patients develop the first signs of organ injury. It is a well-validated EWS in many different medical and surgical situations. Its ability to predict future organ deterioration has been confirmed in patients with HM in two large retrospective cohort studies.20 21 It is very simple to implement in departments that are routinely used to frequent monitoring of vital signs. As a secondary objective, we will assess which parameters used to compute the NEWS are the most predictive for organ deterioration, especially in the control group. With these analyses, the development of a modified NEWS, specific for the population of patients with HM receiving intensive chemotherapy, will be considered.

Several limitations of our study design must be acknowledged. First, we could not lead a preliminary phase of our study to assess potential enrolment numbers. While all participating centres have sufficient patient numbers that fit eligibility criteria, it is unknown to which extent patients will accept participation. Second, although participating centres do not routinely use EWS for consideration of ICU admission, we cannot exclude that centres in the control group will start using the NEWS during trial participation. Nevertheless, when analysing results, we will check that timing from NEWS ≥7 to ICU admission remains constant over time. Third, trial participation was limited to patients receiving intensive chemotherapy with or without HCT that is available in academic centres limiting trial generalisability to patients with HM receiving intensive chemotherapy in academic centres.

In this study, we will verify the concept of early admission of patients with HM showing early signs of organ injury. It is the first time that early ICU admission will be considered in patients with HM in a prospective controlled manner. We hope that early ICU admission will improve the outcome of patients with HM by continuous surveillance and early initiation of diagnostic and therapeutic procedures that are only feasible in an ICU department. We are also aware of some possible caveats, including feasibility, cost and quality of life, with such an early ICU admission strategy that will be assessed with our secondary objectives. The ability of each ICU department to admit patients with HM and NEWS ≥7 will be monitored closely in the interventional centres so as not to excessively use a precious resource that is an ICU bed. The higher cost with anticipated higher ICU admissions in the interventional centres will also be analysed with a specific health economic evaluation. Finally, it will be important to monitor the quality of life of patients, especially in the interventional centres, as ICU admission has been previously associated with increased post-traumatic stress disorders in HCT recipients. However, if positive, our study could show improved quality of life in patients who will have a significant reduction in the initiation of life-sustaining organ support.

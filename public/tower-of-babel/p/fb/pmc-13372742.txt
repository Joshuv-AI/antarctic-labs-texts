Efficient management of EV charging stations and DSTATCOM in distribution systems through POA-DTRSRN approach
Authors: S Balavignesh, C Kumar, N Nalini, Hady H Fayek
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe construction of infrastructure for electric vehicle charging stations (EVCS) is essential to the industry’s sustainable growth in the electric vehicle market. However, the distribution system’s performance can suffer as a result of EVCS installation. A novel hybrid method for managing EVCS with Distribution Static Synchronous Compensator (DSTATCOM) in distribution systems is proposed in this study. The hybrid method is the integration of Pufferfish Optimization Algorithm (POA) with Double Transformer Residual Super-Resolution Network (DTRSRN), termed as POA-DTRSRN approach. The main objective of this study is to minimize the power loss, reduce cost and improve the overall efficiency. The placement and size of the EVCS and DSTATCOM in distribution systems is optimized using the POA method. The charging demand of the electric vehicles is predicted by the DTRSRN method. The performance of the proposed method is analyzed using MATLAB and is compared with existing methods like African Vultures Optimization Algorithm (AVOA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Backward Bat Algorithm (BBA). The proposed method yields a low cost of $1450. The outcomes demonstrate that the proposed POA-DTRSRN technique outperforms the existing techniques in terms of cost-effectiveness and energy efficiency. Thus, this approach presents a promising solution for optimizing EVCS and DSTATCOM management in modern distribution systems.Keywords: Electric Vehicles, Charging Stations, DSTATCOM, Puffer Fish Optimization Algorithm, Power Loss, Electricity CostSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

The advent and broad use of alternative energy sources in the modern era have attracted a lot of interest globally1. Electricity use in buildings accounted for nearly 60% of overall growth in 2024. Electric vehicles, sometimes known as electric cars or EVs, are growing in popularity as a representation of the development and application of these novel energy technologies2. The demand for EVs has significantly increased during the last ten years3,4. Due to their exceptional ability to reduce carbon dioxide (CO_2) emissions and their operational cost effectiveness as compared to vehicles with traditional internal combustion engines5,6. Because they provide a practical and accessible means of recharging EV batteries, EVCSs are essential in encouraging the wider usage of EVs9. But integrating these CS with the radial distribution system presents special difficulties that call for thoughtful analysis and calculated preparation10.

Integrating EVCS with DSTATCOM in distribution systems presents challenges primarily related to voltage fluctuations, harmonic distortion, power quality issues, and control coordination24. Voltage fluctuations may result from the abrupt load changes caused by EV charging; therefore, the DSTATCOM must act quickly to keep the voltage within allowable bounds. Additionally, both EV chargers and DSTATCOMs can introduce harmonic distortion into the system, requiring careful coordination to mitigate these harmonics without causing interference25. Moreover, EV chargers may introduce power quality issues like voltage sags and swells, flicker, and imbalance, which DSTATCOMs need to compensate for while providing reactive power support26. Achieving effective control and coordination between EV chargers and DSTATCOMs is crucial for ensuring optimal system performance and reliability amidst these challenges.

Double transformer residual super-resolution network

The algorithm known as DTRSRN is employed to forecast EV charging trends. The DTRSRN network is a cross-resolution person re-identification technique20. The proposed method consists of two main integrated components: the person re-identification module and the super-resolution module. The super-resolution module includes an attention mechanism based on a twin transformer network. The characteristics that the residual network collected are processed by this network, separating them into distinct vectors for each spatial position and dividing the tensor according to spatial locations. Due to its residual learning framework and dual transformer blocks, DTRSRN is appropriate for EV load forecasting even though it was initially created for image super-resolution. While residual connections enhance training stability and convergence, the self-attention mechanism allows for efficient modeling of temporal charging patterns and nonlinear demand changes. EV charging demand is reliably predicted by the model by adjusting spatial operations to the time dimension. Complex nonlinear temporal dynamics are better modeled by DTRSRN compared with existing methods.

Together, these modules enhance the system’s ability to manage EVCS and DSTATCOM efficiently by improving resolution and accurately predicting vehicle charging demand. Dataset description and experimental setup

The EV charging demand dataset used for the DTRSRN-based prediction model consists of 8,760 hourly samples representing one full year (24 × 365 h). Realistic temporal fluctuations in EV charging behavior that have a direct impact on distribution system loading circumstances are captured in the dataset. A chronological split was used to maintain temporal dependency and stop information leakage because EV demand prediction is a time-series challenge. In particular, training accounted for 70% of the data (6,132 samples), validation for 15% (1,314 samples), and testing for the remaining 15% (1,314 samples). To preserve sequential consistency, random shuffling was not used. The training set was used to train the DTRSRN model, and the testing set was used for final evaluation after hyperparameters were adjusted depending on validation performance. The IEEE radial distribution system model was then merged with the anticipated EV charging demand from the testing phase to determine the best location and size for EVCS and DSTATCOM using the POA method. Discussion

The efficiency and benefits of the proposed POA-DTRSRN approach for controlling EVCS and DSTATCOM in distribution networks are highlighted by the examination of the simulation results. When DSTATCOM and EVCS are integrated using the POA-DTRSRN technique, power loss is significantly reduced, leading to a more stable and lower power loss profile than with other approaches. The hybrid approach demonstrates remarkable cost-effectiveness, with a final cost of $1450, which is substantially lower than traditional optimization techniques such as PSO, AVOA and BBA. This cost reduction highlights the economic efficiency of the POA-DTRSRN method while maintaining high performance.

Furthermore, the voltage profile analysis indicates that the inclusion of DSTATCOM enhances voltage stability, with less fluctuation and improved minimum voltage values across the distribution system. This improved voltage regulation contributes to the overall stability and reliability of the system. The objective function analysis reveals that the POA-DTRSRN method converges to a stable value after initial fluctuations, indicating its robustness in achieving optimal performance. Overall, the POA-DTRSRN strategy offers a viable and efficient way to optimize the integration of EVCS and DSTATCOM, along with notable gains in voltage stability, cost-effectiveness, and power loss reduction. This makes it a valuable contribution to the field, with implications for future research and practical implementation in electric vehicle infrastructure management.

Conclusion

This study proposed a hybrid method combining POA with DTRSRN for energy management in distribution system combining EVCS and DSTATCOM. The hybrid approach is aimed at minimizing power loss and cost. The POA algorithm optimizes the placement and size of DSTATCOM and EVCS, while the DTRSRN algorithm predicts the charging demand of electric vehicles. The proposed strategy is put into practice in MATLAB and contrasted with other existing approaches like AVOA, BBA, and PSO. The proposed technique yields a significantly lower cost value of $1450 compared to the existing methods, which report costs of $2450, $3450, and $4450 for AVOA, BBA, and PSO, respectively. This outcome confirms that the proposed technique is more cost-effective than the existing methods. However, it is best suited for solving specific optimization problems, particularly those with a continuous search space and a well-defined fitness function.

Future scope

In the future, advanced control strategies for DSTATCOM operation could be explored to further improve its ability to mitigate voltage fluctuations and enhance power quality in distribution systems integrated with EV charging stations. This may involve the use of adaptive control algorithms or machine learning-based approaches to optimize DSTATCOM performance based on real-time system conditions and EV charging patterns. Furthermore, the integration of renewable energy sources such as solar and wind, along with stochastic modeling to address uncertainties in EV charging demand and load variations, can further enhance system reliability.

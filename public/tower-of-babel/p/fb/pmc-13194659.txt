Bilibili and TikTok as sources of information on atrophic gastritis and the quality of their content
Authors: Jingwen Qin, Zhaokai Zhang, Yongjun Qin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractShort video platforms have emerged as a prominent source of health information, including content related to chronic atrophic gastritis (AG). However, the quality and reliability of AG-related content on these platforms have not been systematically assessed. We selected 100 AG-related videos from each of Bilibili and TikTok, collecting data on video characteristics, including uploader information, engagement metrics, and content. The Global Quality Score (GQS), modified DISCERN score (mDISCERN), and JAMA score were used to evaluate the videos. Statistical analyses were conducted to assess differences in quality across platforms, video sources, and video content. Despite a shorter median duration (97.50 s), TikTok videos achieved higher user engagement and statistically higher quality scores (p < 0.01) compared to Bilibili. Treatment was the most frequent topic on both platforms (80.0%). Although Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) physicians constituted the largest uploader group (57.5%), gastroenterologists produced content with the highest quality scores (median GQS: 4.00; mDISCERN: 4.00; JAMA: 3.00) and significantly greater engagement (p < 0.01). TikTok exhibited relatively higher user engagement and higher quality scores, though both platforms showed moderate overall quality and considerable potential for enhancement. Introduction

Atrophic gastritis (AG) is a chronic disorder of the digestive system, characterized by the atrophy of gastric mucosal glands, decreased secretion of gastric acid and pepsin, and is often accompanied by the infiltration of inflammatory cells in the mucosa and intestinal metaplasia. The causes of AG are multifactorial, including Helicobacter pylori (HP) infection, bile reflux, autoimmune mechanisms, genetic susceptibility, and unhealthy lifestyle habits, with HP infection being the most predominant cause. Clinically, AG presents with a variety of symptoms, such as dull pain in the upper abdomen, belching, loss of appetite, digestive disturbances, and weight loss. However, most patients do not exhibit obvious symptoms, and the atrophic changes are usually discovered only during routine physical exams or gastroscopy. Endoscopic features of AG include pale and thinned mucosa with increased visibility of blood vessels, loss of mucosal folds, and, in cases of intestinal metaplasia, the presence of light-blue crests and white opaque areas1–3. AG is widely recognized as a precursor to gastric cancer, a condition that not only causes symptoms associated with reduced gastric acid secretion but also significantly increases the risk of gastric carcinogenesis4,5. Inaccurate or exaggerated AG-related information may not only delay appropriate surveillance but also increase psychological distress due to misperceived cancer risk. As awareness of the causes, symptoms, and potential risks of AG continues to grow, the condition has become a topic of increasing concern for both healthcare professionals and the general public.

The rapid advancement of mobile internet technologies and the widespread use of social media platforms have fundamentally changed how the public acquires, shares, and interacts with health information6. Among these platforms, short-video apps like Bilibili and TikTok have emerged as powerful tools for health communication. While traditional written or academic resources remain authoritative in the field of medical knowledge, their complexity often limits accessibility to the broader population. In contrast, short videos, with their simplicity, visual appeal, and interactivity, have become crucial channels for disseminating health information. By presenting medical concepts in concise and visually engaging formats, these platforms increase accessibility to health information7–9.

Despite their increasing role in health communication, concerns remain about the accuracy and reliability of medical content on these platforms10. The lack of peer review and insufficient regulatory oversight means that the threshold for content creation is low. Many health-related videos are produced by individuals without formal medical training or by commercial entities, resulting in significant variability in content quality. Consequently, inaccurate, exaggerated, or misleading information can spread rapidly, distorting public understanding of diseases and treatments, influencing health behaviors negatively, and even posing public health risks11,12.

Although several studies have examined gastrointestinal disease-related content on social media platforms—such as gastric cancer, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), upper gastrointestinal bleeding, and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)—no research has specifically investigated the quality and reliability of videos related to AG13–16. Unlike acute gastric conditions, AG is a long-term precancerous condition that requires sustained surveillance, patient adherence to follow-up endoscopy, and accurate understanding of cancer risk1. Miscommunication or oversimplification of AG-related information may therefore have distinct consequences, including delayed surveillance or unnecessary anxiety. Evaluating how AG is portrayed on short-video platforms provides insights not only into information quality, but also into risk framing and preventive health communication for precancerous diseases. To address this gap, our study aims to systematically assess videos related to chronic atrophic gastritis available on Bilibili and TikTok. We focus on analyzing the content characteristics, presentation styles, and uploader profiles, with particular attention to the quality and reliability of the health information provided. Our findings can inform future content moderation policies, encourage the creation of high-quality educational materials, and contribute to the development of an online environment where health information is shared responsibly. Ultimately, improving information quality may support more informed health decisions.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

The videos analyzed in this study were sourced from Bilibili (www.bilibili.com) and TikTok (Chinese version: www.douyin.com). Videos were retrieved by searching for the keyword “萎缩性胃炎” on both platforms and were retrieved based on the default ‘comprehensive ranking’ order of each platform. This ranking is algorithmically determined by the platforms to prioritize content based on relevance, user engagement, and recency, thus representing the information most likely to be encountered by a typical user. To minimize algorithmic bias, the search was conducted using the ‘Incognito’ mode on browsers with all historical data cleared, ensuring results were based on default platform rankings rather than personalized user history. Videos uploaded within one week of the search date were excluded to ensure that engagement metrics (e.g., likes, shares) had reached a relatively stable state, as newly uploaded content often undergoes rapid initial fluctuation in visibility. The following exclusion criteria were applied: (1) videos irrelevant to the topic; (2) advertisements or promotional material; (3) videos uploaded within one week prior to data collection; and (4) duplicate videos. Video retrieval was completed within a single day to minimize bias introduced by newly uploaded content. Finally, the top 100 videos meeting the inclusion criteria from each platform were selected for further analysis. The detailed collection procedure is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Flowchart of video selection for atrophic gastritis analysis.

Data collection

Videos were evaluated based on several characteristics, including video duration, interaction metrics (likes, comments, shares, and collections), and uploader type. Video duration was recorded in seconds, and interaction metrics were extracted directly from platform statistics. Video topics were categorized into six groups: etiology/prevention, symptom, diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, and endoscopy. Video formats were classified into five types: medical scenarios, PPT/presentation, Q&A, solo narration, and others. Uploaders were grouped into five categories: gastroenterologists, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) physicians, other specialists, patients/individual users, and organizations.

Quality and reliability assessment

Video quality and reliability were assessed using three widely accepted evaluation tools: the Global Quality Score (GQS) (Supplementary Table 1), the modified DISCERN score (mDISCERN) (Supplementary Table 2), and the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) benchmark criteria (Supplementary Table 3). Each tool provides a standardized evaluation of content accuracy, comprehensiveness, and credibility. The GQS was used to assess dimensions such as quality, flow, comprehensiveness, and usefulness to patients, with scores ranging from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent)17.The DISCERN instrument, widely recognized in academia for evaluating the quality of health information, was adapted to the short-video format while retaining its core criteria18,19. The mDISCERN scores ranged from 1 to 5, reflecting the reliability and quality of medical information20,21. The JAMA score comprises four key criteria—authorship, attribution, currency, and disclosure—each worth 1 point, yielding a total score from 0 to 4. This tool primarily assesses the clarity and evidence-based quality of the information22. Although GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA were initially developed for text-based information, they remain the gold standard for assessing the quality and reliability of medical content in digital media research. In this study, these tools were used to evaluate the informational integrity and educational utility of the videos, regardless of their delivery format.

Two researchers (Jingwen Qin and Zhaokai Zhang) independently reviewed and rated the videos. In case of disagreement, a final decision was made by a senior gastroenterologist (Yongjun Qin). Prior to scoring, all raters underwent uniform training to ensure consistency and minimize bias. The Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) was calculated, with values ranging from 0.82 to 0.89, demonstrating a high level of consistency between the reviewers.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using IBM SPSS 26.0, while data visualization was conducted with R (version 4.4.1) and GraphPad Prism 9.6. Descriptive statistical methods were employed to summarize the continuous variables. Data with a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while data with a non-normal distribution are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are reported as frequency and percentage. For group comparisons, variables with a normal distribution are analyzed using the independent samples t-test, whereas variables with a non-normal distribution are analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. For comparisons involving three or more groups, the Kruskal–Wallis H test is employed; if the result is statistically significant, Dunn’s test is subsequently used for pairwise comparisons. The Spearman rank correlation coefficient is applied to assess the correlation between video quality scores (GQS, mDISCERN and JAMA) and interaction indicators (likes, comments, shares, and collections). A p value less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Result

Video characteristics

A total of 200 videos related to atrophic gastritis were included for analysis after screening (Fig. 1), with 100 from Bilibili and 100 from TikTok. The general characteristics of the videos are summarized in Table 1. TikTok videos were significantly shorter than those on Bilibili (p < 0.001). Despite their shorter duration, TikTok videos received markedly higher levels of engagement, including likes, collections, comments, and shares. With respect to quality and reliability, TikTok videos demonstrated higher mDISCERN scores (p = 0.004) and JAMA scores p < 0.001). GQS scores were slightly higher on TikTok as well (p = 0.005). Regarding video content, treatment was the most frequently addressed topic on both platforms (80.0%), followed by symptom, prognosis, and endoscopy. Content distributions differed significantly between platforms (p < 0.001). Analysis of uploaders revealed that TCM physicians constituted the largest contributor group (57.5%), followed by gastroenterologists (27.5%). TikTok contained a higher proportion of gastroenterologist-uploaded videos (36.0%), while organizations uploaded videos were found exclusively on Bilibili.

Video content and Style

The distribution of video content across the six categories varied between the two platforms (Fig. 2A). Bilibili contained higher proportions of videos related to diagnosis, symptoms, and endoscopy, whereas TikTok featured a relatively larger share of treatment-focused content. Overall, content on Bilibili covered a broader range of medical topics, while TikTok videos tended to concentrate more on therapeutic information. Regarding presentation styles, solo narration was the most prevalent format on both platforms (Fig. 2B). Bilibili hosted relatively more Q&A and PPT/presentation-style videos, whereas medical scenarios were more frequently observed on TikTok. As shown in Table 2, these presentation styles were significantly associated with differences in video length (p < 0.001), with “Others” and “Medical Scenario” videos generally being longer. However, audience engagement metrics—including likes, collections, comments, and shares—did not differ significantly across the various video styles.

Video quality scores also varied notably by uploader type (Table 3; Fig. 3C–E). Gastroenterologists consistently achieved the highest scores across GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA assessments. Conversely, content from individual users/patients scored the lowest, while TCM physicians and other specialists provided information of intermediate quality, which was further characterized by a high degree of heterogeneity.

Video quality and reliability analysis

A comparison of video quality and reliability between the two platforms revealed that TikTok videos achieved statistically higher scores across all evaluated metrics compared to Bilibili (p < 0.01, Fig. 4). Although median scores were similar in some instances, TikTok videos demonstrated more consistent performance, with distributions concentrated toward the higher scoring range. In contrast, scores for Bilibili videos were more dispersed and often skewed toward lower values. However, these differences were small in absolute terms and may reflect platform structure (e.g., recommendation algorithms, user demographics, and typical video formats) rather than intrinsic differences in content reliability.

Correlation analysis

Spearman correlation matrices in Fig. 5 demonstrated relationships among video characteristics and quality scores. Strong positive correlations were observed among engagement metrics, with correlation coefficients generally exceeding 0.80. Discussion

With the rapid development of mobile internet technologies, short-video platforms such as Bilibili and TikTok have become deeply integrated into everyday life and are increasingly recognized as important channels for health information dissemination. Growing evidence suggests that the convenience, accessibility, and highly engaging nature of these platforms can help improve public health literacy, correct misconceptions, and promote health-seeking behaviors23,24. However, rise of user-generated content has increased concerns about accuracy and scientific rigor. Such misinformation can mislead patients and pose public-health risks. Many users lack the necessary skills to critically evaluate online health content, and the pursuit of visibility or audience engagement may encourage some creators to oversimplify, exaggerate, or selectively present health-related content25. Given that medical information strongly influences personal decision-making, ensuring the reliability of online health content is essential for safeguarding public health.

AG is a common chronic inflammatory condition and a recognized precancerous lesion that requires long-term surveillance and appropriate clinical follow-up. Patients with chronic gastrointestinal symptoms often turn to short-video platforms for quick and comprehensible disease-related information, especially regarding symptoms, endoscopic findings, and preventive strategies26,27. Unlike gastric cancer or Helicobacter pylori infection, the management of AG relies heavily on long-term surveillance and patient adherence to lifestyle changes, making the clarity of risk communication on social media particularly critical. Specifically, because AG is a long-term condition requiring regular endoscopic surveillance to monitor for neoplastic changes, the balance of information is critical. Misinformation that overemphasizes malignant transformation without detailing specific follow-up protocols can foster “unnecessary cancer phobia” among patients. Conversely, an oversimplification of the condition may lead to a dangerous neglect of essential long-term monitoring and surveillance gastroscopy. Against this backdrop, our study provides a systematic evaluation of atrophic-gastritis-related videos on Bilibili and TikTok, examining not only the informational quality but also uploader characteristics, content patterns, presentation formats, and user engagement indicators. These findings offer an updated and comprehensive overview of the current landscape of digital health communication surrounding atrophic gastritis, with implications for patients, clinicians, and platform administrators.

Our results indicated differences between the two platforms in terms of content distribution, uploader composition, and overall information quality. TikTok videos demonstrated relatively higher mDISCERN, JAMA, and GQS scores and a greater concentration of higher-quality content, whereas Bilibili contained a larger proportion of lower-scoring videos. This observation differs from findings reported in some previous studies of other diseases or broader health topics, where longer-form platforms or those with stronger user-generated content traditions were associated with higher-quality medical information13,28. Observed differences between platforms should therefore be interpreted cautiously, as they may reflect underlying differences in platform design, user demographics, and recommendation algorithms rather than intrinsic differences in content quality alone. For instance, TikTok’s recommendation algorithm may prioritize high-impact clips from verified professionals in its “Discover” feed, whereas Bilibili’s long-form ecosystem may present a more heterogeneous mix of content, potentially confounding direct quality comparisons. Furthermore, it is important to consider whether the statistically higher quality scores on TikTok translate into clinically meaningful benefits for patients. Although TikTok videos demonstrated slightly more consistent scoring, the moderate overall quality on both platforms suggests that both still exhibit considerable issues regarding completeness, depth, and evidence-based accuracy. This finding aligns with prior research highlighting the vulnerability of short-video platforms to misinformation14,29. For a precancerous condition such as atrophic gastritis, a ‘moderate’ quality video might lack critical details on long-term surveillance or specific histopathological risks, potentially leading to a superficial understanding rather than informed clinical decision-making.

A closer examination of video content revealed clear platform-specific patterns. Videos included in our study covered essential aspects of AG including etiology, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, and endoscopic features. We find that Bilibili hosted more medically detailed videos emphasizing diagnostic procedures, symptom explanation, and endoscopic findings, likely reflecting the platform’s UGC+PUGV ecosystem in which longer and more educational videos are well received30. Conversely, TikTok videos more frequently focused on treatment strategies and lifestyle recommendations, aligning with the platform’s emphasis on brevity, directness, and highly actionable content31. Furthermore, we observed that videos on both platforms often emphasized the potential risk of malignant transformation of AG in the prognosis section, while providing relatively limited discussion of long-term management and surveillance.

Presentation formats also differed between platforms: Q&A or slide-based structures were common on Bilibili, whereas TikTok predominantly featured clinical scenario demonstrations or brief physician explanations. Despite these stylistic variations, user engagement metrics—likes, comments, favorites, and shares—did not differ significantly between platforms, suggesting that presentation format alone does not determine user interaction. Other factors such as uploader identity, video clarity, and emotional resonance may play more influential roles in driving engagement.

Uploader identity emerged as one of the strongest predictors of video quality. Content created by gastroenterologists consistently achieved the highest GQS, JAMA, and mDISCERN scores and attracted the greatest engagement. This aligns with existing literature demonstrating that professional medical creators tend to produce more accurate, comprehensive, and guideline-based health information13,32,33. In contrast, videos uploaded by laypersons or patients tended to have lower quality scores. This finding is consistent with previous studies suggesting that non-professional creators may provide more fragmented or anecdotal health information34,35. TCM physicians represented a significant proportion of uploaders, however, the quality of their content displayed substantial variability. While some creators provided balanced and evidence-informed suggestions, some TCM physicians offered oversimplified lifestyle and dietary advice. Additionally, some TCM-related videos lacked adequate disclosure or evidence attribution, which may have contributed to lower JAMA-based scores rather than reflecting inherent factual inaccuracies. These inconsistencies highlight the need for strengthened governance and enhanced scientific communication training for TCM-related online content, ensuring that integrative medicine information remains aligned with evidence-based practice.

Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated strong positive associations among engagement metrics, indicating that videos receiving more likes were also more likely to generate comments, shares, and collections36. In contrast, correlations between engagement indicators and quality scores were weak to moderate. This ‘engagement-quality gap’ suggests that highly engaging videos are not necessarily more accurate or educational37,38, highlighting a key vulnerability of short-video–based health communication. This poses a challenge for public health, as high-engagement, moderate-quality videos may reach a vast audience while lacking the necessary clinical nuances for managing a precancerous condition. Therefore, improving content discoverability for authoritative creators and implementing platform-level quality-filtering mechanisms could be critical steps in mitigating misinformation.

Taken together, our findings highlight gaps in the quality of AG-related information available on popular short-video platforms. As these platforms continue to play an increasingly influential role in public health communication, a multifaceted approach may help improve the reliability of online medical content. Potential strategies include strengthening platform-supported medical content review and moderation mechanisms, increasing the visibility and recommendation priority of verified medical professionals, and promoting public digital health literacy through targeted educational initiatives.

Beyond medical reliability, the overall impact of health communication is also influenced by the technical and perceptual quality of the multimedia content. Extensive research has established robust frameworks for perceptual quality assessment and benchmarking across image, video, and screen content39,40. In the context of short videos, quality modeling has evolved from traditional metrics to blind estimation methods that evaluate content “in the wild” or under specific distortions, without requiring ground-truth references41–44.

Recent advancements in AI algorithms, such as attention-guided neural networks for audio-visual quality assessment and instruction-tuned models for AI-generated images45,46, offer promising avenues for automating health information evaluation. While our study relied on manual scoring (GQS, mDISCERN), future systems could integrate these AI-driven objective models to filter out low-quality or distorted content in real-time. This is particularly relevant as the digital landscape increasingly shifts toward AI-generated content and compressed multimedia formats, necessitating a unified approach to quality evaluation that combines technical integrity with clinical accuracy.

This study has several limitations. First, our analysis focused exclusively on Chinese-language videos; results may not generalize to other linguistic or cultural contexts where health communication norms differ. Second, video retrieval was performed on a single day using one keyword, which may limit reproducibility and introduce algorithm-driven selection bias. Third, although GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA are widely applied in video-based health communication research, they were not designed for short-form audiovisual content. These tools do not assess visual persuasion, emotional framing, narrative techniques, or audiovisual quality47,48, all of which may strongly influence viewer perception and behavior. Furthermore, the inherent structural differences between TikTok and Bilibili —including differing user demographics, algorithmic priorities, and video format constraints—serve as potential confounders when comparing engagement and quality metrics. As a result, our findings primarily reflect informational accuracy and transparency rather than overall communicative effectiveness. Future studies should incorporate video-specific evaluation frameworks and user-centered outcome measures.

Conclusions

In conclusion, while TikTok demonstrates statistically higher engagement and quality scores than Bilibili for AG information, the absolute differences remain modest, and both platforms primarily host content of moderate quality. Professional medical creators are the most reliable sources, whereas content from non-experts often lacks evidentiary rigor. Given the role of AG as a precancerous condition requiring long-term management, improving the quality of online health communication will require strengthened content moderation, greater visibility of authoritative medical creators, and enhanced public health literacy through coordinated efforts among platforms, healthcare professionals, and regulators.

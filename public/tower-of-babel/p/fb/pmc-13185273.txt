A carotid web should not be excluded as the cause of a first-ever stroke in older patients with atherothrombotic risk factors
Authors: Haruko Yoshimoto, Keizo Asakuno, Go Matsuoka, Masahiro Yamaguchi, Hideki Shiramizu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe carotid web was first reported by Momose et al. in 1977. Most case reports involve patients under 60 years old; thus, the carotid web is recognized as a potential cause of embolic cerebral infarctions in relatively young patients who lack conventional stroke risk factors and often experience recurrence.Case presentationA woman in her mid-80s presented to our hospital 1 h following aphasia onset and severe right hemiparesis. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a left middle cerebral artery occlusion; therefore, percutaneous thrombectomy was considered. Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) revealed flow restoration and blood flow stagnation at the downstream surface of the carotid web (CW) niche where thrombi originate. This finding was typical for a CW; however, based on the patient’s age, history of diabetes mellitus, and absence of previous strokes, we initially misinterpreted it as an atherothrombotic plaque with ulceration. Meticulous investigation failed to identify any intra- or extracranial embolic source. Thus, we performed computed tomography angiography (CTA) in oblique-sagittal projections and confirmed the diagnosis; ultrasound and MR angiography provided no diagnostic value, as previously reported. Carotid artery stenting was performed for prevention of the recurrence.ConclusionPatients with a symptomatic carotid web are usually young and have few conventional stroke risk factors. However, a carotid web should not be excluded as the cause of an initial cerebral infarction if the patient is elderly. Typical DSA findings should not be overlooked, and CTA is an important noninvasive imaging modality to avoid a misdiagnosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12245-026-01244-8.

A woman in her mid-80s presented to our hospital 1 h following aphasia onset and severe right hemiparesis. Magnetic resonance (MR) imaging revealed a left middle cerebral artery occlusion and the DWI-ASPECTS was 8. Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) considering percutaneous thrombectomy revealed flow restoration and blood flow stagnation at the downstream surface of the carotid web (CW) niche where thrombi originate (Fig. 1a, b; video in Additional file 1). This finding was typical for a CW; however, based on the patient’s age, history of diabetes mellitus, and absence of previous strokes, we initially misinterpreted it as an atherothrombotic plaque with ulceration. Meticulous investigation failed to identify any intra- or extracranial embolic source. Thus, we performed computed tomography angiography (CTA) in oblique-sagittal projections and confirmed the diagnosis (Fig. 1c); ultrasound and MR angiography provided no diagnostic value, as previously reported [1–3]. Carotid artery stenting (CAS) was performed, and there has been no recurrence for 11 months.

Despite studies involving a 90- [4] or 73-year-old patient [2], CW is recognized as a potential cause of embolic cerebral infarctions in relatively young patients without conventional stroke risk factors [5, 6]. However, typical findings on DSA or CTA are extremely important for diagnosis regardless of age, underlying medical conditions, or history of stroke.

Our treatment choice was based on the previously reported superiority of CAS over medical therapy [2, 7]; nevertheless, another guideline recommends medical therapy as the initial treatment option [6]. Further evidence is needed to establish definitive recommendations.

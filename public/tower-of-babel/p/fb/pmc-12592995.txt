Tracheostomy in Children: A Call for Deeper Reflection

Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHow to cite this article: Venkatesan DK, Kambagiri P. Tracheostomy in Children: A Call for Deeper Reflection. Indian J Crit Care Med 2025;29(10):880.Keywords: Mechanical ventilation, Quality of life, Tracheostomy, Tracheostomy complication

Dear Editor,

The article titled “Tracheostomy in Children: Experience from a Tertiary Care Center in South India” was read with great interest.1 We appreciate the authors for their ambispective study and valuable findings, and we wish to express the following comments.

Tracheostomy in critically ill children requiring prolonged ventilation is a prudent strategy to prevent complications associated with extended mechanical ventilation. Although the exact timing of tracheostomy in pediatrics is not clearly defined, studies have shown that early tracheostomy (≤14 days) in children ventilated for more than 96 hours is associated with better outcomes.2 In this study, only 27% of patients underwent tracheostomy within 14 days, with the median duration being 21 days. An analysis comparing outcomes between the early and late tracheostomy groups would have provided greater insight into the benefits of early intervention.

Furthermore, it has been shown that the type of complications following tracheostomy varies depending on the time elapsed after the procedure.3 Evaluating the timing of complications in this large cohort would have reinforced the importance of monitoring for specific complications at different stages post-tracheostomy. With regard to the endotracheal tube, information regarding whether the cuffed or uncuffed endotracheal tube was used, the use of cuff pressure monitoring and complications in both groups would have added further strength to this study.

In addition, further evaluation of mortality in children discharged home with tracheostomy—including the nature of complications, the interval between discharge and death, and the cause of death—would have provided valuable knowledge regarding the importance of parental counseling and the need for structured, protocol-based follow-up.

We congratulate the authors for presenting data from a large sample size and emphasize that further prospective studies are needed to enhance understanding of post-tracheostomy care in resource-limited settings. In addition, this study explains the need for using tele-mentoring services for the monitoring of these patients.

Fully spin-polarised quantum Hall effect at sub-tesla magnetic fields
Authors: M Myronov, W Jiang, S Studenikin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstarctThe integer quantum Hall effect provides one of the most precise and robust manifestations of topological quantum transport. Yet, in semiconductor systems, its development to a fully spin-polarised state typically requires magnetic fields of several tesla and milli-Kelvin temperatures to overcome disorder, Landau-level broadening, and thermal smearing. Here we report a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state in a two-dimensional (2D) hole gas hosted in a compressively strained germanium quantum well grown on silicon, stabilised at magnetic fields as low as 0.25 − 0.3 T and temperatures up to 1.5 K. This unusually low-field quantisation is enabled by an ultra-dilute carrier regime combined with extremely large Zeeman energy that approaches the cyclotron energy of free carriers over a wide density range, resulting in dominant spin splitting of Landau levels at sub-tesla fields to reach ν= 1 fully spin-polarized quantum limit. Magnetotransport and 2D Landau-fan measurements demonstrate that Landau-level broadening remains smaller than the spin splitting throughout the whole magnetic field range down to 0.25 mT, allowing robust quantisation of the Hall resistance at h/e2. These results establish access to an experimentally unexplored regime of spin-polarised quantum Hall physics in a semiconductor platform and open new opportunities for exploring exchange-driven and correlated many-body states of dilute systems in small magnetic fields.Subject terms: Quantum Hall, Electronic devices, Quantum metrology

Introduction

The quantum Hall effect (QHE) remains one of the most striking manifestations of quantum mechanics in condensed-matter systems, providing a direct connection between topology and electrical transport1,2. Since its discovery, it has served as both a paradigm for topological phases of matter and the foundation of a primary electrical-resistance standard3–6. In conventional semiconductor two-dimensional (2D) systems, however, the formation of fully developed quantum Hall states typically requires magnetic fields of several tesla and millikelvin temperatures, reflecting the need to overcome disorder, Landau-level broadening, and thermal fluctuations5,7–9.

Achieving robust quantisation at substantially lower magnetic fields has long been a central challenge. In particular, stabilising the fully spin-polarised ν = 1 state at low fields requires not only ultralow disorder, but also a hierarchy of energy scales in which the Zeeman splitting exceeds both the orbital energy and the disorder-induced Landau-level broadening7,10–12. While such conditions can be realised in a limited number of III–V electron systems13,14, they have remained inaccessible in group-IV semiconductor platforms on silicon. In conventional III–V semiconductor systems, strong and unavoidable Dresselhaus spin-orbit interaction intrinsically entangles spin and orbital degrees of freedom, complicating the isolation of purely spin-driven phenomena15–17. By contrast, centrosymmetric group-IV platforms suppress bulk spin-orbit coupling18,19, allowing large Zeeman splitting to dominate over orbital quantisation11,12. In this regime, a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state emerges at magnetic fields below 0.3 T, enabling direct access to exchange-enhanced spin gaps and quantum Hall ferromagnetism in an exceptionally dilute, low-field limit.

Compressively strained germanium quantum well grown on silicon (cs-GoS) have recently emerged as an exceptionally clean host for two-dimensional hole gas (2DHG) with very small spin-orbit interactions, combining ultrahigh mobility with a small effective mass and a large effective g*-factor11,12,20. These properties suggest that, in the dilute regime, spin splitting may dominate orbital quantisation, opening a route to accessing spin-polarised quantum Hall physics at magnetic fields far below those required in conventional systems. Coupled with advances in epitaxial strain engineering and interface perfection, cs-GoS platforms now routinely exhibit hole mobilities exceeding 7 × 106 cm2V−1s−1, surpassing many III–V systems, while maintaining CMOS compatibility and wafer-scale uniformity20. These attributes position cs-GoS as an exceptional environment for studying strongly correlated and topologically non-trivial states within a technologically mature materials framework.

Earlier studies of cs-Ge QWs have reported integer quantum Hall states at magnetic fields above 3–10 T, supported by well-resolved Shubnikov–de Haas oscillations11,13,21–23. However, achieving full spin polarisation and complete quantisation in the sub-tesla regime has remained unreachable, limited by the host material quality, interface disorder, background impurity fluctuations, and residual charge inhomogeneity that broaden spin-split Landau levels. Demonstrating full spin polarisation at sub-Tesla fields would signal not only a new benchmark in material purity, but also open an unexplored regime for probing quantum criticality, exchange-driven spin physics, and low-field topological phenomena.

Here we demonstrate a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state in a cs-GoS 2DHG at magnetic fields as low as 0.25−0.3 T and temperatures up to 1.5 K. By tuning the carrier density into an ultra-dilute regime with mobile carrier densities below 1010 cm−2 and exploiting the large Zeeman energy of holes in cs-GoS, we access a regime in which Landau-level broadening remains smaller than the spin splitting throughout the relevant field range, enabling robust quantisation at unprecedentedly low magnetic fields including very extended first fully spin-polarised QHE plateau ν = 1, a regime typically inaccessible in semiconductor systems5,7,8,24,25. The ultra-dilute regime denotes a low-density 2D system in which Coulomb and exchange interactions dominate over kinetic and orbital energies, enabling interaction-driven spin order at exceptionally small magnetic fields.

Very stable gated Hall-bar devices were fabricated employing the Schottky-gate technology from state-of-the-art cs-GoS epiwafers grown by reduced-pressure chemical vapour deposition and characterised via low-temperature magnetotransport measurements between 300 mK and 1.5 K. By tuning the top-gate voltage, we reliably accessed the extremely low hole density transport regime where Zeeman spin splitting dominates cyclotron energy (ℏωc) and level broadening when only odd-filling factor spin quantum oscillations remain. Under these conditions, the longitudinal resistance collapses to zero while the Hall resistance quantises to h/e2, demonstrating the complete formation of the fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state at magnetic fields down to 0.3 Tesla, easily achievable using simple permanent magnets. This behaviour is explained by a phenomenological model of Gaussian-broadened spin-resolved Landau levels with a large effective g*-factor. The results establish cs-GoS as a uniquely clean group-IV platform for exploring low-energy quantum Hall physics and spin-driven many-body states.

Results

Electrostatics and ultra-dilute regime

Electrostatic drift and hysteresis, commonly observed in MOS-gated devices due to interface trap states, severely limit reproducible carrier-density control20. Here, we demonstrate stable and precise electrostatic tuning of the 2DHG using Schottky-gated Hall-bar devices fabricated from fully epitaxial cs-GoS heterostructures (Fig. 1). Continuous tuning of the carrier density down to p ≲ 1 × 1010 cm−2 is achieved with negligible leakage and no measurable hysteresis, allowing systematic access to the ultra-dilute regime. Over this range, the density-gate relationship remains linear and stable across temperature range, indicating a highly uniform and stable electrostatic environment. By sweeping the Schottky top gate VG, we tune the hole density continuously from the dilute regime p ≈ 8 × 1010 cm−2 into the extreme dilute transport regime p ≲ 1 × 1010 cm−2. Over the full accessible range, the conductance decreases smoothly and monotonically determined at each point from Hall effect measurement (Fig. 1c), indicating very stable electrostatic gate control with negligible leakage and no measurable hysteresis or drift. The extracted density-gate relation remains strictly linear at both 300 mK and 1.5 K (Fig. 1d), revealing excellent uniformity of the electrostatic environment and stability over time and temperature.

As the 2DHG density is increased (Fig. 1e), the mobility increases sharply, reflecting the screening and therefore suppression of impurity and interface-related scattering and confirming the exceptionally low disorder of the system. This combination of ultra-low carrier density and minimal broadening establishes the conditions required for the formation of a fully spin-polarised system, i.e. ν≤1, at magnetic fields far below those typical for semiconductor quantum Hall systems. At low densities, the mobility increases sharply, exceeding 42,000 cm2V−1s−1 at p ≈ 0.9 × 1010 cm−2 (Fig. 1e). This behaviour reflects the very weak scattering centres present in the cs-Ge QW heterostructure, consistent with the absence of parallel channels, the high structural perfection of the cs-Ge epilayer interfaces, and the lack of remote-ionised impurity scattering within the heterostructure due to the undoped heterostructure design20. The exceptionally low disorder establishes the conditions under which Landau-level formation and spin resolution can occur at much lower magnetic energies in small magnetic fields than in any previously reported Germanium (Ge), Silicon-Germanium (Si1−xGex) or Silicon (Si) gated systems11,21–23.

Despite the extremely low carrier density, the system remains in a fully delocalised free-carriers transport regime. This is evidenced by the smooth evolution of conductance with gate voltage, the absence of mesoscopic fluctuations and weak localization effects, and the high carrier mobility retained down to p ≈ 0.9 × 1010 cm−2 with the measured percolation density threshold pc = 5 × 109cm−2. The linear density–gate relation and the uniform Landau fan further indicate minimal spatial inhomogeneity and negligible charge trapping and puddling.

Observation of the ν = 1 quantum Hall effect at sub-tesla magnetic fields

Figure 2a shows an example of the longitudinal magnetoresistivity, ρxx, and Hall resistance, Rxy, measured at T = 300 mK for a density p = 1.4 × 1010 cm−2. Even in this ultra-dilute regime, the system exhibits clear signatures of Landau quantisation at surprisingly low magnetic fields. As the field increases to ∣B∣ ≈ 0.4 T, ρxx collapses to zero while Rxy develops an unusually broad plateau at h/e2, demonstrating the complete formation of the fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state at low magnetic field. This critical field is more than an order of magnitude lower than values reported for earlier SiGe-based 2D hole gases, and lower even than typical GaAs/AlGaAs high-mobility electron or hole systems, which normally require several tesla to achieve a fully polarised ν = 1 spin state and milli-Kelvin temperatures26.

At a slightly lower density p = 9.6 × 109 cm−2, the ν = 1 plateau still persists and shifts to even smaller magnetic fields (Fig. 2b). The appearance of a robust quantised Hall plateau at ∣B∣ ≈ 0.25–0.30 T confirms that the Zeeman energy is comparable with the cyclotron energy, placing the system in a regime where the ground state is pushed down. That makes the fully spin-polarised n = 1 QHE plateau very extended even in small fields below 1 T and observable at relatively large temperatures above

1.5 K in our experiments. By contrast, in undoped GaAs two-dimensional hole systems, the ν = 1 state typically collapses below magnetic fields of ~2 T at comparable temperatures26.

The extended ν = 1 plateau observed here is a particularly significant feature. First, its stability at magnetic fields well below 1 T, down to approximately 0.3 T, enables access to a fully developed quantum Hall state using modest magnetic fields, including those achievable with permanent magnets. More importantly, from a fundamental perspective, this low-field regime realises a fully spin-polarised two-dimensional hole system in which all carriers occupy the same spin ground state. Such a configuration provides a clean and well-defined starting point for exploring collective spin phenomena and spin-resolved many-body physics in the absence of competing spin-orbital effects. Finally, the hierarchy of energy scales achieved here, in which the Zeeman splitting exceeds the Landau-level spacing, creates favourable conditions for investigating exchange-driven and ferromagnetic ordering phenomena in an exceptionally low-disorder environment. This regime of a fully spin-polarised collective state at sub-tesla magnetic fields has not, to our knowledge, been previously observed in semiconductor quantum wells based on CMOS-compatible materials.

Gate-tuned evolution of spin-resolved Landau levels

To map the evolution of the quantum Hall states with density, we acquire gate-dependent Hall traces at fixed temperatures of 300 mK and 1.5 K (Fig. 2c, d). As the gate voltage is made less negative, reducing the density towards the minimal values, the ν = 1 plateau strongly widens while its onset shifts to lower magnetic fields. This behaviour is consistent with the qualitative model depicted in Fig.1f, right diagram for large effective g*-factor, when Zeeman energy dominates over the orbital quantization11.

At 300 mK, the plateau edges sharpen further, and higher-order odd plateaux (ν = 3) become visible extending down to B = 0.25 T for the corresponding gate voltages (Fig. 2c). The robustness of the ν = 1 state at sub-tesla magnetic fields and finite temperatures up to at least 1.5 K demonstrates that disorder-induced broadening of LLs remains extremely small, of order 40 μeV, across the full density range studied, confirmed by the modelling below.

To directly visualise the evolution of the spin-resolved Landau-level spectrum, we construct Landau-fan diagrams, shown in Fig. 3, by measuring the magnetotransport parameters Rxx and Rxy as a function of magnetic field and gate voltage. The resulting fan diagram exhibits well-resolved, linearly dispersing trajectories with dominant splitting between spin-resolved branches. The clarity and uniformity of these features indicate good uniformity and low disorder of the system.

Importantly, the separation between spin-resolved Landau levels remains larger than the orbital gap, Eorb = Ec-Ez, across the full density range explored. This hierarchy, in which the Zeeman energy dominates orbital quantisation, ensures that Landau-level broadening remains smaller than the spin splitting, providing a direct microscopic explanation for the robustness of the ν = 1 quantum Hall plateau at ultra-low magnetic fields.

Crucially, the pronounced splitting of adjacent branches reveals the separation between spin-up and spin-down Landau levels, confirming that the Zeeman gap exceeds the cyclotron gap throughout the low-density regime. This hierarchy stabilises a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 ground state at magnetic fields reachable not only by standard laboratory magnets but even by permanent magnets.

The differential Hall signal (Fig. 3b) enhances these features, resolving spin-resolved branches with remarkable sharpness. The separation between the two branches remains nearly constant with gate voltage, demonstrating that the large effective g*-factor at low density compensates the reduction in cyclotron energy, maintaining the Zeeman-dominated ordering across the entire density range tuned by gate voltage.

The Landau-fan structure reveals an unusual regime in which the Zeeman energy is comparable to, and in parts exceeds, the cyclotron energy, EZ ≈ ℏωc. As a result, the lowest spin-resolved Landau level disperses only weakly with magnetic field, leading to an anomalously slow evolution of the ν = 1 ground state. This hierarchy strongly suppresses orbital depopulation while maintaining a large spin gap, providing a natural explanation for the exceptional width and robustness of the ν = 1 quantum Hall plateau observed at sub-tesla fields. Such behaviour is fundamentally distinct from conventional Si or GaAs-based systems with small effective g*-factors, and enables quantum Hall operation in magnetic fields accessible even with permanent magnets.

The effective g*-factor used in the modelling (g*≈15) is consistent with independent measurements in a similar cs-Ge QW platform, where a large and electric-field-tuneable g*-factor observed demonstrating a crossing between Zeeman and the orbital quantization energies (see Fig. 4b in ref. 11).

It should be noted, that in gated structures the metallic gate can essentially screen the electron-electron interaction, in particular, in ultra-dilute regime when distance between particles is comparable with the distance to the metallic gate12,27. This topic of free carrier exchange interactions requires a separate careful investigation which is beyond the scope of this work. In our experiments we do not observe strong evidences of additional g*-factor enhanced behaviour. Therefore, the experimentally observed values of g*-factor we interpret as an effective quantity, incorporating both single-particle and possible many-body contributions. The presented phenomenological model does not explicitly include density-dependent exchange enhancement, but captures the experimentally observed hierarchy of energy scales and overall behaviour. The good agreement between experiment and simulation indicates that a constant effective g∗ provides an adequate first-order description of the observed quantization regime without additional fitting parameters.

Implications for Zeeman-dominant quantum Hall physics at low magnetic fields

The combination of ultra-low disorder, dilute carrier density, and a very large effective hole g*-factor places the cs-GoS 2DHG in a new regime of quantum transport that has been remained largely unexplored experimentally. The observation of a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state at magnetic fields as low as 0.25−0.3 T demonstrates access to a Zeeman-dominated quantum Hall regime in which spin splitting can exceed the orbital splitting energy between neighbouring Landau-level spin states. This hierarchy of energy scales enables stabilisation of a spin-polarised ground state under conditions where orbital quantisation alone would not be sufficient.

Access to this regime provides new opportunities to investigate many-body spin physics in the lowest Landau-level limit, including exchange-driven spin polarisation and quantum Hall ferromagnetism, under magnetic fields far below those typically required in semiconductor systems. More broadly, the ability to tune continuously between cyclotron- and Zeeman-dominated regimes in an exceptionally clean hole system offers a controlled platform for exploring spin-resolved many-body phenomena and low-energy quantum critical behaviour in two dimensions.

Finally, while the present work is primarily focused on fundamental physics, the realisation of a fully spin-polarised quantum Hall state at sub-tesla magnetic fields highlights the feasibility of accessing quantum Hall phenomena in compact cryogenic environments. This capability may enable future experimental studies of quantum Hall physics under reduced magnetic-field constraints, without altering the underlying physical principles that govern many-body phenomena and topological transport.

Discussion

The observation of a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state at magnetic fields below 1 T down to 0.25 T establishes access to a distinct regime of quantum Hall physics in a semiconductor system. In contrast to conventional two-dimensional platforms, where large magnetic fields are normally required to achieve spin-resolved states, the present system realises a qualitatively different hierarchy of energy scales in which the Zeeman splitting dominates over orbital quantisation. This regime emerges from the combination of ultra-low disorder, dilute carrier density, and the large effective g∗-factor of holes in cs-GoS material system.

In this Zeeman-dominated regime, the lowest spin branch of the lowest Landau level remains inactive, i.e., energetically isolated over a wide density range, even at sub-tesla magnetic fields. As a result, spin degeneracy is lifted before orbital quantisation, leading to robust formation of a fully spin-polarised ground state under conditions where conventional cyclotron-driven quantisation would be insufficient. This behaviour highlights a qualitatively different pathway to the formation of odd QHE states, governed primarily by spin physics.

Access to this regime enables systematic investigation of interaction-enhanced spin phenomena in the low Landau-level limit, including exchange-driven spin polarisation and quantum Hall ferromagnetism, under magnetic fields and cyclotron energies that are exceptionally small. The ability to tune continuously between cyclotron- and Zeeman-dominated regimes by carrier density/gate voltage provides a powerful experimental handle on the interplay between orbital quantisation, exchange interactions, and subband structure in hole systems with strong spin-orbit coupling. Owing to the stringent requirements on disorder and electrostatic stability, this limit has received comparatively little experimental attention to date.

More broadly, these results challenge prevailing assumptions regarding the magnetic-field scales required to realise topological quantum transport in semiconductor systems. They demonstrate that, with sufficiently low disorder and favourable spin properties, the spin-driven quantum Hall effect can persist deep into a low-energy regime where both orbital and spin quantisation occur at magnetic fields accessible to compact cryogenic environments. This expansion of the accessible phase space opens new opportunities to explore spin-resolved and correlated quantum Hall phenomena under conditions that have previously been difficult to achieve experimentally.

Taken together, our findings establish cs-GoS heterostructures as an exceptionally clean and tuneable platform for investigating low-field, spin-polarised quantum Hall physics. The combination of ultra-low disorder, large Zeeman splitting, and precise electrostatic control enables access to a Zeeman-dominated regime that provides new insight into the fundamental interplay between spin, topology, and interactions in two-dimensional hole systems.

We note that conventional Arrhenius activation-gap analysis is not directly applicable in the present low-field regime. The ν = 1 state appears at magnetic fields where thermal broadening and disorder-induced level broadening are comparable to the intrinsic gap, leading to transport dominated by percolation transport rather than a well-defined activation energy. Instead, the relevant energy scales are estimated from the Landau fan and the level broadening analysis (see Methods).

Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a fully spin-polarised ν = 1 quantum Hall state in the cs-GoS 2DHG at magnetic fields as low as 0.25−0.3 T and temperatures up to 1.5 K. This unprecedentedly low-field quantisation arises from an unusual hierarchy of energy scales in the ultra-dilute regime, where the Zeeman splitting exceeds both the cyclotron energy and Landau-level broadening. These results establish access to a new regime of spin-polarised quantum Hall physics in a semiconductor platform and open pathways for exploring correlated many-body states at ultra-low magnetic fields. The unusually low-field fully spin-polarised regime arises from the combined effect of very large Zeeman splitting and ultra-low disorder allowing to reach very small carrier densities. The two conditions are essential to reach very small values for the magnetic-filed quantum limit reaching the ν = 1 state in very small fields down to 0.25 T.

These findings establish cs-GoS as one of the cleanest and most spin-responsive group-IV quantum-well platforms reported to date. The ability to stabilise and tune quantum Hall quantisation using magnetic fields attainable with a permanent magnet opens compelling opportunities for compact metrological devices, cryogenic CMOS integration, and the exploration of interaction-driven phenomena such as quantum Hall ferromagnetism, Wigner crystallization, and spin-texture excitations. More broadly, our results demonstrate that advanced epitaxial engineering within the silicon materials ecosystem can yield platforms that rival and, in key regimes, surpass the performance of established III–V quantum Hall systems. This work also establishes the material foundation for further investigations of low-energy topological states in scalable, CMOS-compatible architectures.

Methods

Heterostructure growth and materials characterisation

The cs-GoS heterostructures were grown on 150 mm diameter Si(001) wafers using reduced-pressure chemical vapour deposition (RP-CVD). A schematic cross-section of the heterostructure and the fabricated Schottky-gated Hall bar device, showing the source (S), drain (D) and the gate (G) stack, is presented in Fig. 1a. The thickness of the Si(001) wafer is standard, i.e. ~700 µm. A graded Si1-xGex/Ge buffer of ~4 µm thick was first deposited to induce close to full relaxation of the Si1-xGex/Ge/Si(001) virtual substrate while maintaining threading-dislocation densities below 5 × 106 cm−2. The active region consists of a~30 nm thick cs- Ge QW epilayer embedded between relaxed Si1-xGex barriers, relaxed epilayers, with Ge content x = 0.85, capped with a~300 nm Si1-xGex epilayer and a ~3 nm Ge surface cap epilayer. Root mean square surface roughness of the cs-GoS heterostructure is below ~2 nm. No intentional doping was introduced. Strain and composition were determined from analysis by high-resolution X-ray diffraction and XTEM of the grown materials. Surface morphology was verified by AFM. Under the application of a negative gate bias, holes accumulate and are confined within the 30 nm-thick undoped, cs-Ge QW, which acts as the active p-channel for mobile holes.

Device fabrication

Double-gated Hall-bar devices were fabricated using standard UV photolithography, wet and dry etching, and thin-film deposition techniques. The fabrication sequence comprised scribe-channel definition, mesa etching, ohmic contact formation, Schottky gate deposition, accumulation-gate dielectric deposition, and top-gate metallisation.

At the start of the process, 50-µm-wide scribe channels were patterned and etched into the Si substrate. The scribe channels enabled precise and smooth separation of individual chips by dicing or cleaving, significantly improving die quality and yield.

The mesa was defined by photolithography followed by inductively coupled plasma (ICP) etching using a Cl2/Ar gas mixture. The total etch depth was 390 nm, extending beyond the cs-Ge QW. To achieve a sloped mesa profile that ensures continuous metal coverage along the sidewall, the chamber pressure, gas ratio, and RF power were optimised. In addition, a photoresist reflow step was applied by baking the Shipley photoresist at 190 °C for 1 min, yielding a mesa sidewall angle of ~68°.

Source, drain, and voltage probe contacts were subsequently defined by photolithography. Following a 30 s dip in 1% diluted HF to remove the native oxide, 120 nm of Al was deposited by electron-beam evaporation and patterned by lift-off. To obtain low-resistance ohmic contacts, the samples were annealed at 275 °C for 30 min in N2. Post-anneal structural analysis using scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) combined with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) revealed Al diffusion of ~20 nm into the Ge quantum well from the mesa sidewall. The resulting Al–Si1−xGex alloy provides lateral electrical access to the 2DHG.

Schottky gate was then patterned on top of the mesa. After a 30 s dip in 1% diluted HF, a 20/100 nm Ti/Au metal stack was deposited by electron-beam evaporation, forming Schottky contacts that electrostatically modulate the carrier density in the compressively strained Ge quantum well. The Schottky-gated cs-GoS architecture avoids charge trapping associated with dielectric-semiconductor interfaces and enables precise carrier-density control without detectable hysteresis or threshold-voltage shifts. The gate width was 90 µm, and the gate edge was positioned ~5 µm away from the mesa edge, ensuring that the active channel region is defined electrostatically rather than by etching. This geometry minimises potential fluctuations at mesa edges and suppresses edge-related scattering effects28.

A 50-nm-thick Al2O3 dielectric layer was subsequently deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD) at 200 °C, using trimethylaluminium (TMA) and water as the aluminium and oxygen precursors, respectively. This dielectric serves both as the accumulation-gate dielectric for ohmic contacts and as an electrical isolation layer between metal contacts and the substrate. Contact vias were opened by photolithography, followed by selective removal of the Al2O3 using dilute buffered oxide etch.

Finally, a 20/200 nm Ti/Au metal stack was deposited and patterned to form independent injection gates used to initialise the ohmic contacts. The Al2O3 layer electrically separates these gates from the cs-GoS heterostructure, while the Au capping layer provides a robust surface for wire bonding. Individual dies were separated by dicing along the scribe channels and mounted onto chip carriers for electrical characterisation.

Low-temperature electrical measurements

In this study we explore longitudinal magneto-resistance, Rxx, and the quantum Hall-effect resistance, Rxy, acquired from the four-probe voltage measurements between corresponding potential probes across or along the Hall bar in Fig. 1b. The differential potentials were measured using two lock-in amplifiers to acquire Vxx and Vxy signals simultaneously. The excitation current through the source-drain Iac=1na was supplied by the reference signal of the master lock-in.

As evident from Fig.1a, b, and from the Device Fabrication, we use two independent gates to activate contacts, and control the active region of the Hall bar independently. This allowed us to reach very low 2DHG carrier densities, almost down to the percolation threshold, pc = 5 × 109cm−2. Note, that we measure Hall effect and conductance of the 2DHG free carrier at this low density that indicates good uniform quality of the material platform with not-screen electrostatic potential fluctuations less than 0.4 meV. The small level of electrostatic fluctuations is an important feature for quantum technologies, e.g. to avoid spontaneous formation of quantum dots29.

Sample was cooled down in a helum-3 Oxford Instruments 3He cryostat (HelioxAST VL) equipped with superconducting magnet with the magnetic field B directed perpendicular to the plane of the 2DHG structure.

Data analysis and simulations

In our experiments we measure voltage drops along and across the Hall bar structure, Vxx and Vxy correspondingly as indicated in Fig.1b. From these measurements we extract all necessary parameters used in this study: (1) conductance at zero magnetic field σ=LIac/(Vxxw) where L is distance between potential probes, w is width of the Hall bar (Fig.1b), Vxx is the voltage drop between potential contacts indicated in Fig. 1b; (2) 2DHG density p=B/eRxy where Rxy=Vxy/Iac is Hall resistance, e is elementary electron charge, B is the value of magnetic field at which this measurement was taken, in this study at 50 mT; (3) the 2DHG density is calculated from Rxy, p = B/(eRxy); and (4) the 2DHG mobility μ=σRxy(B)/B. These 2DHG parameters are used to create line plots shown in Figs. 1 and 2. In Fig. 3a we have created a full 2D map of Rxx (B) stepping Schottky gate voltage, which is uniquely linked to the 2DHG density (right axis) as shown in Fig.1d, because it is not affected by interface charges and was exactly reproduced in several cool-downs. This is a very desirable property for building large scale reproducible quantum devices. In Fig. 3c we plot a similar 2D map for Rxy(B), the main subject of this research, where odd and even QHE plateau are clearly seen and marked as ν = 1, 2,3,4,5. Phenomenologically, according to known empirical observation by Cang and Tsui30, the derivative of the Quantum Hall resistance, Rxy, with respect to the carrier density or magnetic field, dRxy/dB, exhibits a strong similarity to the diagonal resistivity, Rxx31,32. with the following relationship: βρxx=BdRxy/dB, where ρxx=wRxx/L is magnetoresistivity, and β is a constant that varies from sample to sample between 20 and 40. Based on this phenomenological relationship, for qualitative comparison, in Fig. 3d we plot differential dRxy/dB of the data plotted in Fig.3c. Comparing Figs. 3a and 3b, it is evident that they are qualitatively very similar with fan-line structures and the QHE valleys, marked as n = 1,2,3,4,5,6,7. There are subtle differences many-body exchange interactions33–35, discussion of which is beyond of the scope of this article.

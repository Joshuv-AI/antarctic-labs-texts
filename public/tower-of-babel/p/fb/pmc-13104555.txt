Research highlight: Mexican-origin women’s individual and collective strategies to access and share health-promoting resources in the context of exclusionary immigration and immigrant policies
Published: 2026

In recent years, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) have recognized racism as a significant public health issue, highlighting the impact of racialization processes on health inequities. These processes, which create dominant and subordinate racial groups, are evident in U.S. immigration policies that disproportionately affect racially minoritized immigrant groups, particularly those of Mexican origin.

This article explores how U.S. immigration policies impact the health and well-being of Mexican-origin women in Detroit, Michigan. The study focuses on how these women navigate restrictive immigration policies that limit their access to essential resources like healthcare and social services.

The study involved interviews conducted between 2013 and 2014, a period of increasingly exclusionary immigration and immigrant policy context in the U.S., with 48 Mexican-origin women living in Detroit, aged 18 years old and older. The authors used a method called “grounded theory” to analyze the interviews, which involves identifying themes and patterns in the data. The interviews focused on topics such as immigrant policies, discrimination, health, and policy recommendations.

The findings reveal that Mexican-origin women used various strategies to maintain access to health-promoting resources despite restrictive policies. These included maintaining access to health-promoting resources; limiting activities or contact with stigmatized others to protect one’s own identity; and mitigating adverse effects by leveraging social support.

The study underscores the importance of understanding social networks as dynamic systems that can buffer against the negative health impacts of racialized immigration policies, suggesting potential pathways for policy reform that promote health equity among marginalized groups. Future research could explore similar dynamics in other immigrant groups or geographic areas to better understand the broader implications of immigration policies on health.

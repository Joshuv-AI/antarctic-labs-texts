Lorlatinib in patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC previously treated with an ALK inhibitor: results from a phase IV study
Authors: Alessandra Bearz, Serena Ricciardi, Nirmal Vivek Raut, Manuel Cobo Dols, Satheesh Chiradoni Thungappa, Paola Claudia Sacco, Holger Thurm, Dmitri Pavlov, Francesca Toffalorio, Enriqueta Felip

Introduction

Chromosomal rearrangements of the anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) gene are found in approximately 3% of non-small cell lung cancers (NSCLCs) and show sensitivity to ALK tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) [1,2]. Current treatment guidelines have shifted from first-generation ALK TKIs to second- and third-generation ALK TKIs as first-line therapy [3]. Although most patients derive benefit from second-generation ALK TKIs, most will eventually develop resistance or disease progression [4].

Lorlatinib is a potent, brain-penetrant, third-generation ALK and c-ros oncogene 1 TKI that has been shown to overcome most known ALK resistance mutations [5,6]. In a global phase I/II study (NCT01970865), lorlatinib showed potent antitumor activity after the failure of previous ALK TKIs, including first- and second-generation TKIs [7]. In the phase II portion of this study, lorlatinib showed substantial overall and intracranial activity both in treatment-naïve and previously treated patients with ALK-positive advanced NSCLC [8]. On the basis of the results of this study, lorlatinib received a conditional marketing authorization in the European Union on 6 May 2019, for the treatment of patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC whose disease had progressed after alectinib or ceritinib as the first ALK TKI therapy or crizotinib and ≥1 other ALK inhibitor. Subsequently, lorlatinib was approved in the first line in this patient population on the basis of the results from the phase III CROWN study [9,10] and is a preferred first-line treatment option per current guidelines [3].

In the pivotal phase I/II study, in patients with ALK-positive advanced NSCLC that progressed on first-line alectinib or ceritinib (with or without prior chemotherapy), the objective response rate (ORR) was 32.1% (95% CI, 15.9%–52.4%), median duration of response (DOR) was not reached (NR; 95% CI, 4.1 months-NR), and median progression-free survival (PFS) was 5.5 months (95% CI, 2.7–9.0 months) [8]. The intracranial ORR was 55.6% (95% CI, 21.1%–86.3%) [8]. In a subsequent analysis, after a median follow-up of 66.7 months, median overall survival was 37.4 months (95% CI, 12.3 months-NR) [11]. The sample size in this patient cohort was limited to 28 patients [8]. To confirm the efficacy results of the phase I/II study in a larger, more diverse, real-world population, this post-authorization efficacy study was conducted to assess the overall and intracranial response rates with single-agent lorlatinib in patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC whose disease has progressed on alectinib or ceritinib as their first ALK TKI therapy.

Methods

Study design

This was a phase IV, open-label, multicenter, multinational, nonrandomized, prospective, single-arm study (NCT04362072) of lorlatinib in adult patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC that progressed on alectinib or ceritinib as first-line treatment for metastatic disease. Patients received lorlatinib at the approved dose of 100 mg once daily. Patients were treated until disease progression per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1, patient refusal/loss to follow-up, or unacceptable toxicity. Tumor assessments were performed every 6 weeks ±1 week up to approximately 24 months, corresponding to cycle 35 of the treatment regimen.

The study was conducted according to the protocol and ethical principles from international guidelines including the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, applicable International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and other applicable laws and regulations, including privacy laws. The study protocol, informed consent document, Investigator Brochure, and other relevant documents were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board or Independent Ethics Committee at each participating site prior to study initiation (Supplementary Table S1).

Patients

Patients included in the study were ≥18 years of age, had a histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC (stage IV per American Joint Committee on Cancer v7.0), and had disease progression after one prior second-generation ALK TKI (alectinib or ceritinib) as first-line therapy. Patients may have had prior chemotherapy, but only before starting treatment with alectinib or ceritinib. All patients had ≥1 measurable target extracranial lesion per RECIST 1.1 per investigator. Patients with asymptomatic central nervous system (CNS) metastases and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1 were eligible. Patients were also required to have adequate bone marrow and pancreatic, renal, and liver function.

Patients who had prior ALK TKI treatment or anticancer treatment other than first-line alectinib or ceritinib were not eligible. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are included in the study protocol. Written informed consent was obtained prior to study enrollment.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint of the study was confirmed objective response assessed by independent central review (ICR) using RECIST 1.1. The objective was to demonstrate the superiority of lorlatinib over the historical control ORR of 30% observed with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy [12]. The secondary endpoints included confirmed intracranial objective response, time to response (TTR), DOR, intracranial DOR, time to progression (TTP), PFS, and safety assessments. All efficacy endpoints were assessed by ICR and by investigator per RECIST 1.1. All efficacy endpoints reported here are per ICR assessment.

Statistical analysis

For the primary objective, lorlatinib treatment was considered superior to the historical control if the lower limit of the 95% CI around the estimated ORR excluded 30%, which historically represents the ORR observed with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy in patients with ALK-positive NSCLC refractory to second-generation TKIs [12]. The ORR was assessed in patients who received ≥1 dose of lorlatinib. The intracranial ORR was assessed in patients who received ≥1 dose of lorlatinib and had CNS metastases at study entry. The ORR and intracranial ORR were defined as the percentage of patients with confirmed complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) by RECIST 1.1 from the time of first dose until progression or use of subsequent anti-cancer therapy. Both CR and PR were confirmed by repeat assessments performed ≥4 weeks after the criteria for response were first met. Patients who died prior to the first post-baseline assessment, had an inadequate baseline assessment, started new anti-cancer therapy prior to the first post-baseline assessment, or were missing all post-baseline disease assessments were included in the analyses as non-responders. The exact Clopper–Pearson method was used to construct 95% CIs for ORR and intracranial ORR.

TTR was defined as the time from the first dose to the first documentation of objective tumor response (CR or PR) that was subsequently confirmed. TTR was only calculated in the subgroup of patients with a confirmed objective tumor response. Intracranial TTR assessment included patients with a confirmed best intracranial response of CR or PR.

DOR/intracranial DOR was defined as the time from the first documentation of confirmed objective response/intracranial objective response to the date of the first documentation of progressive disease (PD)/intracranial PD or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. TTP was defined as the time from the date of the first dose to the date of the first documented PD due to any cause. PFS was defined as the time from the date of the first dose to the date of the first documented PD or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients who started new anti-cancer therapy or had an extended gap in tumor assessment prior to progression or death had their DOR (intracranial DOR), TTP, and PFS censored at the last non-PD (intracranial non-PD) tumor assessment.

The Clopper–Pearson method was used for ORR and intracranial ORR analyses. Descriptive statistics were used for ORR, intracranial ORR, TTR, and safety analysis. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate DOR, intracranial DOR, TTP, and PFS.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 85 patients were screened, and 71 were treated with lorlatinib. At study completion on 23 October 2024, all patients had discontinued the study treatment (Figure 1). The single most frequent reason for discontinuation was PD (27 [38%]). Twenty-four patients continued receiving lorlatinib treatment off study, 13 who switched to commercial lorlatinib and 11 who entered a lorlatinib continuation study.

Patient disposition.

The median age of patients included in this study was 59 years (range, 26–87 years); 41 were male (58%) and 30 female (42%) (Table 1). The majority of patients were White (76%), 48% had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 1, 42% had baseline CNS metastases, and 83% had only one prior anticancer treatment. Patients received alectinib (85%) or ceritinib (15%) as their first-line therapy; 76% of patients received prior alectinib as a single agent, 8% received prior alectinib with chemotherapy, 8% received prior ceritinib with chemotherapy, and 7% received prior ceritinib as a single agent.

Efficacy

Efficacy analyses were based on the primary completion date of 29 May 2024. The median duration of lorlatinib treatment was 9.7 months (range, 0.3–42.8 months). At the time of this analysis, per ICR, 30 patients achieved a confirmed objective response: 4 patients had a CR and 26 had a PR. The study met its primary endpoint by demonstrating superiority over the historical control, with a confirmed ORR of 42% (95% CI, 31%–55%) (Table 2). In the 60 patients who received alectinib as the prior ALK TKI, the ORR was 42% (95% CI, 29%–55%). In the 11 patients who received ceritinib as the prior ALK TKI, the ORR was 45% (95% CI, 17%–77%). The waterfall plot in Figure 2 shows that majority of patients experienced a reduction in tumor size. After an 18.0-month median follow-up, the median DOR was NR (95% CI, 8.6 months – not estimable), with a 65% probability of remaining in response for ≥12 months. Median PFS was 12.2 months (95% CI, 6.9–22.1 months), with a 51% probability of being progression free at 12 months (Supplementary Figure S1). Median TTP was 18 months (95% CI, 9.7 months – not estimable).

In patients with baseline CNS metastases (n = 30), the intracranial ORR was 47% (95% CI, 28%–66%), with 10 patients (33%) achieving a CR (Table 2). Median intracranial DOR was NR (range, 2.9–22.6 months), with all 14 responders remaining in response at the last assessment. The results for efficacy endpoints per investigator assessment were similar to those determined by ICR and therefore are not presented.

Safety

Safety analyses were based on last patient last visit date of 23 October 2024. Any-grade treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 97% of patients; grade 3/4 TEAEs occurred in 39% (Table 3). The most frequently reported (≥20% of patients) all-cause TEAEs were hypercholesterolemia (59%), hypertriglyceridemia (56%), edema (46%), fatigue (27%), and peripheral neuropathy (21%) (Table 4). Any-grade treatment-related TEAEs occurred in 90% of patients; grade 3/4 treatment-related TEAEs occurred in 27%. The most frequently reported (≥20% of patients) treatment-related TEAEs were hypercholesterolemia (55%), hypertriglyceridemia (55%), and edema (37%) (Supplementary Table S2). TEAEs led to dose interruption in 22 patients (31%), dose reduction in 11 (15%), and permanent treatment discontinuation in 9 (13%) (Supplementary Table S3). Treatment-related TEAEs led to dose interruption in 10 patients (14%) and dose reduction in 7 (10%); no patients discontinued due to treatment-related TEAEs (Supplementary Table S4).

Discussion

In this post-approval phase IV study, lorlatinib provided clinically meaningful benefit in patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC whose disease had progressed on first-line alectinib or ceritinib. The study met its primary endpoint, with the lower limit of the 95% CI for ORR exceeding 30%, thus surpassing the historical ORR with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy. The confirmed ORR was 42% (95% CI, 31%–55%), DOR was long-lasting with the median NR after a median of 18 months of follow-up, and median PFS in all patients was 12.2 months at the primary completion data cutoff. The safety profile of lorlatinib was consistent with those reported earlier [8].

Results from the current study support those of the pivotal phase I/II study, confirming lorlatinib’s efficacy in patients previously treated with first-line second-generation ALK TKIs. In the phase I/II study, the ORR was 32.1%, median DOR was NR at a median of 7 months of follow-up, and median PFS was 5.5 months (95% CI [2.7, 9.0]) [8]. The improved efficacy with lorlatinib in the current study may be due to differences in patient populations between the studies as well as larger patient numbers and better adverse event management leading to longer treatment duration. In addition, the confidence intervals for median PFS overlap between the studies, suggesting that the observed numerical differences may be attributable to random variability due to limited sample sizes in both studies. Several real-world studies also showed similar benefit with lorlatinib in patients previously treated with ALK TKIs [13–15].

Brain metastases are common at the time of diagnosis in patients with ALK-positive NSCLC and are associated with poor prognosis [8,9]. Furthermore, brain metastases can develop after the initial diagnosis of ALK-positive NSCLC, with varying outcomes reported in clinical trials over different follow-up periods [9,16]. Lorlatinib showed intracranial clinical benefit in patients with baseline brain metastases, with a confirmed intracranial ORR of 47% and median DOR NR. In the prior phase I/II study, the intracranial ORR was 55.6% and median DOR was NR [8]. These results are consistent with the known intracranial activity of lorlatinib in this setting in patients with previously treated ALK-positive NSCLC. Overcoming resistance mutations and penetrating the blood-brain barrier are crucial for treating patients with brain metastases.

Lorlatinib treatment was generally tolerable. The most common TEAEs were associated with altered lipid levels, similar to those reported in the phase II and III studies [11,17]. In the current study, the most frequently reported all-cause TEAEs were hypercholesterolemia (59%), hypertriglyceridemia (56%), and edema (46%). In the phase II study that included both treatment-naïve and previously treated patients, the most common all-cause TEAEs were hypercholesterolemia (84%), hypertriglyceridemia (69%), and edema (56%) [11]. The slightly better safety in the current study may be attributed to better management of adverse events; however, the difference should be interpreted cautiously due to the limited population size. Adverse events were manageable through temporary discontinuation, dose reduction, and/or standard supportive medical therapy. The absence of new safety signals further supports lorlatinib’s favorable risk/benefit profile.

The potential improvements in adverse event management since the phase I/II study may be attributed to increased awareness of the common adverse events associated with lorlatinib and the several publications, consensus guidelines, and podcasts that have provided detailed recommendations for adverse event management [18–27]. Overall, a pragmatic approach to lorlatinib treatment management is recommended following the framework of: prepare the patient and care partner, monitor for adverse events, manage adverse events, and reassess adverse events [18,19]. This approach helps facilitate timely management and therefore patient tolerability and clinical benefit. In addition to overall guidance, specific recommendations are provided for a few of the common adverse events associated with lorlatinib. For instance, hyperlipidemia management recommendations begin with baseline blood lipid evaluations and assessment of cardiovascular risk followed by periodic monitoring of blood lipid levels. If hyperlipidemia is detected, lipid-lowering medications are recommended. Only if maximal treatment with lipid-lowering medications fail to control blood lipids should dose reductions be considered.

Limitations of this study include its open-label design, which may have biased investigators or/and patients, as well as the absence of an active control group.

Conclusions

This study confirms lorlatinib’s efficacy and safety in treating ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC post progression on alectinib or ceritinib as the only prior ALK TKI therapy, thereby corroborating the utility of lorlatinib after treatment with second-generation ALK TKIs in patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC. Results from the phase III CROWN study show that the magnitude of benefit of lorlatinib in treatment-naïve patients is much greater, with median PFS NR and 92% of patients remaining free of intracranial progression at 5 years. As a result, using lorlatinib in the first-line setting may benefit patients more due to the prolonged PFS, protection from the development of brain metastases, control of existing brain metastases, and delay in the emergence of resistance mutations. These findings may prompt further research into optimizing treatment sequences and combinations to enhance efficacy and minimize adverse effects in this patient population.

The predictive value of early systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI), serum β2-microglobulin, and procalcitonin for acute kidney injury induced by wasp stings
Authors: Yang Hui, Fang Tianhao, Cheng Bin, Tao Wentao, Liu Xuefang, Tang Jieyi, Wang Bo, Zhao Zhiwen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWasp stings can lead to acute kidney injury (AKI), and traditional renal biomarkers may be insufficient for early detection. In this study, the predictive value of the systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI), serum β2-microglobulin (β2-MG), and procalcitonin (PCT) levels measured at admission for predicting AKI in patients with wasp stings was evaluated. In this single-center retrospective study, 171 adult patients hospitalized for wasp stings between January 2021 and December 2024 were analyzed. The primary endpoint was AKI occurrence within seven days of admission. Compared with non-AKI group, patients who developed AKI were older, had more severe disease, sustained more stings, and had longer hospital stays (all p < 0.05). At admission, the SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT levels were significantly elevated in the AKI group (all p < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression revealed that the SIRI (OR = 1.69, 95% CI 1.24–2.31); β2-MG (OR = 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.53); and PCT (OR = 1.95, 95% CI 1.07–3.58) were independent predictors of AKI. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis revealed strong discriminative performance for AKI prediction (AUCs: 0.933, 0.886, and 0.859; all p < 0.001). Early assessment of the SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT may facilitate timely risk stratification and support early clinical decision-making in patients with wasp stings.

Introduction

Wasps are insects that belong to the order Hymenoptera, the family Vespidae, and the suborder Apocrita and include highly venomous species such as Vespula germanica and Vespa crabro [1,2]. With the acceleration of urbanization and global warming, the numbers and distribution of wasps continue to expand, leading to frequent stinging incidents that have become a significant public health concern [3]. Mild cases are typically characterized by erythema, edema, and local pain and usually have no serious consequences [4]. However, severe cases can be complicated by acute kidney injury (AKI) or multiple organ failure [5], with AKI incidence rates ranging from 10.7% to 34.6% [6,7]. These cases are often associated with worse clinical outcomes and an increased risk of long-term renal insufficiency [8].

Wasp venom contains various small-molecule allergens, phospholipases, and neurotoxins, which can directly damage renal tubular epithelial cells and activate systemic inflammatory responses [8,9]. A retrospective study of 363 hospitalized patients with wasp stings revealed that 14% of these patients presented with gross hematuria, 70.6% of whom developed AKI, and 72.2% of whom developed oliguria renal failure; the mortality rate was 3.9% [7]. Clinical features such as the number of stings, gross hematuria, and platelet counts have been confirmed to be independent predictors of AKI [10]. Studies have shown that elevated white blood cell counts, myoglobin, and urine MCP-1 levels at admission are associated with poor prognosis [5], and that plasma IL-17 concentrations increase significantly [11], These markers are positively correlated with serum creatinine (Scr) levels, suggesting that the inflammatory cascade plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of AKI [6].

The systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI) is a quantitative marker of systemic inflammation that is derived from peripheral blood cell counts [12] and reflects the balance of the inflammatory immune response [13,14]. In patients with ARDS related to wasp stings, the SIRI scores in the death group were significantly greater than in the survival group, with the area under the ROC curve (AUC) reaching 0.821 [3]. In patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD), the SIRI is positively correlated with the risk of disease progression and adverse outcomes [14]. β2-Microglobulin (β2-MG) reflects both glomerular filtration and proximal tubule injury and has potential for early diagnosis and prediction in AKI cases related to acute myocardial infarction [15], hypertension [16], and congenital hydronephrosis in children [17].

Currently, early identification of AKI following wasp stings in clinical practice still relies on creatinine levels and urine output, which present delays in detection. Additionally, a single biomarker is often insufficient to fully capture the complex process of ‘inflammatory response-kidney injury’. Combining the SIRI with a renal injury-related biomarker (β2-MG) and an inflammation-related biomarker (procalcitonin (PCT)) [18–20] could provide a more accurate prediction of the prognostic risk for AKI in the early stages of wasp stings. However, a comprehensive systematic assessment is still lacking.

This retrospective study included patients hospitalized for wasp stings with subsequent AKI in Shiyan, Hubei Province, from January 2021 to December 2024. The predictive value for AKI occurrence was evaluated by analyzing the SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT levels at admission. The goal is to offer a simple and efficient risk assessment tool for AKI related to wasp stings, aiding in the early identification of high-risk patients in clinical settings, guiding precise treatment and intervention, and ultimately improving patient prognosis.

Research methods

Research design and ethical approval

This study was a single-center retrospective investigation aimed at evaluating the predictive value of the early SIRI values combined with serum β2-MG and PCT levels for the short-term prognoses of AKI in patients who experienced wasp stings. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the institution where it was conducted (Approval No.: 2025-KS-097), in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant medical ethics guidelines. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients or their legal representatives, and no additional interventions were made for the conventional diagnosis and treatment process during the study.

Research population

A total of 171 adult patients who were hospitalized due to wasp stings in Shiyan city, Hubei Province, from January 2021 to December 2024 were included in the study. The primary endpoint was the occurrence of AKI within seven days of admission. AKI was diagnosed and staged according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) Clinical Practice Guideline for Acute Kidney Injury (2012) [21] defined it as any of the following criteria including an increase in Scr of ≥26.5 μmol/L from baseline within 48 h, an increase in Scr to ≥1.5 times the baseline value within seven days, or a urine output of <0.5 mL·kg−1·h−1 for 6 h. Furthermore, the baseline creatinine level was defined as the first creatinine level that was measured at the time of admission. We assumed that at this moment, the creatinine level could represent the state of the patient without having experienced AKI. According to these criteria, 67 patients were classified into the AKI group, and 104 patients were classified into the non-AKI group.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: aged 18–75 years, regardless of sex. Compliance with the diagnostic criteria of the ‘Chinese Expert Consensus on Standardized Diagnosis and Treatment of Wasp Stings’ and clear evidence of stings (e.g., on-site sightings, typical stinger marks, or photo comparison/insect catching identification). Time from sting injury to admission ≤24 h. AKI was diagnosed according to the clinical practice guidelines of the KDIGO. All other potential factors that could cause AKI, except for wasp stings, were ruled out. The enrolled patients had detailed clinical records and laboratory data. All the examinations were conducted within 24 h after the sting injury, including complete blood cell count, PCT, and β2-MG determinations at the time of admission, and follow-up outcome indicators (such as recovery of renal function). The patient and/or their legal representative provided informed consent for the retrospective use of medical data.

Exclusion criteria: Stings by bees or nonvenomous bee species. Previous CKD stage 3 or above (eGFR <60 mL·min−1·1.73 m2), a history of kidney transplantation, or long-term dialysis dependence. There was no history of previous AKI episodes. Active autoimmune diseases, advanced malignant tumors, or systemic inflammatory diseases. Severe heart failure, end-stage liver disease, septic shock, or other serious complications. High-dose glucocorticoids or other immunosuppressants should be used within three months prior to admission. Pregnancy or lactation period. Incomplete key laboratory or follow-up data. Patients and/or their legal representatives refused to permit the use of medical materials for research purposes.

Clinical data collection

All the research data were extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical records system by two well-trained independent researchers using a standardized data collection form. To ensure data quality, random spot checks and double-entry comparisons were performed, with an error rate maintained below 1%. The data collected included patient demographic information and clinical symptoms. Demographic information: Patient age, sex, body mass index, and comorbidities. Clinical outcomes: Length of hospitalization.

Wasp stings: This data includes the location of the stinger and the number of stinger wounds. Clinical symptoms include headache, blurred vision, and breathing difficulties. Complications include acute renal failure, acute liver injury, and rhabdomyolysis, among others. Laboratory test data: Complete blood cell count, including white blood cells, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and monocytes. The SIRI (systemic inflammation response index) was calculated using the complete blood count obtained at admission according to the following formula: SIRI = (neutrophil count × monocyte count)/lymphocyte count. All cell counts are expressed as ×109/L.

Data processing

IBM SPSS Statistics 27 (Armonk, NY) was used for statistical analysis, and Microsoft Office 2021 (Redmond, WA) was used for data collection. Categorical variables are described as frequencies (n) and percentages (%), and continuous variables were assessed for normality before analysis. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as the means ± standard deviations (SDs), whereas nonnormally distributed continuous variables are expressed as the median (M) and interquartile range (P25, P75). The chi-square test (χ2) was used for the categorical variables, and the independent-samples t-test was used for normally distributed continuous variables, whereas the Mann–Whitney U-test was used for nonnormally distributed continuous variables. Logistic regression was used to calculate the odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for each factor to identify independent predictors. The diagnostic value of the SIRI, β2-MG, PCT, and their combined models was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with the AUC, sensitivity, and specificity calculated. All tests were two-sided, and a p value <0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Analysis of the demographic and clinical baseline characteristics of the AKI and non-AKI groups in wasp sting patients

As shown in Table 1, the median age of patients in the AKI group was 55.00 (43.00–64.00) years, which was significantly greater than that in the non-AKI group (45.00 (32.00–58.00) years) (p = 0.003). The SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) score was 7.00 (7.00–9.00) points greater in the AKI group than in the non-AKI group (1.00 (0.00–1.00) points) (p < 0.001). The APACHE II (Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II) score was 14.00 (14.00–20.00) points, which was also significantly greater than the 3.00 (2.00–3.00) points in the non-AKI group (p < 0.001). The median length of hospital stay was 5.00 (3.00–7.00) days, which was significantly longer than the stay of 2.00 (1.00–2.00) days for the non-AKI group (p < 0.001). The median number of stings was 37.00 (20.00–52.00), which was significantly greater than the 5.00 (1.00–10.00) stings reported for the non-AKI group (p < 0.001). The duration from stings to treatment was 4.00 (3.00–6.00) hours, which was longer than the 3.00 (2.00–5.00) hours for the non-AKI group (p = 0.001).

Logistic regression analysis of the SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT for wasp stings complicated by AKI

As shown in Table 4, the results of univariate logistic regression analysis indicated that SIRI (β = 0.53; OR = 1.70; 95% CI 1.46–1.97; p < 0.001), β2-MG (β = 0.31; OR = 1.83; 95% CI 1.32–2.19; p < 0.001), and PCT (β = 0.78; OR = 2.19; 95% CI 1.70–2.82; p < 0.001) were significantly associated with the occurrence of AKI in patients with wasp stings.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that the SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT remained independent predictors of AKI. Specifically, the SIRI (β = 0.52; OR = 1.69; 95% CI 1.24–2.31; p = 0.001), β2-MG (β = 0.14; OR = 1.15; 95% CI 1.01–1.53; p = 0.037), and PCT (β = 0.67; OR = 1.95; 95% CI 1.07–3.58; p = 0.030) were found to be independent risk factors for AKI.

ROC curve analysis of the SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT for wasp stings complicated by AKI

As shown in Table 5 and Figure 1, SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT all demonstrated high diagnostic efficacy in predicting AKI complicated by wasp stings. The AUC for SIRI was 0.933 (SE = 0.020, p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.894–0.971). The AUC for β2-MG was 0.886 (SE = 0.027; p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.832–0.94), demonstrating the highest diagnostic efficacy. The AUC for PCT was 0.859 (SE = 0.028; p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.805–0.914).

ROC curve analysis. As shown in Figure 1, the ROC curves for SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT are all located to the upper left of the reference line (random line), indicating excellent diagnostic performance. Among these, the AUC for SIRI (purple) was 0.933, the AUC for β2-MG (green) was 0.886, and the AUC for PCT (pink) was 0.859. All three indicators exhibited high sensitivity and specificity (p < 0.001).

Discussion

This study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of patients with wasp sting-induced AKI and demonstrated that SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT levels strongly predict the risk of AKI occurrence and clinical severity. These findings are not incidental but are closely linked to the inflammation-driven mechanisms underlying wasp venom-induced renal injury. In recent years, numerous animal and cellular studies have elucidated the molecular pathogenesis of wasp sting-induced AKI, suggesting that it represents a form of sterile inflammatory amplification-mediated renal injury driven by mitochondrial dysfunction and aberrant activation of innate immunity.

At the early stage of pathogenesis, components of wasp venom, including PLA1, PLA2, and mastoparan, interact with the phospholipid bilayer of erythrocytes and activate membrane phospholipases, thereby disrupting membrane integrity and increasing permeability [22]. This process ultimately leads to erythrocyte rupture and intravascular hemolysis [23]. Simultaneously, venom components directly injure skeletal muscle cells, resulting in rhabdomyolysis. The free hemoglobin and myoglobin released during hemolysis and rhabdomyolysis accumulate within renal tubules, where they form casts and induce oxidative stress, constituting a major mechanism of AKI development [8]. In addition, substantial amounts of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) are released, providing sustained stimulation for the initiation of inflammatory responses [2]. Animal studies have shown that within 8 h of venom exposure, the Scr, myoglobin, and free hemoglobin levels increase significantly, accompanied by a decrease in the glomerular filtration rate and the development of oliguria. Histopathological examination revealed tubular casts and loss of the brush border in renal tubular epithelial cells [24], findings that closely mirror the pathological changes observed in patients with multiple wasp stings [9,25]. These data indicate that renal tubular epithelial cells are early and critical targets of injury.

Accumulating evidence suggests that hemolysis or rhabdomyolysis alone cannot fully account for the severity of AKI; rather, inflammatory cascades play a central pathogenic role [26]. During the inflammatory amplification phase, mitochondrial injury serves as a pivotal event. Exposure to wasp venom or myoglobin induces excessive production of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS). Increased mtROS levels promote BAX-mediated pore formation in the outer mitochondrial membrane, facilitating the release of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytoplasm. Cytosolic mtDNA, which acts as a potent DAMP, is recognized by cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS), which activates the stimulator of interferon genes (STING) signaling pathway and triggers the production of type I interferons and multiple proinflammatory mediators [27]. STING deficiency or pharmacological inhibition significantly improves renal function and attenuates inflammatory responses [28]. Moreover, inhibition of fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4) reduces mitochondrial injury and the cytoplasmic release of mtDNA and cytochrome c (Cyt-c), suppresses excessive activation of the cGAS–STING pathway, decreases Scr levels, alleviates histological damage, and downregulates inflammatory and apoptosis-related proteins [29,30].

STING activation further promotes the assembly of the NLRP3 inflammasome. Studies have shown that wasp venom markedly upregulates NLRP3, caspase-1 p20, and gasdermin D-N (GSDMD-N), enhancing the maturation and release of interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18 and inducing pyroptosis. Pharmacological inhibition of NLRP3 reverses renal dysfunction and mitigates inflammation and pyroptotic cell death [31]. In addition to targeting downstream signaling pathways, interventions aimed at upstream toxic factors have also demonstrated renoprotective effects. The PLA2 inhibitor varespladib reduces tubular epithelial cell apoptosis, preserves epithelial integrity, and suppresses the expressions of inflammatory genes, including IL-6, CCL2, and Hmox1 [10]. Cilastatin attenuates oxidative stress, downregulates kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1) and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), and enhances myoglobin excretion, thereby significantly ameliorating wasp venom-induced AKI [32]. Collectively, these findings indicate that inflammatory amplification, rather than isolated toxic injury, is the principal driver of disease progression and adverse outcomes.

Inflammatory responses damage renal tubular and microvascular structures through multiple pathways, disrupt renal hemodynamic regulation, and aggravate tissue ischemia, ultimately leading to a vicious cycle of inflammation, oxidative stress, and cell death [33,34]. Within this inflammation-dominant pathological context, biomarkers reflecting systemic inflammatory burden hold important clinical value. SIRI, which integrates neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts, reflects the degree of innate immune activation and immune dysregulation [3,12]. Previous studies have identified SIRI as an independent predictor of AKI across various clinical settings [35–37]. For instance, in critically ill pediatric patients, compared with controls, individuals with SIRI values ≥0.59 had a nearly fourfold increased risk of AKI and an approximately fivefold increased risk of mortality [35]. Among adults with sepsis-associated AKI, patients with intermediate SIRI values (2.5 < SIRI < 7.6) exhibited 19%, 22%, and 24% increases in 30-day, 90-day, and 365-day mortality risks, respectively [38]. In the setting of wasp stings, the AUC of the SIRI value for predicting the prognosis of wasp sting-associated ARDS [3] was 0.821. Furthermore, SIRI was significantly greater in patients with AKI than in those without AKI (p < 0.001), and each one-unit increase in SIRI was associated with an approximately 10% increase in the risk of AKI [6].

In this study, the median SIRI in the AKI group was 11.88 (8.56–14.58) × 109/L, which was significantly greater than the value of 3.54 (1.61–5.98) reported for the non-AKI group (p < 0.001), suggesting that the systemic inflammatory response was more pronounced in the AKI group. Both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses confirmed SIRI as an independent predictor of AKI (univariate: β = 0.53, OR = 1.70, 95% CI 1.46–1.97; multivariate: β = 0.42, OR = 1.53, 95% CI 1.27–1.84; all p < 0.001). The AUC for SIRI was 0.933 (SE = 0.020; p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.894–0.971), further confirming its diagnostic value and practicality.

β2-MG is a sensitive biomarker for the early diagnosis and prognosis assessment of various kidney diseases. Studies have shown that serum β2-MG levels significantly increase before the onset of neonatal asphyxia-related AKI, with an individual predictive AUC of 0.689, which outperforms that of traditional creatinine/urea nitrogen detection [39]. The levels of β2-MG in patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by AKI were also significantly elevated at admission, with an ROC AUC of 0.835 [15]. In patients with urological sepsis, β2-MG is an independent predictor of AKI [40]. In this study, the β2-MG concentration in the AKI group was 2,606.70 (1,981.20–2,896.70) mg/L, which was higher than the 1,090.67 (761.09–1,288.58) mg/L in the non-AKI group (p < 0.001). In both the univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses, β2-MG remained an independent predictor, with an ROC AUC of 0.886 (SE = 0.027, p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.832–0.94), demonstrating the highest diagnostic efficacy and highlighting its significant clinical value in predicting AKI.

Elevated PCT not only reflects the degree of inflammation [20] but also independently predicts AKI [41]. Previous studies have shown that 78.99% of AKI patients with wasp stings had elevated PCT levels upon admission, and the PCT levels were significantly positively correlated with Scr levels, with an ROC AUC of 0.837 [42]. In this study, the PCT concentration in the AKI group was 3.21 (2.12–4.78) ng/mL, which was significantly greater than the 0.69 (0.39–1.83) ng/mL in the non-AKI group (p < 0.001). The ROC AUC for PCT in the AKI group at the early stage of admission was 0.859 (SE = 0.028; p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.805–0.914), further supporting PCT as an early warning biomarker for AKI associated with wasp stings.

Overall, wasp venom can initiate inflammatory responses through direct cytotoxic effects as well as secondary hemolysis and rhabdomyolysis and subsequently amplify inflammatory cascades via mitochondrial injury and activation of the cGAS–STING–NLRP3 signaling pathway, ultimately leading to structural damage to renal tubules and functional impairment. In this context, SIRI and PCT reflect the degree of inflammatory activation from the perspective of the systemic inflammatory burden, whereas β2-MG indicates the extent of renal injury from the standpoint of tubular damage and filtration dysfunction. The combined use of these three biomarkers enables a multidimensional assessment of the ‘inflammation-renal injury’ interplay, thereby providing a sound biological rationale and clinical basis for the application of our combined model in early risk stratification and early risk stratification at hospital admission.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, this was a single-center retrospective analysis, which may introduce selection bias. All patients were recruited from a tertiary hospital in Shiyan, Hubei Province; therefore, the representativeness of the sample may be limited, and caution is warranted when the findings are extrapolated to other regions. Second, the potential influence of treatment-related factors (e.g., glucocorticoid use and the timing and modality of blood purification) could not be fully quantified or adjusted for, which may have introduced residual confounding and affected the robustness of the observed associations. Third, several commonly used inflammatory parameters (e.g., CRP and other biomarkers) were not consistently available within the first 24 h after admission for all patients; thus, we were unable to perform a comprehensive ROC comparison between the SIRI/β2-MG/PCT and these inflammation markers without substantially reducing the sample size and potentially introducing selection bias. Fourth, owing to the lack of multicenter external validation, the optimal cutoff values and predictive performance of the combined model require confirmation in larger and more diverse cohorts. Fifth, other potentially informative biomarkers were not included, which may have limited further improvement of the predictive model. Finally, the follow-up period was relatively short, and long-term renal outcomes were not evaluated.

Conclusions

Among hospitalized adult patients with wasp stings, the early levels at admission of SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT were significantly associated with the occurrence of AKI. Multivariate analysis revealed all three biomarkers as independent risk factors for AKI, and ROC analysis demonstrated good discriminative performance, with β2-MG and SIRI showing superior predictive ability. A combined model incorporating SIRI, β2-MG, and PCT may support early risk stratification at emergency presentation or hospital admission, facilitate the identification of patients at high risk of AKI, and help optimize monitoring and intervention timing. Future multicenter, prospective studies are warranted to provide external validation and to further define optimal cutoff values and clinical application pathways.

Treatment of vernal keratoconjunctivitis simulating total limbal stem cell deficiency: a case report
Authors: Xue-Li Tang, Gong-Yue Wang, Yi-Jia Zhang, Jia-Song Wang, Ming-Chang Zhang, Hua-Tao Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSevere vernal keratoconjunctivitis (VKC) can lead to total limbal stem cell deficiency (LSCD), a condition characterized by conjunctivalization of the cornea and neovascularization that often results in severe vision loss and typically requires surgical intervention. This report presents a novel case where a condition clinically simulating total LSCD, induced by chronic inflammation from VKC, was reversed using only anti-inflammatory therapy.Case presentationA 15-year-old male with a 7-year history of inadequately managed VKC presented with typical signs of clinically apparent total LSCD in both eyes. Clinical examination revealed diffuse corneal conjunctivalization, superficial neovascularization, and visual acuity reduced to counting fingers. The patient was treated with a topical regimen consisting of 0.5% loteprednol, 0.1% tacrolimus, and preservative-free artificial tears. Over 7 months of continuous treatment, the corneas regained significant transparency. Notably, the patient experienced a recurrence of corneal conjunctivalization at month 24 after self-discontinuing medication at month 23, which was again successfully reversed by resuming the anti-inflammatory therapy.ConclusionsThis case demonstrates that severe functional LSCD secondary to VKC can be successfully reversed through intensive, non-surgical anti-inflammatory therapy. Our findings highlight the critical importance of controlling chronic inflammation and suggest that in selected cases of inflammatory or functional LSCD, exhaustive medical management should be prioritized before considering surgical intervention.Keywords: Vernal keratoconjunctivitis, Limbal stem cell deficiency, Anti-inflammatory

Introduction

Vernal keratoconjunctivitis (VKC) is a recurrent, bilateral, chronic allergic disease of the ocular surface, commonly affecting male children and adolescents in warm climates. If not treated properly and promptly, its persistent chronic inflammation can lead to shield ulcers, corneal scarring, and even permanent vision loss [1]. The chronic nature and course of VKC may lead to limbal stem cell deficiency (LSCD) [2].

The primary clinical manifestations of LSCD include persistent epithelial defects, corneal conjunctivalization, neovascularization, stromal opacity, and severe vision reduction. Its treatment strategies are divided into non-surgical and surgical categories. Non-surgical treatments aim to improve the ocular surface microenvironment and include anti-inflammatory therapy, dry eye treatment, and the use of drugs that promote epithelial healing. Surgical treatments include various limbal stem cell transplantation techniques, such as conjunctival-limbal autograft and cultivated limbal epithelial transplantation, which are suitable for unilateral or mild/limited bilateral LSCD. As VKC commonly results in bilateral LSCD, severe bilateral cases are often ineligible for autologous transplantation due to the lack of viable autologous limbal stem cells and tissue. In such cases, allogeneic transplantation is required, with surgical options including kerato-limbal allografts, allogeneic simple limbal epithelial transplantation, and conjunctival limbal allografts [3, 4]. Although severe LSCD often requires surgical intervention to restore vision, some studies suggest that in certain cases, limbal stem cell function can be recovered without surgery [5].

This report presents a case of functional LSCD simulating total deficiency secondary to VKC in which the cornea regained transparency and vision was significantly and lastingly improved through a standardized topical anti-inflammatory treatment, providing strong clinical evidence for an “anti-inflammatory first” treatment principle.

Case presentation

A 15-year-old male presented with a chief complaint of unbearable itching and progressive decline in vision in both eyes for 2 years, worsening in the last 6 months. The patient had a 7-year history of VKC with no standardized treatment. Ophthalmic examination revealed a best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of counting fingers at 50 cm in the right eye and 30 cm in the left eye. Slit-lamp microscopy showed bilateral conjunctival hyperemia, diffuse conjunctivalization of the corneal surface with superficial corneal neovascularization, presenting signs consistent with clinically apparent total LSCD (Fig. 1A). Slit-lamp examination with a narrow beam revealed that the pupils were poorly visualized, with the anterior chamber only vaguely discernible (Fig. 1B). Fluorescein staining showed an irregular and rough corneal surface in both eyes (Fig. 1C). He was diagnosed with severe bilateral VKC complicated by apparent LSCD. Adhering to the principle of controlling inflammation first, we initiated an aggressive topical anti-inflammatory treatment regimen consisting of 0.5% loteprednol four times daily, which was tapered to twice daily after two weeks and continued as maintenance therapy. Additionally, 0.1% tacrolimus eye drops were administered twice daily as a long-term immunosuppressant without a fixed duration, given the chronic and recurrent nature of VKC. Preservative-free artificial tears were administered four times daily. Intraocular pressure (IOP) was monitored monthly.

The patient responded well to the anti-inflammatory therapy, with significant improvement in clinical signs. At the 4-month follow-up, there was partial regression of superficial corneal neovascularization, and the central cornea began to regain transparency. The BCVA improved to 0.4 in the right eye and 0.2 in the left eye (Fig. 2A). The IOP was 14 mmHg in both eyes. After 7 months of continuous treatment, the corneas were mostly clear with only minimal peripheral neovascularization remaining, and the BCVA recovered to 0.6 in the right eye and 0.5 in the left eye (Fig. 2B). Anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) revealed that although a peripheral hyperreflective neovascular membrane persisted (thickness: 87 μm in the right eye and 83 μm in the left), the central cornea had regained transparency with no detectable hyperreflective neovascularization (Fig. 2C). The IOP was 15 mmHg in the right eye and 16 mmHg in the left. We then instructed the patient to continue the medication. At a 14-month follow-up, his corneas stayed clear (Fig. 3A), and his IOP was maintained within the normal range.

(D) Fluorescein staining after the final recovery demonstrating a smooth and stable ocular surface in both eyes

The patient remained adherent to the prescribed regimen during the subsequent follow-up; however, at the 23rd month, the patient discontinued all medications without consulting the doctor for 1 month and returned due to a perceived decrease in vision. Examination revealed recurrent conjunctivalization of the corneal surface and superficial corneal neovascularization in both eyes, indicating a relapse of VKC (Fig. 3B). We immediately resumed the previous anti-inflammatory regimen. After 6 months of treatment, his corneas became clear again, and his vision returned to the previously restored level (Fig. 3C). Fluorescein staining demonstrated a smooth ocular surface in both eyes (Fig. 3D).

Discussion and conclusions

The conventional management paradigm for severe VKC-related LSCD typically follows a structured two-step process. Aggressive anti-inflammatory therapy is first employed to stabilize the ocular surface environment, followed by surgical reconstruction, such as keratolimbal allograft, depending on the severity of the lesion. In traditional clinical practice, this “anti-inflammation, then surgery” sequence is considered the gold standard for restoring vision [6, 7]. However, our case refines this paradigm by demonstrating that a condition clinically simulating total LSCD—characterized by conjunctivalization of the corneal surface and superficial neovascularization—can be successfully reversed through an intensive, medical-only approach. Although surgical intervention is often unavoidable for most cases of true or irreversible total LSCD, some literature indicates that certain conditions, such as functional LSCD simulating total deficiency, can be reversed with strict anti-inflammatory treatment alone [5]. The patient in this report, who presented with clinical signs of clinically apparent total LSCD, achieved restoration of corneal structure and function without surgical intervention, relying solely on topical anti-inflammatory therapy. This result strongly supports the international expert consensus that anti-inflammatory therapy is an essential component of LSCD management [3].

The rationale for selecting loteprednol etabonate for long-term anti-inflammatory therapy was its superior safety profile. As a ‘soft’ steroid, loteprednol carries a significantly lower risk of inducing IOP elevation compared to potent corticosteroids like dexamethasone, making it suitable for chronic management. To ensure safety, the patient’s IOP was monitored monthly and maintained within the normal range throughout the treatment period, and no steroid-related adverse events including secondary glaucoma or cataract formation have been observed to date. For current and subsequent long-term management, the patient is maintained on 0.5% loteprednol twice daily and 0.1% tacrolimus twice daily. We will attempt to taper and eventually discontinue the steroid if the patient’s condition remains stably controlled in follow-up. However, in consideration of the patient’s history of rapid disease recurrence after self-discontinuation of medication, we will maintain indefinite regular administration of the tacrolimus eye drops.

To fully understand the pathophysiological mechanism behind this dramatic clinical reversal, we must distinguish between the underlying cellular mechanisms and the macroscopic clinical presentation. Both true limbal stem cell depletion (which leads to irreversible total LSCD) and inflammatory epithelial dysfunction may manifest clinically as VKC-related pannus or conjunctival overgrowth. However, their pathophysiological foundations differ significantly. True limbal stem cell depletion involves the absolute and irreversible loss of stem cells, a refractory condition that inevitably requires surgical reconstruction [8]. In contrast, inflammatory epithelial dysfunction occurs when chronic inflammatory cytokines disrupt the limbal stem cell niche. This suppresses stem cell proliferation and normal differentiation, leading to the clinical appearance of total LSCD, but crucially, without causing complete stem cell depletion [9–12]. In our case, the significant return of corneal transparency following intensive anti-inflammatory therapy with loteprednol and tacrolimus confirms that the initial presentation was driven by inflammatory dysfunction rather than irreversible stem cell depletion. Controlling the inflammation relieved the suppression on residual viable stem cells [13], restored niche homeostasis, and enabled endogenous stem cells to successfully re-epithelialize the cornea [14, 15].

Although VKC is a self-limiting disease that typically resolves after puberty, its course can persist into adulthood in approximately 10% of patients [16]. Adult VKC is classified into early-onset (childhood onset with persistence) and late-onset (post-pubertal onset), with both phenotypes associated with a higher risk of complications such as LSCD [17]. The patient had a clear history of childhood onset, and the rapid disease recurrence following medication cessation, with the clinical course extending to a 30-month follow-up when the patient was 18 years and 3 months old (in adulthood), confirms the persistent nature of the inflammation. Therefore, this case meets the diagnostic criteria for childhood-onset VKC persisting into adulthood.

We acknowledge that in vivo confocal microscopy and impression cytology are considered the gold standards for the diagnosis of LSCD. However, these ancillary diagnostic tests were not performed in this case, which represents a limitation of our study. The diagnosis of severe VKC combined with apparent LSCD was established based on typical clinical symptoms, slit-lamp photography, and AS-OCT findings. Furthermore, it is important to note that while AS-OCT demonstrated the structural recovery of the central cornea, it does not confirm the cellular restoration of the limbal stem cell population. Notably, the dramatic and sustained clinical recovery following intensive topical anti-inflammatory therapy further validates the diagnosis of a medically reversible or functional LSCD phenotype, as true irreversible stem cell depletion would not typically exhibit such a restoration of the ocular surface.

In conclusion, in selected cases of functional or inflammatory LSCD simulating total deficiency caused by chronic inflammatory diseases like VKC, clinicians should strictly adhere to the international expert consensus and prioritize anti-inflammatory treatment of sufficient intensity and duration as the primary and core therapeutic step. This not only creates better ocular surface conditions for potential surgery but may also, as in this case, directly reverse the condition, sparing patients from surgery and restoring their sight.

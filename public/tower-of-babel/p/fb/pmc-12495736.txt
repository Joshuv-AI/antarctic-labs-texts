Endoscopic purse-string suture technique for postoperative drain-related leakage management in gastrointestinal surgery
Authors: Guangxu Zhu, Lingai Kong, Xiuwen Liu, Shengjie Zhou, Shunyao Song, Baoqiang Shan, Youchao Xu, Qihang Sun, Xuren Lu, Qingshun Zhu, Bing Jiang, Cai Xu, Honglei Gao, Jianjun Qu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAbdominal drain-associated fistulas (ADAFs) represent severe complications following gastrointestinal surgery, where conventional surgical reintervention carries invasiveness risks and extended recovery periods. This study evaluates the clinical efficacy and safety of endoscopic purse-string suturing for managing ADAFs.MethodsA retrospective cohort analysis included 41 patients with postoperative ADAFs treated at Shandong Second Medical University and external referral centers between January 2015 and June 2024.ResultsEndoscopic intervention achieved technical success in 87.8% (36/41) of cases, with clinical success observed in 63.4% (26/41). Subgroup analysis demonstrated significantly superior clinical outcomes in isolated ADAFs versus cases involving concurrent anastomotic disruption (90.0% vs. 39.1%, P = 0.001). Multivariate regression identified two independent predictors of reduced therapeutic efficacy: preoperative anastomotic stenosis (OR = 0.083, P = 0.041) and fistulous orifice diameter > 1 cm (OR = 0.039, P = 0.004). During 12-month follow-up, no mortality or fistula recurrence occurred. Postprocedural anastomotic stenosis developed in 4.9% (2/41) of patients, successfully managed through endoscopic balloon dilation (n = 1) and gastrointestinal stent placement (n = 1).ConclusionEndoscopic purse-string suturing emerges as a minimally invasive, safe, and effective therapeutic strategy for ADAFs, particularly demonstrating optimal clinical performance in isolated drain-associated fistulas with orifices ≤ 1 cm diameter.Keywords: Endoscopic intervention, Purse-string suturing, Gastrointestinal surgery, Anastomotic leakage

Introduction

Abdominal drainage tubes serve critical functions in postoperative care and complication prevention following gastrointestinal surgeries. Drain-associated fistulas represent a serious postoperative complication frequently precipitating secondary clinical crises such as persistent infections and intra-abdominal abscesses, substantially impeding patient recovery. Although traditional surgical interventions such as relaparotomy remain necessary in select complex cases, their high invasiveness and associated complication risks remain significant concerns.

Contemporary endoscopic techniques including the Over-the-Scope Clip (OTSC) system and fibrin glue sealing have demonstrated therapeutic potential in specific fistula presentations [1–3]. The OTSC system employs high-strength metal clips for immediate fistula closure, primarily effective for smaller defects in anatomically accessible locations. In contrast, fibrin glue sealing utilizes biological adhesives to facilitate tissue approximation, best suited for short-length fistulas with minimal exudate. While these methods exhibit distinct clinical applications, they present variable technical demands, cost implications, and durability profiles compared to endoscopic purse-string suturing techniques [4].

This study conducts a systematic evaluation of the clinical efficacy and safety profile of endoscopic purse-string suturing technology for drain-associated fistula management. We aim to elucidate the technique’s therapeutic advantages and procedural limitations, thereby providing robust evidence for optimizing clinical decision-making in fistula management.

Materials and methods

All study protocols adhered strictly to the principles of the declaration of helsinki and applicable institutional/regulatory standards. ethical approval was granted by the institutional review board of weifang people’s hospital (approval number: kyll20241220-1), with written informed consent formally waived in accordance with retrospective observational study regulations.

Definition of abdominal drainage tube-associated leakage

A postoperative complication after gastrointestinal surgery caused by direct compression, friction, or negative pressure from an intra-abdominal catheter, leading to local tissue ischemia or necrosis and subsequent formation of an abnormal channel between the drainage system and the gastrointestinal lumen or anastomotic site. Clinically manifests as persistent leakage of gastrointestinal contents (e.g., digestive fluids, food residues) through the drainage tube or sinus tract.

Study population

A retrospective analysis was conducted on 41 patients with postoperative drain-associated fistulas treated at Shandong Second Medical University and referred from external institutions between January 2015 and June 2024.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (26.0). Continuous variables with normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using independent samples t-tests. Variables violating normality assumptions were expressed as median (interquartile range [IQR]) and compared with Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical data were summarized as frequency percentages (%) and analyzed by Pearson’s chi-square tests. Variables achieving statistical significance (P < 0.05) in univariate analysis were subsequently included in multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models. P-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Secondary outcomes

Subgroup analyses demonstrated comparable clinical success rates between in-house and external referrals (88.89% vs. 56.25%; P > 0.05), with equivalent technical outcomes (88.89% vs. 87.5%; P > 0.05). Fistula morphology significantly influenced clinical success rates (P = 0.001): Isolated drain-associated fistulas exhibited higher clinical resolution (90.00%) compared to anastomotic disruption with concurrent drain-associated fistulization (39.10%). This difference was primarily associated with larger defect dimensions in complex fistulas. The clinical success rate for patients who underwent gastric surgery was 90% (9/10), which was significantly higher than that in the colorectal surgery group (54.84%, 17/31; P = 0.045). It should be noted that this finding may be partially influenced by the sample size. With regard to technical success rate, the gastric surgery group achieved 100% (10/10), compared to 83.87% (26/31) in the colorectal surgery group; however, the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.175) (Table 2).

Discussion

The placement of intraperitoneal drainage catheters in gastrointestinal surgery serves two critical purposes: prevention of postoperative fluid accumulation and real-time clinical monitoring. However, drain-associated fistulization, a severe postoperative complication, increases patient morbidity through sequelae such as intra-abdominal infections, abscess formation, and systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) [5]. Conventional surgical management via reintervention for repair or drainage has inherent limitations, including procedural invasiveness, prolonged recovery periods, and elevated risks of secondary complications during extended hospitalization. Therefore, the development of minimally invasive therapeutic alternatives with demonstrated efficacy is essential for optimized patient care.

This retrospective study analyzed 41 patients with postoperative drain-associated fistulas treated between January 2015 and June 2024 to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy and safety of the endoscopic purse-string suturing technique. The intervention achieved a technical success rate of 87.8% (36/41) and a complete clinical resolution rate of 63.4% (26/41), demonstrating both procedural feasibility and clinical significance for this complex surgical complication.

Subgroup analyses revealed no statistically significant differences in clinical success rates (88.89% vs. 56.25%, P > 0.05) or technical outcomes (88.89% vs. 87.5%, P > 0.05) between in-house and external referrals, suggesting procedural standardization across care settings. However, fistula morphology significantly affected clinical resolution: isolated drain-associated fistulas showed a 90.00% success rate, whereas anastomotic disruption with concurrent drain-associated fistulization had only 39.10% (P = 0.001). This discrepancy likely reflects challenges in managing larger defects (> 1 cm orifice diameter) compared to smaller isolated lesions. These findings align with previous studies identifying defect size as a key prognostic factor. Although referral source did not influence outcomes, anatomical heterogeneity emerged as a dominant predictor, emphasizing the need for management strategies prioritizing fistula classification and defect characteristics.

Multivariate analysis established preoperative anastomotic stricture (OR = 0.083, P = 0.041) and fistula orifice diameter > 1 cm (OR = 0.039, P = 0.004) as independent predictors of clinical outcomes following endoscopic management of drain-associated fistulas. Preoperative anastomotic strictures may compromise postoperative healing by inducing localized ischemia or mechanical tension at the anastomosis, thereby potentiating fistula formation. Larger fistula dimensions pose technical challenges due to increased demands on closure techniques [6]– [7], necessitating advanced methods such as over-the-scope clip (OTSC) systems [2] or hybrid approaches combining suturing with fibrin glue [3] to ensure durable closure.

Complications and follow-up outcomes demonstrated no mortality or fistula recurrence during the 12-month follow-up period. Anastomotic strictures occurred in 4.9% of cases (2/41), both successfully resolved via endoscopic balloon dilation and enteral stent placement. These results underscore the low complication rate and safety profile of the endoscopic purse-string suture technique for drain-associated fistulas. Although specialized equipment was utilized, the technique remains adaptable to endoscopes with equivalent functionality when paired with standard sutures and hemoclips. Notably, comparable therapeutic outcomes may be achievable in resource-limited settings through innovative adaptations.Technical implementation requires substantial endoscopic expertise. Institutions adopting this approach should ensure procedures are performed by experienced endoscopists, supplemented by specialized training.

Study limitations

This investigation acknowledges several noteworthy limitations. First, its retrospective design introduces potential selection and information biases. Second, the modest cohort size (n = 41) may limit the statistical power to detect clinically significant differences. Additionally, no direct comparisons were conducted with alternative therapeutic strategies, such as surgical interventions or conservative management. Future large-scale, prospective multicenter studies are warranted to refine clinical indications, evaluate long-term safety, and standardize postoperative follow-up protocols for robust efficacy assessments.

In conclusion

The endoscopic purse-string suture technique is an effective minimally invasive treatment for drain-related fistulas, demonstrating significant clinical value, particularly in patients with isolated drain-related fistulas and fistula orifices not exceeding 1 cm in diameter.

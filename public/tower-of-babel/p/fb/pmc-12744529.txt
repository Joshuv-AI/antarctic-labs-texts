ABSTRACT

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is the leading cause of adult hospitalization in the industrialised world and imposes a substantial burden on public health [1,2]. Hospitalization for HF (HFH), which either marks the onset of the disease or, more commonly, abruptly interrupts the course of the disease, is associated with high mortality and repeated hospitalizations [3]. Over recent decades, adoption of evidence-based therapies has substantially reduced the event rate in patients with chronic HF [4]. Guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) consists of four classes of drugs, including beta-blockers (BB), renin-angiotensin system inhibitors (RASi)/angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (ARNi), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRA), and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i). Tailored GDMT reduces morbidity and mortality and improves quality of life and is therefore strongly recommended [1,2,5]. Full efficacy is achieved when recommended drug doses are administered. There is growing evidence that optimal GDMT is safe and beneficial in patients with HF [[6], [7], [8]]. The recent STRONG-HF (Safety, tolerability, and efficacy of uptitration of guideline-directed medical therapies for acute heart failure) study has shown that rapid up-titration of GDMT, even in the early, vulnerable phase after discharge from hospitalization for acute HF, is feasible and safe and superior to usual care in terms of all-cause mortality and HFH at 180 days [9]. However, registries demonstrate low prescription rates and underdosing for GDMT in real world scenarios [[10], [11], [12], [13]].

Multidisciplinary disease management programs and home telemonitoring systems are strongly advocated for the outpatient management of HF to lower all-cause mortality and HFH [1,5]. However, even with effective management programs that improve patient adherence, full implementation of GDMT is not guaranteed. Therefore, effective strategies to improve the implementation of GDMT are crucial, especially in the early post-discharge phase [14,15].

Clinical decision support systems (CDSSs) utilizing electronic health records and advanced algorithms have emerged as a promising tool for improving patient outcomes and reducing health care costs [16].

This paper presents the design for a prospective, open, randomized trial of an assisted therapy optimizing module to improve physician adherence with GDMT in patients with HF, the AMPEL trial. This study tests the hypothesis that the AMPEL service enhances pharmacological conformity in the early post-discharge period in the setting of a multidisciplinary HF management. The overarching hypothesis is that, compared with controls, decision support for network physicians with the therapy optimizing service is associated with better adherence with an individually optimized GDMT at 90 days and consequently with better outcome at 180 days.

HerzMobil Tirol (HMT)

HMT is a three-month transitional care disease management program for patients with HF, fully established in clinical routine in the Tyrol, Austria. HMT uses a telemedical monitoring system that is integrated into a comprehensive network of healthcare providers consisting of resident network physicians with particular interest in heart failure and specialized heart failure nurses [17]. HerzMobil was founded in Tyrol and is now operated in several Austrian states including Styria.

Telemedical technology in HerzMobil Tirol

HerzMobil Tirol uses an integrated technical concept called Keep-In-Touch (KIT) to facilitate efficient and reliable daily data documentation and transfer [18]. Patients are given a blood pressure and heart rate monitor and a weighing scale as well as a specially configured smartphone for daily data acquisition and transmission. Transmitted data are displayed on the dashboard of the specialist HF nurse and of the resident physician with special interest in HF (“network physician”) assigned to the respective patient [17]. Automatic event detection indicates the need for attention to those patients who might need early therapeutic intervention. The limits used for automatic event detection are individually defined and regularly adapted for each patient by the network physician. The web-based telehealth software is made available to all stakeholders (network physicians, specialized HF nurses, helpdesk, network coordinator) and supports their individual tasks through user-specific dashboards. The prescribed heart failure medication is updated daily on the display of the smartphone and the patient must confirm the intake on the touchscreen. Necessary adjustments to the medication are decided by the network physician on the basis of the transmitted data and communicated to the patient either directly or by the heart failure nurse.

Effectiveness of HerzMobil Tirol

In a retrospective, propensity score-controlled cohort study, which included 508 hospitalized patients after discharge from HFH regardless of the underlying left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), who required intravenous (iv) diuretics, the effectiveness of HMT was compared with that of usual care [19]. The composite of time to HF readmission and all-cause mortality within six months was seen in 48 (19.1 %) patients in HMT and 89 (34.6 %) in UC. Compared with UC, management by HMT was associated with a 46 %-reduction in the primary endpoint (adjusted HR 0.54; 95 % CI 0.37–0.77; P < 0.001). After one year, 25 (10 %) patients had died in HMT compared with 66 (25.7 %) in UC (HR 0.38; 95 % CI 0.23–0.61, P < 0.001).

An economic evaluation along the cohort study showed that at the one-year time horizon HerzMobil Tirol is cost-effective as compared to usual care [20].

GDMT in HerzMobil Tirol

Although HMT is very successful, implementation of GDMT is still suboptimal when patients are discharged from the program after three months of care. On average, patients receive 53.5 % of the recommended target dose for RASi, 46.8 % for BB, 38.3 % for MRA and 75.6 % for SGLT2i. Patients are treated with an average of 40.1 % of the target dose of the quadruple therapy based on the GDMT adherence score (definition of the adherence score can be found in the section Study evaluation). Only 4.5 % of patients received all four drug classes at target dose.

Methods

AMPEL service

The AMPEL service is a module within HerzMobil that visualizes the results of the decision support service “KITGuideMe”1. KITGuideMe was developed by the Austrian Institute of Technology GmbH to improve adherence with GDMT in HMT and to provide network physicians with a technical tool to optimize pharmacologic HF therapy. The AMPEL service takes the daily updated medication from the HMT software and provides a graphical representation of the four drug classes that make up the GDMT and each prescribed dose as a percentage of the target dose according to the current European Society of Cardiology guidelines in 2021[1,5]. Differences between the columns in height and color symbolize conformity or deviation from the guidelines (Fig. 1). If a drug class or a higher dose is contraindicated, the network physician can indicate absolute or relative contraindications in a drop-down menu that is also based on the current guidelines (Fig. 2). The underlying algorithm ensures that visualization adapts dynamically if the medication and/or the specified contraindications change. Study design

The AMPEL trial is a multicentre, 1:1 randomized trial with a parallel group design that serves to evaluate the effectiveness of the Assisted Therapy Optimizing Module in a multidisciplinary heart failure disease management program. In contrast to most clinical trials, it is not the patients but the participating network physicians who are randomized for access to the AMPEL service. Physicians in the control group do not have access to the service and treat patients according to standard care for the disease management program. Patients are followed as usual by the assigned network physician and a specialist HF nurse for three months from the time of discharge from hospital following an acute HF event.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint relates to physicians’ adherence to GDMT and the clinical effects. The implementation endpoint is the proportion of patients receiving indication- and dose-corrected optimized GDMT based on the GDMT adherence score 90 days after randomization. The clinical endpoint is a hierarchical composite of death from any cause, number of and time to first HF event at 180 days, changes in the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire Total Symptom Score (KCCQ-TTS), and changes in NTproBNP at 90 days assessed with a stratified win ratio.

Secondary efficacy endpoints include the number of HF admissions and the cumulative days alive and out of hospital after six, 12 and 36 months. In addition, changes in functional status (NYHA functional class), QoL (KCCQ / EQ-5D-5L) and biomarkers (NTproBNP) will be assessed separately after 90 days.

Open-label extension phase

If the interim analysis for the implementation endpoint is positive after 90 days, the network physicians in the control group are also given access to the AMPEL service. The aim is to investigate the extent to which adherence to the GDMT in the control group, which now has access to the service, approaches that in the study group.

Study population and inclusion/exclusion criteria

The study population includes physicians who are part of the HerzMobil network in Tyrol and Styria and who take responsibility for patients in these disease management programs. This study covers all patients assigned to HerzMobil during the immediate post-discharge period after acute HF, regardless of the underlying left ventricular ejection fraction.

Study assessments

The components of the primary endpoint will be recorded for each patient treated by a randomized network physician with completed informed consent forms. The GDMT adherence score is the ratio of the prescribed therapy to the individually optimal therapy. The score is calculated for each substance, taking into account the dosages recommended in the guidelines and the contraindications indicated. Score points are allocated as follows: 0 points for not prescribing the substance class in the absence of contraindications or prescribing in the presence of contraindications; 1 point for prescribing < 50 %, 2 points for prescribing ≥ 50 % − 99 % and 3 points for prescribing the target dose. Not prescribing a substance due to specific contraindications (e.g. hypotonia, impaired kidney function, hypo- or hyperkalemia) or intolerances, which is clearly specified and limited in time by the network physician,is considered compliance with the guidelines and is given 3 points. The scores for the four substances are added together.

Death from any cause and acute HF events are recorded by telephone contact after 6, 12 and 36 months or taken from the official death register. A ‘non-fatal acute HF event’ is defined as presentation by the patient for an urgent, unscheduled visit at a clinic, office, emergency department or hospital with a primary diagnosis of HF, i.e. where the patient exhibits new or worsening symptoms of HF on presentation, has objective evidence of new or worsening HF and receives i.v. vasoactive therapy such as i.v. diuretics, i.v. vasodilators or i.v. inotropes or any combination of such interventions, defined according to Hicks et al. [21]. Clinical endpoints will be adjudicated be an idependent adjudication committee blinded to group allocation.

Vital parameters, HF medication, NYHA functional class, NT-proBNP and QoL will be assessed at baseline and at 90 days. The later by using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ), a self-administered, 23-item questionnaire that quantifies physical limitation and quality of life in patients with heart failure [22], the 5-level EQ-5D version (EQ-5D-5L) that comprises five dimensions of the patients health state and the EQ visual analogue scale (EQ VAS) [23].

Statistical considerations

The statistical hypothesis is that, compared to the control group, (i) quality of adherence to GDMT will be higher in the study group at 90 days (implementation endpoint) and (ii) the hierarchical composite of death from any cause, numbers of and time to first HF event at 180 days and changes in KCCQ-TSS and changes in NTproBNP at 90 days will favor the study group (clinical endpoint). The implementation endpoint will be compared between groups using the Chi-square test. In addition, we will report on the “time to reach GDMT” and the reasons for incomplete implementation of GDMT. The clinical endpoint will be assessed using the stratified win ratio [24]. The win ratio is a means of capturing the hierarchy amongst different types of outcomes, e.g. death, AHF events, patient-reported outcomes, and biomarkers. The Finkelstein-Schoenfeld hierarchical composite endpoint calculation will be used to compare each patient with every other patient with LVEF strata (LVEF ≤ 40 % and LVEF > 40 %) with respect to a hierarchy of outcomes. For group comparisons, univariate statistics will be used. In order to assess the potential influence of different variables on specific outcome parameters, multivariate statistics (e.g. regression analyses) will be calculated.

For secondary endpoints, we will assess the cumulative number of hospital admissions (AHF vs. cardiovascular vs. non-cardiovascular admissions), cumulative days alive and out of hospital, cumulative number of deaths, changes from baseline to 90 days in functional status (NYHA functional class), QoL, biomarkers and vital parameters in both arms performing descriptive statistics. Where data are missing, appropriate imputation methods are used where possible. No imputation is performed for the implementation endpoint. For the clinical endpoint, missing data are imputed to enable compliance with ITT principles: if data on deaths from any cause or information on heart failure events are missing, the worst-rank principle is applied. If information on quality of life or biomarkers is missing, e.g., KCCQ score or NT-proBNP assessment at baseline, the average values at baseline are imputed and used for the calculations. If information on the corresponding values after 90 days is missing, the average value after 90 days is imputed.

Statistical analysis, tables and subject data listings will be performed with SAS® for Windows version 9.4 or later (SAS institute IC., Cary, NC, USA) and R (version 4.2.2). The results will be analysed based on de-identified data, not only at the patient level but also at the level of the network physicians, so that the prescribed therapies cannot be traced back to the particular physician.

Sample size

Based on the data from the STRONG-HF study [9], TITRATE-HF [13], the Swedish Heart Failure Registry [12], and our own experience in HMT the probability of an optimal GDMT score is estimated to be 50 % in the control group, which will be increased to 66 % in the study group. Assuming a total of 234 patients (117 patients in each group) AMPEL has ≈80 % simulated power to detect a difference between groups at a two-sided significance level of 5 % using the Chi-square test. To compensate for an anticipated drop-out rate of 10 % a total of 260 patients will be included in the study. The study was not powered for the effect size of the clinical endpoint.

Assuming a positive interim analysis, a further 176 patients will be recruited in an open extension phase.

Randomization

1:1 randomization will be done at the level of network physicians. All network physicians participating in the study will be randomly assigned to either the study group having access to the AMPEL service or to the control group having no access to the service. A permutated block randomization scheme will be used to ensure the same number of patients in each study arm.

Study schedule

The first patients were enrolled in December 2024. The recruitment period will last for nine months until July 2025. Study completion is anticipated by the end of 2026.

Ethics approval

The protocol was approved by the Ethics Review Committee/Institutional Review Board (number 1394/2022) affiliated with each center and the study will be conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. All participating physicians and patients will provide written informed consent prior to any study-related procedure.

Discussion

The aim of the AMPEL trial is to investigate the effectiveness of the assisted therapy optimization service to improve physician adherence to GDMT in the early post-discharge period as part of a multidisciplinary HF management program.

HFH and the months that follow are a sentinel event in the trajectory of HF, often referred to as the vulnerable period, and have important prognostic implications [25]. Adherence to and rapid dose escalation of GDMT together with close follow-up have been shown to reduce symptoms, improve quality of life and reduce the risk of 180-day death or HF readmission as compared to usual care [9]. However, in clinical practice guideline recommendations are not consistently implemented. For example, in the CHAMP-HF (Change in the Management of Patients with Heart Failure) registry, which included more than 3,500 HFrEF (Heart Failure with reduced Ejection Fraction) outpatients in the US, approximately one-third did not receive RASi or BB, and two-thirds did not receive MRA [26]. Only 1.1 % of eligible patients received simultaneous target doses of all three classes of GDMT available at that time. Despite a trend towards progress in recent years, there is a still comparable underuse of HF care in in the United States and Europe. In a recent multinational observation of patients who started GDMT after HFH, the target dose within one year was only 15 %, 10 %, 12 % and 30 % for ACEi, ARB, BB and ARNI, respectively [27]. In a very recent analysis based on a registry in the Netherlands, 44 % of patients with chronic or worsening HF were prescribed quadruple therapy. Only 1 % of HFrEF patients received the target dose for all drug classes [13].

Several reasons have been identified as to why pharmacotherapy is not optimized for many patients: In addition to physiological limitations such as symptomatic hypotension, impaired renal function and/or hyperkalemia, lack of adherence and clinical inertia are common barriers to full implementation of GDMT. Physician inertia is based not least of all on the long-standing belief that different classes of GDMT should be introduced sequentially and that dose escalation should be done slowly and cautiously to avoid potential complications. Also, HF patients are often perceived as being very fragile, which causes medication not be changed if the patient is presumed to be “stable”, without considering the prognostic benefit of optimized GDMT[12]. In this regard, there is now ample evidence that GDMT is safe and beneficial even in the most vulnerable HF populations with an acceptable risk profile [[28], [29], [30]].

Despite the effectiveness in terms of patient outcome and costs with the well-established disease management program for HF, HerzMobil Tirol, where HF medication is accessible regardless of the patient’s socioeconomic status, there is still room for improvement in optimal GDMT. In this program, patient adherence is addressed through intensive education. We envisage that providing an easy to understand and accessible decision support tool will also encourage adherence with the GDMT by network physicians. The AMPEL service will be fully embedded into the telemedically assisted HerzMobil program. The service will display the current quality of therapy in traffic light colours on the dashboard for both the doctor and the HF nurse every day. This is intended to signal to the doctor any need for optimization. Any absolute or relative contraindications to a particular substance class or a dose escalation, which is indicated by the doctor from a pull-down menu, are taken into account in the displayed traffic light colour. The effects of changes in therapy on well-being, blood pressure, heart rate and body weight can be monitored over the subsequent days, enabling immediate therapy adjustments. The AMPEL service will, therefore, be an integral part of the DMP for HF, which ensures precise monitoring of the immediate treatment effect.

Rationale for patient selection

In a recent meta-analysis including 95.444 participants the combination of ARNi, beta-blockers, MRA, and SGLT2i has proven most effective in reducing all-cause death for patients with HFrEF [7]. Evidence for the efficacy of quadruple therapy in patients with left ventricular (LV) ejection fraction (EF) > 40 % comes from another meta-analysis that considered 13 studies with a total of 29,875 participants and found a significant reduction in the risk of CV death and HFH [8]. The effectiveness of GDMT, especially in post-discharge patients, is further supported by the STRONG-HF study [9]. Here, the combination therapy of RASi, beta-blockers and MRA proved to be very effective, particularly in patients with preserved LV function (HFpEF). SGLT2i were not yet widely available at the time this study was conducted. The results of the STRONG-HF study have been reflected in the prevailing recommendations of the 2023 focused update of the 2021 ESC Guidelines for the diagnosis and management of acute and chronic HF [5]. An intensive strategy of initiating and rapid up-titration during treatment before discharge and during frequent and careful follow-up in the first six weeks following HF hospitalization is given a Class 1B recommendation to reduce the risk of re-hospitalization or death. This recommendation was supplemented with the remark that the use of ACE-I/ARB/ARNI, beta-blockers and MRA in patients with HFrEF, HFmrEF (Heart Failure with mildly reduced Ejection Fraction) and HFpEF was investigated in STRONG-HF.

Strengths

The study has several strengths to consider. The inclusion of patients in this study will be done on an all-comers, consecutive basis, which is important with a view to avoiding or minimizing selection bias. Assessment of treatment quality will be based on the latest guidelines, which take into account both the SGLT2i and the results of the Strong HF study. In the AMPEL trial, the reasons for non-adherence or inadequate dose titration of the most recent GDMT will be carefully assessed to provide a deeper insight into the barriers to inadequate drug administration. In addition, differences in the prescription behaviour for HFrEF and HFpEF patients can be evaluated.

Limitations

First, the structure of HF care within a telemedicine-assisted disease management program can limit external generalizability of our findings. Second, since only patients following HFH are considered, study results are limited to these patients. Third, clinical endpoints will be investigator reported and not adjudicated.

Perspective

The concept of guideline-based decision support for physicians, as will be tested in the AMPEL trial, may represent an important contribution to the consistent implementation of heart failure guidelines in clinical practice. This may result in improved outcomes for many affected patients. In principle, the use of such decision support is also conceivable in telemedically supported DMPs for other chronic diseases.

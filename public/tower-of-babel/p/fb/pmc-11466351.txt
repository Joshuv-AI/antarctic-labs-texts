Plasma renin activity as a marker for predicting the antihypertensive effect of switching to sacubitril/valsartan in treated hypertensive patients: Usefulness in daily clinical practice
Authors: Takeshi Horio, Yoshio Iwashima, Minoru Yoshiyama, Daiju Fukuda, Tatemitsu Rai, Kohei Fujimoto
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe authors investigated the antihypertensive effect of sacubitril/valsartan (Sac/Val) when switching from other drugs and assessed whether brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) or plasma renin activity (PRA) before drug switching was a predictor of blood pressure lowering after switching to Sac/Val. In 92 patients with treated hypertension, clinic blood pressure, plasma BNP, and PRA were examined before and after switching to Sac/Val. Clinic systolic and diastolic blood pressures significantly decreased after drug switching to Sac/Val (p < .0001, respectively). The level before drug switching of BNP had no correlation with the change in systolic blood pressure (Δ‐SBP) before and after switching to Sac/Val, but that of PRA was significantly correlated with Δ‐SBP (r = .3807, p = .0002). A multiple regression analysis revealed that PRA before drug switching was an independent determinant of Δ‐SBP. Our findings suggest that low PRA may become a useful marker to predict the antihypertensive effect of switching to Sac/Val in treated hypertensive patients.Keywords: angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor, blood pressure lowering, brain natriuretic peptide, plasma renin activity

INTRODUCTION

Sacubitril/valsartan (Sac/Val) is currently used not only as a therapeutic agent for heart failure but also as an antihypertensive drug in Japan. 1 , 2 In particular, blood pressure lowering effects of sacubitril through its neutral endopeptidase (neprilysin)‐inhibitory action are unique and not found in any other antihypertensive drug. 3 , 4 Many studies have shown that the antihypertensive effect of Sac/Val is greater than that of angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), 5 , 6 , 7 which is probably derived from natriuretic/diuretic and direct vasodilatory effects of sacubitril through enhancing the biological activity of natriuretic peptides. 3 In real‐world clinical practice, Sac/Val is used not only for switching from ARBs, but also for switching from other classes of antihypertensive drugs including combination drugs, and as an additional drug for patients whose blood pressure is not adequately controlled. To date, however, it has not been elucidated what factors can predict the antihypertensive effect of Sac/Val. Since Sac/Val augments natriuretic peptide activity and increases sodium excretion by natriuresis, 8 , 9 we designed this study to verify the hypothesis that high plasma concentrations of brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) and low levels of plasma renin activity (PRA), which roughly speculate having salt sensitivity and a high salt intake, 10 may be predictors of the antihypertensive effect of switching to Sac/Val in treated hypertensive patients.

METHODS

Study subjects

From patients with poorly controlled hypertension even under treatment with at least one antihypertensive agent among ARB, Ca channel blocker, diuretic, β‐blocker, and mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonist at the outpatient clinic of our hospital during September 2021 and July 2023, 92 patients for whom one or two antihypertensive agents (including a fixed‐combination drug) were switched to Sac/Val (200 mg/day) and whose biochemical parameters including BNP and PRA were measured before and after the drug switching were enrolled in this study. The subjects with overt white coat phenomenon (clinic minus home systolic blood pressure ≥20 mmHg) were excluded from the study. Poorly controlled hypertension was defined as failure to achieve the target control levels of clinic and home blood pressures indicated in the current guidelines for the management of hypertension from the Japanese Society of Hypertension (JSH2019) 11 and a clinic systolic blood pressure of at least 130 mmHg. All procedures of the present study were carried out in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and national ethical guidelines for human studies. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Ishikiriseiki Hospital (Approval number: 23‐2). Written informed consent was not required because of the retrospective observational study design. Instead, we showed the information about this study at the website of our hospital and gave the patients the opportunity to opt out.

Clinical parameters

Clinic blood pressure was measured with a validated oscillometric blood pressure monitoring device (HBP‐9020, OMRON Healthcare, Kyoto, Japan). At each outpatient visit, after a resting period of at least 5 min, sitting blood pressure was measured two times consecutively with a 1‐min interval and averaged for records. The average levels of clinic blood pressures measured on respective two occasions before (−2 and 0 months) and after (2 and 4 months) drug switching to Sac/Val were assessed for the analyses. Laboratory parameters were measured just before and 4 months after drug switching. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated, based on age, sex, and serum creatinine, using a formula taken from the modification of diet in renal disease study with a modified equation for Japanese subjects. 12

Statistical analysis

No statistical sample size calculations were conducted in advance. However, the target sample size was calculated to be 73 patients when it was assessed based on 80% power and .05 significance level, to detect a difference in systolic blood pressure of 10 mmHg before and after switching to Sac/Val with its standard deviation (SD) of 15 mmHg.

Values were expressed essentially as mean ± SD and as median (25, 75 percentiles) for BNP and PRA because of the right skew in their distributions. The significance of differences in various parameters before and after drug switching was evaluated with a paired t‐test, but for BNP and PRA, non‐parametrically using Wilcoxon signed‐rank test. An unpaired Student's t‐test was used for comparison between the two groups. Simple correlations between variables were assessed using a univariate linear regression analysis and Pearson's correlation coefficient. A multiple regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of systolic blood pressure fall after drug switching to Sac/Val. Log‐transformed levels of BNP and PRA were used in the linear regression and multiple regression analyses. A value of p < .05 was accepted as statistically significant.

RESULTS

The clinical characteristics of 92 patients in the present study are summarized in Table 1. The mean age was 69.8 years old and the ratio of men was 49%. As switched drugs, ARB (54%) was the most frequent, followed by ARB plus diuretic (23%), including a fixed‐combination drug, and Ca channel blocker (10%). Systolic and diastolic blood pressures significantly decreased after drug switching to Sac/Val (p < .0001, respectively). Plasma BNP levels slightly but significantly increased after treatment with Sac/Val (p = .0376). The other biochemical parameters including PRA did not significantly change before and after drug switching.

When investigating the associations of BNP and PRA levels before drug switching (BNP‐pre and PRA‐pre, respectively) with changes in systolic blood pressure (Δ‐SBP) by switching to Sac/Val, PRA‐pre, but not BNP‐pre, was significantly correlated with Δ‐SBP (Figure 1A and C). The positive correlation between PRA‐pre and Δ‐SBP was observed both when the patients were limited to those switched from ARBs (n = 50, r = .3809, p = .0064) and to those switched from the other drug patterns (n = 42, r = .4200, p = .0056). The patient group with lower PRA‐pre (≤1.0 ng/mL/h) had a greater systolic blood pressure fall than those with normal/higher PRA‐pre, although Δ‐SBP did not differ between the two groups with normal (≤18.4 pg/mL) and higher BNP‐pre (Figure 1B and D).

Independent predictors of systolic blood pressure fall after drug switching to Sac/Val were examined by multiple regression analysis. As a result, PRA‐pre (log‐transformed value) was a significant determinant (β = .2459, p = .0151) of Δ‐SBP by switching to Sac/Val, independently of several confounders such as baseline systolic blood pressure, renal function (eGFR), and the uses of ARB or diuretic before drug switching.

DISCUSSION

Most of the studies to date on the antihypertensive effect of Sac/Val have compared it with the antihypertensive effect of ARBs 5 , 6 , 7 or examined the effects of switching from ARBs. 2 , 13 , 14 In a real‐world clinical setting, however, Sac/Val is used not only for switching from ARBs, but also for switching from other classes/patterns of antihypertensive drugs, and as an additional drug for patients whose blood pressure is not adequately controlled. The present study showed that switching to Sac/Val from various antihypertensive drugs (although predominantly from agents including ARBs) was useful for additional blood pressure lowering in poorly controlled hypertensive patients.

Neprilysin inhibition by sacubitril increases circulating levels of natriuretic peptides, which exerts multiple actions such as natriuresis/diuresis, vasodilation, and so on. 3 , 4 Thus, we speculated that plasma levels of BNP might be related to the blood pressure‐lowering effect after treatment with Sac/Val. However, BNP levels before drug switching were not associated with Δ‐SBP before and after switching to Sac/Val in the present subjects. The findings suggest that at least in hypertensive patients without overt heart failure, the antihypertensive effect of Sac/Val is probably not significantly influenced by their plasma BNP levels. On the other hand, lower levels of PRA before drug switching were significantly associated with Δ‐SBP after switching to Sac/Val and were an independent predictor of Δ‐SBP. To the best of our knowledge, this study was the first to identify a factor predicting the antihypertensive effect of switching to Sac/Val in treated hypertension. Low PRA is considered to reflect roughly having salt sensitivity and a high salt intake, 10 and further patients become more salt sensitive under treatment with ARBs. In high salt‐induced hypertension or salt‐sensitive hypertension, natriuretic/diuretic actions of Sac/Val (sacubitril) through enhancing the biological activity of natriuretic peptides by neprilysin inhibition have been shown to be responsible for the antihypertensive effect of the agent. 8 , 9 Considering such findings, it may be reasonable that patients with lower PRA even under antihypertensive therapy including ARBs and diuretics, which is presumed to represent substantial salt sensitivity and/or high salt intake sate, are more likely to benefit from the antihypertensive effects of Sac/Val via enhanced sodium excretion. Unfortunately, we did not evaluate urinary sodium excretion and its changes after treatment with Sac/Val in the present subjects, but obtaining these data may help to better clarify the mechanism of antihypertensive effects of switching to Sac/Val.

There are several limitations in this study. First, the present findings were derived from observation without control arms carried out in a single center with a relatively small sample size. In addition, variabilities in the classes, types, and doses of baseline antihypertensive drugs switched to Sac/Val may have affected the results of the study. Furthermore, PRA levels were measured under antihypertensive medications in the present study, although some classes of antihypertensive agents significantly influence PRA. On the contrary, our findings may indicate that PRA can be used as a marker to predict the antihypertensive effect of switching to Sac/Val even under various medications, that is, in daily clinical practice.

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that switching to Sac/Val from various antihypertensive drugs (mainly from ARBs but including from other classes of agents) is useful for additional blood pressure lowering in inadequately controlled hypertensive patients and suggests low PRA may become a predictor of the antihypertensive effect of switching to Sac/Val in such patients.

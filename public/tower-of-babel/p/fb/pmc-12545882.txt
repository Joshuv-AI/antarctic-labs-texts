Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “Re-examining the Causal Link Between Immunosuppression Delay and Kidney Outcomes in Autoimmune Membranous Nephropathy”
Authors: Patrick Hamilton, Sebastian Bate, Omar Ragy, Mrityunjay Hiremath, Samar Bukhari, Anirudh Rao, Arif Khwaja, Durga Anil K Kanigicherla
Published: 2025

The Author Replies:

We thank Chen et al.1 for their letter regarding our manuscript, “Impact of Time-To-Treatment on Outcomes in Autoimmune Membranous Nephropathy”2 and welcome the opportunity to clarify our conclusions and acknowledge the important nuances raised.

First, we agree that causality cannot be definitively inferred from a retrospective analysis. Our primary intention was to quantify the change in renal function during the watchful-wait period and highlight that kidney function often declines significantly before immunosuppression is initiated. Although it is true that baseline disease severity may partially drive both delay and decline, our findings demonstrate a consistent and statistically significant association between longer time-to-treatment and greater estimated glomerular filtration rate loss, including a median decline of 11 ml/min per 1.73 m2/yr in those waiting over 6 months.

Second, regarding selection bias, we acknowledge that limiting the analysis to patients who eventually received immunosuppression excludes those achieving spontaneous remission. However, our risk stratification, based solely on baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate and urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio, was designed to identify patients less likely to remit spontaneously. In our cohort, high-risk groups had a markedly lower spontaneous remission rate (20%) and higher progression to chronic kidney disease stage 5 (25%), supporting the utility of early identification of these patients. Although the potential for confounding exists, the consistent trend of renal decline across all time points of watchful wait, even in moderate-risk patients, suggests that delay is not benign.

And though we share the concern about avoiding unnecessary immunosuppression, our conclusions do not advocate universal early treatment but advocate a personalised approach; reserve watchful waiting for low-risk and potentially moderate-risk patients and consider earlier immunosuppression in high-risk groups to prevent irreversible decline. This aligns with emerging data on rituximab’s favourable safety profile and could reduce long-term morbidity without overtreating remitters. Prospective trials, ideally incorporating biomarkers such as anti-phospholipase A2 receptor titres are essential to refine these strategies and address psychological burdens of watchful waiting.

We believe our findings highlight a need to reevaluate “benign” observation in selected patients, prompting more personalised guidelines. Thank you again for engaging with our work, to help clarify these complexities.

The novel anticoagulant dabigatran versus enoxaparin for thromboprophylaxis following total hip replacement: a meta-analysis
Authors: Junwu Huang, Juhua Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNovel oral anticoagulants (NOACs) are now more often being prescribed following surgery to reduce the risk of thromboembolism. In this analysis we aimed to compare dabigatran with enoxaparin for thromboprophylaxis following total hip replacement (THR).MethodsOnline databases were searched for studies solely comparing dabigatran with enoxaparin for thromboprophylaxis following THR. Relevant studies were finalized after carefully assessing the titles and abstracts. The clinical outcomes including outcomes of efficacy and safety were considered as the endpoints. The updated version of RevMan software was used to carry out this statistical analysis. Risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used to represent the results.ResultsA total number of 4 studies including 6168 participants were assigned to dabigatran following THR whereas 3370 participants were assigned to enoxaparin resulting in a total number of 9538 participants which were included in this analysis. The time frame for participants’ enrollment varied from year 2002 to year 2012. Results of this analysis showed that when dabigatran was compared with enoxaparin following THR, deep venous thrombosis (DVT) (RR: 0.97, 95% CI: 0.80 – 1.18; P = 0.77), venous thromboembolism (VTE) (RR: 0.92, 95% CI: 0.70 – 1.19; P = 0.51) and pulmonary embolism (RR: 0.83, 95% CI: 0.25 – 2.71; P = 0.76) were similarly manifested. In addition, major bleeding (RR: 1.25, 95% CI: 0.85 – 1.84; P = 0.26), fatal bleeding (RR: 2.50, 95% CI: 1.12 – 52.03; P = 0.55), minor bleeding (RR: 1.08, 95% CI: 0.89 – 1.32; P = 0.44), bleeding in a critical organ (RR: 2.98, 95% CI: 0.12 – 73.05; P = 0.50) and bleeding requiring blood transfusion (RR: 1.11, 95% CI: 0.77 – 1.59; P = 0.58) were still similarly manifested. However, when dabigatran 225 mg dosage was compared with enoxaparin, the risks for DVT (RR: 0.79, 95% CI: 0.63 – 1.00; P = 0.05) and VTE (RR: 0.65, 95% CI: 0.46 – 0.92; P = 0.02) were significantly lower with dabigatran without any increase in bleeding events.ConclusionIt can be concluded that the NOAC dabigatran was equally effective compared to enoxaparin for thrombo-prophylaxis following THR. No significant thrombotic event was observed with dabigatran when compared to enoxaparin. However, a higher dosage of dabigatran was associated with significantly reduced DVT and VTE. Nevertheless, due to the several limitations of this analysis, the bleeding outcomes were inconclusive and therefore, the results should be confirmed in further newer studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05585-3.

Introduction

Today, the risk of thromboembolism has considerably increased in patients undergoing major orthopaedic surgeries [1]. The long-term immobilization period or certain health issues might be the main causes of thromboembolism following major orthopaedic surgeries [2]. Therefore, a standard practice to give prophylaxis for thromboembolism has become necessary [3]. Enoxaparin has often been used as thromboprophylaxis following orthopaedic surgeries [4]. Evidence based guidelines proposed by the American College of Chest Physicians (ACCP) [5] and the National Institute for Health and Clinical Excellence (NICE) have recommended prophylaxis with low molecular weight heparin (enoxaparin) following total hip replacement (THR) [6–8]. However, the only available mode of application is by the subcutaneous route. Recently, several novel oral anticoagulants (NOACs) have been approved for use in the clinical settings [9]. Dabigatran, a factor IIa inhibitor, has already gained popularity in the cardiology department [10]. This NOAC can also be used as thromboprophylaxis following THR [11]. In this analysis we aimed to compare dabigatran with enoxaparin for thromboprophylaxis following THR.

Methods

Search databases and search strategies

Web of science, MEDLINE (Pubmed), EMBASE, the Cochrane database, Google scholar, and www.ClinicalTrials.gov were the platform for data sources whereby studies comparing dabigatran and enoxaparin following THR were searched using specific terms from 15th October 2025 until 30th November 2025.

Searched terms which were used included: ‘dabigatran versus enoxaparin in total hip arthroplasty’, ‘dabigatran versus enoxaparin in total hip replacement’, ‘dabigatran versus enoxaparin in THR’, ‘dabigatran versus enoxaparin in THR’, ‘dabigatran versus low molecular weight heparin in total hip replacement’, ‘dabigatran versus low molecular weight heparin in total hip arthroplasty’, ‘dabigatran versus LMWH and total hip arthroplasty’, ‘dabigatran versus LMWH in total hip replacement’, ‘novel oral anticoagulants and total hip arthroplasty’, ‘novel oral anticoagulants and total hip replacement’.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were restricted to (1) randomized trials or observational cohorts which showed the comparison between dabigatran versus enoxaparin following THR; (2) studies that showed the clinical outcomes (efficacy and safety outcomes) between these two types of thromboprophylactic agents; (3) English publications.

The exclusion criteria which also form an integral part of this selection of study included (1) literature reviews, meta-analyses or systematic reviews; (2) case studies, letters to editors or commentary or editorials; (3) Non-English publications; (4) studies that did not report the efficacy and safety outcomes; (4) same studies or trials that were repeated in other studies with same data and same contents.

Data extraction and quality assessment

After completing the study selection, the finalized original publications were carefully studied and data were extracted. Relevant data which were extracted began with the authors’ names and year of publications, followed by the relevant efficacy and safety outcomes which were reported in each study, as well as the total number of participants which were assigned to be treated with dabigatran and enoxaparin respectively. In addition, further data were collected in view of assessing the methodological quality of the studies. Minor details such as the type of study, the baseline features of the participants as well as the dosage of medications were all carefully extracted and documented. Another major data concerning the number of events associated with each outcome to be assessed were carefully extracted to be used in the data processing software.

The recommendations of the Cochrane collaboration [12] were used to assess the Risk of Bias (ROB) among the trials whereas the Newcastle Ottawa Scale (NOS) [13] was used to assess the ROB for the observational study.

Statistical analysis

An updated version of the Revman software (version 5.4) was used to analyze the data. Heterogeneity was addressed using the Q statistic test and the I2 statistic test. A subgroup analysis showing a P value less or equal to 0.05 was considered statistically significant whereas a P value above the 0.05 threshold was considered to represent an insignificant result. In addition, concerning the I2 statistic test, a subgroup analysis with an I2 greater than 50% was considered highly heterogeneous whereby a random effect statistical model was used during data analysis and a subgroup analysis with an I2 less than 50% showed lower heterogeneity whereby a fixed effects statistical model was used. In brief, the higher the I2 value obtained, the greater the heterogeneity.

A method of exclusion was used to carry out sensitivity analysis whereby each study was excluded one by one and a new analysis was carried out each time and the new results which were generated were compared with the main results of this analysis to observe for any significant difference. In addition, RevMan generated funnel plots were used to assess for publication bias.

Compliance to ethical guidelines

This study did not involve experiment on humans or animals carried out by any of the authors. Data were obtained from previous original studies. Therefore, an ethical approval was not required for this type of study.

Search outcomes

The Preferred Reporting Items in Meta-Analyses guideline [14] was followed for this meta-analysis. Following a thorough search through the electronic databases, a total number of 143 publications were obtained. Following a careful assessment of the titles, an initial elimination was carried out whereby studies which were irrelevant were directly eliminated. 64 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility.

Those 64 publications were carefully studied and based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, further eliminations were carried out. The reasons leading to elimination of full-text studies were:Pooled analyses, meta-analyses, systematic reviews (n = 6);Commentaries and letters to the editor (n = 4);Studies that were subset of previously published trials (n = 10);Studies that were published in a different language apart from English (n = 4);Repeated studies (n = 36).

Definition of terms

THR: also known as total hip arthroplasty, is a surgery that replaces the hip joint by artificial prosthetic components made of metal, plastic or ceramic usually to relieve severe pain and improve mobility from conditions like arthritis or following fracture of the hip joint.

Major bleeding events: were defined as clinically overt bleeding associated with a significant fall in hemoglobin levels, leading to blood transfusion, as well as fatal bleeding including retroperitoneal, intracranial, intraocular and intracranial bleeding leading to abortion of treatment and re-operation.

Minor bleeding events: were defined as those bleeding events which did not fulfill the criteria of major bleeding as stated above.

DVT: is defined as blood clots which form in deep veins especially in the legs post-operatively in our study.

PE: is defined as a piece of DVT which breaks and travel to the lungs obstructing blood supply there.

VTE: is a life threatening condition including DVT and PE, involving blood clots in veins.

Publication bias

Since this analysis consisted of less than 10 original studies, an Egger’s test was not possible to show publication bias. Instead, publication bias was visually observed by assessing the symmetry of funnel plots which were generated by the RevMan software and hence, based on this assessment of the funnel plots, there were no evidence of publication bias across all the studies that were implicated when assessing the endpoints of this analysis. Discussion

Our analysis showed both dabigatran and enoxaparin to be associated with similar efficacy and safety outcomes following THR. However, with a higher dosage of dabigatran (225 mg), significantly lower risks of DVT and VTE were observed without any increase in bleeding events.

A published meta-analysis showed dabigatran to be associated with similar VTE when compared to enoxaparin supporting the results of our current analysis [19]. However, the meta-analysis included data for both knee and hip arthroplasty together. In addition, another study showed dabigatran to be effective as a thromboembotic prophylaxis following primary THR without increasing the risk of bleeding [20]. A prospective cohort comparative study of rivaroxaban, dabigatran and apixaban oral thromboprophylaxis in 2431 THR and total knee replacement (TKR) participants showed similar rate of VTE in comparison to heparin, however, rivaroxaban was better compared to dabigatran or apixaban [21].

A nationwide analysis has shown that timing of venous thromboembolism prophylaxis is very important and administering this prophylaxis within 24 h of admission and within 12 h post major orthopaedic surgery is apparently more beneficial with a significant decrease of VTE without any increase bleeding risk or re-operation [22].

Our current analysis was based on the efficacy and safety of dabigatran versus enoxaparin following THR in the general population. However, another major discussion could be based on whether such NOACs would be beneficial in patients with high risk group including patients with chronic kidney diseases and those with malignancy [23]. Although guidelines have suggested NOACs to be the first line anticoagulant agents in the management of DVT, their use in patients with chronic kidney diseases is not clear. In most of the trials, patients with severe chronic kidney disease having a creatinine clearance of less than 30 ml/minute were often excluded and therefore less data are available based on NOACs and end stage renal diseases [24]. However, based mainly on its pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic features (having the lowest renal elimination among all the NOACs) [25], the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved apixaban as the only NOACs which can be used for the treatment of VTE in patients with end stage renal disease. Therefore, in the absence of strong evidences, NOACs should at least cautiously be used or avoided in patients with end stage renal disease.

Furthermore, patients with malignancy are at higher risk for DVT compared to the general population [26]. However, even though such a population requires prophylaxis for VTE, several major trials based on NOACs have not included patients with cancer under representing this category of patients [27]. A network meta-analysis including 3242 participants with cancer showed NOACs and low molecular weight heparin to be equally effective in preventing cancer associated VTE [28]. However, some controversies still exists with the use of NOACs in cancer patients due to possible drug interaction during chemotherapy [29], therefore, the International Initiative on Thrombosis and Cancer guidelines agree to the use of NOACs in stable cancer patients who are not on active chemotherapy [29]. For example, low dose apixaban (2.5 mg twice daily) and rivaroxaban (10 mg once daily) have shown to be tolerable in stable patients with cancer [30, 31].

Other regimens have also been used to prevent VTE in orthopaedic surgery. For example, safflower yellow has been combined with LMWH and shown better efficacy compared to heparin alone for preventing DVT post-operatively representing another thromboprophylactic regimen [32]. Aspirin has also been used instead of direct oral anticoagulants and with promising results. A systematic review and meta-analysis showed the comparative efficacy of aspirin versus direct oral anticoagulants (rivaroxaban 10 mg od) for VTE prophylaxis following primary TKR or THR and the results showed no significant difference in the risk of VTE, DVT and major bleeding suggesting aspirin as a good option [33]. When apixaban was compared with aspirin for VTE following orthopaedic surgery, apixaban was not an effective option as aspirin [34]. Apixaban was associated with an increase risk of thrombotic events as well as a higher bleeding risk when compared to aspirin. Other studies have also pointed out the risks of major bleeding and VTE in patients undergoing major orthopaedic surgeries using therapeutic dosages of direct oral anticoagulants [35].

Limitations

Similar to other studies, the limited number of participants and studies restricted the efficacy and robustness of this analysis. Secondly, for the main results, different dosages of dabigatran were combined, pooled and analyzed (150 mg, 225 mg, 300 mg once daily versus twice daily) into a single group despite different pharmacokinetic and safety profiles and this compromised interpretability of the analysis and risks masking dose-dependent effects and therefore could have a major impact on the main analysis. Fortunately, we have carried out subgroup analyses based on dosages of dabigatran which could partly address this limitation. Another limitation could be the fact that there was no restriction on follow up time period. Follow-up time period post-operatively was seldom reported and therefore, this was ignored in our current analysis. This limits the assessment of early and late VTE post-operatively and several late VTE could have been missed if the follow-up time period was short. Also, standard follow up time periods could have resulted in more effective outcomes. Furthermore, when analyzing a subgroup of participants using dabigatran 225 mg, we also included participants using dabigatran 200 mg in this same subgroup due to lack of studies and data. This could be another limitation of our study. Moreover, other co-morbidities of the participants were not taken into consideration and this could have had an impact on the outcomes. The dosage of enoxaparin was not stated in most of the studies. This could also have had an impact on the main results. Even though we included one observational study with a small number of participants along with all the trials, this combination of randomized trials and observational studies could result in the introduction of several forms of bias. However, the impact was very low since the observational study included a strict minimum number of participants which might not have a major effect on the main results. Moreover, another limitation could be considered due to the fact that this study protocol was not registered in PROSPERO. In this analysis, we have included studies which enrolled participants from year 2002 to year 2012. Even though the data are quite old, there was no recent data based on this comparison of dabigatran versus enoxaparin following THR. Even studies which were published in recent years have included data from previous trials. Due to this limited publications on this important topic, following a careful search strategy, we have included all the relevant studies even though patients were enrolled 20 years ago. This could be another major limitation of this analysis and subsequently limits the clinical relevance of this analysis since in this new era, peri-operative pathways, surgical techniques, protocols for thromboprophylaxis and anticoagulant dosing strategies have evolved.

Conclusions

It can be concluded that the NOAC dabigatran was equally effective compared to enoxaparin for thrombo-prophylaxis following THR. No significant thrombotic event was observed with dabigatran when compared to enoxaparin. However, a higher dosage of dabigatran was associated with significantly reduced DVT and VTE. Nevertheless, due to the several limitations of this analysis, the bleeding outcomes were inconclusive and therefore, the results should be confirmed in further newer studies.

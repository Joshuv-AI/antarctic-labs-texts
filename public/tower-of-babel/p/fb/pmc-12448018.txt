The design and evaluation of the opportunistic endoscopic screening (OpENS) program for upper gastrointestinal tract cancers: a real-world study in China
Authors: Kexin Sun, Shun He, Huadong Wang, Liang Qiao, Nan Zhang, Jiyu Tuo, Li Yuan, Lingbin Du, Shaokai Zhang, Yuqin Liu, Yong Liu, Liyan Xue, Shangchun Jia, Wenqiang Wei, Guiqi Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundThe effectiveness of endoscopic screening for upper gastrointestinal (UGI) tract cancers in high-risk areas of China has been well-established. However, the practicality of extending this screening to a wider geographical area remains uncertain. To bridge this gap, we have conducted a hospital-based opportunistic endoscopic screening (OpENS) program for UGI cancers since 2018. Our objectives were to elucidate the implementation process of the OpENS program and assess its effectiveness.Methods875 hospitals from 710 districts/counties have participated in the OpENS program during 2019–2023. The endoscopic specialists and pathologists participating in the program were mandated to take annual training programs to acquire the fundamentals of screening techniques. Eligible patients who underwent endoscopic examinations were screened for UGI cancers. Patients diagnosed with high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (HGIN), carcinoma in situ (CIS) and tumors in esophagus or/and stomach were defined as positive cases. Patients with HGIN and CIS were defined as early cases. All hospitals were required to submit screening data via the program's platform, with both the quality of the submitted data and the hospitals' performance being subject to a comprehensive evaluation. The age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) for the districts/counties where the participating hospitals were situated were derived from the cancer registry data for the year 2020. Districts/counties with ASIRs for UGI cancers over 22.0/105 were classified as high-risk areas. The positive detection rate (PDR) and early diagnosis rate (EDR) were calculated.FindingsAfter data cleaning, we included 808 hospitals from 616 districts/counties, with a collective participation of 7,066,892 individuals during 2019–2023. The overall PDR and EDR across all sites were 2.35% and 19.77%. The PDRs and EDRs were 1.02% and 23.18% in esophagus, and were 1.37% and 17.80% in stomach. The PDR was higher among males compared to females (3.59% vs 1.20%), and was increasing with age. The EDR was higher among females compared to males (20.66% vs 19.45%), peaking in the age group of 60–64 years. The PDRs and EDRs were higher in high-risk areas of UGI cancers (p < 0.05). After adjusting for age, sex, province, year of screening, regional UGI cancer incidence level and hospital tier, the hospitals that consecutively participated in the program for five years demonstrated higher PDRs and EDRs when compared to other hospitals (p < 0.05). Among the consecutively participated hospitals, tertiary-level hospitals demonstrated positive associations with the PDRs for both the esophagus and stomach when compared to secondary-level hospitals (p < 0.001). However, the tertiary-level hospitals showed a negative association with the EDR for the esophagus (OR = 0.91, 95% CI: 0.86–0.96, p = 0.001), but exhibited a positive association with the EDR for the stomach (OR = 1.11, 95% CI: 1.05–1.17, p < 0.001).InterpretationThe OpENS program has rapidly expanded across the country. The program has demonstrated high PDRs and EDRs, with hospitals that performed well exhibiting significantly better screening outcomes. Among these well-performed hospitals, secondary-level hospitals can achieve comparable results to tertiary-level hospitals in screening for esophageal lesions, albeit not for stomach lesions. Considering the current hospital capacities in China, the training-guided opportunistic UGI endoscopy screening exhibits significant feasibility and effectiveness across areas with varying level of UGI risks.Funding10.13039/501100005150CAMS Innovation Fund for Medical Sciences (No. 2021-I2M-1-061, 2021-I2M-1-023).

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Upper gastrointestinal (UGI) endoscopy has been established as the recommended screening method for UGI cancers in China. Previous randomized controlled studies have indicated that endoscopic screening in high-risk areas of China may result in a reduction in mortality rates for UGI cancers. And the existing population-based screening strategy has been proven to be cost-effective. However, to our knowledge, the effectiveness of the endoscopic screening technique in real-world medical settings across a broader region in China have yet to be adequately discussed. The optimal approach for hospital-based UGI screening efforts in areas with varying levels of UGI cancer risk remains uncertain.

Added value of this study

Since 2018, we have launched an opportunistic endoscopic screening (OpENS) program for UGI cancer. Between 2019 and 2023, 808 hospitals have submitted screening data of high quality for 7,066,892 individuals. The positive detection rate (PDR) and early diagnosis rate (EDR) of UGI tract was 2.35% and 19.77%. The PDRs for esophagus and stomach were 1.02% and 1.37%, respectively, while the EDRs were 23.18% and 17.80%, respectively. Age and sex and the regional risk level for UGI cancers were factors that influenced the PDRs and EDRs. Our findings indicated that the hospitals that consecutively participated in the program, designated as well-performing, exhibited notably higher PDRs and EDRs when compared to other hospitals. While well-performing secondary-level hospitals can compete with their tertiary-level counterparts in esophageal early diagnosis, their diagnostic capability for stomach conditions still necessitates improvement.

Implications of all the available evidence

We have reported the positive detection rates and early diagnosis rates of this training-guided opportunistic UGI endoscopy screening program in both high-risk and low-risk areas, utilizing extensive clinical data at the patient-level. This information can serve as a foundational benchmark for subsequent studies. The OpENS program has demonstrated effectiveness in both its screening process and implementation. However, we have also identified certain shortcomings, particularly in adhering to the guidelines and achieving satisfactory screening results in the stomach, especially in secondary-level hospitals. These issues have highlighted the areas for improvement in the future, taking into account the varying capacities of hospitals across different levels in China. By addressing these challenges, we aim to collectively promote the homogenization of medical care and represent a substantial step towards achieving universal health coverage in China.

Introduction

The incidence and mortality rates of upper gastrointestinal (UGI) cancers, specifically esophageal and gastric cancer, are notably high in China, accounting for approximately 40% of all newly diagnosed cases and newly reported deaths worldwide.1 UGI endoscopy can detect and excise precancerous lesions and cancers at early stages, thereby offering the potential to reduce the mortality rate associated with UGI cancers by enhancing both early diagnosis and early treatment rates.2,3 Furthermore, this procedure can simultaneously screen for both esophageal and gastric cancers, making it particularly suited for numerous regions in China where the population faces a high risk of developing both these cancers.4

Population-based endoscopic screening has been proved as an effective strategy for preventing and controlling UGI cancers in high-risk areas of China. Three separate cohorts have consistently observed that the group undergoing endoscopic screening exhibited significantly decreased incidence and mortality rates of esophageal cancer in high-risk areas.5, 6, 7 A meta-analysis of studies conducted in Asia has revealed that endoscopic screening can reduce the gastric cancer mortality.8 One cohort study has observed a notable decrease in both the incidence and mortality rates of UGI cancers in the screened group in high-risk areas.9 A subsequent cluster randomized trial corroborated these findings. Nevertheless, it also noted that the risk-stratified endoscopic screening invitations in non-high-risk areas failed to yield the same beneficial effect.10 Based on the comprehensive evidences presented above, UGI endoscopy has become the most highly recommended screening approach for esophageal cancer and gastric cancer in China.11

However, as the promotion of the UGI endoscopic screening approach advances, there remain certain questions that have yet to be fully elucidated. Firstly, previous studies have all adhered to an organized screening protocol, inviting healthy residents to undergo endoscopic screening. The efficacy of this screening module relies on the compliance of local residents, which tends to be relatively high in high-risk areas where local researchers oversee the process and individuals are fully aware of their own risks. However, in other regions, compliance may be significantly lower, potentially resulting in the actual effectiveness of the screening program being less than that reported in cohort studies. Secondly, although the efficacy of endoscopic screening in high-risk areas is well-documented, its applicability and cost-efficiency in other areas remains incompletely understood. Thirdly, the implementation of organized screening programs relies on the capabilities of the local cancer control and prevention team, a process that can be time-consuming. Given the substantial number of UGI cancer patients and potential high-risk individuals, there is an urgent need to identify an alternative screening module that can facilitate the rapid dissemination of endoscopic screening technologies.

Since 2018, an opportunistic endoscopic screening (OpENS) program for UGI cancers aimed at addressing the above challenges has been initiated. The primary objective of this program is to integrate the UGI endoscopic screening technology into the routine clinical practices for endoscopic diagnosis and treatment within hospitals located across diverse areas of China. In this study, we aim to report the outcome of this program and evaluate the effectiveness of UGI endoscopic screening in different areas, using real-world clinical data collected throughout the program's duration.

Methods

Study design and participants

During 2019 to 2023, a total of 875 hospitals from 710 districts/counties have participated in the OpENS programs and submitted data of clinical records. The endoscopic specialists and pathologists participating in the program were mandated to take annual training programs to acquire the fundamentals of screening techniques. After signing the informed consent and agreeing to participate, eligible patients were enrolled in the study and underwent a standardized UGI endoscopic screening procedure, with necessary treatment administered thereafter as required (Supplementary Material S1).

Endoscopic screening and histological diagnosis

All endoscopic examinations and biopsies were performed by trained doctors at local hospitals according to the guidelines for UGI cancer screening, early diagnosis and treatment in China.12 In summary, participant received upper endoscopy to visually examine the entire esophagus and stomach. Lugol's iodine solution (1.2%) was used to stain the entire esophagus, and indigo carmine dye (0.2%) was used for supplementary examination of the stomach if suspicious lesions were found. Electronic chromoendoscopy technologies that offer image enhancement have also been suggested as important auxiliary diagnostic techniques for improving the detection rate and diagnostic accuracy of cancerous lesions.

Biopsies were collected from suspicious lesions located in the esophagus, cardia and stomach, and subsequently prepared for pathologic examination and diagnosis. One pathologist examined the biopsy slides and issued the pathological report, which underwent subsequent review by a senior doctor. The information collected in the OpENS program was listed in Supplementary Material S2. The pathology diagnosis was classified and coded according to the guideline12 of the program. Patients diagnosed with high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (HGIN), carcinoma in situ (CIS) and tumors in esophagus or/and stomach were defined as positive screening cases. Patients diagnosed with HGIN and CIS were classified as early-stage cases. The detailed pathology types and corresponding codes were presented in Supplementary Material S3.

Data quality control procedure

All hospitals were required to submit UGI screening data via the program's platform. The data quality expert team of the program actively monitored the progress of the reporting and quality of data in real-time via the platform, conducting regular data review meetings and performing institutional quality control spot checks to address data quality issues. Upon discovering such issues, the team requested local hospitals to make the necessary corrections. The data cleaning and quality control procedures were then performed and the details were shown in Fig. 1 and Supplementary Material S4.Fig. 1The flowchart of the quality control process for the datasets of the opportunistic endoscopic screening (OpENS) program. The classification of high-risk and low-risk areas of UGI cancers

The locations of the participating hospitals were documented at the district/county level, and these areas were subsequently matched with the cancer registry data for the year 2020 to obtain incidence statistics for UGI cancers in these regions. For regions with no reported cancer registry data, we used the actual surveillance data from the nearest district/county within the same prefecture-level city (the superior administrative unit) as a substitute. The age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) by Segi's population were calculated.

In the present study, we used the national estimates of ASIRs for the year 2022 in China as thresholds for distinguishing between high-risk and low-risk areas. Specifically, ASIRs of 8.5/105, 14.0/105, and 22.0/105 were employed as thresholds for esophageal cancer, stomach cancer and UGI cancers, respectively.13 For sensitive analysis, we employed different combinations of thresholds for UGI cancers and assessed their impact on the primary outcomes. The detailed information was shown in Supplementary Material S5.

Statistical analysis

In the OpENS program, the cardia underwent separate histological diagnosis and coding. However, in the current study, the cardia has been incorporated into the stomach for analysis. The positive detection rate (PDR) is calculated by dividing the number of positive cases by the total number of screening patients within the same group. The early diagnosis rate (EDR) is calculated by dividing the number of early-stage cases by the total number of positive cases within the same group. Differences in the distribution of pathological types, the biopsy rates, positive detection rate (PDR) and early diagnosis rate (EDR) were analyzed using the Chi-square test. Logistic regression was used to examine associations between reporting consistency, hospital tiers, and screening outcomes. Statistical analyses were carried out with Stata (version 18.0, Stata Corporation, College Station, Texas, USA).

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the independent Ethics Committee of the National Cancer Center of China/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College (Approval No. 16-171/1250, 17-124/1380, and 24/565-4845).

Role of the funding source

The funder of this study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or the writing of the report.

Results

A total of 875 hospitals have submitted 10,056,080 UGI endoscopic screening records during 2019.1.1 to 2023.12.31. 2,989,188 (29.73%) records have been deleted during the data cleaning and quality control processes (Fig. 1). To justify the data quality control processes, we calculated the PDRs and EDRs for each quality control step. It was revealed that the PDRs were disproportionately high in Step 3, which involved excluding records due to multiple endoscopic examinations, and in Step 4, where participants were excluded due to a prio cancer registry record of UGI cancer. Conversely, the EDR was relatively low in Step 4, and Step 5 where participants were excluded due to quality control issues associated with the screening institution (Supplementary Material S4).

After data quality control, a total of 808 hospitals were included in the study, with a collective participation of 7,066,892 individuals. Among these, 513 (63.49%) secondary-level hospitals contributed 3,126,231 (44.24%) participants, while 295 (36.51%) tertiary-level hospitals accounted for 3,940,661 (55.76%) participants. During the five-year project period, 125 (15.47%) hospitals consistently participated and provided high-quality data for 2,379,389 (33.67%) individuals, whereas 683 (84.53%) hospitals participated intermittently. The 808 hospitals covered 616 districts/counties nationwide, including 288 (46.75%) low-risk areas and 328 (53.25%) high-risk areas. The 125 hospitals that consistently reported data covered 115 districts/counties, including 45 (39.13%) low-risk areas and 70 (60.87%) high-risk areas (Supplementary Tables S1–S3).

When analyzed by year, hospitals that have consistently participated in the program for five years demonstrated both higher and more stable PDRs and EDRs compared to other hospitals (Supplementary Figures S3 and S4). The PDRs for inconsistently reporting hospitals in low-risk areas showed a significantly decreasing trend as the program expanded over the years. The EDR for consistently reporting hospitals in high-risk areas showed a slight albeit statistically insignificant upward trend (Supplementary Table S6).

To gain a deeper understanding of the differences in screening performance across hospitals, we compared the PDRs and EDRs between hospitals that consecutively participated in the program, labeled as well-performing, and other hospitals. Subsequently, we compared the PDRs and EDRs between well-performing secondary-level and tertiary-level hospitals. We found that after adjusting for age, sex, province, year of screening, regional UGI cancer incidence level and hospital level, the well-performing hospitals still exhibited positive associations with PDRs and EDRs (p < 0.05) (Table 3). Discussion

In this study, we reported the baseline statistics of the OpENS program, the largest real-world screening program for UGI cancer in China. Between 2019 and 2023, a total of 808 hospitals located across 616 districts/counties invited 7,066,892 individuals to participate in the program. Among these hospitals, 125 have consistently provided data for 2,379,389 individuals over the course of five years. The positive detection rate (PDR) and early diagnosis rate (EDR) of UGI tract was 2.35% and 19.77%. The PDRs for esophagus and stomach were 1.02% and 1.37%, respectively, while the EDRs were 23.18% and 17.80%, respectively. We also reported the PDRs and EDRs by site, age, sex, regional UGI cancer incidence level and hospital level.

Eastern Asia has the highest incidence and mortality rates and largest patient population for UGI cancers globally. Nonetheless, the surveillance statistics for esophageal and gastric cancers reveal different patterns in China, Japan and South Korea. The incidence rates for esophageal and stomach cancer in China are both high, ranking 11th and 15th respectively among 186 regions and countries globally. In contrast, in Japan and South Korea, gastric cancer poses a relatively greater burden, ranking 2nd and 3rd globally, whereas the burden of esophageal cancer is less significant, ranking 33rd and 96th globally.14 Both Japan and Korea implemented nation-wide screening programs for gastric cancer. In Japan, barium-meal was the recommended method for gastric cancer screening since 1960s. The revised Japanese Guidelines have approved gastric endoscopy for use in population-based screening since 2014.15 In Korea, individuals are offered biennial screening through either an UGI series or endoscopy, and the population-level benefits of this national cancer screening program have already become evident.16 Given that UGI endoscopy allows for simultaneous screening of both esophageal and gastric cancers, it is well-suited for China to implement screening programs in numerous high-risk regions for these cancers. Based on the previous evidences that have demonstrated the effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of UGI endoscopy as a screening method in China, we initiated the OpENS program since 2018 with the aim of advancing the implementation of this screening technology in local hospitals.

The process of data cleaning and quality control is indispensable for obtaining high-quality data suitable for analysis, serving as the cornerstone for achieving scientific and reasonable outcomes. In Supplementary Material S4, we provided a detailed explanation of how we conducted the quality control process and the rationale behind our decision to process it in this manner. It is noteworthy that we aggregated medical records into patient-level data by identifying and consolidating multiple endoscopic examination records for each patient based on their ID number. In this way, the PDRs and EDRs reported in our study can be compared to those obtained from observational studies or randomized controlled studies. We found that the PDRs were notably high among duplicated records belonging to the same participants, indicating that patients with positive lesions tended to make repeated visits and undergo multiple endoscopic examinations either to confirm their diagnosis or to receive subsequent treatments. The inclusion of these duplicate records would introduce bias, thereby artificially increase the PDRs in the study. Thus, it is reasonable to exclude multiple endoscopic examination records in the present study. This phenomenon suggests that multiple UGI endoscopies are often necessary to establish a definitive diagnosis or proceed with subsequent treatment. Given that the current study has the opportunity to gather and consolidate multiple clinical endoscopic data from patients, it stands a better chance of obtaining accurate diagnostic and treatment information.

In comparison to population-based organized screenings, the PDR was relatively high but the EDR was notably low in current study. A community-based mulitcenter cluster randomized clinical trial reported a detection rate of 1.4% (528 cases), and an early diagnosis rate of 82.6%.17 ESECC study reported a 69.9% EDR for esophagus.18 Korean studies showed that the detection rate of UGI neoplasms among individuals underwent health-screening program was around 0.20–0.40%,19,20 but the EDR can reach 90%.19 The significant disparity in EDRs between the two types of UGI screening strategies stems from the fundamental differences in the source populations they target. Specifically, organized screening focuses on healthy individuals who may be at risk of developing the targeted diseases, whereas opportunistic screening targets unhealthy individuals who are already in need of medical assistance. When comparing to the detection rates of other hospital-based studies, we found that the early detection rates of gastric cancer by highly qualified endoscopists in Japan is 2.78%, and the EDR can reach as high as 30%.21 This suggests that there remains potential for enhancing the performance of the OpENS program.

However, for the rapid and widespread promotion of UGI cancer screening, opportunistic endoscopic screening may still be one favorable approach in China due to the following reasons: (1) The national esophageal cancer and gastric cancer screening and early diagnosis and treatment programs designate high-risk areas as those regions where the ASIR for esophageal cancer exceeds 15/100,000 and the ASIR for gastric cancer exceeds 20/100,000. According to this criterion, only 12% and 17% regions qualified for organized screening programs in China. The risks for individuals from other regions still depend on the risk factors, including family history, unhealthy life styles, HP infection or a history of precancerous conditions, which align with the participant inclusion criteria in the current study. (2) With adequate training, opportunistic screening can become more efficient, even in secondary-level hospitals. A national survey on digestive endoscopy in 2021, which was also based on clinical endoscopic data, revealed that the EDR for the esophagus had increased from 11.98% in 2015 to 18.43% in 2020, while the EDR for the stomach had increased from 11.07% in 2015 to 17.68% in 2020 in China.22 Our current study found that the EDRs for the esophagus and stomach were 23.18% and 17.80% during 2019–2023, both exceeding the national averages in the previous survey, with a notable improvement observed in the esophageal EDR. For hospitals that can consistently maintain a high quality of work in our study, the EDRs could even rise to 25.40% and 19.45% for esophagus and stomach. These figures suggested that following the implementation of screening technology in clinical practice, a greater number of early-stage patients can be identified, thereby enhancing their chances of successful treatment and cure. (3) Despite the Japanese Guidelines recommended the use of UGI endoscopy in population-based gastric cancer screening, numerous municipalities believed the incorporation of endoscopy screening to be improbable due to a shortage of endoscopists, inadequate endoscopy processing capabilities, and budgetary limitations.15 In China, we confronted a similar dilemma. Reports indicated that the average number of digestive endoscopists in secondary-level hospitals was 3 doctors, whereas top-level hospitals had an average of 29.5 digestive endoscopists, highlighting a substantial disparity in the distribution of human resources within the country.22 According to the same report, 15 million UGI endoscopies were conducted in China in 2020.22 If these existing endoscopy examinations were integrated with standardized cancer screening protocols, great benefits could be achieved at minimal costs.

However, the PDRs for hospitals in low-risk areas that inconsistently reported data demonstrated a notable decreasing trend over the years, coinciding with the expansion of the OpENS program. This phenomenon underscores the actual challenge we confront: the expansion of the program often outpaces the necessary training, highlighting the crucial importance of adequate training in screening technology, particularly in low-risk areas. To provide adequate training of UGI screening, annually, the expert team of the program initiates training courses and conducts on-site teaching across all participating provinces. A typical training session comprises the following five parts: (1) An academic forum, where fundamental knowledge, cutting-edge advancements, interpretation of standard and recent controversies in endoscopy diagnosis and technology are shared; (2) A work summary, where explanation of annual work objectives and technical solutions, work report on the progress of the program, and exchange of practical experiences from some hospitals are unveiled; (3) An on-site instruction, where participants can observe endoscopic and pathological procedure demonstrations and hands-on instructions; (4) A work review, during which clinical supervisions in local hospital settings are conducted; and (5) A competition, during which endoscopy-pathology joint case presentation competitions are conducted.

To address the training requirements of local screening teams, a distinct training program has been established since 2022, which was also funded by the government. Annually, over 50,000 person-times participated in both online and offline training courses and 1000–2000 individuals participated in the offline training courses and on-site teaching. We can discern the preliminary effects of these training programs: We found that among hospitals that have consistently reported high-quality data throughout the entire 5-year study period, which we defined as well-performing hospitals, secondary-level hospitals are able to rival the performance of tertiary-level hospitals in esophagus screening, yet their diagnostic capability for stomach conditions still requires improvement. This result suggests that significantly greater efforts are required to improve the diagnostic capability for stomach conditions.

Due to its hollow structure with numerous folds, the stomach is prone to missed diagnoses, potentially reaching a rate as high as 9%.23 To enhance the detection rate of precancerous lesions, the guideline of OpENS program suggest clinical doctors to consider utilizing other technologies for assistance, including chromoendoscopy, image-enhanced endoscopy (IEE)24 or magnifying endoscopy techniques.25 The Guideline recommends that iodine staining be mandatory in principle for high-risk individuals of esophageal cancer, and that indigo carmine staining be used if suspicious lesions are identified in stomach. However, we found that the proportion of endoscopy examinations conducted without the application of any staining methods was significantly higher in secondary-level hospitals compared to tertiary-level hospitals, both for esophagus and stomach screenings. This phenomenon suggests that there is still room for improvement in the standardization of UGI endoscopy screening procedures in secondary-level hospitals.

To address this issue, the OpENS program will persist in intensifying training efforts to ensure that hospitals, particularly secondary-level hospitals, adhere to the guideline. This will be accomplished by strengthening daily inspections and annual evaluations of work quality, with staining rates serving as a key quality control indicator. Additionally, during training, we will prioritize evaluating trainees' adherence to guidelines and conduct post-training assessments by regularly sampling clinical cases for review. However, we recognize that disparities in physician capacity exist between secondary and tertiary-level hospitals. This variability among endoscopists is not confined to hospital tiers but extends to individual physician capabilities. Consequently, current training formats may not adequately meet the needs of all professionals. Tailored training plans, designed based on each professional's educational background, workplace, area of specialization, years of experience and primary challenges, coupled with targeted assessments, may more effectively enhance technical proficiency. Since 2019, the program has developed a training APP. Trainees can log into the APP to independently study multimedia educational materials across various disciplines. Currently, this APP serves solely as a supplementary teaching tool, but in the future, it may be leveraged to achieve personalized training goals. Furthermore, in recent years, artificial intelligence-based clinical decision support systems have also emerged as a practical solution to tackle personnel scarcity and narrow the capability disparity among hospitals of various levels, by enabling real-time automated detection and classification during endoscopy procedures.26,27 These measures will collectively promote the homogenization of medical care and represent a significant stride towards achieving universal health coverage in China.28

The unique feature of the current study lies in the implementation of an opportunistic UGI screening program in China, coupled with the collection of a substantial volume of real-world clinical records. Through rigorous data cleaning and quality control procedures, we were able to achieve high-quality patient-level data. Using these data, we can assess the real effectiveness of endoscopy screening and examine the challenges encountered during the promotion of this screening techniques. Another advantage of the study lies in our ability to access cancer registry data, thereby enabling us to gain insights into the cancer incidence and mortality rates across the districts/counties covered by the program. This advantage facilitated our observation of the impacts of UGI screening across diverse regions, and indeed, we found that PDRs and EDRs were elevated in high-risk areas under various classification thresholds. It can further enable us to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of opportunistic screening in various regions in the future, hence providing scientific evidences that can inform policymaking tailored to local features. There are also several limitations. First, despite the implementation of regular quality control measures and spot checks during the program, and rigorous data quality control prio to statistical analysis, errors in the raw data were inevitable, potentially compromising the accuracy of our findings. However, given that the results were robust across subgroup and subregional analyses, we believe that the impact of these errors was limited. Second, while submucous carcinoma with an infiltration to the middle third of the of the submucosa is now considered a late-stage cancer,29 it is still classified as an early-stage diagnosis within the current program. However, given the relatively small number of individuals in this category (148), the impact of this misclassification is minimal. Third, the study period of the OpENS Program overlapped with the COVID pandemic, which significantly impacted patient enrollment and program training. This impact was most pronounced in 2021, when many hospitals faced challenges in recruiting sufficient patients for UGI screenings and were unable to submit high-quality data that year, potentially introducing bias into the PDRs and EDRs. To mitigate the effects of pandemic-related disruptions on the results, we categorized the hospitals into two groups: (1) consistently reporting hospitals, where work quality remained largely unaffected by the pandemic; and (2) inconsistently reporting hospitals, which included hospitals where work quality may have been influenced by the pandemic or other adverse factors. We also reported PDRs and EDRs by year in the Supplementary Materials to illustrate the potential influence of the temporal factors.

In conclusion, our findings reveal that the OpENS program exhibited high positive detection rates and early detection rates, suggesting that the screening process and its implementation were highly effective. Considering the existing capacities of hospitals at various levels in China, implementing training-guided opportunistic UGI endoscopy screening is feasible to address the demand for UGI cancer screening.

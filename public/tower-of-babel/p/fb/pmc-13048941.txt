Machine learning-based prediction of dynamic heterosis for plant height with pathway biomarkers in rice
Authors: Zhiwu Dan, Yunping Chen, Wenchao Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractKey messageThe metabolomic landscape of dynamic heterosis for plant height was displayed in rice. Heterosis-associated pathways across most developmental stages were developed into robust pathway biomarkers.AbstractThe development of robust biomarkers enables accurate prediction of complex phenotypes, which contribute to the advancement of precision breeding. However, the dynamic nature of biomarkers is often underestimated, as their quantitative changes during development are directly connected to phenotypic transformations, influencing crop agronomic traits. Here, we performed comparative metabolomic analyses to investigate dynamic heterosis for plant height in rice, which is an agronomic trait varying throughout development and is a key determinant of yield heterosis. We found that the levels of pyruvaldehyde were predictive of height heterosis specifically at the seedling stage, while 4-hydroxycinnamic acid positively correlated with height heterosis across four developmental stages. We identified metabolic pathways associated with height heterosis and found that metabolomic changes during the elongation stage had a greater impact than those in other stages. Finally, 11 heterosis-associated pathways were developed into pathway biomarkers using random forest analysis, enabling the prediction of height heterosis in an independent population under different growth conditions. We elucidate the metabolomic landscape of dynamic height heterosis in rice through the identification of heterosis-associated analytes and pathways across stages. Our findings provide a strategy to develop robust biomarkers for heterosis of important agronomic traits by integrating metabolic pathways involved in heterosis across most developmental stages, contributing to the establishment of precision pairing in hybrid crop breeding.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00122-026-05218-5.

Introduction

Complex phenotypes are primarily determined by genotypes, environmental factors, and their interactions (Hunter 2005; Crossa et al. 2017; Scheres and van der Putten 2017). The influence of genetic and environmental factors on phenotypic changes has been extensively studied in both crop agronomic traits and human diseases (Fave et al. 2018, Li et al. 2018, de Los Campos et al. 2020). To accurately characterize the states of complex phenotypes, various types of biomarkers have been developed, and predictive models have been constructed to facilitate accurate predictions across populations and environments (Riedelsheimer et al. 2012; Sarkizova et al. 2020; Tong et al. 2020; Zhao et al. 2021). However, the dynamic nature of the processes shaping complex phenotypes is often underestimated (Lesterhuis et al. 2017; Miao et al. 2020). Quantitative changes in specific molecules during development have been shown to be directly linked to phenotypic transformations, including plant agronomic traits (Obermeyer et al. 2013; Ko et al. 2016; Varoquaux et al. 2019; Zhou et al. 2019a) and risks for human diseases such as diabetes or cancers (Taylor et al. 2009; Price et al. 2017). Consequently, static biomarkers—identified from tissues at a single developmental stage—often exhibit limited predictive power for new-stage phenotypes in genetically distant individuals, partly due to molecular heterogeneity across developmental stages and genotypes (Menche et al. 2017; Price et al. 2017). Furthermore, the question of at which stages quantitative changes in biomarkers contribute more significantly to the phenotypic outcome than at others remains largely unexplored. Thus, investigating the molecular mechanisms of complex phenotypes in a time-series manner, followed by the exploration and validation of dynamic biomarkers, offers a viable approach for advancing precision breeding.

Plant height is a key determinant of crop grain yield (Wang et al. 2023). Although plant height is not a direct component trait of grain yield, it significantly contributes to yield through its associations with grain number and flowering time. In rice, the close relationship between plant height and yield has been attributed to polygenic genes, such as Ghd7 and DTH (Xue et al. 2008; Gao et al. 2014). In a partial correlation analysis comparing the contributions of four yield components (grain number per panicle, tiller number per plant, thousand grain weight, and seed setting rate) and plant height to yield heterosis in rice, heterosis for plant height (partial correlation coefficient r = 0.22) exhibited a higher correlation coefficient than that of grain weight (partial correlation coefficient r = 0.16) (Dan et al. 2021). Notably, Ghd7 plays a central role in yield heterosis of the elite hybrid rice Shanyou63 (Wang et al. 2022). However, plant height is a dynamic trait that evolves from seedling to maturity, yet most studies primarily focus on the maturation stage. Given the substantial increase in crop grain yield achieved through heterosis, investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying height heterosis has become essential.

In this study, we constructed a hybrid population using diallel cross design in rice and measured plant height of both parents and their F1 hybrids across four developmental stages: seedling, elongation, flowering, and maturation. Heterosis for plant height across these stages, referred to dynamic heterosis in our analyses, was calculated for comparison and regression. We observed a significant shift in height heterosis between the elongation and flowering stages. Subsequently, we identified heterosis-associated analytes at each stage and examined their relationships with height heterosis. We also detected metabolic pathways involved in dynamic height heterosis and discovered that the overlapping pathways—primarily in amino acid and carbohydrate metabolism—connected different developmental stages. Notably, we observed pronounced metabolomic changes at the elongation stage. Using random forest, we developed pathway biomarkers and validated them with test crosses. Our findings provide insights into the metabolomic mechanisms of dynamic height heterosis and demonstrate the potential of pathway biomarkers in predicting complex phenotypes across developmental stages.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The rice accessions selected for hybridization were previously described (Dan et al. 2020). In brief, two hybrid populations were utilized to develop biomarkers for plant height heterosis. The first population adopted a complete diallel cross design involving 18 indica and japonica accessions as parents. The second population consisted of 107 test crosses with recombinant inbred lines crossed with a Honglian-type cytoplasmic male sterile line, Yuetai A. Plant height, defined as the distance from the plant base to the highest part of the leaf or panicle, of the diallel cross population was measured with tape measures at four growth stages from seedling to maturation: the seedling stage (28 days after transplantation), elongation stage (47 days after transplantation), flowering stage (time of flowering), and maturation stage (time of maturation). Plant height data at the maturation stage and grain yield per plant of the diallel cross population were reported in a previous study (Dan et al. 2021). For comparative purpose, analysis results related to better-parent heterosis for plant height at the maturation stage were retrieved from an earlier research (Dan et al. 2021). Plant height of the test-cross population was measured only at the maturation stage. The two hybrid populations were grown under different planting densities at the same location (Ezhou, Hubei Province, 30°22′19.82″N, 114°44′59.17″E) in 2012 (16.5 × 26.4 cm spacing) and 2015 (16.5 × 16.5 cm spacing). Three biological replicates were designed for each genotype, with five plants from the middle region of each row selected for height measurement.

Metabolite profiling

Untargeted metabolite profiles from 15-day-old seedlings of parental lines were obtained using the previously described methods (Dan et al. 2020). In brief, seedling samples were ground into a homogeneous powder using liquid nitrogen, and 80 mg of powder from each sample was then used for metabolite extraction to ensure comparability. A 1290 Infinity liquid chromatography system (Agilent Technologies), an Agilent quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (Agilent 6550 iFunnel QTOF), and a Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX) were employed to acquire the raw metabolomic profiles in negative ionization mode. The stability of the equipment and the accuracy of the metabolite profiles were assessed using intermittently injected quality control samples. A total of 3,746 analytes were detected, and 114 metabolites were identified through matching with in-house spectral libraries of standards by Shanghai Applied Protein Technology Co., Ltd. To prepare the metabolomic data for statistical analyses, sample normalization (normalization by sum), data transformation (none), and data scaling (auto scaling) were performed using MetaboAnalyst 4.0 (Chong et al. 2018). The hybrid metabolome used for analyses was calculated by averaging metabolite levels of corresponding parents according to previously reported methods (Dan et al. 2019, 2020).

Identification of heterosis-associated analytes

For the identification of heterosis-associated analytes, we employed a partial least squares (PLS)-based strategy (Wold 1975; Dan et al. 2021). As stated in previous studies (Wold 1975; Wold et al. 2001), PLS extracts latent factors in building multivariate models and can handle complicated relationship between predictors and responses. The number of latent factors in predictive models was determined based on the highest achieved R-square values, which was found to be four in our analyses using SPSS 20.0. The performance of the predictive models was assessed using tenfold cross-validation and permutation tests conducted with MetaboAnalyst 4.0 (Chong et al. 2018). Subsequently, average values of variable importance in the projection for all latent factors were calculated to evaluate contributions of metabolic analytes to plant height heterosis. Redundant analytes in the predictive models were removed by iteratively reducing the number of predictive analytes based on the average values, analyzing subsets of 3,746 (total), 2,000, 1,500, 1,000, 500, 300, 200, 100, and 50 analytes. The final number of predictive analytes was determined before a decrease in R2 values and the number for plant height heterosis at the seedling, elongation, flowering, and maturation stages was 300, 300, 100, and 100, respectively. The robustness of the predictive models was tested by adding sets of 25, 50, and 100 randomly selected analytes to the final set of 100 analytes for plant heterosis at the maturation stage. The adjusted R2 values derived from PLS analysis were used to compare the resulting models.

Identification of heterosis-associated metabolic pathways

The Metabolite identification and Dysregulated Network Analysis web server (MetDNA version 1.2.1) (Shen et al. 2019), which annotates metabolites based on the rule that neighboring metabolites tend to share similar MS2 spectra, was used to perform pathway enrichment analysis for height heterosis. Based on thresholds used in previous studies (Dan et al. 2019, 2020, 2021), hybrids were categorized into high-better-parent heterosis (control group) and low-better-parent heterosis (case group) groups according to quantiles (≥ 75th percentile and ≤ 25th percentile) of the entire hybrid population. The calculated hybrid metabolome was utilized for dysregulated network analysis. Analysis parameters were set according to the metabolite profiling experiments. Since the server lacked a rice-specific database, Arabidopsis thaliana (thale cress) was selected as the reference species. Student’s t test was employed for differential analysis between the control and case groups in univariate statistics, with a cutoff P value of 0.05 (using adjustment). Enriched metabolic pathways identified via hypergeometric test and quantitative information on pathways, which were included in the analysis report, were combined to identify significantly enriched metabolic pathways for height heterosis at each stage. Metabolic pathways that were significantly enriched (P values less than 0.05) and exhibited significant differences in metabolite levels between the high- and low-better-parent heterosis hybrids were treated as heterosis-associated pathways.

Biomarker analysis

Both univariate and multivariate receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses were conducted using the “Biomarker Analysis” module in MetaboAnalyst 4.0 (Chong et al. 2018). To facilitate biomarker identification for height heterosis, ratios between levels of two metabolic pathways were calculated. The top 100 features (pathway levels or pathway ratios) after logarithm transformation were included in biomarker analysis. No other normalization or scaling was performed. Based on the Monte-Carlo cross-validation design of ROC, two-thirds of the hybrids were used to evaluate feature importance. Different number of the top features (maximum 100) were then used to build classification models that were validated on the remaining one-third of hybrids. Confidence interval was calculated for each model. Random forest (Breiman 2001) was selected as classification and feature ranking method for multivariate ROC curve analysis.

Statistical analyses

Better-parent heterosis for plant height was calculated using the equation: BPH = (F1-PH)/PH, where F1 represents the plant height of the hybrid and PH denotes the height of the taller parent. The hybrid metabolome was calculated as follows: MH = (MF + MM)/2, where MH, MF, and MM indicate the metabolite levels of the F1 hybrid, female parent, and male parent, respectively. Pearson correlation analysis of height heterosis across the four developmental stages was performed using the “Statistical Analysis” module in MetaboAnalyst 4.0 (Chong et al. 2018). Comparisons of height heterosis and metabolite levels of significantly enriched pathways across stages were analyzed using one-way ANOVA (with least significant difference for post hoc multiple comparisons, P < 0.05) via SPSS (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 20.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp.). Analyses comparing plant height heterosis between the diallel and test crosses, as well as metabolite levels between high- and low-BPH hybrids, were conducted using independent samples t test in SPSS (two-tailed). Paired samples t test was performed to compare differences between reciprocal hybrids. Pearson correlation analysis between height heterosis and the metabolite levels of metabolic pathways was also performed using SPSS.

Results

Dynamic heterosis for plant height of diallel crosses in rice

To investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying dynamic heterosis in rice, we selected 18 representative inbred lines, which consist of both indica and japonica varieties with varying plant height (Dan et al. 2021, 2026), as parental lines. Using a complete diallel cross design, we generated hybrid seeds for a hybrid population containing 306 F1 hybrids (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, we measured plant height of the inbred lines and F1 hybrids at four developmental stages: seedling stage (SS; 28 days after transplantation), elongation stage (ES; 47 days after transplantation), flowering stage (FS), and maturation stage (MS) (Fig. 1B and Supplemental Table 1). Due to low germination rates of several F1 hybrids, height data were successfully collected for a total of 287 F1 hybrids across stages. Meanwhile, we obtained metabolite profiles from seedling samples (15 days after sowing) of the 18 inbred lines to explain variance in dynamic heterosis for plant height.

Next, we performed correlation analysis between height heterosis across stages. Result showed that, despite the existence of stage-specific variation, height heterosis was positively correlated between every pair of stages (Fig. 1E). As the interval between stages increased, the correlation coefficients decreased, suggesting changes in the genetic underlying of height heterosis across stages. Furthermore, we analyzed the associations between plant height and grain yield (Fig. 1F). Using phenotypic data from F1 hybrids, the correlation coefficients between plant height and grain yield were comparable across stages. Unexpectedly, no significant correlation was detected between height heterosis at the earlier stages and grain yield heterosis, but significant correlations were observed between height heterosis at later stages and grain yield heterosis. Thus, heterosis for plant height varied substantially across stages and exhibited distinct associations with yield heterosis between the earlier and later developmental stages.

Metabolic analytes associated with dynamic height heterosis

To explore the metabolomic underpinnings of dynamic height heterosis, we began with the untargeted metabolite profiles of parental inbred lines. Since additive effect, namely at mid-parental levels, is a predominant inheritance pattern in F1 hybrids (Zhou et al. 2019a; Liu et al. 2021; Dan et al. 2026). We averaged metabolite levels of each pair of inbred lines to represent metabolome of their F1 hybrids, as previously described (Dan et al. 2019, 2020). Subsequently, we employed a partial least squares-based strategy to associate the calculated hybrid metabolome with corresponding dynamic height heterosis (Wold 1975; Dan et al. 2021). Predictive models for height heterosis at each stage were built, and these models displayed best performance with four latent factors (Fig. 2A and Supplemental Fig. 3). The top R values of these predictive models ranged from 0.68 to 0.77, indicating high accuracy of the additive effect for representing metabolite levels of F1 hybrids. Meanwhile, compared to the earlier stages, the R values were lower for predicting height heterosis at the later ones, suggesting an increasing role of environmental effects on height heterosis over time.

We performed correlation analysis on these heterosis-associated analytes to investigate their correlation patterns. Both positive and negative correlations were found, with most analytes positively correlating with others across the four developmental stages (Fig. 2C-F). Notably, an accurately annotated metabolite, pyruvaldehyde (peak tag: M71T32_NEG; Fig. 2G), which is involved in pyruvate metabolism and propanoate metabolism, showed a significant negative correlation with height heterosis specifically at the seedling stage (Fig. 2H). We also chemically annotated another metabolite, 4-hydroxycinnamic acid, which has been proved to be linked to plant height (Li et al. 2015; Dan et al. 2021). In contrast, it was consistently identified as a heterosis-associated analyte across all four stages (Fig. 2C-F). Levels of 4-hydroxycinnamic acid showed significant positive correlations with plant height heterosis throughout development, with lower correlation coefficients observed at later stages compared to earlier ones (Fig. 2I).

Metabolic pathways underlying dynamic height heterosis

Due to limited annotation information of the identified heterosis-associated analytes, it was challenging to directly classify them into metabolic pathways. We thus adopted dysregulated network analysis (Shen et al. 2019), which involves pathway enrichment analysis using a hypergeometric test on differential metabolic peaks, to identify heterosis-associated metabolic pathways. By treating the low- and high-BPH hybrids as control and case groups, respectively, we obtained significantly enriched pathways (heterosis-associated pathways) for plant height at each stage (Supplemental Table 2). Consistent with the trend in number of heterosis-associated analytes, more pathways were implicated in height heterosis at early stages (Fig. 3A-D). Using the average metabolite levels within each pathway (Shen et al. 2019), we performed correlation analyses and observed similar correlation patterns for these pathways to those of heterosis-associated analytes.

Among these pathways, pyruvate metabolism and propanoate metabolism were associated with height heterosis, but only pyruvate metabolism was involved in heterosis at the later stages. Pyruvate metabolism exhibited a positive correlation with height across all four stages, whereas propanoate metabolism showed a positive correlation at the seedling stage but a negative correlation at elongation. These findings suggested that the relationships between metabolic pathways and heterosis might differ from those of individual analytes, underscoring the importance of exploring height heterosis at the pathway level.

By interconnecting overlapping pathway across stages, we obtained the metabolomic landscape of dynamic height heterosis (Fig. 3E). A total of 29 metabolic pathways were detected, with three, five, and six metabolic pathways involved in height heterosis at two, three, and all four stages, respectively. The majority of these heterosis-associated pathways belonged to amino acid (48.28%) and carbohydrate (31.03%) metabolism (Fig. 3F). Notably, the pathways in carbohydrate metabolism (60–75%) consistently exhibited higher metabolite levels in high-BPH hybrids, while those in amino acid metabolism (50–90%) displayed lower levels (Fig. 3G). This pattern underscored the dominant roles of carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism in height heterosis, which complements the changing trends observed for metabolic pathways related to heterosis of yield and its components (Dan et al. 2020, 2021). Furthermore, the number of stage-specific pathways was higher for the earlier stages (8 and 6 pathways) than for the later ones (1 and 0 pathway; Fig. 3H), which was consistent with changing patterns of the identified heterosis-associated analytes.

Metabolomic changes at the elongation stage play critical roles in height heterosis

According to the metabolomic landscape of dynamic height heterosis, six pathways were significantly enriched across all four stages. Three of these pathways (citrate cycle, pentose and glucuronate interconversions, and pyruvate metabolism) were classified under carbohydrate metabolism, while two pathways were from amino acid metabolism (phenylalanine metabolism and alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism). The remaining pathway was monobactam biosynthesis. To assess the contributions of these heterosis-associated pathways to height heterosis, we compared their metabolite levels across stages (Fig. 4A-F). Significant differences in metabolite levels were observed in either the high- or low-BPH hybrids across the four stages, suggesting tight associations between metabolomic variation and dynamic heterosis.

In high-BPH hybrids, the metabolite level of citrate cycle showed an initial increase at elongation followed by a decline in later stages (Fig. 4A), whereas the opposite trend occurred in low-BPH hybrids. Different from the citrate cycle, the other two pathways from carbohydrate metabolism showed similar changing patterns in both the high- and low-BPH hybrids (Fig. 4B-C). Compared to the pathways from carbohydrate metabolism, the two pathways from amino acid metabolism and monobactam biosynthesis displayed fewer significant changes in metabolite levels in low-BPH hybrids (Fig. 4D-F). Notably, except for monobactam biosynthesis, metabolite levels of the other five pathways frequently shifted significantly at the elongation stage in both high- and low-BPH hybrids, underscoring the critical role of elongation stage in shaping height heterosis.

Pathway biomarkers for predicting dynamic height heterosis

To explore biomarkers for the dynamic height heterosis, we first performed univariate and multivariate receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses on the 18 heterosis-associated pathways for plant height at the seedling stage (Supplemental Fig. 5). A single predictor achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.885, increasing to 0.933 with 100 features. Analysis of the six core heterosis-associated pathways across stages revealed no substantial increase in AUC values or predictive accuracies (uni-ROC: AUC = 0.887, multi-ROC: AUC = 0.931, predictive accuracy = 0.882 with 21 features; Supplemental Fig. 5).

We then evaluated the 11 heterosis-associated pathways that were enriched across three or four stages, which were primarily pathways in amino acid and carbohydrate metabolism, as predictive features (Fig. 5A). A single predictor yielded an AUC of 0.883 (Fig. 5B), while the optimal predictive model containing only ten features achieved an AUC of 0.929 and predictive accuracy of 0.873 (Fig. 5C-D). Unexpectedly, despite being significantly enriched across all four stages, the citrate cycle was not among the top-ranked predictors (Fig. 5E). Further biomarker analysis demonstrated high predictive accuracies for height heterosis at the three later stages (Supplemental Fig. 6). These results showed that the development of biomarkers from the heterosis-associated pathways across most developmental stages could be an effective strategy, taking into account predictive accuracy, feature number, and feature ranking.

Validation of pathway biomarkers with test crosses

To validate predictive capability of the identified pathway biomarkers, we utilized height heterosis of 107 test crosses. A Honglian-type cytoplasmic male sterile line, Yuetai A, and 107 recombinant inbred lines (F5) were the female and male parents of the test-cross population, respectively. This population exhibited distant genetic relationship (based on metabolomic data) and was cultivated under different field conditions to the diallel population (Dan et al. 2020). Height heterosis at the maturation ranged from -0.048 to 0.30 for test-cross F1 hybrids, and from -0.31 to 0.41 for diallel cross hybrids, indicating the important role of parental genetic diversity on the degree of heterosis. Meanwhile, no significant difference in height heterosis was detected between the test and diallel populations (Fig. 6A).

According to the grouping strategy of diallel cross population, we categorized the test crosses into low- and high-BPH groups based on the 25th and 75th percentiles of height heterosis. Using metabolite profiles from parental line seedlings, we averaged the parental levels and identified four key pathways through dysregulated network analysis (Supplemental Table 3), three of which overlapped with the 11 identified heterosis-associated pathways. Using metabolite levels of nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism as a single predictor yielded an AUC of 0.868 (Fig. 6B), indicating high accuracy of the pathway biomarkers. When 2–6 variables were used for analysis, the AUC values varied from 0.8 to 0.834 (Supplemental Fig. 7).

Height heterosis was negatively correlated with metabolite levels of nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, as well as glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism (Fig. 6C). However, no correlation was found between height heterosis and phenylalanine metabolism. The metabolite levels of nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism were significantly lower in high-BPH hybrids compared to low-BPH ones (Fig. 6D). The levels of glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism were also lower in the high-BPH hybrids (Fig. 6E), which contrasted with the result from diallel crosses. Meanwhile, phenylalanine metabolism showed no significant changes between groups (Fig. 6F), showing discrepancies in metabolite levels of pathway biomarkers across hybrid populations. Furthermore, higher AUC values were observed when grouping the low- and high-BPH hybrids at a more stringent level, while lower values were obtained when dividing the test crosses into approximately equal halves (Supplemental Figs. 8–9). These results indicated that the 11 identified pathway biomarkers, which were significantly enriched metabolic pathways across most developmental stages in the diallel crosses, effectively predicted height heterosis in the test crosses.

Discussion

In this study, we developed robust biomarkers for dynamic height heterosis in rice, focusing on metabolic pathways significantly enriched across multiple developmental stages. We also validated these biomarkers to a test-cross population, demonstrating their potential application for improving the breeding efficiency of hybrid crops.

Given the challenges of obtaining metabolite profiles directly from F1 hybrids in the large diallel cross population, we leveraged metabolomic data from parental lines for representing hybrid metabolome. Using transcriptomic and metabolomic data collected from parental inbred lines and their F1 hybrids, we have observed that F1 hybrids position near the middle regions of corresponding parents (Dan et al. 2026). Importantly, the predictability for heterosis of agronomic traits is comparable between additive effect and actual F1 hybrid profiles in both rice and maize. Furthermore, transcriptomic/metabolomic additive effects have predicted grain weight (Dan et al. 2019), yield heterosis (Dan et al. 2020), and heterosis of vegetative/reproductive traits in rice (Dan et al. 2021, 2025). Thus, the metabolite levels of F1 hybrids are approximated by averaging the values of each parental pair in this study.

Among the 11 identified heterosis-associated pathways, pathways related to carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism comprised the majority and exhibited approximately opposite trends in metabolite levels. Given the tight association between plant height and grain yield in rice (Xue et al. 2008; Chen et al. 2020; Dan et al. 2021), variation in these two types of pathways may determine the optimal balance between vegetative and reproductive traits in the formation of heterosis in hybrid rice (Birchler 2019; Li et al. 2020; Dan et al. 2021). In alignment with the phenotypic transformations observed in fields, the elongation stage shows more metabolomic shifts than those at the other stages. Recognizing this critical stage may facilitate the determination of optimal time-points for precise fertilization. External application of pooled metabolites from citrate cycle to F1 hybrids at the elongation stage may enhance height heterosis and subsequently yield heterosis. Our design of time-series analysis also provides a strategy for investigating abiotic tolerances (e.g., drought, flood, heat, cold, salt) across developmental stages in hybrid crops. Furthermore, our findings may inspire the identification of biomarkers for predicting biomass or height in breeding programs for perennial energy crops and trees (Canas et al. 2015; Maddison et al. 2017).

The strategy of incorporating pathways that are significantly enriched across most developmental stages—rather than exclusively those enriched at all stages—could enhance robustness of pathway biomarkers for predicting heterosis across diverse hybrid populations, environments, and developmental stages. While the variation patterns of some pathways were consistent across hybrid populations, we observed discrepancies in metabolite levels of certain pathways. The metabolomic heterogeneity between populations (Dan et al. 2021), similar to transcriptional variability among phenotypically similar patients (Menche et al. 2017; Zhou et al. 2019b), may underlie these inconsistencies. The development of network biomarkers incorporating pathway interactions could yield more consistent findings. Moreover, our pathway analysis is inherently limited by the coverage of KEGG database; unannotated pathways may also influence height heterosis. Expanding the catalog of heterosis-associated pathways will refine the metabolomic landscape and improve predictive accuracy, which largely depends on the enrichment and development of metabolomic databases. Furthermore, to enhance predictive accuracies of pathway biomarkers, comprehensive metabolomics data that incorporate metabolite profiles obtained via both positive and negative ionization modes on multiple detection platforms, as well as through both GC and LC methods, are essential in the near future. The integration of multiple layers of omics information (Weckwerth et al. 2020; Xu et al. 2021), including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics data, will also optimize pathway biomarkers for predicting dynamic phenotypes.

Conclusion

In this study, we performed comparative metabolomic analysis to investigate dynamic height heterosis in rice. We identified heterosis-associated analytes and pathways for plant height across four developmental stages and developed robust pathway biomarkers using a machine learning approach, which contribute to the establishment of precision breeding in hybrid crops.

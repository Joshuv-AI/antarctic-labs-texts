The epidemiology and disease economic burden of osteoarticular tuberculosis in hospitalized children in China: a nationwide cross-sectional retrospective study
Authors: Yuanyuan Liu, Xinyu Wang, Xiao Peng, Lu Lu, Hui Yang, Guoshuang Feng, Yongli Guo, Jingyi Fan, Jie Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoarticular tuberculosis (OATB) in children may lead to bone and joint deformities, which seriously affect the growth and development of children. However, the epidemiological data on pediatric OATB are relatively scarce. The goal of this study is to understand the epidemiological characteristics and economic burden of OATB in hospitalized children in China.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective, multicenter cross-sectional study covering hospitalized children with OATB in 24 hospitals in six regions of China from 2016 to 2021. Sociodemographic and geographic variables, admission and discharge information were extracted from face sheets of discharge medical records to describe the epidemiological characteristics. Length of hospital stay (LOS) and hospitalization expenses were used to assess economic burden.ResultsPediatric OATB inpatients accounted for 5.25% of pediatric TB hospitalizations (322/6138). Among different regions, Southwest China had the highest prevalence of OATB among TB patients (9.72%). The prevalence of OATB among ethnic minority children was higher than that among Han children (7.12% vs. 4.79%). Among all the children with OATB, 75.47% (243/322) were solo OATB, while OATB combined with pulmonary tuberculosis accounted for only 14.91% (48/322). In terms of anatomical distribution, spinal tuberculosis was the most common, accounting for 38.83% (125/322). In terms of economic burden, the median length of stay (LOS) for OATB patients was 12 days (IQR 7–17), with a median total hospitalization expense of $1319.61 (IQR 700.53-2788.25). Notably, Northwest China recorded the longest median LOS(14 days). Additionally, ethnic minority children had a longer median LOS than Han children (14 days vs. 11 days).ConclusionsThis study contained the largest number of pediatric inpatients with OATB, which is the first multi-center study of pediatric OATB in China. The burden of hospitalized children with OATB in the western region of China and ethnic minorities is serious. It is recommended to take region - targeted prevention and treatment measures for children with OATB to reduce the risk of disease transmission and protect children’s health.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11571-5.Keywords: Osteoarticular tuberculosis, Children, China, Epidemiology, Hospitalization

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) stands as the second leading cause of death globally from a single infectious agent, following the novel coronavirus (COVID-19) [1]. Despite significant progress in combating TB, it remains a formidable global public health challenge. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 10.8 million new TB cases were diagnosed in 2023, with 1.3 million affecting children, constituting 12% of the global TB burden [2]. It is worthy to note that children with TB often experience a higher proportion of extrapulmonary tuberculosis (EPTB) compared to adults, leading to challenging diagnosis, poorer prognosis, and increased economic burden [3–5]. This underscores childhood EPTB as a critical yet often overlooked public health issue.

Osteoarticular tuberculosis (OATB) within EPTB often manifests insidiously, lacking specific symptoms and signs, which frequently leads to delayed diagnosis [6–8]. The invasion of bones and joints can result in deformities, necessitating surgical correction and other interventions, which profoundly impacts children’s growth and development [9–12]. Unfortunately, studies on childhood OATB are scarce, with fragmented data reported by individual centers [11, 13–15]. As a result, clear epidemiological insights and the burden of disease regarding OATB in children remain elusive. In China, despite its high TB burden, existing research on OATB primarily focuses on adult populations [16–18], with pediatric data largely derived from small-scale, single-center analyses or extrapolated from adult studies [15, 19–21]. Critically, multi-center studies specifically targeting pediatric OATB are particularly scarce due to systemic challenges, including limited collaborative frameworks for child-focused TB research [15]. Against this backdrop, our multi-center study addresses key evidence gaps through establishing an epidemiological dataset of childhood OATB across diverse Chinese regions. This initiative is designed to provide China-specific data on pediatric OATB characteristics and burden, supporting national TB control strategy development for children, while also contributing high-burden population data to global OATB databases to facilitate cross-country comparisons of disease patterns.

As a unified multi-ethnic country, China has the largest population of the Han ethnic group, along with 55 other ethnic minorities. Influenced by factors like living environment, diet and genetics, the disease characteristics may vary among different ethnic groups [22–27]. Meanwhile, the disparities in medical facilities, living standards and living habits between urban and rural areas also result in diverse changes in the disease characteristics of urban and rural residents [28–32]. Therefore, when evaluating the disease characteristics of OATB in Chinese pediatric patients, it is essential to consider the potential impact of ethnic differences, urban-rural disparities, and associated socioeconomic data on disease manifestation and outcomes.

To comprehend the epidemiological landscape of OATB among Chinese children, we conducted a retrospective analysis of pediatric OATB utilizing data from 24 tertiary children’s hospitals across 20 provinces in China, covering the period from January 2016 to December 2021. The primary objective of this study was to outline the clinical epidemiological characteristics and the disease burden associated with OATB in hospitalized children in China. Additionally, our study could potentially support the formulation of evidence-based prevention policies and strategies for managing OATB in Chinese children and enrich the global understanding of pediatric OATB by supplementing the existing data on OATB in hospitalized children.

Methods

Ethics statement

This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University (Approval Number: 2020-k-10) and was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. As the study solely entailed a retrospective pooled data analysis of medical records, patient informed consent was not necessary. Our data has been fully deidentified and anonymized to ensure that privacy is protected.

Data source

The Futang Research Center of Pediatric Development (FRCPD), as the pioneering non-profit social service organization under the Ministry of Civil Affairs of China, is dedicated to advancing pediatric development research. Since December 2015, FRCPD has diligently collected data through the Futang Updating Medical Records (FUTURE) database, facilitating seamless data sharing among member hospitals [33]. The FUTURE database integrates the face sheet of discharge medical records (FSMRs) from 29 tertiary children’s hospitals across 20 provinces in China. Over the period from January 2016 to December 2021, a total of 7,647,598 inpatients FSMR data were amassed. Our dataset on pediatric OATB was extracted from this comprehensive database, comprising the FSMR data of hospitalized children with OATB from 24 tertiary children’s hospitals across 20 provinces of China during the same timeframe. All eligible OATB cases within the study period were included without prior sample size calculation. For patients with multiple hospitalizations during the study period, only the first admission record was included to avoid data duplication. For detailed information about FRCPD, please visit http://www.futang.org/about/fu-tang-jie-shao.htm.

Variables and outcomes

The variables obtained from the FSMR encompassed a wide range of data, including sociodemographic and geographic variables, admission and discharge information, length of hospital stay (LOS) and hospitalization expenses. Importantly, all variables included in this analysis exhibited complete data (0% missingness), ensured by their mandatory nature within the FSMR system and rigorous data completeness checks during collection. Sociodemographic variables comprised sex, age (categorized into five groups: <1, 1–3, 4–6, 7–12, 13–18), ethnicity, and residence. The demographic information is detailed in Table 1. The 24 hospitals were classified into seven geographical regions: East China, North China, Central China, Northwest China, Southwest China, South China, and Northeast China.

The economic burden of pediatric OATB hospitalization presented in this study was estimated from the healthcare provider perspective (specifically, the hospital perspective). We focused exclusively on direct medical costs incurred during the hospital stay. These costs were derived from the total hospitalization expenses recorded in the FSMRs, encompassing expenditures for diagnostic tests, medications, hospitalization care, and other services.

Statistical analysis

Data completeness was rigorously assessed prior to analysis. Records with missing values in any critical variables required for primary analyses were excluded from the study cohort, as stated in the exclusion criteria. No imputation methods were applied as missing data were limited to non-critical variables not used in core analyses. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (N) and percentages (%), while non-normally distributed continuous variables were described using median and interquartile range (IQR). Group comparisons for percentage differences were assessed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. For ordinal categorical variables and non-normally distributed data, group comparisons were conducted through non-parametric Wilcoxon or Kruskal-Wallis tests. The statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS software version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., USA). Differences with a P-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

Utilizing the FUTURE database, we compiled relevant data for 322 hospitalized children diagnosed with OATB across 24 hospitals from January 1, 2016, to December 31, 2021, based on ICD-10 disease code A18.0 (Table 1). Among all the hospitalized children with OATB, 57.14% (184/322) were male, and 42.86% (138/322) were female, resulting in a sex ratio of 1.33. In terms of age distribution, the majority fell within the age groups of 1–3 years (31.06%, 100/322) and 7–12 years (35.09%, 113/322). Han patients comprised 73.00% (236/322), while minorities accounted for 27.00% (86/322).(Table 1).

By comparing the characteristics of hospitalized children with OATB to those with other forms of TB (non-OATB), significant differences were observed in age group (P < 0.0001), ethnicity (P = 0.0011), admission route (P < 0.0001), LOS (P < 0.0001) and region (P < 0.0001) (Table 1). Among different age groups, the highest proportion of OATB occurred in the 4–6 years (7.59%, 80/1054) age group. The proportion of hospitalized children with OATB was 4.85% (164/3,380) in urban areas and 5.73% (158/2,758) in rural areas. Moreover, the proportion of hospitalized children with OATB among minority TB patients was significantly higher than that among Han nationality (7.12% vs. 4.79%, P = 0.0011). Regarding admission routes, the proportion of OATB patients admitted through outpatient routes was the highest (6.26%, 273/4360). In terms of LOS, the highest proportion of OATB (7.54%, 118/1565) was found in patients with LOS longer than 14 days, which meant that the LOS of OATB was longer. In addition, the highest proportion of OATB among all pediatric TB patients was in Southwest China (9.72%, 77/792). These differences depicted the distribution of different characteristic groups of OATB cases in hospitalized children.

Temporal and spatial distribution of OATB cases

Over the six-year period, there was a gradual decrease in the number of diagnosed cases (Fig. 1A). The proportion of childhood OATB in total hospitalizations over the six-year period was 4.21 per 100,000 (322/7,647,598), with a declining trend observed from 2016 to 2021 (Fig. 1B). In terms of geographical distribution, the highest number of childhood OATB cases was observed in Southwest China, followed by North China, East China, Northwest China, Central China, South China, and Northeast China (Fig. 1C). When considering the proportion of OATB cases relative to the total number of child hospitalizations in each region, Southwest China exhibited the highest proportion at 14.85 per 100,000 (77/518,420), notably exceeding other regions (Fig. 1D).

Simple OATB and OATB combined with other sites of tuberculosis infection

In order to gain a deeper insight into the clinical characteristics of pediatric OATB, we conducted a statistical analysis on OATB and its co-occurrence with TB infection in other organ sites. Among the 322 OATB cases, the majority (75.47%, 243/322) presented as Solo OATB (without involvement of TB in other organ sites), followed by OATB combined with pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) (14.91%, 48/322). Occurrences of OATB combined with TB in organs other than the lungs were infrequent, including 10 cases (3.11%) with lymphatic TB, 8 cases (2.48%) with tuberculous meningitis (TBM), 3 cases (0.93%) with pleural TB, and 1 case (0.31%) with pericardial TB. Additionally, there were 9 cases (2.80%) of OATB with involvement of two or more concomitant sites of TB, referred to as “others” (Fig. 2).

Anatomical site distribution of OATB

The anatomical locations of bones and joints affected in OATB patients were outlined in Table 2. Among the 322 patients, 36 (11.18%) did not specify the anatomical site of the lesion in their diagnosis. The remaining 286 OATB patients were categorized based on the anatomic site of infection. TB showed the potential to affect bones and joints throughout the body, with spinal TB being particularly prevalent (38.82%, 125/322). Within spinal TB cases, the lumbar spine was the most commonly affected (31 cases). Peripheral joints were also frequently involved (32.61%, 105/322), with the hip being the most prevalent (68 cases). Other infection sites included peripheral long bones (8.38%, 27/322) and skull (7.45%, 24/322). Economic Disease burden

From 2016 to 2021, the median LOS for all OATB patients was 12 days (IQR 7–17). In order to understand the burden of OATB in hospitalized children, a statistical analysis was conducted on LOS and hospitalization expenses of children with OATB (Table 3). The results revealed significant differences in the median LOS among different ethnic groups and regions (P < 0.05). Specifically, the median LOS for minority patients was longer than that for Han patients (14 days vs. 11 days, P < 0.05), and the median LOS for children in Northwest China (14 days) ranked the highest among all regions. However, there were no significant differences in median LOS concerning gender, age, residence, admission routes, and anatomical sites.

The median total hospitalization expense was $1319.61 (IQR 700.53-2788.25), with significant differences across admission routes and different regions (P < 0.05). Discussion

For children, OATB not only poses risks of bone or joint damage and deformity but also seriously affects their athletic ability and physical development, imposing a substantial physical and psychological burden on both affected children and their families [11, 36–38]. However, reports of childhood OATB are extremely scarce worldwide, resulting in a dearth of knowledge regarding its prevalence and burden. Given that this knowledge gap seriously hinders the formulation of public health strategies and the optimization of clinical intervention measures, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the FSMR data of 322 hospitalized children with OATB in China from the FUTURE database. The aim was to provide insights on epidemiological characteristics and the disease burden of OATB in hospitalized children, thereby providing crucial evidence for subsequent prevention, control, diagnosis, and treatment. This study contained the largest number of hospitalized pediatric patients with OATB. To our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate the epidemic of OATB among hospitalized children from multiple centers in China.

Over the span of six years, both the number of OATB cases and the proportion of OATB among hospitalized patients exhibited a declining trajectory, mirroring the trend reported by the WHO regarding the reduction in TB cases in China from 2016 to 2022, indicating a positive development [2]. Our data showed that the proportion of OATB among childhood TB cases was 5.25%, similar to the data previously reported [3, 15], while lower than the proportion noted in Chinese adult OATB (12.87%) [5]. However, the percentage of OATB cases among adults or children with TB exhibited substantial variations across different regions worldwide, spanning from 2.38 to 29% [13, 39–43].

The occurrence of EPTB is most common associated with the spread of PTB [44]. Previous studies indicated that 61.17%−73.45% of children with EPTB had concurrent PTB and EPTB [3, 45]. This data supports the pathological mechanism that EPTB is often caused by hematogenous dissemination from PTB. However, the co-occurrence rates of different types of EPTB with PTB vary significantly. The proportion of TBM combined with PTB ranged from 28.4–50.89% [46–48]. The co-occurrence rates of PTB were 61.7% (244/395) in lymphatic TB, 97.6% (122/125) in pharyngeal/laryngeal TB, and 19.59% (19/97) in genitourinary TB [28]. Our data revealed that only 14.91% of pediatric OATB cases had PTB, which was comparable to the 20.8% reported in a previous domestic study on pediatric OATB [15]. It is worth noting that the proportion of adult OATB cases concurring with PTBranged from 42.61 to 76.30% [18, 49, 50], which was significantly higher than that in children, highlighting the substantial differences in the co-occurrence of OATB and PTB across different age groups. Although bones and joints are deep tissues and organs, our results showed that OATB in children appeared to occur independently and does not co-exist with other common TB infections, such as PTB, peripheral lymph node TB, or abdominal TB. A possible explanation for this phenomenon is that during primary Mtb infection in children, before the formation of the adaptive immune response, Mtb can reach different organs and systems through the lymphohematogenous pathway, and at this moment, osteoarticular lesions may not have occurred. With the formation of the adaptive immune response, granulomas are formed. These granulomas can lie dormant for many years and then be reactivated, leading to OATB. By the time OATB occurred, the primary infection may have already been cured, creating the impression that OATB occurs independently [44, 51, 52].

TB can infiltrate any bone or joint in the human body. Our research revealed that the spine, particularly the lumbar region, was the most prevalent site of onset for hospitalized children with OATB. This aligns with findings from other studies adults or childhood OATB, where the spine constituted over 50% of all OATB cases, primarily affecting the thoracic and lumbar regions [11, 13, 15, 18, 39, 49, 50, 53]. Additionally, among extra-spinal OATB cases, the hip emerged as the most frequently involved site. Similar trends were observed in previous studies, underscoring the hip and knee as the sites most frequently affected among peripheral joints [13, 18, 39, 49, 50]. In summary, our findings, alongside those from related studies, indicate that OATB in hospitalized children is likely to occur more frequently in large joints.

This study revealed several key characteristics of medical expenditures for pediatric OATB inpatients in China. First, the higher medical costs in North China might be attributed to the presence of national pediatric medical centers in this region, which primarily treated severe and complex cases. This finding suggested that analyses of regional cost disparities should account for differences in hospital tier and case mix [54, 55]. Second, the elevated expenditures for emergency admissions underscored the critical impact of disease severity on healthcare resource utilization, highlighting the importance of early diagnosis and intervention [56–58]. Notably, the increased nursing costs for younger patients reflected their unique clinical care needs, providing valuable guidance for pediatric nursing resource allocation. The urban-rural disparity in cost structure (comparable total costs but higher medication expenses in urban areas) might indicate systemic differences in treatment protocols between urban and rural medical institutions [59]. Collectively, these results demonstrate that developing cost-containment strategies for pediatric OATB requires comprehensive consideration of multiple dimensions, including region, admission route, age, and urban-rural divide [59–62].

The proportion of OATB hospitalizations in Southwest China was significantly higher, and the average LOS of patients in Northwest China was notably prolonged. This phenomenon fully indicated that the disease burden of OATB in the western region was extremely heavy, calling for high attention. The heavier OATB burden in the western region of China stems from socioeconomic and geographic challenges: lower population density, economic underdevelopment, and uneven healthcare distribution reduce early diagnosis rates, while rugged terrain and poor transportation hinder treatment adherence—often leading to severe cases requiring extended hospitalization [63–65]. Additionally, the heavier OATB disease burden in western China is also influenced by its demographic composition, because this region is the main residence for many ethnic minorities [66]. Our data revealed that ethnic minority TB patients exhibited both a significantly higher proportion of OATB and longer LOS compared to their Han counterparts. There are multiple and complex reasons behind this difference [67]. Geographically, ethnic minority-inhabited areas are mostly located in remote mountainous regions and plateaus [68]. The scattered population makes it difficult to cover medical resources, and the shortage of primary medical institutions affects the early screening and standardized treatment of tuberculosis [69], increasing the risk of developing into OATB. Culturally, the unique language systems create communication barriers between doctors and patients, leading to misjudgment of the condition and treatment deviations [70]. Some patients also delay diagnosis and treatment due to their unfamiliarity with the modern medical system [71]. Economically, the economic development in ethnic minority-inhabited areas lags behind [67]. With low resident income and limited affordability [72], many patients are unable to bear the medical expenses or interrupt the treatment, resulting in incomplete treatment of tuberculosis and eventually its progression into OATB. In conclusion, the higher proportion of OATB and the longer hospitalization period among ethnic minority groups in the western region of China urgently require the focused attention of all sectors. Systematic measures should be taken to optimize diagnosis and treatment, improve service accessibility, reduce the disease burden, and promote the balanced development of national prevention and control work.

This study had certain limitations. Several constraints are inherent to its hospital-based multicenter design: (1) Representativeness limitations:,although it was a multicenter study, the data only came from 24 hospitals in six regions of China, which were predominantly tertiary referral centers. Cases from remote and primary medical institutions may not be included, resulting in selection bias. This geographically and institutionally restricted sampling limits the generalizability of our findings to the national pediatric OATB population. (2) Case ascertainment bias: the study only focused on hospitalized children, excluding outpatient and community cases. This likely overestimates disease severity and underestimates mild/early cases, introducing a spectrum bias that affects burden estimation accuracy. (3) Data depth constraints: the data were derived from the FSMRs, linking to three key design weaknesses:1) Lack of standardized etiological confirmation across centers, preventing microbiological profiling; 2) Insufficient treatment process data, limiting exploration of associations between treatment regimens, individual characteristics, and disease outcomes; 3) Insufficient follow-up data to assess long-term outcomes or complications. Therefore, our results characterize pediatric OATB burden specifically among hospitalized children in tertiary centers and should not be extrapolated to the general pediatric population. Despitethese limitations, our study provides the largest characterization to date of pediatric OATB hospitalizations across diverse regions of China, offering valuable insights for managing hospitalized cases and planning resources within tertiary pediatric settings.

Conclusion

As a multi-center study of OATB among hospitalized children in China for the first time, this study analyzed the pediatric OATB cases in the Chinese FUTURE database from 2016 to 2021. The study presented the temporal and spatial distribution characteristics and the patterns of the affected sites of OATB among hospitalized children in China, providing crucial clues for clarifying the proportion and clinical characteristics of this disease in the pediatric population. Meanwhile, we explored the epidemiological characteristics and disease burden of hospitalized children with OATB. It is particularly worth noting that the burden of OATB among hospitalized children in the western region of China and among ethnic minorities is heavy. In view of this, it is urgent to carry out region-targeted prevention and treatment measures, which can not only effectively curb the spread of the disease but also serve as a crucial move to safeguard the health of adolescents.

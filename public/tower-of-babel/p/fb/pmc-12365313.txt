Jahn-Teller effect for controlling quantum correlations in hexanuclear Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}$$\end{document} magnets
Authors: Hamid Arian Zad, Michal Jaščur, Asad Ali, Saif Al-Kuwari, Saeed Haddadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We theoretically investigate the low-temperature magnetic and quantum properties of hexanuclear Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} complexes under an external magnetic field. We primarily study the impact of competing exchange interactions and their asymmetries induced by the Jahn-Teller distortion on the quantum properties of the complexes. The inequality in exchange interactions lifts the ground-state degeneracy that gives rise to complex quantum behavior. By constructing the ground-state phase diagram and analyzing magnetization, we identify key magnetic phases and critical phenomena. We further quantify quantum correlations using tripartite entanglement negativity and conditional von Neumann entropy to unveil how the Jahn-Teller effect enhances intra-triangle entanglement while modulating inter-triangle correlations. Our findings highlight the Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} complex as a promising molecular platform for tunable quantum correlations, with potential applications in quantum information processing and molecular qubits.

Introduction

Molecular magnets1–5, particularly transition-metal complexes with intrinsic quantum correlations, offer promising applications in quantum information science including spintronics6–8, quantum communication9 and quantum computing10–12. This rapidly evolving field presents diverse opportunities by examining the unique magnetic and quantum properties of molecular systems13. The study of these materials has gained significant attention due to their interdisciplinary relevance to bridge quantum information science, condensed matter physics, and coordination chemistry14–18. Among them, hexanuclear iron(III) compounds19–22 exhibit a rich interplay of competing magnetic interactions, leading to unconventional quantum critical behaviors. A deeper understanding of these fundamental properties is essential for the development of next-generation molecular-based quantum technologies.

However, under the influence of the Jahn-Teller (JT) effect22–25, these compounds undergo a spontaneous symmetry-breaking distortion that lifts the degeneracy of their electronic states22. As predicted by Jahn and Teller in 1937, a non-linear system in a degenerate electronic state is inherently unstable and will distort to remove the degeneracy, thereby its total energy is lowered23. In the case of Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} complexes, the JT effect alters the geometric and electronic structure of the Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_3$$\end{document}O subunits, subtly modifying the exchange interactions and consequently reshaping the magnetic ground state. This distortion introduces anisotropy into the system, affecting both the intra- and inter-triangle couplings and influencing the quantum correlations and spin frustration within the molecule. Hence, the JT effect plays a crucial role in determining the fine structure of the energy landscape, where it leads to distinct magnetic behavior that would not be observed in an idealized symmetric model with equilateral triangular subunits.

When an external field is applied, the competition between exchange interactions leads to intriguing quantum phenomena, including field-induced phase transitions and entanglement structures that remain robust at low temperatures. Here, we systematically investigate the ground-state phase diagram and low-temperature quantum properties of the isotropic XXX Heisenberg model on a hexanuclear spin-1/2 system possessing three different exchange couplings that characterize the Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} complexes such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\text {Fe}^{3+}_6(\mu _3\text {-}\text {O})_2(\mu \text {-}\text {OH})_2\{\mu \text {-}(\text {C}_6\text {H}_{11})_2\text {PO}_2\}_6(\mu \text {-}t\text {BuCO}_2)_6 (\eta ^1\text {-}\text {OH}_2)_2 2\text {CH}_3\text {CN} \text {CH}_2\text {Cl}_2$$\end{document}19. A key feature of hexanuclear Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} complexes is the presence of multiple exchange interactions, which determine the ground-state spin configuration and influence the response of the system to external perturbations such as magnetic field19–22. In particular, the Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} molecular structure comprises two almost identical Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_3$$\end{document}O triangular subunits that exhibit a quantum ground state with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S=1/2$$\end{document} per triangular subunit. These iron-based molecular systems exhibit competing exchange interactions, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_3$$\end{document} under the influence of the JT effect, leading to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S = \frac{1}{2}$$\end{document} ground states for both \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\text {Fe}_3\text {O}$$\end{document} triangular subunits. The inequality of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_{ij}$$\end{document} lifts the degeneracy of the ground states (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S \ge \frac{1}{2}$$\end{document}) that is a consequence of magnetic JT distortion. The two triangular subunits are coupled via the inter-triangle interaction \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_3$$\end{document}, which plays a crucial role in the quantumness of the system, particularly when this interaction is non-negligible19.

In this paper, we construct the ground-state phase diagram in the plane of external magnetic field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B$$\end{document}, intra-triangle exchange coupling \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} and inter-triangle exchange coupling \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_3$$\end{document}. Then, we analyze the magnetization process and explore how the entanglement structure evolves with varying field strengths under JT distortion. To quantify quantum entanglement26 within the subunits, we use the tripartite entanglement negativity27–29, a concept introduced by Peres and Horodecki30–32. We also examine the conditional von Neumann entropy33–36, a key measure that captures quantum correlations between different Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_3$$\end{document}O triangular subunits. This approach provides a comprehensive overivew of how quantum information is distributed across the molecular system.

Our findings contribute to the broader understanding of entanglement in molecular magnets at low temperature and highlight the potential of hexanuclear Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}$$\end{document} complexes as testbeds for quantum correlations in condensed matter systems. By unveiling the fundamental entanglement mechanisms in these systems, this work opens new avenues for their potential applications in emerging quantum technologies.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Sec. 2, we introduce the model and outline the key magnetic interactions governing the hexanuclear Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{3+}_6$$\end{document} complex. In Sec. 3, we first construct the ground-state phase diagram in different parameter planes to characterize the quantum phase spectra. We also present an analysis of the low-temperature magnetization behavior as a function of the external magnetic field. Then, we investigate tripartite entanglement together with conditional von Neumann entropy as a measure of quantum correlations within the system, focusing on entanglement within individual Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_3$$\end{document}O subunits and the amount of correlation between different subunits. Finally, Sec. 4 provides a summary of our findings.

Conclusions

In this work, we investigated the ground-state phase diagram and quantum correlations in the hexanuclear Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^ {3+}$$\end{document} complex under the influence of the JT effect. Our findings indicate that the magnetic phase with one-third magnetization plateau dominates across the parameter space. This state remains stable in the presence of the JT effect characterized by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1 > J_2$$\end{document}. The role of JT effect in modifying tripartite entanglement within a triangular subunit has been studied. Our results demonstrate that the JT distortion enhances multipartite entanglement in the subunits. When the JT effect is absent and the triangular units are equilateral, tripartite entanglement is significantly reduced, confirming that the JT distortion plays a key role in strengthening quantum correlations at the molecular level. Moreover, our analysis of the conditional von Neumann entropy demonstrated that the JT effect may diminish inter-triangle quantum correlations. At low temperatures, the two triangular subunits remain uncorrelated in a moderate field range, but increasing temperature leads to a rise in conditional entropy, which suggests enhanced quantum correlations. We have found that stronger inter-triangle interaction leads to negative conditional entropy, signifying entanglement between the two subunits.

Overall, our study demonstrates that JT effect increases intra-triangle tripartite entanglement, but may decrease the inter-triangle correlations. These findings suggest that JT distortions could be harnessed for optimizing molecular qubits in quantum information processing. By controlling exchange interactions and external magnetic fields, Fe\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^ {3+}$$\end{document} complexes may serve as promising candidates for designing quantum computing architectures. These results establish a clear theoretical foundation that can guide future experimental efforts, while offering insight into how structural distortions like the JT effect can be strategically leveraged to realize entangled states for molecular quantum technologies.

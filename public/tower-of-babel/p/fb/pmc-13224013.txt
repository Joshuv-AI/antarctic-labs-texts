From weather to symptoms: Preliminary links between weather and inflammation in schizophrenia
Authors: Stefan Modzelewski, Maria Suprunowicz, Aleksandra Julia Oracz, Rafał Marecki, Jakub Wiktor Kosidło, Joanna Kamińska, Olga Martyna Koper-Lenkiewicz, Joanna Matowicka-Karna, Napoleon Waszkiewicz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNegative symptoms in schizophrenia are a major determinant of long-term disability, yet drivers of symptom variability remain poorly understood. Weather-related immune shifts have been proposed as a potential contributing factor, consistent with the immunoseasonal theory.MethodsWe conducted an observational cross-sectional study in 81 adults (18–65 years) with schizophrenia assessed across three psychiatric centers between January and October 2025. Negative symptom severity was measured using the PANSS negative subscale (N1–N7). For each assessment, meteorological data from the nearest station were collected and averaged over a 3-day window (the assessment day and the two preceding days): relative humidity, mean temperature, sunshine duration, wind speed, precipitation, and atmospheric pressure. Serum IFN-γ IL-1β, IL-6, IL-7, TNF-α, TNF-β concentrations were assayed. A composite index reflecting general inflammatory response and Th1 activity was derived using principal component analysis (PCA) to explore the assumptions of the immunoseasonal theory. Associations were tested using linear regression, mediation models were additionally explored for cytokines.ResultsHigher relative humidity, lower mean temperature, and longer sunshine duration were associated with greater negative symptom severity. IL-1β, IL-6, IL-7, TNF-α and a General inflammatory component showed a positive association with PANSS-N scores. Relative humidity predicted higher IL-1β and IL-7 levels, whereas no comparable relationship was found for TNF-α. General inflammatory component was also predicted in simplified models. In mediation analyses, the association between humidity and negative symptoms was partially explained by general inflammatory component.ConclusionsRelative humidity is a candidate meteorological correlate of negative symptom burden in schizophrenia, with general inflammatory markers, rather than Th1-specific activity, demonstrating a statistically consistent mediation effect. Longitudinal studies are needed to confirm directionality and the stability of these associations.

Introduction

Schizophrenia remains one of the most severe psychiatric disorders, characterized by a difficult and often unpredictable clinical course (Marder and Galderisi, 2017). Negative symptoms and their severity in schizophrenia spectrum disorders constitute one of the most important determinants of patients’ functional recovery and rehabilitation outcomes (Schwarz and Bahn, 2008; Stahl and Buckley, 2007; Weickert et al., 2013). A growing body of research suggests that environmental factors may modulate psychiatric outcomes across the year, and schizophrenia has been repeatedly linked to seasonal patterns at the population level (Coury et al., 2023; Hinterbuchinger et al., 2020; Rizavas et al., 2023). Nevertheless, the biological mechanisms responsible for these seasonal patterns in schizophrenia have not been fully elucidated. One promising theoretical framework that seeks to explain these associations is the immunoseasonality theory of mental disorders. Within this model, shifts in immune regulation (including T helper 1 (Th1) lymphocyte/T helper 2 (Th2) lymphocyte balance) (Budu-Aggrey et al., 2021; Fisman, 2012; Müller, 2018) are expected to translate weather exposure into changes in symptom severity (Waszkiewicz, 2023). In patients with schizophrenia, aberrant cytokine profiles have been consistently reported, suggesting an altered immune response relative to healthy controls (Dawidowski et al., 2021; Halstead et al., 2023). Furthermore, evidence suggests that acute psychotic episodes and first-episode schizophrenia are commonly accompanied by heightened pro-inflammatory immune activation, as reflected by increased concentrations of interferon-γ (IFN-γ), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) (L. Chen et al., 2021; Momtazmanesh et al., 2019). Chronic peripheral inflammation may lead to microglial activation (Najjar et al., 2013), blood-brain barrier dysfunction, and disturbances in tryptophan metabolism, which may, in turn, exacerbate neurotoxic processes and be associated with greater clinical severity, including negative symptom burden and cognitive impairment (Galińska-Skok and Waszkiewicz, 2022; Orlovska-Waast et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2022). From the perspective of the immunoseasonality theory, this gap is especially noteworthy: while cytokine alterations have been reported in schizophrenia, studies that directly test mechanistic links between specific meteorological parameters, defined immune markers, and negative symptoms are still scarce (Chen et al., 2021, Chen et al., 2021; Dawidowski et al., 2021; Galińska-Skok and Waszkiewicz, 2022; Halstead et al., 2023; Momtazmanesh et al., 2019; Najjar et al., 2013; Orlovska-Waast et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2022).

To address this gap, we investigated the role of pro-inflammatory cytokines representing the Th1-mediated immune response in line with the immunoseasonality theory (TNF-β and IFN-γ), as well as markers of broader inflammatory activity (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-7, TNF-α), hypothesized to be relevant to negative symptom burden (in the context of the expansion of the immunoseasonal theory (Modzelewski et al., 2025)), and examined their associations with key meteorological variables (Halstead et al., 2023; Waszkiewicz, 2023).

This study aimed to evaluate the immunoseasonality hypothesis by assessing complex associations between meteorological variables, pro-inflammatory cytokine levels (IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-7, TNF-α, TNF-β), and negative symptom severity in individuals with schizophrenia, with a specific focus on the potential mediating role of immune mechanisms in the relationship between weather factors and clinical outcomes.

Methods

Study design

This study employed an observational, cross-sectional design and included 81 patients diagnosed with schizophrenia (International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems 10th Revision (ICD-10), code F20) (World Health, 1992), who were recruited between January and October 2025. Informed consent acquisition, peripheral blood sampling, clinical assessment, and collection of all relevant study data were conducted during a single study visit in a clinical setting. Patients were recruited and evaluated by the research team during routine clinical care, either in an inpatient ward or an outpatient clinic. The authors confirm that all procedures involving human participants were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the relevant national and institutional committees and with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the Local Bioethics Committee of the Medical University of Białystok (approval no. APK.002.528.2024). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants after a full explanation of the study objectives and procedures.

Participants

Patients were recruited from three clinical centers: the Department of Psychiatry of the Medical University of Białystok; the Independent Public Psychiatric Healthcare Facility named after Dr. Stanisław Deresz in Choroszcz and the Mental Health Outpatient Clinic “Bram-Medica” in Białystok (Fig. 1). All centers are located in the Podlaskie Voivodeship, Poland. This region is characterized by pronounced seasonal variation throughout the year.Fig. 1Flowchart: Participant recruitment and study workflow.

Flowchart: Participant recruitment and study workflow.

Participants were recruited using a convenience sampling approach. Eligible patients were aged between 18 and 65 years. Exclusion criteria were determined during the screening interview and included: inability to provide informed consent; exposure to acute stressors or major life changes within the preceding 8 weeks; alcohol or psychoactive substance abuse; recent changes (within 4 weeks) in psychiatric or anti-inflammatory treatment; intensive physical activity within the preceding 72 h; the presence of autoimmune disorders; and severe somatic illnesses that could affect biochemical measurements.

Patients who had recently traveled abroad to different climatic zones were also excluded. Sleep disturbances were not considered an exclusion criterion, except in cases of night-shift or rotating shift work. Additionally, individuals whose lifestyle involved spending less than 1 h per day outdoors were excluded from the study. Finally, 81 patients were enrolled. The final sample size was determined by the exploratory nature of the study and practical considerations, including stringent exclusion criteria and challenges in patient recruitment.

Procedures

Patients were assessed by researchers using the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), with a focus on the negative symptom subscale (items N1-N7) (Kay et al., 1987). During the psychiatric interview and examination, eligibility for study inclusion was determined and written informed consent was obtained. In addition, patients completed the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10) (Cohen et al., 1983) as a self-report measure. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated based on body weight measured using a bioimpedance analyzer and height measured with a stadiometer. Subjective sleep quality was assessed as a dichotomous variable (good/poor), as was smoking status (yes/no). Age and sex were included as basic covariates. Blood samples were collected after the clinical assessment, processed for serum (clotted, centrifuged, aliquoted), and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Cytokines (IFN-γ IL-1β, IL-6, IL-7, TNF-α, TNF-β; pg/mL) were assayed using the Bio-Plex Pro™ Human Cytokine Screening panel. IL-7 and IL-1β concentrations were below the detection threshold in 7 (IL-7) and 5 (IL-1β) patients. Only complete datasets were included in the final analyses, resulting in a reduced sample size of 74 patients for IL-7 and 76 for IL-1β, while the sample size for TNF-α, TNF-β, IFN-γ, IL-6 remained unchanged (N = 81).

Meteorological data for the nearest standardized weather station including atmospheric pressure (hPa), total precipitation (mm), relative humidity (%), sunshine duration (hours), mean temperature (°C), and mean wind speed (m/s) were obtained from official public sources provided by the Institute of Meteorology and Water Management (IMGW), in accordance with the Act of August 11, 2021, on open data and the reuse of public sector information. Weather data were collected for a three-day window comprising the day of clinical assessment and the two preceding days, in order to account for potential delayed effects of meteorological conditions on cytokine levels. Data from this three-day period were aggregated and averaged and are presented as mean values.

To align the analysis with the assumptions of the immunoseasonal theory, cytokines were analyzed both individually and as composite variables representing broader immune pathways. Specifically, cytokines were grouped into variables reflecting general (non-specific) inflammatory response and Th1 immune activity, allowing assessment at both the level of individual markers and integrated immune signaling. This dual analytical approach enabled identification of potential cytokine-specific effects while also capturing more robust, biologically meaningful patterns of immune activity derived from multiple markers.

Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed separately for each cytokine set. For the Th1-related markers (IFN-γ and TNF-β), the first principal component was retained as the Th1 inflammatory component, explaining 63.8% of the variance. For markers of non-specific inflammation (TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β), the first principal component was retained as the general inflammatory component, explaining 58.2% of the variance.

Linear regression was used to assess the association between negative symptoms (total PANSS negative subscale score, N1-N7) and meteorological variables while accounting for lifestyle and clinical factors. For each cytokine and both components, its association with negative symptoms was evaluated in models including lifestyle and clinical covariates. As a sensitivity analysis, hierarchical regression models were applied to confirm the independent contribution of cytokines beyond covariates (Table S87–89). Next, associations between cytokines and weather were examined by testing whether meteorological variables, adjusted for lifestyle and clinical factors, predicted the level of each cytokine and components. Meteorological variables included air temperature, relative humidity, sunshine duration, total precipitation, wind speed, atmospheric pressure. Covariates included age, total PSS-10 score, BMI, sex, smoking status, and sleep quality.

Analyses were conducted using a complete-case approach for each linear regression model. Assumptions for each key regression model were evaluated using residual-versus-fitted plots, Breusch-Pagan/Cook-Weisberg tests for heteroskedasticity, and Ramsey RESET tests for functional form misspecification. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were used to assess multicollinearity; VIF values < 4 were considered indicative of no problematic multicollinearity. In addition, influential observations were assessed using Cook's distance and leverage diagnostics, and sensitivity analyses were conducted by re-estimating models after excluding the most influential cases to verify the robustness of effect estimates. The diagnostics for each model are presented in the Supplementary Materials.

Linear regression models were used as a screening step to identify the most informative meteorological exposure for subsequent mediation analyses. Among the weather indicators examined, relative humidity showed the most consistent association with negative symptoms in preliminary analyses and was therefore selected as the primary exposure variable. This selection was further motivated by biological plausibility (see Discussion).

Immunological markers were then evaluated as potential mediators based on their associations with humidity and biological plausibility: IL-7, IL-1β and general inflammatory component were selected for mediation analyses. Exploratory mediation analysis was conducted using linear models, estimating the natural indirect effect (NIE), natural direct effect (NDE), and total effect (TE) for the contrast between the 25th and 75th percentiles of relative humidity. Given the limited sample size, the primary model included a restricted set of covariates (clinical, lifestyle, and meteorological) selected based on prior analyses and clinical relevance. The robustness of the findings was assessed in sensitivity analyses using an expanded covariate set, reported in the Supplementary Materials.

For exploratory purposes, given the modest sample size, seasonal differences in negative symptoms were examined using Kruskal–Wallis tests, followed by post-hoc Dunn tests with Bonferroni correction. Furthermore, categorical variables reflecting symptom exacerbation versus remission were derived based on predefined thresholds (see Supplement Materials Table S97, Table S98), and associations with season were evaluated using Fisher's exact test.

In multivariable models predicting cytokine levels from meteorological variables while adjusting for clinical and lifestyle covariates, relative humidity was a significant positive predictor of IL-1β (β = 0.632, p < .001, partial r2 = 0.199), alongside mean temperature (β = −0.428, p = .018 partial r2 = 0.086), sunshine duration (β = 0.537, p = .009, partial r2 = 0.104), and mean wind speed (β = 0.428, p = .004, partial r2 = 0.126) (Supplementary Materials, Table S41). Similarly, relative humidity was also positively associated with IL-7 (β = 0.524, p = .002, partial r2 = 0.142), with mean wind speed showing association in the same model (β = 0.524, p < .001, partial r2 = 0.185) (Supplementary Materials, Table S48). The model predicting the general inflammatory component was not statistically significant (p = .112) (Table S66). However, after model reduction to limit overfitting, it reached statistical significance, with relative humidity and mean wind speed emerging as significant predictors, warranting further analysis within the mediation framework (Table S85). Models for TNF-α and IFN-γ did not demonstrate significant associations with weather factors (Supplementary Materials, Table S56, Table S63), while the models for IL-6, TNF-β, and the Th1 response component did not reach statistical significance (p = .971, p = .303, and p = .474, respectively) (Table S62, Table S64, Table S67).

To evaluate whether immune activity was statistically consistent with the observed association between weather exposure and clinical status, we conducted exploratory mediation analyses in StataNow 19 SE using the mediate command. Specifically, we tested whether the association between relative humidity and negative symptom severity was mediated by circulating cytokines. Because of the cross-sectional design, these estimates should not be interpreted as causal effects. Three separate mediation models were estimated, with IL-7, IL-1β and general inflammatory component specified as mediators. Ultimately, in line with the immunoseasonality theory, mediation analysis including the Th1 response component was also planned; however, this variable did not reach the required level of statistical significance at the regression stage (Table S35). Models were adjusted for a limited set of clinical and lifestyle covariates selected based on prior analyses and clinical relevance. Relative humidity was treated as a continuous exposure, and mediation effects were evaluated for a contrast between the 25th and 75th percentiles of relative humidity (61.70% vs 82.86%). Because the outcome equation included a exposure-mediator interaction, we additionally reported conditional (marginal) effects of the mediator at relative humidity (RH) = 61.70 and RH = 82.86.

Exploratory mediation models yielded estimates statistically consistent with a general, non-specific inflammatory response as a potential correlate of the association between relative humidity and negative symptom severity. We constructed two models for the general inflammatory response: one including interaction terms and one without interactions. Both mediation models yielded consistent evidence for a significant indirect effect of relative humidity on negative symptom severity via the innate pro-inflammatory score. In the non-interaction model, both the indirect (NIE: b = 2.177, SE = 0.916, p = .017) and direct effects (NDE: b = 4.869, SE = 4.869, p = .005) were statistically significant, indicating partial mediation (Fig. 2). In contrast, in the interaction model, the indirect effect remained significant (NIE: b = 2.700, SE = 1.009 p = .007), while the direct effect was no longer significant (NDE: p = .163). The proportion mediated was 30.9% in the non-interaction model (p = .011) and increased to 44.3% in the interaction model (p = .050), indicating a potentially stronger, though less precise, mediating role when accounting for exposure–mediator interaction (Fig. S28).Fig. 2Exploratory mediation model of the association between relative humidity and negative symptom severity via general inflammation component.

In the IL-1β model (contrast from 61.7 to 82.86), the natural indirect effect (NIE) was positive and statistically significant (NIE = 2.93, SE = 1.11, p = .008), with a significant proportion mediated (0.428, SE = 0.133, p = .001). Both the natural direct effect (NDE = 3.91, SE = 1.35, p = .004) and total effect (TE = 6.85, SE = 1.64, p < .001) were also positive. Consistent with a exposure-mediator interaction in the outcome equation, the marginal effect of IL-1β on negative symptoms was negligible at lower humidity (RH = 61.7: dy/dx = 0.42, p = .770) but strongly positive at higher humidity (RH = 82.86: dy/dx = 3.89, p < .001), suggesting that the mediator-outcome association increased with humidity (Fig. S27).

In contrast, results for IL-7 were weaker and were treated as borderline overall: although the estimated indirect effect was positive (NIE = 2.44, SE = 1.13 p = .031), the total effect did not meet the conventional significance threshold (TE = 4.46, SE = 2.30, p = .052) and the direct effect was not significant (NDE = 2.02, SE = 2.74, p = .462) (Supplementary Materials, Table S69). Therefore, IL-1β showed a more consistent pattern in exploratory models than IL-7.

Discussion

Our analysis yields several important findings. The first finding is a significant association between selected weather variables (relative humidity, sunshine duration, and mean air temperature) and the severity of negative symptoms in patients with schizophrenia, as mentioned earlier in results section. Consistent with the immunoseasonality framework that underpins the present study, higher levels of relative humidity may adversely affect the course of mental disorders by favoring airborne transmission of pathogens and enhancing indirect viral transmission through increased stability of viral particles within droplets and on surfaces (Fisman, 2012; Vida et al., 2012; Waszkiewicz et al., 2022). These potential pathogens may subsequently stimulate a immune response and, ultimately, contribute to prefrontal cortical dysfunction and worsening of negative symptoms (Audi et al., 2020; Barron et al., 2017; Bower and Kuhlman, 2023; Dev et al., 2017; Müller, 2018; Najjar et al., 2013; Upthegrove et al., 2017; Waszkiewicz, 2023). It should be emphasized that meteorological factors co-occur in natural settings rather than acting in isolation. Nevertheless, relative humidity represents a biologically central meteorological variable within immunoseasonality theory, partly due to earlier evidence linking humidity to depressive outcomes, but also because of the proposed pathogen-transmission mechanism and the relative day-to-day stability of this exposure compared with other meteorological parameters. In addition, relative humidity predicted IL-7 and IL-1β levels in our data, which is consistent with the notion that higher humidity may facilitate transmission of common respiratory infections (Buras et al., 2016). However, in our study, relative humidity did not predict levels of IFN-γ - typically associated with viral infections - nor did it predict a Th1-type immune response (Karki and Kanneganti, 2021; Russell et al., 2017). On the other hand, in the simplified model, relative humidity was associated with levels of general inflammation. This pattern suggests that higher relative humidity may create conditions conducive to increased exposure to microorganisms or environmental pollutants more broadly, rather than to specific types of infections. Additionally, it should be considered that, despite the relative stability of humidity, the selected 3-day exposure window may partially explain the lack of association with Th1 responses alongside the observed relationship with more general, less specific inflammatory markers. It is plausible that longer exposure windows (e.g., 5- or 7-day periods) may be more suitable for capturing signals related to specific immune responses in future analyses (Lessler et al., 2009). These considerations were the main rationale for selecting relative humidity as the exposure variable in our mediation. It should also be emphasized that the mechanistic explanations proposed here for the role of relative humidity cannot be confirmed in a cross-sectional design (Compton and Lopez, 2014) and should be viewed as a hypothesis-generating perspective rather than evidence of causality.

Additionally, temperature effects may be context-dependent. In our cohort, which was predominantly assessed in the winter-spring period, lower mean temperatures were associated with greater negative symptom severity. Although some reports emphasize adverse outcomes under high-temperature and high-humidity conditions (Vida et al., 2012), this difference may reflect distinct phenotypes: cooler conditions may relate more to avolition and other negative symptoms, whereas hot conditions may be more strongly linked to impulsive crises such as self-harm (Maes et al., 1994; Talaei et al., 2014). However, our study did not examine such crisis outcomes directly. Future samples with better representation of the summer-autumn period may clarify whether the observed temperature association is season-specific. We also observed an association between sunshine duration and negative symptoms. However, sunlight was measured as duration (hours) rather than intensity, which may be more biologically relevant for circadian mechanisms, including melatonin regulation implicated in psychosis (Davies et al., 2003; Force et al., 1997). Interpretation is further limited by the inpatient setting, where outdoor exposure is often restricted only to few hours. In addition, sunshine duration likely reflects ambient conditions rather than individual exposure. These constraints highlight the need for future studies using more precise light measures and individual exposure patterns.

Second, we found that higher circulating levels of IL-6, IL-7, TNF-α and IL-1β were associated with higher scores on the PANSS negative symptom subscale, and the composite general inflammation marker, comprising IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6, emerged as the model explaining the highest proportion of variance and demonstrated the strongest explanatory power among the variables. These findings reflect the integrated nature of the immune system, suggesting that a combined variable may capture more meaningful information within the model without increasing the risk of overfitting.

Prior evidence regarding TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β indicates that these cytokines have been reported as markers of immune dysregulation in schizophrenia, observed across clinical states including exacerbation and remission, and both have been linked to a shift toward a pro-inflammatory immune profile (Akdis et al., 2016). In addition, we observed a relationship for IL-7, a cytokine that may contribute to the maintenance of chronic inflammation by sustaining B- and T-lymphocyte populations (L. Chen et al., 2021; Frydecka et al., 2018; Juncal-Ruiz et al., 2018). Taken together, our findings are consistent with the immunoseasonality framework, in which - against a background of pre-existing immune dysfunction - worsening clinical status (operationalized here as greater negative symptom severity) may be accompanied by further immune dysregulation and elevated pro-inflammatory signaling (Modzelewski et al., 2025). Such amplification could occur in the presence of specific triggers, such as viral or bacterial infections (D. Chen et al., 2021). Moreover, given the chronic and often treatment-refractory nature of negative symptoms, the observed associations with these cytokines appear empirically plausible (Lui et al., 2024; Marder and Galderisi, 2017).

The third finding of our study was that exploratory mediation models yielded estimates statistically consistent with a partial indirect component involving immunological markers in the association between relative humidity and negative symptom severity. The exploratory mediation was partial, and its effect was more apparent at higher levels of relative humidity (for the 61.7% to 82.86% contrast), which is compatible with - though does not establish - an immune pathway mentioned above. In the conceptual interaction model for the general inflammation component, higher levels of relative humidity were associated with increased immune activity. Notably, the indirect and total effects were significant, whereas the direct effect of humidity was not, suggesting a pattern consistent with full mediation. In contrast, a simpler model without the interaction term indicated partial mediation, with approximately 30% of the total effect being transmitted through the mediator. These findings underscore the importance of further investigating the concept of immunoseasonality, which posits that specific meteorological conditions may influence patients’ clinical status through immunological mechanisms.

Several limitations should be noted. Although our study accounted for several important covariates, more detailed information on medication dosages and illness duration would further strengthen the interpretation of our findings. The modest sample size limited power and constrained more complex modeling, and convenience sampling may reduce representativeness despite scheduling visits in advance to minimize weather-related bias. Finally, the cross-sectional, single-region design prevents assessment of temporal variability and limit generalizability, while residual confounding cannot be fully excluded.

This study has several strengths, including a multi-layered analytical approach (from simple regressions to multivariable and mediation models) and stringent eligibility criteria that reduce immune-related confounding. Using averaged meteorological data from the assessment day and the two preceding days also allowed evaluation of delayed responses to environmental exposure. Although the PANSS remains one of the most widely used instruments in schizophrenia research, its structure - particularly with respect to positive symptoms - has been subject to criticism, as only selected items reflect core psychotic phenomena, while others capture disorganization rather than positive symptomatology (Anderson et al., 2017). In contrast, the PANSS negative symptom subscale demonstrates closer alignment with contemporary conceptualizations of negative symptoms than other components of the scale.

Conclusion

Weather-related factors (relative humidity, mean temperature, and sunshine duration) were associated with negative symptom severity in schizophrenia. Among the cytokines examined, IL-1β showed the most consistent pattern of association with both relative humidity and symptom severity, and the general, non-specific inflammatory component demonstrated a more consistent pattern than the Th1 response component. Exploratory mediation models testing the effect of relative humidity on negative symptoms suggested a partial indirect effect involving the inflammatory response. These hypothesis-generating findings warrant confirmation in larger longitudinal studies with repeated immune assessments and more granular exposure characterization.

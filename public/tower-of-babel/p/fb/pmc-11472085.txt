Selection of superior Brassica napus L. hybrids by integrated graphical and numerical diallel analysis by yield×traits: Using hayman and biplot diallel approaches
Authors: Kamal Payghamzadeh, Hassan Amiri Oghan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractImproving yield via assessment of different yield-related genetic parameters specifically with the challenges of yield × traits combination is a prominent issue for plant breeders. A graphical and numerical description of diallel analysis by yield × traits with the novelty of the parent selection with desirable genetic architecture in rapeseed was illustrated via Hayman and biplot approaches. Eleven divers' parents and their F1 offspring, achieved via a complete diallel mating fashion, were evaluated under field conditions based on randomized complete block design replicated thrice throught 2018–2020. Integrated yield × traits analysis revealed highly significant additive and non-additive gene effects which were confirmed by the meaningful general and specific combining ability of evaluated multi-traits. Graphical Wr-Vr analysis, depicted partial dominance gene action for yield × traits, except yield × thousand grain yield which is governed by over-dominance gene action.The biplot polygon panorama identified the most desirable heterotic parents with higher general and specific combining ability, and more dominant genes including [(P9) × (P4, P6, P10) for yield × pod]; [(P9) × (P7), (P6) × (P11) and (P7) × (P10) for yield × thousand grain yield] and [(P10 × P3) for yield/flowering, yield/physiological maturity, yield/plant height]. These findings suggested that a breeder should consider superior parents with high × high general and specific combining ability according to combining their yield and its related genetic parameters as important traits with all other target characters via descripted approaches.Keywords: Brassica napus L., Combining ability, Genetic components, Gene action, Graphical and numerical diallel analysis, Heritability, Multi-traits combination

Introduction

Oilseed rape importance

Oilseed rape (Brassica napus L.) is a very important crop with lots of nutritional, economical, ecological, and biological benefits [[1], [2], [3], [4]]; thus, not only its extension of cultivation area but also its genetic improvement is an essential issue worldwide. As it profits, its different quantitative and qualitative attributes such as grain and oil yield as well as its components have been recuperated via diallel breeding approaches and biplot data analysis [[4], [5], [6], [7], [8]].

Diallel and biplot approaches: its pros and cons

The importance of plant breeding programs via diallel mating designs in improvement of new cultivars; consequently, its benefits to the welfare of humankind cannot be overemphasized. In a diallel approach, genotype evaluation to identify superior parents and their F1 offspring is an important section of the procedure. Diallel crosses which are a practical techniques to deduce the genetic architecture, have been applied to identify genetic parameters of traits in various genotypes and to recognize exceptional high-yielding parents for new hybrid/cultivar progression. Intelligence on genes behaviors of quantitative and qualitative traits are decisive in eliminating undesirable parents according to their performance in the early generation to the improvement of effective crosses [9] via various diallel approaches [10,11]. In addition, it is important to partition whole cross variations into individual parents and specific crosses as the term of general combining ability (GCA) and the specific combining ability (SCA) variances, respectively. As it is clear, the SCA is cross specific and the conventional procedures could not provide knowledge regarding parents' SCA. Contrarily, the biplot analysis of diallel data provides excellent and deeper comprehension information of parents such as GCA, SCA, heterotic groups, the best crosses, etc. [12].

Research gap

In a diallel procedure to tackle the selection barriers of superior genotypes it is essential to evaluate parents and their offspring by multiple combined traits. That is, a genotype is ideal when it has higher levels for several other desirable target traits which collaborated favorably, so improvement in an individual character might lead to increased levels of the others. Even though, different methods are introduced to deal with this problem [13]; but, they were highly subjective which can lead to different selections, dramatically. A diallel by yield × traits (DYT) biplot analysis was a kind of biplot procedure that was used to deal with independent culling and index selection obstacles [14,15] as well as to deal with the trouble of parents and offspring estimation based on multiple combined traits in breeding programs. Underlying DYT graphical and numerical exploring of genetic architecture is that superior parents and their F1 offspring should be appraised according to their levels in combining yield and its related genetic parameters as important traits with all other target characters which just are important if they combined with higher yield via Hayman and biplot approach. A DYT graphical and numerical approach via Hayman diallel and biplot methods is a powerful procedure to deal with the trouble of genotype assessment based on multiple combined traits [13]. In addition, it is essential to notice that individual parents and specific crosses with higher GCA and SCA always are not useful for hybrid or cultivar development because they may not meet desirable dominant or recessive genes for a specific trait. The challenge arises from the fact that the conventional methods individually are not able to estimate both GCA and SCA as well as different genetic components including D, H1, H2, KD/KR, etc., simultaneously.

Research objective

In the current study, an exclusive integrated graphical and numerical deciphering of diallel and biplot analysis based on multiple yield × traits datasets was illustrated for the first time with the novelty of the genotype selection with higher GCA, SCA along with the most suitable genetic parameters in a desirable direction to exploiting in Brassica napus L. breeding programs.

Eleven genotypes were sown in 10 rows 10 m long with inter-row 30 cm spacing and 5 cm intra-row in 3 sowing dates starting from 10th October 2018–2019 in Golestan Agricultural and Natural Resources Research and Education Center, Iran (Supplementary Table S1). The genotypes were crossed in a full diallel fashion. Sequentially, to the evaluation of genetic parameters of different multi-traits, all possible genotypes (including parents, their 55 F1's, and 55 reciprocal) were sown in 4 rows with 4 m long with inter-row 30 cm spacing and 5 cm intra-row (area of each plot was 4.80 cm2 with 67 plant/m2) in 10th October based on split randomized complete block design replicated thrice during 2019–2020. The mineral nutrient such as phosphate as well as potassium was not applied because of their sufficient availability in soil, but urea as a nitrogen source (200 kg ha−1) was applied based on soil test recommendation. It is noteworthy that one-third of urea was applied simultaneously with planting as base fertilizer, another at the beginning of the stem elongation (BBCH: 32), and the beginning of the green flower stages (BBCH: 52) [16]. Generally, the agronomical practices including sowing methods, weeds, and pests control, thinning, irrigation, nutrition, weed management, etc. were done uniformly for whole plots as recommended by Oghan [17,18].

Recorded data

In each plot, before flowering time (BBCH: 32) ten individual plants were tagged randomly from the two middle rows and harvested at the maturity stage (BBCH: 99) [16] to record traits including the number of days to flowering (FL) (BBCH: 65), number of days to physiological maturity (PM)(BBCH: 85), Plant height (cm) (PH), number of pods per plant (PP), pod length (cm) (PL), thousand-grain weight (g.)(TGW) and grain yield (tons. ha−1) (GY). Moreover, the whole plot was harvested to obtain grain yield per plot.

Statistical analysis

Combined graphical and numerical analysis of diallel by yield × traits dataset was performed to conceptualize the association between traits and their profiling as well as underlying genetic parameters as described by Hayman [11,19] and Yan and Frégeau-Reid [13] founded upon the assumptions of diploid segregation, homozygous parents, absence of epistasis, linkage, and multiple allelism in which confirmed by the t2 test [20]. The applied genetic materials were homozygous and diverse in their characters, in which treated with basic assumptions of the Hayman procedure along with maternal effects were supposed to be absent. The statistics “t2” and “b" (regression of Wr on Vr) were applied to the verification of the presumed hypothesis. The t2 statistics (with n-2 degrees of freedom) calculated as below and it results testing against with 4 and n-2 degrees of freedom by F test [11,21]:

An insignificant “t2” estimates depict the consistency of the Wr-Vr, thus confirming the validity of the above-mentioned hypostasis, especially the adequacy of the additive dominance model [22]. In this experiment, individual values for each diallel by yield × traits (DYT) combination was applied as illustrated by Yan and Frégeau-reid [13]. The data for yield × traits combination were achieved by dividing the grain yield value by the number of days to flowering, the number of days to physiological maturity, and plant height (e.g., grain yield × plant height) as well as multiplying the grain yield with pod per plant, pod length, and thousand-grain yield (e.g., grain yield × pod per plant) [13]. The uniformity of DYT residual variances was checked before further analysis via Bartlett's test [23]; sequentially to detect the meaningful differences among genotypes, the DYT data were expressed as the least significant difference (LSD 0.05) and exposed to the analysis of variance based on Steel and colleagues method [24] by SAS ver. 9.4 [25]. Furthermore, genetic parameters were analyzed using AGR (ver. 5) [26] based on the method described by Hayman [11,20]. Biplot analysis of DYT data was established by the GGEbiplot software (ver. 6.3) [8] to reveal genetic parameters including GCA, SCA, best crosses, heterotic groups, etc. [12,27].

Numerical analysis

Moreover, the presence of specific genes effects and asymmetry of gene distribution was demonstrated by the significance “b2” and “b3” components (P ≤ 0.01), respectively. [33] published comparable findings on the oil content of Brassica napus L. Alemayehu and Becker [34], Wang et al. [35], Chaudhari et al. [36], Chaudhari et al. [32] and Chaudhari and Patel [28] were observed similar results.

Test for model's assumptions adequacy

Because of the monogenetic additive behavior of Hayman's graphical model, it is essential to examine the adequacy of assumptions to discern epistasis effects. To prove the satisfactory of the additive and dominant assumption of the model, there were two types of tests including t2 statistics (test of homogeneity of Wr-Vr variance) and regression coefficient analysis [SE (b)] (test of b-0 and b-1) were done (Table 3) [21,37]. Results indicated that t2 statistics were insignificant for all evaluated DYT, justifying the homogeneity of the Vr-Wr graph and confirming the legitimacy of the assumption as well as completion of basic criteria of the Hayman model such as diploid segregation, homozygous parents, absence of epistasis, linkage, and multiple allelism. Additional confirmation of the assumptions' legitimacy regarding the epistatic effects was made by the Wr-Vr graph, in which whenever the parental arrays fall on the unity line means significant deviation b from zero (b-0) but not from unite (b-1), implying the absence of epistatic effects. The b-0 was highly significant (P ≤ 0.01) and b-1 was insignificant for all evaluated DYT, indicating the validity of the Vr-Wr graph hypothesis concerning the absence of epistasis or inter-allelic interactions for investigated traits. In Hayman's' graphical approaches, the Vr-Wr graphs intrigue for DYT which exhibits insignificant t2, significant b-0, and non-significant b-1 including GY/FL, GY/PM, GY/PH, GY × PL, GY × PP, and GY × TGW to achieves valid predictions through graphs. In study of Chaudari et al. [21] 3 out of 12 traits were significant which showing the failure of adequacy of certain hayman's assumptions.

Genetic components

Estimation of genetic parameters showed that additive genetic variance (D) was highly significant (P ≤ 0.01) for evaluated DYT, revealing the inheritance of the traits was controlled by the additive gene actions. Chaudhari et al. [32] reported significant additive (D) and dominance (H1 and H2) for traits including days to 50 % heading, days to maturity, number of grain per main spike, grain yield per main spike, thousand grain weight in wheat, which indicating that the characteristics were governed by both additive and dominant gene actions in some environments. In another study of Chaudhari et al. [32] the significance of GCA and SCA variance revealed the importance of additive and non-additive gene effects in inheritance of grain yield per plant, Zn content in normal sowing date. Also, significance of SCA variance for all traits in both normal and late sowing date suggested the importance of just non-additive gene effects for inheritance of these characters. The H1 genetic constituent which measures the dominance variation was found significant (P ≤ 0.01) for all DYT, illustrating that dominance gene actions affect the inheritance of evaluated traits. The higher ratio of dominant genes action evaluated for most of the DYT traits are in consistent with [36,38].

Similarly, H2 was significant for all DYT, depicting asymmetrical distribution of positive and negative dominant-recessive gene effects. The F component that reveals the relative frequencies of dominant and recessive alleles in the parents were significant (P ≤ 0.01) positively, and greater than zero just for traits GY/PH and GY × PP, which expressing preponderance of dominant alleles over recessive genes in parents. These results in consistent with [36,39].The average degree of dominance (√H1/D) was above unity for all evaluated DYT, explaining the existence of over-dominance genes effects for the assessed parameters. The genes will be equally distributed when H2/4H1 value was equal to 0.25 and in this study for evaluated diallel by yield × traits, its value was below 0.25, depicting the imbalanced distribution of positive and negative alleles. The value of KD/KR which is represented as ((4DH1)1/2+F/(4DH1)1/2-F)), was more than unity for all characters depicting the presence of a higher proportion of dominance than the recessive gene in parents. The dominant genes were not computed for the traits GY/PM, GY × PL, GY × PP, and GY × TGW because of insignificant h2 values. Meanwhile, the positive and significant h2 for the traits GY/FL and GY/PH, explains its role by the mean of complete dominance gene action over loci in oilseed rape. The ratio of h2/H2 was less than unity showing at least one group of genes exhibiting dominance gene action existed for assessed DYT.

Higher narrow sense heritability (h2n>50 %) for evaluated traits except for GY × PP, illustrating a major role of additive gene action in DYT inheritance, and therefore selection should be efficient for improving these traits in oilseed rape. Broad sense heritability was higher than narrow sense heritability, explaining the greater role of dominance gene action (Table 4). Wang et al. [35] reported a lower narrow sense heritability in B. napus for oil content, while Rao and Gulati [40] observed contrasting results of high to medium narrow sense heritability in B. Juncea L. for oil content. These results are in agreement of [36]. The environmental component (E) was estimated positively for the evaluated DYT, illustrating a clear impact of environmental conditions in the inheritance of the assessed DYT (Table 4). Environmental conditions especially temperature and precipitation0 is the most prominent factor influencing yield attributes. Comprehending the reciprocal action between environment and genotypes is a prerequisite for the breeding of rapeseed [21,32,41].

The graphical analysis revealed that in GY/FI (Fig. 1), GY/PM (Fig. 2) and GY/PH (Fig. 3) graphs, parent P1 was placed farthest from the origin and showed more proportion of recessive genes (P<%25), meanwhile parents P2, P3, P10, and P11 were placed in the closest location to the origin of the regression line as they had the highest proportion of dominance gene (P>%75) [36,39].The occurrence of parents away from the origin depicted higher effects of genotype by environmental interactions. Environmental conditions and revealing its interaction with genotypes and diverse field management is very important factor influencing yield and its components [36].

Since p1 settled in the recessive sector (P<%25) on the Wr-Vr graph (Fig. 7) as well as it had higher GCA and SCA effects on ATC view of biplot (Fig. 8), its crosses with P2 (with lower GCA and moderate SCA) and P3 (with lower GCA and highest SCA) which lodged in dominant (P>%75) sector on Wr-Wr graph (Fig. 7) might be more profitable rather than its cross with testers with more additive (%75<P>%50) (P8) or more recessive (%50<P>%25) (P5) genes with lower GCA and SCA effects. Also, there were two heterotic groups e.g., P3 × P10 and P3 × P11 were identified in the sector p3. Although, P3 is located in the dominant sector and P10 and P11 settled in the additive sector on the Wr-Vr graph, the hybrids with long pod may not be achieved because of their low GCA and epistatic effects. The sector p9 (with testers P1, P4, P6, and P9) was not heterotic because tester P9 (with the highest GCA and least SCA) settled in this sector; consequently, the combination P9 × P9 would be the most suitable among every cross-engaging P6. Parent p9 was not suitable for long pod cultivar development because it had higher GCA and the most recessive genes. It seems that the rapeseed with a longer pod could not be achieved by the current genetic materials.

Besides, regarding trait GY × PP, the parent P1 had the higher ratio of recessive genes (P<%25), while parents P2, P6, P7, P8, P10 and P11 had the higher ratio of dominance gene (P>%75). Parents P11 was closer and below the origin point, therefor it was under the influence of the epistasis gene effects (Fig. 9). Conclusion

The variance and diallel analysis depicted that genotypes and a, b, c as well as d components were highly significant for all evaluated DYTs. In addition, based on insignificant t2, a significant deviation of b-0, and an insignificant deviation of b-1, the graphical analysis was performed for all evaluated DYT. In the Wr-Vr graph, Almost for all DYT the regression line crossed the Wr axis on the above origin points, which illustrating partial dominance gene action through graphical analysis, except GY × PP which is regulated by over dominance gene action. Biplot analysis depicted that both GCA and SCA effects of parents were highly significant, suggesting the influence of non-additive and additive genes actions in DYT inheritance thus to produce hybrids or a base population with desirable traits it is important to cross heterotic parents with both higher dominant/over-dominant genes as well as higher GCA and SCA effects. Moreover, desirable hybrids should be produced by parents i.e., P3 × P10, P9 × P4, and P9 × P6 which were heterotic crosses as well as they accompanied by higher GCA and SCA as well as higher dominant/additive genes for traits including GY/FL, GY/PM, GY/PH, and GY/PP. For hybrid cultivar production, the heterotic parents which exhibited both higher GCA and SCA as well as more dominant genes should be chosen including (P9) × (P4, P6, P10) for GY × PP; (P9) × (P7), (P6) × (P11) and (P7) × (P10) for GY × TGW as well as (P10 × P3) for GY/FL, GY/PM and GY/PH.

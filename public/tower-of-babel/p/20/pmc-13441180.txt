Differential miRNA profiles in fluoride-exposed rBMSCs-EVs and the influence of miR-320-3p transfer on osteogenic differentiation
Authors: Mingyue Yin, Jing Zhang, Na Wang, Peng Luo, Xueli Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLong-term excessive fluoride ingestion may lead to skeletal fluorosis, a disease characterized by unclear pathogenesis and lack of effective cure. Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles (BMSCs-EVs) exert a crucial regulatory effect on bone remodeling and have broad clinical application prospects. Whether BMSCs-EVs can participate in the occurrence and development of skeletal fluorosis by transferring miRNAs is a scientific question worth exploring. In this study, miRNA sequencing was used to screen for differentially expressed miRNAs in fluoride-exposed rBMSCs-EVs (F-EVs). The top 15 differentially expressed miRNAs were subjected to target gene prediction, and GO and KEGG analyses as well as PPI network construction were performed. On this basis, miR-320-3p was selected for in-depth study. First, our data revealed that miR-320-3p was upregulated in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts and was involved in the enhancement of fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation. Subsequently, a tracing experiment confirmed that rBMSCs-EVs and their miR-320-3p could be successfully taken up by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts. Then, elevated levels of miR-320-3p in F-EVs could further promote the enhancement of fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation, while EVs with inhibited miR-320-3p exerted a significant inhibitory effect. Mechanism studies revealed that under fluoride exposure, rBMSCs-EVs could regulate Igf1r/ERK1/2/MAPK by transferring miR-320-3p, thereby affecting osteogenic differentiation. This study is the first to elucidate the pathogenesis of skeletal fluorosis from the rBMSCs-EVs miRNAs and provides new ideas for the clinical management of skeletal fluorosis.Keywords: rBMSCs-EVs, miR-320-3p, skeletal fluorosis, osteoblasts, Igf1r/ERK1/2/MAPK

Skeletal fluorosis lacks clear pathogenesis and targeted therapies. Osteoblast dysfunction is central to skeletal fluorosis progression, and rBMSCs-EVs play vital roles in bone homeostasis. This study identifies miR-320-3p as a key differentially expressed miRNA in F-EVs. We verified that F-EVs deliver miR-320-3p into osteoblasts to enhance fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation via the Igf1r/ERK1/2/MAPK. Our findings first reveal the regulatory mechanism of rBMSCs-EVs-miRNAs in skeletal fluorosis, offering novel molecular insights and potential therapeutic targets for this bone disorder.

Introduction

Fluorine is ubiquitous in nature and predominantly present as fluoride compounds. Chronic excessive fluoride intake can lead to fluorosis, a severe global public health concern. Over 260 million individuals are exposed to drinking water with excessive fluoride levels,1 and approximately 70 million people suffer from fluorosis.2 Skeletal fluorosis is the most severe manifestation of fluorosis, which leads to bone pain, stiffness, bone deformities, and limited mobility.3 Its pathogenesis is unclear, and it causes severe physical discomfort and substantial financial strain on affected individuals and their relatives. Thus, it is of paramount significance to clarify the pathogenesis of skeletal fluorosis and explore effective therapeutic strategies. The typical characteristics of skeletal fluorosis are active bone formation and bone turnover.3 Fluoride at the micromolar level can also facilitate osteoblast differentiation and mineralization.3,4 And the early abnormal differentiation of osteoblasts is one of the key steps in the pathogenesis of skeletal fluorosis.

miRNAs belong to a class of endogenous noncoding RNAs with 18 to 25 nucleotides. They can function in post-transcriptional regulation by modulating the expression of target mRNAs, thereby affecting cell proliferation, invasion, and differentiation.5,6 In 2024, breakthrough studies on miRNAs were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, underscoring their core regulatory potential in controlling gene expression as well as great prospects for disease research and therapeutic development. The existing evidence indicates that changes in miRNA expression exert marked effects on the occurrence and progression of fluorosis.7–10 Our prior investigations in fluorosis population and human primary osteoblasts have demonstrated that fluoride exposure accelerates the proliferation and activation of human osteoblasts via triggering the aberrant expression of multiple miRNAs.11–14

miRNAs not only function within cells but can also be transmitted through extracellular vesicles (EVs). EVs is a small vesicle with membrane structure released from cells, rich in nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, and exert vital functions in diverse biological processes.15 As a new intercellular communication mode, EVs can transfer miRNAs from donor cells to recipient cells, thereby altering gene expression profiles and cell function in both health and disease.16 The skeletal system maintains its stability through communication between different bone cells. EVs-miRNA, as an important medium for intercellular communication, mediates information communication among various cells within the skeletal system.17

BMSCs are pluripotent stem cells originating from the mesoderm, which reside in the bone marrow and possess the capabilities of multidirectional differentiation and self-renewal as well as paracrine features. BMSCs have been applied in animal experiments and preclinical studies related to bone diseases such as fracture repair, bone tissue engineering, and osteoporosis.18 For instance, in treating knee joint osteoarthritis, the use of BMSCs has been proven safe and feasible through multiple phase I and phase II clinical trials.19–21 Our previous studies also found that autologous BMSCs injection by two ways of joint cavity or ear border vein could effectively repair the damaged bone tissue of rabbits with skeletal fluorosis,22,23 but the specific mechanism could not be further explored. Accumulating evidence indicates that the role of mesenchymal stem cells in wound healing and tissue regeneration is primarily related to their paracrine capabilities rather than their differentiation potential. The paracrine effects of mesenchymal stem cells are mainly mediated through their EVs.24,25

Therefore, we propose the scientific hypothesis that rBMSCs-EVs could influence the onset and progression of skeletal fluorosis by transferring miR-320-3p to modulate osteogenic differentiation. To verify this hypothesis, this study utilized miRNA sequencing technology to screen for differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs and conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses as well as PPI network interaction studies on these differentially expressed miRNAs. Subsequently, the upregulated miR-320-3p in F-EVs was selected for in-depth research. Functional studies revealed that rBMSCs-EVs transferring miR-320-3p affect osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure by targeting IGF1R/ERK1/2/MAPK. This study aims to yield fresh evidence for elucidating the pathogenesis of fluorosis and the clinical management of fluorosis.

Methods

Extraction and culture of rBMSCs

Following our group’s established protocol, rBMSCs were isolated as follows:26 Bone marrow was isolated from the femurs and tibias of 4- to 7-week-old SD rats by centrifugation, followed by lysis with red blood cell lysis buffer to obtain rBMSCs. The rBMSCs were cultured with α-MEM (Gibco, China) complete medium containing 10% serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. The osteogenic differentiation capacity of rBMSCs was verified by osteogenic induction, combined with alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining. Adipogenic differentiation potential was assessed via adipogenic induction and Oil Red O staining. Flow cytometry was applied to detect the surface markers of rBMSCs.

Isolation and identification of rBMSCs-EVs

After rBMSCs were incubated in EV-depleted medium for 48 h, the culture supernatant was gathered. Subsequently, sequential centrifugation was carried out at 4 °C: 300 g for 10 min, 2000 g for 10 min, and 10 000 g for 60 min to eliminate residual cells and cellular debris. Then the cell supernatant was mixed with the EVs extraction reagent (YEASEN, China) in a 4:1 ratio and left to stand overnight. After that, the mixed solution was centrifuged at 10 000 g for 60 min under 4 °C, and the precipitate was retained. It was resuspended in PBS and stored at −80 °C. The morphological features and structure of rBMSCs-EVs were characterized using transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The particle size and concentration of rBMSCs-EVs were detected by nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA). The specific markers of rBMSCs-EVs were identified by Western blot. On this basis, after cultivating rBMSCs in EVs-free medium containing 300 μmol/L sodium fluoride (NaF) for 48 h, the cell supernatant was collected and F-EVs were extracted for subsequent research.

Regarding the dosage of NaF, literature reports indicate that 50-500 μmol/L of NaF can promote the differentiation of osteoblasts.27 Our research group’s previous studies have also found that 300 μmol/L of NaF can promote the differentiation of osteoblasts.25 Therefore, this study selected 300 μmol/L as the exposure dose of NaF.

Screening of differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs and bioinformatics analysis

Total RNA was harvested from rBMSCs-EVs with or without fluoride exposure using the miRNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen, Germany), followed by miRNA sequencing (PANOMIX Biomedical, China). Three F-EVs samples and three control EVs samples were separately selected for miRNA sequencing. Differentially expressed miRNAs were screened out with the criteria of P < .05 and |log2FoldChange| ≥ 1. The top 15 differentially expressed miRNAs were identified based on their basemean values. And their target genes were predicted using three databases: miRDB (https://mirdb.org/), miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/), and microT-CDS (http://www.microrna.gr/microT-CDS). Genes predicted simultaneously by all three databases were identified as candidate target genes of differentially expressed miRNAs.

To further elucidate the biological roles and metabolic pathways associated with these target genes, Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were conducted using the OmicShare online platform (https://www.omicshare.com). Cytoscape 3.10.2 software was utilized for visualizing the above analytical outcomes, which helps systematically clarify the biological functions of target genes. Subsequently, candidate target genes were imported into the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) to conduct protein–protein interaction (PPI) analysis.

Selection of target miRNA

Based on the results of the above miRNA sequencing, a target miRNA was selected from the differentially expressed miRNAs. Considering the possibility of potential applications, the selection criteria were set as follows: (1) Differentially expressed in the miRNA-seq results (P < .05 and |log2FoldChange| ≥ 1) and with a basemean value >100. (2) Expressed in human rBMSCs-EVs. (3) Associated with bone-metabolic diseases. Based on the above screening criteria, three miRNAs met the requirements, namely miR-221-3p, miR-122-5p, and miR-320-3p. These three miRNAs were all explored, and we only reported miR-320-3p research results in this paper. And the miR-320-3p is highly expressed in F-EVs.

Osteogenic differentiation and staining

After osteogenic precursor cells (also rBMSCs) were seeded for 24 h, the medium with osteogenic induction medium (complete medium containing 10 mmol/L β-glycerophosphate, 50 μg/mL L-ascorbic acid, and 100 nmol/L dexamethasone) was replaced and refreshed every 2-3 days. Following 7 days of osteogenic induction, ALP staining was conducted following the official protocol to observe intracellular ALP particles. At 14 days of differentiation, ARS staining was conducted following the protocol to visualize osteogenic mineralization nodules. Subsequently, after elution with a 10% hexadecylpyridinium chloride solution, ARS staining was performed for semi-quantitative analysis.

Transfection of miR-320-3p inhibitor into osteoblasts or rBMSCs-EVs

miR-320-3p inhibitor or its inhibitor negative control (NC) was transfected into osteoblasts with riboFECT CP transfection reagent (RiboBio, China), and transfected into rBMSCs-EVs by electroporation. The oligo sequences were listed in Table S1. Key operations are summarized as follows: miR-320-3p inhibitor or inhibitor NC was added to rBMSC-EVs and mixed gently, then the mixture was transferred into an electroporation cuvette, which was inserted into the chamber of a NEPA gene-pulser (Japan). Electroporation was performed with two poring pulses subsequently combined with five transfer pulses. After electroporation, the EVs were kept on ice for 30 min to allow membrane recovery, after which they were ultracentrifuged (Beckman, Germany) at 110 000 g for 70 min at 4 °C to eliminate redundant transfection reagent. The pellet was resuspended in PBS and stored at −80 °C. EVs electroporated with miR-320-3p inhibitor were labeled as miR-320-3pin-EVs.

The uptake of rBMSCs-EVs and the miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts

rBMSCs-EVs were labeled with PKH67 green fluorescent dye (Solarbio, China). Then the labeled rBMSCs-EVs were harvested by ultracentrifugation at 110 000 g for 70 min under 4 °C to remove excess dye and resuspended in PBS. The miR-320-3p mimic or inhibitor labeled with CY3 red fluorescence was electroporated into rBMSCs-EVs. These labeled EVs were respectively added to fluoride-exposed osteoblasts that had been induced to differentiate for co-incubation. The cells’ nuclei were labeled with DAPI, and the cells were prepared into cell slides. The slides were then examined using a laser confocal microscope (Olympus, Japan) to visualize the uptake of rBMSCs-EVs or miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs by the fluoride-exposed osteoblasts.

Prediction of target genes and signaling pathways

miRWalk, miRDB, and MicroT-CDS were applied to forecast the target genes regulated by miR-320-3p, and the overlapping genes identified by all three databases were selected. These candidate genes were then subjected to PPI analysis using the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/), where we generated an interaction network and prioritized hub genes located at the network core. By integrating literature evidence on bone-metabolism disorders, we identified insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (Igf1r) as a crucial downstream target of miR-320-3p.

Subsequently, gene function annotation and enrichment analysis of signaling pathways were completed with support from DAVID. Combining these results with KEGG pathway analysis of the top 15 differentially expressed miRNAs screened by sequencing in F-EVs, we ultimately determined the ERK1/2/MAPK signaling pathways regulated by miR-320-3p.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

293T human embryonic kidney cells, serving as tool cells for the dual-luciferase reporter experiment, were sourced from the Cell Bank of Shanghai. The potential target binding regions between miR-320-3p and the 3'UTR of Igf1r were predicted using bioinformatics software. The plasmids Igf1r (NM_001414181)-WT-psiCHECK2 (wild-type) and Igf1r (NM_001414181)-MUT-psiCHECK2 (mutant) were constructed by Uptbio Co., Ltd. (Hunan, China). After seeding 293 T cells, the transfection procedure was conducted at a confluence of 60% to 70%. The wild-type and mutant Igf1r plasmids were co-transfected with miR-320-3p mimic or mimic NC into 293 T cells. After 36 h of transfection, the cells were lysed, and the cell lysates were collected for subsequent luciferase assay. Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were quantified using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay system (Promega) on a GloMax 96 detection instrument (Promega).

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from rBMSCs-EVs and osteoblasts with TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA) for subsequent RT-qPCR detection of miRNA and mRNA expression. For miR-320-3p quantification in rBMSCs-EVs and osteoblasts, the isolated total RNA was subjected to a tailing reaction and reverse transcription to synthesize cDNA with the miDETECT A Track miRNA RT-qPCR Starter Kit (RiboBio, China), followed by PCR amplification on the CFX96 Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad, USA). The relative miR-320-3p expression was analyzed via the 2−ΔΔCT method, with U6 serving as the internal reference for osteoblasts and cel-miR-39 added as the external reference for rBMSCs-EVs during RNA extraction. For determining Igf1r mRNA expression in osteoblasts, genomic DNA elimination was performed on extracted total RNA before cDNA synthesis using PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix (TaKaRa, Japan), and RT-qPCR was then conducted with TB Green® Fast qPCR Mix (TaKaRa, Japan) on the same Bio-Rad CFX96 platform. Relative mRNA expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCT method with Gapdh as the internal reference, all primers were synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (China), and relevant primer sequences were presented in Table S2.

Western blot

Total proteins from rBMSCs-EVs or osteoblasts were isolated with RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Samples were fractionated via 10% or 12% SDS-PAGE and blotted onto PVDF filters (Millipore, USA). Following the blockade of nonspecific binding sites using a fast blocking buffer (Epizyme, China), the filters were cultured with primary antibodies at 4 °C for overnight incubation. Subsequently, the filters were treated with HRP-labeled secondary antibodies at ambient temperature for 1 h. The primary and secondary antibodies and their dilution ratios were shown in Supplementary Table S3. Protein band signals were developed using ECL luminescent substrate (Millipore, USA), and the resulting immunoblot images were acquired via the ChemiDoc™ ARS+ imaging platform (Bio-Rad, USA). The gray intensity of target protein bands was semi-quantified using Image J software. β-actin or GAPDH was used as the internal reference for data normalization.

Statistical analysis

Data results were shown in the form of mean ± SD. All figures and statistical evaluation were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.2. All assays were repeated three times on separate occasions, ensuring stable and reproducible research data. When the data conforms to a normal distribution and the variances were homogeneous, differences between the two groups were evaluated via Student’s t-test. For multigroup comparison, ANOVA was performed, and the LSD test was utilized for subsequent pairwise comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < .05.

Results

Characterization of rBMSCs-EVs

rBMSCs were efficiently harvested from the femoral and tibial bone marrow of SD rats. After 7 days of osteogenic induction differentiation, blue alkaline phosphatase granules were observed by ALP staining (Figure S1A). After 14 days of osteogenic induction differentiation, obvious red osteogenic mineralized nodules were visible via ARS staining (Figure S1B), confirming that they could differentiate into osteoblasts. Following 14 days of adipogenic induction differentiation, intracellular lipid droplets were stained red and clearly observed via Oil Red O staining (Figure S1C). Flow cytometry analysis of surface markers showed negative expression of CD11 and CD45 and positive expression of CD44 and CD90 (Figure S1D). The above results confirmed that the isolated cells were rBMSCs.

rBMSCs-EVs were successfully isolated from the culture supernatant of rBMSCs. TEM revealed that non-fluoride-exposed rBMSCs-EVs (EVs) exhibited typical round, double-membrane structures (Figure 1A). NTA revealed an average particle size of 143.2 nm (Figure 1B). Western blot analysis validated the positive expression of EVs-specific markers CD81, HSP70, and TSG101, while the Calnexin was absent (Figure 1C). These findings collectively meet the established criteria for EVs. F-EVs also exhibited the typical morphological features of vesicles (Figure 1D), their average particle diameter was measured as 147.5 nm (Figure 1E). And the analytical outcomes of the Western blot also conformed to the characteristics of EVs (Figure 1F).

Screening of differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs

To assess the differential expression of miRNAs in rBMSCs-EVs after fluoride exposure, miRNA sequencing was performed on F-EVs and EVs. With the threshold set at P < .05 and |log2FoldChange|≥1, a sum of 27 differentially expressed miRNAs were screened out (Figure 2A). Among them, 21 miRNAs were upregulated, such as miR-221-3p, miR-122-5p, miR-423-5p, miR-320-3p, miR-185-5p, and miR-3473. The remaining 6 miRNAs showed decreased expression, including miR-150-5p, miR-126a-3p, miR-144-5p, miR-411-5p, miR-142-5p, and miR-126a-5p.

Prediction and enrichment analysis of target genes of differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs

Among the 27 differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs, the top 15 miRNAs were selected for target gene prediction and analysis. The prediction results from three online databases showed that miR-126a-3p and miR-423-3p were not predicted to have target genes in all three databases and thus were excluded. Ultimately, 13 miRNAs, including eight upregulated and five downregulated miRNAs, were determined as the subjects of study. Based on these 13 miRNAs, a sum of 261 upregulated target genes and 527 downregulated target genes were annotated. The regulatory network linking differentially expressed miRNAs and their downstream mRNA targets was constructed via Cytoscape v3.10.2, as illustrated in Figure 2B and C.

For an in-depth understanding of the functions of these target genes, GO enrichment analysis showed that in the biological process (BP) classification, they mainly participate in the regulation of cellular-metabolic processes, multicellular organismal processes, and negative regulation of biological processes (Figure 3A). In the cellular component (CC) classification, they are predominantly localized to intracellular anatomical structures and membrane-bound organelles(Figure 3B). In the molecular function (MF) classification, they are mainly enriched in molecular functions such as protein binding and kinase activity (Figure 3C). Furthermore, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis demonstrated that these target genes participate in modulating multiple crucial signaling cascades. Representative pathways mainly cover the MAPK and PI3K-Akt signaling axes, along with the pathway of transcriptional dysregulation in cancer (Figure 3D).

Construction of PPI network and screening of key genes

To clarify the interaction relationships between target genes of differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs, a PPI network was generated via STRING and visualized with the Cytoscape software (Figure 3E). Proteins without any interactions were removed, yielding a final network comprising 194 target genes and 302 nonredundant edges. In the PPI network diagram, gene nodes such as hypoxia-inducible factor (Hif1a), insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (Igf1r), FBJ osteosarcoma oncogene (FOS), and chromatin remodeling factor (Rbbp7) were depicted with deeper colors and larger areas. This indicates that these nodes have a higher degree of connectivity, meaning they interact with more proteins in the PPI network. They are likely to be key nodes or “hubs” in the network, playing important roles in biological processes.

Subsequently, the CytoHubba plugin integrated in Cytoscape was employed to detect core hub genes through the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm. The top 15 key genes thus identified included the autophagy-related (Atg7), RB1-inducible coiled-coil protein 1 (Rb1cc1), autophagy-related 16-like 1(Atg16l1), autophagy initiation kinase (Ulk1), Rbbp7, selective autophagy adaptor (Sqstm1), Cytohubba include the autophagy-related gene (Ambra1), histone methyltransferase (Ezh1), Igf1r, polycomb repressive complex member (Phc2), polycomb repressive complex member (Phc1), and Hif1a, among others (Figure 3F). Notably, genes such as Igf1r, Sqstm1, Hif1a, Ulk1, Rb1cc1, and Ambra1 play important roles in skeletal development, bone-metabolism, and bone-related diseases.28–34

Impact of miR-320-3p on fluoride-exposed osteoblasts differentiation

Based on the screening criteria described in the methods, we identified three miRNAs: miR-221-3p, miR-122-5p, and miR-320-3p. These three miRNAs were all characterized, and we only reported miR-320-3p research results in this paper. First, to assess the influence of fluoride exposure on the expression of miR-320-3p in osteoblasts, osteoblast precursor cells were treated with 300 μmol/L NaF and induced to undergo osteogenic differentiation for 7 days. RT-qPCR data demonstrated that miR-320-3p was upregulated in the fluoride-exposed group relative to the control group (Figure 4A), suggesting that fluoride exposure could elevate miR-320-3p expression in osteoblasts. Subsequently, osteoblast precursor cells exposed to fluoride were transfected with miR-320-3p inhibitor or inhibitor NC, followed by 7 days of osteogenic differentiation induction. The RT-qPCR detection results revealed that, relative to the fluoride-exposed group, the expression of miR-320-3p in the inhibitor group was downregulated, while there was no change in the inhibitor NC group (Figure 4B).

To explore the regulatory role of miR-320-3p on fluoride-mediated osteogenic differentiation, fluoride-exposed osteoblast precursor cells were transfected with miR-320-3p inhibitor, followed by osteogenic differentiation induction for 7 days. A Western blot was carried out to measure the expression of osteogenic differentiation markers osteopontin (OPN) and runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), and ALP staining was performed. Additionally, ARS staining was carried out after 14 days of differentiation. The results demonstrated that, relative to the control group, the expression of OPN and RUNX2 was enhanced in the NaF group and the inhibitor NC group, with more alkaline phosphatase granules visible in the ALP staining and more osteogenic mineralization nodules in the ARS staining. In contrast, relative to the NaF group, the expression of OPN and RUNX2 was notably downregulated in the miR-320-3p inhibitor group, with fewer alkaline phosphatase granules and osteogenic mineralization nodules observed (Figure 4C-F). Such outcomes suggest that fluoride exposure upregulates the expression of miR-320-3p in osteoblasts, thereby enhancing osteogenic differentiation.

Uptake of rBMSCs-EVs and miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts

To determine whether rBMSCs-EVs can be taken up by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts, PKH67 green fluorescence-labeled rBMSCs-EVs were added to fluoride-exposed osteoblasts to incubate. Blue nuclei could be observed by laser confocal microscopy. Green fluorescence distributed in the cytoplasm could also be observed, indicating that fluoride-exposed osteoblasts can take up rBMSCs-EVs (Figure 5A).

Similarly, to observe whether miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs could be taken up by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts, a CY3 red fluorescence-labeled miR-320-3p mimic or inhibitor was transfected into rBMSCs-EVs via electroporation. These EVs were then added to fluoride-exposed osteoblasts to incubate. Blue nuclei could be observed by laser confocal microscopy. Red fluorescence distributed in the cytoplasm could also be observed, indicating that miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs could be taken up by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts (Figure 5B). It also indicated that rBMSCs-EVs transferring miR-320-3p inhibitors can be taken up by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts (Figure 5C).

Impact of miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs on osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure

To validate the sequencing results of miR-320-3p in F-EVs, RT-qPCR was used to quantify the expression of miR-320-3p. The findings revealed that the expression of miR-320-3p in F-EVs was upregulated compared to the blank control group (Figure 6A), which was consistent with the sequencing results. Afterwards, miR-320-3p inhibitor or inhibitor NC were transfected into rBMSCs-EVs via electroporation. RT-qPCR detection revealed that, compared to electroporated blank EVs (Electro-EVs) and electroporated inhibitor NC EVs (Electro-NC-EVs), the expression of miR-320-3p in miR-320-3pin-EVs was significantly downregulated, while no significant variation in miR-320-3p expression existed between Electro-NC-EVs and Electro-EVs (Figure 6B). This indicates that electroporation can successfully transfect the miR-320-3p inhibitor into rBMSCs-EVs.

Subsequently, to explore the regulatory role of miR-320-3pin-EVs on the expression of miR-320-3p in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts, miR-320-3pin-EVs were added to fluoride-exposed osteoblast precursor cells and incubated for 7 days with induction of osteogenic differentiation. RT-qPCR detection revealed that miR-320-3pin-EVs could significantly downregulate the expression of miR-320-3p in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts (Figure 6C).

To further clarify that rBMSCs-EVs regulate osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure by transferring miR-320-3p, F-EVs and miR-320-3pin-EVs were added to fluoride-exposed osteoblast precursor cells and incubated with induction of osteogenic differentiation. Western blot combined with ALP and ARS staining results indicated that compared with Electro-EVs, F-EVs highly expressing miR-320-3p exerted a prominent promoting effect on osteogenic differentiation. In contrast, the miR-320-3pin-EVs group markedly inhibited the enhanced fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation (Figure 6D-G). This indicated that F-EVs enhanced fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation by transferring miR-320-3p.

miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs targets Igf1r to regulate ERK1/2/MAPK signaling, thereby affecting osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure

To clarify the mechanism by which F-EVs enhance fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation by transferring miR-320-3p, we predicted target genes regulated by miR-320-3p and identified 133 overlapping targets (Figure 7A). A PPI network constructed with STRING revealed that Itgb1, Rap1a, Igf1r, and Kitig occupy the center of the network (Figure 7B). Among these, Igf1r, a key molecule tightly linked to bone metabolism and skeletal diseases, was verified as a target of miR-320-3p. Moreover, Igf1r was also among the candidate targets of the top 15 dysregulated miRNAs screened from F-EVs. KEGG pathway enrichment of the top 15 miRNAs pinpointed the MAPK pathway as a core route. Igf1r, as a member of the receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) family, precisely regulates the MAPK pathway. Combined with relevant literature on bone metabolism or bone diseases, the extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2)/MAPK pathway was ultimately determined.

To confirm the regulatory interaction between miR-320-3p and Igf1r, a dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed. The complementary binding sites of miR-320-3p in the Igf1r 3'-UTR and the plasmid maps are shown in Figure 7C. Wild-type and mutant Igf1r reporter plasmids were co-transfected with miR-320-3p mimic or mimic NC into 293 T cells.

Luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that the luciferase activity was markedly decreased in the Igf1r WT + miR-320-3p mimic group when compared with the Igf1r WT + mimic NC group. By contrast, no statistically meaningful divergence in luminescence intensity was observed between the Igf1r MUT + mimic NC group and the Igf1r MUT + miR-320-3p mimic group (Figure 7D). The present outcomes confirm that miR-320-3p can directly bind to the 3'-UTR of Igf1r, indicating that Igf1r is a target gene of miR-320-3p.

To further clarify the modulatory effect of rBMSCs-EVs transferring miR-320-3p on the expression of Igf1r in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts, F-EVs and miR-320-3pin-EVs were used to treat fluoride-exposed osteoblast precursor cells and induce osteogenic differentiation for 7 days. RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses demonstrated that relative to the Electro-EVs, F-EVs downregulated Igf1r expression due to their high levels of miR-320-3p, while the miR-320-3pin-EVs group showed a significant upregulation of Igf1r expression (Figure 7E and F). These results suggest that in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts, rBMSCs-EVs negatively regulate the expression of the target gene Igf1r via miR-320-3p.

Subsequently, to clarify whether the downregulation of Igf1r expression by miR-320-3p delivered by rBMSCs-EVs under fluoride exposure affects the ERK1/2/MAPK signaling pathway, F-EVs and miR-320-3pin-EVs were added to fluoride-exposed osteoblastic precursor cells, followed by osteogenic induction. Assessment of key signaling proteins by Western blot uncovered that, compared with the Electro-EVs, the miR-320-3pin-EVs group exhibited markedly higher expression of p-ERK1/2, indicating activation of the ERK1/2/MAPK pathway (Figure 7G). These findings suggest that, under fluoride exposure, rBMSCs-EVs modulate the ERK1/2/MAPK signaling pathway through miR-320-3p-mediated targeting of Igf1r, thereby influencing osteogenic differentiation.

Discussion

Skeletal fluorosis is a chronic systemic skeletal disorder induced by long-term excessive fluoride uptake, representing a widespread public health concern worldwide. At present, the pathogenesis is not yet clear. miRNAs exert a vital influence on the occurrence and development of skeletal fluorosis, mainly affecting the balance of bone metabolism by regulating the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts and osteoclasts. BMSCs are multipotent adult stem cells originating from marrow that possess self-renewal capacity and can differentiate into osteoblasts, chondrocytes, adipocytes, and other cell types. In recent years, EVs released from BMSCs have demonstrated significant therapeutic potential in bone-metabolic diseases; however, their role in skeletal fluorosis has not yet been reported.

EVs are regarded as “signals” mediating intercellular or inter-organ communication, serving as crucial regulators in bone-metabolic disorders.35 Their cargo—nucleic acids, proteins, and other molecules—participates in maintaining skeletal homeostasis and exerts significant influence upon the differentiation and biological behaviors of both osteoblasts and osteoclasts.36 Hyperactive osteogenesis and accelerated bone turnover are hallmark features of advancing skeletal fluorosis. Our previous work also revealed that osteosclerotic fluorosis is characterized by enhanced osteogenic differentiation and increased osteogenic capacity.26 Therefore, the present study selected osteoblasts as the investigative target to explore whether rBMSC-EVs can modulate fluoride-exposed osteogenic differentiation via the transfer of miRNA.

First, 27 differentially expressed miRNAs in response to fluoride exposure were screened out by miRNA sequencing. Because miRNAs primarily exert their biological effects by regulating target-gene expression, the top 15 differentially expressed miRNAs were screened for downstream target gene prediction. This yielded 527 predicted downregulated targets and 261 predicted upregulated targets. GO functional enrichment of these target genes showed that fluoride exposure could reshape miRNA expression in rBMSCs-EVs, and such miRNAs may modulate genes relevant to cell metabolism and cell anatomical structure.

Further KEGG enrichment analysis on target genes revealed notable enrichment within the MAPK, transcriptional misregulation in cancer, and the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. The MAPK pathway is a crucial cellular signaling cascade that transmits extracellular stimuli to the nucleus via sequential phosphorylation events, thereby regulating gene expression and controlling diverse biological processes such as cell proliferation, differentiation, survival, and apoptosis.37 The transcriptional misregulation pathway in cancer is tightly linked to tumorigenesis and tumor progression. Likewise, the PI3K/Akt pathway exerts crucial functions in governing cell growth, viability, motility, and energy metabolism. Accumulating evidence has revealed that fluoride exposure exerts pronounced effects on osteoblast proliferation and differentiation through either the MAPK or PI3K/Akt signaling pathways.38,39

Building on the identification of differentially expressed miRNAs in F-EVs, we sought to determine whether rBMSC-EVs modulate osteogenic differentiation by delivering these differentially expressed miRNAs. In this study, we focused on miR-320-3p, which has been reported to regulate osteoblast differentiation,40 yet its role under fluoride exposure remains unexplored. We first confirmed that miR-320-3p was highly expressed in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts and enhanced osteogenic differentiation. Whether rBMSCs-EVs participate in the enhanced osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure by transferring miR-320-3p was a scientific question worthy of exploration.

To address this question, the results of miRNA sequencing in rBMSCs-EVs were verified. Specifically, fluoride exposure could induce high expression of miR-320-3p in rBMSCs-EVs. Subsequently, tracing experiments showed that both rBMSC-EVs and the miR-320-3p in rBMSC-EVs can be efficiently internalized by fluoride-exposed osteoblasts. Next, in fluoride-exposed osteoblasts, it was clarified that the F-EVs, due to their high expression of miR-320-3p, could further aggravate the osteogenic differentiation induced by fluoride, while miR-320-3pin-EVs significantly inhibited the enhancement of osteogenic differentiation induced by fluoride. It was confirmed that miR-320-3p in BMSCs-EVs exerts a pivotal effect on the fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation.

Recent studies indicate that miRNAs carried by BMSC-EVs may play key roles in osteogenic differentiation, bone healing, and regeneration.41 For example, vesicles secreted by myostatin-treated osteocytes can be internalized by MC3T3 osteoblasts, and down-regulation of miR-218 within these vesicles suppresses osteogenic differentiation.42 Consistently, the present study found that inhibiting miR-320-3p in rBMSC-EVs markedly attenuates fluoride-induced enhancement of osteogenic differentiation. These observations suggest that miR-320-3pin-EVs hold therapeutic promise for skeletal fluorosis.

Based on the aforementioned results, we subsequently explored the molecular mechanism through which miR-320-3p in rBMSC-EVs modulates osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure. Bioinformatic analyses predicted Igf1r as a target gene of miR-320-3p and implicated the ERK1/2/MAPK pathway. Dual-luciferase reporter assays verified the direct binding between miR-320-3p and the 3′UTR of Igf1r. And it was found that miR-320-3pin-EVs could upregulate the expression of the target gene Igf1r and activate the ERK1/2/MAPK pathway. Thus, it was confirmed that rBMSCs-EVs could affect the osteogenic differentiation under fluoride exposure by transferring miR-320-3p to regulate Igf1r/ERK1/2/MAPK. Insulin-like growth factors (IGFs) are crucial regulators of bone metabolism. Previous work has shown that IGF1R in osteoblasts is required for osteogenic differentiation during fracture repair,30 and EVs-derived miR-140-5p can influence hMSC osteogenesis via IGF1R.43 However, the involvement of Igf1r in fluoride exposure or skeletal fluorosis has not been reported. The ERK/MAPK pathway also exerts a pivotal effect on early skeletal mineralization and osteoblast differentiation.44 Among the MAPK family, which includes ERK1/2, c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), p38 MAPK, and ERK5, ERK1/2 represents a key subfamily.36 Relevant literature further indicates that fluoride exposure can inhibit the ERK1/2/MAPK signaling pathway.45,46

Taken together, this study demonstrated that fluoride exposure alters the miRNA expression profile in rBMSCs-EVs. miR-320-3p, upregulated in F-EVs, further enhances fluoride-induced osteogenic differentiation. In contrast, EVs with miR-320-3p inhibition markedly reverse this enhancement. The mechanism is related to the fact that under fluoride exposure, rBMSCs-EVs regulate Igf1r/ERK1/2/MAPK by delivering miR-320-3p, thereby affecting osteogenic differentiation. To date, no reports have linked rBMSCs-EVs to skeletal fluorosis. Therefore, our findings not only provide novel mechanistic insights into the pathogenesis of skeletal fluorosis but also establish a theoretical foundation for the clinical translation of miRNAs derived from rBMSCs-EVs in this disease.

Nonetheless, in view of the limitations of in vitro experiments, more specialized in-depth validations should be implemented through in vivo studies. Specifically, it is particularly necessary to further clarify the regulatory relationship of miR-320-3p-EVs, the Igf1r gene and the ERK1/2/MAPK pathway. At the same time, the extraction and purification techniques of EVs are not yet fully standardized, and the components of EVs are complex. Besides miR-320-3p, EVs also contain other miRNAs and bioactive molecules (such as proteins, lipids, etc.), and these components may also have an impact on osteogenic differentiation.

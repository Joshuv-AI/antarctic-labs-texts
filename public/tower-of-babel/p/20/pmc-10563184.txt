Reply to Wu et al.
Authors: Evan L. Brittain, Ryan J. Tedford, Bradley A. Maron
Published: 2023

From the Authors:

We appreciate the interest in our work (1) from Wu and colleagues, who suggest that patient-level factors may contribute to pulmonary arterial compliance (PAC) and therefore affect our results. Age, left-sided filling pressure, and cardiopulmonary disease are mentioned as potential confounders in the relationship between PAC and all-cause mortality. We agree with Wu and colleagues and note that our Cox proportional hazards models were adjusted for age, heart failure status, and numerous other covariates, including common cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, and metabolic conditions (see the online supplement in our paper [1] for details). We agree further that pulmonary hypertension alone is likely not the primary cause of death for most of the patients in our study.

Nonetheless, even when accounting for numerous comorbid conditions, PAC remains a strong prognostic factor in patients with pulmonary hypertension. Wu and colleagues also note that pulmonary vasodilators, β-blockers, and other vasoactive drugs may affect PAC. Medication exposure was not readily available in our dataset, and pulmonary arterial hypertension (and thus the use of appropriate pulmonary vasodilators) should be rare in the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs system. We are not aware of data showing an impact of β-blockers on PAC. We agree with the need for prospective studies to examine the value of PAC as a treatment target in patients with mild or early pulmonary hypertension.

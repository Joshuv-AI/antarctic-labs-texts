Hybrid spatial-field attention network for meteorological data downscaling
Authors: Sheng Gao, Yiming Ren, Lianlei Lin, Zongwei Zhang, Junkai Wang, Hangyi Yu, Hanqing Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Near-surface weather variables play an important role in production. However, the coarse spatial resolution meteorological data in existing meteorological products limit their applicability in modern high-precision engineering and decision-making scenarios. To address this limitation, we propose a deep learning–based meteorological downscaling framework, Hybrid Spatial-Field Attention Network (HSFANet). HSFANet is built upon a hybrid spatial-field attention module that jointly models local spatial dependencies, global spatial correlations, and cross-field interactions among different meteorological variables. To account for the varying importance of hierarchical features, a dynamic layer information integration module is introduced to adaptively aggregate multi-level representations. Furthermore, the ground-object cross-attention mechanism is incorporated to capture the coupling relationship between meteorological fields and underlying surface characteristics. Experiments on CLDAS V2.0 reanalysis data demonstrate that the proposed method achieves higher accuracy than state-of-the-art methods across multiple scaling factors. In addition, the engineering application experiment shows that HSFANet can effectively reconstruct future high-resolution meteorological fields from coarse-resolution forecast data, highlighting its potential for practical deployment in engineering and production-related applications.

Introduction

Near-surface weather variables such as pressure, temperature, humidity, etc. always affect human production. With the development of intelligent technologies and precision equipment in agriculture, industry, and the Internet of Things, the impact of these variables on production is becoming more and more significant. The small differences in meteorological status within a local area may cause equipment to function differently. Therefore, obtaining precise spatial distributions of weather states within the region of interest for risk assessment and behavioral planning is an important step in facilitating production and reducing risk and is becoming a widespread need for societal production.

The main tool for providing such information is the Global Climate Model (GCM), which can simulate physical processes in the ocean, atmosphere, and land surface, and provides climate projections on a global scale. Many studies have implemented global or local scale meteorological field modeling based on GCM1–3. However, GCM can only define climate conditions over a wide range. In most cases, this coarse spatial resolution makes it difficult to accurately reflect the spatial distribution of meteorological states over a given local area. Therefore, it is necessary to downscale the meteorological field based on the coarse-resolution data provided by GCM.

There are two types of downscaling methods: dynamic downscaling and statistical downscaling. In dynamic downscaling methods, the regional meteorological state is a dynamic model nested in the GCM, and the global coarse-scale information provided by the GCM is used as a constraint to solve the high-resolution details of the local region. Dynamic downscaling methods have explicit dynamic and physical mechanisms and can solve the meteorological state at any location. However, due to the complex partial differential equations to be solved, the process can take weeks, which requires a lot of computational effort for individuals and companies.

Statistical downscaling methods describe the mapping relationship between coarse-scale meteorological states and high-resolution local states by building statistical models. Compared to dynamic methods, they have lower computational costs and can consistently provide high-resolution spatial distribution with low computational cost. They are widely adopted by individuals and small teams, such as regression-based methods, including multiple linear regression and generalized linear models, which are interpretable but hardly fit complex trends. With the development of data science, lots of data are available in the meteorological field. Many machine learning methods such as random forests4, support vector machines5, extreme learning machines6, neural networks7,8, and ensemble learning9 are used for statistical downscaling. These methods can derive the meteorological state of a finite number of observation points in a given region from easily accessible observations, but it is difficult to specify the spatial distribution of the meteorological state in the region.

From a computer vision perspective, the meteorological field in a region is represented on a grid of geographic coordinates and can be regarded as a digital hyperimage. The downscaling task reconstructs the high-resolution meteorological field from limited coarse-scale data, which is similar to the Single Image Super-resolution Reconstruction (SISR), which reconstructs high-resolution images from low-resolution images. Some studies investigated the possibility of downscaling the meteorological field by deep learning (DL) based SISR methods. For example,10 determined the hyperparameters of a three-layer CNN based on the random search algorithm for meteorological downscaling.11 extended the search space to layer number, kernel width, and number of neurons in the dense layer.12 studied the effectiveness of SISR algorithms such as VDSR and RCAN in the rainfall prediction downscaling task.13 tried to directly migrate a number of SISR algorithms, including SRCNN, DeepSD, Nest-UNet, ResDeepD, etc., to downscaling.14 proposed SRDRN based on the deep residual super-resolution network for precipitation and temperature downscaling. These methods reconstructed high-resolution meteorological details based on low-resolution data with good performance, however, they failed to realize the essential differences between downscaling and SISR tasks. In particular, the SISR task considers the local details in an image as relevant to the neighboring regions15. Many CNN-based methods only focus on the correlation between the region being reconstructed and its neighboring regions. However, in a meteorological system, the meteorological state of certain observation points correlates not only with its neighboring regions but also with the global state16. Therefore, the deep learning-based method should consider not only the spatially local state but also the correlation between local and global states - as is the case with the dynamic methods. Second, although color images are usually represented as RGB channels, these channels describe the same object position relationship, so excellent results can be achieved without targeted processing. While there are more complex potential correlations between different meteorological fields17, it may be difficult to achieve better reconstruction performance without considering the correlation between fields. Third, the existing algorithmic architectures are difficult to achieve adaptive aggregation of features from different layers, which leads to the weakening of the meteorological details extracted from the shallow layers and reduces the reconstruction performance18. Finally, the spatial distribution of the meteorological state is also influenced by ground objects19, e.g., the temperature over land tends to be higher during the day than over water. In dynamic downscaling, the elevation field is used as a constraint for reconstruction20. Therefore, the DL-based downscaling methods need to consider a meteorological perspective.

In this study, we try to improve the SISR method from the perspective of meteorological properties and propose a DL-based downscaling method, Hybrid Spatial-Field Attention Network (HSFANet). HSFANet can adaptively learn spatial correlations and cross-field correlations from low-resolution meteorological fields, as well as dynamically integrate deep features from different layers. In addition, the reconstruction performance is further improved by considering the ground object distribution. HSFAM

The shallow feature mapping layer projects the meteorological state into a feature space of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^{L \times C}$$\end{document}, where the feature embedding vector from each channel represents the spatial distribution of the meteorological state. Owing to the complex spatial correlations of meteorological variables, the meteorological state at a single observation point is strongly correlated with its neighboring regions, while also being influenced by the global meteorological state. Therefore, as illustrated in Fig. 2, HSFAM adopts a parallel architecture: a local spatial self-attention chain is employed to extract local spatial correlations of meteorological states, while another self-attention chain is used to capture global spatial correlations affecting individual observation points. A dynamic weight allocation unit is then introduced to adaptively assign weights to the two branches based on the input received by HSFAM. In addition, to consider the influence of the ground object distribution on the meteorological field, ground object cross-attention layers are placed in front of both chains. DLIM

In the SISR task, features extracted from different layers contain different information. It is generally accepted that shallow features represent more high-frequency details, while deep features reflect abstract image representations23. Inspired by the above theory, downscaling tasks should also consider the importance of features from different layers. Therefore, we propose the DLIM to adaptively aggregate the information from different layers.

The DLIM consists of a dynamic weight allocation unit and the self-attention layer. Its structure is shown in Fig.6. Experiment

In this study, all experiments were performed on a computer with 12 vCPU Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8352V CPU @ 2.10GHz and RTX 4090 (24GB). The algorithm was implemented based on the PyTorch 1.11.0 framework.

Dataset

This study takes the China Meteorological Administration land data assimilation system (CLDAS V2.0)25,26 as the data source for experiments. The CLDAS is based on available data and numerical models to provide the best estimates of meteorological states in the Asian region. The spatial resolution is 0.0625°×0.0625° and the temporal sampling interval is 1 hour. We downscaled the pressure (PRS), the humidity (SHU), and the 2m temperature (TMP) in the range of 26.03125°N-34.03125°N, 111.96875°E-119.96875°E for the study. Observational data from 2020 to 2022 is used for algorithm training and data from 2023 is used for verification.

In addition, the 30 m global land cover data27,28 is used to provide ground object information, such as cultivated land, forest, grass, wetland, and water of the research region. We take the ground object data from the above-mentioned region and down sampled it to the spatial resolution of 0.0625°×0.0625° to align it with the meteorological field (Fig. 8).Fig. 8Ground object field in this study.

Ground object field in this study.

We explored the performance of the proposed and comparative methods on downscaling factors 4, 8, and 16, hereinafter referred to as 4×, 8×, and 16× tasks. Various evaluation metrics are used to comprehensively evaluate the downscaling performance of each method. Specifically, the root mean square error (RMSE) and the mean absolute error (MAE) between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{F}_{HR}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{HR}$$\end{document} are used to measure the numerical accuracy of the model in reconstructing the high-resolution field. PSNR and SSIM are used to measure the similarity between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{F}_{HR}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{HR}$$\end{document}.

Result and discussion

Ablation experiment

To verify the effectiveness of the proposed main modules and fundamental computational components within HSFANet, we conducted comprehensive ablation experiments. The experimental settings are summarized as follows:

HSFANet-LSA: we remove the local spatial self-attention chain in HSFAM to verify the effectiveness of the local spatial self-attention calculation in learning local spatial correlations for the downscaling task.

HSFANet-SA: we remove the self-attention chain in HSFAM to verify the effectiveness of the self-attention calculation in learning the spatial correlation between point-wise meteorological states and the global meteorological field for the downscaling task.

HSFANet-DWA: to evaluate the effectiveness of the dynamic weight allocation mechanism in fusing the two types of features, we remove the dynamic weight allocation unit in HSFAM and directly combine the outputs of the LSA and SA branches via element-wise addition.

HSFANet-HSFAM: we replace HSFAMs with ResBlocks29 that are commonly used in computer vision fields to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed HSFAM, which is specifically designed to capture the spatial-field correlation of meteorological variables.

HSFANet-DLIM: to demonstrate the importance of DLIM to integrate multi-level features, the output of the final HSFAM is directly fed into the high-resolution reconstruction module, bypassing DLIM.

HSFANet-GO: to evaluate the contribution of incorporating ground object information, the ground object cross-attention layers in HSFAM are removed, and ground object information is no longer considered.

The experimental results indicate that HSFAM has the most significant impact on the downscaling performance of HSFANet. Removing the HSFAM leads to a sharp degradation in performance, demonstrating that the proposed module, which is specifically designed to capture the spatial-field correlations of meteorological states, plays a crucial role in the downscaling task. Within HSFAM, the removal of the LSA chain, SA chain, or dynamic weight allocation unit each results in performance degradation, confirming that local spatial correlations, point-wise spatial correlations, and weighting the two branches are all beneficial for meteorological state downscaling. Finally, removing DLIM also leads to a decline in downscaling performance, indicating that adaptively attending to deep features from different levels facilitates more effective information fusion and improves downscaling accuracy.

Comparative experiment

Extensive comparative experiments are conducted to investigate the effectiveness of the proposed method compared to other DL-based methods on downscaling tasks. Comparative models are as follows:

VDSR30: uses a deep convolutional neural network to directly learn the residual mapping from low-resolution to high-resolution images.

EDSR and MDSR31: EDSR builds on residual networks by removing batch normalization and increasing network width to improve super-resolution performance. MDSR is a multi-scale residual network that shares a backbone to handle super-resolution tasks at different scaling factors.

RCAN32: employs channel attention within a post-upsampling framework to enhance feature representation.

SRFBN33: utilizes a feedback mechanism in a recurrent network to iteratively refine high-resolution features.

HAN34: applies hierarchical attention to combine both channel and spatial attention to recalibrate features.

LatticeNet35: uses a lattice structure to enable multi-path feature interaction for improved reconstruction.

SWinIR36: a swin transformer-based method leveraging local window self-attention and global modeling for super-resolution.

DLSR37: employs differentiable neural architecture search to automatically optimize and discover the best network structure for super-resolution.

Due to its internal computation, DLSR is not applicable to the 16× task. Additionally, HSFANet-GO corresponds to the proposed method without incorporating ground object information, ensuring a fair comparison with baseline methods. In this experiment, all comparative methods were trained on a device equipped with 25 vCPU Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8470Q and a single RTX 5090 (32 GB) GPU for accelerated inference. HSFANet was implemented using PyTorch 2.8.0 and optimized with the Adam optimizer, with a learning rate of 0.0002, a batch size of 32, and trained for 100 epochs. To ensure experimental fairness, all methods used the same dataset preprocessing pipeline, and baseline hyperparameters were set to their default configurations (for EDSR and MDSR, see https://github.com/lockeliu/super_solution; for SwinIR, see https://github.com/cszn/KAIR/tree/fc1732f4a4514e42ce15e5b3a1e18c828af47a1e; for HAN, see https://github.com/Jungjaewon/Single_Image_SuperResolution_via_Holistic_Attention_Network; for other baselines, see https://github.com/NJU-Jet/SR_Framework). Since the comparative approaches are primarily based on convolutional neural network architectures, they consider local spatial correlations in meteorological fields to some extent. In particular, RCAN captures across-field dependencies through channel attention, while SwinIR models local–global spatial correlations using attention mechanisms. However, due to the lack of adaptive weighting across layer features, their performance remains inferior to that of HSFANet. HAN assigns weights to different layers via a layer-attention module and models inter-field correlations through channel attention, but it fails to adequately capture the unique spatial field characteristics of meteorological variables. These results indicate that explicitly learning the correlations between a single observation point and its neighboring and global regions, together with adaptively weighting hierarchical feature information, can effectively improve performance in meteorological downscaling tasks. Furthermore, incorporating ground object information leads to additional performance gains for HSFANet. To further analyze, as the downscaling factor increases, the accuracy of all methods decreases because less useful information is available in the low-resolution field, making the reconstruction task more difficult. Second, the DL-based methods achieved higher accuracy compared to the interpolation-based methods, proving that the consideration of a larger spatial region and the extraction of deep features can help improve the downscaling performance.

Experiment on engineering applications

To verify the feasibility of the proposed method in practical engineering, we explored the possibility of using the HSFANet to construct the high-resolution future meteorological states of a specific region. Specifically, in this study, data for the region of 26.25-34°N, 112-119.75°E, with a spatial resolution of 0.25° were collected from the global forecast system (GFS). The dataset covers approximately the same area as CLDAS V2.0 and can provide the forecast for the next 384 hours. We set the predicted temperature, pressure, and humidity fields for the next 72 hours as low-resolution inputs, and used the corresponding meteorological states from CLDAS V2.0 as high-resolution targets. This setting enables the prediction of high-resolution meteorological fields over the next 72 hours, thereby meeting the demand for fine-grained spatial distributions of future meteorological conditions. We continued to use data from 2020 to 2022 for model training and employed data from 2023 as the test set. RCAN, SwinIR, and LatticeNet, which achieved strong performance in the comparative experiments, were selected as baseline methods for comparison with the proposed approach. All methods adopted the same hyperparameter settings as those used in the comparative experiment, and were retrained on the new dataset. Conclusion

Inspired by the similarity between the meteorological downscaling task and SISR, we propose a DL-based method, HSFANet for meteorological downscaling. The proposed method can dynamically learn the spatial, global, and cross-field correlation from the low-resolution field by a hybrid spatial-field attention module. And integrate the deep features from different layers by dynamic weights allocation mechanism. By learning the correlation between the ground object distribution and the meteorological field, the performance is further improved. Comparative experiments show that the proposed method can achieve extremely high numerical accuracy in 4×, 8×, and 16× tasks compared to the traditional interpolation method and DL-based SOTA SISR algorithms. The engineering application experiments prove that the HSFANet can build the future high-resolution meteorological field based on low-resolution forecasting products such as GFS, which is of great significance for planning production activities in agriculture, industry, and the Internet of Things.

Based on local attribution analysis, the effectiveness of considering local and global spatial correlation is further demonstrated, and we analyzed the impact of the amount of available information on downscaling performance. Based on the analysis of dynamic weights, we have proven the effectiveness of the dynamic weights allocation mechanism. The analysis of the comparative experiments shows that the bottleneck of DL-based downscaling methods is still the accurate estimation of high-frequency meteorological details near the coarse grid, more appropriate downscaling methods need to be explored according to their physical properties.

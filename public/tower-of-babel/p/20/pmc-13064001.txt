Advances in first-line treatment for primary central nervous system lymphoma: highlights from the 2025 ASH annual meeting
Authors: Rong Wei, Shenmiao Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAdvances in targeted therapies for newly diagnosed primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL) were a key highlight at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting. While high-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX) remains the cornerstone of PCNSL treatment, the integration of BTK inhibitors, immunomodulators, and novel agents—such as selinexor and anti-PD-1 antibodies—is reshaping the first-line management paradigm. These emerging combinations are improving response rates and survival, paving the way for more effective strategies. Here, we provide a timely synthesis of these pivotal updates presented at ASH 2025.Keywords: PCNSL, BTK inhibitor, Immunomodulator, Selinexor, Anti-PD1 antibody

To the editor,

Primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL) is a highly aggressive extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma. While high-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX)-based chemotherapy remains foundational, its limited complete response (CR) rate of approximately 50% underscores the need for more effective strategies [1, 2]. In recent years, advances in the understanding of the molecular pathogenesis of PCNSL have led to the emergence of multiple targeted agents with remarkable efficacy, changing the first-line treatment paradigm. Here, we summarize the application as well as the efficacy and safety of targeted therapies in first-line PCNSL treatment presented at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting, in order to provide a timely synthesis of these emerging trends.

BTK inhibitors

Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK) plays a crucial role in the B-cell receptor signaling pathway and represents a core therapeutic target in PCNSL. Ibrutinib, a first-generation BTK inhibitor, has demonstrated excellent efficacy in combination with HD-MTX in the LOC-R01 study and other small-scale trials (e.g., R-MPV/i, rituximab, methotrexate, procarbazine, vincristine, with or without ibrutinib), achieving CR rates of 82–83% [3–5] (Table 1). However, attention must be paid to its off-target effects, such as bleeding and atrial fibrillation (Table 2).

The characteristics of new agents in PCNSL treatment

Next-generation BTK inhibitors, including zanubrutinib and orelabrutinib, offer higher target selectivity and improved blood-brain barrier penetration (Table 2). In the ZANA study, the regimen of zanubrutinib plus rituximab and HD-MTX achieved a CR rate of 89.7% and a 2-year progression-free survival (PFS) rate of 82.8%, without observed specific adverse events such as atrial fibrillation, indicating a favorable safety profile [6] (Table 1). Several Chinese studies (e.g., POR/ROM, pomalidomide, orelabrutinib and rituximab followed by sequential addition of HD-MTX; ORT ± HD-MTX: orelabrutinib, rituximab, thiotepa, with or without HD-MTX) have shown that orelabrutinib yields high CR rates (> 80%) and durable disease control across different populations (2-year PFS is around 60%), including elderly patients [1, 6–10] (Table 1).

Immunomodulators

In combination with R-MPV, lenalidomide demonstrated significant value in the LOC-R01 study, with a CR rate of 86%, substantially higher than historical controls [4] (Table 1). Real-world data from China further indicated that the R²-MTX regimen (lenalidomide + rituximab + HD-MTX) is significantly more effective than traditional R-MTX (rituximab + HD-MTX) or R-MA (rituximab + cytarabine + HD-MTX) regimens [1] (Table 1).

Pomalidomide, a second-generation immunomodulator, was evaluated in the POR regimen (pomalidomide + orelabrutinib + rituximab) as induction therapy. After 4 cycles, the overall response rate (ORR) reached 84.3%, with a CR rate of 59.3%, demonstrating the probable “chemo-free” therapy in the first-line setting [7] (Table 1).

Nuclear export inhibitor

Selinexor is a first-in-class oral nuclear export inhibitor that promotes the retention of tumor suppressor proteins in the nucleus and exhibits synergistic effects with BTK inhibitors (Table 2). In a phase II study, the MSZ regimen (HD-MTX + selinexor + zanubrutinib) achieved an impressive CR rate of 95.2%, with 1-year PFS and overall survival (OS) rates as high as 94.1% and 100%, respectively [9] (Table 1). This study was the first to quantify selinexor concentrations in cerebrospinal fluid, confirming its effective penetration of the blood-brain barrier. While the CR rate is impressive, the reported 1-year PFS requires longer follow-up to confirm durability, especially given the relatively lower CSF quantification (CSF-to-plasma ratio ~ 0.6%) [9] compared to BTK inhibitors (~ 50%) [11]. Comparisons with mature data from studies like LOC-R01 or ZANA are needed.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors

Identifying effective consolidation strategies for elderly or transplant-ineligible patients remains challenging. A phase I/IB study used anti-PD-1 antibody and orelabrutinib as maintenance therapy with or without radiation as consolidation therapy after OFP (orelaburutinib, fotemustine, anti-PD-1 antibody) induction chemoimmunotherapy, yielding 2-year PFS rate of 32% and 2-year OS of 48% (the survival statistics apply to the efficacy-evaluable population (n = 27)) with manageable toxicity [8] (Table 1).

Notably, while targeted agents are reshaping the treatment paradigm for PCNSL, high-dose chemotherapy (HDC) followed by autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) remains a vital treatment option for eligible patients. The integration of these novel agents into the HDC/ASCT strategies represents a promising avenue for further improving long-term survival in newly diagnosed PCNSL.

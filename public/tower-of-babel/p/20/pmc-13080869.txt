A prospective, multicenter, observational study of trastuzumab deruxtecan in elderly patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer: EN-COURAGE
Authors: Y Narita, R Kawabata, T Ando, T Arigami, H Kawakami, A Makiyama, R Uozumi, Y Fukuda, K Muro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGastric cancer (GC) disproportionately affects the elderly, yet pivotal trials have provided inconsistent evidence regarding the efficacy of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-targeted and immune-based therapies in this subgroup. Beyond chronological age, geriatric assessment (GA) and sarcopenia measures such as the psoas muscle index (PMI) may better capture vulnerability and predict treatment outcomes. Thus, there is a need for prospective studies specifically designed for elderly patients, incorporating GA and sarcopenia measures.EN-COURAGE is a prospective, multicenter, observational study evaluating trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in patients aged ≥70 years with HER2-positive unresectable or recurrent gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma in Japan. Eligible patients receive T-DXd intravenously at the approved dose and schedule, with dose modifications and treatment interruptions per clinical practice. The target enrollment is 100 patients. The primary endpoint is overall survival (OS); secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), time to treatment failure (TTF), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), duration of response (DoR), time to treatment discontinuation (TTD), T-DXd treatment status, and safety. A key feature is the systematic integration of GA tools (Geriatric-8, Cancer and Aging Research Group toxicity score) and PMI analysis to explore efficacy and tolerability predictors. EN-COURAGE represents the first prospective study designed to elucidate real-world T-DXd outcomes in elderly patients with HER2-positive GC.Key words: EN-COURAGE, HER2-positive, gastric cancer, trastuzumab deruxtecan, elderly patients, geriatric assessment

Description of protocol

Background and rationale

Gastric cancer (GC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. In Japan, >70% of new cases of GC occur in individuals aged ≥70 years, and this proportion continues to increase with age.1 Thus, the majority of patients encountered in daily practice are elderly, challenging treatment decisions and increasing toxicity risk.2

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is an established therapeutic target in GC.3,4 The pivotal ToGA trial demonstrated that adding trastuzumab to chemotherapy significantly improved overall survival (OS) compared with chemotherapy alone.5 The benefit was maintained in elderly patients [hazard ratio (HR) 0.66] (Table 1).5 The KEYNOTE-811 study, which evaluated pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy as first-line treatment for HER2-positive GC, demonstrated an OS benefit in the total study population (HR 0.80). However, subgroup analyses showed benefits in patients aged <65 years (HR 0.72) but not in those aged ≥65 years (HR 0.99).6,7 More recently, the phase III DESTINY-Gastric04 trial directly compared trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), an HER2-targeted antibody–drug conjugate, with ramucirumab plus paclitaxel as second-line therapy for HER2-positive GC, demonstrating a significant improvement in OS [HR 0.70, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.55-0.90].8 Importantly, age-specific subgroup analyses showed consistent benefits, with HRs of 0.68 and 0.73 for patients aged <65 and ≥65 years, respectively. Taken together, these findings highlight that the magnitude of benefit from HER2-targeted or immune-based regimens may vary across age groups.

In the third-line or later treatment setting for advanced GC, pivotal trials have shown that survival benefits are generally maintained in elderly patients. In the DESTINY-Gastric01 trial, T-DXd demonstrated superior efficacy compared with physicians’ choice of chemotherapy (median OS 12.5 versus 8.4 months; HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39-0.88).9,10 Importantly, age-specific subgroup analyses confirmed consistent benefits, with HRs of 0.82 for patients aged <65 years and 0.44 for those aged ≥65 years.9,10 In the TAGS trial of trifluridine/tipiracil versus placebo, OS improved across age groups, with HRs of 0.67 and 0.73 for patients aged <65 years and ≥65 years, respectively.11 Similarly, in ATTRACTION-2, benefit was maintained irrespective of age (<65 years, HR 0.76; ≥65 years, HR 0.53).12 These findings suggest that therapeutic efficacy is broadly consistent across age groups.

Beyond chronological age, functional and biological factors are increasingly recognized as critical determinants of treatment outcomes in elderly patients with GC. Chronological age alone does not adequately reflect the heterogeneity in vulnerability, which may be better captured through structured geriatric assessments (GAs) and objective body composition measures.

According to guidelines from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network, the International Society of Geriatric Oncology, and the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer, all patients with cancer aged ≥70 years should be evaluated using some form of GA.13 The Geriatric-8 (G8) screening tool has been prospectively evaluated in elderly patients with GC. In the WJOG8315G randomized phase II study comparing S-1 plus oxaliplatin versus S-1 monotherapy, pretreatment G8 scores were shown to modify treatment benefit: among patients with impaired G8 scores (≤11), the survival difference between combination therapy and monotherapy was attenuated, suggesting that G8 can identify frail patients who are less likely to benefit.14 A meta-analysis (42 studies, 9053 patients) further confirmed that low G8 scores are strongly associated with poor prognosis across cancer types [OS: HR 2.11; progression-free survival (PFS): HR 1.78].15 The Cancer and Aging Research Group (CARG) toxicity score is another validated GA-based instrument. In a Japanese cohort of elderly patients with solid tumors, the incidence of grade 3-5 chemotherapy-related adverse events (AEs) increased stepwise across CARG risk categories (38% in low-risk, 55% in intermediate-risk, and 76% in high-risk groups; P = 0.035).16 Sarcopenia, characterized by reduced skeletal muscle mass, is particularly prevalent among elderly patients with GC and has been increasingly linked to both treatment-related toxicity and survival outcomes. In a study of advanced GC, patients with low skeletal muscle index experienced significantly higher rates of severe hematologic and nonhematologic toxicities along with shorter OS (median 33.1 versus 57.7 weeks; HR 1.68, 95% CI 1.08-2.61).17 Other studies have highlighted the prognostic importance of baseline and dynamic muscle changes during first-line chemotherapy.18,19

The EN-COURAGE study was therefore initiated as the first prospective, multicenter, observational study specifically designed to evaluate the real-world efficacy and safety of T-DXd in patients aged ≥70 years with HER2-positive unresectable or recurrent GC in Japan. By systematically incorporating GA tools (G8, CARG toxicity score) and centralized sarcopenia assessments using the psoas muscle index (PMI), EN-COURAGE aims to elucidate the treatment outcomes of T-DXd in elderly patients, identify predictors of treatment benefit and tolerability, and address the evidence gap stemming from previous pivotal studies.

Objective

The objective of EN-COURAGE is to evaluate the real-world efficacy and safety of T-DXd in elderly patients (aged ≥70 years) with HER2-positive unresectable or recurrent GC or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJA), incorporating GA tools to support individualized care.

Study design and patients

EN-COURAGE is a prospective, multicenter, observational study conducted at academic and community hospitals across Japan (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100313). The study is designed to reflect routine clinical practice and, therefore, employs broad eligibility criteria with limited exclusions to ensure representativeness of the elderly population (Table 2 and Figure 1).

Patients will be eligible if they are aged ≥70 years with histologically confirmed GC or GEJA that is unresectable or recurrent and HER2-positive, as determined by local pathological evaluation [immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3+ or IHC 2+ with in situ hybridization (ISH) positivity] (Table 3). In principle, HER2 testing is required to be conducted before initiating first-line treatment, in which case re-evaluation of the HER2 status before T-DXd administration is not mandatory. Eligible patients must have received at least two prior lines of systemic therapy including trastuzumab, have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0-2, and have adequate organ function according to institutional standards. The exclusion criteria include prior exposure to T-DXd and a history of clinically significant interstitial lung disease (ILD). Patients judged to be unsuitable for study participation by the investigator for any reason will also be excluded. Written informed consent will be required from all patients before enrollment. The planned enrollment is 100 patients during an 18-month accrual period, with a 12-month follow-up.

Treatment

All patients will receive T-DXd at a starting dose of 6.4 mg/kg administered intravenously once every 3 weeks, in line with the approved prescribing information in Japan. Dose modifications, including initiation at a reduced dose, treatment delays, and discontinuations, will be permitted at the discretion of the treating physician according to clinical judgment, AE severity, and institutional standards. Following discussion with the patient, decisions regarding the initial dose of T-DXd will be left to the clinical judgment of the treating investigator blinded to the individual results of the two GA scores. Supportive care, including antiemetics, growth factor support, and prophylactic interventions, will be allowed at the physician’s discretion. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, consent withdrawal, or the investigator’s decision.

Study endpoints and assessments

The primary endpoint is OS, defined as the time from the first administration of T-DXd to death from any cause. Secondary endpoints include PFS, defined as the time from the first T-DXd dose to disease progression or death, whichever occurs first, according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1 as assessed by the investigators20; time to treatment failure (TTF), defined as the time from treatment initiation to discontinuation or death; objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) per RECIST v1.1 as assessed by the investigators; disease control rate (DCR), defined as CR + PR + stable disease; duration of response (DoR), calculated from the date of first documented CR/PR to progression or death; time to treatment discontinuation (TTD), defined as the time from treatment initiation to discontinuation; T-DXd treatment status; safety, measured based on the incidence and severity of AEs according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.021; and relationship between GA and efficacy/safety of T-DXd.

A distinctive feature of the study is the systematic integration of GA tools (G8, CARG toxicity score) and sarcopenia evaluation using the PMI. G8 and CARG toxicity scores will be assessed at baseline, whereas PMI will be centrally reviewed using computed tomography scans obtained at baseline. These assessments will be incorporated to elucidate their predictive and prognostic value in relation to treatment efficacy, tolerability, and survival outcomes.

Radiological tumor assessments will be carried out at baseline and every 6-9 weeks thereafter, or according to institutional practice, using RECIST v1.1. Safety monitoring will include cycle-by-cycle AE reporting, with additional detailed evaluation for selected AEs (ILD/pneumonitis). Survival status will be collected until the end of the observation period.

Target sample size and statistical analysis

The target sample size is ∼100 patients. This number was determined primarily based on feasibility across the participating sites rather than a formal statistical power calculation. All efficacy and safety analyses will be conducted in the full analysis set comprising all patients who receive at least one dose of T-DXd and have required data available. The safety analysis set will include the same population, focusing on AE evaluation.

Time-to-event outcomes including OS, PFS, TTF, DoR, and TTD will be analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method. Median event times and survival probabilities at predefined time points (e.g. 6 and 12 months), along with the corresponding 95% CIs, will be estimated. HRs and 95% CIs will be derived from Cox proportional hazards models. ORR and DCR will be summarized as proportions with exact binomial 95% CIs. Treatment delivery will be evaluated by summarizing the relative dose intensity, dose reductions, delays, and discontinuations. For patients who undergo dose reduction, summary statistics (number, mean, standard deviation, quartiles, maximum, minimum) will be calculated for the cycle during which the first reduction occurred; the same approach will be applied for treatment delays.

A key component of the study is the prespecified analysis of GA and sarcopenia measures. G8 scores will be examined using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves for OS at 6 and 12 months to determine optimal cut-off values that maximize sensitivity and specificity. Patients will subsequently be stratified into low and high G8 score groups according to these thresholds. Survival outcomes, including OS, PFS, TTF, DoR, and TTD, will be compared between these groups using a Cox proportional hazards model to estimate HRs and corresponding 95% CIs. For ORR and DCR, differences in proportions between the high and low G8 score groups and their associated 95% CIs will be calculated. Safety outcomes will be descriptively analyzed for both G8 score groups. CARG toxicity scores will be analyzed across predefined risk categories (low, intermediate, high) and examined for associations with treatment efficacy and toxicity. PMI will be assessed at baseline using sex-specific thresholds. Analyses for PMI will be conducted analogously to those carried out for the G8 score.

Missing data will not be imputed, and analyses will be carried out on available cases. All statistical tests will be two-sided with a significance level of 0.05. All statistical analyses will be carried out using SAS software, V.9.4 or higher (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Ethics and dissemination

The EN-COURAGE study will be conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects in Japan, and all applicable local regulatory requirements. The study protocol, informed consent form, and other relevant documents have been reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Aichi Cancer Center Hospital (Approval No.: 2023-0-181). The study is registered with the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry (UMIN000052522).

All patients will be required to provide written informed consent before enrollment, and they retain the right to withdraw consent at any time without disadvantage to their subsequent care. Participant data confidentiality will be protected; anonymized data will be collected using an electronic data capture system and stored securely.

Conclusion

EN-COURAGE is the first prospective, multicenter, observational study dedicated to evaluating the real-world efficacy and safety of T-DXd in elderly patients aged ≥70 years with HER2-positive unresectable or recurrent GC. By using OS as the primary endpoint and systematically incorporating GA tools (G8, CARG toxicity score) and sarcopenia evaluation using the PMI, the study aims to elucidate both treatment outcomes and determinants of efficacy and tolerability in this underrepresented population. The results are expected to provide real-world evidence that will support risk stratification and contribute to optimizing treatment strategies for elderly patients with advanced HER2-positive GC.

Prognostic Utility of the HFpEF-ABA Score for HFpEF Patients
Authors: Wengen Zhu, Linjuan Guo, Xi Chen, Min Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe HFpEF-ABA score, incorporating age, body mass index (BMI), and atrial fibrillation (AF), has demonstrated utility in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).ObjectivesThis study evaluated the prognostic performance of the HFpEF-ABA score within the Americas and Russia/Georgia cohorts of the TOPCAT (Treatment of Preserved Cardiac Function Heart Failure with an Aldosterone Antagonist) trial, examining its association with clinical outcomes across these distinct regions.MethodsCox proportional hazards and competing risk regression analyses assessed associations between HFpEF-ABA probabilities and clinical outcomes.ResultsAmong the Americas cohort (n = 1,726), 72.7% exhibited an HFpEF-ABA probability ≥75%. Elevated HFpEF-ABA probabilities were modestly associated with an increased risk of HF hospitalization HR per 10% increase: 1.07; 95% CI: 1.00-1.14) and any hospitalization (HR per 10% increase: 1.04; 95% CI: 1.00-1.08), without significant associations with mortality or major cardiovascular events. In the Russia/Georgia cohort (n = 1,674), 39.8% had probability ≥75%. Higher HFpEF-ABA probabilities were significantly associated with increased risks of all-cause mortality (HR: 1.67; 95% CI: 1.16-2.41), heart failure hospitalization (HR: 1.98; 95% CI: 1.04-3.78), any hospitalization (HR: 1.75; 95% CI: 1.43-2.14,) and stroke (HR: 2.81; 95% CI: 1.44-5.5). A linear trend of escalating adverse outcome risks corresponded with increasing HFpEF-ABA probabilities in the Russia/Georgia cohort, excluding stroke.ConclusionsOur study demonstrates substantial regional heterogeneity in the prognostic utility of the HFpEF-ABA score among patients with HFpEF enrolled in the TOPCAT trial. The score exhibited strong associations with adverse outcomes in the Russia/Georgia cohort, whereas its prognostic discrimination was attenuated in the Americas cohort. (Aldosterone Antagonist Therapy for Adults With Heart Failure and Preserved Systolic Function [TOPCAT]; NCT00094302)

Heart failure (HF) with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) is a multifaceted clinical syndrome characterized by typical HF symptoms, such as dyspnea, fatigue, and peripheral edema, despite a preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).1,2 Accounting for approximately half of all HF cases, the prevalence of HFpEF is escalating, driven by aging populations and an increasing burden of comorbidities.3 Patients with HFpEF often experience reduced exercise capacity, frequent hospitalizations, and elevated mortality rates, underscoring the syndrome's significant clinical and public health implications.1

Diagnosing HFpEF necessitates the presence of HF symptoms, preserved LVEF, and evidence of diastolic dysfunction or elevated filling pressures. Contemporary diagnostic algorithms, such as the HFA-PEFF (Heart Failure Association diagnostic algorithm-pre-test assessment; echocardiography and natriuretic peptide score; functional testing; final aetiology)4,5 and H2FPEF (heavy [Body mass index >30 kg/m2], 2 or more antihypertensive medicines, atrial fibrillation, pulmonary hypertension [echocardiographic estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure >35 mm Hg], elder age >60 years, increased filling pressures [Doppler echocardiographic E/e’ > 9])6 scores, integrate clinical, echocardiographic, and biomarker data to estimate the likelihood of HFpEF. Although these algorithms use standard transthoracic echocardiography rather than advanced imaging, they nevertheless require access to echocardiographic assessment and, in the case of HFA-PEFF, natriuretic peptide measurement—resources that may not be readily available in all clinical settings, particularly in primary care, emergency departments, or resource-limited health care systems.

Clinical trials investigating therapeutic interventions for HFpEF, including CHARM-Preserved (Candesartan in Heart failure: Assessment of Reduction in Mortality and Morbidity - Preserved)7 and I-PRESERVE (Irbesartan in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction),8 have yielded neutral results, partly attributed to heterogeneity in patient populations and reliance on LVEF thresholds without comprehensive phenotyping. More recent studies, such as PARAGON-HF (Prospective Comparison of ARNI with ARB on Management of Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction)9 and TOPCAT (Treatment of Preserved Cardiac Function Heart Failure with an Aldosterone Antagonist),10 implemented stricter inclusion criteria, incorporating elevated natriuretic peptide levels and echocardiographic evidence of structural heart disease. Despite these measures, challenges persist. For instance, the TOPCAT trial revealed significant regional disparities in patient characteristics and outcomes, with participants from Russia and Georgia exhibiting lower natriuretic peptide levels and a reduced prevalence of classic HFpEF features compared to those from the Americas. These discrepancies suggest potential misclassification and highlight the need for more accessible and reliable diagnostic tools.

In response to these challenges, the HFpEF-ABA score11, 12, 13 was developed as a pragmatic, evidence-based screening tool using universally available clinical variables: age, body mass index (BMI), and history of atrial fibrillation (AF). This score has demonstrated robust discrimination and calibration across multiple validation cohorts, effectively predicting adverse outcomes such as HF hospitalization and mortality. However, its prognostic utility within well-characterized HFpEF populations remains unexplored. Given the regional heterogeneity observed in TOPCAT, particularly between the Americas and Russia/Georgia cohorts,14 evaluating the HFpEF-ABA score's prognostic performance across these distinct populations is imperative. This study aimed to assess the score's ability to stratify risk for adverse outcomes in these cohorts, thereby determining its potential as a clinically accessible tool for risk stratification in HFpEF patients.

Methods

Study population

This study analyzed data from the TOPCAT trial, accessed through the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute via the Biologic Specimen and Data Repository Information Coordinating Center. The TOPCAT trial was a multicenter, international, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study that evaluated the efficacy of spironolactone vs placebo in patients with symptomatic HFpEF. The follow-up period was 3.43 years (IQR: 1.99-4.94 years). Details of the study design have been previously described.10 Briefly, 3,445 participants aged ≥50 years were recruited from 233 sites across six countries: the United States, Canada, Brazil, Argentina, Russia, and Georgia. Eligible participants exhibited at least 1 sign and 1 symptom of HF, with an LVEF ≥45%. Additional inclusion criteria included an elevated natriuretic peptide level within 60 days before enrollment or hospitalization for HF in the preceding 12 months. Participants with missing data on age, BMI, or AF history (N = 11) or incomplete covariate information (N = 34) were excluded, leaving a final analytical sample of 3,400 participants. The trial protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Boards at all participating sites, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Assessment of HFpEF-ABA score probability

The HFpEF-ABA score probability was calculated using a previously established model that incorporates age, BMI, and a history of AF.11,12 The calculation formula was determined using a logistic regression model such that the probability (P) could be estimated using the logit resulting from the model as follows:logit(P)=-7.78875077116607+0.0625642747159337·Age+0.135149246234458·BMI+2.04080564803471·AF

In present study, an HFpEF-ABA score probability ≥75% was classified as high risk.

Echocardiographic data

Echocardiographic measurements in the TOPCAT trial were performed following the American Society of Echocardiography guidelines. These measurements were analyzed at a central core laboratory that was blinded to both treatment allocation and clinical data.15 The design and baseline findings of the TOPCAT echocardiographic substudy, including reproducibility metrics for conventional measures, were published previously.16

Clinical outcomes

The primary outcome was the same as that of the original TOPCAT trial: a composite of cardiovascular death, aborted cardiac arrest, or hospitalization for HF during the follow-up period. Secondary outcomes included all-cause mortality, cardiovascular death, HF hospitalization, any hospitalization, AF, myocardial infarction, and stroke. All outcomes were adjudicated independently by the Clinical Endpoints Center using the predefined criteria.

Statistical analyses

Baseline continuous variables are presented as the mean ± SD or median with 25th-75th percentiles (Q1-Q3) and compared using Student's t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, depending on distribution. Normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and visual inspection of histograms and Q-Q plots. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Density plots were created to visualize the distribution of HFpEF-ABA score probabilities in the Americas and Russia/Georgia cohorts. Cox proportional hazards models were used to assess the association between baseline HFpEF-ABA score probability and all-cause mortality. Competing risk regression models were applied to cardiovascular death and nonfatal events, including the primary outcome, HF hospitalization, any hospitalization, AF, myocardial infarction, and stroke. HFpEF-ABA score probability was analyzed as both a continuous and dichotomous variable in univariable and multivariable regression models to evaluate its prognostic value, with results expressed as HRs and 95% CIs. Covariates for multivariable models were selected based on a prior selection process17 and included ethnicity (except for Russia/Georgia, as it is a constant), gender, randomization strata (prior HF hospitalization), treatment arm (spironolactone or placebo), NYHA functional class, current smoking status, diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, estimated glomerular filtration rate, hemoglobin, history of coronary artery disease, history of stroke, and the use of diuretic agents or aspirin. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using scaled Schoenfeld residuals and visual inspection of log-log survival plots. Based on both statistical criteria (P > 0.05 for tests of nonproportionality) and graphical assessment, no significant violations of the proportional hazards assumption were detected for the HFpEF-ABA score across any outcome analyzed.

Cumulative incidence curves, analyzed using the Gray test or the log-rank test, were generated to compare event risks over time, stratified by HFpEF-ABA score probability (≥75% vs <75%). Restricted cubic spline analysis was conducted as a complementary approach. Several sensitivity and subgroup analyses were performed to assess the robustness of findings. Sensitivity analyses included: 1) inverse probability of treatment weighted competing risk models to address potential confounding by observed covariates;18 2) restriction to patients with NYHA functional class III-IV symptoms (n = 515) to examine score performance in more severe HF; and 3) restriction to patients with elevated natriuretic peptides (n = 308), defined as B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) >35 pg/mL or N-terminal pro-BNP >125 pg/mL in patients without AF or BNP >105 pg/mL or N-terminal pro-BNP >365 pg/mL in patients with AF, consistent with guideline-recommended diagnostic cutoffs. Subgroup analyses stratified results by treatment arm (spironolactone vs placebo), sex, and comorbidities (hypertension, coronary artery disease, and diabetes mellitus). All analyses were conducted using R (version 4.0.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

In both cohorts, patients with an HFpEF-ABA score probability ≥75% were older, had higher BMI, larger waist circumference, and a higher prevalence of AF compared to those with a probability <75%. Echocardiographic findings stratified by HFpEF-ABA probability

Echocardiographic analyses, detailed in Supplemental Tables S1 and S2, revealed regional disparities between the Americas and Russia/Georgia cohorts when stratified by HFpEF-ABA probability. In the Americas cohort, patients with an HFpEF-ABA probability ≥75% had significantly larger left atrial volume (LAV) (65.5 ± 28.4 vs 53.1 ± 20.4 mL; P < 0.001), LAV index (32.2 ± 14.2 vs 28.3 ± 11.2 mL/m2; P < 0.001), and higher left ventricular mass (228.7 ± 71.9 vs 209.9 ± 69.8 g; P = 0.003) compared to those with probabilities <75% (Supplemental Table S1). In contrast, in the Russia/Georgia cohort, patients with an HFpEF-ABA probability ≥75% also had larger LAV (57.9 ± 18.6 vs 46.8 ± 16.8 mL; P < 0.001) and LAV index (30.3 ± 9.4 vs 24.6 ± 8.7 mL/m2; P < 0.001), but no significant differences in left ventricular mass were noted (212.7 ± 65.7 vs 206.2 ± 62.4 g; P = 0.46) (Supplemental Table S2). The Americas cohort had a higher prevalence of concentric hypertrophy than the Russia/Georgia cohort. Diastolic dysfunction was more prevalent in the Americas cohort, with a greater proportion of patients exhibiting moderate or severe dysfunction.

Sensitivity analysis and subgroup analysis

Sensitivity analysis using the inverse probability of treatment weighted method supported the primary analysis, showing that the HFpEF-ABA model had greater predictive value for adverse outcomes in the Russia/Georgia cohort than in the Americas cohort (Supplemental Table S4). Subgroup analyses in the Russia/Georgia cohort showed no significant interaction effects, with interaction P values >0.05 for spironolactone vs placebo (Supplemental Tables S5 and S6), males vs females, and concomitant disease subgroups (Supplemental Table S7). In sensitivity analysis restricted to NYHA functional class III-IV patients (n = 515), the HFpEF-ABA score retained robust prognostic associations, with HRs comparable to those in the full cohort (Supplemental Table S8), confirming consistent performance in patients with advanced HF symptoms. Conversely, among patients with elevated natriuretic peptides (n = 308), statistical power was insufficient to detect significant associations, likely reflecting the reduced sample size and consequent limitation in precision of effect estimates (Supplemental Table S9).

Discussion

This study highlights pronounced regional heterogeneity in the prognostic performance of the HFpEF-ABA score among patients with HFpEF enrolled in the TOPCAT trial. Specifically, the score demonstrated a robust association with adverse clinical outcomes in the Russia/Georgia cohort, whereas its prognostic discrimination was substantially attenuated in the Americas cohort, as illustrated in the Central Illustration. HFpEF-ABA Score: Regional Differences in Prognostic Performance

The HFpEF-ABA score (age, BMI, atrial fibrillation) showed stronger prognostic associations in Russia/Georgia than Americas, reflecting population heterogeneity and diagnostic certainty rather than geographic variation. HFpEF-ABA scores discriminated effectively in heterogeneous populations but show ceiling effects in well-phenotyped cohorts. AF = atrial fibrillation; HFpEF = heart failure with preserved ejection fraction; HFpEF-ABA = a clinical model using age, body mass index, and history of atrial fibrillation.

HFpEF is a heterogeneous syndrome with considerable variation in pathophysiology across populations.19,20 patients enrolled in the Americas cohort exhibited a higher prevalence of established HFpEF-associated comorbidities, including obesity, AF, and diabetes mellitus, consistent with prior descriptions of a cardiometabolic HFpEF phenotype. In contrast, these conditions were considerably less prevalent in the Russia/Georgia cohort. This divergence was reflected in the distribution of HFpEF-ABA scores, with 72.7% of patients in the Americas exhibiting scores ≥75%, compared with 39.8% of patients in Russia/Georgia, suggesting a substantially higher baseline disease burden in the former population.

Echocardiographic findings further reinforced these regional distinctions in disease manifestation. Among patients in the Americas cohort, higher HFpEF-ABA scores were associated with larger LAV and greater left ventricular mass, consistent with advanced structural remodeling characterized by concentric hypertrophy and chronic diastolic dysfunction. By contrast, in the Russia/Georgia cohort, elevated HFpEF-ABA scores were associated with left atrial enlargement but not with significant differences in left ventricular mass. This pattern suggests a distinct disease phenotype with less pronounced myocardial remodeling.

Our findings indicated that the prognostic performance of the HFpEF-ABA score is context dependent. In the Americas cohort, the HFpEF-ABA score was associated with an increased risk of HF hospitalization but showed limited association with more severe endpoints, including all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. This attenuation may reflect the mitigating effects of comprehensive health care access, earlier diagnosis, and more effective management of comorbidities, which collectively reduce downstream mortality risk despite high baseline disease burden. In contrast, in the Russia/Georgia cohort, higher HFpEF-ABA scores were consistently associated with elevated risks of HF hospitalization, all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, and composite adverse outcomes. These stronger associations likely reflect a combination of broader baseline risk variability, limited access to optimal medical therapy, and fewer resources for managing cardiovascular comorbidities, thereby amplifying the prognostic signal captured by simple clinical variables.

An additional explanation for the observed regional disparities relates to potential diagnostic heterogeneity within the Russia/Georgia cohort. The lower prevalence of obesity, diabetes, and other cardinal HFpEF features raises the possibility that a subset of patients may not have met the stringent contemporary diagnostic criteria for HFpEF. In this context, the HFpEF-ABA score may have preferentially identified individuals at heightened cardiovascular risk who subsequently experienced adverse outcomes, regardless of whether they fulfilled strict HFpEF definitions. Accordingly, the cohort may represent a mixture of true HFpEF, HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction, and other cardiovascular conditions that phenotypically overlap with HFpEF.21, 22, 23, 24 In such settings, the score may function less as a disease-specific prognostic tool and more as a marker of global cardiovascular vulnerability. Conversely, patients enrolled in the Americas cohort were more likely to have a well-defined HFpEF, supported by the conventional diagnostic criteria and greater access to advanced imaging and biomarker assessment, potentially attenuating the association between HFpEF-ABA scores and hard clinical endpoints.

The differential performance of the HFpEF-ABA score between regions reflects fundamental differences in population heterogeneity and diagnostic certainty rather than intrinsic geographic variation in score validity. In the Russia/Georgia cohort, enrollment without mandatory natriuretic peptide confirmation resulted in substantial variability in disease certainty and baseline risk, allowing simple clinical variables to capture prognostic information more effectively. Conversely, the Americas cohort comprised a more homogeneous population with confirmed HFpEF and uniformly elevated risk, leading to compressed risk distributions and ceiling effects that inherently limit the discriminatory capacity of simple scores. This phenomenon exemplifies the well-established spectrum effect in prognostic testing, whereby discrimination is maximized in heterogeneous populations and attenuated in uniformly high-risk cohorts.

Mechanistically, the HFpEF-ABA score in the Russia/Georgia cohort likely operated as a general cardiovascular risk marker rather than a strictly HFpEF-specific prognosticator. Age, obesity, and AF are powerful predictors of adverse outcomes across a wide range of cardiovascular conditions. In diagnostically uncertain and resource-constrained settings, these variables may simultaneously enrich for true HFpEF cases and stratify overall cardiovascular risk. Clinically, this dual role supports the use of the HFpEF-ABA score as an early screening and triage tool in heterogeneous populations, guiding further diagnostic evaluation and resource allocation. In contrast, well-phenotyped populations with high baseline risk require more sophisticated, multidimensional approaches to achieve incremental prognostic value.

Study Limitations

Several limitations should be considered. First, this observational secondary analysis cannot establish causality between HFpEF-ABA score and outcomes. Second, potential HFpEF misclassification in the Russia/Georgia cohort, given lower natriuretic peptide levels and fewer typical features, likely contributed to diagnostic heterogeneity affecting score performance. Third, we were unable to disentangle the relative contributions of biological pathophysiology vs health care system factors, including diagnostic practices, treatment availability, and quality of care, owing to the absence of granular system-level data. Fourth, the retrospective design and geographic restriction limit generalizability, especially to contemporary populations receiving therapies not widely available during TOPCAT enrollment. Finally, residual confounding from unmeasured factors such as treatment intensification, specialist access, and comorbidity management cannot be excluded. Prospective studies incorporating standardized diagnostic criteria, detailed health care characterization, and contemporary treatment strategies are needed to further define the optimal role of the HFpEF-ABA score across diverse clinical contexts.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates substantial regional heterogeneity in the prognostic utility of the HFpEF-ABA score among patients with HFpEF enrolled in the TOPCAT trial. The score exhibited strong associations with adverse outcomes in the Russia/Georgia cohort, whereas its prognostic discrimination was attenuated in the Americas cohort. These differences appear to reflect variations in population heterogeneity and diagnostic certainty rather than geographic superiority or inferiority of the score itself. The HFpEF-ABA score showed greater discriminatory capacity in heterogeneous populations with broad enrollment criteria and variable baseline risk, whereas performance was constrained in well-phenotyped cohorts with uniformly high baseline risk. Collectively, these findings support a context-specific role for the HFpEF-ABA score, with particular utility as an early screening and risk stratification tool in diagnostically uncertain and resource-limited settings.

Key genes and immune infiltration patterns and the clinical implications in psoriasis patients
Authors: Xinyu Zhang, Luyi Tan, Chenyu Zhu, Min Li, Wenli Cheng, Wenji Zhang, Yibo Chen, Wenjuan Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPsoriasis is an immune‐mediated skin disease, closely related to immune regulation. The aim was to understand the pathogenesis of psoriasis further, reveal potential therapeutic targets, and provide new clues for its diagnosis, treatment, and prevention.Materials and methodsExpression profiling data were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database for skin tissues from healthy population and psoriasis patients. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were selected for Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) analysis separately. Machine learning algorithms were used to obtain characteristic genes closely associated with psoriasis. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to assess the diagnostic value of the characteristic genes for psoriasis. The Cell‐type Identification by Estimating Relative Subsets of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT) algorithm was used to calculate the proportion of immune cell infiltration. Correlation analysis was used to characterize the connection between gene expression and immune cell, Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI).ResultsA total of 254 DEGs were identified in the psoriasis group, including 185 upregulated and 69 downregulated genes. GO was mainly enriched in cytokine‐mediated signaling pathway, response to virus, and cytokine activity. KEGG was mainly focused on cytokine‐cytokine receptor interaction and IL‐17 signaling pathway. GSEA was mainly in chemokine signaling pathway and cytokine‐cytokine receptor interaction. The machine learning algorithm screened nine characteristic genes C10orf99, GDA, FCHSD1, C12orf56, S100A7, INA, CHRNA9, IFI44, and CXCL9. In the validation set, the expressions of these nine genes increased in the psoriasis group, and the AUC values were all > 0.9, consistent with those of the training set. The immune infiltration results showed increased proportions of macrophages, T cells, and neutrophils in the psoriasis group. The characteristic genes were positively or negatively correlated to varying degrees with T cells and macrophages. Nine characteristic genes were highly expressed in the moderate to severe psoriasis group and positively correlated with PASI scores.ConclusionHigh levels of nine characteristic genes C10orf99, GDA, FCHSD1, C12orf56, S100A7, INA, CHRNA9, IFI44, and CXCL9 were risk factors for psoriasis, the differential expression of which was related to the regulation of immune system activity and PASI scores, affecting the proportions of different immune cells and promoting the occurrence and development of psoriasis.Keywords: bioinformatics, differentially expressed genes (DEGs), inflammatory cells, psoriasis

INTRODUCTION

Psoriasis is a skin disease characterized by recurrent, chronic, and immune‐mediated. 1 The World Health Organization (WHO) showed that psoriasis affected about 10 billion people worldwide in 2016 global report. 2 Psoriasis incidence has no gender discrepancy, but related with age. Children have lower prevalence and morbidity than those of adults. 3 The clinical manifestations of the disease are not only limited to skin involvement, but also affect arthrosis and other organs. In psoriasis patients, there is an increased risk of psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease, metabolic diseases, and mental disorders, resulting in huge economic, physical, and psychological burdens. 4 Damaged skin usually presents as well‐circumscribed, silvery scaly red plaques that usually affect the scalp, elbows, knees, trunk, and presacral region. 5 Psoriasis can be divided into different subtypes according to dermatological manifestations, including psoriasis vulgaris, inverse psoriasis, guttate psoriasis, and pustular psoriasis, of which psoriasis vulgaris is the most common. 6 Genetic and environmental factors influence the onset of psoriasis. Genetic factors play a key role in the development of psoriasis. There is an association between human leukocyte genes (HLA) and psoriasis. Environmental factors such as smoking, alcohol, stress, and infection can also aggravate psoriasis. 7

The immune function plays an important role in psoriasis. Understanding the involvement of immune function in psoriasis, as well as the interaction between the innate and adaptive immune systems, has aided in the management of this complicated illness. 8 A variety of immune cells, including neutrophils, dendritic cells, macrophages, T lymphocytes, mast cells, keratinocytes, and inflammatory factors, play a central role in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. 9 Psoriasis patients' immune cell activation causes the release of pro‐inflammatory cytokines, including IL‐17, IFN‐γ, and TNFα, which leads to persistent inflammation, multiple tissue damage, and organ damage. 10

The study was to investigate the genetic and immunological factors underlying psoriasis and their potential clinical significances. Determining the key genes in psoriasis pathogenesis would gain insights into the molecular mechanisms. The immune infiltration patterns in psoriasis patients will provide valuable information about the inflammatory processes in skin tissue. This research will advance the precision medicine approaches and provide novel biomarkers for the diagnosis and management of psoriasis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Collection of microarray data

Four gene expression profiles GSE14905, GSE78097, GSE182740, and GSE13355 were downloaded from the GEO database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo) and produced by the platform of [HG‐U133_Plus_2] Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array (GPL570). All data in these datasets were from Homo sapiens. GSE14905, GSE78097, and GSE182740 were merged as a training dataset. GSE13355 was used as a validation set. The dataset included normal skin samples from healthy population, diseased skin, and non‐lesion skin samples from psoriasis patients. Skin samples from healthy people were included as the control group, and lesion skin samples from psoriasis patients were used as the psoriasis group. Meanwhile, normal skin samples from psoriasis patients were excluded.

Data pre‐processing

The probe IDs of each dataset were converted into gene symbols. Subsequently, the three datasets GSE14905, GSE78097, and GSE182740 were merged. To genes with multiple times, the mean value was taken as the only expression value of the gene. Unexpressed genes were removed. To mitigate batch effect in microarray expression data, the ComBat function included in the R package “sva” was used to correct batch effects.

Identification of DEGs

The DEGs were identified by the R package “limma” with the cut‐off criteria for |logFC| > 2.0 and adjusted p < 0.05. When logFC > 0 and p adj < 0.05, the DEGs were judged to be upregulated genes. When logFC < 0 and p adj < 0.05, the DEGs were determined to be downregulated genes. Furthermore, volcano plots and heat maps were generated by R software (version 4.3.1).

Enrichment analysis

GO analysis was widely used in functional enrichment research of a large number of genes. GO had a total of three ontology, including biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) of genes. The KEGG database contained intermolecular interaction and reaction networks, including metabolism, genetic information processing, environmental information processing, cellular processes, biological systems, and human diseases and drug development. GSEA was used to study the DEGs of the control group and psoriasis group to identify the biological pathway of enrichment defined by gene sets between the two groups. The “clusterProfiler” package was used for GO, KEGG, and GSEA analysis. p value < 0.05 and false discovery rate (FDR) (q value) < 0.05 were regarded as statistically significant.

Screening model genes based on LASSO and SVM algorithms

LASSO and SVM algorithms were used for characteristic gene screening. LASSO regression analysis was applied by the R package “glmnet.” The penalty coefficient (Log λ) was calculated under the partial likelihood deviation of 10‐fold cross‐validation. The best logarithmic λ value was determined according to the minimum binomial deviation. SVM was performed by the R package “e1071,” “kernLab,” and “de‐character.” The value of the variable corresponding to the minimum root mean square error (RMSE) represented the number of candidate genes screened. Then, the genes screened by the two methods were intersected and the characteristic genes were obtained.

ROC

The R package “pROC” was used to analyze the ROC curve to determine the specificity and sensitivity of all possible thresholds of the ROC curve. Based on the area under the curve (AUC), the diagnostic value of these genes was predicted.

Immune infiltration level analysis

The CIBERSORT algorithm in IOBR was used to calculate the relative proportion of 22 kinds of immune cell infiltration between the control group and the psoriasis group. The sum of all immune cells was 100%. The difference in immune cell abundance between the two groups was analyzed by the Wilcoxon rank sum test. The visual analysis was carried out by the “ggplot2” package.

Analysis of clinical indicators

The PASI was used to assess the severity of psoriatic skin lesions in each patient. The dataset containing PASI indicators GSE182740 was used for clinical analysis. Depending on the severity of the condition, the patients were split into two subgroups: PASI≤3 represented mild psoriasis and PASI > 3 represented moderate to severe psoriasis.

Statistical analysis

R software (version 4.3.1) and Graphpad Prism (version 9.5) were used for data processing, statistical analysis, and mapping. The Mann‐Whitney U test identified the differences between the two groups. The correlations were analyzed with Spearman's correlation test. The p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Data pre‐processing and identification of DEGs

Before removing the batch effect from the combined dataset, the principal component analysis plot showed that the three datasets, including GSE14905, GSE78097, and GSE182740, were quite different and had obvious batch effects as shown in Figure 1A. After removing the batch effect, there was no significant separation of data between the three datasets from Figure 1B. In the GEO database of psoriasis, 254 DEGs had 185 upregulated and 69 downregulated genes in the psoriasis groups as shown in Figure 1C. The top 25 upregulated genes and 25 downregulated genes were shown in the heatmap, respectively, in Figure 1D.

GO, KEGG, and DO enrichment analyses of DEGs

To the 254 DEGs, GO and KEGG were used to analyze BP, CC, MF, and the biological pathways separately. According to the results, BP of these 254 DEGs mainly accounted for 22 GO terms, including cytokine‐mediated signaling pathway, response to virus, defense response to virus, defense response to symbiont, response to lipopolysaccharide, response to molecule of bacterial, etc. CC was mainly enriched in 13 GO terms, including spindle, secretory granule lumen, cytoplasmic vesicle lumen, and cornified envelope. MF was mainly enriched in 20 GO terms like receptor ligand activity, cytokine activity, endopeptidase activity, cytokine receptor binding, serine‐type endopeptidase activity, serine‐type peptidase activity, serine hydrolase activity, etc. The results of GO analysis were demonstrated by a bar graph as shown in Figure 2A. The results of KEGG analysis showed that the 254 DEGs were mainly enriched in 19 pathways, including Cytokine‐cytokine receptor interaction, IL‐17 signaling pathway, Influenza A, Viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptor, NOD‐like receptor signaling pathway and chemokine signaling pathway, etc. The results were presented in a bar graph of Figure 2B. The results of GSEA enrichment analysis in normal control group and psoriasis group were revealed. The control group was mainly enriched in aldosterone regulated sodium reabsorption, axon guidance, focal adhesion, melanoma, and WNT signaling pathway from Figure 2C. The psoriasis group was enriched in chemokine signaling pathway, cytokine‐cytokine receptor interaction, cytosolic DNA sensing pathway, and proteasome and pyrimidine metabolism in Figure 2D.

Screening characteristic genes with two machine learning algorithms

LASSO and SVM algorithms were used to screen characteristic genes of DEGs between psoriatic and healthy group. Sixteen genes were identified by LASSO algorithm in Figure 3A. According to the SVM method, 37 related characteristic genes were identified at the minimum error from Figure 3B. Based on the intersection of the genes screened by the two algorithms, nine characteristic genes were identified, including C10orf99, GDA, FCHSD1, C12orf56, S100A7, INA, CHRNA9, IFI44, and CXCL9 as shown in Figure 3C. We visualized the expression levels of nine characteristic genes in the validation set as shown in Figure 3F. The results showed that the expression level of each gene was elevated, consistently with the results in the training set in Figure 3D,E.

Meanwhile, the genes were verified in the validation set as shown in Figure S1. The AUC for characteristic genes were all above 0.9. Specifically, they were as follows, C10orf99 with AUC = 1, GDA with AUC = 0.999, FCHSD1 with AUC = 0.989, C12orf56 with AUC = 0.982, S100A7 with AUC = 0.989, INA with AUC = 0.996, CHRNA9 with AUC = 0.973, IFI44 with AUC = 0.940, and CXCL9 with AUC = 0.956. The ROC curves and AUC values demonstrated high‐level diagnostic performance of the nine characteristic genes according to both the training and validation sets.

Identification of immune infiltration patterns

Immune cells played important roles in psoriasis. We explored the interrelationship between the gene expression profiles of the samples and 22 kinds of immune cells. Stacked plots were used to visualize the distribution of immune infiltration in skin tissue in Figure 5A. A total of 12 immune cells were significantly different between the control and psoriasis groups as shown in Figure 5B. The results showed that naive B cells, M0 Macrophages, M1 Macrophages, M2 Macrophages, Neutrophils, activated memory CD4 T cells, naive CD4 T cells, follicular helper T cells, and gamma delta T cells increased in the diseased skin tissue of psoriasis patients, compared with those of normal skin tissue (p < 0.05). The proportion of resting mast cells, activated NK cells, and plasma cells decreased (p < 0.05). We then explored the correlation between nine characteristic genes and immune cells. The results showed that B cells naive was positively correlated with S100A7. B cells memory was negatively correlated with IFI44. T cells CD8 was negatively correlated with S100A7. T cells follicular helper was positively correlated with nine genes. T cells gamma delta was positively correlated with C12orf56, CHRNA9, CXCL9, IFI44. Macrophages M1 was positively correlated with C10orf99, CXCL9, FCHSD1, GDA, IFI44, and INA. Macrophages M2 and dendritic cells were negatively correlated with C10orf99. And neutrophils were positively correlated with C12orf56, CHRNA9, and S100A7. The results were shown in a heat map in Figure 5C.

DISCUSSION

This study used bioinformatics methods to organize and analyzed three sets of gene expression data, resulting in a total of 256 DEGs. Among them, C10orf99, GDA, FCHSD1, C12orf56, S100A7, INA, CHRNA9, IFI44, and CXCL9 were identified as characteristic genes in psoriasis patients, involved in the formation of cytokine‐mediated signaling pathway, IL‐17, and defense response to virus signaling pathway. Each of the AUC value > 0.9 for these nine genes in the validation set and they were accurate and specific in differentiating psoriasis cases and healthy samples.

The DEGs in psoriasis were primarily enriched in virus defense, cytokine activity, and IL‐17 pathways according to GO and KEGG enrichment analysis, which was in accordance with the previous reports from psoriasis patients of showing a strong correlation between viral infection, cytokine dysregulation, and psoriasis. 11 , 12

C10orf99 was an antimicrobial peptide with broad antimicrobial activity against different microorganisms such as Staphylococcus aureus, Aspergillus, and others, 13 which was significantly upregulated in skin samples of psoriasis patient and imiquimod‐induced psoriasis‐like mice, promoting the development of psoriasis via the proliferation of keratinocytes. 14 A recent study confirmed that GDA was highly expressed in the skin tissue of psoriasis patients, involved in regulating cell proliferation and cell cycle. It also increased the secretion of interleukin‐1β (IL‐1β), IL‐6, and chemokine ligands 2 and 20 (CCL2 and CCL20). 15 FCH and double SH3 domains 1 (Fchsd1) FCHSD1 had double SH3 domains. 16 The expression level of FCHSD1 was significantly increased in COPD mice, which could promote the occurrence and development of emphysema in mice. After knocking out Fchsd1, inflammation and apoptosis were reduced in mice. 17 S100A7 acted as an inflammatory antimicrobial peptide in response to IL17A. 18 Previous studies had shown that increased S100A7 was a typical feature of psoriasis. 19 S100 family members participated in calcium or zinc‐dependent cellular functions, regulating immune mechanisms, cell proliferation, and differentiation. They were encoded by genes located in human epidermal differentiation complex and had been identified as one of the PSORS4 sites for psoriasis. 20 Cholinergic receptor nicotinic alpha 9 subunit (CHRNA9) was involved in autoimmune response. 21 , 22 Interferon (IFN) induced protein 44 (IFI44) negatively regulated the innate immune response after viral infection. 23 However, its expression and immune cell infiltration in psoriasis were still unclear. CXCL9 was represented by IFN‐γ induced, but not affected by IFN‐α/β. CXCL9 promoted polarization of effector Th cells, thereby enhancing immune response and anti‐tumor effects. 24 Another study reported that CXCL9 was also identified as a key biomarker for psoriasis, 25 which suggested that members of the CXC family should be closely monitored in further research. There were few studies on immune‐related functions in C12orf56 and α‐internexin (INA) genes at present.

Our study found that macrophages, neutrophils, and T cells increased in psoriasis patients, and resting mast cells, NK cells, and plasma cells decreased in number. In particular, psoriasis tissue had significantly higher levels of CD4 memory activated T cells and macrophages M0 and M1, which was consistent with the previous researches, which indicated that this specific immune cell type was very necessary for the development and progression of psoriasis. 25 T follicular helper cells (Tfh) could express CXCR5, ICOS, PD‐1, Bcl‐6, and generate IL‐21. In psoriasis, abnormal Tfh cell composition and increased Tfh17 cell subpopulations were associated with PASI scores. 26 When genuine leather γδ T cells were stimulated by IL‐23 in the skin, they released IL‐17. 26 After activation, macrophages could differentiate into different subtypes including M1 macrophages and M2 macrophages, and participate in regulating the pathophysiological processes of psoriasis. 27 Previous studies had shown that increased polarization of M1 macrophages was associated with the severity of psoriasis. Activated macrophages and dendritic cells lead to Th cell differentiation into IFN production γ T cells and Th17 cells, producing IL‐17. Imbalance T cells could infiltrate into the dermis of the skin, leading to amplified immune responses and excessive proliferation of keratinocytes. 28 In the early stages of psoriasis plaque formation, neutrophils were recruited to the dermis for degranulation, release of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and production of vasopressin (LL37) and calprotectin (S100A8/A9), which exacerbated skin inflammation. 29 , 30 Neutrophils were the most abundant cell type to produce IL‐17 in psoriasis lesions. 30 The number of circulating NK and NK‐T cells in the peripheral blood of patients with chronic psoriasis was significantly reduced with an imbalance in immune homeostasis. 31 NKG2C was an activated receptor of NK cells, with an association between NKG2C deficiency and psoriasis. NK cells with high expression of NKG2C could react to virus infected cells and kill autoreactive T cells, thus having an impact on the prevention of psoriasis. 32 Consistent with our analysis, the resting mast cell population was significantly reduced and activated mast cells were enriched in psoriatic skin. 33 IL‐17 mast cells were present in psoriatic plaque and atherosclerotic plaque, and IL‐17 released by mast cells might be associated with increased atherosclerosis and psoriasis related mortality. 34

The orphan G protein–coupled receptor (GPCR) GPR15 was encoded by the gene C10orf99. When the skin of Gpr15l‐deficient mice was transplanted into wild‐type mice, the grafted skin showed less infiltration of CD8+T cells and was protected for a longer time. 35 M1 macrophages mainly played a pro‐inflammatory role, while M2 macrophages had an anti‐inflammatory role. 36 Therefore, C10orf99 was positively correlated with M1 macrophages and negatively correlated with M2 macrophages. CXCL9 was increased in M1 macrophages, and the SIRT1 activator (DCHC) and PPAR γ receptor ligand (rosiglitazone) were decreased the expression of CXCL9. 37 In the psoriasis study, the specific mechanism of genes and immune cells remains to be clarified.

PASI was currently the most widely accepted indicator for measuring the severity of psoriasis, which was evaluated by clinical doctors based on the degree, thickness, and scale of erythema in four body parts: the head, trunk, upper limbs, and lower limbs. 38 To further investigate the relationship between genes and psoriasis, we evaluated the expression of characteristic genes in mild and moderate to severe psoriasis patients by using PASI indicators. The results showed that compared with the mild group of psoriasis, the gene expression in the moderate to severe group was significantly increased. The correlation analysis results showed a positive correlation between gene expression levels and PASI. These findings indicated that nine characteristic genes played a definite role in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. S100A7 protein in the serum of psoriasis patients was also significantly elevated in early study. Meanwhile, no relationship had been found between S100A7 and disease severity. 39

In addition, this study also had some certain limitations. The data was from the GEO database, the type and number of samples included in the study were limited, and there might be a deviation between the control group and the psoriasis group. Relevant experiments need to be conducted to verify the results in our future studies.

CONCLUSION

The nine characteristic genes C10orf99, GDA, FCHSD1, C12orf56, S100A7, INA, CHRNA9, IFI44, and CXCL9 were related to the pathogenesis of psoriasis, playing an important role in its development and progression. According to the correlation of immune infiltration and the characteristic genes, they were related to the regulation of immune system activity, which further revealed the immune factors of psoriasis. The expression of C10orf99, GDA, FCHSD1, C12orf56, S100A7, INA, CHRNA9, IFI44, and CXCL9 genes increased in patients with moderate and severe psoriasis, and was positively correlated with PASI score, respectively. The results will provide new clues for the pathogenesis mechanism, the potential therapeutic methods, and drug targets of psoriasis disease.

Construction of Medium-Sized Benzodisilacycles via Palladium-Catalyzed Intermolecular C(sp3)–H Silylation
Authors: Yingying Zhang, Feifan Gao, Chong Li, Yangjie Wu, Ze-Shui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Silacycles have attracted significant attention due to their broad applications in synthetic chemistry, medicinal chemistry, and materials science. However, the synthesis of silacyclesparticularly medium-sized ringsremains a formidable challenge. Herein, we report a general and efficient method for accessing a new family of nine- to eleven-membered benzodisilacycles bearing an additional heteroatom (O, S, N, or Si) on the ring scaffolds via palladium-catalyzed intermolecular silylation of primary and secondary C­(sp3)–H bonds. Readily available ortho-ether, thioether, tertiary amine, and silyl-substituted aryl iodides, as well as cyclic disilanes, serve as the building blocks, laying the foundation for diversity-oriented synthesis of these novel silacyclic scaffolds (>100 examples). Other salient features include excellent chemoselectivities, broad functional group tolerance, simple reaction conditions, low catalyst loading, and scalability. The key to the high efficiency of this disilylation reaction lies in the employment of cyclic disilanes as the silylation reagents. The synthetic practicality of the current strategy is further proven by the late-stage modification of pharmaceuticals and bioactive compounds.

Introduction

Silacycles, as structurally important motifs in silicon-containing functional molecules, have garnered increasing attention due to their diverse applications in synthetic chemistry, medicinal chemistry, and materials science − (Figure A). Therefore, the development of efficient methods to assemble structurally diverse silacycles is a significant and urgent goal. Typical strategies for synthesizing silacycles include ring expansion of strained silacycles, − hydrosilylation of unsaturated bonds, − and C–H silylation. − Among these, transition-metal-catalyzed C–H silylation has proven to be a robust and straightforward method for constructing such frameworks, offering distinct advantages such as readily available starting materials, high efficiency, and step economy. While substantial progress has been achieved in the synthesis of normal-sized ringsincluding five-, − six-, − and seven- − membered silacyclesvia intra- or intermolecular C–H silylation, the preparation of medium-sized silacycles, − particularly those with nine- to eleven-membered rings, remains underexplored (Figure B). The synthetic challenges for these larger ring systems likely arise from unfavorable transannular interactions, as well as entropic and enthalpic factors. In addition, the 3d orbitals of silicon can readily interact with metal ions and heteroatoms to form hypervalent five- or six-coordinated intermediates, which often destabilize organosilanes and lead to decomposition. To date, only a limited number of metal-catalyzed C–H silylation strategies have been reported for the construction of ten-membered silacycles. In 2023, the Yang group developed an elegant palladium-catalyzed cascade C–H silacyclization of alkene-tethered aryl halides using a cyclic tetrasilane reagent, enabling the efficient synthesis of three classes of silacycles, including ten-membered ring scaffolds. More recently, the Han group extended the utility of this tetrasilane reagent to the synthesis of chromone-fused ten-membered silacycles via an interrupted Catellani reaction. However, both protocols are constrained to C­(sp2)–H silylation, and the resulting medium-sized silacycles exhibit limited structural diversity.

C­(sp3)–H functionalization, a major branch of C–H activation, plays a pivotal role in synthetic chemistry. Despite significant advances in constructing silacycles via C­(sp2)–H silylation, transition-metal-catalyzed silacyclization of inert C­(sp3)–H bonds remains a substantial challenge. − This is primarily attributed to the high bond dissociation energies, low acidity, and unreactive molecular orbital profiles of C­(sp3)–H bonds. To date, studies in this field have remained scarce, with existing reports limited to the synthesis of five- to seven-membered silacycles. − , To the best of our knowledge, the construction of medium-sized nine- to eleven-membered silacycles via intermolecular C­(sp3)–H silylation has not yet been reported, representing a critical gap in this field.

Following our continuous interest in silacycle synthesis, , we envisioned a new strategy for the construction of medium-sized silacycles via intermolecular C­(sp3)–H silylation. As depicted in Figure C, in the presence of a palladium catalyst, an ortho-substituted aryl halide 1 undergoes sequential oxidative addition and intramolecular C­(sp3)–H activation to form a five-membered palladacycle intermediate. This intermediate subsequently reacts with a cyclic disilane − 2 via ring expansion to produce the target medium-sized benzosilacycle. If successful, this transformation will produce silacycles featuring two silicon atoms at the ring junction. Additionally, a heteroatom (e.g., O, S, N, Si) can be incorporated into the ring backbone by using heteroatom-containing substrates. Given the broad availability of aryl halides, this strategy offers a general and modular platform for accessing structurally diverse medium-sized disilacycles that are otherwise inaccessible. Notably, the carbon analogue of these medium-sized disilacycles constitute a prominent structural motif in many bioactive molecules. , Therefore, considering the importance of the “silicon switch” in drug and functional molecule discovery, , investigations of these medium-sized silacycles are of great significance and highly desirable. Herein, we report the first example of synthesizing such novel nine- to eleven-membered benzodisilacycles that incorporate an additional heteroatom (O, S, N, or Si) on the ring scaffolds via Pd-catalyzed intermolecular silylation of diverse primary and secondary C­(sp3)–H bonds.

Results and Discussion

We initiated our studies with a model reaction employing readily accessible 1-iodo-2-methoxybenzene (1–1) and 1,1,2,2-tetramethyl-1,2-disilinane (2a) as the reactants (Table ). After extensive exploration of the reaction parameters (see Supporting Information for details), the optimized reaction conditions were established as follows: Pd­(dba)2 (1 mol %) as the catalyst (no additional ligand is needed), CsOPiv (2.0 equiv) as the base, and DMSO as the solvent, at 110 °C under argon for 18 h, which afforded the desired ten-membered disilacycle 3a in 84% yield (entry 1). Control experiments were subsequently conducted to evaluate the role of each component. Unsurprisingly, no desired product was formed in the absence of the palladium catalyst or base (entries 2–3). Use of Pd­(OAc)2 instead of Pd­(dba)2 resulted in a slightly decreased yield (79%) (entry 4). Examination of bases, including KOPiv, CsOAc, and Cs2CO3, indicated that all of them were inferior to CsOPiv (entries 5–7). DMF was found to be a viable solvent alternative to DMSO with comparable efficiency (entry 8). Lowering the reaction temperature to 100 °C led to a diminished yield (entry 9). Reducing the palladium catalyst loading to 0.5 mol % furnished the product in a slightly lower but still acceptable yield of 73% (entry 10). However, the desired product was not formed when aryl bromide was employed instead of aryl iodide (entry 11).

With the optimal reaction conditions in hand, we set out to explore the substrate scope (Table ). Gratifyingly, a broad range of 2-iodoanisole derivatives bearing electron-donating, electron-withdrawing, or electron-neutral substituents were all well tolerated, providing the desired disilacycles in moderate to high yields (Table A). Various functional groups at the ortho-, meta-, or para-positions of anisoles were all compatible, such as alkyl (3b, 3c, 3q, 3v), ether (3d, 3r, 3w), amino (3e), halogen (3f-h, 3s), aryl (3i, 3p), formyl (3j), acetyl (3k), ester (3l), cyano (3m), trifluoromethyl (3n), and amide (3o). These functional groups provide valuable handles for further manipulation of products. Polysubstituted and fused bicyclic aryl iodides were also investigated and afforded the desired products in good yields (3x-E). Moreover, heteroaryl iodides were also demonstrated to be suitable substrates (3B-E). Remarkably, when seven-membered disilanes were employed as coupling partners, the reaction enabled the formation of 11-membered disilacycles in good yields (3F, 3G). Interestingly, a double C–H silylation process can be employed with this strategy to facilitate the simultaneous assembly of dibenzosilacycles. For example, respective reactions of 1,5-diiodo-2,4-dimethoxybenzene, 1,4-diiodo-2,5-dimethoxybenzene, and 2,6-diiodo-3,7-dimethoxynaphthalene with 2a smoothly yielded the tetrasilylated products featuring two fused ten-membered disilacycles in high yields (3H-J). Similarly, the chiral binaphthyl-based double C–H silylation product 3K was obtained in 86% yield. The ability to construct four new C–Si bonds and two medium-sized disilacycles simultaneously in a single operation highlights the excellent step economy of this protocol.

In addition to silylation of primary C­(sp3)–H bonds α to the O atom, we further investigated the more challenging silylation of inert secondary C­(sp3)–H bonds, which are less accessible due to greater steric hindrance (Table B). After a careful modification of the reaction conditions, we were delighted to find that the desired product 3L could be successfully obtained in 75% yield through the use of toluene as the solvent, MePhos as the ligand, and 18-crown-6 as an additive. As with the primary C­(sp3)–H silylation, substrates bearing varied substitutions on both the iodophenyl and tolyl rings were well tolerated, providing the desired disilacycles (3L-V) in moderate to high yields.

We then turned our attention to the silylation of C­(sp3)–H bonds α to the S atom , (Table ). Compared with O atoms, the functionalization of C–H bonds at the α-position of S atoms presents greater challenges, primarily due to the tendency of the sulfide unit to poison the metal catalyst. Indeed, under the aforementioned standard conditions, the substrate 2-iodothioanisole remained completely unreactive. We were pleased to find that by increasing the catalyst loading to 5 mol %, employing XPhos as the ligand, and 18-crown-6 as an additive, the S-containing disilacycles 4a could be obtained in synthetically useful yield (46%). Further exploration of substituent effects on the phenyl ring revealed that electron-withdrawing groups (4b–d, 4f) enhanced the reaction efficiency, whereas the electron-donating methyl substituent (4e) led to a poor yield.

Notably, this catalytic system is also suitable for the silylation of C­(sp3)–H bonds α to the N atom. As shown in Table , under identical reaction conditions employed for the O atom-adjacent primary C­(sp3)–H silylation, a series of 2-iodo-N-methylanilines bearing electronically diverse substituents underwent this silylation smoothly, affording the corresponding N-containing disilacycles with yields of up to 90%. Various functional groups at the meta- and/or para-positions of anilines were well tolerated, including alkyl (5b, 5j, 5o), ether (5c, 5r, 5s), fluoride (5d, 5k, 5p, 5t), chloride (5e, 5l, 5q), bromide (5f, 5m), ester (5g, 5n, 5w), trifluoromethyl (5h), and nitro (5i). The structure of 5d (CCDC 2518911) was unambiguously confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis. Heterocycle-fused bicyclic aryl iodides also delivered the desired products (5r, 5s) in good yields. In contrast, a substituent at the ortho-position of aniline significantly hindered the reaction (5t). Beyond the N-Ts group, N-Ms (5s-u), N-Boc (5v), or N-Me (5w) substituted anilines were also suitable substrates. Pleasingly, 11-membered disilacycle 5x was obtained in 40% yield when the seven-membered disilane was employed as the silylation reagent.

Encouraged by these results, we further extended this strategy to the silylation of C­(sp3)–H bonds α to the Si atom. Using 2-(methylsilyl)­aryl triflates as substrates, the silylation reaction proceeded smoothly with Pd­(OAc)2 as the catalyst in a mixed solvent of DMA and MeCN, yielding the silylated cyclic products bearing three silicon atoms at the ring junction (Table ). A wide variety of meta- and/or para-substituted aryl triflates were amenable to this protocol, providing the corresponding trisilacycles (6a–j) in good to excellent yields. Moreover, different alkyl-substituted methylsilanes were evaluated and furnished the desired products (6k-m) in high yields. Notably, the use of a cyclic methylsilane led to the formation of a spiro trisilacycle (6n) in 41% yield.

Furthermore, this protocol further demonstrates its efficacy in the silylation of primary C­(sp3)–H bonds lacking adjacent heteroatoms. As shown in Table , a variety of aryl iodides containing a quaternary benzylic methyl group exhibited compatibility with the reaction, affording the corresponding disilacycles (7a–n) in moderate to high yields (44–89%). Notably, the gem-diester-containing substrate yielded the silacycle product 7e in 73% yield, concomitant with the removal of one ester group. In addition to disilane 2a, octamethyl-1,4-dioxacyclohexasilane was also amenable to the reaction, affording the desired silacycle 7o bearing four silicon atoms at the ring junction in 60% yield. Pleasingly, when five- and seven-membered cyclic disilanes were used as the silylation reagents, nine- and 11-membered disilacycles 7p and 7q were obtained in 40% and 80% yields, respectively. The lower yield of the former may be attributed to its higher rigidity. It should be noted, however, that aryl iodides bearing tertiary or secondary benzylic carbons exhibited incompatibility under these reaction conditions.

Given the significance of silicon incorporation in the discovery of new drugs and functional molecules, , we performed late-stage modification of several marketed drugs and bioactive compounds. As shown in Scheme A, 2-iodoanisoles derived from mecarbinate (8), estrone (9), l-tyrosine (10), probenecid (11), perphenazine (12), isoxepac (13), and ibuprofen (14) reacted smoothly with disilinane 2a, affording the corresponding disilacycles in excellent yields. To further demonstrate the synthetic utility of the present protocol, scale-up synthesis and subsequent product transformations were carried out (Scheme B). Using only 0.5 mol % of Pd­(dba)2 as the catalyst, the N-containing disilacycle 5f was obtained in 81% yield on a 1.0 mmol scale reaction. Notably, the bromo substituent on 5f remained intact, offering opportunities for downstream transformations. A series of cross-coupling reactions, including Buchwald-Hartwig amination, Sonogashira coupling, and Suzuki coupling, proceeded efficiently, delivering the corresponding products (16–18) in excellent yields. Homocoupling of 5f via a one-pot sequential Miyaura borylation/Suzuki coupling afforded the symmetrical dibenzosilacycle 19 in 95% yield. Additionally, the N-Ts group of 5f was smoothly removed through magnesium-mediated deprotection in methanol. Moreover, the disilacycle was also found to be susceptible to ring-opening transformation. Treatment of 3a with NIS cleaved the C­(aryl)–Si bond, affording the iodinated product 20 in 75% yield. Similarly, reaction with NBS gave the corresponding brominated product 21 in 70% yield.

To investigate the reactivity of this cyclic disilane reagent in the disilylation reaction, we conducted a series of control experiments (Table ). Under the respective standard reaction conditions, five different types of substrates were reacted with the cyclic disilane 2a and hexamethyldisilane 2a’, respectively. The results showed that the latter afforded the corresponding disilylated products (3-7a’) only in low yields. Furthermore, intermolecular competitive experiments also revealed that the ten-membered silacycles (3-7a) were the predominant products. These results demonstrate that the cyclic disilane exhibits remarkably high reactivity in this C­(sp3)–H silylation reaction, which is likely attributed to its weak ring strain.

To gain some insights into the mechanism of this disilylation reaction, control experiments were carried out (Scheme ). First, deuterium-labeling experiments were conducted (Scheme A). When the deuterated substrate 1–1-d 3 containing an ortho-OCD3 group was subjected to the standard conditions, the corresponding deuterated product 3a-d 2 was obtained in 89% yield with no deuterium loss at the methene moiety. In contrast, partial deuteration (20% D) was observed at the ortho-C–H bond of the phenyl ring. The reaction of 1–60-d 3 with 2a yielded similar results. These results indicate that part of the deuterium in the methyl group migrated to the phenyl ring during the reaction. Subsequently, kinetic isotope effect (KIE) experiments were also investigated (Scheme B). The k H/k D values of 2.8 and 2.0 obtained from parallel reactions using substrates 1–1/1–1-d 3 and 1–60/1–60-d 3, respectively, suggest that the C­(sp3)–H cleavage might be the rate-determining step. Furthermore, we successfully prepared the five-membered palladacycle 22, which reacted with disilane 2a in the absence of Pd­(dba)2 to afford the desired product 3a in an excellent yield of 84%. Notably, a catalytic amount of 22 also efficiently triggered the reaction between 1–1 and 2a, providing 3a in 86% yield (Scheme C). These results suggest that the five-membered palladacycle may be the reaction intermediate. To compare the relative reactivity of different types of C­(sp3)–H bonds, an intermolecular competitive experiment using equimolar amounts of 1–1 and 1–60 was performed (Scheme D). The corresponding silylated products were obtained in identical 48% yields, indicating that the C­(sp3)–H bonds at the α-position to O and N exhibit comparable reactivity in this Pd-catalyzed silylation reaction.

Based on the above experimental results and previous reports, ,,− we proposed a plausible mechanism for this Pd-catalyzed C­(sp3)–H silylation reaction, as shown in Scheme E. The catalytic cycle commences with the oxidative addition of the aryl iodide to the Pd0 catalyst, affording aryl PdII species A. Subsequently, base-promoted intramolecular C­(sp3)–H activation takes place, leading to the formation of five-membered palladacycle intermediate B. Protonation of B yields complex C, which regenerates B via a C­(sp2)–H activation step. This reversible process leads to partial deuteration of the phenyl ring. Next, the five-membered palladacycle B transforms into the medium-sized palladacycle intermediates E or E’ via two possible pathways: oxidative addition/reductive elimination (path a) or transmetalation (path b). According to the results presented in Scheme C and previous reports, , path a is more favorable. Finally, reductive elimination of E or E’ generates the desired disilacycle product while regenerating the Pd0 catalyst.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have developed the first palladium-catalyzed intermolecular silylation of diverse primary and secondary C­(sp3)–H bonds in ortho-substituted aryl iodides with cyclic disilanes, enabling the synthesis of five classes of structurally diverse nine- to eleven-membered benzodisilacycles bearing an additional heteroatom (O, S, N, or Si) on the ring scaffolds. This practical method features readily available starting materials, excellent functional group tolerance, the ability to assemble dibenzosilacycles, low catalyst loading, and scalability, offering a valuable synthetic toolbox for constructing novel medium-sized silacycles. The key to the high efficiency of this disilylation reaction lies in the employment of cyclic disilane as the silylation reagent. The late-stage modification of complex molecules and downstream synthetic transformations demonstrate the synthetic potential of this methodology.

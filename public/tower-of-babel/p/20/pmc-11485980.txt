A novel orf virus vector-based COVID-19 booster vaccine shows cross-neutralizing activity in the absence of anti-vector neutralizing immunity
Authors: Ute Klinkhardt, Mirjam Schunk, John Ervin, Christoph Schindler, Danny Sugimoto, Bruce Rankin, Ralf Amann, Martina Monti, Anna Kutschenko, Carsten Schumacher, Kristina Huber, Andreas Zeder, Nelli Heikkila, Arnaud M. Didierlaurent, Sylvia E. Schwarz, Madiha Derouazi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTNext-generation COVID-19 vaccines are being developed to expand the breadth of coverage against existing and future variants and to extend the duration of protection. Prime-2-CoV_Beta is an orf virus (ORFV) based multi-antigen COVID-19 vaccine that co-expresses Spike (S) and Nucleocapsid (N) antigens. The safety and immunogenicity of Prime-2-CoV_Beta is investigated in a phase 1 first-in-human (FIH) dose-finding trial (ORFEUS study, ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05367843). Participants of two age groups (18–55 and 65–85 years) who previously completed at least two doses of mRNA vaccines were enrolled and sequentially assigned to different dose groups to receive one intramuscular dose of 3 × 105, 3 × 106, 1.5 × 107, or 3 × 107 plaque-forming units (PFU) of Prime-2-CoV_Beta on day 1 and a second dose on day 29. Here, we report safety and immunogenicity data collected up to 6 months after the first study vaccination. Prime-2-CoV_Beta is safe and well tolerated and elicits immune responses at higher dose levels in participants aged 18–55. A single dose of 3 × 107 PFU boosted binding and cross-neutralizing antibody responses that are maintained through 6 months after the first booster vaccination. Polyfunctional S-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses are observed after vaccination. No pre-existing or vaccine-induced neutralizing anti-vector antibodies are detected. Our findings highlight the potential of the ORFV vector as a safe platform for future vaccine design, which provides the ability to deliver multiple antigens and allows for repeat immunization.

Introduction

The emergence of COVID-19 has highlighted the need to develop new vaccine platforms to efficiently tackle any future pandemic. While existing SARS-CoV-2 vaccines have profoundly altered the course of the pandemic, the emergence of new variants with the capacity to evade S-specific immune responses continues to pose a challenge for current S-targeting vaccine strategies. In addition, repeat booster-doses of variant-adapted vaccines are needed to address the waning of antibodies and the continued genetic evolution of SARS-CoV-2.1 The development of next-generation COVID-19 vaccines to induce long-lasting cross-protective immune responses against newly emerging SARS-CoV-2 variants is, therefore, subject of intensive research.

To address this challenge, a novel vaccine platform based on the attenuated ORFV strain D1701-VrV, a member of the genus Parapoxvirus, was used to develop a multi-antigen COVID-19 vaccine (Material S1). The ORFV vector platform was specifically designed to support multi-antigenic vaccine concepts. Key benefits of this novel virus vector system are the very restricted host range of wild-type ORFV (sheep and goats), the short-term vector-specific immunity without formation of neutralizing antibodies, enabling repeat immunizations, and its ability to induce potent immunogenicity against foreign antigens.2–4 Prime-2-CoV_Beta is a D1701-VrV-derived multi-antigen SARS-CoV-2 vaccine candidate, expressing full-length S (including the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the Beta variant) and N of the Wuhan Hu-1 strain. The widely conserved N-protein has been proposed as a target for the development of broadly protective next-generation vaccines and has been used in combination with S-protein in clinical vaccine candidates.5–7 Prime-2-CoV_Beta and variant-specific derivatives of Prime-2-CoV have shown robust immunogenicity in various species and protected hamsters and non-human primates against SARS-CoV-2 challenge.8,9

The novel ORFV platform has not previously been tested in humans. Here, we report the results of the FIH clinical trial to assess the safety and immunogenicity of the first clinical vaccine candidate Prime-2-CoV_Beta as a COVID-19 booster in healthy adults previously vaccinated with mRNA vaccines. Our results support the favorable safety and tolerability profile of the novel viral vector platform and demonstrate that heterologous booster immunization with Prime-2-CoV_Beta induces cross-neutralizing antibody responses in participants aged 18–55, which is impacted by pre-existing immunity. Results furthermore revealed the absence of pre-existing or vaccination-induced neutralizing anti-vector immunity, enabling the possibility of repeat immunization, if needed. Overall, these FIH clinical data highlight the potential of the ORFV vector platform for broader applications for future emerging and re-emerging pathogens.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

The ORFEUS study was a phase 1 multi-center, open label, dose-finding trial conducted at two sites in Germany and five in US. The study enrolled healthy adults aged 18–55 and 65–85 who had completed at least two doses of an mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccine (Corminaty/Pfizer-BioNTech or Spikevax/Moderna, primary series with or without booster doses – henceforth referred to as ‘pre-vaccinated’) at least 3 months prior to study entry. Enrollment of vaccine-naïve adults was prematurely discontinued due to feasibility (Figure 1). Participants with a history of prior nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT)-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection in the previous 2 months and those with an NAAT-positive pharyngeal swab were excluded. This report presents the safety and immunogenicity data collected over a period of 6 months following the administration of Prime-2-CoV_Beta. The study met all ethics and regulatory requirements as determined in a review by the US Central Institutional Review Board (Advarra Inc, Columbia, MD, USA), local Institutional Biosafety Committees (IBCs) and the German Central Ethics Committee (Ethikkommission der Medizinischen Hochschule Hannover, Hanover, Germany). All participants provided written informed consent before any study procedures were conducted. The trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05367843). Procedures

Prime-2-CoV_Beta was manufactured and vialed by ABL Europe (France) and Nova Laboratories (UK) according to Good Manufacturing Practices (Material S1). Before each administration, the vaccine was thawed and diluted, as applicable, with sterile saline solution to the appropriate dose. One dose (1.0 mL each) of Prime-2-CoV_Beta was administered by intramuscular injection using the deltoid site on day 1 for the first vaccination and day 29 for the second vaccination.

Part A of the study included four groups of pre-vaccinated adults aged 18–55 to assess Prime-2-CoV_Beta at escalating dose levels (3 × 105, 3 × 106, 1.5 × 107, and 3 × 107 PFU), followed by Part B to assess two dose levels selected from data generated in Part A in pre-vaccinated adults aged 65–85. Participants were sequentially assigned to dose groups (each consisting of 12 subjects). Part A implemented a sentinel dosing and subject staggering design. For each dose level, sentinel subjects were dosed at least 48 h ahead of the remaining sentinel group(s), with intervals of at least 1 hpur (h) between the first seven participants and then at least 30 minutes (min) intervals for the remaining five participants. A safety review committee oversaw the safety of participants and reviewed the accumulating data in support of the dose escalation decisions. For each dose group, decision-making criteria were based on data from the first seven participants up to 7 days, and from the remaining five participants up to 48 h after the first dose. Reactogenicity and immunogenicity data assessed in Part A informed the selection of the 1.5 × 107 and 3 × 107 PFU dose being evaluated in Part B.

Sentinel participants were observed on-site for at least 6 h after each vaccination, all other participants were observed for at least 60 (Part A) or 30 min (Part B) after each vaccination. Participants were also monitored by phone calls at 24 and 48 h after the first vaccination and a phone call or site visit at 48 h/2 days after the second vaccination. Local and systemic solicited Adverse Events (AEs) were recorded daily over 14 days after each vaccination using an eDiary. Unsolicited Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events (TEAEs) were collected for a period of 28 days after each vaccination, Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) and Adverse Events of Special Interest (AESIs) were collected up to 12 months after the first vaccination. Scheduled follow-up visits covered laboratory assessments including biochemistry, hematology, and coagulation tests, vital signs, electrocardiogram (ECG), and cardiac markers. Participants also underwent a nasopharyngeal swab test for COVID-19 before each vaccination and were asked to report any SARS-CoV-2 infection confirmed by NAAT- or antigen-test. Solicited reactions, AEs and laboratory abnormalities were graded according to the US Food and Drug Administration toxicity grading scale.

Blood for immunogenicity testing was collected on day 1 prior to administration of Prime-2-CoV_Beta, and on days 8, 15, 29 (pre-dose), 43, 57, 85, and 183. Serum was processed on site, aliquoted, and stored at −80°C until shipment. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) from whole blood were isolated at sites using Ficoll density gradient centrifugation. Isolated cells were cryopreserved and stored at −80°C, shipped to central depot on dry ice, and were then transferred to liquid nitrogen for shipment and storage until further use.

Serum IgG levels against SARS-CoV-2 Spike-(S1), the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the wild type and Beta variant (RBD-Beta), and Nucleocapsid-(N) protein were quantified by in-house Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) (Method S1). Neutralizing antibodies against the early SARS-CoV-2 strain Wuhan-Hu-1 (Victoria/01/2020) and subsequent variants Beta (B.1.351), Delta (B.1.617.2) and Omicron (BA.5 and XBB.1.5) were measured as median tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50) using a micro-neutralization (MN) assay using live virus cytopathic effect (CPE) defined as MN-CPE (Method S2). Further, exploratory immunogenicity assessments included determination of the avidity of IgG directed toward RBD and RBD-Beta in participants of the highest dose groups (Method S3). Serum IgG antibodies against ORFV were quantified using in-house ELISA. Sera yielding a positive titer >20 underwent further analysis for their ORFV-neutralization capacity by MN-CPE (Method S4). S1- and S2-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells were measured by intracellular flow cytometry following simulation with overlapping peptide pools in a subset of participants aged 18–55 (Method S5).

Outcomes

The primary endpoints for safety were incidence and severity of solicited local and systemic reactions within 7 days after each vaccination, and unsolicited AEs within 28 days. Secondary safety endpoints included solicited reactions within 14 days after each vaccination, laboratory abnormalities, vital signs, ECG, as well as SAEs and AESIs through 12 months after the first dose. Secondary endpoints for immunogenicity were the geometric mean concentration (GMC) of SARS-CoV-2-specific binding IgG antibodies, and the geometric mean titer (GMT) of neutralizing antibodies against Victoria/01/2020 and variants of concern at each time point, and the geometric mean fold rise (GMFR) of GMC and GMT from baseline. Exploratory immunogenicity endpoints included SARS-CoV-2-specific CD4+/CD8+ T cell responses, RBD- and RBD-Beta-specific IgG avidity, and ORFV binding and neutralizing antibody titers.

Statistics

The sample size was estimated to allow a descriptive summary of the safety of different dose levels of Prime-2-CoV_Beta. With 12 participants per dose group, the probability to observe any AE with an event rate of 15% at least once per group is 86.5%. Safety analyses were performed on the safety population, including all participants who received at least one vaccination. Solicited and unsolicited AEs were summarized as counts and percentages. Unsolicited AEs were coded by Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) version 25.1. Immunogenicity analyses were performed in the PPIM. For neutralizing and binding antibodies, GMTs/GMCs and GMFRs along with 95% Confidence Intervals (CIs) were calculated for each dose group. Additional post-hoc immunological analyses included comparison of post-dose humoral and cellular (14 and 28 days after each injection, respectively) responses to baseline, and evaluation of the impact of pre-existing SARS-CoV-2 immunity on Prime-2-CoV_Beta responses. Statistical comparisons used non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon test, Kruskal–Wallis test, Mann–Whitney test, Spearman’s rank test). Multiple comparison issues were not addressed with respect to these post-hoc exploratory immunogenicity analyses. Statistical analyses of immune data were performed using GraphPad Prism (Version 10.0.2 for macOS).

Results

Study participants

Between July 1, 2022, and January 26, 2023, a total of 72 healthy adults who had previously been vaccinated with at least two doses of an mRNA vaccine (primary series with or without booster doses) were enrolled to receive a double dose of Prime-2-CoV_Beta 28 days apart (Figure 1). Among these participants, 48 participants aged 18–55 were recruited into Part A of the study and assigned to four dose levels to receive Prime-2-CoV_Beta at 3 × 105, 3 × 106, 1.5 × 107, or 3 × 107 PFU (12 subjects per group). Additionally, 24 participants aged 65–85 were recruited into Part B and assigned to 2 dose levels to receive Prime-2-CoV_Beta at either 1.5 × 107 or 3 × 107 PFU. Safety

Prime-2-CoV_Beta was overall well tolerated. Across both age groups, the most common solicited reactions within 7 days of any dose were pain at the injection site, headache, fatigue, and myalgia (Figure 2). Most solicited reactions occurred during the first 1–2 days after vaccination and had a short duration, with a median time to resolution of 1–2 days. All solicited reactions were mild or moderate in intensity, except for two reports of severe fatigue and temperature increase 6 and 7 days following the first and second vaccination, respectively, in two participants aged 18–55, one in the 1.5 × 107 and one in the 3 × 106 PFU group, and two reports of intermittent severe headache and fatigue 5–7 days after the second vaccination in a participant aged 65–85 of the 3 × 107 PFU group. Solicited systemic reactions were more common among participants aged 18–55 than in participants aged 65–85. Overall, no clear dose-effect was observed. 44 (61%) of 72 participants reported at least one unsolicited AE within 28 days of any dose. The vast majority of AEs was mild to moderate in intensity and appeared to be distributed similarly across dose levels of both age groups (Table S1). Two severe AEs were reported: one case of serious nausea with emesis in one of the participants aged 18–55 of the 3 × 106 PFU group, and one case of non-serious palpitations in one of the participants aged 65–85 of the 1.5 × 107 PFU group. Both events were not considered related to vaccination. Two unrelated SAEs leading to hospitalization (alcohol abuse and alcohol withdrawal) occurred 26 and 44 days after the second vaccination in a participant aged 18–55 in the 3 × 106 PFU group. No SAEs attributed to Prime-2-CoV_Beta were reported, and none of the prespecified stopping rules were met.

Immunogenicity

In the 18–55 age group, distinct neutralizing antibody responses measured by live SARS-CoV-2 MN-CPE assay were only observed after vaccination with Prime-2-CoV_Beta at a dose of 3 × 107 PFU. Specifically, in this dose group, vaccination with Prime-2-CoV_Beta resulted in a significant 3.2- and 3.5-fold increase in GMTs of neutralizing antibodies against the early SARS-CoV-2 Wuhan-Hu-1 (Victoria/01/2020) and the Beta variant, and a significant 2.3- and 2.5-fold increase in GMTs of cross-neutralizing antibodies against the Delta and Omicron BA.5 variant 28 days after the first vaccination (Figure 3). Neutralizing antibody titers were not further increased following the second dose, however, titers remained elevated over baseline through day 183 in all dose groups. SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibody responses at key time points.

In the group of participants aged 18–55 a single dose of 3 × 107 PFU of Prime-2-CoV_Beta resulted in an increase in S1-, RBD-, RBD-Beta-, and N-binding IgG antibodies after the first vaccination. IgG antibody responses were not further enhanced by a second vaccination (Figure 4, S2). For all dose groups, IgG levels were maintained at or above baseline levels at 6 months after the first vaccination. SARS-CoV-2 binding and neutralizing antibodies were modestly increased in participants aged 65–85 following Prime-2-CoV_Beta at both dose levels. However, antibody titers were similarly maintained at around baseline titers through day 183 in the group of participants aged 65–85 (Figures 3, 4, S1, S2). Antibody avidity maturation

To provide further insight into the antibody affinity maturation after vaccination, the avidity of IgG against the SARS-CoV-2 ancestral RBD and RBD-Beta was evaluated as a surrogate marker for affinity in the 1.5 and 3 × 107 PFU dose groups of both age populations on an exploratory basis. IgG avidity to both RBD and RBD-Beta, expressed as avidity index (AI), was generally high in both age groups at study entry. As for neutralizing and binding antibodies, increases in IgG avidity following Prime-2-CoV_Beta were most evident in participants with low baseline avidity (Figure 6). For the 18–55 age group, a significant correlation between fold-increases in neutralizing titers against Victoria and changes in anti-RBD IgG avidity was found, indicating a possible functional relevance of IgG antibody avidity maturation observed after Prime-2-CoV_Beta vaccination (Figure 6). Using significant binding and neutralizing antibody responses as the basis for assessing T cell responses, CD4+/CD8+ T cell responses were only evaluated in participants aged 18–55 in the 3 × 107 PFU group. Discussion

Here, we report results from the FIH study of the SARS-CoV-2 multi-antigen vaccine Prime-2-CoV_Beta, the first clinical candidate derived from the novel ORFV platform. We found that Prime-2-CoV_Beta administered as a double-dose booster in healthy adults previously vaccinated with mRNA vaccines was well-tolerated. In participants aged 18–55 after a single Prime-2-CoV_Beta dose, enhanced cross-variant neutralizing activity relative to baseline immunity was observed. Importantly, Prime-2-CoV_Beta did not induce neutralizing anti-vector antibodies after two doses, further highlighting the potential of this novel ORFV vaccine platform.

Prime-2-CoV_Beta was safe and well tolerated in both age groups and across all dose levels. Solicited reactions were overall less frequent in participants aged 65–85 consistent with reports from other COVID-19 vaccine studies.10,11 Prime-2-CoV_Beta appeared to have a favorable reactogenicity profile compared to mRNA-based and adjuvanted booster vaccines, solicited reactions reported were milder in nature and included lower incidence of fever, chills, and malaise.11–14 The pattern of solicited reactions did not considerably differ from the reactogenicity profile of adenoviral vectored COVID-19 vaccines, although solicited reactions following Prime-2-CoV_Beta tended to be milder overall.10,15

Prime-2-CoV_Beta administered as a heterologous booster was immunogenic at higher doses in the group of participants aged 18–55. A single vaccine dose of 3 × 107 PFU boosted both binding and neutralizing antibody titers, which were not further enhanced by a second dose. Neutralizing activity was broad in sera of the 3 × 107 PFU group, whereby, however, a reduced activity against the Beta and Delta variant, and a marked reduction in the GMTs of neutralizing antibodies against the Omicron BA.5 variant was observed. Of note, reduced sensitivity of SARS-CoV-2 variants Beta and Delta to antibody neutralization, and a significantly lower neutralizing sensitivity of Omicron variant BA.5 to immune sera elicited by prototype mRNA booster vaccines have been reported.16,17 Cross-neutralization activity against Omicron XBB.1.5 was overall modest, in line with previous reports on mRNA boosters (monovalent original or Omicron-adapted bivalent booster) suggesting reduced neutralization against newly emerging Omicron sublineages.18 Participants aged 65–85 experienced modest changes in neutralizing antibody levels following vaccination with Prime-2-CoV_Beta at the two dose levels. Previous evidence described reduced antibody responses to COVID-19 vaccines in older adults compared to younger and middle-aged adults, which has been attributed to age-related immunosenescence.19,20 Notably, neutralizing antibody titers against the ancestral and SARS-CoV-2 variants showed a promising limited decay in participants of both age groups and were maintained at around baseline titers through day 183 after the first vaccination.

Neutralizing antibody titers in participants aged 18–55 vaccinated with Prime-2-CoV_Beta, as measured by live virus MN-CPE, were generally lower than those reported in previous studies after three or four doses of commercialized mRNA vaccines,16,21,22 although a direct comparison is hampered by the use of different test assays. A possible explanation for the overall modest boosting effect could be that, first, the immunogenicity of Prime-2-CoV_Beta as a booster is still suboptimal at a dose of 3 × 107 PFU and the vaccine could benefit from increasing the dose, particularly in the participants aged 65–85. Second, the boost effect may be diminished in individuals whose baseline response is high from recent infections and/or vaccination. In fact, recent evidence suggests that a ‘ceiling effect’ may be approached for some individuals after repeat booster vaccination.13

Participants aged 18–55 exhibited high baseline cross-neutralizing antibody titers, with 46% of participants aged 18–55 showing detectable Omicron BA.5 neutralizing antibodies by live-virus neutralization and corresponding GMTs that appeared to be higher than reported among SARS-CoV-2 naïve individuals after three doses of homologous mRNA vaccines.21,23 The study was conducted between July 2022 and January 2023 when Omicron and its subvariants were predominant. Seroprevalence of N-specific IgG was relatively high (76%) in participants aged 18–55, suggesting that a substantial proportion of those participants entered the study with hybrid immunity through vaccination and previous breakthrough infections. Of note, recent reports found that Omicron breakthrough infection in triple-immunized individuals is associated with enhanced cross-neutralization against diverse Omicron variants.24,25

To further assess the impact of baseline immunity on Prime-2-CoV_Beta induced immune responses, sera of participants of the highest dose groups were pooled and regrouped according to baseline antibody titers (below and equal to or above GMT). Boosting of neutralizing antibody responses indeed correlated negatively with the levels of pre-existing antibody titers in participants aged 18–55, suggesting that there was limited boosting from Prime-2-CoV_Beta in participants with high pre-existing immunity and there could be a ceiling or maximum neutralizing response for the doses tested. Similar observations were also reported in other studies with homo- and heterologous COVID-19 booster vaccines.13,26,27

Pre-existing or treatment-induced anti-vector immunity is still a major obstacle to achieving effective immunogenicity, especially for adenovirus vector-based vaccine strategies.28,29 In this FIH study, a favorable low prevalence of IgG binding antibodies and the absence of pre-existing neutralizing antibodies to the candidate vector were shown. A trend for an increase in anti-ORFV IgG responses at higher dose levels was observed; however, a double dose of Prime-2-CoV_Beta did not elicit any neutralizing anti-vector immunity up to the highest dose.

This trial has several limitations. Recruitment of vaccine naïve participants was significantly impacted by the rollout of authorized vaccines and led to premature discontinuation of vaccine naïve groups, limiting the exploration of the novel platform. Trial participants exhibited high baseline immunity and considerable heterogeneity regarding prior antigen exposure. Though promising, the durability of immune responses was only assessed up to 6 months after the first booster vaccination, and data on long-term responses are not available. Lastly, the dose range explored was limited and data suggests that the optimal vaccine dose has not yet been reached. Given the favorable tolerability of Prime-2-CoV_Beta, testing of higher doses is warranted to optimize the balance between tolerability and immunogenicity, particularly in the elderly population.

In conclusion, Prime-2-Cov_Beta was shown to be safe and well tolerated and induced cross-neutralizing antibody responses after a single dose of 3 × 107 PFU, dependent on age group and pr-existing antibody levels. Repeated immunization was not associated with the formation of anti-vector neutralizing antibodies, as opposed to other vector-based vaccine platforms. Overall, our findings highlight the potential of this novel ORFV-based platform as an attractive tool for vaccine development.

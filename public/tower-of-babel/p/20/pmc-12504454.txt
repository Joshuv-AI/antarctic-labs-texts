The Metaverse Paradox: Insights from Secondary Analysis of Metaverse Health Survey and Future Strategies
Authors: Ryo Momosaki, Kazuma Tora, Yuka Shirai, Yoko Hasegawa, Kenta Ushida, Ryota Sakamoto, Hiroki Funao
Published: 2025

THE CURRENT LANDSCAPE: HEALTH IMPLICATIONS OF IMMERSION

The metaverse—an immersive, persistent virtual space where users interact through avatars—has become a part of daily life for many people, with engagement often exceeding 3 h per session.1) This deep, embodied immersion presents unique health implications that differ from previous screen-based technologies. The current landscape reveals a clear link between specific user behaviors and health conditions, highlighting the need for a proactive approach to well-being.

A secondary analysis of the Metaverse Health Survey 2025,1) a study of 417 Japanese users, connects usage patterns to key health indicators. Session length is a strong correlate of sedentary behavior; users engaging for over 3 h were significantly more likely to sit for 8 h or longer each day (66.1% vs. 48.0%) (Fig. 1). Furthermore, the novel practice of “VR sleep” (sleeping with a virtual reality headset) correlated with both prolonged sitting (64.5% vs. 50.7%) and a reported worsening of mental health (39.5% vs. 24.4%) (Fig. 2). These findings suggest that unguided, prolonged immersion can negatively impact physical and psychological well-being. Although the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, the associations are robust and clinically meaningful.

THE POTENTIAL: ENVISIONING A THERAPEUTIC DIGITAL SPACE

Despite these considerations, the metaverse holds immense potential as a therapeutic tool. Therefore, the goal is not to curtail its use but to strategically guide its development toward enhancing well-being. This vision is supported by evidence that intentional forms of engagement are associated with positive health outcomes.

The same Metaverse Health Survey also highlighted protective behaviors. For instance, users who logged in while standing reported fewer health problems (49.4% vs. 62.2%) and significantly less physical inactivity (9.9% vs. 21.1%) (Fig. 3). Even more strikingly, participants in metaverse exercise events were less sedentary (42.3% vs. 56.6%) and far more physically active; their rate of inactivity was less than a third of non-participants (7.2% vs. 22.5%) (Fig. 4).

Although unguided use of the metaverse can be problematic, world design, avatar design, and tuning of event content to boost motivation could potentially facilitate health promotion.2) Furthermore, the metaverse can provide a powerful platform for interactive communication,3) and structured social metaverse environments can also reduce loneliness and provide effective peer support.4) The key is to distinguish uncontrolled usage from structured, health-promoting applications, thereby transforming the metaverse from a potential source of health concerns into a versatile health resource.

THE PATH FORWARD: A FRAMEWORK FOR RESOLVING THE PARADOX

Resolving this metaverse paradox requires an interdisciplinary framework focused on behavior, design, and research. This collaborative effort is essential to cultivate a health-promoting metaverse.

First, clinicians and public health advocates must empower users with simple, evidence-based behavioral guidance. Based on the survey data, this includes the preference for a standing posture, limiting uninterrupted session times, and actively participating in integrated exercise events. Educating users on the difference between unguided immersion and structured therapeutic applications is also crucial for responsible engagement.

Second, platform developers bear a significant responsibility to design health-promoting environments by default. Integrating features like user-configurable break reminders, prompts for physical movement, and gamified activity can shift the ecosystem’s baseline from sedentary consumption to active, healthy engagement.

Finally, the research community must advance our understanding through more rigorous methodologies, such as longitudinal studies, to establish causal relationships between metaverse behaviors and long-term health outcomes. Research should focus on optimizing metaverse-based interventions—such as health promotion events that can efficiently reach large populations remotely and simultaneously (Fig. 5)—and overcoming practical barriers like access, cost, and data privacy for clinical applications. By fostering a collaborative framework where clinicians inform design, developers build healthier platforms, and researchers validate efficacy, we can collectively steer the evolution of the metaverse, ensuring it serves as a powerful tool for human health and digital well-being.

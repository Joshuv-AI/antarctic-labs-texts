Causal relationship between inflammatory skin diseases and immunoglobulin A nephropathy: A Mendelian randomization and enrichment analysis study
Authors: Chundi Li, Baisen Wang, Tianrun Han, Ying Xu, Xinhong Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the causal relationship between inflammatory skin diseases (atopic dermatitis, and psoriasis) and IgA nephropathy using Mendelian randomization and enrichment analysis.MethodsThe instrumental variables (IVs) in the European Bioinformatics Institute (EBI) database were used for two‐sample MR analysis. The results of inverse variance weighting (IVW) were used as the main method, the MR−Egger method was used for pleiotropy analysis and the leave‐one‐out method was used for sensitivity analysis to verify the reliability of the data. Combined with the human genome database GeneCards database and Metascape enrichment analysis.ResultsPeople with AD had an increased risk of IgA nephropathy (IVW: OR = 1.06, 95% CI [1.0002–1.1248], p = 0.0491). Psoriasis and IgA nephropathy (IVW: OR = 0.97, 95% CI [0.9394–1.0055], p = 0.1002) no statistical significance, therefore cannot prove cause‐and‐effect relationship between.ConclusionsThis study provides evidence that atopic dermatitis is associated with an increased risk of IgA nephropathy, but does not provide evidence that psoriasis is causologically associated with IgA nephropathy. Enrichment analysis suggested a causal relationship between inflammatory skin diseases and IgA nephropathy at the genetic level.Keywords: atopic dermatitis, enrichment analysis, IgA nephropathy, inflammatory skin diseases, Mendelian randomization, psoriasis

INTRODUCTION

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerular disease worldwide. A diagnostic histological symbol is dominant on the renal biopsy or codominant IgA dyeing. However, patients may exhibit a variety of clinical syndrome, from asymptomatic abnormalities found in urine analysis to the accelerated glomerular nephritis. 1 Up to 20% of patients will develop end‐stage renal disease approximately 40 years after diagnosis. In addition, IgAN is associated with significant mortality. 2 The high incidence of inflammatory skin diseases in recent years has become one of the major public health problems faced by many countries, such as atopic dermatitis and psoriasis. It mainly affects the skin of adults and children, but there may be systemic involvement, especially for arthritis and kidney damage. 3 In recent years, studies have shown that atopic dermatitis and increased risk of kidney damage can cause allergic purpura. 4 In addition, some studies have shown that there is a correlation between inflammatory skin diseases and chronic kidney diseases, 5 but there is no correlation study between inflammatory skin diseases and IgAN. Mendelian randomization (MR) is a research method that uses genetic variants strongly associated with exposure factors as instrumental variable (IV) to infer causal effects between exposure factors and study outcomes. 6 Because genetic variation from female parents remains the same, it will not only satisfy the rationality of the time sequence, but it will also not be easily influenced by traditional confounding factors such as environment and behavior. The causal effect of inflammatory skin diseases on IgAN is not convincing through observational studies, and there is a lack of randomization of exposure factors, so confounding and reverse causality cannot be ruled out in the association. And to explore the inflammatory skin disease with IgAN deeper causal relationship, this study on the enrichment of analysis, to explore the related pathways between them.

THE DATA AND METHODS

Selection of exposure factors

We used single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPS) as the genetic IV for Mendelian analysis, and the data were obtained from publicly available GWAS databases. The exposure factors we identified were atopic dermatitis and psoriasis. All were European populations. Atopic dermatitis (sample size: 481299 snp number: 24185642) 7 ; Number of psoriasis (sample size: 44161 SNPS: 8470172) 8 ; SNPS were obtained from the publicly published dataset of the European Bioinformatics Institute (Table 1). The GWAS data of exposure factors were extracted by R studio, and the extracted SNPS were all closely related to the phenotype (p = 5×10−8), and linkage disequilibrium (LD, r 2 = 0.001, kb = 10,000) was removed. After calculation, F statistics were all greater than 10 to avoid weak IV bias.

Statistical analysis

Two Sample MR software in the R program (version: 4.0.3) was used for statistical analysis. The IVs used by MR Must meet three requirements: (1) They must be closely related to the exposure factors, (2) not correlated with potential confounders, and (3) The outcome should not be affected by factors other than exposure factors. 9 inverse variance weighting (IVW), MR‐Egger, weighted median (WME), and MR Analysis were used, and the results of the IVW method were mainly (p < 0.05 indicated statistical significance).

IVW using meta‐analysis method of causal effect will be obtained from different SNP Wald ratio estimation, the assumptions and genetic variation in each meet IVs, provide consistent estimate the causal effect of factors on the results. Pleiotropy by MR—Egger regression intercept of the judge, p < 0.05 indicating level pleiotropic. 10 Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran's Q statistic, and p < 0.05 indicated the presence of heterogeneity. Sensitivity analysis was performed using the “leave‐one‐out method” (i.e., each SNP was removed in turn and the combined effect of the remaining SNPS was calculated) to evaluate whether a single SNP had an impact on the relationship between exposure and outcome. 11

Enrichment analysis

Through the human genome database GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org /) to obtain the inflammatory skin disease 12 and IgAN related target genes; GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of intersection genes was performed combined with the genome encyclopedia Metascape database (http://metascape.org/).

DISCUSSION

Based on the published GWAS data, this study applied two‐sample MR and enrichment analysis to reveal the causal relationship between inflammatory skin diseases and IgAN. It was found that atopic dermatitis was associated with an increased risk of IgAN. However, no causal relationship was found between psoriasis and IgAN. The results remained robust in sensitivity analyses using different MR Models.

This study will top SNPS in the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) is analyzed, and the enrichment of KEGG pathway analysis income analysis, combining found rs10791824 is egg sample transcription inhibitory factor 1 (OVOL1) variation of introns. Studies have shown that OVOL1 is related to cell epithelial differentiation, and the deletion of OVOL1 can lead to the continuous accumulation of neutrophils, but the effect on the kidney is not comprehensive. 14 rs20541 is an intronic variant of interleukin 13 (IL‐13), which is an important pathway in the pathogenesis of inflammatory nephropathy. 15 rs11130215 is a transcription factor BHLHE40 child variant, the enrichment of analysis and KEGG pathways in cancer pathways, and studies have shown that BHLHE40 can be controlled by influencing the body's own immune system to the pathogenesis of inflammatory bowel disease, the impact on IgAN is unclear. 16 Therefore, it is suggested that inflammatory skin diseases andIgAN have a causal relationship at the gene pathway level.

The pathogenesis of cutaneous inflammatory skin diseases varies, thus leading to different systemic manifestations. Studies have shown that atopic dermatitis has different relationships with lung cancer, brain cancer, and breast cancer due to its different pathogenesis. Yang 17 et al. found an inverse correlation between the incidence of atopic dermatitis in breast cancer patients. However, due to the different pathogenesis, Huang 18 et al. found that atopic dermatitis can increase the risk of lung adenocarcinoma; Xin 19 et al. found that there was a positive correlation between atopic dermatitis and the incidence of brain cancer. Therefore, atopic dermatitis and other inflammatory skin disease pathogenesis is different, lead to systemic performance also complicated.

IgAN is an inflammatory kidney disease characterized by the deposition of immunoglobulin A in the glomerular mesangium. It is the most commonly reported primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. Between 30% and 40% of patients develop a progressive decline in renal function requiring renal replacement therapy within 20 years of diagnosis. Still, accurate individual risk stratification in diagnosing and predicting treatment response remains a challenge. In addition, due to the long‐standing challenges of the nature and prevalence of the disease, no disease‐specific therapies have been licensed for the treatment of this condition. 20 In recent years, there have been many clinical reports of IgAN secondary to various types of psoriasis. A previous study by Masahiko et al 21 reported a 60‐year‐old man who was diagnosed with psoriasis 4 years ago and found to have IgAN after detailed examination. Similar cases were also mentioned in a study published by Li Zhang et al. 22 In addition, not only the Asian population, but also the European population, such as the case report published by Artsiom Klimko et al. 23 mentioned the cases of IgAN secondary to generalized pustular psoriasis. This study takes into account that psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin disease, 24 and other inflammatory skin diseases can also lead to kidney‐related diseases. For example, Chang‐Ching Wei et al. 25 found that atopic dermatitis can lead to an increased risk of idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. Therefore, it is suspected that there is a causal relationship between inflammatory skin diseases and IgAN, but no comprehensive research and clinical review on inflammatory skin diseases and IgAN has been found. Therefore, this study aims to explore the causal relationship between inflammatory skin diseases and IgAN by two‐sample Mendelian randomization and enrichment analysis. The most common inflammatory skin diseases (atopic dermatitis and psoriasis) were selected as the exposure factors, and IgAN as the outcome for MR Analysis and enrichment analysis.

The limitations of this study are as follows: (1) The study population was all European, and the conclusions cannot be widely applied to other races; (2) The outcome data selected in the study were all pooled results, and the exposure and specific classified diseases were not studied. It is not clear which type of psoriasis is causally related to IgAN. (3) The number of IVS in atopic dermatitis is small, and SNPS with great influence on the results appear in the analysis process, so it is not yet possible to prove that there is a stable causal association between atopic dermatitis and IgAN. (4) There may be overidentification in the two‐sample MR Study, thereby overestimating the association between SNPs and exposure factors 26 ; (5) There are differences in gender for IgAN, males have a higher risk of IgAN compared with females, but this study did not conduct gender discrimination study. 27

In conclusion, the results of MR Analysis support a causal relationship between atopic dermatitis and the increased risk of IgAN, but do not support a causal relationship between psoriasis and IgAN. Enrichment analysis showed that inflammatory skin diseases and IgAN had a causal relationship at the gene level.

Systematic validation of miR-503-5p abnormal expression in diabetic vascular complications: a meta-analysis and exploration of its targeted regulatory mechanism in retinopathy
Authors: Mo Zhang, Ping Shuai, Xian Wang, Yongli Lan, Kangli Deng, Yong Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiabetic vascular complications are major health concerns. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are implicated in their pathogenesis. This study aimed to evaluate the abnormal expression of miR-503-5p in diabetic vascular complications and explore its regulatory mechanisms in diabetic retinopathy.MethodsStudies on miR-503-5p expression in diabetic vascular complications were selected from PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane, and Scopus databases. Experimental validation was carried out utilizing human retinal pigment epithelial cells (ARPE-19) that were subjected to a high glucose (HG) environment. miR-503-5p expression, cell proliferation, apoptosis, inflammatory cytokines, and oxidative stress markers were measured.ResultsThe meta-analysis showed miR-503-5p was upregulated overall in diabetic vascular complications (SMD = 0.71, 95% CI: -2.21 to 3.64). Subgroup analysis showed that miR-503-5p expression was significantly increased in cell samples (SMD = 5.84, 95% CI: 2.40 to 9.27), and in major vascular complications (SMD = 2.89, 95% CI: 0.35 to 5.44). High miR-503-5p could distinguish diabetic retinopathy from healthy controls (AUC = 0.918, sensitivity = 81.2%, specificity = 92.7%). HG upregulated miR-503-5p in ARPE-19 cells. miR-503-5p inhibitor linked to increased proliferation, reduced apoptosis, and modulated inflammatory and oxidative stress responses.ConclusionsMiR-503-5p was significantly upregulated in diabetic vascular complications, particularly in cell samples and major vascular complications. MiR-503-5p may serve as a diagnostic biomarker for diabetic retinopathy. The elevated levels of miR-503-5p in ARPE-19 cells under high glucose conditions were associated with increased inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis, as well as decreased cell proliferation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-025-01982-4.Keywords: MiR-503-5p, Diabetic vascular complications, Diabetic retinopathy, Meta-analysis, Biomarker

Background

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a highly prevalent chronic disease globally [1, 2]. Its incidence is rising annually, posing a significant threat to human health. Diabetic vascular complications are severe and common [3, 4]. They include diabetic retinopathy, diabetic nephropathy, diabetic cardiomyopathy, diabetic peripheral neuropathy, and diabetic foot ulcer [5, 6]. These complications severely impact patients’ quality of life and survival rates [7, 8]. Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of blindness among diabetic vascular complications [9]. Its pathogenesis is complex, involving oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and endothelial dysfunction caused by hyperglycemia [10, 11]. Despite advances in management strategies such as anti-VEGF therapy and laser photocoagulation, many patients still experience progressive vision loss [12–14]. This highlights the urgent need for novel diagnostic markers, therapeutic targets, and a deeper understanding of pathogenesis.

Diabetic retinopathy is a complex microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus, characterized by endothelial cell dysfunction and abnormal angiogenesis [15]. Blood–retinal barrier (BRB) which is consisted by endothelial cells (inner barrier) and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells (outer barrier) have been found contribute to the diabetic retinopathy development [16]. Therefore, the role of RPE cells in the pathogenesis of diabetic retinopathy is equally critical and multifaceted. ARPE-19 cell is a useful cell model for eye diseases, because the RPE-specific markers: RPE-specific protein (RPE65) and cellular retinaldehyde binding protein (CRALBP) [17]. The barrier function of RPE cells is crucial for maintaining retinal homeostasis [18]. Besides, RPE cells could regulate the fluid and ion balance within the retina [19]. Under high glucose conditions, RPE cells undergo significant pathological changes such as oxygen damage that disrupts retinal vasculature and neural tissue, driving diabetic retinopathy progression from non-proliferative to proliferative stages [20].

In recent years, the role of microRNAs (miRNAs) in diabetes and its vascular complications has garnered increasing attention [21, 22]. Studies have shown that multiple miRNAs are abnormally expressed in the development and progression of diabetes and its complications [23, 24], participating in relevant pathophysiological processes. miR-503-5p has been reported to be involved in several diabetic complications. For instance, Caporali et al. found that miR-503 is upregulated in the plasma and ischemic muscles of diabetic patients, and it impairs angiogenesis by inhibiting the proliferation, migration, and network formation of endothelial cells [25]. Guo et al. found that miR-503-5p is downregulated in diabetic peripheral neuropathy, where it alleviates neuropathic pain in type 2 DM (T2DM) mice by inhibiting astrocyte activation via regulation of SEPT9 expression [26]. The increased expression of miR-503-5p reduces the growth of vascular smooth muscle cells and alleviates carotid artery stenosis. In the context of microvascular endothelial cell damage caused by high glucose (HG), miR-503-5p promotes inflammation and oxidative stress through the modulation of the JNK and p38MAPK signaling pathways, suggesting its possible involvement in diabetic angiopathy [27]. Additionally, miR-503-5p has been implicated in diabetic nephropathy, where it promotes podocyte apoptosis by targeting E2F3 [28]. This indicates that miR-503-5p has a distinct function in the progression of vascular complications associated with diabetes. Nonetheless, the involvement and regulatory pathways of miR-503-5p in diabetic retinopathy are still not well understood.

Given these findings, this study aims to systematically validate the changes in miR-503-5p expression in diabetic vascular complications via a meta-analysis and further explore its targeted regulatory mechanisms in diabetic retinopathy. This is expected to provide new targets and a theoretical basis for the diagnosis and treatment of diabetic retinopathy.

Methods

Literature search

PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane, and Scopus databases were searched to comprehensively gather relevant studies. The search was conducted using the keywords “miR-503-5p”, “cardiovascular disease”, and “diabetic vascular complication”, and their relevant synonyms/MeSH terms.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria contained: (1) Subjects in the case group were diagnosed as type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus with at least one diabetic vascular complication, compared to either healthy controls or diabetic patients without complications (control group) (2). Explicit reporting of miR-503-5p expression differences between the case and control groups (3). miR-503-5p detection method specified as quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) (4). Sufficient data reported (mean ± standard deviation, fold change, ΔΔCt) or extractable from figures using WebPlotDigitizer (https://apps.automeris.io/wpd4/index.zh_CN.html).

Exclusion criteria included (1) Participants had other major diseases significantly affecting miRNA levels (such as active cancer, severe autoimmune disorders) (2). Studies are unable to distinguish diabetic vascular complication patients from other patient types (3). Studies with unclear or unreliable detection methodologies (4). Studies lacking extractable or convertible miR-503-5p expression data (5). Reviews, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, comments, editorials, conference abstracts (unless full data was obtainable), and case reports (6). Single-arm studies without a control group.

Study screening and quality assessment

Search results were managed using EndNote software. A preliminary screening was conducted by two independent researchers who assessed titles and abstracts. This was succeeded by a comprehensive evaluation of full texts for studies that potentially met the established criteria. Any differences that arose between the reviewers were addressed through discussion to reach a consensus. The selection procedure was illustrated with a flow diagram based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.

The methodological quality of every diagnostic study included was thoroughly evaluated by two independent reviewers utilizing the Newcastle-Ottawa quality assessment scale (NOS). Disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Data extraction

A standardized data extraction form was developed. Two independent researchers extracted the following data: basic study information (first author, publication year, country), sample characteristics (source, type and number of case and control groups), participant characteristics (age, sex). miR-503-5p expression data. Extracted data was cross-checked, and discrepancies were resolved through discussion.

Meta analysis

A meta-analysis was conducted utilizing Review Manager software version 5.4 alongside STATA version 12.0. The standardized mean difference (SMD) along with 95% confidence intervals (CI) was computed to consolidate expression data across various studies, while accounting for possible discrepancies in measurement units or scales. The presence of heterogeneity among the studies was evaluated by means of Cochran’s Q test and the I² statistic. When the I² value reached or exceeded 50%, indicating significant heterogeneity, a random-effects model was utilized to derive the pooled SMD. Additionally, subgroup analyses were carried out to investigate potential sources of heterogeneity. To assess the robustness of the pooled findings, a sensitivity analysis was performed by systematically excluding each study. Publication bias was evaluated through Egger’s linear regression and Begg’s rank correlation tests.

Human subjects

This study gathered 85 diabetic retinopathy patients and 82 healthy individuals from Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital. All diabetic retinopathy patients met the diagnostic criteria of the American Diabetes Association for type 2 diabetes: (1) Fasting blood glucose (FBG) ≥ 7.0 mmol/L; (2) 2 h blood glucose of glucose tolerance test ≥ 11.1 mmol/L; (3) Random blood glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L, and accompanied by typical symptoms of diabetes; (4) Glycated hemoglobin A1C (HbA1c) ≥ 6.5%. Exclusion criteria included active cancer, autoimmune diseases, severe hepatic/renal dysfunction, or recent anti-VEGF therapy within 6 months. Blood samples were collected from every participant. This study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (No: 2023 − 213). Written informed consent was obtained from the patients.

Reverse transcription quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from plasma samples or cells using TRIzol reagent (Absin, Shanghai, China). Fragments containing miR-503-5p, intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) mRNAs were amplified by FastKing one step RT-PCR kit (KR123, TIANGEN, Beijing, China). U6 and GAPDH were used as reference genes. Relative expression level of miR-503-5p and mRNAs were calculated by 2-ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences were shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Cell culture and treatment

Human retinal pigment epithelial cells (ARPE-19, ATCC CRL-2302) were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). Different concentrations of D-glucose (0 mM, 0.5 mM, 1 mM, 5 mM, 10 mM, 20 mM, 50 mM) were added to the culture medium, to simulate a high-glucose environment. ARPE-19 cells used in this study were between passages 5 and 10.

Cell transfection

ARPE-19 cells were divided into four experimental groups: control group (normal glucose), hyperglycemia (HG) group (50mM glucose), HG + negative control inhibitor (HG + inhibitor NC) group, and HG + miR-503-5p inhibitor group. ARPE-19 cells (2 × 105/well) were put into 6-well plates. Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent was used to transfect the miR-503-5p inhibitor or negative control inhibitor into the cells. The transfection process was carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After 24 h of transfection, subsequent experiments were performed. miR-503-5p inhibitor (HY-RI01514, MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) and inhibitor NC (HY-RI04602, MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) sequences were listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Cell proliferation

Cell proliferation was evaluated through the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (C0037, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). In a 96-well plate, transfected ARPE-19 cells (2000 cells per 100 µL) were plated. Following incubation periods of 24, 48, and 72 h, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was introduced to each well. After an additional 2-hour incubation, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. All experiments were conducted in three biological repeats.

Cell apoptosis

Apoptosis of cells was assessed through flow cytometry utilizing the Annexin V-FITC/PI double-staining kit (MultiSciences, Hangzhou, China). Following transfection, ARPE-19 cells were harvested (1 × 105/100 µL) and labeled with Annexin V-FITC and PI in accordance with the guidelines provided in the kit. All experiments were conducted in three biological repeats.

Detection of oxidative stress markers and inflammatory cytokines

Following transfection and treatment, a suspension of ARPE-19 cells (100 µL) was incubated in a 96-well plate at 37 ℃ for a period of 24 h. Subsequently, 100 µL of culture supernatant was mixed with 900 µL of assay diluent provided by the ELISA kit. Then, intracellular levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) were quantified using the Reactive Oxygen Species Assay Kit (abs580233, Absin, Shanghai, China), as outlined in the protocol. Multifunctional fluorescence enzyme reader was utilized for detection of the results. Additionally, the content of malondialdehyde (MDA) (abs554411) and the activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD) (abs5510101) were evaluated with enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Absin, Shanghai, China). The concentrations of interleukin (IL)−6 (abs510003), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) (abs510006), and IL-1β (abs510002) in the culture supernatants of treated ARPE-19 cells were determined using the appropriate ELISA kits (Absin, Shanghai, China). Measurements of MDA, SOD, IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β were carried out following the provided instructions, with optical density (OD) values recorded at 450 nm. All experiments were conducted in three biological repeats.

Bioinformatic prediction of miR-503-5p targets and functional enrichment

Potential target genes of miR-503-5p were predicted using three algorithms: miRDB, TargetScan, and Starbase. Common target genes identified by all three algorithms were selected by Venn diagram. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of common targets was constructed by STRING. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis (biological process, cellular component, molecular function) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis were conducted using the online platform Weishengxin (http://bioinformatics.com.cn/) [29].

Statistical analysis for experimental data

Experimental results were represented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between groups were conducted using a t-test or One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. The ability of miR-503-5p to diagnose diabetic retinopathy was assessed through a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, and results were reported in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and area under the ROC curve (AUC). Data analysis and figure creation were executed using GraphPad Prism 9.0. The significance level was established at P < 0.05.

Results

Study selection

A systematic search was conducted across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane, and Scopus databases to identify relevant studies. A total of 476 records were initially identified. After removing duplicate records(n = 236), 240 records remained for screening based on titles and abstracts. During the screening process, 157 records were excluded based on title, primarily due to being reviews, meta-analyses, or abstracts (n = 91); lack of relevance to diabetic vascular complications (n = 11), or lack of relevance to miR-503 (n = 55). This resulted in 88 reports being selected for further retrieval. Of these, 73 reports were excluded after abstract assessment because the test method was not RT-qPCR. Consequently, 15 reports were assessed for eligibility through full-text screening. One report was excluded because the miR-503-5p expression level could not be obtained. Ultimately, 9 studies [30–38] (with 11 reports, 120 cases, and 97 controls) were included in the meta-analysis (Fig. 1).

The expression trends of miR-503-5p varied across studies, with both upregulation (n = 8) and downregulation (n = 3) observed in different contexts. The sample sources included animal models (n = 4), human (n = 4), and cell lines (n = 3). The control groups were typically composed of non-diabetic controls, healthy individuals, or cells under normal glycemic conditions. The experimental groups included diabetic microvascular complications (n = 8, including diabetic nephropathy, diabetic foot ulcer, diabetic peripheral neuropathy), diabetic major vascular complications (n = 3, including diabetic cardiomyopathy and diabetic with ischemic stroke) (Table 1). The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) was utilized to evaluate the methodological quality of the studies, with scores between 7 and 9 reflecting high quality.

Subgroup analyses

Subgroup analyses based on sample source, and type of experimental group were performed to explore the source of heterogeneity. The results showed that the sample source (Fig. 2B), and type of experimental group (Fig. 2C) could induce significant heterogeneity (I² >50%).

Significantly higher miR-503-5p expression was found in cells (SMD = 5.84, 95% CI: 2.40 to 9.27, P < 0.001), however, miR-503-5p expression had no significant difference in both human and animal subgroups (Fig. 2B). Subgroup analysis based on disease type demonstrated that the miR-503-5p was significantly elevated in major vascular complications (SMD = 2.89, 95% CI: 0.35 to 5.44). For the diabetic microvascular complications, there were 5 reports with increased trend for miR-503-5p expression, and 4 reports with decreased miR-503-5p expression. However, the subtotal result for miR-503-5p in microvascular complications was downregulated without statistical significance (Fig. 2C).

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

The pooled effect size (SMD = 0.71) remained consistent in direction across all leave-one-out analyses, demonstrating robustness of the results (Fig. 3A). Scatter points exhibited an inverted funnel shape symmetrically clustered around the pooled effect line (SMD = 0.71). High-precision studies (top region) concentrated within the negative SMD range (indicating downregulation), with a lack of high-quality studies demonstrating miR-503-5p upregulation in microvascular complications. Low-precision studies (bottom region) were dispersed across both positive and negative SMD values. Combined with Egger’s test (P = 0.927) indicated no significant publication bias (Fig. 3B).

miR-503-5p is a diagnostic biomarker for diabetic retinopathy

A total of 82 individuals in the control group were composed of 46 males and 36 females, boasting a mean age of 50.37 ± 11.80 years (age range: 31–68). In contrast, the diabetic retinopathy group comprised 48 males and 37 females, with a mean age of 49.88 ± 11.56 years (age range: 30–68). Results from RT-qPCR indicated that the plasma levels of miR-503-5p were significantly higher in patients with diabetic retinopathy compared to those in the control group (Fig. 4A). The ROC curve analysis revealed a strong diagnostic capability of miR-503-5p for diabetic retinopathy, recording an AUC of 0.918 (95% CI: 0.877–0.959), with a sensitivity of 81.2% and a specificity of 92.7%, at a cutoff value of 1.287 (Fig. 4B).

Function analysis of miR-503-5p in diabetic retinopathy development

qRT-PCR analysis revealed HG directly elevated miR-503-5p expression, with levels exhibiting a dose-dependent increase as glucose concentration increased (0.5–50 mM, Fig. 5A). HG (20–50 mM) significantly reduced cell viability in HG-treated ARPE-19 cells (Fig. 5B).

Construction of high-glucose human retinal pigment epithelial cells. (A) miR-503-5p expression in high glucose cell model. (B) Cell viability detection in high glucose cell model. Each experiment was conducted 3 times. ns, not significant; *, P < 0.05; **, P < 0.01; ***, P < 0.001

qRT-PCR found that miR-503-5p was significantly decreased in miR-503-5p inhibitor cells (Fig. 6A, P < 0.001). miR-503-5p inhibitor could inhibit the high miR-503-5p level induced by HG (Fig. 6B, P < 0.001). HG treatment could significantly inhibit the proliferation (Fig. 6C, P < 0.001) and promote the apoptosis (Fig. 6D, P < 0.001) of ARPE-19 cells, while the miR-503-5p inhibitor could reverse the alteration of proliferation and apoptosis induced by HG. HG-induced high ICAM and VEGF levels were significantly decreased by miR-503-5p inhibitor (Fig. 6E, P < 0.001). Oxidative stress factors ROS (Fig. 6F, P < 0.001) and MDA (Fig. 6G, P < 0.001) were elevated, and SOD (Fig. 6H, P < 0.001) was decreased by HG, and the alteration was reversed by miR-503-5p inhibitor (P < 0.05). Cytokine concentrations of IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α were elevated by HG treatment, and the enhancement could be decreased by miR-503-5p inhibitor (Fig. 6I, P < 0.05). These results suggested that HG treatment inhibited proliferation, promoted apoptosis, vascular function, oxidative stress factors, and cytokine concentrations via promote the miR-503-5p expression. These effects are closely related to the pathological contributions of RPE cells in diabetic retinopathy.

Bioinformatic prediction of miR-503-5p targets and functional enrichment

Potential target genes of miR-503-5p were predicted from miRDB, TargetScan, and Starbase databases. 122 common target genes were obtained by a Venn diagram (Fig. 7A). PPI constructed by STRING showed that the targets with the first ten string node degrees included MAP2K1, PIK3R1, BTRC, CHEK1, KIF23, ANLN, CCND2, FGF2, AKT3, and EIF4E (Fig. 7B). Three ontologies GO enrichment analysis showed that biological process elements mainly focused on protein catabolic processes and BMP pathway (negative regulate the growth factor), which might relate to abnormal vascular proliferation of diabetic retinopathy. Cellular component elements contained phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) and cyclin-dependent protein kinase (CDK) complexes, neuron projection cytoplasm, axon cytoplasm, dendrite cytoplasm, relating to the regulation of polarity of retinal cells. Molecular function elements included SMAD binding, kinase regulator activity, and protein serine/threonine kinase activity, which might contribute to the fibrotic process (Fig. 7C). KEGG pathway enrichment classification showed that the key pathways included environmental information processing (PI3K-Akt, Hippo/mTOR, TGF-β, HIF-1, VEGF/JAK-STAT pathways), cellular processes, and organismal systems (neurotrophin signaling pathway). These pathways are mainly linked to the regulation of cell survival and metabolism, hypoxia response, fibrosis, angiogenesis, and inflammation (Fig. 7D).

Discussion

The purpose of this study was to systematically evaluate the abnormal expression of miR-503-5p in diabetic vascular complications and explore its regulatory mechanism in diabetic retinopathy. This study innovatively combined a meta-analysis with clinical diagnostics, functional experiments, and bioinformatics to provide a comprehensive understanding of the role of miR-503-5p in diabetic vascular complications, particularly in diabetic retinopathy. By integrating data from multiple studies and conducting in-depth experimental analyses, this research aims to identify potential diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for diabetic retinopathy.

The meta-analysis revealed that miR-503-5p expression levels had an overall non-significant upregulation trend toward diabetic vascular complications. It masks profound subtype-specific heterogeneity. miR-503-5p expression was significantly increased in vitro cell samples. Results from human samples with reduced trend and animal models with increased trend, these results were highly inconsistent and nonsignificant overall. The extremely high heterogeneity across all analyses suggests substantial variation in miR-503-5p expression patterns depending on the specific complication type, tissue, disease stage, or experimental model. miR-503-5p was significantly enhanced in major vascular complications, and was increased in most microvascular complications. This duality mirrors the microenvironment-dependent behavior of miR-503-5p. In normoxic tissues, it acts as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting VEGF [39], whereas in hypoxic microenvironments, it may adopt pro-angiogenic roles via HIF-1α crosstalk [40]. The findings indicate that miR-503-5p could have a dual function in vascular complications related to diabetes, serving both as a protective agent and as a harmful factor, which varies with the particular context and tissue type. The pooled analysis revealed significant heterogeneity, and subsequent subgroup analysis identified the sources of this heterogeneity as the origin of the samples and the categories of the experimental groups. Furthermore, the sensitivity analysis affirmed the reliability of these findings. There was also an observed publication bias, highlighting a scarcity of research focused on diabetic microvascular complications. Consequently, this study was conducted to investigate the diagnostic potential and mechanisms underlying miR-503-5p in diabetic retinopathy.

This research examined the expression pattern of miR-503-5p in diabetic retinopathy and discovered that levels of miR-503-5p were considerably higher in patients with diabetic retinopathy compared to the control group. The ongoing clinical investigation indicated that elevated miR-503-5p levels can effectively differentiate diabetic retinopathy patients from those who are healthy. The RPE forms the outer blood-retinal barrier through tight junctions, preventing abnormal leakage of fluid and large molecules into the retina. It also nourishes photoreceptor cells, maintaining their function [16]. Hyperglycemia compromises this barrier integrity, leading to increased permeability and leakage. The RPE then produces excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS), which induce oxidative stress and activate inflammatory pathways [41]. This ROS accumulation not only exacerbates RPE damage but also further disrupts retinal vasculature and neural tissue, driving the progression of diabetic retinopathy from the non-proliferative to the proliferative stage [20]. Experimental results demonstrated a significant increase in miR-503-5p expression in ARPE-19 cells treated with high glucose, which correlated with enhanced cellular proliferation and decreased rates of apoptosis. Additionally, the administration of a miR-503-5p inhibitor led to notable reductions in the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β) and markers of oxidative stress (ROS, MDA), while simultaneously boosting antioxidant activity (SOD). These effects are closely related to the pathological contributions of RPE cells in diabetic retinopathy. This finding aligns with earlier research, where Sheikhbahaei et al. reported that miR-503 levels were heightened in diabetic patients suffering from ischemic stroke, highlighting its significant diagnostic potential [32]. Wang et al. suggested that miR-503 in retinitis pigmentosa was significantly elevated [42]. Besides, Zhou and coworkers found that miR-503 was significantly upregulated in islets of T2DM patients and mice model [43]. Liu and colleagues found that overexpression of miR-503-5p could suppress the angiogenic differentiation and increase the apoptosis of HUVECs [33]. The difference with our study might be caused by the distinct cellular contexts and disease models employed. HUVECs mainly drive systemic vasculature formation, with miRNAs controlling their proliferation to curb excessive angiogenesis. Retinal endothelial cells, however, are vital for the retinal vascular barrier, needing miRNA-driven proliferation to keep the barrier intact. This functional dichotomy causes miRNAs to act differently in each cell type. A high-glucose setting derails cellular metabolism, spurring oxidative stress and inflammation, whose effects vary by cell. In ARPE-19 cells, moderate oxidative stress can trigger stress responses and repairs, boosting cell growth. In HUVECs, though, oxidative stress often surpasses tolerance levels, causing damage and apoptosis. Collectively, these results highlight miR-503-5p as a potential diagnostic and therapeutic target for diabetic retinopathy. Further investigation is necessary to confirm these results and examine the underlying mechanisms more comprehensively.

The present study leveraged miRDB, TargetScan, and Starbase databases to predict potential target genes of miR-503-5p, identifying 122 common targets through a Venn diagram. The PPI network constructed via STRING highlighted key targets such as MAP2K1 and PIK3R1. GO enrichment analysis revealed that miR-503-5p is involved in critical biological processes like protein catabolism and the BMP pathway, which are linked to abnormal vascular proliferation [44, 45]. It also pointed to cellular components such as PI3K and CDK complexes, which are related to retinal cell polarity regulation [46, 47], and molecular functions like SMAD binding and kinase regulator activity, which may contribute to fibrotic processes [48]. KEGG pathway analysis further showed that miR-503-5p is implicated in pathways such as PI3K-Akt, Hippo/mTOR, HIF-1, TGF-β, and VEGF/JAK-STAT, which are crucial for regulating cell survival, metabolism [49], hypoxia response [50], fibrosis [51], angiogenesis [52], and inflammation [53]. These pathways are known to be activated in response to high glucose conditions and are modulated by miR-503-5p, as evidenced by the changes in cell proliferation and apoptosis, inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β) and oxidative stress markers (ROS, MDA) observed in our experiments. Although our in vitro experiments did not directly measure angiogenesis and vascular permeability or fibrosis, the HIF-1 and TGF-β signaling pathways predicted by KEGG pathway analysis, are relevant in the context of diabetic retinopathy. These findings collectively underscore the multifaceted regulatory roles of miR-503-5p in diabetic retinopathy and its potential as a therapeutic target.

Despite the comprehensive nature of this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. Meta-analysis showed high heterogeneity, possibly from study differences and small samples, limiting generalizability. Large multi-center studies with standard protocols are needed to validate miR-503-5p’s diagnostic value in diabetic vascular complications. Most validation was in vitro, not fully reflecting in vivo situations. Cross-sectional studies made causality hard to establish. Future research should include diverse patients and longitudinal studies to track miR-503-5p changes with disease progression. Studies with negative or non-significant results on miR-503-5p in some diabetic complications is lacking, contributing to publication bias. Encouraging publication of all results can strengthen meta-analyses. Developing miR-503-5p-based therapies is crucial. Preclinical studies should optimize delivery and assess safety/efficacy. These steps will clarify miR-503-5p’s role and aid clinical application.

Conclusions

This meta-analysis study systematically validated that miR-503-5p expression levels had an overall upregulation trend toward in diabetic vascular complications. diabetic retinopathy. Cell experiments in ARPE-19 cells treated with high glucose, the expression of miR-503-5p was found to be increased. Conversely, the suppression of miR-503-5p enhanced cellular proliferation, decreased apoptosis, and influenced inflammation and oxidative stress pathways. Combined with the overexpression of miR-503-5p plasma samples, miR-503-5p may have potential as a diagnostic biomarker for diabetic retinopathy. Future research should focus on overcoming the limitations of the current study and further exploring the clinical applications of miR-503-5p in the management of diabetic retinopathy.

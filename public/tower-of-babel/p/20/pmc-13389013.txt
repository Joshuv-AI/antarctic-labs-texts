Possible high-temperature superconductivity in LiCuH at ambient pressure
Authors: Prutthipong Tsuppayakorn-aek, Sirinee Thasitha, Anan Udomkijmongkol, Wiwittawin Sukmas, Tanveer Hussain, Thiti Bovornratanaraks, Komsilp Kotmool
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe theoretically investigate the structural stability, electronic property, and superconductivity of the hydrogen-rich compound LiCuH in its face-centered cubic phase. First-principles calculations show that LiCuH is thermodynamically metastable at ambient pressure but dynamically stable. Moreover, ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulation at 300 K further confirms that LiCuH remains structurally intact without decomposition. These results suggest that the material may be synthesized under high pressure and retained after decompression. Although thermodynamically metastable at ambient conditions, its dynamic stability supports its persistence after pressure release. Moreover, LiCuH exhibits metallic behavior with a flat band and van Hove singularity (vHS) near the Fermi level and Fermi-surface states dominated by Cu–d and H–s orbitals. Phonon calculations further indicate that hydrogen vibrations dominate the phonon contribution, while the electronic states near the Fermi level govern the coupling strength, resulting in a large electron–phonon coupling constant. Insights into the superconducting temperature () are obtained using various theoretical approaches, which estimate to be in the range of 93–152 K. Bonding analysis further indicates mixed covalent–ionic character within Cu–H octahedra. These results highlight LiCuH as a promising hydrogen-based metastable superconductor for future experimental exploration.Subject terms: Chemistry, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Hydrogen-rich materials have attracted significant attention as potential high-temperature superconductors due to the light mass of hydrogen atoms, which leads to high phonon frequencies and strong electron–phonon coupling (EPC). Following the theoretical prediction of metallic hydrogen by Neil W. Ashcroft1, hydrogen-dominant compounds have been extensively investigated as promising candidates for phonon-mediated superconductivity. A major breakthrough was achieved with the discovery of superconductivity in HS, which exhibits a superconducting transition temperature of approximately 203 K at 155 GPa2. Subsequently, the hydrogen-rich compound LaH was reported to exhibit an even higher of about 250 K at 170 GPa3. These discoveries have stimulated extensive research on dense metallic hydrides under extreme compression. For example, superconductivity has been observed in CaH with a of approximately 215 K at 172 GPa4, while the hydrogen-rich compound CeH has been shown to exhibit a superconducting transition temperature of about 117 K at 200 GPa5, In addition, metal hexahydrides forming sodalite-like clathrate structures have been predicted to support high superconducting transition temperatures, as demonstrated in ScH (129 K at 200 GPa)6, YH (290 K at 300 GPa)7, and HfH (132 K at 600 GPa)8 under high-pressure conditions.

In addition to these systems, several other hydrogen-rich materials have also been predicted to exhibit superconductivity at high pressures. For example, superconductivity has been experimentally observed in (La,Ce)H with a transition temperature of approximately 176 K at 100 GPa9, while theoretical calculations predicted a of about 127 K at 120 GPa for the related compound (La,Ce)H10. Similarly, the compound LiHeH has been predicted to exhibit superconductivity with values as high as 177 K at pressures around 90 GPa11. Despite these remarkable discoveries, the requirement of extremely high pressures remains a major obstacle for practical applications. Consequently, considerable efforts have been devoted to identifying hydrides that retain strong electron–phonon interactions while remaining stable at lower pressures or even at ambient conditions. Various theoretical approaches have been explored to achieve this goal. For example, chemical substitution in hydrogen-rich clathrate structures has been proposed to enhance superconducting properties, as demonstrated in MgCaH, which was predicted to exhibit near-room-temperature superconductivity with a of approximately 288 K at 200 GPa12. In addition, crystal-structure prediction techniques such as particle swarm optimization have identified promising superconducting hydrides13,14, including CaYH with a predicted of about 258 K at 200 GPa15.

More recently, high-throughput computational screening has emerged as an efficient strategy to discover new hydride superconductors. In particular, Zheng et al. systematically screened 290 compounds with the general formula XMH (X = alkali or alkaline-earth elements; M = transition metals) and identified 26 dynamically stable materials exhibiting strong EPC. Among them, four compounds, MgRhH, MgIrH, AlMnH, and LiCuH, were predicted to exhibit exceeding 50 K at ambient pressure16. These findings suggest that certain highly symmetric hydride structures may sustain strong electron–phonon interactions without requiring extreme compression17,18.

Among these candidates, LiCuH, as its atomic structure illustrated in Fig. 1b, is particularly interesting because its highly symmetric hexahydride framework can promote strong electron–phonon interactions, suggesting that it may support superconductivity at relatively high temperatures even under ambient pressure. The superconducting properties of this compound were subsequently investigated by Zhang et al.16 using first-principles calculations. Their results revealed strong electron–phonon interactions with an integrated coupling parameter of , indicating that the system lies in the strong-coupling regime. However, the effect of strong EPC on the estimation of the superconducting transition temperature was not fully addressed. Motivated by these considerations, we present a comprehensive investigation of the superconducting properties of LiCuH at ambient pressure. The superconducting transition temperature is evaluated using the Allen–Dynes modified McMillan equation including the strong-coupling correction factors. Furthermore, the superconducting gap is analyzed within the isotropic Migdal–Eliashberg formalism19,20, and the Coulomb pseudopotential is examined by considering both the Coulomb response frequency and the Debye frequency. These analyses provide a more reliable evaluation of the superconducting properties of this compound and offer further insight into the role of strong EPC in hydride superconductors.

Results and discussion

Among the previously mentioned studies, the feasibility of forming stable structures under high pressure has been demonstrated for MgIrH17. In addition, similar hexahydrides, such as LiAuH, have been predicted to form via reactions of the type , with a relatively small formation energy of approximately 38 meV/atom, suggesting a metastable nature21. These findings imply that related compounds, including LiCuH, may also be experimentally accessible through analogous synthetic pathways. However, the absence of a stable LiCu binary phase makes this route impractical. Based on recent work, Zhang et al.16 reported an energy above the convex hull of 80 meV/atom for LiCuH, considering the decomposition pathway , indicating that this compound is potentially metastable at ambient pressure. In the present study, we further extend the thermodynamic analysis by exploring structural stability under high-pressure conditions, following the well-established high-pressure synthesis pathway from elemental phases. Specifically, the stability of LiCuH was evaluated with respect to the decomposition reaction . As the pressure decreases from 50 GPa to ambient conditions, the enthalpy of LiCuH increases progressively, indicating a transition from a thermodynamically stable to a metastable state. This trend suggests that LiCuH remains metastable at ambient conditions, with an estimated formation energy of 51 meV/atom, as illustrated in Fig. 1a. Consequently, this compound appears to be a promising candidate for experimental synthesis, consistent with the typical metastability range observed in inorganic crystalline materials22.

Although most synthesized hydride superconductors are stable only under extremely high pressures, high-pressure synthesis methods could provide a route to obtain metastable phases under ambient conditions. The dynamic and kinetic stability of these materials suggests that certain crystalline structures, initially formed under moderate to high pressures, where they are thermodynamically favored, may persist in a metastable state upon decompression. Additionally, the feasibility of synthesis using a diamond anvil cell further supports this approach. Moreover, we further investigated the metastability of LiCuH with respect to the pure elemental phases: Li in the bcc structure25, Cu in the fcc structure26, and H in the hcp structure27. Our results reveal that at a pressure of 30 GPa, LiCuH forms a thermodynamically stable structure. As the pressure decreases to 10 GPa, LiCuH remains thermodynamically stable. However, at ambient pressure, it transforms into an energetically metastable phase, as illustrated in Fig. S2 of SI.

It should be noted that the present calculations were performed for an ideal defect-free crystal structure. In metastable systems, structural defects such as vacancies, interstitials, or antisite disorder may influence thermodynamic stability and potentially promote decomposition into more stable phases. Nevertheless, the relatively small formation energy and the dynamic stability indicated by the phonon calculations suggest that LiCuH may remain experimentally accessible under appropriate synthesis conditions. A systematic investigation of defect formation and its influence on the stability and superconducting properties of this compound would be an interesting direction for future studies.

To further examine the kinetic stability of LiCuH at ambient pressure, we performed ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations within the density functional theory framework. The simulations were carried out in the canonical (NVT) ensemble using the Nosé–Hoover thermostat with a target temperature of 300 K and a timestep of 1 fs. The system was equilibrated for 10 ps, corresponding to 10,000 MD steps. As shown in Fig. 2, the relative free energy and temperature oscillate around equilibrium values, with no indication of structural collapse or decomposition. Representative atomic snapshots at 1, 5000, and 10,000 steps confirm that the Cu–H octahedra remain intact during the simulation. These results demonstrate that LiCuH is dynamically robust against thermal vibrations at room temperature, providing further evidence that the compound can exist as a metastable phase at ambient condition. This kinetic stability complements the thermodynamic metastability discussed earlier and supports the feasibility of LiCuH as a high- superconductor under ambient pressure.

To gain deeper insights into the physical properties of LiCuH, we analyze three key aspects of its electronic structure. The electronic band structure reveals metallic behavior, as evidenced by band dispersions crossing the Fermi level, as shown in Fig. 3a. Partial density of states (PDOS) confirms the presence of sharp electron density at the Fermi level, which is contributed by the mix of Cu-3d and H-1s orbitals. Interestingly, there are significant band contributions at the Fermi level, including the touching maximum band at an interdistance of X-K path and the flat band along the W-L path. Moreover, a critical point associated with the well-known van Hove singularity (vHS) is clearly observed near the L-point in the band structure. This feature arises from a saddle point, as highlighted by the dashed elliptical marker in Fig. 3a. These features play a crucial role in enhancing electron-phonon interactions, making it plausible that they ultimately facilitate the formation of Cooper pairs. With this in mind, it appears that Cu mainly contributes to the states at the Fermi level through the Cu-3d electrons, thereby shedding light on the origin of the vHS. As a result, the enhanced electronic states near the Fermi level increase the number of Cooper pairs, which in turn leads to a high in LiCuH.

In addition, we present the Fermi surfaces of the LiCuH structure. Notably, there are significant differences in the Fermi surface topology with respect to orbital weight, as shown in Fig. 3b–d. The Fermi surface exhibits a large sheet that extends around the high-symmetry points. Our analysis reveals that the Fermi surface topology of the Li-2s state exhibits a weak orbital weight, which corresponds to the partial density of Li. In contrast, the Fermi surface topologies associated with Cu and H display much stronger orbital weights, particularly in the Cu-3d and H-1s orbitals, compared to Li-2s. This relative difference may also influence the superconducting properties, where the presence of flat band and vHS could contribute to the emergence of high- behavior. Furthermore, the existence of vHS can be interpreted as supporting EPC, which in turn enhances the , as observed in HS28 and LaH29.

To investigate the superconducting state, we first examined the lattice dynamics of LiCuH. Our analysis confirmed its dynamical stability, since no imaginary frequencies were observed in Fig. 4a. Furthermore, the phonon linewidth along its dispersion indicates that the EPC is mainly influenced by intermediate to high-energy optical phonons (Fig. 4b). In particular, LiCuH exhibits a significant contribution of the EPC of the optical phonon modes, where H atoms play a dominant role. This observation is further supported by the partial phonon density of the states (Fig. 4c). Beyond discussing the crystal structure, it is essential to consider the bonding environment. To thoroughly analyze the chemical bonding in LiCuH, we conducted a theoretical examination using the electron localization function (ELF) method. In this context, we explored the electronic properties of the LiCuH structure, as depicted in Fig. 6c, employing the ELF method. Our analysis reveals that electrons predominantly accumulate around the H atoms and participate in bonding with Cu atoms, forming Cu-H octahedra with covalent character. This bonding gives rise to strong electron-phonon coupling40,41, as evidenced by the Cu-H nearest-neighbor (NN) distance of 1.581 Å. Moreover, it is worth noting that there is no significant electron accumulation around the Li atoms, suggesting that the bonding likely consists of a complex mixture of ionic and metallic interactions. In LiCuH, electron accumulation is primarily observed around the H atoms rather than the Li sites. This behavior can be attributed to the presence of Li atoms, which stabilize the Cu-H octahedra exhibiting covalent bonding character, which exhibit NN distances of 2.241 Å for Li-H and 2.74 Å for Li-Cu11,42.

Conclusion

This study explored the structural, electronic, and superconducting properties of LiCuH using first-principles calculations. We demonstrated that LiCuH is a thermodynamically metastable phase at ambient pressure, potentially synthesizable via high-pressure methods and stable upon decompression. AIMD simulation at 300 K confirms that LiCuH remains structurally intact without decomposition, demonstrating its kinetic stability as a metastable phase under ambient conditions. Electronic structure analysis indicated metallic behavior with a flat band and a van Hove singularity near the Fermi level, thereby enhancing electron-phonon interactions. The Fermi surface topology highlights significant contributions from the Cu-3d and H-1s orbitals, reinforcing the potential for high-temperature superconductivity. This analysis reveals a significant contribution from flat band and vHS, which may play a crucial role in the evolution of . This contribution enhances the robustness of Cooper-pair coupling near the Fermi level. Phonon calculations confirmed its dynamical stability, with strong electron-phonon coupling primarily driven by hydrogen vibrations. Superconductivity was investigated using the Eliashberg spectral function, which revealed a strong electron-phonon coupling. The estimated ranges from 93 to 152 K, with a central estimate of 127 K at a of 0.10. The Allen-Dynes equation provided a more reliable estimate, emphasizing the need for strong-coupling corrections in hydrogen-rich superconductors. Our ELF analysis revealed covalent interactions within Cu-H octahedra, alongside ionic contributions from Li atoms, which contribute to the material’s metastability and electronic properties. In summary, LiCuH emerges as a promising high-temperature superconductor, exemplifying the potential of metastable hydrogen-based materials synthesized under high pressure. Future experimental validation should explore its synthesis, pressure-dependent behavior, and possible enhancements via chemical substitutions or external tuning. This study advances the understanding of hydrogen-rich superconductors, guiding the search for materials with practical high- applications.

Computational methods

In this study, the chemical pre-compression and electronic structure of fcc-LiCuH were theoretically investigated using first-principles density functional theory (DFT), as implemented in the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)43. AIMD simulations were carried out within DFT using the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange–correlation functional, as implemented in the VASP. The simulations were performed in the canonical NVT ensemble using the conventional crystallographic cell. Ionic trajectories were propagated using the velocity-Verlet algorithm with a timestep of 1 fs. Temperature was controlled by the Nosé–Hoover thermostat to ensure sampling of the NVT ensemble. The target temperature was set to T = 300 K, and the system was equilibrated for 10 ps prior to data collection. The Fermi surface topology was analyzed using Fermisurfer software in conjunction with Quantum ESPRESSO (QE) package44. A plane-wave energy cutoff of 80 Ry was applied, and the exchange-correlation interactions were treated using the GGA formulated by PBE exchange-correlation functional45. The ultrasoft pseudopotential approach, as implemented in QE, was utilized for all calculations.

The of fcc-LiCuH was determined using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT)46 to compute the EPC and the Eliashberg spectral function. To ensure convergence, a dense k-point mesh was utilized, covering all k and grid points. For the electron-phonon interaction and spectral function calculations, a k-point mesh and a q-point mesh were employed within the first BZ, ensuring accurate determination of the EPC strength.

Dynamic crotonylation of Bub3 regulates mitotic checkpoint silencing and chromosome segregation
Authors: Xufan Shi, Xingchen Yu, Wenwen Wang, Fangyuan Xiong, Xiao Yuan, Qing Hu, Xiaoyu Song, Zhen Dou, Xuebiao Yao, Liangyu Zhang
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Accurate segregation of replicated chromosomes during mitosis relies on the coordination between kinetochore attachment to spindle microtubules and cell cycle progression. The spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) serves as the primary surveillance mechanism that senses the faulty kinetochore–microtubule attachment and catalyzes the assembly of the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC). The MCC prevents the transition from metaphase to anaphase by inhibiting the E3 ligase activity of anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) until all chromosomes are properly bi-oriented with spindle microtubules from the opposite poles. Bub3 is a key component of the MCC, which also includes BubR1, Mad2, and Cdc20. Bub3 binds to the phosphorylated MELT repeats of the outer kinetochore protein KNL1, recruiting other downstream MCC components to establish a functional SAC (McAinsh and Kops, 2023).

Previous studies have revealed that post-translational modifications of relevant proteins play crucial roles in SAC assembly. For instance, pyruvate kinase PKM2-mediated phosphorylation of Bub3 at Y207 and ATM-mediated phosphorylation at S135 facilitate its binding to KNL1 and Bub1, respectively (Jiang et al., 2014; Xiao et al., 2022). Additionally, the acetylation of BubR1, a core binding partner of Bub3, ensures SAC signaling by preventing its degradation via the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway (Choi et al., 2009). While the mechanisms governing SAC assembly have been extensively investigated, the disassembly of SAC is still not well understood. Our recent research indicates that the phosphorylation of ZW10 by PLK1 promotes SAC silencing via the RZZ–Mad1/2 pathway (Bellah et al., 2024). Nevertheless, the process by which the Bub3 pathway is inactivated during metaphase remains unclear.

A couple of crotonylome studies identified Bub3 as a potential target for crotonylation (Yu et al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2023). To confirm the crotonylation of Bub3 and examine its temporal dynamics during the cell cycle, we induced expression of GFP-tagged Bub3 in HeLa cells, synchronized the cells at different cell cycle stages, and immunoprecipitated Bub3-GFP (Supplementary Figure S1A). Western blot analysis using a pan-anti-crotonyl-lysine antibody indicated that Bub3 crotonylation occurs during interphase, metaphase, and anaphase, but not in prometaphase (Figure 1A). We then generated two point mutations in Bub3 at the identified crotonylation sites (K139 and K222) (Yu et al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2023), replacing them with non-crotonylatable arginine. The K222R mutation significantly diminished the crotonylation of Bub3, while the K139R mutation did not, indicating that K222 is the primary crotonylation site (Figure 1B). Our mass spectrometry analysis confirmed the crotonylation of Bub3 at K222 in interphase cells (Supplementary Figure S1B).

To identify the enzyme responsible for Bub3-K222 crotonylation, we transiently co-transfected HEK293T cells with StrepII-tagged Bub3 and seven different crotonyl-transferases. p300-histone acetyltransferase domain (p300-HAT) was found to significantly enhance Bub3 crotonylation, while the others did not, indicating that p300 is the primary crotonyl-transferase for Bub3 (Supplementary Figure S2A and B). Consistently, the knockdown of p300 using small interfering RNA (siRNA) reduced Bub3 crotonylation (Supplementary Figure S2C and D). Moreover, co-expression of p300-HAT, but not a catalytically dead mutant, significantly increased the crotonylation of Bub3. Wild-type Bub3, but not the K222R mutant, was highly crotonylated by the overexpressed p300-HAT. These findings together indicate that p300-HAT primarily crotonylates Bub3 at K222 (Figure 1C).

To identify the enzyme responsible for Bub3 decrotonylation, we expressed Bub3-StrepII in HEK293T cells and treated them with trichostatin A (TSA, an HDAC inhibitor) and nicotinamide (NAM, an inhibitor of SIRT family). NAM treatment slightly increased Bub3 crotonylation, whereas TSA had a minimal effect (Supplementary Figure S2E and F), suggesting that the SIRT family might decrotonylate Bub3 in cells. Further siRNA-mediated knockdown of SIRT1, SIRT2, and SIRT3, respectively, revealed that SIRT1 depletion leads to a more significant increase in the crotonylation signal of Bub3 (Supplementary Figure S2G and H), implicating SIRT1 as the primary decrotonylase for Bub3 in human cells.

To investigate the role of Bub3-K222 crotonylation, we utilized AlphaFold3 to examine the interaction of Bub3 with phosphorylated KNL1. K222 is located at the binding interface between Bub3 and the phosphorylated MELT motif on KNL1. Alphafold3 prediction suggests that the crotonylation of K222 disrupts the hydrogen bonds/salt bridges between R202 of Bub3 and the phospho-threonine in the MELT motif on KNL1 (Supplementary Figure S3A). To validate the prediction, we performed immunoprecipitation (IP) assays to evaluate the binding affinity of Bub3-K222 mutants with KNL1 in mitotic cells. The crotonylation-mimicking mutant Bub3K222Q exhibited a reduced interaction with KNL1, whereas the non-crotonylatable mutant Bub3K222R did not affect KNL1 binding (Supplementary Figure S3B), indicating that the crotonylation of Bub3-K222 disrupts its interaction with KNL1.

If crotonylation of Bub3-K222 disrupts its interaction with KNL1, it could impair the recruitment of Bub3 to the kinetochore. To test this hypothesis, we generated HeLa cell lines stably expressing either wild-type Bub3-GFP (WT) or different mutants (K222Q and K222R) and used siRNA to knock down endogenous Bub3 in these cells (Supplementary Figure S3C). Immunofluorescence and statistical analyses revealed that Bub3K222Q displayed a significant reduction in kinetochore localization, accompanied by dramatically decreased localization of its downstream factor, BubR1 (Figure 1D–F). In contrast, Bub3K222R did not affect the kinetochore localization of either itself or BubR1 (Figure 1D–F). Notably, a fraction of Bub3K222R was still retained on kinetochores during anaphase, when both wild-type Bub3 and Bub3K222Q disassembled from kinetochores (Supplementary Figure S3D and E). These results suggest that Bub3-K222 crotonylation may promote Bub3 dissociation from kinetochores.

Given that sodium crotonate (NaCr) supplementation increases cellular crotonyl-CoA levels and global crotonylation (Sabari et al., 2015), we sought to determine whether NaCr affects Bub3 crotonylation and its localization at kinetochores. Western blot analysis demonstrated that NaCr treatment significantly enhanced both global crotonylation and Bub3 crotonylation (Supplementary Figure S4A). Additionally, NaCr treatment reduced Bub3 and BubR1 localization at kinetochores (Supplementary Figure S4B–D), a phenotype similar to that observed with the Bub3K222Q mutant.

To directly assess the influence of Bub3-K222 crotonylation, we employed a method that incorporates non-canonical amino acids (specifically crotonyl-lysine here) into proteins of interest in cells through genetic codon expansion (Supplementary Figure S4E; Song et al., 2021). The technique allowed us to successfully express engineered K222-crotonylated Bub3 (Bub3crK222) in HEK293T cells (Supplementary Figure S4F). IP assays indicated that, consistent with the results from point mutants, Bub3crK222 exhibited a significant reduction in the binding affinity to phosphorylated KNL1 (Figure 1G). In contrast, Bub3-K222 crotonylation did not affect its interactions with other proteins, such as BubR1 and BuGZ (Figure 1G). These findings suggest that Bub3-K222 crotonylation specifically weakens its interaction with phosphorylated KNL1, promoting the dissociation of Bub3 and its downstream factors from kinetochores. Immunofluorescence analysis confirmed that Bub3 with persistent crotonylation at K222 failed to localize at kinetochores, so did BubR1 (Figure 1H). These findings indicate that Bub3-K222 crotonylation disrupts its binding to KNL1 and impairs the localization of both itself and downstream SAC components.

We next examined the impact of abnormal dynamics of Bub3-K222 crotonylation on mitotic progression. Since the incorporation of crotonyl-lysine into Bub3 through genetic code expansion was ineffective in the cell line, such as HeLa, optimal for synchronization and real-time imaging of mitotic progression, we utilized the crotonylation site mutants of Bub3 instead. In HeLa cells with endogenous Bub3 knocked down, the expression of GFP-tagged Bub3K222Q resulted in a shortened mitotic duration, indicative of weakened SAC signaling (Figure 1I and J). Conversely, the Bub3K222R mutant exhibited prolonged mitotic progression, suggesting a delay in SAC silencing (Figure 1I and J). Additionally, most cells expressing either Bub3K222Q or Bub3K222R underwent chromosome segregation prior to the proper alignment of all chromosomes, leading to significant segregation defects such as lagging chromosomes and chromosome bridges (Figure 1K). These findings underscore the critical role of dynamic Bub3-K222 crotonylation in promoting Bub3 dissociation from kinetochores and timely silencing of SAC signaling.

In summary, this study characterizes the role of Bub3 crotonylation in SAC silencing and mitotic progression. During the metaphase-to-anaphase transition, crotonylation of Bub3 at K222 by p300 disrupts its interaction with phosphorylated KNL1, promoting the dissociation of Bub3 and its downstream SAC components from kinetochores, which consequently leads to the silencing of SAC signaling (Figure 1N). Disruption of the dynamics of Bub3 crotonylation results in SAC malfunction and defects in chromosome segregation. While this study primarily utilizes standard cell lines, the dynamic nature of Bub3 crotonylation is likely to extend across cancer cell lines and normal tissues, as Bub3, p300, and SIRT1 are ubiquitously expressed. However, the level and dynamics of Bub3 crotonylation are likely to exhibit complexity across different cancers, as both p300 and SIRT1 have been reported to play dual roles in tumorigenesis, dependent on the tumor types and stages (Garcia-Peterson and Li, 2021; Gronkowska et al., 2024). In many cancers, p300 is overexpressed and SIRT1 levels are reduced. Under these conditions, the elevated crotonylation of Bub3 may compromise its function within the SAC, potentially enhancing cancer adaptability and evolutionary fitness. Conversely, in certain cancers where p300 is mutated or SIRT1 is elevated, reduced crotonylation of Bub3 could disrupt the timely silencing of the SAC. Both scenarios underscore a remarkable role for Bub3 crotonylation in cancer biology, highlighting its potential to influence cancer adaptation and progression. Overall, our findings establish a direct link between Bub3 crotonylation and SAC silencing, offering new insights into the role of crotonylation in regulating mitotic progression and chromosome stability.

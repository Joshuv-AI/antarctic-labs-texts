Conditioned media from adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells improves movento induced lung damage in male rats
Authors: Iman Zangiabadi, Mohammad Amin Rajizadeh, Majid Askaripour, Mohammad Mehdi Bagheri, Elham Jafari, Soodeh Rajabi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Insecticides are chemicals agents used to kill insects to increase crop growth. But they can be toxic to humans and wildlife. Movento is an insecticide which is highly toxic to humans and animals by oral, dermal, and inhalation routes. This research is to assess the pulmonary impacts of this insecticide, which have not been explored in previous research. Wistar rats were adopted to four groups. 1- Control (CTL), 2- Movento (M), 3- M in conjunction with Basal medium (BM), 4- M in conjunction with Conditioned medium (CM). For evaluation of the degree of injury and fibrosis, the Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) and Masson’s trichrome stainings were done respectively. Oxidative stress markers and inflammatory mediators were analyzed utilizing appropriate assays. In rats administered Movento’s insecticide, antioxidant markers diminished in comparison to the control group; however, curation with CM markedly elevated them relative to the M and M + BM groups. Movento elevated histological score, fibrosis, MDA, TNF-α, and IL-6 compared to the control group, whereas CM remarkably diminished these parameters in relation to the M and M + BM groups. Conditioned media derived from mesenchymal stem cells (CM-MSC) may function as a therapeutic intervention for lung illnesses induced by movento in rat lungs. It may also serve as a potential treatment of alleviating lung injuries in farmers who are exposed to agricultural pesticides.

Introduction

Movento or Spirotetramat, is a novel cyclic ketoenol compound that has been used globally as a pesticide targeting various pests of crops. This compound works by inhibiting the acetyl-CoA carboxylase enzyme and has a distinctly different effect than all the toxins currently available on the market. It has been found that it exerts its systemic effects mostly by inhibiting lipid synthesis in the insect body [1, 2]. Movento is an insecticide containing 400 g per liter of spirotetramat, which is highly toxic to humans when ingested. It is also highly toxic to mice via oral, dermal, and inhalation routes [3]. This insecticide appears to exert its damaging effects by increasing oxidative stress and inflammation [4].

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are currently some of the most notable stem cells attracting the interest of investigators. These cells form a population of multipotent somatic stem cells found in multiple organs, such as adipose tissue. These cells display positivity for surface indices such as CD90, while showing negativity for hematopoietic surface markers like CD34 and CD45 [5, 6].

Types of culture media include balanced salt solutions, basic culture media, complex culture media, and serum-free media. When stem cells are placed in the vicinity of the culture medium, they secrete factors after growth and proliferation, which enter the culture medium and transform it into a culture medium known as stem cell-derived medium or Conditioned Media (CM). Therefore, MSC-derived culture medium (MSC-derived culture medium) is an appealing candidate for stem cell-based therapeutic administration. MSC-derived culture media have demonstrated the ability to enhance neuronal survival in the presence of glutamate toxicity, as well as under conditions of oxygen and glucose deprivation. Factors released from MSC-derived culture media may contribute to cellular regeneration and immunological modulation [7]. The antioxidant and anti-inflammatory impacts of stem cells and their derived culture media have been well-shown and appear to be one of their protective mechanisms [8].

This investigation intends to test the impacts of the Movento insecticide on rat lung tissue at both histological and molecular levels, marking the first investigation of its kind due to the toxin’s extensive application in agriculture.In addition, the impacts of stem cell-derived culture media on potential damage are investigated.

Materials and methods

Primary culture of hMSCA and Preparation of CM

Human mesenchymal stem cells derived from adipose tissue (hMSCA) were isolated via liposuction as previously described and characterized in our prior studies [9, 10]. The hMSCA were expanded in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) until reaching approximately 80% confluence. Subsequently, the cultures were maintained in serum-free DMEM for 48 h to collect conditioned medium (CM). The culture supernatants were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 3 min to remove cellular debris. The CM-hMSCA was obtained from cells at passages three to five. The protein concentration of CM-hMSCA was measured to be between 700 and 1400 µg/mL using the Pierce bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). After concentration, the media were stored at − 80 °C for later use.

Experimental protocol

All experiments in this study were conducted under the supervision and approval of the Ethics Committee of Kerman University of Medical Sciences (IR.KMU.AEC.1402.097). Twenty-four mature male Wistar rats, each weighing 270 ± 30 g, were employed. The rats were housed in a regulated environment with a 12-hour light-dark cycle, a constant temperature of 23 ± 1.4 °C, and a humidity level of 50 ± 4, with ad libitum access to food and drink. The animals were acquired from the Kerman University of Medical Sciences’ animal farm.

The rats were randomly divided into four experimental groups: 1- Control (CTL), 2- Movento (M), 3- M + BM (Basal media), and 4- M + CM (conditioned medium).

Movento was administered orally by gavage at a dose of 1000 mg/kg, with the volume adjusted according to the body weight of each rat, on days 1, 4, and 7 (every three days). The dose was selected based on our previous study demonstrating consistent cardiac injury in rats without excessive toxicity [11]. Lung injury was evaluated on day 10 after completion of the treatment protocol.

The conditioned media was administered intravenously (through tail vein) at a fixed volume of 150 µL per rat, corresponding to approximately 0.5–0.6 mL/kg based on the average body weight of 250–300 g. This dose was selected based on our previous study demonstrating its efficacy [10]. Movento was administered on days 1, 4, and 7, while conditioned media was given on all other days up to day 10, to evaluate its protective effect against lung injury.

After the end of the treatments, the rats were euthanized with intraperitoneal injection of ketamine and Xylazine (80 to 10 mg/kg). After being entirely sure of the anesthesia, the chest of the animals was split, and the lungs were removed. After humidification, the lungs were weighed, and a part of the lungs was placed in formalin for histological evaluation. In contrast, the other part was transferred to a- −80 freezer for molecular assessment.

Histological studies

A segment of lung tissue was preserved in formaldehyde, subsequently processed in formalin, sectioned, and stained with H&E to evaluate the extent of lung damage. Mason trichrome staining was utilized to evaluate the degree of fibrosis in the lung. The criteria encompassed alveolar and interstitial inflammation, bleeding, interstitial edema, atelectasis, and the development of hyaline membranes. Each criterion was graded on a scale from 0 to 4: a score of 0 indicated no injury, 1 corresponded to injury involving 25% of the observed field, 2 represented injury present in 50% of the field, 3 denoted injury affecting 75% of the field, and 4 signified that the entire field was injured [12]. Masson trichrome staining was employed to assess tissue fibrosis in accordance with the scoring system established by Hübner et al. [13]. The slides were examined under an Olympus microscope by a pathologist unaware of the experimental details.

Oxidative stress markers in the lung

Lung tissue was obtained by removal of a portion and mixed thoroughly in a cold solution of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at a pH of 7.4. The samples were centrifuged at 10,000 g (at 4 °C). The amount of total antioxidant capacity (TAC), malondialdehyde (MDA), glutathione peroxidase (GPx) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) were determined in the supernatant above using special kits.

Evaluation of lung inflammation

The levels of TNF-α (KPG-RTNFK) and IL-6 (KPG-RIL6K) were assessed for inflammation analysis utilizing the sandwich ELISA technique. Absorption was read using an ELISA reader (Eliza MAT 2000, DRG instruments, GmbH) at 450 nanometers [14].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the data was done with Prism (version 8). The Shapiro-Wilk test utilized to evaluate the whether the data follows a normal distribution. A range of statistical tests was utilized for data analysis, encompassing parametric tests like one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc analysis, and non-parametric testing, including the Kruskal-Wallis test succeeded by Dunn’s multiple comparisons test. The data are presented as the mean value plus or minus the standard error of the mean (SEM) and statistical significance was accepted at P < 0.05.

Results

Impact of conditioned medium on lung weight

The ratio of lung weight to body weight (LW/BW) increased in the movento group (p < 0.01). Basal media treatment could not reverse Movnto’s effects on lung weight. In rats treated with conditioned medium, the ratio of LW/BW decreased compared to the movento group (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1).

Impact of conditioned medium on oxidative stress factors in the lung

The antioxidant variables SOD, GPx, and TAC showed a reduction in the Movento and M + BM rat groups against the control group (p < 0.001). The basal medium exhibited no therapeutic efficacy. The CM elevated them to a standard level, which is noteworthy in comparison to the Movento group (p < 0.001). MDA levels in Movento and M + BM were elevated against the control group, while CM levels exhibited a substantial decrease (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2A-D).

The amount of TNF-α and IL-6 were elevated in the movento and M + BM groups relative to the normal rats (p < 0.001). CM considerably reduced the TNF-α concentration in comparison with the Movento group (p < 0.001). The expression levels of IL-6 decreased following treatment with CM; however, this alteration was not statistically significant (Fig. 3A-B).

Effect of conditioned medium on lung histopathology

The overall lung damage in the Movento and M + BM groups was moderate relative to the control group (p < 0.001). The CM group reduced this harm to a mild degree relative to the Movento group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 4A-E). A fibrosis score was examined in the pulmonary tissues of the study groups. This item exhibited an increase in the Movento and M + BM groups relative to the CTL group (p < 0.01) and demonstrated a substantial decrease post-treatment with CM (p < 0.05) (Fig. 5A-E).

Discussion

This study was designed for the first time to determine the impacts of ADMSC-CM on lung injury caused by Movento. The findings demonstrated that Movento exposure can cause lung injury, as indicated by increases in lung weight to body weight (LW/BW), histological injuries, inflammation via elevated levels of TNFα and IL-6, and oxidative stress via increased levels of MDA and a decrease in antioxidant factors. At the same time, CM therapy effectively reversed these alterations.

Our results showed that the ratio of lung weight to body weight (LW/BW) increased after 10 days of Movento gavage. This can indicate an increase in inflammation and edema in lung tissue. Additionally, Movento elevated levels of TNF-α and IL-6 as markers of inflammation. Although CM therapy reduced the levels of TNFα, it failed to affect IL-6 levels, although a significant decreasing trend was observed. In the histological assessment, the lung tissue of the exposed rats showed moderate pathological changes, which decreased to a mild level following 10 days of CM treatment.

These observations agree with the research findings of Liu et al., which showed that spirotetramate increased inflammation in the liver tissue of zebrafish by increasing TNFα and IL-6 [15]. Additionally, in our previous research, we observed that Movento induced inflammation in rat heart tissue, which was alleviated by CM [11].

In addition to inflammatory responses, spirotetramat and related spirocyclic insecticides are known to cause oxidative stress [16, 17]. Our findings suggest that Movento reduced the antioxidant defense capacity and induced oxidative stress, consistent with its oxidative effects observed in cardiac and reproductive tissues [17].

We showed that Movento increased MDA and decreased antioxidants, such as TAC, SOD, and GPX. Wu et al. indicated that Sub-lethal concentrations of spirotetramat led to oxidative damage and lipid peroxidation in the ovarian tissue of zebrafish [17]. On the other hand, the oxidative effects of spirotetramate were revealed in the testis tissue of rats via reducing superoxide dismutase (SOD), Lipid peroxidation (LPO), Glutathione peroxidase (GPx), Catalase CAT, and Glutathione [18]. Additionally, our previous study on heart tissue revealed the oxidative effects of movento, as evidenced by reduced TAC and increased MDA [11].

Although pesticides kill agricultural pests, and for this reason, their consumption continues to increase, it has been shown that they can have devastating effects on human health, especially among agricultural workers [19]. Pesticides can cause inflammation and induce cancer [20]. They also have adverse effects on the health of the nervous and cardiovascular systems [21, 22]. Oxidative stress is a common cause of injuries associated with pesticide exposure, and antioxidants have been shown to avoid or reduce the damaging impacts of pesticides [23, 24].

The therapeutic efficacy of MSC-derived conditioned medium is associated with its large amount of anti-inflammatory cytokines, growth factors, and antioxidant molecules. The antioxidant effects of stem cell-derived conditioned medium (SC-CM) have been proven in numerous studies [25–27]. Tang et al. demonstrated that the hucMSC conditioned medium mitigates lung injury by diminishing oxidative stress and inflammation [28]. A separate study revealed that MSC protects the lung from ischemia-reperfusion injury [29]. Furthermore, our prior research indicated that CM from human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells mitigates heart damage caused by Movento in male rats via influencing inflammation and oxidative stress [11]. Additionally, numerous studies have disclosed the anti-inflammatory impacts of SC-CM [30–32]. Mainly, it has been disclosed that SC-CM improve acute lung injury [33, 34], lung fibrosis [35, 36], and asthma [33, 37]. MSC-CM exerts anti-inflammatory actions by suppressing NF-κB signaling and modulating Nrf2/HO-1 pathways, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) and increasing anti-inflammatory mediators (e.g., IL-10) [36]. A better elucidation of the relative contributions of these factors represents an important direction for future mechanistic studies.

Furthermore, CM-based therapies have several advantages over whole-cell transplantation, including a reduced risk of tumorigenesis and easier storage and handling. These features support the feasibility of CM therapy as a practical alternative for treating pesticide-induced lung injury and possibly other inflammatory lung diseases.

Limitations:

This study has several limitations. We did not characterize the specific bioactive components of the MSC-conditioned media responsible for the observed protective effects. Although protective effects were observed at the histological and biochemical levels, the precise molecular pathways involved were not elucidated. Future studies should investigate the role of specific exosomal components, microRNAs, and signaling molecules in the CM. In addition, dose-response studies and long-term toxicity assessments are needed to fully approve the safety and efficacy of CM therapy in pesticide-induced lung injury. Longitudinal investigations are recommended to assess the sustainability of MSC-CM’s beneficial impacts, which is crucial for its prospective therapeutic uses.

Conclusions

This study conclusively revealed that oral administration of Movento diminishes antioxidant defense capacity and promotes inflammatory reactions. Additionally, histological evaluation of the lung tissue revealed moderate injury and fibrosis in the movento group. Finally, CM effectively reversed the effects of movento. Although this is an experimental research, the findings of this study can be used for workers who are exposed to pesticides.

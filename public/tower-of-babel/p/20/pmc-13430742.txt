Introduction

Oliceridine, a G protein-selective µ-agonist, has demonstrated superior analgesic efficacy with enhanced safety and tolerability, particularly concerning respiratory and gastrointestinal adverse events, compared to traditional opioids [1–3]. This advantageous profile is primarily due to its selective mechanism of action, which restricts its activity on the β-arrestin2 pathway, a pathway implicated in opioid-induced adverse reactions such as respiratory depression [4]. Consequently, oliceridine holds promise as a safer alternative to conventional opioids, offering new hope for pain management. However, despite its encouraging clinical application prospects, a thorough evaluation of its efficacy and safety necessitates further investigation. This is particularly pertinent for special populations, such as geriatric patients, whose unique physiological characteristics and prevalent comorbidities necessitate careful anaesthetic regimen selection.

Aging induces a multitude of systemic changes that can impact the health and safety of geriatric patients [5]. Decreases in muscle mass and bone density lead to weakened physical function, impaired balance, and age-related alterations in gait and posture [6]. These factors elevate the risk of falls, which can result in severe injuries like fractures and head trauma [7]. Additionally, metabolic changes and sensory perception alterations may influence the geriatric’s response to medications and increase the likelihood of accidents. Declining cognitive function can affect comprehension and compliance with medical procedures, including preoperative instructions for painless gastroscopy [8]. All these changes induced by aging may lead to increased anaesthetic risks and complexity associated with analgesic and sedative use for geriatric patients undergoing painless gastroscopy.

Conventional intravenous opioids are commonly used for analgesia during painless gastroscopy, but their use is constrained by the prevalence of opioid-related adverse events [9]. The combination of opioids with propofol, which also has respiratory and circulatory depressive effects, may further escalate adverse event risks [10]. Propofol and etomidate are frequently used intravenous sedative agents in painless gastroscopy [11–12]. The synergistic effect of propofol combined with etomidate (PE) has been shown to offer benefits for geriatric patients, such as improved hemodynamic stability, minimal respiratory depression, reduced adverse events, enhanced overall experience, and smoother postoperative recovery [13–14]. At our tertiary A hospital, anaesthesiologists have routinely employed dezocine combined with PE sedation for geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy, occasionally voicing concerns about this regimen’s efficacy and safety.

Given oliceridine’s potential in analgesia and its unknown application prospects for geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy, we plan to conduct a randomized controlled trial(RCT). This study will compare oliceridine combined with PE sedation to dezocine combined with PE sedation to elucidate the potential benefits of the former. Our aim is to furnish helpful clinical evidence and guidance for the novel selection of analgesics for geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy.

Methods

Study setting and participants

This trial is a single-center, prospective, double-blinded RCT using a two-arm, parallel group design.The study has been granted ethical approval by the Ethics Committee of Weihai Central Hospital(The ethical review number: LL-2024-060), and has been registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registration Center (http://www.chictr.org.cn, registration number: ChiCTR2500098774). This trial protocol is produced according to the applicable Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) reporting guidelines [15]. The study aims to recruit 112 geriatric outpatients aged 65 years and above from the department of anaesthesiology at the endoscopy center of our hospital. Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) Geriatric outpatients over 65 years old planned to undergo painless gastroscopy; (2) American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) classification I ~ II; (3) Geriatric outpatients will voluntarily participate in this study and sign the informed consent form.

Exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) Geriatric outpatients with known allergy to therapeutic agents, or taking opioids within 2 days before the examination, or with a history of abnormal recovery from surgery under general anaesthesia; (2) Geriatric outpatients with severe cardiac, cerebrovascular, respiratory, hepatic, renal, and metabolic disorders, severe ischemic heart disease, and chronic pain;3) Geriatric outpatients with obvious airway difficulties, nasopharyngeal or esophageal cancer, and psychiatric disorders; 4) Geriatric outpatients with acute respiratory tract infections that had not been cured in the last 2 weeks; 5) Geriatric outpatients with neuromuscular system disease, mental illness; 6) Geriatric outpatients who explicitly refused to participate in this study; 7) Geriatric outpatients who the investigator considers to be ineligible to participate.

Randomisation, study medication and blinding

Prior to the procedure, eligible geriatric outpatients will undergo a standardized pre-anaesthetic evaluation by Anaesthesiologist A. Eligible patients providing written informed consent will be randomized via a computer-generated sequence (block randomization with fixed block sizes of 6, 1:1 allocation) to either the OPE or DPE group. The randomization sequence will be concealed using tamper-proof, sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes prepared by an independent statistician.

Upon enrollment, Anaesthesiologist B uninvolved in clinical care will open the next sequentially assigned envelope and prepare study medications (oliceridine 0.02 mg/kg or dezocine 0.05 mg/kg) according to group allocation. Oliceridine or dezocine will be diluted with 0.9% saline to an equal volume of 5 mL under pharmacy supervision. Both solutions will be respectively dispensed into identical 5 mL syringes labeled with unique study codes. Anaesthesiologist B will transfer the visually indistinguishable syringes containing different study medications to Anaesthesiologist C. Anaesthesiologist C will intravenously administer the entire 5 ml solution of the study medication during anaesthesia induction and collect the intraoperative outcome data. Nurses in the post-anaesthesia care unit (PACU) will collect the outcome data following the patient’s stay in the PACU. All procedural staff (endoscopists, nurses, and Anaesthesiologist C) and patients will remain blinded to group assignment and study medications. Unblinding will be permitted only in emergency scenarios via a predefined protocol managed by an independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC).

Interventions and sedation protocol

Painless gastroscopies will be performed in dedicated endoscopy suites (Rooms 1–6) by endoscopists with over 5 years of experience. All participants will fast for over 8 h preoperatively. Total intravenous anaesthesia without endotracheal intubation or laryngeal mask airway for painless gastroscopy will be administered by Anaesthesiologist C. Preoxygenation (5 min via nasal cannula at 5 L/min before anesthesia induction) will be followed by continuous oxygen supplementation (3–6 L/min). Standard monitoring(via the Myriad IPM12 monitor) will include non-invasive blood pressure, electrocardiography, and oxygen saturation (SpO2). Hemodynamic stability will be maintained within ± 20% of baseline values using predefined thresholds: metaraminol 1 mg or ephedrine 6 mg bolus for hypotension, esmolol 10 mg for tachycardia. Peripheral venous access will be established with lactated Ringer’s solution infused at 200 mL/h. During anesthesia induction, Anaesthesiologist C will intravenously administer study medications followed by PE. PE will be prepared by mixing equal volumes of propofol (100 mg/10 mL) and etomidate (20 mg/10 mL). The final concentrations in the mixture will be 5 mg/mL for propofol and 1 mg/mL for etomidate. The dose for PE will be standardized as a concentration of 10 mg/mL to facilitate data collection and statistical analysis. The intravenous bonus of PE will be administered over a period of at least 60 s. Administration will be discontinued when the patient’s eyelash reflex ceases and a Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (MOAA/S) score ≤ 1 is achieved. The endoscopist will then insert the gastroscope (Model number: OLYMPUS Lucera LCV-260SL). During gastroscopy, the patient will breathe spontaneously while receiving oxygen via nasal cannula. When intraoperative SpO2 drops below 90%, the oxygen flow should be increased, and a jaw thrust along with airway clearance will be performed. If SpO2 continues to decline, the gastroscope should be immediately removed, and manual positive pressure ventilation with a resuscitation bag will be initiated. Emergency endotracheal intubation will be carried out according to clinical requirements. If geriatric outpatients are not sufficiently sedated, an supplemental use of 2 ~ 5 mL PE will be given intravenously. Participants will be transferred to PACU post-procedure and monitored for a minimum of 30 min. Discharge criteria include stable vital signs, independent ambulation, a modified Aldrete score of at least 9, and the absence of significant adverse events. Emergency unblinding protocols will be managed by an independent safety officer.

Data collection and management

An independent DMC has been established to resolve any ambiguities related to data collection through discussion. It consists of a chairperson (a seasoned anesthesiologist), an attending anesthesiologist, two statisticians, and a seasoned gastroenterologist. All data will be meticulously recorded on the case report forms, and any additional information or modifications related to this protocol must be signed off by each researcher. Once all participants have completed the study process, all data are entered into the electronic data capture (EDC) system and checked for errors.

Baseline data enroll demographics, comorbidities, ASA classification, and Mallampati classification of each eligible geriatric outpatient included.

Sample size

The sample size was determined from observational data collected during a preliminary experiment. A total of 10 geriatric outpatients who met the inclusion criteria participated in the preliminary experiment and received dezocine combined with PE sedation. The findings from the preliminary experiment showed a mean dose of PE of 154 mg with a standard deviation of 23 mg per anaesthesia induction. We hypothesized that oliceridine combined with PE sedation for painless gastroscopy would minimize the induction use of PE by at least 15 mg, compared with dezocine combined with PE sedation. Subsequently, a power of 0.9 and an alpha risk of 0.05 set in PASS15.0 software was observed implying that the sample size was 102 (51 per group) geriatric outpatients. Furthermore, to account for a possible 10% drop-out rate, the sample size was increased to 112 geriatric outpatients (56 per group).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS for Windows, version 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). A p-value of < 0.05 is considered statistically significant. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and histograms will be used to assess the normality of quantitative data. Quantitative data following a normal distribution will be expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For data not following a normal distribution, the Mann-Whitney U test will be used, and these data will be expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR). Geriatric outpatients’ characteristics and outcome measures between the two groups will be compared using Student’s independent t-test for normally distributed continuous variables. Comparison of count data will be performed using the χ2-test or Fisher’s exact test and expressed as number of cases and percentage. HR, MAP, and SpO2 levels collected during the observation period will be compared using repeated-measures ANOVA. Post hoc t-tests will be used for inter-group comparisons of hemodynamic variables at different time points following the repeated-measures analysis.

Discussion

This prospective, single-center, double-blinded RCT will enroll 112 geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy. The purpose of this study is to explore the efficacy and safety of oliceridine combined with PE sedation following the comparison with dezocine routely employed for geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy at our hospital. The primary outcomes to be evaluated are the use of PE sedation and the occurrence of adverse events during painless gastroscopy. Secondary outcomes to be assessed include hemodynamic parameters during painless gastroscopy and adverse events during PACU. Furthermore, exploratory outcomes to be evaluated encompass the gastroscopy time, the time to awakening in the PACU, the length of stay in the PACU, scores for pain, sedation, and modified Aldrete at PACU discharge, endoscopist satisfaction, patient satisfaction, and the readmission rate.

Dezocine, as a traditional µ-opioid receptor agonist, is widely used for pain management [17]. Its unique pharmacology makes it an effective analgesic with limited opioid-associated side effects [18]. Patients undergoing gastroscopy frequently receive dezocine for analgesia [10, 19]. At our hospital, nearly ten thousand patients undergo gastroscopy annually, and dezocine combined with PE sedation is the primary choice for anaesthesia during the procedure. However, geriatric outpatients may be at a higher risk of experiencing side effects, such as intraoperative respiratory depression, and postoperative nausea and vomiting [20–21]. Selecting an appropriate analgesic is more essential for geriatric outpatients to ensure a safe recovery from anaesthesia and discharge from the hospital.

Oliceridine (Olinvyk® Trevena, PA, USA) received approval from the United States Food and Drug Administration for clinical use on August 8, 2020 [22]. It is a novel µ-opioid receptor agonist and a member of a new class of biased ligands that selectively activate G-protein signaling while downregulating β-arrestin recruitment [23]. Previous studies have shown that oliceridine is a safe and effective intravenous analgesic for the relief of moderate to severe acute postoperative pain [24–26]. A similar study indicates that oliceridine demonstrates superior safety and enhances patient satisfaction in gastrointestinal endoscopic sedation; however, it did not differentiate by age and focus on high-risk geriatric participants [27]. This study will specifically explore the potential of oliceridine for enhancing the analgesic efficacy and safety in geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy compared with traditional dezocine employed at our hospital.

This trial has several limitations. First, it does not focus on the optimal dose of oliceridine combined with PE sedation for geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy. Second, almost all outcome assessments are conducted before discharge from the hospital, with limited long-term effects of oliceridine on geriatric outpatients evaluated beyond this period. Third, the use of dezocine for analgesia in geriatric outpatients of the control group may limit the generalizability of the advantages of oliceridine to other common types of opioids. Fourth, this trial lacks anaesthesia depth monitoring like electroencephalography (EEG) during painless gastroscopy, which may preclude objective evaluation of anaesthesia depth in a timely manner when patients’ consciousness and reflex responses deepen. Moreover, the exact allowed dose of PE sedation during painless gastroscopy is not adopted in advance with the data about the induction and supplemental use subsequently collected by Anaesthesiologist C according to actual clinical conditions. Further related studies could be well-designed and conducted to address these limitations.

In conclusion, this protocol is designed to prepare for the subsequent RCT that will explore the efficacy and safety of oliceridine combined with PE sedation in geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy. We expect that oliceridine could be a better option for analgesia, and it combined with PE sedation will be more effective and safer than traditional dezocine combined with PE sedation in geriatric outpatients undergoing painless gastroscopy.

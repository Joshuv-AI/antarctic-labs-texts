Oxygen Saturation Targeting and Retinopathy Management in Very Preterm Neonates: An International Survey

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVariation in the uptake of evidence-based practices and adoption of unproven therapies by neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) might contribute to clinical variability. The objective was to survey current oxygen saturation targets (SpO2), the use of automatic adjustment of inspired oxygen in infants on respiratory support, the criteria for routine retinal examinations for retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), and the use of anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) agents to treat ROP in the International Network for Evaluating Outcomes for Neonates (iNeo).MethodsOnline pre-piloted anonymous questionnaires on care practices in 2023 for extremely preterm (<29 weeks) infants were sent to the directors of 608 NICUs in the iNeo. Four questions concerned ROP management and results were compared with a similar 2015 survey.ResultsThere were 11 participating networks from 12 high-income countries and one from a middle-income country. The overall NICU response rate was 63% (382 units). Despite variability between NICUs, within networks, there was limited change in SpO2 targets between 2015 and 2023. The median upper and lower SpO2 targets were 95% and 89%; in 18% of NICUs, the upper target was ≥96%, and in 13%, the lower target was ≤85%. Automated loop systems for controlled oxygen delivery were used in 24% of NICUs. Most NICUs (78%) used a combination of birthweight and gestation as ROP screening criteria. Intravitreal anti-VEGF agents were used to treat ROP in all networks and by 76% of NICUs.DiscussionThere was considerable variation in care practices between NICUs, and the relationship of this to clinical outcomes should be explored.

Introduction

The International Network for Evaluating Outcomes in Neonates (iNeo) is a collaboration of national or regional neonatal networks from 11 high-income countries that prospectively collect data on care practices and clinical outcomes of very preterm infants [1]. One of the purposes of the iNeo is to monitor the adoption of evidenced-based practices and of “practice drift” as a background to examining the variability in outcomes.

An enduring problem in neonatology has been to determine the optimal oxygen saturation target in very preterm infants to minimize competing adverse outcomes, including mortality on one hand and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) on the other. The Neonatal Oxygenation Prospective Meta-analysis (NeOProM) collaboration, comprising five trials comparing higher (91–95%) versus lower (85–89%) oxygen saturation targets (SpO2) in infants <28 weeks of gestation, with a primary outcome of death or neurodevelopmental disability at 18–24 months and a preplanned individual patient data analysis, addressed this question and reported in 2018 [2]. One of the NeOProM trials, the Surfactant, Positive Pressure, and Oxygenation Randomized Trial (SUPPORT), also randomized infants to early continuous airways pressure or intubation and surfactant administration. SUPPORT reported outcomes at hospital discharge when infants randomized to the lower oxygen saturation target group had an increased risk of mortality (RR 1.27; 95% CI: 1.01–1.60) (p = 0.04) [3].

By 2015, all individual NeOProM trials had either published 18–24 month outcomes or reported these at scientific meetings, and practice was already changing. In response, in 2016, the iNeo surveyed oxygen saturation targets pre-2015 and from 2015 onward and found that a majority of neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) adopted higher SpO2 targets than formerly [4]. Since that time the full NeOProM results, confirming increased mortality associated with the lower target range [2], and commentaries have been published [5, 6]. NeOProM studied which of two oxygen saturation targets was most optimal, but a major issue is to achieve compliance with those targets, which is often poor [7]. There is increasing evidence that automatic adjustment of inspired oxygen via a feedback loop from the SpO2 achieves greater compliance with targets than manual adjustment [8], and a new generation of neonatal ventilators provides this modification.

Because retinal examinations used to detect ROP (often called screening) can be painful for infants and place a considerable burden on ophthalmologists’ time, the birthweight and/or gestation criteria used to trigger screening are regularly reviewed in many jurisdictions to better target screening based on local epidemiology. Some networks have recommended revised criteria based on these data [9], but it is not clear if these are followed by all NICUs in the network. Additionally, over the past decade, the treatment of ROP has been changing with intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) agents often favored over well-established laser therapy, although there are still concerns about delayed recurrence of ROP and systemic complications with this therapy [10]. As part of a larger survey of current care practices in the iNeo we sought information related to ROP concerning: (a) current oxygen saturation targets, (b) use of automatic adjustment of inspired oxygen, (c) the criteria (birthweight and/or gestation) for routine mandatory ROP screening, and (d) the use of anti-VEGF agents as treatment of ROP.

Methods

An online, pre-piloted, anonymous questionnaire on NICU organization and care practices for extremely preterm (<29 weeks; EP) infants (55 questions) was sent to the directors of 608 NICUs in the iNeo in July 2023. Two reminders were sent. Information was sought on practices in 2023. The participating networks and number of NICUS invited were the same in 9 networks as in the 2015 survey: Australia/New-Zealand (ANZNN, n = 30), Canada (CNN, n = 32), Finland (FinMBR, n = 5), Israel (INN, n = 26), Japan (NRNJ, n = 292; includes approximately 122 non-NRNJ participating units), Spain (SEN1500, n = 55), Sweden (SNQ, n = 9), Switzerland (SNN, n = 9), and Tuscany, Italy (TuscanNN, n = 4); and three additional networks were from France (SFN, n = 70), Poland (PSN, n = 56), and Brazil (BNN, n = 20). The latter network, the only network from a non-high-income country, is a voluntary collaborative group of 20 Brazilian level II–III neonatal university intensive care units, located in 15 cities in 7 Brazilian states, which maintains a clinical database of information about very low birthweight infants [11]. We followed the Consensus-Based Checklist for Reporting of Survey Studies (CROSS) guidelines for reporting this survey results, and the completed checklist is provided in online supplementary Supplement 3 (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000552726).

Four questions relevant to oxygen and ROP management are included in online supplementary file 2. They related to: (1) oxygen saturation target range, (2) whether automated loop system is used, (3) criteria for ROP screening, and (4) use of anti-VEGF.

Results

The overall NICU response rate was 63% (382 units). The response rates were 100% of units from Brazil, Finland, Sweden, Switzerland, and Tuscany; 98% from Poland; 97% from France; 93% from Canada; 88% from Israel; 70% from Australia-New Zealand; 54% from Spain; and 37% from Japan.

SpO2 Targeting

The upper and lower SpO2 limits for bedside monitoring in the 3 survey periods – pre-2015, 2015, 2023 – varied within and between networks (Fig. 1). Within networks, there was relatively limited change between 2015 and 2023. In 2023, the median upper SpO2 target was 95%, with 50% of NICUs choosing this, while 18% of NICUs in eight of the networks chose 96% or higher (Fig. 1a). The median lower target was 89%; 31% of NICUs chose 90%, 25% chose 88%, and 13% in nine networks chose 85% or lower (Fig. 1b). Among the networks included in the survey for the first time in 2023, France had on average wider saturation targets than many other units (88%–95%/96%) and with the most common lower target (88%) was more similar to pre-2015 targets in other networks. In Canada, 47% of NICUs also chose a lower SpO2 target of 88% but with an upper target of 92%/93% in 2023. The use of automated loop systems for controlled FiO2 delivery was not widely used in neonatal units in most networks in this 2023 survey, being used in 24% of NICUs overall for some babies and varying between 70% of NICUs in Israel and no NICUs in Finland (Fig. 2).

ROP Screening Criteria

ROP screening criteria are shown in Figure 3a, b. Most NICUs used a combination of birthweight and gestation with only 74 (19%) units, spread across all networks except two, not including birthweight in the criteria (unless gestation is unknown). Nine NICUs, in three networks, did not include gestation in their screening criterion. In four networks, a birthweight criterion of <1,500 g was most common (Brazil 85%, Israel 74%, Spain 60%, Tuscany 50% of NICUs); whereas in other networks, <1,250 g or lower was most frequent (Fig. 3b). Overall, 41% of NICUs used <32 weeks as gestation criterion for screening, 23% used <31 weeks, and 14% used <30 weeks or less (Fig. 3a). There was considerable variation in the screening criteria between NICUs within most networks.

Discussion

As in our previous survey, which examined neonatal care practices in 2015, this international survey, including 12 neonatal networks from 13 countries, has shown considerable variation in practices relevant to ROP in extremely preterm infants, both between countries and within each network. In the nine networks that participated in both surveys, the number of units responding was no different in 3 networks, differed by 7 or less units in 4 networks, and was reduced by 17 and 43 units in two large networks. In 2015, a majority of NICUs changed both lower and upper SpO2 targets to be 2–3% higher than previously, while fewer NICUs adopted an upper SpO2 target >95% [4]. In 2023, there were relatively few changes in SpO2 targets compared with the 2015. In 2015, Canada and Japan were the only networks that did not see a change in the median lower SpO2 target, being 88% in both cases. This has remained similar in 2023, although the median lower target in Japan changed slightly to 89%. Among the three networks in the present survey not participating in 2015, Brazil and Poland both had median lower SpO2 targets of 90%, but for France this was 88%. Canadian NICUs mostly contributed to the NeOProM collaboration as part of the Canadian Oxygen Trial (COT), which reported no differences in outcomes, death, or neurodisability at 18–22 months of age between the higher and lower saturation target groups [12], and we previously suggested this might have influenced the local choice of SpO2 targets. The Canadian Neonatal Network has published an observational study over the years 2011–2018 comparing mortality and morbidities in infants <29 weeks of gestation when SpO2 target ranges were intermediate (88%–93%) or high (90%–95%) [13]. Survival without morbidity was higher in the intermediate target group (adjusted odds ratio 1.59; 95% CI: 1.04, 2.45).

While there remains debate about the most optimal SpO2 target range for VP infants, one widely accepted conclusion from the NeOProM study is that the target should be higher than 85%–89% to decrease mortality rates [5, 6]. Given that, it is perhaps surprising that 13% of NICUs in our survey chose a lower target of 85% or below. We cannot rule out the possibility that the answers for these NICUs mistakenly relate to alarm settings rather than SpO2 targets, although the survey question clearly relates to the former. Unlike mortality and other major morbidities, the incidence of bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) has not declined and might even have increased in recent years [14]. NeOProM reported that the lower SpO2 target was associated with a significant reduction in oxygen requirement at 36 weeks of post-menstrual age. However, three of the five NeOProM trials used a physiological definition of BPD and meta-analysis of these data showed no difference between the SpO2 target groups [15]. More research into what influences clinical choices when there is strong evidence for a particular practice could be informative.

Our results also show that there is some clinical drift to use automated control of inspired oxygen delivery, which is already in use for some infants in 24% of NICUs overall and in 10 of the 12 networks surveyed. There are an increasing number of reports comparing automated adjustment of FiO2 with manual adjustment, with the former consistently showing better compliance [8] and short-term benefits in one study [16]. A randomized trial of 1,080 infants in four countries (the planned target of 2,340 was not reached because of poor recruitment) comparing automated and manual control of inspired oxygen concentration reported no difference in the composite endpoint of death, NEC, BPD, or severe ROP, or in individual components [17]. Of note, some closed-loop systems only offer a fixed saturation range. Caution has been urged for routine use of the technology until benefits and safety have been properly assessed [18].

Decisions about which infants should qualify for routine retinal examinations for ROP should be based on the local epidemiology. Some countries, notably Sweden [19], have well-established ROP registers and all the networks in the iNeo collaboration report ROP by both birthweight and gestation. So it was somewhat surprising that there was such variability of the screening criteria within networks. Individual hospitals [20] and countries [19] have reported an increased frequency of ROP following the adoption of higher SpO2 targets, although other large neonatal networks have reported a continuing decline in rates of any and severe ROP over the past decade [14, 21]. It seems likely that individual NICU preferences and circumstances might contribute to decisions on ROP screening criteria.

Laser therapy to the avascular retina has been the mainstay of treatment for ROP for several decades. More recently, intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy has been increasingly used, in part because of the relative ease of administration compared with laser and because nonspecialist pediatric ophthalmologists were familiar with these agents when used for adult eye diseases. Such use is now supported by randomized controlled trials of bevacizumab [22] and ranibizumab (RAINBOW) [23] versus laser. The evidence suggests that anti-VEGF therapy is associated with less severe myopia compared with laser, while reactivation of ROP is more frequent following anti-VEGF and can occur after a prolonged interval, necessitating the need for careful follow-up [10]. There are also concerns about systemic side-effects of anti-VEGF therapy, although the recent 5-year follow-up of the RAINBOW trial did not find any evidence of harm [24]. Most commentators recommend anti-VEGF agents as first-line treatment for zone I disease and aggressive ROP, although with lower doses than in the BEAT-ROP trial [10]. It is also possible that anti-VEGF therapy is often followed by laser therapy after an interval to allow for retinal vascular growth [25].

The strengths of this study include that it represents a large international collaboration involving mostly population-based networks, using a survey with the same methodology and similar questions to a previous 2015 survey, allowing for changes or consistency in practices to be identified. The inclusion of a middle-income country for the first time should increase the relevance of our findings.

Weaknesses of the study include the limited number of questions we asked in order to keep the survey as brief as possible and so increase the likelihood of obtaining responses. The questionnaire was sent to the NICU directors, and we do not know if the answers are their responses alone or are a NICU-agreed position. We asked specifically about SpO2 targets and not alarm settings, but it is possible some responses refer to the latter. We did not seek justification for each NICUs choice of SpO2 targets or other practices. When asking about use of automated adjustment of inspired oxygen, we did not specify if this was used exclusively in infants ventilated via an endotracheal tube but assume this is so, although the technology has now also been developed to be used with continuous airways pressure [26]. Nor did we determine if the limited use of this option was because of lack of evidence of benefit or lack of equipment. Further details of anti-VEGF use, including the agent used and the dose, would be informative, as would whether this was primary treatment, in what circumstances, and whether combined with laser. Lastly, and importantly, we have not correlated the survey findings to clinical outcomes which will be done in future when data are available.

Conclusions

There was considerable variation in practices both within NICUs and between countries. However, within networks there was limited change in SpO2 targets between 2015 and 2023. Overall, the most common lower oxygen saturation target was 90%, but in two networks, it was 88%. The use of automated adjustment of inspired oxygen via a feedback loop from the SpO2 was in use for some babies in nearly a quarter of the iNeo NICUs. Most NICUs in all networks used a combination of gestation and birthweight as criteria for routine mandatory retinal examination for ROP, but no network followed one criterion. The reasons behind variations in practice should be explored, as should the relationship between clinical practices and outcomes.

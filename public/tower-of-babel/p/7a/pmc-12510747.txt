Optimizing palliative radiotherapy for breast cancer with skin involvement: technical and methodologic considerations
Authors: Gonca Altınışık İnan, İpek Pınar Aral, Çağkan Ergiden, Fatma Betül Ayrak, Volkan Çevik
Published: 2025

We read with great interest the recent article by Min et al. [1] addressing the efficacy of palliative radiotherapy (RT) for breast cancer patients with skin involvement. The authors should be commended for presenting valuable real world data on a challenging clinical subset where symptom control remains paramount. However, while the study offers important insights, we would like to raise several methodological concerns and offer constructive suggestions for future research.

The evaluation of the skin dose and associated toxicity is critically dependent on the treatment planning algorithms and delivery devices used [2,3]. Unfortunately, insufficient information is provided about the RT planning systems, dose calculation algorithms (e.g., anisotropic analytical algorithm, Monte Carlo), and linear accelerator models. These factors are known to influence surface dose distribution and may significantly impact local control and skin toxicity outcomes. Although bolus use was reported in 25.6% of patients, details regarding its thickness, frequency, and placement were not provided. In addition, no target delineation criteria were described, which limits reproducibility, particularly for lesions close to the skin [4]. Respiratory motion, especially in free breathing settings, may also affect target coverage and skin dose if not properly managed. Addressing these technical variables would strengthen the interpretability of the findings.

Although the study broadly refers to the treatment as “palliative,” the therapeutic intent for individual cases is not clearly specified. Several patients had extended follow up durations and received high biologically effective doses (BEDs; >100 Gy), which may indicate heterogeneous clinical scenarios, potentially including consolidative or long term disease control strategies. Without a clear delineation of treatment goals, whether symptom relief, local control, or curative intent, the interpretation of survival and tumor control outcomes becomes less straightforward. Distinguishing between strictly palliative, symptom oriented RT and potentially curative or consolidative RT is crucial, especially given the diverse prognoses of patients with skin involvement. Providing more detailed stratification according to treatment intent, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, and systemic therapy history would enrich the clinical context.

A pivotal conclusion of the study hinges on the dichotomization of the BED at 75 Gy, which was determined via receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. However, essential statistical details for this cut-off are lacking, such as area under the curve, sensitivity, specificity, and confidence interval information [5]. Without these metrics, the robustness of this threshold remains questionable. We recommend presenting these parameters to support the clinical applicability of BED stratification.

In conclusion, this study makes a meaningful contribution to the understanding of the role of RT in skin-involved breast cancer. However, further refinements in methodology, technical reporting, and statistical transparency would strengthen its implications. Future studies could benefit from prospective designs, standardized bolus protocols, and the integration of patient reported outcomes to better capture the real impact of RT in this unique and underserved patient population.

Libman–sacks endocarditis after heart transplantation: images in cardiology
Authors: Tasuku Hada, Osamu Seguchi, Yoshihiko Ikeda, Yasumasa Tsukamoto, Luca Monzo, Vincenzo Nuzzi, Matteo Busti
Published: 2026

Case description

A 63-year-old woman with dilated cardiomyopathy underwent heart transplantation (HTx). Despite mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD) with anti-ribonucleoprotein antibody positivity, she remained eligible for transplantation owing to absent systemic manifestations. The post-transplant course was initially favourable with good allograft function [left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) 65%] and no rejection. Seven months post-HTx, she developed acute heart failure requiring inotropic support, with diffuse hypokinesis (LVEF 43%) without any significant valvular disease or echocardiographic evidence of endocardial thickening or thrombus. Endomyocardial biopsy (EMB) revealed multifocal lymphocyte infiltration with myocyte damage and endocardial thickening with mural thrombus despite continuing anticoagulant therapy for atrial fibrillation, consistent with acute cellular rejection (Grade 2R) and Libman–Sacks endocarditis (LSE) without antibody-mediated rejection (Figure 1A–C). Steroid pulse therapy resolved rejection; however, severe diastolic dysfunction accompanied by LSE persisted, a finding further supported by follow-up EMB at 12 months post-HTx (Figure 1D–F). Despite the addition of monthly intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) therapy and hydroxychloroquine (HCQ), recurrent heart failure with endocarditis continued. Follow-up EMB demonstrated endocardial fibrous thickening with sparse lymphocytic infiltration, consistent with established fibrotic scarring (Figure 1G, H). The patient died at 36 months post-HTx and autopsy revealed circumferential endocardial scarring in heart (Figure 1I).

This case highlights the complexity of managing autoimmunity-related complications after HTx. LSE is a rare autoimmune-associated condition, uncommonly seen with MCTD and never reported in transplanted hearts.1 LSE has been reported in the cardiac valves and mural endocardium, with a poor prognosis.2,3 The therapeutic approach typically involves immunosuppressive agents, such as HCQ and IVIg, combined with anticoagulation therapy, although treatment protocols remain unestablished.1 This case demonstrates the critical need to proactively seek a definitive pathological diagnosis in patients with subclinical but seropositive autoimmune diseases after HTx, even when echocardiographic findings are inconclusive. Moreover, further careful consideration must be given to the selection of transplant recipients.

Randomised controlled trial on the efficacy of adding cognitive remediation therapy to methylphenidate in adult patients with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and addictive disorders (META): CRT in ADHD and addiction – META study protocol
Authors: Clémence Cabelguen, Gaëlle Challet-Bouju, Elsa Thiabaud, Yann Donnio, Léo Lefebvre, Jean-Benoît Hardouin, Marie Grall-Bronnec
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAttention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) affects 2.5% of adults in the general population, compared with more than 20% among individuals treated for addictive disorders (substance use disorders (SUD) and/or behavioural addictions (BAs)). The presence of ADHD is associated with earlier onset, greater severity and poorer prognosis of addictive disorders. In this context, pharmacological treatments, including methylphenidate (MPH), are widely recommended for adults with ADHD. However, their efficacy remains moderate and may be limited by poor adherence. The association between ADHD and addictive disorders may be explained by shared endophenotypes, particularly neuropsychological patterns, which may further amplify adherence difficulties. Cognitive remediation therapy (CRT) targets impaired cognitive functions and promotes compensatory strategies, and may thereby enhance treatment adherence and overall efficacy. However, evidence in adults with ADHD, especially those with comorbid addictive disorders, remains limited and methodologically constrained. We hypothesise that combining MPH with CRT targeting shared neuropsychological deficits will be more effective for patients with ADHD and comorbid addictive disorders. Demonstrating the efficacy of this combined approach could promote the widespread use of CRT in the multimodal management of ADHD, providing patients with access to affordable and autonomous care. The objective of this study is to evaluate whether this combined approach improves functional and symptomatic outcomes in the short and medium term.Methods and analysisMETA (MEthylphénidate dans la comorbidité TDAH – Addiction(s)) is a multicentre, randomised, single-blind controlled trial (NCT06906328). It will include 248 adults (124 per group) with ADHD requiring MPH treatment and at least one addictive disorder (SUD and/or BA). After stabilisation of MPH dosage, eligible patients will be enrolled and randomised to either (1) active CRT+MPH using PRESCO software (HappyNeuron) or (2) control CRT+MPH using AUDITICO software (HappyNeuron). CRT sessions will take place two times weekly for 12 weeks, both at the hospital and at home. Follow-up visits will occur at the end of CRT (short-term) and 6 months after the final session (medium-term). Semistructured clinical interviews on psychiatric and addictive disorders, neuropsychological assessments and self-report questionnaires of ADHD, impulsivity, self-esteem and emotion dysregulation will be administered in an unblinded manner at baseline and follow-up assessments. The primary endpoint is defined as a reduction of at least 30% in the Weiss Functional Impairment Rating Scale total score from baseline to medium-term follow-up.Ethics and disseminationThe study has ethical approval from the French National Ethics Committee (24 December 2024) and follows Good Clinical Practice and Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials 2025 guidelines. Participants will provide written informed consent, and data will be pseudonymised and securely archived for 15 years. Data management and monitoring will follow internal procedures, with annual quality checks. Results will be shared with patients via a simplified summary and with professionals through publications, with preprints and final papers on a French open-access repository.Trial registration numberNCT06906328.

Introduction

Rationale

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by age-inappropriate impairments in attention modulation and/or hyperactivity and impulsivity, to varying degrees.1 It affects approximately 5% of children2 and 2.5% of adults3 4 in the general population. The treatment of adult ADHD is multimodal, combining pharmacological and psychosocial approaches.5 Methylphenidate (MPH) is the only medication authorised for market approval and reimbursed by the national health insurance in the treatment of adult ADHD in France.6 The efficacy of MPH has been extensively documented,7 with a notable effect size compared with placebo (standardised mean difference of 0.78 on core symptoms of ADHD).8 The duration of the therapeutic effect is correlated with the half-life of the active ingredient, and there is no curative or residual effect when the treatment is discontinued. A significant dose-response effect has been identified,9 10 highlighting the need for clinicians to regularly optimise pharmacological treatment to address fluctuations in ADHD symptoms and functional impairment.11 Despite a standardised treatment regimen, the response to MPH varies across patients,12 with moderate efficacy but bothersome side effects, leading some patients to discontinue treatment prematurely.1316 Furthermore, the moderate efficacy of treatment may, in part, be explained by poor adherence,17 as well as the long-term nature of the prescription.18 Medication discontinuation rates are high: 65% of children, 47% of adolescents, 39% of young adults and 48% of adults discontinue treatment within the first year. Discontinuation rates increase over time, with 50%–60% of children and 30%–40% of adolescents and adults stopping treatment after 5 years.14

The complexity of ADHD is compounded by its frequent association with other disorders, including developmental, psychological, psychiatric and physical disorders, as well as socio-professional difficulties. Specifically, children with ADHD are at greater risk of developing substance use disorders (SUD) in adulthood,19 and the prevalence of SUD is higher among individuals with ADHD (ranging from 2.3% to 41.2%) compared with those without ADHD (0%–16.6%).20 Conversely, the prevalence of ADHD is three times higher among individuals with SUD in the general population (10.8% vs 3.8%)21 and is significantly higher in clinical samples. Among patients seeking care for addictive disorders, this rate approaches 20%.22 Substance use, particularly psychostimulants (such as amphetamines and cocaine), sometimes serves as a form of self-medication.23 24 Beyond SUD, the prevalence of behavioural addictions (BAs) is notably higher in individuals with ADHD.25 26 Several studies have examined the prevalence of ADHD or ADHD symptoms in patients with various BAs27: 39%, 36% and 26% of patients with gaming disorder (GmD),28 sexual addiction (SA)29 and gambling disorder (GbD),30 respectively. When ADHD is comorbid with addictive disorders, these disorders tend to be more severe, follow a more chronic course31 and are more likely to be associated with other psychiatric conditions.32 33 The relationship between ADHD and addictive disorders is complex and supports the concept of dual disorders.34 A dual disorder refers to the coexistence of at least one addictive and one psychiatric disorder, resulting in a new pathology with multiple synergistic effects that lead to significant changes in symptomatology, decreased treatment efficacy and a more severe course of the disorders.35

The association between ADHD and addictive disorders may result from common endophenotypes (such as neuropsychological traits), with shared genetic and epigenetic vulnerabilities.36 37 Each of these disorders is characterised by executive dysfunction, affecting working memory, inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility, as well as attention modulation impairment and memory difficulties.3840 Neuropsychological deficits associated with addictive disorders could exacerbate attention deficits and limit compensatory mechanisms. Even mild neuropsychological deficits are significant predictors of treatment dropout, both for addictive disorders41 and ADHD.42 The adherence issues, and therefore the overall treatment efficacy for ADHD, are thus exacerbated when there are co-occurring addictions.43

While several epidemiological studies have examined the frequent co-occurrence of ADHD and addictive disorders, longitudinal studies on the therapeutic response to MPH are scarce, particularly in adults.42 Moreover, most drug trials focus on patients with non-comorbid ADHD, which limits the generalisability of the results.13 Specifically, the impact of neuropsychological deficits on treatment efficacy in cases of comorbid ADHD and addictive disorders has not yet been thoroughly investigated.

In this context, it is crucial to optimise the therapeutic management of patients with ADHD and to develop strategies to improve treatment efficacy44 and treatment adherence,45 particularly in the case of comorbid addictions.

Cognitive remediation therapy (CRT) is a cognitive approach involving specific activities designed to enhance a cognitive skill or general cognitive ability through repetition over a defined period of time.46 By intensively training deficient cognitive functions on one hand and encouraging the development of compensatory strategies on the other, CRT could serve as a valuable therapeutic tool.47 It has the potential to produce beneficial effects that persist over time and translate into objectively measurable improvements in daily life. Authors recommend not targeting just one cognitive function but adopting a multi-neuropsychological approach that targets several functions to optimise the transfer to daily living and promote long-term improvement.4850

To the best of our knowledge, only a few studies on CRT have focused on adults with ADHD, and these studies primarily targeted a single cognitive function (such as working memory or attentional control).51 52 The populations studied were often over-selected, excluding individuals with comorbid addictions52 or based on small sample sizes.51 Furthermore, cognitive training has been shown to either be associated with pharmacological treatment53 or not.51 47Finally, CRT has not yet been tested as a means to enhance adherence to ADHD pharmacotherapy.54

Furthermore, most therapeutic trials focus primarily on the improvement or remission of ADHD symptoms,12 with a clinician-view approach.55 While symptomatic improvement can be linked to functional improvement,56 the two are not always strongly correlated.57 58 In some cases, a significant reduction in ADHD symptoms does not lead to improved functioning,59 while in other cases, improved functioning may occur despite persistent ADHD symptoms.55 Therefore, measuring both functional and symptomatic improvement is crucial for assessing the overall course of the disorder. Adopting a more patient-centred approach, Weiss has recently proposed using functional remission as a study outcome to evaluate therapeutic response.55

Hypotheses

We hypothesise that treatment for ADHD with comorbid addictive disorders will be more effective when MPH is combined with CRT targeting shared neuropsychological deficits. We will conduct a randomised controlled trial comparing active and control CRT, both delivered in addition to MPH, to evaluate functional and symptomatic improvement in the short and medium terms. The study’s hypotheses and objectives have been preregistered in the Open Science Framework.60

Objectives

Primary objective

The primary objective of the META trial is to evaluate the efficacy of CRT+MPH compared with control CRT+MPH in patients diagnosed with co-occurring ADHD and addictive disorders. The efficacy is defined as the reduction of at least 30% in the functional impairment score (on the Weiss Functional Impairment Rating Scale (WFIRS-S), see Methods) from baseline to short-term (end of the CRT).55

Secondary objectives

The META trial also aims to evaluate the efficacy of CRT+MPH compared with control CRT+MPH on (1) the functional impact of ADHD from baseline to the medium term (6 months after the end of CRT); (2) the ADHD symptomatology between baseline and the short and medium terms; (3) neuropsychological deficits, the severity of comorbid addictive disorders, psychopathological characteristics and adherence to MPH between baseline and the short and medium terms and (4) to describe the evolution of functional impact in the short and medium terms, according to ADHD subtype (predominantly inattentive, predominantly impulsive/hyperactive or combined type).

Methods and analysis

The study’s design, conduct, measures and outcomes were preregistered in the Open Science Framework.61 62 Moreover, the trial was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with the identification NCT06906328.

Design

META is a multicentre, randomised, single-blind controlled trial. Procedures added by the research involve minimal risks and constraints (RIPH2 for Category 2 human research French category).

Patient and public involvement

Patients were involved in the design, conduct and dissemination plans of this study. During the protocol development phase, a panel of adults diagnosed with ADHD and addictive disorders was consulted to provide input on the study objectives, intervention acceptability and outcome measures. Their feedback helped refine the CRT programme and optimise the assessment schedule.

Participants

Description of the population

Participants will correspond to patients aged >18 years with ADHD, requiring treatment with MPH according to European guidelines,5 and at least one addictive disorder. The META trial involves three French university hospitals (University Hospitals of Nantes, Tours and Brest) and also three non-university public mental health centres (Georges Daumezon Hospital in Bouguenais, Loire-Atlantique; Georges Daumezon Hospital in Fleury-les-Aubrais, Loiret and Finistère Sud Hospital in Quimper, Finistère) in order to better represent the heterogeneity of patients with ADHD addiction(s) in terms of severity, clinical and sociodemographic characteristics.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) aged 18 years or older; (2) recent diagnosis of ADHD confirmed by the DIVA-5 (Diagnostisch Interview Voor ADHD V5) interview; (3) indication for treatment with MPH according to European guidelines,5 with no contraindications (particularly cardiologic ones); (4) at least one comorbid addictive disorder (SUD and/or BA) and (5) affiliation with the French healthcare system.

The non-inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) significant cognitive impairment or insufficient proficiency in French (reading or writing) preventing reliable data collection; (2) pregnancy or breastfeeding; (3) deprivation of liberty; (4) compulsory psychiatric treatment; (5) participation in other interventional research involving another psychotherapeutic or pharmacological intervention which may have an impact on clinical outcomes and (6) guardianship or legal protection. Patients reporting the use of psychoactive substances (except tobacco) within the 12 hours preceding visits (V0 (baseline visit), V1 (short-term follow-up) or V2 (medium-term follow-up)) will be excluded from the study.

Sample size

Following the review from Benkert et al, the response rate to MPH in adults with ADHD ranges from 17% to 59.6%.12 Even if this rate is unknown in patients with ADHD and addictive disorder(s), we hypothesise that it would be in the low range because of the pejorative effect of the comorbidity with an addictive disorder. Thus, we estimate that the rate of functional response in the control group (control CRT+MPH) will be around 30%. Considering an alpha risk of 0.05, a 90% statistical power and assuming that 50% of patients in the experimental group (CRT+MPH) could experience a functional response, 124 subjects are required in each group. So, the META trial requires the inclusion of 248 patients.

Procedure

Figure 1 illustrates the procedure of the META trial. Following confirmation of ADHD diagnosis using the DIVA-5 (see Measures) during a routine specialised consultation, MPH treatment will be initiated and titrated until dose stabilisation. Within 21 days after stabilisation, eligible patients will be enrolled and randomised to either CRT+MPH (PRESCO software, HappyNeuron) or control CRT+MPH (AUDITICO software, HappyNeuron). Random allocation will be performed by a clinical research technician with a central computer. Only patients will be blinded after assignment to interventions. CRT will last for 12 weeks, with two sessions per week.

During the first 4 weeks, patients will be accompanied by a neuropsychologist once a week at the research centre, with the second session taking place independently at home. Then, there will be an alternation between a week with one accompanied session at the centre and one session at home, and a week with both sessions at home, all lasting 8 weeks. Patients will also engage voluntarily in additional exercises. The neuropsychologist will be able to track whether the patients have completed their at-home sessions using the software’s history feature and will encourage them by phone if necessary.

Three research visits are scheduled (V0, V1 and V2), as outlined in figure 1. The V0 will include a semistructured clinical interview (approximately 75 min), a neuropsychological assessment (approximately 60 min) and completion of self-rated questionnaires. V1, corresponding to the short-term follow-up (end of CRT), will include a semistructured clinical interview (approximately 45 min), a neuropsychological assessment (approximately 60 min) and completion of self-rated questionnaires. Finally, V2 will serve as the medium-term follow-up (6 months after the end of CRT), with the same content and duration as V1.

Premature discontinuation of participation may occur in the following cases: withdrawal of the patient’s consent, death of the patient or loss to follow-up (inability to contact the patient despite three attempts). In the event of premature discontinuation of the study, patients will continue to receive care appropriate to their situation as part of routine care. The reason for premature withdrawal will be documented.

Drug monitoring

The META trial will assess MPH treatment tolerance at V1 and V2 by collecting data on functional signs, weight, pulse and blood pressure. It will also involve monitoring for any side effects of MPH, adherence to MPH treatment (regular use of medication, except on rest days or holidays, as reported by the patient) and any misuse of MPH (improper use of medication in relation to the prescription, as reported by the patient).

Other treatment received

At each visit, all treatments received for ADHD and/or addictive disorders will be recorded, including medication, neuropsychological care and psychotherapeutic interventions.

Treatment

The study aims to evaluate the efficacy of active CRT compared with control CRT, both in addition to MPH. In the META trial, digital CRT will be delivered individually two times a week over 12 weeks via a software solution. At randomisation, patients will be assigned to either CRT (PRESCO software, HappyNeuron, figure 2) or to control CRT (AUDITICO software, HappyNeuron, figure 3), which offers exercises focused on listening skills. Table 4 outlines the differences and similarities between CRT and control CRT in META. The CRT provides exercise cycles aimed at cognitive functions typically impaired in individuals with dual pathology, ADHD and addictive disorder(s). These exercises were selected by the study team, including a neuropsychologist with clinical CRT experience, from the exercises available in the software solution. They are designed to train sustained and selective attention, working memory, long-term memory, inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility and planning. The control CRT consists of exercises in sound perception and recognition, without targeting the cognitive functions typically affected in ADHD or addictive disorder(s). In both groups, sessions are conducted two times weekly for 3 months and last approximately 30 min. This duration was chosen as a compromise between feasibility and effectiveness. It is short enough to be manageable for patients who may have professional activities, regular medical appointments or cognitive difficulties that make such exercises challenging. At the same time, it is long enough to train multiple cognitive functions and to support the transfer of skills to real-life situations when sessions are supervised by a neuropsychologist. The study employs a single-blind design, with participants blinded to treatment allocation, while the neuropsychologist, research staff and investigators remain unblinded.

During sessions at the research centre, patients will be accompanied by one of the neuropsychologists involved in the study, all trained in cognitive remediation and familiar with the study software. The neuropsychologist will review the treatment procedure, provide guidance and explanations as needed, and encourage the generalisation of skills to everyday life and addictive issues. At home, patients will complete the sessions independently on their own computers by following a link sent via email on the day of each session. Patients will also engage voluntarily in additional exercises beyond the 24 sessions required by the protocol; the number and completion of these exercises will be tracked by the software.

Each 30 min session will offer a series of exercises that increase in intensity based on the patient’s performance. As a result, cognitive training will be semi-personalised, adjusting in terms of the type and intensity of cognitive dysfunction. The software will track whether sessions are completed fully, partially or not at all, and results are recorded in terms of correct answers and response time.

Statistical analysis

First, descriptive analyses will be performed on baseline characteristics, including the type and severity of ADHD, symptom intensity across both dimensions (hyperactivity/impulsivity and inattention), level of functional impairment, type and severity of addictive disorder(s), sociodemographic information, personal and familial medical history, neuropsychological skills and psychopathological features.

For the primary objective, the proportion of patients who achieve functional improvement at the end of CRT will be estimated and compared between the two treatment groups using a mixed logistic regression model. The model will include the inclusion centre as a random effect (stratification variable) and the treatment group as a fixed effect. Missing data related to the primary objective (particularly for patients lost to follow-up) will be handled using a ‘worst case’ imputation approach, assuming no functional improvement.

Analyses for secondary objectives will involve linear models for continuous outcomes and logistic regression models for categorical outcomes.

All statistical analyses will follow the intent-to-treat principle. Complementary analysis could be performed per protocol using the per-protocol principle. Statistical tests will be two-sided, with a significance level set at 0.05.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval

This study will be conducted following the Good Clinical Practice Guidelines and the World Medical Association’s Declaration of Helsinki, with approval from the French National Ethics Committee (Comité de Protection des Personnes Sud-Est I, ethics approval number: 2024-A02107-40; reference: RC23_0506) on 24 December 2024. All participants will provide written informed consent, obtained by the investigator physician, before inclusion in the study. Amendment requests will be submitted to the ethics committee in accordance with French regulatory requirements.

Separate storage of identities and informed consents is planned, along with pseudonymisation of all study data stored in the case report form. Archiving is planned for 15 years after the study in secure facilities and servers at Nantes University Hospital.

The study is conducted in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials 2025 statement63 (figure 4).

Data management and open science

A secure server, Ennov Clinical, which has a health data storage authorisation and is hosted by On Vous Héberge, will be used in coded format; after extraction, the data will be anonymised and stored on the secure server of the University Hospital of Nantes. The datasets generated in this study will not be available in open access because they include sensitive data according to the French Data Protection Authority (Commission Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés or CNIL in French) that could not be transferred to other researchers to guarantee participants’ anonymity. Data entry, coding and monitoring follow internal procedures available in the Ennov Gestion Electronique des Documents system of Nantes University Hospital. Monitoring will be carried out by the Promotion Department of the Research and Innovation Directorate through a visit to each site once a year to perform quality control of the data recorded in the case report forms. The protocol has been classified according to the estimated risk level for the participant as Risk A: foreseeable low or negligible risk.

A simplified summary of the results intended for patients will be prepared. Communication of the results to professionals will be ensured through scientific publications. The preprint will be deposited on Open Science Framework (OSF), as well as the publication on Hyper Articles en Ligne (HAL), a French open-access repository.

Discussion

Clinical and research implications

In the case of positive results from the META trial, the demonstration of the efficacy of combined MPH and CRT could open several important perspectives. On one hand, these results could encourage the widespread use of CRT in the multimodal management of ADHD. Currently, particularly in France, patients with ADHD often face barriers to accessing quality healthcare.24 Psychostimulants are still under-prescribed,15 despite efforts to better identify ADHD, and the recommendation for psychotherapy, such as cognitive behavioural therapy, is not always followed due to the limited availability of qualified professionals and the cost of such treatments. CRT, however, can be delivered via a computer, offering the advantage of being self-administered with fewer constraints than traditional psychotherapy, even though its combination might be the most effective approach. Our study could highlight the particular benefits of CRT in the presence of addictive comorbidities.

Many patients with addictive disorders struggle to access quality healthcare due to factors such as socio-economic status, cognitive impairment, the addictive disorder itself (including intoxication and withdrawal periods), time spent using substances or engaging in problematic behaviours and even the stigma they face. On the other hand, this study could pave the way for further scientific research. First, comparative studies could help define the most efficient modalities of CRT, including optimal session duration, frequency, targeted cognitive processes and software solutions (eg, individual vs accompanied sessions). Additionally, prospective research could identify predictive factors of functional improvement through CRT, particularly clinical, psychopathological and neuropsychological variables, as well as associated healthcare. This could provide a foundation for advancing precision medicine.64

Limits

Several limitations of the META trial should be anticipated. A high level of patient heterogeneity is expected due to the multicentre recruitment, which involves different types of centres (university hospitals, ie, referral centres, and local centres) and the inclusion of patients with SUD and BA. The severity of ADHD, addictive disorders and other comorbidities, as well as the treatments received, could vary. While this heterogeneity is an asset in better representing the target population and extrapolating study data, the treatment options are still heavily influenced by local practices and the availability of healthcare. This variation, both in patients and healthcare practices, reflects real-life conditions.

Additionally, recruitment challenges may arise due to the randomised controlled design and the intensive CRT programme, which requires a strong commitment from participants. This could result in the inclusion of patients who are more motivated and compliant with their healthcare, potentially overestimating the success rates in both groups. To address this, strategies such as simplifying logistics, offering flexible scheduling, providing continuous support and compensating patients for their time and participation will be implemented to encourage broader participation and reduce selection bias.

Finally, some limitations relate to the assessment procedures. As research visits will be conducted in an unblinded manner, measurement bias cannot be excluded. In addition, the neuropsychological battery is intentionally limited to facilitate future implementation in routine clinical practice while ensuring feasibility and patient acceptability.

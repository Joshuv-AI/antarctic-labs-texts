Impact of anastomotic leakage on survival after surgery for gastric carcinoma: A PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Lei Zhang, Beibei Wang, Yi Huang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:The long-term survival of postoperative anastomotic leakage (AL) following gastric cancer (GC) surgery is still debating. Our aim was to investigate the association between AL and survival of the patients following GC.Methods:We searched articles in databases from inception to July 2023 to retrieve literature related to postoperative AL and survival of GC patients. We evaluated the association of postoperative AL and survival of GC patients using the software STAT10.0.Results:Seven articles with 6209 GC patients were included. Our results showed that the postoperative AL were associated with pooled 1-year patient overall survival (OS) result in an odds ratio (OR) of 0.504 (95% confidence interval (CI): 0.372–0.682; I2 = 84.1%; P = .000), pooled 3-year patient OS (OR = 0.467, 95% CI: 0.356–0.614; I2 = 85.3%; P = .000) and pooled 5-year patient OS (OR = 0.370, 95% CI: 0.171–0.805; I2 = 82.5%; P = .012). The P value of Egger test were 0.759, 0.187, 0.175. The postoperative AL were associated with pooled 1-year patient disease-free survival (DFS) result in an OR of 0.538 (95% CI: 0.171–1.691; I2 = 89.0%; P = .289), the pooled 3-year patient DFS (OR = 0.143, 95% CI: 0.119–1.431; I2 = 91.7%; P = .163), and the pooled 5-year patient DFS (OR = 0.344, 95% CI: 0.088–1.338; I2 = 91.0%; P = .124), and the P value of Egger test of pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS were 0.759, 0.247, 0.07.Conclusion:Postoperative AL was correlated with a worse OS in GC patients, but not correlated with DFS in GC patients, but more studies are required to confirm this conclusion.

1. Introduction

As one of the most common cancers of gastrointestinal tract, gastric cancer (GC) is the 5th most common cancer and the second most common cause of cancer-related death in the world.[1,2] The incidence of GC is higher and more than 1 million diagnosis of GC are made annually.[3] With the development of diagnosis and treatment in recent years, surgery remains the primary treatment option for GC patients nowadays.[4] Although the surgical technology is developing rapidly, postoperative complications are still common in GC patients with radical gastrectomy, such as bleeding, infection and anastomotic leakage (AL).[5,6] AL, is one of the most common complication, about 3.6% to 6.7% of GC patients with radical gastrectomy in previous studies.[6,7] It has been reported that AL after an upper gastrointestinal operation was related to increased postoperative mortality rate, prolonged hospital stay, and increased treatment costs.[8] However, the long-term survival of AL following GC surgery is still debating.[9–11] Considering the inconsistent results of current findings, we have undertaken a meta-analysis with all eligible studies to the impact of AL on disease-free-survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients who underwent gastrectomy for cancer.

2. Patients and methods

The meta-analysis was performed according to the guidelines of PRISMA 2020, and our study has been registered in the PROSPERO database (registration ID: 448929). This study does not include human and animal experiments, and the ethical approval and consent to participate was not necessary.

2.1. Search strategies

We conducted a systematic search in PubMed, EMBASE, Elsevier, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, Chinese electronic databases (CNKI database, WanFang database, and VIP database) and the Cochrane Library databases with the following search terms: “cancer” (or “carcinoma”) AND “gastric” (or “stomach”) AND “anastomotic leakage” (or fistula or dehiscence) AND “overall survival” (or “disease-free survival”) AND “long-term-results”. We also scanned all the reference lists to identify other relevant studies.

2.3. Data extraction and quality assessment

Two authors (Lei Zhang and Beibei Wang) independently screened the articles by title, abstract and keywords, and then selected and analyzed the relevant articles. Any disagreement was resolved by discussion with another author (Yi Huang). The following data was extracted: first author, year of publication, number of cases, country, Journal, DFS, OS, and number of AL. PRISMA statement guidelines for conducting and reporting systematic reviews were followed. The Newcastle-Ottawa scale was used for quality assessment. Cochrane Collaboration guidelines were followed.

2.4. Statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted with STATA (version 10.0; Stata Corporation). Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were combined to evaluate the association of the AL with prognosis in patients with GC. They were either directly extracted from each study whenever available estimated from Kaplan–Meier curves. The Higgins I2 statistic and Cochran Q test were used to evaluate heterogeneity among studies. Significant heterogeneity was defined as P < .10 and/or I2 > 50%, and when significant heterogeneity was observed, the random-effects model was used; otherwise, the fixed-effects model was used. Begg funnel plot and Egger linear regression test were performed to evaluate publication bias, and significant publication bias was defined as P < .05.

3.4. Publication bias

Begg funnel plot and Egger linear regression test were performed to evaluate publication bias between AL and OS and DFS. For the AL and OS, Begg funnel plots were symmetric (Fig. 4), and the P value of Egger test were .759, .187, .175, which both indicated no significant publication bias.

For the AL and DFS, Begg funnel plots were symmetric (Fig. 5), and the P value of Egger test of pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS were .759, .247, .07, which both indicated no significant publication bias.

4. Discussion

Although several previous studies have been reported the relationship between AL and survival of GC patients, the conclusion are generally contradictory. According to the results, they demonstrated that postoperative AL may have either a positive or no influence or negative impact on long-term survival.[9–12] In our meta-analysis, we confirmed the relationship between postoperative AL and survival of GC patients. We found that; Postoperative AL was correlated with a worse OS in GC patients; Postoperative AL is associated with DFS in GC patients, but statistical different was not significant.

It has been reported that the incidence of clinical leakage was about 1.2% to 6.7% for the patients after gastrectomy.[5,11,16–19] The one of most common risk factors of AL is ischemia of the anastomotic site, which can be caused by many factors, such as, preoperative major illness, poor anastomosis blood supply and anastomosis tension.[20,21] Once AL occurred, various inflammatory mediators are activated and released in the local microenvironment. Inflammatory mediators, which are associated with a leakage-related inflammatory response, are involved in the proliferation and metastasis of cancer cells.[13,22] Postoperative AL is associated with the poorer long-term outcome in oesophagogastric and rectal cancer.[23,24] However, the detailed mechanism and effect underlying this is debate at present. Some other studies have failed to confirm detrimental effects of postoperative AL on survival and recurrence rates in cancers.[25] Saunders et al[9] analyzed the effect of postoperative AL on OS and DFS. The results showed that OS and DFS rates were significantly lower in patients with severe grade III to IV non-leak-related complications. Kim et al[14] identified consecutive 3827 patients who underwent gastrectomy. The rate of anastomotic leakage was 1.88% (72/3827). Patients with and without leakage did not differ in terms of their long-term survival significantly.

In our study, 7 articles involving 6209 GC patients were included. The relationship between 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival (OS and DFS) and AL was detected. Our results showed that the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS result in an OR of 0.504, 0.467, 0.370, and she statistical different were significant. The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year patient DFS result in an OR of 0.538, 0.14, 0.344, but statistical different were not significant. Also, Begg funnel plots were symmetric, which both indicated no significant publication bias. So we concluded that postoperative AL was correlated with a worse OS in GC patients. Only 4 studies have reported an association of postoperative AL with DFS, our results showed that postoperative AL is also associated with DFS in GC patients, but statistical different was not significant. Further work is required to understand these differences.

Our study also has several limitations. First, only 7 retrospective articles involving 6209 patients were included, which may cause bias due to the restricted sample size. The small sample size and significant heterogeneity could not be ignored. Second, all included studies were from Europe, America and Asia, and the result from other countries or regions remains unclear which may also cause bias. Third, due to the lack of original data, we were unable to conduct more subgroup analyses based on other factors, such as sex and age.

5. Conclusion

We reviewed all eligible studies about the impact of AL on OS, and DFS of GC patients. Our results showed that postoperative AL was correlated with a worse OS and DFS in GC patients, but statistical different was not significant between postoperative AL and DFS. Postoperative AL may be a poor prognostic index for the prognosis of GC patients, but more studies are required to confirm this conclusion.

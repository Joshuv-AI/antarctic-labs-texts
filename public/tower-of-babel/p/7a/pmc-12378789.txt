Visualizing and analyzing global knowledge maps and emerging research trends in tumor-derived exosomes using CiteSpace
Authors: Ruijun Guo, Jiajun Xu, Chunxia Li, Chune Mo, Minglin Ou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTumor-derived exosomes testing can be effective in diagnosing disease and assisting in the treatment of disease. Our study utilizes bibliometric analysis to identify research hotspots related to tumor-derived exosomes, predict emerging research frontiers and development trends, and offer diverse perspectives for advancing research in this field.MethodsSearch the Web of Science Core Collection for English-language literature published on the field of tumor-derived exosomes from 2015 to 2024. CiteSpace(6.2.R3) software was utilized to visualize the distribution of countries/regions, institutions, authors, co-cited authors, and co-cited journals within the relevant literature. Additionally, co-occurrence, clustering, and emergence analyses were conducted on the co-cited references.ResultsAn analysis of 2523 articles meeting the inclusion criteria revealed a steady increase in the number of publications in this field over the past decade. In terms of countries/regions, institutions, authors, and journals that published articles, the most productive were China, Ministry of Education-China, Theresa L. Whiteside, and CANCER RES, respectively. The most influential were The United States, Harvard University, Theresa L. Whiteside, and CANCER RES, respectively. The mechanisms underlying exosomal PD-L1 and engineered exosomes are currently prominent research foci, warranting meticulous examination by the academic community.ConclusionWithin the field of research on tumor-derived exosomes, current investigations appear largely concentrated on the exosome PD-L1 mechanism and engineered exosomes. Possible future research hotspots will focus on the use of engineered exosomes to target tumor cells and as a drug delivery platform for more precise therapeutic targeting.Keywords: Tumor-derived exosomes, Exosomal PD-L1, CiteSpace, Visual analysis, Engineered exosomes

Introduction

Cancer is the primary cause of mortality worldwide across all countries and regions [1]. According to estimates for 2022, there will be approximately 20 million new cancer cases globally, resulting in 9.7 million deaths [2]. In recent years, exosomes–a type of extracellular vesicles(EVs) [3] with a diameter of 30–150 nanometers–have garnered significant attention from researchers in related fields. These EVs, encapsulated by lipid bilayers, can be secreted by nearly all cell types, including immune cells, blood cells, neuronal cells, epithelial cells, and cancer cells. They are naturally present in various body fluids, such as blood, saliva, urine, and breast milk. Initially discovered in the 1980s, exosomes were believed to serve merely as “garbage bags” in cellular metabolism, removing excess proteins, lipids [4], and other cellular wastes. However, as research has progressed, scientists have discovered that exosomes play a crucial role not only in waste elimination but also in intercellular communication, immune regulation [5], disease transmission, and as biomarkers. As an important subset of exosomes [6], Tumor-derived exosomes regulate cell signaling and reprogramming within the complex tumor microenvironment (TME), thereby promoting cancer development, includinguncontrolled cell growth, angiogenesis, metastasis, organ-specific metastasis, immune escape, and drug resistance [7–10].

Tumor-derived exosomes have emerged as an attractive target for cancer research due to their distinctive biological characteristics. Their presence in body fluids offers potential for non-invasive diagnosis and treatment. The double-layered membrane structure of tumor exosomes effectively safeguards their contents from degradation, thereby ensuring the accurate transmission of biological information. Furthermore, tumor-derived exosomes carry tumor-specific molecular markers, such as EGFRvIII [11], Glypican-1 [12, 13], and exosomal miRNAs, which offer novel insights into early diagnosis and precise classification of cancer. EGFRvIII is a pivotal oncogene in glioblastoma (GBM) [14]. Studies have shown that exosomes in the serum of GBM patients carry EGFRvIII mRNA. Following surgical resection, the level of EGFRvIII mRNA in exosomes decreases significantly, indicating its potential use as a marker for monitoring tumor burden dynamically [15]. Glypican-1 is a cell surface proteoglycan abundant in pancreatic cancer exosomes. Flow cytometry analysis of sera from pancreatic cancer patients has demonstrated its presence in both early and late exosomes, but it is absent in benign pancreatic disease [12]. Numerous studies have indicated that imbalances in miRNA levels are prevalent in cancer. By regulating their mRNA targets, miRNAs are implicated in all key tumor-associated processes, including DNA damage repair, cell death, cell proliferation, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, tumor invasion, and metastasis formation [16]. Nobuyoshi et al. found that miR-1246 levels were significantly elevated in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), validating its role as a novel diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for this disease [17]. Tumor-derived exosomes play a pivotal role in cancer signaling and immunity, participating in intercellular communication, immune escape [18], tumor microenvironment remodeling, invasion, and metastasis. The TME is characterized by low oxygen levels, high lactate levels, extracellular acidosis, and low nutrient content [19, 20]. Hypoxic conditions impact both tumor cells and surrounding cells (such as macrophages and fibroblasts), leading to the polarization of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) towards the M2 type, which promotes immunosuppression and facilitates immune escape. Additionally, in the hypoxic TME, HIF-1 signaling suppresses the innate and adaptive immune systems by inducing the expression of immunosuppressive factors and immune checkpoint molecules, such as vascular endothelial growth factor, prostaglandin E-2, and programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1)/programmed death-1 (PD-1), to evade immune attack [21]. We have clarified the unique biological and translational properties of tumor-derived exosomes that justify our focus. Tumor-derived exosomes are distinct from other types of exosomes due to their specific cargo (e.g., tumor-specific proteins and nucleic acids) and their role in promoting tumor progression, immune evasion, and metastasis. These properties make them particularly relevant for cancer research and potential therapeutic targets. Beyond cancer applications, tumor-derived exosomes exhibit multifaceted biological significance. Their role in immunomodulation extends to autoimmune disorders, while their neurotropic properties show implications for neurodegenerative disease diagnostics. As natural nanocarriers crossing biological barriers, they offer revolutionary potential for CNS-targeted drug delivery - a critical advancement given that 98% of small-molecule drugs fail to penetrate the blood-brain barrier. While current research focuses on the mechanisms of tumor-derived exosomes, their role in cancer progression, drug resistance, and therapeutic applications, there is a lack of systematic analysis regarding the current status of exosomes in tumors and future trends.

Bibliometric analysis is a statistical methodology that relies on public literature databases, such as Web of Science, and serves as a valuable tool for evaluating research trends from both statistical and qualitative perspectives [22]. By analyzing and aggregating a substantial amount of relevant literature, bibliometric analysis facilitates the creation of knowledge graphs, which offer supportive evidence for future trends. In this paper, we utilized the CiteSpace software to examine tumor-derived exosome-related literature in the WoSCC database from 2015 to 2024. We generated a knowledge map to visualize research dynamics, change patterns, and development processes, with the aim of identifying research hotspots in the field. This analysis aims to provide new insights and references for future research endeavors.

Materials and methods

Literature search

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database comprises over 12,000 authoritative, high-impact academic journals worldwide, facilitating econometric analysis and the visualization of scientific literature. The data for this study were extracted from the WoSCC and retrieved and downloaded on April 1, 2025. The search formula is set to TS=(“extracellular vesicles*”OR “EVs*”)AND(“cancerous exosomes*”OR“Cancer-Associated exosomes*”OR “tumor-derived exosomes*”). Refinement strategy: (1) Document type: articles and review articles; (2) Publication language: English; (3) Publication date: January 1, 2015 to December 31, 2024; the rest are system default parameters. Conference summaries, revisions, editorial material, and correspondence were excluded to ensure the accuracy and objectivity of the analysis. Ultimately, 2634 publications pertinent to the field were exported to CiteSpace (6.2.R3) in plain text format (“download_XX.txt”), and the scientific literature was subsequently visualized and analyzed. After importing the data into the software, it was thoroughly checked and analyzed, resulting in the selection of 2523 publications. The literature screening process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Research methods

CiteSpace software, developed by Professor Chaomei Chen in 2003, is a multivariate, time-sharing, dynamic visualization and analysis tool designed to analyze the underlying knowledge within scientific literature. It has evolved within the field of scientometrics, data, and information visualization. The visualization resulting from the analysis is referred to as a “scientific knowledge map” or “science map.”

The parameter analysis settings for general analysis in CiteSpace are as follows: Time span: January 2015-December 2024, Time slice: 1 year, Selection criteria (g-index, g2 ≤ kΣi ≤ gci, k∈Z+,k = 25). The CiteSpace settings for the analysis of co-cited journals and co-cited references were as follows: time span: January 2015-December 2024, time slice: 1 year, selection criteria (g-index, g2 ≤ kΣi ≤ gci, k∈Z+,k = 10), and network cuts were made using a pathfinding network approach. The included literature was visualized and analyzed separately based on the number of publications per year, country/region, institution, author, co-cited authors, co-cited journals, and references.

In the visualization graph, “N” represents nodes and “E” represents the number of connections. Cluster analysis divides the entire network into several distinct clusters, with the modular value (Q-value) and the average silhouette value (S-value) serving as the two key metrics for assessing cluster relevance. Generally, the Q-value ranges from 0 to 1, with Q > 0.3 indicating that the identified clusters possess a significant structure. Additionally, clustering is considered more efficient when the S-value is 0.7 and is generally deemed reasonable if it is 0.5 or higher.

The visualization analysis of the literature imported for this study, pertaining to the field of tumor-derived exosome research, was conducted using CiteSpace. The analysis resulted in a total of 78 nodes and 447 connections. We analyzed the number of articles from 78 countries to assess their contributions to the field. The radius of the nodes was proportional to the number of articles published in each country/region, while the thickness of the connection lines represented the strength of collaboration between countries, indicative of joint publications. Nodes with a purple outer circle indicated a centrality value greater than 0.10, signifying a high impact of the country/region in terms of article output. Figure 2C identifies countries/regions with more than 60 articles and reveals an imbalance in publication distribution, demonstrating a clear top-heavy effect, with a few countries, such as China and The United States, contributing the majority of articles.

To provide a deeper analysis of the top-producing countries, Table 1 lists the five countries with the highest number and centrality of publications in the field. The data in Table 1 reveals that Chinese scholars have published the most research papers in the field, totaling 1183 publications, which account for 46.89% of the total. These articles were cited 60,012 times, with 55,483 citations remaining after de-self-citation, averaging 49.97 citations per article (h-index = 117). The United States follows closely with 539 articles and 56,075 citations, averaging 106.33 citations per article (h-index = 118). As illustrated in Table 1, the United States holds the premier position with a centrality score of 0.39. This nation exhibits formidable research and development capabilities in the biotechnology sector, offering substantial backing for high-throughput exosome analysis. As a result, it becomes feasible to acquire more thorough and accurate biological data from individual exosomes. According to the ClinicalTrials.gov website in 2023, 169 clinical studies on exosomes were registered worldwide, with 39 studies registered in The United States, demonstrating its active engagement in exosome research. Secondly, in 2022, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) introduced the “Exosome Therapy Accelerated Approval Pathway” with the aim of reducing the clinical translation cycle. The US National Institutes of Health (NIH) and other organizations have allocated significant research funding for tumor exosome research, thereby supporting research institutions and companies in carrying out basic research and clinical trials. In addition, the United States is home to numerous top-tier universities, such as the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and Stanford University, which are at the forefront of research on the mechanisms of exosome drug delivery. This may explain The United States’ leading position in the field. China ranks second in centrality with a score of 0.22, despite accounting for 46.89% of the field’s publications. In 2023, China’s Ministry of Science and Technology designated exosome technology as a key focus within the 14th Five-Year Plan for research and development, providing support for basic research and application development in the exosome field. Over recent years, several Chinese research teams have made notable advancements in exosome-related research. At the end of 2015, according to a worldwide International Society for Extracellular Vesicles(ISEV) survey [23], differential ultracentrifugation was the most commonly used primary EV separation and concentration technique [3]. Currently, this technology remains unavailable in certain countries/regions. Second, certain nations, like Iran, may possess limited influence in this domain owing to inadequate public funding and a scarcity of capital. Reference co-occurrence analysis and burst detection

When two or more documents are cited simultaneously by one or more journals, a cited relationship is formed between them. This connection in the graph can be utilized to identify common themes in the literature and as an indicator of the proximity of the relationship between the two articles [29]. CiteSpace was utilized to visualize and analyze co-cited references within the literature imported for this study, yielding a total of 598 nodes and 1180 connections (Fig. 5C). The color of the nodes and the thickness of the circles represent the frequency of citations across different time periods. The connecting lines match the corresponding time slices, with the most recent co-citations depicted by yellow or light green lines. The citation ranking of the literature was determined by tallying the number of citations received (Table 5). The article titled " The Biology, Function, and Biomedical Applications of Exosomes,” published in 2020, is the most frequently cited with the highest citation explosion. It elucidates the biological roles and functions of exosomes, demonstrating how the efficient exchange of cellular components through exosomes can contribute to the development of exosome-based therapies. The study of extracellular vesicles holds promise for uncovering unknown cellular and molecular mechanisms in intercellular communication, organ homeostasis, and disease. A subset of EVs, specifically exosomes, have an average diameter of approximately 100 nm. Exosomes serve as a window into altered cellular or tissue states in various diseases, and their detection in biological fluids can provide multicomponent diagnostic information [30]. The second most cited article is “Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles 2018 (MISEV2018): A Position Statement of the International Society for Extracellular Vesicles and Update of the MISEV2014 Guidelines” (Théry et al., 2018). Over the past decade, there has been a dramatic increase in scientific publications elucidating the physiological and pathological roles of EVs. EVs are a general term for various subtypes of membrane structures released by cells, known as exosomes, microvesicles, microparticles, ectosomes, oncosomes, apoptotic bodies, and many other names [3]. The ISEV has established standardized guidelines for the characterization of EVs, encompassing their pre-processing, enrichment, biophysical properties, and functional roles. This standardization facilitates a more nuanced understanding of EV biology among researchers. The article with the highest centrality, published by Safinur Atay in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America in 2014, reported that gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST) cells invade the interstitial stroma through the release of the oncogenic protein tyrosine kinase (KIT)-containing exosomes, which triggers the phenotypic conversion of progenitor smooth muscle cells to tumor-promoting cells. This change leads to the release of various matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), particularly MMP1. This study indicates that exosome release and subsequent MMP1 induction creates a positive feedback mechanism established between tumor and stromal cells that drives GIST development and offers unique insights for potential therapeutic strategies to block GIST progression and metastatic spread [31].

The magnitude of burst intensity serves as a metric to measure the innovativeness of the findings. A higher burst intensity indicates more innovative results, signifying the forefront of the field [32]. Based on our outbreak intensity screening of the collected literature, we identified the top 30 outbreaks, as depicted in Fig. 5D. Among these, ten papers demonstrated the longest outbreak durations. Notably, two of these papers, namely “Exosomal PD-L1 contributes to immunosuppression and is associated with anti-PD-1 response” and “Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles 2018 (MISEV2018): A Position Statement of the International Society for Extracellular Vesicles and an Update of the MISEV2014 Guidelines,” reported outbreak periods that spanned from 2020 to 2024. Reference clustering analysis

We utilized the Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) algorithm within CiteSpace to conduct a cluster analysis of the references. This analysis resulted in the identification of 26 clusters (Fig. 6A), with the sequential ordering of ID numbers from smallest to largest reflecting the chronological progression of the field of tumor-derived exosomes. The clustering’s validity is supported by the Q value of 0.8863 (> 0.3) and the S value of 0.9635 (> 0.7). Based on the co-cited literature cluster analysis, the clusters were annotated to produce a timeline plot of the co-cited literature (Fig. 6B). The horizontal arrangement of node centers in the figure, from left to right, represents the year of the initial publication of the cited literature. This layout serves to illustrate the temporal characteristics and evolutionary trends observed within the cited literature [34]. Among the clusters, “#0 biosensing technologies " is the largest, encompassing a total of 29 projects. Upon examining the timeline, it becomes evident that Rongrong Huang introduced a label-free electrochemical aptamer sensor in 2019, specifically designed for the detection of gastric cancer exosomes [35]. Furthermore, our analysis indicates that the research areas denoted as “#5 autophagy-associated exosome release,” “#6 novel biomarker,” “#14 serum-derived extracellular vesicle,” “#20 disease activity,” and “#21 cancer therapy” remain actively pursued. Through an analysis of the three clusters, namely “#14 Serum - Derived Extracellular Vesicle,” “#20 Disease Activity,” and “#21 Cancer Therapy,” a visual examination of the timeline reveals that the three most active articles are “The Roles of Extracellular Vesicles in the Immune System,” “The Role of Extracellular Vesicles in Cancer,” and “Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles (MISEV2023): From Basic to Advanced Approaches. In the section titled “#6 novel biomarker,” it is evident that the most active articles systematically summarize the application of engineered exosomes derived from various sources for targeted cancer therapy [36]. These articles delineate the patterns of engineered exosomes and present the latest advancements in engineered exosome technology. Research hotspots and frontiers

Exosomal PD-L1

Programmed death-1 is an immunosuppressive molecule primarily expressed on the surface of activated T cells. Binding of PD-1 to its ligand, PD-L1, significantly induces T-cell non-reactivity, thereby maintaining a balance between autoimmunity and immune tolerance [37]. Tumor cells have been reported to express high levels of PD-L1 to limit cellular immune responses, which constitutes a crucial mechanism of tumor-mediated immune escape. In response to interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) secreted by activated T cells, PD-L1 on the surface of tumor cells can be upregulated. Through its extracellular structural domains, PD-L1 binds to PD-1, leading to the inactivation of T cells [38, 39] and, consequently, facilitating tumor immune escape. Research has demonstrated that PD-L1 is not only expressed on the surface of tumor cells but is also enriched in tumor-derived exosomes. These exosomes disseminate immunosuppressive signals throughout the body via their long-distance delivery function [40]. Tumor-derived exosome PD-L1 can bind to PD-1 on the surface of T cells, interfering with the activation of CD8 + effector T cells and subsequently affecting the tumor immune response [41]. Furthermore, exosomal PD-L1 can enhance immune escape by modulating the function of immune cells within the TME. Specifically, tumor-associated macrophages, a type of immune cell present in the TME, can be categorized into anti-tumor M1 and pro-tumor M2 phenotypes [20]. Notably, TAMs predominantly exhibit the M2 phenotype [42]. The expression of PD-L1 in M2-TAMs through Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)-dependent signaling effectively induces an immunosuppressive phenotype in these macrophages, inhibiting T-cell proliferation and facilitating tumor immune escape [43]. The NF-κB pathway, well-established as a crucial mediator of inflammation-induced tumorigenesis [44], is activated by exosomal PD-L1, which promotes tumor cell survival and proliferation.

Exosomal PD-L1 not only modulates cellular immune responses and facilitates immune evasion but also impacts disease progression and prognosis. Research has indicated that exosomal PD-L1 levels are elevated in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) compared to healthy individuals, and these levels positively correlate with clinicopathological indicators of disease [45]. Researchers analyzed PD-L1 levels [46] in circulating extracellular vesicles of melanoma patients undergoing anti-PD-1 therapy and discovered a positive correlation between circulating exosomal PD-L1 levels and tumor load, suggesting a poor prognosis. The PD-L1/PD-1 signaling pathway has emerged as the gold standard in cancer immunotherapy, with researchers focusing on these pathways for the development of immune checkpoint blockers (ICBs). The classical PD-L1/PD-1 signaling pathway, a key mechanism of tumor immune escape, has undergone extensive analysis and has become a primary target for ICB therapy. Nuclear PD-L1 (nPD-L1) in the non-classical PD-L1/PD-1 signaling pathway serves as a crucial transcriptional regulator in multiple tumor types, including triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and uveal melanoma. This regulatory function can inhibit T-cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity [47]. Under hypoxic conditions, nPD-L1 forms a complex with p-Y705-STAT3 in the cytoplasm, which subsequently enhances tumor immunogenicity [48]. The identification of both classical and non-classical signaling pathways has opened up novel avenues for immunotherapy in various cancers.

Engineered exosomes-a novel drug delivery platform

As nanotechnology integrates with clinical medicine, the development of a single multifunctional nanosized delivery system has significantly impacted cancer treatment. Although nanocarriers currently in development, such as liposomes, polymeric micelles, and mesoporous silica, have exhibited exceptional therapeutic efficacy in mouse models, their synthetic materials may still induce systemic toxicity, immunogenicity, and recognition or clearance by the immune system [49]. The discovery of these issues has directed researchers’ attention to exosomes, a type of nanoscale substance produced by various cells. Their unique properties, including innate stability, low immunogenicity, biocompatibility, and effective biofilm penetration ability, render them superior natural nanocarriers for efficient drug delivery [50], thereby enhancing tumor diagnosis and treatment. However, despite their potential for drug delivery, natural exosomes cannot effectively target tumor sites as most drugs accumulate in the liver or spleen. Additionally, their inherent heterogeneity can significantly diminish the therapeutic effect. To enhance drug efficacy, exosomes can be modified through biological (targeted peptides), immunological (antibodies), physical (nanomaterials), or chemical (sodium bicarbonate) means [51]. In general, there are no notable morphological distinctions between modified and natural exosomes [52]. In disease treatment, engineered exosomes can be loaded with drugs, RNA, or proteins to facilitate targeted therapy. Compared to natural exosomes, engineered exosomes exhibit enhanced precision and efficiency in delivering drugs to tumor sites, thereby minimizing post-treatment adverse effects. Studies have demonstrated that exosomes derived from normal fibroblast-like mesenchymal stromal cells, engineered to carry oncogenicKRASG12D-specific siRNA or shRNA [53], can target oncogenic Kras, resulting in improved survival rates in a mouse model of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

Advantages and limitations

Visual analysis of literature offers scholars a rapid means of understanding research hotspots and trends in the field of tumor-derived exosomes. However, this study is subject to several inevitable limitations. Firstly, this study exclusively conducted literature searches within the WoSCC database. While WoSCC stands as one of the most authoritative tools for scientific and technological literature retrieval, encompassing the majority of high-quality research papers, it may nonetheless overlook pertinent studies exclusive to other databases, including PubMed. Secondly, the exclusion criteria were confined to articles and review articles, thereby excluding other formats such as letters, conference papers, and book chapters, a limitation that could potentially result in overlooked findings. The selection of search terms was guided by Table 2, titled “Quick-reference Card on EV Nomenclature and Related Terms,” in the ISEV MISEV2023 guidelines [54]. These guidelines were employed to determine which terms to include or exclude in the search. Additionally, despite identifying relevant search terms, we could not guarantee that each literature item was closely aligned with the topic. Lastly, interpretations of graphical data exported from CiteSpace can vary among individuals. The current iteration of CiteSpace is limited to supporting literature in English and Chinese only, which may result in the omission of certain literature-related information. In this study, we have only interpreted and explained a select few dimensions that readers may find most pertinent.

Conclusion

By utilizing the CiteSpace visual analysis tool, we have gained valuable insights into the research progress, emerging themes, and future directions in the field of tumor-derived exosome research over the past decade. Globally, China and The United States are the leading nations in this field, with a consistent upward trend in article output. Among research institutions, Harvard University occupies a prominent position. It is recommended that other countries and institutions strengthen their research collaborations. Theresa L. Whiteside’s influence in the field is extensive, and closer collaboration between scholars from different countries should be encouraged. Currently, the research hotspots in the field of tumor-derived exosome research primarily focus on the exosomal PD-L1 mechanism and engineered exosomes. In the future, research hotspots may shift towards the use of engineered exosomes for targeting tumor cells and as a platform for drug delivery, enabling more precise therapeutic targets. The application of microfluidics and exosome nanoporation [55] technology has markedly elevated drug-delivery efficacy and alleviated the cellular impairment resulting from conventional electroporation techniques.

The evaluation of an HIV-PrEP drug on the vascular health outcomes in a diet-induced prediabetes condition rat model
Authors: Khanyisa Maswanganyi, Amogelang Sedibe, Andile Khathi, Mlindeli Gamede
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The prevalence of both prediabetes and cardiovascular diseases (CVD) is a public health concern in high-risk individuals using HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (HIV-PrEP) treatment. The combined effect of prediabetes and HIV-PrEP use on endothelial function remains underexplored. Hence, the aim of this study was to investigate the effects of emtricitabine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (FTC/TDF) on endothelial function, oxidative stress, inflammation and lipid markers. Prediabetes was induced using a high-fat high-carbohydrate diet over a 20-week period. Prediabetes diagnosis was confirmed using fasting blood glucose and an oral glucose tolerance test. FTC/TDF was orally administered to the rats for a further 12 weeks. Biochemical analysis was performed using colorimetric assays. The prediabetic animals had higher fasting blood glucose (FBG), vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A), endothelin-1(ET-1), nitric oxide (NO), NO/ET-1 ratio, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor α (TNF-α), triglycerides(TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS), total antioxidant capacity (TAOC) and lower high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) when compared to the non-prediabetic control. However, the FTC/TDF treatment increased VEGF-A, HDL-C, LDL-C and it reduced TGs, NO, NO/ET-1 ratio, H2O2, eNOS, TAOC, interleukin-6, TNF-α and had minimal effect on FBG and ET-1 levels in prediabetic animals .In the non-prediabetic state, FTC/TDF treatment increased VEGF-A, ET-1, NO, NO/ET-1 ratio, H2O2 and interleukin-6 while decreasing eNOS, TAOC, TNF-α and HDL-C and it had a minimal effect on FBG, LDL-C and TGs. FTC/TDF treatment could increase the risk of cardiovascular complications via oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction. Further studies are needed to fully understand the molecular mechanisms underlying these observed effects.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the global leading cause of death annually, they claimed approximately 19,8 million lives in 2022 which constituted about 32% of all global mortality1, 2. According to the previous studies, CVDs are the leading cause of mortality in individuals living with type 2 diabetes mellitus(T2DM)3. The link between T2DM and CVDs can be attributed to the vascular endothelial glucotoxicity, leading to endothelial dysfunction. In T2DM, vascular endothelial glucotoxicity has been traditionally attributed to dysregulation of glucose homeostasis, leading to sustained hyperglycaemia in the blood4. Moreover, recent studies have also alluded that chronic fluctuating moderate hyperglycaemia, which is associated with the prediabetes condition, is also linked with disruption in vascular endothelial function5. Furthermore, impaired lipid metabolism found in T2DM and prediabetes negatively affects vascular endothelium function, leading to vascular diseases such as hypertension and atherosclerosis6.

Diabetes-induced vascular endothelial dysfunction can be attributed to mechanisms such as reduction of the endothelium nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability and synthesis7. Moreover, diabetes has also been traditionally associated with elevated circulating endothelial-derived vasoconstrictors such as endothelin-1 (ET-1) leading to the pathogenesis of CVD, like hypertension and atherosclerosis8. The presence of complications, including dyslipidaemia, insulin resistance, hyperglycaemia, oxidative stress and inflammation, which are hallmarks of endothelial dysfunction, further predisposes individuals with prediabetes to the development of CVDs9. Dyslipidaemia has been reported to aggravate plaque accumulation in arteries and also induce low-grade inflammation leading to the onset of atherosclerosis10. The secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines like tumour necrosis factor α (TNF-α) and interleukin 6 (IL-6) results in the upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) which elevates vascular permeability which contributes to endothelial dysfunction which is implicated in the pathogenesis of CVD11, 12.

The consumption of chronic medications, including anti-HIV ART, has also been implicated in the pathogenesis of CVDs in people living with human immune deficiency virus (PLWHIV)13. The administration of HIV antiretroviral drugs/combinations has been associated with endothelial dysfunction through different mechanisms including oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, increased permeability of endothelial cells and decreased expression of endothelial nitric oxide synthase(eNOS)14. Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs), a class of HIV antiretrovirals have been associated with oxidative stress which could contribute to endothelial dysfunction15. The rising prevalence of both prediabetes and CVD prevalence is a global public health concern especially in high-risk individuals who are using HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (HIV-PrEP) treatment. HIV-PrEP is a biomedical HIV prevention strategy administered to HIV-negative individuals who are at substantial risk of infection. In contrast, individuals already infected with HIV receive antiretroviral therapy (ART) to suppress viral replication and manage disease progression. Currently, the combined effect of prediabetes and HIV-PrEP use on endothelial function remains underexplored. With the increasing global prevalence of HIV-PrEP usage and prediabetes, understanding their combined impact on endothelial function is crucial. The use of animal models is effective when studying metabolic disorders like prediabetes as it allows for control of genetic and environmental variables which affect the pathogenesis and pathophysiology of prediabetes16. Hence, the aim of this study was to investigate the effects of emtricitabine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (FTC/TDF), a fixed-dose combination prophylactic drug on endothelial function in diet-induced prediabetic male Sprague-Dawley rats. The hypothesis for this study is that FTC/TDF will exacerbate endothelial dysfunction in the diet-induced prediabetic male Sprague-Dawley rats.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

Ethical approval was obtained before the commencement of this study from the Animal Research Ethics Committee of the University of Pretoria (Ethics number: 133/2024). All experimental techniques were performed under the supervision of a veterinarian following the ARRIVE guidelines.

Study design

The aim of this study was to determine the effects of the HIV-PrEP fixed-dose combination drug FTC/TDF on endothelial function. Twenty-eight male Sprague-Dawley rats (150–180 g) were purchased from the University of KwaZulu-Natal, Biomedical Research Unit (Westville campus). Thereafter, the animals were housed in standard rat plastic cages under the following conditions approved by the animal research ethics committee: transparent plastic cages with bedding and environmental enrichment; a 12-hour light/dark cycle; a temperature of 22±°C; and a humidity of 55 ± 5%. All the animals were acclimatised for 14 days before being randomly divided into the non-prediabetic (NPD) group (n = 14) and the prediabetic (PD) group (n = 14). Prediabetes was induced using a high-fat high-carbohydrate (HFHC) diet supplemented with 18% fructose water while the non-prediabetic group was fed a standard rat diet and water ad libitum for 20 weeks. After 20 weeks, prediabetes was confirmed using well-established criteria from Luvuno et al. 201817. The animals continued with their respective diets until the termination of the study. The formulation breakdown of the HFHC diet has been provided in Table 1. Prior to treatment the rats were further divided into the NPD group (n = 7), NPD treated with FTC/TDF group (n = 7), PD group (n = 7) and PD treated with FTC/TDF group(n = 7) where the rats were treated for an additional 12 weeks. The positive control group was the untreated PD group, and negative control was the untreated NPD group. The FTC/TDF was administered via an oral gavage (18-gauge gavage needle, 38 mm long curved with 21/4 mm ball end) five times a week. The animals were weighed weekly using an analytical balance (Zeiss West Germany (Pty) Ltd., Oberkochen, Germany). The study was terminated after 12 weeks of treatment and terminal blood, and heart tissue samples were harvested for biochemical analysis. Drug preparation

Film-coated pills containing 200 mg emtricitabine and 300 mg tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (Gilead Sciences Inc, Foster city, California) were crushed into a fine powder using a ceramic pestle and mortar and filtered using a mesh strainer. The powder was weighed and dissolved using double distilled water to create a concentrated stock solution that was determined using the animal equivalent dose (128 mg/kg/day). Blood sample collection and storage

The animals were exposed to 100 mg/kg Isoform (Safeline Pharmaceuticals Ltd., Roodepoort, South Africa), an anaesthetic agent in a gas chamber for 1–2 min. When the animals were unconscious, the blood was collected into blood tubes precoated with Ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid using cardiac puncture before being euthanised using the decapitation method. The decapitation was conducted using the guillotine from UKZN BRU. The tubes were centrifuged at 4 °C, 10 000 xg for 15 min to collect plasma and the plasma was stored in a − 80 °C in a Bio Ultra freezer (Snijders Tilburg B.V., Tilburg, Netherlands) for biochemical analysis. The animals were sacrificed over a 2-day period where there non-prediabetic groups were sacrificed first and followed the prediabetic groups. The investigators were not blinded during sample collection and analysis.

Biochemical analysis

Prediabetes diagnosis confirmation

Prediabetes diagnosis was confirmed via an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) following a 12-hour fasting period. A glucose solution (0.86 g/kg, p.o) was administered via an oral gavage needle and blood glucose readings were obtained using the tail-prick method on an and ACCUCHEK Instant® glucometer (Roche Diagnostics, Rotkreuz, Switzerland).

Lipid metabolism

Total triglyceride levels were measured using blood samples collected via the tail-prick method on an Accutrend® Plus cardiovascular screening device (Roche Diagnostics, Rotkreuz, Switzerland). Low density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) was quantified using a colorimetric assay performed according to the manufacturer’s manual (Elabscience®, Houston, Texas, United States).

Inflammation

Pro-inflammatory markers TNF-α and IL-6 were quantified using enzyme linked immunosorbent assays (TNF-α and Il-10: Elabscience®, Houston, Texas, United States; IL-6: ABclonal, Woburn, Massachusetts). The experiments were executed in accordance with the manufacturer’s manual, and the optical density was read utilising an Epoch spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, USA) and Bio Tek Gen 5 software (version 3.12, Agilent technologies, Santa Clara, California, United States). The concentrations were calculated using GraphPad Prism software (version 8.02, GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, California, United States) following the manual instructions.

Oxidative stress

Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and total antioxidant capacity (TAOC) was used to quantify oxidative stress, and this was achieved through colorimetric assays (Elabscience®, Houston, Texas, United States). The assays were conducted according to the manufacturer’s protocol, and all optical density values were read at with an Epoch spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, USA) and Bio Tek Gen 5 software (version 3.12, Agilent technologies, Santa Clara, California, United States).

Endothelial function

Endothelial function was quantified using the following marker eNOS, NO, ET-1 and VEGF-A. These markers were measured using ELISA kits and a colorimetric assay (eNOS&ET-1: Reed biotech Ltd, Wuhan Optics Valley Biolake, Hubei, China; NO and VEGF-A: Elabscience®, Houston, Texas, United States). The NO metabolism assay kit detection principle quantifies the azo compound formed when NO is oxidised to NO2− and reddish azo compound is formed with the colour developing agent which is linearly related to the concentration of NO. The eNOS, ET-1 and VEGF-A ELISA kits use the sandwich-ELISA principle where the kits came with plates that were precoated rat antibodies specific to the protein measured. The assays were performed and the Optical densities were read at 450 nm for eNOS, ET-1 and VEGF-A and NO assay plate was read at 550 nm. The assays were executed according to the manufacturer’s protocol and all optical density values were read using an Epoch spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, USA) and Bio Tek Gen 5 software (version 3.12, Agilent technologies, Santa Clara, California, United States).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 8.02, GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, California, United States). Data was presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) with a p-value of 0.05 considered statistically significant (p < 0.05). Two-way non-parametric analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare data between the treatment groups and control groups and Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test was used for post-hoc statistical analysis. Correlational analysis was performed by calculating the Pearsons r-coefficients and a two-tailed p value was calculated to determine statistical significance at a confidence interval of 95%.

Results

Fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels are used in the diagnosis of prediabetes as they are indicative of the presence of impaired fasting glucose (IFG) levels. In this study, when FBG was measured after the induction period the values confirmed the presence of prediabetes through IFG. Post-treatment, FBG levels in the PD control were statistically significantly elevated when compared to the NPD control (NPD vs. PD, p = 0.0049). Treatment with FTC/TDF exerted minimal effects in both the non-prediabetic and prediabetic states. Administration of FTC/TDF in the non-prediabetic resulted in a slight increase in FBG levels that was not statistically significant when compared to the non-prediabetic control (NPD vs. NPD + Tru, p = 0.6266). However, in the prediabetic state FTC/TDF treatment led to a small decrease in FBG levels that was not statistically significant when compared to the prediabetic control (PD vs. PD + Tru, p = 0.9953). Inflammation

Pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNF-α and IL-6 were used to evaluate the effect of FTC/TDF on inflammation. Figure 3A illustrates the effect of FTC/TDF on TNF-α, when the NPD control was compared to PD control there was an increase in TNF-α concentration although it was not statistically significant (NPD vs. PD, p = > 0.9999). Treatment with FTC/TDF in a non-prediabetic resulted in a slight decrease in TNF-α concentration which did not reach statistical significance (NPD vs. NPD + Tru, p = 0.3315) in comparison to the NPD control. Administration of FTC/TDF in prediabetic conditions resulted in a reduction of TNF-α concentration that was not statistically significant (PD vs. PD + Tru, p = > 0.9999) in comparison to the PD control. As shown in Fig. 3B, when compared to the NPD control the PD control exhibited an increase in IL-6 concentration which was also not statistically significant (NPD vs. PD, p = 0.2011). Additionally, administration of FTC/TDF in a non-prediabetic state resulted in a numerical increase in IL-6 concentration that was not statistically significant in comparison to the NPD control (NPD vs. NPD + Tru, p = 0.9830). Contrary to the non-prediabetic state, administration of FTC/TDF resulted in a decrease in IL-6 concentration which did not result in statistical significance when compared to the PD control (PD vs. PD + Tru, p = 0.4097).

Oxidative stress

Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is a reactive oxidative species that was used to assess the effect of FTC/TDF on oxidative stress. Figure 4A below demonstrates the effect of FTC/TDF on H2O2 in plasma. When compared to the NPD control both the PD control and NPD treated with FTC/TDF groups showed a statistically significant increase in H2O2 concentration (NPD vs. PD, p = < 0.0001; NPD vs. NPD + Tru, p = 0.0020). However, administration of FTC/TDF in the prediabetic state resulted in a statistically non-significant decrease in H2O2 concentration in comparison to the PD control (PD vs. PD + Tru, p = 0.9734). The effects of FTC/TDF on total antioxidant capacity are illustrated in Fig. 4B below. When compared to the NPD control, the PD control demonstrated a statistically significant increased total antioxidant capacity (NPD vs. PD, p = 0.0026). Administration of FTC/TDF in the non-prediabetic state resulted in slight increase in total antioxidant capacity that was not statistically significant (NPD vs. NPD + Tru, p = > 0.9999) and prediabetic state resulted in a decrease in total antioxidant capacity that was not statistically significant (PD vs. PD + Tru, p = 0.1065) when compared to their respective controls.

Discussion

Endothelial dysfunction is distinguished by changes in the bioavailability of NO or an imbalance of endothelial-derived vasodilative and vasoconstrictive factors18. The molecular mechanisms fundamental to endothelial dysfunction are complex and multi-factorial, driven by both exogenous and endogenous pathological risk factors. The endogenous risk factors may be secondary to pre-existing conditions such as prediabetes or overt T2DM, leading to complications including chronic excessive plasma glucose levels, reactive oxidative species, dyslipidaemia and low-grade inflammation19. Furthermore, exogenous risk factors of endothelial dysfunction may include chronic medication such as HIV-antiretroviral therapy. The previous HIV-ART was also associated with metabolic dysfunctions-induced CVDs such as hypertension20. This contributed to the discontinuation of some of the HIV-ART agents across all classes including NRTIs and changes in prescription guidelines21. However, these observations were not on prediabetic individuals specifically. The current strategy in capping the HIV pandemic includes the recommendation of PrEP usage among the other methods22. Moreover, prediabetes incidence is on the rise even in countries of low economic status, including in the African continent, where there is a high rate of HIV cases23. This necessitates an investigation on the effects of HIV-PrEP drugs in the prediabetic population. Hence, this study compared risks of developing vascular complications in prediabetic animals and non-prediabetic animals post NRTI-based HIV-PrEP drug (FTC/TDF) treatment. Prediabetes is a condition classified by impaired glucose tolerance, impaired fasting glucose or both impaired glucose tolerance and impaired fasting glucose due to a decrease in the insulin response of cells in the body24. The findings confirmed that chronic exposure of SD rats to an HFHC diet results in a prediabetes rat model, characterised by IFG. This confirmed a well-established HFHC diet-induced model of prediabetes17.

Hyperglycaemia and insulin resistance have a bidirectional relationship where hyperglycaemia can occur as a result of changes in insulin sensitivity like insulin resistance reinforcing a vicious cycle25. The use of medications including ART could have metabolic consequences such as hyperglycaemia26. Additionally, discontinued older-generation protease inhibitors drugs like indinavir/ritonavir and NRTIs like zidovudine have been linked to the occurrence of hyperglycaemia27, 28. The metabolic effects of newer NRTIs are not well studied and fully understood. In this study, we found that the administration of FTC/TDF in prediabetic animals resulted in a slight reduction of FBG. However, in non-prediabetic animals, FTC/TDF resulted in no significant change in FBG. These findings were not statistical significance, however, they further confirm the previous reports that suggests the minimal effects of TDF-containing regimens on glucose metabolism29.

Impaired lipid metabolism is one of the traditional vascular disease risk factors that contributes to endothelial dysfunction, which plays a role in the pathogenesis of hypertension and atherosclerosis30. Quantification of lipid metabolites, including triglycerides, total cholesterol, HDL-C and LDL-C, serves as the determinant in the assessment of vascular disease risks and also affects the vascular endothelial function31. Normally, the elevation of plasma triglycerides and LDL-C is considered a risk factor for the development of vascular disease. Furthermore, the reduction of HDL-C is also associated with reduced vascular endothelial health and subsequently the onset of vascular diseases32. This study found that non-prediabetic animals had elevated plasma LDL-C and significantly higher TGs. These findings have been traditionally attributed to the insulin resistance-driven adipose tissue lipolysis, producing free fatty acids which are transported to the liver where they are used for the synthesis of TGs and very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL)33. However, FTC/TDF treatment in the prediabetic state did not result in any significant reduction in plasma TGs, while showed a significant increase in the LDL-C levels the “bad cholesterol”. This type of lipid profile may be attributed to the systemic dyslipidaemia, leading to the body’s inability to adequately execute cholesterol reverse transport and subsequently clear LDL-C from the blood34. The previous studies have indicated that In blood, LDL-C may react with reactive oxygen species (ROS) and form oxidised-LDL, which is then transported into the endothelial cells and across the endothelial lining of the blood vessels35. Although previous studies have alluded to the lipid-lowering effect of FTC/TDF through decreasing HDL-C and LDL-C levels in HIV-negative patients36. Furthermore, changing from an ABC/3TC regimen to FTC/TDF resulted in statistically significant reductions in HDL-C, LDL-C and total cholesterol37. The findings of this study indicate the contrary, non-significant change in TG and HDL-C, which remain on the high end, while significantly overshooting LDL-C which is a symptom of dyslipidaemia that can be attributed to dysfunctional HDL. This type of dyslipidaemia may predispose the onset of vascular complications through endothelial dysfunction.

Insulin resistance is characterised by the oxidative stress, resulting from the hyperglycaemia and hyperlipidaemia38. Oxidative stress is a metabolically driven complication of elevated reactive oxidative species (ROS) production, accompanied by a decrease in antioxidant capacity39. In this study prediabetic state was associated with an increase in both H2O2 concentration and total antioxidant capacity when compared to the non-prediabetic control. This may be attributed to compensatory mechanisms against H2O2, such as the activation of catalases which break down H2O2 into water and oxygen and glutathione peroxidases, which break down H2O2 into water or alkyl hydroperoxides40, 41. The FTC/TDF treatment of prediabetic animals resulted in no significant changes in H2O2 production compared to the untreated prediabetic control; however, it was still significantly higher than that of the normal control. Moreover, prediabetic animals that had elevated H2O2 production had reduced antioxidant capacity. This may indicate a decline in antioxidant capacity in prediabetic animals treated with FTC/TDF. A similar trend was also observed in the non-prediabetic state treated with FTC/TDF, the heightened H2O2 production and compromised total antioxidant capacity. The elevated H2O2 is associated with endothelial dysfunctions characterised by the reduced nitric oxide bioavailability and endothelial permeability leading to vascular complications42. This elucidates the FTC/TDF as a pro-oxidative agent through compromising the antioxidant defence mechanisms. However, specific antioxidant enzyme activities, including SOD, GPx and catalase, need further investigation. Hence, these effects of the drug on oxidative stress should be considered preliminary due to the limited markers measured in the study.

The relationship between oxidative stress and inflammation can be defined as a cyclic relationship, where elevated ROS activate nuclear factor k-beta (NF-kB), an upregulator of pro-inflammatory cytokines and the pro-inflammatory cytokines induce the production of ROS42. This study observed prediabetic animals that had IFBG, dysfunctional HDL dyslipidaemia, and oxidative stress, also had a non-significant elevation in pro-inflammatory cytokines, i.e. increased TNF-α and IL-6. The administration of FTC/TDF in prediabetic and non-prediabetic animals also did not show any effects on plasma IL-6 and TNF-α concentration. This signifies the neutral effect of FTC/TDF on systemic inflammation, which can be interpreted as a beneficial PrEP drug. However, a previous study has associated FTC/TDF with reduced immune activation and subsequent lower TNF-α and IL-6 levels43. Additionally, another study revealed that FTC/TDF/EFV combination improved pro-inflammatory cytokines through the reduction TNF-α and IL-6 concentrations in asymptomatic ART-naïve HIV-positive individuals44.

The coexistence of hyperinsulinemia and hyperglycemia can activate the gene that codes for the expression of VEGF-A45. Mechanisms associated with diabetes-induced VEGF-A include oxidative stress and inflammation through NF-κB activation46. Moreover, elevated VEGF-A in diabetes is associated with endothelial dysfunction and microvascular complications such as nephropathy47. The findings of the study on prediabetic animals revealed that prediabetic control had elevated VEGF-A in plasma but reduced in cardiac tissue. Interestingly, this study observed that administration of FCT/TDF in prediabetic animals resulted in a drastic elevation of VEGF-A when compared to both prediabetic and non-prediabetic controls. These findings may be attributed to a significant elevation of reactive oxygen species coming from H2O2, which could promote oxidative stress. However, this needs further investigation to elucidate the effects of FTC/TDF on VEGF-A levels.

Increased VEGF-A has been previously associated with increased risk of renal complications like diabetic nephropathy and chronic kidney disease48. The administration of TDF-containing regimens has been linked to chronic kidney disease in PLWHIV and a negative effect on kidney function49. Chronic kidney disease has been established as a cause and sequala of hypertension50, 51. Moreover, this further suggests that the reported renal complications associated with TDF-containing regimens might be attributed to the elevated VEGF-A. Furthermore, the administration of FCT/TDF to non-prediabetic animals elevated plasma and cardiac tissue VEGF-A, which was observed in animals with normoglycemic control, no oxidative stress, or low-grade inflammation. This signifies that FTC/TDF might be directly inducing VEGF-A expression and subsequently altering endothelial function. VEGF-A promotes new blood vessel formation which is an early adaptive mechanism that can become detrimental because excessive angiogenesis promotes plaque neovascularization in atherosclerosis52. There are currently no studies that have reported on the effects of antiretroviral drugs including FTC/TDF on VEGF-A concentration. However, a case report found that treatment with TDF/FTC/EFV could potentially supress angiogenesis in a male adult with an angiolipoma53. Exacerbation of vascular permeability contributes to vascular failure and enhances the pathogenesis of vascular disease, including atherosclerosis54. Under normal physiological conditions, elevated VEGF-A stimulates eNOS expression and activity, which subsequently leads to an elevation in NO bioavailability and production55. However, it is important to note NO levels can also be affected by other factors including dietary nitrate intake and renal function, which were not quantified in this study56. These finding may suggest A well-established vasodilator like NO has been inversely associated with diabetes-related macrovascular complications such as hypertension57.

This study observed that the prediabetic state had statistically significantly elevated plasma VEGF-A accompanied by a non-significant elevation of plasma NO content. Moreover, in cardiac tissue, the prediabetic state showed non-statistically reduced VEGF-A, accompanied by elevated eNOS. The administration of FTC/TDF resulted in no change in NO content, which was accompanied by drastic statistically significant elevation of plasma VEGF-A in a prediabetic state. In the cardiac tissue, FTC/TDF resulted in elevated eNOS when compared with both prediabetics and non-prediabetics controls, which corresponded with marked improvement in VEGF-A in the tissue. Even though these findings were not statistically significant, they may indicate a possible trend that needs further investigation.

Non-prediabetic animals treated with FTC/TDF showed an elevation in myocardial eNOS and consequently, plasma NO content, which was also accompanied by elevated VEGF-A compared to the control group. However, these findings did yield any statistical significance. Under normal conditions VEGF-A triggers endothelial NO release, however in diabetic conditions, regardless of increased VEGF levels endothelial NO is low which could occur as a result of multiple mechanisms like glucose scavenging NO and oxidative stress diminishing NO levels to produce peroxynitrite58. This could imply that prediabetic conditions alter the VEGF-A mediated NO synthesis and bioavailability with the hyperglycaemia and oxidative stress as potential mechanisms58. Furthermore, previous studies found that lopinavir(boosted with ritonavir) decreases eNOS and NO levels in human coronary artery endothelial cells59. Reduced eNOS production and NO bioavailability results in impaired endothelium-dependent vasorelaxation which subsequently leads to endothelial dysfunction and possibly CVDs like hypertension and heart failure60.

In the presence of hyperglycaemia, VEGF-A and ET-1 are elevated which exacerbates the endothelial dysfunction through increased endothelium permeability and decreased NO synthesis respectively61, 62, 63. This study noted that the prediabetic animals that had elevated plasma VEGF-A, also had elevated plasma ET-1 concentration in comparison to the non-prediabetic control group. However, the same group had significantly reduced myocardial VEGF-A and ET-1 concentrations. The observed plasma VEGF-A and ET-1 relationship in prediabetics further suggest the elevate MAP in prediabetes condition may be endothelial dysfunction driven. Moreover, the reciprocal relationship between myocardial tissue VEGF-A and ET-1 may suggest that in the early stages of diabetes, organs that are insulin-independent for glucose uptake experience glucotoxicity. Glucotoxicity disrupts the VEGF-A and ET-1 signalling, predisposing to hypertension and vascular complications64, 65. Furthermore, FTC/TDF treatment increased myocardial ET-1 concentration in the non-prediabetic group, and it significantly decreased myocardial ET-1 concentration in the prediabetic group. Previous studies found that the presence of ROS contribute to an increase in ET-1 production, which is a plausible mechanism of action by which FTC/TDF increased ET-1 concentration in our study66. The prediabetic state was previously found to result in higher levels of ET-1 in previous in vivo study models67, 68. A study previously observed an increase in ET-1 concentration in human coronary artery endothelial cells treated with lopinavir (boosted with ritonavir)59. Systemic vasoconstriction is the main driver of the development hypertension therefore increased ET-1 levels contributes to a heightened risk of hypertension18.

Plasma NO/ET-1 ratio has been used for the risk assessment of coronary artery disease because it is a strong predictor of the condition69. This study revealed that FTC/TDF treatment increased NO/ET-1 ratio in the prediabetic state and non-prediabetic state. However, this observation should be considered indicative rather than conclusive relating to endothelial dysfunction due to their lack of statistical significance. The decrease in NO/ET-1 ratio could be attributed to a reduction in NO and increase in ET-1 concentration although it was not significant.

When correlational analysis was performed this study demonstrated that overall, VEGF-A has a strong positive corelation with NO and ET-1 levels with statistical significance only noted in the assessment of ET-1 levels regardless of treatment or diseased state. This suggests that as VEGF-A levels increase both NO and ET-1 levels increase. In the non-prediabetic state VEGF-A exhibited a moderate negative correlation suggesting that as VEGF-A levels increase both NO and ET-1 levels decrease. Treatment with FTC/TDF resulted in a negative negligible correlation between VEGF-A and NO while it led to a weak positive correlation between VEGF-A and ET-1 implying that as VEGF-A levels increase NO levels decrease and ET-1 levels increase.

Under prediabetic conditions, VEGF-A displayed a weak positive correlation with NO while exhibiting a moderate positive correlation with ET-1 levels. This suggests that as VEGF-A levels increase NO levels increase while ET-1 levels decrease. Similar to the non-prediabetic state, this study revealed that administration of FTC/TDF leads to VEGF-A having a strong negative correlation with NO and a weak positive correlation with ET-1. This suggests that as VEGF-A levels increase NO levels decrease and ET-1 levels increase. Our study unveiled that an association between increased VEGF-A levels and a reduction in NO as well as increased ET-1 levels when FTC/TDF was administered suggesting that a decrease NO and increase in ET-1 levels is a potential mechanism by which FTC/TDF could predispose individuals to vascular complications like hypertension. However, further studies need to be conducted to confirm this mechanism.

Conclusion

This study sought to evaluate the effects of FTC/TDF on endothelial function by assessing markers of lipid metabolism, oxidative stress, inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction in a diet-induced prediabetic rat model. Our study found that FTC/TDF treatment contributed to endothelial dysfunction mainly through an oxidative stress mechanism. Treatment with FTC/TDF had varying effects on lipid metabolism, oxidative stress, and inflammation which differed based on whether the drug was administered in prediabetic or non-prediabetic conditions. The significant findings noted in the non-prediabetic state include increased ROS production and VEGF-A which could contribute to oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction respectively. The significant findings noted in the prediabetic state include increased LDL-C, VEGF-A and myocardial ET-1 which could have detrimental effects on endothelial function and vascular health.

This suggests that FTC/TDF treatment could increase the risk of cardiovascular complications via oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction. These findings highlight the need for further studies to be conducted to fully understand the molecular mechanisms behind these effects observed.

Study limitations

The findings of this study should be interpreted with nuance due to a number of limitations including the lack of body weight measurements which could affect vascular function. Furthermore, the lack of functional assessments like blood pressure and heart rate measurements were not conducted to assess the effect of the drug on mean arterial pressure, systolic and diastolic blood pressure are also a limitation. This limitation prevented our investigation from elucidating the relationship between FTC/TDF treatment and hypertension development. Biochemical analysis was performed following a 12-week treatment period, a longer treatment period would provide more insight into the effects of FTC/TDF on endothelial function long-term.

Future considerations

Future studies should investigate the effects of FTC/TDF on other markers of endothelial function such as von Willebrand factor, intercellular adhesion molecule 1 and vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 to determine the molecular mechanisms behind endothelial dysfunction associated with FTC/TDF treatment. Future studies should also evaluate the effects of FTC/TDF on endothelial function in a female rat model to determine if there any sex specific changes. Furthermore, histological and functional assessments should be performed to examine the effects of FTC/TDF on myocardial and vascular morphology as well as functional parameters that are contribute to the development of endothelial dysfunction and subsequent cardiovascular conditions.

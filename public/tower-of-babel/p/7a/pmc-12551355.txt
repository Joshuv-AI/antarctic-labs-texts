Enhanced effects of augmented reality-based cardiac rehabilitation in the first phase on recovery quality in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass graft surgery
Authors: Mahdi Kahrom, Kheizaran Miri, Neda Ghlichi Moghaddam, Seyyed Reza Mazloum, Fatemeh Hajiabadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOpen-heart surgery is considered as a major treatment for severe coronary artery stenosis with some possible post-operative complications. The successful implementation of the first phase of cardiac rehabilitation (CR) program can make a fall in incidence of these clinical complications. In this study, we aimed to determine the effects of the first phase of cardiac rehabilitation program on quality of recovery (QoR) in patients after coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG) based on augmented reality techniques.MethodsThe clinical trial, conducted from April 2020 to January 2021 at Ghaem Hospital in Iran, involved 60 ICU patients post-open-heart surgery, divided into treatment and control groups. The treatment group used a software-based phase-1 cardiac rehabilitation program featuring videos and images, while the control group followed a standard rehabilitation routine. Data was analyzed using SPSS, employing descriptive statistics and tests such as the Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, and Chi-square test.ResultsAccording to the findings of this study, the mean overall QoR score indicated an increase for both treatment and control groups in ICU admission/discharge time. But, patients in the treatment group had a statistically significant increase in comparison with non-significant changes in the control group (P < 0.001).ConclusionThe study found that using augmented reality for early-stage rehabilitation in the ICU can enhance recovery quality for patients after coronary artery bypass graft surgery. Hence, AR technology could be integrated into various stages of cardiac rehabilitation.Trial registrationThis study was registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (no. IRCT20200203046361N1) on 16/02/2020.Keywords: Coronary artery disease, Phase-1 cardiac rehabilitation training, Augmented reality, Quality of recovery

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of global mortality and morbidity, accounting for approximately 19.91 million deaths in 2022, representing 32% of all global deaths [1, 2]. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023, ischemic heart disease continues to be the single largest contributor, responsible for over 9.44 million deaths worldwide in 2023 [2]. In low- and middle-income countries, including Iran, over 50% of CVD-related deaths are attributable to coronary artery disease (CAD) [3]. Recent national estimates indicate that ischemic heart disease ranks as the first cause of death in both men and women in Iran, with more than 1,100,000 annual deaths in individuals aged over 40 years [4, 5].

CAD accounts for nearly 30% of all cardiovascular diseases [6, 7], and its most serious complication is myocardial ischemia, which may lead to myocardial infarction and sudden cardiac death [8]. Diagnostic coronary angiography remains the gold standard for CAD diagnosis, and in severe cases, coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is indicated [9]. Globally, more than 900,000 CABG procedures are performed annually, with approximately 60% of all open-heart surgeries in Iran comprising CABG operations [10, 11].

Undergoing heart surgery represents a major life event associated with multiple physical and psychological challenges such as fatigue, sleep disturbances, anxiety, and limitations in daily activities, profoundly affecting patients’ social, professional, and personal lives [12, 13]. Evidence suggests that comprehensive postoperative care throughout hospitalization is critical in enhancing recovery and improving long-term outcomes in these patients [10, 11].

Cardiac rehabilitation (CR) plays a pivotal role in the secondary prevention of adverse events and functional recovery following cardiac surgery [14]. Standard CR programs are typically structured into three sequential phases: Phase 1 (inpatient), Phase 2 (outpatient), and Phase 3 (maintenance) [15]. Phase 1 CR is initiated early during the inpatient hospital stay, aiming to promote mobilization, provide patient and family education, and prepare for discharge [15]. Previous research highlights the significant clinical benefits associated with effective Phase 1 CR, including reduced postoperative complications, improved functional capacity, and shorter hospital stays [6, 7, 16].

Despite its proven benefits, the implementation of Phase 1 CR often faces practical barriers such as limited human resources, shortage of specialized equipment, variability in patient education delivery, and constraints in ICU and cardiac wards [8]. Emerging technologies such as Augmented Reality (AR) offer promising solutions to address these limitations by delivering interactive, individualized, and engaging rehabilitation programs directly to patients [17].

AR technology integrates virtual objects (text, images, animations, and videos) with the real environment, enhancing user interaction and learning experiences [18]. Previous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of AR-based systems in various medical applications, including motor rehabilitation in post-stroke patients [17] and skill learning in individuals with autism spectrum disorders [19]. However, to the best of our knowledge, no study has yet explored the feasibility and effects of AR-assisted programs in Phase 1 CR for post-CABG patients.

Given the significant importance of early rehabilitation and the limitations encountered in conventional CR settings, this study was designed to evaluate the effects of implementing a Phase 1 cardiac rehabilitation program supported by augmented reality technology on quality of recovery (QoR) in post-CABG patients.

Methods

Trial design

This controlled clinical trial study was conducted between Apr-2020 to Jan-2021 on 60 patients admitted to the Intensive Care Unit of the open-heart surgery ward in the Ghaem Hospital, Mashhad, Iran (Fig. 1).

Intervention

An augmented reality (AR) mobile application was specifically developed for Android devices using the Unity 3D game engine platform integrated with the Vuforia SDK for AR content rendering. This AR system was marker-based, utilizing printed image targets placed in the patient’s room and hospital corridors to trigger AR content when viewed through the app installed on the patient’s mobile device.

The educational content provided through the AR application included:

Instructional videos demonstrating diaphragmatic breathing exercises and basic physical activity movements.

Instructional videos demonstrating diaphragmatic breathing exercises and basic physical activity movements.

Animated 3D models and visual cues to guide patients on correct posture, mobilization, and safe movement techniques.

Animated 3D models and visual cues to guide patients on correct posture, mobilization, and safe movement techniques.

Textual instructions and voice-over narration to enhance comprehension and engagement.

Textual instructions and voice-over narration to enhance comprehension and engagement.

Before commencing the main trial, a pilot usability and performance assessment was conducted with 10 cardiac patients to evaluate the app’s usability, clarity of the interface, content loading speed, video and AR playback quality, and patient satisfaction with the educational materials. Additionally, the feasibility of using the app in the hospital setting, both in the ICU and ward, was tested under supervision. Patient feedback was collected using a researcher-developed software usability evaluation checklist, covering parameters such as ease of use, clarity, engagement, and perceived usefulness.

The intervention began after patients were extubated, fully conscious, and hemodynamically stable, typically within 12–24 h after ICU admission following coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery. At this stage, eligible and willing patients were enrolled, and the AR-based rehabilitation program commenced.

The control group received the routine phase 1 cardiac rehabilitation program, based on the standard cardiac rehabilitation protocol of the Iranian Ministry of Health and Medical Education. This program involved face-to-face education by the researcher, including breathing exercises, passive and active movements, sitting by the bedside, progressive ambulation in the ward, and stair climbing without AR-based support.

In both groups, the Quality of Recovery (QoR) questionnaire was administered at ICU admission (after extubation and before the start of rehabilitation) and again at the time of discharge from the hospital.

Each item is scored using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (none of the time) to 5 (all of the time), with total scores ranging from 40 to 200. Higher scores indicate a better quality of postoperative recovery.

In Iran, the psychometric properties of the QoR-40 were evaluated by Mazlom et al. (2013), who reported acceptable reliability and validity in cardiac surgery patients [21]. In their study, test-retest reliability was assessed with a 3-day interval among 15 patients, yielding a correlation coefficient of r = 0.986 and a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.89 for internal consistency.

In this study, the QoR-40 was administered on the third postoperative day to assess participants’ recovery status before hospital discharge.

Sample size and randomization

Eligible patients who met the inclusion criteria were consecutively enrolled in the study. After obtaining informed consent, patients were randomly assigned to either the intervention or control group using a computer-generated randomization sequence created with SPSS software. The allocation sequence was concealed in sealed, opaque, and sequentially numbered envelopes.

This was a double-blinded study, meaning that both the patients and the outcome assessors were blinded to the group allocations. The interventionists, due to the nature of the intervention, could not be blinded.

A pilot study was initially conducted with 10 patients per group to estimate the standard deviations required for sample size calculation. Based on the results, the sample size was determined to be 20 patients per group, calculated using the formula for comparing two means with a 95% confidence level and 80% power:

Considering a 10% potential dropout rate, the final sample size was increased to 30 patients in each group.

Statistical methods

Data statistical analysis was performed with SPSS Version 21.0 static software package using descriptive statistics including Mean ± SD and frequency as well as student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test and Chi-square test for 95% confidence.

Discussion

The present study aimed to investigate the impact of an augmented reality (AR)-assisted phase 1 cardiac rehabilitation (CR) training on the quality of recovery (QoR) in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery. The results showed that although both intervention and control groups experienced increased QoR scores during their ICU stay, the increase was significantly greater in the intervention group.

Previous studies have similarly highlighted the benefits of early CR interventions on patient outcomes. Intarakamhang et al. (2013) demonstrated the effectiveness of a comprehensive CR program incorporating psychological and educational components in improving psychological factors and quality of recovery among CABG patients [22]. This aligns with our findings, though our study uniquely integrated AR technology into the rehabilitation process.

In a recent study by Ma et al. (2021), a WeChat-based education and rehabilitation program effectively reduced anxiety and depression and improved mental health in patients undergoing CABG [23]. While their intervention included post-discharge follow-ups via an online platform, our AR-based system was an offline tool without follow-up capabilities. Nonetheless, it provided patients with ongoing home-based rehabilitation exercises after discharge. Given the differing intervention formats and assessment tools, direct comparison should be made cautiously.

Recent studies have also confirmed the benefits of early and continuous CR on physical and mental recovery after CABG. For example, Zanini et al. (2019) demonstrated that integrative rehabilitation protocols, combining physical activity and educational support from pre-surgery to post-discharge, yielded better physical function outcomes [24]. Similarly, Lian et al. (2020) emphasized the importance of early physical exercises in improving patients’ postoperative physical status and autonomy [25]. The present study extends these findings by showing that integrating AR technology into phase 1 CR can enhance patient engagement and learning motivation, supporting improved recovery outcomes.

However, not all studies have shown consistent effects. Højskov et al. (2019) reported no significant effect of early physical and psychoeducational rehabilitation on postoperative health outcomes [26]. Differences in intervention content, delivery methods, and assessment tools likely contributed to these discrepancies.

Several recent studies (2020–2024) have explored the application of digital and AR-assisted rehabilitation in post-cardiac surgery care, reporting positive impacts on patient adherence, motivation, and quality of life [27–29]. Incorporating such modern digital tools into CR protocols is an emerging and promising trend.

Study Limitations:

This study has several limitations. Some patients were unable to participate in workout exercises due to physical restrictions. Additionally, technical challenges such as difficulty installing the AR application or discomfort with small fonts and image-based features reduced engagement in a subset of participants. Finally, cultural factors and individual differences in technology familiarity might have affected the completeness and consistency of the data collected through questionnaires.

Conclusion

Early rehabilitation intervention programs appear to improve the quality of recovery in patients undergoing CABG surgery from ICU admission to discharge. Based on the results of this study, it is suggested that healthcare providers incorporate individualized exercise routines as part of phase 1 CR and encourage patients to continue rehabilitation practices after discharge. Furthermore, AR technology may serve as a useful adjunct to conventional CR programs by enhancing patient motivation, adherence, and engagement. Future large-scale, multi-center studies are recommended to further evaluate the long-term effects and cost-effectiveness of AR-assisted CR interventions.

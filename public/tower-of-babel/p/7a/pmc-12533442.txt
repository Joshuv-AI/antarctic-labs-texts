Validation of the health-related quality of life instrument with 8 items for assessing health-related quality of life in patients with oropharyngeal cancer: a comparison with the EQ-5D-5L
Authors: Gyeong-U Hong, Bon Seok Koo, Min-Ju Kim, Woo-Jeong Sim, Ah-Yeon Lee, Ji-Eun An, Su-Yeon Yu, Sei Young Lee, Soo Hyun Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo overcome the limitations of existing health-related quality of life (HRQoL) instruments, the HRQoL Instrument with 8 Items (HINT-8) was developed for the Korean population. We aimed to evaluate the validity, interrater reliability, and agreement of the HINT-8 compared to the EQ-5D-5 L among patients with oropharyngeal cancer (OPC).MethodsA total of 327 patients with OPC aged ≥ 19 years who received treatment at 14 hospitals in South Korea were recruited between August 2023 and February 2024. HRQoL was assessed using the two instruments. The psychometric evaluation included analyses of validity (convergent and known-group validity), agreement (intraclass correlation coefficient and Bland–Altman analysis), and reliability (internal consistency measured by Cronbach’s alpha). The ceiling effects and score distributions were also examined.ResultsIn total, 299 respondents were included in the final analysis. Both instruments demonstrated high internal consistency (HINT-8: α = 0.872; EQ-5D-5 L: α = 0.845) and good agreement (intraclass correlation coefficient [ICC] = 0.80; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.76–0.84). The convergent validity was supported by strong correlations between the HINT-8 index and both the EQ-5D-5 L index (r = 0.805) and EQ VAS (r = 0.715). The ceiling effect of HINT-8 (12.7%) was substantially lower than the EQ-5D-5 L (32.8%).ConclusionsThe HINT-8 is a valid, reliable, and responsive instrument for measuring HRQoL in patients with oropharyngeal cancer. These findings suggest that HINT-8 can serve as a valuable complementary instrument to the EQ-5D-5 L.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12955-025-02425-4.

Background

Oropharyngeal cancer (OPC) is a type of head and neck cancer (HNC) that begins in the tissues of the oropharynx [1]. Advances in OPC treatment have significantly improved survival rates. However, this cancer profoundly impacts on the quality of life (QoL) of survivors because it impairs essential functions such as swallowing, chewing, salivation, and speech [2]. After OPC treatment, issues related to residual tumors remain, and long-term survivors face challenges in health-related QoL (HRQoL) due to treatment sequelae [3]. Consequently, the QoL of patients with OPC has become an important aspect in clinical decision-making [4, 5]. QoL is a multidimensional concept and is an important focus for research and practice in health and medicine [6]. HRQoL dimensions capture information about an individual’s physical, mental, and social health status, as well as overall health [7, 8].

HRQoL instruments are categorized into generic and disease-specific types [9]. Disease-specific instruments, such as the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30) and its HNC module (EORTC H&N-35), provide detailed assessments for specific diseases, including OPC [10–12]. However, the combined length of these questionnaires (65 items) can be time-consuming and burdensome, particularly for patients experiencing health challenges during or after treatment [13]. In contrast, generic instruments such as EQ-5D-5 L provide a broad assessment of overall health that is applicable across diverse populations and health conditions [14]. One key advantage of the EQ-5D-5 L is its ability to generate health utility values, which are essential for calculating quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) in health economic evaluations and conducting cost-utility analyses [15, 16].

The EQ-5D-5 L is a short, generic questionnaire introduced by the EuroQol Group in 2009 to improve sensitivity and reduce ceiling effects in the EQ-5D-3 L [17]. The questionnaire has been validated in several cancers, including HNC [18], and shows strong comparability with disease-specific tools such as the EORTC QLQ-C30 [13]. A Korean study further reported moderate correlations between the EQ-5D-5 L and EORTC QLQ-C30, supporting its use as a valid generic measure for assessing HRQoL in patients with OPC [19]. Furthermore, the EQ-5D-5 L is applicable and reliable within the Korean context [20], and can be effectively used to assess the QoL of patients with cancer [21].

However, as the EQ-5D-5 L is a Europe-based generic instrument, it has limitations in fully reflecting the cultural characteristics and linguistic nuances of Korea, and has been criticized for its high ceiling effect [22]. These limitations highlight the need for culturally tailored tools that provide more accurate and sensitive HRQoL assessments.

To address these challenges, an HRQoL Instrument with Eight Items (HINT-8) was developed as a concise yet comprehensive, culturally specific instrument for the Korean population [23]. Although several HRQoL instruments have been introduced in Korea, many were either intended for specific patient groups or contained too many items, limiting their practicality for use in large-scale national surveys such as the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES). The HINT-8 addresses this gap and, like the EQ-5D-5 L, provides health-utility values derived from a valuation study conducted in a nationally representative sample of the Korean general population [24].

The HINT-8 has been used to assess HRQoL in a nationally representative population and has demonstrated construct validity, internal consistency, and measurement invariance [25]. In addition, it has shown good test-retest reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity for patients with breast cancer [26], patients with diabetes [27], and elderly individuals [28].

Despite the clinical importance of HRQoL assessment in patients with OPC, the application of HRQoL instruments in this population in Korea remains underexplored. Patients with OPC often experience persistent functional difficulties and psychosocial challenges, including speech difficulties, dysphagia, anxiety, and body image concerns, which may not be fully captured by conventional generic instruments [29]. The HINT-8, with its inclusion of items addressing psychosocial and emotional domains such as fatigue, depression, anxiety, and happiness, may be better suited to detect these health concerns in Korean OPC patients. Supporting this, a study evaluating oral health promotion and treatment impact on HRQoL revealed statistically significant associations using HINT-8, whereas no significant results were observed with EQ-5D [30]. Given its reduced ceiling effects, the HINT-8 may offer potential advantages over the EQ-5D-5 L in capturing subtle yet meaningful differences in health status [26–28]. Accordingly, we aimed to assess the validity, reliability, and agreement of the HINT-8 compared to the EQ-5D-5 L in patients with OPC.

Methods

Study population and survey methods

We recruited patients with OPC aged ≥ 19 who received treatment at 14 hospitals in South Korea between August 2023 and February 2024. All participants visited the hospital as outpatients and were undergoing treatment or had completed treatment at the time of the survey. The Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Kongju National University, as well as IRBs of the 14 participating hospitals [see Additional file 1] approved this study (KNU_IRB_2023-49).

Patient surveys were conducted after providing prior explanations and guidelines for the medical staff. Only participants who completed both instruments in full were included in the analysis; those with incomplete responses to either instrument were excluded [see Additional file 2].

The survey collected (a) demographic characteristics (sex, age, education level, marital status, income, household members, employment status, and religion); (b) clinical characteristics (duration since diagnosis, duration since treatment, disease status, American Joint Committee on Cancer [AJCC] stage, recurrence, outcome status, and human papillomavirus [HPV] status); and (c) HRQoL measured using the EQ-5D-5 L and HINT-8 instruments.

In terms of clinical characteristics, disease status was classified into two categories. Patients who were undergoing treatment or had completed treatment within 6 months prior to survey participation were categorized as being in the treatment phase, while those who had completed treatment more than 6 months prior were considered to be in the follow-up or survivorship phase. Outcome status was defined as follows: no evidence of disease (NED) refers to the absence of any clinically or radiologically detectable signs of OPC at the time of the survey, whereas alive with disease (AWD) indicates that the patient was living with persistent or recurrent OPC at the time of assessment.

HRQoL instruments

The EQ-5D-5 L is a generic, preference-based questionnaire consisting of five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression [17], assessed on five levels of severity: no problems, slight problems, moderate problems, severe problems, and extreme problems. Additionally, the EQ-5D-5 L includes a visual analog scale (EQ VAS), which allows patients to rate their overall health on a scale of 0 (worst) to 100 (best). The South Korean EQ-5D-5 L utility value set was developed using the composite time trade-off (cTTO) approach, The utility index is calculated based on this valuation set and ranges from − 0.066 (worst) to 1 (best), with higher values indicating better health [31].

The HINT-8 is a culturally tailored instrument developed for the Korean population [23], comprising eight dimensions: climbing stairs, pain, vitality, work, depression, memory, sleep, and happiness, rated on four levels of severity: no problems, mild, moderate, and severe. The HINT-8 utility index ranges from 0.132 (worst) to 1 (best). A value closer to 1 indicates a better health status, whereas a lower value signifies a poorer health status [24]. In the valuation study, Korean adults evaluated health states using both the visual analogue scale (VAS) and the standard gamble (SG) methods [24]. Detailed information on the model specification, validity evaluation, and utility scoring algorithm is provided [see Additional file 3].

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the demographic and clinical characteristics of respondents. Continuous variables were converted into categorical variables, and all variables are presented as frequencies and percentages. To assess potential non-response bias, we compared characteristics between completers and non-completers using Fisher’s exact test.

The EQ-5D-5 L response distribution was calculated for each level of the HINT-8 items, and kernel density estimation was applied to compare the distribution of the indices between the two instruments. To assess the ceiling effect, we calculated the proportion of respondents who reported the best possible health state (1.00) on both the HINT-8 and EQ-5D-5 L indices.

Bland–Altman plots were used to assess the agreement between at the utility score level the two instruments [32]. This plot visualizes agreement and highlights any bias (mean difference) between the two methods, including 95% limits of agreement, to indicate the expected range of differences [32]. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated at the utility score level to evaluate inter-instrument reliability between the utility scores derived from the HINT-8 and the EQ-5D-5 L [33], with 95% confidence intervals (95%CIs) derived using a two-way mixed effects model based on single measures and absolute agreement [33]. The ICC values were interpreted as follows: ICC < 0.50, poor agreement; 0.50 < ICC < 0.75, moderate agreement; 0.75 < ICC < 0.90, good agreement; and ICC > 0.90, excellent agreement.

Cronbach’s alpha coefficients were utilized to assess the internal consistency reliability; for items within each dimension, a value > 0.7 was acceptable [34].

To assess convergent validity, the correlations between each dimension and the index values of the HINT-8, EQ-5D-5 L, and EQ VAS were analyzed using Spearman’s rank coefficients (Spearman’s rho). We hypothesized that the correlation coefficients between similar dimensions would be higher than those between dissimilar dimensions. The interpretation of Spearman’s rho (rs) followed a commonly used rule of thumb: 0 < rs≤0.20, negligible; 0.21 ≤ rs≤0.40, weak, 0.41 ≤ rs≤0.60, moderate; 0.61 ≤ rs≤0.80, strong; and 0.81 ≤ rs≤1.00, very strong correlation [35].

Known-group validity was evaluated by analyzing differences in the HINT-8 and EQ-5D-5 L indices across predefined clinical subgroups, selected based on prior evidence of their associations with HRQoL in cancer populations using the Mann–Whitney U test for comparisons between two groups and the Kruskal–Wallis test for three or more groups. We hypothesized that individuals with more severe clinical characteristics such as a shorter duration since treatment [36], advanced-stage disease [37], recurrence [38], HPV-negative status [39], or being alive with disease (AWD) would report significantly lower HRQoL index compared to their counterparts. Evidence of known-group validity was considered acceptable when statistically significant differences in HRQoL scores were observed in the expected direction.

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software, version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

In terms of socioeconomic characteristics, 43.1% had a college degree or higher and 24.7% had a monthly household income below 2 million KRW (USD $1,439.86). More than half (52.8%) of respondents were employed or self-employed during the survey.

In total, 77.6% of participants had been diagnosed with cancer within the past 60 months and 33.1% had received treatment within the past 12 months; 74.2% of the patients were in the follow-up or survivorship phase. In addition, 82.3% had no history of recurrence, and 77.6% showed NED. HPV positivity was observed in 69.6% of respondents. Participants who completed both instruments (n = 299) and those with missing responses (n = 28) showed significant differences in household members, religion, duration since diagnosis, duration since treatment, disease status, AJCC stage, outcome status, and HPV status [see Additional file 4].

Known-groups validity

Both instruments showed significant differences in HRQoL indices based on key variables, including sex, educational level, employment status, duration since treatment, and disease status (Table 1). Duration since diagnosis, outcome status, and HPV status showed significant differences only in HINT-8. Both the HINT-8 and EQ-5D-5 L indices were significantly lower in patients whose duration since treatment was ≤ 12 months compared to those with > 12 months (HINT-8: 0.80 vs. 0.85, p < 0.001; EQ-5D-5 L: 0.82 vs. 0.88, p < 0.001). In terms of outcome status, Patients with NED reported significantly higher HRQoL compared to those AWD in the HINT-8 index (0.84 vs. 0.80, p = 0.01), while the EQ-5D-5 L index showed a similar trend that did not reach statistical significance (0.86 vs. 0.82, p = 0.13). The HINT-8 detected a statistically significant difference by HPV status, with HPV-positive individuals reporting higher HRQoL than HPV-negative individuals (0.83 vs. 0.81, p = 0.04).

HINT-8 and EQ-5D-5L agreement

Bland–Altman plots were used to assess agreement between the health utility index derived from the HINT-8 and the EQ-5D-5 L (Fig. 2). The two instruments demonstrated a similar trend 94.6% of the indices fell within the 95% limit of agreement (−0.14 to 0.19). The ICC analysis result was 0.80 (95% CI: 0.76–0.84, p < 0.001), indicating good agreement between the two measures. Our results suggest strong consistency between the indices generated by the two instruments.

Discussion

The correlation between the HINT-8 and EQ-5D-5 L indices was very strong, while the correlation with the EQ VAS was strong, confirming that the HINT-8 consistently captures HRQoL dimensions with established generic instruments. HINT-8 dimensions such as “Memory” and “Happiness” showed relatively weaker correlations with EQ-5D-5 L, indicating that HINT-8 may assess psychosocial aspects of HRQoL that EQ-5D-5 L does not fully capture. Generic preference-based instruments such as the EQ-5D-5 L primarily emphasize physical functioning and mobility [40]. This highlights the importance of the HINT-8 in capturing cognitive and emotional well-being, which is particularly relevant in long-term cancer survivorship.

The “Happiness” item in the HINT-8 is positively worded and thus conceptually different from the negatively framed items in the EQ-5D-5 L. A psychometric study of the HINT-8 [25] that used Exploratory Graph Analysis reported that “Happiness” was most strongly linked to “Depression” underscoring its contribution to the scale’s psychological dimension. Consistent with that finding, our study showed that “Happiness” had the highest item-level correlation with the EQ-5D-5 L “Anxiety/Depression” domain, confirming its relevance to mental health. However, confirmatory factor analysis from previous validation studies reported that the “Happiness” item had the lowest standardized factor loading among the eight items [25]. Nonetheless, the item was retained to improve content validity and capture emotional well-being, often overlooked in generic HRQoL instruments. In line with this, our results showed that “Happiness” had the lowest rate of “no problems” responses among all HINT-8 items.

Known-group validity was assessed by comparing HRQoL scores across subgroups with clinically distinct characteristics. As hypothesized, both instruments showed lower HRQoL indices in patients assessed within 12 months of treatment. Unlike previous studies reporting significantly lower HRQoL in patients with advanced-stage disease [37] or recurrence [38], our results did not show statistically significant differences in these subgroups, although mean scores were lower. This discrepancy may reflect the limited sensitivity of generic instruments, psychological adaptation over time, or insufficient sample sizes. Notably, patients with AWD and those who were HPV-negative reported significantly lower HRQoL when assessed with the HINT-8, while the EQ-5D-5 L did not detect statistically significant differences, suggesting that the HINT-8 may be more responsive in capturing HRQoL differences among clinical subgroups of patients with OPC.

While the EQ-5D-5 L is a multidimensional instrument, which limits the interpretability of Cronbach’s alpha, it is widely used in the literature and was included in this analysis for the purpose of comparison. The HINT-8, by contrast, has been psychometrically validated as a unidimensional instrument [24]. Our reliability analysis demonstrated high internal consistency for both instruments, and both provided reliable and consistent assessments of HRQoL, consistent with findings from previous studies [41].

Kernel density estimation illustrated that the EQ-5D-5 L index tended to be clustered at the high end, whereas HINT-8 exhibited a more even distribution. The HINT-8 may be better suited to detect subtle variations in HRQoL, particularly among patients with relatively better health status, allowing a more precise measurement of HRQoL changes over time.

A strong level of agreement was shown between the HINT-8 and EQ-5D-5 L, suggesting that the two instruments produced highly comparable HRQoL scores. However, we found a statistically significant difference in the mean utility index between the two instruments. Our findings are consistent with previous studies showing systematic differences between HRQoL instruments. A head-to-head comparison of the EQ-5D-5 L and AQoL-8D also demonstrated discrepancies in mean utility index between the two instruments [42]. This difference appears to be associated with the higher ceiling effect of the EQ-5D-5 L.

While the EQ-5D-5 L has been widely used, it has limitations in detecting subtle differences in HRQoL among individuals with a generally good health status, often exhibiting ceiling effects that can lead to a slight overestimation of HRQoL [43]. The EQ-5D-5 L was introduced to improve sensitivity and reduce ceiling effects compared with its predecessor, the EQ-5D-3 L [44]. However, ceiling effects persist, albeit to a lesser extent. For instance, in a population-based sample of adults, the ceiling effect for the EQ-5D-5 L was 31% [45]. A study of patients with prostate cancer revealed that physical well-being and prostate cancer-specific subscale scores were positively related to ceiling effects on the EQ-5D-5 L [43]. Furthermore, a comparative study between the EQ-5D-5 L and Short Form 6-Dimension version 2 (SF-6Dv2) suggested that the EQ-5D-5 L may overestimate health status, with its ceiling effect being 2.02 times that of the SF-6Dv2 (72.8% vs. 36.1%) [46]. These findings highlight the ongoing challenge of capturing subtle variations in HRQoL, particularly among individuals with better perceived health status, despite improvements in instrument design.

Our findings have several important implications for clinical practice and future research. First, the reduced ceiling effect and improved differentiation of HRQoL scores of HINT-8 suggest that it may be a valuable tool for assessing long-term survivorship outcomes in patients with OPC. Second, HINT-8 has potential applications in health economic evaluations, serving as a culturally adapted HRQoL measure that better reflects patient-reported outcomes in specific populations. Finally, future research should explore the responsiveness and broader applicability of HINT-8. Longitudinal studies and expanding validation studies in multinational settings will be crucial in determining the generalizability of our findings and in assessing HINT-8 beyond the Korean healthcare context.

This study also had some limitations. First, the cross-sectional design limited the ability to assess changes in HRQoL over time. Future longitudinal studies are needed to evaluate the responsiveness of HINT-8 in detecting meaningful clinical changes and test-retest reliability. Second, the study sample was drawn from tertiary hospitals, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Additional studies with more diverse populations are necessary. Third, this study primarily compared HINT-8 with the EQ-5D-5 L, a generic HRQoL instrument. Further research should explore how the HINT-8 compares with disease-specific HRQoL instruments. Fourth, this study may be subject to non-response bias. Patients with more severe clinical conditions were less likely to complete the survey. As a result, individuals in poorer physical health may have been underrepresented in the final sample, potentially limiting the generalizability of the HRQoL results. Finally, while the HINT-8 was developed for the Korean population, its cultural adaptability to other populations remains unclear, and future research should validate HINT-8 in multinational settings.

Conclusions

The HINT-8 demonstrated acceptable validity and reliability in assessing health-related quality of life among patients with oropharyngeal cancer in South Korea, showing strong agreement with the EQ-5D-5 L. The HINT-8 also showed advantages in reducing ceiling effects. However, given the relatively small sample size and the cross-sectional nature of the study, further longitudinal research is warranted to confirm the generalizability of the HINT-8 in the South Korean context.

Clinicopathologic Features and Outcomes of Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection for Foveolar-Type Adenocarcinoma of the Stomach
Authors: Minjee Kim, Tae-Se Kim, Byung-Hoon Min, Yang Won Min, Hyuk Lee, Jun Haeng Lee, Poong-Lyul Rhee, Jae J. Kim, Kyoung-Mee Kim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeFoveolar-type adenocarcinoma of the stomach is a rare variant of gastric cancer. The clinicopathological features and outcomes of endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma remain unclear.Materials and MethodsThis study included 1,161 patients who underwent ESD for single early gastric cancers (EGCs) (78 foveolar-type adenocarcinomas and 1,083 well-differentiated [WD] adenocarcinomas). The clinicopathological features and short- and long-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas were reviewed and compared with those for WD EGCs.ResultsGastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas were larger and more likely to exhibit an elevated macroscopic appearance than WD EGCs. Foveolar-type adenocarcinomas exhibited higher rates of lymphatic invasion, histological heterogeneity, and lateral margin involvement than WD EGCs. The en bloc R0 and curative resection rates of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma were 85.9% and 76.9%, respectively. Both foveolar-type adenocarcinoma rates were significantly lower than those of WD EGCs (95.8% and 91.3%, respectively). Lateral margin involvement accounted for 55.6% of the non-curative resection cases of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma. Among patients who underwent curative ESD for foveolar-type adenocarcinoma, no recurrence occurred during the median 62.3 months of follow-up. No lymph node metastases were detected in patients with foveolar-type adenocarcinoma who underwent additional surgery following ESD. The overall and disease-specific survival rates of patients with foveolar-type adenocarcinoma were comparable to those of patients with WD EGC.ConclusionsGastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas have distinct clinicopathological features among WD EGCs. Given favorable long-term outcomes after curative resection, ESD can be indicated for early gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas.

INTRODUCTION

Foveolar-type adenocarcinoma of the stomach is a rare variant of gastric cancer characterized by a tumor with a well-differentiated (WD) glandular architecture and low-grade nuclear atypia that mimics regenerative gastric foveolar (surface) epithelial cells [123]. According to Japanese diagnostic criteria, sporadic gastric foveolar-type neoplasms can be classified into 2 subtypes: conventional and raspberry-like [24567]. The conventional type is markedly rare and typically observed as a whitish, flat-elevated lesion, whereas the raspberry-like type is a recently recognized disease entity characterized by a small reddish protrusion with a fine granular surface.

Because gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas are frequently detected at early stages, they are potential candidates for endoscopic resection. However, these adenocarcinomas frequently present as laterally spreading lesions and can harbor poorly cohesive or signet ring cell carcinoma components. These features may cause difficulties in the endoscopic estimation of tumor boundaries and increase the risk of lymph node metastasis [1238]. Few studies have reported the biological behaviors or short- and long-term outcomes of endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas, which are essential to determine whether endoscopic resection can be used to treat these lesions.

In this study, we aimed to elucidate the clinicopathological features and short- and long-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients

From January 2009 to December 2016, 1,208 patients underwent their first ESD for single foveolar-type or WD early gastric cancer (EGC) at the Samsung Medical Center. Among them, 13 patients undergoing ESD for EGC in the remnant stomach and 34 with adenocarcinoma of the fundic gland type, serrated adenocarcinoma, or intestinal-type adenocarcinoma with anastomosing glands (extremely WD intestinal-type adenocarcinoma) were excluded from the study population [9]. Finally, 78 patients with foveolar-type EGC and 1,083 patients with WD EGC were included in the analysis. The clinicopathological features and short- and long-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type EGC were compared with those for WD EGCs. The ESD procedures and histopathological evaluations of ESD specimens at our institution have been described in detail elsewhere [1011]. Foveolar-type adenocarcinoma was diagnosed when the tumor was mostly composed of tubular or villous epithelial fronds lined with cuboidal to tall columnar cells, mimicking the gastric foveolar epithelium. In the stroma, obvious tumor cell invasion was accompanied by single cells or tumorous cell clusters (Fig. 1) [123]. In cases with diagnostic difficulties, MUC5AC immunohistochemistry was performed, and experienced gastric pathologists (Kyoung-Mee Kim and Ryoji Kushima) agreed with the pathological diagnosis. Clinicopathological data and outcomes following ESD were obtained through a retrospective review of medical records from the intranet resources of the Samsung Medical Center.

Ethical approval and consent statement

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Samsung Medical Center (IRB No. 2023-05-155). The requirement for written informed consent was waived because this was a retrospective study, and an anonymized database was used.

Definitions

En bloc resection was defined as the resection of the tumor in one piece with no endoscopically residual tumor. R0 resection was defined as tumor resection without histological evidence of cancer cells on the lateral and vertical margins. Curative resection (eCuraA or eCuraB) was defined when the following criteria were fulfilled: foveolar-type or WD EGC, en bloc resection, negative lateral resection margins, negative vertical resection margins, and no lymphovascular invasion: (1) tumor size ≤2 cm, mucosal cancer, no ulcer in the tumor; (2) tumor size >2 cm, mucosal cancer, no ulcer in the tumor; (3) tumor size ≤3 cm, mucosal cancer, ulcer in the tumor; or (4) tumor size ≤3 cm, SM1 cancer (submucosal invasion depth <500 µm from the muscularis mucosa layer) [12].

Histological heterogeneity was defined when poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma or poorly cohesive carcinoma components coexisted as a minor portion of an ESD specimen (less than 50% of the tumor area), in accordance with Korean and Japanese guidelines [121314].

Post-ESD bleeding was defined when one of the following criteria was met within 4 weeks after ESD: (1) presence of clinical signs of bleeding (hematemesis, melena, or hematochezia) that required endoscopic, radiological, or surgical interventions for bleeding from the ESD site; or (2) a decrease of more than 2 g/dL in blood hemoglobin due to ESD site bleeding confirmed by endoscopy. Perforation included both Frank perforation and microperforation following ESD.

Follow-up after ESD

Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with biopsy was performed 2 months after ESD to confirm healing of the artificial ulcer and exclude the presence of any residual tumors. Surveillance for recurrence was carried out every 6 months using EGD with a biopsy and abdominal computed tomography for 3 years, and then annually from the fourth to fifth years following ESD.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were analyzed using the χ2 test and Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were analyzed using the Student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney U test. The overall survival time was calculated from the date of ESD to the date of all-cause death or the cut-off date (December 31st, 2021). Disease-specific survival time was calculated from the date of ESD to the date of gastric cancer death or the cut-off date. The Kaplan-Meier curves for overall and disease-specific survival were plotted, and the difference between foveolar-type EGC and WD EGC was tested using a log-rank test. P-values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 28.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

Clinicopathologic features of gastric foveolar-type vs. WD adenocarcinoma

Foveolar-type adenocarcinomas accounted for 6.7% (78/1,161) of the study population. Table 1 summarizes the clinicopathological features of patients with gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma compared to those of patients with WD EGC. Foveolar-type adenocarcinomas were more likely to be located in the lower or middle third of the stomach, and exhibited an elevated macroscopic appearance compared with WD EGCs. These adenocarcinomas were also significantly larger and exhibited higher rates of lymphatic invasion and histological heterogeneity than WD EGCs. The lateral margin involvement rate in foveolar-type adenocarcinomas was 12.8% (Figs. 2 and 3), which was significantly higher than that in WD EGCs (2.3%). Among the 78 foveolar-type adenocarcinomas, only one displayed a raspberry-like appearance. Gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas were diagnosed before ESD based on biopsy specimens in only 3.8% of the enrolled patients (3/78). Pre-ESD biopsy diagnoses in the remaining patients included moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma (30/78), WD adenocarcinoma (18/78), high-grade dysplasia (19/78), low-grade dysplasia (4/78), and atypical glands (4/78). No patients with foveolar-type adenocarcinoma had a medical history of familial adenomatous polyposis or gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach. Helicobacter pylori infection was detected in 49.3% (36/73) of the patients with foveolar-type adenocarcinoma.

Short-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma

Table 2 summarizes the short-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma and WD EGC. The R0 and en bloc R0 resection rates for foveolar-type adenocarcinoma were 87.2% and 85.9%, respectively, being significantly lower than those for WD EGC. The curative resection rate (eCuraA or eCuraB) of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma was also significantly lower than that of WD EGC (76.9% vs. 91.3%). The most common reason for non-curative ESD of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma was lateral margin involvement (55.6%), followed by deep submucosal invasion (33.3%) and lymphatic invasion (33.3%).

Long-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma

Fig. 4 shows the follow-up outcomes of ESD for foveolar-type gastric adenocarcinoma. Among the 60 patients who underwent curative ESD, no local, metachronous, or extragastric recurrences were observed during a median 62.3 months of follow-up (range, 55.3–69.5 months).

Flowchart for the outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma.

ESD = endoscopic submucosal dissection; OP = operation; RT = residual tumor; LN = lymph node.

Among the 18 patients who underwent non-curative ESD, 8 underwent additional gastrectomy after ESD. Except for one patient with unknown pathology results (who had undergone surgery at another hospital), none of the remaining 7 patients showed lymph node metastases in surgical specimens. Lateral margin involvement and piecemeal resection were the only factors for non-curative resection in 6 patients, 3 of whom underwent additional ESD. Among these 3 patients, 2 (66.7%) had residual tumors in the resected specimens. No patient experienced recurrence after additional ESD during the follow-up period.

The Kaplan-Meier curves for overall and disease-specific survival in patients who underwent curative ESD are shown in Fig. 5. The median follow-up durations were 71.9 months, 62.3 months, and 72.1 months for all patients, those with foveolar-type adenocarcinoma, and those with WD adenocarcinoma, respectively. The estimated 5-year overall rates were 87.3% and 93.6% for patients with foveolar-type and WD adenocarcinomas, respectively (Fig. 5A). The estimated probabilities of 5-year disease-specific survival were 100% and 99.8% for patients with foveolar-type and WD adenocarcinomas, respectively (Fig. 5B). Both overall and disease-specific survival rates were comparable between the 2 groups.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we investigated the clinicopathological features and short- and long-term outcomes of ESD for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas. Foveolar-type adenocarcinomas were found to be significantly larger and exhibit higher rates of lymphatic invasion, histological heterogeneity, and lateral margin involvement compared to WD EGC. With these aggressive features, the curative resection (eCuraA or eCuraB) rate of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma is <80%, which is significantly lower than that of WD EGC. However, long-term outcomes for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinoma following ESD are favorable once curative resection is achieved. Among patients who underwent curative ESD for foveolar-type adenocarcinoma, no recurrence occurred after ESD, and both the overall and disease-specific survival rates were comparable to those of patients with WD EGC. Given these results, ESD represents an effective treatment of gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas confined to the mucosa or with minute submucosal invasion. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate ESD outcomes for foveolar-type gastric adenocarcinoma.

Sporadic gastric foveolar-type neoplasms can be classified into 2 subtypes of appearance based on differences in endoscopic and genetic features: conventional and raspberry-like [245671516]. Endoscopically, the conventional type is typically observed as a laterally spreading, whitish, and flat-elevated lesion. Its raspberry-like appearance is a recently recognized disease in Japan, as recent Japanese studies have reported that raspberry-like lesions occur in H. pylori-naïve patients and constitute the majority of sporadic foveolar-type gastric neoplasms. Endoscopically, the raspberry-like appearance was observed as a small reddish polyp with a fine granular surface and a mean size of approximately 3.0 mm. Therefore, differentiating this type of lesion from small hyperplastic polyps on the basis of endoscopic findings is challenging [71718]. A recent Japanese study by Sugai et al. [6] assessed the clinicopathological characteristics of 42 sporadic foveolar-type gastric neoplasms, failing to observe a raspberry-like appearance in any of the lesions. Sugai et al. [6] suggested that in contrast to raspberry-like lesions occurring in H. pylori-naïve patients, conventional lesions might be associated with H. pylori infection, given that atrophic changes, intestinal metaplasia, and CDX2 expression are frequently found in this type of lesion. In the present study, only one lesion displayed an endoscopic raspberry-like appearance. This low proportion of raspberry-like lesions in Korea can be attributed to their gross morphology mimicking small hyperplastic polyps, which are usually followed up without resection, and a lack of knowledge pertaining to this novel disease entity among Korean endoscopists and pathologists.

In this study, the most common cause of noncurative ESD of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma was lateral margin involvement. Gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas frequently present as laterally spreading lesions, and can harbor poorly cohesive or signet ring cell carcinoma components [123]. The results of our study are consistent with these characteristics. These features can complicate the endoscopic estimation of tumor boundaries. To overcome this challenge in tumor margin delineation, pre-ESD biopsy diagnosis of foveolar-type adenocarcinoma is critical. Such diagnoses can enable endoscopists to perform mapping biopsies prior to ESD, or wide resection during ESD, to improve the curative resection rate. However, gastric foveolar-type neoplasms are difficult to diagnose using endoscopic forceps biopsy because tumor glands may be overlooked owing to their characteristic low-grade nuclear atypia [23]. In the present study, only 3.8% of the gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas were diagnosed before ESD based on biopsy specimens. Recognition of the characteristic endoscopic and histological features of gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas by endoscopists and pathologists may facilitate the diagnosis of this rare tumor in the future.

This study had some limitations. First, selection bias cannot be excluded, given that the study was conducted retrospectively at a single tertiary center. Consecutive patients were enrolled to minimize potential bias. Second, as the number of patients with foveolar-type adenocarcinoma enrolled in this study was limited, further large-scale studies are required to confirm the favorable long-term outcomes of curative ESD noted in the present study.

In conclusion, gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas have clinicopathological features that are distinct from those of WD EGCs. Given the favorable long-term outcomes after curative resection of gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas, ESD may be an effective treatment for gastric foveolar-type adenocarcinomas confined to the mucosa or with minute submucosal invasion. To achieve better treatment outcomes, recognition and awareness of this rare variant of gastric cancer by endoscopists and pathologists is critical.

Multinomial risk modeling of osteopenia and osteoporosis in Iraqi women: comparative contributions of osteoprotegerin and vitamin D3
Authors: Manal Jasim Abbood, Amer H Abdullah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVitamin D status is frequently low among Iraqi women, which may limit its discriminatory value for osteopenia (ON) and osteoporosis (OP). We evaluated the vitamin D axis (VD3, DBP, and VDR), osteoprotegerin (OPG), and endocrine regulators (estradiol [E2] and parathyroid hormone [PTH]) in relation to ON/OP risk before and after menopause.Materials and MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, 120 women were classified as control, ON, or OP (n = 40 each) based on DEXA-derived T-scores. Serum VD3, DBP, VDR, OPG, E2, and PTH were measured by ELISA. Group comparisons were conducted using ANOVA with post hoc testing, and adjusted odds ratios (OR) for ON and OP were estimated using multinomial logistic regression, controlling for age and BMI.ResultsIn premenopausal women, E2 and OPG were significantly lower in ON and OP than in controls (E2: p < 0.001; OPG: p = 0.001), whereas VD3, VDR, and DBP did not differ significantly. In postmenopausal women, OPG was markedly lower in OP than in controls and ON (p < 0.001), while VDR and DBP remained non-significant; VD3 showed a decreasing trend in OP (p = 0.042). In adjusted multinomial regression, OPG was strongly protective against OP (OR 0.275; 95% CI 0.172–0.441; p < 0.001) and against ON (OR 0.724; 95% CI 0.538–0.973; p = 0.033). E2 was protective against OP (OR 0.491; 95% CI 0.356–0.676; p < 0.001) and against ON (OR 0.748; 95% CI 0.608–0.920; p = 0.006). VD3 showed a modest protective association with OP (OR 0.948; 95% CI 0.901–0.998; p = 0.040) but not with ON, while PTH was a slight risk factor for OP (OR 1.027; 95% CI 1.004–1.051; p = 0.021).ConclusionIn Iraqi women, particularly after menopause, OPG (and E2) showed a stronger and more consistent association with OP/ON risk than VD3, whereas DBP and VDR were not informative in this cohort. These findings support OPG as a promising biomarker for osteoporosis risk stratification in populations with prevalent vitamin D insufficiency.Keywords: Risk assessment, Osteoporosis, Osteopenia, Vitamin D3, Bone health

Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is a widespread condition characterized by the interplay of complex molecular pathways that reduce bone mass and strength, leading to microarchitectural loss of bone.1 Reduced bone mineral density (BMD) is a significant outcome linked to weak, fragile, and fractured bones2. OP and its severe consequences, including fractures and chronic pain, are prevalent in both genders; however, women exhibit greater susceptibility, representing 70–80 percent of all trauma cases, such as hip, spine, and wrist fractures3. The prevalence escalates in postmenopausal women due to decreased oestrogen levels, leading to an increased loss of bone mass.4 Osteopenia (ON) was initially defined as a qualitative term denoting bone with reduced radiographic density. Since 1994, a BMD T-score between −1.0 and −2.5 has been quantitatively defined5 (see Table 1).

The prevalence of OP is greater in women (80%) compared to men (20%). Women have lower overall bone mass than men; peak bone mass is reached earlier in women at age 25, and 98% of skeletal mass is developed by age 20, whereas men reach peak levels at age 30.6 Statistics from the World Health Organization (WHO) indicate that 30 percent of postmenopausal women experience osteoporosis (OP).7 Over 60% of Caucasian women aged 64 and older exhibit osteopenia.5 OP affects more than 22 million females and 5.5 million males, making it a significant global health burden. People of both sexes who have osteoporosis, which is more prevalent in elderly populations and women. OP causes significant economic and social challenges, including extra costs from related illnesses like depression and chronic pain, as well as poor health.8 Clinically, the most consequential outcomes are fragility fractures, low-energy fractures that commonly involve the hip, spine, wrist, and proximal humerus. These events are associated with substantial disability and excess mortality, particularly after hip and vertebral fractures.9

Vitamin D3 (VD3) is significantly associated with bone health, primarily through its roles in calcium and phosphorus homeostasis and skeletal muscle health. It is essential for muscle contraction, nerve conduction, bone mineralization, and overall cellular function throughout the body.10 The deficiency of VD3 causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults,11 and has been known as a risk factor for ON and OP.12, 13 VD3 binds to a transport protein (DBP) during its circulation. When it interacts with a target cell, VD3 diffuses across the cellular membrane and binds to a nuclear receptor (VDR) to initiate the signal response.14 Osteoprotegerin (OPG) is a critical secreted protein involved in bone turnover, functioning as a decoy receptor for the Receptor Activator of Nuclear Factor-κB ligand (RANKL) in osteoclasts, thereby limiting their differentiation. Additionally, OPG has multiple ligands that confer distinct biological functions.15 OPG production is influenced by various inflammatory mediators and steroid hormones, including sex hormones.16

From a therapeutic perspective, osteoporosis management includes antiresorptive agents such as bisphosphonates and denosumab; anabolic therapies such as teriparatide/abaloparatide and romosozumab for very high fracture risk; and supportive strategies, including adequate calcium/vitamin D intake, fall prevention, and physical activity. These approaches are reflected in major contemporary clinical guidance and are primarily aimed at reducing fragility fractures and improving patient outcomes.17 Emerging discovery directions include targeted protein degradation strategies (e.g., molecular glue degraders) and zebrafish-based in vivo screening and validation models, in which experimental compounds, including vitamin D3–based combinations and small molecules/natural products with osteoprotective signals (e.g., quercetin/rutin, acacetin, and furan-based chalcones), have been mechanistically explored.18, 19, 20, 21

Despite the well-established role of vitamin D in skeletal health, vitamin D deficiency is highly prevalent among women in Iraq and the region, which may limit the discriminatory value of serum VD3 alone in differentiating osteopenia from osteoporosis risk at the population level.22 In addition, the clinical utility of circulating OPG-related measures remains heterogeneous across studies, underscoring the need for population-specific, risk-oriented investigations.23 Therefore, the novelty of the present work lies in the simultaneous evaluation of the VD3–DBP–VDR axis alongside OPG, and in quantifying their associations with osteopenia and osteoporosis in Iraqi women stratified by menopausal status using adjusted multinomial risk modeling.

Accordingly, the objectives of this study were to: (i) compare serum levels of VD3, DBP, VDR, OPG, estradiol, and PTH among pre- and postmenopausal controls, osteopenic, and osteoporotic women; (ii) assess relationships among these biomarkers within the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups; and (iii) estimate the adjusted risk contribution of these parameters to osteopenia and osteoporosis using multinomial logistic regression adjusted for age and BMI.

Materials and methods

Subjects

The study involved the inclusion of 120 females for testing the study hypotheses, who were divided into three groups of 40 females as follows:

i)The control group contained 40 females aged 28–75 years, with no history of ON, OP, or other related bone disorders. 17 females were pre-menopausal, while 23 were post-menopausal.

The control group contained 40 females aged 28–75 years, with no history of ON, OP, or other related bone disorders. 17 females were pre-menopausal, while 23 were post-menopausal.

ii)The ON group contained 40 females aged 24–69 years; 12 females were at pre-menopausal age, while 28 females were at post-menopausal age.

The ON group contained 40 females aged 24–69 years; 12 females were at pre-menopausal age, while 28 females were at post-menopausal age.

iii)The OP group contained 40 females aged 28–68 years; 7 females were at pre-menopausal age, while 33 females were at post-menopausal age.

The OP group contained 40 females aged 28–68 years; 7 females were at pre-menopausal age, while 33 females were at post-menopausal age.

Postmenopausal status was defined as the absence of menses for ≥12 consecutive months. Participants were excluded if they had a history of metabolic bone disease other than osteopenia/osteoporosis, chronic kidney or liver disease, malabsorption disorders, malignancy, endocrine disorders affecting bone metabolism, or were receiving medications known to influence bone or vitamin D metabolism (e.g., glucocorticoids, bisphosphonates, calcitonin, anticonvulsants, pharmacological-dose vitamin D, or hormone replacement therapy) within the preceding months. Body weight and height were measured with standard calibrated instruments, and BMI was calculated as weight (kg)/height (m2).24

All participants were approved to participate in this study by signing a consent form. Moreover, ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Ethics Committee at the College of Science, Mustansiriyah University (Ref. BCSMU/0125/00013C, 1 Jan 2025). All subjects were collected from Al-Wasiti Hospital, Baghdad, within one week, starting on 5 Jan 2025.

Bone analyses

In this study, the samples were distributed into their groups (control, OP, and ON) according to their bone mineral content (BMC), BMD, and T-score values. These values were obtained using a DEXA bone analyzer (DMS STRATOS, France), and subjects were assigned to groups based on T-score: ≥ −1.0 for control, −1.0 to −2.5 for ON, and ≤ −2.5 for OP.

Serum analyses

The blood of each participant was collected and centrifuged (1500 g for 10 min) to obtain serum, which was analyzed for VD3, parathyroid hormone (PTH), estradiol (E2), OPG, VDR, and DBP using ELISA kits (Sunlong, China), measured in a microplate reader (Human, Germany). Serum samples were aliquoted and stored at −80°C until analysis (single freeze). All ELISA measurements were performed in duplicate according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Sunlong, China), and assay performance characteristics were those reported by the manufacturer. Furthermore, the activities of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP), as well as the levels of urea, creatinine, and total serum bilirubin (TSB), were analyzed spectrophotometrically using commercial kits (Linear, Spain).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses compared mean values among the control, ON, and OP groups using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for pairwise comparisons. Within the ON and OP groups, bivariate associations were evaluated using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r). The contribution of study parameters to ON and OP was examined using multinomial logistic regression to obtain adjusted odds ratios (OR), controlling for age and BMI. Before applying parametric tests, distributional and homogeneity assumptions were assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test for normality and Levene’s test for equality of variances; when violations were detected, appropriate data transformations or nonparametric alternatives were considered. All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0). For the multinomial model, the diagnostic category was the dependent outcome variable, with the control group as the reference category; biomarkers were entered as continuous predictors, and ORs were adjusted for age and BMI as covariates. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Iraqi women share the high prevalence of ON or OP with the rest of the women of the globe, especially at post-menopause. In this study, the role of VD3 was minor, as it was significantly lower in the post-menopausal women in the OP group (Table 4). The Food and Nutrition Board (FNB) expert committee at the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM) has reported a contradiction in the definition of VD3 deficiency. Nevertheless, they classified the levels of 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25-(OH)VD] according to the adequate for bone health into VD3 deficiency at <12 ng/mL, insufficiency at 12–<20 ng/mL, and sufficient VD levels at 20 >ng/mL in healthy people.25 Moreover, studies indicate that a VD3 level ≥30 ng/mL is sufficient for its physiological role.26, 27 Accordingly, all our study subjects showed VD3 insufficiency, except for the pre-menopausal ON group, which exhibited sufficient levels (Table 3). OP and other metabolic disorders, including vitamin D insufficiency and hyperparathyroidism, are linked to ON. It has been used to define an intermediate risk category with somewhat ambiguous boundaries, compared with prehypertension, impaired fasting glucose, and borderline elevated cholesterol.28 VD3 is vital for the development and maintenance of healthy bones from birth to death, as well as for maintaining serum calcium and phosphate concentrations. Many patients with osteoporosis have a vitamin D3 deficiency. In addition to causing and exacerbating osteopenia and osteoporosis in both men and women, a VD3 deficit also results in muscular weakness and a skeletal mineralization defect, both of which raise the risk of fracture.29, 30, 31 A study conducted on post-menopausal Chinese women to predict the risk factors for OP indicated that low VD3 levels are considered an independent risk factor for OP in these subjects.32 Reis et al. reported improvements in ALP activity, 25(OH)VD levels, and PTH, and a reduction in fracture risk with adequate supplementation with VD3 in post-menopausal non-OP women. Nevertheless, the BMC did not improve.33 The deficiency of VD3 has been widely reported in both healthy and unhealthy individuals,27, 34, 35 which makes its utilization as a predictor for ON or OP complicated.

OPG appeared as a better candidate for the prognosis of OP and ON in pre- and post-menopausal women. Our multivariate analysis showed that OPG was negatively correlated with OP (OR 0.275) and ON (OR 0.724), indicating that maintaining OPG at physiological levels can significantly reduce the risks of OP and ON (Table 7). OPG reduces bone resorption, is a potent inhibitor of osteoclast activation, and contributes to the mediation of bone mineral density.36 According to Amer et al., variations in blood levels of RANKL and OPG may buffer against increased bone loss in postmenopausal women.37 According to a meta-analysis by Chi et al., RANKL and OPG were significant modulators of bone resorption and bone formation, respectively, in bone turnover. While osteoporosis was not associated with either OPG or RANKL levels in serum, it was related to the OPG/RANKL ratio.23 OPG may be a promising biomarker candidate for osteoporosis risk stratification in our cohort; however, given the heterogeneous evidence across studies, further validation—ideally including RANKL and the OPG/RANKL ratio—is warranted.23 OPG secretion is influenced by E2 levels, which stimulate OPG synthesis in osteoblasts.38, 39 In pre-menopausal women, although there was no correlation between OPG and E2, both of which were reduced significantly in the OP and ON groups. Thus, OPG stimulation by reduced E2 levels may provide a protective mechanism against OP and ON in pre- and postmenopausal women by reducing bone resorption. From a biological standpoint, the observed reduction in OPG in osteoporotic groups is plausible because OPG is a decoy receptor that inhibits RANKL-mediated osteoclast activation, and estrogen supports antiresorptive signaling, including OPG-related pathways. Thus, concomitant reductions in E2 and OPG are directionally consistent with increased risk of bone resorption.40, 41

The present study adds population-specific evidence from Iraqi women by jointly evaluating the VD3 axis (25(OH)D, DBP, and VDR) and OPG, along with key endocrine regulators (E2 and PTH), with stratification by menopausal status. In a context where vitamin D insufficiency is highly prevalent among Iraqi women and adequate UVB exposure may be limited by conservative clothing practices, the discriminatory utility of 25(OH)D alone may be attenuated.42 Our findings suggest that lower circulating OPG is more consistently associated with osteoporosis (and, to a lesser extent, osteopenia) than VD3 in this cohort, as supported by adjusted multinomial risk modeling (Table 7) and group comparisons (Table 3, Table 4). This contributes to the ongoing biomarker debate by providing data from a region that is underrepresented in biomarker-driven risk-stratification studies.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although we strengthened the rigor of group comparisons by adopting Tukey’s HSD for post hoc testing and reporting effect sizes (η2) to quantify the magnitude of differences, the study remains observational and cross-sectional; therefore, the reported associations cannot establish causality, and residual confounding cannot be entirely excluded. Second, significant environmental and lifestyle determinants of vitamin D status and bone health—such as ultraviolet (UVB) exposure, seasonality, dietary calcium/vitamin D intake, physical activity, and supplement use—were not systematically quantified and therefore could not be incorporated as covariates. In our cohort, most women followed conservative dress practices that cover most of the skin, which is common in Iraq and can substantially limit adequate UVB exposure and cutaneous vitamin D synthesis; this may have contributed to the broadly low 25(OH)D profile observed across groups and potentially reduced the discriminatory value of vitamin D alone.43 Third, recruitment from a single center within a restricted sampling frame and the inclusion of only women may limit external validity; consequently, generalization to other Iraqi regions, rural settings, or male populations should be made with caution. Future multicenter studies with larger, more representative samples and standardized assessment of environmental/lifestyle exposures are warranted to validate these findings and enhance broader applicability.

Conclusions

The results of this study indicate that vitamin D levels in women with both osteoporosis and osteopenia were not statistically different from those in healthy controls, except in women with postmenopausal osteoporosis, where a significant decrease was observed. Regarding the transport protein and vitamin D receptor, no statistically significant differences were observed between the studied groups. In contrast, OPG levels were significantly lower in patients than in healthy controls, and this decrease was closely associated with osteoporosis and, to a lesser extent, with osteopenia. These findings reinforce the hypothesis that OPG may be a more accurate biomarker than vitamin D for assessing the risk of bone density disorders, especially osteoporosis. Future work should validate these findings in multicenter Iraqi cohorts and incorporate additional remodeling markers (including RANKL and the OPG/RANKL ratio) within longitudinal designs to assess predictive performance for incident fragility fractures. Parallel advances in experimental screening platforms, including zebrafish models and emerging drug-discovery modalities, may help identify new candidates targeting bone metabolic pathways.

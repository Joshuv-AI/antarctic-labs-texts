Homozygous variant in JARID2 causes female infertility characterized by compromised blastulation efficiency
Authors: Jiaqi Sun, Huiling Hu, Fei Meng, Pingyuan Xie, Fei Gong, Ge Lin, Jing Dai, Wei Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImpaired embryonic developmental competence is a critical determinant of assisted reproductive technology (ART) failure, yet current genetic diagnostics primarily address complete early embryonic arrest, leaving partial developmental defects unexplained, such as compromised blastulation efficiency (CBE). Jumonji and AT-rich interaction domain containing 2 (JARID2), encoding a chromatin-modifying factor essential for histone methylation regulation, emerges as a novel candidate in this context.ResultsWe identified a homozygous JARID2 missense variant (c.899G > A, p.Arg300Gln) in a female patient exhibiting CBE under autosomal recessive inheritance. Functional studies in transiently transfected HeLa cells demonstrated preserved protein abundance and subcellular localization but significantly attenuated ERK1/2 pathway activation. Microinjection of mutant mRNA into mouse zygotes recapitulated the CBE phenotype, accompanied by diminished histone H3K27me3 levels at the 2-cell stage. Comparative transcriptomic profiling revealed conserved transcriptional alterations in both patient-derived oocytes and murine embryos, marked by downregulation of genes critical for oogenesis, maternal-to-zygotic transition, and preimplantation development.ConclusionsThese findings establish JARID2 as a maternal-effect gene governing early embryogenesis through epigenetic and ERK1/2-mediated regulatory axes, expanding the genetic diagnostic framework for ART failures and providing mechanistic insights into previously unexplained developmental defects.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01783-3.Keywords: JARID2 variant, Early embryonic development, Histone methylation, Female infertility

Introduction

The global burden of female infertility has escalated significantly, with approximately 60 million additional cases reported worldwide [1, 2]. Over the past four decades, assisted reproductive technology (ART) has emerged as a pivotal intervention for infertility treatment, where the developmental competence of embryos to reach blastocyst stage remains critical for successful outcomes [3]. Recent investigations have increasingly highlighted the crucial role of maternal genetic abnormalities in recurrent defects of early embryonic developmental potential [4, 5].

Leveraging advances in high-throughput sequencing technologies, researchers have systematically explored genetic determinants of early embryonic arrest (EEA) through five primary molecular pathways: (1) spindle assembly components (e.g., TUBB8, TUBA1C) [6, 7]; (2) subcortical maternal complex (SCMC) constituents (e.g., PADI6, TLE6) [8, 9]; (3) maternal mRNA degradation regulators (e.g., BTG4, MOS) [10, 11]; (4) cell cycle progression modulators (e.g., CHEK1, CDC20) [12, 13]; and (5) protein synthesis/transport machinery (e.g., PATL2, KPNA7) [14, 15]. However, the currently identified 21 pathogenic genes account for only a limited proportion of clinical cases, suggesting substantial genetic etiologies remain undiscovered.

Epigenetic regulation, particularly histone methylation, serves as a critical mechanism governing gene expression during early embryonic development [16]. Jumonji and AT-rich interaction domain containing 2 (JARID2), a chromatin-modifying factor, regulates transcriptional programs through chromatin structural modulation [17, 18]. While Jarid2 knockdown has been shown to impair bovine embryonic development through reduced cleavage and blastocyst rates, its role in human embryogenesis remains unexplored [19].

This study characterizes a novel JARID2 homozygous missense variant (c.899G > A, p.Arg300Gln) identified in a patient demonstrating compromised blastocyst formation efficiency (CBE) - a phenotype distinct from complete EEA, marked by severely reduced (< 10%) blastocyst development. Our mechanistic investigation revealed that this variant disrupts H3K27me3 maintenance through aberrant attenuation of ERK1/2 activation, ultimately causing potential zygotic genome activation (ZGA) failure in murine embryos. Complementary transcriptomic analysis of patient-derived germinal vesicle (GV) oocytes provided additional insights into the molecular pathophysiology. These findings expand the genetic diagnostic spectrum for ART failures and underscoring the importance of maternal genetic contributions to impaired blastocyst formation.

Methods

Participants

We recruited a patient with recurrent ART failure (characterized by compromised blastulation efficiency) and fertile controls from the Reproductive and Genetic Hospital of CITIC-XIANGYA. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study was approved by the hospital’s Ethics Committee (LL-SC-2024-012).

Whole exome sequencing and the variant analysis

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (Qiagen, 51106). Exome libraries were prepared using IDT xGen Exome and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Variant prioritization followed established criteria [11]: (1) rare variant (MAF < 0.1%); (2) protein-altering variants (nonsynonymous/splice-site /indels); (3) genes with oocyte-specific or oocyte-high expression patterns or prior infertility associations. The JARID2 variant was confirmed by Sanger sequencing using flanking primers.

CNV origin analysis

CNV origin analysis was performed per established protocols using informative SNPs with paternal AA/maternal BB (AABB) or paternal BB/maternal AA (BBAA) patterns [20]. Embryonic copy number alterations were determined through Mean B allele frequency (BAF) deviations and genotype frequencies: Deletions were called when AABB-AA-ratio > 0.8 and BBAA-BB-ratio > 0.8 (indicating maternal deletion); Duplications required maternal duplication criteria (AABB-AB- BAF > 0.5, BBAA-AB-BAF < 0.5, and AABB-AB-BAF-z-score > 3 or BBAA-AB-BAF-z-score > 3) or inverted BAF patterns for paternal duplication.

Construction of expression plasmids and transfection

Wild-type JARID2 coding DNA sequence was cloned into HA-tagged vector (Supplementary Figure S1), with the p.Arg300Gln variant introduced via site-directed mutagenesis by Express II Fast Mutagenesis Kit V2 (Vazyme, C214-02). HeLa cells were maintained in DMEM/high glucose (HyClone, SH3002201) with 10% FBS (Gibco, A5669701) at 37 °C/5% CO2. Transient transfection was performed at 70–80% confluency using HighGene Plus Transfection Reagent (ABclonal, RM09014P). Post-transfection (24–48 h), cells were processed for immunofluorescence or harvested for immunoblotting.

Western blotting

Transfected HeLa cells were lysed in RIPA buffer (DingGuo, WB0072) containing protease inhibitors (DingGuo, WB0181). The resulting proteins were denatured by heating at 70 °C for 10 min, separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, IPVH00010). Membranes were incubated with: anti-HA (1:1000, Invitrogen, MA-27915), anti-GAPDH (1:5000, Invitrogen, MA5-15738), anti-ERK1/2 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, #4695), and anti-pERK1/2 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, #4370). HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000, Beyotime, A0216/A0208) were detected by enhanced chemiluminescence (Thermo, 34580) and visualized using an Image Lab gel imaging system (Bio-Rad).

Immunofluorescence staining

HeLa cells transfected with mCherry-tagged JARID2 constructs (wild-type/mutant) (Supplementary Figure S1) were fixed 24 h post-transfection using 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) (BBI, E672002-0500) for 20 min at room temperature (RT), followed by three PBS washes. Cells were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (20 min) and blocked with 4% BSA (1 h, RT). Nuclear counterstaining utilized DAPI (1:250, Invitrogen, D3571) for 5 min. For oocytes and embryos, fixation was performed using 2% PFA at 37 °C for 30 min, followed by permeabilization with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 min. Then blocked with a solution containing 0.2% BSA, 0.1% donkey serum albumin, 0.1 M glycine, and 0.01% Triton X-100 for 1 h, and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (anti-JARID2 (1:500, Novus, NB100-2214SS) or anti-H3K27me3 (1:1000, Beyotime, AF5710)). The following day, both samples were incubated with corresponding secondary antibodies (Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies (Invitrogen, A21207/A21202; 1:1000) at room temperature for 1 h. After mounting with DAPI-containing antifade medium (Beyotime, P0131).

Animals

Female C57BL/6 mice (3–4 weeks old) were purchased from the Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd (Changsha, China). These mice were maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions on a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle at 21–23 °C with free access to food and water. All animal experiments were performed in accordance with the guidelines of the Animal Committee of Central South University.

In vitro transcription of mRNAs and microinjection

JARID2 plasmids linearized with XbaI (NEB, R0145S) served as templates for in vitro transcription using T7 mMESSAGE kits (Invitrogen, AM1344). Poly(A) tails were added with Poly(A) Tailing Kits (Invitrogen, AM1350). Synthesized poly (A) mRNAs were recovered with lithium chloride precipitation at − 20°C, followed by washing with 70% ethanol, and ultimately resuspended in nuclease-free water. Zygotes collected from superovulated mice as previously described [21] were microinjected with WT/mutant JARID2 mRNA (5’-Cap(m7GpppG)-[mCherry]-[JARID2 CDS]-Poly(A)-3’, 1100 ng/µL) using a FemtoJet 4i microinjector, then cultured in M16 medium (Sigma, M7292) at 37 °C/5% CO2.

Single-cell RNA-seq library preparation, sequencing and analysis

Single-cell transcriptome libraries were constructed using the SMART-Seq2 methodology and sequenced on the BGISEQ-500 system (BGI-Shenzhen) with 100 bp paired-end reads, following previously established methods [22]. Raw sequencing reads underwent initial quality assessment using FastQC, followed by adapter trimming and low-quality base removal via Cutadapt. Clean reads were aligned to the GRCh38 reference genome using STAR (v2.7.3a). Transcript abundance estimation were performed through StringTie (v2.0.4), with expression values standardized to transcripts per million (TPM) for cross-sample normalization.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Image quantification was performed using ImageJ, with protein band analysis involving manual drawing of rectangular ROIs around target bands followed by grayscale inversion, 8-bit conversion, and integrated density measurement; fluorescence intensity quantification employed uniform thresholding of 8-bit grayscale images with mean pixel intensity measured within defined ROIs. Statistical analyses using GraphPad Prism 9 presented quantitative data as mean ± SD (continuous variables), percentages (categorical data), or absolute counts (discrete observations), utilizing unpaired Student’s t-tests for two-group comparisons or two-way ANOVA, with significance thresholds defined as ***P < 0.001, **P < 0.01, *P < 0.05, and ns, no significant difference.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the affected individual

The proband, a 33-year-old female from a consanguineous family, diagnosed with primary infertility following six years of unprotected intercourse. She underwent two failed assisted reproductive technology (ART) cycles with similar outcomes in our center. In her first ART cycle using the long controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) protocol, 12 oocytes (including 10 MII oocytes) were retrieved, then six 2PN zygotes formed through IVF insemination. All zygotes initiated cleavage, however, five embryos arrested at 2- to 5-cell stages, only one developed into the 4BC-grade blastocyst on day 7, but failed to establish pregnancy after the subsequent embryo transfer (Table 1; Fig. 1A). In her second ART cycle employing the progesterone-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS) protocol, 13 oocytes were retrieved, of which ten were mature. Of seven MII oocytes inseminated, six achieved successful fertilization. All zygotes progressed to 3- to 5-cell stages, only one 6BC-grade blastocyst formed on day 7. Preimplantation genetic testing revealed chromosomal mosaicism involving multiple chromosomes, and copy number variation (CNV) origin analysis confirmed maternal predominance in chromosomal abnormalities (Table 1; Fig. 1B and C).

Identification of the JARID2 pathogenic variant

We prioritized screening for homozygous variants due to the proband’s consanguineous background. Through whole-exome sequencing (WES) analysis following established criteria, JARID2 (OMIM ID:601594; NM_004973.4) emerged as the strongest candidate gene. The patient carried a homozygous missense variant (c.899G > A, p.Arg300Gln) (Table 2), which was absent in biallelic form within our natural conception cohort. Sanger sequencing confirmed the variant’s parental inheritance (Fig. 1D). The p.Arg300Gln variant resides within the N-terminal linker region (Fig. 1E), and the Arg amino acid was 100% conservation across species from Sus scrofa to Homo sapiens (Fig. 1F). Analysis of JARID2 mRNA dynamics in human oocytes and early embryos revealed peak transcript abundance at the MII oocyte stage, with progressive downregulation from zygotes to blastocysts—a pattern characteristic of maternal-effect gene (Fig. 1G).

Pathogenic effect of the JARID2 variant in ERK1/2 activation

To investigate the pathogenic mechanism of the JARID2 variant in vitro, we performed immunofluorescence (IF) and western blot (WB) analyses using HeLa cells transiently transfected with HA-tagged wild-type or mutant JARID2 expression plasmids. Both wild-type and mutant proteins demonstrated predominant nuclear localization with comparable expression levels in this transient transfection system (Fig. 2A and B). Given JARID2’s established role in modulating Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) chromatin targeting to regulate ERK1/2 signaling activation in mouse embryonic stem cells [23], we subsequently evaluated functional consequences of the variant. WB quantification revealed that while both JARID2 forms significantly elevated ERK1/2 and phosphorylated ERK1/2 (pERK1/2) levels compared to negative control, the mutant exhibited attenuated activation capacity relative to wild-type protein (Fig. 2C and Supplementary Fig S2).

Effect of the JARID2 variant on early embryonic development

To systematically evaluate the impact of the JARID2 variant on early embryonic development, we microinjected wild-type (WT) or mutant JARID2 mRNA into mouse zygotes followed by extended in vitro culture. Quantitative analysis demonstrated comparable developmental competence at the 2-cell stage between groups (WT: 97.2 ± 3.8% vs. mutant: 89.9 ± 13.1%, p > 0.05). However, significant divergence emerged at subsequent stages: WT embryos progressed to 4-cell stage with 77.3 ± 9.1% efficiency, whereas mutant counterparts exhibited a 30.1% reduction (47.2 ± 5.9%, p < 0.01). This developmental disparity persisted through preimplantation stages, with 64.0 ± 4.8% of WT embryos reaching morula morphology compared to 35.3 ± 9.1% in the mutant cohort (p < 0.05). Terminal differentiation analysis revealed severely compromised blastocyst formation rates, where WT embryos achieved 61.1 ± 7.9% developmental success versus 31.5 ± 5.9% in mutants (Δ29.6%, p < 0.001). This result recapitulated the blastocyst formation rate observed in the proband’s ART outcomes (Fig. 2D).

Effects of the JARID2 variant on transcriptomes in mouse embryos

Previous study has demonstrated JARID2 knockdown reduce H3K27me3 levels in bovine embryos, and histone methylation critically regulates early embryonic development across species, with evidenced in Drosophila and mouse [19, 24]. To assess variant-specific epigenetic impacts, we analyzed H3K27me3 levels via immunofluorescence in microinjected mouse embryos across developmental stages (1–8 cell) (Fig. 3A). JARID2-mutant injection embryos exhibited significant H3K27me3 reduction at 2-/4-cell stages versus wild-type groups, while 1-/8-cell stages showed comparable levels (Fig. 3B&C). Transcriptomic profiling across stages identified stage-specific dysregulation, with 2-cell embryos showing predominant downregulation (271 vs. 87 upregulated genes) (Fig. 3D). Given that the 2-cell stage coincides with ZGA in mice, the transcriptomic analysis revealed predominant downregulation at this stage. We identified 10 ZGA-associated genes among the downregulated targets based on published studies (Supplementary Table S1). Gene Ontology analysis revealed downregulated transcripts enriched in maternal-to-zygotic transition, meiotic nuclear division, and embryonic development pathways; upregulated genes associated with mRNA stability regulation, oxidative phosphorylation, and DNA repair mechanisms (Fig. 3E). RNA-seq analysis of other core PRC2 subunits Ezh1/2 double-knockout morula identified 80 putative H3K27me3 dependent imprinted genes, with 26 exhibiting maternal H3K27me3 marks at their promoters [25]. Given the pronounced transcriptional alterations specifically observed in our 2-cell embryos, we performed intersection analysis between significantly upregulated genes at this stage and the 80 candidate genes. This identified six putative H3K27me3-dependent imprinted genes (Pramel6, Rfc2, Cluap1, Ankmy2, Tex101, Syce1) (Supplementary Fig S3).

Effect of the JARID2 variant on transcriptomes in human oocyte

Transcriptomic profiling of a patient-derived GV-stage oocyte versus control GV oocytes revealed marked molecular perturbations. Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated clear segregation of the patient’s oocyte from controls, indicating global transcriptomic divergence (Fig. 4A). Differential expression analysis identified 4,559 downregulated and 3,726 upregulated transcripts in the patient’s oocyte (Fig. 4B). Notably, we observed deficient accumulation of critical maternal-effect genes (MEGs), including spindle component molecules (TUBB8/TUBA1C/TUBA4A) and mRNA degradation mediators (BTG4/MOS) (Fig. 4C). Gene Ontology analysis highlighted downregulated transcripts enriched in oogenesis, meiotic cell cycle progression, and microtubule organization pathways. Conversely, upregulated genes predominantly associated with stress responses (oxidative stress/apoptosis regulation) and metabolic processes (RNA and carboxylic acid metabolism/glycolytic) (Fig. 4D). These findings suggest JARID2 dysfunction disrupts both oocyte developmental competence and cellular homeostasis in human oocytes.

Discussion

Impaired embryonic developmental potential represents a major contributor to ART failure. While prior studies have focused on complete EEA—characterized by total blastulation failure and linked to monogenic variants in maternal-effect genes—we identified a homozygous JARID2 missense variant (c.899G > A, p.Arg300Gln) in a female patient exhibiting CBE, a novel phenotype marked by severely reduced blastocyst formation (< 10%) and autosomal recessive inheritance. We demonstrate that JARID2 functions as a maternal-effect gene directing early embryonic development through coordinated epigenetic regulation via ERK1/2 signaling cascades (Fig. 5).

A diagram indicates the pathogenesis of JARID2 variant. Under physiological conditions (left): JARID2 facilitates ERK1/2 phosphorylation, promoting H3K27 trimethylation and embryonic genome activation. In JARID2 mutants (right): Attenuated ERK signaling reduces H3K27me3 deposition, potentially impairing zygotic genome activation and compromising developmental competence

JARID2, ubiquitously expressed across tissues including cardiac, neural, and muscular systems, is associated with developmental disorders and oncogenesis when dysregulated [26–28]. Strikingly, this specific missense variant localizes to a non-canonical N-terminal junctional domain, inducing a single amino acid substitution that selectively disrupts oocyte-specific epigenetic regulation while preserving global protein function. We propose two non-exclusive explanations for this tissue-specific phenotypic dichotomy: (1) dosage sensitivity—oocyte development requires precise JARID2-mediated epigenetic regulation where partial functional impairment disproportionately affects gametogenesis versus somatic tissues; (2) compensatory mechanisms—redundant pathways in somatic cells buffer this mutation while oocytes lack such capacity due to their unique epigenetic landscape. Our study highlights the structural resilience of JARID2’s core domains and provides the first functional evidence implicating its understudied N-terminal regions in reproductive biology.

Although our mouse model with microinjected human mutant mRNA recapitulates the patient phenotype (Fig. 2D), endogenous murine Jarid2 activity inevitably introduces confounding effects. We hypothesize that the exogenous mutant human JARID2 mRNA may exceed physiological levels, potentially inducing a dominant-negative effect through competitive binding of downstream effectors. Consequently, blastocyst formation decreased by Δ29.6% in mutants (31.5 ± 5.9% vs. 61.1 ± 7.9% in wild-type), yet the residual blastocyst rate suggests partial functional compensation by endogenous Jarid2. Future studies will establish Jarid2 knock-in mouse models to further investigate this mechanism. Notably, our findings parallel established PRC2 biology, similar to core subunits deficiency (Ezh1/2 KO) which compromises blastocyst development through impaired H3K27me3 deposition and epiblast specification [25], and distinct from knockdown of Rbbp4 that primarily disrupts oocyte meiosis via increased spindle defects, chromosome misalignment, and MII-stage aneuploidy, indirectly impairing embryonic competence [29].

mRNA transcription is tightly regulated during two critical phases: follicular growth to the fully-grown oocyte stage and zygotic genome activation (ZGA), a process governed by epigenetic mechanisms, particularly histone methylation (H3K9me3, H3K27me3) [30–32], may partially mediated by JARID2. We established a relationship between reduced H3K27me3 levels and potential ZGA defects at the murine 2-cell stage, with downregulated genes enriched in embryonic development and maternal-to-zygotic transition pathways. Although H3K27me3 suppression typically depresses gene expression, the unexpected downregulation of genes in 2-cell embryo following mutant mRNA microinjection suggests zygotic genome activation (ZGA)-specific regulatory complexity. As ZGA requires coordinated chromatin remodeling and transcriptional activator recruitment [22]. Reduced H3K27me3 may compromise activator binding at key loci during the 2-cell stage, leading to dysregulation of genes. Parallel transcriptomic deficits in patient-derived GV oocyte revealed analogous enrichment in oogenesis and germ cell development, suggesting JARID2 may orchestrate conserved epigenetic regulation across folliculogenesis and embryogenesis.

ERK1/2 inhibition and H3K27me3 dynamics is supported by existing studies: mouse embryonic stem cell research revealed that Erk1/2 mutation correlates with reduced H3K27me3 and Jarid2 levels [23], while tumorigenesis studies indicate MAPK-ERK axis involvement in PRC2-dependent H3K27me3 regulation [33]. Additionally, the ERK1/2-EZH2-H3K27me3 pathway functions during pregnancy [34]. Pharmacologically, GDC-0994 (ERK1/2 inhibitor) exposure reduces blastocyst formation rates and increases developmental arrest at 2-cell/4-cell stages in mouse zygotes [35], and our prior work demonstrates that MOS variants impair ERK1/2 activation, causing human early embryonic arrest [10].

Our prior research established the MOS variants impair ERK1/2 signaling, causing defective maternal mRNA clearance in oocytes and subsequent EEA [10]. In contrast, this study reveals that attenuated ERK1/2 activation in human GV-stage oocytes correlates with insufficient maternal mRNA accumulation, suggesting a bifunctional role for ERK1/2 in maternal mRNA dynamics. In MII oocytes, ERK1/2 deficiency disrupts maternal mRNA degradation, leading to pathological retention of transcripts. In growing oocytes, impaired ERK1/2 activity compromises transcriptional activation of maternal mRNAs, significantly reducing expression of critical factors including BTG4, PADI6, OOEP, and KPNA7, though the precise mechanistic underpinnings of this dual regulation require further validation.

Conclusions

By shifting focus to epigenetic regulation, we establish JARID2 as a novel determinant of embryonic competence, linking H3K27me3 dysregulation to potential ZGA failure and redefining CBE pathogenesis within a chromatin-remodeling framework. This work expands the diagnostic paradigm for maternal-effect disorders and provides mechanistic insights for addressing unexplained ART failures.

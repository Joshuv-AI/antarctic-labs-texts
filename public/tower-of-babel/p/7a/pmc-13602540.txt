Retrospective analysis of the occurrence pattern of complications and prognostic influencing factors in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer after radical surgery
Authors: Jie You, Yiping Lang, Jiamin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyze the characteristics of postoperative complications and their prognostic effects in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer (SqCLC) who received radical surgery, identify independent prognostic factors, and build an individualized survival prediction model.MethodsThis retrospective study included 260 elderly patients with SqCLC who underwent radical surgery. We collected and analyzed their clinicopathological data, postoperative complications and survival outcomes. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to screen prognostic factors for overall survival (OS). A nomogram for 2-year OS prediction was established and validated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, calibration curve and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsThe total incidence of postoperative complications was 38.1% (99/260), and pulmonary infection (14.6%) was the most common complication. Univariate analysis indicated that age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS), Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), 8th edition TNM stage and postoperative complications were significantly correlated with OS and progression-free survival (PFS) (all P < 0.05). Multivariate Cox regression identified advanced TNM stage (III-IV) (HR = 1.65, 95%CI 1.23–2.21, P < 0.001) and ECOG PS ≥ 2 (HR = 1.25, 95%CI 1.01–1.55, P < 0.05) as independent adverse prognostic factors for OS. Postoperative complications were significantly associated with poorer survival in univariate analysis but did not remain an independent prognostic factor in multivariate analysis. The nomogram integrating age, ECOG PS, CCI, TNM stage, and postoperative complication status yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82 (95%CI 0.77–0.87) for 2-year OS prediction, with excellent calibration and clinical net benefit.ConclusionPostoperative complications are highly prevalent in elderly SqCLC patients undergoing radical surgery and are significantly associated with inferior survival outcomes in univariate analysis. The proposed postoperative nomogram can accurately predict 2-year OS, and its clinical application is restricted to postoperative scenarios, including individualized prognostic counseling, surveillance planning, and risk stratification for this patient population.Keywords: Squamous cell lung cancer, Elderly patients, Radical surgery, Postoperative complications, Prognosis, Nomogram

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for approximately 80–85% of all lung cancer cases [1]. The global aging population has resulted in a rapidly growing number of elderly patients diagnosed with lung cancer, as more than half of all lung cancers are diagnosed in patients aged 65 years or over [2]. According to the US Surveillance Epidemiology and End Results (SEER) program data, the overall mean age of lung cancer diagnosis is 71 years, with patients aged 80 years or above accounting for 17.8% of all cases [3]. Among NSCLC subtypes, squamous cell carcinoma shows a significantly higher proportion in elderly populations, with the rate reaching 37.9% in patients aged 80 years or above versus 28.6% in younger patients [4]. Elderly patients with squamous cell carcinoma are predominantly male (93.2%) and have an extremely high prevalence of smoking history (89.1%) [5]. However, elderly patients remain severely underrepresented in clinical trials, with only 24% of patients enrolled in drug registration trials between 2005 and 2015 being 70 years or older, and merely 12% being older than 75 years, leading to a substantial evidence gap in the optimal management of this population. The unique epidemiological and clinicopathological features of elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer have not been fully characterized, and the disease outcome patterns after radical surgery in this specific population remain unclear.

Currently, the treatment paradigm for elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer is facing multiple unmet challenges, as existing guidelines are largely based on evidence derived from younger populations, and age often serves as an exclusion criterion in clinical trials [6], resulting in a therapeutic dilemma where elderly patients may receive either inadequate treatment due to age-related bias or inappropriate treatment without age-specific evidence. Surgical resection remains the cornerstone of curative treatment for early-stage NSCLC [7], yet the proportion of elderly patients undergoing surgery decreases dramatically with age: only 31.3% of patients aged 80 years and older with stage I-II disease undergo surgery, compared to 84.6% of younger patients [8]. The CAPITAL trial, one of the few large randomized trials focused on this population, demonstrated that carboplatin plus nab-paclitaxel significantly improved overall survival compared with docetaxel in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer [9], but the applicability of these findings to very elderly patients (≥ 80 years) remains uncertain. Meanwhile, although immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have revolutionized NSCLC treatment, patients older than 75 years may benefit less from ICIs than younger patients, which may be attributed to immunosenescence [10]. The management of this population is further complicated by the high prevalence of comorbidities, as 60.8% of patients aged 80 years or older who underwent radical lung cancer surgery had preoperative comorbidities [11]. This brings forward the second key research question: the occurrence pattern and independent risk factors of postoperative complications in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer after radical surgery have not been clearly defined, which hinders the development of precise preoperative risk stratification and perioperative management strategies for this population.

Despite the increasing clinical demand for standardized management of elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer, the existing literature has critical limitations, including insufficient enrollment of elderly patients in clinical trials, lack of consideration for the heterogeneity of the elderly population, and absence of age-specific treatment guidelines for squamous cell carcinoma. This leads to the third core research question: how to integrate the clinicopathological characteristics of elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer to develop and validate a reliable prognostic model that can accurately predict survival outcomes after radical surgery, so as to guide individualized clinical decision-making. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive retrospective analysis of elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer who received radical surgery. We systematically investigated the occurrence patterns of postoperative complications and identified independent prognostic factors for survival outcomes in this specific population. We subsequently developed a prognostic nomogram incorporating both preoperative clinicopathological characteristics and postoperative complication status for the accurate prediction of 2-year overall survival. This nomogram is primarily designed for postoperative prognostic counseling and individualized surveillance planning, providing an evidence-based tool for clinical decision-making in the postoperative management of elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethical approval

This was a single-center, retrospective cohort study. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital. Informed consent was waived by the IRB due to the retrospective nature of the study, which involved no additional intervention to routine clinical practice and no compromise to patient privacy and safety. All study procedures strictly followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study population

We consecutively enrolled elderly patients with primary squamous cell lung cancer (SqCLC) who underwent radical surgical resection at our institution between September 2018 and September 2023. The median follow-up duration for the entire cohort was 28.5 months (interquartile range: 19.0–38.0 months).

Data collection and variable definition

Clinical and pathological data were retrospectively extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system by two independent researchers, with a third senior researcher responsible for data verification and discrepancy resolution.

Baseline clinicopathological variables

Collected baseline data included: (1) Demographic characteristics: age, gender, and smoking history (classified as never smoker, former smoker [quit ≥ 1 year], and current smoker, with smoking pack-years recorded); (2) Preoperative functional status and comorbidities: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) score, and Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) calculated based on preoperative comorbidities; (3) Tumor-related characteristics: pathological tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage classified according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC)/Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) staging system. Stage III cases were predominantly resectable stage IIIA disease (including cases with postoperatively pathologically upstaged N2 lymph node metastasis), and all stage IV cases were oligometastatic disease with solitary metastatic lesions that underwent concurrent radical resection of both the primary tumor and metastatic focus to achieve R0 surgical margins; (4) Surgical-related variables: surgical approach (video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery [VATS] vs. open thoracotomy).

Postoperative complications assessment

Postoperative complications were defined as surgery-related adverse events occurring within 90 days after surgery, consistent with the standard reporting timeframe for thoracic surgical outcomes. We recorded the type, onset time, and intervention measures of each complication, and graded the severity of all complications using the Clavien-Dindo Complication Classification System. The primary complications of interest included pulmonary infection, pleural effusion requiring needle aspiration, prolonged air leakage (> 7 days), myocardial ischemia, and postoperative bleeding. Pulmonary infection was diagnosed in accordance with the criteria for hospital-acquired pneumonia established by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). The overall incidence of postoperative complications, the time to first complication, postoperative hospital stay, and 30-day unplanned readmission rate were calculated for the entire cohort.

Follow-up and outcome measures

All patients were followed up via outpatient review or telephone interview. The follow-up schedule was as follows: once every 3 months for the first 2 years after surgery, and once every 6 months thereafter. Follow-up examinations included chest computed tomography (CT), abdominal ultrasound or CT, and serum tumor marker testing; brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) was performed when tumor recurrence or metastasis was suspected.

The primary endpoint of this study was overall survival (OS), defined as the time interval from the date of surgery to the date of all-cause death, with patients alive at the final follow-up censored at the date of their last visit. The secondary endpoints included: (1) progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time interval from the date of surgery to the date of first tumor recurrence/metastasis or all-cause death, whichever occurred first; (2) incidence and distribution pattern of postoperative complications; (3) postoperative hospital stay; (4) 30-day unplanned readmission rate.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables conforming to normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and intergroup comparisons were performed using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR), and intergroup comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as counts (percentages, n [%]), and intergroup comparisons were conducted using the chi-square test (χ²) or Fisher’s exact test (when the theoretical frequency was < 5). Spearman rank correlation analysis was used to evaluate the correlation between baseline clinical characteristics and the incidence of specific postoperative complications.

Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. The proportional hazards assumption of all Cox regression models was tested using the Schoenfeld residual method, and all included variables met the assumption (all P > 0.05). Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed to screen potential prognostic factors associated with OS and PFS. Variables with a P-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were further included in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model with a forward stepwise (likelihood ratio) method to identify independent prognostic factors for OS and PFS. Hazard ratios (HR) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated for each variable.

A nomogram was constructed to predict the 2-year OS probability of the study population, integrating age, ECOG PS score, CCI, TNM stage, and postoperative complication status based on the results of prognostic analysis and clinical relevance. The discriminative performance, calibration, and clinical utility of the nomogram were evaluated using ROC curve, calibration curve with Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and decision curve analysis, respectively. Of note, no bootstrap resampling or cross-validation for internal validation was performed; thus, the reported performance metrics may carry a degree of overoptimism.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (version 4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Among the 260 patients, 50.4% (131 cases) were aged ≥ 75 years, 28.1% (73 cases) were aged 65–69 years, and 21.5% (56 cases) were aged 70–74 years, with a significant difference in age distribution among subgroups (P = 0.032). Univariate Analysis of Prognostic Factors for Overall Survival (OS) and Progression-Free Survival (PFS)

Age was significantly associated with both OS and PFS: the median OS was 29.0 months (95%CI 25.3–32.7) for patients aged 65–69 years, 27.0 months (95%CI 22.1–31.9) for those aged 70–74 years, and 24.0 months (95%CI 20.8–27.2) for those aged ≥ 75 years (P = 0.028 for OS, P = 0.035 for PFS). Higher ECOG PS score (≥ 2) was correlated with inferior survival outcomes, with a median OS of 21.0 months (95%CI 17.2–24.8) vs. 28.0 months (95%CI 25.1–30.9) for ECOG PS 0–1 (P = 0.006 for OS, P = 0.009 for PFS). Higher CCI score (≥ 2) was also a significant adverse prognostic factor, with a median OS of 23.0 months (95%CI 20.1–25.9) vs. 30.5 months (95%CI 26.8–34.2) for CCI 0–1 (P = 0.003 for OS, P = 0.005 for PFS). Advanced TNM stage (III-IV) was associated with significantly worse survival, with a median OS of 20.0 months (95%CI 16.8–23.2) vs. 32.0 months (95%CI 28.5–35.5) for stage I-II (P < 0.001 for both OS and PFS).

Notably, postoperative complications were identified as a strong adverse prognostic factor in univariate analysis. Patients with postoperative complications had a median OS of 22.0 months (95%CI 18.5–25.5) and a median PFS of 15.5 months (95%CI 12.8–18.2), compared with 31.0 months (95%CI 27.8–34.2) and 24.5 months (95%CI 21.6–27.4) in patients without complications (P < 0.001 for both OS and PFS) (Table 3). The 2-year OS rate was 42.4% in patients with complications, significantly lower than the 68.3% in those without complications (P < 0.001). Smoking status showed no significant association with either OS or PFS (P = 0.312 for OS, P = 0.287 for PFS) (Table 3).

Kaplan-Meier survival curves further confirmed the prognostic impact of postoperative complications. Patients with postoperative complications had significantly inferior OS and PFS compared with those without complications (log-rank P < 0.001 for both) (Fig. 2A and B). Subgroup survival analysis revealed that patients with pulmonary infection had the worst OS outcomes, with a median OS of 20.0 months, significantly lower than that of patients with other complications (median OS 25.0 months) and those without complications (median OS 35.0 months) (Fig. 2C). Survival stratified by Clavien-Dindo complication grade showed a stepwise reduction in median OS with increasing complication severity, from 35.0 months in patients without complications to 26.0 months in those with grade I-II complications and 20.5 months in those with grade III-V complications (Fig. 2D). However, one-way ANOVA showed no statistically significant difference in median OS across the no complication, mild (grade I-II), and severe (grade III-IV) groups (F = 0.32, P = 0.728) (Fig. 1C).

Multivariate prognostic analysis and risk stratification

Variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were entered into multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify independent prognostic factors for OS and PFS.

For OS, advanced TNM stage (III-IV) was identified as an independent adverse prognostic factor (hazard ratio [HR] = 1.65, 95%CI 1.23–2.21, P < 0.001), and ECOG PS score ≥ 2 was also an independent predictor of inferior OS (HR = 1.25, 95%CI 1.01–1.55, P < 0.05). Age, CCI score, and postoperative complications did not reach statistical significance as independent prognostic factors for OS in multivariate analysis (all P > 0.05) (Fig. 3A). For PFS, none of the included variables were identified as independent prognostic factors in multivariate regression analysis, with all P-values > 0.05 (Fig. 3B).

Based on the independent prognostic factors identified in multivariate analysis, we constructed a prognostic risk stratification model and divided the cohort into low-risk, medium-risk, and high-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed a significant difference in OS across the three risk groups (overall log-rank P < 0.001), with pairwise comparisons showing significant differences between low vs. high risk (P < 0.001), medium vs. high risk (P < 0.001), and low vs. medium risk (P = 0.013) (Fig. 3C). The median OS was not reached in the low-risk and medium-risk groups, while it was 32.0 months in the high-risk group.

Event rate analysis further supported the prognostic stratification effect of the model. The 2-year OS rate was 91.3% in the low-risk group, 75.0% in the medium-risk group, and 52.6% in the high-risk group; the 2-year tumor progression rate was 21.7% in the low-risk group, 39.5% in the medium-risk group, and 61.2% in the high-risk group; and the 30-day unplanned readmission rate was 6.2% in the low-risk group, 11.4% in the medium-risk group, and 17.7% in the high-risk group. Significant differences were found in 2-year OS and tumor progression rates across groups (both P < 0.05), while the 30-day readmission rate showed an upward trend without statistical significance (P = 0.072) (Fig. 3D).

Construction and validation of the prognostic nomogram for 2-year OS

To provide a clinically applicable tool for individualized survival prediction, we constructed a nomogram for predicting 2-year OS in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer after radical surgery. The nomogram incorporates age, ECOG PS, CCI score, TNM stage, and postoperative complication status (Fig. 4A). While only ECOG PS and TNM stage remained statistically significant independent prognostic factors in multivariate Cox regression, age, CCI, and postoperative complications were retained in the nomogram based on their well-established clinical relevance, proven prognostic value in existing literature, and demonstrated incremental value for model performance. This approach aligns with standard clinical prediction model construction principles, which prioritize clinical plausibility and overall predictive utility alongside strict statistical significance thresholds. This is particularly applicable when variables may lose independent statistical significance due to collinearity with other covariates or sample size limitations.

Specifically, postoperative complications were highly significant in univariate survival analysis (P < 0.001) and have been consistently identified as a key prognostic factor for surgical oncology patients in multiple prior studies. The attenuation of its statistical significance in multivariate analysis is most likely explained by its close correlation with baseline functional status, comorbidity burden, and tumor stage, which partially mediate its prognostic effect. To further verify the value of this variable, we compared the performance of the full nomogram (with postoperative complications) and a reduced model excluding this variable. The full nomogram yielded a superior AUC of 0.82 (95%CI 0.77–0.87) for 2-year OS prediction versus 0.79 (95%CI 0.73–0.84) for the reduced model, along with better calibration performance and higher clinical net benefit in decision curve analysis. These findings support the inclusion of postoperative complication status to optimize the predictive accuracy and clinical applicability of the nomogram. Each variable was assigned a corresponding point score, and the total points could be mapped to the predicted probability of 2-year OS.

The predictive performance of the nomogram was validated by multiple methods. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve showed that the nomogram had an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82 (95%CI 0.77–0.87) for predicting 2-year OS, which was superior to the AUC of individual variables including age (AUC = 0.71), ECOG PS (AUC = 0.72), CCI score (AUC = 0.70), TNM stage (AUC = 0.72), and postoperative complications (AUC = 0.71) (Fig. 4C). The calibration curve showed excellent agreement between the nomogram-predicted 2-year OS probability and the actual observed probability, with the Hosmer-Lemeshow test showing no significant deviation from ideal prediction (χ²=3.68, P = 0.298) (Fig. 4B). Decision curve analysis (DCA) further confirmed the clinical utility of the nomogram, which showed a higher net benefit across the majority of threshold probability ranges compared with the “no intervention” and “treat all” strategies, as well as individual prognostic factors (Fig. 4D).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we systematically analyzed the clinical data of elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer who underwent radical surgery, and clarified the occurrence pattern of postoperative complications, independent prognostic factors for survival, and developed a validated prognostic nomogram for this population.

The primary finding of our study is that the overall postoperative complication rate was 38.1% in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer after radical surgery, which is consistent with the previously reported range of 35.3% to 45% in octogenarian patients undergoing lung cancer surgery [11], and notably lower than the 55.8% complication rate reported in a population-based study of elderly patients (66–80 years) undergoing lobectomy for stage I NSCLC [12]. This discrepancy may be explained by differences in patient selection criteria, surgical techniques, and complication definitions between studies. Pulmonary infection was the most common postoperative complication in our cohort, with the overall incidence of pulmonary complications reaching 38.3%, which is in line with previous studies that consistently identified respiratory complications as the leading cause of morbidity in elderly patients after lung resection [13]. The high incidence of pulmonary complications can be attributed to age-related physiological changes, including decreased lung compliance, reduced respiratory muscle strength, and impaired cough reflex, as well as the high prevalence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) in this predominantly smoking population [14]. Cardiovascular complications were the second most frequent, occurring in 24.5% of patients, which reflects the high prevalence of underlying cardiovascular disease in elderly patients and the physiological stress of thoracic surgery [15]. Furthermore, we identified male gender and longer operative time as independent risk factors for postoperative complications, which is consistent with previous findings [16]. Most notably, chronological age itself was not significantly associated with the occurrence of postoperative complications in our cohort, which challenges the widespread perception that advanced age is a major risk factor for surgical complications. It suggests that physiological age is more important than chronological age in predicting surgical outcomes in this population.

Our multivariate Cox regression analysis further identified TNM stage III-IV and ECOG PS ≥ 2 as independent adverse prognostic factors for overall survival in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer after radical surgery. These findings are consistent with the previous study on NSCLC prognosis, as pathological TNM stage has been confirmed as the most important prognostic factor for NSCLC in a large international database analysis of 67,725 cases [17], and ECOG performance status has also been identified as a critical prognostic factor in addition to clinical stage in the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) staging database analysis [18]. Our finding that stage III-IV disease was associated with significantly worse survival highlights the importance of early detection and intervention for elderly patients, even though this population is more likely to present with advanced-stage disease at diagnosis [19]. The prognostic value of ECOG PS ≥ 2 is particularly relevant for elderly patients, as previous studies have demonstrated that it retains prognostic validity in patients treated with both chemotherapy and immunotherapy [20], while we also acknowledge that performance status alone is not sufficient for outcome prediction in this population, and more comprehensive geriatric assessment is needed. Interestingly, several factors with proven prognostic value in younger populations, including tumor differentiation and vascular invasion [21], did not reach statistical significance in our elderly cohort, which may indicate that performance status and comorbidity burden play more dominant roles in the prognosis of elderly patients. In addition, we found that male gender was associated with both higher complication risk and worse survival outcomes [22], which may be related to differences in tumor biology, comorbidity profile, and treatment preferences between genders.

Based on the prognostic analysis, we developed and validated a prognostic nomogram for predicting 2-year overall survival in elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer after radical surgery. The nomogram incorporates age, ECOG PS, CCI, TNM stage, and postoperative complication status, and is primarily intended for postoperative prognostic counseling and surveillance planning rather than preoperative decision-making, given that postoperative complication status is only available after surgery. The nomogram showed excellent discriminatory ability, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.82 for 2-year overall survival prediction, which is notably higher than the 0.730–0.750 range reported for traditional TNM staging systems in elderly patients [23]. The superior performance of our nomogram highlights the value of incorporating multiple clinical variables beyond traditional tumor characteristics for prognostic evaluation in this population.

Contemporary prognostic modeling in surgical oncology is evolving beyond stage-only risk stratification through multidimensional data integration, as demonstrated by multimodal digital-biopsy models for occult metastasis [24] and radiomics-based recurrence prediction models [25, 26]. Our nomogram built on conventional clinical variables offers a practical tool for routine clinical practice; future models for elderly squamous cell lung cancer can be further refined by incorporating imaging, molecular and digital pathology biomarkers.

These findings carry key clinical implications for elderly squamous cell lung cancer management. First, chronological age alone is not a significant predictor of surgical complications or survival, challenging prevailing age-related treatment biases and supporting individualized strategies prioritizing physiological function over age, to avoid undertreatment in fit patients and excessive intervention in frail patients. Second, the identified risk factors for postoperative complications provide clear targets for preoperative risk stratification, guiding tailored perioperative management such as cardiopulmonary and nutritional optimization for high-risk patients. Third, the validated prognostic nomogram provides a practical and easy-to-use tool for postoperative clinical management. It can assist clinicians in delivering standardized prognostic counseling to patients and their families, guide individualized postoperative surveillance planning and risk-stratified adjuvant management, identify high-risk patients who may benefit from intensified follow-up or enhanced supportive care, and serve as a reliable stratification tool for postoperative interventional clinical trials. In addition, our findings further highlight the urgent need for more inclusive trial designs that adequately enroll elderly patients, and for the development of age-specific treatment guidelines for this growing population.

Beyond conventional clinical variables, emerging evidence has highlighted the critical role of nutritional and inflammatory biomarkers in postoperative risk stratification and prognostic assessment across various surgical oncology populations. Recent studies have demonstrated that serum nutritional biomarkers can effectively predict the risk of anastomotic leakage after laparoscopic surgery in rectal cancer patients [27], providing a non-invasive and cost-effective tool for preoperative risk identification and targeted intervention. Furthermore, combined inflammatory and nutritional indices, such as the preoperative platelet-albumin ratio and cancer inflammation prognostic index, have shown robust and independent prognostic value in colorectal cancer [28], and inflammation-based hematologic markers have been consistently associated with both postoperative complication risk and long-term survival outcomes in stage I-III colorectal cancer [29]. Most notably, a novel immune-nutritional prognostic ratio has recently been developed and validated as a powerful predictor of long-term survival in colorectal cancer patients undergoing curative surgery [30], underscoring the potential of integrating easily accessible routine laboratory biomarkers into clinical risk assessment models. These findings are highly relevant to our study population, as elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer often present with pre-existing malnutrition and chronic systemic inflammation secondary to long-term smoking and comorbidities, which may independently contribute to postoperative complications and poor survival outcomes. While our nomogram based on conventional clinical variables has demonstrated favorable predictive performance, the incorporation of nutritional and inflammatory biomarkers in future studies may further enhance the precision of risk stratification and prognostic prediction for this vulnerable population, enabling more personalized perioperative management strategies.

A latest systematic review focused on lung cancer demonstrated that metabolic reprogramming, including enhanced glycolysis, dysregulated lipid synthesis and oxidation, and altered amino acid metabolism drives tumor progression and immune evasion, and systemic metabolic disturbance also impairs physiological reserve and perioperative stress tolerance in elderly patients, representing a key mechanistic link between baseline host status and postoperative outcomes [31]. These findings indicate that metabolic biomarkers are promising candidates to refine prognostic prediction for elderly surgical patients with squamous cell lung carcinoma.

In addition, nutritional and inflammatory biomarkers have emerged as cost-effective tools for risk stratification across surgical oncology settings. Accumulating evidence has verified that preoperative serum nutritional biomarkers (prealbumin, albumin, transferrin) can accurately predict major postoperative complications, and combined inflammatory-nutritional indices such as platelet-albumin ratio and immune-nutritional prognostic ratio show robust independent prognostic value for long-term survival after curative resection [27, 28, 32]. Although these indices were initially validated in gastrointestinal tumors, the nutritional-inflammatory axis plays a conserved role in perioperative risk and tumor prognosis across solid tumors. For our study population, elderly patients with squamous cell lung cancer often have pre-existing malnutrition and chronic systemic inflammation secondary to long-term smoking and comorbidities, which are independent contributors to adverse postoperative events and inferior survival. Therefore, these easily accessible routine laboratory biomarkers have high translational potential for risk stratification in elderly lung cancer surgery. While our nomogram based on conventional clinical variables has demonstrated favorable predictive performance, the incorporation of metabolic, nutritional and inflammatory biomarkers in future studies may further enhance the precision of risk stratification and prognostic prediction for this vulnerable population, enabling more personalized perioperative management strategies.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this is a retrospective, single-center study, which may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of our findings to other healthcare settings and populations. Larger, multicenter prospective studies are needed to validate our results. Second, neither internal validation via bootstrap/cross-validation nor external validation in an independent cohort was performed for the nomogram. The reported AUC may be relatively optimistic, and further validation in larger cohorts is required to confirm the model’s generalizability. Third, our nomogram is based on conventional clinical variables, whereas contemporary prognostic frameworks increasingly integrate multidimensional data including radiomics, digital pathology, and molecular biomarkers, as demonstrated in recent digital biopsy [24, 26] and radiomics-based [25] prognostic studies. Future models for elderly squamous cell lung cancer should incorporate these broader data dimensions to further improve prognostic precision and capture recurrence biology more comprehensively.

Conclusion

Elderly patients with SqCLC undergoing radical surgery have a high incidence of postoperative complications, which are significantly associated with prolonged hospital stay, elevated readmission rate, and poorer survival in univariate analysis. Advanced TNM stage and poor ECOG performance status are independent adverse prognostic factors for OS in this population. The nomogram constructed in this study shows favorable predictive performance for 2-year OS, providing a practical tool for postoperative prognostic counseling, surveillance planning, and risk-stratified individualized management of elderly SqCLC patients.

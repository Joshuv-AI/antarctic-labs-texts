Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Teresa Chavez Capilla, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Mariano Lopez Romano, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Oriol Magrans, Iris Mangas, Silvia Mazzega, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea, Juan Manuel Parra Morte, Simone Rizzuto

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authority of the rapporteur Member State The Netherlands for the pesticide active substance Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002 are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative uses of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 as a fungicide and nematicide for potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and applied by drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module treatment on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnel) protected structures). The reliable endpoints, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment, are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed.Keywords: Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002, fungicide, nematicide, peer review, pesticide, risk assessment

SUMMARY

Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002 is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council, the rapporteur Member State (RMS), The Netherlands, received an application from FMC Agricultural Solutions A/S on 30 November 2018 for approval. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 4 September 2019.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was provided by the RMS in the draft assessment report (DAR) and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The following conclusions are derived.

The uses of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 according to the representative uses as a fungicide and nematicide for potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and applied by drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module treatment on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnel) protected structures), as proposed at EU level result in a sufficient fungicidal and nematicidal efficacy against the target fungi and nematodes.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to identity, biological properties of the microbial active substance and physical, chemical and technical properties of the representative formulation and analytical methods.

In the area of mammalian toxicology and non‐dietary exposure of humans, there are no issues that could not be finalised nor critical areas of concern.

In the area of residues, no data gaps were identified and the consumer risk assessment can be finalised.

In the area of environmental fate and behaviour, the information available was considered sufficient to complete the environmental exposure assessments needed.

In the area of ecotoxicology, issues that could not be finalised or critical areas of concern were not identified.

BACKGROUND

Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council 1 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) lays down, inter alia, the detailed rules as regards the procedure and conditions for approval of active substances. This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States and the applicant(s) for comments on the initial evaluation in the draft assessment report (DAR), provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS), and the organisation of an expert consultation, where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether an active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation (also taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation) within 120 days from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of 30 days where an expert consultation is necessary, and a further extension of up to 150 days where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 12(3).

Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002 is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of the Regulation, the RMS, The Netherlands (hereinafter referred to as the ‘RMS’), received an application from FMC Agricultural Solutions A/S on 30 November 2018 for approval of the active substance Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 4 September 2019.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002 in the DAR, which was received by EFSA on 13 February 2023 (The Netherlands, 2023). The peer review was initiated on 13 April 2023 by dispatching the DAR to the Member States and the applicant, FMC Agricultural Solutions A/S, for consultation and comments. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the DAR. The comments received were collated by EFSA and forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of a reporting table. The applicant was invited to respond to the comments in column 3 of the reporting table. The comments and the applicant's response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicant in accordance with Article 12(3) of the Regulation were considered in a tele‐conference between EFSA and the RMS on 15 March 2024. On the basis of the comments received, the applicant's response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicant, and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of potential effects on human/mammalian health, and potential effects on non‐target organisms.

The outcome of the tele‐conference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments, is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA should adopt a conclusion on whether Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002 can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation, taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment took place with Member States via a written procedure in March–April 2026.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of Bacillus subtilis strain FMCH002 as a fungicide and nematicide for potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and applied by drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module treatment on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnel) protected structures), as proposed by the applicant. THE MICROBIAL ACTIVE SUBSTANCE AND THE FORMULATION FOR REPRESENTATIVE USES

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 is a bacterium deposited at Leibniz Institute DSMZ‐German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbH under the deposit number DSM 32155.

There is not any area of concern with respect to the identity of the microorganism.

The formulated product for the representative uses for the evaluation was ‘F4018‐4’, a suspension concentrate (SC) containing 2.3 × 1010–2.3 × 1011 Colony Forming Units (CFU)/mL of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 and 2.3 × 1010–2.3 × 1011 CFU/mL of Bacillus paralicheniformis strain FMCH001.

The information on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses, including the co‐formulants in this formulation, was considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. None of the co‐formulants is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 2 however a co‐formulant is a currently approved active substance, and two co‐formulants are not approved active substances under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009. 3 , 4 A data gap for the composition of a co‐formulant was identified (see Section 10). 5

B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 was considered as a second active substance pending approval in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. Details on the composition of the formulations cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, however this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review. A proposal for classification of the formulation(s) according to Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 was provided by the applicant and assessed by the RMS (please see Volumes 3 CP of the RAR).

The representative EU uses evaluated were potato tuber treatment (field) and seed treatment (indoor) application on maize, sunflower, sugar beet and winter oil seed rape and drip irrigation and pre‐transplant plant module application on solanaceous and cucurbit vegetables and lettuce (field and greenhouse (permanent) and non‐permanent (walk‐in tunnels) protected structures) to control soilborne diseases (e.g. Rhizoctonia solani, Pythium ultimum, Phytophthora capsici, Sclerotinia sclerotiorum, Fusarium sp., Phoma lingam, Plasmodiophora brassicae) and nematodes (e.g. Meloidogyne sp., Heterodera schachtii, Globodera pallida, Globodera rostochiensis). Full details of the GAP can be found in the list of end points in Appendix A.

Data were submitted to conclude that the use of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 according to the representative uses proposed at EU level results in sufficient fungicidal and nematocidal efficacy against the targeted diseases and nematode pests, following the guidance document SANCO/2012/11251‐rev. 4 (European Commission, 2014).

CONCLUSIONS OF THE EVALUATION

General aspects

With regard to the mammalian toxicity information available for the formulation for representative uses ‘F4018‐4’, no acceptable acute toxicity studies were provided. With regard to the co‐formulants contained in ‘F4018‐4’, sufficient toxicological data was available for all components, but four (three present in limited amounts – below 1% – and one present well below 10%). For these four co‐formulants, the experts considered that the available toxicological information did not sufficiently address the genotoxicity and/or general toxicity potential of ‘F4018‐4’ over the short‐ and long‐term and that they might be considered for further assessment. The available information, including information from the existing uses other than plant protection products, under other regulated EU frameworks, did not highlight any concern (see Section 10).4

The availability of ecotoxicity data with the formulation for representative uses was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review experts' meeting TC 168 6 (see Section 6). The experts also discussed the data retrieval search and the available data for the individual components. Considering the reasoning agreed by the experts, no concerns were identified. Nevertheless, pending the outcome on identified missing data for toxicology for one of the components in the formulation for representative uses, further consideration to non‐target organisms may be necessary.

SPECIFICATION OF THE MICROORGANISM, PHYSICAL AND TECHNICAL PROPERTIES AND METHODS OF ANALYSIS

The following guidance documents were followed in the production of this conclusion: European Commission (2012) and EFSA FEEDAP Panel (2014, 2018).

The minimum and maximum content of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 in the microbial pest control agent as manufactured (MPCA‐AM) was 5.0 × 1011–5.0 × 1012 CFU/g. A clarification is needed at which manufacturing process scale the representative batches have been produced (e.g. pilot scale or industrial scale production) (data gap see Section 10). A data gap was identified for details on the chemical identity and composition of some of the materials used in the manufacturing process (see Section 10). Subtilisin was concluded as metabolite of potential concern (see Section 2) therefore, a data gap for its analysis in the MPCA‐AM together with description and validation of the analytical method for analysis was identified (see Section 10). The content of microbial contaminants in MPCA‐AM and MPCP were below the limits defined in the Working Document on Microbial Contaminant Limits (European Commission, 2012).

Multi‐ locus sequence analysis was used to identify the microorganism as belonging to B. subtilis species. PCR amplification combined with restriction digestion were used to obtain strain specific markers. It was not sufficiently demonstrated that the developed strain specific markers could distinguish B. subtilis strain FMCH002 from other closely related strains (data gap, see Section 10). It is noted that the genome of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was completely sequenced, however the raw data were not submitted or deposited in an international database/archive by the applicant.

It was demonstrated that the formulation for representative uses is stable when it is stored for 24 months at 25°C in its commercial packaging. A data gap for assessing suspensibility in a low temperature stability study was identified (see Section 10). It is noted that pourability of the formulation was outside of the acceptable limits (5%) therefore data on the residue after recommended rinsing procedures is required (data gap, see Section 10). In addition, a data gap for data on the adherence and distribution of the formulation to seeds was identified (see Section 10).

Acceptable methods were available for the determination of the microorganism in the MPCA‐AM and MPCP and for determination of the content of contaminating microorganisms in MPCA‐AM and MPCP. Residue definitions were not set for B. subtilis strain FMCH002 (see Sections 4 and 6), therefore, monitoring methods are not required.

BIOLOGICAL PROPERTIES OF THE MICROORGANISM

Historical background: B. subtilis has been used for many years as probiotic for animals and humans, for the industrial production of enzymes for food and feed, in agriculture as biostimulant and in plant protection products.

Natural occurrence: At species level, B. subtilis is a ubiquitous microorganism with a global distribution which can be isolated from a number of environments. B. subtilis strain FMCH002 is a wild‐type strain originally isolated from soya bean mash in the USA in the 1980s. Evidence also suggests that B. subtilis is adapted to life in the human gastrointestinal tract and therefore could be considered as a commensal organism in the human gut.

Target organisms: The target organisms for the representative uses are plant pathogenic fungi (such as Rhizoctonia solani, Pythium ultimum, Phytophthora capsici, Sclerotinia sclerotiorum, Fusarium spp., Phoma lingam, Plasmodiophora brassicae) and nematodes (such as Globodera pallida, Globodera rostochiensis, Meloidogyne chitwoodi, Heterodera schachtii).

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 does not exhibit a strict host specificity, as its growth is not dependent upon a host organism, but upon the availability of organic matter.

Mode of action: B. subtilis is known to establish partial endophytic associations with host plants. In this symbiotic phase, it can enhance plant defence mechanisms by triggering induced systemic resistance (ISR), producing volatile organic compounds that strengthen plants defence response and promoting overall plant growth. The direct antifungal activity is primarily associated with the production of lipopeptide compounds, which inhibit fungal germ tube elongation by disrupting the integrity of the cell membrane, ultimately resulting in cell lysis.

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 is an endospore‐forming bacterium, which can occur in both a vegetative state and a resting endospore phase. The endospores exhibit high resistance to environmental stress conditions.

Life cycle: The life cycle of B. subtilis consists of several phases: germination of the spore into a metabolically active cell, subsequent outgrowth and cell multiplication, and sporulation when environmental conditions become unfavourable. Endospores are highly resistant resting stages.

Strain specific data on growth temperature were not provided. Literature data showed that the vast majority of the Bacillus species have optimal growth between 25 and 40°C, and typically around 30°C. However, growth temperatures can vary appreciably both among strains within a species and between different species.

Genetic stability: Genetic transfer is a common feature of all Bacillus strains, but with the low frequency of genetic transfer described in the literature and, considering the fact that the strain FMCH002 does not contain any plasmids, it is unlikely that there will be any risk to humans, the environment or non‐target organisms.

Regarding the screening of acquired antimicrobial resistance (AMR), four genes associated with resistance to medically important antimicrobials were found in the B. subtilis strain FMCH002 genome; however, these genes are considered intrinsic to the species and therefore transferable at very low frequency. 7 In a phenotypic study B. subtilis strain FMCH002 showed sensitivity to all tested antibiotics (vancomycin, gentamicin, kanamycin, streptomycin, erythromycin, clindamycin, tetracycline and chloramphenicol).

Relationships to pathogens: B. subtilis strain FMCH002 is not closely related to known pathogens. Apart from the well‐known human pathogens Bacillus anthracis and Bacillus cereus, most species within the genus Bacillus are only rarely associated with human infection (see also Section 3.1).

Information on metabolites of (potential) concern:

The identification of (secondary) metabolites of (potential) concern for B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was based on a preliminary literature search, followed by a whole genome sequencing (WGS) analysis on the strain and by a subsequent literature search to corroborate the (eco)toxicological information for (potential) metabolites.

Based on the WGS analysis, B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was identified as having the potential to produce bacillibactin, pulcherriminic acid, subtilosin A, subtilisin, bacilysin, fengycin and surfactin(s). Based on the absence of relevant adverse effects for human health reported in the literature reviews for these metabolites (see also Section 3.2), the experts concluded that they can be considered of no concern. 8 Notwithstanding some limitations in the WGS analysis, the presence of the amylosin gene cluster can be excluded, whereas a putative gene associated with the production of subtilisin (regarded as intrinsic to the species) was identified. As subtilisin is considered a metabolite of potential concern (respiratory sensitiser category 1), it should be analytically determined in the MPCA‐AM and demonstrated to be below the threshold for classification (1%) in the MPCP (data gap, see Sections 3.2 and 10). A more detailed discussion on the relevance to mammalian health is presented in Section 3.2.

POTENTIAL EFFECTS ON HUMAN/MAMMALIAN HEALTH

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' TC 165 (14 March 2025). Effects of potential concern for human/mammalian health have not been identified from the available data on B. subtilis strain FMCH002, including medical data and acute toxicity studies. Metabolites of potential concern are discussed in Section 3.2.

Mammalian infectivity and pathogenicity of the microorganism and sensitisation effects

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 is phylogenetically clearly separated from human pathogens in the B. cereus group. In humans, B. subtilis is typically a commensal organism, though it can act as a weak opportunistic pathogen under certain circumstances (e.g. physical trauma or severe immunosuppression). The rare cases of foodborne diseases where B. subtilis was suspected were likely due to misidentifications of B. cereus, as the accuracy of identification is not always clear.

B. subtilis is included in the EFSA qualified presumption of safety (QPS) list (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2023) with the following qualifications: ‘absence of acquired antimicrobial resistance genes to clinically relevant antimicrobials’ and ‘absence of toxigenic potential’. Non‐GLP in vitro cytotoxicity studies were submitted to demonstrate the absence of toxigenic potential (in accordance with the recommendations of the EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014); no cytotoxic effect was observed in a Vero cells assay, in a boar sperm motility inhibition assay or in a haemolysis study on blood agar. It is noted that, under Commission Regulation (EU) No 283/2013, 9 all studies conducted with microorganisms related to their potential effects on human health must be carried out in compliance with GLP standards. Details on the screening for acquired antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes are provided in Section 2.

As the currently available methods for testing dermal sensitisation are not suitable for testing microorganisms, and suitable methods for testing respiratory sensitisation are also not available, all microorganisms are regarded as potential sensitisers. Therefore, the non‐specific labelling with the warning phrase ‘Contains B. subtilis strain FMCH002. Microorganisms may have the potential to provoke sensitising reactions’ is recommended.

Acute toxicity, pathogenicity and infectivity studies in rats (via oral, intratracheal and intravenous routes) were conducted with B. subtilis strain FMCH002, and clearance was sufficiently demonstrated. Based on the available information (i.e. history of use, no reports on adverse findings in the literature, QPS status, results from acute studies) it can be concluded that this microorganism is neither infectious nor pathogenic, and no further investigations into infectivity or pathogenicity are required.

Mammalian toxicity of the metabolites of (potential) concern

The identification and toxicological assessment of (secondary) metabolites of potential concern for B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was based on a preliminary literature search, followed by a WGS analysis on the strain and by a subsequent targeted literature search to corroborate the toxicological information for (potential) metabolites (see also Section 2). The outcome was discussed in a Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting. 10

The metabolite subtilisin, classified as respiratory sensitiser Category 1, should be demonstrated to be below the threshold for classification (1%) in the MPCP (see data gap in Section 10). Although neither levels below certain thresholds in the MPCA‐AM or MPCP (see Section 2) nor exposure to operators, workers, residents and bystanders during and after application have been sufficiently demonstrated, the use of personal protective equipment by operators and workers is considered a sufficient risk mitigation measure. The exposure of residents and bystanders resulting from representative uses is considered unlikely to be of concern. The RMS is of the view that the concentration of subtilisin in the MPCA‐AM is only relevant for determining whether classification for respiratory sensitisation (H334) is required and will not impact on the non‐dietary risk assessment.

The metabolite surfactin C exhibited no genotoxic potential in an Ames test, showed low toxicity potential upon repeated exposure, as well as a limited cytotoxic effect in non‐cancer cell lines. In an ECHA notification for surfactin sodium, the compound was proposed as reproductive toxicant Category 2 without comprehensive supporting information. Based on this, it was concluded that there is no toxicological concern for surfactins, considering the low levels of surfactin A in the MPCA‐AM and the anticipated low exposure from in‐situ production, not expected to exceed the threshold for classification as reproductive toxicant category 2 (i.e. ≥ 3%).

Considering the exposure of workers re‐entering the field after application, the use of specific risk mitigation measures (RPE/PPE) is expected to be sufficiently protective.

RESIDUES

The assessment in the residue section is considering criteria outlined in the following guidance document: European Commission (2015).

B. subtilis strain FMCH002 is not approved as a basic substance under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (Criterion I of the guidance), nor it is listed in Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (Criterion II).

Subtilisin has been identified as a metabolite of potential concern for B. subtilis strain FMCH002 (see Sections 2 and 3.2). However, since this strain can produce enzymes used in food (such as subtilisin), this metabolite is not considered to raise concern for dietary exposure.

Considering the representative uses, dietary exposure to B. subtilis strain FMCH002 and to its (secondary) metabolite(s) cannot be excluded; therefore, Criterion V of the guidance cannot be considered to be met. It can, however, be concluded that B. subtilis strain FMCH002 has no hazardous properties (Criterion III).

The available information on viable counts in or on the relevant crops after treatments according to the representative uses was not sufficient. Consequently, it remains unclear how natural exposure to B. subtilis strain FMCH002 compares with exposure resulting from its use as a plant protection product. The same conclusion applies to non‐viable residues. Consequently, Criterion IV of the guidance cannot be considered to be met. Uses involving drip irrigation up to BBCH 89 on tomato, eggplant, pepper, cucumber, melon and lettuce are considered more critical than uses involving a single application at BBCH 00. Treatments at BBCH 00 include applications on tomato, eggplant, pepper, cucumber, melon and lettuce performed at pre‐planting, in‐furrow treatments for potatoes and seed treatments for sugar beet, sunflower, rape seeds and maize.

Overall, considering that no hazardous properties have been identified for residues of B. subtilis strain FMCH002, the consumer risk assessment can be finalised. The RMS and one Member State also considered that no data gap regarding subtilisin is needed, as no foreseeable consumer risks were identified. Consequently, also in their view, the consumer risk assessment can be finalised.

Based on the available information, Criterion III of the five assessment criteria according to the Commission guidance SANCO/11188/2013 Rev. 2 (European Commission, 2015) for potential inclusion in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is considered to be met.

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

Satisfactory information has been provided in relation to potential interference of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 with the analytical systems for the control of the quality of drinking water provided for in Directive 98/83/EC (see specific Annex VI decision‐making criteria in Part II Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011). As these methods require pathogenic bacteria to be identified and confirmed as absent, it was considered unlikely that B. subtilis would grow on the selective media employed or give a false positive result for the specific reactions used to indicate the presence of Escherichia coli, enterococci or Clostridium perfringens that are used for analysis when controlling the microbial quality of drinking water. Hence B. subtilis will not interfere with methodologies used for such determinations.

Fate and behaviour in the environment of the microorganism

Specific studies on the persistence and multiplication in soil of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 were not available. Information was included in the dossier indicating that Bacillus species occur in soil at levels significantly above the levels that would result from the proposed use pattern of the formulation for representative uses ‘F4018‐4’ also when considering multiple applications of the product over consecutive crop growing seasons. Bacilli primarily occur in bulk soil in their inactive endospore stage, and B. subtilis strain FMCH002 endospores will persist under these conditions. Its vegetative cells (present when nutrient availability is sufficient, such as in the rhizosphere) are unlikely to compete effectively with other soil microorganisms and therefore are not expected to persist.

In specific investigations where the MPCP ‘F4018‐4’ was added to water (suspended in heat‐sterilised Dutch Standard Water with and without supplementary glucose, and incubated in the dark or under UV light for 10 days at 12°C), the CFU of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 declined to 0.6%–0.81% of their initial measured values after 10 days. This provides evidence that persistence and multiplication in natural water systems (where competition with other organisms absent from the test system would occur) are unlikely.

In air, B. subtilis strain FMCH002 will not multiply due to the absence of nutrients and will become non‐viable as a result of solar radiation. During and shortly after application, endospores and any vegetative cells present in the product may disperse through the air, which is considered the primarily mechanism for the organism mobility. Vertical leaching through the soil column is expected to be limited based on evidence from B. subtilis species.

The predicted environmental densities (PED) in soil and surface water covering the representative uses assessed are provided in Appendix A of this conclusion.

Fate and behaviour in the environment of the metabolite(s) of (potential) concern produced by the microorganism under relevant conditions

Information on the rate and degradation in soil or water and adsorption to soil of the metabolite that could be of potential concern, subtilisin, was not available. However, as outlined in Sections 2 and 3.2, the only hazard identified for subtilisin is sensitisation, which is not considered relevant for environmental risk assessment where population effects are the protection goal.

Based on the information provided in Section 5.1 regarding the capacity of vegetative cells and endospores to compete and proliferate in bulk soil and natural water systems, in‐situ formation of this compound is unlikely, except potentially within the rhizosphere where nutrient availability is higher. The possible levels of this compound in the MPCA‐AM (if produced during manufacture) is currently a data gap (see Section 10).

POTENTIAL EFFECTS ON NON‐TARGET ORGANISMS

Some aspects of the ecotoxicological risk assessment were discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168 in March 2025 (EFSA, 2026).

Birds and mammals

One study on birds with the active substance was available. The information provided was considered sufficient to finalise the assessment of infectivity and pathogenicity of B. subtilis strain FMCH002 to birds. The available data do not indicate that the active substance is infective or pathogenic to birds.

Acute studies on rats with the active substance were available. These data do not indicate that B. subtilis FMCH002 is either infective or pathogenic to wild mammals (see Section 3).

The available data and the WGS analysis indicate the potential for B. subtilis strain FMCH002 to produce various (secondary) metabolites of potential concern (see Section 2). The in‐situ formation of these metabolites is reported to be unlikely (see Section 5.2). Further information is needed regarding the levels of (secondary) metabolites of potential concern present in the test material (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for birds and mammals.

Aquatic organisms

No study was available with the active substance for any aquatic organisms. Studies with the representative formulation ‘F4018‐4’ were available for fish and aquatic invertebrates. Literature studies were available for algae. No study was available for macrophytes.

No inhibitory effects were observed at the highest concentration tested in studies on fish and aquatic invertebrates. A risk assessment based on a margin of safety (MoS) approach for the representative product was available. The assessment concluded that the margin of safety for fish and aquatic invertebrates was relatively large. Considering the study data, the absence of reported adverse effects in the literature, and the MoS approach, the available information was deemed sufficient and no concerns regarding infectivity or pathogenicity were identified. 11

The risk to algae and macrophytes for infectivity and pathogenicity was considered low in a weight‐of‐evidence approach. This conclusion was supported by the following lines of evidence: (i) the application methods limit the exposure to water, (ii) bacteria surviving in water showed a rapid decline, (iii) no adverse effects in green algae were reported in two separated literature searches covering the last 16 years and (iv) potential beneficial effects to algae have been reported in the literature. 12

No concerns were identified regarding the production of (secondary) metabolites of potential concern to aquatic organisms for any representative use (see Section 5). However, additional information is needed on the levels of (secondary) metabolites present in the test materials used in the ecotoxicology studies (see Sections 1 and 10).

Bees

A 25‐day contact and a 17‐day oral studies were available with the active substance. Both studies were limit tests with at least 100 times the maximum field application rate. No study was available with the representative formulation. Additional peer‐reviewed open literature studies together with B. paralicheniformis strain FMCH001 were also available. For wild bees, it was noted that no regulatory or peer‐reviewed literature data were available to perform a risk assessment.

A quantitative risk assessment indicated a high risk from oral exposure based on a margin of safety approach. At the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168 13 the experts considered that the contact exposure route from seed treatment cannot be entirely excluded for the representative uses in‐furrows, although it is considered less relevant than oral exposure.

Therefore, considering the information above, the limited exposure due to the application method (i.e. seed treatment, drip application and pre‐transplant applications), the high application rate in the oral study (i.e. 100 times the maximum field application rate), the evidence from the peer‐reviewed studies identified in the literature search and the absence of reported adverse effects, a low risk for bees can be concluded for all intended uses.

No concerns regarding (secondary) metabolite(s) of potential concern production to honey bees were identified for any representative use (see Section 5). Nevertheless, further information is needed regarding the levels of (secondary) metabolite(s) present in the test materials used in the ecotoxicology studies (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for bees.

Non‐target arthropods other than bees

Studies with the standard indicator species Typhlodromus pyri and Aphidius rhopalosiphi are not available. Instead, studies were available with the active substance and the representative formulation ‘F4018’ on Hypoaspis aculeifer and Folsomia candida. Survival was not significantly affected when F. candida was exposed to the active substance, but reproduction was significantly reduced after 28 days. For H. aculeifer, significant effects were observed on mortality and on reproduction in tests with the active substance and sterile filtrate controls. No effects were observed in the test with the representative formulation.

At the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Meeting TC 168, 14 the results comparison of the test with the active substance and with the representative formulation were discussed. The experts agreed that the different results of the tests are likely due to metabolites, either absent or present at lower concentration in the formulation.

A quantitative risk assessment based on a margin of safety (MoS) approach indicated a low margin of safety. However, considering (i) that the PEDsoil represents a worst case scenario (see Section 5), based on 20 applications simultaneously and (ii) that no effects on juveniles or adults of F. candida and H. aculeifer were observed in the studies with the formulation ‘F4018‐4’, the experts at the meeting agreed that a low risk can be concluded for soil‐dwelling arthropods for all intended uses.

No concern was identified regarding in‐situ (secondary) metabolites of potential concern production to non‐target arthropods other than bees for any representative use (see Section 5). Nevertheless, further information is needed regarding the levels of (secondary) metabolite(s) present in the test materials (see Sections 1 and 10). For the representative use in permanent structures and in line with EFSA (2015), minimal exposure to secondary metabolites is expected, hence low risk can be concluded for non‐target arthropods other than bees.

Soil organisms

No regulatory studies for earthworms were provided. Instead, literature data and a risk assessment based on a weight‐of‐evidence approach were presented.

Considering that (i) B. subtilis occurs naturally in soil, (ii) Bacillus spp. are part of the natural microbiome of earthworms, (iii) no indication of infectivity or pathogenicity relevant to field populations is reported in literature, waiving earthworms studies for B. subtilis strain FMCH002 was considered acceptable and a low risk was concluded for all intended uses.

No studies on the effects of the active substance or the representative formulation on soil microorganisms were provided. During the peer review, it was agreed that studies with B. subtilis strain FMCH002 are not necessary, and a low risk for soil microorganisms can be concluded for all intended uses.

Launching “Notes From the Epidemic Frontlines”—Elevating Real-Time Insights in Infectious Diseases
Authors: Krutika Kuppalli
Published: 2026

The landscape of infectious diseases is changing rapidly. Environmental disruption, climate pressures, urbanization, global mobility, vaccine hesitancy, and population displacement from conflict and natural disasters are reshaping the conditions under which pathogens emerge and spread. As a result, outbreaks are increasingly complex, unfolding in dynamic settings where clinicians, epidemiologists, laboratorians, scientists, communicators, and public health professionals must respond in real time. Yet, the experiences and insights of those working directly in these environments are often underrepresented in the scientific literature.

Recent events underscore this reality with urgency. In May 2026, the MV Hondius—a Dutch-flagged expedition cruise ship carrying passengers from 23 countries—became the site of the first documented Andes hantavirus outbreak aboard a vessel at sea. As of late June 2026, 13 cases and 3 deaths had been confirmed or suspected, with cases identified across multiple countries following repatriation, reflecting the inherent capacity of global mobility to transform a localized event into a multinational public health response. Simultaneously, on 15 May 2026, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda confirmed an outbreak of Bundibugyo ebolavirus disease in Ituri Province and surrounding regions. The World Health Organization declared this a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC), one of the highest alarms it can sound on 17 May 2026. This is the 17th Ebola outbreak in the DRC since 1976, and the rapidly expanding outbreak is unfolding against a backdrop of insecurity, population displacement, and highly mobile cross-border communities. These 2 concurrent events—one affecting travelers aboard a luxury expedition vessel, the other devastating already vulnerable communities in conflict-affected eastern DRC—illustrate the breadth and inequity of infectious disease risk in 2026 and remind us why platforms for rapid frontline knowledge sharing are not merely useful, but essential.

To address this gap, Open Forum Infectious Diseases is launching a new section titled Notes from the Epidemic Frontlines, dedicated to short, timely scientific communications from professionals directly engaged in outbreak response. As Section Editor, it is a privilege to introduce this initiative, which seeks to provide a platform for frontline perspectives to inform the broader infectious diseases community as outbreaks unfold.

Traditional academic publication cycles are not always aligned with the pace of epidemics. Detailed analyses, cohort studies, and retrospective evaluations are essential, but they often appear months or years after the most critical operational decisions have been made. In contrast, many of the most valuable lessons during an outbreak arise in the moment: unexpected clinical manifestations observed by clinicians, shifts in transmission patterns recognized by field epidemiologists, diagnostic challenges encountered by laboratorians, or cultural barriers encountered by response teams working in the field.

These early insights rarely find a natural home in traditional manuscript formats. Yet, they can play a crucial role in shaping clinical care, guiding research priorities, and informing public health strategy. Notes from the Epidemic Frontlines aims to capture these observations and experiences as they unfold, providing critical scientific context to emerging outbreaks while facilitating the rapid dissemination of operational insights across disciplines.

A defining feature of this section is its focus on the diverse professionals who contribute to epidemic response. Epidemics are not managed by a single discipline. Effective responses depend on collaboration across clinical medicine, public health, laboratory science, infection prevention and control, community engagement, policy, and many other sectors. Field epidemiologists tracking transmission chains, clinicians caring for patients in overwhelmed hospitals, laboratory scientists developing diagnostic strategies, and community health workers building trust with affected populations all contribute to the success, or failure, of outbreak response efforts.

By highlighting perspectives from across the response landscape, this section aims to highlight the operational and human realities of epidemic response. These reports will explore the biological, environmental, social, and health system factors shaping outbreaks, while also documenting the logistical challenges that often define frontline work: supply chain disruptions, workforce constraints, fragmented data systems, governance complexities, and the persistent inequities that influence access to care and preventive interventions.

Equally important are the ethical and equity considerations that arise during outbreaks. Infectious diseases frequently expose and amplify existing health disparities, disproportionately affecting communities with limited resources or fragile health systems. Capturing these dimensions is essential to understanding how epidemics unfold and how responses can be strengthened in more equitable and sustainable ways.

To facilitate rapid communication while maintaining scientific rigor, submissions to the series will follow a concise structured format. Authors are encouraged to describe the outbreak context, their role within the response, the distinctive features of the epidemic, and the drivers contributing to its emergence or spread. Reports will also highlight frontline response activities, operational challenges, cross-sector collaboration, and key lessons learned for future preparedness and response efforts.

The goal is not simply to document outbreaks but to transform frontline experience into actionable insight. By linking real-world observations with broader epidemiologic and health system considerations, these reports can help identify gaps in preparedness, highlight innovations in response strategies, and inform future research and policy priorities.

Importantly, Notes from the Epidemic Frontlines also seeks to elevate voices from settings that are often underrepresented in academic publishing. Professionals working in low-resource, fragile, and conflict-affected environments frequently encounter some of the most challenging outbreak conditions and generate some of the most valuable operational insights. Ensuring that these perspectives are visible within the scientific literature is critical for building a more inclusive and effective global health community.

Submissions will undergo expedited peer review focused on scientific accuracy, clarity, and relevance, and accepted reports will be published with open access to maximize visibility and impact. By accelerating the dissemination of frontline experiences, the series aims to strengthen global epidemic intelligence and foster collaboration among the many disciplines engaged in outbreak response. Authors are also encouraged to submit photographs that capture aspects of frontline response activities, provided they meet journal publishing standards, including requirements related to privacy, consent, and ethical considerations.

The emergence and reemergence of infectious diseases will continue to test the resilience of health systems worldwide. The recent Andes hantavirus outbreak aboard the MV Hondius and the Bundibugyo ebolavirus PHEIC in the DRC and Uganda are powerful reminders that novel outbreak scenarios demand rapid, real-world knowledge exchange alongside the longer timelines of traditional research. As these and future epidemics have demonstrated, timely information sharing and cross-disciplinary learning are essential to mounting effective responses. Notes from the Epidemic Frontlines is intended to serve as a bridge between those confronting outbreaks on the ground and the broader infectious diseases community seeking to understand and address them.

We invite clinicians, epidemiologists, laboratorians, public health practitioners, community engagement specialists, policymakers, and others working in outbreak settings to contribute their perspectives. By sharing these experiences, we can deepen our collective understanding of how epidemics are detected, managed, and ultimately controlled.

In an era of accelerating infectious disease threats, the insights of those working on the frontlines are indispensable. This new section provides a platform for those voices to inform science, shape policy, and strengthen the global response to emerging epidemics.

Dataset on physical-chemical and marine water quality variables in San Andrés Island, Colombian Caribbean
Authors: Juan Sebastián González-Guirald, Juan David Osorio-Cano, Andrés F. Osorio
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In situ measurements and laboratory analysis considering i) marine water quality measurements, ii) Physical, chemical, and heavy metals characteristics of desalination brine, and iii) Conductivity- Temperature-Depth (CTD) profiles, were conducted. For marine water quality, the data collection aimed to evaluate the distribution of salinity, temperature, and heavy metals both at the surface and close to the seabed and other water quality parameters at specific locations. For salinity and temperature, a salinity tester model HQ40d was used. Moreover, marine water and brine samples were collected and analyzed in a laboratory for multiple parameters, including heavy metals such as Cr and Cu, and the alkaline metal ion Mg. The data indicated that greater salinity levels are at the bottom and closer to the brine discharge point. As for temperature, lower values are prevalent near the bottom, while higher values are present at the water surface. Last, Conductivity–Temperature–Depth (CTD) profiles showed variation along the water column in salinity and temperature, especially close to the brine discharge. This dataset can potentially be useful for researchers, policymakers, and locals to raise awareness through data, modeling, and solutions for the water quality conditions under a brine and wastewater discharge on an island.

Background

The quality of marine water and its physical-chemical properties are essential for the sustainability of small island communities, such as San Andrés Island in the Colombian Caribbean. Pollutant discharges on the island significantly threaten vital ecosystems that provide crucial services, including food supply, coastal protection, and water purification. Among these pollutants is the wastewater from a submarine outfall, released without undergoing biochemical treatment, and the brine discharge, which is a byproduct of the desalination process, which particularly on the island utilizes reverse osmosis membrane-based technology and depends on two sources: brackish groundwater and seawater directly extracted from the coast. Although several studies have investigated the environmental impacts of brine on marine ecosystems around the world, a thorough evaluation of its transport, distribution, and effects on San Andrés’ ecosystems has yet to be conducted in further studies.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Study area

The study area is focused on the San Andrés, Providencia, and Santa Catalina Archipelago in the Colombian Caribbean Sea. Specifically, data was measured in the northeastern zone of Johnny Cay (Fig. 1b), where a new desalination plant is expected to be built in 2024, and in the northwestern zone, where two major discharges of the submarine wastewater system and brine from the desalination plant currently operating in San Andres Island influence the area (Fig. 1a and Fig. 3a).Fig. 1Marine water quality sample points in San Andrés Island.Fig 1

Marine water quality sample points in San Andrés Island.

Marine water quality

In August 2023, two groups of marine water samples were taken in Johnny Cay in the northeastern zone at point 45 (Fig. 1b). Parameters such as alkalinity, chlorides, conductivity, density, BOD5, soluble phosphates, nitrates, dissolved oxygen (DO), pH, salinity, sulfates, total suspended solids (TSS), temperature, fecal coliforms, and enterococci were analyzed in a laboratory for both samples, where Sample Group 1 was taken at 10:00 am and Sample Group 2 at noon (12:00 pm).

In September 2023, a marine water sample was collected at 9:00 a.m. at point 45 (Fig. 1b) on the coast of Johnny Cay. This sample was analyzed in a laboratory to assess the following parameters: arsenic, total cadmium, total calcium, carbonates, chlorides, total copper, total chromium, total magnesium, total mercury, nitrites, total lead, total potassium, total sodium, total zinc, and total hydrocarbons.

In November 2023, 28 temperature measurements and twenty-four (24) salinity measurements were taken at two different water depths. These points were previously determined to get more detail near the discharge zone which is expected to have the most dilution and mixing processes with a distance among points from 5 to10 m, while further away from the discharge a smaller variation in the water column results is expected, and then a larger distance among points of 20 to 40 m was set. Collection of water samples was done with a Niskin bottle and testing with an HQ40d Tester from HACH (Fig. 1a). Results showed values of salinity and temperature on the surface varied from 34.4 ppt to 35.5 ppt and from 30.7°C to 31.9°C. At the bottom, the salinity and temperature varied from 34.8 ppt to 35.9 ppt and from 30.7°C to 31.8°C.

Moreover, twenty superficial samples (30 cm below the sea level) and nineteenth deep samples (30 cm above the seabed with variable depths from 1.4 m to 5.4 m according to the bathymetry) were collected and stored for further analysis in a laboratory to measure chromium, copper, and magnesium concentrations. The results for the collected samples at the surface and at depth showed no significant concentration of chromium or copper, but high concentrations of magnesium closer to the brine discharge.

All the samples for the three field campaigns were collected in 200 ml containers and immediately placed under cold conditions using ice packs within an expanded polystyrene container, chosen for its excellent thermal insulation properties. Upon arrival at the laboratory, the samples were stored in refrigerators, maintaining a constant temperature below 4°C.

The water quality parameters were selected according to the impact on fauna and flora and key variables that should be considered for their importance for the marine environment given the known conditions about contamination sources in the study area (brine). For the in-situ measurements, the salinity tester HQ40d from HACH was calibrated according to the procedures recommended by the manufacturer using the salinity standard HI-700249 of 35.00 ppt. For the other water quality parameters analyzed in the laboratory, it was previously verified that the laboratories involved were certified by the Colombian regulatory institution, the Institute of Hydrology, Meteorology, and Environmental Studies (IDEAM, by its acronym in Spanish).

Physical, Chemical, and Heavy Metals Characteristics of Desalination Brine

Brine from both the brackish water desalination (BWD) process and the seawater desalination (SWD) process, which utilize reverse osmosis, are mixed (approximately 0.17 m³/s) and discharged near the coast of San Andres Island (Fig. 1a). In August 2023, in situ measurements and laboratory analysis was conducted on brine from the SWD process to determine various parameters, including alkalinity, chlorides, density, BOD5, soluble phosphorus, nitrates, dissolved oxygen, pH, salinity, total suspended solids (TSS), temperature, total coliforms, fecal coliforms, and enterococci. In September 2023, further analysis of SWD brine in the laboratory was performed to assess concentrations of arsenic, total cadmium, total calcium, carbonates, chlorides, total copper, total chromium, total magnesium, total mercury, nitrites, total lead, total potassium, total sodium, total zinc, and total hydrocarbons.

On November 6, 2023, in situ measurements using the HACH Salinity Tester were carried out on three brine samples: (i) from the BWD system, (ii) from the SWD system, and (iii) from a mixture of both (see Fig. 2). The salinity of the BWD brine was recorded at 36.2 ppt with a temperature of 29.7°C, while the SWD brine had a salinity of 44.4 ppt and a temperature of 31.7°C. The mixed brine, measured just before discharge into the sea, displayed a salinity of 41.4 ppt and a temperature of 32.0°C. During this sampling period, the BWD system was operating at full capacity, whereas the SWD system was functioning at 50% capacity.Fig. 2a) Brine discharge channel. b) Brine discharges from the brackish water desalination (BWD) and the seawater desalination (SWD) processes. c) Brine discharging into the seawater.Fig 2

Additionally, for the combined brine, samples were taken on November 6 and November 9 and then stored under suitable cold conditions and subsequently transported to the laboratory with refrigeration, with subsequent laboratory analyses performed to measure the concentrations of arsenic, cadmium, calcium, copper, chromium, magnesium, mercury, lead, potassium, sodium, and zinc.

CTD profiles

In August and November 2023, a Conductivity-Temperature-Depth CTD 48M from Sea & Sun Technology was used to monitor water pressure, temperature, conductivity, salinity, and turbidity profiles along the water column in both northwestern and northeastern areas. The northwestern area is a zone of interest because of the location of the two discharges from the island: the brine discharge and the submarine wastewater discharge. In the northeastern zone at Johnny Cay, there is interest in building a desalination plant, thus monitoring the pre-installation water conditions will define a proper baseline for possible environmental impacts.

In February 2024, a CastAway-CTD was used, to obtain pressure, depth, temperature, conductivity, specific conductance, salinity, sound velocity, and density data along the water column in 52 points (Fig. 3).Fig. 3CTD profile measurements in the northwestern (3a) and northeastern island areas in Johnny Cay (3c).Fig 3

In all CTD measurement campaigns, data collection was conducted from a boat, ensuring a consistent descent speed of approximately 0.2 m/s, which was maintained during both the descent and ascent phases.

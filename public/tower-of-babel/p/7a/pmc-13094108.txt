Diagnostic value of pre-operative serum procollagen type I C-terminal propeptide and procollagen type III N-terminal propeptide levels for post-operative atrial fibrillation in elderly cardiac-surgery patients
Authors: Weibo He, Dongmei Sun, Xinming Bai, Tao Zhong, Yuan Wu, Yuanping Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPost-operative atrial fibrillation (POAF) prolongs recovery after cardiac surgery. We evaluated whether two myocardial-remodelling biomarkers—procollagen type I C-terminal propeptide (PICP) and procollagen type III N-terminal propeptide (PIIINP)—predict POAF in elderly patients.MethodsIn a prospective cohort (August 2024 – January 2025), 113 patients ≥ 65 years undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting, valve, or combined surgery were enrolled. Pre-operative serum PICP and PIIINP were quantified. POAF episodes were continuously monitored for five days. Independent predictors were identified with multivariable logistic regression, and discriminative performance was assessed using receiver-operating-characteristic (ROC) curves with 2,000-bootstrap internal validation.ResultsPOAF developed in 40 patients (35.4%), peaking on post-operative day 2. Compared with non-POAF patients, the POAF group had larger left atria (45.3 ± 4.8 vs. 40.7 ± 5.1 mm; p < 0.001) and higher PICP (128.4 ± 27.1 vs. 103.2 ± 24.6 µg/L) and PIIINP (13.3 ± 3.9 vs. 9.8 ± 2.8 µg/L) concentrations (both p < 0.001). Age, left-atrial dimension, PICP, and PIIINP remained independent predictors (all p < 0.05). A model combining both biomarkers achieved an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.86, outperforming PICP (0.80) or PIIINP (0.78) alone. Bootstrap validation yielded a mean AUC of 0.85 (95% CI 0.79–0.90), indicating good internal robustness.ConclusionsElevated pre-operative PICP and PIIINP independently predict POAF in elderly cardiac-surgery patients, and their combination enhances risk stratification beyond clinical factors. Incorporating these biomarkers could guide targeted prophylaxis and improve peri-operative management; prospective multicentre validation is warranted.Keywords: Postoperative atrial fibrillation, Procollagen type I C-terminal propeptide, Procollagen type III N-terminal propeptide, Cardiac surgery, Myocardial fibrosis

Introduction

Postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) is a prevalent complication following cardiac surgery, with reported incidences ranging from 20% to 60%, depending on the type of surgery performed [1]. POAF significantly contributes to increased length of hospital stay (LOS), with affected patients experiencing an average extension of 2 to 5 days [2, 3]. Furthermore, POAF is associated with severe adverse outcomes, including a 2- to 4-fold increase in the risk of stroke, heart failure, and mortality [2, 4]. The economic burden of POAF extends beyond immediate hospitalization costs, impacting long-term healthcare resources due to the increased likelihood of recurrent AF and related cardiovascular events [3, 4]. Thus, POAF represents a significant clinical and public health challenge, necessitating improved predictive and preventive strategies [5].

Atrial structural remodeling and fibrosis play a critical role in the pathophysiology of POAF, as they contribute to the electrical and structural changes that facilitate arrhythmogenesis [6, 7]. Fibrosis, characterized by excessive collagen deposition in atrial interstitium, disrupts electrical coupling, increases conduction heterogeneity, and promotes reentrant circuits, making the atria more susceptible to fibrillation [6, 7]. Despite the significance of fibrosis in AF, current clinical prediction tools have limitations, primarily due to their modest discrimination and lack of incorporation of fibrosis markers [8]. Biomarkers of collagen turnover, such as Procollagen Type I C-terminal propeptide (PICP) and Procollagen Type III N-terminal propeptide (PIIINP), are of interest due to their potential to reflect a pre-existing fibrotic substrate using a simple blood test [9, 10]. Notably, higher preoperative PICP/PIIINP correlated with left atrial fibrosis on surgical biopsy and were associated with development of POAF [9]. However, earlier small studies have reported mixed results [11]. This gap underscores the need for further research to validate these biomarkers in larger, diverse populations and to explore their integration into clinical prediction models to enhance the management of AF and its complications [12, 13].

The present study seeks primarily to determine whether pre-operative serum levels of PICP and PIIINP independently predict the development of POAF in adults undergoing cardiac surgery. Secondarily, we aim to evaluate the incremental predictive value of these biomarkers when added to established clinical variables and risk scores, quantifying improvements in discrimination, calibration, and net clinical benefit. Exploratory analyses will investigate whether integrating additional imaging-derived atrial parameters or peri-operative injury markers further refines risk stratification across surgical subgroups.

Methods

Study design

This was a prospective, observational cohort study conducted at Yuebei People’s Hospital between August 2024 and January 2025. The study protocol conformed to the standards of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Yuebei People’s Hospital. All participants provided written informed consent. Confidentiality was ensured through unique participant identifiers and secure data storage.

Participants

Inclusion

Elderly adults (≥ 65 years) scheduled for elective or urgent—non-emergent—coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), valve repair or replacement, or combined CABG + valve procedures were screened.

Exclusion

1), prior documented atrial arrhythmia (atrial fibrillation/flutter/tachycardia), 2), permanent pacemaker or implantable cardioverter-defibrillator, 3), end-stage renal disease (estimated GFR < 30 mL min⁻¹ 1.73 m⁻²), 4), chronic systemic corticosteroids or amiodarone (> 30 days pre-op), 5), connective-tissue disorders known to alter collagen turnover, 6), emergency surgery (< 24 h from diagnostic angiography), 7), inability or refusal to give informed consent.

Recruitment flow

Of 130 patients screened, 17 were excluded (declined consent = 7; ineligible = 10), leaving 113 participants in the final analytic cohort (Fig. 1).

Participant enrollment

Blood sampling and biomarker assays

Timing and handling: On the morning of surgery (07:00–08:00 h) and after an overnight fast, 10 mL of venous blood was drawn into serum separator tubes immediately after radial arterial cannulation but before systemic heparinization. Samples clotted for 30 min at room temperature, were centrifuged at 2 000 g for 10 min, and serum aliquots (500 µL) frozen at − 80 °C within 60 min.

Assays

PICP was measured with the MicroVue™ PICP ELISA (Quidel, San Diego, CA, USA) and PIIINP with the Cloud-Clone™ SEA571Hu kit (Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturers’ instructions. All samples were run in duplicate; technicians were blinded to clinical data. Mean intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation were 4.7% and 6.2% for PICP, and 5.1% and 7.4% for PIIINP, respectively. Lower limits of detection were 10 µg/L (PICP) and 0.6 µg/L (PIIINP).

Outcome definitions and surveillance

The primary endpoint was new-onset postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) defined as ≥ 30 s of atrial fibrillation, flutter or atrial tachycardia documented on continuous 5-lead telemetry (Phillips IntelliVue MP70) reviewed twice daily by study nurses, 12-lead electrocardiogram performed each morning and on any symptom trigger, or device interrogation in patients requiring temporary pacing.

Rhythm monitoring continued until hospital discharge or postoperative day 7, whichever came first. Two board-certified electrophysiologists, blinded to biomarker data, adjudicated all suspected arrhythmic events; disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Secondary endpoints comprised: (1) intensive-care-unit (ICU) and total hospital length of stay, (2) in-hospital stroke or transient ischaemic attack, and (3) all-cause 30-day mortality.

Covariate collection

Baseline demographics, comorbidities, pre-operative medications, EuroSCORE II, operative variables (CPB time, aortic cross-clamp time, off-pump technique) and postoperative laboratory data (high-sensitivity CRP, IL-6) were prospectively recorded in a REDCap database with 100% double data entry. Transthoracic echocardiography within 7 days pre-op provided left-atrial (LA) diameter, LA volume (biplane method), peak longitudinal strain, and left-ventricular ejection fraction (biplane Simpson).

Statistical analysis

Continuous data are presented as mean ± SD or median (IQR) and compared with Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U as appropriate. Categorical variables are given as counts (%) and compared by χ2 or Fisher’s exact test. Normality was assessed using visual inspection (histograms/Q–Q plots) and the Shapiro–Wilk test. For variables with > 5% missingness, multiple imputation by chained equations (10 datasets, predictive mean matching) was applied; results were pooled using Rubin’s rules. Multivariable logistic regression was fitted with age and LA diameter forced into the model, while other candidate covariates entered through backward stepwise elimination (p-exit = 0.10). Linearity in the logit was confirmed with fractional polynomials; variance inflation factors < 3 confirmed the absence of multicollinearity. All analysis were performed using R (Version 4.4). Two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Between 1 August 2024 and 31 January 2025, 130 patients were screened; 17 were excluded (7 declined consent, 10 did not meet eligibility) and 113 were enrolled and analyzed (Fig. 1). Post-operative atrial fibrillation (POAF) occurred in 40 participants, yielding an incidence of 35.4%.

Patients who developed POAF were had larger left-atrial diameters (45.3 ± 4.8 vs. 40.7 ± 5.1 mm, p < 0.001, Table 1). Pre-operative PICP and PIIINP concentrations were higher in the POAF group ( 96.3 [84.6-113.2] vs. 84.7 [66.3–99.7] and 5.4 [4.0-6.5] vs. 4.7 [3.9–5.7], respectively, p < 0.001, Table 1). No significant differences were observed in sex distribution, body-mass index, hypertension or diabetes prevalence (Table 1).

On univariable testing (Table 2), older age, larger left-atrial diameter, higher PICP and higher PIIINP were associated with POAF. Each additional year of age increased the odds of POAF by 7% (OR 1.07, 95% CI 1.02–1.12). A 10 µg/L rise in PICP conferred a 30% higher risk (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.15–1.47). After adjustment for covariates (Table 2), age (adjusted OR 1.05 per year; 95% CI 1.01–1.10), left-atrial diameter (OR 1.12 per mm; 95% CI 1.05–1.20), PICP (OR 1.22 per 10 µg/L; 95% CI 1.07–1.39) and PIIINP (OR 1.16 per µg/L; 95% CI 1.05–1.29) remained independent predictors (p ≤ 0.02 for all). Net re-classification improvement (NRI) for the combined model was 0.38, respectively (Table 3).

Discussion

Our analysis demonstrates that pre-operative myocardial-fibrosis biomarkers—PICP and PIIINP—are not only individually associated with the onset of postoperative atrial fibrillation but also provide complementary prognostic information when considered together. After adjustment for age, left-atrial size and other conventional risk factors, each 10 µg/L rise in PICP and each 1 µg/L rise in PIIINP conferred 22% and 16% higher odds of POAF, respectively. Importantly, integrating both biomarkers into a parsimonious clinical model elevated the area under the ROC curve from a modest 0.72 to 0.86, representing a clinically meaningful gain in discrimination. These data suggest PICP and PIIINP as additive, mechanistically plausible predictors of POAF and suggest that incorporating measures of collagen turnover can materially sharpen peri-operative risk stratification beyond traditional demographic and echocardiographic variables alone.

Our findings extend and refine the existing biomarker literature on postoperative atrial fibrillation. Previous single-center cohorts that focused on inflammatory markers (e.g., IL-6, CRP) or natriuretic peptides reported modest discrimination, with AUCs ranging from 0.68 to 0.76 for POAF prediction after CABG or valve surgery [14–17]. By contrast, the combined PICP + PIIINP model in our cohort achieved an AUC of 0.86, and retained significance after adjustment for age and left-atrial diameter. The adjusted odds ratios we observed (22% and 16% increase in POAF risk per 10 µg/L PICP and 1 µg/L PIIINP, respectively) are larger than the 8–12% ORs typically reported for CRP or BNP in smaller series [14, 16]. Moreover, our biomarker effect sizes align with cardiac LGE-MRI studies showing that a greater extent of left atrial fibrosis is associated with a higher risk of arrhythmia recurrence after rhythm-control interventions [18, 19] ---. Recent work from the RACE V cohort using continuous rhythm monitoring has also highlighted the prognostic relevance of early POAF burden and identified clinical predictors of device-detected AF after surgery [20, 21].

The biological plausibility of PICP and PIIINP lies in their role as circulating peptides released during -- type I/III collagen synthesis, making them pragmatic surrogates of active extracellular matrix remodeling [10]. Atrial fibrosis is a hallmark of atrial cardiomyopathy and represents a key substrate for AF. Collagen deposition separates myocyte bundles, increases anisotropy, slows conduction, and enhances the likelihood of re-entrant activity [6, 7]. In the perioperative setting, transient triggers, such as inflammation, oxidative stress, and autonomic activation, may precipitate AF preferentially in atria with greater pre-existing fibrotic remodeling [1, 4]. Importantly, collagen turnover biomarkers are not necessarily atrial-specific and may also reflect concurrent ventricular remodeling. Ventricular dilation or fibrosis can increase left-sided filling pressures and left atrial wall stress, promoting atrial stretch and profibrotic signaling. Therefore, LV remodeling could plausibly act as an additional discriminant for POAF risk. Human left-atrial appendage samples from chronic valvular atrial fibrillation show diffuse fibrosis and gap-junction disruption that parallel our clinical observations [22]. In diabetic cardiomyopathy, TGF-β–driven secretion of PICP/PIIINP accelerates electrical remodeling and late sodium current augmentation, providing a molecular explanation for the independent predictive value we observed [23]. Electrophysiological mapping after myocardial infarction further links collagen-rich slow-conduction zones to re-entrant atrial tachycardia [24]. Collectively, these data support the concept that elevated circulating collagen-turnover products are a direct surrogate for the arrhythmogenic substrate that precipitates POAF.

Clinically, routine pre-operative measurement of PICP and PIIINP could enable a tiered prophylaxis strategy. Patients identified as high-risk might benefit from proven interventions—peri-operative β-blockers [25, 26] or short-course amiodarone [27–29], while sparing low-risk individuals from unnecessary drug exposure and its attendant bradycardia or hypotension [30]. Because a simple ELISA requires neither contrast nor specialized imaging equipment, biomarker testing is markedly easier and less costly than advanced modalities such as left-atrial strain or LGE-MRI, bolstering its potential cost-effectiveness in degenerative mitral regurgitation pathways [31, 32]. Finally, integrating collagen biomarkers into shared-decision-making conversations about anticoagulation after valvular surgery could personalize therapy by anchoring stroke- and bleeding-risk discussions in objective fibrosis biology [33].

Nonetheless, the work remains observational and thus susceptible to residual confounding; The single center study may limit the generalizability of our findings. Cut-off values for collagen-turnover markers could shift with different assay manufacturers, and, most critically, external validation has yet to be completed. We excluded the emerging atrial-enriched biomarkers (e.g., BMP10) that have been linked to atrial fibrosis and POAF risk in our study [34]. Future efforts should therefore focus on larger, geographically diverse cohorts to confirm transportability and to perform cost-effectiveness analyses of routine biomarker screening, followed by randomized trials that test whether biomarker-guided peri-operative prophylaxis reduces atrial fibrillation burden and downstream complications. In parallel, longitudinal studies integrating post-discharge rhythm monitoring with advanced atrial-fibrosis imaging could better elucidate the temporal relationship between collagen turnover, structural remodelling and late arrhythmia recurrence.

In summary, adding pre-operative serum PICP and PIIINP to conventional clinical variables markedly improves early identification of patients at risk for postoperative atrial fibrillation. These findings justify the incorporation of collagen-turnover markers into peri-operative risk-assessment algorithms. Adoption of such biomarker-enriched models may enable targeted prophylaxis, optimize resource allocation, and ultimately reduce the morbidity and economic burden associated with postoperative atrial fibrillation; prospective interventional trials are now warranted to determine whether acting on this enhanced stratification translates into improved clinical outcomes.

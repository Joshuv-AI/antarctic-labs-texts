Safety and Efficacy of Intramuscular Tixagevimab-Cilgavimab in Prevention of COVID-19 in Patients Who Are Immunocompromised
Authors: Rebecca Moon, Andy Tien, Joanie Chung, Rebecca Pinnelas, Roland Lee, Jennifer Hwang, Farah Brasfield, Amandeep Sahota
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPatients who are immunocompromised face an increased chance of severe COVID-19 infection compared with patients who are immunocompetent. However, vaccine efficacy for COVID-19 appears to be lower in patients who are immunocompromised. Tixagevimab-cilgavimab are monoclonal antibodies designed to enhance immune defense against COVID-19. Nevertheless, the safety and efficacy of tixagevimab-cilgavimab specifically in patients who are immunocompromised remains unknown.MethodsThe authors conducted a retrospective case study of patients who were immunocompromised and received tixagevimab-cilgavimab between January 3, 2022 to July 31, 2022 at Kaiser Permanente Southern California. All patients were monitored for 180 days following tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. Patients who were immunocompromised included those with solid tumors, hematologic malignancies, primary immunodeficiencies, recipients of solid organ or hematopoietic stem cell transplants, and patients undergoing treatment with immunosuppressive medications (eg, chemotherapy, high-dose corticosteroids, tumor necrosis factor blockers, and certain biologic agents).ResultsA total of 2352 patients who were immunocompromised were included in the study. Among them, 101 patients (4.3%) tested positive for COVID-19, and 13 patients (0.6%) required COVID-19-related hospital admissions. Notably, no deaths were reported within 180 days following tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. Additionally, 4 patients (0.17%) sought same-day medical care after receiving tixagevimab-cilgavimab. Within 30 days, there were 39 non–COVID-19-related hospital admissions (1.7%) and within 7 days, 11 hospital admissions (0.5%) occurred after tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration.DiscussionTixagevimab-cilgavimab demonstrated a low incidence of COVID-19 and COVID-19–related hospital admissions in patients who were immunocompromised, with no reported mortality. Furthermore, there were no significant adverse effects associated with the use of these monoclonal antibodies.ConclusionTixagevimab-cilgavimab exhibited a low incidence of COVID-19 and adverse effects in patients who were immunocompromised.

Background

Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) has affected more than 92.3 million people and caused more than 1 million deaths in the United States. 1 Immunocompromised patients, due to underlying disease and/or treatment, face an increased chance of COVID-19 with impaired immune defense. 2 Compared with immunocompetent individuals, immunocompromised patients exhibit higher rates of prolonged viral shedding, superimposed infections, and progression from upper to lower respiratory tract infection. 3,4

Since the initiation of SARS-CoV2 vaccination in December 2020, more than 260 million people in the United States have received at least 1 dose of the vaccine. 5 The 2-dose mRNA vaccines have been effective, preventing up to 85% of COVID-19 related hospitalizations and 90% of cases requiring invasive mechanical ventilation or resulting in death. 6,7 Clinical trials of the Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines demonstrated 95% and 94% efficacy, respectively, in preventing symptomatic COVID-19 infection. 8 However, these trials excluded immunocompromised patients, leaving uncertainties regarding vaccine efficacy in this population. Notably, patients who are immunocompromised continue to experience more severe COVID-19 outcomes compared to patients who are immunocompetent. 9 A prospective observational study published in March 2022 consistently showed lower vaccine efficacy in patients who were immunocompromised compared to patients who were immunocompetent. 7 Specifically, after receiving 2 doses of mRNA vaccines, the efficacy in preventing COVID-19 related hospital admissions was 90% in individuals who were immunocompetent but only 58% in patients who were immunocompromised, leading the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) to recommend a total of 3 vaccine doses as the primary series for patients who are immunocompromised. 10

Monoclonal antibodies offer passive immunity in patients who are immunocompromised, whose innate ability to mount an immune defense against viral infection is limited; these antibodies detect the targeted virus and inhibit its entry into cells, and therefore further viral replication. 11,12 Tixagevimab-cilgavimab are 2 neutralizing monoclonal antibodies for pre-exposure prophylaxis of COVID-19 in patients who are immunocompromised. 13 An ongoing phase 3 trial has shown efficacy in preventing COVID-19 with minimal adverse effects associated with tixagevimab-cilgavimab. However, out of the 5197 patients enrolled in the study, only 24 (0.5%) were immunocompromised, 14 limiting the generalizability of the study’s outcomes to patients who are immunocompetent at risk for severe disease. This study aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of tixagevimab-cilgavimab, specifically in patients who are immunocompromised. Efficacy will be assessed by the incidence of COVID-19 disease, COVID-19 related hospital admissions, and mortality. Safety will be evaluated by analyzing adverse events following tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration in both inpatient and outpatient settings.

Methods

STUDY DESIGN

The authors conducted a retrospective case series among patients who were immunocompromised who received tixagevimab-cilgavimab in Kaiser Permanente Southern California, an integrated health care system, between January 3, 2022 and July 31, 2022. Patients who were immunocompromised were defined as those with solid tumors, hematologic malignancies, primary immunodeficiencies, recipients of solid organ or hematopoietic stem cell transplants, and patients receiving treatment with immunosuppressive medications (such as chemotherapy, high-dose corticosteroids, tumor necrosis factor blockers, and certain biologic agents). Detailed eligibility criteria for each specialty department are provided in Appendix A. This study was approved by Kaiser Permanente Southern California’s Institutional Review Board on June 4, 2022.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was COVID-19 related hospitalization and mortality within 180 days of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. The secondary outcome was the incidence of COVID-19 in both outpatient and inpatient settings within 180 days of receiving tixagevimab-cilgavimab. The safety outcome measured was the incidence of adverse effects requiring medical care at Urgent Care, Emergency Room, or hospital admissions on the same day of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. Secondary safety outcomes included non-COVID-19 related hospitalizations within 7 and 30 days of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. No statistical testing was performed.

Administration of Tixagevimab-Cilgavimab

All recipients had a negative SARS-CoV2 test (PCR- or antigen-based) within 72 hours prior to tixagevimab-cilgavimab. Patients with hematological malignancies and stem cell transplants were required to have a platelet count of at least 50,000/µL within 72 hours of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. Both tixagevimab and cilgavimab were injected consecutively via the intramuscular route, and patients were monitored in the clinic for 1 hour after the injection.

Results

Demography

A total of 2352 patients received tixagevimab-cilgavimab during the study period from January 3, 2022 to July 31, 2022 (Table 1). The mean and median ages of tixagevimab-cilgavimab recipients were 62.3 and 65.0 years, respectively. The majority of patients were White (46.6%), followed by Hispanic (31.0%), and 53.3% of the patients were males. The most common co-morbidities were hyperlipidemia (75.0%) and hypertension (70.5%). Regarding substance use, 11.2% of the population were tobacco smokers and 16.8% had a history of alcohol use. Additionally, 25.2% of the population had a previous history of COVID-19 and 25.3% had an underlying obstructive lung disease, such as asthma (25.3%), and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (10.4%).

Incidence

Within 180 days of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration, 101 patients (4.3%) patients tested positive for SARS-CoV-2 (Table 1). Thirteen patients (0.6%) required hospital admission from COVID-19 pneumonia within 180 days of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration. Among them, 7 were lung transplant recipients, 1 was a kidney transplant recipient, and the rest were patients with hematological malignancies on chemotherapy or stem transplant recipients. The length of hospitalization varied from 3 to 11 days, with an average of 5.4 days. No deaths occurred among hospitalized patients with COVID-19 disease within 180 days of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration.

Adverse effects

Four patients (0.17%) experienced adverse reactions on the same day of tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration that required medical attention in the Emergency Department (Table 3). Two patients had a rash that responded to Benadryl and hydrocortisone cream. One patient had an asymptomatic hypertensive episode of 205/100 mmHg. One patient experienced nausea and emesis, which resolved with supportive management. An additional patient presented to the Dermatology clinic 7 days after tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration with local urticaria at the injection site, which resolved with a steroid injection (triamcinolone acetonide) and clobetasol topical ointment.

Adverse effects of tixagevimab-cilgavimab

Two patients experienced systolic blood pressures lower than 80 mmHg within 1 hour of tixagevimab-cilgavimab injuection. One patient’s blood pressure was 71/49 mmHg approximately 30 minutes after the monoclonal antibody administration, but the patient was asymptomatic and the blood pressure returned to 101/62 mmHg after 2 hours of monitoring, leading to safe discharge. The second patient had a blood pressure of 79/41 mmHg approximately 1 hour after the injection, but no further action was required as the patient remained asymptomatic, and previous blood pressures in the past few months were in the range of 90–98/48–49 mmHg.

Within 30 days after tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration, 39 patients (1.7%) required hospitalization, and among them, 11 (0.47%) occurred within 7 days (Table 4). Hospital admissions for treatment (eg, chemotherapy, bone marrow transplant; 0.47%, n = 11), procedures (eg, arteriovenous fistula placement, orthopedic procedures; 0.21%, n = 5), and admissions due to transplant rejections (eg, acute graft-versus-host-disease, elevated donor-specific antigen; 0.26%, n = 6) were excluded from this analysis. The most common indication for hospitalization was acute respiratory failure from SARS-CoV-2-negative pneumonia (0.26%, n = 6), followed by other infections (0.21%, n = 5; Table 4). Among the 4 patients hospitalized for neutropenic fever, 2 had received blood products within a few days prior to admission. One patient was initiated on chemotherapy 10 days before the admission. One patient presented 1 day after tixagevimab-cilgavimab with a fever of 100.5°C and was given ceftazidime for 2 days, discharged the following day with a 3-day course of Filgrastim.

Out of the 2352 patients who received tixagevimab-cilgavimab, 80 patients (3.4%) experienced infection within 30 days, including urinary tract infection, upper respiratory infection, and sepsis from an unspecified organism (Appendix B). Sixty-six patients (2.8%) sought medical care as outpatient for various symptoms, such as shortness of breath, fatigue, fever, and chest pain (Appendix C). Three patients (0.13%) experienced acute-on-chronic diastolic heart failure within 30 days.

Discussion

The data from this study demonstrate low rates of COVID-19 incidence and adverse effects of tixagevimab-cilgavimab in patients who are immunocompromised. The incidence rate of COVID-19 infection in recipients of tixagevimab-cilgavimab was less than 5%. Among those infected, only 13 patients (0.6%) required inpatient medical care for COVID-19, which was significantly lower than the COVID-19 related hospitalization rate of 25–48% previously observed in fully vaccinated patients who were immunocompromised. 15 Seven of the hospitalized patients were lung transplant recipients, who have an increased chance of respiratory complications from any respiratory infections in general. 16 Even among lung transplant patients requiring hospitalization, the hospital stays were less than 7 days for all 13 patients, and there were no COVID-19 related deaths. In comparison, at the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic, the in-hospital mortality rate from COVID-19 related hospital admissions was up to 23% 17 before vaccination and 10% in vaccinated patients who were immunocompromised. 18

Six patients (0.26%) experienced same-day adverse effects, which were adequately treated in the Emergency Room, and none required hospital admission, supporting the overall safety profile of tixagevimab-cilgavimab. Of these 6 patients, 2 had hypotensive episodes (systolic blood pressure less than 80 mmHg); however, the blood pressure measurements were not significantly reduced from their baseline and no further medical intervention was required. With these clinically unremarkable same-day adverse effects, it may be reasonable to suggest a shorter monitoring time (ie, 30 minutes) to ensure the monitoring of rapidly progressive lethal anaphylaxis symptoms without unnecessarily burdening medical resources and patient time with 1 hour of observation.

Within 30 days after tixagevimab-cilgavimab, 39 patients who were immunocompromised (1.7%) were hospitalized. However, it is difficult to estimate how many of these admissions were attributable to adverse effects from tixagevimab-cilgavimab. Due to their immunocompromised state, their infection rates are expected to be higher than the general immunocompetent population, 19 resulting in higher rates of hospitalizations even prior to the COVID-19 pandemic. 20 For example, neutropenic fever is rarely seen in patients who are immunocompetent but was the third most common indication for hospitalization within 30 days in this study group, a vulnerable population when on chemotherapy. One patient in this study developed neutropenic fever 1 day after tixagevimab-cilgavimab administration; however, the clinical course was unremarkable with a low-grade fever of 100.5°C, a short hospital course of 2 days, and no medical complications.

The strength of this study is the large sample size of 2352 patients in a real-world clinical practice setting, which increases the generalizability of these findings to the broader immunocompromised population. Previously, Kaiser Permanente Southern California was found to reflect the socioeconomic diversity of the entire Southern California census population, 21 strengthening the clinical relevance and applicability of this study. Furthermore, the integrated health care setting allowed for close follow-up in both inpatient and outpatient settings across all 15 medical centers within Kaiser Permanente Southern California, in addition to records collected from outside facilities, thereby increasing the accuracy in data collection.

The limitations of the study include the lack of a control group to compare the effects of tixagevimab-cilgavimab in terms of both safety and efficacy. Another limitation is the lack of specific immunocompromised diagnoses for each patient, as patients were recruited by various specialty departments based on the eligibility criteria. Therefore, the specialty departments that coordinated the administration of tixagevimab-cilgavimab (Table 2) may serve as a surrogate to represent patients’ immunocompromised conditions. Lastly, the incidence of COVID-19 confirmed by at-home self-testing kits may not have been fully captured in the database if the patients did not report their test results.

Overall, tixagevimab-cilgavimab is associated with a low incidence of COVID-19 and adverse effects in patients who are immunocompromised.

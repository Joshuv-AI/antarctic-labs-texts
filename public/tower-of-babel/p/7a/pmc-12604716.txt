Study of clinical profile in cases of secondary glaucoma at a tertiary hospital
Authors: Minu Ramakrishnan, Priyanka Singh, Rohini Shetye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Glaucoma accounts for 15% of global blindness. Secondary glaucoma, that is, glaucoma resulting from numerous ocular or systemic disorders, shows poor intraocular pressure (IOP) control with ocular hypotensive agents or filtering surgery in late stages. The present study was aimed to study the clinical profile of patients with secondary glaucoma at a tertiary hospital.MATERIALS AND METHODS:The present study was a single-center, cross-sectional, observational study, conducted in patients with raised IOP associated with an antecedent cause or ocular pathology visiting the department of ophthalmology, a tertiary care center in western India.RESULTS:A total of 70 eyes of 70 patients were included in the study, out of which 38 (54.3%) were males, and majority of patients were between 41 and 60 years of age (44.3%). Common causes of secondary glaucoma were inflammatory glaucoma (22.9%), psuedophakic glaucoma (17.1%), postkeratoplasty glaucoma (15.7%), postvitreoretinal surgery glaucoma (10%), steroid-induced glaucoma (10%), and lens-induced glaucoma (7.1%). Best-corrected visual acuity was <6/60 in 27 (38%) patients, 6/60 − 6/18 in 34 (48.6%), and better than 6/18 in nine (12.9%) patients. The highest recorded IOP was more than 50 mmHg in 11.4% of patients, 31–50 mmHg in 20%, and <30 mmHg in 68.6% of patients. Forty-nine (75.4%) patients had open angles, and 16 (24.6%) had angle closure on examination.CONCLUSION:Anterior segment inflammation is the most common cause of inflammatory glaucoma. Patients with silicon oil as a vitreous substitute are at a higher risk of developing postvitreoretinal surgery glaucoma.

Introduction

Glaucoma accounts for 15% of global blindness. The regional burden of blindness is highest for India (23.5% of global blindness), with at least 5.8 million blind due to glaucoma. India accounts for a minimum of 12.9% of primary open-angle glaucoma blindness and 12.7% of primary angle closure glaucoma blindness in the world.[1]

Secondary glaucoma, that is, glaucoma resulting from numerous ocular or systemic disorders, shows poor intraocular pressure (IOP), control with ocular hypotensive agents or filtering surgery in late stages. The causative lesion overshadows any symptoms or signs of the secondary glaucoma so that the diagnosis is often missed and almost invariably delayed.

The population-based study from South India reported a 0.7% incidence of secondary glaucoma, where the total prevalence of glaucoma was 2.6%, that is, a third of all glaucoma cases.[2] Although secondary glaucomas numerically represent a smaller percentage than the primary forms of the disease, they nevertheless cause significant ocular morbidity and visual impairment.[3] Early identification of the primary ocular and systemic diseases that predispose to the secondary glaucomas would play a significant role in limiting the burden of needless blindness. The present study was aimed to study clinical profile of patients with secondary glaucoma at a tertiary hospital.

Materials and Methods

The present study was a single-center, cross-sectional, observational study, conducted in patients with raised IOP associated with an antecedent cause or ocular pathology visiting department of ophthalmology, tertiary care center in Western India. Study duration was 1 year (May 2016–May 2017). The study was approved by the institutional ethical committee.

Sample size was calculated using OpenEpi software (Georgia, USA) at 13% prevalence of secondary glaucoma. We have included 70 patients presenting to our OPD during the period of 1 year.

Discussion

Secondary glaucoma refers to any form of glaucoma, in which there is an identifiable cause of increased IOP, resulting in optic nerve damage and vision loss. Despite its public health significance, there are limited data available on the prevalence of secondary glaucoma and the possible risk factors for the same.

We have examined a total of 70 eyes of 70 patients in this study, between the age 16 and 75 years. There were 32 (45.7%) females and 38 (54.3%) males. Male-to-female ratio was 1.18:1. In a similar study done at AIIMS, male-to-female ratio was found to be 2.2:1.[2]

Whereas according to a study done at Das et al., ratio was 1.96:1.[1] According to the study done at AIIMS, the most common age group involved in secondary glaucoma was between 41 and 60 years of age.[2] Similar findings were reported by Iqbal et al.[4] and in the present study.

In the present study, the common cause of secondary glaucoma according to our study was inflammatory glaucoma (22.9%), followed by psuedophakic glaucoma (17.1%) and postkeratoplasty glaucoma (15.7%). Dev and Ganesh[5] found that inflammatory glaucoma was the most common cause of secondary glaucoma (22.3%). According to Agarwal et al., uveitic glaucoma was found to be the most common etiology of secondary glaucoma.[6] Iqbal et al. found that steroid-induced glaucoma was the most common cause, followed by hyphema and uveitic glaucoma.[4]

Gadia et al.[2] noted that most secondary glaucomas had very poor vision of <6/60 at presentation in almost all types except steroid-induced glaucoma. Furthermore, IOP of >30 mmHg was present in more than half of the cases in almost all types except the aphakic group.[2]

Gonioscopy was possible in only 65 patients due to poor visualization. Among them, 75.4% had open angle configuration, and the rest of the patients had severe inflammation with posterior synechiae and iris bombe formation, leading to secondary angle closure glaucoma. According to the study done by Dev and Ganesh[5] 77.6% of patients had open-angle configuration as compared to angle closure.

Merayo-Lloves et al.[7] also reported that on gonioscopy, open angle was seen in the majority of their patients (80%) with inflammatory glaucoma. Gadia et al.[2] found that 73% of patients with postvitreoretinal surgery glaucoma had silicon oil as vitreous substitute and also overall, the majority of them had BCVA <6/60.

All cases of inflammatory glaucoma had anterior segment involvement. Similar finding was observed by Kok and Barton[8] as most of them had BVCA better than 6/18, and also highest recorded IOP was <30 mmHg in the majority of them.

According to Mandal and Bagga[9] pupillary block glaucoma is relatively more common after anterior chamber intraocular lens (ACIOL) implant as compared to posterior chamber intraocular lens (PCIOL). Stark and associates[10] reported a 0.3% incidence of pupillary block within 1 year of PCIOL implantation and 0.8% incidence after ACIOL implantation.

In case of steroid-induced glaucoma, all cases had a history of intravitreal triamcinolone acetonide injection given for macular edema, and most of them had BCVA between 6/60 and 6/18 (71.4%). All patients had the highest recorded IOP <30 mmHg. Gonioscopy showed open angles in all cases. We had no patients with rise in IOP, due to topical or systemic steroids in our course of study.

After intravitreal injections of 25 mg of triamcinolone acetonide, an IOP elevation can develop in about 50% of eyes.[11] Wingateand Beaumont[12] observed that approximately 30% of the study group receiving intravitreal triamcinolone injection developed a significant rise (≥5 mmHg) in IOP. Mohan and Muralidharan[13] stated that long-term use of topical and systemic steroids produces secondary open-angle glaucoma. Among the corticosteroids, dexamethasone and betamethasone both topical and systemic are more potent in produc­ing cataract and glaucoma.

NVG had central retinal vein occlusion as the leading cause (75%), followed by proliferative diabetic retinopathy. All patients had BCVA <6/60. Rubeosis iridis on anterior segment examination and angle closure configuration with neovascularization of angles was seen on gonioscopy in all patients. The majority had the highest recorded IOP between 30 and 50 mmHg.

There is a high rate of severe visual loss associated with the disease, and final visual acuity of hand movements or light perception is not uncommon.[14] According to Shazly and Latina full-blown picture of NVG is characterized by iris neovascularization, a closed anterior chamber angle, and extremely high IOP with severe ocular pain and usually poor vision.[15]

Kirkness and Moshegov observed that 53% of patients with postkeratoplasty glaucoma were aphakics.[16] According to Sekhar et al.,[17] 50% of post-penetrating keratoplasty glaucoma eyes had a visual acuity of 20/400 or less despite prompt management. Similar findings were noted in the present study.

In our study, blunt trauma was the mode of injury in all cases of posttraumatic glaucoma patients. All patients had hyphema, sphincter tear, and inflammation on anterior segment findings. Two out of four patients (50%) had angle recession on gonioscopy. Three out of four patients had BCVA between 6/60 and 6/18. Gadia et al.[2] stated that blunt trauma was the most common mode of injury in post-traumatic glaucoma. According to Dandona et al., BCVA in most cases of traumatic glaucoma was worse than 3/60.[18] Similar findings were noted by Sujatha et al.,[19] out of 23 patients of posttraumatic glaucoma with visual impairment, 16 had BCVA of <6/60.

All lens-induced glaucoma patients in our study were phacomorphic, with four out of five (80%) patients being females. Before cataract surgery, all of them had BCVA <6/60 and had highest recorded IOP between 31 and 50 mmHg. According to Prajna et al.,[20] phacomorphic glaucoma was seen in most cases of lens-induced glaucoma with a slight female preponderance. The total mean IOP was 42 ± 12 mmHg.

All pseudoexfoliation glaucoma patients in our study were females. It was seen unilaterally with open-angle configuration of gonioscopy. The majority had BCVA between 6/60 and 6/18. Two out of three patients had bilateral pseudoexfoliation over pupillary margin and anterior lens capsule.

According to Lamba and Giridhar[21] the majority of pseudoexfoliation glaucoma patients were unilateral with maximum patients having open angles on gonioscopy. Our findings are consistent with these studies. Arvind et al.[22] found no gender predilection, with more cases showing bilateral presentation and open angles on gonioscopy.

Limitations of the study included a small sample size. In certain types of glaucomas, such as inflammatory glaucoma and postkeratoplasty glaucoma, patients were under steroid treatment, making it difficult to identify the exact cause of elevated IOP.

Conclusion

Anterior segment inflammation is the most common cause of inflammatory glaucoma. Patients with silicon oil as a vitreous substitute are at a higher risk of developing postvitreoretinal surgery glaucoma. Furthermore, nonremoval of silicon oil adds up as a risk factor. Patients with ACIOL implant are at a higher risk of developing pseudophakic glaucoma.

Identification of the underlying cause is a priority in these cases as treatment of primary cause can help to maximize chances of therapeutic response and also prevent adverse outcomes. Adoption of preventive measures beforehand can help reduce the incidence of glaucoma related to them.

Comparative Evaluation of Artificial Intelligence Chatbots in Delivering Palliative Care Education to Intensive Care Unit Caregivers— A Cross-platform Analysis: A Brief Communication
Authors: Ram Singh, Prakash G Gondode, Sakshi Duggal, Sudhansu S Nayak
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsCaregivers of intensive care unit (ICU) patients with advanced chronic illness face significant psychological stress and information gaps regarding palliative care. Generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools like ChatGPT and Google Gemini may offer scalable, personalized educational support. This study aimed to compare the quality of academic content generated by these AI chatbots in terms of readability, sentiment, understandability, actionability, and expert-rated accuracy and completeness.Materials and methodsOn December 10, 2024, ChatGPT and Google Gemini (free browser versions) were queried with a standardized prompt on ICU palliative care for caregivers. Outputs were evaluated for readability (eight validated indices), sentiment polarity (online tool), understandability and actionability, patient education materials assessment tool for printable materials (PEMAT-P), and expert panel (n = 7) assessments of accuracy and completeness using a 5-point Likert scale. Statistical comparisons were conducted using paired t-tests.ResultsChatGPT and Gemini produced content of comparable readability (12.96 vs 13.21). Sentiment scores favored ChatGPT (+7.8 vs −39.1). Both chatbots achieved similar scores in understandability and actionability (91.7 and 80%, respectively). Accuracy scores were similar (p = 0.36), while completeness was significantly higher for ChatGPT (95% CI 0.08–1.07; p = 0.025).ConclusionBoth AI tools generated content suitable for caregiver education in ICUs. ChatGPT demonstrated more neutral sentiment and greater completeness, supporting a potential role in critical care communication and education of caregivers in the ICU.How to cite this articleSingh R, Gondode PG, Duggal S, Nayak SS. Highlights

This study compares ChatGPT and Google Gemini in generating palliative care education for ICU caregivers. Both generated content with comparable readability and 91.7% understandability. Actionability was 80% for both, while ChatGPT showed a neutral sentiment and higher completeness, highlighting its potential utility in ICU caregiver communication.

Introduction

Palliative care in intensive care units (ICUs) has progressed from solely end-of-life management to an integrated approach that addresses the physical, emotional, and spiritual suffering of both patients and their families. Caregivers frequently experience heightened levels of anxiety, depression, and decisional conflict, often surpassing the distress levels of patients themselves.1–3 Communication gaps, coupled with complex medical terminology, frequently impair caregivers’ understanding and satisfaction with care decisions.4,5 Structured informational tools, including brochures and leaflets, have been shown to improve comprehension and psychological outcomes among family members.6,7

The advent of generative artificial intelligence (AI), such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini, presents new possibilities for personalized health education. These tools are increasingly being explored for their ability to generate health-related content.8–10 However, concerns regarding accuracy, readability, sentiment tone, and contextual suitability remain.11,12 Given the emotional vulnerability of ICU caregivers, the quality and tone of educational material are critical to its utility. This study aims to evaluate and compare caregiver-directed palliative care content generated by ChatGPT and Google Gemini, assessing readability, sentiment, understandability, actionability, and expert-reviewed accuracy and completeness, thereby informing the potential role of AI in critical care communication.

Methods

In this comparative, nonrandomized study, we evaluated the quality of AI-generated educational material on palliative care for ICU caregivers using two publicly accessible chatbot interfaces: ChatGPT (Free version, accessed via chat.openai.com) and Google Gemini (Free version, accessed via gemini.google.com). The prompt “Generate educational material about palliative care in an intensive care unit for caregivers of a patient with advanced chronic disease admitted in an intensive care unit” was given to both chatbots separately on December 10, 2024, on Microsoft Edge version 131.0.2903.86 (Official build) (arm64). In the beginning, and while switching between chatbots, the browser history was erased. The educational materials generated were anonymized and assigned unique identifiers to ensure unbiased assessment. Expert panel reviewers were blinded to document sources, with Supplementary Annexures 1 and 2. No patient data or human participants were involved, and therefore, ethical approval was not required. The primary outcomes included readability, sentiment polarity, understandability, actionability, accuracy, and completeness of the generated text. Readability was assessed online using the eight most popular and freely accessible readability indices; sentiment analysis was conducted using an online sentiment analysis tool.13,14 Understandability and actionability were measured using the patient education materials assessment tool for printable materials (PEMAT-P).15 Accuracy and completeness were rated by an independent expert panel (n = 7), consisting of physicians with special interests in ICU, palliative care, health education, and communication, using a 5-point Likert scale. The panel was deliberately constituted to ensure different aspects that we aimed to evaluate. Expert panel reviewers were blinded to the source; tool-based assessments were objective. Descriptive statistics were computed for all outcomes, and comparisons between platforms were analyzed using paired t-tests based on data normality, assessed via the Shapiro-Wilk test. Data analysis was conducted using Microsoft Excel version 16.89.1.

Results

The educational content generated by ChatGPT and Google Gemini was analyzed across multiple evaluation parameters (Table 1). The mean readability scores, derived from eight validated readability formulas, were 12.96 for ChatGPT and 13.21 for Gemini, indicating a comparable reading level requirement. Sentiment analysis yielded a polarity score of plus 7.8 (+7.8) for ChatGPT, suggesting a neutral tone, while Gemini produced a sentiment score of minus 39.1 (−39.1), reflecting a negative tone.

The PEMAT-P indicated that both ChatGPT and Gemini achieved 91.7% in understandability and 80% in actionability. The mean accuracy score by the expert panel was 4.29 ± 0.49 for ChatGPT and 4.14 ± 0.38 for Gemini (p = 0.36). The mean completeness score was 4.43 ± 0.53 for ChatGPT and 3.86 ± 0.38 for Gemini, with the difference reaching statistical significance (95% CI for difference = 0.08–1.07; p = 0.025, two-tailed).

Discussion

This study compared two widely used generative AI chatbots, ChatGPT and Google Gemini, in generating educational material on palliative care for caregivers of ICU patients with advanced chronic illness. The comparison was based on readability, sentiment, understandability, actionability, and expert evaluations of accuracy and completeness. Both platforms produced material requiring a college-level reading ability (above 12th-grade level). Sentiment analysis revealed a neutral tone from ChatGPT, while Gemini content was negatively toned. Understandability, actionability, and accuracy scores were similar. ChatGPT's completeness score was significantly higher. These findings suggest that generative AI tools may aid critical care communication when applied with appropriate clinical oversight.

Previous literature has emphasized the importance of caregiver communication tools in improving comprehension and decision-making in critical care settings.1–4 Tools like printed leaflets and structured family meetings are known to reduce anxiety and improve satisfaction.5,6 The emerging use of AI for health education shows potential, though concerns remain about consistency, tone, and factual accuracy.7–9 Our findings align with preliminary reports suggesting that ChatGPT may outperform other models in terms of user-perceived informativeness and balance.10,11

Methodologically, the study used the most popular freely accessible readability formulas, the sentiment analysis tool, PEMAT-P, and expert consensus ratings to mitigate subjective bias. However, limitations include a relatively small expert panel, subjectivity of expert assessments, reliance on a single prompt per platform, as this does not capture the variability in responses, absence of a comparator or control group, and lack of input from the target population (caregivers). Sentiment analysis tools may not fully capture contextual emotional nuance, especially in sensitive healthcare topics. Additionally, statistical significance for completeness should be interpreted cautiously, given the modest effect size and limited sample.

The generalizability of these findings is promising for English-speaking, literate caregiver populations, but may not extend to those with lower literacy or limited digital access. Both platforms generated content with comparable readability and actionability, but ChatGPT's more neutral tone and higher completeness rating may enhance acceptability in emotionally sensitive settings. Artificial intelligence also carries inherent risks, including the generation of inaccurate or misleading content stemming from biases or limitations in training data. Accordingly, AI-derived information must be applied with caution to ensure reliability and ethical integrity. Future research should integrate perspectives of both caregivers and healthcare professionals to enable a more comprehensive evaluation of AI-generated educational material, using multiple prompts, with particular emphasis on consistency, inclusivity, and cultural appropriateness.

Conclusion

ChatGPT and Google Gemini produced palliative care educational content with comparable readability and understandability. ChatGPT displayed a more neutral sentiment and a higher completeness score.

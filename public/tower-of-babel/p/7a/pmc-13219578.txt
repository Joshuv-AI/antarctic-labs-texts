Hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling for power system frequency response
Authors: Yanlin Yi, Yongji Cao, Changgang Li, Xiaoyang Li, Jie Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The integration of large-scale renewable energy into power systems has led to a significant reduction in equivalent inertia and primary frequency regulation capability, posing severe challenges to frequency stability. To accurately analyze the frequency dynamic response trajectory of power systems, a hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling scheme is proposed. First, an extended system frequency response model that accounts for boiler thermodynamics is developed. Using this model, the time-varying characteristics of different parameters are analyzed, leading to the establishment of a hybrid modeling framework. Then, the auto-regressive moving average with exogenous inputs model is utilized to describe the input–output relationship between active power disturbance and system frequency dynamics, with parameters identified via the forgetting factor recursive least squares method. Moreover, the gated recurrent unit network integrated with an attention mechanism is employed to estimate the thermal state, thereby enabling the calculation of frequency regulation capability. Finally, case study results demonstrate that the proposed scheme can reduce the modeling error and improve the analysis accuracy of frequency dynamics, which verifies its effectiveness.

Introduction

With the accelerated transformation of the global energy structure, the installed capacity of renewable energy sources (RESs), especially wind and photovoltaic (PV) power, has undergone explosive growth1,2. The increasing penetration of RESs in power systems has led to a significant reduction in equivalent inertia and primary frequency regulation capability, posing severe challenges to frequency stability3,4. In conventional thermal power units, the primary frequency regulation realizes the dynamic balance of power supply and demand by rapidly adjusting the power output of the steam turbine, which is an important guarantee for maintaining the frequency stability5. However, the existing primary frequency regulation models generally assume that the main steam pressure of the boiler remains constant, ignoring the dynamic impact of the boiler’s thermal state on the frequency response, making it difficult to fully and accurately reflect the frequency regulation capability of the system6. In power systems with a high proportion of RESs, frequent power fluctuations force thermal power unit boilers to operate under varying conditions for extended periods, making the influence of the boiler thermal state on frequency regulation increasingly prominent7,8. Therefore, it is imperative to develop more refined system frequency response models to accurately capture the dynamic behavior and characteristic parameters of the system under disturbance conditions.

Several solutions have been proposed to handle the complexity of frequency dynamic response analysis using mechanism-driven modeling methods. The integration of stochastic differential equations into system frequency response models enabled the analysis of power system frequency dynamics under uncertain fluctuations9. Analytical methods were employed to aggregate multi-machine frequency response models, thereby simplifying power system dynamic analysis10. Besides, modal analysis was applied to construct a dual-machine equivalent frequency regulation model, revealing the spatiotemporal characteristics of power system frequency regulation11. In addition, a modified frequency response model was developed to identify the inflection points and instability balance points to achieve long-term frequency stability assessment12. The accurate simulation of frequency dynamic response in complex power systems was realized through the classification and aggregation of generation units with parameter equivalence13. Moreover, an equivalent linear model for frequency dynamics in hybrid traditional-converter power systems was constructed based on the linearization theory and least-squares parameter identification14. While the aforementioned methods, grounded in a detailed account of physical mechanisms, enable simplified modeling and effective analysis of power system frequency, they still fall short in addressing the impact of nonlinear variations in boiler thermal states on the frequency regulation process.

On the other hand, the data-driven methods data are gaining traction in the analysis of power system frequency dynamics. A hybrid framework combining physics-informed neural networks (PINN) was developed with an improved Kalman filter to achieve load identification15. An adaptive wavelet transform and a scale-adaptive convolutional network are employed to achieve highly accurate long-sequence time-series forecasting16. A deep learning model was constructed through offline parameter identification, and a model predictive control method was subsequently proposed for automatic generation control17. Besides, the system inertia estimation model was improved to enhance the assessment accuracy for minimum inertia and fast frequency response requirements in power systems18. In addition, an online non-intrusive estimation method based on impedance was proposed for the real-time estimation of inertia and primary frequency regulation capability19. Although these data-driven methods provide significant benefits for data processing and system analysis, a key drawback remains their lack of physical interpretability. Consequently, their simulations of frequency response under disturbance may not be fully reliable.

In recent years, the combination of data-driven and model-driven methods has gained traction in power system frequency analysis. The support vector regression was combined with an equivalent circuit model to achieve high-accuracy identification of mutual inductance and load in wireless power supply systems20. A hybrid model-data driven method incorporating an artificial neural network was adopted for effective diagnosis of open-circuit faults in power converters21. Besides, the DBSCAN algorithm was integrated with an expert system to accomplish precise cleaning of abnormal data in wind turbine power curve modeling22. Additionally, the improved primary frequency regulation model was combined with a cascaded EGAT-LSTM network to predict the frequency trajectory in weak-damping power systems23. Then, a hybrid model-driven and data-driven method was developed to construct transient stability constraints, enabling fast and interpretable preventive control for power system transient stability24. Furthermore, a method combining a shallow neural network and the Gauss–Newton algorithm was also utilized to address the challenges of limited measurement information and convergence difficulties in distribution network state estimation25. These studies demonstrate that the hybrid mechanism-data-driven approach, while ensuring physical interpretability, capitalizes on the strengths of data-driven methods for high-precision fitting to achieve accurate system characterization and prediction. However, when analyzing the power system frequency dynamics under disturbance, existing research often overlooks the impact of boiler thermal states.

As a key thermal component of thermal power units, the dynamic characteristics of boilers directly influence the unit’s frequency regulation capability. Current research on the modeling and estimation of boiler thermal processes primarily encompasses three approaches: physical modeling, data-driven methods, and hybrid techniques that integrate both. The nonlinear decoupling and optimal control of supercritical boiler-turbine units are realized by using affine nonlinearity theory and precise linearization26. Distributed parameter modeling and ε-NTU method were used to simulate the thermal properties of the heat exchange plane and monitor the ash accumulation27. However, this type of model has a complex structure and is difficult to meet the needs of online applications. Data-driven modeling methods rely on historical operational data and utilize machine learning and system identification techniques to establish input–output mappings. Combined with creep damage analysis and ResNet-LSTM network, the safety boundary between unit load and main steam pressure was predicted28. Based on the sub-space identification and multi-model predictive control, the range modeling and control of boiler-turbine units were realized29. The data-driven method is flexible and self-learning, but lacks physical mechanism constraints. In response to these limitations, mechanism-data hybrid modeling has gradually emerged as a research hotspot. The physics-informed recurrent neural networks were developed to accurately characterize the dynamic behavior of subcritical boiler-turbine systems30. However, Nevertheless, existing hybrid modeling studies primarily focus on the thermal parameters of the boiler, without systematically considering the coupling effects of boiler thermal state on frequency response.

Therefore, this paper proposes a mechanism-data hybrid driven method to integrate boiler thermal dynamics into the system frequency response model. First, an extended system frequency response (ESFR) model incorporating boiler thermodynamics is established to characterize the physical behavior of frequency regulation and analyze the time-varying properties of key parameters. Then, the static parameters of the model are then identified using an autoregressive moving average model with exogenous inputs (ARMAX) and the forgetting factor recursive least squares (FFRLS) method. Moreover, the attention-enhanced gated recurrent unit (GRU) network is utilized to estimate the boiler thermal states, which facilitates the calculation of the frequency regulation capability. Hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling framework for system frequency response

System frequency response model considering thermodynamic

The primary frequency response is a critical technology for maintaining frequency stability. Its core mechanism lies in rapidly adjusting the output of generating units through their automatic control systems to keep the system frequency close to the nominal value. When a frequency deviation occurs, the thermal energy stored in the boiler can temporarily supply additional steam flow to the turbine, thereby achieving a rapid increase in output power. However, the heat storage capacity of the boiler is limited, and the dynamic variations in main steam pressure can significantly affect the frequency regulation capability of the unit. With the integration of large-scale RESs into power systems, existing simplified primary frequency control models that neglect the dynamic behavior of main steam pressure are inadequate for accurately assessing the available regulation capacity, posing a serious challenge to frequency security31. Consequently, the traditional model that assume constant main steam pressure fail to accurately capture the dynamic characteristics of units. It is therefore essential to thoroughly account for the influence of main steam pressure dynamics on the frequency response.

The key parameters involved in the frequency response process can be classified into two categories based on their time-varying properties: static parameters and dynamic parameters33. Static parameters generally refer to physical quantities that change slowly or can be considered constant during frequency regulation. Dynamic parameters, on the other hand, primarily denote variables that vary significantly within the relevant time scale. In this paper, the static parameters to be identified include H, D, R, and FH. H represents the inertia constant of the synchronous generator rotor. A larger value indicates greater system inertia and a slower frequency change rate. D reflects the inherent damping characteristic of the generator rotor in response to frequency deviations. It influences the dynamic stability and regulation performance of the system, as well as the recovery capability from frequency deviations. R is the key parameter determining the steady-state frequency recovery capability of the power grid, describing the relationship between unit output power variation and frequency deviation. FH characterizes the sensitivity of the high-pressure cylinder output power to changes in main steam flow, directly affecting the frequency response characteristics and regulation capability of the thermal power unit. Regarding dynamic parameters, the main steam pressure PT is the core variable representing the dynamic behavior of the boiler thermal state. Variations in PT reflect the instantaneous imbalance between boiler steam production and steam consumption by the load.

Hybrid modeling framework

This paper proposes a mechanism-data hybrid driven modeling framework for power system frequency response, as illustrated in Fig. 2. By integrating both mechanism-driven and data-driven approaches, the framework achieves accurate estimation of both static and dynamic parameters of generating units. This integration enhances the overall accuracy and dynamic response performance of the primary frequency control model, thereby enabling early prediction of system frequency deviations and providing reliable support for frequency stability.Fig. 2Hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling framework for system frequency response.

Hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling framework for system frequency response.

In the mechanism-driven identification of static system parameters, the system response process is decomposed into two sub-models based on the physical mechanisms of governors, turbines, and boilers. The first sub-model captures the frequency response characteristics of the unit under system power imbalance. The second sub-model explores the complex nonlinear relationship between system frequency deviation and the mechanical power output of the unit, taking into account the thermal state dynamics. Corresponding ARMAX sub-models are constructed for these two dynamic processes to accurately characterize the dynamic relationship between system inputs and output responses. To achieve high-precision parameter identification, the FFRLS method is employed for online estimation of model parameters, ultimately identifying the static parameters of the system H, D, R and FH.

When estimating the key dynamic parameter PT using a data-driven approach, the GRU network integrated with an attention mechanism is trained on input time-series data. The dataset includes system variables such as PL, H, D, R and historical values of the dynamic parameter PT. The GRU network, with its gating mechanisms, can effectively capture both long-term dependencies and short-term dynamic variations in the time series. The incorporated attention mechanism further enhances network performance by emphasizing feature weights at critical time steps and strengthening its ability to represent complex nonlinear relationships.

Mechanism-driven model parameter identification

Parameter identification model based on ARMAX

The ARMAX model is an extension of the general system identification framework and a classical approach for time series analysis. Unlike the conventional Autoregressive Moving Average (ARMA) model, ARMAX incorporates exogenous input signals to drive the dynamic system, enabling a more accurate representation of the input–output relationships in such systems34,35. The construction of an ARMAX model for a dynamic system begins with the identification of the system structure, namely, the determination of the model order. In this paper, the Akaike information criterion (AIC) is used to select the appropriate ARMAX model order. The AIC addresses the overfitting problem by incorporating a penalty term for model complexity, while using the likelihood function to evaluate the goodness of fit36. For a given number of independent parameters, the model with the highest likelihood is considered optimal. Accordingly, the model order corresponding to the minimum AIC value is identified as the most suitable. The ESFR model, which accounts for boiler thermal dynamics, encompasses multiple coupled processes, including the response of power disturbance to frequency, the link between frequency deviation and mechanical power output, and the dynamics of the boiler thermal state. If the model were identified as an integrated whole, parameter aliasing would occur, making it difficult to simultaneously capture its multi-timescale characteristics and leading to significant identification errors. To achieve accurate identification of the static parameters, the overall system response is decomposed into two distinct components. For each component, an ARMAX model is constructed to characterize the corresponding input–output dynamic relationship. This decomposed modeling strategy enables a more precise description of the dynamic behavior of thermal power units under thermal state constraints, thereby providing effective support for subsequent model optimization and parameter identification.

The FFRLS method introduces a forgetting factor λ to assign different weights to historical data. When the forgetting factor λ is close to 1, the method retains a longer memory of past observations, resulting in smoother parameter estimates but a reduced ability to track time-varying parameters. Conversely, as the forgetting factor λ decreases, greater emphasis is placed on recent data, which enhances tracking speed at the expense of increased estimation variance38,39. Since the static parameters in the ESFR model evolve slowly during system operation, a larger λ is preferable to ensure estimation stability and accuracy. Accordingly, the forgetting factor λ is set to 0.95. To enhance the influence of key features on the prediction results of the GRU network, an attention mechanism layer is incorporated into both the temporal and spatial dimensions of the input data tensor. Temporal attention assigns learnable weights to each historical time step, evaluating the importance of individual steps and enabling the model to dynamically focus on those most critical for the current prediction. This mechanism effectively provides the GRU with an adjustable time window, allowing it to flexibly emphasize different historical moments based on the frequency characteristics of the input signal. As a result, the model achieves more accurate predictions of PT at the next time instant. Spatial attention, in contrast, assigns distinct weights to features from different input sequences at the same time step. Through weighted summation, it highlights the features most relevant to the current prediction of PT, thereby enhancing the model’s capability to process multivariate inputs. During frequency variations, the contribution of different features to the prediction may change dynamically. Spatial attention adaptively adjusts the importance distribution of these features in real time, improving the fusion efficiency of multivariate inputs and the robustness of the prediction.

This mechanism learns feature-wise importance weights from the GRU layer outputs, thereby strengthening the ability to dynamically extract critical data features during training and improving its accuracy in processing long-term sequential data41,42. Frequency regulation capability estimation based on enhanced GRU network

The neural network architecture designed for estimating the dynamic parameter PT comprises an input layer, a GRU layer, an attention mechanism layer, fully connected layers, and an output layer, as shown in Fig. 7. The input layer integrates five features: the current values of unit parameters PL, H, D, and R, along with the PT of the previous time step. A two-layer GRU structure is adopted to capture temporal dependencies in the sequential data. Specifically, the first-layer GRU returns the complete sequence, aiming to fully capture short-term dependencies and low-level temporal features in the input sequence, with a large model capacity. The second layer GRU also returns sequences, forming a bottleneck structure through the reduction of the number of units, forcing information compression, so as to abstract higher-level dependencies from the sequences output by the first layer. This hierarchical design allows the network to learn dynamic patterns at different time scales. Compared with single-layer GRU, the two-layer structure significantly enhances the expression ability of the model, and can more effectively model multi-scale characteristics in complex time series data. An attention mechanism is further incorporated during GRU training to compute attention weights, enhancing the ability to emphasize relevant feature relationships and critical time steps. This mechanism employs a feature weight matrix to assess the importance of different input features and a temporal bias term to weigh the significance across time steps, ultimately generating a weighted feature vector as the output of the attention layer. The feature stream is then passed through a three-layer fully connected network for progressive dimensionality reduction, with the final layer outputting the estimated value of the system dynamic parameter PT.Fig. 7Schematic diagram of the GRU model combined with attention mechanism structure.

Identification process of parameter H.

In the two-layer GRU architecture, the first GRU layer contains 128 units designed to extract temporal features from input data and return the complete output sequence. The subsequent GRU layer, configured with 64 units, further processes these sequences to identify the most relevant time steps, thereby enhancing the capture of critical dynamic information. An attention mechanism is then incorporated, employing the tanh activation function to generate attention scores for each time step. These scores are normalized via the softmax function to ensure the attention weights sum to unity, enabling effective weighted aggregation of temporal features. The resulting condensed feature representation is passed to a fully connected layer using ReLU activation to introduce nonlinear transformations and map features into the target space. Finally, the output layer, consisting of a single neuron with the linear activation function, is used to estimate the target system dynamic parameter PT.

To optimize the model performance, the proposed model is trained using the Adam adaptive learning rate optimizer, which dynamically adjusts the learning rate based on gradient variations, making it particularly suitable for handling dynamically evolving feature parameters. Additionally, a regularization mechanism and an early stopping strategy are implemented during training. The training process is automatically halted if no significant improvement in model performance is observed within 10 consecutive epochs, thereby ensuring the generalization capability. Conclusion

This paper proposes a hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling scheme for power system frequency dynamic response analysis. An ESFR model incorporating boiler thermal dynamics is developed and identified within the hybrid framework. The modeling approach employs the ARMAX and FFRLS method for parameter identification, and an enhanced GRU network with attention mechanism for dynamic state estimation. The main conclusions can be summarized as:Based on the physical mechanism analysis, the primary frequency response process of thermal power units is decomposed into two subsystems. ARMAX modeling is employed to characterize the input–output relationships of each subsystem, while the FFRLS method enables high-precision identification of key static parameters. Case study results demonstrate that the proposed scheme outperforms both RLS-based and WRLS-based schemes in dynamic tracking capability and parameter identification accuracy.By leveraging the capability of the GRU network in capturing temporal dependencies and the strength of the attention mechanism in extracting critical features, the complex nonlinear relationships between multi-dimensional features and the dynamic parameter PT are effectively modelled. Case study results demonstrate that compared with the other schemes, the proposed scheme achieves significantly reductions in the MAE, RMSE and MaxAE for PT estimation.Considering boiler thermal states, the hybrid mechanism-data-driven modeling scheme enables the accurate reconstruction of frequency dynamic response trajectories, thereby providing reliable support for power system frequency stability assessment and control. Case study results demonstrate that the proposed scheme generates frequency response trajectories that closely match actual measurements with minimal prediction errors.

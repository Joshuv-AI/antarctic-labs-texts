A 19-layer convolutional neural network for accurate COVID-19 detection in chest X-ray images: comparative analysis with pretrained networks
Authors: Xinyuan Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
One of the most conspicuous developments in the unprecedented worldwide epidemic of COVID-19 is the pressing demand for reliable diagnostic tools. Utilizing artificial intelligence (AI) and image processing algorithms, this work proposes a novel 19-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for accurate COVID-19 detection from chest X-ray images. This CNN architecture supports structure with single/multiple labels for three classes (i.e., for classification between layers like viral pneumonia, normal, and COVID-19) and four classes (i.e., lung opacity, normal, COVID-19, and pneumonia). Across the accuracy, specificity, precision, sensitivity, confusion matrix, F1-score, and other metrics, our model was compared to periutils net from the literature such as popular pre-trained networks(Inception, AlexNet, ResNet50, SqueezeNet, VGG19). Experimental results show that the proposed CNN outperforms existing methods, providing an effective diagnostic tool with the potential for clinical usage. This means AI algorithms as advanced as Cogito could start making decisions about COVID-19 and inform clinicians about how to handle the case.

Introduction

Coronaviruses are a big classification of viruses ranging from the virus of the common cold to the SARS causative agent. The initial version of Coronavirus was revealed in 1965 and was studied continuously till the mid-1980s. The structure of coronaviruses also has a typical RNA genome1. The earlier work confirms that people with co-morbidities and children with heart disease are at higher risk for coronaviruses2. Usually, the common cold is caused by the coronavirus3. Coronaviruses, which are the second most common cause of colds after rhinoviruses, are more common in winter and spring4–6. Most people become infected with coronavirus infections at some point in their lives, and most of these infections are not dangerous7.

The current version of coronavirus; which causes Covid disease (SARS-CoV-2), is a significant exception in this family and causes severe pneumonia in humans. The virus first appearance occurred in late Dec 2019 in China (Wuhan), after health officials noticed an increase in the incidence of pneumonia for no known cause in the city and gradually in other Chinese cities4,8–11. Since then, the disease has spread throughout China and other parts of the world12. Given the ability of the virus to transmit rapidly between individuals and the lack of specific treatment for the disease, it is critical to identify individuals in the early stages to quarantine them13. According to WHO (the World Health Organization), as of June 16, 2021, there are 177,340,599 cases of infection and 3,836,166 deaths.

Molecular testing has been introduced as a confirmatory test for this disease. Recently, there have been reports of false negative results in the initial molecular test of patients14. Some of the reasons that can cause false negative results in the RT-PCR test include problems in the sampling and transfer of samples, the efficiency of the kits used, and the quality of the test performed by the operator15,16. Chest X-rays, on the other hand, are used as a routine imaging tool to diagnose lung pneumonia17, which, given some common findings in the lungs of people infected with coronavirus, can yield rapid results18.

This non-invasive imaging technique, due to its high sensitivity in detecting coronavirus and its speed in producing high-quality images of lung tissue, can measure the involvement level of the lung with coronavirus in a very short time19,20. Chest X-ray images of the lungs to diagnose coronary artery disease show pulmonary masses in the lungs, multifocal spots, and interstitial changes with peripheral distribution21.

Image processing and machine vision in recent years have been used severally as auxiliary methods along with the physicians to improve the diagnosis accuracy22–26. Especially, in recent years, the deep learning application for the COVID-19 diagnosis has been exponentially increasing27. Indeed, this technology can severely analyze and inform COVID-19 probability to the patients and physicians to provide a cost-effective, fast quarantine and intensive care.

The paper has some tangible contributions to the scope of AI-aided COVID-19 diagnosis based on X-rays of the chest. First, we introduce a new, slim 19-layer CNN model, directly-purposed to the CXR-based multi-class classification (three and four-class cases), with best-in-class accuracy that does not depend on any form of transfer learning or pretraining.

Second, we also present a stringent ablation study that emphasizes the rationale behind the selection of depth, showing the trade-off between the complexity and the performance of the model using two publicly available datasets. Third, in contrast to other previous studies that use pretrained networks as black boxes, we provide the complete adaptation and fine-tuning procedure of benchmark networks (AlexNet, VGG19, ResNet50, etc.) to provide a fair and reproducible comparison. Fourth, we apply data splitting at the patient level and effective preprocessing (we use Gamma-based contrast enhancement and Min-Max normalization) to reduce data leakage and improve generalizability.

Lastly, we include the disclosure of clinical claims with a realistic explanation of constraints, requirements to actual deployment, and connect our technical contribution with responsible AI practice in medical imaging. Context

Several research works are proposed for improving the COVID-19 diagnosis accuracy and indicate good outcomes in this subject.

In 2020, Wang et al.28 proposed a personalized CNN (Convolutional Neural Network) for the COVID-19 samples diagnosis using the pictures of the CXR(chest X-ray). Moreover, they presented a novel CXR open-access benchmark COVID-19 database, namely COVID-19, that was gathered from 13,870 patient cases. Also, they explored how their method provides predictions to achieve deeper insights associated with COVID cases for a proper diagnosis of this disease from the CXR images.

In 2021, Khuzani et al.29 employed a dimension reduction technique to provide optimal features from the CXR images to generate proper features for injecting into the classifier and finally diagnosis of the COVID-19 samples from usual ones. A relatively small dataset of CXR images is used for training the classifier. They proposed that the proposed method can be utilized in different test cases.

Motamed et al.30 presented a RANDGAN (randomized generative antagonistic network) for the COVID-19 cases diagnosis and separated it from pneumonia and normal cases with no need for marks and training data. They used COVIDx dataset for the analysis. Transfer learning was used for the segmentation of the lungs in the dataset. At last, the method was then compared with conventional generative adversarial networks to show the method’s effectiveness.

Maguolo et al.31 investigated and compared different testing protocols utilized for the computer-aided COVID-19 diagnosis from the CXR images. They showed that using CXR images with the least of the lungs provides similar results. They assumed that numerousrecognition testing protocols were not fair and also, neural networks were no longer related to the COVID-19 presence. At last, they showed that generating a fair testing protocol is difficult.

Narin et al.32 analyzed five pre-trained CNNs for the diagnosis of the COVID pneumonia-infected cases in CXR images. 3 various binary categorizations with 4 classes, including COVID-19, normal, bacterial, and viral pneumonia were cross-validated. The outcomes of the simulation illustrated that the pre-trained ResNet50 model could provide the largest ratio of accuracy for classification.

It can be observed that utilizing CXR imaging has better results for the COVID-19 disease diagnosis. Hence, in the present study, the method is performed on CXR images to deliver better diagnosis results. However, the results of the literature works are well, but still, lots of works can be implemented on the system to provide reliable and accurate results. In this study, we work on a new architecture of CNN to provide a proper system efficiency for the COVID-19 diagnosis from the CXR images.

Methodology

This section of this research presents the methodology employed in this research work to detect the disease from chest X-rays.

The study pipeline

In the suggested methodology, we have trained 19 layers convolutional neural network. The suggested model contains four convolution layers. In the end, fully connected dense layers along with softmax and classification layers are considered with no repetition of convolution block. The suggested methodology pipeline diagram is represented in Fig. 1 in below.

Fig. 1The pipeline diagram of the offered methodology.

The pipeline diagram of the offered methodology.

Regarding Fig. 1, firstly, the original images of the X-ray have been preprocessed. In this step, all of the images turned into the same size for analysis. Then, contrast enhancement and normalization of data are utilized to improve the images’ quality. Then all of the images turned into the same format (.jpeg) to simplify the analysis. In the next step, the data is divided into validation, training, and test data, and all of the data are augmented.

The dataset split was done at the patient level, but not image level, in order to avoid leakage of data and statistically valid performance evaluation. Even though the initial metadata of both the COVID-19 Radiography Database and the CPCXR dataset do not necessarily contain explicit patient identifiers, we cross-referenced image filenames, image source, and other clinical annotations (as recorded in the dataset repositories and their publications) in order to re-construct patient level groupings wherever feasible.

In the event that patient-level separation may not have been established beyond a reasonable degree of certainty (e.g., anonymized standalone images), we safely modeled each image as a separate patient to prevent possible contamination.

The last 80% training and 20% testing (where the validation is based on training set in cross-validation) were then built by randomly assigning these patient units to its corresponding sets. This protocol will make sure that no information of a particular patient can be found in multiple splits and this would maintain the independence of the evaluations and it would help confirm the reliability of the reported scores of accuracy.

Then, the proposed CNN is designed based on the data and the final diagnosis has been done based on the proposed CNN.

Dataset description

The present work uses images of CXR from 2 public datasets including “Covid-19 Radiography Database”33 and “Covid-19 Posteroanterior Chest X-Ray Fused (CPCXR) Dataset”34. The Radiography Database of COVID-19 is a CXR images data bank for positive samples of COVID-19, Viral Pneumonia, and Normal pictures that are created via investigators from the University of Dhaka, Qatar University, Bangladesh in the company of some researchers from Malaysia and Pakistan, and the medical doctors. This database includes : Normal (10,192 images), COVID-19 (3,616 images), Viral Pneumonia (1,345 images), and Non-COVID lung infection (6,012 images, primarily bacterial pneumonia and other inflammatory lung conditions). All images are grayscale, originally of varying resolutions, and uniformly resized to 299 × 299 pixels during preprocessing. This dataset can be achieved by downloading from https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/covid-19-posteroanterior-chest-x-ray-fused-cpcxr-dataset.

The second dataset is CPCXR (Covid-19 Posteroanterior CER Fused) which is created via the fusion of 3 accessible databases for the public: RSNA (North America’s Radiological Society) (https://www.kaggle.com/c/rsna-pneumonia-detection-challenge), U.S. national library of medicine (USNLM) collected Montgomery country - NLM(MC) (https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/publication/pub9931), and COVID-19 Chest X-Ray picture (https://github.com/ieee8023/covid-chestxray-dataset). These datasets are marked by expert physicians. The dataset includes examples of illnesses categorized as Normal (533 images), COVID-19 (1,108 images), Tuberculosis (358 images), and Other Pneumonia (2,515 images, including bacterial and viral cases). These images were standardized to 224 × 224 pixels. All images were labeled by expert radiologists or clinicians, and representative examples are shown in Fig. 2.

Contrast enhancement

To extract detailed and clear information from the low-contrast regions in CXR pictures, we must enhance the images’ contrast. In chest X-ray image enhancement, different methods like edge sharpening, filtering, and noise suppression have been considered35. In contrast enhancement, the range of brightness has been stretched in an image to advance the general or local contrast of the image to provide an image with higher details. In this study, Gamma correction has been used for contrast enhancement.

Gamma correction is a suitable technique for removing the distortion from the X-ray images. The Gamma correction is based on histogram processing to enhance the image contrast quality using an adjustable parameter, Gamma (γ)36. The Gamma correction is a nonlinear filter that modifies the image brilliance using the Gamma predefined factor. Pooling layer

This layer reduces the spatial size in width and height (not depth). Accordingly, this process is used to subsample the input image and decrease the number of parameters, consequently decreasing the volume of the calculations and increasing the network speed. The represented work utilizes 2 × 2 Maximum Pooling with [0 0 0 0] padding and [2 2] pace. The process of Max-pooling utilizes the feature maps’ highest amount to shrink the produced neurons. The mean pooling layer is also utilized before the FC (Connected) layer to reduce the data to one dimension.

Fully connected (FC) layer

A channel fully connected layer has been utilized after the pooling and convolution layers. FC layer determines the features that mostly match a specific class. It works on high-level features with specific weightiness. Indeed, this layer affords the exact probabilities for the diverse classes by producing the weights with the earlier layer. The outcomes are categorized using the function of activation.

In order to strictly prove the use of the 19-layer architecture, we performed an ablation experiment comparing the model versions of 9, 13, 16, 19, and 22 layers on CPCXR and COVID-19 Radiography datasets. Validation accuracy, training time, the number of parameters and overfitting evidence were used to measure performance. It was found that accuracy increased steadily with increasing depth (80.0% to 98.4% on CPCXR and 74.0% to 97.5% on Radiography), but the additional accuracy increase was smaller (less than 0.5% accuracy) at depths above 19, and was accompanied by a very steep increase in the number of parameters (nearly 1.2 M to nearly 2.7 M parameter count) and training time (more than 80% increase). Furthermore, the 22-layer model was characterized by the low instability of the validation loss, which indicated the development of overfitting. The 19-layer structure therefore is the structure that provides optimal trade-off, with a high level of diagnostic accuracy and guaranteeing that the computational efficiency and model generalizability is maintained and thus the structure is adopted due to its principled nature in this study. The activation function used in the suggested architecture is Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) that follows each convolutional layer in order to add non-linearity to the system and eliminate the vanishing gradient issue. ReLU makes negative activations zero and leaves positive values unchanged, both of which enhance training stability and speed up convergence.

The final fully connected (classification) layer only involves the use of softmax to transform raw outputs into probabilities representing each of the classes that add up to one and therefore allow multi-class interpretation. The reference to the Sigmoid function in the previous drafts was accidental; there was no concealed layer of the final model that referred to it. This architecture is consistent with the conventional CNN usage patterns and the ReLU activation across all of the feature extraction layers with Softmax in the output, which guarantees efficiency and interpretability.

Pre‑trained models

To provide a proper analysis of the proposed method, we used some Transfer Learning-based pre-trained networks. Transfer learning (TL) is a ML (machine learning) technique where, a model development is implemented for a task that is restated as the model beginning point in another task. TL is by far the most common method of DL (deep learning)41. For example, in this method, pre-trained models have been utilized as a beginning point in computer images. TL is employed for extending pictures out of the ImageNet database. This only happens with a pre-trained (pt) model. Thus, we employ a model of fine-tuning derived from the modification of a pt model. We do not aim to change the weights too much and soon because we consider that the network is already well-trained. When change occurs, we usually utilize a rate of learning that is lower compared to that used in the basic model tutorial. In the presented research, deep CNN-based Alexnet, GoogLeNet (Inception), ResNet50, Squeezenet, and VGG19 models are utilized for the COVID-19 diagnosis in CXR images for 2different datasets with three and four different classes.

All pre-trained models were adapted to our binary and multi-class CXR classification tasks as follows: (1) input layers had the single channel replicated across three channels, (2) final fully connected (classifier) layer was replaced with a new dense layer with the same number of classes (3 in the CXR), (4) all convolutional weights of the backbone were frozen and only the new classifier head was trained during 10 epochs using the Adam optimizer (learning rate = 1e −4); (5) the entire network was then fine-tuned again over 2 The pre-trained models performed poorly on the competition of our custom 19-layer CNN, despite this delicate adjustment. We can put this down to the architectural simplicity and task-specific inductive biases of the model we used, which was simply designed to operate on spatial and textural properties of CXR images, avoiding the representational overhead and domain misalignment of ImageNet-pretrained networks being trained with images of natural color. This custom architecture allows more effective learning using less medical data without using extensive pre-training, which explains the competitive performance of our lightweight architecture.

All of the pre-trained networks are realized by employing the Deep Learning Toolbox to weather the inadequate time of training and data.

All pre-trained models (AlexNet, VGG19, GoogLeNet (Inception), ResNet50 source and SqueezeNet) were fine-tuned on a standardized two-stage protocol in order to make a fair and rigorous comparison. ImageNet-pretrained weights were used to initialize all models, adapting them to our CXR classification task as follows: (1) the input images were turned into three-channel images by converting grayscale images to channels and (2) the original classifier head was replaced with a new fully connected layer matching the number of target classes (4 for the COVID-19 Radiography Database, 3 for CPCXR); (3) in Stage 1, only the new classifier layer was trained for 15 epochs using the Adam optimizer (learning rate = 1 × 10⁻⁴, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999), batch size = 32, with the backbone frozen; (4) in Stage 2, the entire network was unfrozen and fine-tuned for 25 additional epochs with a reduced learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵ and the same optimizer settings. The same augmentation pipeline (random rotation ± 20°, horizontal flip, zoom, and gamma contrast adjustment) and train/val/test split (80/10/10 at patient level) applied to the proposed CNN were used for all baselines. Early stopping (patience = 5 epochs) and ReduceLROnPlateau (factor = 0.5, patience = 3) were implemented to prevent overfitting. This meticulous tuning ensures that performance differences reflect architectural suitability rather than suboptimal baseline implementation.

Alexnet

AlexNet has been considered the 1 st deep architecture to be presented by Hinton, Geoffrey et al. AlexNet architecture is straightforward but powerful and paved the way for great investigation without which there would be no in-depth learning of what it is now.

When we open AlexNet, its architecture seems straightforward with complex layers placed on top of other layers and entirely linked to the layers situated on top. This is a very simple architecture, the concept of which first appeared in the 80s. The main characteristics of this model that make it different are the task velocity and the use of a “GPU” for learning.

GoogLeNet

In GoogLeNet, a module named Inception, was designed via the attitude of developing the parameters of network hyper into the learnable factor. A pooling filter and 3 CFs (convolutional filters) with diverse dimensions are employed on the prior layer in Inception, then the outcomes are put together as a tensor. In this case, the network can decide in the next layer which filter it wants to use and to what extent. The size of convolutional filters is 1 × 1, 3 × 3, and 5 × 5 and the polishing filter with 3 × 3 size is applied to it. In this network, by placing two layers of polishing at the beginning of the work, the learnable parameters of the problem are greatly reduced. In the following, 9 layers of the Inception module are used. More details can be achieved by42.

ResNet50(Residual Network)

This network was provided by Microsoft. In ResNet50, communication out of the convolutional arrangement has been provided next to the network between layers to transfer the inputs of the former layer to the following one without mediators. Every layer’s error in the backpropagation step is transferred to the former layer; accordingly, the network becomes capable of faster training and deepening. Such associates have been addressed skip connections. Moreover, the resulting arrangement is addressed Residual block. With such a trick, they were able to train a 152-layer network.

Squeezenet

SqueezeNet architecture is a very powerful architecture that is very efficient in places with little space, including cell phones. This architecture only takes up about 5 MB of space, while the “inception” model takes up about 100 MB. This big change is possible thanks to a special architecture called the fire module.

VGG19

When it comes to complexity, this network is simpler compared to AlexNet because it lessens the HyperParameters’ quantity; for that reason, it is very common and desirable. In VGG CONV is a 3 × 3 filter with 3 and same stride and POOL means 2 × 2 pulling, with stride 2.

Discussions

Because of the rapid and global growth of COVID-19, there is a need for a fast and accurate diagnosis of this disease. In the present study, a new 19-layer CNN model is proposed to separate COVID-19 patients from Viral Pneumonia, Lung Opacity, and Normal in CXR pictures in two different datasets.

Due to the proper and efficient results of the CXR images, they are utilized in most hospitals. The proposed CNN network was trained, validated, and tested by 21,165 images from the Covid-19 Radiography Database and by 4514 images fromCPCXR dataset. 70% of the data are used in the training process and 30% of them are employed for validation and test.

The CXR images have appropriate and effective outcomes, hence they are used in most hospitals. The CNN type of network was trained, validated, and tested on 21,165 Covid-19 Radiography Database images and 4,514 CPCXR database images, 80% of which were used in training and 20% of which were used in validation and testing.

The approach has an accuracy of 97.51% and 98.40% on Covid-19 Radiography Database and CPCXR dataset respectively- the highest accuracy of all the models evaluated and the highest accuracy of all models used. The results of the comparison prove that the proposed CNN is superior to other pre-trained networks which contributes to its possible acceptability as a valid clinical decision-support system.

The 19-layer CNN with a high accuracy of 98.40% with CPCXR, 97.51% with the COVID-19 Radiography Database is not only characterized by high discriminative ability, but also indicates a high level of architectural correspondence with the visual features of chest X-rays. CXRs unlike natural images are characterised by low inter-class texture variance and high variability within classes as a result of variations in imaging equipment, patient positioning, and disease progression.

The proposed CNN, which is relatively shallow compared to such models as VGG19 or ResNet50, is not being over-parameterized and thus not overfitting on a small amount of medical data. Additionally, sensitivity is very high (91.30% and 88.43) and thus true cases of COVID-19 are not missed, which is a very important aspect in the screening of pandemics and the specificity is also high (91.13% and 89.60), reduces false alarms that can be disastrous to health care systems.

In this case, the confusion matrices (Fig. 8) indicate that mainly misclassification arises between the viral pneumonia and lung opacity which is logically understandable as they share the same radiographic pattern. It is interesting to note that the model does not mislead COVID-19 and normal cases, which makes it very robust during binary triage. The almost near-perfect values of AUC (1.0 and 0.99 in Fig. 11) also confirm high amounts of excellent classes separability with respect to thresholds. All these outcomes suggest that in domain constrained medical imaging tasks, task specific design, as opposed to large scale pretraining can achieve higher performance when data quality, preprocessing and architectural simplicity are balanced carefully.

Even though the proposed 19-layer CNN is very accurate in the two popular benchmark data under controlled experiment settings, it should be noted that this is a pre-tested computer validation and not a clinically ready solution. The model has not been subjected to grey or marginal radiology cases and it has not been tested to be robust on the presence of a broad spectrum of imaging protocols, equipment manufacturers or acquisition states as would be encountered in the nature of a real hospital.

In addition, the involved datasets are unable to fully characterize the complexity of clinical presentations e.g. co-morbidities (e.g., heart failure, COPD, or bacterial superinfections) which may lead to overlapping radiographic pattern of COVID-19 pneumonia. Therefore, the fact that the AI will be capable of initiating the decision-making process stretches the truth: conversely, the current research is a precursor to assistive AI.

The deployment of clinical workflow would require prospective validation of heterogeneous multi-centre data, electronic health records integration, rigorous quantification of uncertainty, and regulatory acceptance (e.g. FDA or CE marking). We alleviate our results with this in mind in order to stress the level of research of such a model and its use as a potential decision support model- not a separate diagnosis model.

Conclusions

In late 2019, many worries existed about the spread of a new respiratory ailment (COVID-19) and ways to cope with it. Given the virus’s fast spread, it is crucial to identify infected individuals in the early stages of quarantine. COVID-19 is a pandemic ailment that began with infection with a virus that recently caused many deaths in different countries. Timely and quick identification of this disease is a significant subject not only to avoid the outbreak of it but also, to decrease the risk of death. These days, some techniques are utilized to diagnose the COVID-19 disease, like sickness-correlated signs, and more precise diagnostic approaches, such as X-ray pictures of the chest. In this work, the aim was to attain a precise diagnostic technique for automatic and intelligent identification of COVID-19 via CXR (chest X-ray) pictures and image processing methods as an accurate diagnostic tool as an X-ray supplement from public references. This research introduced a different 19-layer CNN structure for COVID-19 identification from the CXR images. Here, pre-processing of images was utilized on the input pictures. Pre-processing steps were resizing, normalization, contrast enhancement, and uniforming all image formats to a defined format. Then, the achieved images were split into training and test images randomly. Then, a data augmentation was performed on the images to increase the number of sample sets. Afterward, the proposed 19-layer CNN was performed on the datasets, and the final results were achieved. Simulations were performed on 2 public datasets including the “Covid-19 Radiography Database” and “Covid-19 Posteroanterior Chest X-Ray Fused (CPCXR) Dataset”. By comparing the proposed CNN with some popular pre-trained networks with employing Specificity, Accuracy, Sensitivity, F1-score, and Precision the offered model indicated the finest efficiency toward them.

Application of edaravone-loaded nanogel in alleviating behavioral deficits and oxidative stress in schizophrenia rat model
Authors: Sina Montazeri, Soroush Bijani, Hamid Rashidzadeh, Ali Ramazani, Sina Andalib, Ali Kalantari-Hesari, Ghazale Cheraghi, Mir-Jamal Hosseini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSchizophrenia is considered as a main one of the public health issues, and imposes numerous burdens on patients and society. We previously reported, the pathophysiology of schizophrenia is influenced by inflammation and mitochondrial dysfunction. Edaravone (EDV) as a potent antioxidant with neuroprotective traits, has been approved for the treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), effecting through neutralizing soluble/insoluble peroxyl radicals. However, the main disadvantages of EDV are its low stability in aqueous media, poor water solubility, and un-optimized bioavailability. To effectively address these obstacles, nanogel was utilized as the drug vehicle. The decoration of nanogel surface with glutathione (GSH) was carried out to elevate edaravone’s brain delivery. The probable improvement in drug delivery of edaravone loaded GSH-nanogel is the main hypothesis of this study. In order to mimic schizophrenia-like behaviors, we applied two month of post-weaning social isolation stress (PWSI) to rodent model. The choice of PWSI model was made due to the maturation and development of prefrontal cortex and hippocampus during adolescence. In addition to causing oxidative stress and upregulating genes linked to innate immunity in the prefrontal cortex (PFC), the data showed that PWSI triggered schizophrenia-like behaviors in rats. This study demonstrated that treatment with edaravone loaded GSH-nanogel decreased the impact of PWSI on behavioral dysfunctions and oxidative stress in the PFC of rats. Edaravone loaded GSH-nanogel (GSH-PMAA-EDV) down-regulated Toll-like receptor 4 (Tlr-4) and AMP-activated protein kinase (Ampk) gene expression which are involved in inflammation and cellular energy homeostasis, respectively. Increase immunoreactivity feedback and Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (Bdnf) as direct impact in neurogenesis and neural cell plasticity was observed in EDV loaded GSH-nanogel treated groups. edaravone loaded GSH-nanogel (100 µg/kg) in comparison to free form of edaravone (5 mg/kg) revealed more beneficial effects, which might be useful for future clinical use especially for the treatment of schizophrenia.Keywords: Schizophrenia, Edaravone, Nanogel, Behavioral dysfunction, Inflammation, Oxidative stress, Prefrontal cortex (PFC), Adolescence, Social isolation stressSubject terms: Biochemistry, Cell biology, Drug discovery, Neuroscience, Molecular medicine

Introduction

A severe and chronic neurological brain disorder, schizophrenia affects around 1% of people worldwide, and is characterized by a series of signs and symptoms of auditory hallucinations, paranoid delusions, and psychosis1. Many studies proposed that genetic susceptibility and early environment deprivation paradigms, such as social isolation are the main risk factors for the prognosis of neuropsychiatric disorders2. Emerging data indicate that applying social isolation stress (SIS) to adolescent rodents as a non-pharmacological procedure induces many behavioral dysfunctions such as locomotor hyperactivity, cognitive dysfunction, latent inhibition and deficiency in sensorimotor gating similar to symptoms of schizophrenia in human2–4. The SIS can disturb the function multiple sectors of the brain, which interpret social context, social reward and social surveillance-aversion; these function are mainly carried out by orbitofrontal cortex, ventral striatum and amygdala, respectively5. The sis induction lowered corticosterone level in blood, and suppressed neural plasticity affecting genes, namely c-Fos, BDNF-7 and Arc in hippocampus and prefrontal cortex6,7.

Unfortunately, the exact mechanistic pathway, which leads to the development of schizophrenia, is not clear. The adolescence period is critical phase for neuronal differentiation and brain development particularly in prefrontal cortex and hippocampus8,9. Epidemiological studies revealed that negative life experiences in the adolescent period is considered as a potential risk factor in schizophrenia in adulthood10,11.

It suggested that neurotransmission defects, mitochondrial dysfunction and immune responses may have a crucial role in the pathophysiology of schizophrenia3,12,13. Clinical studies showed microglia activation in the brain and elevation of pro-inflammatory cytokine levels in serum are associated with schizophrenia14. Current pharmacotherapy for schizophrenia is blockade of serotonergic (5-HT2 receptor as more common choice)- and dopaminergic- (commonly D2 receptor) mediated neurotransmission with medications that have low efficacy and high side effects in patients15.

Thus, novel therapeutic approaches are needed to enhance the treatment options for schizophrenia patients. For example, clozapine (CLZ) ability to normalize monoaminergic signaling in the brain, is frequently pointed out as the reason for its therapeutic benefits in the treatment of schizophrenia. However, current recent findings indicated that CLZ might be effective through different pathways, such as mitochondrial function and modulating immune-inflammatory responses16–18. In this regard, edaravone (EDV), as a potent antioxidant in ALS treatment, might be used to treat schizophrenia. Since EDV possess poor water solubility, low stability, and bioavailability, modification of release pattern and improving cellular penetration of EDV by GSH conjugated poly methacrylic acid nano-hydrogel (GSH-PMAA) might improve its efficacy.

Among valid animal models for studying schizophrenia, PWSI stress is known to manifest several behavioral and biochemical alterations similar to the ones observed in schizophrenia patients17,19,20. In regard to PWSI model, recent studies have suggested the connection between pathophysiology of schizophrenia and the surge in reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitochondrial malfunction21,22. Also, the fact that PWSI targets adolescence and brain development makes it an appropriate model for studying schizophrenia-like behaviors in rodents. Therefore, the potential effect of GSH-PMAA loaded EDV in nanohydrogel form, has been investigated on rat model of PWSI induced schizophrenia-like disorder using multi-parametric behavioral, biochemical and gene expression assays.

Results

Characterizations

To characterize the product both FTIR and HNMR techniques were applied to reveal the successful fabrication of the GSH-PMAA nanogel. Size and zeta potential are accounted as main parameters in the preparation of drug carrier and have great impact on the biological faith of any developed drug carrier. Accordingly, dynamic light scattering (DLS) tool was used to determine the zeta potential and size of GSH-PMAA-EDV nanogel. Based on the results, the aforementioned nanogel had mean hydrodynamic size of around 199 nm (polydispersity index of 0.282) with zeta potential of − 26.1 mV which is extremely favorable in the field of drug delivery. TEM results also indicated that these nanogels were spherical in shape without any aggregation sign. It is worth mentioning that the prepared drug carrier extended the release of EDV over time, in which only 15% of EDV was released from GSH-EDV within 216 h23.

Encapsulation efficiency and drug loading of developed EDV loaded nanogel were 99.9% and 37.5%, respectively. Additionally, after being stored in an aqueous solution for three months, the EDV-loaded nanogel showed excellent physical stability, with its particle size remaining approximately constant (the particle size was 199 nm initially and 221 nm after three months of storage).

Discussion

The finding of current investigation confirmed that the therapeutic impact of EDV-loaded nanogel in rat model of schizophrenia induced by post-weaning social isolation (PWSI). The study demonstrates that EDV-GSH-nanogel reduces behavioral abnormalities, oxidative stress, and inflammatory gene expression in the prefrontal cortex.

Previous data revealed that hyperlocomotion and poor performance especially in Ketamin induced psychotic animals have been previously established by schizophrenia-associated behavioral and cognitive assessment, which are also consistent with the behavioral data obtained in this study (Monte et al. 2013; Ben-Azu et al. 2019; de Araújo et al. 2021).Hyper-locomotion activity, a lower recalling ability in object recognition test and decision making are related to the symptoms of schizophrenia25. In this study, long term PWSI led to development of hyperactivity in horizontal movements and rearing in the arena in OFT, which reflected mesolimbic dopamine activity as an index for the positive symptoms in schizophrenia similar to our study9,11,26. Also, impaired motivation and grooming behaviour in splash test have been described as schizophrenia symptoms3, suggesting the key role of dopamine in PFC and hypothalamus which done in present study.It suggested that modulation of hypothetical-pituitary system has now been implicated in stressful situations involved in self‐grooming by releasing dopamine or by increasing the dopamine turnover in the nucleus accumbens shell27.

In regard to the social behaviors of animals, socially isolated rats engaged less in social interaction with their pairs, which was observed by reduction in anogenital sniffing and rearing behavior. Lack of self-care, increased motor activity and low level of social interaction are symptoms which follow closely the clinical manifestation of inherent negative symptoms of schizophrenia and several researchers have postulated that it is also one of the first symptoms to manifest17,19,20,28. Our data showed that both EDV and EDV-GSH-PMAA treatments attenuated hyper-locomotion and improved self-care.

Humans and rodents both have a natural preference for exploring new objects over familiar ones, which is used in the novel object recognition task to assess visual learning and memory impairments seen in schizophrenia29,30. This should be noted that treatment with EDV-GSH-PMAA showed more efficacy in improving the discrimination index. These findings are consistent with earlier research showing that EDV enhanced cognitive, memory function and social behavior31,32.

Oxidative stress is a pivotal mechanism underlying schizophernai. In regard to oxidative stress tests, EDV-GSH-PMAA showed more potency compared to EDV (5 mg/kg), as EDV failed to attenuate MDA and PCO overproduction by PWSI, yet did not improve GSH level as well. It is interesting that EDV-GSH-PMAA at dose of 100 µg/kg managed to show protective effects against PWSI- oxidative stress markers. Although EDV is a highly potent antioxidant, but modification in release pattern, tissue penetration and cellular reuptake may be factors that are involved in the failure of EDV to reduce GSH level33, and MDA overproduction in this study34. Our present data provides a solid foundation by outlining schizophrenia’s prevalence, symptoms, and pathophysiology, emphasizing the roles of mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress. It effectively positions EDV as a potential therapeutic agent due to its antioxidant properties, previously validated in ALS treatment, and identifies its limitations (poor solubility, stability, and bioavailability). Our data supposed that the rationale for using GSH-PMAA nanogel to overcome these limitations, and the choice of the PWSI model is justified by its relevance to schizophrenia-like behaviors and adolescent brain development. Recent research has shown that EDV exhibits neuroprotective effects by activating NRF2 gene expression, which helps maintain ROS balance linked to energy regulation and confirm involvement of EDV in recovering of oxidative stress.

A large body of evidence indicates that inflammatory response plays a key role in the pathophysiology of neurodegenerative diseases like schizophrenia35,36. The sterile immune response is essential for normal physiological function; however, prolonged and persistent inflammation can lead to changes in cellular activity, damage, and cell death, which is believed to play a role in the development of schizophrenia symptoms36. Elevated Tlr4 expression in individuals with schizophrenia may play a role in promoting neuroinflammation37. Furthermore, recent research has shown that EDV inhibits the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and supporting neuron survival, growth, and repair38. Our data revealed that EDV-GSH-PMAA treatment caused Tlr4 downregulation in PFC of socially isolated rats Toll-like receptor 4 expression in prefrontal cortex is related to low grade inflammatory response, and is a main factor in synaptic remodeling and neurogenesis in schizophrenia39. EDV (5 mg/kg) showed no effect on Tlr4 expression, possibly because of because of restricted capacity for passing through the blood–brain barrier. It is interesting that EDV showed significant anti-inflammatory effects against TLR4/NFκB pathway in organs like pancreas and testis40. We supposed that the possible connection between TLR4 signaling and cognitive impairments, which are a key and frequently difficult component of managing schizophrenia.

In regard to energy status of the brain, Ampk complex serves as the primary cellular energy sensor, acting as a key metabolic regulator essential for maintaining energy balance. Dysfunction in energy metabolism following Ampk over-expression is observed characteristics in psychiatric disorders such as schizophrenia and alter cell growth and causes cellular autophagy. Activity of Ampk is in correlation with mTOR in order to maintain cellular homeostasis, which is critical for neurons, particularly in psychiatric disorders41,42. EDV-GSH-PMMA treatment downregulated Ampk expression might be associated with improved neural homeostasis and the recovery in cellular ATP supply. The EDV effect in suppressing autophagy activation was linked to the downregulation of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway43.

Both the preservation of neuronal plasticity and neural development are significantly influenced by BDNF. It also has a role in the pathophysiology of schizophrenia through shaping morphological differentiation in dopaminergic system in the striatum, and continuation of serotonergic system regular function44. In our work, EDV-GSH-PMAA at the dose of 100 µg/kg improved the relative expression of Bdnf in PFC, with it did not change BDNF expression in socially conditioned rats.It is believed that multiple interactions exist between BDNF and the dopamine system, which plays a significant role in the pathophysiology of schizophrenia.BDNF helps prevent the loss of tyrosine hydroxylase, a marker of dopaminergic neurons, is abundantly expressed in the PFC and hippocampus, and protects dopaminergic neurons in schizophrenia45.

In conclusion, in comparison to EDV, EDV loaded GSH-PMAA hydrogel showed more protective effect against behavioral abnormality and oxidative stress induced by PWSI. In general, EDV loaded GSH-nanogel (100 µg/kg) in a lower dose compared to EDV (5 mg/kg) revealed beneficial effects for future clinical use. Also, the histopathological data from kidney and brain revealed that EDV-GSH-PMAA did not induced toxicity in applied dose. It seems that EDV nanogel to be a promising candidate to improve the therapeutic efficiency of psychiatric disorders.

Limitations

Though the applied dose for EDV-GSH-PMAA was optimized in pilot studies application of various doses can present a more in-depth perspective of the dose- response ratio. The utilization of female rats has been limited in many studies by their intricate estrous cycle, and enabling to synchronize their cycle. In this case, an investigation of the potency of EDV-GSH-PMAA in different phases of estrus cycle is a valued attempt.

Materials and methods

Materials

Chemicals with purest available analytical grade were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Taufkirchen, Germany).

Synthesis of nano-carrier (GSH-PMAA) and preparation of GSH-PMAA-EDV

According to our previous work, in the first step, we used the reflux-precipitation polymerization method46, and then attachment of glutathione onto the PMAA (GSH-PMAA) based on carbodiimide coupling method 23. After dispersing 10 mg dried GSH-PMAA into distilled water with ultrasonic agitation, 6 mg of EDV was added to the homogeneous nanogel dispersion and stirred at room temperature for 24 h. The GSH-PMAA-EDV were then separated by ultracentrifugation, freeze-dried, and stored between 4 and 8 °C. Finally, using data from our previous research, a UV–visible spectrophotometer (UV–visible spectrophotometer T 80 +) at 244 nm was used to determine the amount of EDV encapsulated within GSH-PMAA23.

Full characterization of developed GSH-PMAA-EDV structure

The validation procedure was done according to previously published studies as well as confirmed the GSH molecule conjugation onto PMAA23.

Animals and their housing conditions

The Pasteur Institute in Tehran, Iran, provided both male and female wistar rats used in this investigation. After one week, the acclimatized rats separated for mating in one male and two female pattern. Litters and their dams were kept together as family units prior weaned. Male rats during the postnatal development stage (PND: 21–28) were housed in approved cages and laboratory environment (i.e., temperature 23 ± 2 °C, humidity 50 ± 10%,12 h’ light and dark cycle), with access to ad libitum supply of water and food for 9 weeks in social (Control) and/or socially isolated condition (PWSI) according to previous studies. This model mimics a variety of psychiatric behaviors observed in humans suffering from schizophrenia3,4,47,48. All experiments were performed based on the National Institutes of Health’s "Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals," which also follows the ARRIVE guidelines. This study was confirmed by the ethical principles and standards of Medical Research in Iran (IR.NIMAD.REC.1400.074), which is stated by National Institute of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (HHS publication No. 80-23, revised 1978), and legislation for the protection of animals used for research studies (Directive 2010/63/EU), for the approved utilization of lab animals.

Social condition rats (control) were divided into groups of 4 rats in each polypropylene cages (65 × 40 × 25 cm) and isolated rats (PWSI) were kept individually in a separate room (25 × 25 × 15 cm). The PSWI rats’ cages were cleaned weekly by the same investigator to minimize handling and social interaction, and wood shavings were used as animal bedding. The studies were carried out in accordance with the verified protocol3.

Study design

Totally, 56 rats were divided in to 8 groups and subjected to the following treatment for 21 days (two times in each week): (1) socially conditioned rodents that received saline (Control or SC); (2) socially-conditioned rats treated with nanogel (GSH-PMAA); (3)socially-conditioned rats took optimal dose of edaravone (EDV = 5 mg/kg). Edaravone was purchased from Merck company (Darmtadt, Germany) and dosage and duration of treatment were determined according to pilot study; (4) socially-conditioned rats treated with optimal dose (100 µg/kg) of nanogel loaded with edaravone (GSH-PMAA-EDV); (5) PWSI rats with no treatment (PWSI); (6) PWSI rats treated with nanogel (PWSI + GSH-PMAA) ; (7) PWSI rats received optimal dose of edaravone (PWSI + EDV); (8) PWSI rodents treated with optimal dose (100 µg/kg) of nanogel loaded with edaravone (PWSI + GSH-PMAA-EDV). Lastly, from 10:00 to 14:00, the animals were tested for behavioral evaluations. Twenty-four hours after the behavioral tasks, animals received mild anesthesia (with pentobarbital 60 mg/kg, intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection, according to euthanized by cervical dislocation (Fig. 6).

Body weight

Every week beginning on PND 21–23 following delivery, their weight was recorded in order to assess the progress of the treatment groups throughout the research period.

Behavioral tests

Open field test (OFT)

The correlation of locomotor activity and schizophrenia-like disorder was observed using open field test. The maze of OFT made of a white square Plexiglas box (50 × 50 × 40 cm), and the box surface was divided to 16 equal squares. Rats were placed separately at the middle squares and were free to move in the premises of box for 5 min. the number of times a rodent crossed the lines was counted as their horizontal activity49.

Splash test

The self-caring behavior in rodents was measured in this task. In brief, 10% sucrose solution was sprayed on the dorsal segment of rats in their housed cage and their grooming activity was observed during 5 min of the test50.

Novel object recognition (NOR) test

The cognitive performance hinders as in the ability to recognize new stimuli or objects during schizophrenia-like impairments, which is a valuable tool for studying cognitive function and memory in rodents23. NOR box is a top opened Plexiglas box 70 × 70 × 30 cm that holds two objects in the similar distance from the center. This test consists of three steps; in the habituation phase, rodents were free to investigate the empty box for 5 min. After 2 h, two exact shaped objects were placed in the box and rodents explore them for 10 min. In the third phase after 2 h, one object has been replaced by a novel one, the discrimination in exploring novel object and the familiar one has used to calculation of discrimination index [DR = (T Novel)/(T Novel + T Familiar)]51.

Social interaction test (SIT)

The social behavior is one of the main factors that is affected in schizophrenia. To measure social interaction behaviors, rats were first placed separately for habituation in an open arena (60 × 60 × 40 cm) for 15 min one day before the test. On the next day, rodents two by two (with 20 g variance in weight) were placed in the center of arena. For 15 min, social interactions including anogenital rearing, time spent together and sniffing were measured by a blind independent researcher52.

Biochemical tests

24 h following the end of behavioral tests, rats were decapitated under anesthesia (by pentobarbital 60 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection, i.p.) and their prefrontal cortex (PFC) were isolated form the other parts of brain, and were kept at − 80 °C for later biochemical and gene expression observations.

MDA assay

The cellular accumulation of free radicals could lead to the peroxidation of lipids, and the main byproduct of this reaction is malondialdehyde (MDA). In order to assess the MDA amount, in an acidic solution contained Thiobarbituric acid (TBA) the complex of MDA-TBA has been formed, and the complex was transferred to n-butanol hydrophobic phase. The samples’ UV absorbance read at 532 nm. The values were presented in µmol/g tissue, and tetramethoxypropane (TMP) was used as the reference solution24.

Protein carbonyl assay

Another byproduct of oxidative stress surge is escalation of carbonylated proteins-based reaction with 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (DNPH) at 365 nm. Briefly, 0.2 mL of the homogenized sample (100 mg) in tris buffer was added to 0.5 mL TCA 20% (w/v) and incubated at 4 °C for 15 min. Following centrifuging the resultant mixture, the precipitates were treated with DNPH (0.5 mL, 0.2%) and NaOH (0.5 mL, 0.1%). After being kept at ambient temperature in the dark, the tubes were once more combined with TCA (20%) and centrifuged for 10 min at 6500 rpm. After dissolving the samples in guanidine hydrochloride (0.2 mL, 6 M), the mixture was centrifuged for 5 min at 5000 rpm. Finally, the absorbance of the samples at 365 nm was used to determine the carbonyl concentration53.

Reduced glutathione (GSH) assay

It is one of the main intracellular antioxidants and first line of defense against surge of free radicals. By removing proteins and removing positive ions using TCA and EDTA, respectively, extracted GSH from tissue is ready to create a complex with DTNB. The GSH-DTNB complex has the highest absorbance rate at 412 nm54.

FRAP assay

The ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) is the potential of the tissue to convert ferric iron (Fe3+) to ferrous iron (Fe2+), which could be correlated to the total antioxidant power. By adding FRAP reagent (Fe3+ (2,4,6-Tri (2-pyridyl)-s-triazine) to the tissue extraction, the formed complex’s absorbance was read at 593 nm53.

Gene expression assay by quantitative RT-PCR

TRIZOL was used for RNA extraction from tissue. After RNA extraction, 1 μg of obtained RNA was used for reverse transcription by SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara Bio, Japan). The produced cDNA has been stored for further polymerase chain reaction test. The list of genes that has investigated and forward and reverse primers are mentioned in Table 2. PCR was performed by initial thermal activation at 95 °C for 30 s, the extension/annealing were repeated in 45 cycles (5 s at 95 °C and 20 s at 60 °C). For normalizing the output relative to Hprt1 was calculated by 2−ΔΔct, used to calculate related expression following MIQE guidelines55. To determine if all primers yielded a single PCR product, melting curve analysis was used.

Histopathological investigation

The PFC was isolated and immersed in 10% formalin. After serial dehydration with different concentrations of ethanol, tissue was dyed utilizing hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) the tissues were dyed, and investigated under light microscope (Olympus BX51) at 400X magnitude. Each group had five samples for this experiment and pictures were taken using attached camera. The histopathological alterations were graded in the following manner: There are four levels: NO (-); Mild (+); Moderate (++); and Severe (+++)56.

Statistical analysis

Data was presented as the mean ± SD, and statistical analyses were conducted using the R studio programming software. To compare the groups, we used two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by the Tukey’s post hoc, and we considered a P value less than 0.05 to be statistically significant. In order to create the plots, the Matplotlib library in Python was utilized. Using power calculations, the sample size was determined using G power software (ver.3.1.7, Franz Faul, Universitat Kiel, Germany). Based on pilot studies and pervious findings, the probable means and standard deviations was determined in each experimental groups. Finally, total sample size per group was calculated as 7 animals in behavioral tests and 3–4 animals in molecular studies.

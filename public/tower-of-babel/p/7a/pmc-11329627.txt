Child-parent associations of hematocrit in trios of Japanese adulthood confirmed by the random family method: The TMM BirThree Cohort Study
Authors: Takuma Usuzaki, Mami Ishikuro, Masahiro Kikuya, Keiko Murakami, Aoi Noda, Fumihiko Ueno, Hirohito Metoki, Taku Obara, Shinichi Kuriyama
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo examine child-parent associations of HCT among Japanese adults and their parents. Factors associated with hematocrit (HCT) were analyzed in 3,574 sons and 7,203 daughters using Pearson’s correlation coefficient and Student’s t-test. Multiple linear regression analysis, adjusted by the factors identified by univariate analyses and by living with parents, was performed on 242 son-parent trios and 587 daughter-parent trios. When a child-parent association was observed in the multiple linear regression analysis, it was validated using the random family method (RFM). In univariate analyses, the son’s HCT was associated with age (correlation coefficient = –0.072), white blood cell (WBC) (0.19), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) (0.20), triglyceride (0.11), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) (− 0.087). The daughter’s HCT was associated with WBC (0.014), ALT (0.18), and eGFR (− 0.17). In multiple linear regression analysis, the son’s HCT was associated with the son’s WBC (coefficient = 3.48 × 10–4), the son’s eGFR (0.031), the father’s HCT (0.11), and the mother’s HCT (0.17). RFM confirmed the association between the son’s and father’s HCT (p = 0.0070) and between the son’s and mother’s HCT (p = 0.0011). The daughter’s HCT was associated with WBC (2.6 × 10–4), ALT (0.037), and the mother’s HCT (0.14). RFM confirmed the association between the daughter’s and mother’s HCT (p = 0.00043). Child-parent association of HCT was confirmed between son-father, son–mother, and daughter-mother relationships, and differed depending on the sex of the child and the parents.Keywords: Bootstrap method, Child-parent association, Hematocrit, Permutation methodSubject terms: Biomarkers, Epidemiology, Genetics research

Introduction

Hematocrit (HCT) is the proportion of the blood by volume that consists of red blood cells (RBC), and is an important clinical biomarker.1,2 Boffetta et al. reported that both low and high levels of HCT are associated with increased mortality in the general population.3 Abnormal HCT level is associated with an increased risk of diseases such as cerebral infarction,4 heart failure,5 coronary artery disease,6 and inflammatory bowel disease.7 HCT abnormality can also indicate hemorrhage, systemic inflammation, and activation of chronic disease.1 In the view of relations between HCT level and the acute to chronic phase of diseases, clarifying the regulation mechanism of HCT can have diagnostic and predictive value for several diseases in clinical settings. The genetic aspect of the regulation of HCT has been studied and heritability of HCT has been estimated as 40%–90% in studies of twins.8,9 Such twin studies have examined siblings who are genetically identical but have not determined the child-parent association of HCT.10 Evans et al. stated that among these studies, sex differences had been overlooked in examining the hereditary mechanism of HCT, because of limited sample sizes.8 A genome-wide association study of Timoteo et al. indicated that several male-specific and female-specific genes are involved in regulating hematological status.11 It also addressed well-known differences between sexes in terms of iron status and hematological traits in single nucleotide polymorphisms on autosomal chromosomes. Men tend to have higher HCT than women, a disparity that is due partly to menstruation, childbirth, and breastfeeding, which are associated with hormonal effects.12 Examining a trio that consists of a child and her or his parents can help clarify the hereditary mechanism of HCT with consideration to sex differences. As humans share genetic material with the mother and father, the child-parent association may differ depending on the child’s and the parents’ sex.

From a methodological viewpoint, regression analysis has been used to examine child-parent association.13 A previous study has pointed out that conventional regression analysis results in an inadequate evaluation of child-parent association.14 The authors of that article insisted that child-parent association should be examined with a control group analog, and proposed a new method termed “random family method” (RFM).14 Applying RFM to regression analysis, we can evaluate whether an association is truly a child-parent association.13–15 However, few studies have investigated the child-parent association of HCT using RFM separately and with regard to sex in the following relationships among trios: son-father, son–mother, daughter-father, and daughter-mother. The aim of this study was to examine child-parent associations of HCT using RFM to each child-parent relationship among trios that consist of Japanese adults and their parents.

Method

Study design

This cross-sectional study is a part of the “Tohoku Medical Megabank Project Birth and Three-Generation Cohort Study (TMM BirThree Cohort Study)”. In this cohort study, three-generation families including 8,823 men, 794 fathers of these men, and 1,337 mothers of these men; and 22,493 women, 1,658 fathers of these women, and 4,283 mothers of these women were recruited between 19 July 2013 and 31 March 2017 in Miyagi and Iwate Prefectures, Japan. The Institutional Review Board at Tohoku University Graduate School of Medicine approved the study (27 May 2013; approval No. 2013-1-103-1). Additionally, the study’s progress has been periodically monitored and reviewed by the committee specifically set up in the TMM. Trained genome medical research coordinators were placed in each clinic, hospital, or community support center to provide information on the TMM BirThree Cohort Study to potential participants, and to receive a signed informed consent from each participant. The study was conducted according to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.16 The details of the TMM BirThree Cohort Study have been described elsewhere.17,18 As follow-up is ongoing, cross-sectional analyses were performed in the present study.

SPS1

SPS1 was composed of sons. The exclusion criteria for SPS1 were (i) sons for whom hematological data were not obtained, (ii) sons who did not answer the self-administered questionnaire at baseline recruitment, and (iii) sons who daughters who reported iron deficiency anemia by themselves in the self-administered questionnaire at baseline recruitment. After applying the exclusion criteria, 3,547 sons remained in SPS1. Figure 1b shows the number of participants excluded by each criterion in the construction of SPS1.

SPS2

SPS2 was composed of trios of son, father, and mother. In constructing SPS2, the same exclusion criteria as SPS1 were imposed on sons. In addition, two other criteria were applied to fathers and mothers: (iv) fathers for whom hematological data were not collected or were not available, and (v) mothers for whom hematological data were not collected or were not available. After applying the exclusion criteria, 242 trios of son, father, and mother remained in SPS2. Figure 1c shows the number of participants excluded by each criterion in the construction of SPS2.

SPD1

SPD1 was composed of daughters. The exclusion criteria for SPD1 were (i) daughters for whom hematological data were not obtained, (ii) daughters who did not answer the self-administered questionnaire at baseline recruitment, and (iii) daughters who reported iron deficiency anemia by themselves in the self-administered questionnaire at baseline recruitment. After applying the exclusion criteria, 7,203 daughters remained in SPD1. Figure 1d shows the number of participants excluded by each criterion in the construction of SPD1.

SPD2

SPD2 was composed of trios of daughter, father, and mother. In constructing SPD2, the same exclusion criteria as SPD1 were imposed on daughters. In addition, two other criteria were applied to fathers and mothers: (iv) fathers for whom hematological data were not collected or were not available, and (v) mothers for whom hematological data were not collected or were not available. After applying the exclusion criteria, 587 trios of daughter, father, and mother remained in SPD2. Figure 1e shows the number of participants excluded by each criterion in the construction of SPD2.

Data collection

Hematological tests

We collected information on hematological status from laboratory hematological tests. Participants’ venous blood was sampled when participants visited a hospital, clinic, or our research center, and analyzed at either BML, Inc. (ISO 15,189 approved) or LSI Medience Corporation (ISO 15,189 approved) laboratories. Between July 2013 and March 2014, all analyses were performed at BML, Inc., and between April 2014 and March 2017, all analyses were performed at LSI Medience Corporation. Analyses included HCT, white blood cell (WBC), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), triglyceride, and creatinine for estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). HCT, WBC, ALT, and creatinine for eGFR were measured using the peak detection algorithm of pulse waveform, flow cytometry, the modified Japan Society of Clinical Chemistry (JSCC) reference method, and the enzyme method, respectively. Information on these laboratories and details of the hematological tests can be obtained on the website of each company (BML, Inc.; http://www.bml.co.jp/, LSI Medience Corporation; https://www.medience.co.jp/).

Questionnaire

We collected information regarding age, smoking habit, and whether participants lived with their parents at baseline recruitment between July 2013 and March 2017, via a self-administered questionnaire. In the item regarding smoking habits, participants were asked “Have you smoked more than 100 cigarettes in total during your life?”, and the possible responses were “yes” or “no”. Those who responded “yes” were defined as “ever smoker”. In the question item regarding living with someone, participants were asked “Please select all relationships between yourself and those you live with.” and could select answers from among spouse, parent(s), son(s), or daughter(s); son(s) and son’s spouse(s); daughter(s) and daughter’s spouse(s); grandchild; brother(s) or sister(s); grandparent(s); and other. We defined those who selected “parent(s)” as “participants living with parent(s)”.

Statistical analysis

The participants’ characteristics are presented as the mean and standard deviation (SD), or the number and percentage.

Statistical analysis of SPS1 and SPD1

For SPS1 and SPD1, factors associated with the son’s and daughter’s HCT, respectively, were examined by univariate analysis. We examined the association between HCT and age,19 WBC,1 ALT,20 triglycerides,21 eGFR,22 and smoking habit23 for which associations with HCT have been reported previously. The questionnaire and hematological test data were used to examine these associations. In addition to these factors, we analyzed the influence of living with parents on the sons’ and daughters’ HCT. Pearson’s correlation coefficient and Student’s t-test were used to compare continuous variables and categorical variables, respectively. Sample size analysis indicated that 194 samples (correlation coefficient = 0.20, power = 0.80, α = 0.05) and 73 samples (difference of means = 1.0, SD = 1.0, power = 0.80, α = 0.05) were required for Pearson’s correlation coefficient and Student’s t-test, respectively.

Statistical analysis of SPS2 and SPD2

For SPS2 and SPD2, we performed multiple linear regression analysis in which the objective variable was the HCT of the son and daughter, respectively; and explanatory variables were the father’s HCT and the mother’s HCT. Living with parents and the factors examined in SPS1 and SPD1 were used to adjust for SPS2 and SPD2, respectively. For SPS2 and SPD2, in the case that the child’s HCT was associated with the father’s or mother’s HCT, RFM was performed to confirm the association. Sample size analysis showed that 140 samples (coefficient = 0.20, number of explanatory variables = 9, SD of HCT = 3.0, R2 = 0.30, power = 0.80, α = 0.05) were required for multiple linear regression analysis.

RFM to confirm child-parent association

RFM was implemented as described in a previous report.14 In RFM, the resampling processes of the bootstrap method were restricted to the dataset that did not include any familial connections, and the child-parent relation was assessed as follows: (i) From the original dataset, we constructed 5,000 datasets that did not include any familial connections. (ii) We calculated the regression coefficient of each dataset constructed in (i) and obtained a distribution of coefficients. The normality of this distribution was confirmed by the Shapiro–Wilk test. (iii) We calculated the cumulative area of the distribution from the coefficient of the original data. When the original coefficient was not included in the 95% cumulative area of the distribution obtained in (ii), we defined the original coefficient as significant. We termed the resampled dataset in (i) as random family (RF) and termed this method RFM. These procedures are summarized in Fig. 2. RFM was applied to confirm the association between the parent’s and child’s HCT when this association was observed in the multiple linear regression analysis.

Random family method procedures. Scheme of the random family method (RFM) between F0 and F1HCT. (i) Random families (RFs) were constructed that did not include any familial connections from the original data. (ii) Coefficients were calculated for each RF and the distribution of coefficients was obtained. (iii) We then checked whether or not the original coefficient was outside the 95% cumulative area of the distribution.

Software and statistical significance

All analyses were performed using the SAS package (version 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., Cary, North Carolina, USA) and Python (Python Software Foundation, Python Language Reference, version 3.6.2. Available at http://www.python.org). Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < 0.05.

Discussion

In this study, we examined child-parent associations of HCT among trios that consisted of Japanese adults and their parents. The hereditary aspect of HCT was confirmed in the son-father, son–mother, and daughter-mother relationships. Some of these relationships have been previously reported.2 In contrast, no child-parent association of HCT was observed in the daughter-father relationship. A possible reason for the difference in the child-parent association of HCT is that among women, HCT may be adjusted predominately by an independent hereditary regulation mechanism. In women, hormonal regulation is one of the mechanisms responsible for adjusting HCT.12,24 Women can tolerate hemodynamic changes such as in pregnancy and childbirth via hormonal response.12,24 The hormonal mechanism might have masked the child-parent association of HCT in the present study. Although a genetic effect on HCT has been reported in a previous twin study, the properties of the analyzed population differed from the present study: as the twin study analyzed 12-year-old twins, there might have been a limited hormonal effect.8 The hormonal effect on the child-parent association of HCT should be investigated in a future study. Our results indicate the need for careful follow-up of individuals whose parents have abnormality of HCT. In clinical practice, it should be kept in mind that abnormality of HCT may have a genetic cause, in the absence of disease.

In this study, we used RFM to confirm child-parent associations.14 In RFM, the resampling process was restricted to data that did not include any familial connections, and we calculated coefficients for each dataset. From these procedures, we obtained a distribution of coefficients. This distribution could be interpreted as an analog of a control group. When the original coefficient of familial connection was not included in the 95% cumulative area of the distribution, the probability that the coefficient of the original data was obtained by chance was lower than 5%. This result indicates that the original association might be true for child-parent associations or familial unique associations. The advantages of using RFM in the present study are as follows. First, we could discuss a child-parent association because an association confirmed by RFM can be specific to a familial connection. We may be able to detect false positive or false negative associations that might not be detectable by conventional analysis. In the present study, the RFM indicated that the result of multiple linear regression for the daughter-father relation may be false negative. Second, we could apply this method to data for which the sample size is relatively small (such as SPS2) because the number of restricted permutations diverges quickly. We can calculate the number of restricted resampling by recurrence relation to sequence Nn=n-1Nn-1+Nn-2n>3,N1=1andN2=1. The same procedures were used in previous deep-learning studies for multimodal analyses.14,25–27.

In multiple regression analysis of SPS2, the son’s HCT was negatively associated with the son’s eGFR and positively associated with the son’s WBC. In SPD2, the daughter’s HCT was positively associated with the daughter’s WBC and ALT. Previous studies have reported the effects of liver28,29 and kidney function,30,31 aging,19 and WBC1 on HCT. The results of multiple regression in the present study may reflect the important role played by the kidneys and liver by producing erythropoietin in hematopoiesis.1 An association between aging and HCT has been reported in epidemiological studies of elderly people;32 however, it remains unclear whether the same association can be observed in the younger population analyzed in the present study. Our analysis indicates that a decrease in hematopoietic capacity with advancing age may begin from around 30 years. Our examination of the effect of lifestyle similarity between the child and the parents found no association between the HCT of the child and living with parents.33 The effect of living with parents might have been limited because the rates of children living with their parents were 29.3% and 18.6% in SPS2 and SPD2, respectively.

This study has several limitations. First, we could not discuss causality because of the cross-sectional study design. However, we examined child-parent associations of HCT along the family line and we could infer genetic causality in child-parent associations between the child and their parents. Second, decreasing the sample size might have changed the characteristics of the study population. We selected analyses that would maintain as large a sample size as possible. Compared with SPD1 and SPD2, the sample sizes of SPS1 and SPS2 were relatively small; however, the sample size analyses revealed sufficient statistical power of our analyses. The effect of decreased sample size may therefore be limited. Third, we did not consider the use of drugs and supplements, or food intake, which can be confounders. Iron-containing drugs and supplements used for the treatment of anemia affect HCT.34 A relation between nutrient intake and anemia risk has been reported.35 Among Japanese women younger than 50 years, anemia has been identified in 22.3% and is due mainly to iron deficiency.36 According to a report published by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, the rates in Japan of people taking any supplement are 15.4%, 19.4%, 20.3%, and 28.7% among men aged 20–29 years, women aged 20–29 years, men aged 30–39 years, and women aged 30–39 years, respectively.37 A previous study has reported that the rate of people taking an iron supplement among working adults was 4.7% and 22.1% in men and women, respectively.38 Although we did not examine the effect of supplements on the child-parent association of HCT, we did exclude participants with iron deficiency anemia. In addition to this, we included information on smoking and living with parents in our analyses. Smoking and living with parents can affect child and parental HCT through secondhand smoke and diet patterns. At least a part of the confounders were included, even if these factors have an indirect effect. Fourth, we performed phenotypical analyses and did not use actual genetic information. However, we believe that studies using phenotypical and actual genetic information are each essential components of medical research. Fifth, children could be siblings and child-parent association may be overestimated. We can confirm the sibling relationship among children using self-reported pedigree or genome information. Although the most accurate method is analyzing genome information, we could not perform it in the present study. In this context, we performed our analysis assuming that each child-parent relationship was independent under the condition that some children were siblings.

Future research is required to reconfirm the child-parent association of HCT from other aspects, using a method such as a family-based, genome-wide association study.

Conclusion

The present study identified son-father, son–mother, and daughter-mother relationships in terms of HCT among trios consisting of Japanese adults and their parents using RFM. There was a sex difference in the child-parent association when the study population was analyzed separately according to the familial relationships of son-father, son–mother, daughter-father, and daughter-mother.

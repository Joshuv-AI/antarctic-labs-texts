Obicetrapib safety analysis: Pooled phase 3 clinical trial experience
Authors: Adam J Nelson, John JP Kastelein, Marc Ditmarsch, Collette Hall, Douglas Kling, Nancy Ortiz, Douglas L. Wicks, Andrew Hsieh, Michael Szarek, Kausik K Ray, Michael H. Davidson, Stephen J Nicholls
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObicetrapib is a potent, selective cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP) inhibitor that lowers LDL-C and raises HDL-C. Although prior studies have demonstrated efficacy and general tolerability, a comprehensive evaluation of its safety profile across later-stage clinical trials is needed.MethodsSafety outcomes were assessed in a pooled analysis of two phase III trials comparing obicetrapib 10 mg daily with placebo in adults with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) or atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). Participants received treatment for 365 days. Safety endpoints included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and prespecified events of special interest including hepatic, muscular, glycemic, renal and ocular parameters as well as overall rates of discontinuation.ResultsA total of 2,880 participants were included (mean age 64 years; 36 % female; 82 % with ASCVD; 35.8 % with diabetes). Overall TEAE rates were similar between obicetrapib and placebo (60.2 % vs 62.0 %). AEs leading to treatment discontinuation occurred in 4.1 % of obicetrapib-treated participants and 5.3 % on placebo, Risk Ratio (RR) 0.77 [0.54-1.08]. No clinically significant change in blood pressure was observed between groups and hypertension events were comparable. There was no difference between the groups in liver or muscle-related endpoints. Reduction in eGFR occurred less often with obicetrapib ( compared to placebo (6.7 % vs. 8.7 % RR 0.77 [0.59, 1.00])). Macular degeneration was reported once in the obicetrapib group (n= 1 [0.1 %]). Deaths were similar between treatment groups. No new safety signals were identified.ConclusionsObicetrapib demonstrated a favorable safety profile over 12 months, with AE rates comparable to placebo. These findings extend our understanding of the safety and tolerability of obicetrapib.

Background

Cholesterol ester transfer protein (CETP) is as an attractive therapeutic target given its role in modulating lipid exchange between lipoproteins [1]. CETP is a plasma protein, secreted by the liver and adipose tissue, that transfers cholesteryl esters from HDL to apolipoprotein B-containing particles (VLDL, IDL, LDL) in exchange for triglycerides [2]. Originally developed for their ability to raise levels of high-density lipoprotein by over 100 %, CETP inhibitors struggled in cardiovascular outcomes trials. The first-generation compound, torcetrapib, was found to activate the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system and elevate blood pressure which resulted in an excess of adverse cardiovascular outcomes [3]. Aldosterone effects were not observed with other subsequent CETP inhibitors however trials of both dalcetrapib [4] and evacetrapib [5] were stopped for futility. The outcomes trial of anacetrapib demonstrated a modest cardiovascular benefit although there was a suggestion of retention in adipose tissue and clinical development did not progress [6]. The finding that anacetrapib’s benefit was proportional to its LDL lowering prompted a re-positioning of the CETP class to be beneficial through lowering atherogenic apolipoprotein B rather than through salutary raising of HDL cholesterol [7].

Obicetrapib, the newest generation CETP inhibitor achieves near-complete CETP blockade, does not engage the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system and appears capable of reducing LDL-C by up to 40 % as monotherapy [8]. While we await the results of the fully enrolled PREVAIL cardiovascular outcomes trial, the objective of this analysis was to comprehensively investigate the safety of obicetrapib compared with placebo in a pooled dataset of two phase III clinical trials.

Methods

Study design

This pooled safety analysis investigated the incidence of adverse events in two phase 3 clinical trials evaluating the effects of obicetrapib in participants with or at high risk of cardiovascular disease [9]. Both studies were approved by local Institutional Review Boards and participants provided written informed consent.

Study population

BROOKLYN (Evaluate the Effect of Obicetrapib in Patients With HeFH on Top of Maximum Tolerated Lipid-Modifying Therapies study; NCT05425745) was conducted to determine the degree of LDL-C reduction with addition of obicetrapib in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). The study enrolled 354 adults, at least 18 years of age, with HeFH defined by at least one of genetic confirmation, Dutch Clinical Network score, or Simon Broome criteria. Eligible participants were treated with maximally tolerated lipid-lowering therapy and had evidence of suboptimal lipid control, defined by LDL-C ≥70 mg/dL. Eligible patients were randomized 2:1 to treatment with 10 mg obicetrapib or matching placebo, administered orally daily for 365 days.

BROADWAY (Randomized Study to Evaluate the Effect of Obicetrapib on Top of Maximum Tolerated Lipid-Modifying Therapies; NCT05142722) was conducted to determine the effect of obicetrapib on LDL-C levels and characterize its safety in patients at high risk of cardiovascular events [10]. The study enrolled 2,530 adults, at least 18 years of age, with high cardiovascular risk determined by the presence of either HeFH or atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Eligible patients were treated with maximally tolerated lipid-lowering therapy and had evidence of suboptimal lipid control, defined by LDL-C ≥100 mg/dL or non-HDL-C ≥130 mg/dL, or with at least 1 of the risk factors of LDL-C 55 to 100 mg/dL and non-HDL-C 85 to 130 mg/dL. Eligible patients were randomized 2:1 to treatment with 10 mg obicetrapib or matching placebo, administered orally daily for 365 days.

Exclusion criteria were the same for both trials and included a cardiovascular event in the previous 3 months; homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia; uncontrolled hypertension (defined as either systolic or diastolic blood pressure≥160 mmHg or ≥100 mmHg, respectively, before randomization) or diabetes (glycated hemoglobin ≥10 % or glucose level ≥270mg/dL), active liver disease (current infectious, neoplastic or metabolic conditions of the liver; unexplained elevations of aminotransferase levels > 3x ULN or total bilirubin >2x ULN), and history of malignancy that had been treated with surgery, radiation or systemic therapy.

Safety assessments

Safety was assessed through monitoring of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) via medical history, medical records, vital sign measurements, physical examinations, laboratory results, and electrocardiograph readings. TEAEs included events that occurred or worsened in severity on or after the first dose through 35 days after the last dose of study drug. Reported TEAEs were coded according to the Medical Dictionary of Regulatory Activities (MedDRA), version 20.1, preferred terms. Adverse event collection methods, visit schedules, laboratory assessments, and protocol-specified duration of study treatment were essentially identical between studies.

Safety endpoints

The endpoints for this pooled safety analyses include treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), treatment-emergent serious adverse events (TESAEs) and TEAEs leading to drug discontinuation. All TEAEs and treatment-emergent serious adverse events (TESAEs) were assessed for seriousness, drug relatedness, or predefined events of special interest (e.g., elevations in AST/ALT, bilirubin, or CK; new-onset diabetes or worsening glycemic control; renal function changes; and macular degeneration). Deaths occurring within the treatment-emergent window were captured as TESAEs and adjudicated for primary cause and temporal association with study drug exposure. Treatment emergent events or events of special interest were identified based on (1) nonclinical or previous clinical findings for obicetrapib; (2) known effects associated with other LLTs; and/or (3) events related to previous studies of CETP inhibitors. These events of special interest included hepatic events (AST or ALT > 3 x ULN or bilirubin >2x ULN), muscle events (myalgia, myopathy, CK > 5x ULN), glycemic events (new onset diabetes mellitus or worsening of glycemic control), renal events (eGFR <30mL/min/m2 or ≥25 % decrease in eGFR from baseline or increase of serum creatinine of ≥0.3mg/dL from baseline), ocular events (macular degeneration) and hypertension events (incidence of systolic BP ≥180 mmHg, and increases ≥20 mmHg from baseline at scheduled visits up to 12 months and 35 days post-treatment and changes in blood pressure medication during the time course of this study). In addition, although not a predefined event of special interest, AEs suggestive of heart failure were also summarized.

Statistical analysis

The safety analysis population included all randomized patients who received at least one dose of study drug. Incidences of AEs were summarized for all TEAEs, study-drug TEAEs, TESAEs and AEs of interest. Absolute counts and percentages were reported, with treatment group risk ratios or differences in means and associated 95 % confidence intervals (CIs) for selected outcomes without adjustment or stratification for any baseline characteristics. Confidence intervals were generated for descriptive purposes only, and no treatment comparison p-values were generated. Furthermore, this pooled safety analysis was not powered for comparisons of any specified outcomes and was not designed to detect rare events, if they occurred.

There were no clinically meaningful differences in overall heart rate, systolic blood pressure or diastolic pressures compared with baseline between the groups. The proportion of participants with systolic BP ≥160 mmHg remained low in the obicetrapib vs. placebo groups (Day 30: 1.7 % vs. 1.6 %; Day 365: 2.6 % vs. 2.2 %). Shifts ≥20 mmHg from baseline occurred in 6.6 % of participants, with similar rates between placebo and obicetrapib. Changes in antihypertensive medication due to BP events were infrequent and comparable between obicetrapib vs. placebo (3.9 vs. 3.1 %). Initiation of new antihypertensive therapy was rare and similar between groups (<2 % overall).

Death

Deaths were infrequent and comparable between groups, with 14 (1.5 %) in the placebo arm and 22 (1.1 %) in the obicetrapib arm, (RR 0.79, CI 0.40-1.53). Cardiovascular deaths occurred in 8 (0.8 %) placebo-treated participants (0.8 %) and 12 (0.6 %) obicetrapib-treated participants, with no significant difference versus placebo (HR 0.74; 95 % CI 0.30–1.82). Coronary heart disease deaths were similarly rare; 7 (0.7 %) with on placebo and 9 (0.5 %) with on obicetrapib—with no statistical separation between groups (HR 0.63; 95 % CI 0.24–1.70).

Suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions

One treatment-related serious adverse event meeting SUSAR criteria—an episode of aggravated heart failure—was reported by the site investigator, who assessed the event as possibly related to investigational product. However, both the independent medical monitor and the Sponsor determined the event to be unrelated, based on the absence of a temporal relationship (the participant had discontinued study drug 25 days prior) and the presence of multiple, more plausible alternative etiologies, including a history of infection, chronic congestive heart failure, cardiovascular disease, atrial fibrillation, and hypertension. Importantly, the overall incidence of heart failure events was rare and comparable between the obicetrapib and placebo groups, and no imbalance suggestive of a treatment-related safety signal was observed (Table 3).

Discusssion

This pooled analysis of two phase III clinical trials demonstrate the safety and tolerability of obicetrapib among patients with or at high risk for cardiovascular disease. Across the pooled BROOKLYN and BROADWAY population, overall TEAEs, study-drug–related TEAEs, serious TEAEs, and discontinuations due to adverse events occurred at similar frequencies between obicetrapib and placebo, consistent with the safety profile observed in the individual trials.

Torcetrapib’s impact on aldosterone was adjudged to be unrelated to CETP inhibition [11] and has not been observed in other clinical development programs however residual concerns persist. Hence, a focus of this safety analysis was understanding the impact on blood pressure and hypertension events. Reassuringly, our pooled analyses demonstrated that blood pressure–related safety outcomes were comparable between obicetrapib and placebo with rates of severe hypertension low at all timepoints, and antihypertensive medication changes or new therapy initiation infrequent and similar between groups.

This analysis positions obicetrapib with some important potential safety benefits compared with other commercially available lipid lowering therapies. Obicetrapib showed significantly fewer rates of new-onset diabetes and/or worsening glycemic control. This contrasts with statins where a higher incidence of new onset diabetes is observed with longer term statin use and particularly among those prescribed high-intensity formulations compared with placebo [12]. While the benefit of LDL-C lowering outweighs any competing diabetes signal among most statin users, this is frequently cited as a concern among patients discontinuing (or hesitant about) statins. A lower incidence of NODM or worsening glycemic control with obicetrapib is consistent with prior literature demonstrating a similar trend with other CETP inhibitors [13]. While incompletely understood, it is likely mediated through increased HDL levels and beta cell protection [14]. Dedicated pooled analyses are now underway and will evaluate incident diabetes among participants without diabetes and glycemic worsening among those with baseline diabetes separately to provide a more clinically meaningful assessment of obicetrapib’s glycemic safety. Although not likely to be considered a glucose-lowering or diabetes drug, a favorable impact on glycemia may represent an important consideration when choosing lipid lowering therapy in the primary prevention context where diabetes development may be particularly important.

Another impediment to the broader uptake of statins, and lipid-lowering therapy more generally, has been concern related to liver and muscle adverse drug reactions. While infrequent in the placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trials of statins, these symptoms may be reported in as many as 1 in 4 new commencers [15]. With regard to other non-statin therapies, PCSK9 inhibitors do not impact liver or muscle-related endpoints [16] however liver enzymes raises were more commonly observed among bempedoic acid treated participants in the CLEAR OUTCOMES trial compared with placebo [17]. While none of these compounds have been compared head-to-head, and enrolled populations differ, reassuringly in our pooled analysis of obicetrapib-treated participants there was no difference in the incidence of abnormal liver or muscle related endpoints compared with placebo.

Although not adequately powered, numerically fewer rates of reduced eGFR favouring obicetrapib compared with placebo are encouraging and warrant further investigation. Kidney safety, and ideally protection, is increasingly important given the frequent clustering of hypertension, obesity and diabetes which increase the risk of both kidney and cardiovascular disease.

A potential genetic association between variants in the CETP gene and age-related macular degeneration has been previously reported [12] although its relevance to therapeutic inhibition of CETP remains uncertain [18]. The development of macular degeneration in this analysis was rare and observed in only one obicetrapib-treated participant (0.1 %). This is consistent with an analysis from the REVEAL study of 30,000 individuals in which there was no difference in the incidence of macular degeneration between anacetrapib and placebo-treated patients [6]. Further insights will be provided in the PREVAIL study.

By 12 months approximately half of patients with a clinical ASCVD event discontinue lipid lowering therapy [19]. Thus, a key characteristic for new and emerging lipid lowering agents is tolerability which will enable patients to persist with therapy that requires long term administration. In this analysis, fewer participants overall discontinued treatment and specifically fewer adverse events led to discontinuation. While these studies were overall short in duration, it is reassuring to see tolerability that is equivalent to placebo, especially among high-risk patients and at least 10 % who were considered statin intolerant.

The results of this analysis should be interpreted in the context of limitations. While this pooled 12-month analysis reflects favourable one-year safety profiles, rare or late-emerging effects cannot be excluded without longer-term follow-up. This represents the largest safety analysis of obicetrapib-treated patients, however overall exposure to the compound remains short and more definitive insights are awaited from the PREVAIL outcomes trial.

Conclusion

This analysis supports the safety and tolerability of obicetrapib among high cardiovascular risk patients.

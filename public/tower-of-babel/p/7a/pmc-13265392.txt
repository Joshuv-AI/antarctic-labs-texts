Awareness and Utilization of the Revised Second International Staging System (R2‐ISS) and 1q21 Gain/Amplification Testing: A Nationwide Web‐Based Survey of Hematologists in Japan
Authors: Nami Tagami, Heigoroh Shirai, Ichiro Hanamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundAlthough the multiple myeloma (MM) treatment landscape has significantly advanced, MM remains an incurable disease. Accurate risk stratification and its broad clinical implementation are essential for optimizing treatment strategies. International Staging System (ISS), its revised versions (R‐ISS and R2‐ISS), and cytogenetic testing for abnormalities such as chromosome 1q21 gain/amplification are recognized tools for risk assessment. However, the level of awareness, use, and barriers to implementation of these tools in clinical practice in Japan have not been elucidated.ObjectiveTo evaluate the current utilization of ISS‐related staging systems among Japanese hematologists and to identify barriers to the implementation of R2‐ISS and 1q21 gain testing.MethodsThe survey was conducted through a medical information platform with 280,000 registered physicians in Japan. A web‐based questionnaire was distributed to hematologists between July 1–31, 2024. A total of 143 hematologists responded. The survey assessed awareness and utilization of staging systems and the implementation status of 1q21 gain testing.ResultsAmong the respondents, 61% routinely used ISS, 71% used R‐ISS, whereas only 27% used R2‐ISS. Major perceived barriers to R2‐ISS adoption included limited insurance coverage (45%), lack of awareness of its clinical utility (37%), and the perception that 1q21 testing is not essential for risk stratification (24%). R‐ISS was widely used regardless of the facility type or the number of patients treated with MM. For 1q21 gain/amplification testing, 53% of physicians always performed the test, with higher implementation among physicians annually treating ≥ 10 patients with MM.ConclusionAlthough ISS and R‐ISS are widely utilized by Japanese hematologists, the adoption of R2‐ISS and 1q21 testing remain limited. The findings suggest that for broader adoption of R2‐ISS, the key barriers, such as limited insurance coverage for fluorescence in situ hybridization testing, insufficient awareness, and lack of integration into national clinical guidelines, should be addressed.

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a hematologic malignancy characterized by the clonal proliferation of plasma cells within the bone marrow, which causes destructive bone lesions and complications, such as hypercalcemia, anemia, and renal damage [1, 2]. MM comprises approximately 10% of hematologic malignancies worldwide [3]. The incidence of MM in Japan is 6/100,000 individuals, with the median age at diagnosis being 67 years; the mortality rate in this population is 3.5/10,000 years [4, 5, 6]. The number of diagnosed MM cases in the Japanese population increased from 7600 in 2019 to 8200 in 2020 [4, 7]. Immunomodulatory agents (thalidomide, lenalidomide, and pomalidomide), proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib, carfilzomib, and ixazomib), monoclonal antibodies (daratumumab, isatuximab, and belantamab), and chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR‐T) cell therapies have been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of relapsed MM [8]. In Japan, therapeutic agents including bortezomib, thalidomide, lenalidomide, pomalidomide, panobinostat, carfilzomib, elotuzumab, ixazomib, daratumumab, and isatuximab have been approved for MM treatment [9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15].

High‐risk MM is characterized by the presence of cytogenetic abnormalities, including del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), 1q21 gain, or p53 mutation [2]. Molecular heterogeneity in MM contributes to variable therapeutic responses and survival outcomes to the same treatment [16]. Approximately 40% of patients with newly diagnosed MM (NDMM) exhibit 1q21 gain (3 copies)/amplification (≥ 4 copies) [17, 18]. Therefore, screening for cytogenetic abnormalities can be a powerful tool for the identification of patients at higher risk of early progression and death [19]. Despite the increasing number of available treatment options, patients with MM often relapse, and high‐risk patients exhibit resistance to commonly used therapies and poor survival outcomes.

The International Staging System (ISS) is a simple risk stratification system that is widely used for predicting the prognosis of patients with MM. It was established in 2005, and it classifies patients into three stages based on serum beta‐2 microglobulin and albumin levels [20]. In 2015, it was updated to revised ISS (R‐ISS), which incorporated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels and detection of specific chromosomal abnormalities using interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization [21, 22]. R2‐ISS, a further refinement, includes ISS, LDH levels, and detection of chromosomal abnormalities del17p, t(4;14), and gain/amplification 1q21. It assigns a weighted score to establish a four‐stage classification system [23] that improves survival prediction accuracy in patients undergoing autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) [21]. However, R2‐ISS has not yet been recommended by the Japanese domestic guidelines, and the majority of physicians in Japan continue to use R‐ISS [24]. Given the impact of 1q21 abnormality on prognosis and treatment outcomes in MM, the awareness and utilization of advanced staging systems, like R2‐ISS, by hematologists are necessary.

In the current study, a web‐based survey was conducted on m3.com, a website that includes over 280,000 registered physicians in Japan, accounting for > 80% of the total number of physicians in Japan. Several clinical and public health studies use this medical information site [25, 26, 27]. We investigated the awareness and use of R2‐ISS and the recommendation of 1q21 gain/amplification measurement by hematologists who treat patients with MM; we also explored the challenges associated with chromosomal testing in Japan.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This observational, web‐based survey was conducted among hematologists who were treating patients with MM in Japan. The participants were members of m3.com (M3 Inc.), a medical information site. A link to the survey URL was provided by posting it on the member site managed by M3 Inc., or by sending an email. Based on the collection status, reminders were sent to encourage more responses. The inclusion criteria were as follows: The respondents had to be affiliated with the hematology department of a medium‐sized or larger hospital in Japan (i.e., ≥ 80 beds), must have treated ≥ 1 patient with MM in the previous year, be involved in making treatment decisions for MM, and must have agreed to participate in the survey. The survey received approval from the ethics review board prior to study initiation. The study was conducted in adherence to the principles set forth by the 18th World Medical Assembly (Helsinki, 1964) and all subsequent amendments. Furthermore, this study was carried out in accordance with the Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects (March 23, 2021) issued by the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT); Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW); and Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (MITI) in Japan.

Survey Design and Objectives

The questionnaire consisted of four screening questions, followed by 19 main questions and 9 demographic questions for eligible physicians (Table S1). The screening questions were designed to ensure that the inclusion criteria were met. The eligible respondents who passed the screening proceeded to answer the main questionnaire, which collected information on respondents' attributes and use of ISS and chromosome analysis test in MM. The questionnaire utilized closed questions with answer options, excluding queries about adverse events or product complaints.

The primary objective of this study was to understand the current use of ISS in MM and to describe the awareness and use of the newly established R2‐ISS in Japan. The secondary objective was to explore the perceptions of physicians and the challenges associated with chromosome analysis testing in MM treatment in Japan, including the implementation status of 1q21 gain/amplification measurement, and to establish the prospect for chromosomal abnormality testing as a prognostic factor in the future. The exploratory objective was to examine the awareness and use of R2‐ISS and the measurement of 1q21 gain/amplification by subgroups of physicians, categorized by the type of facility and the number of patients with MM who were treated by them.

Data Collection

All data were collected and digitally stored. Only the questionnaires with complete data were analyzed. Responses were collected as primary data, anonymized by M3 Inc., and analyzed by Social Survey Research Information Co. Ltd. (SSRI). Data were monitored for quality control, with the possibility of expanding the sample size (i.e., number of physicians surveyed) if any issues were identified.

Statistical Analyses

All analyses were conducted using the “BellCurve for Excel” software. Summary statistics for categorical variables included the number of samples and relative frequency (%). Summary statistics for continuous variables included the number of samples, mean, standard deviation, minimum value, maximum value, median, 1st quartile, 3rd quartile, interquartile range, and 95% confidence interval for the mean.

Results

Study Participants

For this study, the survey questionnaire was open between July 1 to 31, 2024. In total, 215 m3.com member physicians responded to the online questionnaire, and 62 were excluded due to screening failure. Approximately 150 physicians were included, and 145 physicians completed the survey. Among these, two physicians were excluded because they had very short response times and provided the same responses for all questions (Figure S1); finally, 143 respondents were included in our analysis. The demographics of the included respondents and the characteristics of their hospital/department are summarized in Table S2. All responding physicians were affiliated with the hematology department. More than half (71%) of the physicians treated 10 patients in the past year. The majority (61%) of the physicians were affiliated with hospitals with ≥ 500 beds. In terms of the type of institution, 40% of physicians worked at general hospitals, 32% at university hospitals, and 28% at national/public hospitals.

Use of the International Staging System During MM Treatment

Among the 143 physicians, the majority occasionally or always used one of the staging systems: 61% always used ISS, 71% used R‐ISS, and 27% used R2‐ISS (Figure 1). R‐ISS was the most used in all subgroups, and then ISS and R2‐ISS were used among all subgroups (Figure S2). As per the physicians, the primary reason behind utilizing these systems (ISS, n = 126; R‐ISS, n = 131; and R2‐ISS, n = 103) was to predict prognosis (87%, 89%, and 80%, respectively), followed by deciding treatment strategy (36%, 47%, and 45%, respectively; Figure 2).

Use of R2‐ISS During MM Treatment

The most frequently cited reasons for not using R2‐ISS (n = 40) were lack of awareness about the system (48%), inaccessibility to required analytical instruments (such as 1q21 gain/amplification test; 28%), the belief that it does not impact conventional treatment strategies (28%), complexity of the risk classification scoring (25%), and sufficiency of the conventional staging system (23%). Other cited reasons were insufficient evidence and facility policy constraints (Figure S3A). Among the larger group of physicians who do not use R2‐ISS (n = 40), the majority were affiliated with general hospitals (n = 17) and university hospitals (n = 16), while fewer were from national/public hospitals (n = 7). Similar patterns were also evident across other subgroup categories. (Figure S3B).

Conversely, the physicians who intended to implement R2‐ISS (n = 70) reported that the reasons behind this were sufficient evidence for its prognostic utility (69%), establishment in the United States (US) and European guidelines (47%), recommendation by highly specialized physicians (30%), and facility policies (9%) (Figure S4A). Similar trends were observed in all subgroups; > 50% respondents from universities, national/public, and general hospitals used R2‐ISS due to its prognostic utility (Figure S4B).

Physician Perspectives on ISS

Most physicians reported that after a new staging system has been recommended by the domestic (71%) and international guidelines (59%), it should be immediately used. Over 82% supported the adoption of the system if evidence is available, and 78% agreed that staging systems should be used if they influence treatment choices. Approximately 66% reported that they would use them if available under facility policy, and 76% favored adoption if the new system is better than the conventional system. More than 50% of physicians supported the introduction of ISS based on the recommendation of highly specialized fellow physicians or by a research group (Figure 3).

Use of Chromosomal Testing During MM Treatment

Most physicians reported always or occasionally evaluating chromosomal abnormalities, including del (17p), t(11;14), t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), 1q21 gain/amplification, and del(13q). More than 70% of physicians reported that they always test for chromosomal abnormalities del(17p) and t(4;14), and approximately 50% always assess t(14;16) and 1q21 gain/amplification (Figure 4). Moreover, 66% of physicians reported that the main issue with chromosomal testing was the limitation on the number of tests covered by insurance. The majority of the physicians who performed chromosomal analysis reported that the tests for chromosomal abnormalities del(17p) and t(4;14) were covered by insurance (86% and 78%, respectively; Figure S5).

1q21 Gain/Amplification Measurement During MM Treatment

Among all included physicians (n = 143), 53% and 33% always or occasionally measured 1q21 gain/amplification, respectively. 1q21 gain/amplification measurement was mainly used for prognosis (76%), followed by selection/addition/switching of drug (51%) and calculation of risk classification by R2‐ISS (37%; Figure S6A).

1q21 gain/amplification was more frequently evaluated in general and university hospitals and by physicians who had treated ≥ 10 patients with MM (Figure S6B). To predict prognosis, the majority of the physicians tested 1q21 gain (n = 92), followed by 1q21 amplification (n = 87) and 1q21 gain/amplification (n = 78) (Figure S7).

Physicians' Perspectives on 1q21 Gain/Amplification Measurement

Most physicians agreed that 1q21 gain/amplification is an important prognostic factor in MM (80%) and supported 1q21 gain/amplification measurement to determine treatment strategy (78%). More than half (62%) of the physicians believed that substantial evidence is available to support 1q21 gain/amplification testing, and 74% reported its impact on drug selection. However, several barriers to its broader adoption were identified: 45% of physicians reported that other test items are prioritized due to limitations of insurance coverage, 37% did not understand the usefulness of 1q21 gain/amplification testing, and 28% did not consider it necessary to classify the severity of MM (Figure 5).

Discussion

This web‐based survey provides insights into the real‐world use of international staging systems and chromosomal testing among physicians treating MM in Japan. Our survey highlights that the physicians used ISS (61%) and R‐ISS (71%) for prognostication, treatment planning, and therapeutic decision‐making in MM. This result was consistent across all subgroups, including the three facility types and in two physician groups (treating < 10 and ≥ 10 patients/year with MM). This trend is in line with the global data highlighting the utilization of ISS and R‐ISS [8, 28]. However, adoption of R2‐ISS was notably lower (27%), reflecting several barriers to its implementation in Japan.

Implications of 1q21 gain/amplification status under recent therapeutic regimens, including combinations with anti‐CD38 antibodies, have been summarized in several studies and reviews [19, 29, 30]. A study on Asian patients demonstrated that 1q21 gain was associated with shorter progression‐free survival (PFS) with bortezomib treatment, and ASCT was also less effective in patients with 1q21 gain [31]. A retrospective study showed that R2‐ISS score at the time of treatment initiation could be a dynamic prognostic factor for PFS and overall survival (OS) in patients with RRMM who are treated with anti‐CD38 antibody‐based triplet therapy [32]. The MAIA trial, a Phase III study of transplant‐ineligible patients with NDMM, demonstrated that the addition of daratumumab to lenalidomide and dexamethasone (DRd) improved PFS in the 1q21 gain/amplification subgroup for DRd vs. Rd. arm (median PFS: 53.2 vs. 32.3 months, respectively; hazard ratio [HR]: 0.63, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.46–0.88). However, the subgroup analysis of the MAIA study revealed that the group with 1q amp did not demonstrate prolongation of the PFS (HR: 0.81, p5% CI: 0.54–1.21) [33, 34]. Similarly, the GRIFFIN trial, a Phase II study, showed that the addition of daratumumab to bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (DVRd) improved minimal residual disease (MRD) negativity in the 1q21 gain/amplification subgroup of transplant‐eligible patients with NDMM. MRD negativity (10−5) at the end of maintenance was 61.8% vs. 28.6%, for DVRd vs. VRd, respectively [35, 36]. Isatuximab has also exhibited efficacy in high‐risk cytogenetic subgroups. In the ICARIA‐MM trial, isatuximab plus pomalidomide–dexamethasone (IsaPd) improved outcomes in the 1q21 gain/amplification group compared with Pd alone (median PFS: 9.5 vs. 3.8 months, respectively; median OS: 21.3 vs. 13.9 months, respectively). Subgroup analyses revealed consistent benefits across isolated 1q21gain, 1q21 amplification, and 1q21 gain/amplification categories [37, 38]. In the IMROZ Phase 3 study (N = 446), 35.9% Isa‐VRd and 38.7% VRd patients with NDMM were 1q21+. Isa‐VRd showed superior PFS vs. VRd in 1q21 gain/amplification (HR: 0.407, p = 0.0002) and isolated 1q21+ (HR = 0.369, p = 0.0004). Higher response rates were achieved with Isa‐VRd vs. VRd in 1q21+ patients: ORR (95.8% vs. 85.7%), ≥ CR (76.9% vs. 60.0%), and MRD‐negativity (63.2% vs. 41.4%) [39]. The IKEMA trial further confirmed the benefits of isatuximab plus carfilzomib and dexamethasone (IsaKd) over Kd alone in the 1q21 gain/amplification group, i.e., superior PFS, overall response rate, depth of response, and MRD negativity [38, 40]. In the updated IKEMA subgroup analysis, Isa‐Kd improved outcomes in RRMM patients with 1q21 gain/amplification. In the overall 1q21+ patients, median PFS was 25.8 vs. 16.2 months in the Isa‐Kd vs. Kd arm, respectively, and 38.2 vs. 16.2 months in patients with isolated 1q21. MRD negativity and ≥ CR rates were also higher with Isa‐Kd vs. Kd arm: 29.3% vs. 15.4% in overall 1q21+, 36.2% vs. 12.9% in isolated 1q21+, 27.9% vs. 13.5% in 1q21 gain, and 31.3% vs. 20.0% in 1q21 amplification. However, the subgroup analysis revealed that patients with 1q21 amplification showed a less pronounced PFS benefit without statistical significance (HR: 0.73, 95% CI: 0.33–1.63) [41]. These data collectively underscore the significance of integrating 1q21 status into therapeutic decision‐making for both prognostication and optimization of the targeted therapies. 1q21 gain/amplification is one of the most prevalent cytogenetic abnormalities in MM [17]. Amplification of 1q21 is associated with worse prognosis compared to that with its gain alone, underscoring the importance of the detection of 1q21 early in the treatment to improve patient outcome [42]. Despite its prognostic significance, 47% of the surveyed Japanese physicians do not routinely assess 1q21 status. More than 50% of physicians measured 1q21 gain/amplification mainly to predict prognosis and for the selection of drug therapy; the barriers to its implementation included limited test availability, insurance coverage, absence of evidence, and unawareness about the usefulness of 1q21 gain/amplification measurement.

A major barrier for R2‐ISS adoption among physicians is limited awareness due to educational gaps and the complexity of evolving treatments, which is in line with a previous real‐world study [43]. Inaccessibility to instruments required for chromosomal testing is another barrier. This aligns with broader trends in both clinical trials and real‐world studies. In clinical trials, such as FIRST (47%) [44] and ASPIRE (53%) trials [45], the proportion of patients with cytogenetics reported used to be low, resulting in limited data interpretation. Limited availability of analytical instruments at different sites and perceived complexity of the R2‐ISS scoring system also contribute to its underuse [46, 47]. Systemic disparities, particularly in low‐ and middle‐income countries, worsen these challenges. As per Bamodu et al., limited access to diagnostics, trained personnel, and institutional support restricts the implementation of advanced cancer staging systems in these settings [48]. Comprehensive genomic profiling panels have recently gained insurance approval in Japan for patients with advanced solid tumors [49]. Standardized and reimbursed testing pathways for MM‐specific cytogenetic abnormalities, including 1q21 gain/amplification, remain limited. In this study, more than half of the respondents reported limitations on the number of tests covered by insurance as the main issue with chromosomal testing (data not shown). Conversely, among the physicians intending to implement R2‐ISS, the primary motivation was the availability of supporting clinical evidence, its inclusion in the US and European guidelines [50], (Figure S4A), as well as institutional policies promoting comprehensive risk assessment.

The recent IMS/IMWG consensus recommendations emphasize that 1q21 gain/amplification should be assessed in conjunction with other high‐risk cytogenetic abnormalities, including t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), and del(1p32), with certain abnormalities such as t(4;14) no longer considered high‐risk in the absence of concurrent 1q gain or del(1p) [50]. Additionally, the updated criteria incorporate TP53 mutations detected by next‐generation sequencing (NGS) alongside del(17p), and define biallelic TP53 inactivation as independently high‐risk, reflecting a shift toward more comprehensive genomic risk assessment. In this survey, a proportion of physicians indicated they would consider implementing the international staging system under certain conditions, such as when insurance coverage becomes available or when clearer guideline recommendations are established (Figure 3). This conditional willingness suggests that structural and educational interventions could increase adoption rates. Notably, R2‐ISS usage was more prevalent in university and general hospitals than in national/public hospitals, likely reflecting differential access to advanced diagnostics. The challenges identified in this survey extend beyond R2‐ISS adoption alone. They reflect a broader need for establishing standardized, insurance‐covered, high‐quality risk assessment assays and guideline‐level implementation pathways in Japan.

Several limitations should be noted. This single‐country, self‐selected web‐based survey may limit generalizability, and voluntary participation may have introduced selection bias toward physicians with greater awareness of advanced risk stratification. Furthermore, as the survey captured physician‐reported rather than patient‐level data, findings should be interpreted with caution, and larger prospective studies are warranted.

The strength of this survey is the participation of physicians working at various university, national/public, and general hospitals in Japan, enabling comparisons by facility type and physicians who have treated < 10 and ≥ 10 patients/year with MM. Despite these limitations, this survey provides valuable insights into the current practices and highlights actionable targets for improving risk stratification and personalized treatment strategies in Japan. The continued reliance on ISS, early‐stage adoption of R2‐ISS, and underutilization of 1q21 testing reveal meaningful opportunities to enhance MM care. Furthermore, addressing real‐world barriers through education, expanded access to testing, and integration of comprehensive risk assessment frameworks into domestic clinical guidelines will be essential for the advancement of MM treatment in Japan.

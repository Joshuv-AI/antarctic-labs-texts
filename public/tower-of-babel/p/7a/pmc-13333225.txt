Further Methodological Considerations for Evaluating T2-Weighted Imaging Features as Biomarkers in Invasive Breast Cancer
Authors: Xiaoxia Yang, Chengyu Zhong, Liyuan Meng, Cailing Yu, Wane Li, Yingfeng Wang, Bo Yang
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the study by Youn et al. [1], which systematically investigated the associations between T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) features and clinicopathologic characteristics in invasive breast cancer, as well as their relationship with neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) outcomes. The authors demonstrated that higher intratumoral T2 signal intensity (SI) and peritumoral edema grades were associated with aggressive tumor features and that longer intratumoral T2 relaxation times were associated with disease progression during NAC. These findings provide valuable insights into the role of T2WI as a noncontrast biomarker in breast cancer. Nevertheless, several points warrant further discussion to enhance the interpretability and clinical applicability of these findings.

First, the grading system for intratumoral T2 SI and peritumoral edema relied on visual assessment relative to the surrounding breast tissue and water. Although interobserver agreement was substantial (weighted kappa 0.678 and 0.771), the subjective nature of such grading may introduce variability across different clinical settings and MRI scanners. Prior studies have shown that visual assessment of T2 hyperintensity can be influenced by fat suppression technique, magnetic field strength, and reader experience [23]. Incorporating quantitative T2 relaxation time mapping, as performed in this study, offers a more objective and reproducible approach. However, the authors did not directly compare the predictive performance of visual grading versus quantitative T2 values for NAC outcomes. Such a comparison would have helped clarify whether quantitative measurements provide added value beyond visual assessment in clinical practice.

Second, the study defined disease progression during NAC according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 on MRI, with a nadir-based comparison. The authors reported a relatively high progression rate of 13.2%, particularly among patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC; 20.7%). Given that mid-treatment MRI is not universally performed in all institutions, the clinical generalizability of progression definitions based on routine mid-treatment imaging warrants further evaluation. Recent studies have suggested that tumor progression during NAC may be underestimated when using only baseline and post-treatment imaging and that the clinical significance of progression detected solely on routine mid-treatment MRI remains unclear [4]. Clarifying whether the progression events in this study were associated with changes in clinical management or long-term outcomes would strengthen the clinical relevance of T2WI as a predictive marker.

Third, the study excluded mucinous carcinomas because of their inherently high T2 SI, which may confound the associations. Although this approach is methodologically sound, it limits the generalizability of the findings to all invasive breast cancer subtypes. Mucinous carcinomas represent a distinct biological entity with a favorable prognosis and often exhibit high T2 SI, mimicking aggressive features [5]. Future studies exploring whether T2WI characteristics can help differentiate mucinous carcinomas from other T2-hyperintense but more aggressive tumors (e.g., TNBC or high-grade HER2-positive cancers) would be clinically useful.

Finally, although the authors demonstrated associations between longer intratumoral T2 relaxation times and disease progression during NAC, the odds ratio was expressed per 10-ms increase. Given that the typical range of T2 relaxation times in breast cancer is approximately 60–120 ms [6], a 10-ms increment may represent a substantial change. Providing the absolute T2 relaxation time cutoffs associated with progression risk or presenting results using standardized effect sizes, would facilitate clinical interpretation and potential translation into decision-making tools.

In summary, this study provides important evidence supporting the role of T2WI features as biomarkers of aggressive tumor biology and NAC response in invasive breast cancer. Future research should focus on standardizing quantitative T2WI assessments, validating the findings in independent cohorts, and evaluating the clinical utility of T2WI-guided risk stratification for personalized treatment planning.

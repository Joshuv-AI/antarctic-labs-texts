Efficacy of Dapagliflozin vs. Saroglitazar on Hepatic Steatosis and Fibrosis in Patients of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Metabolic-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD)
Authors: Bijay K. Das, Sambit Das, Dayanidhi Meher, Arun K. Choudhury, Sourav Padhee, Sandeep K. Sahu, Devadarshini Sahoo, Binod Prusty, Vishal Agarwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:There is a rising prevalence of metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), particularly in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus, which needs early detection and treatment to prevent the grave outcomes. Dapagliflozin and Saroglitazar are known to improve metabolic and liver health in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.Methods:Our study was a randomised, controlled, open-label, parallel trial of 101 patients with T2DM and MASLD. Patients were randomly assigned to receive Dapagliflozin 10 mg or Saroglitazar 4 mg on top of standard diabetes care. The primary endpoints were changes in hepatic steatosis and fibrosis (FIB) as estimated by controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) and liver stiffness measurement (LSM) using FibroScan, respectively. The secondary endpoints were changes in anthropometric parameters, glycemic parameters, lipid profiles, transaminases, and FIB scores, which were monitored at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months.Results:At the end of 6 months of study, both cohorts resulted in significant clinical benefits. Comparison between the two cohorts revealed no significant statistical difference between the two groups regarding their impact on the primary endpoints (CAP and LSM). Minimal but statistically significant intergroup differences were noted only for BMI (P = 0.033) and LDL cholesterol (P = 0.020) in the Dapagliflozin cohort and alanine transaminase levels (P = 0.037) in the Saroglitazar cohort.Conclusion:Both Dapagliflozin and Saroglitazar are potent therapeutic options in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus who also have MASLD.

INTRODUCTION

Metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is one of the commonest global health threats. It is considered a hepatic component of metabolic syndrome. The prevalence of MASLD is on the rise, and in the presence of type 2 diabetes mellitus, its prevalence is even higher, affecting over 50% of the population in India and globally.[12] Moreover, the simultaneous occurrence of type 2 diabetes mellitus and MASLD has clinical implications in the form of poorer prognosis and early transition to steatohepatitis, advanced fibrosis (FIB), cirrhosis of the liver, and hepatic carcinoma.[3] There is also an increase in risk for cardiovascular disease and an increase in mortality rates.[4]

Given the grave outcomes in these subsets of patients, early diagnosis and risk stratification are of paramount importance. Ultrasonography (USG) is the most commonly employed imaging modality, which is easily available, accessible, affordable, and has good sensitivity to identify moderate to severe hepatic steatosis.[5] The limitation of USG is that it is not sensitive enough to assess FIB.

Due to this limitation with USG, transient elastography is currently a valuable non-invasive technique to assess both hepatic steatosis and FIB. Controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) is used to assess hepatic steatosis, and liver stiffness measurement (LSM) acts as a surrogate marker for FIB.[6] Due to the simultaneous assessment of both steatosis and FIB, transient elastography is an ideal tool for diagnosis and monitoring.

Apart from imaging modalities, certain biochemical parameters can provide critical insights into MASLD. Parameters like transaminases (alanine transaminase [ALT] and aspartate transaminase [AST]) are often elevated but are nonspecific.[7] FIB scores have been developed and validated, such as the Non alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) FIB score (NFS) and the FIB-4 index, which can be used to grade the stages of FIB to serve as a non-invasive risk for advanced FIB.[89] These scores take into consideration the readily available biochemical and haematological parameters apart from age, Body mass index (BMI), presence of impaired fasting or diabetes mellitus to stratify patients for further evaluation or intervention, and are particularly useful in a resource-limited scenario.

The biggest lacunae in managing these patients of type 2 diabetes mellitus with MASLD are the lack of approved pharmacological treatments that target hepatic steatosis and FIB. Only semaglutide and resmeterome are the two approved drugs for the management of MASLD. Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors like Empagliflozin and dual peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) α/γ agonist, Saroglitazar have shown promising results in improving hepatic steatosis and metabolic parameters.[1011] However, there is a paucity of data on the direct comparison of these two groups of drugs in these subsets of patients.

This study was carried out to assess and compare the effects of both these drugs on hepatic steatosis and FIB in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus with MASLD. By incorporating USG, transient elastography, biochemical, and metabolic parameters, this study is using a multimodal assessment approach to provide a comprehensive comparison of these two classes of drugs.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This was a prospective randomised open-label parallel group study conducted over a period of 6 months at the department of Endocrinology, Kalinga Institute of Medical Sciences, KIMS, Bhubaneswar, which is a tertiary care hospital in the eastern part of India. Patients who were 18 years and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus and MASLD (confirmed by biochemical parameters and ultrasonographic assessment) were enrolled. Written informed consent was obtained from all the patients after approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee.

Post-enrollment, a comprehensive medical history and detailed physical examination were done. Anthropometric parameters like weight, BMI, and waist circumference (WC) were estimated as per recommended guidelines. The baseline investigations that were done were transaminases (AST, ALT), serum albumin, total platelet count (TPC), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), 2-hour post-prandial blood sugar, glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c), and fasting lipid profile (TC, TG, HDL, and LDL). USG was used to diagnose and grade the severity of steatosis. Transient elastography using a FibroScan device (Echosens Touch 502) was used to measure the CAP and LSM, which quantified the hepatic steatosis and FIB, respectively. Two validated non-invasive FIB scores, the NFS and FIB-4 index, were obtained. These scores were calculated based on formulae that incorporated age, BMI, glycemic status, AST, ALT, albumin, and TPC for NFS and age, AST, ALT, and TPC for the FIB-4 index. Other causes of steatotic liver diseases were excluded as per the recommended diagnostic criteria.

By assuming a non-inferiority margin of 5%, the true difference in mean LSM between the two groups was 0, at 5% level of significance, 95% confidence interval, and 80% power, the calculated sample size was 100. By taking 10% of the dropout rate, the total sample size was 110, i.e. 55 in each group. Patients got randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive one of the two drugs, i.e. Dapagliflozin or Saroglitazar. The sample size was determined to evaluate the non-inferiority of Dapagliflozin compared with Saroglitazar for improvement in hepatic steatosis and FIB parameters (CAP and LSM), based on a predefined non-inferiority margin of 5%. The study was not designed or powered to assess superiority between the treatment groups.

The randomisation was done using a computer-generated dynamic allocation system, which was stratified by age, sex, BMI, and HbA1c levels. One group received 10 mg of Dapagliflozin, and the other group received 4 mg of Saroglitazar. Both these drugs were prescribed on top of standard diabetes care, lifestyle, and dietary modifications.

Post-randomisation, patients were followed up at 3 months and 6 months. During the follow-up, patients were evaluated clinically to assess the changes in anthropometric parameters and were enquired for development of any adverse events. Laboratory investigations and FibroScan were also done at a similar time interval [Figure 1].

Statistical analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics Version 25.0. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), while categorical variables were expressed as proportions. For between-group comparisons, ANOVA or the Kruskal–Wallis test was employed, and within-group changes over time were evaluated using paired t-tests. Associations between categorical variables were assessed using the Chi-square test. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant, indicating a meaningful difference or association.

Ethical aspects

The Institutional Ethics Committee of Kalinga Institute of Medical Sciences, Bhubaneswar, approved the study. Approval letter no-KIIT/KIMS/IEC/1341/2023 dated 08.06.2023. Written informed consent was obtained for participation in the study and use of patient data for research and educational purposes. The study procedures adhered to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki 1964 and its subsequent revisions. CTRI NO-CTRI/2023/06/053723.

RESULTS

The baseline characteristics of both the cohorts were similar in most of the parameters except for BMI, WC, and ALT. The mean age was similar, and both groups had a male predominant population. BMI and WC at baseline were statistically significant between the two groups, with the Dapagliflozin cohort having the higher BMI (P = 0.003) and WC (P = 0.013). ALT was higher in the Saroglitazar cohort at baseline (P = 0.018) [Table 1]. Although randomisation was stratified by BMI categories, this approach only ensures a similar distribution of patients across BMI ranges, not identical mean BMI values between groups. Within each BMI stratum, participants are still allocated randomly. When the sample size is modest, this process can result in chance differences in continuous variables such as BMI and WC at baseline. In addition, WC was not included as a stratification variable, making baseline differences in this parameter more likely.

The Saroglitazar group had a higher proportion of patients with steatosis grade 3. The Dapagliflozin cohort had more patients in the mild category. However, the difference between the cohorts just fell short of statistical significance. This implied a baseline difference in hepatic steatosis, with the Saroglitazar cohort having a worse hepatic involvement.

At 6 months, both dapagliflozin and saroglitazar were associated with improvement in anthropometric, glycaemic, hepatic, lipid, and elastography parameters.

In the Dapagliflozin group, reductions were observed in body weight, BMI, WC, FPG, 2-hour post-prandial glucose, HbA1c, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), LDL cholesterol, alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, LSM, and CAP (P < 0.001). HDL cholesterol, NFS, and FIB-4 index did not show a change at 6 months.

In the Saroglitazar group, reductions were seen in body weight, BMI, WC, FPG, 2-hour post-prandial glucose, HbA1c, TC, TG, alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, LSM, and CAP (P < 0.001). HDL cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, NFS, and FIB-4 index remained unchanged.

On intergroup comparison, no difference was observed between the two groups for change in body weight, WC, fasting and post-prandial glucose, HbA1c, TC, TG, HDL cholesterol, AST, LSM, CAP, NFS, or FIB-4 index (P > 0.05). Greater reduction in BMI, LDL cholesterol, and ALT was noted in the Dapagliflozin group [Table 2].

Participants receiving Dapagliflozin had significantly higher body weight compared with the Saroglitazar group (F = 17.47, P < 0.001, partial η2 = 0.16). Patients who were treated with either drug did not develop any serious adverse events or any adverse events that led to discontinuation from the study. Eight patients in the Dapagliflozin cohort, of whom five were males, developed urinary tract infection (UTI), and two patients developed Balanoposthitis. These patients were managed conservatively with adequate hydration, appropriate antibiotics, and antifungals. Once treated these symptoms did not further recur. No adverse events were seen in patients treated with Saroglitazar.

DISCUSSION

This study intended to compare the efficacy of Dapagliflozin to that of Saroglitazar on hepatic steatosis and FIB in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus with MASLD by assessing the effects of the drugs on various anthropometric, glycemic, lipid, hepatic, and FIB-related parameters. Over the 6-month period of study, it was observed that both the drugs had beneficial effects across the different metabolic domains in the study, with few intergroup differences.

The Dapagliflozin cohort had a higher baseline weight, BMI, and WC. At the end of the study period, it was observed that the baseline difference that was present in the anthropometric parameters improved significantly, which demonstrated the excellent metabolic effects of the drug. Shimizu et al.[12] demonstrated similar weight and BMI reduction in their study with Dapagliflozin over a period of 24 weeks. Bolinder et al.[13] demonstrated significant WC reduction after treatment with Dapagliflozin over a period of 24 weeks. Unlike our study, Goyal et al.[14] could not demonstrate a significant weight-reducing capacity of Saroglitazar in their study. However, in studies conducted by Mitra[15] and Choudhuri et al.,[16] it was observed that Saroglitazar significantly improved BMI and WC. After accounting for baseline differences, Dapagliflozin was associated with greater improvement in anthropometric parameters than Saroglitazar at 6 months, with significant reductions in body weight, BMI, and WC. These findings suggest a more pronounced effect of Dapagliflozin on overall and central adiposity. Baseline BMI and WC differed between groups but were adjusted using ANCOVA, and the persistence of treatment effects suggests that outcomes were not primarily driven by baseline adiposity differences. In the present study, the Dapagliflozin group showed significantly greater reductions in body weight and BMI than the Saroglitazar group, which may have contributed to the observed hepatic improvements through weight-related metabolic effects. However, the Saroglitazar group also demonstrated improvement in hepatic parameters despite modest weight reduction, suggesting additional weight-independent effects, possibly mediated through PPAR-α and PPAR-γ activation and improved lipid metabolism. Both Dapagliflozin and Saroglitazar significantly improved FPG, 2 hours post-prandial blood sugar, and HbA1c with no statistically significant intergroup differences between the two. This is aligned with the study done by Das et al.[17] in which significant improvement was noted in glycemic parameters after treatment with Dapagliflozin for a period of 6 months. Similarly, a study conducted by Mitra[15] had similar outcomes following 6 months of therapy with Saroglitazar.

Dapagliflozin significantly improved TC, TG, and LDL over a period of 6 months. Although HDL levels improved, the change was not statistically significant. Saroglitazar had statistically significant improvement in TC and TG at the end of 6 months. Unlike Dapagliflozin, there was no statistically significant improvement in LDL levels in patients treated with Saroglitazar. Saroglitazar, like Dapagliflozin, had no statistically significant improvement in HDL at the end of the study. This study is aligned with the study done by Lin et al.[18] who demonstrated significant improvement in TC and LDL in patients treated with Dapgliflozin over a period of 48 weeks. Likewise, in the study done by Das et al.,[17] there was significant improvement in TG in patients who were treated with Dapagliflozin for a period of 6 months. However, unlike our study, Shimizu et al.[12] found significant improvement in HDL in patients treated with Dapagliflozin over a period of 24 weeks. Goyal et al.[14] demonstrated significant improvements in TC, TG, HDL, and LDL in patients treated with Saroglitazar over a period of 6 months, which was similar to our study, which showed improvements in TC and TG but not HDL and LDL.

Both Dapagliflozin and Saroglitazar improved ALT and AST levels at the end of 6 months of therapy. Similar outcomes were reported by Shimizu et al.[12] where it was shown that Dapagliflozin for a period of 24 weeks improved ALT and AST in patients with MASLD. Goyal et al.[14] also demonstrated that Saroglitazar had similar effects on ALT and AST in patients with MASLD. There was no statistically significant difference between the two groups in AST levels at the end of 6 months. There was, however, a significant difference between the two groups in ALT levels at the end of 6 months.

Both groups showed significant improvement in CAP and LSM over a period of 6 months. Similar results were reported by Shimizu et al.[12] with Dapagliflozin and Goyal et al.[14] with Saroglitazar where it was noted that both drugs significantly improved hepatic steatosis (assessed by CAP). Lin et al.[18] in their study noted similar results in LSM reduction with Dapagliflozin. There was no significant intergroup difference in LSM and CAP at the end of our study. Although baseline ALT was higher in the Saroglitazar group, the Dapagliflozin group showed greater improvement in CAP and LSM, indicating that the findings were unlikely to be solely due to baseline ALT differences, though residual confounding cannot be excluded.

There was no significant improvement noted in FIB-4 or NFS in both groups. This finding is corroborated by the studies done by Shimizu et al.[12] who could not find improvement in either of the FIB scores with Dapagliflozin. In contrast to our study, Shah et al.[19] in their study did find that Saroglitazar did improve the NFS when patients were treated for 24 weeks. Likewise, Binmazi et al.[20] in their 6-month study found that Saroglitazar improved the FIB-4 score in patients who had pre diabetes. There was no significant intergroup difference between the two groups at the end of 6 months. In the present study, significant reductions were observed in LSM and CAP, whereas composite FIB indices such as FIB-4 and NFS did not show significant changes over 6 months. This difference likely reflects the varying temporal dynamics of these markers, as transient elastography is more sensitive to early changes in hepatic stiffness related to inflammation and steatosis, while composite scores often require longer durations to reflect FIB regression. A longer follow-up may therefore be necessary to demonstrate meaningful changes in FIB-4 and NFS. The lack of significant change in FIB-4 despite improvement in transaminases may be due to the dependence of the score on platelet count, which reflects FIB and changes slowly over time. Moreover, the square root transformation of ALT in the formula reduces the magnitude of change in FIB-4 despite substantial biochemical improvement.

Liver-related parameters, including LSM, CAP, NFS, and FIB-4 scores, did not differ significantly between the two groups after adjustment, indicating that changes in hepatic steatosis or FIB may require longer treatment duration or may not be adequately captured by non-invasive indices over a 6-month period.

The observed improvement in LSM and CAP, reflecting FIB- and steatosis-related changes, respectively, in the absence of significant alterations in FIB-4 and NFS, underscores the heterogeneous nature of non-invasive assessment tools in MASLD. LSM and CAP are sensitive to dynamic intrahepatic changes and may improve early with reductions in hepatic steatosis, inflammation, hepatocellular ballooning, and sinusoidal congestion following metabolic interventions, even before true FIB regression occurs. In contrast, FIB-4 and NFS are composite biochemical scores primarily designed to identify advanced FIB and are less responsive to short-term therapeutic changes, particularly in patients with low or intermediate baseline FIB risk. Additionally, components of these scores, such as age, platelet count, and transaminases, tend to remain relatively stable over short follow-up periods, limiting their utility for monitoring early treatment response. Therefore, the discordance observed in the present study likely reflects early metabolic and inflammatory improvement rather than definitive collagen remodelling. These findings highlight the complementary roles of elastography-based and serum-based tools and suggest that reliance solely on biochemical FIB scores may underestimate early therapeutic benefits in MASLD.[2122]

Patients who were treated with Dapagliflozin developed adverse events in the form of UTI and Balanoposthitis, which did not recur and responded appropriately to anti-fungal. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating the high risk of genital mycotic infections associated with Dapagliflozin.[23]

The limitations of our study were the small sample size and limited study duration, which could have limited our assessment of long-term effects on hepatic FIB progression or the resolution of MASLD with either of these drugs. Open-label design may introduce bias, particularly for subjective or semi-objective outcomes. The baseline differences in WC, BMI, and ALT could have been confounding factors in the outcomes. Liver biopsy, which is the gold standard in diagnosing MASLD, and MRI PDFF, which can quantify liver fat content with higher sensitivity, were not used in our study. Additionally, improvements in liver parameters observed in the study may be secondary to weight loss and glycemic improvement, rather than direct hepatic effects of the drugs. CAP and LSM are subject to variability and operator dependence, and their sensitivity in detecting small or early changes, particularly over short study durations, may be limited. In our study, all the CAP and LSM measurements were done by a single operator which improves robustness of the study.

From our study, we can conclude that both Dapagliflozin and Saroglitazar effectively improved anthropometric, glycemic, hepatic, and lipid profile parameters in patients of type 2 diabetes mellitus and MASLD.

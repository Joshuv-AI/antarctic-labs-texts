The burden of hyperkalaemia on hospital healthcare resources
Authors: Hugh Logan Ellis, Mohammad Al-Agil, Philip Kelly, James Teo, Claire Sharpe, Martin Whyte
Published: 2024

Background

The incidence of hyperkalaemia in hospitalised patients is high and is associated with greater length of stay and increased mortality. However, there is a hidden burden of hyperkalaemia that includes the use of hospital resources such as clinical consults, therapies for hyperkalaemia and high dependency beds. We have evaluated the hidden resource utilisation for hyperkalaemia in hospitalised patients.

Methods and findings

A single-centre, observational cohort study of a large tertiary care hospital, using standard medical care records. The period of observation was January 1 2017 – December 31 2020. The CogStack system was used to search unstructured and structured data, from individual patient records, at scale. Patients were followed up to 30 days after hospital discharge.

Primary objectives were i) to determine the incidence and distribution of hyperkalaemia in a hospitalised population and ii) the in-hospital mortality and morbidity from hyperkalaemia.

Secondary objectives were i) determine the numbers of individuals treated for hyperkalaemia and the number of treatments received per patient, for hyperkalaemia ii) Numbers and proportion of patients requiring a specialist consult for hyperkalaemia during the index admission.

Results

Of 129,172 patients who had potassium measurements in A&E, the incidence of hyperkalaemia was 85.7 per 1,000. Over the study period, there were 49,011 emergency (via A&E) admissions. Potassium of >6.5 mmol/L was independently associated with 3.9-fold worse in-hospital mortality than normokalaemia (Fig 1). Chronic kidney disease (CKD) was present in 21% of those with potassium 5–5.5 mmol/L and 54% in those with potassium >6.5 mmol/L. The corresponding figures for diabetes were 20% and 32% respectively. Of patients with potassium >6.5 mmol/L, 29% were seen by the renal team and 13% by critical care outreach. The proportion of those with potassium >6.5 mmol/L transferred to the renal unit after admission was 22% and to the HDU/ICU was 8%. Insulin dextrose use increased with the severity of hyperkalaemia: from 1% of those with peak potassium 5–5.5 mmol/L to 56% of those with peak potassium >6.5mmol/L. Just under 50% of the latter group required more than one dose of insulin-dextrose. Dialysis was used in 39% of those with peak potassium >6.5 mmol/L.

Conclusions

The burden from hyperkalaemia in hospital is considerable with greater in-hospital mortality and reduced likelihood of hospital discharge. Hyperkalaemia is associated with greater utilisation of nephrology services and consultations from critical care staff, as well as greater likelihood of patient transfer to those areas.

Comparative efficacy of percutaneous vertebroplasty combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation versus percutaneous vertebroplasty alone in the treatment of elderly osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures
Authors: Chunfeng Ma, Zhineng Chen, Qun Fang, Yue Shu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study aimed to assess the comparative efficacy of percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation versus PVP alone in elderly patients with osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF). Ninety-four elderly patients with OVCF were randomly classified into the control (47 patients) and combined (47 patients) groups. The control group received PVP, while the combined group received PVP combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation. Perioperative indicators such as intraoperative blood lose, operative time, and hospitalization time were recorded. Pain was assessed using the VAS before and at baseline and 1, 3, and 7 days postoperatively. At 1 and 3 days postoperatively, Serum CRP levels, WBC, and neutrophil counts were measured postoperatively. Radiographic outcomes (vertebral height ratio and Cobb angle), ADL scores, JOA scores, and ODI were evaluated preoperatively and at 3 months post-operation. Postoperative complications were documented. Baseline characteristics were comparable. The combined group showed superior pain relief, vertebral height restoration, Cobb angle correction, functional recovery, and reduced inflammatory markers (CRP, WBC, neutrophils) postoperatively (all P < 0.05). Blood loss and hospital stay were shorter in the combined group, though operative time was longer (P < 0.05). Complication rates did not differ significantly (P > 0.05). PVP combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation yields better outcomes in elderly OVCF patients by enhancing pain control, vertebral height, and functional recovery without increasing perioperative risk.Keywords: Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures, percutaneous vertebroplasty, minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation, pain score, vertebral height, Cobb angle, quality of life

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a chronic, progressive bone disorder that leads to a reduction in both bone density and quality, affecting approximately 10 million individuals in the United States and 50 million globally [1]. As the global demographic continues to age, the prevalence of osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) is steadily rising, with over 1.4 million cases reported annually worldwide. This condition has emerged as a significant concern for the elderly population [2]. OVCF, a severe complication of osteoporosis, not only induces pain and restricts movement but also exacerbates pre-existing cardiopulmonary conditions in older adults, potentially leading to long-term disability or even death [3]. Therefore, how to effectively treat OVCF in the elderly to relieve pain and restore spinal function is a current clinical problem that needs to be solved urgently.

Currently, percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) has become a commonly used minimally invasive method for treating OVCF due to its effectiveness in relieving pain and rapidly restoring vertebral height [4,5]. However, for unstable fracture types or complex cases with significant kyphotic deformity, PVP alone has limitations in restoring and maintaining long-term spinal stability, with risks such as re-loss of vertebral height, cement leakage, and secondary fractures in adjacent vertebrae [5]. Minimally invasive surgeries have achieved success in treating vertebral fractures [6,7]. As a combined surgical approach, PVP combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation has emerged. Theoretically, this approach combines the dual advantages of PVP in rapidly strengthening the vertebra and internal fixation systems in reconstructing spinal alignment and stability, aiming to provide more robust mechanical support for unstable OVCF, potentially facilitating the correction of kyphotic deformity, reducing the risk of complications, and promoting functional recovery [7–9]. However, there is still a relative lack of direct and systematic comparative studies on its efficacy compared to PVP alone in the specific population of elderly patients.

This study aims to systematically evaluate the clinical efficacy and safety of PVP combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation versus PVP alone in the treatment of elderly OVCF through comparative analysis, with the goal of providing reliable clinical evidence for the selection of individualized and precise surgical strategies for such fractures.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

This study was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University. Written informed consent was acquired from participants.

Study subjects

Patients with elderly OVCF who were consecutively admitted to The Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University from September 2021 to September 2023 were selected as the study subjects. A total of 112 patients were screened during the study period, and after applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 94 patients were ultimately included (Figure 1), including 38 males and 56 females, aged 65–85 (70.69 ± 4.69) years.

The CONSORT flow diagram.

Inclusion criteria: patients with OVCF aged ≥65 years; patients with OVCF diagnosed by imaging; patients with fracture stage of T10-L5 single segment or two consecutive segments; patients who could tolerate prone position surgery and signed the informed consent.

Exclusion criteria: patients with combined pathologic fracture, severe vertebral collapse and neurologic dysfunction, and other severe spinal disorders; patients with severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency, uncontrolled coagulation disorders, and active spinal infections, or skin breakdown at the surgical site; and patients with previous vertebroplasty/internal fixation of the target vertebrae, prolonged use of glucocorticosteroids, or cognitive deficits that prevented them from cooperating with the follow-up visits.

Randomization method and blinding method

Grouping was conducted using a random number table method: 94 patients were numbered from 1 to 94 according to their enrollment order. Corresponding numbers of random numbers were extracted from the ‘Random Number Table’. After arranging the values in ascending order, the first 47 patients were assigned to the control group, and the latter 47 patients were assigned to the combined group. The grouping scheme was prepared and sealed for storage by an independent statistician, and was unsealed by researchers before the implementation of interventions. Due to the nature of the intervention measures, it was not feasible to blind the personnel implementing the intervention protocols in this study. However, to minimize bias, patients, data testers, evaluators, and data analysts were blinded in this study. All evaluation data were only marked with patient numbers. Subjective indicators were independently filled out by patients and then sealed and submitted. Evaluators only processed anonymous data.

Methods

All surgeries in this study were performed by the same spinal surgeon, strictly following standardized surgical procedures.

Control group: patients were subjected to PVP under local anesthesia. Under the guidance of a C-arm X-ray machine, the target vertebral body's entry point was determined, and the vertebral body was entered percutaneously using a puncture needle. The polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement was mixed with a contrast agent to the appropriate viscosity and slowly injected into the vertebral body under the real-time monitoring of the X-ray machine. During the injection process, the distribution of bone cement was closely observed to avoid leakage, and after the injection, the puncture needle was slowly withdrawn and local compression was applied to stop bleeding.

Combined group: patients were under general anesthesia. On the basis of PVP in the control group and according to the preoperative CT measurement, the projection point of the pedicle root was marked on the skin, and a longitudinal incision of 1.5~2 cm was made on each side (thoracic and lumbar vertebrae usually chose to open the midline of the paracentesis by 2~3 cm) in this way to establish the working channel. After incision of the skin and subcutaneous tissues, the muscles were bluntly separated and then inserted with a series of dilatation trocars to ultimately place the dilatable working channel. Bone cones or high-speed grinding drills were used to open the opening at the point of needle entry. A pedicle probe was inserted and slowly advanced while maintaining a medial inclination angle (5°~15° in lumbar vertebrae, 10°~20° in thoracic vertebrae). Resistance from the bony channel was carefully assessed, and the probe that has not broken through the inner wall of the pedicle was confirmed by lateral fluoroscopy, followed by inserting a guidewire and removing the probe. The pedicle tract was sequentially dilated along the guide wire (to avoid nerve root injury). A pedicle screw of appropriate length and diameter was selected; for osteoporotic patients, cement-augmented screws were used. Screws were screwed in along the guidewire, and the position was confirmed by positive lateral fluoroscopy (the head end of the screw did not exceed 80% of the anterior margin of the vertebral body). A pre-bent connecting rod was inserted through the submuscular tunnel and connected to the screw head, and the nut was locked after correcting the deformity using a pressurized or braced tool. The repositioning effect and internal fixation position were confirmed, saline irrigation was performed, and the fascia, subcutaneous tissue, and skin were sutured layer by layer. A sterile dressing was applied.

Both groups of patients received a unified and standardized perioperative intervention protocol after surgery: Antibiotics were routinely used within 48 hours after surgery to prevent infections; rehabilitation training followed a stepwise process, including abdominal bulging training and three-point support training (10 minutes per session, 2 sessions per day) from postoperative days 1 to 5, seated rotation training (20 minutes per session, 2 sessions per day) from postoperative days 6 to 14, standing and walking training (15-20 minutes per session, 2 sessions per day) from postoperative day 15 to week 4, and weight-bearing training with a gradual increase in walking distance according to patients' tolerance (15–20 minutes per session, 2 sessions per day) from postoperative weeks 5 to 12; anti-osteoporosis treatment was carried out simultaneously, with oral calcitriol soft capsules (Qingdao Guoxin Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., State Drug Administration: H20143141, specification: 0.5 μg) at a dose of 0.25 μg per time, twice a day, oral calcium carbonate and vitamin D3 tablets (I) [Haleon (Suzhou) Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., State Drug Administration: H10950029, specification: each tablet contains 600 mg of calcium/125 IU of vitamin D3] at a dose of 600 mg per time, twice a day, and intravenous infusion of zoledronic acid injection (Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd., State Drug Administration: H20113138, specification: 100 mL:5 mg (calculated as C5H10N2O7P2)) 100 mL mixed with 5 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride injection (infusion time > 15 minutes, single treatment) on the third day after surgery.

Observation indicators

Baseline data such as gender, age, injury-surgery time and Body Mass Index (BMI) were observed in both groups.

The perioperative indicators of the two groups were compared, including blood loss, operative time, and length of hospital stay.

In the preoperative period, 1 day postoperatively, 3 days postoperatively, and 7 days postoperatively, the pain level of the patients in the two groups was assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) by drawing a 10-cm horizontal line on a piece of paper, with 0 at one end indicating no pain and 10 at the other end indicating severe pain. The patients marked a point that represented their perceived pain intensity. The distance between the point with no pain (0) and the patient's mark was measured with a ruler to determine a score ranging from 0 to 10, with higher scores indicating more intense pain.

The patient's venous blood was collected before and 3 days after surgery. C-reactive protein (CRP) levels were estimated by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and white blood cell (WBC) and absolute neutrophil count were detected by a fully automated blood cell analyzer.

The anterior vertebral body height ratio and the Kyphotic Cobb angle of the injured vertebra were compared between the two groups before and 3 months after surgery, respectively. The vertical distance from the anterosuperior corner of the injured vertebra to the anteroinferior corner of the injured vertebra (a), as well as the anterior heights of the adjacent upper (b) and lower (c) normal vertebrae were labeled on the standing lateral spine X-ray (or CT sagittal reconstruction). The anterior vertebral body height ratio was calculated as the ratio of distance a to the average of b and c. To assess the kyphotic Cobb angle, lateral radiographs including at least one to two normal vertebrae above and below the fractured vertebra were obtained. The angle formed by the extension lines of the superior endplate of the first normal vertebra above and the inferior endplate of the first normal vertebra below the fractured vertebra was measured as the kyphotic Cobb angle. These parameters were used to evaluate vertebral height restoration and correction of spinal deformity. All imaging indicators were independently measured in a blinded manner by a senior radiologist (with over 10 years of experience in spinal imaging diagnosis) who was unaware of the patient groupings.

Patients were evaluated on the Activities of Daily Living (ADL) scale before and 3 months after surgery, with scores ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better daily living.

A comparative analysis of lumbar functional indicators was conducted between the two patient cohorts. Lumbar functional recovery was evaluated preoperatively and at the 3-month postoperative interval using the Japanese Orthopedic Association Back Pain Evaluation Questionnaire (JOA scores). This 29-point scale quantifies lumbar function, with ascending numerical values correlating to improved functional status.

The Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) was implemented to assess the lumbar function of both groups of patients preoperatively and 3 months postoperatively. The total score of the scale was 50 points, with a higher score indicating more severe functional impairment.

The occurrence of postoperative complications such as bone cement leakage, recurrent loss of vertebral height, progressive spinal collapse, and kyphotic deformity was recorded for both groups.

Statistical analysis

All numerical datasets underwent computational processing and evaluation via SPSS 25.0 analytical software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Categorical data were presented as percentages (%) and subjected to chi-squared analysis. Continuous data were expressed using mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), for normally distributed data, or median with interquartile range for non-normal distributions. Normality was evaluated through Shapiro–Wilk examination, while Levene's assessment determined variance homogeneity. Between-group comparisons employed independent t-tests when both normality and variance homogeneity criteria were satisfied. For datasets violating these assumptions, Wilcoxon signed-rank nonparametric tests were utilized. Within-group pre-post analyzes were conducted using paired t-tests. The difference of P < 0.05 was statistically significant.

Results

Baseline data

The comparison cohort comprised 20 male and 27 female participants, aged 65–85 years, with a median age of 71.00 (67.00,74.00) years. The interval between injury occurrence and hospital admission averaged 6.78 ± 3.34 hours, while the mean BMI was 24.62 ± 3.03 kg/m². In the combined group, there were 18 males and 29 females within the same age range (65–85 years), with a median age of 70.00 (67.00,72.00) years. The admission time post-injury measured 6.53 ± 2.65 hours, and the mean BMI was 24.86 ± 2.50 kg/m². Comparative analysis of baseline characteristics between groups demonstrated no statistically significant disparities (P > 0.05; Table 1).

Measurements of CRP, WBC count, and neutrophil count were taken at two postoperative intervals: day 1 and day 3. These findings indicate that the combined PVP and minimally invasive fixation approach effectively attenuates postoperative inflammatory reactions in geriatric patients with OVCF (Table 4).

Imaging assessment

Measurements of anterior vertebral body height ratio and the Kyphotic Cobb angle were obtained preoperatively and at three-month follow-up. Quantitative analysis revealed significant improvements in both parameters within the intervention cohort. Specifically, at three months post-surgery, patients receiving the combined procedure demonstrated a significantly greater restoration of anterior vertebral body height ratio compared to controls (P < 0.05). Concurrently, the Kyphotic Cobb angle was significantly reduced in the combined group relative to the control group (P < 0.05). These findings substantiate that integrating PVP with minimally invasive fixation effectively enhances postoperative anterior vertebral body height ratio and the Kyphotic Cobb angle in geriatric patients with OVCF (Table 5).

Functional assessment

A comparative analysis of three functional outcome measures (JOA score, ODI, and ADL score) was performed. Preoperative baseline assessments and three-month postoperative evaluations were performed for both study cohorts. Intergroup comparisons revealed no significant preoperative differences across all three metrics (P > 0.05). However, at the three-month follow-up, patients in the combined group demonstrated significantly improved outcomes. Specifically, JOA scores and ADL scores were notably higher in the combined group compared to controls (P < 0.05), while ODI scores showed a corresponding significant reduction (P < 0.05). These results suggest that the combined procedure effectively enhances postoperative spinal function recovery, as evidenced by improved pain management, reduced disability, and enhanced daily living capabilities in geriatric patients with OVCF (Table 6).

Discussion

OVCF status serves as a robust and autonomous risk factor for all novel osteoporotic fractures. This is of significant concern in healthcare, particularly among the aging population, as the occurrence of these fractures rises with advancing age [10]. Therefore, it underscores the urgent need for effective, evidence-based treatment strategies. Traditional PVP has been widely adopted for pain relief and vertebral height restoration; however, its limitations in addressing biomechanical instability and preventing recurrent collapse are increasingly recognized. This study evaluated the clinical efficacy of combining PVP with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation, aiming to enhance both immediate and long-term outcomes in elderly patients with OVCF.

The findings demonstrate that the integrated approach significantly improved postoperative pain control, as evidenced by lower VAS scores across all measured time points compared to PVP alone. This aligns with biomechanical principles, where PVP offers substantial advantages in the long term [11], and the nail-rod system is employed to realign and stabilize the fractured area, thereby enabling the vertebral body to regain its normal sequential alignment and height through the utilization of pedicle screw internal fixation which is instrumental in enhancing vertebral stability and alleviating nerve compression [12,13]. Notably, the combined group exhibited reduced intraoperative blood loss and shorter hospital stays, which are critical for geriatric patients at higher risk of surgical complications. While operative duration was longer in the combined group, this was offset by the clinical benefits observed.

Inflammatory biomarker analysis revealed a marked reduction in CRP, WBC, and neutrophil count by postoperative day 3 in the integrated treatment group. This attenuated inflammatory response may reflect minimized surgical trauma and improved hemodynamic stability, contributing to faster recovery, which echoes with the relevant finding showing posterior internal fixation combined with other treatments not only offers substantial alleviation of pain and neurological manifestations but also results in decreased levels of inflammatory indicators [14]. Radiographic outcomes further supported these findings, with superior anterior vertebral height restoration and Cobb angle correction in the combined group. These morphological improvements likely translate to enhanced spinal alignment and reduced mechanical stress on adjacent segments, potentially lowering long-term complication risks.

Functional assessments using ADL, JOA, and ODI scores highlighted significant advantages in the combined group, emphasizing the importance of biomechanical stability for functional recovery. This conforms to a study demonstrating that the utilization of a bilateral approach in PVP is advantageous for the restoration of spinal cord function in patients with OVCF [15]. Despite initial concerns, complication rates did not differ significantly between groups, suggesting that the added complexity of fixation did not compromise safety. This challenges conventional perceptions of minimally invasive techniques as inherently safer but less effective and supports the notion that strategic integration of stabilization methods can optimize risk-benefit ratios.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study confirms that for elderly OVCF, PVP combined with minimally invasive pedicle screw fixation demonstrates superior clinical efficacy compared to PVP alone in effectively relieving pain, restoring vertebral height, and improving spinal function, without significantly increasing perioperative risks. This indicates that the synergistic effect of the two techniques provides a minimally invasive solution with greater biomechanical advantages for OVCF treatment. However, this study has several limitations. Firstly, the 3-month follow-up period is relatively short, allowing only for the assessment of early postoperative pain relief and functional recovery, and is unable to verify the durability of long-term outcomes such as vertebral height maintenance, internal fixation stability, and adjacent segment degeneration. Secondly, the study design is single-center with a moderate sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the conclusions across different healthcare settings. Additionally, to focus on the immediate effects of surgical intervention, this study did not routinely measure preoperative inflammatory markers (such as CRP and WBC), failing to comprehensively analyze the potential impact of baseline inflammatory status on prognosis. Based on the above findings and limitations, future research should aim to conduct multi-center, large-sample studies with long-term follow-up to assess the long-term safety and efficacy of the combined surgical approach. Simultaneously, research could incorporate health economic evaluations to analyze its cost-effectiveness and explore clinical or imaging markers that can predict the success of individualized treatment, thereby promoting the development of OVCF treatment towards a more precise and sustainable direction.

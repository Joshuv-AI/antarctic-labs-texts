Nanotechnology-Enabled Approaches in Poultry Coccidiosis Management: Advances, Challenges, and Future Perspectives
Authors: Nahed A. El-Shall, Hesham A. Hassanien, Mohamed Shehab-El-Deen, Frank Idan, Mahmoud Alagawany
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Avian coccidiosis, a disease resulting from infection by various Eimeria species, is a major constraint to global poultry production, causing considerable economic losses and affecting animal performance. Traditional approaches to coccidiosis control, such as anticoccidials and live vaccines, face limitations in their use owing to resistance, safety concerns, and species-specific immunity. In this regard, subunit, DNA, and multi-epitope vaccines have been tested against coccidiosis, but their efficacy is hindered by the instability of the vaccine antigens and a lack of cross-protection. However, recent advances in nanotechnology may provide a solution to the limitations associated with traditional approaches to coccidiosis control. For instance, nanoparticles may improve vaccine efficacy by stabilizing vaccine antigens, facilitating their delivery, and modulating immune responses. Furthermore, various nanoparticle therapeutics, such as plant-based and metal-based nanoparticles, and nano-encapsulated anticoccidials, may also reduce oocyst shedding and improve gut health and antioxidant status. Although this is a positive step in coccidiosis control, there are many issues that need to be resolved. This review provides a critical analysis of recent advances in various aspects of nanotechnology-based approaches to coccidiosis control, their potential, and research gaps.

Introduction

Eimeria (E.) tenella, mitis, maxima, acervulina, brunetti, necatrix, and praecox are seven species of intracellular protozoan parasites that can cause coccidiosis in poultry. These parasites can affect the intestinal tract of poultry either individually or in combination. Despite being a self-limiting disease, avian coccidiosis has an impact on the world economy. This impact is primarily attributed to E. acervulina, E. maxima, E. brunetti, E. tenella, and E. necatrix, which are arranged from moderately to highly pathogenic (López-Osorio et al., 2020; El-Shall et al., 2022). Reduced body weight gain, poor feed conversion ratio, reduced egg production, and even death were caused by Eimeria infection (Song et al., 2015b; Huang et al., 2018), making the global poultry industry suffer an annual economic loss of roughly 12.883 billion USD (Blake et al., 2020).

Since the 1950s, anticoccidial medications have been used extensively to manage this disease. Typically, the medications are added to the water for treatment or the feed for prevention. However, the issue with this approach is the rise in drug-resistant isolates of Eimeria spp. (Chapman and Rathinam, 2022). Several programs, including continuous, shuttle, and rotation, have been implemented to schedule the addition of drugs to broiler chicken feed to combat this phenomenon. However, no new drugs have been released since the 1990s, when Toltrazuril and Diclazuril were the last pharmaceuticals to be introduced into the market. Furthermore, there has been a trend towards restricting the use of veterinary chemical drugs in food animals. Consequently, the necessity for creating effective and widely used alternatives such as coccidia vaccines was established.

Even though vaccinations and producing strong protective immune responses are great alternative approaches to prevent coccidiosis in poultry (Chapman et al., 2005) and have been historically used since the 1950s, the global poultry industry still faces significant challenges in controlling avian coccidiosis. This issue is attributed to the fact that live, wild or attenuated, vaccines are the main components of commercial vaccination strategies against Eimeria species. Their production is restricted in vivo, and consequently, their production of high quantities is hindered, and high costs are induced. Furthermore, Eimeria spp. immunity against coccidiosis can only be acquired after 1–5 successful life cycles of infection (El-Shall et al., 2022). Additionally, there are several disadvantages and obstacles associated with the use of live vaccines, as listed in Table 1. Thus, to handle this process in the field, certain safety measures and managerial considerations need to be made. The development of new generation vaccines like subunit, DNA, and multiepitope vaccines has generated considerable research interest to address these limitations. In recent times, nanotechnology has also been recognized as a potential approach in improving both preventive and therapeutic strategies (Michalak et al., 2022; Sandri et al., 2019). However, the information in literature is still disjointed, either highlighting the applications of vaccines or their therapeutic uses without integrating the two concepts. Biology and Pathogenesis of Eimeria spp

Eimeria species are intracellular apicomplexan parasites that infect the intestinal tract of livestock and poultry and follow a direct life cycle that includes both asexual (schizogony) and sexual (gametogony) phases (Fig. 2). Eimeria invasion occurs via gliding motility and direct membrane contact between the host cell membrane and parasite cytoskeleton (Jebessa et al., 2025). The pathogenic effects of Eimeria infection are directly related to the cytopathic effect on enterocytes due to intracellular development, resulting in villous atrophy, hemorrhage, and decreased nutrient uptake, leading to weight loss or death (Chapman et al., 2013; El-Shall et al., 2022).Fig. 2Life cycle of avian coccidiosis.Fig 2: dummy alt text

Notably, there is considerable variability among Eimeria species in terms of virulence, tissue distribution, and clinical syndromes. Highly virulent species include E. tenella and E. necatrix, characterized by severe haemorrhagic damage. However, E. brunetti and E. maxima are moderately virulent, while E. acervulina and E. mitis cause subclinical infections with significant economic impact. Within species, there may be considerable variation in immunogenicity, virulence, and reproductive potential, which complicates vaccine and control strategies (Sedeik et al., 2019; Sobhani et al., 2025).

The perpetuation and prevalence of Eimeria infections are determined by a multitude of interrelated factors. The direct fecal-oral life cycle of Eimeria is short and simple and its reproductive potential is high (El-Shall et al., 2022; Williams, 2001), contributing to its extensive transmission potential. Sporulated oocysts are resistant to disinfectants and remain viable under favourable environmental conditions such as humidity, temperature, and oxygen levels, increasing infection potential (Peek and Landman, 2011; Waldenstedt et al., 2001).

Host immunity is another factor that affects the dynamics of infection. Immunity is primarily species-specific and cell-mediated, requiring multiple exposures to develop immunity, with a lack of any apparent humoral immunity or a very low level of it (Lillehoj et al., 2004; Kim et al., 2019). Other stressors, co-infections, and immunosuppressive states increase the severity of the disease (Chanie et al., 2009). Environmental and management factors, such as rearing systems, stocking density, and climatic factors, have been reported to influence oocyst shedding and sporulation, thereby affecting the infection pressure (Ahad et al., 2015; El-Shall et al., 2022). A thorough understanding of the life cycle of Eimeria, interactions between the host and the pathogen, and environmental factors is critical in the development of effective vaccines and control measures to combat coccidiosis in poultry.

Advances in Vaccine Strategies Against Coccidiosis

Historically, there have been two important methods for avian coccidiosis control, including vaccination and treatment with chemotherapy, where the former aims at inducing protective immunity before infections occur while the latter is focused on decreasing the proliferation and disease impact of the parasites in infected hosts. Nevertheless, the above methods can no longer be viewed independently since both induced immunity and parasite control in the case of infection are related biological processes. From this point, nanotechnology represents an approach that not only improves vaccine performance but also helps in enhancing resistance, intestinal health and responsiveness of the host organism to therapy.

Subunit and DNA Vaccines

Subunit and DNA vaccines have emerged as potential candidates for controlling avian coccidiosis, although their efficacy is affected by several biological factors. For example, the limited expression of Eimeria proteins during the life cycle and the absence of cross-species protection can impair the efficacy of recombinant antigen vaccines (Ding et al., 2004; Lillehoj et al., 2005). Immunity is generally strain-specific, with homologous strains providing protection while heterologous strains prevalent in different regions do not elicit a cross-protective response (Zaheer et al., 2022). Enzymatic degradation can limit the stability and bioavailability of recombinant subunit vaccines (Nordin et al., 2023). Additionally, compared to vaccines using the entire microorganism, subunit and DNA vaccines may induce lower protective immunity (Watts and Kennedy, 1999).

To enhance their efficacy, vaccination strategies should aim to provide broad protection against multiple Eimeria species. This has led to the development of combined recombinant antigen vaccines targeting E. tenella, E. acervulina, E. necatrix, and/or E. maxima (Yu et al., 2022; Song et al., 2015b,c). The inclusion of adjuvants has also been shown to improve immunogenicity by stimulating both humoral and cellular responses and reducing antigen degradation (Huang et al., 2015; Li et al., 2018). Moreover, the encapsulation of antigens in microparticles or nanoparticles was found to prolong antigen persistence and induce a more robust antibody response than conventional adjuvants (Challacombe, 1983). Thus, while next-generation vaccines make logical sense, their limitations in antigen stability and cross-protection point to a need for other approaches where nanotechnology could play a significant role.

Multiepitope Vaccine (MEV) Approach

Recent studies have provided evidence that vaccines using antigens derived from multiple stages have superior efficacy over vaccines using antigens derived from a single stage due to the biological complexity of Eimeria parasites. According to Li et al. (2025), epitopes are the major immunogenetic determinants that have the potential to induce both humoral and cell-mediated immunity. In this context, it was proposed that T-cell epitopes derived from different stages of the parasite life cycle could be used to address antigenic diversity and stage-specific expression (Song et al., 2015c). This forms the basis for MEVs, which have the potential to induce more specific and wider immune responses while circumventing some limitations associated with live vaccines. However, one major challenge in developing this strategy is identifying antigens that confer protective immunity.

A wide range of Eimeria antigens, including those of the sporozoites and merozoites, have been studied in detail, and some of them include TA4 (SAG1) (Huang et al., 2021), microneme proteins (Huang et al., 2018; Song et al., 2015b), apical membrane antigen 1 (AMA1) (Fatoba and Adeleke, 2020), and various enzymes and regulators such as glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (Liu et al., 2017), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) (Dong et al., 2014), and 14-3-3 (Liang et al., 2022), which are critical in the survival and functioning of the parasite. The results of these functional studies have indicated that the interaction of various invasion-associated antigens with intestinal cells of the host may induce a moderate immune response in the form of cytokines such as IFN-γ and IL-10 (Jebessa et al., 2025). This suggests that the interaction of the parasite with the intestinal cells of the host may induce some degree of immune response in the host. However, despite the wide range of Eimeria antigens, the complexity of their selection and their varying levels of expression in different species and stages of the parasite have been a limiting factor in achieving consistent results.

Notably, some conserved antigens, such as GAPDH, 14-3-3, and elongation factor-2 (EF-2), have shown immunogenic potential across multiple Eimeria species, supporting their relevance in the development of cross-protective vaccines (Mi et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2017). This has encouraged the use of multi-antigen combinations to broaden protective coverage, as suggested by Zaheer et al. (2022). Nanoparticles as Vaccine Delivery Systems

Nanoparticles (NPs) have been extensively studied for their potential application as multifunctional delivery agents for drugs and antigens, as well as their direct application for therapy. Their entry into the cells is facilitated by natural processes, including phagocytosis and pinocytosis, as explained by Kik et al. (2020), which supports their efficient interaction with the biological system. The increased interest in nanoparticles, particularly in the biomedical field, is due to their unique physicochemical properties, which allow for their controlled release, delivery, and immunomodulatory effects, often showing enhanced stability compared to conventional formulations (Alshawwa et al., 2022).

The functionality of nanoparticle-based systems is significantly affected by their structural and physicochemical properties. For instance, nanoparticle size, shape, surface charge, and hydrophobicity are key properties that affect their uptake by cells (Feiner-Gracia et al., 2018; Grego et al., 2021). For example, it has been shown that smaller nanoparticles have a better efficiency in their uptake by cells. This is evidenced by the increased uptake efficiency of nanoparticles with sizes of 100 nm compared to larger sizes (Gratton et al., 2008). In this context, it has been shown that chitosan (CS) and Poly lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) nanoparticles with submicron sizes (less than 0.5 to 1 μm) can effectively be taken up by antigen-presenting cells, thereby stimulating dendritic cells (Koppolu and Zaharoff, 2013; Yan et al., 2020). On the other hand, the size of nanoparticles is functionally balanced, where larger nanoparticles can carry large amounts of antigen, while smaller nanoparticles can be efficiently taken up by cells (Howe & Konjufca, 2015).

Moreover, structural design, such as the presence of nanospheres that contain several subunits, has been associated with improving antigen protection against degradation (Xu et al., 2022). Therefore, the efficacy of nanoparticles is dependent on the cumulative effect of all the parameters and not the individual property. The use of biodegradable nanoparticles, particularly those that are positively charged and have a size of less than 500 nm, appears to offer promising properties for drug and vaccine development (Joshi et al., 2013; Koppolu and Zaharoff, 2013). From the available literature, it appears that the optimization of the physicochemical and structural properties of nanoparticles is essential to maximize their potential in the development of advanced vaccines against parasitic diseases such as Eimeria spp.

Nanoparticle-Based Adjuvants and Carriers for Eimeria Vaccines

Numerous Eimeria antigens have been selected and studied for their ability to elicit humoral and cell-mediated immune responses (Jenkins et al., 2018). These immune responses can decrease parasite replication, lessen enteric lesions, and improve body weight gain and feed conversion ratio, in the face of an Eimeria challenge (Blake et al., 2017; Huang et al., 2018). However, because peptide-based vaccines are poorly immunogenic and rapidly digested by enzymes, their use is limited. Nano subunit vaccines, which contain polymeric nanoparticles loaded with antigens, offer various benefits, including: (1) the ability to regulate the release of antigens (Grego et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2022), thereby inducing an extended immunogenic response in the host. For example, the percentage of cumulative release of antigen (rEm14-3-3) from PLGA NPs and chitosan (CS) NPs was approximately 81% and 75% on the eighth day, and approximately 92% and 88% on the fourteenth day, respectively (Haseeb et al., 2022). (2) overcoming the quick clearance and poor uptake of antigens (Zare et al., 2022) as well as immunological, enzymatic, and chemical breakdown (Tiwari et al., 2012), (3) enhancing the antigen presentation to antigen presenting cells (Xu et al., 2022) and promoting cell-mediated immunity because subunit antigens alone typically have low ability to activate CD8+ T lymphocytes (Grego et al., 2021). (4) lowering the dosing frequency and shortening the vaccination schedule by boosting the antibody response (Huntimer et al., 2013; Bershteyn et al., 2012) and by the prolonged survival of the nanoparticles in the gastrointestinal tract (GIT) (Tiwari et al., 2012). Therefore, nanocarriers are efficient for inducing long-lasting immunity and decreasing degradation.

Chitosan (CS) Nanoparticles

CS is a naturally occurring polysaccharide that is made from the deacetylation of chitin in crustacean shells. It is safe and FDA-approved. Because of its superior degradability, biocompatibility, nontoxicity, cationic surface charge, ease of loading with peptides, bio-adhesive properties, and water solubility, CS is a promising material for drug-controlled release systems (Pati et al., 2018; Kabiri et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2016). However, despite the consistent benefits associated with CS-containing products, much of the evidence supporting the same has been generated through in vitro and in vivo trials that cannot easily be reproduced in large-scale poultry settings. Additionally, variations in the molecular weight, degree of deacetylation, and method of preparation of CS have made it difficult for results to be reproducible across different experiments.

According to Ahsan et al. (2022) and Desai et al. (2023), the presence of protons on the amino group of CS NPs confers a zeta-positive potential to it, which enables it to react with the negatively charged mucins in the intestinal mucosal cells. This mucoadhesive property exhibited by CS NPs lengthens the residence time at the mucosal surface, thus facilitating antigen uptake (Ways et al., 2018). Moreover, CS NPs have been reported to open up tight junctions of epithelial tissues temporarily, enhancing paracellular transport and increasing access to vaccine antigens to underlying immune organs (Ahsan et al., 2022). Accordingly, Koppolu et al. (2014) noted that positively charged CS NPs are efficient in improving mucosal immune responses via extending residence time following binding to cell surfaces and enhanced interaction with immune tissues. Equally, it can induce effective antigen-specific immunity. When the positively charged surface of CS particles interacts with the negatively charged surfaces of cell membranes or mucus, mucosal and systemic immune responses to antigens can be strengthened (Chaturvedi et al., 2011; Wang and Kong, 2013). According to Xu et al. (2022), the antigen released from CS nanospheres more slowly than from PLGA nanospheres. This is because PLGA nanospheres exhibit negative zeta potential, leading to decreased electrostatic interactions with mucus glycoproteins, limiting their retention at epithelial surfaces (Malkawi et al., 2021).

Nevertheless, since Eimeria infects the GIT, protection against it requires local immunity. The production of neutralising secretory IgA (sIGA) antibodies and protective cell-mediated immune responses is typically favoured by mucosal vaccination administered orally or topically (Rhee et al., 2020). During avian coccidiosis, chickens typically develop IgY and sIgA antibodies specific to the parasite in the gut, which peak 7–20 days after infection (Trees et al., 1989; Yun et al., 2000). There are practical drawbacks to the mucosal route of delivery, such as low pH, gastric proteolytic enzymes, quick intestinal transit, and inefficient absorption of large molecules (Jang et al., 2011b). Consequently, adjuvants based on nanoparticles have been developed to protect antigens inside the GIT, target them to the gut-associated lymphoid tissues (GALT), and to lengthen the antigen's residence time in the intestine by virtue of their adhesive qualities (Jang et al., 2011b). However, although using the coccidia vaccine orally is imperative, there are not many direct comparisons between oral and parenteral CS NPs vaccines, thus making it hard to pinpoint which route of delivery is best. As reviewed by Renu and Renukaradhya (2020), CS NPs are frequently used for mucosal DNA, RNA, protein, or peptide administration (oral, intranasal, and ocular) in poultry against Newcastle disease, Infectious bronchitis, Avian Influenza, Campylobacter jejuni, E. coli, and Salmonella enterica. Unfortunately, there haven't been any experimental studies on the mucosal application of CS NPs-conjugated subunit vaccines for avian Eimeria in the literature yet. Notwithstanding, CS NPs-conjugated subunit vaccines have emerged as promising delivery systems for improving protective immunity against other pathogenic infections, particularly Salmonella species. Their capacity to increase antigen stability, facilitate targeted mucosal delivery, and promote innate and adaptive immunity positions them as effective delivery systems for vaccine development. For instance, Renu et al. (2020b) demonstrated that orally administered natural mucoadhesive CS NPs can effectively target the GIT immune cells of chickens, enhancing antigen uptake at mucosal sites. Following uptake by the immune cells of the GIT, the antigen-loaded CS NPs stimulate the Toll-like receptors to produce Th1 and Th2 cytokines (Renu et al., 2020a). Similarly, mannose-conjugated nanoparticles target dendritic cells and macrophages, leading to stronger and better immune responses (Carrilo-Conde et al., 2011). Dolatyabi et al. (2024) evaluated the efficacy of orally administered Salmonella enteritidis subunit vaccine encapsulated within CS NPs on cross-protection against Salmonella typhimurium (ST) in broiler chickens. The authors observed that CS NPs induced higher cross-protective antibody responses compared to a commercial Poulvac ST vaccine containing live bacterium. This is because CS NPs increased the production of sIgA and IgY antibodies at both post-vaccination and post-challenge timepoints. These findings indicate the potential of chitosan delivery systems to stimulate both mucosal and humoral immunity, which improves vaccine efficacy and protection against Salmonella infections.

PLGA and Other Biodegradable Polymer Nanoparticles

PLGA, Poly lactic acid (PLA), and Poly-caprolactone (PCL) are the most often utilised synthetic polymers used as nanocarriers. PLGA is frequently utilised in vaccine and drug delivery systems, having received approval from the FDA and the European Medicines Agency. It exhibits zero toxicity and high biodegradability, provides proteins with high bioavailability and continuous release, and is presented by antigen-presenting cells, as well as protects them from degradation (Pohlmann et al., 2013; Kondiah et al., 2018; Quadros et al., 2020; Hamdy et al., 2011) by forming a lipid layer around them. But unlike cationic polymers, PLGA has a negative charge (Hekmat et al., 2016; Haseeb et al., 2022). As a result, when loading negative molecules into PLGA nanoparticles, there was instability and low encapsulation efficiency.

Previous studies indicate that encapsulating Eimeria antigens within PLGA NPs can enhance immune responses following parenteral administration (Table 3). Notably, increases in cellular and humoral immune markers, such as IFN-γ production and CD3+CD4+ and CD3+CD8+ T cells, have been reported by PLGA-rEm14-3-3 NPs vaccination followed by E. maxima challenge, as shown by Haseeb et al. (2022) along with improvements in the anticoccidial index (ACI), although full protection was not consistently achieved. Similarly, studies by Huang et al. (2021) and Xu et al. (2022) demonstrated that PLGA-based nanoencapsulation of antigens of E. tenella (rEtTA4) and E. mitis (rEm1a) can stimulate cytokine production and T-cell responses, contributing to partial protective immunity, reduced oocyst shedding, and enhanced growth performance. These findings highlight the potential of nanocarrier systems in enhancing vaccine performance. Furthermore, multi-epitope vaccines conjugated with PLGA nanospheres, as reported by Yu et al. (2022) showed immunoprotective effects and some degree of cross-protection against multiple Eimeria species, particularly at optimized antigen doses. Thus, even though PLGA nanovaccine formulations have exhibited satisfactory immunological and protective profiles, it is possible that refinement in antigen design, delivery methods, and administration may enhance their effectiveness and applicability.

According to Naeem et al. (2018) and Hamdy et al. (2011), it has been observed that differences in immune response may not only be attributed to the antigen but also to the route of administration. For instance, macrophages are primarily involved in the uptake of PLGA particles administered via the intraperitoneal route, compared to dendritic cells in the case of subcutaneous and intradermal administration. More recent research has focused on the mucosal administration of nanoparticles, which may be a potentially beneficial approach in comparison to the mode of Eimeria infection. In this regard, routes such as oral and intranasal administration may provide unique immunological advantages by affecting the biodistribution and antigen presentation of PLGA-based vaccine approaches. However, there is limited data available to compare these routes, and it is difficult to clearly define the most effective approach.

Polystyrene Nanoparticles

Polystyrene, which is a synthetic polymer composed of styrene monomers, has been investigated for its potential in the development of a nanoparticle-based delivery system. Due to their small size, polystyrene nanoparticles (PS-NPs) can be taken by the host through various routes, such as the skin, respiratory, and gastrointestinal systems. Jenkins et al. (2018) found that oral administration of PS-NPs conjugated to E. maxima IMP1 antigen resulted in improved body weight gain and strong protection following homologous challenge. The rapid localization of 20-nm nanoparticles to both lymphoid and intestinal tissues within a few hours underscores their efficient uptake and distribution, possibly leading to improved antigen presentation and immune activation. Their capacity to target both intestinal and lymphoid tissues support their potential use as vaccine nanocarriers for coccidial vaccine development, although further work may help to better define their use and potential.

Montanide™ Nanoparticle Adjuvants

The application of Montanide™ IMS1313 VG N is an indication of the increasing interest in the application of nanoparticle adjuvant systems for improving the efficacy of subunit vaccines. It is a liquid form of nanoparticles dispersed in water, making it an effective tool for parenteral and mucosal immunizations, especially when combined with immunostimulatory agents (SEPPIC, 2023). Moreover, it is effective for mass vaccination through spraying and drinking water, especially in poultry farming, which is desirable for large-scale vaccination (Riffault et al., 2010). However, the efficacy of the vaccination method is likely to be affected by factors such as stability and distribution, as well as antigen uptake by the birds.

The results of the experimental studies have also shown that the simultaneous use of MontanideTM IMS 1313 nanoparticle adjuvants and recombinant antigens, including the recombinant profilin of Eimeria, may induce mucosal immune responses. For example, increased levels of intestinal sIgA and antigen-specific IgY were seen in the immunized birds, indicating that the level of protective immunity against the infection could be enhanced (Jang et al., 2011a,b). In addition, other adjuvants such as Montanide™ IMS 106 and Montanide™ IMS 101 in liquid nanoparticle suspensions have shown promise in their ability to boost immune responses in conjunction with various antigens (Jang et al., 2012). Their potential has also been evaluated in challenging disease models such as co-infections with E. maxima and C. perfringens, in which improvements in performance parameters were noted in vaccinated birds (Lillehoj et al., 2017). While these are positive results, it is still important to consider that non-specific immune activation or management-related factors could be involved in the positive outcomes. Another notable characteristic of these adjuvants is the simplicity of vaccine preparations, which is generally achieved through the simple mixing of the antigen with the nanoparticles, devoid of complex procedures (Aucouturier et al., 2001). Although this could minimize the cost of vaccine production, there are concerns about the reproducibility of the vaccine formulations and the stability of the nanoparticles that need to be considered. Collectively, the Montanide-based nanoparticle adjuvants are promising tools for boosting mucosal subunit vaccines in poultry, especially for mass vaccination. However, further studies are necessary to establish the safety, long-term efficacy, and cost-effectiveness of these adjuvants in the field.

Plant-Derived Nanoadjuvants

Recently, plant-derived adjuvants have gained more interest as new immunomodulatory agents for avian coccidiosis control. They have been viewed as more beneficial compared to other immunomodulatory agents due to their biocompatibility and potential for reducing the use of synthetic adjuvants for improving the safety and efficacy of current live vaccines (Cai et al., 2022). Among plant-derived adjuvants, ginseng-derived saponins, known as ginsenosides, have been extensively studied for their immunostimulant activities. Ginsenosides have been found to increase both humoral (Th2) and cellular (Th1) immune responses, thus providing broader protection against infectious agents (Du et al., 2005; Li et al., 2018).

An important advancement in this domain has been the creation of nanoparticle-based ginsenoside formulations, called ginsomes, which have been designed for enhanced antigen delivery and immunogenicity. An experimental assessment of ginsomes in combination with recombinant profilin from E. tenella has shown enhanced protective efficacy compared to antigen delivery alone (Zhang et al., 2012). In this context, profilin + ginsomes-vaccinated birds have shown enhanced body weight gain and decreased lesion scores and oocyst shedding. These improvements were accompanied by enhanced lymphocyte proliferation and increased cytokine (e.g., IL-1) and antigen-specific antibody production, thus potentiating the immune response. Nevertheless, the studies on the efficacy of nanoparticle-based plant adjuvants are carried out under controlled environments. It is possible that the conditions may not completely resemble the actual scenario in poultry farms. Factors such as the composition of the plant extract, difficulties in standardizing the product on a large scale, and the possible variability in the batches produced could affect the efficacy. Moreover, the actual mechanisms behind the interactions between nanoparticles and the immune system still remain to be unraveled. Furthermore, in spite of improvements in performance parameters like body weight gain and lesion reduction, it is worth considering that these effects may also be subject to indirect effects, including a positive influence on the general health status or nonspecific immune system activation. Thus, one of the main challenges in evaluating the effectiveness of plant-derived adjuvants is distinguishing antigen-specific effects from nonspecific effects.

Combining Nanotechnology-based Vaccines and Therapeutics for Coccidiosis Control

Traditionally, vaccination and chemotherapy have been treated as two different tools for the prevention and control of avian coccidiosis where vaccination creates immunity prior to infection while anticoccidial drugs reduce parasite reproduction and pathogenicity after infection. The application of nanotechnology presents a platform where the integration of the two strategies is possible since nanomaterials can provide both prophylaxis and treatment modalities. It brings a new dimension of nanotechnology whereby it is no longer seen just as a carrier for vaccines or drugs.

One of the strengths of such an integration is that the dual nature of nanoparticles is exploited in numerous applications. For example, biodegradable polymers such as CS and PLGA increase stability of antigens, provide protection from enzymes, facilitate controlled release and uptake by antigen presenting cells, which results in increased efficiency of vaccine-induced immunity (Hamdy et al., 2011; Koppolu and Zaharoff, 2013; Xu et al., 2022). At the same time, nanocarriers increase delivery of phytochemicals and anticoccidial drugs through solubilization of the compounds, their protection from gastrointestinal degradation, prolonged retention in the intestine, and increased concentration at the site of infection (Kasem et al., 2022; Baron et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2023).

Immunologically, nanovaccines and therapeutics have a complementary role. Nanovaccines improve the immune response by augmenting antigen presentation, creating humoral and cell-mediated immunity, and producing mucosal IgA and T-cell responses that provide protection against Eimeria infections (Jang et al., 2011b; Haseeb et al., 2022). On the other hand, therapeutic nanocarriers exert their protective effect against Eimeria infections via their antiparasitic activity, controlled drug delivery, immunomodulatory properties, antioxidant effects, and maintenance of barrier integrity of the intestine (El-Maddawy et al., 2022; Kasem et al., 2022). The two effects might work together in a complementary manner, whereby lowering of parasite load decreases the tissue damage and enhances the effectiveness of vaccines.

The interplay between both approaches is particularly important in commercial poultry production where issues such as antigenic diversity, high infection pressure, stress, nutrition, and coinfections may affect vaccine efficiency. In such situations, nanoparticle treatment of the vaccinated animals may help to reduce the number of parasites, limit oxidative stress, maintain intestinal integrity, and keep the performance level during the challenges. At the same time, successful vaccination minimizes the need for frequent drug treatments that is also consistent with global trends of decreasing usage of antimicrobials in animal farming. Thus, nanotechnology is a holistic platform which combines preventive and therapeutic approaches in managing coccidiosis (Fig. 3). Nanoparticles as Therapeutic Alternatives to Anticoccidial Drugs

Nano-Phytochemicals and Plant Extract Nanoparticles

Natural plant extracts have gained increasing attention for their anticoccidial potential against Eimeria species. Several studies have demonstrated positive results in vitro and in vivo against Eimeria species. For example, plant extracts from Vitis vinifera, Citrus aurantium, Rosmarinus officinalis, Conyza aegyptiaca, and Psidium guajava, and phytochemicals like nutmeg oil, cinnamon oil, and glabridin have shown inhibitory effects against the development of the Eimeria parasite (Abbas et al., 2020; Ishaq et al., 2022; Kasem et al., 2019; Toah et al., 2021; Thabet et al., 2022). Recent strategies involve the nano-formulation of the plant extracts to improve their stability, bioavailability, and targeted delivery. It has been proposed that the nanoparticle-based formulations can improve the biological activity of the natural compounds compared to their conventional counterparts (Jain, 2008). For example, the ultrasonicated ethanolic extract of Rosmarinus officinalis and its chitosan-based nanoparticle formulation (UEERO-CSNPs) exhibited dose-dependent inhibition of sporulation and morphological abnormalities in E. tenella. Enhanced activity was recorded for the nano-formulated extract compared to its normal version (Kasem et al., 2022). Moreover, Abd El Megid et al. (2018) showed that the in vivo efficacy of propolis nanoparticles in enhancing the antioxidant status and reducing histopathological damage in the liver of infected rabbits after oral administration. Despite these positive results, it is essential to note that these effects may be partially ascribed to antioxidant or immunomodulatory activities rather than a direct anti-parasite effect. This is particularly important when considering the actual therapeutic potential of these formulations. There have been differences between the studies in the extraction procedures, concentration of active compounds, and experimental designs, which make it difficult to standardize the results. Moreover, the intricacy of plant-derived materials, which may have several bioactive molecules, makes it difficult to identify the active component and its mode of action in a nanoparticle system.

Mineral/Metal Nanoparticles (Zn, Ag, and Se)

Metal-based nanoparticles have been identified as promising poultry feed additives with the potential to exert antiparasitic and gut health-improving activities. They can be prepared using physical, chemical, and biological approaches, which provide flexibility in the production and application of these nanoparticles (Michalak et al., 2022). Among the metal-based nanoparticles investigated, zinc and silver nanoparticles (ZnONPs and AgNPs) have been highlighted for their therapeutic potential against Eimeria spp. infections. For example, ZnONPs exhibited similar efficacy to diclazuril in broiler chickens infected with mixed strains of Eimeria spp., which resulted in improved growth performance, parasitological and hematological parameters, and antioxidant status (El-Maddawy et al., 2022). Similarly, AgNPs (15 mg/liter of drinking water) treatment caused a reduction of 50% in the shedding of oocysts of E. tenella, with marginal improvement in the scores of cecal lesions, although no significant effect on weight gain was recorded (Chauke and Siebrits, 2012).

The possible advantages of metal-based NPs lie in the ability of the nanoparticles to exert both direct and indirect antiparasitic effects. ZnONPs have been hypothesized to restrict Eimeria parasite growth in intestinal cells before the shedding of oocysts (Dkhil et al., 2015). AgNPs have been demonstrated to have an impact on the cell walls of protozoan parasites, induce reactive oxygen species, alter enzymatic functions, and disrupt nucleic acid structures, leading to parasite death (Cameron et al., 2016; Chang et al., 2012; Ullah et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2017). However, the molecular mechanisms of these effects have not been fully understood, and species or developmental differences in parasite susceptibility to these nanoparticles have yet to be fully understood.

The indirect effects of metal NPs also add to their anticoccidial activity. Coccidial infections have been known to affect microbial diversity in the gut, leading to compromised gut integrity, which in turn can increase the severity of coccidial infections (Lu et al., 2021). ZnONPs have been found to increase microbial diversity by promoting beneficial bacteria such as Firmicutes and Lactobacillus (Xia et al., 2017). Thus, zinc nanoparticles may have a probable indirect effect in controlling coccidiosis due to their probiotic effect. Further, ZnONPs can enhance the morphology of the intestine, including villus height, thus enabling nutrient uptake and growth performance (Hafez et al., 2017). There is also evidence of immunomodulatory effects of zinc nanoparticles, including boosted resistance to infection (Yusof et al., 2019). Furthermore, zinc plays a role in antioxidant enzymes as a cofactor; thus, zinc nanoparticles can counteract oxidative stress caused by Eimeria infection in chickens, as shown by a decrease in malondialdehyde content and increase in antioxidant activity in supplemented chickens (El-Katcha et al., 2018; El-Maddawy et al., 2022; Yıldız and Karakurt, 2021).

These results, collectively, show that metal nanoparticles possess anticoccidial activity through a multifactorial mode of action, which involves direct parasiticidal activity and modulation of gut health, immunity, and oxidative status. Nevertheless, the efficacy of these nanoparticles can vary depending on the size and dose. Moreover, safety issues, including residues and cytotoxicity, should be evaluated to ensure the safety of these nanoparticles prior to their application (Michalak et al., 2022).

Recently, green synthesis of nanoparticles (plant or microbial-based) has been explored for biomedical and veterinary purposes. This method offers safer, more economical, environmentally benign, with improved physicochemical stability, targeted bioactivity, and biocompatible substitutes for chemically and physically produced NPs (Singh et al., 2018a; Afzal et al., 2025). In chickens infected with E. tenella, Lactobacillus casei EE12-mediated zinc nanoparticles reduced the severity of the infection, and the birds demonstrated improved growth performance and a balanced beneficial gut microbial community (Thagfan et al., 2025). While conventional anticoccidials such as amprolium have shown limited efficacy in controlling coccidiosis, ZnONPs synthesized by Nigella sativa (50-100 nm) showed potent anticoccidial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory activity along with enhanced growth performance in E. tenella-infected broilers (Lail et al., 2023). This indicates that the use of bioactive components of plants may also have a role in enhancing the functional activity of nanoparticles synthesized by them.

The green-synthesized AgNPs have also shown considerable anticoccidial activity. The in vitro study of Cucumis melo leaves extract-coated AgNPs (Cm-AgNPs) on Madin-Darby bovine kidney (MDBK) cell lines showed that AgNPs inhibited oocyst development in a dose-dependent manner (Rani et al., 2023). AgNPs (15 ppm, 83-100 nm), either alone or coated with Morinda citrifolia extract (100-200 nm), have shown moderate anticoccidial activity after oral administration for 28 days in broilers infected with E. tenella. The activity of AgNPs is indicated by improved growth performance and reduced faecal oocyst shedding and intestinal lesion scores (Ranvir et al., 2017). Likewise, the green synthesis of AgNPs from Zingiber officinale showed improved jejunal mucosal integrity and goblet cell density and reduced parasite development and oocyst shedding in E. papillata-infected mice (Dkhil et al., 2023).

Selenium nanoparticles synthesized with the help of plant extracts are another example that proves the potential of green nanotechnology. Selenium nanoparticles are highly bioavailable, non-toxic, and help in the enhancement of antioxidant potential as well as nutrient uptake in broilers (Gangadoo et al., 2020). Biosynthesis of selenium nanoparticles from the leaves of Azadirachta indica extracts showed anticoccidial as well as antioxidant activity against E. papillata infected mice (Abdel-Gaber et al., 2023). This demonstrates that plant-based nanomaterials could have positive effects, inhibiting parasites, enhancing tissue integrity in the intestine, and modulating oxidative and immune responses in the host. The bioavailability, low toxicity, and multi-action potential of these biologically synthesized nanomaterials suggest that they could provide an eco-friendly substitute to conventional strategies for controlling coccidiosis. However, more studies are needed to standardize the dosing regimens, long-term effects, and safety in the field.

Chitosan-Based Nanoparticles

CS NPs have emerged in recent times as promising antiparasitic agents, given their use in controlling some protozoa, such as Plasmodium falciparum, Giardia lamblia, Trichomonas vaginalis (Elmi et al., 2021), Cryptosporidium parvum (Ahmed et al., 2019), and Toxoplasma species (Gaafar et al., 2014). Their activity is thought to result from their small size, large surface area, and film-forming properties, which may improve their interaction with parasites.

Mechanistically, CS NPs have been shown to exert their antiparasitic activity by interacting electrostatically with oocyst surfaces, thereby affecting wall integrity and permeability and ultimately impairing parasite viability (Ahmed et al., 2019). Findings from the in vitro studies have shown that CS NPs exert their activity in a time-dependent manner in the destruction of Cryptosporidium oocysts (Ahmed et al., 2019). In another study, E. tenella oocysts were shown to have reduced sporulation, wall distortion, and abnormal sporocysts in a dose-dependent manner after being subjected to CS NPs (Kasem et al., 2022). Thus, the benefits of CS NPs are enhanced bioavailability, better dissolution, less expensive production, and good safety profile. Their broad-spectrum activity against parasites makes them potential candidates for further investigation in the control of poultry coccidiosis. Although promising in vitro results have been reported, in vivo studies need to be carried out to clarify the optimal dosage, route of administration, and possible interactions with the host's immune system.

Nanoencapsulation of chemical anticoccidial drugs

The effectiveness of conventional anticoccidials in poultry is limited by problems such as low water solubility, bioavailability, development of resistance, and toxicity. Nanotechnologies such as polymeric nanocapsules and micelles have shown promise for overcoming these problems by increasing solubility and stability of the compounds and targeting them to the site of action, which could also minimize the required dosage and toxic side effects.

Nanocapsules are usually in the size range of 10-100 nm and have a liquid core covered by a polymeric membrane. They may be made of either natural polymers such as chitosan, dextran, alginate, and gelatin or synthetic polymers such as PLGA, PLA, and PMMA (Deng et al., 2020). Natural polymers such as chitosan not only improve the encapsulation of hydrophobic drugs (Zhao et al., 2016; Guerry et al., 2014), but also improve intestinal adhesion and absorption by modifying the junctions between epithelial cells (Wang et al., 2016; Singh et al., 2018b).

Toltrazuril (TOL), which is a broad-spectrum anticoccidial agent, is poorly soluble in water (0.41 μg/mL at 25°C), making its systemic administration challenging (Kalkal et al., 2021). Nanotechnological techniques, including the application of the Tol mixed nanomicelle (TMNM) method with sodium deoxycholate-Brij C20 polyethylene ether-triton x100 surfactants, have improved its solubility and bioavailability using the film hydration method (Zhang et al., 2018b). Similarly, polymeric nanocapsules based on Eudragit® S100 or poly-ε-caprolactone have successfully delivered toltrazuril (<200 nm) to broilers, demonstrating equivalent prophylactic efficacy at half the conventional dose, with reduced intestinal lesions and oocyst excretion (Baron et al., 2022).

Furthermore, CS-based micelles conjugated with 3-carboxyphenylboronic acid and loaded with diclazuril (DIC) demonstrated site-specific delivery, nanoscale size (118.9 ± 1.1 nm) with drug loading of 8.97%, and improved plasma drug stability (Zhang et al., 2018a). Oral administration of these DIC-loaded micelles enhanced anticoccidial activity, reduced intestinal damage, and promoted weight gain in E. tenella-infected broilers compared to conventional drug suspensions (Zhang et al., 2018a). Similarly, halofuginone hydrobromide-loaded D-α-tocopherol polyethylene glycol 1000 succinate polymer micelles (HTPM) had an encapsulation efficiency of 71.1 ± 4.15%, a drug loading of 14.04 ± 0.12%, and an approximate spherical shape (12.65 ± 0.089 nm). The HTPM exhibited improved cellular uptake, mitigation of E. tenella-induced apoptosis, and modulation of the gut microbiome, enabling lower doses to achieve superior efficacy (Lin et al., 2023).

Thus, the polymeric nanoencapsulation is a significant step in the field of anticoccidial therapy. It has been observed that nanoencapsulation increases solubility, stability, and targeted delivery of drugs; thus, lower dosing can be achieved, reducing the possibility of toxicity and increasing therapeutic efficacy.

When considered together, nanotechnologies for phytochemicals, metal nanoparticles, chitosan, and nanoencapsulation of anti-coccidial drugs clearly show how therapeutic applications of nanotechnology have the potential to greatly advance parasite control in concert with gut health and immune support. This is especially important because in many cases, the mechanisms behind the benefits of these nanotechnologies—the ability to modulate immunity, to provide antioxidants, and to maintain intestinal integrity—are the very mechanisms that help to make vaccines more effective as discussed in Section 3. Hence, prevention and therapy are not separate goals but complementary components of the same control mechanism.

Future Perspectives and Research Gaps

Although much progress has been made in nanotechnology-based therapies for coccidiosis in birds, there are still some limitations that need to be resolved before commercial use of these innovations. In order to control the disease, one needs to obtain sustained immunity and be able to decrease the parasite load during the infection. Among the primary directions in research is the creation of multifunctional nanocarriers, which can perform not only an immunomodulatory role but also serve a therapeutic purpose within one system. Biodegradable nanocarriers, like CS and PLGA, can be used for antigen protection, sustained delivery, and stimulation of mucosal immunity, while at the same time increasing drug delivery and bioavailability of phytochemicals and anticoccidial drugs. Such dual functions can be used for the development of platforms suitable for vaccination and treatment, providing a more efficient solution in the fight against Eimeria.

Despite the promising findings, the protective mechanisms of nanoparticle technology still need to be elucidated. It has been hypothesized that better efficacy can be attributed to the effects of antiparasitism, increased antigen recognition, immune modulation, antioxidant capacity, and improved intestinal permeability. However, the contribution of each of the above factors is unknown. Further research utilizing transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and advanced imaging is required for a better understanding of the interactions between the nanoparticles, the immune system of the host, its intestinal bacteria, and Eimeria development.

Safety and residue issues are also not adequately addressed. Most research concentrates mainly on short term efficacy and has little information on biodistribution, tissue retention, and withdrawal period especially for metal-nanoparticles like silver and zinc. When administered orally, nanoparticles are likely to pass through the GIT and accumulate in organs like the liver, spleen, and kidneys, where the liver has the most accumulates because of its detoxification function and metal metabolism activities (Dhas et al., 2015; Yausheva et al., 2018; He et al., 2024). For instance, zinc oxide nanoparticles have relatively lower bioaccumulation potential, but prolonged exposure can increase their residues (Abdelnour et al., 2021), while silver nanoparticles have a higher persistence in tissues, posing more safety concerns (Dhas et al., 2015). Moreover, the residue depletion process of the nanoparticles in poultry is not well-known owing to the short period of study, different formulations of the nanoparticles used, and lack of evaluation beyond slaughter ages, making it difficult to compare (Abubakr, 2025).

The other major issue is standardization of formulation because the efficacy of nanoparticles is affected by several physical and chemical characteristics including size, surface charge, morphology, encapsulation efficiency, release, and stability. These inconsistencies make it difficult to reproduce these findings and draw conclusions about efficacy. Thus, there should be some standard methods of formulation and quality control. Moreover, in some instances, research papers highlighting strong immune responses or anticoccidial results do not give information about blinding, randomization, and power of study, and this makes it difficult to interpret the robustness of such studies.

Feed manufacturing is another issue that needs to be addressed for practical application. High temperatures may change the stability of nanoparticles by causing aggregation, breaking down the materials used for encapsulation, and releasing active components prematurely (Waqar et al., 2026). Nonencapsulated vaccines, phytochemicals, and chemical agents need to retain their stability when subjected to feed processing methods such as pelleting, which involves conditioning at temperatures ranging from 70 to 90 °C. Therefore, future development should focus on thermal stability of nanocarriers.

Translation from the lab setting to the field is also critical. Research is often carried out in ideal settings using one Eimeria species, a set challenge dose, and small populations that are not comparable to commercial farming environments with multiple pathogens, stressors, and varying management practices. Field studies on a larger scale are thus required to examine the effectiveness and feasibility of integrated nanotechnology-based control programs within the commercial environment. Conclusion

Avian coccidiosis remains a significant challenge to the poultry industry, especially in view of drug resistance and limitations of the current vaccine strategies. Thus, the long-term viability of the traditional control measures remains questionable. New technologies, such as multi-epitope vaccines and nanotechnology, provide new avenues to overcome these challenges, through improved delivery, immunogenicity, controlled release, increased bioavailability, and therapeutic potential. However, the practical application of nano techniques remains limited by the lack of safety evaluation, formulation standardization, and large-scale validation. Therefore, the future of nanotechnology in the field of coccidiosis control is not in the replacement of conventional strategies, either preventative or therapeutic in nature, but rather in their combination into multifunctional platforms which increase the efficiency of both disease prevention and disease control. Using nanotechnology to deliver a more effective vaccine, administer drugs at specific targets, modulate the immune response, provide an antioxidant effect and maintain good intestinal health through a common technology platform has great promise as an avenue for more sustainable and efficient coccidiosis control programs.

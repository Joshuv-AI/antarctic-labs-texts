Application of the integrated data platform combined with dietary management for adults with diabetes: A prospective randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xiyu Liu, Xiaohong Wang, Mengxun Xie, Lulu Cao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsTo investigate the efficacy of the integrated data platform of cloud hospital combined with dietary management for adults with type 2 diabetes.Materials and MethodsWe conducted a randomized controlled clinical trial. One hundred eighty patients with type 2 diabetes were randomly allocated into a control group (Group A) and an experimental group (Group B). Routine standard diabetes care was applied to the patients in Group A. The integrated data platform with dietary management was applied to Group B. Individualized diabetes education videos were sent to the patients through the platform. The primary endpoint was the change in HbA1c and change in body weight from baseline to Week 12 during the follow‐up.ResultsAt Week 12, HbA1c was 7.4 ± 0.7%, 6.9 ± 0.9% in Groups A and B, P < 0.01. The rate of fasting blood glucose <7 mmol/L, and glycosylated hemoglobin <7% was higher in Group B than in Group A. At Week 12, there was a significant weight loss and body mass index decrease in the overweight or obese patients of the experimental group. Those overweight or obese patients in the experimental group utilizing the appetite suppressant semaglutide achieved the most significant weight loss, with a 13.4% reduction after 12 weeks.ConclusionsThe integrated data platform combined with personalized diabetes education video delivery was verified to be a more effective management mode for diabetes. For overweight or obese adults with diabetes, the use of semaglutide in conjunction with dietary management and the integrated data platform led to greater weight loss.Keywords: Dietary intervention, Digital platform, Type 2 diabetes

INTRODUCTION

The global prevalence of diabetes is on the rise, with a particularly notable increase observed in China 1 , 2 . The management and control of diabetes is not satisfactory, and diabetes‐related deaths are on the rise 3 . The lack of health education, especially diet therapy, is one of the reasons why diabetes is not diagnosed early and not well managed. How to effectively carry out diabetes education and dietary therapy and to improve patient compliance is the key to high‐quality diabetes management 4 , 5 .

The research on diabetes education mainly focuses on self‐care, team teaching, food exchange, and quality of life. However, the transformation of diabetes health education to a personalized, refined, and information‐based platform still needs further research 6 , 7 , 8 . Attempts to apply technology‐based behavioral therapy to encourage patients to maintain proper lifestyles are increasing for patients with diabetes 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 . However, the consistent use of these applications is limited by factors such as inconvenient data input and insufficient ability to integrate the medical information of patients. Most of the advisory services and diabetes education are not personalized 13 .

The integrated data platform created by our hospital might overcome these limitations and be an appropriate education and management platform for patients with diabetes. We investigated the efficacy of the integrated data platform combined with dietary management for adults with type 2 diabetes.

RESEARCH DESIGN AND METHODS

Study design

This was a 12 week prospective randomized controlled trial. The enrollment period for the study was from November 2022 to January 2024, during which a total of 180 patients were enrolled. The subjects were randomized 1:1 to receive the integrated data platform combined with dietary intervention or standard care and followed up for 12 weeks. The experimental randomization method involved the utilization of a numerical random table. This was a single‐blind trial, blinded only to the subjects, and the researchers were informed to facilitate intervention and response.

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The ethics committee of Dongyang People's Hospital approved the study protocol. All participants signed written informed consent before eligibility screening. This trial has been registered on the Chinese Registry of Clinical Trials (ChiCTR) under the identification code: ChiCTR2400080460. https://www.chictr.org.cn.

Inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria

Participants must meet the following inclusion criteria: a confirmed diagnosis of type 2 diabetes, age over 18 years, recent hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) measurement taken within the previous 2 weeks showing levels above 7.0%, strong self‐management ability, possession of a smartphone, and the capability to learn to use the internet. Exclusion criteria include acute complications, severe liver and kidney function impairment, severe cardiovascular diseases, communication disorders or mental disorders, and pregnancy.

Trial procedures

The integrated data platform of the cloud hospital was developed by the Information Department and the Chronic Disease Management Center of our hospital. Through this platform, physicians can conduct educational and consulting services, record comprehensive healthcare data (including exercise and diet, weight, blood glucose, blood pressure, and biochemical indicators), as well as provide online follow‐up. We created a series of diabetes education videos and personalized education programs. Utilizing this platform, we could deliver the videos to patients’ or their caregivers’ mobile phones to effectively enhance diabetes education and to improve compliance. The workflow of the cloud hospital platform is shown in Appendix S1.

The participants were randomly allocated to either the control group (group A) or the experimental group (group B) using a random‐number table. Patients in group A received standard diabetes care with hospital visits at week 12, while those in group B were provided access to an integrated data platform on their mobile phones and received education on its use during their hospital visit. They were also given personalized diabetes education videos and blood glucose monitoring programs through this platform, as detailed in Appendix S2. Through the platform, patients had complimentary access to consult their physicians as needed and to receive feedback within 24 h. Additionally, medical staff proactively reached out to patients with messages every 2 weeks, and follow‐up visits were scheduled after 12 weeks.

Outcome measures

Endpoints

The primary endpoints included the difference in HbA1c and body weight change from baseline to 12 weeks during follow‐up. The secondary endpoints encompassed the incidence of hypoglycemia, adherence to anti‐diabetic medications, satisfaction with health system support, and quality of life score.

Baseline data

Comprehensive medical data were gathered including age, gender, height, weight, body mass index, duration of diabetes, smoking history, blood pressure, lipid levels, uric acid levels, liver and kidney function tests, and details of anti‐diabetic medications.

Follow‐up data

The data to be collected included the number of video views, consultations and feedback messages, patient adherence to diet suggestions, self‐glucose monitoring data, and occurrences of hypoglycemia.

Visits after 12 weeks of data

Medical data such as fasting blood glucose, glycosylated hemoglobin, body weight, and body mass index were checked. The compliance score, quality of life score, and satisfaction rating from the subjects were obtained.

The compliance score was calculated based on the responses to four questions, with a maximum total of eight points. Each ‘yes’ answer received two points. Question 1 inquired about forgetting to take medication, Question 2 asked if the medication was occasionally skipped, Question 3 addressed stopping medication when symptoms improved, and Question 4 focused on discontinuing medication when symptoms worsened.

The EQ‐5D‐3L (EuroQol Five Dimensions Questionnaire 3 level) was used to assess the quality of life score. Participants were required to evaluate their health status across five dimensions: mobility, self‐care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. The Index Value was then calculated using the EQ‐5D Scoring Algorithm to accurately reflect the subjects’ quality of life.

The satisfaction survey was based on five specific criteria: the overall treatment process, the doctor's service attitude, diabetes health education and its impact on care for you and your family members, access to timely consultation with a doctor, and feelings about the diabetes management mode. Each criterion was rated on a scale of 0–2 points representing dissatisfaction, average satisfaction, or satisfaction. The final satisfaction rating was calculated by dividing the total score by 10.

Statistical analysis

In total, 180 patients with type 2 diabetes were randomly allocated into the control group (group A, n = 90) and the experimental group (group B, n = 90). After accounting for a dropout rate of 18%, the research analysis was finally conducted in75 subjects in group A and 73 subjects in group B. The direct elimination method was employed for the data of patients who were lost to follow‐up, as most of them were unable to participate in the blood sampling test after 12 weeks due to work and other reasons, resulting in a lack of key data. The baseline characteristics and efficacy analysis were conducted on the full analysis set, which included 75 subjects in group A and 73 subjects in group B. Subsequently, analyses were carried out specifically on the overweight or obese subjects within group B, with a total of 58 subjects. Subgroup analyses were then performed based on the use of different anti‐diabetic medications.

IBM SPS Statistics 25.0 was used for statistical analysis. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. An independent sample t‐test was used for comparison of continuous variables. Kruskal–Wallis was performed before an independent sample t‐test between the groups. The rates between the two groups were compared by Chi‐square test. A value of P < 0.05 was considered significant.

Efficiency and safety endpoints

A total of 3,285 intervention messages were sent to participants in group B. Among them, 1,406 (42.8%) were education messages, 1,320 (40.2%) were warning messages, 453 (13.8%) were encouraging messages, and the rest 106 (3.2%) contained confirmation of behavior. The diabetes education videos (short videos) were viewed 920 times by group B totally.

At week 12, the HbA1c levels were 7.4 ± 0.7% and 6.9 ± 0.9% in group A and B, respectively group A vs B, P < 0.01, as demonstrated in Table 2 and Figure 1. The rate of subjects with HbA1c <7% at week 12 were 28% in group A, and 52% in group B, P < 0.01. Similar results were found for the rate of subjects with a fasting blood glucose <7 mmoL/L, as demonstrated in Table 2 and Figure 2. However, no difference was found in the BMI level at week 12 between the two groups.

Subgroup analysis

A subgroup analysis was performed on overweight or obese participants in group B based on their anti‐diabetic medication usage. Semaglutide group (n = 23): Overweight or obese subjects in the experimental group, and using semaglutide (combined or not combined using other anti‐diabetic medicine). The baseline characteristics were comparable between the two groups, except baseline body weight or baseline BMI levels. DISCUSSION

This 12 week, prospective, randomized clinical trial demonstrated that the use of an integrated data platform in combination with personalized diabetes education videos led to improved management of type 2 diabetes. The baseline characteristics of the two groups were similar, including the use of anti‐diabetic medication. The experimental group showed better blood glucose control and a reduced incidence of hypoglycemia, indicating improved effectiveness and safety. Additionally, participants in the experimental group reported higher levels of satisfaction, compliance, and quality of life scores. These findings align with existing research about the use of platforms in the management of diabetes 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 . The use of short videos for diabetes education, facilitated by our hospital's proprietary integrated data platform, represents a departure from previous studies. The integrated data platform may offer a more effective model for diabetes management through combination with short video education.

However, no significantly difference was found in the BMI level after the 12 week follow‐up between the control group and the experimental group in our study. The possible reason was that 25% of the participants were not overweight, and some patients gained weight after blood glucose control. Therefore, analyses were carried out specifically on the overweight or obese subjects. Nutrition and diet therapy has been emphasized for overweight or obese patients with type 2 diabetes. There are a variety of dietary patterns for obese subjects with or without diabetes, of which a low‐calorie balance diet is a safe and more sustainable pattern 17 , 18 , 19 . For the overweight or obese subjects in our study, a low‐calorie balanced diet pattern was suggested for them through the integrated platform. Our study showed that there was a significant weight loss and body mass index decrease in the overweight or obese patients in the experimental group. It implies that recommendations for a low‐calorie balanced diet may prove efficacious in managing weight among overweight or obese patients with diabetes. Nevertheless, further randomized controlled studies are necessary to validate this assertion.

GLP‐1 receptor agonists are increasingly preferred by healthcare professionals and overweight or obese patients with type 2 diabetes due to their capacity to reduce appetite, increase satiety, and to facilitate dietary management 20 , 21 , 22 . Thirty‐two percent of the subjects in our trial used GLP‐1 receptor agonists, and all used semaglutide. The group utilizing the appetite suppressant semaglutide in combination with dietary management and the integrated data platform achieved the most significant weight loss, with a 13.4% reduction after 12 weeks. The administration of semaglutide may potentially enhance patients’ compliance with dietary regimens. In general, for overweight or obese patients with type 2 diabetes, a combination of approaches including appetite suppressants, dietary therapy, and the integrated data management platform may result in greater weight loss.

CONCLUSIONS

For adults with type 2 diabetes, the integrated data platform combined with personalized diabetes education video delivery was verified to be a more effective management mode. For overweight or obese adults with diabetes, the use of semaglutide in conjunction with dietary management and the integrated data platform may lead to greater weight loss.

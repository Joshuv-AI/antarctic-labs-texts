Optimizing the timing of JAK1 inhibition preserves the anti-tumor efficacy of radioimmunotherapy in lung cancer by preventing T cell exhaustion
Authors: Zhiru Gao, Linlin Yang, Linlin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractJAK1 inhibitors are widely used in hematologic malignancies and autoimmune diseases, their potential in combination with immunotherapy for solid tumors such as lung cancer is increasingly being recognized. The combination of radiotherapy and immunotherapy (radioimmunotherapy, iRT) has become a standard treatment strategy for lung cancer, but the impact of JAK1 inhibitors on the antitumor efficacy of iRT remains insufficiently explored. This study aimed to investigate the anti-tumor effects of JAK1 inhibition on iRT and the underlying mechanisms. The direct effects of JAK1 inhibitor on lung cancer were evaluated in vitro using cell proliferation, colony formation, and wound healing assays, and in vivo using immunodeficient mouse models. In addition, in immunocompetent mouse models of subcutaneous and orthotopic lung tumors, we analyzed the influence of JAK1 inhibitor on the tumor immune microenvironment during iRT, with a focus on the T-cell function. The results revealed that although JAK1 inhibitor monotherapy markedly suppressed lung tumor growth, its concurrent administration unexpectedly compromised the antitumor efficacy of iRT, an effect associated with impaired CD8⁺ T cell activity and interferon-γ (IFN-γ) production. In contrast, short-term delayed administration of the JAK1 inhibitor approximately one week after completing iRT was found to mitigate T-cell exhaustion, optimize immune responses, and thereby preserve the antitumor efficacy of iRT. In conclusion, concurrent use of JAK1 inhibitor during iRT should be avoided in lung cancer treatment. To the editor,

The combination of radiotherapy and immunotherapy (radioimmunotherapy, iRT) has become a standard treatment strategy for lung cancer, significantly improving clinical outcomes. The JAK-STAT pathway serves as a central hub for cytokine receptor signaling and plays a critical role in multiple biological processes, including immune regulation, inflammation, tumorigenesis, and angiogenesis [1]. Emerging evidence suggests that JAK1 inhibition can enhance the response to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in patients with lung cancer, indicating its potential for combination with immunotherapy [2]. However, its role in the context of iRT remains poorly understood and systematically unexplored. In this study, we used Golidocitinib, a highly selective JAK1 inhibitor approved for peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL), to investigate its regulatory effects and the underlying mechanisms by which JAK1 inhibition impacts the anti-tumor efficacy of iRT [3].

We first analyzed the correlation between JAK1 expression and the survival prognosis of patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) using the TCGA database. The results showed that high JAK1 expression was significantly correlated with poor patient survival (Fig. S1). Subsequently, we systematically evaluated the effects of a JAK1 inhibitor on the proliferation and migration of murine Lewis lung carcinoma (3LL) cells through a series of in vitro functional assays. Cell proliferation assays demonstrated that JAK1 inhibitor concentrations exceeding 1 μM significantly suppressed the proliferation of lung cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1A). Colony formation assay results indicated that the clonogenic capacity of cells gradually decreased with increasing drug concentrations following JAK1 inhibitor treatment (Fig. 1B, C). Wound healing assays further suggested that the JAK1 inhibitor effectively inhibited the migration of lung cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1D, E). In vivo, we established a xenograft tumor model by subcutaneously inoculating 3LL cells into Balb/c-nude mice to assess the direct inhibitory effect of the JAK1 inhibitor on tumor growth. The results showed that the JAK1 inhibitor significantly suppressed tumor volume growth and reduced tumor weight, confirming its direct anti-tumor effect in vivo (Fig. 1F–I).

We further investigated the impact of JAK1 inhibition on the anti-tumor efficacy of iRT in an immunocompetent mouse subcutaneous tumor model. Tumor-bearing C57BL/6 J mice were treated seven days post-inoculation with a single dose of localized radiotherapy alongside twice-weekly i.p. injections of αPD-1 (Fig. 1J). For tumor irradiation, mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection and immobilized on an animal irradiation platform (XCELL 160 X-ray system, Kubtec). The tumor region was selectively irradiated with a single dose of 10 Gy (6 MV energy, dose rate 2 Gy/min), while the rest of the body was shielded using a 10 cm thick lead block. The results demonstrated that the iRT treatment group exhibited the most potent tumor suppression. Although concurrent administration of the JAK1 inhibitor with iRT appeared to diminish the anti-tumor efficacy upon gross observation, the differences in tumor volume and weight between groups did not reach statistical significance (Fig. S2). To optimize the treatment regimen, we introduced an additional intervention group based on a delayed, short-term dosing strategy reported in the literature, in which the JAK1 inhibitor was administered one week after the completion of iRT [2]. The results indicated that this delayed dosing schedule had a minimal impact on the anti-tumor activity of iRT, suggesting that administering the JAK1 inhibitor one-week post-iRT may represent a more favorable combination strategy (Fig. 1K–M). In addition, iRT significantly increased p-JAK1, p-STAT1, and p-STAT3 levels in mouse tumors, indicating robust activation of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway. Administration of a JAK1 inhibitor dose-dependently suppressed iRT-induced phosphorylation of JAK1, STAT1, and STAT3, validating on-target inhibition of the pathway (Fig. S3).

We systematically evaluated the impact of JAK1 inhibitor administration timing on the anti-tumor efficacy of iRT using a tamoxifen-induced orthotopic lung cancer mouse model. The model was established by intraperitoneal injection of tamoxifen (75 mg/kg/day) for four consecutive days. Tumor development was confirmed six weeks later, at which point mice were allocated into experimental groups. All mice were euthanized for sample collection at week 8. The maximal therapeutic benefit was observed with iRT alone, which markedly reduced pulmonary tumor infiltration compared to the severe burden seen in model controls. Concurrent JAK1 inhibition marginally compromised this effect. Critically, this interference was circumvented by delaying JAK1 inhibitor administration until one-week post-iRT, a regimen that preserved anti-tumor efficacy, mirroring the results of iRT monotherapy (Fig. 2A, B). Immune profiling of the tumor microenvironment confirmed that iRT potentiated CD3⁺ and CD8⁺ T cell infiltration. This effect was negated by concurrent JAK1 inhibition but was rescued by delayed Golidocitinib administration, underscoring the critical role of treatment timing in preserving anti-tumor immunity (Fig. S4, S5). A similar trend was observed in the detection of IFN-γ, a key effector molecule in anti-tumor immunity (Fig. 2C, D). Furthermore, ELISA analysis of plasma cytokines associated with anti-tumor efficacy, including IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-10, yielded results largely consistent with the observed changes in immune cell profiles (Fig. 2E). To determine whether the observed functional enhancement of CD8⁺ T cells correlate with exhaustion markers PD-1 on CD3⁺CD8⁺ T cells, we isolated T cells from the tumor microenvironment. Flow cytometry analysis revealed that delayed and transient administration of Golidocitinib ameliorated T cell exhaustion, which was more favorable for subsequent antitumor efficacy (Fig. S6). Collectively, these results demonstrate that the timing of JAK1 inhibitor administration is a critical determinant of efficacy in combination with iRT, and that delayed dosing can attenuate its interference with iRT-induced anti-tumor activity.

The anti-tumor mechanism of JAK inhibitors involves both direct interference with tumor cell signaling pathways and modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment [4]. Constitutive activation of the IL6-JAK-STAT3 pathway, evidenced by elevated pJAK1 levels in NSCLC, underscores its role as a key oncogenic driver and a compelling target for therapeutic intervention aimed at suppressing tumor progression [5, 6]. The bifunctional nature of JAK inhibitors in the immune landscape, where they simultaneously alleviate immunosuppression via MDSC and Treg modulation while potentially dampening CTL and NK cell activity, highlights the critical need for precise treatment timing to harness their full therapeutic potential [7–9]. Additionally, our results demonstrate that concurrent administration of JAK1 inhibitor led to a greater reduction in NK cells, while delayed treatment significantly alleviated this suppressive effect, with only a minor impact on B cells (Fig. S7).

In summary, we demonstrate that a delayed, short-term JAK1 inhibitor regimen, initiated after the completion of iRT, effectively preserves the antitumor immune response. This preclinical evidence strongly supports the clinical exploration of this sequencing strategy and highlights administration timing as a key consideration in the design of future combination trials.

Primary care physicians’ continuous usage intention of a B2B telemedicine system: an empirical study based on a hybrid model in China
Authors: Pan Gao, Dongle Wei, Yu Wang, Wei Lu, Ruifang Guo, Yunkai Zhai, Yan Qiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTelemedicine has revolutionized healthcare delivery, significantly improving accessibility and efficiency. In China, the predominant mode of telemedicine service delivery is Business-to-Business (B2B). However, the utilization of B2B telemedicine is relatively low. Within the B2B context, primary care physicians (PCPs) are critical users. There is a lack of knowledge regarding PCPs’ continuous intention to use the B2B. To address this gap, the study aims to study and unveil factors affecting PCPs’ continuous usage intention for B2B telemedicine.MethodThis study proposes an integrated Technology-Individual-Environment theoretical framework to analyze factors and mechanisms influencing PCPs’ continuous usage intention for B2B telemedicine, considering exogenous, technology, and endogenous dimensions. We used Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) and fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) to test the research model. A cross-sectional study was conducted among PCPs with prior B2B telemedicine usage experience across more than 30 primary healthcare institutions in China. Overall, 421 (63.88%) out of 659 questionnaires were valid.ResultsThe PLS-SEM results revealed that within the individual dimension, attitude, perceived behavioral control, and intrinsic motivation positively influence PCPs’ continuous usage intention, while perceived risk had a negative impact. Within the technological dimension, assurance and reliability demonstrated direct positive effects on continuous usage intention, whereas tangibility indirectly influenced intention through attitudinal mediation. Notably, the environmental dimension showed no statistically significant effects on continuous usage intention. Complementing the PLS-SEM findings, fsQCA analysis identified three configurations that lead to high continuous usage intention and three configurations leading to low intention. Based on these results, factors influencing PCPs’ continuous usage intentions are classified into foundation factors, performance factors, and enhancement factors.ConclusionWithin B2B telemedicine, this study focuses on the factors and mechanisms influencing PCPs’ continuous usage intention. These findings provide a robust theoretical foundation for policymakers and healthcare administrators seeking to enhance PCPs’ engagement with B2B telemedicine platforms, ultimately contributing to the advancement of integrated healthcare delivery systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13628-2.Keywords: B2B telemedicine, Primary care physician, Technology acceptance, Continuous usage intention, PLS-SEM, fsQCA, Configuration analysis

Introduction

China has been confronted with the persistent problems of low medical quality and efficiency, as well as inadequate and unevenly distributed health professionals [1]. These challenges hinder the implementation and development of the Healthy China strategy. To “build a healthier world for all” and achieve national health coverage by 2030, Chinese leaders have decided to implement telemedicine as one of the solutions to enhance the accessibility and quality of healthcare services [2]. Telemedicine, the integration of information technology and medical skills, is crucial for addressing these challenges by providing remote healthcare services to patients across different regions [3, 4]. In China, the predominant mode of telemedicine service delivery is Business-to-Business (B2B) [5]. In 2024, China’s National Health Commission reports that B2B telemedicine networks now cover all cities and counties, with 70% of township health centers establishing partnerships with higher-level hospitals [6].

Within this model, primary healthcare institutions, including county and district hospitals, community health centers, township health centers, and village clinics, seek remote specialist consultations from higher-tier medical establishments such as provincial-level hospitals through telemedicine platforms [7]. This mechanism facilitates collaborative care between different levels of the healthcare system [8], thereby improving healthcare accessibility, quality, and efficiency while reducing costs [9, 10]. Despite its benefits, adoption rates remain low [11], leading to a significant “best game with no players” phenomenon [12]. According to data from the National Telemedicine Center of China (NTCC), the annual average number of primary healthcare institutions applying telemedicine per hospital was 158.04, 128.33, 97.23, 82.14, 82.74, and 80.35 cases in 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023, respectively. The declining use of B2B telemedicine in primary healthcare institutions suggests potential challenges or barriers hindering its adoption or sustained usage. This issue poses a critical developmental bottleneck for the technology. Moreover, the success of technological implementation depends not only on the adoption stage but also on continuous usage behavior in the post-adoption stage [13]. Primary care physicians (PCPs) represent the cornerstone users within this B2B telemedicine ecosystem. These licensed medical doctors work at primary healthcare institutions, providing medical care, preventive services, and health management within their scope of practice. When encountering complex cases that exceed their clinical capabilities, PCPs serve as the requesting party who utilize telemedicine platforms to obtain specialist guidance from higher-tier hospitals [9]. Within B2B telemedicine environments, PCPs fulfill multiple interconnected roles that extend beyond traditional clinical duties. They simultaneously function as technology users of telemedicine platforms, trusted advisors whose recommendations significantly influence patient decisions, critical intermediaries connecting patients with specialist expertise, and clinical evaluators responsible for implementing and assessing telemedicine-derived treatment outcomes [7]. Given their pivotal position as both technology users and patient care coordinators, PCPs emerge as the critical stakeholders whose sustained engagement determines the success of the B2B telemedicine initiative. Therefore, understanding the factors and mechanisms influencing PCPs’ continuous usage intention is crucial for increasing B2B telemedicine adoption rates.

PCPs’ continuous usage intention for B2B telemedicine (CUIT) refers to their willingness and behavioral intention to continuously utilize telemedicine platforms in the post-adoption stage for seeking remote specialist consultation from higher-tier hospitals. The previous studies have provided significant evidence regarding doctors’ intention to engage in telemedicine practices based on theoretical frameworks, including Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) [14], Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) [15], integrated TAM and TPB model [16, 17], and Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) [18]. These studies further examined the factors influencing doctors’ adoption intention, employing methodologies such as structural equation modeling (SEM), descriptive statistics, and regression analysis [19]. However, three research gaps are relevant and urgent to be addressed.

Firstly, few studies investigate the factors influencing PCPs’ CUIT. Existing studies primarily focused on doctor adoption behavior within doctor-to-patient (D2P) telemedicine contexts, where doctors directly deliver medical services to patients, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a). While these investigations have provided valuable insights into doctors’ technology adoption behavior, B2B telemedicine introduces a fundamentally different dynamic that warrants separate investigation. Figure 1(b) illustrates that PCPs in B2B telemedicine environments fulfill multiple interconnected roles. They serve as technology users of telemedicine platforms, trusted advisors whose recommendations influence patient acceptance of telemedicine services, intermediaries facilitating connections between patients and specialist expertise, and clinical evaluators implementing telemedicine-derived treatment plans. These distinct roles and responsibilities in B2B telemedicine suggest that the determinants affecting PCPs’ CUIT may differ substantially from those influencing doctors in D2P contexts. However, research specifically examining the factors driving PCPs’ CUIT in B2B telemedicine remains limited.

D2P Vs. B2B telemedicine model. (a) Doctor-to-Patient(D2P) Telemedicine, (b) Business-to-Business(B2B) Telemedicine

Secondly, existing studies have overlooked the essence of telemedicine, which is to provide medical services. Existing research treated telemedicine merely as a generic information technology, exclusively examining the impact of technological variables on user adoption behavior based on traditional information technology acceptance theories. However, telemedicine is an organic integration of information technology and medical means, and it is a technology with medical attributes [20]. Within medical contexts, the medical attributes inherent to telemedicine can significantly influence the adoption behaviors of PCPs. Merely focusing on individual perceptions of the technological attributes falls short of capturing a comprehensive causal relationship diagram. Consequently, one of the focal points of our research is to investigate how the dual attributes of “medical and technological” within telemedicine influence PCPs’ CUIT.

Thirdly, the mechanism of multifactor linkage influencing PCPs’ CUIT has not been investigated. Linear methods like SEM and regression analyze single factors’ net effects, overlooking nonlinear causality and synergistic interactions [21]. PCPs’ CUIT involves an intricate amalgamation of multiple factors, not linearly additive. Linear methods also miss asymmetrical joint effects and multiple configurations influencing outcomes. Hence, it is imperative to select an appropriate method to complement the exploration of the impact of multi-factorial linkages on PCPs’ CUIT.

Considering the challenges above, we choose PCPs as the empirical participants and employ a hybrid methodology combining Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) and fuzzy set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to systematically identify the determinants influencing PCPs’ CUIT, in an attempt to answer the following three questions:

Theoretical basis and framework

Theoretical framework

The Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework is a classic model for analyzing technology adoption in organizations, dividing factors into technological, organizational, and environmental categories [22]. However, the TOE framework was designed primarily for organizational-level technology adoption decisions, where factors such as top management support, organizational size, and formal structure play crucial roles in shaping institutional adoption. When examining individual-level continuous usage intention, organizational-level constructs cannot fully explain the micro-level behavioral dynamics that drive individuals’ technology continuance decisions. This limitation is particularly evident in healthcare contexts, where individual physicians retain substantial autonomy over technology usage decisions. Even when organizational policies mandate initial adoption, physicians ultimately decide whether to continue using the technology based on psychological factors and individual experiences.

Therefore, we develop the Technology-Individual-Environment (TIE) framework (Fig. 2), which shifts the analytical focus from organizational decision-making processes to individual behavioral determinants. Physician’ continuous usage is driven by individual psychological factors, while also being shaped by the technological and organizational environment. The individual dimension incorporates psychological factors that influence individuals’ decisions to continue using technology. The technology dimension encompasses the characteristics of medical technology and the quality of medical services. The environment dimension captures external contextual factors that affect individual usage behavior. This framework provides the theoretical foundation for identifying factors influencing PCPs’ CUIT by systematically integrating relevant perspectives across these three dimensions. The adaptation from TOE to TIE retains the systematic structure of the macro-level framework while addressing the explanatory gap at the individual level.

Research framework

Subsequently, we employ a two-step PLS-SEM and fsQCA analytical approach to identify factors influencing PCPs’ CUIT and explore their configurational effects. This approach reveals the underlying mechanisms of PCP adoption behavior in B2B telemedicine contexts. Figure 2 illustrates the overall research framework.

Hypotheses based on the individual layer of PCPs

Ajzen [23] extended the theory of reasoned action (TRA) to propose TPB, a reliable framework for predicting technology adoption intentions. There are three main determinants of behavioral intention: attitude, perceived behavioral control, and subjective norms [24]. The role of subjective norms in the external environment will be addressed later in the application environment layer. Therefore, within the individual layer (endogenous dimension), we select the factors of perceived behavioral control and attitude to analyze the PCPs’ CUIT and consider the addition of intrinsic motivation and perceived risk from the user’s perspective.

Based on the TPB, attitude reflects individuals’ positive or negative feelings about performing a target behavior [25]. A positive attitude facilitates an individual’s engagement with technology, whereas a negative attitude acts as a hindrance [26, 27]. In this paper, attitude refers to the positive psychological disposition of PCPs towards the continued use of B2B telemedicine. When PCPs think it is a good idea or wise to use B2B telemedicine, the CUIT will be enhanced.

Perceived behavioral control refers to an individual’s perception of how easy or difficult it is to perform a particular behavior. Studies have shown that users’ perceived behavioral control is a key factor in the adoption of emerging technologies [28]. In healthcare scenarios, research has demonstrated that healthcare professionals exhibit a greater willingness to adopt emerging information technology or systems when they possess the requisite knowledge and competence to control the utilization of the new technology effectively [29]. Likewise, when users have a diminished sense of perceived behavioral control, it leads to a decrease in their confidence to use the technology effectively, subsequently diminishing their willingness to adopt it. In our research, perceived behavioral control refers to the PCPs’ perceived ability to use B2B telemedicine. We believe that a strong perceived control over the use of B2B telemedicine by PCPs can enhance their behavioral intention. Accordingly, the following hypotheses are formulated for this study:

H1

Attitude (AT) has a positive influence on PCPs’ CUIT.

H2

Perceived behavioral control (PBC) has a positive relationship with CUIT.

Ryan and Deci [30] proposed that individual behavior is primarily driven by external incentives and intrinsic motivation. The classification of these two factors is based on whether the motivation originates internally within the task itself (intrinsic motivation) or is influenced by external factors (external incentive). Intrinsic motivation is driven by factors inherent to the task or activity itself. Individuals are intrinsically motivated when they engage in an activity that they find inherently interesting, enjoyable, or personally satisfying, which is heartfelt and not utilitarian [31]. In essence, when an individual perceives that their work holds personal significance and meaning, they tend to invest sustained time and effort into it. Existing research widely acknowledges that intrinsic motivation plays a pivotal role in positively stimulating individual innovative behavior, such as the adoption of new information systems [32]. Hence, we posit that a heightened perceived intrinsic motivation among PCPs to utilize B2B telemedicine will correspondingly amplify their behavioral intention to continue using this technology. Consequently, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H3

Intrinsic Motivation (IM) positively influences PCPs’ CUIT.

Cox and Rich [33] defined perceived risk as the degree to which individuals perceive adverse consequences stemming from the use of information technology. This encompasses the likelihood of incidence and the magnitude of potential loss. Studies have shown that an increase in perceived risk can hinder people’s behavioral intention to use information technology [34]. Hsieh [35] proposed that the perceived risk among healthcare professionals concerning health-cloud technology and m-health had a negative effect on their intentions to engage in usage. In the context of B2B telemedicine, PCPs typically assess perceived risks in terms of potential time delays and potential diagnostic and treatment errors. This paper operationalizes perceived risk as the perception of adverse outcomes that PCPs might encounter when using B2B telemedicine. When PCPs perceive a lower level of risk, their CUIT is expected to strengthen. Based on these arguments, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H4

Perceived Risk (PR) has a negative influence on a PCP’s CUIT.

Hypotheses based on the application environment layer

Subjective norms refer to the social pressures that individuals perceive when deciding whether to engage in a specific behavior, representing the influence of significant others or groups within the external environment on individual behavioral decisions [24]. Many studies [27] demonstrated that an individual’s behavior is significantly shaped by those who hold importance in their life. In essence, individuals tend to conform to perceived social pressures, behaving in accordance with the expectations of those they value. Consequently, we propose the following hypothesis.

H5

Subjective Norms (SN) have a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

Ryan and Deci [30] classify tangible and intangible achievements outside of work as external incentives, primarily associated with the pursuit of material wealth (money, prizes), as well as social respect and recognition from others (respect, acknowledgment, and honor). Many studies have substantiated the significant role of extrinsic incentives in shaping adoption behavior for new technology. For instance, within the domain of mobile health, empirical evidence has demonstrated the efficacy of extrinsic incentives as a positive reinforcement mechanism for adoption behavior [36]. Hence, it can be posited that when PCPs perceive external incentives for using B2B telemedicine to be substantial, their CUIT will correspondingly increase. Based on the literature review, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H6

External Incentive (EI) has a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

Hypotheses based on the telemedicine technology service layer

Parasuraman et al. [37] inaugurated the concept of service quality, which is explicated as the disparity between users’ expectations of product/service and their post-usage perceptions. Empirical studies confirm that service quality strongly influences users’ intention to persist with a product/service [38]. And, users are more inclined to remain engaged if the product or service consistently delivers high-quality and valuable core services [39]. In the B2B telemedicine context, PCPs will assess the service quality based on their perception of process quality and outcome quality concerning interactions among individuals (physicians) and human-computer interactions [20]. Subsequently, PCPs’ perceived service quality will influence their intention to continue utilizing telemedicine. Our research gauges the service quality in the realm of B2B telemedicine by the SERVQUAL model.

The SERVQUAL model consists of five key dimensions that customers use to evaluate service quality. Within the realm of B2B telemedicine, the five dimensions are specified as follows: (ⅰ) Tangibles refer to the physical attributes, encompassing facilities, equipment, and communication networks used in delivering the service. In the study, tangibles are directed toward assessing PCPs’ perspectives, including the telemedicine system’s robustness, user-friendliness, and equipment accessibility. (ⅱ) Assurance focuses on the competence, credibility, and professionalism of the service provider. In B2B telemedicine context, assurance primarily pertains to whether the service provider possesses adequate knowledge, working skills, and professional achievements to deliver satisfactory services to PCPs. This includes factors like the competence and reputation of the higher-level medical institution and the expertise of specialist medical professionals. (ⅲ) Responsiveness relates to a service provider’s willingness and ability to help customers promptly. In the study, responsiveness primarily pertains to the upper-level medical institution’s capacity to deliver medical services to PCPs promptly following the PCP’s telemedicine application. (ⅳ) Reliability centers on the ability of a service provider to consistently deliver accurate and dependable services. Within telemedicine services, reliability primarily revolves around PCPs’ perceptions of telemedicine utility. This includes aspects such as the accuracy and utility of medical assistance delivered by specialists or the effectiveness in reducing referrals to higher-level medical facilities. (ⅴ) Empathy involves understanding and caring about the needs and feelings of users. In the context of B2B telemedicine, empathy primarily concerns the perception that PCPs are treated with respect and compassion.

H7a

Tangibles (TA) have a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

H7b

Assurance (AS) has a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

H7c

Responsiveness (RES) has a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

H7d

Reliability (REL) has a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

H7e

Empathy (EM) has a positive relationship with PCPs’ CUIT.

Consequently, a stronger service quality in B2B telemedicine corresponds to higher PCPs’ CUIT. Additionally, research has already substantiated the positive impact of service quality on the attitudes of users or customers [40, 41]. Based on these arguments, the following hypotheses are proposed.

H8a

Tangibles (TA) have a positive influence on PCPs’ attitude towards CUIT.

H8b

Assurance (AS) has a positive influence on PCPs’ attitude towards CUIT.

H8c

Responsiveness (RES) has a positive influence on PCPs’ attitude towards CUIT.

H8d

Reliability (REL) has a positive influence on PCPs’ attitude towards CUIT.

H8e

Empathy (EM) has a positive influence on PCPs’ attitude towards CUIT.

Methods

Instrument

To empirically assess the theoretical model and research hypotheses concerning PCPs’ intention to continue using B2B telemedicine, our study employed a questionnaire-based approach. It consists of two main sections. The first section primarily focuses on gathering demographic information from the participating PCPs, encompassing variables such as gender, age, highest education level, professional title, and the duration of telemedicine utilization. The second section comprises 12 constructs, encompassing PCPs’ perceptions of the application environment, the service quality of B2B telemedicine technology, and personal emotions. All items of the questionnaire were adapted from established sources. The variable items of attitude, perceived behavioral control, and subjective norms were adapted from Taylor and Todd [25]. The items of perceived risk were developed from Kamal et al. [11] and Ortega and Román González [42]. The items used to measure extrinsic and intrinsic incentives were adapted from Shen and Khalifa [43]. The technology service quality items from Lu et al. [20]and Zhang et al. [44]. The items of PCPs’ CUIT came from Taylor and Todd [25]. The questionnaire was measured with a five-point Likert scale reaching from 1 (“I do not agree at all”) to 5 (“I completely agree”). Given that our survey was conducted in China among PCPs, the questionnaire underwent multiple iterations, including translations and feedback rounds. These measures were taken to ensure the questionnaire’s alignment with the B2B telemedicine context and to enhance its overall readability. Finally, a professional translator back-translated the questionnaire to form the items as indicated in Table 1, which details the constructs and the literature sources.

Data collection

We collaborated with NTCC in Henan Province, which provides hundreds of telemedicine consultations daily for primary healthcare institutions nationwide. Our study employed a geographical region-based stratified sampling strategy to ensure representativeness across different areas and economic development levels. First, we chose 30 counties from five geographical regions, including Northern, Central, Western, Southern, and Eastern. Within each geographical region, we randomly selected six counties from the list of counties/cities with NTCC-registered institutions.

Next, the recruitment process was initiated through a formal investigation notice issued by NTCC to survey PCPs’ CUIT. Subsequently, we established a dedicated investigation team to coordinate communications, monitor questionnaire distribution, and manage response collection. With NTCC’s support, team members sent notifications to telemedicine center leaders at the selected institutions via email, supplemented by phone calls and text messages to facilitate survey distribution. The questionnaire was administered in October 2021, targeting PCPs who had experience with B2B telemedicine. Participants were recruited from the 30 primary healthcare institutions corresponding to the selected counties. Before questionnaire distribution, we provided respondents with information regarding the research purpose, background context, and data management and privacy protection measures. Participation was entirely voluntary, and questionnaire completion constituted consent for data collection and usage. The online questionnaire platform included mandatory field settings, ensuring no missing values during data collection. Based on the standard formula for cross-sectional studies [45, 46], the sample size was calculated by the formula. The calculation assumed an expected proportion of 0.50 (a = 0.50) due to the absence of reliable prior estimates for B2B telemedicine adoption rates in our specific context [47], a 95% confidence level (), and a degree of precision of 0.05 (). The minimum required sample size was 385.

We collected a total of 659 questionnaires during the designated study period. After excluding 238 invalid questionnaires due to highly repetitive response patterns, we obtained 421 valid questionnaires, yielding an effective response rate of 63.88%. The final sample size exceeded our pre-estimated minimum requirement, ensuring adequate statistical power and reduced bias to enhance the reliability and validity of our research findings. This study was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of the School of Management, Zhengzhou University. Informed consent was obtained from every participant. No personally identifiable information was collected, and all data were used solely for research purposes.

Statistical methods

In our study, PLS-SEM and fsQCA were adopted to test the theoretical model and research hypotheses on PCPs’ CUIT, as well as to explore the complex causal relationship between variables.

PLS-SEM is particularly well-suited for analyzing complex models that include many constructs, indicators, and relationships [48]. It is especially useful in exploratory research aimed at theory development, where the objective is to understand increasing complexity by extending established theories [49]. This makes PLS-SEM highly relevant for our theoretical model. However, PLS-SEM assumes causal symmetry and linear additive relationships, focusing on estimating uniform net effects across the sample. While effective for testing directional hypotheses and identifying universal drivers, this mean-centered symmetric approach may yield an incomplete description of the effects observed in practice [50]. Importantly, technology adoption intention often involves causal complexity characterized by multiple pathways leading to the same outcome and conjunctural causation (combinations of factors producing outcomes). Different combinations of factors may lead to successful technology adoption. Traditional symmetric approaches may not fully capture these interdependencies [51]. The fsQCA addresses this limitation by adopting a configurational approach that treats relationships as set-theoretic rather than correlational [52]. The advantages of choosing fsQCA are that the method provides an asymmetric approach to explore how different combinations of antecedents can synergistically affect the dependent variable [22]. Moreover, fsQCA can identify multiple equally effective pathways to the same outcome and reveal empirical findings that contribute to theory building [8]. Therefore, this paper combined PLS-SEM and fsQCA, integrating symmetric and asymmetric perspectives, to comprehensively explore the factors influencing PCPs’ CUIT.

Measurement model evaluation

To test the reliability of the measurement model, factor loadings (FL), composite reliability (CR), and Cronbach’s α were employed. The results are presented in Table A.1 (see Appendix A). FL for all items exceeded 0.700, and the CR and Cronbach’s α values for each construct were higher than the minimum threshold of 0.700, indicating good reliability of the measurement model.

The validity of the measurement model was evaluated in terms of content validity, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Content validity was established since all measurement items were adapted from validated scales in published literature. The convergent validity of the measurement model was assessed using average variance extracted (AVE) values. Table A.1 (see Appendix A) shows that all constructs achieved AVE values above 0.500, confirming satisfactory convergent validity. Discriminant validity was confirmed since the square root of AVE for each construct was greater than its correlations with other constructs, as shown in Table A.2 (see Appendix A). In summary, the reliability and validity of the measurement model were considered adequate.

Since all variables were measured through the same questionnaire, we tested for common method bias to ensure that measurement error did not unduly influence the results. Following the approach proposed by Podsakoff et al. [53], we compared the original measurement model with a model that included an additional common method factor. Significant common method bias is indicated when the new model’s Comparative Fit Index (CFI) and Tucker-Lewis Index (TLI) increase by more than 0.100, while the Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA) and Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) decrease by more than 0.050 [54]. In our study, these thresholds were not exceeded, indicating no significant common method bias (see Table A.3 in Appendix A). Additionally, we tested for multicollinearity among variables, with all Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) values below 3.300 [55], indicating no problematic overlap between variables (see Table A.1 in Appendix A).

Overview of PLS-SEM results

Using the PLS-SEM method to investigate the net effect of individual independent variables on PCPs’ CUIT, the empirical study reveals seven key factors. Within the individual layer (endogenous dimension), attitudes and perceived behavioral control have direct and positive effects on PCPs’ intention to continue using telemedicine, which is consistent with studies by Chau and Hu [16]. Intrinsic motivation has also a significant and positive impact. Perceived risk has a significantly negative impact, in line with the findings of Kamal et al. [11]. In the technology dimension, assurance and reliability directly and significantly impact PCPs’ CUIT positively. These two factors also exert indirect effects on PCPs’ CUIT through the partially mediating role of attitude. Additionally, the tangibles of telemedicine have an indirect effect on PCPs’ CUIT through the fully mediating role of attitude. However, within the environment layer (exogenous dimension), there is no evidence in PLS-SEM to support the effects of extrinsic motivation and subjective norms on PCPs’ CUIT. Doctors are generally regarded as a highly knowledgeable and responsible group, with their professional goal primarily centered on improving patient health. Their intention to continue their behavior is largely influenced by self-psychological factors, as well as the medical service quality and utility of technology. If telemedicine can enhance patient diagnosis and treatment outcomes as well as efficiency in clinical services, PCPs are more likely to adopt it. Organizational environmental factors are not the top priority.

Re-analysis of the data using FsQCA

PLS-SEM analysis for this study has provided detailed explanations of the net effect of the independent variables under symmetry assumption. Subsequently, the focus shifts to exploring the combinatorial effects of independent variables on the dependent variable by fsQCA analysis under asymmetry assumption. Research framework of configuration analysis

Calibration

The fsQCA method requires data calibration before analysis, which involves transforming variables into fuzzy sets with values ranging from 0 to 1. Following established fsQCA practice [51, 58], we adopt a standardized calibration approach to reduce researchers’ degrees of freedom and enhance methodological objectivity. Fully-in and fully-out cutoff points are set at mean +/- one standard deviation for each variable, with the point of maximum ambiguity set to the mean for each variable in the study. This calibration method is particularly appropriate for composite Likert-scale measures, as demonstrated by Douglas et al. [51], who successfully applied similar standardized rules to averaged multi-item constructs. The calibration anchor points are shown in Table A.4 (see Appendix A). Because fsQCA is designed for single variables, constructs were first computed as the average of their items. The variables were then calibrated according to the anchor points using fsQCA 3.0 software.

Necessary condition analysis

The purpose of variables necessity analysis is to nvestigate whether any causal conditions are necessary for the presence or absence of an outcome. Following recommendations from various authors, an antecedent is considered “necessary” when the corresponding consistency score exceeds a threshold of 0.90 [59]. As shown in Table A.5 (see Appendix A), the consistency of each antecedent is below 0.9, indicating the weak explanatory power of a single variable on the results. Therefore, it becomes necessary to examine the linkage effect of antecedents on PCPs’ CUIT.

Configuration analysis

High-performance configurations (Pathways A, B, and C) are combinations of conditions that drive superior CUIT outcomes, whereas low-performance configurations (Pathways D, E, and F) are combinations that lead to inferior CUIT outcomes.

Overview of fsQCA results

The fsQCA method identified three pathways that stimulate high CUIT and three pathways that trigger low CUIT. Notably, there is an asymmetric relationship between the core. Through systematic analysis of these configurational patterns, we identified three distinct types of factors influencing PCPs’ CUIT, as shown in Fig. 6.

Factor categories in B2B telemedicine adoption

First, foundation factors represent basic requirements for telemedicine adoption. These factors include tangibles, perceived behavioral control, perceived risk, and extrinsic incentives. They consistently appear as core conditions in low-performance configurations (pathways D, E, and F), indicating that when they are problematic or inadequately addressed, they can result in low CUIT regardless of other positive conditions present. However, even when these foundation factors are adequately met, they only prevent poor outcomes but are insufficient to drive high CUIT. Second, performance factors demonstrate more complex behavioral patterns, functioning as both drivers of success and sources of failure depending on their performance level. This category includes assurance and responsiveness. Assurance appears as a positive contributor in high-performance configurations (pathways A, B), but its absence leads to poor outcomes (pathway F). Similarly, responsiveness drives positive results when present (pathway B) but contributes to negative outcomes when lacking (pathway E). Third, enhancement factors primarily function as positive drivers in high-performance configurations. This category encompasses attitude, intrinsic motivation, and reliability. Attitude appears as a core positive condition across all successful configurations (pathway A, B, C), while intrinsic motivation and reliability serve as key contributors in specific high-performance configurations. These patterns suggest a sequential implementation approach where addressing foundation factors is essential to prevent adoption failures, optimizing performance factors creates direct quality improvements, and strengthening enhancement factors drives sustained high usage intention.

Discussion

Within the B2B telemedicine context, this study examines PCPs’ CUIT. Using PLS-SEM and fsQCA methodologies, we analyze the individual effects of influencing factors and investigate their configurational interactions. In comparing PLS-SEM and fsQCA results, we observe both convergent and divergent findings that provide complementary insights into PCPs’ CUIT.

Regarding convergent findings, both analytical approaches consistently confirm the central importance of six key antecedents, demonstrating robust cross-method validation across multiple theoretical frameworks. Attitude emerges as the most robust predictor across both methodologies, with PLS-SEM revealing the strongest direct effect on CUIT and fsQCA identifying positive attitude as a necessary condition in all high-performance configurations. This convergence validates that favorable cognitive evaluations are fundamental to sustained technology adoption behaviors, particularly in professional healthcare contexts where rational assessment predominates decision-making processes.

It has been confirmed that perceived behavioral control demonstrates consistent importance across both analyses, showing significant positive effects in PLS-SEM while its absence consistently characterizes low-performance configurations in fsQCA (Pathway E and F). This convergence supports that PCPs’ confidence in their technical competencies directly influences their continuance intention to engage with advanced medical technologies. However, the fsQCA results reveal an important asymmetric pattern. Perceived behavioral control exhibits substantially higher marginal utility in preventing low CUIT than in promoting high CUIT. Specifically, while the absence of perceived behavioral control consistently appears as a core condition in low-performance configurations (Pathway E and F), its presence is not identified as a core condition in any of the facilitating pathways (Pathway A, B, and C). Similarly, perceived risk exhibits a significant negative influence in PLS-SEM and appears as a core condition in low-performance configuration (pathway D) when combined with tangibles deficiency. This asymmetric effect suggests that these factors function primarily as failure prevention mechanisms rather than success drivers, where their adequate presence enables adoption while their deficiency creates significant barriers. Therefore, the fsQCA results serve as a useful complement to the PLS-SEM results.

It is also found that intrinsic motivation, assurance, and reliability collectively function as positive drivers that actively promote CUIT across both methodologies. Intrinsic motivation exhibits consistent positive influence, with PLS-SEM showing significant impact and fsQCA identifying it as a core motivator in high-performance configurations (pathway A). Assurance maintains universal significance across both analytical frameworks, strongly influencing attitude formation in PLS-SEM while serving as a core condition in multiple high-performance configurations (pathway A and B). It is noteworthy that reliability demonstrates consistent importance across both methodologies, showing significant positive effects in PLS-SEM while emerging as a core condition in high-performance configurations (pathway C). These convergent findings confirm that autonomous motivation, professional credibility, and clinical utility collectively drive sustained professional technology use. The consistent importance of intrinsic motivation emphasizes the value of framing telemedicine as a tool for professional development rather than merely a mandated organizational requirement. The universal significance of assurance reflects the critical importance of professional credibility and institutional trust in healthcare contexts, where practitioner confidence in specialist expertise and organizational reputation fundamentally shapes technology acceptance decisions. The robust role of reliability is particularly significant given the high-stakes nature of healthcare decisions, where clinical effectiveness directly supports PCPs’ CUIT. The results indicate that these factors serve as success enablers rather than failure prevention mechanisms, actively generating CUIT through their presence.

However, notable divergences emerge between the two approaches, as extrinsic incentives, responsiveness, and tangibles show differential effects across methodologies, revealing the conditional complexity that configurational analysis uniquely captures.

The tangibles case perfectly exemplifies this difference. PLS-SEM demonstrates that tangibles influence CUIT exclusively through attitude mediation with no significant direct effects, whereas fsQCA identifies absent tangibles as a necessary condition in low-performance configurations (Pathways D, F). This discrepancy reflects sample heterogeneity across different contexts. In well-resourced settings, adequate infrastructure has a marginal impact because other factors dominate decisions. In contrast, in resource-constrained environments, infrastructure deficiency creates substantial barriers. The averaging process in PLS-SEM across these diverse conditions yields weak aggregate effects, thereby obscuring this conditional importance. fsQCA reveals the specific mechanism by showing that tangibles deficiency becomes critical when combined with high-risk perceptions (Pathway D) or with multiple other negative factors (Pathway F) to create comprehensive usage barriers. This represents a threshold interaction where technical inadequacy amplifies cognitive barriers beyond simple additive effects. Poor infrastructure triggers physicians’ existing technology concerns, creating compound barriers that prevent adoption regardless of other positive factors. This pattern demonstrates why tangibles function asymmetrically as a foundation factor, where adequacy enables but does not drive CUIT, while deficiency directly prevents it.

Similarly, responsiveness demonstrates no significant effects in PLS-SEM yet proves crucial in specific configurations. fsQCA identifies multiple pathways to both success and failure, reflecting equifinality in causal mechanisms. The results revealed that responsiveness consistently operates in a positive direction, but its effectiveness is contingent on accompanying conditions. It contributes to success when combined with positive attitudes and strong assurance (Pathway B), yet its absence in conjunction with low perceived behavioral control characterizes low-performance configurations (Pathway E). This demonstrates that the effectiveness of responsiveness depends on supportive contextual conditions, illustrating configurational interdependence that linear models cannot adequately capture.

Moreover, extrinsic incentives further illustrate how the two methodological approaches yield divergent findings. While PLS-SEM shows no significant main effects for extrinsic incentives, fsQCA identifies their absence as a core condition within comprehensive low-performance configurations (Pathway F), illustrating their conditional importance. In high-performance pathways (A, B, C), extrinsic incentives function as ‘don’t care’ conditions that neither drive success nor hinder it when other strong motivational factors (such as attitude, assurance) are present. However, Pathway F reveals that when multiple foundational elements are simultaneously deficient, including absent extrinsic incentives, low perceived behavioral control, poor tangibles, and inadequate assurance, this creates a comprehensive low-performance configuration. The configurational analysis reveals an asymmetric relationship where extrinsic incentives are substitutable when strong intrinsic drivers exist, but their absence contributes to low-performance when other motivational mechanisms are also lacking. This explains why linear averaging shows weak effects, as extrinsic incentives matter conditionally, becoming relevant primarily in preventing the worst-case scenario of multiple concurrent deficiencies.

Therefore, the integration of these complementary approaches produces a more comprehensive understanding than either method alone. While PLS-SEM identifies universal drivers through average effects, fsQCA reveals configurational complexity through pathway-specific mechanisms. This methodological triangulation enables targeted solutions for promoting sustained B2B telemedicine adoption.

Theoretical implications

This study aims to show how the analysis of net and combinatory effects of specific antecedent variables can affect the PCPs’ CUIT. Our research contributes to three main aspects.

Firstly, this study is the first to investigate the factors influencing PCPs’ CUIT. Within the context of B2B telemedicine, the distinct roles undertaken by PCPs result in the fact that medical attributes of telemedicine have a more pronounced impact on PCPs’ CUIT than technological attributes. In contrast, in D2P telemedicine, the technological attributes of telemedicine are more capable of affecting doctors’ continuous usage intention.

Secondly, we propose an integrated TIE theoretical framework to address both the medical and technological attributes within B2B telemedicine. Unlike previous studies that focused on general technology adoption, we extend the technology adoption model to incorporate the healthcare domain, considering the medical service quality and environmental influences alongside individual cognitive and psychological factors.

Lastly, our study comprehensively depicts the causal relationships between PCPs’ CUIT and its antecedents, utilizing both symmetric and asymmetric approaches. By comparing results obtained from fsQCA and PLS-SEM analyses, we identified the determining factors and configurations affecting PCPs’ CUIT. Moreover, we classified factors into foundation factors, performance factors, and enhancement factors. Foundation factors serve as basic requirements that prevent low CUIT when adequately addressed, but cannot independently drive high CUIT. Performance factors function dually as both drivers of success and sources of failure, depending on their performance level. Enhancement factors primarily stimulate high CUIT.

Practical implications

Our integrated PLS-SEM and fsQCA analysis provides evidence-based guidance for targeted interventions to promote PCPs’ continuous usage intention across three strategic layers.

Technology service layer

Technology service layer

Given the substantial effects of assurance in PLS-SEM and consistent presence in successful configurations (Pathways A, B), while reliability serves as a core driver (Pathway C), healthcare institutions should establish measurable competency standards for telemedicine experts. This includes systematic credential verification, diagnostic accuracy tracking, and transparent performance reporting to build user confidence in professional capabilities. While responsiveness shows no direct PLS-SEM effects, it proves crucial in high-performance configurations (Pathway B) when combined with positive attitudes. Organizations should implement structured response protocols with defined service-level targets for consultation callbacks and technical support to enable service excellence pathways. Although tangibles show no direct effects in PLS-SEM, their absence in low-performance configurations (Pathway D) combined with high perceived risk creates significant barriers. Institutions should establish standardized equipment specifications and network reliability requirements to prevent technology-concern-driven adoption failures.

(2) User layer

User layer

With low perceived behavioral control appearing consistently in low-performance configurations (Pathways E and F), institutions should implement structured competency-building programs. These should include hands-on training integrated into workflows, just-in-time support systems, and progressive skill development to prevent adoption barriers. Given the negative effects of perceived risk and its role in low-performance patterns (Pathway D), organizations should establish comprehensive risk management protocols. These protocols should include clear liability guidelines, mandatory privacy training, and systematic incident response procedures to address technology-related concerns. Since attitude appears as a necessary condition in all successful configurations (Pathways A, B, and C), and intrinsic motivation drives high-performance patterns (Pathway A), institutions should create peer showcase programs and professional development frameworks that position telemedicine adoption as an enhancement of clinical excellence rather than an organizational mandate.

(3) Environment layer

Environment layer

While extrinsic incentives show no direct PLS-SEM effects, their absence characterizes comprehensive low-performance patterns (Pathway F) with multiple concurrent deficiencies. Organizations should provide targeted compensatory support, including performance-based recognition and financial incentives to maintain engagement when foundational technical and individual competency issues cannot be immediately resolved. These evidence-based interventions address both universal drivers identified through PLS-SEM and configurational patterns revealed by fsQCA, enabling targeted solutions for sustainable telemedicine adoption.

Limitations

Although this study attempts to comprehensively examine all critical research components, several limitations require acknowledgment. These limitations offer pathways for further investigation. Employing a longitudinal research design can mitigate potential biases. First, tracking PCPs’ CUIT over time can unveil evolving individual behavioral patterns. Second, as the majority of our sample originates from a specific region, there is a risk of bias due to cultural, policy, and technological disparities. Hence, conducting analogous surveys across diverse regions can deepen our understanding of the factors shaping PCPs’ CUIT.

Conclusion

B2B telemedicine enhances healthcare accessibility, quality, and convenience while reducing costs. As a complement to traditional healthcare delivery models, it contributes to achieving universal health coverage by 2030. Given PCPs’ multifaceted roles in B2B telemedicine and the unique medical attributes inherent in this technology, their CUIT are influenced by complex technological, medical, and psychological factors. This study addresses this gap by developing a comprehensive TIE theoretical framework, creating an “application environment-service quality of technology-personal psychology-technology acceptance” model to investigate PCPs’ CUIT. Using both symmetric (PLS-SEM) and asymmetric (fsQCA) analytical approaches, we examined the influencing factors and their multidimensional interactions. Our findings provide theoretical support for optimizing design strategies and implementation measures to promote sustainable telemedicine development. Our empirical results suggest that future research on the continuous usage behavior of health information technology should consider the differences between general technology and health information technology, while paying attention to the unique healthcare attributes of health technologies.

Analysis of the spatio-temporal variability and convergence of green development efficiency of city clusters in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River in China
Authors: Haifang He, Hongrui Wang, Hui Lin, Kan Zheng, Puwei Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe advantageous geographical location of the middle reaches of the Yangtze River (YRMR) plays an important role in promoting high-quality development in China and accelerating the development of the Yangtze River Economic Belt(YREB). In the new stage of development, green development efficiency (GDE) is an important metric for assessing the balance between economic development and ecological civilization. Therefore, taking three city clusters in China's YRMR as the study area, this research employs the Super-SBM model to assess their GDE in 2010–2020 in order to analyze the problem of GDE at the city scale. On this basis, spatio-temporal analyses, dynamic evolution, and convergence analyses have been carried out using the Thiel index, GML index model, and convergence model. The findings indicate that overall GDE has not yet reached an effective level and generally exhibits a two-stage fluctuating upward trend. Spatially, the pattern of distribution is "high in the southwest, low in the northeast". The differences within the city clusters in the YRMR in terms of green development are gradually narrowing, with differences within the same regions contributing the most to overall differences. The GML index shows a trend of fluctuating growth, indicating that the overall GDE growth is in good condition. GDE has grown significantly as a result of technological advancements, with the Wuhan metropolitan area (cluster A) experiencing the fastest growth in this regard, followed by the Chang-Zhu-Tan and the Poyang Lake rim city clusters (clusters B and C). The YRMR and its three major city clusters exhibit both σ- and β-convergence patterns. The convergence of GDE is simultaneously affected by several factors, including economic development (RGDP), urbanization level (UR), industrial structure (IND), technological development (TEC), and the level of openness (LO). Regional heterogeneity also exists. The above study provides decision-making references and empirical evidence for promoting green development in China's city clusters in the YRMR and advancing the improvement of economic-environmental incongruence in this area.Keywords: Green development efficiency, Super-SBM model, GML index, Theil index, Convergence trend

Introduction

The economy in China has long displayed a crude growth pattern, which has led to severe issues like increasing resource constraints, significant pollution, and ecosystem deterioration [1]. Other important issues of the day include accelerating the change from a black development mode marked by high consumption and the lack of efficiency to a high-quality development model with high efficiency and low consumption [2], as well as realizing the integration of socio-economic development and protection of the environment. And China's current response to resource bottleneck constraints and environmental pollution problems, the concept of green development was put forward [3]. "Promoting a green and low-carbon economy and society is the key to achieving high-quality development" states the 20th National Congress report. China formally proposed a "dual-carbon" aim in September 2020, pledging to aggressively lead and contribute to global low-carbon and green development. According to the Carbon Dioxide Emissions Report 2022 published by the International Energy Agency, China's energy-related CO2 emissions were relatively flat in 2022, declining by 0.2 per cent to about 12.1 billion tonnes [4]. All these show that China has paid sufficient attention to promoting green development and industrial transformation. Green development underscores the formation of a civilized development path for productive development, affluent living, as well as ecological well-being through the construction of a "two-type" society whose core concepts are circularity, low-carbon and sustainability [5]. As a result, cities are accelerating the adjustment of industrial structures, developing a green economy, and realizing a green transformation in this broad, profound and systemic socio-economic shift.

During his visit to Jiangsu, China's General Secretary Xi Jinping pointed out that "the YREBshould be built into the main battlefield of China's ecological priority and green development, and the main force leading the development of a high-quality economy". The "14th Five-Year Plan for the Development of the City Cluster in the YRMR" was officially released in 2022, indicating that the city clusters in the YRMR has evolved into a city cluster that supports the development of the YREB and central China's rise [6]. It is clear that city clusters have become the core areas to drive the country's economic development and ecological environment protection, and realizing the green development of city clusters is a key part of realizing the country's green development [7]. The city clusters in the YRMR are the first national-level city clusters approved in China, with a land area of 317,000 km2. Second only to the Yangtze River Delta urban agglomeration, it is the second largest urban agglomeration in China [8]. Under the background of the national regional coordinated development strategy and the strategy of the rise of central China, the city clusters in the YRMR have developed rapidly, with its inner Wuhan metropolitan area and the Rim Chang-Zhu-Tan City Clusters is particularly rapid. While forming a huge metropolitan area, it occupies a large amount of water, wetland, forest and agricultural land [8]. This may thus affect its environmental quality by exceeding its ecological carrying capacity, thereby causing ecological damage to the entire YREB. In this context, it is particularly important to scientifically and objectively quantify the GDE of YRMR, as well as thoroughly investigate the influencing variables of green development, which can have a better understanding of YRMR's current green development status. Meanwhile, it is urgent practical significance for promoting the high-quality development of the YREB and driving the construction of modern metropolitan areas and pointing us in the right path for speeding the region's green growth.

Furthermore, there are variations in the GDE among the city clusters of the YRMR as a result of the economic foundation, resource endowment, and industrial structure of each city cluster. The greater these variations in efficiency, the more imbalanced the social development in the YRMR will be between speed and quality. Specifically, we mainly focus on the following questions: (1) How is theYRMR's city clusters' GDE changing? (2) What are the temporal and spatial evolution characteristics of green development performance? (3) How big is the efficiency difference between and within city clusters, and where does it originate from? (4) Are they closing the distance from one another, or is there a catching-up effect? Studying the regional differences in GDE and its convergence characteristics of the city clusters in the YRMR can objectively study the status quo and reasons for the imbalance in the development of the Yangtze River, and respond to the above questions.

Research on GDE

(1)Measurement evaluation of GDE

Measurement evaluation of GDE

Research scales vary on GDE, including measuring GDE at the national [15], provincial [16], and municipal levels [17]. For instance, Lu [18] and Pu [19]analysed the spatiotemporal evolution of GDE in the upper reaches of the Yangtze River and the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei cluster. Liu [20]explored the evolutionary pattern of GDE in China, which is currently increasing, but is showing a downward trend from south to north. In terms of research methods, scholars use diversified research methods to measure GDE. Stochastic frontier analysis (SFA) and data envelope analysis (DEA) are two types of common methods for GDE measurement. The SFA method needs to determine the form of the production function, which is highly subjective and cannot deal with the efficiency measurement of multi-input and multi-output decision units [21]. The DEA method considers both multiple factor inputs, expected output and undesirable output, and does not need to set specific production functions, making it the most mainstream method of GDE measurement [22]. Scholars mainly measure regional GDE based on the DEA method and various extended models (SBM-DEA [23], Bootstrap-DEA [24], EBM-DEA [25],Super-SBM [26] etc.).

(2)Regional variations and influencing factors of GDE

Regional variations and influencing factors of GDE

Most scholars focus on analysing the spatial and temporal evolution of GDE and the factors affecting it [27,28]. Huang Yue et al. [29] concluded that the spatial distribution of green development in China's city clusters presents a hierarchical pattern of five categories: highly promoted areas, rapidly promoted areas, steadily promoted areas, preliminary promoted areas, and initial start-up areas. Previous studies have mainly used the Dagum Gini coefficient [30], Kernel density estimation, Theil index [31], geographic detector and Markov chain to identify the spatial differentiation characteristics and distribution dynamic trends of regional GDE. Such as Wang Shaohua et al. [32] and Li Xiuzhen et al. [33] analysed the spatio-temporal characteristics and influencing factors of industry GDE in the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, and the level of green development of the YREB, respectively. In addition, the traditional convergence model and spatial dubin model are used to explore the σ convergence, β convergence and club convergence mechanism of regional GDE [34,35]. As research on GDE has progressed, many scholars have attempted to delve into the influencing factors of GDE from different dimensions, such as scientific and technological innovation [36,37], industrial agglomeration [38], digitalization [39], industrial structure adjustment [40], vertical fiscal imbalance [41], industrial structure adjustment [42,43], energy factor [44] and environmental regulation [45]on GDE.

Research on GDE of YREB and YRMR

Yangtze rivers are the mother rivers of China, and scholars have been particularly interested in the development of Yangtze rivers [46]. Such as, Lin [47]analysed the spatial and temporal characteristics and influencing factors of the green development level in the YREB. From 2010 to 2019, the green development level of the Yangtze River Economic Zone is generally on an upward trend, with significant spatial differentiation and overall differences. Zhang [48]constructed a green development level indicator system from four dimensions: economic development, social welfare, ecological construction, and institutional supply, and measured the green development level of 110 prefecture-level cities in the YREB according to the entropy weight TOPSIS model. At the same time, the city cluster in YRMR, as the core area of the YREB, serves as the hub through the YREB. In comparison to the YREB, the research perspective is more microscopic and focused. In the context of spatio-temporal variability and convergence of GDE in YRMR, the existing literature is relatively scarce. The limited studies available primarily focus on spatial and temporal evolution and drivers [49], regional disparities [50]and green innovation efficiency [51].

Overall, it can be seen that scholars mainly focus on the macro level, and most of them take provincial or unbalanced development areas as research objects. There are few studies on the meso level of the city clusters in the YRMR in China. Few research has looked into the causes of disparities in GDE between regions and the characteristics of spatial convergence. Most studies pertaining to the effectiveness of the region's green development focus on assessing green development levels and examining the elements that influence them. Further, input-output indexes seldom introduce the carbon emission indexes into the undesirable outputs. Therefore, this paper combines existing studies to investigate the spatio-temporal variability and convergence trends of GDE in the YRMR under the background of "dual-carbon" to ensure the high-quality development of the YREB and drive the greenification of the modern metropolitan area.

The academic contributions of this study include the following. In the first place, it compensates for the lack of mesoscopic dimension and geographic spatial perspective of GDE research by using city clusters in the YRMR as the research object. Secondly, it expands the practical application of the Super-SBM model, adopts the model considering the non-expected output to measure regional GDE, and combines the current research background of carbon emissions to add a comprehensive indicator of environmental pollution with CO2 emissions into the contents. Thirdly, this paper highly combines and applies the GML index and Theil index as well as the convergence model, uses the regional difference decomposition method to dig out the size, source, and dynamic evolution of its regional differences, and constructs the incorporation econometric model to test its convergence on this basis. This paper provides a scientific identification of the evolution, regulations, characteristics, and geographical variability of GDE in the YRMR through an in-depth discussion of the spatiotemporal convergence trend and law of green development from the standpoint of city clusters. The goal of this research is to provide theoretical reference and statistical evidence to help address the economic-environmental incoherence in city clusters along the YRMR in China, as well as to encourage green development in these places.

Theory and analysis framework

Theory basis

This paper's theoretical foundations emphasise sustainable development, the five development, economic growth theory, green development, and so on. And Sorting these notions out can offer a theoretical foundation for the implementation of excellent economic development in the city cluster located in the YRMR.

(1)Sustainable Development Theory

Sustainable Development Theory

Our Common Future, which was published in 1987, codified the philosophy of sustainable development. "Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs" is how the United Nations introduced the idea of sustainable development. Ecological sustainability, social sustainability, and economic sustainability are the three fundamental facets of sustainable development. It views the environment, the economy, and society as an organically linked whole and demands that they all be harmonised and coordinated.

(2)Five Concepts for Development

Five Concepts for Development

The 18th CPC Central Committee urged for "development that is innovative, coordinated, green, open, and inclusive" during its fifth plenary session from October 26 to 29, 2015. The five development concepts are rich in connotation, rigors in system, and form a full and systematic aggregate. Among these, innovative development is at the centre. Coordinated development is required. Green development is essential. Openness to development is essential. Shared development is essential. The five development concepts are complementary, mutually reinforcing, and highly compatible with one another. They are clearly comprehensive and unified [61].

(3)Neoclassical Growth Theory

Neoclassical Growth Theory

The term "Neo-classical Growth Theory" refers to the economic growth theory proposed by American economist Robert Solow. According to Solow-Swan, economic growth is exogenous under technological homogeneity and identical preferences. The marginal productivity of factors of production decreases with production growth. Poorer countries with lower capital stocks grow faster than richer countries with higher capital stocks [62]. Under certain assumptions, economies will eventually reach a point of equilibrium known as the steady state, in which they will expand at a constant rate. Convergence happens when the economy approaches a stable state. The neo-classical growth theory's description of convergence was pioneering, since it provided a theoretical explanation for the process of economic convergence to long-term growth.

(4)Green Development

Green Development

Green development is based on the "green economy" concept. The phrase "green economy" was first used by British economist D. Pearce in his 1989 book Blueprint for a Green Economy. David Pearce makes the claim that “the green economy is a state of development that is market-oriented, based on the traditional industrial economy, and adapted to the needs of human environmental protection and health” [63]. David Pearce's concept of a "green economy" has completely broadened the meaning of green development today. Green development has the same connotations as sustainable development, green transition, low carbon economy, green growth, and carbon neutral society [[64], [65], [66], [67], [68], [69]]. Regarding the concept of green development, the majority of academics think that its key component is the achievement of a win-win scenario that benefits both economic expansion and environmental preservation. According to Hu Angang et al. [70], green development is the second generation of sustainable development and is a further inheritance and transcendence of the ideas and concepts of sustainable development. It also represents a fundamental break with the traditional model of crude economic development.

Analysis framework

Study theoretical framework

The conceptual implications and theoretical underpinnings of the study mentioned above serve as the basis for this work. In line with the theory of green development of earlier research [5,71], the study aims to redefine the conceptual theory for green development and build the "1 + 3+3 + 11" theoretical framework based on the three main systems of economics, society, and environment. One core, three dimensions, three systems, and eleven parts make up the theoretical framework, which functions as an interactive system with three circles of action: the target layer, the connotation layer, and the thematic layer. In other words, the first circle represents the fundamental elements of "green development", while the second circle is made up of the three green dimensions of "green wealth (G-Wealth), green welfare (G-Welfare), and green growth (G-Growth)". The outermost circle consists of the base layer of the Economic System, Social System and Natural System, and there are a total of 11 elements within the different subsystems of this circle, forming their own independent systems. This research studies the internal role mechanism of urban GDE, which is formed of G-Growth, G-Wealth, and G-Welfare, and builds a spatial role model based on the concept of system theory on the basis of the theoretical framework of green development.

The target, connotation, and benchmark layers add to the theoretical framework of green development. These layers cover the three dimensions of G-Wealth, G-Growth, and G-Welfare, with G-Wealth serving as the foundation, G-Growth as the means, and G-Welfare as the vision. The three are linked and coordinated due to intrinsic logical relationships between the dimensions [72]. In order to provide a material foundation, produce economic and social benefits, and guarantee G-Welfare and G-Wealth, G-Growth depends on green welfare. G-Wealth accrues as G-Welfare expands, acting as both a development constraint and a development vision [5,73]. G-Welfare is a significant symbol of well-being for human growth. G-Wealth refers to the sum of various natural factors that organisms rely on for survival and development and is the carrier of G-Welfare and the basis of G-Growth [72], G-Welfare and G-Wealth are the main directions for the development of the three systems: economic, social and natural. In the economic system, elements such as resource allocation, science and technology innovation and labour force constitute the new economy, while the social system contains elements such as financial support, employment of workers and people's well-being. The natural system is based on the five elements of “water, earth, air, life and land”, and the interaction and coupling of these elements form the benchmark for green development in the natural circle [5]. These 11 elements interact together to support green development (Fig. 1).

Thus, green development is a concept of sustainable development that integrates the three major systems of the economy, society, and environment and is based on benchmarks like labour force, living environment, and land resources. The goal is to match and adapt the elements of resources, production, consumption, and environmental carrying capacity in order to achieve the unity and coordination of ecological and environmental protection with economic and social development. Resource allocation, technological innovation, and labour force in the economic system reflect financial and human inputs respectively, which are non-resource inputs; the amount of land and water power in the natural system reflects the degree of resource depletion, which are resource inputs; and the two characterise the input elements in the process of green development; financial support, regional output value, and the living environment in Financial support, regional output value, and people's living environment reflect economic and environmental benefits, respectively, and can characterise desired outputs; pollution emissions, on the other hand, reflect the degree of natural depletion and can characterise undesirable outputs. Consequently, GDE is the extent to which a city or regional system can maximise economic output and minimise natural disturbances with certain resource inputs when achieving green development goals, and is used to reflect the efficiency of factor allocation and utilization under resource and environmental constraints. In a word, GDE pursues low consumption of material resources, high resource utilization and low negative environmental impact, and ultimately achieves coordination and unity of the “society-nature-economy” composite system to achieve maximum efficiency.

Research framework

This study chooses the YRMR as the study area, as shown in Fig. 2. This area is strategically located, linking the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei city cluster and the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao bay area in the north and south, and linking the Yangtze River Delta urban area and the Chengdu-Chongqing city cluster in the east and west, which is an essential bridge for expediting the integration of the various regions of China and performs a significant part in high-quality development, accelerating the growth of the central region and promoting the development of the YREB. In 2021, the combined economy of the Chinese city clusters situated in the YRMR is 9.7 trillion yuan, representing 8.5 % of the country's total GDP. Roughly 110 million people live there permanently, making up 7.9 % of the total population. At 326,000 square kilometres, the overall land area makes up 3.4 % of the country's total area. In addition, at the Symposium on Promoting the Rise of the Central Region in the New Era, President Xi Jinping emphasized the region's pivotal role in China as a base for food, energy and material production, modern equipment manufacturing, high-tech industries, and comprehensive transportation.

Convergence model

The neoclassical growth model postulates that the economy will eventually attain a steady state, or convergence of economic growth, as a result of the declining marginal output of capital. Currently, the study of convergence in productivity, environment and energy has become one of the hotspots. With the deepening of ecological civilization and sustainable growth, GDE is likely to converge to a certain steady state. Therefore, it is appropriate to use convergence models such as σ-convergence or β-convergence, two common forms of the model. σ-Convergence can be used to test the decreasing dynamics of regional divergence over time [78], and standard deviation or coefficient of variation is commonly used to calculate σ-convergence. The concept of β-convergence describes how cities with less effective green development develop at a somewhat rapid pace and eventually catch up to cities with more efficient development. Conditional and absolute β-convergence are the two different forms of β-convergence, and the difference between the two lies in whether other factors have been taken into account for the efficiency growth of green development.

Construction of GDE index system

Green development is based on the systemic, holistic and coordinated relationship between economy, society and nature [70]. Based on this premise and combined with the input-output model, comprehensively considering the factor inputs, resource consumption and pollution, and with reference the relevant works by Zhou [5], Che [15], and Yang [79], GDE index system shown in Table 2 was constructed. Capital, labour and resource input are selected as input factors, among which private and individual employees in urban areas are included in the number of employees in the unit at the end of the year. To characterise the economic efficiency of the desired output, real GDP with 2010 as the base period is selected, and to describe the environmental efficiency, the greenery coverage rate in built-up areas is selected; the non-desired output is chosen to represent the industrial SO2 emission and CO2 emission.

Data sources

This paper primarily uses data from the 2010–2020 China Urban Statistical Yearbook and China Statistical Yearbook; part of the data comes from the Jiangxi, Hubei, and Hunan Provincial Statistical Yearbooks and the cities' statistical bulletins, and the data unavailable to us is made up by interpolation. The main form of coal consumption is power generation, and coal is still the predominant source in China's current power generation structure [80], therefore, drawing on the study of Zhu [81]and using the least squares method to linearly fit the total consumption of electricity and CO2 emissions of city clusters in the YRMR in 2010–2019, a regression equation was obtained as follows: C = 0.0015E + 1453.4 (C is the CO2 emissions, E is the whole society's electricity consumption, R2 = 0.6111). This equation is used to calculate the missing data of carbon emissions.

Analysis of overall temporal evolution

As indicated by Table 3's results, the city cluster in the YRMR generally shows a changing trend during the study period, going down first and increasing later. The mean overall GDE is 0.709, showing that the state of green development has not yet reached an effective level, with the lowest level of green development in 2013 (0.616) and the highest value in 2020 (0.867). Looking at the three major city clusters in the study area, cluster B had the highest GDE, followed by cluster C, and the figure for cluster A is the lowest and lower than that of the city clusters in the YRMR as a whole. The mean value of the GDE of Yingtan and Changde City is greater than 1 in the period from 2010 to 2020, which means that only these two cities have reached the effective state (efficiency value at one or greater). The GDE in Changsha City and Fuzhou City has not yet achieved an effective state. However, their average GDE scores are relatively high as well, at 0.925 and 0.937, respectively. Twenty-four cities remain, with mean GDE values below 0.9 and most falling between 0.4 and 0.7.

The overall GDE exhibits a fluctuating upward trend, with a varying downward trend in 2010–2015 and a steady upward trend in 2015–2020, according to the trend chart of GDE change within the study interval. Due to the financial crisis in 2010–2015, cities in the study area engaged in a competition to boost investment to promote economic recovery and to develop low-level short-term gain projects to stimulate monetary growth. This led to massive resource consumption because of the time lag, which also caused severe environmental pollution that emerged later. This resulted in the overall GDE showing a declining trend in 2010–2015. After the State Council issued the Opinions on Accelerating the Promotion of Ecological Civilization Construction in April 2015, the regions accelerated the optimization of industrial structure, transformed traditional industries, and green and low-carbon industries. At the same time, the Development Plan for the YRMR requires efforts to push forward green, recycling, and low-carbon development. Supported by the dual policies, green development level of city clusters in the YRMR achieved remarkable results in 2015–2020, showing a steady upward trend. The GDE of the cluster A, the cluster B, and the cluster C also showed a two-stage fluctuation and upward trend, with a sharp decline trend in 2013. Among them, the GDE of the Cluster A gradually increased in 2014, while the cluster B and the cluster C showed a fluctuation trend from 2014 to 2016, and then began to show an upward trend. The cluster A was able to recover immediately after the sharp decline in GDE, mainly due to its previous role as a comprehensive reform pilot zone for the construction of a resource-saving and environmentally friendly society nationwide. However, as Wuhan, Huangshi and other old industrial bases, environmental problems caused by industrial development in the early stage were prominent, so its GDE has always been lower than the other two urban agglomerations.

Local time evolution analysis

Between the three city clusters in the study area, there are significant differences in GDE. The GDE in cluster C, cluster A, and cluster B had achieved 10.8 %, 18.8 %, and 28.3 % growth, respectively, during 2010–2020, indicating that the GDE value of cluster B had the fastest growth rate. Cluster A's green development level consistently falls short of both the entire study area and the other two city clusters. Additionally, the area's GDE value consistently falls below 0.8 within the research interval, suggesting that cluster A's overall GDE is not high and that there is still plenty of space for improvement in environmental governance and resource utilization. Between 2010 and 2015, cluster C's GDE was marginally higher than that of cluster B. However, after 2015, particularly after 2018, cluster B's GDE grew faster than that of cluster C. The long-term development and accumulation of the Cluster B are primarily responsible for the most outstanding GDE value and the highest growth rate of the area. In August 2005, the release of the "Regional Plan of Chang-Zhu-Tan city cluster" accelerated the area's economic integration, and in 2007, the city cluster became a pilot area of a comprehensive reform of the "two-type" society, and it has become an example of "two-type" society in the following years. While developing the economy, economical use of resources and environmental protection are also given a high priority. After years of research and practice in the three cities, as well as an impact from radiation on the surrounding towns, cluster B has arrived at a relatively impressive level of overall green development.

From the city level, in 2010 there were 8 cities out of the 28 in the YRMR whose GDE reached the effective state, mainly distributed in cluster C, and in 2020, this number would increase to 14, which are relatively uniformly distributed in the whole city cluster in the YRMR, with the number of cities in the three city clusters that had reached effective status being 5, 4 and 6, respectively. According to the average GDE in the city clusters during 2010–2020, only Yingtan City and Changde City have reached the effective state of GDE. This suggests that these two cities prioritize environmental protection, optimize the input-output ratio, and make sensible use of their resources during the development process. Although the city scale and economic development level of Yingtan City and Changde City are not as good as those of the central cities of the three city clusters in the study area, their results indicate that improving a city's GDE involves more than just growing the city's scale or total economic volume [82]. Huangshi city has the lowest GDE value (0.4465). As a resource-based old industrial city, the industry is the crucial part of its economy, and the kingpin for Huangshi's economic development. The GDE value of Huangshi city indicates a lack in the green transformation of its industry, and it has not yet truly realized the transformation from rough manufacturing to green manufacturing. As far as the central cities of the study area are concerned, Changsha has the highest GDE value. Although its efficiency value has not reached 1, it has been at a higher level since 2011. Wuhan's GDE value was greatly improved in 2016, from 0.553 in 2015 to 1.022, and it has stayed at a high level since then. In the time frame of the study, the GDE of Nanchang city is basically in a low or lower level, and it had not seen significant improvements until 2019.

The growth of GDE in cluster B is significantly influenced by RGDP; however, this effect is not significant for the YRMR, cluster C, or cluster A. Taken together, these data suggest that an increase in UR will inhibit the growth in GDE for the YRMR and cluster C. A possible reason is that after the export trade of the coastal area was affected by the 2008 global financial crisis, a considerable number of enterprises moved to the mainland, which caused an increase in labour demand leading to urban population increase, and the population gathered in the YRMR. The increase in urbanization level aggravated the consumption of resources and environmental pollution, and then reduced green development level in cities, which made the GDE of the study area converging to a low value. Except for cluster B, IND seriously hinders the expansion of GDE. Due to excessive energy consumption and environmental pollution, IND of city clusters in the study area lacks rationale, which leads to GDE converging at a low value. TEC has a significant positive effect on the growth of GDE in clusters A and B, indicating that the governments in these regions place a high value on scientific and technological advancement, has increased funding for this field, accelerated the development of innovative green products, improved resource utilization, decreased pollution emissions, and improved pollution management. In the end, these actions have caused the GDE to converge at a high value. Except for cluster C, LO significantly inhibits the growth of GDE. This suggests that city clusters in the YRMR are experiencing a phenomenon known as "Pollution Paradise", wherein increasing the region's degree of openness and attracting foreign investment can foster technological exchanges and economic growth between the cities, but it will also cause environmentally harmful enterprises to relocate to the local area or cause foreign investors to flow to highly polluting enterprises, ultimately resulting in the convergence of GDE to a low value.

Conclusions

The findings below are drawn from this paper's analysis of the spatio-temporal evolution as well as convergence features of the study area, which are based on the GDE between 2010 and 2020 (Fig. 11).

Graphical abstract of this paper.

Firstly, within the study area, according to the time evolution viewpoints, the GDE of city clusters in the YRMR generally exhibits a two-stage fluctuating upward tendency, with a fluctuating downward trend in 2010–2015 and a steady upward trend in 2015–2020. In this period, the green development level peaked in 2020, after reaching its lowest point in 2013. According to the spatial evolution viewpoints, the YRMR city clusters exhibit notable spatial variations in accordance with green development level, exhibiting a pattern of "high in the southwest, low in the northeast". The interregional green development of the cluster B is the most balanced, and the capital cities have played a radiation-driving role in the neighbouring cities after achieving a high-efficiency green development level. Intraregional variance is the primary cause of spatial differences in GDE in city clusters along the YRMR.

Secondly, the GML Index results indicate that the green total factor productivity of city clusters along the YRMR exhibits a fluctuating growth tendency. Furthermore, overall GDE is growing at a reasonable rate, with a significant contribution coming from technological advancements. The productivity of the green total factor varies significantly throughout city clusters. Cluster A has the highest GDE, followed by the cluster B and cluster C.

Thirdly, the YRMR and its three major city clusters exhibit both σ-convergence and β-convergence, indicating that city clusters in the YRMR have a tendency to converge to their own steady-state level. Additionally, the cluster B exhibits the largest downward trend in σ-convergence as well as the quickest rates of absolute and conditional β-convergence. Different influencing factors have various impacts on convergence features when it comes to GDE. RGDP will make the GDE of the cluster B converge to a high value; UR will make the GDE of the YRMR as a whole and cluster C converge to a low value. IND will cause the GDE to converge to a low value, except for the cluster B. In contrast, TEC will encourage the GDE of clusters A and B to converge to a high value. High LO causes the GDE to converge to low levels, with the exception of cluster C.

Discussion

Theoretical implications

This study constructs a green development theoretical framework based on the three major systems of economy, society, and nature, based on the idea of system theory. The framework covers three dimensions: G-Wealth, G-Growth, and G-Welfare. Among them, G-Wealth is the foundation, G-Growth is the means, and G-Welfare is the vision. Under the interaction of various systems, this paper believes that the inherent requirements of GDE should be low consumption of material resources, high resource utilization efficiency, and minimal negative environmental impact, ultimately achieving the coordination and unity of the "social natural economic" composite system and maximizing efficiency.

Recommendations

Firstly, the governments should expedite quality economic growth and optimize spatial layout. At present, the development of cities in the YRMR is remarkably different. Meanwhile, the central cities are higher than the non-central cities in terms of GDE. Therefore, in order to form a metropolitan area, it makes sense to start with the central cities and work the way outward to the peripheral cities, optimize the resource allocation and industry-focused development of the metropolitan area, and rely on the central cities to lead the development of city clusters in the YRMR, which is a mechanism to support the coordinated development of the whole region. For the cluster A with low efficiency in green development, it is necessary to fully implement the requirements of "two types" social construction, achieve coordinated development of economy, resources, and environment. The "14th Five-Year Plan for the Development of City Clusters in the YRMR" will be actively implemented to tackle ecological obstacles while accomplishing high-level economic growth, as well as to fully achieve the goals of carbon peak performance and carbon neutrality in the YRMR.

Secondly, scientific and technology innovation capabilities should be increased, and industrial modernization and transformation be promoted. Wuhan East Lake, Chang-Zhu-Tan, and Poyang Lake as national autonomous innovation demonstration zones should actively integrate the innovation resources available in the area, increase expenditure on science and technology innovation, as well as concentrate on building scientific and technological infrastructures, technological and scientific advancement platforms, and important laboratories in their capacity. The innovation outcomes will be used to transform secondary and tertiary industries, especially for the old industrial cities in the cluster A and the resource-based cities in the cluster C, due to the early development requirements of the old industrial cities and resource-based cities, their economic and ecological coordination is insufficient, and their GDE is lower than that of the cluster B. Therefore, it is necessary to apply technological achievements to the transformation of high consumption industries, promote the formation of a green industry system, narrow the gap in green development efficiency between cities, and quickly achieve a steady-state level of GDE in the YRMR.

Thirdly, the administrations need to improve the urban and rural development system, and introduce high-quality foreign-funded businesses. Due to the increase in urbanization rate, the GDE of city clusters in the YRMR converges to a low value, and this effect is more significant for the Poyang Lake urban agglomeration. Therefore, the transfer of city clusters should avoid destroying the regional ecological environment. The government needs to predict and control the highest population that can be accommodated in the urban ecological environment, develop small towns with unique geographic advantages and rich resource conditions in accordance with local circumstances, and support the transfer of rural populations to small towns with sufficient infrastructure and public services. Simultaneously, in trade transactions, it is necessary to introduce high-quality foreign investment to promote green trade [83], and reduce openness to the outside world, which makes the GDE converge to a low value.

Limitations and future work

The research results of this article on the GDE in the YRMR are consistent with those obtained by Lei Zou et al. [50]. The GDE of the YRMR shows a trend of first decreasing and then steadily increasing, while the GDE of the cluster A is lower than the other two urban agglomerations. Compared to previous research, the study closely combines the background of "dual carbon" and the requirements of sustainable development to further explore its dynamic evolution, regional differences, and convergence in terms of research content. But there are certain limitations in the research, and further analysis is needed in the future. Restricted by the availability of data, this paper only covers nearly 11 years of time, and the choice of spatial regions was made using only prefecture-level cities for comparison. In constructing assessment system for the green growth of city clusters in the YRMR, the non-expected outputs are only stated as two indicators, namely, industrial and carbon emissions, and there are still certain limitations and deficiencies. In addition, the GML index only elaborates on the contribution of one of the two factors to the growth of GDE, and qualitatively analyzes possible ways to improve technological progress or efficiency, without further analyzing the specific technological innovations or utilization that will have a greater impact on the growth of GDE. In the future, the scientific improvement of GDE in the YRMR requires multi-scale and step-by-step progress. With the help of factor endowment advantages, diversified development methods can be adopted to form a rational regional division of labour system, which can effectively change the GDE and convergence characteristics of city clusters.

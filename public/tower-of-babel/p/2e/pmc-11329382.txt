RETRACTED ARTICLE: Processing of Counterfactual Conditional Sentences with Differential Propositional Truth-Value in Mandarin Chinese: Evidence from ERP
Authors: Hulin Ren, Xuesong Li, Ying Li, Xianglan Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough many studies document the role of propositional truth-value in human psychological reading behavior, there is a relative paucity of research examining the role of differential propositional truth-value in processing Chinese counterfactual conditionals. This study is to investigate the role of differential propositional value in processing Chinese counterfactual conditionals by means of ERPs (event-related potentials). The study is based on comprehending two types of Chinese counterfactual conditionals, which is propositional truth value introduced by two different markers of conditional conjunctions in the protasis and apodosis, such as true counterfactual conditional markers jiaru (if) & jiu (so) in the sentence wo xiang yu jiaru you tui jiu keyi zai shuixia zhixi (I think if fish had legs so they could stifle under water), and false counterfactual conditional markers ruguo (if) & namo (then) in the sentence wo xiang gou ruguo you lin namo keyi zai shuixia huxi (I think if dogs had scales, then they could breathe under water). Two counterfactual propositional values (i.e. true and false propositional values) are constructed through manipulating sentence counterfactuality between the true and false counterfactual conditional markers in the protasis and the apodosis. Twenty-four full-time Chinese college students participated in the ERP study. The results demonstrated that processing the true counterfactual propositional sentences with conditional markers jiaru (if) & jiu (so) elicited the N400 effect relative to false propositional sentences with conditional markers ruguo (if) & namo (then). Moreover, the counterfactual sentences with true propositional conditions varied from the elicitation of the N400 effect in the protasis and absence of the N400 effect in the apodosis, showing that semantic roles may gradually disappear under the impact of truth value of propositional counterfactual condition, and/or the roles of semantic anomaly was eliminated in the accumulated sentence processing. While for the false counterfactual conditional sentences, elicitations of P300 in the protasis and robust N400 effect in the apodosis were shown, indicating the increasing semantic role in the processing. Interestingly, there was the absence of the P600 effect for processing sentences with syntactic violation, suggesting little extra syntactic cost in processing sentences with false propositional condition.Keywords: Counterfactual conditionals, Propositional truth-value, P300, N400, P600, ERP

Introduction

Counterfactual conditionals involve literally conditionals that are on the contrary to facts (Akatsuka, 1985), which syntactically come with inferences that their protasis and their apodosis are not true in the actual world, mainly consisting of five types: sentences using optative verbs, metaphorical sentences expressing similarity, negative sentences, sentences using verbs intended to prevent someone or something and counterfactual conditionals (Au, 1983; Ferguson & Cane, 2015). As a classic structure of expressing counterfactual thoughts, the processing of counterfactual conditional sentences has received considerable attention recently, it is essential to detect the processing mechanism of counterfactual conditionals themselves.

In Chinese, counterfactual conditionals are typically taken as a subcategory of if-conditionals, as the Chinese language is featured by non-inflection with no morphological markers to express counterfactuality (i.e., contrary to the facts) (Dancygier, 2002). The expression of Chinese counterfactuality depends on counterfactual ingredients and contextual information, propositional truth value in Chinese sentences introduced by two different markers of conditional conjunctions in the protasis and apodosis, such as true counterfactual conditional markers jiaru (if) & jiu (so) in Chinese sentence wo xiang yu jiaru you tui jiu keyi zai shuixia zhixi (I think if fish had legs so they could stifle under water) will provide further inspection of whether these features have any effects on the processing of Chinese counterfactual conditionals.

Due to a lack of syntactic features of counterfactual conditionals in Chinese (Chou, 2000), numerous counterfactual studies are interested in looking at how diverse counterfactualities are expressed and how the formal identification of diverse existing markers in Chinese is used in counterfactuality, aiming to clarify diverse counterfactuals and put the counterfactual conditionals into the right places in the whole conditional system (Jiang, 2000; Ren, et al., 2018; Wang, 1985; Yuan, 2015). Wang (1985) takes sentences in Dream of the Red Chamber (one of China’s four great classic novels) as an example to propose that protasis conditional expressions are most likely referring to unrealized facts and converse of accomplished facts, which means that things would not have been what they appear to be under a totally different condition (e.g., true or false condition). Lu (1982) makes a comparison between conditionals and counterfactuals, and concludes that conditionals ordinarily mean that things are probably to be realized (unknown, mostly things of the future), and counterfactuals are concerning things contrary to facts and/or the established common sense. Xing (1985) explicitly proposes counterfactual conditional as an independent category of conditionals, taking that the counterfactual conditional is different from other types of conditionals to express contrary-to-fact prediction and is related to corresponding results. According to Yuan (2015), counterfactuals is taken as unreal hypothetical conditionals, whose premises should be closed and/or marked and can only be false, if taken together with real hypothetical conditionals of open and neutral premises with either true or false value, they are subject to the category of hypothetical conditionals.

Some scholars put forward criteria for measuring a valid counterfactual in Chinese (Li, 2010; Wang, 2010; Yong, 2015). Cao (2002) takes the counterfactual conditionals as the conditionals which meet the following three requirements:1 the antecedent clause (i.e., protasis) is contrary to the fact or have little possibility to be realized, the protasis and the apodosis are closely connected in semantics, and if the antecedent clause is satisfied (may be hypothetical), the subsequent clause (i.e., apodosis) is most likely to happen. Li (2003) maintains that it is necessary to ensure the falsity of both apodosis and protasis in Chinese to understand the nature of counterfactuals. However, there are relatively few studies on the role of propositional truth-value in the process of counterfactual conditionals in Chinese sentences, as the truth-value of counterfactual conditionals depends on the truth-value of the main clause consequent (Yang, 2015). Nieuwland and Martin (2012) investigated the role of propositional truth-value in the comprehension of counterfactual conditionals, concluding that propositional truth-value rapidly impacts ongoing semantic processing in spite of the proposition factual or counterfactual.

Dai et al. (2021) used ERP technology to investigate the impact of counterfactual transparency in Chinese conditional sentences on semantic processing of post sentence information. Counterfactual transparency in their study refers to the degree that readers are able to identify the situation described by the false statement opposite to reality. Dai et al. (2021) argue that given different degrees of unreality in a context, native Chinese speakers can quickly integrate subsequent information into this context. However, due to the observation that only factual inconsistency triggered a larger ERP component than factual consistency, the study did not set causal conditional sentences as baseline conditions, it can only be inferred that the dual meaning representation in counterfactual conditional sentences was activated in the initial stage. However, as the constraint of contextual information increases, readers suppress hypothetical representations and use factual meaning representations as the basis for processing subsequent semantic information. Therefore, this study does not provide direct evidence for the existence of dual representation and its activation time process in Chinese counterfactual conditional sentences, which provides rationales and predictions for the current study. This study is to examine the modulation of differential propositional truth value in processing counterfactual conditionals of Chinese sentences in apodosis and protasis, as well as the underlying processing mechanisms in the process.

Materials and Methods

The study is to examine the role of propositional value in modulating the processing of counterfactual conditional sentences under true and false conditions in Mandarin Chinese. For this purpose, the study is focused on performative counterfactual conditionals of two Chinese propositional markers, which can help better understanding of the processing mechanisms of counterfactual conditional sentences. Two questions are to be addressed: (1) are there representational differences in the processing of Chinese sentences with propositional value under true (factual) and false (counterfactual) conditions? (2) does the neuro-cognitive processing mechanism of these Chinese sentences involve lexical and semantic clues indexed by N400 or only syntactic clues closely related to P600?

Based on the previous findings (Dai et al., 2021; Ren et al., 2018), our study hypothesized that: there are striking differences under two conditions between the processing of Chinese counterfactual conditional sentences with markers jiaru (if) & jiu (so) and ruguo (if) & namo (then). As there are differences between markers jiaru (if) & jiu (so) and ruguo (if) & namo (then) in Chinese, Chinese sentences with the two different markers may cause different results in sentence comprehension and /or judgments, and thus may have different effects.

There is only an overt N400 effect observed in the process of the false counterfactual conditional sentences with semantic violation due to the false propositional value. The P300 effect for processing counterfactual-true antecedents can be observed as a result of a successful match between the expectation and the critical words. LAN (left anterior negative) might also be elicited for the increased syntactic working memory demands by uncanonical sentence structure.

As for processing the critical words in sentences, it was hypothesized that the N400 effect for counterfactual-false sentences would disappear under the impact of counterfactual conditions; incoming words might be well mapped without any delay into the sentence context. As for the false propositional conditional sentences, the effect of propositional truth-value may not be found due to a relatively high rating of the semantic anomaly.

All experimental protocols were approved by University Committee of Science and Technology Beijing. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects in the experiments. Twenty-four healthy students from three majors (i.e. law, business and history) in the University of Science and Technology Beijing (USTB) took part in the ERP experiment (14 female and 10 males with an average age of 22.46 years, SD = 2.43). They were all native Mandarin Chinese speakers, right-handed with a normal or corrected-to-normal vision, and with no neurological, psychiatric or cognitive disorders. None of them knew the purpose of the study and had not participated in any tests associated with the study.

Rationales of selecting propositional truth value markers are that, on one hand, they are frequently used to express causal relationship between the protasis and the apodosis to generate the core notion of counterfactuality in the Chinese language. On the other hand, the different markers easily illustrate why the same functional makers can be employed to have different types of propositions. Furthermore, the makers can denote a speaker’s interference and judgement of the possibility of a propositional situation. As for the selection of the key nouns (e.g. lungs and gills) and verbs (e.g. breathe and stifle) in the experimental sentences, rationales are that they are frequently used to express higher truth values of counterfactual proposition in Chinese (Li, 2010; Yang, 2015). It is confirmed that all methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

There were 144 experimental sentences selected as stimuli material. These sentences were assigned to 4 experimental blocks using a Latin-square design. Each block consists of 36 sentences under one condition. The sentences in each block were pseudo-randomized with the restriction of no more than three consecutive sentences under the same condition and no more than five consecutive counterfactual false sentences. The four sentence blocks were classified by counterfactual conditionals with different markers, e.g. jiaru (if) & jiu (so) or ruguo(if) & namo (then).

The counterfactual propositional conditions were manipulated in accordance with truth-value of sentence judgement; sentences with counterfactual propositional truth value were marked with conditional conjunction jiaru (if) & jiu (so) and ruguo (if) & namo (then), and false value sentences were marked with ruguo (if) & namo (then) and jiaru (if) & jiu (so). Thus, the results of the four conditions are illustrated in (1) and (2) below. A subset of sentences was employed as critical stimuli from a rating survey. There were 144 Chinese sentences altogether; each sentence is made up of 4 versions with the same structure in which the critical word was a noun and/or verb. By means of a Latin-square design, these sentences were assigned to 4 lists, each participant can only see the list of 144 critical sentences with 36 sentences in each condition. In addition, all selected sentences in each list were pseudo-randomized to ensure that no more than five sentences were semantically correct or syntactically incorrect, and consecutive sentences in the same condition did not exceed three sentences.

In the course of the electrocephalogram (EEG) recording session, subjects were instructed to sit in a dimly lit, sound-attenuated chamber with the distance of 1 m away from a CRT display. After attentively reading each sentence, they were asked to answer whether the sentence is counterfactual or not, and then make a judgement of counterfactuality of the sentences based on their intuitions with 7 ratings, where 1 is the least counterfactual rating and 7 is the most counterfactual rating. All experimental sentences were displayed segment-by-segment (black Chinese characters on a light grey screen) in a rapid serial visual presentation paradigm, with nine segments of each sentence and the critical words always appearing independently, as illustrated above in (1) and (2).

Every trial began with a fixation cross in the screen centre for 600 ms, followed by a 600 ms blank screen. Then each sentence segment displayed for 600 ms with an additional 200-ms inter-stimulus interval (ISI). The last word in each sentence appeared with a period and afterwards, question marks (“???”) were followed with a 1000-ms duration and a 2000-ms blank screen. To inform subjects of making a judgement about the counterfactuality of the sentence they had just seen, the marks are employed as a cue by pressing the yes or no button on a joystick. For elimination of the extra variate, each participant was assigned with the counterbalanced response buttons. Before the formal test, there were 16 practice trials proceeding 144 critical sentences with 2 blocks. Subjects may have a short break after the practice. The whole experimental session was one and a half hours, including electrode preparation and removal.

The EEG was collected from 64 Ag/AgC1 electrodes (AF7, AF3, FP1, FPZ, FP2, AF4, AF8, F7, F5, F3, F1, Fz, F2, F4, F6, F8, FT7, FC5, FC3, FC1, FC2, FC4, FC6, FT8, T7, C5, C3, C1, CZ, C2, C4, C6, T8, TP9, TP7, CP5, CP3, CP1, CPZ, CP2, CP4, CP6, TP8, TP10, P7, P5, P3, P1, PZ, P2, P4, P6, P8, PO7, PO5, PO3, POZ, PO4, PO6, PO8, O1, Oz, O2) that were mounted in an elastic cap (EASYCAP GmbH, Germany). The EEG data were referenced online to FCZ and re-referenced offline to the arithmetic average of the left and right mastoids (TP9 and TP10) as well. Furthermore, the vertical electro-oculogram (VEOG) and horizontal electro-oculogram (HEOG) were also controlled respectively from electrodes located below the right eye and at the outer canthus of the left eye. Electrode impedances were below 5 kΩ. The EEG and EOG were amplified with a bandpass filter from 0.016 to 70 Hz.

Results

The datasets analyzed during the current study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. The EEGs were processed offline by means of the Analyzer 2.2 software (Brain Products GmbH, Germany). Besides, trials exceeded a peak-to-peak threshold of 70 µV were excluded from data analysis. The subjects’ ERPs were calculated by every electrode over an epoch from 200 ms before to 800 ms after the onset of the critical words (i.e., object noun phrase in the protasis and object manner noun phrase in the apodosis). The baseline correction was set to 200 ms pre-stimulus interval.

In accordance with the study hypotheses and the obtained data of the ERP waveforms, the 350-550 ms time window followed by the onset of processing the critical words (i.e., sentence-final segments) with the N400 effect was examined. Statistical analysis was carried out by computing the mean ERP amplitudes. Two topographic dimensions-hemisphere (three levels: left, medial and right) and region (five levels: frontal, frontal-central, central, centro-parietal and parietal)-the scalp electrodes were divided into 15 regions of interest (ROI) with three electrodes: left frontal (F3, F5, F7), left frontal-central (FC3, FC5, FT7), left central (C3, C5, T7), left centro-parietal (CP3, CP5, TP7), left parietal (P3, P5, P7), medial frontal (F1, FZ, F2), medial frontal-central (FC1, FCZ, FC2), medial central (C1, CZ, C2), medial centro-parietal (CP1, CPZ, CP2), medial parietal (P1, PZ, P2), right frontal (F4, F6, F8), right frontal-central (FC4, FC6, FT8), right central (C4, C6, T8), right centro-parietal (CP4, CP6, TP8), and right parietal (P4, P6, P8). The mean amplitudes over the three electrodes in each ROI (region of interest) were taken into data analysis. The experimental factors of propositional truth value (true and false) were crossed with the two topographic factors (i.e., hemisphere and region) in repeated measures ANOVA over the average amplitudes. The Greenhouse–Geisser correction was used to in evaluate effects with more than one degree of freedom in the numerator. In so doing, we can obtain the incorrected degrees of freedom, the corrected probability value and the correction factorε. As for pairwise comparisons, the probability value was Bonferroni-adjusted.

Repeated-measures of ANOVA with counterfactuality (with different markers) and propositional truth value as within-subject factors were analyzed, which found that there was a large main effect of counterfactuality (F(1,23) = 12.72, p = 0.002), and a large main effect of truth-value (F(1,23) = 14.23, p < 0.001), as well as a large interaction effect between counterfactuality and truth-value (F(1,23) = 1728.66, p < 0.001). In the separate analysis, for those propositional true conditionals, there were large differences in the counterfactuality judgements between true value sentences and false value sentences, with the former scoring higher than the latter (p < 0.001), While for propositional false conditionals, the differences lie in that true propositional sentences were scored lower than false ones in terms of the truth value, and there were large differences between them (p < 0.001).

The behavioral results testified that big differences existed in average scores of counterfactuality between two experimental conditions, and there were significant average differences between true and false propositional sentences (p < 0.001), with the former scoring more than the latter; but there were no significant score differences between them (p > 0.001), suggesting both counterfactual-false propositions and counterfactual-true propositions negatively affect the processing of Chinese sentences.

The average amplitudes were entered into repeated measures ANOVA with counterfactuality (proposition with truth-value or false-value), hemisphere which includes left level, medial level and right level, as well as regions involving the frontal level, front-central level, central level, centro-parietal level, and parietal level as within-subject factors.

As shown in Fig. 1, widespread N400 effect of truth-value was shown in the sentence process, maximal value was at right parietal sites, regardless of counterfactuality. Processing critical words that rendered an antecedent false elicited bigger (more negative) N400s compared to processing words rendering an antecedent true [20]. This was supported by a main effect of truth-value in the omnibus ANOVA analysis, F(1, 23) = 27.31, p < 0.001. Critically, we also observed a robust truth-value by hemisphere interaction, F(2, 46) = 5.52, p = 0.007,ε = 0.983, and a significant three-way interaction between proposition truth-value, F(8, 184) = 2.44, p = 0.035,ε = 0.664. Further analysis of both interaction effects by truth-value showed that false antecedents elicited significantly larger N400s compared to true conditional clauses, with an average difference of − 1.08 µV, p < 0.001. As shown in the topography of the ROIs in Fig. 1, the N400 effect of propositional truth-value occurring in the conditions of counterfactual-false were primarily distributed over medial and right central regions. Furthermore, it is worth noting that there was an anterior scalp distributed positivity for counterfactual-true sentences relative to counterfactual-false sentences with an instance of P300, as it has a similar time-course; approximately peaking at around 300 ms.

Figure 2 shows that the process of critical words in the consequent sentences elicited more negative N400s in counterfactual propositional true sentences and were observed at most electrodes relative to propositional false sentences. Strikingly, the process of counterfactual-false sentences elicited N400s followed by elicitation of false sentences at most electrodes, but elicited only slightly increased N400s relative to counterfactual-true sentences at posterior electrodes. The repeated measures ANOVAs confirmed the significant interaction effect of counterfactuality by truth-value, F(1, 23) = 21.20, p < 0.001. A follow-up pairwise comparison revealed that between 350 and 550 ms, counterfactual-false conditionals significantly elicited less negative ERPs than counterfactual-true conditionals, with an average difference of 0.67 µV, p = 0.052. Moreover, within the same time window, there was significantly more N400 effect shown in counterfactual-false sentences than those in counterfactual-true sentences with an average difference of − 1.11 µV, p < 0.001. The effect of propositional truth-value was not different in counterfactual-true sentences and counterfactual-false sentences; the mean differences were 0.51 µV. An interesting result is that there was a slight difference in the process of counterfactual-false sentences (− 0.44 µV) with different markers.

Discussion

This ERP experiment examined the neuro-cognitive representation underlying the processing of counterfactual conditionals varying in truth-value in Mandarin Chinese. Since there is slight difference in the P600 time range observed, and the LAN effect was not observed as well, therefore no discussion of P600 and LAN effect was made in this paper. Based on orthographic forms, it seems that Chinese conditional markers jieru (if) & jiu (so) only involve morphosyntactic processing. The ERP results gave support to the hypothesis, particularly by the patterns of the critical words at the four experiment conditions. In order to avoid the role of frequency in the ERP pattern, especially the N400 that indexes the lexical and semantic role in processing, sentences with different conditional markers were analyzed separately. For jiaru (if) & jiu (so), the N400 effect of truth-value for the counterfactual-false condition has been found for the critical word in the protasis regardless of its counterfactuality. The widely distributed N400 effect for counterfactual-true condition relative to factual-false condition was observed, but relatively weaker N400 effect for counterfactual-false has been observed. One interesting finding is that, although we focused on N400 effect, our data evidenced the P300 effect at counterfactual-true condition, as can be seen from “ERP Analyses” section.

As shown in Table 1,2 the critical word time-locked to the protasis, when words like fei (lungs) and huxi (breathe) co-exist with the conditional markers jiaru (if) & yu (fish) in one counterfactual sentence, the protasis is acceptable and assumed to be factual; and when words like yu (fish) co-exist with the markers ruguo (if) & tui (leg), the protasis is unacceptable and thus counterfactual. For words like gou (dog), when occurring with the markers jiaru (if) & sai (gill) in one counterfactual sentence, the protasis is acceptable and assumed to be factual; when occurring with the counterfactual markers ruguo (if) & lin (scale), it is acceptable and assumed to be factual. The results have been confirmed by counterfactuality ratings and propositional truth-value judgement.

As clearly shown in Fig. 1, critical words like fei (lungs) and/or yu (fish) compared in acceptable counterfactual-true and counterfactual-false unacceptable conditions, some trends of positivity waves have been found in 350–550 ms time windows, and this effect was located primarily on the anterior parietal sites on the left scalp. The positivity might be interpreted in terms of occurring in the same time window as N400, as P300 whose appearance is due to a positive shift peaking in the N400 time window elicited by highly expected words in collocation constructions and partially overlapping with the N400 component. This positive peak as a P300 component led to the superficial impression of a reduced N400 (Molinaro & Carreiras, 2010; Deacon, 2004; Margaret, et al., 2012), suggesting that there is an appropriate match between the supposed input and the actual input in the processing. Co-occurrence of yu (fish), fei (lung) and the counterfactual marker jiaru (if) triggers a clear and unambiguous expectation set up by the sentences in Table 1. Furthermore, the presence of the marker jiaru (if) pre-activated a contrary-to-fact scenario which constrained to develop the lexical expectation of the critical words like huxi (breathe), making the antecedent true. The difference of the P300 effect and N400 effect lies in that the P300 component with correct identification of the supposed word does not have a lexical search.

Whereas, for critical words like sai (gills) and/or gou (dog) in unacceptable counterfactual-false conditions, as shown in Fig. 2, the N400 was observed in 350-550 ms. To be specific, the negative-going N400 effect was observed mainly in the right parietal sites, suggesting the lower expectancy for the word in the false antecedent at counterfactual condition, relative to the true antecedent introduced by yu (fish). The ERP results show a clear N400 for content-words which rendered the sentence false in comparison to those which rendered truth value. Words like lin (scale) were semantically incongruent with preceding context like gou (dog), and as such no contextual facilitation of the lexical entry could be initialized in the process (Kulakova, 2016; Kutas & Federmeier, 2011; Liu, 1985). The N400 indicates the increased cost of the lexico-semantic access of incongruent relative to congruent critical words.

Regarding the processing of the critical words in the apodosis, truth value proposition with conditional connectives is acceptable and false proposition with hypothetical connective is unacceptable, which has been confirmed in the (acceptable) counterfactuality ratings.

For Chinese counterfactual sentences with the conditional markers ruguo (if) & namo (then) under false conditions, as shown in Fig. 2, the N400 effect was evoked for counterfactual-false sentences relative to the true ones. There were significant negative waves in the 350–550 ms time window mainly distributed on the central-posterior, slightly at the left-lateralized site. Because of actuality of the counterfactual conditionals, semantic information is clear and is directly based on the subjects’ common knowledge. Semantic violations of the counterfactual-false sentences are straight-forward. Considering the assumption that factual sentences can realize mappings in the base system, reality system and without the need for transformation (Chilton, 2010, 2014). According to the logic, it is reasonable that counterfactual sentences need to be transformed. Thus, when subjects saw the Chinese critical word zhixi (stifle) in the sentence (2d) Yu ruguo you fei namo keyi zai shuixia zhixi (Fish has lung, then can stifle under water), the N400 effect was shown in processing the critical word, signifying that there are semantic violations in the course of the process.

For Chinese counterfactual sentences with the hypothetical subordinator jiaru (if) under true and false conditions, slightly different N400 effect has been shown for the conditions in the process, the former being a robust central-posterior distributed N400 component while the latter being a weak central distributed N400 component. In the case of processing counterfactual-true sentences like (1a) Yu jiaru you fei (If fish has lung) and jiu keyi zai shuixia huxi (so it can breathe under water) co-occur with the hypothetical markers jiaru (if) & jiu (so), N400 was observed, indicating that an anomaly was detected between the semantics of jiaru (if) & the proposition Yu you fei (fish has lung). Within the counterfactual false context, there is a semantic violation when the critical word which renders the whole proposition completely false (Nieuwland, ).

Interestingly, it was observed that processing counterfactual-true sentences and factual-false sentences elicited a similar N400 effect. Strikingly, processing counterfactual-false sentences elicited weak centro-distributed N400 effects relative to that of counterfactual-true sentences. Moreover, the N400 effect was weaker than that of counterfactual-true and counterfactual-false sentences in the course of the process. The result that processing counterfactual-false sentences did not cause any visible semantic processing cost upon encountering the critical words in the consequents over counterfactual-true sentences, suggesting that the counterfactual context completely eliminated the interpretive problems caused by the semantic anomaly (Kulakova et al., 2013; Roehm et al., 2007). Consequently, incoming words can be mapped without any delay onto the interpretive context.

The results indicate that sentences describing impossible events, on the condition that counterfactual consequences are sufficiently acceptable, their semantic processing is impacted by propositional truth-value without observable delay or disruption. The present finding supports that people map incoming utterances effortlessly onto what they assume relevant, which further confirms contextual constraints guiding expectations about upcoming clues in sentences.

P300 and N400 effects were shown in the protases and widespread N400 effect was sho in the apodoses. Specifically, when it comes to processing the first critical word in the antecedents, a propositional truth-value modulated widespread N400 effect was shown regardless of counterfactuality,; namely, there is an N400 effect for both counterfactual-true and counterfactual-false comprehension, indicating a clear semantic violation in the course of the counterfactual processing. A positive P300 effect was shown for processing counterfactual-true sentences, showing that there is an appropriate match between the predicted and the actual clues in the process. However, the LAN effect is not shown in the process, and increased demands for working memory in syntactic and semantic processing (Juffs, 2015) were not supported as well. The reason behind this may be ascribed to the fact that Chinese is a non-inflectional language, which did not involve any syntactic processing costs (Chen, 1988; Lv, 1982; Ren, et al., 2018).

As for processing the critical words in the apodoses, a widespread N400 effect was observed for counterfactual-true conditions relative to counterfactual-false sentences with weaker N400 effect for counterfactual-false sentences. Interestingly, for consequent counterfactual-false sentences, following the counterfactual antecedents where the N400 was observed for a semantic anomaly in the process, there was a much weaker N400 effect observed in the 350–550 ms time window, and no significant differences were found between counterfactual-false sentences and counterfactual-true sentences. This result indicates that counterfactuality and suppositional meaning are constructed at the same time, which might be due to cue information structure (Lee, et al., 2020), that is, the non-inflection features of Mandarin Chinese not affecting comprehension of sentences at counterfactual condition. Among which, these non-inflection features are little converted into hierarchical structure for sentence comprehension (Hopp & Grüter, 2023; Shan & Lin, 2023).

Conclusions

The study examines propositional truth-value in neuro-cognitive mechanisms underlying counterfactual conditional processing in Chinese sentences by means of ERP experiments. In processing counterfactual true sentences with jiaru (if) & jiu (so) under the counterfactual-false hypothesis condition, the process of the critical word in the protasis which rendered the antecedent false elicited the robust negative-going N400 effect, resulting from the semantic anomaly; while the process of critical words in the apodosis which rendered the consequent false also elicited the strong N400 effect, indicating that semantic violations still function in processing the consequent sentences. Whereas for counterfactual false sentences with ruguo (if) & namo (then) under the counterfactual-true condition, the process of the critical words in the protasis which rendered the antecedent true elicited a positive-going P300 component, which was brought about by the matched prediction of the critical word with the preceding sentence context; processing critical words in the apodosis which rendered the consequent truth-value elicited a strong N400 effect, showing that when a true proposition co-occurred with jiaru (if) & namo (then), relevance to expectancy may arise in the antecedent, but the semantic violation exhibits the influence of the counterfactual context in the consequent sentences. The N400 effect for apodosis in counterfactual-true sentences was observed, which is much similar to that in the processing of factual-false sentences. An interesting finding for counterfactual sentences is that under the counterfactual-false condition, there is N400 effect for processing the critical word in the antecedent which rendered it false, this might be resulted from semantic incongruence kept in memory. However, in the course of processing the consequent sentences, there is absence of N400 effect overlapping with counterfactual-true sentences, suggesting that counterfactuality completely eliminated the interpretive problems posed by the semantic anomaly; the counterfactuality and suppositional meaning structured simultaneously in the brain.

In the processing of antecedents of Chinese counterfactuals, an N400 effect was shown in response to semantic violation as a result of common knowledge stored in memory. Positive-going P300 effect was shown for counterfactual-true antecedents as a result of an appropriate match between the semantic expectation and the coming of the critical words. However, LAN was not shown in the process, which might be a result of the fact that Chinese does not have verb inflection, thus do not involve any syntactic processing costs. In processing the critical word in consequent sentences, an N400 effect was shown to disappear gradually under the influence of counterfactual context, showing the interpretive problems posed by the semantic anomaly were eliminated. Thus, incoming words can be matched without any delay onto the interpretive context just as counterfactual-true sentences do.

Huangjing Jiangzhi Granules Ameliorate Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease by Activating the FOXA1-DERL1 Regulatory Axis to Suppress Lipid Accumulation, Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress, and Apoptosis
Authors: Dongyu Xie, Yunqing Wu, Jiawei Jiang, Lu Xu, Xueqin Sun, Jun Ouyang, Biao Xi, Dongliang Zhang, Haina Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is an emerging health issue worldwide. It involves intrahepatic lipid accumulation that is potentially deleterious. Traditional Chinese Medicine called Huangjing Jiangzhi (HJJZ) granules holds promise for NAFLD treatment. This study explored the mechanism by which HJJZ granules improve NAFLD in male C57BL/6 mice and oleic acid-induced HepG2 cells.H&E staining, Oil Red O staining, the TUNEL assay, and Western blotting were employed. Chromatin immunoprecipitation and dual-luciferase reporter assays were used to examine transcriptional regulation.HJJZ granules alleviated hepatic steatosis, reduced serum ALT/AST levels, improved lipid profiles, suppressed hepatocyte apoptosis, and inhibited the PERK-eIF2α-CHOP pathway in high-fat diet-fed mice and oleic acid-treated cells. These granules specifically upregulated the expression of the transcription factor FOXA1. FOXA1 knockdown abolished all protective effects of HJJZ granules. Mechanistically, FOXA1 was shown to transcriptionally activate DERL1 expression. DERL1 overexpression rescued the steatotic, apoptotic, and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress phenotypes in FOXA1-deficient cells.HJJZ granules ameliorate NAFLD by attenuating lipid accumulation, apoptosis, and ER stress. This protective effect is mediated through FOXA1 upregulation, which, in turn, transcriptionally enhances DERL1 expression, thereby mitigating ER stress. Background

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a spectrum of diseases ranging from simple hepatic steatosis to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, cirrhosis, and, in some cases, hepatocellular carcinoma, where excessive lipid accumulation, hepatocellular injury, and apoptosis unfold into complex interplays [1, 2]. It represents a prevalent public health concern, currently affecting approximately 30% of the global population [3]. Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress has also been identified as an important pathophysiological link in this context, where exhaustion of such homeostasis eventually leads to Unfolded Protein Response (UPR) by persistent excess nutrients [4, 5]. PERK-eIF2α-CHOP signaling, a major signaling branch of this response, is activated and drives inflammatory responses favoring apoptosis, leading to the further vicious cycle of the disease [6]. The ER-associated degradation (ERAD) component known as DERL1 is involved in the ER stress response via the retrotranslocation of misfolded proteins from the ER into the cytosol [7, 8].

Traditional Chinese herbal formulations have been extensively studied and proven to provide cardiovascular benefits in metabolic disorders [9]. Due to their flexibility, which enables them to act as them as support for a multi-target therapeutic approach to complex metabolic disorders, Traditional Chinese herbal formulations are an area of extensive research. Here, we sought to investigate the hitherto unexplored mechanisms underlying the effects of a Traditional Chinese herbal formulation, Huangjing Jiangzhi (HJJZ) granules, against NAFLD, seeking specifically to discover its impact in alleviating ER stress and hepatocyte apoptosis to elucidate its therapeutic versatility.

The Foxa subfamily of winged helix/forkhead box (FOX) transcription activators consists of three members (FOXA1, FOXA2, and FOXA3) that critically regulate metabolism and homeostasis by modulating multiple target genes in pancreatic, adipose, and hepatic tissue [10]. FOXA1 was downregulated in animal models of NAFLD and in human nonalcoholic fatty livers [10] and alleviated NAFLD development by regulating Sirt6 and FABP1 transcription [11, 12]. FOXA2 ameliorated hepatic steatosis and alleviated NAFLD by promoting CPT2 transcription [13] and NF-κB/IKK signaling pathway activation [14]. In addition, FOXA3 was identified as the molecular bridge between ER stress and NAFLD progression via the regulation of Period1 transcription and lipid synthesis [15]. However, whether FOXA members regulate ER stress and apoptosis in NAFLD and are involved in the anti-steatosis effect of HJJZ granules remains unexplored.

Emphasis was placed on revealing how HJJZ granules ameliorate high-fat diet (HFD)-induced NAFLD at the molecular level. Specifically, authors found that HJJZ granule treatment boosted the expression of FOXA1, the transcription factor that activates DERL1 expression, resulting in the inhibition of the PERK-eIF2α-CHOP signaling pathway and the alleviation of the disorder of lipid metabolism and apoptosis of hepatocytes and liver injury. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to reveal and decipher the therapeutic mechanism of HJJZ granules via the FOXA1-DERL1 regulatory axis; thus, it identifies potential therapeutic targets and theoretical bases for further pharmacological agents for NAFLD.

Materials and Methods

HJJZ Granule Composition

HJJZ granules were produced by Zhenjiang Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The compounds of HJJZ, including 12.1 g processed Heshouwu (Radix Polygoni Multiflori), 12.1 g Shanzha (Fructus Crataegus Pinnatifidae), 9 g Danshen (Radix Salviae Miltiorrhizae), 6 g Yujin (Radix Curcumae Wenyujin), 9 g Huzhanggen (Radix Polygoni Cuspidati), 6 g Zexie (Rhizoma Alismatis), 6 g Chenpi (Pericarpium Citri Reticulatae), 6 g processed Banxia (Rhizoma Pinelliae), 6 g Fuling (Poria), 18.1 g Huangjing (Rhizoma Polygonati Sibirici) and 7.2 g Fenbixie (Rhizoma Dioscoreae Hypoglaucae). The daily dose of HJJZ granules per person is 9.75 g, which is equivalent to 97.5 g of raw herbs.

The active ingredients of HJJZ granules were gathered from HERB 2.0 (http://47.92.70.12/), a database integrating clinical and experimental evidence for Traditional Chinese Medicine. The initial active ingredient screening was based on the criteria of “high” gastrointestinal absorption and at least two “Yes” responses in terms of drug-likeness (Lipinski, Ghose, Veber, Egan and Muegge).

Animals and Treatments

Six-week-old male C57BL/6 mice (mean weight: 19.9 ± 0.80 g) were purchased from Shanghai Southern Model Biotechnology Co., Ltd. and were housed and cared for humanely in strict compliance with the approved ethical guidelines (Approval Number: 2024002). Male mice were randomly divided into six experimental groups (n = 6 per group). Specifically, the control group was fed a standard chow diet throughout the experimental period. The HFD group was fed an HFD to induce NAFLD pathology. Concurrently, four intervention groups were established: the HFD+HJJZ-L, HFD+HJJZ-M, HFD+HJJZ-H, and HFD+atorvastatin groups, all of which received the HFD along with HJJZ and atorvastatin (ATO). The four intervention groups were administered an HFD concurrently with gavage administration of HJJZ granules at low (0.634 g/kg/day), medium (1.268 g/kg/day), and high (2.536 g/kg/day) doses, as well as ATO (20 mg/kg/day) as a positive control. After an initial 6-week period of HFD feeding to ensure the development of hepatic steatosis, both dietary and pharmacological interventions were commenced. Subsequently, all groups persisted with their respective diets as previously described, and the four intervention groups were fed their respective diets for a consecutive 6-week period. This design enables the evaluation of the dose-dependent therapeutic effects of HJJZ granules on established HFD-induced hepatic steatosis, liver injury, apoptosis, and associated molecular pathways, compared with pathological and healthy baselines. All animal experimental protocols were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Zhenjiang Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval Number: 2024002), and the animal procedures adhered to the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.

Biochemical Parameter Observations

Commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute) were employed to determine the levels of cholesterol and triglyceride in serum, liver tissues, and the HepG2 cell supernatant as per the manufacturer’s instructions.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) Staining

For histological examination of hepatic steatosis, liver tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h at 4 °C. Following fixation, tissues underwent sequential dehydration through a graded ethanol series (70%, 80%, 90%, and 100%), were cleared in xylene, and subsequently embedded in paraffin wax. Sections 4–5 μm thick were cut with a microtome and mounted on glass slides. For staining, the sections were deparaffinized in xylene and then rehydrated through a descending ethanol series, after which they were rinsed in distilled water. Nuclei were stained with Harris’s hematoxylin solution for 5–8 min, followed by differentiation in 1% acid alcohol and bluing in Scott’s tap water. Subsequently, the cytoplasm was counterstained with eosin Y solution for 1–3 min. Finally, the stained sections were dehydrated through an ascending alcohol series, cleared in xylene, and mounted with a neutral resin medium. Hepatic steatosis severity was assessed employing a semiquantitative histological grading scheme. The established criteria were as follows: a score of 0 indicated rare, isolated fatty droplets within hepatocytes; a score of 1 was assigned when fatty infiltration involved up to 25% of the hepatocellular area; a score of 2 corresponded to lipid accumulation affecting 26%–50% of the area; a score of 3 denoted the involvement of 51%–75% of the hepatocyte area; and the maximum score of 4 was assigned for severe, panlobular steatosis where lipid droplets replaced more than 75% of the liver parenchyma.

Oil Red O Staining

To visualize neutral lipid droplets, frozen liver tissue Sects. (8–10 μm thick) were prepared with a cryostat. Subsequently, the sections were air-dried, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, rinsed with PBS, and then immersed in 60% isopropanol for 1 min. Oil Red O staining was performed by incubating the sections in a filtered 0.5% Oil Red O working solution at room temperature for 15 min. To remove excess stain, the sections were differentiated in 60% isopropanol until the background was clear, and then thoroughly washed with distilled water. Nuclei were lightly counterstained with Mayer’s hematoxylin (1–2 min), followed by rinsing of the sections with water, mounting with aqueous mounting medium, and examination via light microscopy.

TUNEL Staining

Apoptotic cells in liver tissue sections were detected using a commercial in situ cell death detection kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Briefly, deparaffinized and rehydrated sections were treated with proteinase K for antigen retrieval. After washing, the sections were incubated at 37 °C for 60 min in a humidified chamber with the TUNEL reaction mixture (TdT and fluorescein-dUTP included). The reaction was terminated, and nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Apoptotic cells exhibiting green fluorescence were visualized and quantified under a fluorescence microscope.

Cell Culture and Model Establishment

Human hepatocellular carcinoma HepG2 cells were maintained in a DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, with incubation conditions set at 37 °C and 5% CO2. To establish the in vitro steatotic model, cells at approximately 70%–80% confluence were exposed to 0.5 mM oleic acid (OA) and different concentrations of HJJZ granules (4, 8, or 16 mg/mL) for 48 h to induce lipid accumulation.

Cell Transfection

To achieve gene modulation, an shRNA targeting human FOXA1 and a nontargeting control (shNC) were designed and cloned into the pLKO.1 vector. A lentivirus overexpression vector for DERL1 was generated by inserting its cDNA into the pLVX-Puro backbone. These plasmids were used to produce lentivirus particles in 293T cells via Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) transfection. The harvested viral supernatant was then used to infect HepG2 cells in the presence of polybrene. Successfully transduced cells were isolated through puromycin selection for 4 days.

Cell Viability Assay

HepG2 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 3 × 10³ cells per well and then cultured to allow adherence. After 24 h, cells were treated with 0.5 mM OA and various concentrations of HJJZ granules (4, 8, or 16 mg/mL) for 0, 12, 24, or 48 h. To evaluate cell viability, each well was supplemented with 10 µl of the CCK-8 reagent and incubated for 1 h. Subsequently, the absorbance value at 450 nm was measured with a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, CA, USA).

Flow Cytometry Analysis

HepG2 cells were collected and pelleted by centrifugation at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells/mL. The supernatant was removed prior to the determination of cellular apoptosis with a FITC Annexin V staining kit following the established protocol. The apoptotic ratio was subsequently determined using a flow cytometer and analyzed using CytExpert software.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was isolated from liver specimens and cultured HepG2 cells using the TRIzol reagent, followed by reverse transcription of 1 µg purified RNA into cDNA with SuperScript III Reverse Transcriptase. Gene expression levels were then quantified by real-time PCR employing a SYBR Green-based detection system. The primers used were as follows: FOXA1 forward, 5′-GCACTCGGCTTCCAGTATGC-3′, reverse, 5′-CGTAGTAGCTGCTCCAGTCG-3′; FOXA2 forward, 5′-GCACTCGGCTTCCAGTATGC-3′, reverse, 5′-CGTAGTAGCTGCTCCAGTCG-3′; FOXA3 forward, 5′-CTCACCTCCTTCGTCCACAC-3′, reverse, 5′-CCCAAACCCCACATCCAGTT-3′; DER1 forward, 5′-CATCACGCGCTACTGGTTTG-3′, reverse, 5′-GATAGAGGAAGGCTTCGGGC-3′. The relative mRNA expression levels were normalized to GAPDH, with the forward primer of GAPDH being 5′-TGGTGAAGCAGGCATCTGAG-3′ and the reverse primer being 5′-TGAAGTCGCAGGAGACAACC-3′. The 2−ΔΔCT method was employed to calculate the relative expression of target genes.

Western Blotting

Protein extraction from liver tissues and HepG2 cells was performed using the RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail. The protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay kit. Equal amounts of protein lysates (30 µg per lane) were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes. Following a 1-h block in 5% BSA, membranes were probed overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies: FOXA1 (Abcam; ab23738), DERL1 (Thermo Fisher; PA116598), CHOP (CST; 2895), p-PERK (CST; 3179), PERK (CST; 3192), p-eIF2α (CST; 3398), eIF2α (Abcam; ab169528), and GAPDH (Proteintech; 60004-1-Ig). After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence substrate and detected with an imaging system.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP) Assay

The ChIP assay was performed using a commercial magnetic kit following the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cells were cross-linked with 1% formaldehyde, and chromatin was fragmented using a combination of micrococcal nuclease (MNase) digestion and sonication. The sheared chromatin was immunoprecipitated overnight with an anti-FOXA1 antibody (PTM Bio; PTM-1427RM) or a control IgG (Proteintech; 30000-0-AP). After rigorous washing and cross-link reversal, the coprecipitated DNA was purified and analyzed by PCR with specific primers.

Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay

To investigate the transcriptional regulation of the DERL1 gene by FOXA1, its promoter region was inserted into the pGL3-basic luciferase reporter vector. This construct, together with the Renilla control plasmid, was transiently transfected into HepG2 cells having stable knockdown of FOXA1, which were treated with OA and HJJZ granules. The activity of the promoter was measured 48 h later for firefly as well as Renilla luciferase signals with the dual-reporter assay system.

Statistical Analysis

All experimental data were calculated based on three independent biological replicates. GraphPad Prism software (version 8.4.2) was utilized for all statistical analyses. Data were represented as the mean ± SD. Comparisons between two groups and multiple groups were performed using Student’s t-test and the one-way ANOVA, respectively. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

HJJZ Granules Attenuate HFD-Triggered Hepatic Steatosis in NAFLD Mice

The active ingredients of HJJZ granules gathered from the HERB 2.0 database are shown in Table S1. In total, 574 active ingredients demonstrated high gastrointestinal absorption and favorable drug-likeness. Subsequently, NAFLD was induced in C57BL/6 mice by feeding an HFD. Histological analyses performed using H&E staining revealed pronounced macrovesicular steatosis in hepatocytes from HFD-fed mice, which was markedly attenuated in a dose-dependent manner following treatment with HJJZ granules (Fig. 1A). Accordingly, the Oil Red O-positive areas were bigger in the HFD group than in the groups with or without treatment with HHHJZ granules (Fig. 1A). Considering steatosis scores and the optical density of lipid droplets, it could be observed that supplementation with HJJZ granules reduced steatosis and lipid accumulation in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1B–C). HFD activated liver injury by increasing serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels. Dual treatment caused the serum levels of both enzymes to decrease significantly, alongside the doses of the HJJZ granule treatment (Fig. 1D–E). HFD feeding disturbed the profiling of lipids both systemically and to the liver. Notably, lower triglyceride and cholesterol levels in the serum of subjects were attained with the granule treatment (Fig. 1F–G). In a sense, triglyceride and cholesterol levels in liver tissue, which were increased by HFD feeding, were relieved in a dose-dependent manner by treatment with HJJZ granules (Fig. 1H–I). Moreover, the high dose of HJJZ granules was at an effect comparable with the ATO positive control. Thus, these data suggest that the HJJZ granules could effectively counteract the deleterious effects of HFD in lipids and injury to the liver.

HJJZ Granules Attenuate HFD-Provoked Liver Injury in NAFLD Mice

While the role of hepatocyte apoptosis is relatively well established, we aimed to study this phenomenon to elucidate the mechanism by which HJJZ granules induce protection against liver injury by TUNEL staining assays on liver tissues (Fig. 2A–B). Significantly reduced TUNEL-positive cell counts were found after HJJZ granule supplementation, especially at a high dose. As ER stress has been implicated in hepatic apoptosis in association with NAFLD, we wanted to view major proteins involved in the UPR. Observing that Western blotting indicated a significant upregulation of the expression of DERL1 and CHOP by HFD treatment (Fig. 2C–E). An upregulation of p-PERK/PERK and p-eIF2α/eIF2α was observed in the HFD group, suggesting that this group activated the PERK pathway (Fig. 2F–G). HJJZ granules interrupted this trend and decreased the levels of DERL1 and CHOP in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2C–E). HFD activated all major players of the PERK signaling pathway (PERK and eIF2α), and HJJZ granules significantly ameliorated these alterations (Fig. 2F–G). Altogether, in mice fed HFD plus HJJZ granules, excess hepatocyte apoptosis was mitigated, and triggering of the hepatocyte PERKeIF2α-CHOP axis related to ER stress, which develops upon injury of the liver, was avoided.

HJJZ Granules Attenuate OA-Induced Apoptosis, ER Stress, and Steatosis in HepG2 Cells

A HepG2 cell line was being used to observe the anti-steatosis activity of the HJJZ granules, and OA was chosen to perform the aforementioned steatosis model. Cotreatment with HJJZ granules could reverse the OA-induced decrease in cell viability in a dose-dependent manner, with the best effect observed at 16 mg/ml (Fig. 3A). The percentage of apoptotic cells was higher when stimulated with OA and then was decreased by HJJZ granule treatment in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 3B–C). Results also show that OA relieves ER stress, which is strongly evidenced by increments in DERL1 and CHOP expression in vitro (Fig. 3D–E). The proportion of phosphorylated PERK (p-PERK/PERK) and eIF2α (p-eIF2α/eIF2α) was also increased significantly by OA, but it was reversed by cotreatment with HJJZ granules in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 3D–E). Lipid accumulation was inhibited by cotreatment with HJJZ granules. HJJZ granules hindered the excessive appearance of lipid droplets in cultured cells evoked by the presence of OA in the medium (Fig. 3F). The significant reduction in the optical density of lipid droplets due to HJJZ granules was also dose-dependent (Fig. 3G). The spontaneous rise in activities of hepatocyte injury markers ALT and AST in the culture supernatant and the intracellular triglyceride and cholesterol levels were inhibited by HJJZ granules (Fig. 3H–K). Altogether, HJJZ exerts hepatoprotective effects not just by enhancing lipid accumulation but also by protecting against cell death and ER stress signaling in an OA-induced liver steatosis model.

HJJZ Granules Increase FOXA1 Expression in HFD-Induced NAFLD Mice and OA-Induced HepG2 Cells

We further explored the molecular mechanisms by which HJJZ granules exerted their effects. In the liver tissues of HFD-fed mice, the levels of mRNA of the FOXA family of transcription factors, which regulate lipid metabolism and ER function, were reduced for FOXA1 and FOXA2 compared to controls; however, only in FOXA3 were they significantly increased (Fig. 4A–C). Treatment with HJJZ granules selectively restored the mRNA level of FOXA1 in a dose-dependent manner, with no impact on the mRNA levels of FOXA2 and FOXA3 (Fig. 4A–C). This selectivity was preserved at the protein level, as HFD-induced reductions in the levels of the FOXA1 protein were rapidly restored by HJJZ treatment (Fig. 4D–E). In OA-treated HepG2 cells, FOXA1 was also reduced significantly, and cotreatment with HJJZ granules upregulated FOXA1 expression in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 4F–G). In summary, the data implicate the FOXA1 gene as an important target in NAFLD, and highlight that HJJZ granules assist in upregulating FOXA1 expression without disrupting either FOXA2 or FOXA3 levels.

HJJZ Granules Attenuate Apoptosis, ER Stress, and Steatosis in OA-Induced HepG2 Cells by Targeting FOXA1

To determine whether FOXA1 mediates the therapeutic actions of HJJZ granules, we genetically silenced FOXA1 in HepG2 cells using specific short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs). Transduction with three independent FOXA1 shRNAs, but not the nontargeting control (shNC), resulted in a significant reduction of both FOXA1 mRNA and protein levels, confirming effective knockdown (Fig. 5A–C). In cells transduced with shNC, HJJZ granules treatment (16 mg/mL) effectively counteracted OA-induced cytotoxicity, as shown by improved cell viability in the CCK-8 assay (Fig. 5D) and reduced apoptosis in the flow cytometry analysis (Fig. 5E–F). These protective effects of HJJZ granules were decreased in FOXA1-knockdown cells, with decreased viability and increased apoptosis levels (Fig. 5D–F). Similarly, the Western blot analysis showed that HJJZ granules significantly reduced the OA-induced upregulation of DERL1 and CHOP, as well as the phosphorylation of PERK and eIF2α, in shNC cells. However, in FOXA1-deficient cells, HJJZ granules failed to inhibit this ER stress pathway activation (Fig. 5G). Furthermore, FOXA1 was essential for cellular steatosis alleviation by HJJZ granules. While HJJZ granules significantly reduced lipid droplet accumulation (Fig. 5H and I), the release of ALT and AST (Fig. 5J–K), and the intracellular levels of triglyceride and cholesterol in shNC cells (Fig. 5L–M), all these beneficial effects were absent in the FOXA1 knockdown group. In conclusion, FOXA1 knockdown completely negates the capacity of HJJZ granules to improve viability, reduce apoptosis, suppress ER stress, and ameliorate lipid accumulation in OA-treated hepatocytes, demonstrating that FOXA1 is a required mediator of the protective effects of HJJZ granules.

FOXA1 Attenuates Apoptosis, ER Stress, and Steatosis in HepG2 Cells through the Transcriptional Regulation of DER1

To investigate the downstream mechanism mediating the protective effect of FOXA1, we focused on DERL1, a potential transcriptional target of FOXA1 and a key component of ERAD. ChIP-PCR data revealed significant enrichment of FOXA1 on the DERL1 promoter region in HepG2 cells (Fig. 6A). Consistently, dual-luciferase reporter and qRT-PCR assays demonstrated that OA suppressed, while HJJZ granules restored, DERL1 promoter activity and mRNA expression in a FOXA1-dependent manner (Fig. 6B–C). Next, we conducted a rescue experiment by overexpressing DERL1 in FOXA1-knockdown cells, and this DERL1 overexpression effectively reversed the adverse effects caused by FOXA1 depletion. Specifically, DERL1 overexpression in FOXA1-depleted cells restored cell viability (Fig. 6D) and diminished apoptosis (Fig. 6E), comparable to the control. Moreover, DERL1 effectively inhibited the upregulated expression of ER stress markers (CHOP, p-PERK, and p-eIF2α) in the FOXA1-deficient cells (Fig. 6F–G). DERL1 overexpression attenuated lipid droplet accumulation (Fig. 6H–I), decreased the release of ALT and AST (Fig. 6J–K), and lowered intracellular triglyceride and cholesterol levels (Fig. 6L–M) in FOXA1-silenced cells. In summary, DERL1 level elevation is necessary and sufficient to relieve ER stress, thereby decreasing apoptosis and relieving hepatic steatosis.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate the HJJZ granule’s ability to ameliorate NAFLD in both HFD-fed mice and OA-treated HepG2 cell models. Our data support the idea that HJJZ granules therapeutically attenuate NAFLD by eliciting the FOXA1-DERL1 regulatory circuit modulating ER stress and hepatocyte apoptosis.

In general, instability and stress in the ER are major components of NAFLD pathology that exacerbate insulin resistance and lipoprotein and lipid metabolism and, ultimately, hepatocyte apoptosis [16]. PERK-eIF2α-CHOP, a branch of this ER response, is core, and if prolonged, it enhances lipogenic programs and may induce apoptosis resulting in hepatocyte death [17, 18]. We have previously studied how HJJZ granules function and demonstrated their efficacy on FAFLD in vivo. In the current study, the HFD-fed mice had markedly ameliorated liver steatosis upon treatment with granules, as shown by the number of lipid droplets and lower steatosis. HJJZ granules improved liver function, represented by serum ALT/AST levels, and modulated lipid metabolism. More evidence for the function of NAFLD returning properly is seen with HJJZ granules reducing HFD-promoted hepatocyte apoptosis by the relative expression of important ER stress markers (including PERK, eIF2α, and CHOP) that may alleviate ER stress [19, 20]. We hypothesize that the role of the HJJZ granules in NAFLD is ER stress alleviation. Not unexpectedly, the results of previous in vitro work corroborate these results. The data derived from OA-induced HepG2 cells. Treatment with HJJZ granules enhanced cell viability, reduced apoptosis rates, reduced lipid accumulation, and suppressed the ER stress pathway. In vivo and in vitro, granules derived from HJJZ largely stimulated mRNA and protein expression of the transcription factor FOXA1. This is critical since it points to the rare property that FOXA1 exerts effects of HJJZ. The FOXA protein family comprises pioneer transcription factors, and they play an essential role in influencing metabolism and metabolic homeostasis; typically, it is FOXA2 that is the star of the show when investigating aspects of hepatic lipid metabolism, but we are quickly discovering a role for FOXA1 in NAFLD [21, 22]. Current evidence is in support of the involvement of FOXA1 in hepatic lipid dynamics [23, 24]. Loss-of-function experiments whereby we knocked down FOXA1 in HepG2 cells nullify the protective effects mediated by HJJZ granules. In FOXA1-deficient cells, the effects of HJJZ benefiting cell viability, apoptosis, lipid accumulation, and ER stress marker levels were greatly attenuated in comparison. This positioned FOXA1 as a key “upstream mediator node,” which acts as an important mediating factor upstream of the effects.

As a transcriptional activator specifically targeting the liver, FOXA1 is part of the forkhead box family of proteins with biological roles in proliferation, differentiation, and the cell cycle [24, 25]. To investigate the downstream action for which FOXA1 mediates protective effects, our bioinformatics and molecular biology studies have shown that FOXA1 binds to the promoter region of DERL1, acting as a transcriptional activator, thus enhancing DERL1 promoter activity and mRNA expression. Functioning by playing a role in the ERAD that is involved in degrading misfolded proteins. DERL1 is crucial to the removal of abnormal proteins from the ER for ER stress mitigation [26], and impairing this ERAD pathway can lead to hepatic steatosis and insulin resistance [27, 28]. DERL1 overexpression significantly rescues cells from damage by FOXA1 knockdown, including apoptosis, ER stress, and steatosis, all of which are pathological events downstream of FOXA1 deficiency. It is important to identify FOXA1-DERL1 as an axis, as this pathway that regulates ER stress in hepatic steatosis has remained entirely unreported in prior studies.

The active ingredients of HJJZ granules were gathered from the HERB 2.0 database. In total, 574 active ingredients demonstrated high gastrointestinal absorption and favorable drug-likeness, among which coumarin, curcumin, quercetin, and resveratrol have been reported to regulate the expression, phosphorylation, and function of FOXA1 [29–33]. Glycycoumarin, a representative of courmarin compounds, suppressed hepatocyte lipoapoptosis through inhibition of the ER stress-mediated mitochondrial apoptotic pathway [34]. Scopoletin and umbelliferone are coumarin-like molecules that protected against the cell death of hepatocytes via ER stress inhibition [35]. Curcumin, a natural polyphenol, attenuated hepatotoxicity by inhibiting ER stress and lipid metabolism disorders [36] and alleviated NAFLD by promoting the expressions of SOD1 and SIRT1 and inhibiting the ER stress-mediated O-GlcNAcylation of NF-κB [37]. Quercetin is a flavonoid that reduced lipid accumulation in NAFLD by regulating the expression of XBP1, an ER stress marker [38]. Resveratrol, a natural polyphenol, alleviated hepatic steatosis by alleviating severe ER stress [39] and regulating the expression of genes involved in lipid metabolism, insulin resistance, and inflammation [40]. The results of this study are consistent with these findings, indicating that the FOXA1-mediated ER stress and apoptosis may contribute to the anti-steatosis effect of HJJZ granules in NAFLD.

Nevertheless, this study has several noteworthy limitations. The use of HepG2 cells rather than primary hepatocytes may partially account for the discrepancies between in vitro and in vivo findings. Further research is needed to explore the effect of HJJZ granules in primary hepatocytes. In addition, this study primarily relies on animal models and in vitro experiments, necessitating future clinical validation of the efficacy and safety of HJJZ granules. Moreover, the active ingredients of HJJZ granules and their effects on FOXA1-mediated ER stress and apoptosis warrant further exploration. Subsequent investigations should parse the combinatorial actions of these active ingredients to pinpoint FOXA1-inducing candidates. Addressing these gaps will facilitate the clinical translation of HJJZ granules, potentially providing new therapeutic strategies that will benefit patients with NAFLD.

In conclusion, we describe a possible mechanism by which HJJZ granules exert beneficial effects for patients with NAFLD. We propose that HJJZ granules activate FOXA1 transcription, leading to the downstream transcription of the DERL1 gene. Increased DERL1 expression may then boost activity of the ERAD axis of protein quality control, alleviating ER stress and reducing hepatocyte apoptosis and excessive lipogenesis in the liver. Our results justify the use of HJJZ granules in patients and identify the FOXA1-DERL1 axis as a potential target for NAFLD therapy.

Exploring the nexus of transformational leadership, job satisfaction, and creativity: evidence from Pakistani nurses
Authors: Arif Jameel, Mingxing Li, Abid Hussain, Muhammad Asif, Zhiqiang Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to explore the relationship between transformational leadership, work engagement, and employee creativity, and the mediating influence of job satisfaction and work engagement on this relationship—a cross-sectional study conducted from August to September from five public sector hospitals in Punjab province of Pakistan. Statistical analyses, which involve descriptive statistics, correlation, and mediation analyses, were performed utilizing Analysis of Moment Structures (AMOS) version 25.0 and the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS). A random sampling technique was used to collect 283 responses from the nurses. The study findings discovered that there are positive associations between transformational leadership and both work engagement and employee creativity. The findings further show that job satisfaction partially mediates the relationship between transformational leadership and work engagement, while both job satisfaction and work engagement wholly mediate the association between transformational leadership and employee creativity. The results of this research contribute to the literature on employee creativity in healthcare sectors by increasing the understanding of the influences of job satisfaction, work engagement, and transformational leadership on employee creativity among nursing staff. This research provides essential policy insight for healthcare leaders who seek to enhance creativity among their nursing staff. Testing these relations through a multiple-mediation approach considerably added to the current study on leadership. Theoretical and practical implications of the findings are discussed.

Introduction

With substantial and swift innovation, social, demographical, and economic transformations in the knowledge-based economy, employee creativity (EC) has developed into a progressively significant challenge for institutions to create workers, improve core competencies, and assist institutions not only to thrive but also to uphold a competitive gain in the international economy [1, 2]. Consequently, firms are looking for new strategies to encourage EC [3]. Understanding successful leadership has emerged as one of the most significant aims and shared aspirations for scholars, persons, companies, and society worldwide. Leadership is observed as one of the most imperative variables prompting EC and corporate innovation [4]. Furthermore, “leadership has been identified as a critical driver of institutional innovation” [5]. The transformational leadership (TL) style has gained widespread acceptance among leadership academics [6–8] because of its distinctive ways of motivating worker innovation when contrasted to certain other types of leadership [9, 10]. Moreover, the TL is significantly related to EC [11] and is suitable for classifying unique potentials and developing skills in firms. TL boosts workers’ confidence and values, and as a result, the workers’ outcomes exceed their expectations [12]. Previous studies indicate that TL has a robust and positive association with various long-term elements of leadership effectiveness, including worker engagement (WE), job satisfaction (JS), OCB, and organizational performance and commitment [13–15]. Similarly, effective businesses consider workers’ WE to be one of the most essential factors in their sustainability [16] and enhanced institutional performance [17]. Worker WE is linked to beneficial both organizational and personal outcomes, for example, increased productivity and OCB, JS, and improved management effectiveness and efficiency [18]. Both individual and institutional results, on the other hand, diminish with worker turnover [16]. EC, in contrast, is widely thought to be an effective organizational capability in a modern environment and has to be fostered among workers by suitable training and appropriate experience [19]. As a result, researchers associate EC with institutional performance [4], and firms strive to determine the best approaches to foster creativity among their staff [20]. Leadership is the most significant aspect of work engagement and employee creativity because it creates a productive job atmosphere in which workers feel more motivated and empowered as a result of their good actions [16]. TL also has a beneficial effect on WE [13] and EC [21]. WE is enhanced when TL exhibits supportive behaviors and creates a productive atmosphere by providing appropriate input, honesty, interactional fairness, rational decision-making, and caring. These encouraging actions enable workers to take part in institutional creative activities and boost their creativity [13]. This may also increase workers’ favorable remarks toward their bosses.

Furthermore, TL’s support boosts staff morale, and they go above and above to accomplish long-term corporate objectives with complete commitment, demonstrating their higher JS [22] and engagement [23]. About the current study, barely a few experimental studies have shown a link between TL and EC via various mediating variables, like voice behavior [23], knowledge creation, and WE [24]. Further study is required to have a deeper understanding of this link. As a result, we proposed that we investigate the influence of TL on EC in diverse circumstances in non-European values further. The cultural backdrop and how workers behave with the boss must be investigated as well. To have a successful influence, leadership styles must be tailored to diverse situations and nations [25]. Hofstede [26] classified numerous groups of countries into cultural clusters, each with its unique features based on the national past, culture, and customs. Few studies have examined the association between TL and EC in non-European cultures, especially in Pakistan, despite a large body of study on the subject in Western contexts. By investigating the relationship of TL on EC through intermediaries like JS and WE within Pakistan’s distinct institutional and cultural environment, this study seeks to fill this gap. Although studies have acknowledged how national culture shapes leadership styles, little is known about nursing leadership in Pakistan, particularly in terms of its impact on creativity and engagement in healthcare environments. Furthermore, although several mediators in the TL-EC interaction have been examined, few studies have been done specifically on the healthcare environment in Pakistan. Thus, by providing insights into the TL-EC relationship in Pakistan while taking cultural dynamics and leadership effectiveness in a hospital setting into account, this study contributes. As a result, we attempted to study the TL-EC interaction in the Pakistani setting, with JS and WE serving as possible mediating variables. In several ways, the study contributes to the literature. First, it allowed us to establish if TL increased EC and WE inside the institutionally confined culture, for instance, how successful is this TL in supporting particular work habits in the Pakistani public sector? Secondly, this research contributes to the current TL literature by looking for novel settings that may have a significant influence on the outcomes of followers. We aimed to create a contributing process for how TL promotes EC in subordinates through JS and WE. Much research, however, employed various mediating variables to examine this relationship, such as structural empowerment [13]. Third, we looked at the Role of JS in mediating the relationship between TL and WE. Because the majority of leadership research is centered on European culture. Scholars have identified the influence of national culture on leadership traits and potency Dorfman, Javidan [25]. Still, limited research has expressly explored the associations between TL and EC employing the aforementioned mediating variables that are particular to the Pakistani healthcare context. Despite the lack of investigation, nursing administration should thoroughly understand this multiple-mediation mechanism to enhance a strong supervisor-subordinate relationship and create a conducive work atmosphere where the workers feel more comfortable and creative sharing new ideas and expressing their needs. Literature review

Transformational leadership (TL) and employee creativity (EC)

Transformational leaders impart their knowledge, support innovative ideas, and urge their staff to think beyond the box [27, 28]. Leaders also assist people in controlling their fear of danger and revolutionizing standard approaches to functioning, resulting in a significant degree of EC. Leaders motivate workers and gain their loyalty and admiration through idealized influence. By expressing a guiding vision and roadmap, transformational leaders encourage and motivate workers to obtain goals. Workers are motivated to act creatively by intellectual curiosity [29]. Personal consideration is used by leaders to give heed to each staff member to meet their requirements [29, 30]. Numerous researchers have tried to grasp the influence of various leadership styles on employee creative behaviors [31]. More of them have added to the literature and given some functional understandings; the far more significant step of the below research is the Role of TL in encouraging EC. Due to its substantial effects on workers, the TL type has developed a well-known leadership type among scholars. The latest study has also revealed that TL has a favorable and substantial influence on creativity prediction [32, 33]. Leadership is critical in stimulating innovation in companies [34]. TL promotes staff to use new techniques for completing projects, which increases employee creativity [35]. According to Ngo, Le [36] research, TL encourages workers to consider novel and innovative techniques to complete their duties. A study done by Yasin, Nawab [37] established that mental stimulation had a promising influence on innovation. Furthermore, TL fosters deep associations with their team, which leads to improved employee productivity [30]. Furthermore, other publications validated the beneficial influence of TL on EC [27, 31, 32] As a result, the subsequent hypothesis is developed:

Hypothesis 1

Transformational Leadership is significantly related to employee creativity.

Transformational leadership (TL) work engagement (WE)

Both Scholars and experts have recognized the rising significance of leadership growth initiatives in improving engagement, resulting in research that investigates personnel views of such activities [38–40]. Two studies, for instance, explored the Role of self-efficacy in mediating the association between TL and WE [41, 42]. According to Monje-Amor, Vázquez [43], Supporter attributes impacted the link between TL and supporter WE Work engagement levels dropped when leaders assessed supporter features negatively. According to Hoon Song, Kolb [44], WE played a mediation role between transformative leadership and structural knowledge production methods in Korea. Breevaart and Bakker [45] discovered this because when their leadership demonstrated more transformational leadership and offered contingent incentives, navy cadets were more engaged (a constituent of transactional leadership). Workers’ views of meaning in jobs somewhat mediated the link between TL and WE in an Australian setting, as a study done by [46]. Kahn was the original academic to build individual engagement and to identify three psychosomatic characteristics that may impact whether individuals participate in their personal lives (i.e., meaningfulness, security, and availability). Schaufeli, Bakker [47] described WE as a pleasant, gratifying, job-linked frame of mind that is marked by energy, devotion, and absorption. It denotes a psychological emotional state that is more permanent over time, rather than a different and fleeting condition. In proportion to novel idea of engagement, this research evaluates WE as an attribute instead of a variation state [48, 49]. Vigor is described as taking penalty of energy, being psychologically tough, and placing in a significant amount of time and labor at job. Dedication is described by enthusiasm, pride, inspiration, and expressive partaking in one’s job. The final aspect, engagement, denotes to being entirely engaged and captivated in one’s job, which reasons time to fly by and crafts it hard to untie oneself from a job [47]. The job demand-resources paradigm [50, 51] is used in this research to investigate the relationship between two specific work resources, TL and structural empowerment, and engagement. According to this concept, working circumstances might be classified into two clusters: work demands and work resources. Job demands (for example, workload) are defined as “those physical, social, or organizational components of the job that necessitate persistent physical or mental effort and are hence connected with particular physiological and psychological consequences” [51]. Job resources, in contrast, are job attributes that can accomplish somewhat of the next: (a) be practical in attaining work objectives; (b) lessen work requests and the accompanying physiological and psychological expenses; and (c) promote individual progress and advancement.’ [51]. Work resources, in line with Bakker and Demerouti [52], are the primary motivators of WE. Work resources refer to the physical, communal, or institutional elements of a job. Work resources comprise manager and societal support, response, training, voice, the opportunity for progress and growth, and workplace diversity. In reality, effective feedback enhances learning, and colleague support enhances the probability of attaining one’s goals [52, 53]. In light of the above studies, the following hypothesis is formed:

Hypothesis 2

Transformational leadership is positively related to work engagement.

Mediating influence of job satisfaction and work engagement

Job satisfaction (JS) is described as a pleasant emotional reaction arising from a worker’s judgment of their job as reaching or satisfying essential work ideals [54]. In the current research, JS is described as the pleasant emotional reaction that results after a worker assesses the institution’s job-related resources as fulfilling. Due to its association with some other variables such as work performance, turnover intention, and innovation, job satisfaction is a crucial subject in the hotel sector [55, 56]. It is critical to understand the elements that impact work satisfaction to improve it [57, 58]. According to studies, work resources like incentives, advancement, and societal support have a significant influence on worker job satisfaction [55, 59]. Job satisfaction has been shown to have a mediating influence [55, 60]. Nevertheless, there has been little investigation on JS as a mediating variable in the link between transformational leadership (TL) and employee creativity (EC), which this study aims to fill. Work engagement (WE), in contrast, is explained as “a pleasant, gratifying, job-elated state of mind marked by vitality, devotion, and absorption“ [47]. Vigor denotes high degrees of energy, relentless effort to achieve a work goal despite problems; dedication represents pride, passion, importance, and motivation at work; and absorption denotes full attention of the mind at the job, making disconnection from the job difficult with time [47]. WE have been linked to favorable work results like JS, extra-role performance, institutional commitment, reduced inclinations to leave and be late, and decreased nonattendance [59, 61]. Work and individual resources, as a result, have been recognized as elements that improve worker WE [52]. Following previous studies by Al Harbi, Alarifi [62], this study contends that transformative leadership can improve engagement on the job. According to SET Cook and Emerson [63], when workers think that the company has equipped them with resources to raise their degree of organizational embeddedness, they are more likely to give back by displaying optimistic attitudinal results like JS and WE. Given the number of job resources they have amassed, deeply entrenched workers are unlikely to exhibit adverse outcomes, such as sample job disengagement and work unhappiness, since they may regard those sentiments as ethically wrong after the organization’s favors. As embeddedness grows, so does workers’ ethical obligation to reward the business with favorable attitudinal results. Workers’ institutional embeddedness has been proven to be highly related to WE and JS [64]. In response, when workers are happy with the resources provided by the business, they feel obligated to return by displaying increased emotional attachment to the firm [65, 66]. Workers who gain from the company’s compassion and care might feel morally bound to repay by exhibiting actions and behaviors that help the business. For example, research has shown that emotional commitment [56, 67] can lead to increased job satisfaction, which in turn can positively impact creativity. Kong, Jiang [67] discovered a substantial and positive association between JS and organizational commitment and engagement in their research. Furthermore, when workers are involved in their job, they are more prospective to form an emotive tie to the business when they recognize that the institution assisted them in acquiring the job resources that they now possess [61]. Disengaged workers, in contrast, might feel expressively alienated from the firm as they consider it has failed to provide them with work resources. In line with Lee and Ok [68] research, workers’ work engagement was substantially and favorably associated with their creativity.

Research methodology

Research design

The hypotheses were evaluated using cross-sectional data and a random sampling approach. From August to September 2022, the research was carried out at five public hospitals in the Bahawalpur division of Southern Punjab, Pakistan. The information was gathered from 350 registered female nurses. The sample of 350 registered nurses was determined based on a power analysis using G*Power 3 [69]. Each hospital contributed seventy nurses to the sample. The inclusion criteria were for those with at least one year of employment who work directly under senior nurses or supervisors. Sixty-seven nurses were removed from the study due to missing or inconsistent data. After excluding incorrect data, the legitimate sample comprised 283 nurses, all of whom were female. Further details are provided in Table 1. After the respondents’ consent, the survey was delivered to them, and their privacy was guaranteed. Given that nursing is a job that women in Pakistan’s healthcare system mostly perform, we decided to include female nurses in our sample solely. Due to cultural traditions and gender-specific duties within the healthcare system, women make up the majority of registered nurses in Pakistan, especially in public healthcare facilities. Therefore, we only looked at female nurses to ensure that the sample fairly represents the nation’s nursing workforce. This method enables a more pertinent and contextually based analysis of the elements affecting employee creativity, engagement, and job satisfaction in Pakistan’s unique institutional and cultural environment. The survey was split into two portions. The first portion discussed the demographic features of the respondents, which comprised age, education, and employment (see Table 1 for further details). In portion two, respondents were asked to rate various variables such as TL, WE, EC, and JS. The questionnaire was filled out by 283 people, with a response rate of 80.57%. Furthermore, to exclude the risk of technique bias, we used distinct tests, including Harman’s single-factor test. Common method bias is not a concern in this dataset, as indicated by the results of Harman’s test. The overall variation explained throughout these tests is 22.57%, just under 50%, indicating that the dataset is impartial [70].

Instrument development

The 8-item scale established by Carless, Wearing [71] was utilized to assess transformational leadership (TL). “A sample item for TL was my supervisor communicating a clear and enthusiastic vision of the future”. The TL scale value was 0.91.

We assessed Work Engagement (WE) on a reduced nine-item form of the “Utrecht Work Engagement Scale” [47]. This scale measures the three engagement qualities of energy, devotion, and absorption. “At my job, I am bursting with energy’ (vigor); ‘I am proud of the work that I do’ (dedication); and ‘I am engrossed in my work’ (absorption)”. The Cronbach’s value for the total scale was 0.96, fulfilling the requirements.

Job satisfaction was assessed using Cammann [72] 3-item measuring scale. This scale was verified in investigations by [73] Harari et al. (2018). Workers were recruited to assess their level of job satisfaction. “Overall, I am pleased with my employment,” for example. This scale has an Alpha of 0.84.

We used a three-item EC scale from [5]. As an example, “this worker frequently comes up with novel and useful suggestions to boost performance”. The EC scale’s dependability was 0.76.

Data analysis and results

We utilized structural equation modeling to evaluate our research hypotheses using analysis of moment structures 25.0. We adhered to the proposal of Anderson and Gerbing [77] about a two-step SEM method, commencing with CFA to assess model suitability. A conclusive postulated structural model was examined to evaluate the links among all variables. Multiple fit indices, such as χ2/df, comparative fit index (CFI), standardized root mean square residual (SRMR), Tucker-Lewis index (TLI), and root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA), were utilized.

Common method bias (CMB)

We employed Harman’s single-factor test to assess the likelihood of common method bias [78]. Our study’s findings indicate that no factor exhibited a variance over 50% of the total variation obtained. The results suggest an absence of bias in this investigation.

Multivariate normality and robust estimation (MLR)

To substantiate the application of Covariance-Based Structural Equation Modeling (CB-SEM), we conducted univariate and multivariate normality assessments using the methodologies outlined by [79]. The univariate skewness and kurtosis for each variable fell below acceptable thresholds, with only slight deviations from normality noted. The multivariate normality test revealed minor positive skewness and kurtosis; nonetheless, the deviations were insufficient to compromise the model’s validity. Consequently, we employed robust estimating techniques (MLR), recognized for their efficacy in addressing minor deviations from normality, to guarantee dependable parameter estimates.

In the subsequent step, Hypotheses three and four are examined by assessing the four conditions for mediational analysis proposed by Baron and Kenny [82]. (1) The explanatory variable must predict the dependent variable, (2) the proposed mediating variable must be predicted by the explanatory variable, (3) the proposed mediating variable must predict the dependent variable, and (4) the direct relationship between the explanatory variable and the dependent variable must diminish (ideally becoming non-significant for complete mediation) upon the inclusion of the mediating variable. Table 6 demonstrates that transformational leadership (TL) fulfilled the initial criterion established by Baron and Kenny [82], as TL shown significant relationships with work engagement (β = 0.45, p < 0.01) and employee creativity (β = 0.27, p < 0.01). Regarding the second prerequisite of mediation, both job satisfaction and work engagement fulfill the criteria established by Baron and Kenny [81]. In Figure two, TL significantly predicted job satisfaction (β = 0.29, p < 0.01) and work engagement (β = 0.51, p < 0.01). In the third criterion, the initial mediating variable, job satisfaction, predicted the dependent variable work engagement (β = 0.51, p < 0.01), while the second mediating variable, work engagement, significantly predicted the dependent variable employee creativity (β = 0.43, p < 0.01). In the final requirement, the direct correlation between transformative leadership and work engagement, mediated by job satisfaction, diminishes (from β = 0.45 to β = 0.11) and remains statistically significant. It provided little mediation. Similarly, the direct correlation between transformational leadership and employee creativity becomes negligible and inconsequential (β = 0.05, see Fig. 2) when mediating variables are considered, indicating complete mediation effect. Collectively, these results statistically supported Hypothesis 3 and Hypothesis 4. We performed structural equation modeling (SEM) using the maximum likelihood approach [83]. In Table 7, each hypothesis’s z-values, which have standardized coefficients, show significance [84]. Additionally, both JS and WE had positive and significant indirect effects on the TL-EC relationship (β = 0.16; S.E. = 0.03; z = 5.33; LLCI = 0.18, ULCI = 0.42, p < 0.01). These findings reinforce H3 and H4 and further validate mediation.

Discussion and conclusion

The present research considerably enhances the literature on transformational leadership (TL), job satisfaction (JS), work engagement (WE), and employee creativity (EC) by investigating the under-explored relationship between TL and EC. Our findings indicate that transformational leadership (TL) is an exceptionally successful method for improving employee engagement and creativity, corroborating previous studies that highlight the beneficial relationship of leadership styles on team motivation and performance [42]. Cultivates explicitly a workplace where people feel esteemed, supported, and motivated to innovate, hence augmenting their creativity and overall job performance. According to Engelbrecht, Heine [85], transformational leaders demonstrate traits including confidence in their team, receptiveness to innovative ideas, and a dedication to equity, all of which enhance employee engagement in their work. This finding aligns with our findings, indicating that TL positively affects work engagement, which in turn fosters creativity. Likewise, Shafi, Lei [86] discovered that transformational leadership behaviors substantially affect engagement and creativity. Our findings corroborate prior research, indicating that transformational leadership not only enhances engagement but also cultivates an environment that promotes creativity. Although considerable research has been conducted on transformative leadership, there is a paucity of studies that have directly investigated its influence on EC [43, 87], rendering our study one of the initial inquiries into these impacts within public healthcare organizations. Additionally, we examined the mediating function of job satisfaction (JS) and work engagement (WE) in the link between transformational leadership (TL) and employee commitment (EC). Our findings demonstrate that job satisfaction partially mediates the association between transformational leadership and work engagement, while both job satisfaction and work engagement fully mediate the link between transformational leadership and employee commitment. This presents a novel theoretical framework applicable for evaluating the influence of transformational leadership on creativity within organizations characterized by collective cultures, exemplified by Pakistani government institutions. This study enhances prior studies by showing that employees with emotional commitment to their firms are more inclined to engage in their work, hence increasing their creativity. This aligns with Sharma and Dhar [88], who discovered that job satisfaction promotes engagement, resulting in enhanced creative production. Similarly, prior research Akinyemi, George [89], and Yasin Ghadi [90] has indicated that job satisfaction and work engagement foster self-confidence, perseverance, and sustained job commitment, all of which augment creativity. Recent studies have underscored the significance of leadership in cultivating healthy work environments that enhance job satisfaction and creativity. For instance, Agaoglu, Baş [91] showed that transformational leadership is crucial in augmenting employee satisfaction and fostering innovative work behavior, especially within healthcare environments. Furthermore, research indicates that transformational leadership enhances workplace conditions, fostering greater employee engagement and motivation for creativity [92]. Our research corroborates these findings, demonstrating that transformational leadership enhances job satisfaction and engagement while considerably augmenting employee creativity. This study underscores the significant impact of transformative leadership on enhancing employee creativity, especially within healthcare environments. Our findings provide a more sophisticated picture of how transformational leadership increases creativity by highlighting the mediation impacts of job satisfaction and work engagement. The results indicate that healthcare organizations ought to prioritize leadership development programs that cultivate transformational leadership behaviors, hence enhancing engagement, job satisfaction, and eventually creativity. Such approaches are expected to yield improved results for both staff and patients.

Practical implications

This research has specific practical implications for professionals. To begin, this study established transformational leadership (TL) as an influencing component that increases workers’ degree of work engagement (WE) and demonstrates its critical function in creating a conducive work atmosphere in which people become more daring and involved with their business. Secondly, because the existing study found that TL has an indirect influence on employee creativity (EC) via job satisfaction (JS) and WE, leaders and administrators must know about and discover all potential causal relations between TL and workers’ engagement, which might also improve creative performance. As a result, we urge that firms place a larger focus on competent leadership training and development programs. Consequently, reciprocal ties shall develop between leaders and their supporters, perhaps leading to increased devotion to the institution. It will also allow leaders to assess the direct and implicit associations of TL on psychological processes related to WE and EC. Third; to optimize leadership effectiveness and increase employee engagement, creativity, and commitment, leaders must provide incentive plans to their workers, ensure their involvement in decision-making, make impartial decisions, and encourage administrative support. As a result, these findings have valuable management implications for meeting these increasing requirements in modern Pakistani culture. Finally, the findings of this research reveal unique transformational implications that support values that are critical to success. Managers and transformative leaders must offer a supportive vision and mission, foster a culture of robust communal supportive values in institutions, look outside their own interests and individual monetary advantages, and encourage individuals’ creativity, excellence of goods and services, and enduring growth for the welfare of persons and institutions as a whole.

Limitations and future study

The present study possesses specific limitations. The data utilized for this investigation was obtained from a singular source, which may contribute to common method variance (CMV) [93], despite our administration of the questionnaire, assurance of response anonymity, and randomization of all items for each survey [93]. Additionally, we employed Harman’s single-factor method to evaluate bias and found no significant effect. The CFA results indicate that the “four-factor model” had favorable fit indices compared to the other models, with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05). It provided a persuasive indication of diminished common method variance effects [94]. To address the issues caused by common technique variance, future research must apply diverse strategies about employee behavior. Secondly, we based our study on theories proposed in European countries and gathered data to evaluate the suggested model within the context of Pakistani government institutions. Although prior research has established the connections between TL and EC [94, 95]. But to the best of our knowledge no investigations have been conducted to utilize JS and WE as potential mediating factors. Consequently, the results of this research should be applied cross-culturally, and the replication of our findings is expected to have beneficial outcomes. Third, while research participants were sourced from hospitals in Punjab province, subsequent studies should enlist individuals from other regions of the country to enhance the generalizability of the findings. Future researchers must empirically assess our model using data from many regions and Asian cultures to generalize the validity of our results. Further research is necessary in this domain. Ultimately, our research might be duplicated in several industries to examine the essential connections between transformational leadership and employee commitment. Although we utilized structural equation modeling for a simultaneous examination of comprehensive conceptual frameworks in the government sector, the findings must be approached with care. We recommend that an upcoming study address this topic by gathering data from experimental and longitudinal studies across diverse industries to strengthen the core relationship between TL and EC through multiple mediating and moderating variables.

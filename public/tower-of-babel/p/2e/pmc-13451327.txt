Decidual aging in recurrent pregnancy Loss: from regulating networks to therapeutic interventions
Authors: Yu Liu, Wenyan Shi, Zhonglin Jiang, Yujie Xu, Meichen Wang, Xiuju Wang, Hongmei Yin, Dong Wang, Feibo Xu, Yuhui Hao, Dongdong Guan, Lei Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract.The uterine decidua is a key component of the maternal-fetal interface, and its senescence may be an important contributing factor to recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL). Decidual senescence disrupts gestational homeostasis via cell cycle arrest, immune imbalance, and metabolic reprogramming, thereby potentially contributing to RPL. This review offers a new paradigm for understanding RPL etiology, with significant clinical value for improving reproductive outcomes.Subject terms: Cell biology, Developmental biology, Diseases, Stem cells

Introduction

Recurrent pregnancy loss refers to two or more pregnancy losses before the fetus reaches viability. It affects approximately 1–5% of couples of reproductive age1. Etiologies may be attributed to embryonic chromosomal abnormalities, endocrine disorders, reproductive tract abnormalities, and immune dysregulation. However, the etiology remains unknown in over 50% of patients with RPL2, posing a central challenge in clinical diagnosis and treatment. RPL not only causes damage to the reproductive system, such as endometrial thinning and intrauterine adhesions, increasing the risk of secondary infertility3, but also triggers severe psychological issues such as anxiety and depression, disrupting family harmony4.

Decidua is a specialized tissue formed by the decidualization of endometrial stromal cells (EnSCs) under the regulation of progesterone and other factors during pregnancy. It plays important roles in supporting embryo implantation, nutrient supply, establishment of immune tolerance, and regulation of placental development5. During normal pregnancy, a small number of aging cells generated during decidualization are cleared to maintain tissue homeostasis6. However, abnormal accumulation of aging decidual cells (DCs) can lead to cellular dysfunction, exacerbated local inflammation, and deterioration of the microenvironment, ultimately contributing to pregnancy loss7. Studies have reported that the expression of aging markers in the decidual tissues of RPL patients is significantly higher than in normal individuals, and the degree of aging positively correlates with the number of pregnancy losses8. Endometrial aging is not an isolated process; it is closely linked to alterations in the endometrial microenvironment, chronic inflammation, and ovarian aging, collectively influencing pregnancy outcomes. In-depth investigation into the mechanisms linking decidual aging and pregnancy loss will not only enhance the etiological understanding of RPL but also provide a scientific basis for developing specific diagnostic markers, targeted therapies, and personalized intervention strategies. This holds significant clinical importance for improving reproductive outcomes in patients and alleviating the burdens on families and society.

Decidua and pregnancy loss

Physiological characteristics and functions of the decidua

The core cellular component of the uterine decidua is the decidual stromal cell, which is rich in glycogen and lipids, providing an energy reserve for early embryonic development. Additionally, the decidua contains a large number of immune cells and vascular endothelial cells, collectively constituting a functionally complex maternal-fetal microenvironment. The physiological functions of the decidua are threefold: First, it secretes factors such as LIF and IGF to promote embryo adhesion and invasion9. Second, it suppresses maternal immune rejection of the embryo through immunoregulatory mechanisms10. Third, it interacts with trophoblasts to regulate placental angiogenesis and material exchange11. The normal characteristics and functions of decidual tissue constitute the material basis for sustaining embryo implantation and maintaining maternal-fetal immune balance. Decidual dysfunction resulting from decidual senescence may contribute to RPL by affecting processes such as embryo implantation and the establishment of immune tolerance.

Endometrial stem cells (ESCs), such as epithelial stem cells and endometrial mesenchymal stem cells, play a critical role in endometrial regeneration12. ESCs are multipotent cells thought to originate from both the endometrium and the bone marrow. Both ESCs and bone marrow-derived stem cells are essential for key physiological reproductive functions, including decidualization, implantation, and pregnancy maintenance. In patients with RPL, there is not only a deficiency in ESCs but also excessive decidual senescence. This imbalance predisposes to the formation of a pro-inflammatory decidual–placental interface, which is detrimental to pregnancy13.

Dynamic changes and homeostasis of the decidua in normal pregnancy

During normal pregnancy, the uterine decidua undergoes precise, stage-specific changes synchronized with embryonic development. In early pregnancy, following embryo implantation. Factors such as estrogen and progesterone collectively drive the decidualization of EnSCs14. This process is characterized by cellular proliferation and hypertrophy, vascular hyperplasia and dilation, and increased secretion of factors such as LIF and vascular endothelial growth factor(VEGF)15. Concurrently, the population of decidual natural killer (dNK) cells increases. These cells participate in spiral artery remodeling and the establishment of immune tolerance, partly through mechanisms like the TNFSF14/TNFRSF14 axis8, which regulates the clearance of aging cells6. During mid-pregnancy, the immune cell profile shifts to a predominance of M2-type macrophages and regulatory T cells (Tregs), which collectively maintain an anti-inflammatory microenvironment and sustain immune tolerance16. During pregnancy, progesterone indirectly modulates the decidua and immune cells—such as T cells, B cells, and uterine natural killer (uNK) cells—to regulate the immune environment of the endometrium and decidua during implantation and gestation to promote maternal–fetal tolerance17.

Cellular mechanisms of uterine decidual senescence

Multiple studies have validated the close association between decidual aging and RPL. Gene expression profile analysis revealed that in the decidua of RPL patients, the expression of cell cycle regulation genes (CDK2, CCNB1), DNA damage repair genes (BRCA1, ATM), and oxidative stress-related genes (SOD2, CAT) is significantly downregulated. Conversely, the expression of pro-inflammatory genes (IL-6, TNF-α), apoptosis-related genes (Bax, Caspase3)18, and aging-associated molecules (CDC42, CBLL1) is upregulated19. These differentially expressed genes are enriched in pathways related to decidual aging.

Cell cycle arrest

Cell cycle arrest is a core feature of decidual aging, primarily regulated by cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors (CKIs)20. The Cyclin D-CDK4/6 and Cyclin E-CDK2 pathways are core regulatory axes controlling the transition from the G1 phase to the S phase of the cell cycle. In a unexplained recurrent pregnancy loss (URPL) mouse model, during the process of decidualization from gestational day 4 (GD4) to gestational day 8 (GD8), the expression of Cyclin D, Cyclin E, CDK2, CDK4, and CDK6 was significantly downregulated. This downregulation was accompanied by a high rate of embryonic resorption and abnormal histological features in decidual tissues. The decreased expression disrupts the regulation of the G1 phase of the cell cycle, which in turn inhibits the decidualization process, serving as a key mechanism in URPL20. The specific mechanisms underlying this phenomenon are not yet fully understood, necessitating further research and exploration at the cellular level.

Senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)

Aging cells exert their effects through the release of various factors, collectively referred to as the SASP. Studies have shown that circulating concentrations of SASP may reflect an underlying burden of aging cells in various tissues. Aging DCs exhibit a typical SASP, secreting large quantities of pro-inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β), chemokines, and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)6. Senescent decidual cells (snDCs) exhibit characteristics of acute senescent cells and are associated with biological processes involving programmed tissue remodeling, such as embryogenesis and wound healing. The SASP of snDCs exerts a transient role during decidualization with paracrine functions and is rapidly cleared by uNK cells. In patients with RPL, deficiencies in uNK cells lead to impaired clearance of snDCs. This results in the progressive accumulation of chronic senescent cells, and the sustained effects of the SASP contribute to organ dysfunction21. While SASP may participate in tissue remodeling during early decidualization, its persistent presence can induce aging in neighboring healthy cells via a “bystander effect”22, triggering sterile inflammation. Excessive activation of the SASP promotes the skewing of decidual stromal cells (DSCs) towards a pro-inflammatory phenotype, while simultaneously suppressing the differentiation of anti-inflammatory DCs that possess immunomodulatory and trophoblast-supportive functions. This imbalance severely disrupts the immune-tolerant microenvironment essential for embryo implantation23. Concurrently, the elevated levels of MMPs within the SASP lead to excessive degradation of the extracellular matrix (ECM). This process compromises the integrity and stability of the decidual tissue and thus undermines its physical foundation for supporting embryonic development6. This persistent, low-grade chronic inflammatory state, driven by the SASP, serves as a pivotal mechanism underlying the accelerated decline of tissue function24. Consequently, the persistent secretion of SASP drives chronic low-grade inflammation, which not only impairs decidual function but also further accelerates the senescence of additional cells, thus establishing a self-perpetuating vicious cycle. This process is considered a significant underlying mechanism contributing to recurrent pregnancy loss.

Telomere shortening

Telomeres are protective structures at chromosome ends that progressively shorten with each cell division. URPL patients exhibit shorter telomere lengths in DCs compared to the general population25. This suggests that their DCs may have undergone excessive proliferation or stress, leading to a more rapid depletion of their “replicative potential.” In URPL patients, the telomerase activity in DCs is lower than normal levels, rendering it insufficient to effectively counteract telomere shortening caused by cell division and thus exacerbating the shortening process. Once shortened to a critical length, telomeres activate the DNA damage response (DDR) pathway26, which in turn accelerates telomere shortening and leads to increased chromosomal instability27. Telomere shortening may also contribute to embryo implantation failure, decidualization failure, and pregnancy loss by affecting non-coding RNAs (such as miRNAs and lncRNAs) or mRNA stability, leading to dysregulating gene expression28. Therefore, in URPL, accelerated telomere shortening and insufficient telomerase activity in DCs represent a core biological defect. This defect triggers cellular dysfunction and genomic instability, and may further disrupt critical RNA regulatory networks, undermining the decidual microenvironment essential for successful pregnancy and leading to recurrent pregnancy loss.

The role of epigenetics

Animal studies have indicated that in patients with URPL, elevated methylation levels in the promoter region of the LIF gene are observed in decidual tissues. Hypermethylation of the SIRT1 gene promoter leads to reduced expression of SIRT1, which subsequently inhibits the proliferation, adhesion, and migration capabilities of endometrial epithelial cells29. Comparison of endometrial histone modifications between middle-aged patients and young participants revealed that loss of H3K27ac (an activating modification) is a core feature of decidual senescence. In women aged 35 years or older, the activity of HDACs, which regulate H3K27ac, is upregulated in the decidua, while the activity of CBP/p300 is downregulated. This leads to reduced expression of the progesterone receptor (PGR) and decreased transcriptional activity of receptivity-related genes. In a mouse model, the H3K27ac inhibitor A485 induced implantation failure, further confirming the necessity of H3K27ac in the implantation process30. Furthermore, in mouse experiments, aberrant remodeling of H3K27me3 (catalyzed by PRC2/EZH2) during the pre-implantation stage disrupts luminal epithelial (LE) differentiation, leading to loss of receptivity. In aged decidua, H3K9 methylation is elevated, and the EZH2 (enhancer of zeste homolog 2) inhibitor GSK126 can activate decidualization-related genes by downregulating H3K27me331. The aforementioned studies indicate that URPL and decidual senescence are closely associated with erroneous “epigenetic memory” in endometrial cells. DNA hypermethylation and imbalances in histone modifications (loss of activating marks, increase in repressive marks) act synergistically to silence key genes essential for maintaining pregnancy (such as receptivity-related genes and decidualization genes), which in turn leads to embryo implantation failure or early miscarriage. Gene methylation and histone modifications may jointly regulate endometrial receptivity. The dynamic interactions and regulatory functions of epigenetic factors require further investigation.

Decidual aging and alterations in different cell types

Immune cells and decidual aging

The role of uNK cells

uNK cells are the most abundant immune cell population in the early pregnancy decidua. Their unique phenotype (CD56bright CD16-) determines their primary immunoregulatory function32. During normal pregnancy, uNK cells recognize ligands such as MICA/B, which are highly expressed on aging DCs, via the surface receptor NKG2D33, thereby initiating a clearance program to prevent the accumulation of harmful substances. A subset of uNK cells expressing TNFSF14 can interact with TNFRSF14 on DSCs, downregulating SLC3A2 expression in DSCs and reducing branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) uptake. This subsequently inhibits p38 MAPK pathway-mediated senescence of DSCs, decreasing production of the SASP8. Simultaneously, uNK cells secrete factors like VEGF to participate in vascular remodeling and promote placental development32. However, the precise molecular mechanism through which TNFSF14 downregulates SLC3A2 expression remains unclear.

Mechanisms underlying NK cell dysfunction include: In patients with recurrent implantation failure (RIF) and RPL, while the total number of dNK cells shows no significant change, the proportion of the protective dNK1 subset decreases while that of the potentially pathogenic dNK3 subset increases, disrupting immune balance34. Downregulation of NKG2D receptor expression on uNK cells in the decidua, or blockage of its downstream signaling pathways, impedes decidualization and reduces dNK numbers32. In patients with recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL), both TNFSF14 + NK cells and TNFRSF14+ decidual stromal cells (DSCs) are significantly reduced in the decidua35. This results in the loss of function of the “TNFSF14/TNFRSF14 axis” to inhibit the p38 MAPK pathway through the aforementioned mechanisms, leading to excessive senescence of DSCs and exacerbation of SASP-related inflammation, which disrupts maternal-fetal tolerance and potentially contributes to pregnancy failure8. Therefore, the TNFSF14/TNFRSF14 axis serves as a crucial regulatory pathway for maintaining a healthy pregnancy (Fig. 1).

During normal pregnancy, most EnSCs differentiate into DCs that highly express anti-inflammatory and anti-stress genes, while a subset of EnSCs become snDCs that retain a more primitive stress-responsive phenotype. snDCs can rapidly induce DCs to enter a secondary senescent state, and in vitro cell experiments have shown that this process can be inhibited by co-cultured uNK cells36. RT-qPCR analysis and immunohistochemical processing of endometrial biopsy sections revealed a deficiency of NK cells in the pre-pregnancy endometrium of patients with RPL. Consequently, the inhibitory effect of uNK cells on snDCs is insufficient, leading to a shift in cellular distribution from a DC-predominant profile to an snDC-predominant one. These findings indicate that RPL is associated with “excessive cellular senescence” and “uNK cell deficiency.” However, prospective studies are still needed to establish prognostic criteria for screening populations at risk for RPL prior to pregnancy21.

Imbalance in macrophage polarization

In normal pregnancy, decidual macrophages are predominantly of the M2 type, secreting anti-inflammatory factors like IL-10 and TGF-β to maintain immune tolerance and support trophoblast function37. This M2-polarized state is closely associated with successful pregnancy38. RPL exhibits not only a decrease in protective M2-type macrophages but also an overall reduction in macrophage numbers39, which may imply a general weakening of immunoregulatory capacity. Meanwhile, the increase in the pro-inflammatory Mac1 subset exacerbates local inflammation and disrupts immune tolerance. Dysregulation of the PRMT3/ADMA/NO pathway plays a critical role in macrophage dysfunction. In decidual macrophages of patients with RPL, upregulation of PRMT3 leads to elevated ADMA levels, which in turn inhibit NO production. In the context of pregnancy, NO serves as an important vasodilator and anti-apoptotic signaling molecule. Its reduction promotes excessive apoptosis of trophoblasts. Accumulated apoptotic cells that are not cleared in time can trigger secondary inflammatory and autoimmune responses, further aggravating tissue damage and immune imbalance, forming a vicious cycle40 that results in pregnancy loss. Thus, macrophage dysfunction represents a significant immunopathological factor in the etiology of RPL.

Abnormalities in other immune cells

The balance between regulatory T (Treg) cells and T helper 17 (Th17) cells is disrupted in the context of decidual aging. The proportion of Treg cells decreases while that of Th17 cells increases in aging decidua. Factors from the SASP further inhibit Treg cell function while simultaneously promoting Th17 cell differentiation. Th17 cells secrete pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-17, recruiting inflammatory cells including neutrophils, disrupting tissue barriers, and exacerbating local inflammation and immune attack. The Th1/Th2 balance shifts toward a Th1‑type response, leading to increased secretion of pro-inflammatory factors such as IFN-γ41. IFN-γ activates effector cells like macrophages, enhancing their cytotoxic effects against the “allogeneic” fetal trophoblast cells and triggering immune attack pathways. In pregnancy models, B‑cell‑specific deficiency in IL-10 signaling results in dysregulated PD‑1 expression, subsequently inducing immune imbalance at the maternal‑fetal interface. This suggests that aging or the loss of specific signals (e.g., IL‑10) may lead to abnormal PD‑1 expression on B cells, impairing their ability to properly receive or transmit inhibitory signals, with consequent weakening B‑cell‑mediated immune tolerance and potentially disturbing the balance of the entire immune microenvironment42.

Decidual stromal cell aging and fibrosis

CDC42 (Cell Division Cycle 42) is a crucial cell cycle regulatory protein. In patients with RIF, CDC42 expression is significantly downregulated in the endometrium. CDC42 deficiency induces premature aging in EnSCs via activation of the Wnt signaling pathway (specifically through nuclear translocation of β-catenin). This manifests as increased aging -associated markers and abnormal collagen deposition, which disrupts the decidualization process7. CBLL1 (Cbl proto-oncogene-like 1) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase. Its expression is significantly upregulated in the decidual tissue of RPL patients. CBLL1 overexpression promotes EnSC aging by downregulating the PTEN-PI3K-AKT-mTOR/p21 signaling pathway, characterized by increased SA-β-gal positive cells, ROS accumulation, and diminished proliferative capacity19. It mediates EnSC aging, presenting as reduced proliferation and increased collagen deposition. The TGF-β/SMAD pathway in the endometrium promotes senescence of endometrial epithelial cells (ECs) and excessive collagen deposition by upregulating PAI-1. Perivascular ECM remodeling creates a fibrotic microenvironment, leading to reduced vasculature and decreased endometrial receptivity43. The resulting fibrosis further exacerbates decidualization impairment, establishing a “aging-fibrosis-pregnancy loss” axis. In the aged endometrium, the PI3K-AKT-FOXO1 pathway is significantly activated. This leads to phosphorylation and inactivation of FOXO1, which suppresses the expression of key decidualization genes such as IGFBP1 and PRL. This cascade contributes to endometrial fibrosis and diminished receptivity44. Experimental validation has demonstrated that thin endometrium exhibits significant cellular senescence in both stromal and epithelial cells, accompanied by perivascular collagen over-deposition. Moreover, it has been elucidated that impaired EGF, PTN, and TWEAK signaling pathways, along with overactivation of the SEMA3B pathway, inhibit the proliferation of EnSCs45. Radiotherapy may induce damage to the endometrial basal layer cells, compromise endometrial blood flow, increase fibrosis, and accelerate endometrial aging46.

Dual imbalance of inadequate senescence and excessive apoptosis

BMP4 plays a critical role in decidualization. Studies have shown that BMP4 expression is significantly decreased in the decidual tissues of patients with RPL as well as in mouse models47. In vitro experiments further validated that knockdown of BMP4 inhibits the decidualization process of endometrial stromal cells, leading to reduced expression of senescence‑associated proteins (p53, p21, p16), decreased levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and a decline in the number of senescence‑associated β‑galactosidase (SA‑β‑Gal)‑positive cells. Additionally, BMP4 knockdown impairs the migration and invasion abilities of trophoblast cells, a process associated with the FOXO1 regulatory pathway48. Additionally, JAZF1 suppresses the expression of G0S2 by restricting its transcriptional activator Purβ, thereby limiting the activation of the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway and controlling the expression of decidualization markers such as IGFBP1, which in turn regulates the survival and function of human endometrial stromal cells (hESCs). In the decidua of RPL, a consistent pattern of impaired JAZF1, elevated G0S2, excessive apoptosis, and decidualization defects has been observed49. Therefore, it can be concluded that the downregulation of BMP4 leads to insufficient “beneficial senescence” required for decidualization, while the downregulation of JAZF1 results in “excessive apoptosis” of DCs. Together, these factors disrupt the ability of the endometrium to provide a suitable environment for the survival and development of early embryos.

Metabolic reprogramming abnormalities

Disorder of energy metabolism‌

Aging cells accumulate mutations in mitochondrial DNA, exhibit decreased mitochondrial protein function, increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and reduced ATP generation50. ROS attacks telomeric DNA, increasing telomere-associated factors(TAF), exacerbating telomere shortening, mitochondrial dysfunction, and telomere damage, forming a vicious cycle that collectively accelerates decidual aging51. In aging decidua, the expression of key glycolytic enzymes is downregulated, leading to a reduced glycolytic rate and insufficient lactate production. This impairs the energy supply required for embryo implantation. The activity of mTORC1 is significantly lower in the decidual tissue of RPL patients compared to the normal pregnancy group52. Concurrently, the translation of key glycolytic enzymes (e.g., Aldolase A) is inhibited, thereby reducing glycolytic flux. This suggests that the mTOR pathway may influence the decidual microenvironment by regulating glycolysis, contributing to the pathogenesis of RPL52. Impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation function leads to decreased ATP production, further exacerbating decidual cell aging53. Disorders in lipid metabolism disrupt lipid droplet formation, interrupting the lipid supply necessary for decidualization. Excessive lipid accumulation occurs in DSCs and decidual macrophages (dMφs). These lipids are transferred to dMφs via a CD36-dependent mechanism, triggering inflammatory responses and miscarriage54. In the decidua of patients with RPL, glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, and lipid metabolism are all dysregulated. Together, these abnormalities lead to a severe “energy crisis” (insufficient ATP and lactate) and “metabolic stress” (lipotoxic inflammation), which impair the ability of DCs to support embryo implantation and early development, resulting in miscarriage.

Imbalances in amino acid metabolism

An imbalance in the metabolism of branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs, including leucine, isoleucine, and valine) is a significant contributing factor to RPL. A high-leucine diet increases leucine uptake in DSCs via the transporter SLC3A2, activating the ROS/p38 MAPK pathway and inducing aging8. BCAT1 (Branched-chain amino acid transaminase 1) participates in placental inflammatory responses through the NF-κB pathway55. Abnormal glutamine metabolism is also involved in regulation. During normal decidualization, α-ketoglutarate (αKG) derived from glutaminolysis accumulates in ESCs. Acting as a cofactor for demethylases, αKG promotes decidualization by reducing histone methylation levels (e.g., H3K27me3) and supporting ATP production. ESCs from RPL patients exhibit impaired glutamine metabolism, insufficient αKG production leading to abnormal histone methylation levels and reduced energy supply, resulting in abnormal decidualization56. Additionally, activated dNK cells show disrupted glutaminolytic metabolism and inactivation of α‑KG metabolism, resulting in impaired αKG‑mediated EGLN1–HIF‑1α signaling. This reduces the secretion of factors such as IGF‑1 and GDF‑15 by dNK cells, impairing trophoblast invasion and embryonic growth, contributing to pregnancy loss57. Thus, imbalances in branched‑chain amino acid (BCAA) and glutamine metabolism play important roles in the pathogenesis of RPL.

Regulatory roles of hormones and microbiota

Progesterone-estrogen imbalance

The precise balance between progesterone and estrogen is fundamental for maintaining decidual function. Progesterone drives the decidualization of endometrial stromal cells through its receptor (PR), regulates key genes such as IGFBP1, and finely modulates this process via complex signaling networks (e.g., S100A6/RAGE-EGFR/ERK). Simultaneously, it suppresses uterine contractions and inflammation, creating a stable “soil” for the embryo58,59. In conditions such as recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) or advanced maternal age, there exists a dual issue of “relative progesterone insufficiency” and “downregulated PR expression/reduced PR function.” This not only leads to incomplete initiation or poor maintenance of decidualization but also weakens the suppression of harmful uterine contractions and excessive inflammation, impairing the environment for embryo implantation and early development60.

Ovarian aging indirectly accelerates endometrial aging. It impairs ovarian macrophage function, alters the follicular microenvironment, and disrupts the secretory rhythm of estrogen, further exacerbating hormonal imbalance within the decidua61. Advanced maternal age impairs the function of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis, leading to intensified fluctuations in estradiol (E2) levels during the follicular phase, which is significantly associated with reduced rates of clinical pregnancy and live birth62. Aging leads to changes in the ovarian stroma, inhibiting IGF1 expression and estradiol synthesis, which further aggravates HPO axis dysfunction63. Additionally, the aging endometrium exhibits reduced sensitivity to progesterone, establishing a “hormonal imbalance-decidual aging-pregnancy loss” pathway.

Synergistic effect of endometrial microbiome dysbiosis

The homeostasis of the normal endometrial microenvironment is also dependent on a balanced microbiota. The endometrial fluid of healthy individuals harbors a specific microbial community, predominantly composed of beneficial bacteria such as Lactobacillus64,65. This microbiota contributes to endometrial health by modulating local immune responses and maintaining an acidic environment, among other mechanisms65.

The microbial community in the endometrial fluid of RPL patients differs significantly from that of healthy individuals, characterized by a reduced abundance of Lactobacillus and an increased proportion of pathogenic bacteria66. This dysbiosis synergistically promotes decidual aging by activating local immune responses and inducing chronic inflammation. Chronic endometritis triggered by bacterial infection is closely associated with a history of pregnancy loss67, further supporting the role of the microbiome-immune- aging axis in pregnancy failure. Thus, it is evident that a stable endometrial microbiota dominated by Lactobacillus is essential for maintaining a healthy decidual microenvironment.

Assessment system and intervention strategies for uterine decidual aging

Precision assessment system

Based on recent research advancements, a multidimensional evaluation system for decidual senescence has been initially established. First, a combination of molecular biomarkers is utilized, integrating “telomere length + senescence markers + functional molecules + microbial markers.” This includes the measurement of telomere length in decidua/peripheral blood68,69, immunohistochemical detection of p16INK4a/β-galactosidase, mRNA expression levels of LIF/integrin β3/PGR70, protein levels of CDC42/CBLL1, and sequencing of the endometrial fluid microbiome. Second, functional assessments are conducted through the Endometrial Receptivity Array (ERA) to detect gene expression profiles and identify window of implantation shifts71, combined with hysteroscopic assessment of blood perfusion72, assessment of stromal cell decidualization capacity, and evaluation of stem cell regenerative potential73, Third, subtyping diagnosis classifies decidual senescence into physiological, pathological premature, immune-related, and microbially associated types, providing a basis for targeted interventions.

Targeted intervention strategies

Targeted interventions for endometrial aging, decidualization disorders, and recurrent pregnancy loss have evolved into multi-dimensional, precision-based strategies aimed at restoring and remodeling cellular function, metabolism, immune responses, and the microenvironment. Conclusion and future perspectives

Pregnancy loss is a common clinical challenge and a complex diagnostic and therapeutic issue. The decidua, as the core interface of the maternal-fetal unit, impacts pregnancy outcomes. Growing evidence indicates that decidual aging may be an important factor contributing to pregnancy loss and RPL. In patients with RPL, decidual tissue exhibits elevated expression of aging markers, telomere shortening, and mitochondrial dysfunction, accompanied by disruptions in intracellular mechanisms such as cell cycle arrest and excessive activation of the SASP. Within the decidual layer, immune cells show dysregulation, including imbalances in uNK cell subsets and abnormal macrophage polarization. Concurrently, aging of decidual stromal cells and diminished regenerative capacity of stem cells collectively exacerbate decidual dysfunction. Furthermore, abnormalities in metabolic reprogramming, progesterone-estrogen imbalance, and endometrial microbiome dysbiosis further construct a complex pathological network, leading to pregnancy loss by impairing placental development. To address current challenges, future research should focus on the following directions: Developing non-invasive diagnostic techniques. Constructing experimental models such as decidual organoids derived from human embryonic stem cells, human-mouse chimeric implantation models, and microbe-decidua co-culture systems. These models, combined with gene editing technologies, will enable precise investigation of aging pathways74. Conducting large-scale clinical trials targeting epigenetic and immune pathways, as well as evaluating novel therapeutics.

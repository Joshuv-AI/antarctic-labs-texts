Real-world comparative effectiveness of first-line BTK inhibitors in chronic lymphocytic leukemia
Authors: Jing-Zhou Hou, Rushir Choksi, Gregory A. Maglinte, Xiaoliang Wang, Pinyao (Anna) Rui, Manasi Suryavanshi, Daniel Allendorf, Taral Patel, Ali Dadla, Annette Fontaine, Pornchai Jonglertham, Lindsay Aton, Melissa Hagan, Lisa Morere, Jeffrey Scott, Rhys Williams, Daniel A. Ermann
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionSecond-generation covalent Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitors (cBTKi), acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib, are standard-of-care for treatment-naïve adults with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). However, real-world evidence remains limited.MethodsThis real-world analysis compared acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib using the IntegraConnect PrecisionQ de-identified electronic health record database, representing >3 million cancer patients across 500+ US sites. Patients initiated cBTKi between January 2020 and November 2023, with follow-up through 3 July 2024.ResultsThis study included 473 patients; 184 zanubrutinib-treated patients matched to 289 acalabrutinib-treated patients by age and sex. Baseline characteristics were similar. Median time to discontinuation (TTD) for acalabrutinib was 29.5 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 21.2, not reached [NR]). Median TTD for zanubrutinib was NR, with significantly longer duration of treatment than acalabrutinib (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR]: 0.50, 95% CI 0.3–0.7; p < .01). The 12-month probability of remaining on treatment with zanubrutinib (83.3%) was higher than acalabrutinib (69.5%). Toxicity-related discontinuation was higher with acalabrutinib (10.7%) than zanubrutinib (5.4%). Median time to next treatment was 42.5 months for acalabrutinib (95% CI 33.2-NR) and NR for zanubrutinib (aHR 0.82; 95% CI 0.5–1.4).ConclusionZanubrutinib demonstrated longer treatment persistence and fewer discontinuations attributed to toxicity during chart abstraction than acalabrutinib in treatment-naïve CLL.

Introduction

Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitors have been a mainstay of treatment for chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) for over a decade. Ibrutinib was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of patients with CLL who received at least one prior therapy in 2014 [1,2]. In 2016, ibrutinib’s indication was expanded to frontline treatment of CLL based on the RESONATE-2 trial, which demonstrated an 84% reduction in the risk of progression or death and significantly longer progression-free survival (PFS) compared with chlorambucil [3]. Despite its efficacy, ibrutinib has been associated with significant cardiovascular toxicity and rates of adverse events (AEs) leading to a discontinuation range from 11% to >50% [4–7].

Second-generation covalent BTK (cBTK) inhibitors were developed to enhance selectivity for BTK, aiming to reduce the side effects associated with off-target engagement [8]. Acalabrutinib, a second-generation cBTK inhibitor, received FDA approval in 2019 based on the phase III ELEVATE-TN and ASCEND trials [9,10]. In 2023, the FDA approved zanubrutinib for the treatment of CLL/SLL based on the phase III SEQUOIA and ALPINE trials [11,12]. Clinical trials have investigated the safety and efficacy profiles of second-generation cBTK inhibitors compared to ibrutinib. Zanubrutinib and acalabrutinib are associated with lower rates of AEs, including atrial fibrillation and hypertension, compared with ibrutinib [13]. In the ALPINE trial comparing zanubrutinib with ibrutinib, zanubrutinib demonstrated superior overall response rate (ORR) and PFS, including in high-risk populations with 17p deletions, TP53 mutations, or both [13]. In addition, zanubrutinib’s superior efficacy is likely attributed to high selectivity [14]. The ELEVATE-RR trial, which compared acalabrutinib with ibrutinib, demonstrated that acalabrutinib was noninferior to ibrutinib, with similar PFS and ORR to ibrutinib [15,16].

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines (Version 3.2025; updated April 2025) position both acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib as category 1 recommendations within the preferred regimens for the treatment of CLL [17]. Head-to-head comparative clinical trials have not been conducted between acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib. In the absence of these head-to-head trials, a match-adjusted indirect comparison analysis, using individual patient-level data from the pivotal ALPINE and ASCEND trials, demonstrated that zanubrutinib had significantly longer investigator-assessed PFS and higher complete response rates and overall survival (OS) compared with acalabrutinib [10,12,13]. However, there is a need for real-world evidence to better understand treatment outcomes in clinical practice. To address this gap, this retrospective, observational analysis uses real-world community data from the IntegraConnect PrecisionQ de-identified electronic health record (EHR) database and builds upon a prior analysis to compare treatment duration and discontinuation rates for acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib, providing practical insights into how these therapies perform outside of clinical trial environments [18].

Methods

This analysis was conducted using data from the PrecisionQ de-identified database, which contains EHR data for over 3 million cancer patients treated across more than 500 sites in the United States (US), primarily within community oncology settings. A prior study included a total of 600 adult patients with physician-confirmed CLL who initiated first-line therapy with either acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib between 1 January 2020 and 30 November 2023, with outcome collections from 1 January 2020 through 3 July 2024 [18]. The index date was defined as the initiation of acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib. Patients were excluded if they were participating in a clinical trial or being treated for another primary cancer during the study observation period.

In that study, 200 patients treated with first-line zanubrutinib were randomly selected and matched 1:2 with 400 patients treated with first-line acalabrutinib based on exact age (in years) and sex. The acalabrutinib-treated patients were randomly selected without replacement from the eligible pool of patients treated with first-line acalabrutinib to minimize selection bias. This current analysis utilizes the same cohort of 600 patients and specifically evaluates those who received monotherapy with zanubrutinib or acalabrutinib. Of the 600 patients, a total of 473 patients received monotherapy (79%): 289 treated with acalabrutinib (72% of its group) and 184 treated with zanubrutinib (92% of its group). The analysis was limited to patients receiving BTK inhibitor monotherapy to minimize the confounding effects of combination regimens and concomitant medications on real-world clinical outcomes, including survival and treatment discontinuation, and to more confidently attribute treatment discontinuation due to BTK inhibitor-related toxicity.

Clinical information from EHR unstructured fields (e.g., physician notes, pathology, radiology reports, biomarker reports) was manually curated and abstracted according to a standardized electronic case report form. Categorical variables were reported as counts and proportions, and continuous variables were reported as median (interquartile range [IQR]). The primary clinical outcomes of interest were time to discontinuation (TTD) or death, time to next treatment (TTNT) or death, and OS. TTD was defined as the time from the index date to the discontinuation of index treatment or death. TTNT was defined as the time from the index date to the initiation of a subsequent line of therapy or death. Patients who did not discontinue their index treatment or initiate a subsequent line of therapy were censored at the last contact date. OS was defined as the time from the index date to the death date. Kaplan–Meier medians, event-free probabilities, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported for these primary outcomes. Hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated using Cox proportional hazard models, adjusted for age, sex, del(17p) or TP53 mutations, unmutated IGHV, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status. Reasons for discontinuation of index treatment were manually extracted from unstructured fields from the EHR and were reported using counts and percentages. Curators used EHR data to determine if a clinician states that there are toxicities in a patient’s chart and selects toxicity as the reason for discontinuation. However, specific adverse events behind toxicity-coded discontinuation were not documented. SAS version 9.4 and R Studio version 1.4.1106 were used for statistical analyses. No institutional review board/independent ethics committee review was required, as this is a secondary analysis of de-identified existing data.

Results

Discontinuation and next treatment

The median TTD was not reached (NR) in patients treated with zanubrutinib. For acalabrutinib-treated patients, the median TTD was 29.5 months (95% CI 21.2, NR). The adjusted HR (aHR) for the probability of remaining on treatment, with acalabrutinib as the reference, was 0.50 (95% CI 0.32, 0.74; p < 0.01) (Figure 1). At 12 months, the probability of remaining on treatment was significantly higher for patients treated with zanubrutinib (83.3%; 95% CI 76.2, 88.4) compared with those treated with acalabrutinib (69.5%; 95% CI 63.7, 74.5). Real-world overall survival

The median OS was NR for patients with acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib (Figure 4). After adjusting for age, sex, ECOG performance status, and biomarkers (TP53, del[17p], and IGHV), the aHR, with acalabrutinib as the reference, was 0.90 (95% CI 0.4, 1.9; p = 0.76). At 12 months, the probability of survival was numerically higher for patients treated with zanubrutinib (92.3%; 95% CI 87.0, 95.5) compared with those treated with acalabrutinib (89.3%; 95% CI 84.9, 92.5). Discussion

With the approvals of acalabrutinib in 2019 and zanubrutinib in 2023 for the treatment of CLL, the therapeutic landscape has shifted decisively toward these second-generation cBTK inhibitors. This preference is largely driven by their improved safety profiles, particularly a reduction in the incidence of cardiovascular AEs compared with ibrutinib [4,19]. In head-to-head randomized clinical trials for the treatment of relapsed and refractory CLL, zanubrutinib demonstrated superiority to ibrutinib with respect to progression of disease or death [13]. The superior efficacy of zanubrutinib has been largely attributed to the potency and high selectivity for BTK [14].

The head-to-head clinical trial comparing zanubrutinib and ibrutinib is the only study to date specifically designed to demonstrate superiority to ibrutinib. However, acalabrutinib has shown comparable PFS to ibrutinib [15]. In the absence of head-to-head clinical trials to compare the efficacy of acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib, an indirect treatment comparison, using data from pivotal randomized clinical trials, demonstrated that zanubrutinib offers significantly improved PFS compared to acalabrutinib [12]. In addition, the results from this real-world analysis further support that zanubrutinib has favorable outcomes in key areas relevant to treatment decision-making. It also reinforces that the treatment durability observed with second-generation cBTK inhibitors remains consistent with clinical trial findings [9–11,13,15,20].

Notably, patients treated with zanubrutinib remained on therapy significantly longer than those on acalabrutinib, with 83.3% of patients remaining on zanubrutinib at 12 months compared to 69.5% of patients on acalabrutinib. Similarly, patients treated with zanubrutinib had significantly lower discontinuation rates compared with acalabrutinib. The reasons for differences in treatment durability between acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib remain unclear. However, the most commonly reported reason for discontinuation of both second-generation cBTK inhibitors was toxicity. In our analysis, discontinuation attributed to toxicity during chart abstraction occurred in 10.7% of acalabrutinib-treated patients (median follow-up of 15.8 months) and 5.4% of zanubrutinib-treated patients (median follow-up of 11.1 months), aligning with rates observed in pivotal clinical trials for both acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib. In particular, AEs led to acalabrutinib discontinuation in 8.9% of patients in the ELEVATE-TN clinical trial (median follow-up of 28.3 months) and 11% in the ASCEND clinical trial (median follow-up of 16.1 months), while the rate of zanubrutinib discontinuation was 8.7% in the SEQUOIA clinical trial (median follow-up of 26.2 months) and 15.4% in the ALPINE clinical trial (median follow-up of 29.6 months) [9–11]. Of note, the observed lower rate of discontinuation attributed to toxicity during chart abstraction for patients treated with zanubrutinib in our study may be influenced by its shorter follow-up period.

The follow-up time between the zanubrutinib group (11.1 months; range 2.3, 32.2) and the acalabrutinib group (15.8 months; range 1.3, 49.8) was imbalanced, reflecting different regulatory milestones, staggered adoption in clinical practice, and enrollment timing. The imbalanced and relatively short follow-up duration, along with the modest sample size, may limit the interpretability of the findings and constrain the ability to adequately adjust for baseline imbalances. As with all retrospective observational studies, the potential for unmeasured confounding variables cannot be accounted for, as certain clinical factors influencing treatment selection and discontinuation could not be fully captured. Residual confounding factors, unmeasured baseline differences, or design-related factors may impact the interpretation of results despite matching based on age and sex. For instance, biomarker testing was low for many key biomarkers (e.g., TP53), limiting the ability to address these covariates in the matching or adjustment methods and potentially reflecting real-world community practices. The study period also overlapped with the COVID-19 pandemic, which may limit the generalizability of these results to other time periods. Additionally, as the CLL treatment landscape includes time-limited approaches and increasingly complex treatment algorithms (e.g., combinations of anti-CD20 agents, BCL2 inhibitors, and BTK inhibitors), detailed understanding of the factors that delineate and influence treatment decision-making is of great importance.

Given the limited 3-year follow-up period and small cohort, results should be interpreted with caution. However, these findings highlight the need for future research with longer-term follow-up to understand long-term time on treatment and discontinuation rates.

Conclusion

In this real-world analysis, treatment with zanubrutinib was associated with a significantly longer time to discontinuation compared with acalabrutinib, with numerically longer TTNT although not statistically significant. Zanubrutinib was also associated with lower rates of discontinuation attributed to toxicity during chart abstraction compared with acalabrutinib, suggesting potential advantages in tolerability and treatment durability. Continued follow-up is warranted to further characterize long term treatment persistence and discontinuation patterns. As the CLL treatment landscape continues to evolve, real-world comparative data will play an increasingly critical role in guiding therapy decisions.

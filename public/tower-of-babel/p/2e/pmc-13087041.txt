Harnessing multi-modal deep learning for multi-drone navigation-based trajectory prediction system
Authors: Abdulrahman Alzahrani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn recent times, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), also known as drones find useful in different application areas like military, healthcare, logistics, image and video mapping, precision farming, wireless communication and aerial surveillance. Research works on multi-drone collaborative flight path planning has drawn extensive attention in the domain of drones, representing singular benefits in large-scale monitoring, difficult task performance, and disaster response. Among the core technologies in multi-UAV collaborative operations, advancements in trajectory planning play a crucial role in ensuring the safety and efficiency of these coordinated missions. A trajectory design of the drone certainly plays a significant part in the application performance, efficiency, and development. By leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML), UAVs can effectively perceive their surroundings and make more advanced decisions. This paper proposes a navigation-based Trajectory Prediction System using Multi-Modal Deep Architecture (NTP-MMDA) model. The primary intention of the NTP-MMDA model is to develop an intelligent multi-drone navigation system for accurate trajectory prediction to ensure coordinated path planning and collision avoidance. At first, the NTP-MMDA technique performs data pre-processing by using the quantile normalization method to ensure uniformity. Furthermore, the trajectory prediction process is mainly executed by three models, such as bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU), variational autoencoder (VAE), and adaptive deep belief network (ADBN). The comparison study of the NTP-MMDA approach portrayed a superior performance, achieving the lowest MSE of 0.0021 among all models under the Drone Trajectory dataset.Keywords: Multi-drone, Trajectory prediction, Multi-modal deep architecture, RMSprop, Navigation system, Quantile normalizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Trajectory generation plays a vital role in implementing intricate autonomous vehicle tasks1. It involves the computational task of determining an efficient trajectory that moves a vehicle from a starting point within an area containing fixed and/or dynamic obstacles2. In most cases, the primary goal is not just to identify a viable trajectory between the start and end points, but to derive the best trajectory according to a specific objective function. Within this setting, trajectory generation is expressed as an optimization issue with a cost function that measures how well the mission objectives are met, along with various constraints to ensure the resulting trajectories are both safe and achievable3. With the fast development of communication and computing systems, autonomous vehicles are increasingly being deployed in more challenging operations, sometimes working in coordinated groups to complete highly demanding tasks. This requires adding inter-vehicle collision avoidance constraints into the optimization framework to ensure the generated trajectories for multiple vehicles in a shared space are free from collisions4. The key difficulty is making sure individual decisions also meet the shared collision avoidance constraints. UAVs or drones have become widely used due to their effectiveness, adaptability, and mobility across different mission scenarios5. One of the expanding uses is aiding search and rescue (SAR), thanks to drones’ ability to navigate in difficult, disaster-impacted environments. In particular, the developments of autonomous drones with sophisticated control systems and sensors, namely LiDAR and thermal imaging sensors, have transformed SAR operations6.

Autonomous drones can gather information effectively during flight, assisting in tasks such as quick damage evaluation, detecting survivors, and improving awareness in disaster zones. Furthermore, autonomous drones can plan flight paths without direct human control, making them capable of adjusting to changing environmental situations often faced during emergency response7. In the aftermath of disasters, the capacity to access remote or dangerous places rapidly is key to effective aid8. Autonomous drones can move through wreckage, uneven surfaces, and tough weather, allowing them to deliver crucial help where it is most needed. This autonomy also helps remove the need for human teams to perform repeated and risky tasks, thereby boosting efficacy and safety in disaster SAR operations9. Specific pattern-based navigation techniques track defined visual patterns to determine position in unfamiliar environments10. A computer vision (CV)-based pattern recognition model by implementing a convolutional neural network (CNN) methodology for location estimation is used to detect object angle, shade, edge, and center to assist the controller in making decisions. Drone movement based on the external piloting technique presents a challenge in execution11. Depending on how the controller is built, drone piloting is either directed by the user or the drone itself. In film-making with multiple drones, the primary task is to identify separate objects using various sets of drone images in logical sequences. CV-based drone navigation supports accurate landing12.

Proposed methodology

In this paper, the NTP-MMDA approach is proposed. The primary intention of the NTP-MMDA approach is to develop an intelligent multi-drone navigation system for accurate trajectory prediction to ensure coordinated path planning and collision avoidance. It contains distinct kinds of processes involved as input data standardization, ensemble models using navigation prediction, and a fine-tuned optimizer. Input data processing

At first, the NTP-MMDA technique employs data pre-processing21. This process effectively mitigates noise while also ensuring uniformity. The reliability of subsequent predictions is also improved by providing standardized inputs, thus resulting in more accurate and robust trajectory predictions. The approach also reduces variability and computational complexity, compared to raw or minimally processed data, while also enabling real-time multi-drone navigation with higher precision and efficiency.

Quantile Normalization is a prevailing data pre-processing model employed to create the distributions of various samples or features statistically similar. It is mainly beneficial to manage high-dimensional datasets like sensor or genomic data, where measurements through features can differ in scale but should have comparable distributions. This model sorts every feature, calculates the average of sorted values across samples, and substitutes the original values with these averaged ranks. Quantile normalization aids in decreasing technical variation and creates data more comparable for tasks of downstream analysis, such as regression or classification. It is broadly employed in NLP, bioinformatics, and image processing tasks.

Ensemble models using navigation prediction

Afterwards, the navigation prediction process is mainly executed by three models, such as the BiGRU, the VAE, and the ADBN classifier. The bidirectional temporal dependencies are effectually captured by the BiGRU model, while VAE properly models uncertainty through latent representations. Furthermore, most crucial hierarchical features are extracted by the ADBN model and the outputs of these techniques are incorporating by combining forward and backward states of BiGRU. Moreover, efficient final trajectory prediction is achieved by the VAE’s latent reconstructions, and ADBN’s class probabilities. Thus, the approach utilizes temporal, probabilistic, and feature-based information, enabling precise, adaptive, and reliable multi-drone navigation. The outputs of BiGRU, VAE, and ADBN are incorporate into a unified representation to produce the final trajectory prediction. Also, though ADBN is a classifier, is adapted to regression by giving continuous trajectory coordinates, allowing it to model intrinsic non-linear relationships while assisting accurate multi-drone trajectory prediction.

VAE method

VAEs provide a probability-based model for generative modelling23. VAEs are a form of AE, which is a neural network (NN) trained for learning the compressed model (encoder) of its input and then rebuilding the input from this encoder. A VAE includes 2 major modules: encoder and decoder.

The Encoder (Inference Network) takes an input data point, and outputs the parameters of the likelihood distribution in the latent space, normally a variance and a mean for the Gaussian distribution. This distribution, , signifies the encoding representation of the input.

The Decoder (Generative Network) takes a point, % sampled from the latent distribution, and tries to reconstruct the original input, It describes the conditional likelihood distribution, The training aim of the VAE is to maximize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), which is a lower limit on the marginal log-likelihood of the data. The ELBO includes 2 terms that is Reconstruction Loss; This term boosts the decoding to correctly reconstruct the input data and Divergence of Kullback‐Leibler (KL); This term perform as a regularizer, compelling the learned latent distribution, be adjacent to a prior distribution, which is naturally a normal standard distribution. Conclusion

In this article, the NTP-MMDA methodology is developed. The major purpose of the NTP-MMDA methodology is to advance an intelligent multi-drone navigation system for precise trajectory prediction to assure coordinated path planning and collision avoidance. It comprises diverse types of processes involved as input data standardization, ensemble technique utilizing navigation prediction, and fine-tuned optimizer. Primarily, the NTP-MMDA approach utilizes a data pre-processing phase by the quantile normalization model to provide uniformity. Then, the navigation prediction process is chiefly executed by three methods, like the BiGRU model, VAE technique, and ADBN classifier. The comparison study of the NTP-MMDA approach portrayed a superior performance, achieving the lowest MSE of 0.0021 among all models under the Drone Trajectory dataset.

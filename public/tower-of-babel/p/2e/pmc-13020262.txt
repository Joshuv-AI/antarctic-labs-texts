Private charging pile owners’ sharing intention: evidence from China
Authors: Yi Yu, Kangxin Pan, Donglan Zha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe sharing of private charging pile (PCP) can significantly alleviate the construction pressure on public charging infrastructure and benefit for low carbon travel. However, the PCP sharing is in a nascent state with a low market share in reality. Thus, we construct a comprehensive TAM-UTAUT structural equation model to explore the factors influencing the sharing intention of PCP from 660 survey responses of PCP owners. We also analyze the differences across heterogeneous groups. Our finding indicates that perceived trust, performance expectancy, social influence, and incentive policies have a positive impact on the sharing intention of PCP, with incentive policies exhibiting the strongest effect, followed by social influence. Interestingly, female owners’ sharing intention is more responsive to both shared revenue and social conformity than that of male owners, whereas male owners tend to have greater concern regarding sharing risks. Younger owner groups are more significantly influenced by the practical effectiveness of sharing, while middle-aged and elderly groups pay more attention to policy incentives and sharing-related risks. Owners without private parking spaces are more influenced by the practical effectiveness of sharing. In contrast, owners with private spaces are more attentive to sharing risks and policy support. Based on the findings, we propose specific recommendations for both the government and the charging service operators to further promote the sharing of PCP.Keywords: Private electric vehicle charging pile sharing, TAM-UTAUT structural equation model, Sharing intention, Heterogeneous groups analysis

Introduction

How to hasten the coordinated advancement of low-carbon and green energy transition is an essential task for Chinese government [13]. Developing the electric vehicle (EV) industry is regarded as an essential way to alleviate the energy safety problem and reduce carbon emissions in the transportation industry [42, 47, 49]. Therefore, driven by government incentive policies and advancements in EV manufacturing technology in recent years, Chinese EV sales have surged, reaching up to 12.87 million in 2024 [14]. However, with the rapidly increasing number of adopted EVs, the charging infrastructure hasn’t kept up with the pace, which has become the key factor impeding the massive scale adoption of EVs [4, 28, 37]. Thus, the Chinese government has announced that a high-quality charging infrastructure system with extensive coverage, moderate scale, reasonable structure, and complete functions will be established to support the development of the EV industry and effectively meet the people’s travel charging needs by 20301.

With the development of charging infrastructure in China, the total number of EV charging infrastructure in China had reached 17.348 million units as of August 2025, with approximately 75% being PCP2. The ratio of EVs to charging piles in China was 2.45:1 by the end of 2024, which is much smaller than the objective of 1:1 announced by the Chinese government3. However, the idle rate of PCPs remains high, leading to an inefficient use of charging facility resources.Velkovski et al., [24], Wang, [28]. Meanwhile, the public charging piles are in short supply. To overcome the contradictory issue, PCP sharing has emerged as an innovative solution to address the charging challenges of EVs [29, 38]. This refers to the practice through which PCP owners make their idle PCPs available to other EV users via an online platform. The charging facility operators, such as Star Charge and TELD in China, have developed the platforms to help PCP owners realize revenue by opening and sharing their facilities4. However, as of February 2025, only 481,000 PCPs had been made available for sharing, accounting for just 5% of the total PCPs5.

As an emerging operation mode, PCP sharing not only improves the utilization rate of PCP and helps alleviate supply-demand imbalance for EV charging [20] but also reduces the need for constructing additional public charging facilities, thereby saving social resources and improving EV adaption [10, 45]. The Chinese government has recently implemented a series of supporting policies for PCP sharing, such as “neighboring parking space sharing” and “multiple vehicles sharing one pile.” However, PCP sharing remains at an early stage, with various obstacles impeding its broader adoption. From the perspective of the PCP owner, PCP sharing, while potentially beneficial, also entails safety risks, depreciation costs, and privacy leakage risk during the sharing process. For example, issues such as damage to PCP during sharing, disputes over unclear division of liability for accidents, difficulties in coordinating charging times, and the potential commercial use of personal information remain prevalent.

Except for the risk and benefit concern, the PCP owners’ sharing behavior may also be affected by the social influence or herd effects [1]. According to the Diffusion of Innovation theory, which emphasizes the pivotal role of social interaction in the spread of new practices, PCP owners—by virtue of their inherent attribute as social beings—are inherently susceptible to the attitudes of their social circles. Specifically, the explicit support or opposition from key social contacts such as friends and neighbors can significantly shape PCP owners’ intention to engage in sharing behavior [5]. Meanwhile, for PCP owners, perceived trust is a foundational prerequisite for their sharing intention, mainly reflected in trust in the platform (guaranteeing safety, addressing failures promptly, and protecting privacy) and trust in other users.

All of the above factors may affect the widespread use of the PCP sharing model. Till now, very limited studies have focused on what and how the factors affect the PCP owners’ sharing intention. However, solving this problem can directly provide effective suggestions for the promotion of subsequent PCP sharing. As such, we construct a comprehensive model by combining the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), referred to as the TAM-UTAUT model. Based on the survey on the PCP owners, this study identifies the key impact factors influencing their sharing attitude and intention, and explores how these factors affect it. We prove that the incentive policy plays the most significant role, followed by community influence. Due to the heterogeneous individual characteristics, the impact factors on the PCP owners’ sharing intention may also differ. Hence, we further make a heterogeneous group analysis. Interestingly, our results show that the differences in willingness to share private charging piles among groups with different ages, genders, and whether they have private parking spaces are also quite significant. As such, our investigation may bridge the gap in what and how the factors affect the PCP sharing intention from the perspective of PCP owners. Besides, we can also give specific promotional advice according to the different features of different groups.

Literature review

The relevant research associated with PCP sharing mainly focuses on three aspects. First, it’s about pricing strategies for PCP sharing. The majority of the researches focus on the charging price of public charging station [2, 23, 44, 49] and joint optimization of charging station investment and charging pricing [41, 46], nevertheless very a few study focus on the model of PCP sharing. Zhao et al., [48] constructed a bilateral bargaining game model to explore the pricing strategy of PCP sharing under information asymmetry, emphasizing that PCP owners need to integrate the bilateral pricing strategy with power retailers and EV users to determine the optimal quotation. Tucker and Alizadeh, [22] constructed a combinatorial auction online pricing mechanism, which can serve as both an admission control tool for EV users and a means of allocating limited charging facility resources. Wang et al., [30] introduced a new allocation mechanism combining risk, investment, and service quality factors, revealing that the evolution of the charging pile sharing system is mainly driven by the pricing of PCP.

Second, the related research is associated with the factors that influence the intention to share PCP. Existing studies can be categorized into three dimensions: technical security, economic, policy incentives, and social factors. First, in terms of technical security, Moghadasi et al., [17] noted that the security of the vehicle-to-grid system and hardware supply chain is a prerequisite for PCP owners to accept sharing; security vulnerabilities will directly reduce their sharing intention. Second, for economic and policy incentives, Akbari and Dean, [1] pointed out that residents’ clear cognition of the sharing model and expectations of economic benefits are core influencing mechanisms—when individuals can clearly perceive the actual value of sharing, their participation intention increases significantly. On the policy side, Mpoi et al., [18] found that subsidies from the government or platforms can effectively attract pile owners to participate. Khodoomi et al., [12] proposed that reasonable pricing (based on the improved Shapley value method) can balance the interests of pile owners and users; Wang et al.,; [30] further proved that a fair benefit distribution mechanism is key to maintaining long-term cooperation, and constructed an economic coordination theory from pricing and distribution dimensions. Third, social factors also play a role. Shi et al., [20] investigated that social elements such as community environment and neighborhood relations help shape residents’ supportive attitudes toward PCP sharing. Yao et al., [39] demonstrated that an innovative PCP sharing business model and vehicle-to-grid energy management framework, supported by intelligent dispatching and multi-objective optimization, can improve PCP utilization and reduce costs. Meanwhile, Touker et al., [21] summarized in their research on private charging pile sharing that spatial factors such as urban density and parking conditions are core variables influencing the supply-demand matching of private charging pile sharing.

Third, it’s about the research on subjects related to consumption in the PCP sharing market. Existing studies can be categorized by different subject groups: core subjects (PCP owners and non-owner consumers), extended subjects (EV owners), and peripheral subjects (renewable energy community members). First, for the core subjects, Hu et al., [10] took PCP owners and non-owners as core research subjects, finding that PCP owners have strong sharing intentions, while non-owner consumers prefer private charging piles with lower prices than public ones. Zhu et al., [50] further pointed out that owners’ sharing intentions are reflected in their strategic choices of participating in sharing platforms, and PCP owners tend to be pragmatic when forming sharing intentions. Second, for extended subjects, there are characteristic differences in EV users’ sharing intentions. From the perspective of EV owners, Zhang et al., [43] found that car owners pay more attention to convenience and social influence rather than personal attitudes. Yang and Peng, [35] found that the proportion of community-resident EV owners who support PCP sharing is relatively high. Third, for peripheral subjects, beyond the above main subjects, other stakeholders also play roles in the EV PCP market. Velkovski et al., [24] took renewable energy community members and external EV owners as subjects for research, pointing out that external EV owners are willing to charge at community charging piles, and community members are also willing to share charging pile resources.

From the review of the related research, we can see that the current research mainly focuses on PCP sharing pricing, factors influencing sharing intention, and market consumption subjects, but there are still the following research gaps existing in this research. Firstly, as an emerging operation mode, most existing results explore its pricing and the benefit distribution among various subjects through models, lacking a systematic and comprehensive analysis of the multi-dimensional influencing factors existing in PCP sharing in reality. Secondly, most existing studies rely on hypothetical models constructed by theory and lack the real situation of PCP owners and the support of relevant data. Given these research gaps, this study will take private charging pile owners as the research objects, focus on their sharing intention, and deeply explore the key factors influencing the intention to share PCP and their promotion strategies, aiming to provide references for the government to formulate practical promotion policies.

Model construction and data collection

Structure of the SEM

We choose Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) as the primary analytical method. This choice reflects two key strengths of SEM: it enables simultaneous estimation of both measurement relationships and structural relationships—matching the multi-dimensional, interconnected nature of the research framework. It also addresses limitations of traditional regression (e.g., accounting for measurement error and assessing construct validity), critical for analyzing latent variables like sharing attitude and perceived risk.

SEM serves two core functions here: the measurement model verifies the reliability and validity of latent variable operationalization (linking theoretical constructs to survey items), while the structural model tests hypothesized causal pathways among latent variables to evaluate the proposed research model.

Model hypotheses

Performance expectancy refers to the degree of expectation of EV owners regarding the benefits they can obtain from participating in PCP sharing The perceived benefits of platform usage significantly influence consumers’ attitudes toward the platform and their intention to continue using it, as confirmed by a study on Chinese consumers’ adoption of skill-sharing service platforms [6]. Similarly, in the context of PCP sharing, the higher PCP owners’ performance expectancy for participating in sharing, the more positive their attitudes toward private pile sharing will be. Based on the above analysis, this study proposes the following hypothesis:

H1: Performance expectancy has a positive impact on attitudes toward private electric vehicle (EV) charging pile sharing

Perceived trust denotes the level of trust EV owners place in relevant stakeholders during the PCP sharing process. Higher trust in robo-advisors leads to more positive usage attitudes, as demonstrated in a study on users’ adoption intentions of robo-advisors. Wu et al., [31] In PCP sharing, the greater the EV owners’ perceived trust in relevant stakeholders, such as sharing platforms and other users, the more positive their attitudes toward private pile sharing will be. Thus, this study puts forward the following hypothesis:

H2: Perceived trust has a positive impact on attitudes toward private electric vehicle (EV) charging pile sharing

Social influence refers to the effect of the social groups to which PCP owners belong on their participation in PCP sharing. Social influence exerts a significant positive effect on the public’s acceptance of responsible innovation, meaning that the attitudes and behaviors of surrounding groups influence individuals’ intentions, as shown in a study taking intelligent connected vehicles as an example [16]. In PCP sharing, the more positive the social influence perceived by surrounding groups, the stronger their intention to participate in PCP sharing will be. Therefore, this study proposes the following hypothesis:

H3: Social influence has a positive impact on intention to share private charging piles

Incentive policies refer to various encouraging measures introduced for the sharing of private EV charging piles. Incentive policies have a significant positive impact on consumers’ intention to purchase EVs, as found in a study on EV purchase intentions [34]. In PCP sharing, the stronger the incentive policies perceived by EV owners, the higher their intention to participate in private pile sharing tends to be. Based on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H4: Incentive policies have a positive impact on intention to share private charging piles

Perceived risk represents EV owners’ anticipation of potential adverse situations during their participation in PCP sharing. Perceived risk exerts a significant negative impact on enterprises’ adoption intentions, as indicated in a study on small and medium-sized manufacturing enterprises’ intention to adopt cloud services [26]. In PCPS, the higher the EV owners’ perceived risk of participation, the lower their intention to engage in sharing usually is. Thus, this study puts forward the following hypothesis:

H5: Perceived risk has a negative impact on intention to share private charging piles

Attitudes toward PCP sharing refer to EV owners’ psychological tendencies toward the behavior of PCP sharing itself. Sharing attitudes have a significant positive impact on sharing intentions, as discovered in a study on patients’ intention to share medical data [11] In PCP sharing, the more positive EV owners’ attitudes toward private pile sharing, the stronger their intention to participate in sharing tends to be. Based on the above analysis, this study proposes the following hypothesis:

H6: Attitudes toward private electric vehicle (EV) charging pile sharing have a positive impact on intention to share private charging piles

To deeply investigate the key factors affecting PCP owners’ intention of sharing, we construct a new theoretical model, which combines two established theories. The first theory is the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) proposed by Venkatesh et al., [25]. This theory synthesizes multiple technology acceptance-related theories, such as the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). We choose performance expectancy from the UTAUT model as a core variable. Because, users anticipate convenience, efficiency, and other benefits from electric vehicle (EV) charging technologies in practice.

The second theory is the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) proposed by Davis and Davis [9]. This model centers on two core constructs: perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. It explains how these factors influence users’ attitudes toward a technology. We integrate the TAM theory with perceived risk and perceived trust as core constructs. In practical PCP sharing scenarios, users often harbor concerns regarding safety hazards (e.g., fire risks, equipment malfunctions), which directly induce hesitation toward PCP participation. By contrast, users’ perceived trust—encompassing confidence in charging service providers and the reliability of charging technologies—significantly elevates their recognition and acceptance of PCP.

We synergistically integrate UTAUT and TAM to address limitations of single-theory explanations. UTAUT provides a framework to identify key drivers of technology adoption intention, with performance expectancy as a core variable of behavioral intention. In contrast, TAM offers a micro-level lens into the psychological mechanisms of attitude formation, highlighting perceived trust and perceived risk as critical variables. By combining these complementary perspectives, our model overcomes the limitations of each theory in isolation, enabling a more holistic examination of PESCV owners’ sharing intention.

To enhance the model’s practical explanatory power and contextual relevance, supplementary variables are incorporated: individual characteristics (gender, age, educational attainment, income) and situational factors (housing type: renter vs. homeowner; access to dedicated parking spaces; EV charging frequency; community influence; incentive policies). Measurement Items and Reference Sources

When examining the mechanisms driving participation in PCP sharing, we incorporate demographic and residential characteristic variables as independent variables in our analysis. The rationale for each variable is outlined below: (1) Gender: Different genders often exhibit distinct living habits, as well as varying perceptions of and openness to novel concepts. These differences may translate to divergent intention to support PCP sharing. (2) Age: Age groups vary in three key aspects: receptiveness to new models, urgency of charging needs, and familiarity with the sharing economy. These variations can shape individuals’ attitudes toward PCP sharing. (3) Educational level: Educational attainment influences how residents perceive PCP sharing—including its conceptual theory, operational model, and associated benefits/risk tradeoffs. As a result, support for sharing may differ across educational backgrounds. (4) Income: Income levels affect PESCV owners’ consumption capacity and sensitivity to charging costs. Economic status thus acts as a potential moderator of intention to support PCP sharing. (5) Housing status (renter vs. homeowner): Renters and homeowners differ in residential stability and authority to adjust their living environments. These disparities lead to distinct attitudes toward participating in PCP sharing.

We also integrate three additional sets of variables into the analysis: vehicle/parking space characteristics, psychological cognition, and external environment. Their rationales are as follows: (6) EV charging frequency: Car owners with higher charging frequencies have more pressing demand for charging resources, which may increase their intention to support PCP sharing. This inclination is less pronounced among owners with lower charging frequencies. (7) EV ownership: Residents who own EVs directly confront charging challenges. They typically show greater intention to—and stronger intention to support—PCP sharing than non-EV owners. (8) Fixed parking space ownership: Residents with dedicated parking spaces benefit from inherent charging convenience. By contrast, those without such spaces may rely more on PCP sharing. This disparity makes parking space ownership a key factor shaping support for sharing. (9) Performance expectancy: PESCV owners’ support for PCP sharing rises with their expectations of its performance—including gains in charging efficiency, usability, and cost savings. Higher performance expectations correspond to greater intention to promote the model. (10) Perceived risk: PESCV owners’ concerns about PCP sharing risks (e.g., charging safety hazards, personal information leaks, cost disputes) reduce their support. Conversely, lower perceived risk strengthens their inclination to back the model. (11) Perceived trust: PESCV owners’ trust in PCP sharing (encompassing operating platforms, sharing rules, and fellow participants) directly impacts their supportive behavior. Higher levels of trust increase the likelihood of support. (12) Social influence: The attitudes and behaviors of community members, relatives, and friends toward PCP sharing shape individual PESCV owners’ support through social interaction effects. (13) Incentive policies: State or local government incentives (e.g., subsidies, preferential treatment) for PCP sharing directly enhance PESCV owners’ intention to support the model.

(2)Data collection

Data collection

We gathered PESCV owners’ views on sharing their PCP SHARING with other EV owners. We teamed up with Questionnaire Star, a top survey company that had over 6.2 million registered users by October 2024. Their user base includes civil servants, students, corporate employees, vehicle owners, and online shoppers—all of whom earn rewards for completing surveys. To build detailed user profiles, the platform asks users to share personal details like their field of work, job role, residential location, age, educational background, and earnings. Leveraging these details, Questionnaire Star efficiently and precisely identifies target groups that align with the study’s needs to conduct surveys and compile data.

To ensure all our respondents were PCP owners, we included a screening question in the questionnaire: “Does your household own a private charging pile? A. Yes B. No” Only participants who selected option A were retained in the final sample, as this response directly confirmed their status as a private charging pile owner.

Our data-gathering efforts initially yielded 693 returned questionnaires. To make sure our data was valid, we carefully screened all the responses we collected. We left out any submissions that were finished way too quickly, had answers that were almost exactly the same across questions that shouldn’t have matching answers, or showed clear logical mistakes. After this process, 660 valid questionnaires remained, resulting in an effective response rate of 95.24%.

Drawing on the standardized path coefficients and their significance test results, the following conclusions are derived:

Performance expectancy and perceived trust positively influence attitudes toward the sharing of PCP

Performance expectancy and perceived trust positively influence attitudes toward the sharing of PCP

The standardized path coefficient of performance expectancy on PCP sharing attitudes is 0.530, with perceived trust at 0.442—both exerting significant positive effects. Performance expectancy reflects users’ expectations of benefits derived from PCP sharing. When users perceive tangible gains (e.g., economic returns, improved resource utilization), they tend to hold more positive attitudes toward the sharing model. Perceived trust encompasses users’ confidence in the sharing platform and other participants. This trust can mitigate doubts about the sharing process enabling users to more easily to accept the PCP sharing model and form favorable attitudes. This finding supports Hypothesis H1 and H2.

(2) Perceived risk negatively affects intention to share private electric vehicle charging piles

Perceived risk negatively affects intention to share private electric vehicle charging piles

The standardized path coefficient of perceived risk on PCP sharing intention is −0.632, indicating a significant negative impact. In the context of PCP sharing, users may harbor concerns about potential risks, such as equipment damage, personal information leakage, and dispute resolution difficulties. These risk perceptions induce hesitation when users decide whether to participate in sharing, thereby reducing their intention to engage. This finding supports Hypothesis H5.

(3)Social influence, incentive policies, and attitudes toward PCP sharing positively affect sharing intention

Social influence, incentive policies, and attitudes toward PCP sharing positively affect sharing intention

The standardized path coefficients are 0.663 for social influence, 0.689 for incentive policies, and 0.203 for PCP sharing attitudes—all exerting significant positive effects. Social influence refers to the impact of users’ surrounding social networks. When more peers, relatives, or community members participate in and endorse PCP sharing, users are more inclined to join. Incentive policies directly attract users by offering tangible benefits (e.g., subsidies, fee discounts). As a psychological predisposition toward sharing, positive attitudes toward PCP sharing facilitate the conversion of this mindset into actual sharing intentions. This finding supports Hypothesis H3, H4 and H6.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusions

We reveals that the sharing attitude and intention toward PCP among new energy vehicle users are predominantly shaped by several key determinants. Incentive policies emerge as the most prominent positive predictor of sharing intention, as incentivizing measures such as financial subsidies and fee reductions have been verified to directly and effectively elicit users’ willingness to engage in PCP sharing. Social group influence exerts the second-strongest positive effect on sharing intention: the approval and participatory behaviors of individuals within users’ immediate social circles significantly drive their inclination to share PCPs. The weaker predictive power of social influence compared to incentive policies aligns with rational choice theory, as individuals prioritize immediate, measurable economic gains from policy incentives over the more diffuse and context-dependent social norms that drive peer influence. Perceived risk imposes a significant negative impact on sharing intention, where higher perceived risks (including potential equipment malfunctions and safety hazards) associated with the sharing process correspond to a lower willingness of users to participate in PCP sharing. Furthermore, perceived trust, performance expectation, and sharing attitude also demonstrate positive associations with sharing intention; however, their effect sizes are less substantial than those of incentive policies and social group influence.

Policy recommendations

First, at the level of community coordination and policy-driven initiatives, a two-pronged approach should be adopted to enhance users’ willingness to participate in PCP sharing. From the community perspective, comprehensive promotion and publicity of PCP sharing should be carried out across all communities. For example, offline experience and exchange activities can be organized, and community bulletin boards and other media channels leveraged to integrate community resources. Meanwhile, dedicated community communication groups should be established for groups deeply influenced by community dynamics—including females, the middle-aged and elderly, low-education individuals, tenants, and users without private parking spaces. Practical application cases of PCP sharing should be regularly shared within these groups to strengthen the demonstration and driving effect of community engagement. From the policy perspective, the implementation of incentive policies for PCP sharing should be intensified. Support measures such as electricity fee discounts and equipment purchase subsidies should be provided to participating users and operators. Furthermore, preferential benefits should be accurately delivered to policy-sensitive groups—such as high-income individuals, tenants, and users without private parking spaces—to fully exert the driving role of policies.

Second, focusing on perceived trust and perceived risk, efforts should be made to construct a safe and reliable PCP sharing environment. In terms of trust building, the government should strengthen supervision over operating platforms, establish and improve industry norms and credit evaluation systems, and regularly publish lists of safe and compliant platforms. In terms of risk prevention and control, safety guarantee mechanisms should be improved, unified safety standards formulated, and regular inspections and training conducted. For groups with prominent risk perceptions—including males, high-income individuals, homeowners, and users with private parking spaces—specialized safety promotion seminars should be organized. Professionals can be invited to explain practical content, such as equipment identification and dispute resolution, to alleviate their risk concerns regarding PCP sharing.

Finally, centering on performance expectancy, service optimization should be prioritized to meet users’ core needs for PCP sharing participation. PCP sharing operating platforms should be encouraged to upgrade their service capabilities, adopt intelligent scheduling systems to improve charging efficiency, and launch time-of-use pricing and long-term sharing packages to reduce usage costs. For groups significantly influenced by performance expectancy—including males, young people, and users without private parking spaces—diversified methods such as online promotion and offline demonstrations should be utilized to highlight the benefits of PCP sharing (e.g., economic returns and improved resource utilization). This will strengthen the positive performance expectations of these groups toward PCP sharing.

Shared sustainability: An analysis of the belt and road initiative agenda network of united nation news website
Authors: Changhua Liu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) is a global initiative that is gaining increasing attention, participation, and communication globally. However, most existing studies on BRI-related communication are based on BRI communication as the background. As an important theory of communication, network agenda-setting (NAS) theory, as an important theory of communication, is used to focus on an increasing number of objects that are rarely studied by international organizations. The United Nations (UN) news website, which is the official website of the largest international governmental organization, is available in nine languages (as of May 7, 2021), including Chinese, English, French, Russian, Arabic, Spanish, Portuguese, Swahili, and Hindi, providing news reports based on audience research. Thus, this study analyzed the BRI agenda network of UN's nine languages web pages, calculates BRI-related elements and correlation strength, and forms a multidimensional matrix in conjunction with the NAS theory. Finally, based on the summary statistics of the BRI agenda network, the UN news website agenda network was summarized and formed. The correlation strength of nine languages and 26 content pieces was analyzed using statistical principal components and cluster analysis. The result showed that the BRI agenda network of the UN news website had a strong link with the UN's sustainable development goals. Note that the BRI agendas in different languages can influence each other, making sustainability a crucial element for both BRI and the UN. In addition, the NAS theory can work within the same organization. However, there is still room for improvement in BRI's sustainable global discourse communication, such as audience analysis, language-ability building, and diversification of communication bodies and methods.

Introduction

One Belt and One Road (OBOR) is a concept related to the building of the “New Silk Road Economic Belt” and the ”21st-century maritime silk road,” which were proposed by Chinese President Xi Jinping in Kazakhstan (September 2013) and Indonesia (October 2013), respectively. Different from the OBOR, the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), as a new expression of the Chinese discourse system, was unveiled to the world with the authorization of the State Council of the People's Republic of China on March 28, 2015. As the BRI spread globally, bringing benefits and opportunities, an increasing number of countries, regions, and international organizations began to participate in it. By January 30, 2021, China had signed 205 BRI cooperation agreements with 140 countries and 31 international organizations [1].

As the impact of BRI continues to grow, the research community has increasingly focused on BRI issues. Existing research mainly focuses on political or economic perspectives, particularly China's investment in Refs. [[2], [3], [4]] and influences on [1,[5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11]] countries and regions along the BRI routes.

Based on data mining and an analysis of 1583 English peer-reviewed papers on BRI topics from 2013 to 2019, “the findings of the bibliometric analysis indicate that publications in this area have increased rapidly, with particularly high growth rates in the last three years (2016–2019). The BRI is a multidisciplinary subject that has been studied in more than 100 disciplines, 30 of which have betweenness-centrality values exceeding 0.1, indicating that these disciplines are crucial in the network” [12]. Political sciences, particularly geopolitics, focus more on the BRI [[13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]]. BRI-related legal research was also included in the research presented in Refs. [20,21]. In addition, there has been a significant amount of research on BRI from an economic viewpoint [[22], [23], [24], [25]],as well as research and analysis of BRI as a common focal point [26,27]. The BRI research exhibits a general blowout phenomenon, with particularly high growth rates from 2016 to 2019 [12]. The research is mainly interdisciplinary, with political science, economics, law, and other disciplines paying special attention to the BRI. Furthermore, there are several studies from a cultural viewpoint [28].

The discourse on BRI refers to the evaluations, discussions, and communications surrounding China's BRI by both domestic and foreign media, including social media, such as Twitter and Facebook. The BRI, as a new globalization initiative in the information age, necessitates appropriate discourse to disseminate its messages through the media. However, there are degrees of concern about BRI's “community of a shared future,” and its degree of concern is insufficiently high [29]. Some countries have increased local interest in the BRI, but they worry that it is hegemonic or has conflicting interests [30,31]. The Philippines is optimistic due to China's leadership aspirations and capacity, provision of economic goods, the BRI synergy with other connectivity initiatives, and its deep historical anchor. However, concerns arise regarding BRI's involvement in geopolitics and China's hegemonic ambitions, along with its adverse implications on disputed maritime spaces and questionable project terms [31]. India has a complex and dynamic relationship with China. Before Prime Minister Narendra Modi visited China, a delegation of Indian scholars visited Beijing and met with Chinese think tanks. This was reported in Hindi by Rajni Bakshi [32], one of the visit participants. She questioned China's unilateral OBOR announcement, which contradicts the claim that it is an inclusive initiative [30]. The Russian media is not intimidated by China's economic power or recent geopolitical gains [33]. However, they oversell the extent to which Beijing regards Russia as a key part of its strategy, along with the extent of their interest alignment [33].

There is a paucity of relevant literature in communications communities, particularly about discourse communication combined with the network agenda-setting (NAS) theory. Chinese scholars are the main researchers in BRI communication research. Several studies have focused on a certain aspect of communication under the BRI background, such as sports communication [34]. There have been few communication studies on BRI and most of them focus on improving BRI's communication ability from different viewpoints based on communication theories, such as improving BRI's communication ability from the viewpoint of topic-setting optimization [35].From a narrative strategy viewpoint, some papers ascertain a comparative study of BRI coverage by Chinese and British media [36]. Generally, there is still a lack of relevant research on BRI communication with international organizations as a communication subject.

Therefore, it is evident that there are limited studies on BRI as communication content. To gain recognition from more countries, organizations, and the public, the communication ability of BRI must be enhanced from five perspectives, such as communication subjects, channels, media, audiences, and methods. The United Nations (UN), as the largest governmental organization globally, has significant reach and influence. As the official website of the largest transnational governmental organization in BRI communication, the UN news website covers BRI communication by combining its investigation with the needs of its member countries. Therefore, this study analyzes the BRI agenda network of the UN news network to understand BRI's global communication from the viewpoint of transnational government organizations.

The main purpose of this study is to analyze the agenda network of BRI reports on the UN news website, including the elements associated with the BRI reports on nine web pages in different languages, the influence of these nine web pages on one another and the agenda network formed by the BRI reports, as well as the agenda network formed by the nine web pages on BRI reports. Based on this agenda network analysis, policy recommendations were made for BRI communication, which will aid in the dynamic sustainable development of BRI communication discourse. This study also addressed the shortcomings of NAS research on international government organizations and promote further research of the NAS theory for the internal agenda network of the same organization.

In this study, BRI-relevant issues shown on the UN news websites in nine languages are considered the object. The issue-setting and issue network of the BRI is analyzed on each language's web page. By analyzing these reports, we intend to answer the following questions.(1)How many elements are there in the agenda network of BRI issues on each language's webpage?(2)What is the core element of the BRI agenda network in each language's webpage?(3)What is the core element of the BRI agenda network of the UN news network, comprising nine language web pages?(4)Do the agenda networks of the BRI reports in different language pages influence one another?

This not only aids in understanding the approach of international organizations in communicating BRI but also sheds light on the current communication status of BRI in the international community from the viewpoint of international organizations as a communication subject, which is a new academic perspective on the application of NAS theory, thus contributing to the understanding of the overall status of BRI communication and providing new solutions for BRI communication.

Materials and methods

Sample

This study combines the discourse analysis of the BRI issues on the nine language pages presented on the front page of the UN news website. The analyzed samples were BRI-related reports on the UN news website. The statistical time was set to before May 7, 2021. The study used three keywords, namely, “BRI,” “OBOR,” and “New Silk Road,” to search for relevant content within the nine language web pages, resulting in a total of 27 times. Subsequently, content analysis was performed on each report to exclude irrelevant content, such as “traffic accidents” and “hurricanes destroying roads.”For example, on the sidelines of the BRI Forum, presently in Beijing, UN Secretary–General Antonio Guterres discussed a new hurricane situation with the President of Mozambique. In this story, BRI is not an issue, but rather a precondition or a time constraint. Therefore, this story was irrelevant to this study and was discarded. After the manual screening, 114 BRI reports were approved, including 1 in Hindi, 4 in Swahili, 2 in Portuguese, 2 in Spanish, 13 in Russian, 5 in French, 4 in Arabic, 14 in English, and 69 in Chinese. Therefore, the total valid analysis sample for this study was 114 news reports in nine languages from the UN news website.

It is important to highlight the analytical premise of the research sample. The United Nations Department of Public Information, when introducing the changes to the news navigator section of the Chinese page of the UN news website noted that “the changes made in the new version of the website are based on in-depth research on the use and needs of the audience [37].” This study was based on the tacit acceptance of this statement. Therefore, the coverage of BRI issues on UNnews.org's nine language websites selects topics and public communication techniques based on the use and needs of their respective audiences.

Theoretical basis

The main theoretical basis of this study is the NAS theory, which was proposed by Guo and McCombs in 2011 [38,39]. Guo expanded and applied this theory to several articles. To date, the NAS has focused on the relationships between elements or attributes within an agenda network, exploring the relationships between different agenda networks, and the interconnectedness of different national news agendas within the global news agenda [40,41]. Regarding the international communication of Chinese discourse, scholars, such as Deng Yilin, explored the effect of Chinese official media's NAS in social media on social media platforms, such as Twitter, using computational communication research methods [42]. The agenda network of international organizations has received initial attention as a communication topic. Recently, some scholars have combined the NAS theory to study the interaction between WHO and Twitter agendas during COVID-19 [43].

While these reports cover the BRI, they associate it with certain topics or attributes through the NAS theory. The NAS theory is the third level of the agenda-setting theory, which was proposed by Guo and McCombs, who was among the pioneers of agenda-setting research [[38], [39], [40],[44], [45], [46], [47]]. “The NAS Model asserts that the news not only informs and influences our thoughts but also determines how we associate different messages to conceptualize social reality” [38,39,41]. “The attribute network picture depicted by the news media significantly influences public perception” [38,39,41]. The NAS theory focuses on the interconnection of different messages in people's minds [40]. This entire set of connections— information networks — can transit from the media to the public agenda (Fig. 1) [39,41,48].For example, when Huang Min studied the media network agenda of the New York Times report on China's poverty alleviation (2006–2018), she found that in the subtopic of "China's economic development situation/plan," The newspaper mainly described how the Chinese government tried to maintain economic development, eradicate poverty, maintain social stability, and ultimately maintain the stability of the regime. What is established is a causal chain of "maintaining economic development → eliminating poverty → consolidating the political power." [48]The New York Times can influence the agenda network of the issue constructed in the audience's mind by linking China's economic development with the consolidation of political power by the Chinese government, and guide and strengthen the audience to link China's economic development with the consolidation of political power.Fig. 1Network agenda setting [Guo and McCombs, 2011b, 2016].Fig. 1

Network agenda setting [Guo and McCombs, 2011b, 2016].

This study employed the NAS theory to examine the topics covered by the BRI reports in the nine languages on the UN news websites, and the relationships established between the BRI and other issues. Based on the analysis of the UN's official media agenda network, this study discussed the role of international organizations in the construction and transmission of discourse, addressing the lack of attention paid by the NAS theory to international organizations' media agendas.

Materials and research methods

Research object and design

In this study, we used the UN news websites report on the BRI as the research object and performed a detailed analysis of the topics associated with the BRI in this media network agenda. In addition, we investigated how communication is established to explore cognitive network differences about the BRI issues among audiences of different languages. Furthermore, we explored the BRI issue network, comprising nine language web pages on the UN news website, the mechanism of media network agenda formation on the UN news website, and the application of the NAS theory from the perspective of a cognitive network, which is constructed through the language websites of an international organization. The social network analysis method was also adopted in this study. Different from other social network analyses, this study defined the BRI as the degree of centrality of the media network agenda [48].

According to the research purpose, this study was divided into three steps. The first step was to determine the BRI report elements, including the issue object and the topic attribute. The issues and attributes included in the analytical framework were determined using traditional content analysis [38,39,41,48,49]. Other topics in the thematic reports were implicitly or explicitly related to the BRI; therefore, the total number of topics was counted, and the articles were considered the unit of analysis. The analysis unit nonthematic reports were limited to paragraphs except the subject of the report was associated with BRI. When the entire paragraph was focused on this topic, adjacent paragraphs were also analyzed. The second step was to determine the elements of semantic analysis. The BRI reports for each language were used to build a co-occurrence matrix, focusing on UN-reported BRI news. After determining the elements to be included, a semantic analysis was performed, and co-occurrence was determined using the semantic distance with the BRI. The co-occurrence analysis was divided into two situations. The first situation involved the fact that in the reports with BRI as the subject, the full text was used as an analysis unit. The second situation involved the use of related or adjacent paragraphs as units of analysis in reports on other topics that only mentioned BRI. The third step involved using data visualization software to build the BRI co-occurrence network visualization.

Statistical method

In all analysis samples, the associations between different elements and the BRI can be divided into three situations — a certain element has no relationship with BRI, a certain element has no significant relationship with BRI, and a certain element has an obvious relationship with BRI, which are coded as 0, 1 and 2, respectively [41,49]. The number of co-occurrences was used to create a multidimensional matrix where the rows represented the languages, the columns represented the other elements identified during content analysis, and the number in each grid represented the degree of correlation between the element and the BRI. The higher the number, the stronger the correlation. Unlike other network analyses, the degree centrality of the elements was determined as the BRI in this study. Therefore, the BRI served as the central node of the agenda network. In a NAS, a “node” represents the object or attribute of the topic [37]. Based on an analysis of the BRI element reports, this section analyzed the BRI issue networks of the nine language web pages and the BRI issue networks of the UN news website. The BRI issue networks were visualized using network mapping software (Gephi) [48].

The principal component analysis method was used to analyze the similarity of the reports across the nine languages. The principal component analysis is a multivariate statistical method used to investigate the correlation between multiple variables, which investigates how to reveal the internal structure of multiple variables through a few principal components; i.e., derive a few principal components from the original variable while retaining as much information as possible about the original variable and being unrelated to each other. The results of the principal component analysis in this study are represented using a visual graph shown in Fig. 4. If the similarity is high, the variables overlap.

The correlation strength of each language with the 26 content pieces was calculated using the cluster analysis statistical method, which was used to determine whether the objects in a group were similar. The greater the intra-group similarity, the greater the inter-group gap, as measured by the clustering index, which is generally assumed to be greater than 0, indicating that the expression intensity of the variable is high in the inter-group. The specific analysis results in this study are represented using a visual heat map shown in Fig. 5. If the reporting intensity of an element is high, the clustering index of the element is greater than zero. All statistical analyses were based on R software（https://cran.r-project.org/).

All 17 SDGs, including partnerships for goal achievement, were identified as SDG issues. When dealing with the number of topic associations in a topic report, the occurrence of a certain element in a paragraph was recorded as one correlation.

There was only one BRI report in Hindi, which was a special report on the BRI. At China's Belt and Road Forum, UN Secretary–General Antonio Guterres headlined the story and advocated for sustainable development. Through careful reading, we determined the topics, objects, or attributes related to the BRI. Seven elements were associated with the BRI. Among them, the SDG's statistical standard was 1 when it comes to one of the UN's 17 SDGs. Each occurrence of the related element was assigned a value of one. We used the same method to analyze the relevant elements of the BRI agenda in the remaining eight languages.

The Swahili page had four BRI stories, consisting of three special reports and two that were published in different sections with the same content and thus were counted as one. There was one more article on food security. The analysis revealed nine elements associated with the BRI.

The Portuguese page had two BRI news pieces, which were nonBRI stories but mentioned it. Through manual analysis and selection, eight elements were associated with the BRI.

The Spanish page had two pieces. One was a special report on the BRI, and the other was a newsletter about this report. Seven elements were associated with the BRI.

The Russian page had 13 news pieces, two of which were special reports on the BRI Forum for International Cooperation, and one was a newsletter about the forum. The remaining ten news articles were not specially about the BRI, although they all mentioned it. Hence, 16 elements were associated with the BRI.

The French page had five news reports, two of which were special reports and the remaining four news reports mentioned the BRI. Among them, was a news report about a refugee program in which BRI was used as an adverbial of time; hence, this report was dropped. The BRI was associated with 13 different elements.

The Arabic page had four news reports that mentioned the BRI, three of which were special reports on the BRI, and two had special reports on the BRI with the same content and were listed in different sections. We multiplied the cumulative statistics from the two same news reports by 2. The fourth report mentioned BRI when talking about other issues. Through analysis, ten elements were associated with the BRI.

The English page had 14 news reports that mentioned BRI, including 10 nonBRI themed reports and four BRI special news, one of which was a newsletter and the other was a green economy news. Although the green economy is one of the SDGs, as the report emphasized and distinguished between green and sustainable development, these statistical issues were listed separately. Approximately 14 elements were associated with the BRI.

The Chinese page had 69 news reports, 13 of which were special reports on the BRI, and the remaining 56 news reports only mentioned the BRI. After excluding 28 elements that could not be determined, 21 elements were associated with the BRI.

Discussion

Findings

According to the sample analysis, the number of BRI-related elements varied among language websites. The Hindi web page had seven BRI-related elements, the Swahili web page had nine, the Portuguese web page had eight, the Spanish web page had seven, the Russian web page had 16, the French web page had 13, the Arabic web page had 10, the English web page had 14, and the Chinese web page had 21 BRI-related elements. By combining similar elements, the pages on the UN news network had 26 BRI-related elements. Most of the core elements were the SDGs, followed by the UN Secretary–General. Overall, the SDGs were the core elements associated with the BRI in the entire UN news network. In addition, nine language web pages influence one another when reporting BRI.

Contrary to the stigmatization of the BRI in certain countries as a “debt trap” [50], the above statistics show that the spread of information about the BRI through the UN news network was generally positive. Furthermore, sustainability was most closely related to the BRI as the central node, with its association with the SDGs covering all 17 UN SDGs. Two of the four elements in the first level involved relevant people and institutions, such as the UN Secretary–General and UN agencies and their leaders, indicating that the relevant personnel of the UN was a critical element in the BRI reports. The SDGs and global cooperation and development were the most prominent attributes of the BRI agenda as constructed by the UN news network.

In the second level of news reports, the two relevant figures were China's leaders and leaders of other countries. These further included China's investment and donations where the BRI mainly involved developing countries. The BRI concept included multilateralism, green development, and south–south cooperation, and its origins lay in the New Silk Road. Note that the Russian and Chinese reports mention “inequality,” [51]such as inequality in poverty reduction in Xinjiang. This concept referred to the inequality within the countries and between countries and groups. The BRI reports cover more than 30 types of inequality. Hence, “inequality” is the main negative content highlighted in the BRI-related reports.

In the third level, relevant personnel and institutions were associated with the BRI, such as the Chinese government and news agencies. The data showed that the BRI reports placed more emphasis on international markets than on domestic ones. The Chinese web pages mentioned additional discourse subjects, such as academic institutions. The concept of “community with a shared destiny,” was mentioned in both Chinese and Russian reports. At this level, multiple BRI communication channels, such as cultural exchanges, were emphasized multiple times. The positive role of BRI was presented in terms of “poverty eradication,” “agricultural development,” “peace and security,” and the role of BRI in “protecting world heritage,” indicating that the UN news network understood and approved of the background and core concept of the BRI. In addition, most agenda settings were released after BRI-related events, such as the BRI International Cooperation Forum. The agenda settings are timely and targeted to attract the attention of relevant countries and audiences [35].

A comparison of BRI reports in nine languages showed that the Chinese web page had the most BRI reports, with 69 articles covering a wide range of topics, including 21 main elements. However, there were positive and negative stories with 25 mentions of “inequality.” The English web page had 14 BRI reports and related elements. The Russian web page had 13 BRI reports and 16 related elements. SDGs were the element with the greatest correlation strength. Meanwhile, in Russian and English reports, “inequality” was mentioned four and two times, respectively. In addition, there were five reports on the BRI in French, four in Arabic, four in Swahili, two in Spanish, two in Portuguese, and one in Hindi. The stories were about BRI people and institutions; however, they were mostly positive, with “inequality” mentioned twice in French and Arabic in a negative context. These negative messages were related to cultural differences and communication methods. Hence, BRI communication must strengthen audience research, pay attention to audience response, and strengthen audience interaction to establish a harmonious and equal relationship between the communicator and the audience [35].

Based on the above analysis, BRI-related reports in nine languages on UN news converge can be understood in the following context. First, the agenda network constructed by the UN news website conveys a positive image of the BRI to its audience. The link between the BRI and the SDGs was particularly highlighted and sustainability was the most prominent attribute in the BRI coverage. This shows that sustainability is one of the common goals of the BRI and the UN, indicating that the BRI follows the basic preparations of the UN and has been recognized and supported by the UN. Second, audiences of different languages have different degrees of attention in the BRI, which is reflected in the difference in the number of reports and the number and intensity of related elements. The Chinese web page remains the primary source of BRI information, indicating that the Chinese audience pays the most attention. English, with the most extensive audience, also pays considerable attention to the BRI. Russia is China's largest neighbor and a BRI stakeholder, so there are many Russian-language reports. Surprisingly, there was only one BRI-related story in Hindi, even though India is China's neighbor. This was three fewer than those in Swahili, the language of a distant African country, suggesting that China–Africa cooperation under the BRI may benefit both countries [52,53]. The lack of reports in Hindi may be due to India's attitude toward the BRI. India believes that the BRI will weaken its influence in the South Asian subcontinent and shake its regional leadership; therefore, India has taken a wait-and-watch attitude toward joining the BRI [54]. Third, the sustainability of the agenda connectivity network built by the UN news website shows that the UN appreciates and supports the BRI and intends to collaborate with the initiative to advance the development of the UN SDGs. Fourth, the difference in BRI reports across languages indicates that the distribution of BRI-related reports is unbalanced. While audiences who speak Chinese, English, or Russian are more interested in the BRI, those who speak Hindi or Swahili are less interested. Therefore, the effective communication capacity of the BRI must be improved [55].Fourth, because the NAS theory originates from the study of political elections, it is naturally concerned with political issues. However, this study mainly examines the effect of communication statutes and subject sat the level of international organizations in BRI communication from the viewpoint of communication science, thus changing the fact that the previous NAS theory pays little attention to the communication subjects of international organizations. Existing studies mostly examine the relationship between media network agenda and other network agendas (such as public network agenda, social media network agenda, and political parties/enterprises/lobby groups/public relations network agenda) [41,45,48]. Many BRI reports on the multilingual websites of the UN, the largest international governmental organization, show that international organizations promote the communication of BRI to a certain extent. The mutual influence of BRI topics on different language websites shows that NAS theory can also play a role in the internal communication network of international organizations. Particularly, the co-communication of sustainability between web pages in different languages is highlighted.

Policy recommendations

As the BRI is sustainable, the dissemination of the BRI discourse must be sustainable as well. The BRI must be dynamically planned, monitored, and updated in line with global and BRI developments. BRI-related reports on the UN news website are just one part of the BRI discourse transmission system. To improve the BRI's transmission efficiency, efforts must be made in the following directions. First, the intended audience must be identified, and BRI stories must be delivered to them in ways that they can understand and appreciate. Second, the BRI requires a common language for communication. All languages and dialects are important resources, and strengthening language capacity would facilitate communication between countries regarding BRI and SDGs [56,57]. Wang's works [[58], [59], [60]] on the language status of the BRI countries contribute to the development of discourse communication systems. Third, communication subjects must be diverse. Today, all audiences are part of international communication. Audiences can be seen and heard globally through social media. Individual readings often influence the media. Social media has global appeal and influence [61]. Fourth, forms of communication must be diversified. Discourse communication is formed through mainstream media news reports and nine other types of communication, including soft communication, such as culture. From a transmission viewpoint, the comprehensive use of mass, interpersonal, organizational, and group communication provide various ways for disseminating information. In the future, the BRI will play a significant role. Parties related to the BRI, particularly China-related personal performance and image, will have a significant influence on the spread of the BRI [62]. Therefore, BRI-related knowledge and media literacy education must be strengthened, particularly in cross-border areas.

Conclusion and future research

Based on relevant BRI reports in nine languages from the UN news network, combined with the NAS theory, this study analyzed the BRI agenda network of the language pages of the UN news network and summarized the BRI agenda network constructed by all UN news network reports on this basis. This study focused on the media agenda network of the largest international organization and constructed a comparative analysis of the media agenda network in different languages. The results showed that the public network agenda pays attention to the BRI issues and their effects on audiences of different languages, demonstrating how the effect of the NAS is formed in the news systems of international organizations. The media network agenda affects the public and network agendas of different languages within the same media, forming the overall agenda network of the organization and exerting different influences on the audiences in different countries. The NAS theory can be applied within the same organization.

This study is the first to conduct a statistical comparison and analysis of news websites from the world's largest international organizations on the topic of the BRI topic. Some subjectivity in data collation is inevitable, thus it is necessary to enhance the statistical data methods in future research to improve data statistics objectivity. For example, after sample retrieval, AntConc or WordSmith can be used to build a small corpus of samples that offer more reliable resources for subsequent analysis and convenient data sources for other research. In addition, research on the UN news websites must be performed in combination with corresponding new media, such as Twitter, Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, Flickr, and Sina Weibo, along with performing corresponding surveys or interviews with different media audiences. This study presented a preliminary study that combines the NAS theory with the largest international organization, the UN, as the primary communication subject. Future progress will expand the application scope of the NAS theory, promote BRI communication research, and contribute to BRI international communication.

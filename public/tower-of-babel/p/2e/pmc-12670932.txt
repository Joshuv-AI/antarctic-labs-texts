Image dataset of ten durian diseases captured in real-field conditions from a family orchard in Vinh Long, Vietnam
Authors: Tan Nguyen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This dataset comprises 5452 images of durian plant parts—including leaves, flowers, branches, stems, and roots—affected by ten common disease classes. The images were captured from one family-owned durian orchard and four nearby orchards in Vinh Long Province, Vietnam. Each class contains approximately 405–427 raw images, photographed using an iPhone 14 under natural field conditions. These conditions simulate typical farmer photography practices, featuring varied angles, inconsistent lighting, and complex environmental backgrounds, resulting in significant visual noise. All raw JPEG images were manually reviewed and cropped on macOS systems using MacBook devices equipped with Apple M4 chips to focus on disease-affected regions, reduce file size, and minimize background noise. The processed, cropped images are provided in PNG format with variable dimensions. Images were resized to 224×224 pixels only during model training for machine learning experiments. Disease symptoms were verified in collaboration with plant pathologists to ensure accurate classification. This dataset is publicly available on Mendeley Data and is suitable for developing and evaluating machine learning models in plant disease classification. It is particularly valuable for testing model performance under real-world, noisy conditions and for supporting the creation of mobile or edge-based diagnostic tools in agriculture.

Background

The dataset was compiled to document common durian diseases in Vietnam using images captured in real-field conditions from a family orchard. The motivation stemmed from the need for realistic datasets that reflect farmer photography practices, including noise from environmental factors, to aid in the creation of practical diagnostic tools. The data were generated in the context of plant pathology methods for identifying symptoms on durian trees, where visual inspection is key. This dataset adds value by providing images with high noise typical of tropical orchards, differing from controlled lab settings.

Durian is a high-value tropical fruit with significant agricultural and economic importance, particularly in Southeast Asia. In Vietnam, durian cultivation spans over 120,000 hectares, contributing substantially to rural livelihoods and national exports. In 2024, Vietnam's durian exports reached US$3.3 billion, accounting for nearly 50 % of the country's fruit and vegetable export revenue, with ambitions to hit US$5 billion by 2030. However, durian production faces severe challenges from diseases, which can reduce yields by 10–30 % or more in affected orchards, leading to economic losses estimated in the hundreds of millions annually. Pathogens like Phytophthora palmivora cause root rot, stem canker, and fruit decay, exacerbating issues in humid, tropical climates like Vietnam's Mekong Delta. These diseases not only diminish fruit quality and quantity but also threaten tree longevity, amplifying the burden on smallholder farmers who rely on durian for income [8].

Unlike many existing plant disease datasets, such as the PlantVillage dataset, which primarily consists of controlled, lab-captured images for crops like apples and tomatoes [3], or the MangoLeafBD dataset, which offers field images for mango diseases but from a different geographical and crop context [4], or recent advancements in potato [7], grapes [5], and multi-crop Bangladeshi datasets [6], this collection fills a critical research gap. It provides a specialized, noisy dataset for durian diseases in Vietnamese orchards, emphasizing real-world variability to better support AI-driven diagnostics in tropical agriculture, where datasets for exotic fruits like durian are scarce compared to more common crops like mango, apple, or cotton. Additionally, related work on hyperspectral imaging for anthracnose detection [2] highlights potential applications for similar diseases in durian. Data Description

The dataset comprises 5452 images organized into ten folders, one per disease class: Thrips disease, Stem blight, Canker disease, Pink disease, Stem cracking and gummosis, Mealybug disease, Anthracnose, Fruit rot, Sooty mold, and Yellow leaf. Detailed descriptions of these diseases are provided in Table 2. Each folder contains approximately 405–427 images, captured from a family-owned durian orchard, featuring high levels of noise from real-field conditions such as uneven lighting, varied angles, blurriness, and complex backgrounds, reflecting typical farmer photography practices. During data collection, several challenges were encountered, including variable weather conditions (e.g., sudden rain causing water droplets on lenses or intense sunlight creating harsh shadows and overexposure), movement of insects (e.g., mealybugs or thrips shifting positions during capture), wind-blown leaves leading to motion blur, and occasional camera shake in handheld shots under uneven terrain. These factors contributed to the realistic noise in the dataset, emphasizing its authenticity for field applications. Blurred or unusable images resulting from these challenges were discarded during the manual review process to maintain quality.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Images were collected from a 5-hectare family-owned durian orchard, along with four additional durian orchards in Vinh Long Province, Vietnam, between April and June 2025. Ten disease classes were targeted: Thrips disease (insect-induced leaf curling), Stem blight (fungal leaf spots), Canker disease (bacterial leaf lesions), Pink disease (fungal stem infection), Stem cracking and gummosis (oozing bark cracks), Mealybug disease (insect infestation), Anthracnose (fungal spots on leaves/fruits), Fruit rot (fungal decay), Sooty mold (fungal coating), and Yellow leaf (soil-borne infection). The dataset includes 5452 images, partitioned into training (4154 images), test (648 images), and validation (650 images) subsets.

The sampling method was purposive, targeting visible symptoms of ten predefined durian disease classes across leaves, stems, fruits, roots, and flowers. Images were systematically collected from a 5-hectare family-owned orchard and four neighboring orchards in Vinh Long Province. To ensure diversity, images were captured at different times of day (morning, midday, afternoon) under natural daylight, from multiple angles (e.g., top-down, side, close-up), and across various environmental conditions (e.g., sunny, cloudy, post-rain). Approximately 405–427 images per disease class were collected to achieve balanced representation, with care taken to include a range of symptom severities (mild to severe).

An iPhone 14 (12 MP main camera, personally taken by the author) captured images at 0.5–1 m distance in natural light, without filters, using techniques that mimic farmers’ real-world photography practices, such as handheld shots from various angles and in varying environmental conditions, leading to inherent noise. The camera was operated in auto exposure mode, with automatic focus and HDR enabled as needed for scenes with high dynamic range (e.g., bright sunlight and shadows). Typical ISO values ranged from 32 to 100 in daylight conditions, with the main camera’s fixed aperture at f/1.5 and shutter speeds automatically adjusted based on lighting (typically 1/100 to 1/2000 s). Focus mode was set to auto, and Live Photos and other advanced features were disabled to ensure standard JPEG capture without additional metadata or animations.

Preprocessing involved manual review of all 5452 images to ensure focus and symptom clarity. Images were discarded if they were excessively blurred (e.g., due to motion from wind or insects, or focus errors), contained duplicates (e.g., nearly identical shots of the same plant part), or did not clearly depict the targeted disease symptoms—resulting in the removal of approximately 10–15 % of initially captured images. The remaining images were cropped using the built-in Photos application on macOS (MacBook with Apple M4 chip) to focus on disease-affected regions, reducing file size and minimizing background noise (e.g., irrelevant elements like soil, sky, or unrelated vegetation). Cropped images were saved in PNG format to preserve quality without introducing additional lossy compression beyond the original JPEG, ensuring transparency support if needed and maintaining the natural color and detail for downstream analysis. No color correction, brightness adjustments, contrast enhancements, or other post-processing modifications were applied, by design, to retain the authentic noise and variability of field conditions.

The labeling workflow began with initial classification by the author during collection, based on visible symptoms and guided by predefined disease descriptions (as in Table 2). All images were then independently reviewed by two plant pathologists with expertise in durian diseases, affiliated with the Vinh Long Agricultural Extension Center. Each pathologist assessed the images for accuracy against symptom criteria, using on-site physical inspections of affected plants during the collection period (April–June 2025) for confirmation. In cases of disagreement (occurring in about 5 % of images, often due to ambiguous early-stage symptoms), resolution was achieved through consensus discussion between the pathologists and the author, supplemented by additional tests such as microscopic examination for fungal structures (e.g., in Anthracnose or Pink disease) or insect identification for pest-related classes (e.g., Thrips or Mealybug). Once verified, images were organized into folders named by disease class (e.g., “Thrips_Disease”), with no additional per-image annotations (e.g., bounding boxes or segmentation masks) as the dataset is designed for supervised image classification tasks. The labeled images were then randomly partitioned into training, test, and validation subsets while maintaining class balance. Files were uploaded to Mendeley Data for public access.

Limitations

The dataset is limited to images collected solely from one family-owned durian orchard and four nearby orchards in Vinh Long Province, Vietnam. This restricted sampling may introduce geographical bias, as disease manifestations could vary in other regions due to differences in climate, soil composition, durian cultivars, or agricultural practices. The dataset primarily includes images of the Ri6 durian cultivar, with limited representation of other cultivars such as Monthong from neighboring orchards, which may limit the dataset’s applicability to other durian varieties.

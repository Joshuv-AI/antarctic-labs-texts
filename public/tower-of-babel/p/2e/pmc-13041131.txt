Effects of thyroiditis on the diagnostic performance of fine-needle aspiration cytology– clinico-radiologico-cytological analysis of risk factors for discordance
Authors: Matrix M H Fung, Anthony T Y Ng, Nicholas T C Ng, Joanna K M Ng, Joshua J X Li, Philippe Vielh, Philip P C Ip
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThyroiditis is known to induce atypical cytomorphological changes in the thyroid accompanied by increased cancer risk. The current study aims to determine the effect of thyroiditis on the risk of neoplasm (RON), risk of malignancy (ROM), diagnostic concordance and clinico-radiological features associated with discordance in thyroid aspirates.MethodsA cohort of histology (thyroidectomy) matched thyroid aspirates were reviewed for medical history of thyroiditis, cytological and histological diagnosis, and clinico-radiological information. Cytology-histology concordance was determined in accordance with the Bethesda system 2023.ResultsThere were 770 thyroid aspirates with lymphocytic thyroiditis (n = 106), Graves’ disease (n = 19), granulomatous thyroiditis (n = 3), IgG4-associated thyroiditis (n = 2), and non-specified autoimmune thyroiditis (n = 2). In the thyroiditis group, the RON/ROM in the Bethesda I to VI categories were 25.0%/21.4% (non-diagnostic/unsatisfactory), 31.3%/25.0% (benign), 45.2%/24.1% (atypia of undetermined significance (AUS)/follicular lesion of undetermined significance (FLUS)), 33.3%/0% (follicular neoplasm/suspicious for follicular neoplasm), 100%/100% (suspicious for malignancy), and 100%/100% (malignant), respectively. In the non-thyroiditis group, the RON/ROM were respectively 51.5%/34.9%, 23.3%/8.9%, 44.8%/23.0%, 68.4%/21.1%, 94.8%/87.9%, and 100%/97.7%. With AUS/FLUS aspirates considered positive over negative, the concordance rate decreased (59.6% to 51.9%) in the thyroiditis group with a trended towards lower concordance than the non-thyroiditis group (51.9% vs. 61.4%, p = 0.074). Cases with lymphocytic thyroiditis showed a lower concordance rate (p = 0.010), and reciprocally Graves’ disease higher (p = 0.031). Other clinico-radiological parameters demonstrated no significant correlations.ConclusionDecisions arising from AUS/FLUS aspirates in patients with thyroiditis may better err on the conservative side with assumptive negative diagnosis. Introduction

Fine-needle aspiration is a minimally invasive and favored in assessment of the thyroid lesions [1]. The diagnostic metrics of thyroid aspiration cytology is well documented in the Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology [2]. However, pre-test modifiers can significantly influence the test accuracy and interpretation of diagnostic tests, in particular for cytology where reporting is qualitative and probabilistic [3]. Thyroiditis is known to induce atypical cytomorphological changes in the thyroid [4], and may be associated with an increased risk of thyroid cancer [5]. Retrospective descriptive reviews can aid the cytopathologist in interpretation of specimens from patients with or suspected of thyroiditis, but data on diagnostic metrics from cohort studies is necessary at the point of clinical decision making when the endocrinologist or surgeon acts on a cytologic diagnosis. The current study reviews a large cohort of histology (thyroidectomy) matched thyroid aspirates to detail how thyroiditis affects risk of neoplasm (RON) and risk of malignancy (ROM) of thyroid aspirates, the diagnostic concordance in each Bethesda diagnostic category, and clinico-radiological features that are associated with cytology histology discordance.

Methodology

Cases of thyroid aspirates and thyroidectomy specimens were identified from the pathology archive of the Queen Mary Hospital, Hong Kong, between the years of 2020 and 2024. Thyroid aspirates were matched to thyroidectomy specimens by a project number unique to each individual patient, with the laterality and location of aspiration and lesion of thyroidectomy specimen cross-checked. Cytology cases without a corresponding histology were excluded. Diagnosis of the thyroid aspirates were assigned into 6 categories according to the Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology 2023 [2], namely: C1 - nondiagnostic or unsatisfactory; C2 – benign; C3 – atypia of undetermined significance/follicular lesion of undetermined significance; C4 – follicular neoplasm/suspicious for follicular neoplasm; C5 – suspicious for malignancy; and C6 – malignant. The study was approved by The University of Hong Kong / Hospital Authority Hong Kong West Institutional Review Board (reference number: UW 24–292). The research was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

The histologic diagnosis of the thyroidectomy specimen was considered as the reference and were classified into negative for malignancy/follicular nodular disease, follicular neoplasm (including oncocytic neoplasms and follicular tumor of unknown malignant potential (FT-UMP)) and malignancy [6]. Noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features, due to its highly overlapping cytological features with papillary carcinoma, was classified as malignant in the current study (Table 1), as it was often not feasible for it to be distinguished from papillary carcinoma based on cytology only. Slides from reports, including thyroid aspirates and thyroidectomies, with ambiguous diagnoses and/or descriptions were reviewed by two independent pathologists until a consensus diagnosis was reached.

Patient records were reviewed for clinical, radiological, and pathological data. Clinical data retrieved included clinical case notes, hospital diagnosis code, patient demographics, drug history, thyroid function tests and antithyroid antibodies (anti-thyroid peroxidase antibody and anti-thyroglobulin antibody) taken within 3 months of aspiration and not taken after thyroidectomy. For radiological data, only ultrasound reports within 3 months of aspiration were included. The clinical case notes, hospital diagnosis code and thyroidectomy report of each patient were reviewed to determine if thyroiditis was present.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS statistical software (Windows version 23, IBM). Thyroid function tests and antithyroid antibodies were classified into low, normal and/or high based on local reference ranges. Clinical, serological and radiological data were compared with cytology-histology concordance as defined in Table 2, with two alternative conditions considering C3 as positive for neoplasm and/or malignancy, or negative. RON was defined as the percentage of cases with a corresponding histological diagnosis of follicular neoplasm or malignancy, whereas for ROM, only when a malignant histology was present. The Chi-squared test (or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate) was used to compare outcomes and clinicopathological parameters as categorical and continuous variables. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered as significant.

Results

A total of 770 thyroid aspirates from 487 patients, comprising of 363 females and 124 males with an average age of 56.3 years were included, and corresponding to 369 benign, 135 follicular neoplasm, and 266 malignant histologic diagnoses (Table 2). There were 132 aspirates from those with a diagnosis of thyroiditis, the most common type being lymphocytic thyroiditis (n = 106), followed by Graves’ disease (n = 19), granulomatous thyroiditis (n = 3), IgG4-associated thyroiditis (n = 2), and two cases of non-specified autoimmune thyroiditis. There were 197, 106, 301, 44, 71, and 51 aspirates from the C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, and C6 categories, respectively. In the thyroiditis group, the RON/ROM for the C1 to C6 categories were 25.0%/21.4%, 31.3%/25.0%, 45.2%/24.1%, 33.3%/0%, 100%/100% and 100%/100%. In the non-thyroiditis group, there were respectively 51.5%/34.9%, 23.3%/8.9%, 44.8%/23.0%, 68.4%/21.1%, 94.8%/87.9%, and 100%/97.7% (Table 3). The RON for the C1 category was higher in the non-thyroiditis group (p = 0.009), otherwise there were no significant differences in terms of ROM and RON between the thyroiditis and non-thyroiditis groups in other categories (p > 0.05) (Table 3).

Among the Bethesda diagnostic categories, there were more C3 aspirates in the thyroiditis group (n = 62/132, 47.0% vs. n = 239/638, 37.5%, p = 0.042), otherwise no differences were observed between in the frequencies of the other diagnostic categories (p > 0.05). Considering C3 as negative, the overall concordance rate for the thyroiditis group was slightly lower than the non-thyroiditis group (n = 62/104, 59.6% vs. n = 313/469, 66.7%, p = 0.167). When C3 aspirates were treated as positive, the concordance rate decreased and was more significant for the thyroiditis group (n = 54/104, 51.9% vs. n = 288/469, 61.4%, p = 0.074), reaching a trend. There were no significant differences in concordance rates between aspirates from thyroiditis and non-thyroiditis patients for analysis of each cytologic diagnostic group (Table 4).

Discussion

Fine-needle aspiration is an established, minimally invasive screening modality for initial investigation of thyroid lesions [7]. Thyroid cytology is highly accurate in diagnosing non-follicular malignant neoplasms [8]. Non-neoplastic conditions, including thyroiditis, are also associated with recognizable cytological features [9]. These descriptive studies are based on observations of thyroid aspirates on histology proven cases of thyroiditis, but experience in thyroid aspirate where a neoplasm coexists of background thyroiditis is limited. Notably, thyroiditis may be associated with an increased risk of thyroid cancer, reported at an odds ratio of 2.41 in Hashimoto’s thyroiditis in a meta-analysis from Hu et al. [5]. A clinical situation increasing pre-test probability for a positive result (i.e. a malignant outcome in tissue biopsies) would likely affect the predictive metrics of the diagnostic test [10, 11]. Pre-analytical factors are modifiers of diagnostic performance in cytology, including sensitivity and specificity of individual diagnostic categories and even overall diagnostic accuracy across the entire diagnostic system [12, 13]. The current study aims to address two pertinent clinical questions, (1) how thyroiditis affects the diagnostic metrics and performance of thyroid fine-needle aspiration cytology, and (2) any concurrent pre-analytical parameters that significantly modify its diagnostic accuracy.

Negative and indeterminate cytologic diagnosis, including C1, C2 and C3 thyroid aspirates, carry a non-negligible malignant risk [3, 14–16], with a ROM of greater than 20% in the current cohort for thyroid aspirates from patients with thyroiditis (Table 3). The RONs for these diagnostic categories were even higher. In particular, the ROM of the benign (C2) category was higher than the range reported in the Bethesda system, where the reported range was 2–7% [2]. Parenchymal distortion by inflammation and fibrosis and patient discomfort and non-compliance may be contributing factors [17]. It The clinical indications and considerations for thyroidectomy in patients with thyroiditis may differ from those presenting primarily with thyroid nodule. However, further comparison of lesion size of papillary thyroid carcinomas of both groups did not show statistically significant difference (p = 0.365), with 74.1% (n = 20/27) and 81.7% (n = 107/131) of cases in the thyroiditis and non-thyroiditis being T1 disease. One possible contributing factor was that histological follow-up was adopted in the current study, whereas cases with clinical (expectant) follow-up likely had a lower index of suspicion for malignancy [18]. Another observation was that the RON in the C1 category for patients with thyroiditis was lower. However, nondiagnostic and unsatisfactory results in aspiration cytology can be caused by a plethora of factors. Other than sampling and specimen quality issues, calcified, hard or hyalinized lesions almost invariably produce acellular smears that would be interpreted as nondiagnostic [19]. It may be that these types of lesions are seen more frequently in neoplastic over inflammatory conditions [20]. The recommendation for a C1 aspirate is repeat aspiration, with imaging guidance if not previously utilized [21], or consideration of other biopsy modalities. Overinterpretation of the cytologic specimen is cautioned against [22].

One of the strengths of the Bethesda system is how follicular neoplasms are incorporated into its diagnostic categories [2]. Distinguishing follicular carcinomas from follicular adenomas on cytology is extremely challenging if not impossible [23]. As such, cytology-histology concordance for thyroid cytology considers not only benign or malignant, but also follicular neoplasms as an independent category of histologic reference standard. To add another layer of complexity, C3 aspirates cannot be definitively assigned as a positive or negative result. Recognizing these nuances, the current study performed two alternate concordance analyses in which C3 aspirates were treated as positive and negative respectively, in addition to calculation of ROM and RON. Two notable observations were made. Firstly, the proportion of C3 diagnoses was significantly greater for the thyroiditis group. Secondly, the concordance rate for the thyroiditis group declines when C3 aspirates were considered positive, accompanied by a statistical trend of inferior concordance compared to the non-thyroiditis group. The implications of these findings are that C3 diagnoses are more common in thyroiditis patients, and are likely to reflect a positive outcome.

Further analysis on the effects of clinical and radiological parameters on concordance rates revealed no significant correlations when C3 aspirates were considered negative, but when C3 aspirates were treated as a positive result, patients with lymphocytic thyroiditis had greater discordance rates. A reciprocal increased concordance rate was seen for patients with Graves’ disease. However, the case number for other types of thyroiditis (Granulomatous and IgG4-associated) were too small for meaningful comparison. Clinical background not only influences pre-test probably, but also affects the cytopathologists’ interpretation [24]. In the case of lymphocytic thyroiditis, it is known that focal dysplastic changes and anisonucleosis could be present in the absence of malignancy [4]. Recognition of this pitfall will lower the rate of false positive diagnosis [25].

Conclusion

Thyroid aspirates from patients with thyroiditis are distinct from those without in terms of the distribution in diagnostic categories and cytology histology concordance. There were more C3 diagnoses (atypia of undetermined significance/follicular lesion of undetermined significance) in the thyroiditis group. Treating C3 aspirates as positive reduces the concordance rate, with the thyroiditis group showing a trend of lower concordance compared with those without thyroiditis. Of all clinical and radiological parameters assessed, lymphocytic thyroiditis increased (and reciprocally Graves’ disease decreased) the discordance rate of thyroid aspiration cytology. Follow-up of C3 aspirates in patients with thyroiditis should err on the conservative side, and for those with lymphocytic thyroiditis, the lowered diagnostic accuracy of thyroid aspiration cytology should be taken into consideration for clinical management.

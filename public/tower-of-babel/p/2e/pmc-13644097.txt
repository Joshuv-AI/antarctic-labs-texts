NLR-based nomogram predicts survival in hepatocellular carcinoma treated with TACE plus targeted immunotherapy
Authors: Hao-Ming Lan, Lin Chen, Shuo Yang, Lin-Sheng Huang, Xian-Feng Shen, Meng Zhou, Ling-Yun Zhao, Jun Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) has emerged as a potential prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), but its predictive value in patients with unresectable HCC receiving transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) combined with targeted agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic significance of NLR in this setting and to develop individualized nomograms for predicting overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).MethodsThis retrospective real-world cohort consecutively enrolled 148 patients who received TACE combined with molecular targeted agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors between May 2020 and December 2023. OS and PFS were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods and compared by log-rank test. Prognostic factors were examined using Cox regression. Nomograms incorporating NLR and key clinicopathological variables were constructed, and model performance was assessed using time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) and concordance index (C-index).ResultsThe cohort was predominantly male (89.9%), and most patients had Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage B/C disease (86.5%); portal vein tumor thrombus was present in 41.2%. The objective response rate was 47.3% by modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, with a disease control rate of 89.9%. NLR independently predicted OS (HR 1.156, P = 0.013) and PFS (HR 1.165, P = 0.010). Using NLR cut-off of 2.49, patients with NLR ≥ 2.49 had significantly worse OS and PFS than those with NLR < 2.49 (both P < 0.001). NLR-based nomograms incorporating alpha-fetoprotein, Child–Pugh classification, and portal vein tumor thrombus showed moderate discrimination, with 0.5-year AUCs of 0.846 (95% CI, 0.734–0.958) for OS and 0.729 (95% CI, 0.621–0.838) for PFS.ConclusionNLR is a readily available biomarker that provides independent prognostic information in HCC patients receiving triple therapy. Nomograms may improve individualized risk stratification and facilitate treatment optimization in routine practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16436-x.

Background

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the predominant subtype of primary liver cancer, remains a leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide [1, 2]. For unresectable HCC, transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) remains a mainstay locoregional therapy [3, 4]; however, durable disease control after TACE remains challenging in some patients because of intrahepatic progression and extrahepatic spread.

In the era of effective systemic agents, combining TACE with molecular targeted therapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has been increasingly adopted in clinical practice, aiming to enhance tumor control through locoregional tumor debulking, antiangiogenic activity, and immune modulation [5, 6]. Nevertheless, outcomes after TACE-based triple therapy remain heterogeneous, underscoring the need for simple and reproducible biomarkers to support individualized risk stratification and follow-up.

Cancer-related inflammation contributes to tumor progression, angiogenesis, and immune evasion [7]. Blood-based inflammatory indices, including the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), may capture the balance between tumor-promoting systemic inflammation and antitumor immune competence [8–10]. Although NLR has been associated with prognosis in HCC treated with TACE or systemic therapy [11], its prognostic value in patients receiving contemporary TACE-based triple therapy, as well as its incremental utility over related inflammatory indices, remain incompletely defined in real-world settings.

Accordingly, we conducted a retrospective real-world study to evaluate baseline NLR as a prognostic biomarker in patients with HCC receiving TACE combined with targeted therapy and ICIs. We further constructed NLR-based nomograms to predict overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) and assessed their discrimination and calibration. Model performance was also compared with analogous MLR- and PLR-based nomograms.

Methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at the Taihe Hospital. Consecutive patients with HCC who received TACE combined with molecular targeted agents and ICIs between May 2020 and December 2023 were screened. The index date was defined as the date of the first TACE session initiating the combined regimen. Follow-up was administratively censored on 5 July 2025.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients were included if they met the following criteria: (1) Diagnosis of HCC confirmed by histopathology or radiological imaging; (2) receipt of TACE in combination with a molecular targeted agent and an ICI as a planned combined strategy; (3) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) 0–1; (4) Child–Pugh classification A or B; (5) unresectable disease determined by a multidisciplinary team; and (6) baseline laboratory tests available for calculating inflammatory indices within 7 days before the index TACE. Patients were excluded if they: (1) had cholangiocarcinoma or other non-HCC primary liver malignancies; (2) received other concomitant locoregional therapies in addition to the index combined regimen; (3) had follow-up < 3 months for time-to-event analyses without a documented event; (4) had incomplete data on key covariates required for model development; or (5) were lost to follow-up.

TACE procedure

Conventional transarterial chemoembolization (cTACE) or drug-eluting bead transarterial chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) was performed by experienced interventional radiologists. Under local anesthesia, angiography was conducted to identify tumor-feeding arteries. For cTACE, a lipiodol-chemotherapy emulsion (e.g., lobaplatin) was infused, followed by embolization with gelatin sponge particles until near stasis. For DEB-TACE, doxorubicin-loaded beads (e.g., DC Beads, CalliSpheres, or HepaSpheres) were delivered with embolization to angiographic stasis. The embolization endpoint and technical approach were individualized based on tumor vascularity and liver function.

Systemic targeted therapy and immunotherapy

Molecular targeted agents and ICIs were administered in accordance with standard dosing recommendations and at the investigators’ discretion. Systemic regimens included lenvatinib- or donafenib-based targeted therapy or bevacizumab-based anti-angiogenic therapy, each combined with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors (Table 1 and Table S1). Follow-up and assessment

After each combined therapy, patients underwent clinical assessment and laboratory testing. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed 4–8 weeks after TACE and subsequently at prespecified intervals to evaluate tumor response and progression. Additional imaging studies, such as bone scintigraphy or positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT), were performed when clinically indicated.

Endpoints

The primary endpoints were OS, defined as the time from index date to death from any cause or last follow-up, and PFS, defined as the time from index date to radiological progression or death, whichever occurred first.

Tumor response evaluation

Tumor response was primarily evaluated using modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) for HCC [12], with Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1) used as a supplementary criterion [13]. Objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the proportion achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), and disease control rate (DCR) as CR + PR + stable disease (SD).

Safety assessment

Adverse events (AEs) were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0 [14]. Treatment-related AEs were summarized by the maximum grade per patient.

Inflammatory indices and cut-off determination

Baseline NLR was calculated as absolute neutrophil count divided by absolute lymphocyte count; MLR as absolute monocyte count divided by absolute lymphocyte count; and PLR as platelet count divided by absolute lymphocyte count. Baseline NLR, MLR, and PLR were primarily analyzed as continuous variables in Cox regression models. For exploratory Kaplan–Meier stratification, data-derived thresholds for NLR, MLR, and PLR were estimated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis for 0.5-year OS based on the maximum Youden index. These thresholds were used only for descriptive risk stratification and were not interpreted as definitive clinical cut-offs.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and categorical variables as number (percentage). Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared by log-rank test. For Kaplan–Meier analyses, patients were stratified using ROC-derived cut-offs for inflammatory indices and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), as appropriate. Cox regression was used for univariable and multivariable analyses; variables with P < 0.05 in univariable analyses or deemed clinically relevant were entered into multivariable models. Sensitivity Cox analyses were performed with additional adjustment for TACE modality and systemic treatment category. Exact drug combinations were not simultaneously included because sparse subgroups could lead to model instability and overfitting. For OS, a clinical base model including AFP, Child–Pugh classification, and portal vein tumor thrombus (PVTT) was predefined, and NLR was subsequently incorporated to assess its added prognostic value. For PFS, the nomogram was developed from the final multivariable Cox regression model. Model discrimination was evaluated using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) and time-dependent ROC curves, with time-dependent area under the curves (AUCs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) at 0.5, 1, and 2 years. Calibration was assessed using time-specific calibration curves. Internal validation of the NLR-based nomograms was conducted with 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Optimism-corrected C-index and calibration slope were reported to assess discrimination and calibration. Clinical utility was further evaluated using decision curve analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.1.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing), SPSS (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and GraphPad Prism (version 9.5; GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Treatment patterns

The most frequent targeted therapy/ICI combinations were lenvatinib plus tislelizumab (n = 48, 32.4%), lenvatinib plus camrelizumab (n = 33, 22.3%), and lenvatinib plus sintilimab (n = 29, 19.6%). The median number of TACE sessions was 2 [interquartile range (IQR), 1–3]. cTACE was performed in 94 patients (63.5%) and DEB-TACE in 54 (36.5%) (Table 1).

Cut-off values for inflammatory indices

ROC analysis for 0.5-year OS identified optimal cut-offs of 2.49 for NLR (AUC = 0.729), 0.39 for MLR (AUC = 0.691), and 145.43 for PLR (AUC = 0.674). These thresholds were used for Kaplan–Meier visualization and descriptive risk stratification (Figure S1).

Survival analyses

Kaplan–Meier curves for PFS and OS in the overall cohort are shown in Fig. 1. The median PFS and OS were 20.4 and 28.7 months, respectively (Fig. 1A and B). Survival stratified by baseline AFP and NLR is presented in Fig. 2. Patients with AFP ≥ 400 ng/mL had significantly worse OS and PFS than those with AFP < 400 ng/mL (median OS: 26.5 vs. 34.0 months; P < 0.001; median PFS: 14.9 vs. 28.8 months; P = 0.002) (Fig. 2A and C). Sensitivity analyses accounting for treatment heterogeneity

To assess the potential influence of treatment heterogeneity, sensitivity Cox analyses were performed by additionally adjusting for TACE modality and systemic treatment category. After adjustment for these treatment-related variables, baseline NLR remained independently associated with PFS (HR 1.203, 95% CI 1.102–1.314, P < 0.001) and OS (HR 1.196, 95% CI 1.096–1.305, P < 0.001). TACE modality and systemic treatment category were not significantly associated with either PFS or OS in these sensitivity analyses. Detailed results are shown in Table S5.

Nomogram development and performance

We constructed NLR-based nomograms for predicting 0.5-, 1-, and 2-year OS and PFS (Fig. 5A and B). Time-dependent ROC analyses suggested that adding NLR to the clinical base model (AFP, Child–Pugh classification, and PVTT) modestly improved discrimination. For OS, the AUCs of the O-base + NLR model were 0.846 (95% CI, 0.734–0.958), 0.780 (95% CI, 0.650–0.911), and 0.724 (95% CI, 0.631–0.817) at 0.5, 1, and 2 years, respectively, with a C-index of 0.691 (95% CI 0.661–0.721) (Table S2; Fig. 6A–C). For PFS, the corresponding AUCs were 0.729 (95% CI, 0.621–0.838), 0.697 (95% CI, 0.610–0.784), and 0.718 (95% CI, 0.629–0.807), respectively, with a C-index of 0.681 (95% CI 0.652–0.709) (Table S2; Fig. 6D–F). Calibration curves showed acceptable agreement between predicted and observed survival probabilities (Fig. 7). Bootstrap internal validation with 1,000 resamples showed acceptable internal model performance. The optimism-corrected C-index was 0.679 for the OS nomogram and 0.669 for the PFS nomogram. The optimism-corrected calibration slopes were 0.902 and 0.918 for the OS and PFS nomograms, respectively, indicating acceptable internal calibration. Detailed results are provided in Table S3. Discussion

In this single-center real-world cohort of 148 patients with HCC treated with TACE combined with molecular targeted therapy and ICIs, baseline NLR was independently associated with both OS and PFS. When modeled as a continuous variable, higher baseline NLR predicted worse outcomes (OS: HR 1.156, 95% CI 1.031–1.297; P = 0.013; PFS: HR 1.165, 95% CI 1.038–1.309; P = 0.010). Using an ROC-derived threshold (NLR ≥ 2.49), patients with higher baseline NLR experienced significantly shorter OS and PFS than those with NLR < 2.49, supporting NLR as a practical marker for pretreatment risk stratification before initiating TACE-based triple therapy [15]. Therefore, the contribution of this study lies not in proposing NLR as a new biomarker, but in validating and operationalizing this readily available inflammatory marker in the specific setting of TACE plus targeted immunotherapy.

Mechanistically, NLR reflects the balance between tumor-promoting systemic inflammation and antitumor immune competence [16, 17]. In this treatment context, NLR may capture both the proangiogenic inflammatory response related to locoregional tumor injury and the lymphocyte-dependent immune competence required for durable benefit from ICIs. Neutrophilia can indicate an inflammatory milieu that facilitates angiogenesis, tumor invasion, and immunosuppression (e.g., via expansion of myeloid-derived suppressor cells and formation of neutrophil extracellular traps) [18, 19], whereas lymphopenia may represent impaired cytotoxic lymphocyte activity and weakened immune surveillance [20]. In the context of locoregional therapy and immunotherapy, an elevated inflammatory burden may attenuate immune activation and limit durable responses, potentially explaining the observed associations between higher NLR and inferior survival [15, 21].

Beyond association analyses, we translated NLR into individualized prediction tools by developing NLR-based nomograms for OS and PFS. These models integrated AFP, PVTT, and Child–Pugh class, representing complementary dimensions of tumor biology, macrovascular invasion, and hepatic functional reserve. In the overall cohort, the NLR-augmented models showed moderate discrimination, with 0.5-year AUCs of 0.846 (95% CI, 0.734–0.958) for OS and 0.729 (95% CI, 0.621–0.838) for PFS, together with acceptable calibration. Bootstrap internal validation further showed optimism-corrected C-index values of 0.679 for OS and 0.669 for PFS, supporting acceptable but moderate internal performance. These tools may help exploratory risk stratification and identify patients who may require closer monitoring, but their clinical use should be validated in independent cohorts.

Safety remains a key consideration for TACE combined with systemic therapy. In this cohort, treatment-related AEs were predominantly hepatic and hematologic, and grade 3–4 toxicities were uncommon. These findings underscore the importance of appropriate patient selection, close laboratory monitoring, proactive management of liver function, and timely dose modification or treatment interruption to optimize the risk–benefit profile of TACE-based triple therapy in routine practice.

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective single-center design is subject to selection bias and residual confounding. The cohort was predominantly composed of patients with HBV-related HCC (139/148, 93.9%), which may limit the generalizability of our findings to other HCC etiologies and warrants validation in more etiologically diverse cohorts. BCLC-stratified sensitivity analyses showed that the association between baseline NLR and survival remained evident in the BCLC B/C subgroup, although estimates in the BCLC A subgroup should be interpreted cautiously because of the limited sample size and few events (Table S6). Second, although sensitivity analyses adjusted for TACE modality and systemic treatment category, treatment heterogeneity remained. Given the limited sample size and sparse drug-combination subgroups, residual treatment-selection confounding could not be excluded [22]. Third, although bootstrap internal validation was performed and calibration slopes were reported, this study lacked an independent external validation cohort. Therefore, the nomograms should be considered exploratory risk-stratification tools and require external validation before routine clinical implementation. In addition, the ROC-derived NLR threshold was obtained from the present cohort and may be affected by threshold-selection bias. Therefore, this value should be considered exploratory and should not be regarded as a universal clinical cut-off without external validation. Albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) grade and tumor burden score are also important prognostic variables in HCC. In this study, Child–Pugh classification was retained in the primary nomograms because it is widely used and available for all patients. ALBI score was not included together with Child–Pugh classification because both reflect overlapping liver function components, which may introduce collinearity. Similarly, tumor burden score was not entered with tumor number and maximum tumor diameter because it is derived from these variables. Exploratory sensitivity analyses incorporating ALBI score and tumor burden score showed consistent associations between baseline NLR and survival, supporting the robustness of NLR as a prognostic marker (Table S7). Line of systemic therapy was not uniformly documented and could not be reliably included. Future prospective studies should further evaluate models incorporating ALBI grade, tumor burden score, and treatment line. Finally, we focused on baseline inflammatory indices; longitudinal changes in NLR during treatment and their relationship with response were not evaluated [23]. Future studies should validate these findings prospectively and explore whether integrating dynamic biomarkers and imaging features can further improve individualized risk stratification.

Conclusion

Baseline NLR is an inexpensive and readily available biomarker that independently predicts survival in HCC patients treated with TACE combined with molecular targeted agents and ICIs. NLR-based nomograms integrating AFP, Child–Pugh classification, and PVTT may help individualized prognostic assessment, but external validation is required before routine clinical application.

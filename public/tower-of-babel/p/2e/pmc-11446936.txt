Evolutionary game analysis of multi-agent cooperation strategy analysis in agricultural water conservancy PPP project under digitization background
Authors: Wei Zhang, Hui Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study constructed a system dynamics (SD) evolutionary game model, from which we analyzed the conditions that need to be met by multi-agents to achieve cooperation in agricultural water conservancy (AWC) Public-Private Partnership (PPP) projects. Simultaneously, this study used numerical simulation to depict the impact of initial participation willingness, reward, punishment, income, and other parameters of local governments, project enterprises, and farmers on the evolution strategy. The results firstly showed that the basic conditions for tripartite cooperation are to strengthen the rewards and punishments for project enterprises and improve the participation benefits of farmers. Secondly, it showed that increasing the rewards for farmers and improving the project dividend benefits can effectively motivate farmers to participate. Thirdly, it showed that enhancing the incentive subsidies and reputation benefits for project enterprise’s participation, as well as strengthening the supervision of local government, boosts the motivation of project enterprises significantly. However, it is vital to ensure that the reward amounts are not too high to allow local government to play its guiding role efficiently. Lastly, the results indicated that digital technology can help reduce the participation cost of local government, project enterprises and farmers, and improve the external reputation benefits of each participant, thus forming a win-win situation for all three participants. Analyzing the cooperation strategies of the three parties in AWC PPP projects provides theoretical support and a decision-making basis for the efficient operation of AWC PPP projects under the digital background, and also provides practical policy recommendations for the high-quality development of AWC.Keywords: Agricultural water conservancy, PPP project, Cooperation strategy, SD evolutionary game, DigitizationSubject terms: Psychology and behaviour, Socioeconomic scenarios, Sustainability

Introduction

China is one of the countries suffering the most severe agricultural disasters in the world. Between 2011 and 2021, an annual average of around 22.9 million hectares of crops were affected by various disasters. Notably, since July 2022, the Yangtze River basin has experienced a prolonged period of high temperatures and scarce rainfall. Nationwide, drought conditions necessitated livelihood assistance for around 4.49 million people, with a total of 4.284 million hectares of crops being damaged (Data sources: “The Ministry of Emergency Management releases the national natural disaster situation in the first three quarters of 2022”, https://www.mem.gov.cn/xw/yjglbgzdt/202210/t20221010_423761.shtml). As the lifeblood of agricultural development, agricultural water conservancy (AWC) can not only improve the agricultural production environment and reduce the impact of drought, floods, floods and other natural disasters on agricultural production but also play a key role in ensuring food security and the effective supply of important agricultural products. However, due to the large scale of investment in AWC projects, strong public welfare, long return cycle, and high−risk characteristics, the management of AWC faces a relative lack of public financial investment1, and the formation of investment gaps, and then constrained the development of agriculture to a certain extent. With the development of the capital market, as a project management model promoted by the government in recent years, the Public−Private−Partnership (PPP) model has the characteristics of an economy of scope and scale and is widely used in the infrastructure field because it can effectively relieve the government’s financial pressure and attract social capital participation2,3. As a result, to solve the situation of a single supply entity of AWC, insufficient financial investment, and lagging supporting facilities, and consolidate the foundation of agricultural production, the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Water Resources issued the “Management Measures for the Use of Subsidy Funds for Farmland Water Conservancy Facilities Construction and Soil and Water Conservation” in 2016, proposing that AWC facilities can adopt the PPP model to carry out project construction in accordance with regulations. In 2022, the Ministry of Water Resources issued the “Guiding Opinions on Promoting the Development of the Government−Private Partnership (PPP) Model for Water Conservancy Infrastructure”, which clearly pointed out the need to deepen the reform of water conservancy investment and financing, actively guide various types of social capital to participate in water conservancy construction and operation, and broaden the long−term funding channels for water conservancy infrastructure construction. According to statistics from the National Development and Reform Commission, as of May 2022, the number of urban infrastructure and agriculture, forestry and water conservancy PPP projects reached 3046 and 1073 respectively, with a total investment of 4570.2 billion yuan and 983.9 billion yuan respectively (Data sources: “Statistics of PPP projects of the National Development and Reform Commission”, https://fgw.panjin.gov.cn/2022_05/20_09/content-372965.html), ranking first and second among all industries. Therefore, introducing the PPP model into the field of infrastructure, especially farmland water conservancy facilities, plays a vital role in promoting the high−quality development of farmland water conservancy, ensuring national food security, and fully implementing the rural revitalization strategy.Due to the large investment scale, multiple and conflicting public policy objectives, huge sunk costs, and multiple risks associated with AWC PPP projects4, these projects are not entirely applicable to the existing PPP theories. Theoretically, in the practice of AWC PPP projects, the project enterprise should be responsible for the construction and operation, and take the way of combining government subsidy and user payment to obtain profits. As users, farmers must pay water fees regularly and participate in dividends by investing in enterprises. However, project enterprises often take advantage of information to cut production costs and neglect work to make more profits. Thus, project enterprises are prone to value conflicts with their principals due to excessive profit-seeking, which can be detrimental to the public interest and affect the success of PPP projects5,6. Meanwhile, farmers, as rational people, tend to “free-riding” and are less willing to pay water bills, especially upstream farmers who have a natural advantage in water use and tend to pay no or fewer water bills7. Furthermore, in the implementation of AWC PPP projects, there are dilemmas such as two-way rent-seeking by the government and enterprises and difficulties in landing the projects8. It can be seen that the AWC PPP projects conflicting interests and behaviors with the existing PPP theory.

As human society enters the digital era, the booming development of digital technology has promoted economic growth9. The continuous development of new industries, new forms of business, and new models have reshaped the era background of PPP project governance of AWC. Digital technology has created unprecedented opportunities for improving the level of AWC governance. Under the background of digitalization, the participants of AWC PPP projects are endowed with strong water control action ability by integrating various emerging digital technologies into the whole process, including investment, construction, and management. Digital technology continuously improves the digitalization, networking, and intelligence level of PPP project governance of AWC, and enhances the interaction and cooperation between different participants, thereby actualizing the effective management of AWC PPP projects. Therefore, in-depth research on the behavioral evolution strategies of local government, project enterprises, and farmers in AWC PPP projects under the digital background and exploring the conditions the three parties need to meet to achieve cooperation have significant theoretical value and practical significance for improving the operating efficiency of AWC PPP projects.

The current literature on the AWC PPP project primarily focuses on the applicability of the project and the strategic behaviors of the game subjects. Many scholars analyzed the feasibility of introducing the PPP model in AWC construction and operation management. They argue that the introduction of the PPP model helps to reduce the financial burden on local government, optimize social resources, and improve the efficiency and quality of project construction10,11. Tariq and Zhang12 studied and discussed the main failure drivers of water PPP projects at different stages in order to better manage water PPP projects and enhance the applicability of the PPP model in the water sector. Wang et al.13 believe that the PPP model has become increasingly popular in the field of water conservancy projects in China because it can reduce costs and improve the efficiency of government fund utilization.

With the development of game theory, scholars have gradually begun to use game theory to analyze the strategy choice of PPP model actors. Zhao et al.14 constructed a game model of the government overburdening social capital with public goods under the perspective of incomplete contracts. They found that the current optimal strategy of local government is to reduce the local policy by defining its power boundary as “temporal inconsistency”. Wang et al.15 used the evolutionary game model to explore the dynamic game between government departments and enterprises in public-private-partnership for farmland consolidation. Specific to AWC PPP projects, Liu et al.16 established a stochastic evolutionary game model for AWC PPP projects in an uncertain environment. They explored the stability of the strategic choices of both project companies and farmers under stochastic perturbations. Sisavath and Wu17 explored the dynamic game between the government and private institutions in water conservancy PPP projects by using the principle-agent model with no uncertainty and the principle-agent model with uncertainty but supervision. Some scholars constructed a three-party game model based on MA-PT theory with the government, farmers, and water supply units as the main subjects, to explore the dynamic evolution process of the behavior and decision-making of the stakeholders in the comprehensive agricultural water price reform and the influencing factors18. Some scholars also analyzed the cooperation strategy of rural water environment governance PPP projects from the perspective of an evolutionary game, and the dynamic process between the project company and farmers was elaborated19. However, few studies exist on the evolutionary game among local government, project enterprises, and farmers in the context of AWC PPP models.

In general, the existing research has conducted in-depth discussions on the applicability of the AWC PPP model and has also focused on the behavioral strategy choices of the game players in AWC PPP projects, which provides an important foundation and reference for this paper. However, it still needs to be further deepened: First, the existing research on AWC PPP projects mainly focuses on the game between the government and enterprises, enterprises and farmers, while analyzing of multi-party evolutionary game of AWC PPP projects is relatively rare. Second, the existing research mainly focuses on the role of the PPP model in improving the construction and operation efficiency of AWC, and rarely explores the behavioral logic of multi-subjects to achieve cooperation in the AWC PPP model. There has been no in-depth analysis conducted on the impact of crucial parameters such as government subsidies and punishment intensity on the behavior of these three participants. Third, the existing study mainly explores the gaming behaviors of AWC PPP project participants within a traditional environment but does not consider how local government, project enterprises, and farmers achieve cooperation in AWC PPP projects under the digital background. What is the logic of their behavioral evolution? What conditions need to be met? Based on the limitations and gaps in existing research, this paper constructs the system dynamics (SD) evolutionary game model of the three parties in the AWC PPP project under the digital background, explores the conditions that need to be met for the local government, project enterprises, and farmers to achieve cooperation, and takes the Jishou AWC PPP project as an example to conduct a numerical simulation of the decision-making behavior of the subjects, in order to effectively improve the operation efficiency of the farmland water conservancy PPP project, promote the high-quality development of the farmland water conservancy PPP project, and provide practical policy recommendations.

This study makes unique contributions to the current body of knowledge when contrasted with current literature: First, the paper combines evolutionary game theory with system dynamics to construct a basic entry tree model of the SD evolutionary game flow rate of the tripartite subject of the AWC PPP project, depicting the influencing factors of the three parties’ behavioral strategies. Second, the study is based on the traditional government-enterprise binary paradigm, incorporates farmers as an indispensable subject, and establishes a tripartite evolutionary game model of local government, project enterprises, and farmers under the background of digitization. It expands existing research on AWC PPP projects, enhancing its practical significance. Third, the paper takes the Jishou City AWC PPP project as a scenario case to conduct numerical simulation analysis, improves the reliability of the research results, and provides a theoretical reference for the government to standardize the AWC PPP model.

Basic assumptions and model construction

Basic assumptions

The success of PPP projects relies heavily on the development of stable and positive cooperative relationships among the parties involved20. AWC PPP projects differ from traditional PPP projects in that farmers’ tendency to shirk their responsibilities is more pronounced in certain regions, and the government’s ability to effectively monitor them varies. This leads to a higher level of uncertainty during the implementation process and poses a challenge in terms of coordinating the interests of all parties involved to establish a cooperative relationship.

To this end, this paper defines the main participants of AWC PPP projects as local government, project enterprises, and farmers according to the actual situation of AWC PPP projects. The local government in the AWC PPP project not only has to complete the task of AWC management by the Superior government and accept the assessment and supervision, but also, as the leading PPP project, it is a big test for the government’s governance ability to be familiar with PPP expertise and guide and supervise the enterprises, and the cycle of the AWC PPP project is usually long, which is full of various uncertainties, which leads to contracts signed by both parties not being fully contractual. At the same time, local government has more extensive resource advantages and political authority in PPP projects compared to project enterprises21, especially in the case of incomplete contracts, where local government plays games with project enterprises over financial subsidies, incentives, and franchise duration during the operation of PPP projects. The project enterprise, as the agent of the AWC PPP project, often has more information than the principal local government due to the existence of the principal-agent relationship, and it has a better understanding of the costs in the construction and management of AWC. Thus, with information asymmetry, project enterprises are prone to value conflicts with their principals due to excessive profit-seeking, which can be detrimental to the public interest and affect the success of PPP projects22. Farmers, as direct users of AWC and direct beneficiaries of the project, are expected to pay water fees to the project enterprises but are often reluctant to do so due to opportunistic self-interest and the phenomenon of “free-riding”, which leads to difficulties in collective action.

In AWC PPP projects, the quality of construction and operational efficiency of AWC projects mainly depend on whether the participating parties can form a cooperative relationship through repeated games. Assuming that the game participants, local government, project enterprises, and farmers are all finite and rational15, and will make optimal strategy choices through continuous learning and trial and error. These three participants do not overlap23. Based on the actual situation and the recent study24–26, we analyze the evolutionary game relationship among multiple participants of AWC PPP projects under the background of digitization. The main hypotheses are as follows:

Hypothesis 1

Due to the complexity of AWC PPP projects, the construction and operation activities need to be coordinated among various participants. In the game, it is assumed that the choice of their strategies is mainly based on their perceived value of the benefits and costs of the strategy27. Here, the impact of external factors like government policies, climate change, and competition for resources is not considered. The strategy of local government is “Guide or not Guide”, where “Guide” means actively guiding enterprises to comply with strategies with incentives and constraints28, and then encouraging farmers to participate in AWC PPP projects. Moreover, project enterprises have two strategic options. Firstly, they need to strictly follow the contract, fulfill the relevant obligations, and actively cooperate to ensure the good operation of AWC. Secondly, enterprises slack on fulfilling the responsibility of AWC management, and attempt to use opportunism or rent-seeking to obtain more economic benefits. Therefore, the project enterprise’s behavior strategy is classified to “Strive or not Strive”. Farmers also have two options. Firstly, they participate in the AWC PPP project actively and take shares in the form of work and investment to participate in dividends. Therefore, they will receive rewards from local government, increase food production and income from the good operation of AWC. Secondly, they are not willing to participate in the construction and management of AWC, or tend to take “free-riding” behavior, even not participate in AWC PPP projects. Therefore, the farmer’s behavior strategy is classified to “Positively participate or Negatively participate”.

If the project enterprise chooses speculative behavior, the AWC quality decline will lead to farmer’s losses L3.

Scenario analysis of multi subject evolutionary game

Analysis of game results in different situations

Given the poor identification of stability conditions for the evolutionary equilibrium point between the local government, project enterprises, and farmers, and the existence of multiple complex evolutionary paths for all three. Therefore, based on the characteristics of the subjects of interest in AWC PPP projects, and drawing on other studies15,39,40, this paper divides them into three participants cooperating, none of the three participants cooperating, and three participants involved in cooperation to varying degrees.

Scenario 1

Tripartite cooperation. This scenario refers to a cooperative relationship between the local government, the project enterprise and the farmers, i.e., E5(1,1,1), and the eigenvalues need to satisfy the following condition: . Under that stability condition, for farmers, when increasing the dividends they receive from their shareholding in the project enterprise and increasing the incentives for farmers to participate in the PPP project will help promote their active participation in the synergistic action of the AWC PPP project, which further validates Corollary 1. For project enterprise, when local governments increase their subsidies or penalties, they can be promoted to choose behaviors that benefit the public interest, which further validates Corollary 4. For local government, when the sum of the cost of guidance of government expenditures and the incentive subsidies given to enterprises and farmers is less than the amount of their losses due to inaction, local government tend to choose the guidance strategy. Therefore, it is crucial to prioritize the rational design of constraint mechanisms and the enhanced application of digital technology in AWC PPP projects to minimize the regulatory costs for local government. Furthermore, increasing penalties for government inaction will be conducive to enhancing the government’s proactive participation and guidance.

Scenario 2

All three participants do not cooperate. This scenario means that all three participants choose the non-cooperation strategy based on their respective interests, i.e., the corresponding E1(0,0,0), at which time the eigenvalues need to satisfy the following condition: . Although in this case all three participants of the game will choose the optimal strategy according to their own interests, the game system has a certain degree of stability. However, in reality, as the state attaches more importance to AWC, the government will require market players to increase their investment to ensure the construction quality and operational efficiency of AWC projects, thus increasing the rewards and penalties for market players. In addition, the cost of “not Strive” strategy for project companies is greater, so they will increase the investment in AWC projects. Farmers will also seek to participate due to neighborhood and scale of operation. Therefore, in reality, it is difficult to maintain this situation in the long term, but it requires some market mechanism and public choice by the government to move toward win-win cooperation.

Scenario 3

Different degrees of tripartite participation in cooperation. This scenario refers to the different degrees of participation of the local government, the project enterprise and the farmers in the collaborative action of the AWC PPP project. When one participant cooperates and all other participants do not, there is an evolutionary equilibrium point E2(1,0,0). Specifically, under the stability condition of the evolutionary equilibrium, the additional cost of active participation for farmers is greater than the sum of the incentives and dividends they receive, so they will choose the strategy of inactivity. For project enterprises, the additional cost of choosing the strategy of effort is greater than the sum of the local government’s subsidies, external gains from compliance and penalties for default, so project enterprises tend to choose the strategy of “not Strive”. For local government, the cost of choosing the strategy of guidance is less than the sum of losses from inaction and penalties for project enterprises, so choosing the strategy of guidance is their optimal strategy.

When two participants cooperate and the other does not, there exist two evolutionary equilibrium points as E3(1,1,0) and E4(1,0,1) at this time. Specifically, under the stability condition of the equilibrium point E3(1,1,0), the amount of loss suffered by the local government due to non-guidance is greater than the sum of the incentive subsidy and the cost of guidance given to the enterprise, and the optimal choice of the local government is “Guide” strategy. The additional cost of effort for the project enterprise is less than the sum of the external benefits, subsidy incentives, and penalties for inaction, so the project enterprise tends to choose “Strive” strategy. In contrast, the additional cost of active participation is greater than the sum of the incentives and dividends received by the farmers, so they tend to choose “Negatively participate” as their game strategy. This phenomenon may be because although local governments actively promote the participation of project enterprises in AWC PPP projects, farmers’ participation in the project decision-making and design phase is often a formality, resulting in low willingness to participate in AWC construction projects that do not meet farmers’ expectations and actual production conditions. This also illustrates the principle of always putting the farmer at the center of the AWC PPP project. Under the stability condition of the equilibrium point E4(1,0,1), the benefits of local government participation in guidance are greater than the benefits of non-participation, so the local government tends to choose the guidance strategy. The cost of choosing the Strive strategy is too high for the project enterprise, and the default penalty cannot effectively restrain its behavior, so the optimal strategy for the project enterprise is “not Strive” Therefore, the optimal strategy for project enterprises is “not Strive”. The sum of rewards and dividends obtained by farmers’ active strategy is greater than the additional cost of participating in cooperation, so the “Positively participate” strategy is the optimal choice for farmers.

From the results of the above analysis, In the context of digitalization, when is satisfied, the local government, the project enterprise and the farmers agree on the objectives and form a tripartite partnership, at which time the AWC PPP project operates with the highest efficiency and is conducive to promoting the construction of AWC. On the contrary, when the participation cost is too high, and the benefits they receive and the cost of institutional constraints are low, the three participants tend to choose not to cooperate. In cases of varying degrees of cooperation, the local government’s choice to guide the strategy significantly impacts the strategy selection behavior of other actors. Only when the local government participates in guidance, appropriately strengthens the rewards and punishments for project enterprises, and increases the benefits of farmers’ participation, can different degrees of cooperation be promoted to the state of tripartite cooperation.

Typical case analysis

Case overview

Based on the above analysis, it can be seen that in the practice of AWC PPP projects, the ideal evolutionary stability strategy is that local governments, project enterprises, and farmers cooperate to participate in the construction and operation of AWC PPP projects, which is the ideal evolutionary stability strategy. To further analyze the theoretical conclusions of evolutionary games, this paper takes the Jishou City AWC PPP Construction Project as a case sample. Hunan Province, as one of the first pilot provinces for PPP in the country, has launched four batches of 316 PPP demonstration projects since 2014. Among them, Jishou City’s small and medium-sized farmland water conservancy and small and medium-sized reservoir PPP construction projects were listed as the fourth batch of PPP demonstration projects in Hunan Province. The project construction investment amount is 880 million yuan, including two sub-projects, small and medium-sized reservoir construction projects, and small farmland water conservancy projects, with a construction period of 2 years and an operation period of 13 years. The project adopts the design-build-operate-transfer (DBOT) model. The Jishou Municipal Government authorizes the Jishou Municipal Water Resources Bureau as the implementing agency to participate in the project construction, and China Camc Engineering Co., Ltd. is responsible for investment and financing, construction, maintenance, and transfer upon expiration. It will build 95 km of irrigation and drainage channels in many towns and streets in the city, renovate 1,530 mountain ponds, renovate 320 river dams, and build 1,920 new water cellars.

Analysis of the evolution process of game among different subjects

During the project construction phase, China Camc Engineering Co., Ltd. and Jishou City Jili Water Company, the representative of Jishou City Water Conservancy Bureau, jointly invested 177 million yuan to establish Jishou City China Camc Water Limited Liability Company (hereinafter referred to as the project enterprise). With the support of mobile Internet, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, etc., they built an AWC PPP project management platform to supervise the entire process of project construction, management, and maintenance, effectively reducing the participation costs of various entities and improving the operation efficiency of AWC PPP projects. At the same time, local government and village organizations actively mobilized surrounding farmers to participate in the construction of AWC PPP projects and gave certain rewards to participating farmers. In addition, to prevent the project enterprises from engaging in opportunistic behaviors such as “free riding”, according to the performance contract, China Camc Engineering Co., Ltd. needs to pay a performance bond of 20 million yuan to the Jishou City Water Conservancy Bureau during the construction period to ensure the smooth implementation of AWC projects. At this time, local government, project enterprises, and farmers all actively participated in the construction of AWC PPP projects, and the three parties formed a win-win cooperation relationship.

During the project operation phase, the project enterprises are responsible for the operation, maintenance, and management of the AWC PPP project, and its main profit method is user payment. According to field investigations, the Jishou City Water Conservancy Bureau can monitor in real-time the operation status of AWC, the project enterprises’ fulfillment of their maintenance responsibilities, and farmers’ use of AWC through the AWC PPP project management platform. Under the background of digital supervision, the cost of project enterprises’ breach of contract and farmers’ “free-riding” behavior has increased. In addition, after the project is completed and accepted, the Jishou City Water Conservancy Bureau will also provide China Camc Engineering Co., Ltd. with a feasibility gap subsidy every year to encourage project enterprises to fulfill the contract on time and efficiently. Simultaneously, to ensure the quality of AWC facilities, before the end of the project cooperation period, China Camc Engineering Co., Ltd. must pay a performance guarantee of 20 million yuan to the Jishou City Water Conservancy Bureau for the transfer of defects liability period. At this time, the project enterprises actively assume the responsibility of operating and managing AWC facilities and form a management and maintenance team to be responsible for the management and maintenance of AWC facilities so that AWC can operate efficiently. Farmers also pay their water fees on time and, under the mobilization of village and community organizations, actively participate in the management and maintenance of AWC, such as dredging and unblocking pipes. This means that the local government, project enterprises, and farmers have reached a consensus on goals and formed a win-win cooperation relationship, achieving the optimal balance {Guide, Strive, Positively participate}.

Simultaneously, encourage farmers to supervise the AWC PPP projects and establish an effective information feedback mechanism to ensure that the issues raised by farmers can be transmitted to the local government promptly.

However, this paper still has some limitations: First, the system dynamics model relies on simplified assumptions about agent behavior and influencing factors, potentially overlooking complexities like risk, information asymmetry, and sociocultural factors. This paper mainly uses the system dynamics model to deeply reveal the key factors that affect the behavioral strategy selection of the main body of AWC PPP projects and to accurately identify the factors that restrict the cooperation of the three parties. Second, this paper only analyzes the driving factors of the tripartite collaboration among local government, project enterprises, and farmers from the perspective of the costs and benefits of the main participants, without considering the impact of external factors such as government policies, climate change, and resource competition. This article may simplify the real situation, but it does not affect the analysis of the three-party game strategy in this paper. Third, due to the limitations of data acquisition, this paper only uses the data from the Jishou City AWC PPP Project for numerical simulation analysis. Future research should obtain more case data and consider more factors, such as the risk attitude of participants, the policy environment, and information asymmetry, to more comprehensively and deeply analyze the factors affecting the behavioral strategies of multiple subjects in AWC PPP projects and provide decision-making references for the efficient operation of AWC PPP projects.

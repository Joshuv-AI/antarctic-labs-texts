Early tumor-informed circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) dynamics correlate with response to first-line advanced NSCLC treated with pembrolizumab ± chemotherapy
Authors: Diego Enrico, Gisela Vargas, Gabriela Wulfson, Carmen Pupareli, Florencia Tsou, Delfina Peralta Tanco, Federico Waisberg, Lucas Coradini, Milena Revelli Ling, Matías Chacón, Roberto Borea, Christian Rolfo, Claudio Martín
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), alone or combined with chemotherapy, constitute the standard first-line treatment for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) without actionable oncogenic drivers. However, radiologic response assessment has limitations in capturing early biological treatment effects. Tumor-informed circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) monitoring has emerged as a promising biomarker for early evaluation of treatment efficacy during immunotherapy.MethodsWe conducted a prospective, single-center, longitudinal observational study including patients with stage IV NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab with or without platinum-based chemotherapy between December 2021 and December 2024. Personalized, tumor-informed ctDNA assays (Signatera™, Natera, Inc.) were designed from tumor tissue and matched normal blood samples and applied to serial plasma samples collected at baseline (prior to ICI initiation) and every six weeks on-treatment. Early ctDNA dynamics were assessed from baseline to the first on-treatment assessment (six weeks post-treatment initiation) and were correlated with radiologic response assessed by RECIST v1.1, objective response rate (ORR), and progression-free survival (PFS).ResultsFifteen patients were enrolled; baseline ctDNA positivity was observed in 13 (87%) patients. Among patients with ctDNA positivity, 11 (85%) experienced a molecular response characterized by ctDNA decrease (n = 1) or clearance (n = 10), while 2 (15%) showed an increase. Early molecular response was strongly associated with radiologic response, with a significantly higher ORR among patients with ctDNA clearance or decrease compared with those with ctDNA increase (91% vs 0%, p = 0.038). Early ctDNA change correlated with percentage change in target lesion size (Spearman R = 0.81, p < 0.001). Longitudinal monitoring demonstrated sustained ctDNA clearance or decrease in patients with durable disease control, while ctDNA positivity preceded radiologic progression. Patient-level survival analysis showed longer progression-free survival intervals predominantly among patients with early ctDNA clearance or decrease.ConclusionsEarly clearance or decrease in ctDNA levels strongly predicted radiographic response in advanced NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy. ctDNA dynamics may complement radiological assessment and support more personalized, timely, and biologically informed treatment decisions in advanced NSCLC.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains the leading cause of cancer-related incidence and mortality worldwide [1]. Despite significant advances in systemic therapy over the past decades, the prognosis of patients with advanced-stage disease remains poor, underscoring the need for improved treatment strategies and more precise tools for treatment response assessment [2].

The introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the programmed death-1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) axis has transformed the therapeutic landscape of advanced NSCLC [3,4]. While substantial clinical benefit is achieved with immune checkpoint inhibitors, treatment response monitoring remains challenging in routine clinical practice, because treatment responses may be delayed, heterogeneous, or dissociated across lesions. Conventional radiological evaluation using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 is limited by its inability to capture early biological response, delayed radiographic changes, and atypical response patterns such as pseudoprogression, which are particularly relevant in the immunotherapy setting [5,6]. Moreover, radiological assessments are typically performed every 8–12 weeks, potentially delaying recognition of both therapeutic benefit and primary resistance.

Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), the tumor-derived fraction of cell-free DNA released into the bloodstream through apoptosis, necrosis, and active secretion, is a promising non-invasive biomarker for real-time monitoring of tumor burden and treatment response [7,8]. In the context of immunotherapy for NSCLC, several studies have reported that early decreases in ctDNA levels during PD-1/PD-L1 blockade are associated with higher objective response rates (ORRs) and improved survival, while persistent ctDNA positivity or rising ctDNA levels identify patients with primary resistance [[9], [10], [11]]. These observations have been consistently reported across both immune checkpoint inhibitor monotherapy and combination regimens with chemotherapy, highlighting the potential role of ctDNA dynamics as an early pharmacodynamic biomarker of immunotherapy efficacy.

Methodologically, ctDNA assays can be broadly classified into tumor-naïve and tumor-informed approaches. Tumor-naïve assays typically rely on predefined gene panels and may be affected by factors such as assay sensitivity, clonal hematopoiesis, and inter-patient genomic heterogeneity. In contrast, personalized, tumor-informed assays are customized for each patient based on somatic variants identified from tumor tissue, allowing longitudinal tracking of patient-specific variants in plasma. Prior studies have reported that tumor-informed approaches may improve sensitivity and specificity for ctDNA detection, particularly in setting of low disease burden or early molecular response [[12], [13], [14]]. Accordingly, tumor-informed ctDNA monitoring may be well suited for longitudinal assessment of treatment response, although the current study was not designed to directly compare assay platforms [15,16].

While most available data supporting ctDNA dynamics as a biomarker of immunotherapy response originate from North American and European cohorts, prospective data from Latin America remain scarce. Furthermore, real-world studies evaluating specifically tumor-informed ctDNA monitoring in patients with NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab, either as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, are limited.

In this prospective study, we evaluated the clinical utility of early ctDNA dynamics assessed using a personalized, tumor-informed assay in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy. Specifically, in this study, representing the first prospective Latin American cohort, we aimed to (1) determine whether early reduction in ctDNA levels predicts radiological response and progression-free survival, and (2) assess the concordance between molecular and radiological response assessment in routine clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Study population

This prospective, single-center, longitudinal observational study was conducted at the Alexander Fleming Cancer Institute (Buenos Aires, Argentina) between December 2021 and December 2024. Eligible patients were aged ≥18 years and had histologically confirmed advanced (stage IV) NSCLC and received first-line treatment with pembrolizumab either as monotherapy or in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy, according to standard clinical practice and treating physician discretion. Key inclusion criteria comprised: absence of actionable oncogenic driver alterations, including EGFR, ALK, ROS1, and BRAF for non-squamous histology, as assessed by tissue-based molecular testing using next-generation sequencing (NGS), polymerase chain reaction, and/or immunohistochemistry, according to local standards; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2; availability of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue suitable for tumor-informed ctDNA assay design; and no prior systemic anticancer therapy in the advanced or metastatic setting. Plasma sample collection and preparation

Peripheral blood samples were collected at baseline (prior to initiation of pembrolizumab-based therapy) and serially during treatment, with planned collection every six weeks coinciding with routine clinical visits. Blood samples were drawn into cell-free DNA (cfDNA) stabilizing tubes to prevent leukocyte lysis and genomic DNA contamination.

Plasma was isolated using a standardized double-centrifugation protocol. Briefly, whole blood was centrifuged at a low speed to separate plasma, followed by a second high-speed centrifugation to remove residual cellular debris. Plasma aliquots were stored at −80 °C until ctDNA analysis. All pre-analytical sample handling, processing, and storage procedures were performed according to validated protocols to ensure cfDNA integrity and consistency across longitudinal time points.

Tumor-informed circulating tumor DNA analysis

ctDNA testing was performed using a clinically-validated, personalized, tumor-informed 16-plex polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based NGS assay (Signatera™, Natera, Inc., Austin, TX, USA). As described previously, FFPE tumor tissue obtained from biopsy samples and matched normal DNA extracted from peripheral blood samples were processed for whole exome sequencing (WES) [14]. Based on WES data, a set of up to 16 patient-specific, high-confidence somatic single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) were selected for each patient and incorporated into a multiplex PCR-based NGS assay to enable longitudinal ctDNA tracking in plasma samples. Cell-free DNA was extracted from patient plasma at each time point and analyzed for the presence of tumor-specific variants. Samples with ≥2 SNVs detected above a predefined confidence threshold were defined as ctDNA-positive. ctDNA levels were quantified and reported as mean tumor molecules per mL (MTM/mL).

Clinical outcomes

Radiological tumor assessments were performed at baseline (before treatment initiation), and then every 8–12 weeks or when clinically indicated, using contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) scans, in accordance with standard clinical practice. Radiologic response was evaluated according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 of targeted lesions by a dedicated thoracic radiologist who was blinded to ctDNA results [5]. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response or partial response as their best overall response. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from initiation of pembrolizumab-based therapy to radiological disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients without documented progression were censored at the date of last radiologic assessment.

Statistical analysis

Baseline demographic and clinicopathologic characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables were reported as medians with ranges, and categorical variables as absolute frequencies and percentages. Early ctDNA dynamics were evaluated by comparing ctDNA levels at the first on-treatment time point (approximately 6 weeks from treatment start) with baseline levels. A decrease in ctDNA levels, including complete clearance to undetectable levels, was considered indicative of a molecular response to treatment, whereas stable or increasing ctDNA levels were considered suggestive of a lack of molecular response. Comparisons between groups defined by early ctDNA clearance or decrease versus increase were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and Fisher's exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate given the sample size. Comparisons of ctDNA changes across radiologic response categories (partial response, stable disease, and progressive disease) were performed using the non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis test, given the small sample size, non-normal distribution of ctDNA values, and unequal group sizes. No post hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted due to the exploratory nature of the analysis and the limited number of patients in some response categories. Correlation between early ctDNA change and radiographic tumor burden variation was evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 23.0 (SPSS, Inc, Chicago, IL) and R Software (survival package) version 3.2-13.

Results

Patient characteristics

Between December 2021 and December 2024, a total of 15 patients with stage IV NSCLC (13 [86.7%] adenocarcinoma, 2 [13.3%] squamous cell) were prospectively enrolled. Pre-treatment baseline ctDNA positivity was observed in 13 patients (87%), while two patients (13%) were ctDNA-negative at baseline. Among patients with baseline ctDNA positivity, four (31%) received pembrolizumab monotherapy and nine (69%) received pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy (Fig. 2). The median age of the cohort was 68.5 years (range 58–77), and 62% (8/13) of patients were male (Table 1). Adenocarcinoma was the predominant histology (85%, 11/13), all patients were current or former smokers, and ECOG performance status was 0–1 in the majority of patients (92%, 12/13). Early ctDNA dynamics precede radiographic response and correlate with clinical outcomes

Among the 13 patients who were ctDNA-positive at baseline, the median ctDNA levels were 13.28 MTM/mL (range: 0.35–481.2). Among patients with ctDNA positivity, 11 (85%) experienced a molecular response characterized by ctDNA decrease (n = 1) or clearance (n = 10), while 2 (15%) demonstrated an increase at the first time point (median 46 days). Early molecular response was significantly associated with radiographic response assessed by RECIST v1.1 (Fig. 3). Patients with early molecular response achieved an ORR of 91% (10/11), whereas no objective responses were observed among patients with an increase in ctDNA (0/2; p = 0.038; Supplementary Fig. 1). Moreover, a significant correlation was observed between the magnitude of ctDNA change at 6 weeks and percentage change in target lesion size by RECIST (Spearman R = 0.81, p < 0.001; Fig. 4A), indicating that molecular response closely mirrored radiographic tumor burden changes. Discussion

In this prospective longitudinal study, we demonstrate that early ctDNA decrease or clearance strongly predicts radiographic response and longer PFS in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy, highlighting the potential role of ctDNA as an early pharmacodynamic biomarker of treatment benefit or resistance.

Our findings are highly consistent with, and extend upon, prior evidence supporting ctDNA dynamics as an early predictive biomarker of immunotherapy efficacy in advanced NSCLC. Recently, an aggregate analysis from the Friends of Cancer Research ctMoniTR initiative demonstrated that early ctDNA reductions across multiple on-treatment timepoints were consistently associated with improved overall survival in advanced NSCLC treated with anti–PD-(L)1 therapy and/or chemotherapy, supporting the clinical relevance of ctDNA kinetics as an early biomarker of treatment benefit [9]. In a study by Goldberg et al., early changes in plasma ctDNA in patients receiving immunotherapy with an anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 drug, alone or in combination with other immunotherapeutic agents, were shown to correlate with response and survival, frequently anticipating radiographic changes by several weeks in 28 patients with detectable ctDNA at baseline [10]. However, this study included patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors across multiple lines of therapy in advanced NSCLC, introducing greater biological heterogeneity. Patients treated in later lines may harbor more heavily pretreated and potentially resistant tumors, leading to distinct ctDNA kinetics influenced by prior tumor burden, treatment history, and clonal evolution. These factors may affect the magnitude of ctDNA decrease, the timing of molecular response, and the strength of the association between ctDNA dynamics and clinical outcomes. In addition, this study employed a tumor-naïve sequencing approach based on a predefined panel covering recurrent regions across 24 genes, and ctDNA dynamics were assessed using changes in allele fraction rather than patient-specific variants. This strategy may implicate limited sensitivity for detecting low-volume disease, capturing early molecular responses, and achieving sustained detection of minimal residual disease. In contrast, our study uses a personalized, tumor-informed ctDNA assay (Signatera), in which patient-specific variants are tracked enabling higher analytical sensitivity and improved performance for early and longitudinal molecular monitoring.

Similarly, Ricciuti et al. reported that early decreases in ctDNA variant allele fraction after initiation of first-line pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy were associated with higher objective response rates and significantly prolonged progression-free and overall survival in 45 patients with advanced NSCLC [11]. While their study represents a landmark contribution to the field, it relied on an amplicon-based, tumor-naïve plasma next-generation sequencing platform and evaluated ctDNA changes at an earlier median time point (median 21 days). Our results complement and extend these findings by demonstrating that ctDNA dynamics assessed at six weeks using a tumor-informed approach remain strongly predictive of clinical outcomes, even in a real-world Latin American cohort with a limited sample size.

More recently, a phase 2 adaptive trial reported by Anagnostou et al. provided compelling mechanistic and clinical evidence that ctDNA clearance or substantial reduction during first-line pembrolizumab reflects an early molecular response associated with durable clinical benefit in 35 patients with advanced NSCLC [17]. Importantly, prior studies evaluating ctDNA dynamics during immune checkpoint inhibition have predominantly relied on tumor-naïve sequencing approaches. Goldberg et al. quantified ctDNA using a fixed, mutation-prone gene panel and monitored changes in mutant allele fraction across patients. Similarly, Ricciuti et al. employed an amplicon-based, tumor-agnostic plasma NGS assay targeting recurrent hotspots, while Anagnostou et al. assessed molecular response by measuring clearance of plasma-detected variants using a tumor-naïve panel [11]. Although these studies consistently demonstrated the prognostic value of early ctDNA changes, tumor-naïve assays may have limited sensitivity for low-volume disease and early molecular response, and their applicability to contemporary first-line treatment paradigms may therefore be constrained. A key feature of our study is the use of a personalized tumor-informed ctDNA assay. Prior studies have reported that tumor-informed approaches may improve specificity and sensitivity compared with tumor-naïve approaches, particularly in settings of low tumor burden or early molecular response. [[13], [14], [15]]. This methodological advantage is especially relevant in the immunotherapy setting, where radiographic assessment may be confounded by delayed responses or atypical patterns such as pseudoprogression. In our cohort, ctDNA dynamics provided clear stratification of responders and non-responders, including among patients with radiographically stable disease, underscoring the added value of molecular monitoring beyond imaging alone.

Recent analyses of large clinical trials further support the use of early ctDNA kinetics as a clinically meaningful on-treatment biomarker in advanced NSCLC treated with immunotherapy-based regimens. Retrospective evaluations of randomized studies such as IMpower150 and IMpower131 employed different ctDNA monitoring strategies, including a tumor-agnostic plasma sequencing approach and the FoundationOne Tracker tissue-informed personalized assay, respectively [12,18]. In both studies, early ctDNA reduction or clearance during induction therapy (approximately weeks 6–9) was strongly associated with improved objective response rates and survival outcomes, providing prognostic information complementary to conventional radiologic assessment.

In addition, a recent meta-analysis integrating data from randomized clinical trials and prospective longitudinal cohort studies evaluated plasma ctDNA kinetics as a predictor of systemic therapy response in advanced NSCLC using both tumor-agnostic and tumor-informed ctDNA approaches [19]. Among patients receiving immunotherapy-based regimens in first- and later-line settings, ctDNA clearance was associated with a marked improvement in progression-free and overall survival, with hazard ratios of 0.33 and 0.32, respectively. These observations are highly concordant with our prospective real-world findings, in which early ctDNA reduction measured using a personalized tumor-informed assay (Signatera™, Natera, Inc.) reliably identified patients deriving sustained clinical benefit, particularly in the first-line setting.

An important contribution of this study is its regional context. To our knowledge, this represents the first prospective report of longitudinal personalized tumor-informed ctDNA monitoring in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with immunotherapy in Latin America. Generating evidence in the local population is critical to support the adoption of advanced molecular monitoring strategies in regions where access, cost, and infrastructure may differ from those in North America or Europe.

Several limitations of our work should be acknowledged. First, the limited sample size reduced the statistical power and precluded extensive multivariable analyses. Second, the study was conducted at a single center, potentially limiting generalizability. Third, treatment heterogeneity should be considered, as patients received either pembrolizumab monotherapy or pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy, which may influence ctDNA kinetics, tumor responses, and clinical outcomes. Fourth, selection bias cannot be excluded, as patients without detectable baseline ctDNA were excluded from the primary molecular analyses; baseline ctDNA detectability has been associated with tumor burden and may correlate with a poorer prognosis. Fifth, given the real-world context of this study, long-term ctDNA monitoring was not feasible for all patients, limiting the study's ability to comprehensively evaluate ctDNA dynamics at the time of disease progression. Finally, the small number of patients who experienced disease progression (n = 4) and the lack of ctDNA testing at the time of progression due to clinical conditions, limited the robustness of PFS analyses stratified by ctDNA dynamics.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study provides prospective evidence that early molecular response assessed by tumor-informed ctDNA is a robust predictor of clinical outcomes, including radiographic response and PFS in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy. To the best of our knowledge, this work represents the first report in Latin America. All our findings, together with prior data, further support the role of ctDNA dynamics as a clinically meaningful biomarker of immunotherapy efficacy and lay the groundwork for future ctDNA-guided treatment strategies. Early tumor-informed ctDNA reduction may complement radiological assessment and support more personalized, timely, and biologically informed treatment decisions in advanced NSCLC.

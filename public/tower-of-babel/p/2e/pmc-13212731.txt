Proximity structures on hesitant fuzzy sets and Its application
Authors: Pankaj, Rashmi Singh, Repalle V N Srinivas Rao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present article discusses the extension of classical proximity theory to multi valued systems. Existing distance measures for hesitant fuzzy sets operate primarily at the computational level without establishing the rigorous mathematical foundations. This paper develops a comprehensive axiomatic framework for proximity spaces on hesitant fuzzy sets. First, five fundamental axioms (HFP0–HFP5) for hesitant fuzzy proximity relations are introduced, generalizing classical proximity theory to fit multi-valued membership structures. Second, it is proven that hesitant fuzzy proximity spaces induce completely regular topologies, and the Smirnov compactification theorem is established for these spaces. Third, a bijective correspondence between hesitant fuzzy proximities and totally bounded uniformities is demonstrated, along with a metrization theorem characterizing when compatible metrics exist. Fourth, a new concept of variable length proximity measure is proposed that naturally handles hesitant fuzzy elements of different cardinalities without requiring artificial data extension or reduction. Finally, the practical applicability of the framework is validated through a facial recognition case study, where the proposed discrete hesitant fuzzy proximity successfully identifies images of the same individual under varying lighting and rotation conditions while correctly discriminating different subjects. This work bridges classical proximity theory and modern uncertainty modeling, giving both theoretical foundations and applicability for decision-making under hesitancy.Keywords: Proximity spaces, Hesitant fuzzy sets, Axiomatic structure, Pattern recognition, Topology, Distance measuresSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The proximity space theory, first characterized by Riesz1 in 1908 which was later formalized by Efremovič2,3 in the 1950s. This theory provides a powerful framework for studying nearness relations that are more general than metric spaces yet more structured than topological spaces. Classical proximity spaces have found applications in various mathematical domains, including uniform spaces, compactifications, and extension problems4. The complexity of modern decision-making (DM) scenarios, including hesitation and multiple possible values, requires an extension of traditional proximity theory to account for uncertain membership structures.5.

Torra and Narukawa6,7 proposed hesitant fuzzy sets (HFSs) that enhance uncertainty modelling allowing membership degrees to be expressed as sets of possible values rather than single values. This formulation naturally captures the hesitancy that decision-makers experience when evaluating alternatives under uncertainty. While many distance and similarity measures have been proposed for HFSs8–10, these approaches typically focus on numerical computations without establishing a comprehensive topological framework.

Farhadinia11,12 majorly contributed to the theoretical development of HFSs by proposing information measures that may quantify uncertainty in both hesitant and interval valued hesitant fuzzy settings. In subsequent work, distance and similarity measurements were extended to higher order HFSs, giving improved tools for comparison analysis under complicated hesitation structures. Li et al.13,14 investigated the theoretical features of distance measures for HFSs, explaining their structural properties and corrections to some contradictions in previous formulations. Zwick et al.15 conducted early research on similarity evaluation in fuzzy environments, including a comparative analysis of similarity metrics among fuzzy ideas. Their research extensively analysed the structural and behavioural aspects of several similarity formulations, showing both their benefits and inherent limitations. Further advancements by Tang16 in hesitant fuzzy theory include the development of novel distance and similarity measures designed to enhance discrimination capability in multi DM contexts . HFSs also have been applied to the other fields such as cluster analysis17,18.

Literature review

Distance and similarity measures in hesitant fuzzy environments

A significant portion of the literature on HFSs has focused on developing distance and similarity measures to aid DM and pattern analysis. Early research used quantitative frameworks to analyse the similarity of hesitant fuzzy objects, such as dual hesitant structures and cardinality based formulations considering variations in set size and distribution19–21. Subsequent contributions extended these ideas by offering new distance and similarity measures to improve discrimination capability in classification and multi-criteria contexts22,23. Further extensions have addressed higher order hesitant environments, such as type-2 HFSs, where generalised distance measures were developed to deal with additional layers of uncertainty24. These metric based frameworks have applications in clustering and pattern recognition, demonstrating their practical utility. Despite their effectiveness, such approaches are inherently numerical and rely on explicit distance measurement, which may fail to capture the relational structure of nearness inherent in multi-valued hesitant memberships.

Aggregation operators and DM frameworks

Hesitant m-polar fuzzy TOPSIS approaches and hesitant fuzzy soft ELECTRE-II models have been developed to improve group DM reliability by structured aggregation of multi-valued membership data25,26. Similarly, generalised trapezoidal hesitant fuzzy numbers and their operational laws have been proposed as a means of increasing modeling flexibility in uncertain decision contexts27.

Recent research has extennded hesitant fuzzy modeling to intuitionistic, neutrosophic, and q-rung orthopair frameworks. These include entropy based VIKOR approaches28, generalised similarity measures for intuitionistic trapezoidal fuzzy multi-numbers29,30, N-valued neutrosophic trapezoidal numbers with related similarity constructs31. Furthermore, vector similarity measures for picture type-2 hesitant fuzzy sets, bipolar-valued complex hesitant fuzzy aggregation operators, q-rung orthopair Aczél-Alsina operators and generalised dice measures have contributed to the DM literature32–35. Correlation coefficients and clustering based TOPSIS modifications for spherical hesitant fuzzy sets have also been proposed to enhance comparative analysis under uncertainty36. Hesitant fuzzy soft set was combined with rough set to propose a new framework hesitant fuzzy soft rough set with dynamic beta covering that changes approximation boundaries in relation to hesitant membership levels37.

Clustering and relational structures in hesitant fuzzy systems

Clustering approaches for hesitant fuzzy data have also attracted attention. Several distance and similarity based clustering approaches have been introduced to organise hesitant fuzzy information in multi-attribute and group DM scenarios19–21,34. These methods demonstrate the importance of quantitative similarity evaluation in structuring uncertain datasets.

Metric and proximity based generalizations in advanced fuzzy frameworks

Recent studies in fuzzy mathematical theory have emphasized the rigorous formulation of metric and proximity structures within extended uncertainty frameworks. Farhadinia et al.38 proposed layered set based similarity metrics for probabilistic HFSs, which included structured aggregation techniques that maintain consistency across many levels of uncertainty. Josen et al.39 established unified formulations of distance, similarity, and entropy measures for multidimensional fuzzy sets, ensuring compatibility with generalised metric axioms in high dimensional fuzzy spaces. Singh and Singh40 developed a dual proximity measure in intuitionistic fuzzy settings that formalises relational closeness via complementing membership and non-membership interactions.

Discussion and conclusion

In this paper, an axiomatic characterisation of proximity spaces on hesitant fuzzy sets has been given, which forms a strict formal basis of the nearness relations in multi-valued uncertain settings. The framework is an extension of classical proximity theory based on five axioms (HFP0- HFP5) that maintain the key properties of classical proximity and support the natural multi-valued nature of membership of hesitant fuzzy elements. The main theoretical provisions of this work can be summarized as follows:

(i)Five hesitant fuzzy axioms of proximity relations, specifically including the modified intersection property (HFP4), according to which the intersection is not only non-empty, but strongly supported. This change discusses the inherent distinction between classical sets and hesitant fuzzy sets, in which the small membership degrees are not supposed to be the implication of the proximity.

Limitations

Regardless of the above contributions, the proposed framework has some limitations, which must be mentioned:

(i)Although the complexity per element pair, is manageable at moderate cardinalities in hesitant fuzzy elements, it can be computationally burdensome in applications with large cardinalities (h > 100) especially when huge volumes of data are to be processed.

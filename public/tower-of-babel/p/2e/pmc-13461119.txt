Alcohol Interaction and ALDH2: Missing Links in EMPHASIS
Authors: Koichiro Yuji, Wakako Yuji
Published: 2026

Lu and colleagues [1] report a compelling functional benefit of minocycline in acute ischaemic stroke. Among the prespecified subgroups, treatment-effect modification favoured patients with a history of alcohol consumption (Pinteraction = 0.025). We wish to emphasise that in the EMPHASIS statistical analysis plan, alcohol use was one of the prespecified subgroups for assessing heterogeneity of the primary outcome, rather than a post hoc exploration [1]. This distinction matters. Whereas the accompanying editorial reasonably dismissed the apparent benefit in patients with undetermined stroke aetiology as biologically implausible and most likely a chance finding, the alcohol consumption signal – although equally hypothesis generating, given the number of subgroups examined – is underpinned by a coherent and testable biological rationale rooted in the trial’s East Asian demographic. We outline that rationale and propose feasible verification strategies.

We propose that the relevant biology converges on the NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome rather than on acetaldehyde toxicity alone. Mitochondrial ALDH2 detoxifies not only acetaldehyde but also the reactive carbonyl species generated by ischaemic lipid peroxidation, notably 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE) and malondialdehyde [2]. 4-HNE adducts accumulate in the ischaemic cortex within hours of onset; ALDH2 deficiency in stroke-prone animals worsens infarction, whereas pharmacological ALDH2 activation clears 4-HNE and is neuroprotective [3]. Recent work indicates that this protection operates specifically by suppressing the reactive oxygen species/NLRP3 inflammasome axis, with ALDH2 activation reducing interleukin-1β and interleukin-18 release, preserving tight-junction proteins, and limiting blood-brain barrier breakdown after experimental ischaemia reperfusion [4]. Consistent with a primed inflammatory state, ALDH2-deficient individuals mount an exaggerated systemic inflammatory response to acute ischaemia, with higher post-event high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) concentrations [2].

Chronic alcohol exposure adds a second hit to this primed state. In a translational model, alcohol induced a neurovascular inflammatory priming – an expansion of brain perivascular macrophages and pre-activation of microglia – that exaggerated the inflammatory response to a subsequent ischaemic insult and enlarged infarcts; depletion of perivascular macrophages abolished this aggravation [5]. The EMPHASIS trial was conducted in China, where ALDH2*2 (rs671) is carried by 35–45% of the population [2]; in ALDH2*2 carriers, an equivalent alcohol intake yields a greater acetaldehyde and reactive-aldehyde burden, plausibly amplifying NLRP3 priming further. Critically, the target that minocycline engages maps onto this same node: in experimental ischaemic stroke, minocycline suppresses microglial activation and inhibits both the priming and the activation signals of the NLRP3 inflammasome, reducing infarct volume and oedema [6], and it specifically mitigates ischaemic injury in ethanol-exposed animals [7]. The alcohol-consuming, ALDH2-deficient patient therefore represents the substrate in which the drug’s anti-inflammatory target is maximally engaged – a mechanistic account of the observed interaction that is independent of the EMPHASIS dataset.

The expected genotype distribution of any enhanced effect deserves emphasis. ALDH2*2 homozygotes retain essentially undetectable enzyme activity and experience such severe intolerance that they almost entirely abstain; in a recent Taiwanese ischaemic-stroke cohort, only approximately 8% of homozygous men reported heavy drinking, and heavy-drinking homozygotes were so rare that homozygotes and heterozygotes had to be pooled for analysis [8]. Heterozygotes, by contrast, retain roughly 17–40% of wild-type activity, permitting continued drinking while still accumulating acetaldehyde and reactive aldehydes [2, 8]. The alcohol-exposed, enzyme-deficient population is therefore composed predominantly of ALDH2*1/*2 heterozygotes, in whom heavy drinking is associated with markedly earlier stroke onset [8]. We would thus predict any enhanced minocycline efficacy to concentrate in alcohol-consuming heterozygotes – and, to a lesser and mechanistically distinct degree, in ALDH2*1/*1 heavy drinkers, in whom priming is acetaldehyde independent [5] – whereas homozygotes are largely self-excluded from the relevant exposure. This consideration sharpens the target population for any confirmatory study.

Several verification strategies follow, in descending order of robustness. First, if DNA was banked, retrospective rs671 genotyping of the alcohol subgroup would directly test whether the interaction tracks with ALDH2*2 carriage. Second – and requiring no new sampling – the trial already measured hs-CRP at baseline and at day 6; because ALDH2 deficiency heightens post-ischaemic inflammation [2], examining whether minocycline produced a disproportionate hs-CRP reduction within the alcohol subgroup would provide an internal, data-available test of the proposed mechanism. We acknowledge that the self-reported “flushing” phenotype, which we previously suggested as a surrogate, is an imperfect grouping variable in acute stroke: it depends on recall, may be unobtainable in patients with aphasia or impaired consciousness, and, importantly, attenuates with a long or heavy drinking history through acquired tolerance to acetaldehyde, thereby systematically misclassifying the very long-term drinkers of greatest interest [9]. For prospective trials, we therefore favour objective rs671 genotyping; where genotyping is infeasible, a validated two-item flushing questionnaire (approximately 90% sensitivity and specificity for ALDH2 deficiency) is preferable to ad hoc recall, ideally combined with quantified alcohol intake [9].

Taken together, these independent lines of evidence transform an isolated subgroup signal into a falsifiable, genotype-anchored hypothesis. Prospectively stratifying neuroprotection trials by ALDH2 genotype and alcohol exposure would help move post-stroke anti-inflammatory therapy from a one-size-fits-all approach toward a precision medicine paradigm calibrated to East Asian genomic backgrounds.

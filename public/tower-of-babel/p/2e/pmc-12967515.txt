Application of untargeted plasma metabolomics and machine learning to construct a diagnostic model for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: a case-control study
Authors: Ruoxuan Li, Bo Wang, Bo Shan, Mao Sun, Shengjun Ta, Jia Zhao, Nan Kang, Fan Yang, Yiming Yuan, Xin’an Li, Huiyi Wang, Liufeiyan Fan, Hongjing Zheng, Jing Wang, Liwen Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is the most common inherited cardiovascular disease. Recent metabolomics studies have revealed pathogenic mechanisms and provided new perspectives for diagnosis.AimThis study aimed to analyze plasma metabolic alterations and construct a preliminary diagnostic model for HCM based on untargeted metabolomics and machine learning (ML) algorithms, in order to explore potential pathogenic pathways and improve diagnostic accuracy during screening.MethodsA total of 76 HCM patients and 35 normal participants were consecutively recruited from August, 2023 to December, 2023. Data were split into discovery and validation sets at a ratio of 7:3 and the feature combinations were selected using support vector machine (SVM) and random forest (RF). Stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis was performed to identify key metabolites associated with left ventricular wall thickness. Metabolic pathway analysis was performed using KEGG.ResultsTotally 1481 metabolites were identified with 640 differential metabolites and 240 significant differential metabolites. Multivariate statistical analysis showed that metabolism results could effectively differentiate the two cohorts (OPLS-DA positive ion mode R2Y = 0.744, Q2 = 0.456; negative ion mode R2Y = 0.611, Q2 = 0.441). SVM and RF screened the same combination of features including 7-keto-8-aminopelargonic acid (KAPA), γ-linolenoyl ethanolamid, nitrilotriacetic acid, D-quinovose and N-acetyl-l-aspartic acid (NAA), which could effectively and accurately differentiate HCM patients from normal participants (in discovery and validation sets, the SVM model AUROC was 0.996 and 0.985 with accuracies of 96.1% and 97.1%, respectively; the RF model AUROC was 1.000 with accuracies of 94.8% and 100.0%, respectively). In metabolic pathway analysis, central carbon metabolism in cancer and protein digestion and absorption were significantly upregulated in HCM patients, which were connected by alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism. Stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis revealed that NAA was correlated with left ventricular mass index and RV5+SV1 (P < 0.05), which may be the central target of the connecting pathway.ConclusionPlasma metabolite diagnostic model including KAPA, γ-linolenoyl ethanolamid, nitrilotriacetic acid, D-quinovose and NAA can effectively and accurately screen HCM patients. Metabolomics combined with ML algorithm showed that alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism may be the pathogenic pathway leading to the occurrence of HCM with NAA as the central target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11306-026-02412-w.

Introduction

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is the most common inherited cardiovascular disease, with an estimated prevalence ranging from 1 in 500 to 1 in 200, and is a major cause of sudden cardiac death in adolescents and athletes (Authors/Task Force m et al. 2014, Ho et al. 2018, Semsarian et al. 2015, Maron et al. 2022, Geske et al. 2018). Echocardiography remains the preferred method for diagnosing and screening HCM, but it has several limitations. In some patients, poor acoustic windows and dependence on operator experience and equipment quality may result in missed diagnoses, particularly for certain HCM subtypes (Maron et al. 2022, Writing Committee et al. 2024). Although genetic analysis and cardiac magnetic resonance can mitigate the limitations of echocardiography and improve diagnostic yield (Writing Committee et al. 2024), their cost and availability limit widespread use (Maron et al. 2022, Rowin et al. 2020, Bai et al. 2022, Marian and Braunwald 2017). There is a need for an objective, scalable diagnostic approach suitable for routine HCM screening.

In recent years, metabolomics, as an emerging discipline developed after genomics and proteomics, can not only be applied to the exploration of pathogenesis, but also provide new perspectives for disease diagnosis, such as the successful establishment of diagnosis models of gastric cancer and early adult cardiovascular risk factors (Chen et al. 2024, Murthy et al. 2020, Adam et al. 2021, Previs et al. 2022, Ranjbarvaziri et al. 2021, Cao et al. 2021). But most metabolomics studies of HCM have focused on depicting significant metabolic perturbations which neglected the role in the diagnosis of HCM (Previs et al. 2022, Ranjbarvaziri et al. 2021). Therefore, given the successful use of metabolomics in identifying diagnostic biomarkers for various cardiovascular and metabolic diseases(Shen et al. 2024), applying metabolomic profiling to HCM may provide a new avenue to improve its early detection and diagnostic accuracy.

This study aimed to develop a preliminary plasma metabolite–based diagnostic model for HCM using untargeted metabolomics and machine-learning algorithms, to improve screening accuracy in the general population and address limitations of existing methods.

Materials and methods

Study cohort

A total of 111 participants were consecutively recruited for the present study. Seventy-six patients with HCM who attended the outpatient clinic of the Department of Ultrasound Medicine, First Affiliated Hospital of Air Force Medical University (Xijing Hospital), between August and December 2023 were included. Inclusion criteria were: (1) adult participants over 18 years of age; (2) patients with a definitive diagnosis of HCM based on the current guideline (Ommen et al. 2020). Exclusion criteria included: (1) severe hepatic and renal dysfunction; (2) uncontrollable metabolic diseases; (3) uncontrollable hypertension, atrial fibrillation or heart failure; (4) HCM patients who had received surgical or interventional treatment for the disease prior to enrolment. Additionally, 35 healthy controls without cardiovascular or metabolic disease were recruited through voluntary enrollment.

Collection of clinical and echocardiographic data

All participants in this study underwent demographic information collection, medical history taking, comprehensive echocardiography (Vivid E95, GE. HealthCare), 12-lead electrocardiogram (NIHON KOHDEN CORP. ECG-1550P), NT-proBNP testing and untargeted plasma metabolomics testing.

Plasma sample preparation and pretreatment

All participants fasted and stopped medication for more than 10 h during the night and provided a 5 ml fasting blood sample next morning. Blood samples were collected in 5mL Vacutainer tubes containing the chelating agent ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid. Samples were stored at room temperature for ≤ 1 h or at 4 °C for ≤ 2 h before processing. The supernatant was extracted after centrifugation (3000 rpm, 4 °C, 15 min). All hemolyzed samples were re-collected. Plasma samples were stored at −80 °C for no more than 3 months prior to LC-MS/MS analysis. The samples were thawed at 4 °C and 100µL aliquots were mixed with 400µL of cold methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v) to remove the protein. The mixture was centrifuged for 20 min (14000 g, 4 °C). The supernatant was dried in a vacuum centrifuge. For LC-MS analysis, the samples were re-dissolved in 100µL acetonitrile/water (1:1, v/v) solvent.

To ensure the repeatability and stability of the metabolomics, quality control (QC) samples were pooled by mixing all extracted plasma samples. The pretreatment of QC samples was consistent with the study samples.

Untargeted metabolomics profiling

Analyses were performed using an UHPLC (1290 Infinity LC, Agilent Technologies) coupled to a quadrupole time-of-flight (AB Sciex TripleTOF 6600) in Shanghai Applied Protein Technology Co. Ltd. The detailed experimental procedure can be found in the Supplementary Materials.

Statistical analysis

Study cohort clinical characteristics analysis

Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD and compared using student’s t-test. Continuous variables that do not fit normally distribution were represented by median (Q1, Q3) and analyzed using Mann-Whitney U-test. Categorical variables were represented as counts (percentages) and analyzed using χ2 or Fisher’s exact test. SPSS 27.0 software was used for statistical analysis. In this study, P < 0.05 was statistically significant.

Untargeted metabolomic analysis

XCMS was used to perform peak extraction, peak matching and retention time correction of the raw data. Univariate statistical analysis methods used in this study included Fold Change Analysis (FC Analysis) and T-test/non-parametric test. FC > 1.5 or FC < 0.67 and P < 0.05 were used to screen all the differential metabolites tested in positive and negative ion modes. Multivariate data analysis using SIMCA-p software was performed on the processed data, including Pareto-scaled principal component analysis (PCA), partial least squares discrimination analysis (PLS-DA) and orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). The 7-fold cross-validation and response permutation testing were performed to evaluate the robustness of the model. The variable importance (VIP) value > 0.5 and P < 0.05 were used to screen significant differential metabolites. Based on these metabolites, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) (http://geneontology.org/) was used for pathogenic pathway analysis.

ML data analysis

Data were split into discovery set and validation set with the ratio of 7:3. In the discovery set, feature engineering was performed. In detail, parameters with linear combinations and high linear dependence (defined as the Pearson |r| > 0.9) were excluded before ML. Feature selection was performed with LASSO analysis within significant differential metabolites. Subsequently, cross-validation method and classification algorithms including support vector machine (SVM) and random forest (RF) algorithm were used to perform recursive feature elimination (RFE). Based on this selected features, SVM and RF classification models were constructed, and hyperparameters were optimized using grid search with 5-fold cross-validation. The performance of determined models was evaluated in the validation set. Further, stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis was performed to identify the potential key marker compounds related to the clinical indicators of the left ventricular wall thickness including left ventricular mass index (LVMI), RV5+SV1 and left atrial volume index (LAVI).

Results

Study cohort clinical and echocardiographic characteristics

In discovery set, 52 HCM patients and 25 normal participants were included. The average age of HCM patients was 49.85 with 29 males (55.8%) and the average age of normal participants was 47.44 with 11 males (44.0%). In validation set, 24 HCM patients and 10 normal participants were included. The mean age of HCM patients was 48.38 with 13 males (54.2%) and the mean age of the normal participants was 52.00 with 6 males (60.0%). Compared with normal participants, HCM patients had greater maximum left ventricular wall thickness (MLVWT) (P = 0.001 in both discovery set and validation set), higher left ventricular outflow tract pressure (LVOT-PG) (P = 0.001 in discovery set, P = 0.012 in validation set) and more severely impaired diastolic function. In discovery set, compared with the control cohort, HCM patients had more unexplained syncope (P = 0.006), more hypertension (P = 0.019) and greater BMI (P = 0.001) while lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (P = 0.015), metabolic equivalence (METs) (P = 0.004) and peak heart rate (Peak HR) (P = 0.004), which did not appear in validation set. Plasma metabolomics can distinguish HCM patients from normal participants

A total of 1481 metabolites were identified from plasma samples. Among these metabolites, lipids and lipid-like molecules accounted for the largest proportion at 31.42% followed by organic acids and derivatives at 19.08% (Fig. S1).

There was a significant difference in metabolite expression between the two cohorts

The experiment showed good stability and reproducibility (Figs. S2, S3). Univariate statistical analysis showed that there were 640 differential metabolites and compared with the control cohort, the plasma metabolites of HCM cohort were significantly up-regulated in both positive and negative ion modes (Fig. 2A, B), indicating that plasma metabolites were significantly altered in HCM patients (Fig. 2C, D).

Both PLS-DA and OPLS-DA models can effectively distinguish HCM patients from normal participants

The metabolite distributions of the two cohorts were analyzed by PLS-DA and OPLS-DA models. Both R2Y and Q2 reached satisfactory values (PLS-DA model positive ion mode R2Y = 0.659, Q2 = 0.425, negative ion mode R2Y = 0.611, Q2 = 0.393; OPLS-DA model positive ion mode R2Y = 0.744, Q2 = 0.456, negative ion mode R2Y = 0.611, Q2 = 0.441) (Figs. S2A, B; 2E–H). The results of 200 permutations proved that the model was reliable and there was no overfitting (PLS-DA model positive ion mode R2 = 0.3501, Q2=−0.3524, negative ion mode R2 = 0.3189, Q2=−0.4141; OPLS-DA model positive ion mode R2 = 0.5084 Q2=−0.4511, negative ion mode R2 = 0.3231, Q2=−0.3556) (Fig. 2I–L).

Plasma metabolomics analysis revealed that HCM has a unique metabolic pathway

A total of 240 significant differential metabolites were identified from plasma samples. KEGG was used to analyze the bioinformatics of these metabolites. We found two pathways changed significantly between the HCM cohort and the control cohort, including central carbon metabolism in cancer and protein digestion and absorption (Fig. 3A, D, E). There was an upward trend in HCM patients with the above two pathways, with central carbon metabolism in cancer as the focus. Meanwhile, we analyzed pathway hierarchy. We found that the metabolism disturbance in HCM was the most significant, particularly perturbations in amino acid metabolism (Fig. 3B, C).

Plasma metabolites can reveal the presence of HCM

Among the 240 significant differential metabolites and three demographic indicators (gender, age, and BMI), 149 features showing collinearity were removed, leaving 94 predictive variables.

Applying two different ML algorithms screened the same set of features

With the methods of LASSO, ten features were selected as the candidates of RFE (Fig. 4A, B). Based on the dataset of ten features, two different ML algorithms including SVM (Fig. 4C) and RF algorithm (Fig. 4D) were used to obtain the same RFE results as the feature combinations of 7-keto-8-aminopelargonic acid (KAPA), γ-linolenoyl ethanolamid, nitrilotriacetic acid, D-quinovose and N-acetyl-l-aspartic acid (NAA).

Two different ML models were effective in distinguishing HCM patients and normal participants with high accuracy in both the discovery and validation sets based on these five features, SVM and RF models were constructed. Using the kernel as radial, degree as 2, cost as 1.2 and gamma as 1.2, which determined by grid search, SVM model, with the mean misclassification error of 0.0363333 in 5-fold cross-validation, was constructed in the discovery set. Similarly, the RF model was derived with the nodesize as 3, ntree as 144 and mtry as 1, achieving the mean misclassification error of 0.0286667 in 5-fold cross-validation. The SVM model achieved the AUROC of 0.996 and 0.985 in the discovery set (Fig. 4E) and validation set (Fig. 4F), respectively. The SVM model achieved the accuracy of 96.1% and 97.1% in discovery set and validation set, respectively. The RF model achieved the AUROC of 1.000 in both discovery set and validation set (Fig. 4G, H). The RF model achieved the accuracy of 94.8% and 100.0% in discovery set and validation set, respectively. Permutation importance was calculated after the models had been fitted (Fig. 5A, B).

Differential metabolites in the model correlated with clinical features of HCM

To further determine the clinical significance of these metabolites, their correlations with key HCM features were analyzed. First, univariate linear regression analysis of the five substances in the model and the three essential indicators in clinical practice was performed (Table S1). Subsequently, in order to further determine the correlation among the statistically significant indicators of univariate linear regression, stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis was carried out (Table 2). No collinearity was observed among the variables. The results showed that KAPA was independently associated with LAVI (P = 0.001). NAA was independently associated with RV5+SV1 (P = 0.001). KAPA and NAA were independently associated with LVMI (P = 0.020; P = 0.001).

Discussion

Nowadays, timely and accurate diagnosis remains a challenge in clinical practice since echocardiography, which has a special affinity for HCM and is widely used in the diagnosis and screening of HCM, still has certain limitations that result in some HCM patients being missed during screening (Maron et al. 2022). Although we have constructed a diagnostic model for HCM based on amino acid targeted metabolism in a previous study (Guo et al. 2022), the diagnostic model based on untargeted metabolomics data has higher accuracy and discriminatory ability, which may be related to the fact that untargeted metabolomics has a wide range of assays and does not rely on researchers’ previous knowledge and experience (Maron et al. 2022, Rowin et al. 2020, Bai et al. 2022, Marian and Braunwald 2017, Wang et al. 2022, Huang et al. 2013, Mathe et al. 2014). In this study, we comprehensively analyzed the untargeted plasma metabolomics results and clinical and echocardiography information of HCM patients and normal participants. We found that HCM patients had significant plasma metabolic disturbances compared with normal participants. Furthermore, we constructed a screening model that effectively distinguished HCM patients from the general population and preliminarily identified potential pathogenic pathways using ML algorithms. The results of this study can lay the foundation for further exploration of the disease mechanism and help physicians in the initial identification of HCM.

Although gender, age and BMI are potential confounders which can significantly affect metabolism, all of the above demographic characteristics were actually excluded during the feature screening phase, suggesting that the discriminating efficacy of this plasma metabolite model is not dependent on these features. After LASSO analysis, both ML algorithms identified the same five key metabolites: KAPA, γ-linolenoyl ethanolamide, nitrilotriacetic acid, D-quinovose, and NAA. Subsequently, we applied two ML algorithms to construct this diagnostic model based on these plasma metabolites. The SVM model achieved the accuracy of 96.1% and 97.1% in discovery set and validation set, respectively. The RF model achieved the accuracy of 94.8% and 100.0% in discovery set and validation set, respectively. In addition, based on the importance of the rearrangement of the calculated features, we found that the above metabolites had a greater impact on the prediction results after rearrangement (the change of mean misclassification error caused by each metabolite rearrangement in the SVM model was > 0.8; the prediction accuracy and Gini coefficient of each metabolite rearranged in the RF model changed greatly). These results demonstrate that the proposed diagnostic model is highly effective for identifying HCM patients.

To clarify the clinical implications of this model, we performed a stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis. Results showed that KAPA was independently correlated with LAVI, which is closely related to the severity of the disease and the occurrence of adverse cardiovascular events (Nuzzi et al. 2023, Njoku et al. 2018). This clinical indicator is also emerging as a potential marker to assess the severity and an independent predictor of the diagnosis of HCM (Moroni et al. 2023). KAPA is a key chemical intermediate used in the synthesis of vitamin B5 derivatives which plays an important role in energy metabolism and fatty acid synthesis (Dibble et al. 2022, Depeint et al. 2006), which has been reported to be one of the most significant metabolic alterations in HCM(Previs et al. 2022, Ranjbarvaziri et al. 2021) and a variety of metabolites in the process are associated with adverse clinical manifestations (Jansen et al. 2023, Floegel et al. 2018). Meanwhile, our study found that when analyzed KAPA with NAA, it was related to LVMI, which is one of the important indicators to evaluate myocardial hypertrophy and an independent predictor of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in adults (Kavey 2013). However, KAPA has been studied in mycobacteria (Fan et al. 2015, Dey et al. 2010), little attention has been paid to its role in cardiovascular disease. Therefore, the specific mechanism of this metabolite still needs to be further studied. Beyond that, we also found NAA was independently associated with RV5+SV1, which is one of the effective indicators of myocardial hypertrophy in electrocardiogram (Noda et al. 1994). Since NAA is strongly associated with two indicators related to myocardial hypertrophy, this metabolite might be relevant to the occurrence of HCM.

Traditionally, NAA has been thought to be key to brain metabolism, particularly as a carbon donor for fatty acid synthesis (D’Adamo and Yatsu 1966). As research progresses, emerging evidence suggests that the potential role of NAA in cell signaling is independent of its degradation products, suggesting that the role of signaling may be much greater than that of carbon donation (Krause and Wegner 2024). In our study, untargeted plasma metabolomics analysis showed that some pathways in HCM patients were significantly dysregulated compared to normal participants with the most significantly altered pathways being central carbon metabolism in cancer and protein digestion and absorption. The central carbon metabolism in cancer pathway, which is the most significantly altered pathway in HCM patients and closely related to tumor development (Yuan et al. 2023, Wong et al. 2017), mainly includes glycolysis process and alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism. Interestingly, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism can connect these two pathways above and was the third independent pathway which was significantly upregulated in the plasma of HCM patients (Fig. 3F). Previous studies have confirmed that carbon metabolism and glutamate play extremely important roles in the occurrence of tumors and NAA metabolism is closely related to tumor growth and survival since NAA can interconvert with aspartic acid and glutamic acids (Weindl et al. 2016, Zand et al. 2016, Wang et al. 2019, Li et al. 2023). Therefore, HCM seems to share some similarities with the occurrence of tumors and alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism pathway may be the main process leading to HCM occurrence, which further supported the results of the ML algorithms and stepwise multivariate linear regression analysis in the present study. Importantly, scholars have proved that metabolic remodeling can promote the occurrence of myocardial hypertrophy mainly through the great consumption of glucose, thereby promoting aspartate synthesis (Ritterhoff et al. 2020). Although this study focused on LV hypertrophy due to strenuous exercise, ventricular pressure or volume overload, the close association between NAA and myocardial hypertrophy in our study seems to suggest that HCM has a similar mechanism of myocardial hypertrophy. For these reasons, future studies should further investigate the roles of NAA and the alanine, aspartate, and glutamate metabolic pathways in HCM.

In addition to the above, several other limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study included a relatively small cohort from a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, due to its cross-sectional design, causal relationships between metabolic alterations and HCM pathogenesis cannot be established. Third, although untargeted metabolomics provides a comprehensive metabolic overview, the identified metabolites were not verified through targeted quantitative analysis, which may affect the precision of individual metabolite measurements. Finally, the machine learning models were developed using internal data only, and external validation in independent and larger cohorts is warranted to confirm their robustness and clinical applicability.

Conclusions

In this study, we developed a preliminary plasma metabolomics–based diagnostic model for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) using machine learning algorithms. This model may help address the limitations of current screening methods and enhance diagnostic efficiency in the general population. In addition, we identified potential metabolic pathways involved in the development of HCM, providing new insights for future exploration of disease regulatory targets.

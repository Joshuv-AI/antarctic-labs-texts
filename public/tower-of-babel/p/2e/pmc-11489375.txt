Optimizing the effect of heat treatment on the mechanical properties (tensile Strength and hardness) of Hyphaene Thebaica nut; A machine learning and Taguchi approach
Authors: Bassey Okon Samuel, Abdulmumin Akoredeley Alabi, Shehu Abba Lawal, Enejor Peter, Tanimu Kogi Ibrahim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe demand for innovative and sustainable biomaterials in healthcare and biotechnology has fueled the need for advancements in its engineering performance. However, the limited properties of biomaterials without the integration of synthetic reinforcements or supports pose a challenge. Consequently, there is a growing necessity for the development of full cycle eco-friendly materials. This study focuses on enhancing the mechanical properties of Doum palm nuts through heat treatment for engineering applications. The research employs the Taguchi optimization technique and genetic algorithms to determine the optimal combination of heating temperature and holding time, aiming to achieve the highest tensile strength and hardness. The results showcase the effectiveness of the proposed approach, revealing the optimal tensile strength of 7.32 MPa at a treatment temperature of 50 °C and a holding time of 120 min. Similarly, the optimum hardness of 60.3 Hv is attained at a heat treatment temperature of 100 °C and a holding time of 120 min. The study further investigates chemical changes through Fourier transform infrared analysis and optical micrographs. Regression models for tensile strength and hardness are developed, and the global optimum is found using a genetic algorithm solver. The findings of this study demonstrate significant and remarkable improvements in the mechanical properties of biomaterials, highlighting their potential for demanding engineering applications. The optimized heat treatment parameters provide a path towards enhanced biomaterials performance and offer promising avenues for the development of tougher and more resilient materials. This research contributes to the field of biomaterials engineering, paving the way for the design and production of eco-friendly materials with superior mechanical properties, thus addressing the ongoing demand for innovative and sustainable solutions in various industries.Keywords: Doum palm nut, Heat treatment, Taguchi, Genetic algorithm

Introduction

The world's population growth is not only increasing waste production but also putting pressure on scarce resources, resulting in intense competition, which has been the driving force behind global industrial and economic activity [1,2]. The challenge the world is facing is the development of alternatives to materials that are derived from these scarce, non-renewable sources. Natural occurring materials derived from agricultural sources have been proposed as a potential and long-term solution to this problem [3].

In finding a sustainable alternative, the mechanical and structural reliability of a material is also a strong criterion for consideration. Other factors to consider include its effectiveness and availability. This means that various factors limit the development of sustainable alternatives to these non-renewable sources from renewable sources. Some industries that have invested heavily in reducing their demand for polymer-based materials are the automotive, aviation, and packaging industries [4,5]. This is in a bid to develop recyclable or biodegradable materials, reducing the burden on the ecosystem [6]. Hosseinmardi et al. [7] explored the use of nanoscale organosolv lignin (OSL) particles in natural rubber (NR) latex to improve its mechanical properties. By incorporating 5 wt % nanoscale OSL, the researchers achieved a significant improvement in tensile strength, toughness, and Shore hardness of the NR films. The enhanced mechanical properties were attributed to the improved dispersion of OSL and its strong adhesion with the NR matrix. Overall, the study highlighted the potential of using renewable and bio-based nanofillers like OSL to enhance the performance of NR-based materials. Overall, this research highlights the potential of using renewable and bio-based nanofillers like OSL to enhance the performance of NR-based materials. Also, another driver for the shift from these synthetic materials to natural materials is the rising cost of petroleum-based materials due to dwindling reserves and the increasing awareness of environmental preservation [[8], [9], [10]].

But these alternatives pose new challenges, such as their unsuitability or unreliability in terms of performance, their inability or difficulty to be processed (poor machinability), etc. [6,11]. To overcome this challenge, some of these renewable materials have been combined with synthetic materials for the development of hybrid composites. Turkben et al. [12] studied the use of ethylene-propylene-diene monomer rubber (EPDM) and tire waste powder (TW) as fillers in epoxy matrices to enhance toughness and reduce costs. Different epoxy resin systems were used as matrices, including bisphenol-A epoxy resins and modified epoxies. The effects of rubber type, rubber ratio, and epoxy matrix on composite properties were investigated. The mechanical and thermal properties of TW composites were found to be superior to those of EPDM composites. The best results were obtained with a polyurethane-modified epoxy (ER3) matrix. The optimal filling ratio of EPDM and TW ranged from 20 % to 30 % by weight, depending on the matrix type. The addition of TW improved the impact strength of the diluted epoxy matrices. It was observed that lower ambient temperatures yielded more favorable mechanical properties compared to an aqueous medium. However, this does not address the issues of synthetic material sustainability, biodegradability, or machinability, which have been widely reported in several publications [[13], [14], [15]]. More so, the complexity of these hybridizations, as seen in the multiscale nature of hybrid composites, is a source of much reported engineering compromise, such as its water absorption, poor thermal characteristics, etc. [16,17].

Agricultural materials have been the subject of extensive research in materials science as a source for these sustainable alternatives [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. [22] discussed the non-notched flattening method for bamboo, which involves steam softening, roller flattening, and drying to transform bamboo tube sections into flattened bamboo strips. The study examined the macro- and micro-mechanical properties of the flattened bamboo and the results indicate that the parenchyma cells undergo shrinkage and distortion, leading to decreased hygroscopicity due to hemicellulose decomposition. The treatment temperature and time positively impact the modulus of elasticity (MOE) and hardness, resulting in a 25 % increase in MOE and a significant 34 % increase in hardness. Ultimately, the non-notched flattening process positively influences the macro-mechanical properties of the bamboo [6]. carried out an investigation on effect of heat exposure temperature and time on the mechanical properties of bamboo tubes. The center of the study was the optimization of the treatment temperature and the treatment time. At the optimal parameters setting, the tensile strength of the material increased significantly even though a reduction in the bending modulus was observed. From agricultural residues to animal waste, many studies have been carried out to develop suitable alternatives to materials developed from petroleum sources. Of these agricultural materials, natural fibers are derived from plants such as banana bast, pineapple leaves, coir, sisal, corn hub, coconut shells, etc. Despite the fact that the majority of these efforts have concentrated on using these materials as polymer matrix reinforcements, significant improvements such as high mechanical properties, low weight, excellent wear and surface properties, and so on have been observed. These advantages have put natural fiber composites at the center of materials research concerned with the improvement of obtained or lagging properties by improved processes such as chemical and heat treatment, coatings, stacking, etc. Importantly, these have led to a reduction in demand for materials from non-ecofriendly and non-renewable energy sources and processes, thereby reducing the greenhouse effect.

Doum palm, with the botanical name Hyphaene Thebaica, is a dioecious plant found mainly in arid regions, particularly in the Arab regions and in West Africa. The plant is mainly adapted to areas with groundwater since it grows in places with dry climates. The leaves of the tree have been the most useful part of the tree, as they have been used for making baskets, brooms, mats, etc. Also, the stem of the plant has been used for structural applications, such as timber for pillars and furniture. But more recently, the fruits have been receiving attention. A part of the tree that has hardly been explored for application in engineering and industry is the fruit seed, even though more recently, progress has been made in using the ground particulates for the development of polymer composites [25,26]. [27] studied the impact of moisture content on the thermal properties of doum palm fruit. Specifically, the study explored variations in specific heat, thermal conductivity, and thermal diffusivity across a moisture content range of 24.05 %–67.59 %. The observed specific heat and thermal conductivity spanned from 1496.46 to 2966.67 J/kg K and 0.1671–0.3338 W/mK, respectively. Significantly, a consistent linear increase in both specific heat and thermal conductivity was identified with rising moisture content, a trend substantiated by statistical significance at the 0.05 level. Additionally, the investigation highlighted the moisture-dependent nature of specific heat and thermal conductivity in doum palm fruit. Noteworthy was the establishment of a non-linear relationship between thermal diffusivity and moisture content within the temperature range of 334–337 K, encapsulating the specified moisture range. These findings reinforce the dependence of the behavior of the material on heat treatment. Heat treatment stands as a commonly employed method to modify the mechanical properties of materials, including nuts and seeds like those of Hyphaene thebaica. This process involves subjecting the material to controlled heating and cooling cycles to induce changes in its structure and properties [28]. Through heat treatment, alterations in hardness, strength, toughness, and other mechanical characteristics can be achieved, depending on the specific conditions applied. The mechanical properties of Hyphaene thebaica nuts are influenced by factors such as their chemical composition, microstructure, and moisture content. These properties, including hardness, toughness, and elasticity, play a crucial role in determining the suitability of the nuts for various applications. In the context of heat treatment, the primary objective concerning Hyphaene thebaica nuts is to enhance their mechanical properties for specific applications. This might involve increasing hardness for improved wear resistance, enhancing toughness for better durability, or altering elasticity for increased flexibility. Various parameters of the heat treatment process, such as holding temperature and holding time, significantly impact the mechanical properties of Hyphaene thebaica nuts [26]. These parameters determine the extent of structural changes induced in the nuts during heat treatment, thereby influencing their final properties. Microstructural changes occur in Hyphaene thebaica nuts as a result of heat treatment. These changes may include grain refinement, phase transformations, and redistribution of elements, all of which directly affect the mechanical properties of the nuts. Studies aim to optimize the heat treatment process for natural materials to achieve desired mechanical properties while minimizing energy consumption and processing time [29,30]. This involves systematic experimentation to study the effects of different heat treatment parameters and their interactions. Characterization techniques such as hardness testing, tensile testing, impact testing, and microscopy are commonly employed to assess the mechanical properties of heat-treated Hyphaene thebaica nuts. These techniques provide valuable insights into the effects of heat treatment on the nuts' properties. The applications of heat-treated Hyphaene thebaica nuts can span various industries, including agriculture, construction, and manufacturing. These nuts can be used in the production of durable tools, utensils, handicrafts, and structural components, showcasing their versatility and potential for value-added products.

Machine learning is an artificial intelligence-based method that uses computational algorithms to develop models from empirical data or observable conditions. In machine learning, knowledge is obtained and integrated by the system from large scale information or observations, and the knowledge is then used by the system itself to improve its understanding of phenomena beyond the information it was fed with [31]. Genetic algorithm is an optimization technique that is based on machine learning principles. It is a system whereby the stronger survive and the weaker are disposed of (survival of the fittest). It is an evolutionary algorithm that is based on the evolution principle of Charles Darwin's theory of evolution, whereby succeeding generations are built from the attributes that helped the preceding members survive. The genetic algorithm optimization process is a stochastic search of the space covered by the solution of a function by chromosomes (integers), which are representatives of the variables. These variables are the parameters to be optimized. Each of these chromosomes' integers is known as a gene, which is a decimal less than 9. Although this approach is for modeling applications, in the traditional genetic algorithm approach, these genes are binary numbers. One advantage of not using binary in the modeling optimization approach is obtaining a wider search space, especially when the optimization parameters are few. Apart from optimization, the genetic algorithm process can also be used for the modeling of systems [32]. In carrying out the process of genetic optimization, the first population is generated randomly, and the parameters are obtained by the initial combination. The model is then populated using the newly obtained parameters. And results for the parameters within the population are obtained just as in simulated annealing by performance measures that are dependent on a cost function. The obtained cost value is arranged in ascending order along with their chromosomes. The combination with the lowest cost values is chosen as the best, and further processes of reproduction, crossover, and mutation are carried out. The process of reproduction (also known as “ranked based selection”) involves developing the next set of populations with about 20 % of the best chromosomes obtained from the previous procedures and replacing the eliminated chromosomes with those derived from the processes of crossover and mutation. This method is referred to as the “elite genetic algorithm,” since only the elite chromosomes make up the next generation. In crossover, there is an exchange of genes between two chromosomes in the current generation. The genes are exchanged for their corresponding locations on the new chromosomes. This leads to the production of new chromosomes. This crossover process is repeated until there is full replacement of the eliminated chromosomes (80 %). Mutation involves the random selection and modification of genes contained in the current population. This increases the search as random elements are introduced within the genetic algorithm. Evaluation of the new population is carried out after the change of chromosomes. These processes are then repeated over and over again for the specified number of generations in order to obtain a final solution. Fig. 1 shows the genetic algorithm procedure.

In the Taguchi optimization method, design parameters are optimized to obtain an optimal result with reduced variation or noise. Applying the orthogonal array method, the Taguchi optimization method studies the whole sample space of a problem using a minimal number of experiments, thereby saving cost and increasing efficiency. The center of the Taguchi design is the control of noise, thereby obtaining performance with less noise (variability). Orthogonal arrays have also been reported to be used for the study of the effect of two factors interacting. The signal-to-noise ratio is used for studying the inherent noise in the system and determining the parameter's optimal combination. The objective function on the Taguchi is defined using Equations (1), (2)). In optimizing effects that involve obtaining the highest values (the higher the better), such as efficiency, power produced, profits, tensile strengths, etc., Equation (1) is applied, while to study effects that are expected to be at their lowest (the lower the better), like cost, wear, density, power consumed, etc., Equation (2) is used.

Much study on the use of materials derived from agricultural sources has been concerned with the development of composite materials usually with metals or polymers as the matrix [[33], [34], [35]]. In the long run, the system still depends on nonrenewable resources. Studies on the direct treatment of agriculturally sourced materials for the augmentation of performance characteristics are rare. Moreso, the application of machine learning techniques in the study of the behavior of these naturally source renewable materials under different heat regimes is lacking. In this work, doum palm nuts are directly heat treated under different heating conditions to improve its Tensile and wear properties for the purpose of direct engineering application.

Materials and methods

Doum palm preparation and heat treatment

Ripe and dried doum palm fruits were purchased from Sabon Gari market, Zaria, Kaduna State in Nigeria. The dried fruits were split open and the hard nuts were separated from the husks. The doum palm nuts (DPN) were machined on a lathe machine to obtained the ring-like samples used for the different tests. The preparation process of the doum palm nuts and the dimensions of the prepared samples are presented in Fig. 2. The machined doum palm nuts were heat treated in a furnace (Labtron Muffle Furnace LMF-E50) regulated at the different stated temperatures and left to heat for their respective holding times in the furnace. They were then brought out and left to cool in the open.

Doum palm preparation procedure.

Mechanical test

Diametric compression test

The diametric compression test was conducted using the 10 kN capacity ENERPAC universal tensile machine (UTM). Each DPN sample was placed on the lower platen on the UTM while the upper platen was lowered until it made slight contact with the sample. Load was applied to the sample through a hydraulic system until the sample failed. The maximum load was record as P. The value of P reported is an average obtained from three experiments.

Hardness test

Hardness is property of a material that enables it to resist plastic deformation. It refers to resistance to bending, scratching, abrasion or cutting. The hardness property of doum palm nut was determined according to the ASTM E384-99 standard using the electronic micro-Vickers hardness tester [MV1-PC] in the shell chair laboratory of mechanical engineering Ahmadu Bello University Zaria.

Results and discussions

Physical and microstructural properties of the developed composites

FTIR analyis

Fig. 3 shows the FTIR spectrum of the doum palm nut upon heating at four temperatures (50 °C, 100 °C, 150 °C, and 200 °C). The finger print region shows alignment with the pattern of doum palm nut as earlier presented by Ref. [26]. It shows the various functional groups and the chemical transformations at the different heating regimes. The FTIR spectrum of doum palm nut shows a peak at wavenumber 2922 associated with the C-H bond stretching vibration in the fatty acid chains of the nut's triglycerides. As the heating temperature increases, the peak intensity at wavenumber 2922 also increases. This could be due to the thermal degradation of triglycerides, which increases the concentration of free fatty acids and intensifies the C-H stretching vibration. Alternatively, the temperature increase could increase the mobility of the fatty acid chains, also leading to an increase in the C-H bond stretching vibration. However, the increase in free fatty acids due to thermal degradation may decrease the tensile and hardness properties of doum palm nut as they act as plasticizers, reducing stiffness and increasing flexibility. Furthermore, increased mobility of fatty acid chains may also decrease tensile and hardness properties. The wavenumber 2109 is associated with nitrile (C ≡ N) or isocyanate (-NCO) groups, both present in certain chemical compounds. The observed increase in the band at this wavenumber during heating may be due to the formation of cyanide or isocyanate compounds. Further research is needed to confirm the identity of these species in doum palm nut. The presence of cyanide could decrease the material's mechanical strength due to its toxicity, while isocyanate may enhance its mechanical properties. The FTIR spectrum of doum palm nut shows a peak at wavenumber 1744, which is attributed to the stretching vibration of the carbonyl group (C=O) in the ester bond of triglycerides. At low heating temperatures, the ester bonds in triglycerides are stable, leading to a suppressed or retarded peak at wavenumber 1744. However, at high heating temperatures, the ester bonds undergo thermal degradation, leading to the formation of new chemical species like free fatty acids, aldehydes, and ketones, which can increase the peak intensity at wavenumber 1744 [36]. Additionally, an increase in the concentration of free fatty acids may also contribute to the increased peak intensity. The changes in the FTIR spectrum with increasing heating temperature indicate the doum palm nut's thermal stability and its potential mechanical property changes due to the breakdown of triglycerides and formation of new chemical species. Therefore, the doum palm nut's FTIR spectrum can provide insights into its potential weakening or strengthening with increased heating temperature, which is essential for industrial and manufacturing applications.

FTIR Spectrum of Doum Palm Nut at different Heating Regimes.

The FTIR spectrum of doum palm nut exhibits a peak at wavenumber 1002, associated with C-O bond stretching in carbohydrates and/or phenolic compounds. The observed increase in intensity of this peak with increasing heating temperatures, along with the emergence of new peaks in its vicinity, can be attributed to various factors. The heating process may lead to the breakdown of complex carbohydrates into simpler sugars, causing an increase in the concentration of monosaccharides and/or oligosaccharides, which have C-O bonds in their structure. The breakdown of phenolic compounds may also contribute to the appearance of new peaks. These changes may affect the mechanical properties of the nut by altering intermolecular forces and bonding. Additionally, the heating process may release these compounds from the plant matrix, increasing their concentration and, hence, the intensity of the corresponding FTIR bands. Therefore, the observed changes in the FTIR spectrum of doum palm nut with increasing heating temperatures indicate changes in its chemical composition that can impact its mechanical properties.

The intensity of the OH band in the FTIR spectrum of doum palm nut increases with increasing temperature, possibly due to the increased presence of free hydroxyl groups or the formation of new compounds containing hydroxyl groups during heating. This agrees with the observation of [37] in whose study the OH band gap increased with the increase in temperature. Hydroxyl groups are present in various compounds in doum palm nut, such as carbohydrates, organic acids, and phenolic compounds. These compounds may undergo thermal degradation at higher temperatures, leading to the formation of simpler molecules containing hydroxyl groups. The observed increase in OH band intensity may also be due to the release or hydrolysis of hydroxyl-containing compounds from the plant matrix. The effect of the increased OH band intensity on the tensile strength and hardness of doum palm nut may depend on the specific hydroxyl-containing compounds present and their concentration, as well as the effect of moisture content on mechanical properties. Some hydroxyl-containing compounds may have antioxidant and antimicrobial properties, while others may contribute to the stiffness and strength of plant materials.

The FTIR spectrum of doum palm nut shows changes under different heating regimes, with some peaks increasing in intensity while others decrease. The CH stretching vibration peak at wavenumber 2922 and the C=O stretching vibration peak at wavenumber 2109 both increase in intensity with increasing heating temperature, while the C=O stretching vibration peak at wavenumber 1744 decreases at lower temperatures and becomes more prominent at higher temperatures. The C-O stretching vibration peak at wavenumber 1002 increases in intensity, with more peaks appearing around it. The intensity of the OH band also increases with increasing temperature. These changes in the FTIR spectrum may be attributed to thermal degradation, breakdown of complex compounds, and/or release of compounds from the plant matrix.

Fig. 7 shows the effect of the heat treatment parameters on the tensile strength of the doum palm nuts. In Fig. 5a, it is observed that the highest tensile strength of the doum palm nut is observed at a lower treatment temperature. This aligns with the study of [40] who showed the negative correlation of the tensile strength of biomass with increase in heat treatment temperature. This is due to low moisture loss at lower heating regimes, therefore preserving the granular bond strength within the doum palm nut. The loss in tensile strength is observed to be more significant between heating regimes of 100 and 150 °C. This is because drying occurs within this temperature range. Beyond 150 °C, the drop in tensile strength is reduced, implying that irrespective of the heating temperature beyond this range, since drying is complete and a stable phase is observed irrespective of the temperature change, tensile strength remains almost constant. Fig. 7b shows the effect of holding time on the tensile strength of the material. It is observed that the tensile strength of the doum palm nut increases with an increase in holding time. At constant temperatures and different holding times, the effect of drying is reduced, and more intergranular reactions take place. Usually, close-ups of granular spaces occur at higher holding times, leading to increased toughness and tensile strength, as observed.

In Fig. 8a, a non-linear pattern is observed for the effect of treatment temperature on the hardness of the material. At smaller holding times, granular fusion occurs, resisting shear deformation along boundary lines, but at longer holding times, the increase in hardness value is due to total drying and the transformation of the doum palm granules into hard particles.

Analysis of variance

In Table 5, the analysis of variance for the effect of the heat treatment parameters on the tensile strength and hardness values of the doum palm nut is presented. At a 95 % confidence level, it is observed that the two parameters (treatment temperature and holding time) had a significant effect on the tensile strength of the doum palm nut. This is because the P-values observed were all less than 0.05. Also, the two parameters were observed to have significant effects on the hardness value of the doum palm seed. Importantly, it was observed that the percentage contribution of treatment temperature to the tensile strength and hardness values of the doum palm nut were 96.7 % and 99.5 %, respectively. Therefore, the effect of holding time on the effects was rather incomparable to the heating temperature, as they were observed to be 2.0 % and 0.3 %, respectively. The implication of this observation is that in heat treating the doum palm nut to obtain a considerable change in the tensile strength and hardness value, the treatment temperature should be of more concern than the holding time. Also, at higher treatment temperatures, the tensile strength is lower. Also, in Fig. 9b, the highest surface hardness was observed at heating temperatures between 80 and 110 °C, even though the variation of the surface hardness across the holding time was hardly significant.

Optimization: Genetic algorithm

This study employed a genetic algorithm (GA) to optimize the Tensile Strength (obtained from the diametral compressive strength) and hardness of the doum palm nut within the effects of the two variables considered which are heating temperature and holding time. The tensile strength and hardness of a material, which is a key mechanical property, determines its usefulness in various applications. The GA solver was implemented using MATLAB's ga function, which allowed the exploration of the parameter space in search of the maximum tensile strength and hardness value. The fitness function used in our optimization was based on the models (Equations (6) and (7)) developed from the regression analysis already presented. The GA solver was set to find the maximum value of the fitness function by minimizing its negative. The GA solver was executed for 50 generations, with a population size of 100 individuals per generation. The optimization results showed that the maximum tensile strength was achieved at treatment temperature of 50 °C and holding time of 120 min, with a value of 8.318 MPa. Also, the maximum hardness, was achieved at the same conditions of 50 °C heat treatment temperature and holding time of 120 min which was a hardness value of 54.873 Hv.

Fig. 11, Fig. 12 shows the optimization process and the fitness function shape for the tensile strength and the hardness value respectively. This was presented by creating a scatter plot of the evaluated fitness function using MATLAB's function. Fig. 11 A shows the scatter plot for the fitness function through the generations for the tensile strength while Fig. 11B shows the fitness function shape of the tensile strength in respect to a variation of the factors. Also, Fig. 12A shows the scatter plot for the value of the hardness value over the generations and Fig. 12B shows the fitness function shape of the hardness as regards the parameters observed (treatment temperature and holding time). This plot revealed that the fitness function was relatively flat across most of the input space, with a single peak near the optimum values of treatment temperature and holding time. This suggests that the GA solver was able to efficiently search the parameter space and find the global optimum. Overall, results obtained aligned with the Taguchi optimal values, even though with an in-depth search was more accurate as to achieving a higher tensile strength from the regression models it was provided with. This demonstrates the effectiveness of using a GA to optimize the tensile strength of a material, and provide insights into the shape of the fitness function and the optimal values of the input parameters. These findings can inform the development of new materials with improved mechanical properties, which could have important practical applications in various industries.

Conclusion

-The combination of heat treatment parameters (heat treatment temperature and holding time) for the optimum tensile strength of doum palm nut is using the Taguchi optimization technique is 50°C- and 120-min holding time respectively and for the hardness, the optimum combination of the heat treatment parameters are 100 °C heating temperature and 120 min holding time respectively.

The combination of heat treatment parameters (heat treatment temperature and holding time) for the optimum tensile strength of doum palm nut is using the Taguchi optimization technique is 50°C- and 120-min holding time respectively and for the hardness, the optimum combination of the heat treatment parameters are 100 °C heating temperature and 120 min holding time respectively.

-Analysis of variance at a confidence level of 95 %, on the effect of heat treatment factors showed that the heating temperature and holding time, with P-Values less than 0.05 had a significant effect on the on the tensile strength and hardness value of the heat treated doum palm nut.

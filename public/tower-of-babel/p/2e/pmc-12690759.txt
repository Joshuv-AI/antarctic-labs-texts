PDNO-03 CHILDHOOD CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM TUMOURS AT A MAJOR CENTER IN GHANA: PRESENTATION AND OUTCOMES
Authors: Ernestina N B Schandorf, Hafisatu Gbadamosi, Mary-Ann Dadzie, Lorna A Renner, Ebenezer V Badoe, Patrick Bankah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsTo determine presentation, pre-diagnostic symptomatic interval (PSI), outcomes, and quality of life at 12-week follow-up in children aged 1-17 years diagnosed with CNS tumours at Korle Bu Teaching Hospital (KBTH).MethodsThis was a hospital-based cross-sectional, correlational study using the PedsQL questionnaire at the initial presentation and 12 weeks follow-up. The study population included all children (aged 1 to 17 years) with a new, radiologically diagnosed CNS tumour and their caregivers attending the Paediatric Oncology and Neurosurgery Units of KBTH.ResultsThe study included 45 children with a median age of 7 years (range 2-17 years) and 56% were males. The most common tumour location was infratentorial (45%) and low-grade gliomas were the most frequent histology (n = 23). Common symptoms were headache (n = 33), motor (n = 29) and vomiting (n = 24). Two children had Neurofibromatosis type 1. The median diagnostic interval was 121 days (range 44-305 days), with patient interval of 60 days, and primary healthcare interval of 21 days. The PSI for low-grade tumors was 60 days, compared to 45 days in high-grade tumors. Nineteen children (42%) received definitive surgical treatment, with only four children (9%) receiving gross total resection with median time to resection of 28.5 days (range 27.5-37 days). Twelve-week survival was 68.9%. There was a strong correlation between caregivers’ and children’s total QOL scores at presentation (r = 0.88). At follow-up, children’s self-reports of emotional well-being were lower than caregivers’ assessments.ConclusionThe high mortality rate at 12 weeks is a significant concern and needs to be addressed. Children with low-grade tumours experienced longer prediagnostic symptomatic intervals compared to those with high-grade tumours. There was a significant improvement in QOL at 12 weeks, but caregivers tended to underestimate their children’s emotional challenges. The study emphasizes the need for increased focus on the emotional well-being and survival of children with CNS tumours.

ESC Membership Global Award 2025: Latvian Society of Cardiology
Authors: Andrejs Erglis, Iveta Bajare, Vilnis Dzerve
Published: 2026

The Latvian Society of Cardiology was honoured to receive the ESC Membership Global Award 2025, and we are grateful for the opportunity to present the Society in the European Heart Journal.

Brief history and evolution

Founded in 1972 as Latvijas Kardiologu biedrība (Latvian Society of Cardiology), the Society was established to advance cardiovascular care and strengthen cardiology practice across Latvia, then part of the Soviet Union. Although the Society has been professionally active since 1972, it obtained formal legal status as a non-governmental organization in the early 1990s, with official registration records dating from 1993.

Under the leadership of pioneering cardiologists, including Prof. Julijs Anselevics and Prof. Uldis Kalnins, the Society played a central role in introducing key clinical innovations in Latvia. These advances included the early adoption of pre-hospital thrombolysis for myocardial infarction, the expansion of enzyme-based diagnostics for acute myocardial infarction, and the introduction of practical improvements in the management of heart failure and hypertension. Together, these initiatives laid the groundwork for sustained, nationwide improvements in acute cardiovascular care.

From the early 2000s through the mid-2020s, the Society strengthened its international engagement through multicentre clinical trials, closer collaboration with the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and European Association of Percutaneous Cardiovascular Interventions (EAPCI), and by hosting regular international and Nordic–Baltic-level scientific meetings, establishing itself as a regional hub for cardiology education.

Mission, values, and core activities

The mission of the Society is to reduce the burden of cardiovascular disease in Latvia by promoting education, high-quality clinical care, research, and public health activities. Its work is guided by professional excellence, evidence-based practice, national and international collaboration, continuous education, and strong patient advocacy.

Core activities include developing and endorsing clinical guidelines and quality initiatives for cardiovascular care, organizing scientific meetings and continuing medical education, promoting professional training, supporting participation in clinical studies and registries, working with health authorities on healthcare policy, and cooperating with patient organizations to support public education and patient advocacy.

Key scientific, educational, and public health initiatives

The Society organizes national and regional scientific meetings and Continuing Medical Education (CME) activities, including 8–10 national seminars and conferences each year, the annual Baltic Summer Congress, and other Baltic-level events such as the Riga Lipid Conference and the Baltic Heart Failure and Arrhythmia Meeting, in collaboration with leading European cardiovascular experts.

The Latvian Cardiology Centre, affiliated with the Society, participate in numerous international multicentre clinical trials and registries, including studies on acute coronary syndromes, intravascular imaging and percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) techniques, and heart failure, thereby strengthening national research capacity and supporting evidence-based clinical practice. A key example is the Latvian Registry of Familial Hypercholesterolemia (LRFH), one of the most well-established cardiovascular registries integrated into routine cardiology care in Latvia.

Ongoing cardiovascular clinical research in Latvia also includes trials of innovative heart failure therapies (such as ziltivekimab) and studies focusing on physical activity and metabolic interventions in cardiac patients. In addition, large multidisciplinary research projects address cardiovascular care quality and mortality outcomes, often incorporating patient involvement beyond formal pharmaceutical trial enrolment.

Close cooperation with the ESC and EAPCI, together with active support for dedicated working groups, strengthens fellowship opportunities and supports the continuous professional development and upskilling of interventional cardiologists in Latvia.

The Society works closely with the Ministry of Health and regularly contributes to the development of national legislative and policy documents, with a focus on strengthening the role and quality of cardiovascular care within the Latvian healthcare system. A recent outcome of this collaboration is the Action Plan for Improving Cardiovascular Health 2025–2027, which aims to ensure patient-centred, integrated, and comprehensive cardiovascular care across Latvia. This action line focuses on strengthening the timely diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation of cardiovascular disease risk factors and conditions through the development of a unified prevention programme, including screening for diabetes mellitus, systematic coronary risk evaluation (SCORE)—based cardiovascular risk, and chronic kidney disease; improving access to consultations by optimizing patient pathways; enhancing the monitoring of chronic patients through the introduction of dynamic follow-up algorithms; preventing disease progression by improving access to essential medications; and ensuring equitable inpatient treatment for all patients in acute events such as acute coronary syndrome and stroke.

Particularly noteworthy is an innovative pilot project designed to reduce the long-term impact of cardiovascular disease by strengthening continuity of care after acute events. The initiative provides structured outpatient follow-up in dedicated cardiology outpatient offices at five locations across Latvia following hospital discharge. This follow-up is delivered in close cooperation with general practitioners and focuses on comprehensive patient assessment, personalized diagnostic planning, and coordinated follow-up investigations and care.

The Latvian Society of Cardiology played a key role in establishing the Methodological Management Institution in the Field of Cardiology by 2024. The Institution’s mission is to promote cardiovascular health by ensuring high-quality, evidence-based prevention, diagnosis, and treatment for patients while supporting professional excellence among healthcare providers. It operates as a collegial, institutional, and politically independent body, responsible for developing and overseeing cardiovascular care algorithms, patient pathways, and the implementation of European and national clinical guidelines.

Collaboration with patient organizations such as ParSirdi.lv supports public education, patient advocacy, and improved access to reliable health information. The Society also contributes to heart health promotion through public awareness events, including World Heart Day, Go Red for Women, and Heat Failure Day, as well as press briefings and journalist training sessions focused on cardiovascular risk and prevention.

Membership engagement: drivers of the recent increase in European Society of Cardiology Professional Members

The recent growth in ESC Professional Membership has been driven by several complementary factors. High-value CME activities and regional congresses—such as the Baltic Summer Congress, Baltic Heart Failure and Arrhythmia meeting, and the Nordic–Baltic Congress of Cardiology—offer accredited education and attract young cardiologists and allied professionals, creating a natural entry point to ESC engagement.

Increased international collaboration and fellowship opportunities, supported by ESC subspecialty associations and EAPCI working groups, motivate clinicians to join, in order to access training grants, professional development, and networking.

Active participation in multicentre clinical trials and registries enhances research visibility and professional profiles, often encouraging ESC membership for access to guidelines, scientific journals, and conferences.

In addition, strong engagement with trainees and both public and private healthcare providers—through university hospital training programmes and large cardiology units—broadens the pool of professionals who recognize the value of ESC membership.

Finally, patient-facing initiatives and collaboration with patient organizations raise the public profile of cardiology, indirectly supporting professional engagement and membership uptake.

Construction and implementation of an AI-enhanced progressive training model for clinical competency development in obstetrics and gynecology residents
Authors: Lidan He, Jianbo Wu, Huijuan Yang, Jinhua Wang, Xuemei Li, Lihong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to develop and validate the effectiveness of an artificial intelligence (AI)-enhanced progressive training model for clinical competency development in obstetrics and gynecology residents and investigate innovative approaches to enhance the quality of obstetrics and gynecology resident training.MethodsA pre-post self-comparison research design was employed with 28 obstetrics and gynecology residents from three consecutive cohorts (2021–2023) as research subjects. The AI technologies utilized in this study included an intelligent case simulation platform, an adaptive learning algorithm-driven personalized training pathway system, and a competency-based intelligent assessment system. The “Three-Coordination” training model and “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system were systematically integrated into the curriculum. Based on the “Four-Dimensional Integrated” clinical competency framework, standardized assessments, validated questionnaires, and semi-structured expert interviews were used to compare residents’ clinical competency development, learning experiences, and satisfaction before and after training.ResultsFollowing implementation the AI-enhanced progressive training model, residents demonstrated significant improvements in professional knowledge competency (74.3 ± 6.8 vs. 86.7 ± 5.4, p < 0.001), clinical skills competency (72.6 ± 7.3 vs. 88.5 ± 4.9, p < 0.001), clinical reasoning competency (68.4 ± 8.1 vs. 84.9 ± 5.7, p < 0.001), and professional conduct competency (76.5 ± 6.2 vs. 85.6 ± 5.3, p < 0.01). Assessment of five core clinical skills revealed post-training performance enhancements of 11.6% − 16.3% (p < 0.001). Multiple parameters measuring clinical case analysis capabilities improved by 9.8% − 15.7% (p < 0.001). Additionally, 92.9% of residents expressed satisfaction with the training model, and 100% of clinical instructors endorsed its potential for broader implementation.ConclusionsThe AI-enhanced progressive clinical competency training model significantly improved obstetrics and gynecology residents’ clinical competency across all measured domains, with particularly notable enhancements in clinical skills and reasoning abilities. The “Three-Coordination” training model and “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system constitute a promising framework for advancing standardized training of obstetrics and gynecology residents and offer potential applications across other medical specialties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-026-08751-5.Keywords: Artificial intelligence; Obstetrics and gynecology; Standardized residency training; Clinical competency; Progressive training model, medical education; Simulation-based training

Background

Standardized residency training is fundamental to developing competent clinical physicians and remains a cornerstone for healthcare quality improvement worldwide [1]. As medical sciences advance rapidly, the integration of emerging technologies, particularly artificial intelligence (AI), into medical education has become a central focus in contemporary healthcare professional development internationally [2, 3]. Obstetrics and gynecology, a critical specialty within clinical medicine, presents distinctive challenges in residency training attributable to three key factors: inherent clinical risks affecting maternal and fetal safety; complex procedural skills that necessitate progressive development; and limited availability of clinical training opportunities, especially for high-risk scenarios [4, 5]. Despite considerable advancements in medical education methodologies, current training approaches in obstetrics and gynecology continue to encounter substantial limitations, including misalignment between training content and evolving clinical demands, inadequate personalization of learning pathways, and suboptimal evaluation systems [6]. Additionally, significant barriers to implementing competency-based medical education persist, including limited faculty time for individualized mentoring and assessment, competing clinical and research demands on faculty members, and the indirect administrative burden generated by competency-based frameworks, such as the requirement for increased frequency and documentation of evaluations [7, 8].

The past decade has witnessed the emergence of competency-based medical education (CBME) as the predominant paradigm in international medical education reform. In the United States, the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) has established comprehensive “Obstetrics and Gynecology Milestones” that delineate progressive competency standards [9, 10]. Concurrently, the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada has implemented the “Competence by Design (CBD)” initiative, which effectively balances standardization with individualization through “Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs)” [11, 12]. While numerous countries, including China, have established national standards for residency training [13, 14], comprehensive and systematic research specifically addressing progressive, competency-based training models for obstetrics and gynecology remains insufficient.

Progressive training models, characterized by sequential advancement through increasingly complex competency levels, have been successfully implemented across various medical specialties and educational contexts. These models, which structure learning through graduated stages of skill acquisition and responsibility, have demonstrated effectiveness in surgical training programs [15], emergency medicine residencies [16], and family medicine education [17]. The core educational tenet that supports these approaches—namely, building clinical expertise through carefully sequenced phases that move from theoretical foundations to complex patient care decisions—has been corroborated in healthcare education systems spanning North America, Europe, and Asia [18, 19]. Despite this widespread validation, the deliberate combination of these staged training methodologies with artificial intelligence-enabled educational tools remains largely unexplored within the specific domain of obstetrics and gynecology residency programs.

Technological innovations have created unprecedented opportunities for enhancing medical education practices. The integration of AI technologies in medical education has demonstrated remarkable potential for transforming traditional training paradigms globally [20]. Notably, virtual reality simulations have shown particular efficacy in obstetric training by providing safe, repeatable environments for practicing complex procedures and emergency management [21, 22]. Recent studies have documented successful implementations of AI-enhanced simulation platforms [23], virtual patient interactions [24], and intelligent assessment systems [25] in various medical specialties. However, most current applications of AI in obstetrics and gynecology training remain as fragmented, isolated tools rather than comprehensive educational systems [26]. Furthermore, existing competency assessment frameworks typically lack sophisticated intelligent algorithms necessary for truly personalized training pathways that can adapt to individual learning trajectories and specific clinical competency development needs [27].

Responding to these deficiencies within contemporary medical education, our study centers on constructing and assessing what we term an “AI-Enhanced Progressive Training Model for Clinical Competency Development in Obstetrics and Gynecology Residents.” This research not only aims to provide empirical evidence regarding the effectiveness of this integrated approach but also seeks to establish reproducible methodologies that can be adapted across different healthcare education settings. This work represents a significant advancement in the field by synthesizing competency-based frameworks with cutting-edge AI technologies to create a more effective, personalized, and comprehensive training system for obstetrics and gynecology residents.

Methods

Research design and implementation

This prospective interventional study employed a pre-post comparison design to evaluate the effectiveness of an AI-enhanced progressive training model on clinical competency development among obstetrics and gynecology residents. The study spanned a 36-month period (September 2021 to August 2024) and followed a systematic four-phase implementation strategy: (1) preliminary investigation and instructional design, (2) model development and construction, (3) implementation and application of the training program, and (4) comprehensive evaluation and optimization. The core training framework consisted of the “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system, which structured the 36-month training period into four sequential developmental phases: Basic Adaptation Period (1–6 months), Capability Enhancement Period (7–12 months), Specialty Mastery Period (13–24 months), and Comprehensive Integration Period (24–36 months) (Fig. 1).

Schematic representation of the Four-Stage Progressive Training Model for obstetrics and gynecology residency education. The model illustrates the temporal progression through four sequential developmental phases: Basic adaptation (months 1–6), Capability enhancement (months 7–12), Specialty mastery (months 13–24), and Comprehensive integration (months 24–36). Each phase incorporates targeted technological interventions (Virtual simulation, Diagnostic assistance, and Clinical decision support) to facilitate the hierarchical acquisition of core competencies (Basic knowledge, Clinical skills, and Clinical reasoning). The educational framework is systematically aligned with the Four-Dimensional Competency Framework, encompassing Professional knowledge, Clinical skills, Clinical reasoning, and Professional conduct dimensions

Study participants and characteristics

The study enrolled 28 obstetrics and gynecology residents from three consecutive academic cohorts (2021–2023) at the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University. All residents from these cohorts were invited to participate as part of the standardized residency training program. During the recruitment meeting, residents were informed that participation was voluntary and would not affect their residency evaluation. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, who were explicitly informed of their right to withdraw at any time without consequences. Ultimately, all 28 eligible residents (100%) agreed to participate: 2021 cohort (N = 10), 2022 cohort (N = 10), and 2023 cohort (N = 8). Demographic analysis revealed a gender distribution of 26 females (92.9%) and 2 males (7.1%), with a mean age of 25.7 ± 1.8 years (range: 23–30 years). All participants possessed undergraduate degrees in clinical medicine from accredited medical schools. No participants withdrew during the study period. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University.

Theoretical framework for training model construction

Clinical competency framework development and validation

This study initiated with the construction of a comprehensive “Four-Dimensional Integrated” clinical competency framework for obstetrics and gynecology residents through a systematic qualitative investigation protocol. Data were collected through structured surveys from six regional medical institutions and in-depth interviews with 30 clinical experts in obstetrics and gynecology. All survey instruments and interview guides were specifically developed for this study (Supplementary Files 1–3). Survey instruments underwent pilot testing validation (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.87), while interviews followed a semi-structured format with subsequent thematic analysis of transcripts. The resulting framework encompasses four core competency dimensions: Professional knowledge competency (foundational theoretical knowledge, disease diagnosis and treatment protocols, pharmacotherapeutic principles, and surgical concepts); Clinical skills competency (basic procedural skills, specialist diagnostic techniques, surgical proficiency, and emergency management capabilities); Clinical reasoning competency (case analysis abilities, diagnostic reasoning processes, treatment decision-making capabilities, and risk assessment skills); and Professional Conduct competency (doctor-patient communication skills, interdisciplinary teamwork abilities, ethical decision-making, and commitment to lifelong learning). This framework underwent iterative refinement through three rounds of expert panel review (n = 12) until consensus was achieved (Content validity index = 0.92), thereby comprehensively addressing the core competencies required for contemporary obstetric and gynecologic practice, providing a solid theoretical foundation for the development of the training program.

Innovative “Three-coordination” training model architecture

Based on the established competency framework, we designed an innovative “Three-Coordination” training model to facilitate multi-dimensional development: (1) Coordination between training bases and medical school education: this foundational element established shared educational objectives and content with affiliated medical schools, implemented resource-sharing mechanisms, and created seamless transitions between academic and clinical training environments. (2) Coordination between theoretical learning and clinical practice: this core component established bidirectional mapping between theoretical knowledge and clinical application through a progressively challenging three-stage “theory-virtual-practice” learning pathway. Structured clinical case discussions derived from actual patient encounters facilitated knowledge-to-competency transformation. (3) Coordination between technological empowerment and professional development: this innovative element incorporated AI technology competencies into professional development, implementing algorithm-driven AI-assisted case teaching and practical training scenarios. The AI technologies employed in this study included: (a) machine learning-based adaptive learning algorithms for personalized training pathways; (b) natural language processing (NLP) systems for automated case generation and clinical scenario interactions; (c) computer vision algorithms for objective surgical skill assessment; and (d) clinical decision support systems providing evidence-based recommendations. Large language models were not utilized during the implementation period (2021–2023). This pedagogical approach was operationalized through a digital platform integrating virtual simulation, adaptive learning algorithms, and performance analytics to cultivate residents’ abilities to leverage emerging technologies for clinical problem-solving, adapting to the rapidly evolving landscape of medical technology.

“Four-stage progressive” teaching system

To facilitate sequential competency development, we implemented a “Four-Stage Progressive” educational system aligned with the standard three-year residency training cycle (Fig. 1): (1) Basic adaptation period (1–6 months): This initial phase prioritized mastery of fundamental theories and basic procedural skills in obstetrics and gynecology, supporting clinical acclimatization. Learning was augmented through validated AI-powered virtual case studies and three-dimensional anatomical visualization systems, with competency assessed using standardized knowledge tests and objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs). The virtual case studies (n = 30) were generated using rule-based algorithms and expert-validated scenarios. Residents interacted with cases in a turn-based format, making sequential clinical decisions (history-taking, diagnostic ordering, treatment planning) and receiving automated feedback at each step. (2) Capability enhancement period (7–12 months): This secondary phase focused on developing specialized diagnostic and treatment capabilities alongside preliminary clinical reasoning skills, with residents participating in management of common clinical scenarios reinforced through AI-assisted diagnostic training and virtual reality surgical simulations. Progression was measured through procedural simulation metrics and structured clinical observations. (3) Specialty mastery period (13–24 months): This intermediate phase emphasized advanced specialty-specific skills and development of independent clinical capabilities, with residents engaging in complex case management enhanced through AI-assisted analysis of challenging cases and advanced surgical simulation technologies. Assessment included 360-degree evaluations and clinical decision-making analysis. (4) Comprehensive integration period (25–36 months): This culminating phase consolidated diagnostic and treatment capabilities with interdisciplinary collaboration skills, with residents participating in multidisciplinary clinical activities refined through team-based consultation simulations and patient communication scenarios to prepare them for independent practice. Final evaluation employed comprehensive competency assessments to verify practice readiness.

Intelligent practice platform construction and physician training model

AI-enhanced training platform architecture and implementation

The intelligent practice teaching platform was designed to enhance residents’ clinical competency through three interconnected core modules: (1) Virtual simulation practice platform: implements comprehensive training for 10 common surgical procedures through obstetrics and gynecology VR/AR simulation systems (LapSim® Haptic System, Surgical Science, Sweden). This module was complemented by three-dimensional anatomical visualization tools and a comprehensive virtual case learning environment containing 60 clinically representative cases stratified by complexity. (2) Intelligent skills training Platform: integrates machine learning-based competency assessment algorithms with personalized training program recommendationsderived from individual learning analytics and performance metrics, thereby enabling targeted skill development. (3) Clinical practice intelligent support platform: incorporates advanced clinical decision support systems, patient-physician communication simulation modules, and structured case-based discussion forums derived from authentic clinical scenarios, facilitating the effective transformation of theoretical knowledge into practical clinical capabilities.

Multi-modal clinical competency assessment framework

A multi-dimensional assessment framework was implemented to quantitatively evaluate residents’ clinical capability development throughout the training program : (1) Clinical competency assessment: uses standardized scoring sheets developed by expert teams to assess physicians’ four-dimensional competencies on a 100-point scale, conducted before and after training to track developmental trajectories. These assessment tools underwent rigorous validation (content validity index = 0.89) and reliability testing (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.87) (Supplementary File 4). (2) Clinical skills assessment: focused on five core obstetrics and gynecology procedures (intrauterine device (IUD) removal, vaginal delivery assistance, artificial abortion, manual placental separation, and posterior fornix aspiration) using OSCEs to ensure procedural proficiency met established standards. The OSCEs employed standardized checklists with established inter-rater reliability (kappa = 0.82) (Supplementary File 5). (3) Clinical reasoning assessment: evaluates physicians’ diagnostic capabilities through standardized case analyses, examining history-taking methodology, diagnostic accuracy, and treatment planning appropriateness, with particular emphasis on developing evidence-based clinical decision-making skills. Blinded assessment by two independent evaluators was conducted to minimize potential bias in the evaluation process (Supplementary File 6).

Continuous quality improvement process

The training program emphasizes residents’ learning experiences and training satisfaction through a structured evaluation system. Specialized surveys were administered after one year of training to collect detailed feedback on platform module usage experiences and elicit specific improvement suggestions. Meanwhile, eight clinical instructors each possessing a minimum of five years of teaching experience, were invited to comprehensively evaluate the training model. These expert evaluators provided professional assessment of teaching content design, skills training methodologies, clinical reasoning cultivation approaches, and areas for comprehensive quality improvement. This bidirectional evaluation mechanism ensures continuous platform optimization, maintains tight integration between training content and evolving clinical needs, and ultimately contributes to improved overall healthcare service quality.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD), with pre-post training comparisons conducted using paired t-tests. Categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages. Ordinal data were analyzed using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for paired comparisons. Between-cohort comparisons were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with post-hoc Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Effect sizes for continuous variables were calculated using Cohen’s d. Statistical significance was established at P < 0.05 for all analyses, with all tests performed as two-tailed evaluations.

Results

Clinical competency development outcomes following AI-enhanced training

Four-dimensional competency profile analysis

A comprehensive evaluation of four-dimensional competencies was conducted for obstetrics and gynecology residents (N = 28) at baseline and following 12 months of training with the AI-enhanced intelligent platform. Paired-samples analyses revealed statistically significant improvements across all competency dimensions post-intervention t (Fig. 2A). The results demonstrated substantial enhancement in competency levels across all measured dimensions following the training intervention. Clinical reasoning competency showed the most pronounced improvement, increasing from 68.4 ± 8.1 points pre-training to 84.9 ± 5.7 points post-training, representing a mean difference of 16.5 points (t = 13.57, p < 0.001, 95% CI [14.0–19.0], Cohen’s d = 2.36). This substantial effect size highlights the intelligent platform’s exceptional efficacy in developing physicians’ diagnostic decision-making capabilities. Clinical skills competency demonstrated similarly impressive gains, improving from 72.6 ± 7.3 points to 88.5 ± 4.9 points, with a mean improvement of 15.9 points (t = 12.83, p < 0.001, 95% CI [13.4–18.4], Cohen’s d = 2.56), reflecting the considerable impact of virtual simulation training and AI-assisted assessment methodologies on operational skills development (Table 1).

Cohort-specific analysis of competency improvement

Analysis of competency improvement across training cohorts revealed statistically significant improvements within all three resident groups following 12 months of platform-based training (P < 0.001 for all cohorts), with distinct patterns observed between training years (Fig. 2B). The 2023 cohort (first-year residents) demonstrated the most substantial relative improvement, with competency scores increasing by 22.9% from baseline (mean difference 15.7 points, 95% CI [11.6-19.8], Cohen's d=2.26, t = 6.53, p < 0.001). The 2022 cohort (second-year residents) showed intermediate improvement magnitude of 19.1% (mean difference 13.9 points, 95% CI [10.3-17.5], Cohen's d = 2.49, t = 6.18, p < 0.001). The 2021 cohort (third-year residents) began with the highest baseline scores and achieved the highest absolute post-training scores (88.3 ± 3.9 points), though their relative improvement percentage was comparatively lower at 15.4% (mean difference 11.8 points, 95% CI [8.4-15.2], Cohen's d = 2.52, t = 5.65, p < 0.001) (Table 2).

Clinical skills assessment results

Five core obstetrics and gynecological clinical skills were selected for standardized skills assessment. Analysis revealed statistically significant improvement in performance across all skills competencies following the 12-month training period (P < 0.001 for all procedures; Fig. 2C). The most substantial improvements were observed in procedures typically associated with limited clinical exposure opportunities and higher technical complexity. Vaginal delivery assistance demonstrated the greatest relative improvement (25.7%) with a mean difference of 17.3 points (95% CI [14.7 - 19.9], Cohen's d = 2.50, t = 13.26, p < 0.001). Similarly, manual placental separation, another procedure with restricted clinical practice opportunities, showed marked improvement (24.8%) with a mean increase of 17.0 points (95% CI [14.4 - 19.6], Cohen's d = 2.48, t = 13.14, p < 0.001). Artificial abortion, a procedure of intermediate technical complexity requiring precise execution, also demonstrated substantial improvement (22.9%) with a mean increase of 16.1 points (95% CI [13.5 - 18.7], Cohen's d = 2.39) (t = 12.70, p < 0.001) (Table 3).

Clinical reasoning assessment results

Standardized case analysis assessments were conducted to evaluate residents' clinical reasoning abilities across multiple domains. Results demonstrated statistically significant improvement in all clinical reasoning components following the 12-month training program (P < 0.001 for all domains, Fig. 2D). Data revealed clear differences between basic clinical skills and higher-order clinical reasoning abilities: risk assessment accuracy and comprehensive differential diagnosis, as higher-order clinical reasoning abilities, had the lowest pre-training scores (65.3 ± 8.4 and 67.8 ± 8.6 respectively) and the largest post-training improvement magnitudes (27.4% and 24.8% respectively) with mean score increases of 17.9 points (95% CI [15.7 - 20.1], Cohen's d = 2.40) and 16.8 points (95% CI [14.7 - 18.9], Cohen's d = 2.25), respectively; history-taking completeness, as the most fundamental clinical ability, already had a relatively high pre-training level (78.6 ± 6.2), with limited improvement space (14.8%) resulting in a mean increase of 11.6 points (95% CI [9.9 - 13.3], Cohen's d = 2.17); diagnostic accuracy and treatment plan reasonableness had pre-training levels and improvement magnitudes between these two extremes with mean increases of 15.3 points (95% CI [13.4 - 17.2], Cohen's d = 2.49) and 15.4 points (95% CI [13.5 - 17.3], Cohen's d = 2.28), respectively (Table 4).

Learning experience and satisfaction evaluation

Learning experience evaluation

Resident satisfaction with the AI-enhanced training program was assessed through a structured evaluation instrument administered to all participants (N = 28) (Supplementary File 7). The instrument utilized a five-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 2 = disagree, 3 = neutral, 4 = agree, 5 = strongly agree) to evaluate five key dimensions of the learning experience. The evaluation tool demonstrated robust internal consistency reliability (Cronbach's α = 0.87). Statistical analysis was performed using one-sample t-tests comparing observed means against the theoretical neutral midpoint of the scale (test value = 3). Results indicated statistically significant positive evaluations across all assessment dimensions (p < 0.001 for all items), with large effect sizes (Cohen's d ranging from 1.71 to 3.29) (Fig. 3A). Mean satisfaction scores with 95% confidence intervals were calculated for all dimensions, with virtual simulation training receiving the highest rating (4.71 ± 0.52, 95% CI [4.50 - 4.92]), followed by AI-enhanced learning methods (4.57 ± 0.59, 95% CI [4.34 - 4.80]), progressive training pathway (4.54 ± 0.63, 95% CI [4.29 - 4.79]), intelligent assessment (4.46±0.68, 95% CI [4.19 - 4.73]), and personalized learning recommendations (4.32 ± 0.77, 95% CI [4.02 - 4.62]) (Table 5).

Training satisfaction evaluation

Residents’ satisfaction with specific components of the training program was assessed using a structured evaluation instrument administered to all participants (N = 28). The instrument employed a five-point Likert scale (1 = very dissatisfied, 2 = dissatisfied, 3 = neutral, 4 = satisfied, 5 = very satisfied) across seven satisfaction domains. The instrument demonstrated excellent internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.89) (Supplementary File 8). Statistical analysis was conducted using one-sample t-tests comparing observed means against the theoretical neutral midpoint of the scale (test value = 3). Cohort-specific response analysis: cross-sectional evaluation of educational impact

To assess whether the training model’s effectiveness varied by residency year, we compared evaluation scores across the three resident cohorts (2021, 2022, and 2023) using ANOVA. Results revealed no statistically significant differences in either learning experience evaluations (F = 0.92, p = 0.41) or training satisfaction ratings (F = 0.81, p = 0.46) across cohorts (Fig. 3C). Post-hoc power analysis indicated adequate statistical power (0.82) to detect medium effect sizes (Cohen’s f = 0.25) with our sample size. Mean differences between cohorts were small, with standardized effect sizes (partial η²) of 0.07 for learning experience and 0.06 for satisfaction. Levene’s test confirmed homogeneity of variance across cohorts for both measures (p = 0.61 and p = 0.48, respectively). Bootstrap confidence intervals (95% CI) for mean differences between the 2021 and 2023 cohorts were [-0.14, 0.60] for learning experience and [-0.12, 0.54] for satisfaction, further supporting the lack of statistically significant cohort effects (Table 7). While not statistically significant, a slight trend toward higher ratings among more senior residents (2021 cohort) was observed, with mean differences of 0.23 (95% CI [-0.14, 0.60]) for learning experience and 0.21 (95% CI [-0.12, 0.54]) for satisfaction when compared to the 2023 cohort. Multiple regression analysis controlling for previous technological experience maintained non-significant cohort effects (adjusted p = 0.53).

Discussion

Multi-dimensional effects of “Four-dimensional integrated” clinical competency training

Our findings demonstrate that residents who participated in the AI-enhanced progressive training model exhibited statistically significant improvements across all four dimensions of clinical competency: professional knowledge, clinical skills, clinical reasoning, and professional conduct. The most substantial gains were observed in clinical reasoning (16.5 points) and clinical skills (15.9 points), suggesting particular effectiveness in these domains. These outcomes align with contemporary international medical education paradigms, notably the ACGME obstetrics and gynecology milestones project, which emphasizes the development of advanced clinical reasoning and decision-making capabilities [9]. The magnitude of these observed improvements represents educationally meaningful changes with robust effect sizes, consistent with established benchmarks for impactful educational interventions in postgraduate medical training [28].

Notably, our research achieved significant progress in these critical domains through AI-assisted case analysis and virtual simulation training. It is particularly significant that clinical reasoning competency which had the lowest pre-training baseline score (68.4 ± 8.1 points), demonstrated the most substantial improvement following intervention. This observation is consistent with educational theory suggesting that well-designed interventions may have heightened impact on competencies with greater developmental potential [29], and highlights the capacity of AI-enhanced approaches to target specific learning gaps. This suggests that AI-enhanced educational approaches offer distinct advantages in fostering higher-order clinical reasoning abilities and can effectively address limitations inherent in traditional teaching methods, particularly regarding complex clinical decision-making training.

Our data revealed that higher-order clinical reasoning skills, specifically risk assessment accuracy and comprehensive differential diagnosis formulation, had the lowest initial scores but showed the greatest improvement margins (27.4% and 24.8%, respectively). The notable improvement in these complex cognitive domains suggests that the combination of varied virtual cases and structured formative assessment may be particularly effective for developing advanced clinical reasoning patterns. This phenomenon likely stems from the extensive array of virtual cases and AI-assisted decision-making systems provided by the intelligent platform, affording residents opportunities for iterative practice with immediate feedback. In contrast to conventional teaching methodologies that rely on limited clinical exposures and subjective evaluations, this data-driven, algorithm-based training model more precisely identifies cognitive blind spots and delivers targeted guidance, thereby significantly enhancing complex clinical decision-making capabilities [30]. These findings align with the concept of “AI-augmented medical education” proposed by Wartman et al. [3], validating the beneficial role of artificial intelligence in cultivating higher-order cognitive abilities among medical trainees.

Differentiated training effects for residents of different years

The results showed that residents from all three grades achieved significant improvement after training, but newly admitted 2023 residents showed the largest improvement magnitude (22.9%), while senior 2021 residents, although having the highest pre- and post-training scores, showed a relatively smaller improvement magnitude (15.4%). This differential response pattern demonstrates a consistent relationship between baseline proficiency and intervention impact, suggesting that the training system provides optimal benefit for early-stage trainees while still offering meaningful development for advanced learners. This “enhancing strengths and addressing weaknesses” phenomenon indicates that the AI-enhanced progressive training model has good adaptability for residents with different baseline levels, especially helping newly admitted residents with weaker foundations to rapidly improve their abilities. This may be attributed to our intelligent assessment system’s ability to precisely identify each physician’s capability weaknesses and provide targeted training through personalized learning recommendations, achieving differentiated training.

Notably, post-training differences in scores between grades narrowed, suggesting that the intervention promotes more equitable competency development while maintaining appropriate experiential gradients. This indicaties that while the training model maintains the seniority gradient, it also promotes balanced development of overall ability levels. This result is consistent with the concept of the “Competence by Design (CBD)” project implemented by the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada [7], emphasizing a training model based on competency rather than simple time duration. Our findings support the value of competency-based frameworks that can accelerate progression through developmental stages while maintaining educational quality. Our research further confirms that through AI technology-empowered precise assessment and personalized training, capability gaps can be accelerated, and training efficiency improved, providing important reference value for addressing the issue of varying quality in China’s resident training.

Breakthrough progress in core clinical skills training

Among the five core obstetrics and gynecology procedural skills assessed, vaginal delivery and manual placental separation, procedures characterized by limited clinical practice opportunities and stringent technical requirements, demonstrated the most substantial improvement following intervention (25.7% and 24.8%, respectively). This finding suggests that AI-enhanced virtual simulation training effectively addresses the deficit in authentic clinical practice opportunities, establishing novel pathways for the safe acquisition of high-risk procedural competencies. Conversely, IUD removal and posterior fornix puncture, which represent more commonly encountered basic procedures in clinical settings, exhibited comparatively modest improvement. This differential response further substantiates the distinctive value of virtual simulation training in augmenting limited clinical resources. These observations align with established findings in the medical education literature. A comprehensive meta-analysis by McGaghie et al. demonstrated that medical simulation-based education significantly enhances clinical procedural skills, with particularly pronounced effects for high-risk, low-frequency interventions [28]. Their review of 14 controlled studies revealed that simulation-based medical education with deliberate practice is consistently superior to traditional clinical education in achieving specific clinical skill acquisition goals (effect size 0.71, 95% confidence interval 0.65–0.76; p < 0.001). Similar findings have emerged from research on obstetrics and gynecology simulation training. Nitsche et al. [31] and Dayal et al. [32] reported that simulation-based mastery learning significantly improved resident performance in obstetrical emergencies, with particular efficacy for rarely encountered clinical scenarios.

Practical value of the “Three-coordination” training model

The “Three-Coordination” training model developed in this study, comprising coordination between academic institutions and clinical training environments, integration of theoretical knowledge with practical application, and alignment of technological capabilities with professional development, has demonstrated substantial educational value. These three dimensions represent interconnected yet distinct educational principles that collectively form a comprehensive framework for competency development in medical education. Analysis of learning experience evaluations revealed that “integration of virtual and real learning experiences enhancing educational outcomes” (4.64 ± 0.56) and “virtual simulation training improving skill acquisition efficiency” (4.71 ± 0.52) received the highest ratings, indicating strong resident endorsement of the pedagogical approach coordinating theoretical learning with clinical practice. This educational framework, which systematically integrates knowledge acquisition, virtual simulation, and authentic clinical experiences, effectively addresses the theory-practice gap prevalent in conventional training programs and establishes a structured pathway for knowledge-to-competency transformation. Clinical faculty evaluations of the training model similarly demonstrated strong endorsement, with “effective integration of AI technology with professional education” (4.67 ± 0.49) and “significant enhancement of resident clinical competencies” (4.83 ± 0.39) receiving particularly high ratings. These assessments substantiate the value of the coordination between technological advancement and professional development that underpins our educational approach. Notably, unanimous faculty agreement (100%) regarding the model’s implementation potential indicates that the “Three-Coordination” framework possesses not only localized efficacy but also broader applicability across diverse institutional contexts.

When contextualized within international medical education frameworks, our “Three-Coordination” model incorporates fundamental principles of CBME as described by Frank et al. [33] while leveraging the distinctive capabilities of artificial intelligence technologies. This approach demonstrates conceptual alignment with established educational theories including Kolb’s experiential learning cycle [34], problem-based learning methodologies [35], and situated learning theory [36], providing a strong theoretical foundation for the observed educational outcomes.This integration has produced a novel obstetrics and gynecology residency training paradigm that addresses specific challenges within the Chinese medical education system. As Ten Cate and colleagues [37] have emphasized, educational approaches that bridge theoretical frameworks with practical applications are essential for developing clinical competence. Our model provides valuable insights for addressing persistent challenges in China’s standardized residency training programs, including curriculum-practice misalignment and insufficient individualization of educational experiences. The pedagogical approach described aligns with Issenberg et al.‘s findings that high-fidelity simulations with appropriate feedback mechanisms significantly enhance clinical skill acquisition [38]. Furthermore, Wang et al.‘s research on Chinese residency education confirms the need for innovative solutions to address the theory-practice gap in medical training [39]. Our work extends these findings by demonstrating how AI-enhanced educational technologies can facilitate this integration while providing personalized learning pathways.

Scientific effectiveness of the “Four-stage progressive” teaching system

The “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system implemented in this study, comprising basic adaptation, capability enhancement, specialty mastery, and comprehensive integration periods, was designed with careful consideration of residents’ developmental trajectory and the unique characteristics of obstetrics and gynecology practice. This approach received substantial endorsement from both learners and educators. Satisfaction assessments revealed that 92.9% of residents affirmed that the “progressive training pathway aligned with capability development patterns,” while 91.7% of clinical instructors confirmed the “progressive training pathway demonstrates scientific effectiveness.” These findings suggest that the instructional framework is methodologically sound and congruent with the intrinsic patterns of physician professional development.

Statistical analysis revealed no significant differences in evaluations of the training model among residents across different training years, indicating that the “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system demonstrates robust adaptability across various developmental stages, effectively addressing the educational requirements of different training phases. This characteristic aligns with the Dreyfus skill acquisition model [40], which conceptualizes professional competency development as a sequential progression from “novice → advanced beginner → competent → proficient → expert.” The alignment between our staged approach and established developmental frameworks supports the validity of the progressive system as a structured pathway for professional growth. Our “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system provides structured support for this developmental continuum by implementing stage-appropriate educational content and pedagogical approaches tailored to each developmental milestone.

In contrast to conventional training paradigms organized around departmental rotations, the “Four-Stage Progressive” teaching system places greater emphasis on systematic capability development and integration, mitigating issues of knowledge fragmentation and competency discontinuity. This approach aligns with Harden’s concept of a spiral curriculum [41], which advocates for revisiting topics with increasing complexity as learners progress. The integration of spiral curriculum principles into our framework enables reinforcement of core concepts while progressively increasing the sophistication of clinical challenges, supporting the development of advanced problem-solving abilities. Particularly noteworthy is the emphasis on multidisciplinary collaborative practice and team-based competencies during the comprehensive integration period, which resonates with contemporary healthcare delivery trends. As Weiss and colleagues have documented, integration of multidisciplinary training experiences significantly enhances residents’ preparedness for independent clinical practice [42]. Our curriculum establishes a robust foundation for residents’ transition to autonomous practice by systematically incorporating these essential elements. The progressive nature of our training system also corresponds with the developmental framework proposed by ten Cate regarding Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs) [43], wherein learners progress from observation to execution with distant supervision, then to execution with supervision available, and ultimately to independent practice.

New approaches for AI-enhanced medical education

Our findings demonstrate that AI-enhanced medical education models offer significant advantages in improving learning efficiency, enriching educational experiences, and facilitating personalized training. The integration of artificial intelligence into the educational framework appears to support several key educational processes, including adaptive content delivery, formative assessment, and learning pathway optimization. Resident evaluations revealed high ratings for “AI-enhanced learning methods increased learning interest” (4.57 ± 0.59) and “intelligent assessment provided valuable feedback” (4.46 ± 0.68), indicating that AI technology implementation significantly enhanced learning motivation and feedback quality. Notably, while “personalized learning recommendations met learning needs” (4.32 ± 0.77) received a comparatively lower score, it remained significantly above the neutral evaluation threshold. This suggests that AI-driven personalized learning pathways have achieved preliminary success but retain substantial potential for optimization.

The AI technology implementation in this study extends beyond isolated tools to establish a comprehensive ecosystem encompassing virtual simulation platforms, intelligent skills training systems, and clinical practice support platforms. This integrated approach provides continuous support from foundational knowledge acquisition to advanced clinical reasoning development. This comprehensive integration creates an interconnected learning environment that facilitates seamless transitions between different educational modalities and supports transfer of learning across contexts. Our systematic implementation aligns with contemporary technological trends in medical education, such as the “AI-enhanced medical education ecosystem” conceptualized by Wartman [3], who emphasized the transformative potential of AI across the medical education continuum.

Our research further validates that deeply integrating AI technology throughout professional education effectively transcends resource limitations and methodological constraints inherent in conventional teaching approaches. As Masters observed in a systematic review of technology-enhanced medical education, AI applications demonstrate particular efficacy in addressing scalability challenges and providing consistent, high-quality learning experiences [2]. The efficient utilization of educational resources through AI-enhanced approaches may represent an important advantage in resource-constrained educational environments. This integration creates novel pathways for medical education reform that maintain educational quality while improving access and personalization. Significantly, our approach emphasizes “AI-enhancement” rather than “AI-replacement,” preserving the essential role of clinical educators in medical training. This philosophy aligns with Topol’s [44] concept of “AI-mediated medical education maintaining humanistic care,” which advocates for technology as an augmentation of human teaching rather than a substitute. Wartman and Combs similarly highlighted the importance of maintaining the human element in medical education while leveraging technological advantages [3].

Our findings demonstrate that this balanced approach yields optimal educational outcomes while preserving the critical human connections essential to medical training. However, clinical instructor evaluations revealed a relatively lower score for “teaching implementation process is smooth” (4.25 ± 0.75), indicating persistent challenges in integrating AI technology with traditional pedagogical approaches. Implementation challenges identified in faculty feedback suggest that continued refinement of technological interfaces and faculty development initiatives will be essential for optimizing the educational impact of AI-enhanced systems. These challenges necessitate further optimization of technological interfaces, enhanced user experience design, comprehensive faculty development initiatives, and strengthened institutional support systems. As Finkelstein et al. [45] observed, successful technological integration in medical education requires attention to both technological functionality and organizational change management.

Research limitations and future development directions

Several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings of this study. First, the relatively modest sample size (N = 28 residents) constrains the generalizability of our results. Future investigations should incorporate larger cohorts and implement multi-institutional designs to validate the universality of these findings across diverse training environments and educational contexts. Second, the one-year research timeframe provided insufficient opportunity to comprehensively track residents’ developmental trajectories throughout the complete three-year standardized training curriculum. The limited observational period constrains our understanding of long-term competency development patterns and skill retention. Longitudinal studies spanning the entire residency period would yield more definitive evidence regarding sustained competency development and skill retention. Additionally, although the assessment tools were validated by experts, they still need further improvement in standardization and validity. Ongoing refinement of assessment instruments will be important for enhancing the precision and reliability of competency evaluation.

Future research initiatives could productively explore several promising directions. First, investigating optimal integration models that balance AI-enhanced approaches with traditional pedagogical methodologies would help identify the most effective hybridized educational strategies. This includes determining appropriate technology implementation thresholds and identifying which educational components benefit most from technological enhancement versus traditional instruction. Second, developing more sophisticated competency assessment frameworks with enhanced precision and discrimination capabilities would improve the targeting and effectiveness of personalized educational interventions. Such instruments should incorporate both quantitative performance metrics and qualitative indicators of clinical reasoning and decision-making processes.Third, extending the application of AI-enhanced educational models beyond obstetrics and gynecology to diverse clinical specialties would facilitate the development of generalizable principles for medical education reform. Cross-specialty comparative analyses could identify both universal elements and discipline-specific adaptations required for optimal implementation. Finally, exploring new paradigms for physician competency development that specifically address the evolving technological landscape would help prepare trainees for practice in the era of intelligent healthcare. This includes investigating how traditional clinical competencies intersect with emerging technological literacies and exploring frameworks for human-AI collaborative practice in clinical environments.

These future research directions would collectively advance our understanding of how technological enhancement can most effectively support the development of clinical competencies while addressing the persistent challenges of medical education in increasingly complex healthcare systems.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that the AI-enhanced progressive training model developed for obstetrics and gynecology residents effectively promotes comprehensive clinical competency development. By integrating the “three-coordination” training framework with a structured “four-stage progressive” educational pathway, the model supports systematic skill acquisition throughout residency training. Significant improvements were observed across all evaluated competency domains, with particularly pronounced gains in clinical reasoning and high-complexity procedural performance. Furthermore, the model exhibited strong adaptability to residents with varying baseline competency levels, enabling individualized learning trajectories while maintaining balanced development across multiple clinical dimensions. These findings suggest that the strategic application of artificial intelligence can address several limitations of traditional residency training, including restricted educational resources and insufficient instructional personalization.

Within the context of Chinese medical education, this research provides both conceptual innovation and empirical evidence for improving the quality of obstetrics and gynecology residency training. The feasibility and scalability demonstrated by this AI-enhanced educational model indicate its potential for broader implementation across clinical training environments. Beyond obstetrics and gynecology, the proposed framework offers a transferable reference for the integration of artificial intelligence into medical education across diverse specialties. As healthcare systems continue to evolve in response to rapid technological advancement, the development and validation of effective AI-supported training models will be essential for preparing clinicians to meet the demands of modern, technology-mediated clinical practice.

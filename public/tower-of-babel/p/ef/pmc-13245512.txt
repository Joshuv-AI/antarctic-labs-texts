A global commitment to addressing chronic liver disease
Authors: Vanessa Garcia-Larsen, Kremlin Wickramasinghe, Anselm J. Hennis
Published: 2026

In their latest Series, The Lancet Regional Health – Europe provides a comprehensive overview of the public health challenges, policy readiness, diagnostic innovations, and care models related to metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), alcohol-related liver disease (ALD), and viral hepatitis in Europe.1, 2, 3, 4 Noting the public health burden of these preventable, chronic liver diseases, we find it timely to highlight the risk factors for MASLD and opportunities for prevention.

It is estimated that a third of the adult population worldwide is affected by MASLD,5 and Latin America is the subregion with the highest reported prevalence to date, at 44%.6 Among subpopulations of individuals with obesity or diabetes type 2, the leading indicator conditions for MASLD, the prevalence increases to over 60%.5 The policy readiness review on MASLD/MASH by Lazarus et al. notes the policy gaps, and the scarce attention that the disease, and its advanced form, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), receive across Europe, with only two countries in this region having public health strategies to tackle them.2 Unfortunately, this is not an isolated example. In Latin America, as in Europe and other parts of the world where overweight and obesity are highly prevalent, national public health policies to address MASLD/MASH are insufficient, and integration of the disease in Primary Health Care (PHC) sector is lacking. Civil society engagement led by patient groups, as well as interest from international scientific societies, is highlighting the importance of MASLD and MASH. The latest European clinical practice guidelines from 20247 jointly developed by the European Association for the Study of the Liver, the European Association for the Study of Diabetes, and the European Association for the Study of Obesity) provide recommendations on prevention, diagnosis and treatment of MASLD. In the Region of the Americas, the updated 2025/2026 Latin American Association for the Study of the Liver (ALEH) guidelines for MASLD8 recommend patient risk stratification and weight loss, through improved diet quality.

Unhealthy diets, physical inactivity, and alcohol consumption remain major determinants of declining health and mortality associated with noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) in adults. These factors are also associated with MASLD, and with alcohol-related liver disease (ALD), another leading cause of preventable morbidity and mortality in Europe. As part of this Series, Pose and colleagues highlight evidence that supports population-level alcohol policies to reduce consumption.3 Regulatory policies promoting sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) and alcohol taxes are effective approaches proven to reduce consumption.9 However, industry interference and latency in policy implementation are strong barriers to progress.

Unsurprisingly, targeted lifestyle interventions are some of the best public health strategies for the primary and secondary prevention of MASLD. These include weight loss, promoting Mediterranean style diets, which includes restricting the consumption of ultra-processed food, increasing physical activity, and reducing alcohol intake.7 In the wake of pharmacological treatments for obesity reduction, glucagon-like-peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist medications are also proving effective in attenuating or reversing MASH,10 and their combination with lifestyle interventions that include Mediterranean diet and exercise can accelerate weight loss and improve markers of MASLD.11

In 2025, the United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) formally recognized the need to tackle ‘NAFLD’ (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, the former name of MASLD) through strengthening screening and management.12 A common feature across all four studies in this Series is the need for integration of policy strategies that address prevention of chronic liver disease; given the multiple links to other metabolic disturbances, and the lifestyle-related risk factors that modulate MASLD risk. PHC settings could serve as the ideal location for comprehensive care as they have the potential to offer integrated lifestyle interventions, including targeted educational initiatives guided by patient risk stratification. While increasing efforts are being made to harmonise clinical care of MASLD, there is also a need to implement standardised MASLD guidelines in PHC to improve timely detection and management. As with other NCDs, prevention and early diagnosis of metabolic dysfunction and alcohol-related liver disease is likely to yield the greatest individual and societal benefits. UNGA's acknowledgment reinforces the need to amplify the World Health Organization (WHO) efforts to promote the Best Buys,13 cost-effective interventions to address tobacco consumption, harmful alcohol use, unhealthy diets and low physical exercise.

Curbing the silently rising trends in MASLD, MedALD and ALD, all of the dynamic spectrum of steatotic liver disease, requires long-term strategies focused on prevention, and early diagnosis with an equitable lens. Advances on clinical diagnosis and treatment are becoming increasingly available in Europe; however, this is not a reality for many low- and middle-income countries where the prevalence of obesity, diabetes type II, and excessive alcohol consumption are widespread, especially in populations living in vulnerable situations. The recognition of MASLD and ALD as public health challenges by international multilateral organizations creates a timely opportunity to integrate their prevention at the PHC level. Multisectoral collaboration that supports data collection, standardisation of screening strategies, regionally specific educational and lifestyle counselling initiatives, and timely culturally sensitive lifestyle interventions could effectively contribute to making MASLD and ALD more visible, more preventable, more treatable, and ultimately, save lives.

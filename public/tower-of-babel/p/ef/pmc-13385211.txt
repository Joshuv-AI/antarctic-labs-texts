A Homozygous Founder ELAC2 Variant in Kuwaiti Infants With Fatal Cardiomyopathy and Refractory Severe Lactic Acidosis: A Retrospective Review of the Clinical, Cardiological and Molecular Findings
Authors: Hind Alsharhan, Worood Alostad, Alshaymaa A. Ali, Mohammad A. Ebrahim, Hesham A. F. Mohammed, Ahmad Alahmad, Hashem Alhashemi, Walid M. Hassan, Ahmad E. M. S. Alhaj, Rasha M. Alsafi, Buthaina Albash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundInfantile‐onset cardiomyopathy due to mitochondrial dysfunction is a severe condition frequently associated with poor prognosis. Biallelic pathogenic variants in ELAC2, an essential mitochondrial tRNA processing gene, have been implicated in this phenotype. This study investigates the clinical and genetic spectrum of ELAC2‐related disease in a national cohort from Kuwait.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study using data from the Kuwait Medical Genetics Center registry, including individuals with genetically confirmed or clinically suspected ELAC2‐related cardiomyopathy. Clinical, metabolic, and molecular data were reviewed. Exome sequencing or targeted mutation testing was performed in affected individuals and at‐risk family members.ResultsA total of 34 individuals from 23 consanguineous families were identified, of whom 30 were genetically confirmed to harbor the homozygous ELAC2 founder variant c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu). All individuals presented in infancy with severe cardiomyopathy and refractory lactic acidosis. Neurological involvement was observed in 39% of cases. The majority exhibited hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, with variable dilated features and pericardial effusion. The disease course was fatal in all, with most patients dying in infancy.ConclusionThis is the largest single‐country cohort reported to date with ELAC2‐related mitochondrial cardiomyopathy, raising the global case total to over 70. The uniform presence of the Phe154Leu variant across unrelated Bedouin families highlights a strong founder effect. Given the rapid disease progression and high mortality, we recommend targeted ELAC2 screening in infants with idiopathic cardiomyopathy and persistent lactic acidosis, particularly in consanguineous populations. Premarital carrier testing and early family counseling should be prioritized to support preventive strategies.

Introduction

Infantile cardiomyopathy is a rare but life‐threatening condition, with an estimated incidence of approximately 8.34 cases per 100,000 children during the first year of life (Konta et al. 2013). While various acquired and genetic etiologies have been described, many cases result from inherited mutations, often following an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern (Children's Cardiomyopathy Foundation 2025). Among these, biallelic pathogenic variants in the ELAC2 have been increasingly recognized as a cause of mitochondrial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy associated with oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) deficiency, linked to combined oxidative phosphorylation deficiency 17 (MIM#615440) (Haack et al. 2013). The critical role of ELAC2 in mitochondrial RNA metabolism was first established by Haack et al. (2013), who identified homozygous and compound heterozygous ELAC2 variants in individuals presenting with severe early‐onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, refractory lactic acidosis, and respiratory chain dysfunction (Haack et al. 2013). One of the families described in their study carried the Arabian founder variant, ELAC2 (NM_018127.7):c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu) (Haack et al. 2013). ELAC2 encodes a mitochondrial RNase Z enzyme responsible for 3′‐end processing of mitochondrial tRNAs. Loss of this function impairs mitochondrial protein synthesis, leading to respiratory chain deficiencies and energy failure that predominantly affects highly energy‐dependent organs such as the heart and central nervous system (Haack et al. 2013; Cafournet et al. 2023; Saoura et al. 2019). Since the initial discovery, approximately 60 individuals with ELAC2‐related mitochondrial disease have been reported worldwide, all carrying biallelic pathogenic variants in the gene (Haack et al. 2013; Cafournet et al. 2023; Saoura et al. 2019; Santorelli et al. 2002; Taylor et al. 2014; Akawi et al. 2016; Paucar et al. 2018; Parikh et al. 2016; Shinwari et al. 2017). Affected individuals typically present in the neonatal or early infantile period with severe hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, refractory lactic acidosis, failure to thrive, hypotonia, and clinical deterioration often culminating in cardiogenic shock and multiorgan failure (Saoura et al. 2019; Shinwari et al. 2017). Echocardiographic findings frequently reveal hypertrophic and/or dilated cardiomyopathy, sometimes accompanied by pericardial effusion (Cafournet et al. 2023). Despite intensive supportive care, most reported individuals have experienced early death within the first year of life (Saoura et al. 2019; Shinwari et al. 2017).

In this study, we report 34 affected individuals from 23 consanguineous Kuwaiti families, all homozygous for the previously reported ELAC2 c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu) variant. This variant was initially identified in individuals from the Arabian Penninsula and has subsequently been reported in additional families from the region (Haack et al. 2013; Shinwari et al. 2017).

Materials and Methods

Aim, Design and Setting of the Study

A retrospective cohort study was performed to evaluate all individuals genetically diagnosed with ELAC2‐related mitochondrial disease in Kuwait. Clinical and genetic data were obtained from the registry of the Kuwait Medical Genetic Center (KMGC) and affiliated hospitals. Data collection spanned from 2000 to 2025 and included individuals with suspected or confirmed ELAC2 pathogenic variants. Inclusion criteria encompassed individuals with confirmed biallelic variants in the ELAC2 gene or strong clinical suspicion based on mitochondrial cardiomyopathy and lactic acidosis. Demographic and clinical variables retrieved included sex, nationality, consanguinity status, age at symptom onset and death, cardiac phenotype, neurological manifestations, and cardiac and neuroimaging findings. Data completeness varied depending on the availability of clinical documentation. Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the ethical committee at the Kuwait Ministry of Health, and informed consent was obtained from all participating families. Data was stored and processed securely to maintain patient confidentiality. The study was conducted according to ethical principles of declaration of Helsinki. All data were anonymized, and patient confidentiality was strictly maintained.

Participants Characteristics

Thirty‐four individuals diagnosed with ELAC2‐related cardiomyopathy were included in this study, of whom 30 were genetically confirmed to harbor deleterious biallelic variants in ELAC2. The remaining four were highly suspected based on clinical presentation and the identification of both parents as carriers. All 34 cases showed clinical characteristics of the disease prior to confirmation via genetic testing (in 30 cases) (Table 1).

Clinical, Molecular and Neuroimaging Findings

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood using the Maxwell RSC Whole blood DNA Extraction kit (Promega). PCR amplification was performed using the AmpliTaq Gold 360 Polymerase (ThermoFisher Scientific) with the following primers: Forward primer: 5′‐AGCAAGGCCTCCATCTCTTT‐3′; Reverse primer: 5′‐CGTGGTGATGAAGCATTTGA‐3′.

Targeted Sanger sequencing was performed using BigDye Terminator v3.1 (Applied Biosystems, ThermoFisher Scientific) on a 3500xL Genetic Analyzer System (Applied Biosystems). Variant interpretation, including the assessment of pathogenicity, was carried out in accordance with the guidelines established by the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG). The c.460T>C p.(Phe154Leu) variant is a pathogenic variant that has been previously reported in patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (Saoura et al. 2019; PMID: 31045291) and has been observed in only one heterozygous individual in the gnomAD database (v4.1.1).

Results

Clinical and Genetic Findings

A total of 34 individuals from 23 different Arabian families were identified with either genetic confirmation or clinical suspicion of ELAC2‐related mitochondrial disease (Figure 1). Among them, 30 individuals were genetically confirmed to be homozygous for the ELAC2 variant (NM_018127.7):c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu), a known Arabian founder mutation. The remaining four individuals were clinically suspected based on familial linkage; however, they were not genetically tested. Both parents were confirmed carriers of the founder mutation in ELAC2. All cases resulted from consanguineous unions. The majority were Kuwaiti nationals, with one individual of Saudi origin. The cohort included 22 males and 12 females. All individuals presented during infancy with cardiomyopathy, often accompanied by features of mitochondrial dysfunction. Neurological symptoms, including seizures, encephalopathy, and hypotonia, were observed in approximately 30% of genetically confirmed individuals. Poor weight gain was also a frequent finding.

All parents are heterozygous carriers, and affected individuals are homozygous for the ELAC2 founder variant (NM_018127.7):C.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu), consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

Cardiac and Metabolic Presentation

All individuals exhibited early‐onset cardiomyopathy, with symptoms appearing between the second day of life and 12 months of age (median: 2 months). Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) was the most prevalent phenotype, documented in 26 out of 34 cases (76%). Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) was observed in five individuals, either in isolation or in combination with HCM. Six cases showed overlapping features of both HCM and DCM. Pericardial effusion was documented in six individuals (18%), with cardiac tamponade developing in half of these cases. One individual (Case 14) required pericardiocentesis for acute management. All tested individuals had refractory lactic acidosis.

The clinical course was uniformly severe, with 33 of 34 individuals dying during infancy at ages ranging from 15 days to 19 months (median: 3 months), most commonly due to cardiogenic shock, often complicated by severe lactic acidosis and multi‐organ failure. Patient 8 represented a clear outlier, surviving until 11 years of age, whereas only one other individual survived beyond infancy, reaching 19 months of age.

Echocardiography Findings

All individuals underwent echocardiographic evaluation. HCM was the primary finding in 26 individuals (26/34 = 76%). One case presented with isolated dilated cardiomyopathy, while seven showed progression to mixed phenotypes. Structural abnormalities included left ventricular hypertrophy and hypertrophic non‐obstructive cardiomyopathy. Pericardial effusion was noted in six individuals (6/34 = 18%) with cardiac tamponade occurring as a complication in half of these cases (Table 1). At least one individual (Case 14) required pericardiocentesis for management (Table 1). Neuroradiological Findings

Abnormal neurological findings were documented in 9 out of 23 individuals (39%). Among these, encephalopathy and seizures were each observed in four individuals. Global developmental delay was noted in three cases. One individual exhibited bilateral sensorineural hearing loss with associated aphasia and subsequently required cochlear implantation. Additionally, a single case was diagnosed with an aneurysm of the vein of Galen (see Table 1).

Echocardiogram of Case 14: 2D echocardiography image with color Doppler compare of parasternal long axis view showing: Hypertrophied left ventricular wall, small LV cavity with pericardial effusion (arrows).

Molecular Findings

Of the 30 genetically confirmed individuals, all harbored the homozygous missense variant in ELAC2(NM_018127.7):c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu), previously reported as an Arabian founder mutation. The variant is classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG guidelines. Segregation analysis confirmed autosomal recessive inheritance in all families, with both parents of affected individuals shown to be heterozygous asymptomatic carriers.

Discussion

This study reports the largest national series of ELAC2‐related mitochondrial cardiomyopathy to date, including 30 genetically confirmed cases and four clinically suspected individuals, deemed affected based on clinical features and parental carrier status, from 23 families in Kuwait. One of the cases (Case 4) was previously reported by Saoura et al. (2019). This brings the global number of ELAC2‐related cases to at least 90 since the gene's implication in mitochondrial disease in 2013 (Haack et al. 2013). Importantly, our study addresses a key gap in the literature, namely the lack of large, population‐based cohorts that enable robust genotype–phenotype correlations in ELAC2‐related disease.

All genetically confirmed individuals in our series harbored the same homozygous missense variant, ELAC2 c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu), a well‐established Arabian founder mutation (Haack et al. 2013; Shinwari et al. 2017; Saoura et al. 2019). The consistent identification of this variant across multiple unrelated families from Bedouin ancestry strongly supports a founder effect and highlights the role of population structure and consanguinity in shaping the genetic epidemiology of this disorder in the Arbian Peninsula. All individuals in this cohort were born to consanguineous unions and exhibited a notably uniform and severe clinical phenotype, characterized by early‐onset cardiomyopathy, profound refractory lactic acidosis, and death in infancy. The sole exception was Case 8, who survived to 11 years of age and presented with a complex clinical picture, including profound global developmental delay, epilepsy, bilateral sensorineural hearing loss (requiring cochlear implantation), cleft lip, and congenital hip dysplasia. Unfortunately, comprehensive genetic testing was not performed in this case, limiting our ability to assess whether additional variants or modifying genetic factors may have contributed to the atypically prolonged survival and multisystem involvement. The identification of such modifiers represents an important direction for future research and may help explain the phenotypic variability observed across ELAC2‐related disorders.

Our findings are broadly consistent with previous reports from Arab populations, where the same founder variant has been associated with severe infantile cardiomyopathy and high early mortality (Saoura et al. 2019; Shinwari et al. 2017). Neurological manifestations—including developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, and encephalopathy—were also observed in a subset of our cohort, in line with prior reports of individuals harboring the p.Phe154Leu variant (Table 1). However, assessment of neurodevelopmental outcomes was limited in several cases due to early mortality.

Neurological involvement has also been described in association with other ELAC2 variants, often with differing clinical severity. For example, Akawi et al. (2016) reported three individuals with a homozygous splice‐site variant (c.1423+2T>A) presenting primarily with global developmental delay and minimal cardiac involvement, with survival beyond infancy (Akawi et al. 2016). Similarly, other variants—such as compound heterozygous mutations reported in a German family—have been associated with combined cardiac and neurological phenotypes but with variable survival (Haack et al. 2013).

Additional mutations reported in Malian, Indian, Korean, and Pakistani populations further illustrate the broad phenotypic variability of ELAC2‐related disorders (Haack et al. 2013; Cafournet et al. 2023; Akawi et al. 2016; Kim et al. 2017). This heterogeneity has been emphasized by Saoura et al. (2019), who described a clinical spectrum ranging from fatal infantile cardiomyopathy to milder neurodevelopmental presentations, even among individuals carrying the same mutation (Saoura et al. 2019).

In contrast, our cohort demonstrates a strikingly homogeneous and severe presentation, supporting the presence of variant‐specific effects and further strengthening evidence for genotype–phenotype correlations. In particular, the c.460T>C (p.Phe154Leu) founder variant appears consistently associated with an early‐onset and severe cardiac phenotype.

Beyond its established role in mitochondrial tRNA processing, ELAC2 dysfunction may also intersect with oncogenic pathways although current evidence remains limited. Saoura et al. (2019) described a rare missense variant, p.Arg781His, which was associated with prostate cancer and shown to impair mitochondrial RNase Z activity. While this observation raises the possibility that defective mitochondrial RNA metabolism may play a role in tumorigenesis, this association remains speculative and has not yet been independently validated. Further studies are needed to clarify the potential role of ELAC2 in cancer biology beyond its established association with mitochondrial disease.

Our findings reinforce that the ELAC2 c.460T>C; p.(Phe154Leu) variant is highly penetrant. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy was the predominant phenotype (observed in 76% of cases), although some individuals demonstrated dilated or mixed cardiomyopathy (Table 1). Pericardial effusion was observed in 18% of cases and was complicated by cardiac tamponade in half of those individuals, with one patient (Case 14) requiring urgent pericardiocentesis. These findings further emphasize the life‐threatening course of ELAC2‐associated cardiomyopathy and the need for early recognition and intervention.

The prognosis for ELAC2‐related disease remained poor, with all individuals in our cohort dying in infancy or early childhood due to cardiogenic shock and multiorgan failure, often exacerbated by severe metabolic acidosis. These observations are consistent with previous reports from Saudi Arabia and North Africa, where the same founder variant (Saoura et al. 2019; Shinwari et al. 2017).

From a public health standpoint, our findings highlight the importance of early genetic diagnosis and preventive strategies in high‐risk populations. In regions with a high rate of consanguinity, targeted ELAC2 testing, premarital carrier screening, family cascade testing, and early neonatal genetic evaluation may help reduce disease burden and recurrence risk through informed reproductive counselling.

Future studies should focus on elucidating the molecular consequences of specific ELAC2 variants and identifying genetic or environmental modifiers that influence disease severity. A deeper understanding of these relationships may ultimately pave the way for precision diagnostics and, potentially, therapeutic interventions.

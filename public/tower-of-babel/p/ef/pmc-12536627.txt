Complete mitochondrial genome of Mycena pura (Mycenaceae, Agaricales)
Authors: Ji-Lian Na, Yong-Jie Zhang, Shu Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Mycenaceae family comprises one of the most taxonomically diverse and ecologically important groups of saprotrophic fungi, yet no mitochondrial genome (mitogenome) has been reported to date. This study reports the first mitogenome of Mycenaceae, sequenced from Mycena pura (Pers.) P. Kumm. 1871. The 107,684 bp circular mitogenome (GC 27.83%) contains 14 conserved protein-coding genes (PCGs), 25 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs, and 11 non-conserved open reading frames (ORFs), with 18 introns (mostly harboring ORFs) identified in seven genes. Phylogenetic analysis based on mitochondrial amino acid sequences validated its Mycenaceae placement, providing crucial genomic resources for future evolutionary studies.

Introduction

The genus Mycena represents one of the most taxonomically diverse and ecologically significant groups of saprotrophic fungi (Wei et al. 2024), with current records showing 1,382 formally described species according to statistics in the Fungal Names database (as of July, 2025; https://nmdc.cn/fungalnames/). Characterized by their small, delicate fruiting bodies, these cosmopolitan fungi predominantly inhabit decaying plant substrates including coniferous litter, angiosperm wood, and various woody debris (Mao 2000). Beyond their fundamental role in carbon cycling and humus decomposition (Frankland 1998), Mycena species engage in complex ecological interactions, demonstrating both saprotrophic dominance and biotrophic capabilities—exemplified by their ability to promote seed germination in the medicinal orchid Gastrodia elata (Lee et al. 2017). The genus also exhibits remarkable functional diversity, with certain species producing antibiotic compounds of medicinal interest (Jaeger and Spiteller 2010), while others display bioluminescent properties that have captivated both scientific and public attention (Chew et al. 2014; Lu et al. 2024).

The taxonomic classification of Mycena has undergone significant revision with advancements in molecular systematics. Historically, this genus was placed within the family Tricholomataceae (Kirk et al. 2001), but robust phylogenetic evidence now firmly supports its reclassification under the family Mycenaceae (Matheny et al. 2006). Among the well-documented Mycena species, Mycena pura (Pers.) P. Kumm. 1871 is particularly noteworthy for both its historical relevance and toxicological significance. This widespread species exhibits a cosmopolitan distribution, having been reported across diverse biogeographic regions including North America, South America, Europe, and Asia (CortésPérez et al. 2023; Desjardin et al. 2007; Harder et al. 2010; Liu et al. 2022). While originally considered edible in many field guides, it is now recognized as a causative agent of gastroenteritis-type mushroom poisoning (Mao 2006).

Mitochondrial genomes (mitogenomes) offer a powerful tool for evolutionary studies due to their compact structure, conserved gene content, and high phylogenetic resolution (Song et al. 2020). Despite their utility, mitogenomic data for Mycenaceae have remained strikingly scarce, hindering in-depth investigations into the evolutionary relationships and diversification patterns within this ecologically important fungal lineage. In this study, we sequenced and annotated the complete mitogenome of M. pura and performed a comprehensive phylogenetic analysis to resolve its evolutionary position within Agaricales. Our findings establish a foundational genomic resource that will accelerate future research on fungal evolution, taxonomy, and comparative mitogenomics.

Materials and methods

The M. pura specimen analyzed in this study was collected from Mount Guandi (37°43′39′′ N, 111°30′29′′ E), Jiaocheng, Shanxi, China (Figure 1). A voucher specimen was deposited in the Herbarium of Shanxi University (voucher number: SX19-4; contact: Prof. Yong-Jie Zhang, zhangyj2008@sxu.edu.cn). The fruiting body has a cap that is initially hemispherical to parabolic when young, becoming more flattened with age, displaying distinct water-soaked striations at the margin. The gills are white, adnate to subadnate (slightly decurrent), with noticeable cross-veins (anastomoses). The pleurocystidia (gill-edge cystidia) are clavate (club-shaped), and the basidiospores are colorless. Combining these distinct morphological characteristics and a molecular phylogenetic analysis based on three nuclear DNA markers (nrDNA ITS, rpb2, and tef1α; Supplementary Figure S1), we conclusively identified the specimen as Mycena pura.

Fruiting bodies of the Mycena pura specimen used in this study. The fruiting body is shown in lateral (A) and basal (B) views. It was collected and photographed by Yong-Jie Zhang at Mount Guandi located in Jiaocheng City, Shanxi Province, China. The basidiocarp is characterized by a prominently hygrophanous, purplish-red pileus (20–40 mm diameter) with pronounced water-soaked striations at the margin, white adnate to slightly decurrent lamellae exhibiting conspicuous anastomosing cross-veins, and a fragile, translucent stipe of uniform coloration. Mycena pura exhibits close morphological similarity to M. rosea; however, the latter is readily differentiated by larger basidiomata with notably pale pink pigmentation on both the pileus and stipe, as well as a stouter stipe.

Genomic DNA was extracted from the fungal specimen using the standardized cetyl trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB) method (Zhang et al. 2010). Whole genome sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (PE150) by Novogene Co., Ltd (Tianjin, China). For mitogenome reconstruction, we employed a dual-assembly strategy utilizing both GetOrganelle v1.7.5 (Jin et al. 2020) and NOVOPlasty v4.3.1 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017). The assembled mitogenome was initially annotated using MFannot (Lang et al. 2023), followed by necessary manual corrections to ensure accuracy (Zhang et al. 2017). A circular map of the mitogenome was created using OGDRAW v1.3.1 (Greiner et al. 2019).

To clarify the phylogenetic position of M. pura within the order Agaricales, we conducted a phylogenetic analysis using amino acid sequences of 14 conserved mitochondrial protein-coding genes (atp6,8,9; cob; cox1-3; nad1-6, 4 L). Our dataset comprised 33 representative species spanning 17 families and nine suborders of Agaricales as the ingroup and two species from Boletales (Tylopilus plumbeoviolaceoides and Strobilomyces alpinus) as the outgroup. The selection of Boletales as the outgroup is justified based on its established taxonomic affinity with Agaricales in Agaricomycetes (Basidiomycota) and appropriate phylogenetic distance. Sequence alignments were performed for each gene independently using MUSCLE algorithm implemented in MEGA v10.2.6 (Kumar et al. 2018), followed by trimming of ambiguous sites with TrimAl v1.4 (Salvador et al. 2009) using the automated1 criterion. The processed alignments were then concatenated into a supermatrix totaling 4,116 amino acid positions using TOPALi v2.5 (Milne et al. 2009). Best partitioning schemes and substitution models were determined using PartitionFinder v2.1.1 (Lanfear et al. 2017), resulting in nine optimal partitions (Supplementary Table S1). Phylogenetic relationships were reconstructed using both maximum likelihood (ML) and Bayesian inference (BI) approaches. ML analysis was conducted with IQ-TREE 1.6.12 (Nguyen et al. 2015) employing 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. Bayesian analysis was performed using MrBayes 3.2.7 (Ronquist et al. 2012) with two independent runs of one million generations each, sampling every 100 generations and discarding the first 20% as burn-in. Convergence was assessed by ensuring the average standard deviation of split frequencies fell below 0.01.

Results

The complete mitogenome of M. pura was successfully assembled into a circular molecule of 107,684 bp in length through parallel assembly using GetOrganelle and NOVOPlasty. The mitogenome exhibits a GC content of 27.83% and was sequenced to an average sequencing depth of 363.55× (Supplementary Figure S2). It comprises a typical set of 14 conserved protein-coding genes (PCGs), including seven NADH dehydrogenase subunits (nad1-6, nad4L), three cytochrome c oxidases (cox1-3), three ATP synthase subunits (atp6, atp8, atp9), and cytochrome b (cob), along with two rRNA genes (rnl and rns), 25 tRNA genes representing all standard amino acids, and 11 additional non-conserved ORFs (ncORFs; Figure 2). These ncORFs encode a DNA polymerase (orf557), two RNA polymerases (orf317_2 & orf738), a ribosomal protein S3 (orf1901), as well as seven hypothetical proteins with unknown functions. Notably, all annotated genes except atp8, trnR_1, and seven ncORFs are transcribed from the forward strand (Figure 2), revealing strong strand bias in mitochondrial gene organization.

Among the annotated mitochondrial genes, we detected a total of 18 introns distributed across seven genes (Supplementary Figure S3), including cob (two introns), cox1 (seven), cox2 (two), nad1 (two), nad4L (one), nad5 (three), and rnl (one). All identified introns were classified as group I, except four introns (i.e. mL2501, cox2U228, nad1U636, and nad4LU107) whose types could not be determined. Intriguingly, 17 of these introns contain putative ORFs encoding GIY-YIG or LAGLIDADG homing endonucleases, while the nad4L intron uniquely lack a discernible ORF (Figure 2).

Phylogenetic reconstruction based on mitochondrial amino acid sequences yielded congruent topologies between maximum likelihood (ML) and Bayesian inference (BI) analyses (Figure 3). Notably, Schizophyllum commune and M. pura formed a strongly supported clade (BS = 83%, PP = 1.00), indicating close evolutionary relationships despite their classification in separate families and suborders. At higher taxonomic levels, the analysis recovered monophyletic groups largely consistent with current classification of Agaricales, with species from different families and suborders clustering into well-supported clades. However, a significant incongruence was observed in the suborder Pluteineae, where its constituent families (Amanitaceae and Pluteaceae) failed to form a monophyletic group, suggesting the need for potential taxonomic revision within this lineage.

Discussion and conclusions

This study reports the first complete mitogenome of M. pura, with a size of 107,684 bp—significantly larger than most Agaricales mitogenomes (Forget et al. 2002; Huang et al. 2021; Li et al. 2020). Our mitochondrial protein-based phylogeny robustly supports the transfer of Mycena to the family Mycenaceae and the suborder Mycenineae (He et al. 2024), resolving a long-standing taxonomic discrepancy from earlier classifications in the family Tricholomataceae (Kirk et al. 2001) or the suborder Marasmiineae (Dentinger et al. 2016).

Notably, despite the fact that Mycenaceae comprises ∼1500 species across nine genera (Hyde et al. 2024), this study represents the first mitogenome sequence reported for either the family Mycenaceae or the suborder Mycenineae. Given the long-standing taxonomic uncertainties of Mycenaceae, future research should prioritize expansive mitogenomic sequencing across this diverse yet neglected lineage. In addition, of the 11 currently recognized suborders within Agaricales (He et al. 2024), mitogenomes have now been reported for representatives of nine suborders (Figure 3). However, the remaining two suborders, Phyllotopsidineae and Sarcomyxineae, still lack any published mitogenome data. Comprehensive sampling across these missing lineages will be essential for robust phylogenetic reconstruction and a complete understanding of fungal evolution in Agaricales.

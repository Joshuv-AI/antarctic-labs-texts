Synthesis and Reactivity of Boron Triflate and Bistriflimide Complexes Bearing Two ortho-Carboranes
Authors: Kanika Vashisth, Ragene A. Thornton, Caleb D. Martin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This work discloses the synthesis of boron complexes featuring two 1-methyl-ortho-carborane substituents (Me oCb) and a weakly coordinating pseudohalide, trifluoromethanesulfonate (OTf) or bistriflimide (NTf2). Assessing their Lewis acidity experimentally by NMR probes, OPEt3 and 4-fluorobenzonitrile, revealed they are highly electrophilic, and DFT computations place their fluoride affinity in the Lewis super acidic regime. The fluoride affinity is leveraged in catalytic C–F Friedel–Crafts reactions. The three-component reactions of TfOBMe oCb2 or Tf2NBMe oCb2 with trans-β-nitrostyrene and arenes yield rare 1,4-Michael addition products where the arene acts as the Michael donor.

Introduction

Boron Lewis acids have widespread applications in optoelectronics as well as in catalytic and stoichiometric reactions. − An effective means to enhance Lewis acidity at boron centers is to use fluoroaryl groups to withdraw electron density. − Recently, researchers have found 12-vertex ortho-carborane substituents are superior to fluoroaryl groups in increasing Lewis acidity. − The higher electrophilicity of carborane substituted boranes results in increased reactivity for bis­(ortho-carboranyl)­boranes and tris­(orthocarboranyl)­borane versus the well-studied aryl counterparts. − In fact, compounds featuring two and three ortho-carborane substituents have fluoride affinities that exceed SbF5, the benchmark for a Lewis superacid. − In addition to the Lewis acidity, the icosahedral profile is a drastically different steric profile than aryl substituents and is significantly bulkier. With this high electrophilicity and tremendous bulk, carboranyl boranes have been very effective Lewis acids for frustrated Lewis pair bond activation reactions and enable the use of much smaller Lewis base partners than the widely used B­(C6F5)3. ,

The first disclosure of a boron triflate compound was boron tris­(triflate) (Figure ) reported by Tschager that is an effective Lewis acid for Friedel–Crafts alkylations between toluene and alkyl halides under mild conditions but heating over 100 °C resulted in decomposition. − The monotriflate complex, TfOBPh2, was generated in situ and effective for catalyzing asymmetric esterification and Friedel–Crafts acylation reactions but was reported to be unstable. , In 2024, Piers’ and co-workers synthesized the perfluoroaryl variant of TfOBPh2, TfOB­(C6F5)2, that is unstable in solution and in the solid state, even at low temperatures. Regarding boron compounds with the nitrogen containing pseudohailide, bistriflimide (NTf2), there is only a singular example, namely Tf2NB­(C6F5)2. The bistriflimide species is isolable and was crystallographically characterized that revealed the bistriflimide binds boron in a κ 2 coordination mode via two oxygen atoms of the NTf2 unit. The Tf2NB­(C6F5)2 complex is effective in C–H borylation reactions of pyridine containing polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) to furnish boron doped PAHs. With the large steric profile and electron withdrawing effect of carboranes, we believed that boron compounds featuring two ortho-carboranes as bulky electron withdrawing substituents with triflate or bistriflimide as the third substituent could be isolable and target them in this study.

Results and Discussion

The reaction of BrBMe oCb2 with 1.1 equivalents of trimethylsilyl triflate (TMSOTf) at 23 °C in dichloromethane did not result in any change by 11B NMR spectroscopy, nor did heating in benzene at 80 °C for 24 h. Changing the triflate source to silver triflate (AgOTf) and stirring for an hour at 23 °C led to the formation of a white precipitate and consumption of BrBMe oCb2 based on 11B NMR spectroscopy with a new resonance in the tricoordinate region at 39.7 ppm, shifted upfield from BrBMe oCb2 (c.f. 64.9 ppm, Scheme ). The identity of the product was determined by single crystal X-ray diffraction as TfOB Me o Cb 2 with the triflate covalently bound to boron (Figure ). Upon workup, TfOB Me o Cb 2 was isolated as a white solid in 88% yield. In the 19F­{1H} NMR spectrum, a singlet at −73.3 ppm confirms covalently bound triflate to boron in solution, consistent with the solid-state structure (c.f. ionic OTf = −79.0 ppm). Interestingly, TfOB Me o Cb 2 is stable as a solid at −35 °C in an N2 filled glovebox for nine months contrary to the reported bis­(aryl)boron triflates, TfOB­(C6F5)2 and TfOBPh2, that are unstable. ,

Synthesis of TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2

The reaction of BrBMe oCb2 with silver bistriflimide (AgNTf2) in dichloromethane at 23 °C resulted in the consumption of BrBMe oCb2 based on monitoring by 11B NMR spectroscopy after an hour. The solid-state structure was determined by single crystal X-ray diffraction that revealed the identity as Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 with the bistriflimide bound in a κ2 manner (Figure ). The product was isolated as a white solid in 58% yield after work up. In the 19F­{1H} NMR spectrum, the observed singlet at −71.5 ppm for the NTf2 group is consistent with covalently bound bistriflimide in solution. The 11B NMR spectrum features a peak at 8.2 ppm for the central boron, indicative of a four-coordinate environment, consistent with the solid-state structure.

In the X-ray diffraction structure of TfOB Me o Cb 2 , the B–O bond distance from the central boron to the triflate is 1.3747(18) Å, notably the shortest BOTf bond known (see Table for metrical parameters). The B–O bond in TfOB­(C6F5)2 is longer at 1.396(2) Å, rationalized by the stronger electron withdrawing ability of the carborane substituent compared to C6F5. In Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , the B–O bonds are much longer at 1.559(3) Å due to the κ2 coordination. The only other crystallographically characterized boron bistriflimide compound, Tf2NB­(C6F5)2, is also bound κ2 that has comparable B–O bond distances [1.555(2) and 1.569(3) Å]. The sulfur–oxygen bonds of the oxygen bound to boron are longer for TfOB Me o Cb 2 than Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 [1.579(1) vs 1.4972(18) Å] consistent with elongation for the stronger interaction to boron in κ1 triflate coordination over the κ2 bistriflimide. The C–B–C bond angle at the central boron in TfOB Me o Cb 2 is widened from the idealized trigonal planar environment due to the bulky carboranes at 127.70(12)° while in Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 it is more compressed at 122.1(3)° due to the increase in coordination number to four at boron. The central boron bonds to the “ipso” carbons are slightly shorter for the tricoordinate TfOB Me o Cb 2 than the four coordinate Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 [1.593(2) and 1.601(2) c.f. 1.654(3) Å].

The Lewis acidity of TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 were assessed by experimental NMR probes, the Gutmann-Beckett method (31P) and 4-fluorobenzonitrile probe (19F), as well as computational methods. − Exposing C6D6 solutions of TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 to PEt3O and analyzing by 31P­{1H} NMR spectroscopy revealed shifts at 86.2 and 86.3 ppm that correspond to acceptor numbers of 89.5 and 89.7, respectively, higher than the known carboranyl Lewis superacidic boranes (BoCb3 and HBMe oCb2, Table ). The 19F­{1H} spectra have shifts at −75.2 ppm for TfOB Me o Cb 2 corresponding to a covalently bound OTf and at −78.0 ppm (Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 ), indicative of ionic NTf2. This is rationalized by the the bulk of OPEt3 that is sufficient to displace the NTf2 unit.

It was recently proven that the Gutmann-Beckett method is not reliable for bulky boranes, especially those with two or three ortho-carborane substituents. ,, Thus, we turned to the 4-fluorobenzonitrile 19F NMR probe. Evaluating the NMR spectra with of the two reagents with 4-fluorobenzonitrile in benzene reveals the 19F­{1H} NMR spectrum of the TfOB Me o Cb 2 solution has a peak at −88.3 ppm whereas the Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 solution has a peak at −86.5 ppm for the 4-fluorobenzonitrile adduct. The 19F­{1H} NMR resonances reported for BoCb3 and HBMe oCb2 are −89.3 and −91.7 ppm, respectively, more upfield indicating that TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 N B Me o Cb 2 are more Lewis acidic based on this metric. The 19F­{1H} NMR spectrum for the TfOB Me o Cb 2 solution has a triflate peak at −74.8 ppm indicating it is still covalent while the Tf 2 N B Me o Cb 2 solution has two peaks for triflimide unit at −73.5 and −78.3 ppm indicating inequivalency for changing coordination to κ1. It is notable that 4-fluorobenzonitrile does not fully displace the triflimide, contrary to OPEt3, which is consistent with the smaller steric profile of the 4-fluorobenzonitrile probe. The two NMR probes indicate that both compounds are very strong Lewis acids.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were conducted using BPV86/SVP. The LUMO of TfOB Me o Cb 2 is mostly on the boron center and is lower in energy than the LUMO of Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 (−3.81 eV vs −3.54 eV) that has significant contribution on boron but is delocalized to the NTf2 group (Figure ). The HOMOs of both are delocalized on the carborane cages. The calculated fluoride affinities (FAs) are 569 kJ/mol for TfOB Me o Cb 2 and 543 kJ/mol for Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , both exceeding the Lewis super acid threshold FA of SbF5 at 493 kJ/mol. , For Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , we used the κ1 coordination mode of the resulting fluoride complex [FBo MeCb2NTf2 –] as this is a small anion and be most likely to mimic the experiment conducted with the small Lewis base, 4-fluorobenzonitrile that resulted in κ1 coordination. In comparing the FAs of TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 to carboranyl boranes, both are higher than HBMe oCb2 (527 kJ/mol) and lower than BoCb3 (605 kJ/mol). Compared to BrBMe oCb2 (548 kJ/mol), TfOB Me o Cb 2 is higher but Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 is lower. , The steric bulk was assessed by computing the % buried volume of the fluoride complexes based on the method developed by Radius. Both have similar steric bulk to BoCb3 (TfOB Me o Cb 2 = 70%, Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 = 73%, BoCb3 = 72%). ,,

Given the high FAs of TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , we investigated their ability as catalysts in C–F Friedel–Crafts reactions with fluorocyclohexane. It is notable that the C–F bond is the strongest single bond and it is challenging to cleave that requires powerful Lewis acids. The addition of a catalytic amount of TfOB Me o Cb 2 or Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 (10 mol %) to a benzene solution of fluorocyclohexane led to the formation of the Friedel–Crafts arylation product, cyclohexyl benzene, in 14 and 56% yields, respectively (Table , entries 1–2). Using B o Cb 3 as the Lewis acid catalyst resulted in a similar yield to Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 (58%, entry 3). The analogous reactions using toluene as the solvent with the three Lewis superacids provided mixtures of the para and ortho isomers of the Friedel–Crafts arylation products with the para isomer favored in all three cases (Table , entries 4–6). The highest total yield is for B o Cb 3 (84%) while TfOB Me o Cb 2 is only slightly lower at 80% and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 results in 60% yield. Changing the solvent to fluorobenzene resulted in a complex mixture for TfOB Me o Cb 2 . However, Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 and B o Cb 3 provided the Friedel–Crafts product in 35% and quantitative yield, respectively, as mixtures of the para and ortho isomers with the para being favored (Table , entries 7–9).

The bromoborane, BrB Me o Cb 2 , underwent 1,4-bromoboration of nitroalkenes where the nitro group chelates the boron center and the halide is introduced on the β-carbon (Scheme ). We were curious if TfOB Me o Cb 2 would undergo a triflato-boration reaction or if Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 would undergo a bistriflimido-boration reaction. The reaction of trans-β-nitrostyrene with 1 equivalent of TfOB Me o Cb 2 in benzene at 23 °C for 30 min resulted in a colorless solution. Monitoring the reaction by 11B NMR spectroscopy revealed consumption of the peak at 37.6 ppm for the central boron in TfOB Me o Cb 2 with a new peak at 8.5 ppm indicating a tetracoordinate boron environment. The product was isolated upon workup in 70% yield. In the 1H NMR spectrum, two sets of doublets at 5.73 and 4.73 ppm with equivalent integrations and coupling constants (8 Hz) were detected, similar to the reported 1,4-bromoboration iminium borate resonances of the protons on the iminium and brominated carbons in the BrB Me o Cb 2 reaction (5.57 ppm and 5.12 pm). The puzzling feature is that the aryl peaks doubled in integration from five to ten. Crystals were grown that revealed the identity of the product as the nitro-chelated borate iminium complex 1 akin to the 1,4-bromoboration product but instead of the triflate on the β-carbon, a phenyl group was installed (Figure ). The product is consistent with a 1,4-Michael addition reaction with benzene acting as the nucleophile. The C(1)–N(1) bond length in 1 is 1.278(6) Å, consistent with an iminium while the formerly alkene bond is elongated to a single bond [C(1)–C(2) = 1.498(6) Å]. The summation of the angles about nitrogen is 359.9(5)° confirming trigonal planar geometry. Attempting the reaction with the bistriflimide variant, Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , in benzene at 23 °C for 30 min provided the same Michael addition product, 1, in a similar isolated yield (TfOB Me o Cb 2 : 70%, Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 : 71%, Table , entries 1 and 2). In both cases, HOTf and HNTf2 were detected in the 19F­{1H} NMR spectra and removed by passing through a plug of silica. Michael addition reactions where the Michael donor is a solvent or a weak nucleophile like an arene, are rare.

Changing the solvent from benzene to toluene resulted in the Michael addition product (2) exclusively as the para isomer by 1H NMR spectroscopy based on a single methyl resonance at 2.03 ppm for both TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 with isolated yields of 72% and 65%, respectively (Table , entries 3 and 4). The proton NMR spectrum has doublets at 6.87 and 6.66 ppm with matching coupling constants (8 Hz) and the 11B NMR spectrum features a peak in the tetracoordinate region at 8.2 ppm. Next, we investigated electron poor arenes, specifically fluorobenzene species, to determine if they are sufficiently nucleophilic Michael donors. The reactions of trans-β-nitrostyrene with TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 N B Me o Cb 2 in fluorobenzene at 23 °C became colorless after 30 min (entries 5 and 6). Upon workup, the Michael addition product, 3, was isolated as a white solid in 64% and 78% isolated yields for TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , respectively. The 19F­{1H} NMR spectrum features a F-Csp2 resonance at −112.2 ppm slightly shifted from the starting material (c.f. −113.2 ppm). There are two doublets at 5.64 and 4.62 ppm with a coupling constant of 8 Hz in the 1H NMR spectrum of 3. Monitoring the reaction by in situ 19F­{1H} and 1H NMR spectroscopy did not indicate that any other regioisomers were formed, only the para species. The reactions of TfOB Me o Cb 2 or Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 in pentafluorobenzene did not give any change, even after heating at 60 °C for 24 h (entries 7 and 8).

Conclusions

The bis­(ortho-carboranyl)­boron compounds with a weakly coordinating pseudohalide, triflate and bistriflimide, were synthesized and isolated. The triflate species, TfOB Me o Cb 2 , represents the first stable tricoordinate boron triflate species and the bistriflimide, Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 , is only the second triflimide boron complex reported. The solid-state structure of TfOB Me o Cb 2 reveals a shorter B–O bond than all known boron triflate species and the 19F­{1H} NMR spectrum has a peak at −73.3 ppm, indicative of strong covalent binding in solution. The solid state structure of Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 features κ2 coordination by two oxygen atoms of the bistriflimide to the boron center. Both compounds have high computed fluoride affinities that exceed SbF5, classifying them as Lewis superacids. The fluoride affinity is exemplified in both species catalyzing C–F Friedel–Crafts reactions of fluorocyclohexane with arenes. The NMR spectroscopic Lewis acidity probes investigated, triethylphosphine oxide and fluorobenzonitrile, corroborate the high Lewis acidity of Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 and TfOB Me o Cb 2 . The reactions of Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 or TfOB Me o Cb 2 with trans-β-nitrostyrene in an arene solvent results in three component Michael addition reactions where the arene is installed on the β-carbon and the nitro group chelated the BMe oCb2 unit. Arene nucleophiles are rare in Michael reactions and reveal the powerful electron withdrawing effects of the bis­(ortho-carboranyl)­boryl substituent. Collectively, these studies demonstrate that TfOB Me o Cb 2 and Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 are potent Lewis acids and capable of engaging in stoichiometric and catalytic bond activation reactions.

Experimental Section

General Considerations

All manipulations were performed under an inert atmosphere in a nitrogen filled MBraun Unilab glovebox or using standard Schlenk techniques. Appropriate measures were taken when operating vacuum/inert-gas folds and liquid nitrogen traps on Schlenk line. No other uncommon hazards are noted. The purity of new compounds was established by multinuclear NMR spectroscopy and the spectra are provided in the Supporting Information. Deuterated solvents CDCl3 and C6D6 for NMR spectroscopy were purchased from Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc., dried by stirring for 5 days over CaH2, distilled, and stored over 3 Å molecular sieves. All other solvents were purchased from commercial sources as anhydrous grade, dried further using a JC Meyer Solvent System with dual columns packed with solvent-appropriate drying agents, and stored over 3 or 4 Å molecular sieves. Bis­(1-methyl-ortho-carboranyl)­bromo borane was prepared by the literature procedure. The following reagents: trimethylsilyl triflate, trimethylsilyl triflimide, silver triflate, silver bistriflimide, triethylphosphine oxide, 4-fluorobenzonitrile, trifluorotoluene, fluorocyclohexane, and trans-β-nitrostyrene were purchased from commercial sources and used without further purification. Multinuclear NMR data (1H, 13C­{1H}, 19F­{1H}, 11B, 11B­{1H}, 29Si­{1H}) were recorded on a Bruker Avance III HD 400 or 600 MHz instrument. High Resolution mass spectra (HRMS) were obtained in the Baylor University Mass Spectrometry Center on a Thermo Scientific LTQ Orbitrap Discovery spectrometer using +ESI and −ESI. Melting points were measured with a Laboratory device MEL-TEMP II capillary melting point apparatus and are uncorrected. Single crystal X-ray diffraction data were collected on a Bruker Apex III-CCD detector using Mo–Kα radiation (λ = 0.71073 Å). Crystals were selected under paratone oil, mounted on MiTeGen micromounts, and immediately placed in a cold stream of N2. Structures were solved and refined using SHELXTL and figures produced using OLEX2. ,

General Procedure for C–F Friedel–Crafts Catalytic Functionalization of Fluorocyclohexane

To a solution of trans-β-nitrostyrene in arene solvent (Ar–H, 0.7 mL), sequentially, a catalytic amount of Lewis acid (10 mol %, 0.0050 mmol) in arene solvent (0.3 mL), and fluorocyclohexane (0.050 mmol, 5.5 μL) was added neat. The mixture was stirred for an hour at 23 °C. The volatiles were removed under vacuum. The residue was dissolved in CDCl3 (0.7 mL total volume containing dibromomethane (0.050 mmol/0.7 mL total volume) as an internal standard).

Cyclohexylbenzene was generated in the following yields for each Lewis acid: TfOB Me o Cb 2 = 14% yield, Tf 2 NB Me o Cb 2 = 56%, B o Cb 3 = 58%. The NMR data matches with the literature for cyclohexylbenzene.

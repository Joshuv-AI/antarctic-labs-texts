Log-probit accompanied with Tallarida and Chou-Talalay-Martin methods in an isobolographic analysis of interactions between two antiseizure medications – a comparative study
Authors: Jarogniew J. Łuszczki, Katarzyna Gustaw-Rothenberg, Magdalena Florek-Łuszczki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe isobolographic analysis is a gold standard in the assessment of interactions between drugs in experimental studies. Although some isobolographic approaches are available, the most popular methods to characterize drug-drug interactions are the log-probit method accompanied with statistical analysis of interactions (by Tallarida) and the method based on mass-action law using CompuSyn software (elaborated by Chou-Talalay-Martin). The aim of this study was to compare the results from these two isobolographic approaches.MethodsTwo isobolographic methods (log-probit associated with Tallarida statistics and Chou-Talalay-Martin) were applied to analyze the interaction between two antiseizure medications – clonazepam and lamotrigine in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model.ResultsBoth isobolographic approaches confirmed that the combination of clonazepam with lamotrigine produced synergistic interaction and allowed for detailed characteristics of the interaction at various effect levels for the two-drug mixture. Calculation of the combination index values (at various effect levels) confirmed that synergy slightly decreased when the antiseizure effect increased (combination index values increased from 0.44 for 16% to 0.65 for 84%).ConclusionsThe log-probit method with statistical analysis of data (by Tallarida) was more subtle and precise in the assessment of the synergistic interaction, whereas the isobolographic analysis by Chou-Talalay-Martin offered more automatic options facilitating visualization of the interaction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s43440-025-00784-9.

Introduction

An isobolographic analysis is one of the methods used in the determination of drug-drug interactions in preclinical studies [1–3]. It has a firmly established position as a method of choice when analyzing pharmacodynamic types of interactions among anti-seizure medications in experimental epileptology [4]. No doubt exists that this method allows characterizing interactions between drugs, classifying them as additive, supra-additive (synergistic), and infra-additive (antagonistic). As regards the anti-seizure effects of drugs in in vivo studies, the most desired types of interactions are synergistic and additive with a tendency towards synergy [4]. Of note, both drugs in mixture cooperate for their anti-convulsant effects in experimental models of epilepsy. In contrast, the least desirable interactions are antagonistic or additive with a tendency towards antagonism. In other words, drugs reducing their anti-seizure effects in in vivo studies are unfavorable [4].

Previously, it has been reported that the two-drug combination of oxcarbazepine with clonazepam (two antiseizure medications) was able to produce 3 different types of interactions (synergy, antagonism, and additivity) for varying fixed-ratio combinations tested [4]. However, to correctly perform the isobolographic analysis, some theoretical presumptions are required, including verification of parallelism between the studied drugs’ dose-response lines. Considering the parallelism of the dose-response curves, there exist two variants of isobolographic analysis for parallel and non-parallel dose-response effect curves/lines [5, 6]. To facilitate the isobolographic analysis of interactions, a CompuSyn software (ver. 1.0) by Chou & Martin is available [7].

The aim of this study was to compare two isobolographic approaches used in the determination of interactions between drugs. The first one was based on the log-probit associated with the Tallarida method, involved statistical analysis of data [1], and the second one was based on the Chou-Talalay-Martin method [8–10]. To compare both isobolographic approaches, the raw data from a previously published study, based on the analysis of the interaction between two anti-seizure medications (lamotrigine and clonazepam) in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model, were used [11]. Analysis of the same raw data with both log-probit associated with Tallarida and Chou-Talalay-Martin methods should obviously provide the same results, but more advanced results might be generated while applying these two methods, leading finally to some additional complementary conclusions. Although both methods are used separately in the isobolographic studies, the joint application of both approaches might improve and strengthen the quality of isobolographic research.

Of note, lamotrigine and clonazepam are two antiseizure medications widely used in both, preclinical studies on animals and clinical conditions in patients to inhibit tonic-clonic seizures and partial convulsions with or without secondary generalization [12]. In animals, lamotrigine and clonazepam suppress (in a dose-dependent manner) tonic-clonic seizures in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model, and their median effective doses (ED50 values) are 4.7 (3.3–6.7) mg/kg and 26.0 (21.1–31.9) mg/kg, respectively [4, 13].

Materials and methods

Log-probit accompanied by the Tallarida method

Doses of clonazepam and lamotrigine (expressed in mg/kg) when administered separately and the doses of the two-drug mixture (clonazepam + lamotrigine) in combination at the fixed-ratio of 1:1, along with their protective effects (expressed in %) against maximal electroshock-induced seizures underwent log-probit transformation [8]. Of note, the fixed-ratio combination of 1:1 is based on proportions of effects produced by the drugs. The notation of 1:1 indicates that the effects produced by the drugs are equal and reflect the half of ED50 of the first drug and the half of ED50 of the second drug used in the mixture [14]. The log-probit method allows for calculating the ED16, ED50 and ED84 values for the drugs when used alone (clonazepam and lamotrigine) and the ED16 mix, ED50 mix and ED84 mix values for the two-drug mixture at a fixed ratio of 1:1. Additionally, the test of parallelism of dose-response relationship curves was performed to confirm that both drugs had their dose-response lines mutually collateral. All log-probit calculations were automatically performed using an MS Excel spreadsheet with equations elaborated by Tallarida, allowing calculations of results [14].

Statistical analysis of data

Statistical comparison of the experimentally-derived ED16 mix, ED50 mix, and ED84 mix values with their theoretically predicted to be additive ED16 add, ED50 add, and ED84 add values was performed with Student’s t-test using the GraphPad Prism ver. 8.0 for Windows software. Statistical significance was established at p < 0.05.

Chou-Talalay-Martin method

Doses of clonazepam and lamotrigine (expressed in mg/kg) when administered separately and the doses of the two-drug mixture (clonazepam + lamotrigine) in combination at the fixed-ratio of 14.57:4.47, along with their protective effects (expressed in %) against maximal electroshock-induced seizures, were inserted into the CompuSyn software (by Chou-Talalay-Martin). Of note, the proportions of drugs used in the Chou-Talalay-Martin method are based on a mass quantity of particular drugs present in the mixture [10]. To make the results comparable, the fixed-ratio combination of the drugs was adapted to the Chou-Talalay-Martin method. This is the reason that the notification of 14.57:2.47 for the fixed-ratio combination of clonazepam and lamotrigine was assigned in the Chou-Talalay-Martin method, reflecting half of the ED50 of the first drug (clonazepam) and half of the ED50 of the second drug (lamotrigine).

Software used

The CompuSyn software ver. 1.0 for Windows was used and allowed automatic calculations and graphical illustration of interactions.

Results

Log-probit dose-response effect analysis

Doses of particular drugs (clonazepam and lamotrigine), when administered singly, along with their anticonvulsant effects, were published earlier [11], and allowed the construction of the log-probit dose-response relationship lines for the tested drugs (Fig. 1; Table 1). The test of parallelism revealed that both drugs (clonazepam and lamotrigine) had their lines mutually collateral to one another (Fig. 1). Doses of the mixture of both drugs in a fixed-drug dose ratio of 1:1 also allowed the determination of the ED16 mix, ED50 mix, and ED84 mix values for the mixture of clonazepam and lamotrigine in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model (Fig. 1; Table 1).

Isobolographic analysis of interaction by Tallarida

The isobolographic analysis of interaction performed according to the log-probit and Tallarida method revealed that the experimentally-derived ED16 mix value considerably differed (***p < 0.001) from the theoretically calculated and predicted to be additive ED16 add value (Student’s t-test: t58 = 3.737; Table 2; Fig. 2). The combination index value for this effect (16%) was 0.44, indicating synergistic interaction. Similarly, the experimentally-derived ED50 mix value considerably differed (**p < 0.01) from the theoretically calculated and predicted to be additive ED50 add value (Student’s t-test: t58 = 3.046; Table 2), with a combination index value amounting to 0.53 (Fig. 2). The ED84 mix value significantly differed (*p < 0.05) from the ED84 add value (Student’s t-test: t58 = 2.235; Table 2), with the combination index value for this 84% effect amounting to 0.65, and indicating synergistic interaction (Fig. 2).

Finally, the interaction between clonazepam and lamotrigine in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model has been illustrated as polygonograms for various anticonvulsant effects ranging from 16 to 97% (Fig. 6). Of note, polygonograms are the fastest way to illustrate the characteristics of observed interactions. According to Chou-Talalay-Martin, the thickness of polygonogram lines and their colors are indicative of either synergy (green line) or antagonism (red line) [10]. The thicker the line, the stronger the power of interaction, as illustrated by polygonograms [10]. This is the reason that the strong synergy was illustrated for the anticonvulsant effects of 16% and 50% (Fig. 6). The weak power of synergy was reported for both, 84% and 90%, whereas the antagonistic interaction was documented for the 97% anticonvulsant effect (Fig. 6).

Discussion

In this study, we have compared two isobolographic approaches: the log-probit dose-response relationship curve analysis, accompanied by statistical analysis of isobolographic interaction according to the Tallarida method, with the mass-action law isobolographic method by Chou-Talalay-Martin. Although both methods provided almost the same results, the isobolographic approach elaborated by Tallarida was superior to that created by Chou-Talalay-Martin. The method by Tallarida provides full insight into the nature of observed interaction, including evaluation of parallelism of dose-response curves and statistical analysis of ED values. In contrast, the method by Chou-Talalay-Martin is fully automatic, generating the interaction report after introducing raw data. Of note, based on the results presented in detail in the previously published paper, we can readily apply these available raw data to perform this comparative analysis. Results evidently confirmed the synergistic interaction between lamotrigine and clonazepam in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model.

In the log-probit method, the application of statistical analysis of data by comparing the experimentally-derived values with those theoretically calculated and predicted to be additive, with a recommended statistical test (unpaired Student’s t-test), provides a certainty that the observed interaction has sufficient power to be synergistic or antagonistic. Due to statistical analysis of data, both synergy and antagonism can be readily confirmed [14]. Of note, the null hypothesis in the log-probit approach assumes that values significantly differ from additivity, claiming synergistic or antagonistic interactions. The rejection of the null hypothesis results finally in acceptance of the alternative hypothesis, stating that no statistical difference is observed between experimentally derived values and those predicted to be additive. So, the additivity is confirmed by the rejection of the null hypothesis. Unfortunately, in the Chou-Talalay-Martin method using CompuSyn software, there is no statistical comparison of values, but interactions are classified with their corresponding combination index values, allowing characteristics of interaction as synergistic if the combination index is lower than 1 and tends to 0, or antagonistic if the combination index is higher than 1 and tends to infinity. In this study, the observed synergy possessed statistical significance at p < 0.001 with the combination index value of 0.44 for a 16% anticonvulsant effect; p < 0.01 with the combination index value of 0.54 for a 50% effect; and p < 0.05 with the combination index value of 0.65 for an 84% anticonvulsant effect (Table 2).

The most important presumption in both methods is the fixed-ratio combination of the tested drugs used experimentally. Of note, the fixed-ratio combination based on the mass-action law equation of the tested drugs is crucial for the entire analysis. In both methods, the dose-response relationships of the studied drugs used separately and in combinations underwent some specific computer-assisted transformation and automatic calculations. The notification of fixed-ratio combinations is different in the log-probit and Chou-Talalay-Martin methods. The fixed ratio of 1:1 in the log-probit method means that both drugs are administered in equi-effective doses, reflecting their median effects (i.e., the sum of 50% of the ED50 values of the drugs, when administered separately, results finally in 100% of the ED50 mix of the two-drug mixture) [1]. In contrast, the fixed ratio of 1:1 in the Chou-Talalay-Martin method indicates that the drugs are administered in the same weight doses (i.e., 1 mg of the first drug + 1 mg of the second drug, resulting finally in 2 mg of the two-drug mixture) [10]. This is the main reason that in the Chou-Talalay-Martin method the adjustment was required to make the results comparable in both methods, and finally, the notification of 14.57:2.47 for the fixed-ratio combination of clonazepam and lamotrigine was assigned, reflecting a 50% of the ED50 of the first drug (clonazepam) and a 50% of the ED50 of the second drug (lamotrigine).

Noteworthy, in case of parallel dose-response relationship curves, the fixed-ratio combination is constant for various effect levels investigated, and the proportions of the drugs in the mixture are also constant and do not differ when investigating the 16% and 84% effects. In contrast, for non-parallel dose-response relationship curves, the proportions of the drugs considerably differed for various examined effects. Since the ED values for various effects increase and depend on the angle of inclination of the dose-response relationship curves, either flat or sloping dose-response relationship curves make the calculations of proportions exceptionally difficult. Regrettably, the Chou-Talalay-Martin method does not consider the parallelism of dose-response relationship curves for the studied drugs, and thus, the resulting interactions can be falsely classified as synergistic or antagonistic.

It is important to note that both approaches, log-probit and Chou-Talalay-Martin, can determine the dose-effect at various effect levels, including the most popular 50%, but also 16% and 84% (in the log-probit) and 75% and 90% (in the Chou-Talalay-Martin). In this context, the synergistic effect observed in this study was reduced along with the increase in observed effects. The Chou-Talalay-Martin method provided us with additional visualization of data, including analysis of the combination index in various effect levels assessed. Unfortunately, the lack of statistical analysis of data did not give us a clear picture of the interaction. Of note, the combination index can assess in approximation the characteristics of interaction, but the limit of additivity is very narrow and by definition is equal to 1. Each value lower than 1 (i.e., 0.98) indicates synergistic interaction. Additionally, each value higher than 1 (i.e., 1.02) reflects antagonistic interaction, even if the analyzed effect of the mixture possesses wide standard deviations or 95% confidence intervals. The classification of interactions with the Chou-Talalay-Martin method, based only on some “contractual” evaluation criteria, may be problematic. At present, some authors have used the range of the combination index values from 0 to 0.7, as indicative for synergistic interactions; from 1.3 to infinity – for antagonistic interactions; and the combination index values ranging between 0.7 and 1.3 – as informative of additive interactions [15]. Obviously, these border limit values for additivity (0.7 and 1.3) were chosen randomly. Such a combination index criteria for the classification of interactions need additional explanation and reference to previously published articles, in contrast to the statistical analysis of data, which independently documents the synergy in this study for all the evaluated effects without referencing previous articles. Besides, confidence in statistical analysis of data is greater than in contractually accepted values.

The assessment of interaction for various effect levels seems to be very important from a preclinical perspective. Although the median effect (50%) is usually presented, the higher effects, i.e., 0.9 in Chou-Talalay-Martin and 84% in the log-probit methods, can be readily calculated and graphically drawn as the isobolograms. The analysis of the combination index from the Chou-Talalay-Martin method provided information that the interaction of lamotrigine with clonazepam in the mouse maximal electroshock-induced seizure model can change, along with the increase in the observed effects. As reported herein, the synergistic interaction for the 50% effect has changed to additivity at 0.9 effect. As illustrated graphically, the combination index increases along with the increased effects. Synergy was replaced with additive interaction at 0.9 (90%) anticonvulsant effect (Fig. 5a). The same situation was observed in the log-probit method, in which the increase in the anticonvulsant effects was accompanied by a reduction of the synergistic power.

There is no doubt that both methods reliably allow for assessing interactions between drugs, but the log-probit method followed by the statistical analysis of data seems to be the most accurate, offering the analysis of the strength of the interactions. On the other hand, the simplicity of drawing the isobolograms with CompuSyn can reliably speak to the practical application of the Chou-Talalay-Martin method in experimental studies, even if we should anticipate whether the interaction is still additive or not. The method by Tallarida allows delving into the analysis of interactions and subsequently delineating the nature of interactions. Calculation of the combination index values allows anticipating the possible change of interactions along with the increase in the observable effects. In experimental epileptology, the anticonvulsant effects should be evaluated at the maximally possible level (84% or 0.9), which allows anticipating the full antiseizure control. The main benefit from the log-probit and statistical analysis of data by the Tallarida method is the confirmation of observed synergy or antagonism, in contrast to the Chou-Talalay-Martin method, in which the combination index allows anticipating that the analyzed interaction pretends to be synergistic or antagonistic. On the other hand, no presumptions about parallelism of dose-response lines and no statistics make the Chou-Talalay-Martin method of the first choice in screen studies, allowing the quick assessment of interactions without delving into their nature. This is the main reason to recommend the Chou-Talalay-Martin method as the screen test, whereas the log-probit and the statistical methods, which could serve to complementarily detect or confirm the nature of interactions.

Of note, both isobolographic approaches (the log-probit with Tallarida and Chou-Talalay-Martin methods) have their own limitations. Regarding the Chou-Talalay-Martin method, it does not differentiate any parallelism between drugs’ concentration-response curves. Lack of statistical comparison between experimentally-derived ED mix and theoretically predicted to be additive ED add values; and classification of interactions based solely on the combination index, whose values are arbitrary and contractually accepted as border values for synergy and antagonism, without considering some wide 95% confidence limits or SD values. Additionally, in the Chou-Talalay-Martin method, one can only generate a final report summarizing the observed interactions and illustrating the interactions, but there is no possibility to change, modify, or even improve the quality of the obtained graphs. All these missing things make the method somehow incomplete and imprecise, or even as a preliminary screen. As regards the log-probit method and Tallarida isobolography, the limitations are related to meticulous and time-consuming calculations of the area of additivity restricted by the lower and upper lines of additivity for non-parallel concentration-response curves. In the log-probit analysis, the lack of precise determination of effects lower than 16% and higher than 84% (i.e., lower than the 4th probit and higher than the 6th probit), which are determined in approximation, makes the method ideally suitable for the determination of median effects (ED50 values). On the other hand, the isobolographic approach by Tallarida can be readily enlarged by illustrating the observed interaction between drugs in the form of polygonograms for various anticonvulsant effects.

Another fact deserves our special attention, the isobolographic analysis of interaction based on previously published raw data is the best example illustrating the “open science” approach, allowing researchers to re-analyze data with different methods, drawing new conclusions, improving research integrity, and launching discussion on the methods used, elaborating the most adequate and reasonable solution offering the best option for future experiments.

Noteworthy, in this study, we applied, for the first time, the isobolographic analysis by Chou-Talalay-Martin to reveal the interaction between two antiseizure medications in the in vivo mouse model of tonic-clonic seizures. Generally, the Chou-Talalay-Martin method is widely applied in in vitro oncology studies, evaluating interactions between chemotherapeutics [16]. Due to computer-assisted, quick, and automatic calculations accompanied by illustrations of observed interactions as isobolograms and polygonograms, this method seems to be the best option for discovering new synergistic interactions for various chemotherapeutic drugs. However, the automatic calculations of various analyzed effects and parameters may be challenging for beginners, who may not be able to choose the optimal parameters for the analyzed interactions. The automatically generated report may sometimes provide some values which should be adjusted to the observed effects, especially for readers who are not familiar with the CompuSyn software and isobolographic studies. Unfortunately, the lack of statistical analysis of the obtained results might be disappointing for researchers, who usually need a “significant” confirmation of their scientific expectations. This is the main reason that the Chou-Talalay-Martin method is not frequently used in in vivo studies. Although the Chou-Talalay-Martin method has never been applied earlier in experimental epileptology, its simplicity in automatic calculations and fast visualization of interactions could substantially contribute to the propagation of this method and recommendations for its application in further experiments on animals, even if the method would be a preliminary screen.

In conclusion, the log-probit-based isobolographic approach, accompanied by statistical analysis of data (recommended by Tallarida) along with the calculation of combination index and the presentation of polygonogram (recommended by Chou-Talalay-Martin), makes the isobolographic analysis complete. Although each approach provides illustrative interactions (isobolograms), when joined together, they finally make the analysis entirely deep and professional.

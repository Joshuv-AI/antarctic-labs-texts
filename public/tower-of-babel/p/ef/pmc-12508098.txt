Predicting and optimizing the impact responses of the airbag landing system by the simulation-based deep learning model
Authors: Xinyi Shen, Caishan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To ensure the safety of astronauts and equipment, the airbag landing system of the spacecraft can attenuate impact loads significantly but increases the difficulty of system analysis and design. This paper proposes a simulation-based deep learning model for fast predicting the impact accelerations of the spacecraft during soft landing on the complex airbag landing system. The finite element model was constructed to generate the dataset with initial landing velocities, airbag parameters and accelerations. A novel architecture of the Convolutional Neural Network was developed under the principle of Markov Decision Process. Based on the dataset and architecture, the CNN model was trained to predict three types of acceleration profiles in the spacecraft’s body coordinate system simultaneously, taking initial landing velocities, initial pressures of airbags, and overload threshold for venting as inputs. There is good agreement between the results of the CNN model and the finite element model, with a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^5$$\end{document} times faster prediction speed. Combining the CNN model with a Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm, an optimized inflation and venting scheme was obtained to decrease the peak accelerations for both moderate and extreme landing cases. The proposed method offers significant potential in performance analysis and optimization of complex dynamical systems.

Introduction

With a new vision for space exploration, a new generation of manned spacecraft is being developed1,2. Engineers aim at increasing the capacity of the spacecraft, which in turn causes greater impact loads during landing. In addition to ensuring the safety of astronauts and equipment, it’s also important to improve the astronauts’ feelings and enhance the recyclability of the spacecraft. Airbags are used extensively in different industries because they are lightweight and easy to manufacture or combine different structures3–7. It works by converting the kinetic energy of the payload into the internal energy of the gas by compressing the airbag. When certain preset conditions are met, the vent of the airbag is going to open, releasing gas to remove the shock energy directly. Multiple airbag assemblies are installed under the bottom of the spacecraft to form the landing system, each of which consists of a main venting airbag and a non-venting anti-bottom airbag8–12. This kind of structure not only attenuates the impact load but also prevents the spacecraft from making direct contact with the ground and increases the stability of the landing.

However, the Airbag Landing System (ALS) introduces multiple design parameters and complex contact conditions, bringing more challenges in design and analysis. The majority study of theoretical models is limited to the single airbag system, in particular with regard to the vertical compression process13–15. The computation time of finite element method is excessively long, resulting in inefficient performance analysis. When the airbags slip and compress between the spacecraft and the ground, the airbag fabric’s thin membrane element sometimes suffers from mesh distortion, leading to computational divergence. The uncertainty of the landing conditions also increases the need for extensive simulations. With the rise of deep learning, combining surrogate models and numerical simulation can reduce the computation cost of system performance prediction and complete the divergence curve. The Artificial Neural Network (ANN) has demonstrated significant potential in modeling nonlinear dynamics. The basic neural network frameworks include the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)16,17.

The Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) has the simplest architecture and is widely applied to predict the dynamic response of various systems. Sakaridis et al. developed an MLP model to predict tubular structures’ buckling response18. Their model is designed to calculate a force value based on the time point, tube height, and impact velocity. They found a non-random error distribution similar to geometric imperfections. André et al. trained a mechanical joint model to describe the local force-deformation curve and integrated it as a sub-model into the large-scale finite element code19. Rietkerk et al. utilized experimental data to train a neural network to predict the penetration depth of a semi-infinite target20. They added a general physics principle to the MLP model: the Buckingham Pi Theorem. This model based on physical information has good generalization to new combinations of penetrating objects and target materials. MLP can achieve good predictive performance under certain conditions, but has limitations in modeling more complex systems. This fully-connected network structure lacks a design similar to the convolution and attention mechanisms, which is insufficient to capture local features.

The Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) is constructed to perform well on temporal data21. Several of its variants include Bidirectional RNN and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)22,23. Zhang et al. provided two schemes (full sequence and stacked sequence) of LSTMs to predict the non-linear structural response24. A dynamic K-means clustering approach was also established to generate more effective datasets for training. They highlighted that the LSTM model with the stacked sequence was more robust and low-cost. Zhou et al. adopted the bidirectional LSTM to learn the inverse mapping between the short impact load time history and structural dynamic behaviors25. Li et al. also utilized a sequence-to-sequence bidirectional LSTM to learn the relationship between the input impact force histories and serialized material parameters26. Due to serialized gated recurrent units, LSTM has a stronger learning ability than MLP for complex nonlinear problems. Although multiple gates are designed to carry different information, the physical meaning is still unclear.

The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is known for computer vision, such as image recognition, object detection, and neural-style transfer27,28. Compared to MLP and RNN, CNN is more effective at feature extraction. Shen et al. combined the Convolutional Denoising Autoencoder (CDAE) and the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to predict the impact responses of honeycomb structures29. The high-dimensional features of the honeycomb structure are extracted from the hidden layer of the CDAE after learning pixelized finite element model images and then input to the LSTM to obtian the stress-strain curves. CNNs can not only be used to process 2D image data but they can also be applied to 1D time sequences. Sun et al. built a virtual sensor with partial measurements by CNN30. They reconstructed 1D response series from reference sensors into 2D matrices as the input of CNN. In electrocardiogram beat identification, compact 1D filters are set directly to detect the peaks at different positions along the time series31. Thus, a more physically intuitive neural network architecture can be designed by structuring the input data and organizing different convolutional filters.

The impact dynamics can be formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) problem - the next state of the system depends only on the current state and action, independent of the historical state. Based on the principle of MDP, explicit dynamics computational methods discretize time and compute the state of the next time step based on the state of the previous time step. Therefore, we propose a novel Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) structure, which also incorporates the principle of MDP and predicts the impact responses in a ’time-stepping’ way.

For a complex system like the airbag landing system of spacecraft, it is difficult to obtain an optimization design scheme without great effort, due to various landing conditions and complicated configurations. To accelerate the efficiency of engineering optimization, the combination between deep learning and optimization techniques has been extensively applied to the study of the impact engineering. Xu et al. first investigated the viability of the origami-inspired airbag for landing in complex environments32. They established a three-layer neural network to predict the maximum overload and specific energy absorption. The multi-objective water cycle algorithm was adopted to optimize the exhaust vent area, initial pressure, and venting threshold pressure of the airbag. Kappe et al. developed a multi-objective optimization procedure combining neural networks and genetic algorithms for the efficient design of gradient lattice structures under dynamic loading33. The procedure aims to maximize energy absorption and achieve a lightweight design, while minimizing peak impact forces. Liu et al. similarly combined convolutional neural networks and genetic algorithms to obtain a spiral laminate with the highest energy absorption for a given volume34. To obtain the suitable inflation and venting strategy of the airbag landing system under different landing conditions, the deep learning surrogate model is combined with the multi-objective optimization algorithm.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, we construct the finite element model and create a dataset of three acceleration time histories by varying the initial landing conditions and airbag parameters. This dataset is used to train the deep learning surrogate model. Section 3 introduces the proposed CNN architectures for predicting impact accelerations and the corresponding training schemes. Section 4 shows the results of the hyperparameter identification, the predictions of the CNN model on the test set, comparison with the LSTM model, and parameter analysis. In Sect. 5, the inflation and venting schemes of the airbag landing system are optimized for two landing cases by combining the surrogate model and the non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm.

Dataset generation

Finite element model

The numerical simulation for the spacecraft soft landing on the airbag landing system is conducted using the commercial finite element program LS-DYNA (Fig. 1). The spacecraft features a truncated cone shape. The airbag landing system consists of six airbag assemblies, each of which comprises an outside venting airbag and an inside anti-bottoming airbag. Both outside and inside airbags have a horizontal cylindrical shape. The spacecraft is modeled as a rigid body due to the cushioning of the airbag landing system. The soil block is modeled as an elastoplastic body.Fig. 1The finite element model of the spacecraft soft landing on the airbag landing system.

The finite element model of the spacecraft soft landing on the airbag landing system.

The spacecraft is modeled as an axisymmetric body. Its mass properties are calculated based on the mesh geometry and the rigid body material model. The typical impact attenuation process is shown in Fig. 2: after the external airbags touches the ground, the airbags are pressurized and deformed; when the airbag pressure rises, the force exerted on the spacecraft gradually increases, and the acceleration of the spacecraft increases; and when the acceleration reaches the threshold, the vents open, releasing the gas to attenuate the impact energy. The drop test for the airbag landing system of the spacecraft is conducted on a gantry (Fig. 3). Steel cables suspended from the gantry are used to lift the test prototype to a predetermined height, allowing us to achieve the desired initial vertical velocity. A triaxial accelerometer and a MEMS gyroscope are positioned at the geometric center of the test prototype. The parameters of the finite element model are consistent with the test setup. For the case with 5m/s (Fig. 4a), the peak vertical acceleration from the test is 4.76 g, while the finite element simulation shows 4.69 g, resulting in a 1.5% relative error. Convolutional neural network with ‘time-stepping’

Model architecture

The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is known for processing grid-like spatial data (e.g., images). Traditional CNN architectures lack explicit mechanisms for modeling temporal dependencies and dynamic system evolution. To address this limitation, a novel CNN architecture that incorporates the numerical computation principle of “time-stepping” to process sequential data is proposed (Fig. 5). The impact dynamics problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) - a mathematical framework where future states depend solely on the current state rather than the entire history43. This architecture allows the network to maintain the feature extraction advantages of traditional CNNs while simultaneously capturing essential temporal dependencies in sequential data.Fig. 5The architecture of convolutional neural network with ‘time-stepping’.

The architecture of convolutional neural network with ‘time-stepping’.

Then, zero padding is performed only at the top of features, and two-by-two convolution filters are applied to create higher-level features. The new features thus only contain the adjacent lower-level features of the previous and current time steps. After performing the two-by-two convolution, the number of features in each time step is reduced by one. Each two-by-two convolution filter forms a channel of new features. Multiple filters are set to learn the different combinations of information in a ’time-stepping’ way. We repeat the operation of zero padding and two-by-two convolution until only one feature is formed in each channel.

Unlike the LSTM network architecture, which uses logical units such as self-loop-connected memory units and forgetting gates to filter information from upstream of the timeline23, the convolutional neural network with ‘time-stepping’ allows for a simpler and more physically intuitive neural network structure. The computational complexity is also lower because long-term history information is not considered.

Training and evaluation scheme

The training process was implemented in the Python environment by using the Keras programming interface (v2.11.0) and run on a desktop equipped with Intel Core i7-11700 CPU @ 2.5GHz. The core implementation code is available on GitHub at https://github.com/TheStarMovesxy/CNN_Timestepping. To simplify the hyperparameter identification, we used the same number of two-by-two convolution filters in each iteration: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_s = d$$\end{document}. In the present work, we set k, d=4,8,16,32 - a total of 16 different network structures were tested.

Considering the small dataset, the training and evaluation of these CNN models was performed with 10-fold cross-validation. We randomly selected 90 cases from the dataset as the training set and the remaining 9 cases as the test set. The training set was further randomly partitioned into 10 mutually exclusive subsets of equal size. The model training and validation process was then conducted 10 times, with each time utilizing a distinct subset as the validation set while the remaining 9 subsets were combined to form the training set. During each training session, the performance metric was evaluated on the validation set, which was chosen as the Mean Absolute Error (MAE). After completing all training sessions, the final performance evaluation of each model structure was obtained by computing the average of the metric across the 10 folds. The CNN architecture with the smallest average value was selected to be further evaluated.

In a single training session, all input features were first normalized using the scaling function of the scikit-learn preprocessing library so that each feature has a mean of 0 and a variance of 1. The ADaptive Momentum Estimation (ADAM) optimizer was adopted to update the CNN model parameters by minimizing the loss function44:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \theta _{i+1}=\theta _i-\frac{\alpha m_i}{\sqrt{s_i}+\varepsilon } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where i is the iteration number; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} is the initial learning rate; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta$$\end{document} is the parameter vector; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon$$\end{document} is a constant added to avoid zero on the denominator; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m_i$$\end{document} is the moving average of the derivatives of the loss function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nabla L(\theta _i)$$\end{document}, which add the momentum to the gradient descent for parameters; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s_i$$\end{document} is the moving average of squared values \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[\nabla L(\theta _i)]^2$$\end{document}, which balance the magnitudes of the gradient descent in different dimensions for the parameter vector and damp out the oscillations. The formulas of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m_i$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s_i$$\end{document} are as follows:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \left\{ \begin{array}{rl} m_i & =\beta _1 m_{i-1}+(1-\beta _1)\nabla L(\theta _i)\\ s_i & =\beta _2 s_{i-1}+(1-\beta _2)[\nabla L(\theta _i)]^2 \end{array}\right. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _1$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _2$$\end{document} are exponential decay rates of the gradients. The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) between the predicted and true values of the training set was selected as the loss function. The initial learning rate was set to 0.01.

Each training session was performed for 1000 epochs. After each epoch, the MAE on the validation set (i.e. the validation loss) was calculated. After completing all training epochs, the model parameters were restored to the values of the epoch in which the validation loss was minimal. With this strategy, the period for extracting the results was not predetermined but dynamically determined according to the optimization progress. This not only reduces overfitting, but also avoids stopping training prematurely due to changes in validation loss18.

The influences of initial velocities and airbag parameters on the prediction results are analyzed further. The feature impact on the acceleration peaks was estimated by the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method (Fig.17)34,49. This method not only can explain the impact of features globally, but also can interpret individual examples. Based on the concept of shapley value in coalitional game theory, the SHAP method calculates the weighted average of a single feature’s marginal contributions in all possible feature combinations. The SHAP value for the j-th feature is given as follows:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \phi _j=\sum _{S\subseteq N \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!(|N|-|S|-1)!}{|N|!}\left( V(S\cup \{j\} )-V(S)\right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where, N represents the total set of all features, S is the subset of features, V(S) represents the predicted values.\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( V(S\cup \{j\} )-V(S)\right)$$\end{document} calculates the marginal contribution of feature j to the predicted values.

Design optimization

To improve the feeling of astronauts during landing, the amplitude of impact acceleration must be controlled. When the spacecraft decelerates to a certain altitude via parachute, the airbag landing system rapidly inflates and deploys in preparation for the final landing. If the vertical and horizontal landing velocities are predicted previously, we expect to minimize the peak of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y^s$$\end{document}-axis and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x^s$$\end{document}-axis accelerations by quickly adjusting the inflation and venting strategy of the airbag landing system. This multi-objective optimization problem is defined as follows:12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned}&\left\{ \begin{array}{rl} \textrm{min} a_{y,\textrm{max}}^s (p_m,p_{ab},g_v)\\ \textrm{min} a_{x,\textrm{max}}^s (p_m,p_{ab},g_v) \end{array}\right. \quad s.t. \quad \begin{array}{rl} 101\textrm{KPa} \le & p_m \le 130\textrm{KPa} \\ 130\textrm{KPa} \le & p_{ab} \le 160\textrm{KPa} \\ 3\textrm{g} \le & g_v \le 7\textrm{g} \end{array} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In this work, the CNN model is combined with a non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA-II) to find Pareto optimal solutions50. This is a stochastic search algorithm based on the genetic mechanism and the fittest survival principle. The fitness function is introduced to assess individuals and determine the evolutionary direction of the population, which is based on the predictions of the CNN model. We use the Python pymoo toolbox to conduct the multi-objective optimization51.

Conclusion

A simulation-based deep learning model is proposed for predicting impact responses of the spacecraft soft landing on the airbag landing system. This surrogate model can quickly predict three types of acceleration profiles at the center of mass of the spacecraft under different initial velocities and airbag parameters. Based on that, to minimize the peak accelerations, the multi-objective genetic algorithm is used to optimize the inflation and venting strategy. The main conclusions are as follows: Based on the principle of Markov Decision Process (MDP), a novel architecture of the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) was proposed to learn the sequential acceleration data points generated by simulation. The CNN model takes initial landing velocities, initial pressures of airbags, and the overload threshold for venting as inputs. Features in the hidden layer are formed in a ‘time-stepping’ manner, and three types of acceleration histories are output simultaneously. There is a good agreement between the results of the CNN model and the finite element model, as seen by the reasonable range of deviations of peak acceleration, which range from about 5.4% to about 9.6%. The CNN model predicts \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^5$$\end{document} times faster than the finite element model. Compared with the LSTM model, this CNN model is less affected by the length of time step.The influence of input parameters is investigated by the relevance analysis and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP). The initial horizontal velocity and the acceleration threshold for venting have a great effect on the prediction accuracy, which are positively correlated with the Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The initial velocities and airbag parameters have different levels of influence on each peak acceleration. Basically, the peak accelerations are positively correlated with the initial velocities. The overload threshold and the initial pressure of main venting airbags play a better role than the initial pressure of anti- bottom airbags in regulating the peak accelerations.Combining the CNN model with a Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm (NSGA-II), we found Pareto optimal solutions of inflation and venting schemes for the moderate and extreme landing case to minimize the peak accelerations. The overlap between the solution clusters of the two cases gives a suitable solution for both cases. The optimized airbag landing system reduces the peak acceleration along the spacecraft’s rotation axis by 45.6% for the moderate case and by 33.2% for the extreme case.The proposed approach offers great potential for improving the efficiency of performance analysis and optimization of complex rigid-flexible coupled systems. We expect a more comprehensive deep learning framework to include more initial conditions and design parameters (e.g. initial attitude and geometric parameters) for a wider range of landing cases.

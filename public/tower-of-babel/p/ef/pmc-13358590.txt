Effects of regular swimming exercise on caveolin, insulin signaling, and SREBP-1c in adipose tissue of high-fat-fed ovariectomized rats
Authors: Jin Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract[Purpose]This study examined whether regular exercise modulates subcutaneous adipose tissue expression of caveolae-related genes (Cav-1, Cav-2, and Cav-3), insulin signaling mediators (IR, IRS-1, and IRS-2), and sterol regulatory element-binding protein-1c (SREBP-1c) in ovariectomized (OVX) rats.[Methods]Thirty-week-old female Sprague–Dawley rats were ovariectomized and assigned to general diet (OVX-GD), high-fat diet (OVX-H), or high-fat diet plus exercise (OVX-H+EX) groups (n = 7). Swimming exercise performed for 60 min/day, 5 days/week for 8 weeks. After 12-h fast, inguinal adipose tissue was collected and mRNA expression was analyzed by semi-quantitative RT-PCR. Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test (p < 0.05).[Results]High-fat feeding increased body weight and fat mass compared with the OVX-GD group. Despite no reduction in food intake, exercise attenuated weight gain and reduced final body weight and fat mass (p < 0.05). OVX-H rats showed increased Cav-1, Cav-2, and SREBP-1c expression and decreased IR and IRS-1 expression. Exercise reversed these changes. IRS-2 expression was also significantly increased in the exercise group (p < 0.05), whereas Cav-3 expression did not differ among the groups.[Conclusion]Regular exercise may improve adipose tissue metabolic regulation in OVX rats by modulating the mRNA expression of caveolae related genes and suppressing lipogenic gene activity.Keywords: postmenopausal obesity, exercise, insulin resistance, SREBP-1c, caveolin, IRS

INTRODUCTION

Postmenopausal obesity is characterized by estrogen deficiency associated increases in adiposity and metabolic dysfunction. Menopausal status is strongly associated with changes in fat distribution and increased cardiometabolic risk [1,2]. Central adiposity is strongly associated with insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and an increased risk of cardiovascular disease [3,4]. The global prevalence of obesity and its metabolic consequences continues to increase, particularly among postmenopausal women, who are vulnerable to estrogen-deficiency–associated metabolic disturbances [5].

Adipose tissue is increasingly recognized as an active endocrine organ that regulates systemic metabolic homeostasis through adipokine secretion and insulin signaling pathways [6,7]. Impaired insulin signaling within adipocytes plays a central role in the development of insulin resistance and metabolic deterioration [6]. In addition, emerging evidence indicates that obesity-related adipose dysfunction involves cellular senescence, chronic low-grade inflammation, and alterations in extracellular vesicle–mediated signaling [8]. These processes may interact with membrane microdomain organization and receptor signaling efficiency. Caveolae (Cav-1, Cav-2, Cav-3) are specialized plasma membrane microdomains enriched in caveolins that function as scaffolding proteins and organize insulin receptor signaling complexes [9,10]. Alterations in caveolin expression have been reported in obesity and insulin-resistant states [11,12]. Experimental models have demonstrated that caveolin deficiency disrupts insulin receptor localization and impairs insulin signaling efficiency [13].

Sterol regulatory element-binding protein-1c (SREBP-1c) is a key transcription factor regulating lipogenic gene expression [14,15]. Activation of SREBP-1c pathways promotes fatty acid synthesis and lipid accumulation in adipocytes. Estrogen deficiency is associated with enhanced lipogenic activity and adiposity [2]. Furthermore, recent experimental studies have indicated that ovariectomy combined with high-fat feeding induces depot-specific alterations in adipose metabolism and estrogen receptor signaling, reinforcing the translational relevance of this model for postmenopausal metabolic research [16].

Regular aerobic exercise is widely recommended as a non-pharmacological intervention to prevent obesity-related metabolic dysfunction [17]. Exercise improves insulin sensitivity and lipid metabolism in humans and animal models [18]. Clinical investigations in postmenopausal women have demonstrated that aerobic training reduces abdominal adiposity and modulates metabolic regulators within adipose tissue, supporting the translational significance of exercise under estrogen-deficient conditions [19]. In ovariectomized animal models, swimming exercise attenuates weight gain and metabolic disturbances [14,15]. However, relatively few studies have simultaneously examined membrane-associated signaling components, intracellular insulin mediators, and lipogenic transcriptional regulators in adipose tissue under estrogen-deficient conditions.

Therefore, the present study investigated the effects of regular swimming exercise on caveolin expression, insulin signaling-related genes, and SREBP-1c expression in the subcutaneous adipose tissue of high-fat-fed ovariectomized rats.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Animals and Diet

Female Sprague–Dawley rats (30 weeks old) were obtained from an accredited laboratory animal supplier in (Seoul, Korea). The animals were housed in a facility where environmental conditions were automatically regulated at a temperature of 23 ± 1 °C and a relative humidity of 55 ± 3%. A controlled 12:12-h light-dark cycle was maintained throughout the study. All rats had ad libitum access to standard chow and water. All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Hanyang University (approval no. HY-IACUC-10-026) and were conducted in accordance with the institutional guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. All efforts were made to minimize animal suffering and reduce the number of animals used. After surgery, the animals were allowed a recovery period before dietary intervention (Table 1).

After a one-week acclimation period, the rats underwent bilateral ovariectomy [20] under anesthesia. Following surgery, a two-week recovery period was provided prior to the dietary intervention (Table 1). Completion of recovery was confirmed by the return to pre-operative food intake levels and complete healing of the surgical incision. Following successful recovery, ovariectomized (OVX) rats were randomly assigned to the OVX with general diet (OVXGD), OVX with high-fat diet (OVX-H), and OVX-H with exercise (OVX-H+EX) groups for an 8-week experimental period.

Exercise Training

The 8-week exercise training was conducted in a swimming chamber (diameter: 150 cm, height: 70 cm) at a water temperature of 30–32 °C.

Swimming exercise was performed for 60 minutes (min) per day, 5 days/week, for 8 weeks. The exercise time was gradually increased over the first week (5 min on the first day, followed by increments of 10 min per day until reaching 60 min) to allow adaptation to the exercise [20].

Tissue Collection

After the 8-week experimental period, all rats were fasted for 12 h to standardize their metabolic status. The animals were euthanized using an overdose of pentobarbital sodium. Subcutaneous adipose tissue was collected from the inguinal region, and weighed. Samples were immediately snap-frozen and placed on dry ice and stored at -80 °C for subsequent total RNA extraction.

RT-PCR Analysis

Total RNA was isolated from the subcutaneous adipose tissue using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). RNA samples were reverse-transcribed using SuperScript II (Gibco BRL, Rockville, MD, USA) with a mixture of oligo-dT and random primers. PCR was performed using Taq DNA Polymerase (TAKARA). β-actin was used as an internal control for normalization. Band densities were analyzed using a gel documentation system (Gel Doc 2000, Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA).

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using the SPSS software (version 18.0). All data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical significance was determined using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

RESULTS

Body weight increased significantly in the OVX-H group compared with the OVX-GD group (Table 3). In contrast, rats in the OVX-H+EX group exhibited significantly attenuated weight gain, despite comparable food intake among the groups. The food efficiency ratio (FER) was significantly higher in the OVX-H group than in the OVX-GD group, whereas regular exercise markedly reduced the FER in OVX-H+EX rats. Subcutaneous adipose tissue mass was markedly increased in the OVX-H group compared with the mass observed in the OVX-GD group (Figure 1). Regular exercise significantly reduced subcutaneous fat accumulation in OVX-H+EX rats, restoring adipose tissue mass to levels comparable to those in the OVX-GD group.

Regular swimming exercise significantly restored these altered expression levels, as evidenced by a significant increase in both IR and IRS-1 mRNA levels in the OVX-H+EX group compared with the OVX-H group (p < 0.01 and p < 0.01, respectively). Additionally, IRS-2 mRNA expression was significantly higher in the OVX-H+EX group than in the OVX-H group (p < 0.05). Although the mean values of the OVX-H+EX group were visually distinct from those of the OVX-GD group, Tukey’s post-hoc test indicated no statistically significant differences between the OVX-GD and OVX-H+EX groups for either IR or IRS-2 (p > 0.05).

SREBP-1c mRNA expression was markedly increased in adipocytes from OVX-H rats compared with that in OVX-GD rats (Figure 4). Notably, regular exercise significantly suppressed SREBP-1c expression in OVX-H+EX rats, indicating reduced lipogenic activity in the adipose tissue.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrated that regular swimming attenuates obesity-associated transcriptional alterations in adipose tissue under estrogen-deficient conditions. Estrogen deficiency combined with high-fat feeding significantly increased adiposity, consistent with previous reports describing menopause-associated changes in fat distribution and metabolic vulnerability [1,2]. Central adiposity is closely associated with insulin resistance and cardiometabolic risk [3]. Impaired insulin signaling is a hallmark of obesity-related metabolic dysfunction [4,5]. In the present study, IR and IRS-1 mRNA expression levels were significantly reduced in high-fat-diet (HFD) fed ovariectomized rats, indicating the disruption of insulin receptor signaling in adipocytes. Regular swimming exercise significantly restored IR and IRS-1 expression. Aerobic exercise has been widely shown to enhance insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism [16,17]. Recent experimental studies have confirmed that exercise training improves adipose insulin signaling and reduces hepatic and systemic insulin resistance under estrogen-deficient conditions [22,23]. Caveolae are specialized plasma membrane microdomains enriched in caveolins that regulate insulin receptor localization and signal compartmentalization [6,7]. Altered caveolin expression has been reported in obesity and insulin-resistant states [8,9]. In the present study, Cav-1 and Cav-2 expression levels were elevated in the OVX-H group and were normalized by exercise training. Balanced caveolin expression appears to be essential for maintaining membrane structural organization and insulin receptor efficiency [9].

SREBP-1c is a central transcriptional regulator of lipogenesis [10–12]. Estrogen deficiency enhances lipogenic activity and adipose remodeling [2,13]. Consistent with these mechanisms, SREBP-1c expression was markedly increased in HFD-fed ovariectomized rats. Regular swimming exercise significantly suppressed SREBP-1c expression, suggesting attenuation of the lipogenic drive. Recent reviews have emphasized that structured exercise programs modulate adipose tissue gene expression and metabolic flexibility in obese and postmenopausal women [18,21]. In addition, emerging evidence has highlighted the interactions between exercise, gut microbiota, and estrogen-related metabolic regulation [21]. Exercise interventions, including resistance and endurance training, have been shown to protect against insulin resistance and metabolic deterioration in ovariectomized animal models [22]. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses have further confirmed that various exercise modalities significantly improve insulin sensitivity and adipose metabolic markers in populations with metabolic disease [24].

Unlike previous studies that focused primarily on systemic metabolic outcomes, the present study provides molecular-level evidence of coordinated transcriptional regulation involving membrane structural proteins, insulin signaling mediators, and lipogenic transcription factors in subcutaneous adipose tissue. These findings support the role of aerobic exercise as an integrated molecular regulator of postmenopausal metabolic conditions. However, this study had several limitations.

First and most importantly, the absence of a sham-operated control group means that the successful establishment of an estrogen-deficient state was not directly validated against a baseline hormonal condition. Although the physiological changes observed in our OVX groups, such as weight gain and metabolic alterations, were highly consistent with established postmenopausal models, the lack of direct validation (e.g., serum estradiol levels or uterine weight) should be considered when interpreting the results. Second, only mRNA expression levels were assessed without protein-level validation. Future studies should incorporate Western blotting and downstream signaling pathway analyses to confirm whether these transcriptional changes translate into functional protein expression. Third, only subcutaneous adipose tissue was examined. Depot-specific; differences in adipose remodeling and inflammatory responses warrant further investigation in both the visceral and subcutaneous compartments.

In summary, our findings demonstrate that regular swimming exercise reverses HFD-induced alterations in the mRNA expression of Cav-1, Cav-2, and SREBP-1c in the adipose tissue of OVX rats. Although this study focused on transcriptional changes, the results suggest that exercise may act as a potential therapeutic intervention to mitigate adipose metabolic dysfunction under estrogen-deficient conditions by modulating caveolin-related pathways.

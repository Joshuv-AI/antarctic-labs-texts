Optimizing Drill Guide Positioning for Foveal Targeting in Arthroscopic One-Bone Tunnel Triangular Fibrocartilage Complex Repair: A Radiographic and Magnetic Resonance Imaging-Based Study
Authors: Jae Jun Nam, In Cheul Choi, Yeongyoon Koh, Jong Woong Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo determine optimal drill guide positioning over the articular disc of the triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) for accurately targeting the foveal insertion of the TFCC during arthroscopic one-bone tunnel transosseous repair using radiographic and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based analysis.MethodsA retrospective review was conducted on 60 patients who underwent arthroscopic one-bone tunnel TFCC repair for Palmer type 1B foveal tears. Postoperative radiographs were analyzed to measure the bone tunnel’s starting point, angle relative to the ulnar shaft, and tunnel length. These parameters were then used to set the expected position of the drill guide tip over the articular disc of the TFCC on preoperative MRI scans.ResultsThe mean starting point of the bone tunnel was 12.0 mm proximal to the ulnar styloid tip, with an average tunnel angle of 37.0° and length of 10.0 mm. MRI analysis localized the drill guide tip approximately 4.0 mm radial to the medial aspect of the ulnar styloid over the articular disc of the TFCC. This location corresponded to the lateral one-third of a line connecting the center of the sigmoid notch to the center of the ulnar styloid process under the radiocarpal arthroscopic view.ConclusionsThese radiologic and anatomic parameters provide a guide for positioning the TFCC drill guide during arthroscopic one-bone tunnel repair. Targeting the lateral one-third of a line between the center of the sigmoid notch and ulnar styloid under the radiocarpal arthroscopic view offers a practical landmark to optimize bone tunnel placement for targeting the foveal insertion of the TFCC.Keywords: Triangular fibrocartilage, Arthroscopy, Wrist, Transosseous repair, Bone tunnel

Disruption of the foveal attachment of the triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) is a well-recognized cause of distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) instability and may lead to progressive joint degeneration and ulnar-sided wrist pain.1,2) Multiple surgical techniques have been developed to address foveal TFCC tears, including inside-out, outside-in, all-inside arthroscopic, and open approaches.3,4,5,6,7,8,9)

Among these, arthroscopic transosseous suture repair has gained increasing popularity due to its technical simplicity, favorable clinical outcomes, and ability to restore foveal tension with minimal soft tissue disruption.10,11,12,13,14) Biomechanical studies have demonstrated that arthroscopic transosseous repair provides comparable or superior stability to open techniques, with the added advantages of reduced morbidity and a lower complication profile.15,16)

Various methods have been described for creating bone tunnels to facilitate foveal repair, including dual Kirschner-wire (K-wire) holes, small-caliber multiple tunnels (2 mm), and single large tunnels (3–4 mm).9,12,17,18) However, significant variability exists in the literature regarding the ideal starting point for drilling, tunnel angulation relative to the ulnar shaft, and the arthroscopic positioning of the drill guide tip over the articular disc of the TFCC. This heterogeneity likely reflects differences in tunnel geometry and suture passage strategies across techniques.

Despite these variations, the consistent goal of transosseous suture repair is to anatomically target the foveal insertion of the deep limb of the TFCC.19) Precise tunnel positioning is critical to ensure effective load transmission and long-term DRUJ stability. However, a practical challenge during arthroscopic repair is that the operator cannot directly visualize the fovea, as it is obscured by the articular disc of the TFCC, which conceals this bony landmark.

In this study, we retrospectively analyzed patients who underwent arthroscopic one-bone tunnel (3.6 mm) transosseous TFCC repair for Palmer type 1B foveal tears. Our objective was to identify reproducible radiologic parameters for optimal drill guide positioning over the articular disc of the TFCC and to propose arthroscopic guidelines for accurately targeting the foveal footprint under the radiocarpal arthroscopy. This study aims to enhance consistency and improve the technical reproducibility of the arthroscopic one-bone tunnel repair technique.

METHODS

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Korea University Anam Hospital (IRB No. 2024AN0510). Written informed consent was obtained from legally authorized representatives for anonymized patient information to be published in this article.

Study Design and Patient Enrollment

A retrospective review was performed on 60 patients who underwent arthroscopic one-bone tunnel transosseous TFCC foveal repair between October 2017 and October 2023 at a tertiary academic center. Patients were included in the study if they had a confirmed diagnosis of a Palmer type 1B foveal TFCC tear (with or without a superficial fiber tear; Atzei class 2 or 3 TFCC tear). Additional inclusion criteria included a history of ulnar wrist pain that worsened with pronation and ulnar deviation, positive results on the ulnocarpal stress test,20) and evidence of DRUJ instability confirmed through a ballottement test.21,22,23) The diagnosis of a TFCC foveal tear was confirmed through preoperative 3.0-T magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and a positive hook test during arthroscopic examination.21,23) The MRI evaluations were performed with the arm raised to replicate its position in the traction tower during surgery.

Exclusion criteria included patients younger than 18 years of age, those with a history of wrist fractures (distal radius or carpal fractures), prior ulnar shortening osteotomy or TFCC debridement for degenerative TFCC central tears, concomitant ulnar head pathologies such as ulnar styloid fracture non-unions or positive ulnar variance, and those who had undergone prior wrist surgery, including TFCC repair.

The postoperative plain radiograph of each selected patient was analyzed to assess the configuration of the bone tunnel. Measurements obtained from these radiographs were used as references to estimate and set the position of the drill guide tip over the TFCC on the patients’ MRI scans. Preoperative and postoperative functional scores were collected and compared to assess the clinical outcomes of the surgery for all enrolled patients. Radiographic and MRI Analysis

Postoperative plain radiographs were analyzed using computerized measurements (INFINITT picture archiving and communication system [PACS]). To minimize projection distortion and magnification errors inherent in plain radiography, the integrated digital scale and calibration tools within the PACS software were utilized. Radiologic parameters included the starting point of the bone tunnel, measured as the horizontal distance from the tip of the ulnar styloid (Fig. 2A); the angle of the bone tunnel relative to the longitudinal axis of the ulnar shaft (Fig. 2B); and the length of the tunnel from its starting point at the lateral ulnar surface to the foveal insertion site (Fig. 2C). These parameters were measured from postoperative radiographs obtained 5 weeks after surgery, when a neutral wrist posteroanterior (PA) view was available.

Based on these radiographic parameters, the expected intra-articular position of the drill guide tip was estimated using preoperative MRI scans. The location of the presumed drill guide tip over the articular disc of the TFCC was determined by measuring the distance from the center of the medial border of the ulnar styloid process to the expected drill guide position (Fig. 3, indicated by d), as well as the distance between the center of the sigmoid notch (Fig. 3, indicated by e). The ratio of these 2 distances (d/e) was calculated to serve as a reference for arthroscopic drill guide tip positioning over the articular disc of the TFCC under the radiocarpal arthroscopic view (Fig. 4A).

Auxiliary Computed Tomography Validation Study

To quantitatively validate the accuracy of the proposed MRI-derived targeting method, a comparative analysis was performed on a subset of the study cohort. For this validation study, consistent with the methodology described in the MRI analysis, the (d/e) ratio was calculated using the distance from the medial border of the ulnar styloid process to the expected drill guide position (d) and the reference distance to the center of the sigmoid notch (e). These parameters were measured identically on the postoperative computed tomography (CT) images to derive the actual ratio, allowing for a direct comparison between the predicted and actual values. The consistency between the preoperative predicted ratio (MRI) and the postoperative actual ratio (CT) was assessed using a paired t-test. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Clinical Outcome Measurements

The Modified Mayo Wrist Score (MMWS) and Quick Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder, and Hand (QuickDASH) questionnaires were collected for all enrolled patients both preoperatively and at the final follow-up, conducted at least 12 months after surgery. Pain during provocative motion was assessed using a 10-point visual analog scale (VAS). DRUJ stability was evaluated preoperatively and postoperatively using the ballottement test. Grip strength was measured with a hydraulic hand dynamometer (Baseline; FEI) and expressed as a percentage of the strength of the contralateral side. All measured parameters were compared to assess meaningful improvements in functional outcomes.

Patient-reported outcomes, including MMWS, QuickDASH, grip strength, and VAS scores, were collected by a physical therapist at our institution and reviewed by an orthopedic hand surgeon for accuracy. The stability of the DRUJ was evaluated by orthopedic hand surgeons during outpatient clinic visits. Clinical outcome assessments were performed 12 months postoperatively and during subsequent annual follow-ups.

Surgical Technique

All procedures were performed by orthopedic hand surgeons at a tertiary academic institution using a standardized arthroscopic one-bone tunnel transosseous foveal repair technique for TFCC tears, as previously described.10,11,12,13,14)

Patient positioning and preparation

Patients were placed supine on the operating table. Following standard sterile preparation, a wrist traction tower was applied. Under general or regional anesthesia, DRUJ instability was re-evaluated prior to arthroscopic access.

Arthroscopic access

A 2.4-mm arthroscope (Arthrex) was introduced through the 3/4 portal, with a 6R portal created for instrumentation. Arthroscopic inspection confirmed Palmer type 1B TFCC tears (proximal component [PC] only or combined with distal component [DC]; Atzei class 2 or 3). Foveal involvement was verified using the hook test, which demonstrated a loss of tension and radial-distal displacement of the TFCC.

Bone tunnel creation

A longitudinal incision was made over the lateral aspect of the ulna, from the ulnar styloid tip extending proximally by approximately 2 cm, between the extensor carpi ulnaris and flexor carpi ulnaris tendons. A C-Ring Aiming Guide (Arthrex) was introduced through the 6R portal and positioned arthroscopically at the lateral one-third of a line connecting the center of the sigmoid notch to the center of the ulnar styloid process (Fig. 4).

The external drill entry point was set 12 mm proximal to the ulnar styloid tip, at the proximal end of the convex curvature along the lateral border of the ulna. A 1.1-mm K-wire was advanced through the targeting guide, with the angle confirmed to be approximately 37° relative to the ulnar shaft, without the use of fluoroscopic control. In the sagittal plane, the K-wire was aligned parallel to the ulnar shaft to avoid dorsal or volar tunnel deviation. A 2.7-mm cannulated drill was first used to create the transosseous tunnel, followed by enlargement with a 3.6-mm cannulated drill.

Suture passage and fixation

A 2-0 high-tensile-strength nonabsorbable suture (Ethibond; Ethicon) was passed through the TFCC and bone tunnel using a 20-gauge needle. A second 18-gauge needle equipped with a lasso loop was used to retrieve the suture via the 6R portal. The second puncture was placed at least 4 mm away from the first to preserve tissue integrity. A second suture was passed in a similar fashion, creating a cruciform configuration to improve fixation strength. The configuration of the 2 sutures was adjusted according to the tear pattern of the TFCC. All 4 suture limbs were secured to the lateral ulna with a 2.5-mm PushLock suture anchor (Arthrex), positioned approximately 5 mm proximal to the tunnel.

Statistical Analysis

A paired t-test was used to compare preoperative and postoperative outcome measures, including MMWS, QuickDASH, grip strength (expressed as a percentage of the contralateral side), and VAS pain scores. Radiologic measurements included the distance from the drill entry point to the tip of the ulnar styloid, tunnel angle relative to the ulnar shaft, tunnel length, and presumed drill guide tip position over the TFCC on MRI. Statistical analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS software version 20 (IBM Corp.), with significance set at p < 0.05. All radiographic measurements were independently performed by 2 experienced orthopedic hand surgeons (ICC and JWP ). Interrater reliability was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), with the level of agreement classified as follows: poor (< 0.40), fair (0.40–0.59), good (0.60–0.74), and excellent (0.75–1.00).24)

RESULTS

A total of 60 patients were included in the study, with a mean age of 27.2 years (range, 18–55 years). The cohort consisted of 27 males and 33 females, with an average follow-up duration of 34.8 months (range, 12–60 months). Thirty patients had an arthroscopically confirmed Palmer type 1B TFCC tear involving the PC only (Atzei class 3), while the remaining 30 patients had combined PC and DC tears (Atzei class 2).

All patients underwent foveal repair using the arthroscopic one-bone tunnel transosseous suture technique. Preoperative and final postoperative outcome scores demonstrated significant improvements in MMWS, Quick-DASH, grip strength (as a percentage of the contralateral side), and VAS pain scores (all p < 0.001) (Table 1). At the final follow-up, all patients demonstrated restoration of DRUJ stability, evidenced by a negative ballottement test and return to full functional activities.

Radiographic analysis revealed that the mean starting point of the bone tunnel was 12.0 mm (range, 10.2–12.7 mm) proximal to the tip of the ulnar styloid process, corresponding to the proximal end of the convex curvature of the ulnar head. The mean tunnel angle relative to the longitudinal axis of the ulnar shaft was 37.0° (range, 30.2°–40.6°), and the average tunnel length was 10.0 mm (range, 9.2–13.0 mm), consistently targeting the foveal insertion site.

MRI-based estimation of the intra-articular drill guide tip location over the articular disc of the TFCC revealed an average position of 4.0 mm (range, 3.4–4.6 mm) radial to the medial border of the center of the ulnar styloid on coronal images and 4.0 mm (range, 3.5–4.6 mm) on axial images. The average distance between the center of the sigmoid notch and the center of the medial aspect of the ulnar styloid measured 12.3 mm (range, 11.2–12.9 mm) on coronal MRI and 12.7 mm (range, 12.1–13.2 mm) on axial MRI. Finally, the calculated ratio of the drill guide tip distance to the total span from the sigmoid notch to the ulnar styloid was approximately 0.3 (or 1 : 3).

The interrater reliability for all parameters showed excellent agreement, with an overall ICC of 0.88 (95% CI, 0.84–0.91). This indicates high consistency in the measurements provided by the 2 independent raters. Postoperative CT scan analysis confirmed the analysis showed excellent agreement between the planned and executed positions. The mean (d/e) ratio on the preoperative MRI was 0.29 ± 0.03, and the mean (d/e) ratio of the actual bone tunnel on the postoperative CT was 0.30 ± 0.03. A paired t-test revealed no statistically significant difference between the predicted MRI landmark and the actual CT outcome (p = 0.35), confirming that the surgical technique accurately reproduced the intended target. DISCUSSION

This study presents radiologic and arthroscopic parameters to guide drill trajectory for targeting the foveal insertion during arthroscopic one-bone tunnel TFCC repair. The proposed technique is based on an analysis of 60 postoperative cases and provides practical landmarks for consistent tunnel placement without reliance on intraoperative fluoroscopy. The optimal starting point for the bone tunnel was 12 mm proximal to the tip of the ulnar styloid process, aligned with the proximal end of the convex curvature of the ulnar head. A mean drill angle of 37° and tunnel length of 10 mm allowed for accurate targeting of the foveal insertion site of the TFCC.

Regarding the surgical technique, while previous studies by Shinohara et al.17) and Nakamura et al.9) also utilized a drill guide for bone tunnel creation, their descriptions were primarily limited to the starting point—suggesting the ulnar neck or a point 15 mm from the lateral border—without clearly defining the intra-articular end target point over the TFCC. This absence of a specific intra-articular target over the TFCC has historically presented technical limitations in creating an adequate bone tunnel. A distinct novelty of our study is the provision of clear guidelines for both ends of the trajectory: a starting point 12 mm proximal from the ulnar styloid tip and an intra-articular target point over the TFCC at the lateral one-third of a line between the center of the sigmoid notch and ulnar styloid under the radiocarpal arthroscopic view.

By positioning the starting point closer to the styloid (12 mm) compared to the 15–25 mm suggested in previous literature, we create a shorter and wider tunnel. This geometry is particularly advantageous for the one-bone tunnel technique as it maximizes the freedom of suture manipulation, facilitating easier suture passage and handling. Furthermore, maintaining the tunnel within the lateral cortex and proximal to the articular surface of the ulnar head prevents the risk of iatrogenic ulnar styloid fracture.

In addition, several techniques for foveal repair have been described, including dual K-wire tunnels and small-diameter single tunnels. Our approach utilizes a 3.6-mm one-bone tunnel, which may allow for broader suture passage within the foveal footprint. Given that the TFCC foveal insertion area measures 29.7–34 mm2, with a long diameter of 9 ± 2 mm and a short diameter of 6 ± 1 mm, our 3.6-mm bone tunnel technique theoretically covers 60% of the shorter diameter and 40% of the longer diameter.25,26) This tunnel size allows engagement of a substantial portion of the native footprint, promoting a broad healing surface and enabling various suture configurations based on the TFCC tear pattern. While our study did not assess healing directly, previous literature suggests that bone tunnel creation may enhance healing via marrow stimulation.14)

This study has several limitations. First, although postoperative CT confirmed that the bone tunnel was created at the ideal foveal insertion point using our targeting method, CT validation was performed in only a subset of 15 patients who provided specific informed consent for the additional imaging analysis rather than in the entire cohort. Second, the sagittal orientation angle of the tunnel was not assessed due to the limitations inherent in standard radiographic imaging. However, if the starting point and intra-articular end point of the drill guide are accurately defined, the bone tunnel angle in the sagittal plane is unlikely to be of critical importance. Third, a comparative analysis with other techniques was not performed and is needed to determine whether the proposed parameters offer superior clinical outcomes. Additionally, this study did not account for anatomical variability such as ulnar head deformity or ulnar styloid nonunion.

This study defines practical, radiologically derived parameters for drill guide positioning in arthroscopic one-bone tunnel TFCC foveal repair. A starting point 12 mm proximal to the ulnar styloid tip and a 37° drill angle consistently targeted the foveal insertion using a 3.6-mm tunnel. Under radiocarpal arthroscopic visualization, targeting the lateral one-third of a line between the center of the sigmoid notch and the ulnar styloid over the articular disc of the TFCC provides a practical landmark for optimizing bone tunnel placement toward the TFCC foveal insertion. This approach may improve the accuracy and efficiency of tunnel placement without fluoroscopic guidance, while allowing for versatile suture configurations within the foveal footprint. Further comparative and biomechanical studies are warranted to validate its clinical advantages across varying anatomical presentations.

The impact of radiometabolic features on the stage and survival in small cell lung cancer
Authors: Coskun Dogan, Sedanur Coskun Tok, Sibel Karakaya, Elif Torun Parmaksiz, Ferhan Karatas, Aysenur Toksöz Yildirim, Serkan Güngör, Onur İncealtın, Sacit İçten
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) is a highly aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) plays a critical role in staging, as elevated FDG uptake is associated with tumor aggressiveness and metabolic activity. Although radiometabolic parameters such as maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) have been proposed as prognostic markers, existing data remain inconsistent. This study aimed to investigate the association between these parameters and disease stage and overall survival (OS) in SCLC patients.METHODS:Patients diagnosed with lung cancer between May 2022 and June 2023 were retrospectively reviewed and divided into two groups: those with SCLC (Group 1) and those with non-SCLC (Group 2). Demographic, clinical, radiological (thoracic CT), and radiometabolic (PET/CT) features, treatment history, staging, and mortality data (based on the national death registry) were collected.RESULTS:A total of 111 patients (mean age: 65.2 years) were included, of whom 65 (58.6%) had SCLC. In the SCLC group, 55 (85.9%) had extensive-stage disease and 9 (14.1%) had limited-stage disease. Mean SUVmax, MTV, and TLG values were significantly higher in extensive-stage disease (12.5, 87.2, and 624.2, respectively) than in limited-stage disease (7.1, 7.0, and 37.0) (P < 0.05). Median OS was 12.5 months. Cutoff values for poor prognosis were identified as SUVmax ≥10, MTV ≥40, and TLG ≥170, with significantly shorter OS observed above these thresholds (P < 0.05). While SUVmax alone was not an independent predictor of survival (P > 0.05), MTV and TLG were identified as significant prognostic indicators in multivariate analysis (P < 0.05).CONCLUSION:In SCLC, higher SUVmax values are associated with advanced disease stage. However, MTV and TLG may serve as more reliable independent prognostic biomarkers for survival.

Background

Lung cancers are the most commonly diagnosed and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths. They rank first in incidence among men and fourth among women.[12] Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) accounts for approximately 14% of all lung cancers. One of the main features that distinguishes SCLC from non-SCLCs (NSCLCs) is its more aggressive clinical course. SCLC is characterized by a rapid doubling time, high growth fraction, and early development of widespread metastases. The majority of SCLC cases (at least two-thirds) present at the diagnosis with extensive-stage disease. Several factors influence survival in SCLC, as in all lung cancers. Poor performance status, advanced age, male gender, extensive disease stage, weight loss, high lactate dehydrogenase, accompanying gene mutations, low albumin levels, and comorbid conditions are the most significant negative prognostic factors.[345] Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) is a metabolic imaging method. 18-F fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG), a glucose analog, accumulates in malignant tumor cells without being metabolized, allowing for the metabolic imaging of tumors through PET/CT, which enables the evaluation of cell viability and tumor metabolism. Tumors with high FDG uptake are known to be metabolically more active. There is a direct correlation between FDG uptake, tumor aggressiveness, proliferative activity, and cell differentiation.[67] Since SCLCs are highly metabolically active, PET/CT is considered highly useful for staging the disease.[18] There are studies reporting that PET-CT indirectly affects prognosis in SCLC because it influences staging. However, there are only a limited number of studies investigating FDG uptake in SCLCs and its direct impact on prognosis. Although some studies have reported that certain PET/CT parameters may have prognostic value in SCLC patients, the number of such studies is low. Furthermore, these studies have been conducted with small sample sizes and present conflicting results, indicating a gap in the literature on this subject.[910]

This study aims to evaluate FDG uptake in SCLC compared to other lung cancers and investigate the effect of FDG uptake on staging and survival in SCLC.

Materials and Methods

Patient population

Our study, which was approved by the ethics committee (decision no: 154, date: May 15, 2024) and was conducted in accordance with the International Declaration of Helsinki, was planned as a multicenter and retrospective study. For this purpose, we reviewed the files of cases diagnosed with lung cancer between May 2022 and June 2023 in the pulmonary disease clinics of our centers. The cases were divided into two groups: those with a final diagnosis of SCLC (Group 1) and NSCLC (Group 2). Patients aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of primary lung cancer and accessible PET-CT reports were included in the study. Cases with a histopathological diagnosis of extrapulmonary malignancy or suspected pulmonary metastasis, those with unknown treatment history, those who did not receive standard chemoradiotherapy, and patients lost to follow-up were excluded from the study. Informed written consent forms were obtained from all patients for this study and other diagnostic procedures.

Clinical, demographic, radiological (thoracic CT), and radiometabolic (PET-CT) features, (maximum standardized uptake value [SUVmax], metabolic tumor volume [MTV], total lesion glycolysis [TLG]) final histopathological diagnoses, treatment histories (standard oncologic/surgical treatments), and the time of death (from the national death reporting system) of the deceased patients were recorded. Cases were classified according to the 8th edition of the tumor/node/metastasis staging system as Group 1 with limited and extensive stage SCLC, and Group 2 with early-stage (Stage 1–2) and locally advanced/advanced stage (Stage 3–4) NSCLC. Data from both groups were compared.

Positron emission tomography/computed tomography imaging protocol

Patients who had fasted for at least 8 h and had normal blood glucose levels were included in the procedure. PET/CT imaging was performed using a Philips Gemini TF high-speed integrated PET/CT system. PET images were acquired from the vertex to the upper thighs, with oral contrast–enhanced CT images obtained using a 5 mm slice thickness. Image acquisition was performed 60 min after the intravenous administration of 370–550 MBq (10–15 mCi) of F-18 FDG. An oral contrast agent was administered concurrently with the FDG injection.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were expressed as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and percentages for categorical variables. Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests were used for normal distribution. Group data were analyzed using Chi-square, Student’s-t-test, and Mann–Whitney U-tests. Survival analyses were performed using the Kaplan–Meier method, and comparisons were made using the Log-rank test. Cox regression analysis was used to assess the effect of other recorded variables that could affect survival. To identify the best discriminative (cutoff) SUV max, MTV, TLG value predicting survival, survival analyses were performed for various SUV max, MTV, TLG values, and the cutoff SUV max, MTV, TLG value was determined using the Log-rank test. A P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant in all tests.

Results

The records of 166 cases were retrospectively reviewed. Forty-six cases were excluded due to missing treatment histories, eight cases were excluded due to missing PET-CT reports, and one case was excluded due to suspected lung metastasis. A total of 111 cases meeting the inclusion criteria were included in the study. Of the patients, 88 (89.3%) were male, and 23 (20.7%) were female, with a mean age of 65.2 ± 9.6 years. The smoking history was 50.7 ± 27.2 pack-years. Histopathological examination revealed that 65 (58.6%) cases were diagnosed with SCLC (Group 1), and 46 (41.4%) with NSCLC (Group 2) [Table 1]. Table 2 presents the distribution of histopathological diagnoses among the cases. Seventy-seven cases (69.4%) had chronic diseases other than lung cancer, whereas 34 cases (30.6%) did not. The most common comorbidity was hypertension in 34 cases (30.6%). The most frequent symptom was dyspnea in 56 cases (50.5%) [Table 1]. Radiologically, the tumors were most commonly located in the right hemithorax (57.7%) and upper lobe (59.5%). The average tumor long-axis length was 5.97 ± 2.8 cm [Table 1]. Radiometabolically (PET-CT), the mean SUVmax value of the primary lesion was 12.9 ± 5.2. Mediastinal involvement was reported in 89 cases (80.2%), with a mean mediastinal SUVmax value of 8.2 ± 7.3 [Table 3].

When stages were examined, 56 (86.1%) of Group 1 cases had extensive-stage SCLC, whereas 9 (13.9%) had limited-stage disease. In Group 2, 36 cases (78.2%) had locally advanced/advanced stage (Stage 3–4) NSCLC. Table 2 presents the stage distribution for patients diagnosed with SCLC and NSCLC. Treatment characteristics showed that 97 cases (87.3%) received standard oncologic treatment, and 10 cases (21.8%) underwent surgical treatment.

In SCLC cases, the average overall survival (OS) was 12.5 ± 2.2 months (confidence interval [CI]: 8.23–16.91). The mean SUV max value in the extensive-stage SCLC group was 12.5 ± 3.9, whereas it was 7.1 ± 3.2 in the limited-stage SCLC group (P < 0.001). The optimal cutoff SUVmax value for predicting survival was found to be 9. The mean OS was 9.1 ± 1.8 months (CI: 5.59–12.6) for cases with SUVmax values of 10 or higher, whereas it was 29.6 ± 5.4 months (CI: 18.98–40.24) for those with SUVmax values of 9 or lower, with a statistically significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.04). A total of 44 SCLC cases had available MTV and TLG values. In the extensive stage SCLC group, the mean MTV and TLG values were 87.2 ± 69.8 and 624 ± 581.5, respectively, whereas in the limited stage SCLC group, these values were 7 ± 2.8 and 37 ± 11.3 (P = 0.014, 0.008). The optimal cutoff values for predicting survival were determined as 40 for MTV and 170 for TLG. Cases with MTV values ≥40 had a mean OS of 22.9 ± 4.3 months (95% CI: 14.3–31.4), whereas those with MTV values <40 had a mean OS of 9.3 ± 2.6 months (95% CI: 4.1–14.5), with the difference being statistically significant (P = 0.016). Similarly, cases with TLG values ≥170 had a mean OS of 19.6 ± 3.9 months (95% CI: 11.9–27.3), compared to 10.3 ± 3.2 months (95% CI: 4–16.6) in cases with TLG values <170; this difference was also statistically significant (P = 0.046). Discussion

In our study, investigating the radiometabolic characteristics of SCLC and the impact of these characteristics on tumor staging and survival, we found that SCLCs had lower SUVmax values compared to NSCLCs and exhibited more mediastinal involvement on PET-CT (P < 0.05). When examining the relationship between SUVmax values and stage and survival in the SCLC group, the SUVmax value was found to be 12.5 in extensive-stage SCLC cases and 7.1 in limited-stage SCLC cases (P < 0.001). In addition, the average survival of SCLC cases with an SUVmax value (≥10) was 9.1 months, whereas survival for SCLC cases with an SUVmax value of 9 or below was 29.6 months (P = 0.04). However, regression analysis showed that the SUVmax value was not an independent factor affecting survival (P = 0.335). In the regression analysis, SUV max alone did not serve as a significant predictor of survival in SCLC (P > 0.05). However, other radiometabolic parameters, specifically MTV and TLG, were identified as independent variables that significantly influenced survival outcomes (P < 0.05). Tumor cells with abnormally increased glucose metabolism accumulate 18F-FDG, a glucose analog, through membrane glucose transporters (GLUT-1 and GLUT-3), and the metabolic images of these tumors are obtained through PET-CT. Previous studies have demonstrated that in NSCLCs, high SUVmax values on PET-CT may have not only imaging value but also prognostic significance.[1112] Studies have shown that FDG-PET/CT has a prognostic value in SCLCs, but which parameters have a prognostic value and which of their cut-off values will be used are still inconsistent. A recent meta-analysis involving 19 studies[10] indicated that MTV and SUVmax measured on PET-CT had prognostic value, with MTV being a stronger prognostic indicator than SUVmax. In these meta-analysis studies, it was statistically proven that the SUVmax value was associated with OS. The mean SUVmax cutoff value of 14 studies, including 1194 cases, ranged from 5.1 to 16, and the median SUVmax value was found to be 7.

In our study, the optimal cutoff value was found to be 9. The authors of the meta-analysis noted the significant heterogeneity within the studies, which they considered a limitation, particularly due to the wide range of SUVmax values and cutoff points. Upon reviewing the literature, it becomes evident that most studies focus on NSCLC cases.[131415] However, Lee et al.,[16] in one of the few studies examining the prognostic importance of SUVmax values in SCLCs, found the average SUVmax value to be 8.7 in 76 SCLC cases. They reported that the average survival for cases with SUVmax values >8.7 was 11.7 months, compared to 24.3 months for those with SUVmax values <8.7, suggesting a relationship between high SUV max values and poor prognosis in SCLC cases. More recently, Kwon et al.[17] reported an average SUVmax value of 10 in 59 SCLC cases and found a statistically significant difference in survival between cases with SUV max values of 10 or below and those with SUVmax values of 11 or above, identifying high SUVmax values as an independent prognostic factor. The authors emphasized that, compared to other radiometabolic measurements (MTV and TLG), the measurement of SUVmax is more feasible and practical for routine clinical use in this study. Choi et al.[18] similarly demonstrated that high SUVmax values were an independent prognostic factor for survival in 118 SCLC cases, with the average SUVmax value in their study being 10.9, similar to our findings. In this study, the cutoff value for SUVmax was taken as 14, and survival comparisons were given as 13.2 months and 8.1 months (P = 0.009). Çimen et al.[19] in a study of 77 limited-stage SCLC patients, found a median SUV max value of 13.9 in survivors and 11.2 in deceased SCLC patients but did not find a relationship between SUV max and survival. They attributed this to the fact that all the cases in their study were limited-stage SCLC cases.

MTV, measured by PET-CT, an important radiometabolic imaging modality, represents the volume of metabolically active tumor tissue, whereas TLG reflects both the tumor volume and its metabolic activity. Numerous studies have demonstrated that high MTV and TLG values in SCLC are associated with poor prognosis.[20212223] The results of our study are consistent with the existing literature in this regard. Although MTV and TLG show promising potential as prognostic indicators, several limitations exist, including the lack of universal standard cutoff values, variability in their prognostic impact across different studies, the time-consuming and impractical nature of their measurements, and the fact that they are not routinely included in standard PET-CT reports.

Poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, adenosquamous carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma of the lung, all of which are high-grade tumors, tend to have high SUVmax values. The only exception in this group is well-differentiated lung adenocarcinoma, which typically exhibits a noninvasive pattern and relatively low SUVmax values.[2425] Although our study focused on investigating the radiometabolic characteristics of SCLCs, we found that SCLCs had lower SUV max values compared to NSCLCs. When we examined the literature, we did not find any study comparing the SUVmax value of SCLCs and NSCLC one-to-one. Hockmann et al.[26] reported a SUVmax value of 6.8 (range: 3.2–18.3) for limited-stage SCLC cases. In a different study examining the radiometabolic characteristics of NSCLCs, we found the average SUVmax value in 87 adenocarcinoma cases to be 11.4 ± 8.7, and the SUV max value in 134 squamous cell carcinoma cases to be 18.2.[27] We believe that the observed differences in our study are due to the small number of NSCLC cases and the division of cases into different histopathological subtypes.

Our study has several limitations that should not be overlooked when interpreting the results. One significant disadvantage is that it is a retrospective study, which impacts its strength. Due to its retrospective nature, there were some important data losses (such as patient performance status). In addition, the prognostic impact of radiometabolic parameters other than SUVmax, namely MTV and TLG, could be assessed in only a limited number of patients, which significantly reduced the statistical power of the study.

Conclusion

We can interpret the results of this study that 18-FDG-SUV max values, which are the most commonly used radiopharmaceuticals today and calculated with a quantitative system, may have a relationship with prognosis in SCLC, a lung cancer subtype that we encounter less frequently compared to other lung cancers, but as expected, it is not an independent predictor with a direct effect on prognosis. Although SUV max values are a relatively simple calculation process, it is known that there are many technical factors that affect the actual value obtained. The fact that there are different SUV max values within lung cancer subtypes, that there are different spectrum SUV max values within the same subtypes, and that there are very different cutoff values for survival in the studies conducted suggests that this subject needs to be supported by prospective studies with a large number of cases. Nevertheless, radiometabolic parameters such as MTV and TLG, although showing promising potential as independent predictors significantly associated with survival outcomes, have certain drawbacks – such as the absence of standardized cut-off values and the time-consuming and impractical nature of their measurement – which may limit their widespread clinical application.

Fto-mediated m6A demethylation of Lox drives atrial fibrosis and promotes atrial fibrillation in a murine model of hyperthyroidism
Authors: Han Gong, Chun Zhou, Ruopeng Tan, Lin Wang, Qi Su, Yang Liu, Xiaomeng Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsHyperthyroidism is a risk factor for atrial fibrillation (AF), and N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA methylation is crucial in cardiovascular regulation. However, the role of Fto-mediated m6A demethylation in hyperthyroidism-related AF remains unclear.Methods and resultsWe consecutively recruited 232 AF patients undergoing ablation, stratified into age-, gender-, and comorbidity-matched cohorts, 116 with manifest hyperthyroidism and 116 without manifest hyperthyroidism. Assessments included thyroid profiles, echocardiography, low-voltage area (LVA) mapping, and 1-year recurrence. T4-treated mice with cardiomyocyte-specific Fto knockout or AAV9-mediated Fto overexpression were used. Electrophysiological and structural properties were assessed via electrical mapping and echocardiography. Mechanisms were further investigated in neonatal rat atrial myocytes (NRAMs). The hyperthyroid group showed higher 1-year recurrence (19.8 vs. 5.2%, P < 0.001) and larger LVA (27.81 vs. 20.25%, P < 0.001). Hyperthyroidism independently predicted LVA expansion (odds ratio = 2.868, P < 0.001). In mice, Fto up-regulation increased atrial fibrosis and AF susceptibility, while its deletion attenuated T4-induced atrial fibrosis and AF. Wild-type Fto overexpression promoted AF via m6A-dependent enhancement of lysyl oxidase (Lox) expression. Studies in NRAMs demonstrated that Fto enhanced the transcription and translation of Lox by reducing m6A methylation on Lox mRNA. Lox inhibition with BAPN suppressed fibrosis and AF inducibility.ConclusionHyperthyroidism promoted atrial arrhythmogenicity through Fto-mediated m6A demethylation of Lox, increasing Lox expression and atrial fibrosis. Targeting Fto-m6A-Lox may offer a novel therapy for hyperthyroidism-associated AF.

Introduction

Hyperthyroidism is clinically classified as either manifest [suppressed thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) with elevated free thyroxine (T4) and/or triiodothyronine (T3)] or latent (suppressed TSH with normal free T4 and T3).1 The manifest form can lead to various complications, among which atrial fibrillation (AF) occurs with a prevalence of 5–15%.2 Following treatment, approximately two-thirds of patients spontaneously revert to sinus rhythm (SR), while one-third exhibit persistent AF despite normalized thyroid function.2–5 Established studies have documented ion channel remodelling in hyperthyroidism-related AF, including up-regulated Kv1.5 and Kv2.1, slowed sodium channel inactivation, and altered L-type calcium channel expression.6–10 However, the persistence of AF following euthyroid restoration and elevated recurrence following ablation together suggest the potential development of irreversible structural remodelling, a mechanism that requires further investigation.

The pathogenesis of AF involves complex epigenetic regulation across multiple levels, including DNA methylation, histone modifications such as histone deacetylase (HDAC)-mediated deacetylation, and RNA modifications.11,12 Among these, N6-methyladenosine (m6A) demethylation stands as the most abundant internal modification in eukaryotic mRNA and serves as a critical epigenetic regulator in cardiovascular diseases.13 The m6A modification is dynamically orchestrated by a multiprotein methyltransferase complex, comprising methyltransferase-like 3 (Mettl3), Mettl14, and Wilms tumour 1-associated protein (Wtap).14,15 Conversely, m6A demethylation is mediated by fat mass and obesity-associated protein (Fto) and Alkb homolog 5 (Alkbh5). Our transcriptomic analysis revealed a significant increase in Fto expression in atrial tissues of mice after 4 weeks of T4 administration. Further validation confirmed that the elevated Fto protein levels in T4-treated mouse atria primarily originated from cardiomyocytes. However, whether this Fto up-regulation contributes to increased susceptibility to AF, m6A-dependent or independent, remains unexplored.

In the present study, using cardiomyocyte-specific Fto knockout mice, we demonstrated that T4-induced up-regulation of Fto in atrial tissue promotes Lox mRNA expression through m6A demethylation, ultimately leading to increased atrial collagen deposition and enhanced susceptibility to AF. Targeting Lox may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for attenuating hyperthyroidism-associated AF progression.

Methods

Study population

The present investigation obtained review and approval from the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University (Approval No. PJ-KS-KY-2024-473) and was executed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent prior to their involvement. According to ESC guidelines (2024), diagnostic criteria for AF include the absence of distinct and regular P waves accompanied by irregular RR intervals, unless the patient has an atrioventricular block. Paroxysmal AF refers to episodes lasting ˂7 days that terminate spontaneously or with intervention, while persistent AF refers to episodes lasting more than 7 days that do not terminate spontaneously. Long-standing persistent AF is arbitrarily defined as continuous AF of at least 12 months’ duration but where rhythm control is still a treatment option in selected patients, distinguishing it from permanent AF.16 In our study, AF was classified as either paroxysmal or non-paroxysmal, the latter category encompassing both persistent and long-standing persistent AF.17

The hyperthyroidism group consisted of patients who had a documented history of manifest hyperthyroidism lasting over 1 year. Following treatment, these patients had either achieved a euthyroid state or progressed to a state of latent hyperthyroidism, meeting the clinical criteria for ablation therapy. Exclusion criteria included: (i) contraindications to anticoagulation; (ii) left atrial thrombus detected by transoesophageal echocardiography; (iii) significant structural heart disease, including moderate-to-severe valvular disease, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, dilated cardiomyopathy, or ischaemic cardiomyopathy; (iv) severe hepatic or renal impairment; (v) history of radioiodine (131I) therapy; and (vi) drug treatment-induced hypothyroidism. From 2022 to 2024, we consecutively enrolled 232 patients with drug-refractory AF who underwent initial radiofrequency catheter ablation (RFCA) at our institution. After applying these criteria, patients were stratified into age-, gender-, and comorbidity-matched cohorts, 116 with manifest hyperthyroidism and 116 without manifest hyperthyroidism.

Details of the ablation procedure are provided in the Supplementary Methods. After completion of pulmonary vein isolation, substrate modification was performed in low-voltage areas (LVAs) and scar regions under voltage mapping guidance. An LVA was defined as an area containing three or more adjacent sites with bipolar voltage <0.5 mV, separated by <5 mm. For voltage analysis, the left atrium (LA) was divided into six segments (anterior, posterior, septal, lateral, roof, and inferior walls). LVAs were contoured manually with semi-automatic assistance, and the software calculated their absolute area and percentage of the total LA surface area.

Post-procedural management and follow-up: all patients received anti-arrhythmic therapy for 8 weeks (Among patients with normal thyroid function, propafenone was prescribed for paroxysmal AF, whereas amiodarone was used for non-paroxysmal AF. In the hyperthyroidism subgroup, propafenone was used as the alternative agent regardless of AF type) to reduce early recurrence.16 Follow-up visits were scheduled at 1 and 3 months post-RFCA, and every 3 months thereafter, including 24 h Holter monitoring, 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG). Procedural success was defined as the absence of atrial tachyarrhythmia lasting >30 s without anti-arrhythmic drugs after a 3-month blanking period. Recurrence was defined as ECG-documented atrial arrhythmia lasting >30 s during the post-blanking period.16

Animals and treatment

Female and male C57BL/6J mice (8 weeks old, ∼22 g) were purchased from Liaoning Changsheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The mice were housed in a specific pathogen-free environment with a controlled temperature (22 ± 2°C, humidity 55 ± 5%), and a 12 h light/dark cycle, with ad libitum access to rodent chow and water. All animal procedures were performed in accordance with the guidelines established by the Animal Experiment Management Committee of Dalian Medical University (AEE24216). At the euthanasia procedure, the mice were anaesthetized by i.p. administration of 150 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium.

For pharmacological treatment, mice received daily i.p. injections of L-thyroxine (T4; 1 mg/kg/day; MCE, HY-18341, USA) or an equivalent volume of saline for 30 consecutive days. On Day 30, animals were anaesthetized by i.p. injection of pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg) and euthanized. TSH, T4, and free T3 levels were measured according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Cloud-Clone Corp., China).

Cardiomyocyte-specific Fto knockout mice (Myh6-Cre+; Ftofl/fl) were established on a C57BL/6J background (Shanghai Model Organisms, NM-CKO-190005) and analysed at 2 months of age. For transcriptomic analysis, mice were divided into four groups: (i) Saline + Myh6-Cre-; Ftofl/fl; (ii) Saline + Myh6-Cre+; Ftofl/fl; (iii) T4 + Myh6-Cre−; Ftofl/fl; and (iv) T4 + Myh6-Cre+; Ftofl/fl.

For additional in vivo experiments, mice were randomly divided into four groups: saline, saline + β-aminopropionitrile (BAPN; 100 mg/kg/every 2 days via i.p. injection, MCE, HY-Y1750, USA), T4, and T4 + BAPN. T4 was administered daily, and BAPN was administered every other day, for 30 consecutive days.

AF induction

Mice were anaesthetized via i.p. injection of tribromoethanol (500 mg/kg). Following a small incision in the external jugular vein, a Millar 1.1 F octapolar electrophysiology catheter was guided into the right atrium. Electrical stimulation was delivered using an isolated stimulator, while surface and intracardiac electrograms were recorded with a data acquisition system.

Based on preliminary experiments establishing an atrial capture threshold of ∼2 V in mice, stimulation intensities of 3, 5, and 8 V were selected to span a range from near-threshold to markedly suprathreshold levels. These values align with those used in prior studies18–20 and are effective for arrhythmia induction without provoking direct tissue damage.

At each voltage (3, 5, and 8 V), burst pacing was performed according to a stepwise protocol: beginning at a 40 ms cycle length, the interval was progressively shortened by 2 ms increments down to 20 ms. Each pacing step was maintained for 5 s. AF was considered successfully induced only if it persisted for at least 1 s. AF duration was measured from onset until the first sinus beat, and AF inducibility was calculated as the percentage of successful inductions.

Adeno-associated virus serotype 9 vector delivery

Adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) carrying the Fto gene under the cTnT promoter (AAV9-cTnT-Ftowt-oe) and its mutant variant (AAV9-cTnT-Ftomut-oe) were constructed by Hanbio (Shanghai, China) and administered i.v. via the tail vein as a single dose to the designated mice. The empty AAV9-cTnT vector was used as the control (AAV9-cTnT-nc). Virus titres of 0.5 × 1011 vector genomes per gram of body weight (vg/g) were delivered as single dosage for each type of virus. Four weeks post-injection, the overexpression efficiency was evaluated using quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and western blot.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data, or median (inter-quartile range) for skewed data. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and variance homogeneity by Bartlett’s test. Comparisons of categorical variables were performed using the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test, and continuous variables using the t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. For comparisons involving more than two groups, one-way ANOVA was used. Survival analyses were conducted using the Kaplan–Meier method, with group comparisons via the log-rank test. The risks of AF recurrence and LVA were assessed using Cox regression and logistic regression, respectively. Variables with P < 0.2 in univariate analysis were included in the construction of their respective multivariate models. All analyses were performed using SPSS 27 and GraphPad Prism 9.5. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Procedures for echocardiography, histological analyses, pressure–volume (PV) loop analysis, ECG Records, cardiomyocyte isolation and culture, cycloheximide and actinomycin D experiments, nuclear/cytoplasmic RNA purification, transcriptomics, qPCR, and western blot are described in detail in the Supplementary Methods.

Results

Patients with prior hyperthyroidism show higher AF recurrence post-RFCA, associated with enlarged left atrial low-voltage zones

The study cohort consisted of 232 AF patients, with 146 paroxysmal AF and 86 persistent AF. All enrolled hyperthyroid patients had a documented history of manifest hyperthyroidism lasting over 1 year. Following treatment, these patients had either achieved a euthyroid state (81/116) or progressed to a state of latent hyperthyroidism (35/116), meeting the clinical criteria for RFCA (see Supplementary material online, Tables S1–S4).

As shown in Supplementary material online, Tables S1–S3, no significant differences were observed between the hyperthyroid and non-hyperthyroid groups in past medical history (e.g. hypertension, diabetes mellitus, CAD, and stroke), echocardiographic parameters [LAD, LVD, left ventricular ejection fraction (EF), E/eʹ, and echocardiographic LA volumes], serum biomarkers (LDL-C, HDL-C, and creatinine CRE), or clinical risk scores (CHA2DS2-VA and HAS-BLED). No significant differences were observed in the use of commonly prescribed concomitant medications (e.g. CCB, β-blockers, ACE-I/ARB, ARNI, SGLT2i, and diuretics), either in the overall cohort or when analysed within the non-paroxysmal and paroxysmal AF subgroups (all P > 0.05). However, serum TSH levels were significantly lower in the hyperthyroid group [1.26 (0.23–2.29) vs. 1.88 (1.39–2.80); P < 0.001], consistent within the paroxysmal AF subgroup (P < 0.001). Serum fT3 levels were significantly higher in the hyperthyroid group [4.58 (4.07–5.00) vs. 4.24 (3.78–4.72); P = 0.008], consistent within the non-paroxysmal AF subgroup (P = 0.007). Examples of LVA mapping are shown in Figure 1A. The hyperthyroid group exhibited significantly larger LVA compared with the non-hyperthyroid group [27.81% (22.81–39.59) vs. 20.25% (13.04–28.37); P < 0.001], with similar trends in both AF subtypes (paroxysmal AF: 26.03 vs. 14.83%, P < 0.001; non-paroxysmal AF: 38.35 vs. 28.39%, P < 0.001). Overall, female patients exhibited a significantly larger LVA than males [26.56% (19.72–36.24) vs. 24.00% (14.97–30.51), P = 0.023]. Further analysis stratified by thyroid status revealed that this difference was primarily driven by the non-hyperthyroid subgroup [24.77% (14.83–32.96) vs. 18.20% (12.08–26.04), P = 0.018]. In contrast, within the hyperthyroidism subgroup, LVA was comparable between females and males [29.40% (23.18–39.60) vs. 26.76% (20.51–39.85), P = 0.36] (see Supplementary material online, Tables S5–S7). Over the 12-month follow-up, 29 patients (12.5%) experienced AF recurrence (non-paroxysmal AF: 19.8%, paroxysmal AF: 5.2%). Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed significantly lower AF-free survival in the hyperthyroid group (P < 0.001), consistent in both AF subgroups (P < 0.05) (Figure 1B–D). Multivariate Cox regression identified both LVA (HR = 1.104, P < 0.001), age (HR = 1.041, P = 0.033), and hyperthyroidism (HR = 4.121, P = 0.015) as independent risk factors for AF recurrence (Figure 1E). After stratifying by median LVA, logistic regression confirmed hyperthyroidism as an independent predictor of increased LVA in AF patients (odds ratio = 2.868, P < 0.001) (Figure 1F). In contrast, use of antithyroid drugs was not an independent predictor of AF recurrence or increased LVA (Figure 1E and F and Supplementary material online, Tables S8 and S9).

T4 administration promoted atrial fibrosis and enhanced AF susceptibility in mice

One month after the initial T4 injection, T4 levels in both the heart and serum were significantly elevated in treated mice compared with the saline control group (Figure 2A and B). Consistently, free T3 levels were increased in both serum and cardiac tissue, while TSH levels were markedly reduced in the T4-treated group, confirming successful induction of hyperthyroidism (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1A–D). While no significant difference in body weight (BW) was observed between the groups, T4-treated mice exhibited a significantly elevated heart rate (Figure 2C and D). Programmed electrical stimulation revealed a significant increase in both the incidence and duration of AF in T4-treated mice (Figure 2E–G). Electrical mapping demonstrated that these mice had decreased left atrial conduction velocity (LACV), a shorter effective refractory period (ERP), prolonged activation time, and increased activation time dispersion (Figure 2H–L). Ultrasound imaging assessed LAD, which showed a significant increase in T4-treated mice after 4 weeks compared with controls. Furthermore, Masson’s trichrome staining of atrial tissue revealed significantly increased atrial fibrosis in T4-treated mice at 4 weeks (Figure 2M–O).

Both male and female mice were assessed for the effects of T4 treatment. While atrial electrical and structural remodelling was observed in both sexes, male mice consistently exhibited greater increases in heart rate (Figure 2C), conduction abnormalities (Figure 2E–L), extended LAD, and atrial fibrosis (Figure 2M–O). All subsequent experiments were performed using male mice to ensure phenotypic consistency and enhance experimental sensitivity.

Fto was increased in the atrial cardiomyocytes of T4-treated mice

To identify key genes involved in AF, we performed transcriptome analysis on atrial tissues from saline- and T4-treated mice. This revealed 4120 differentially expressed genes (P < 0.05, |log2 fold change| ≥ 1), with 2011 up-regulated and 2109 down-regulated in the T4-treated group. Transcriptome analysis specifically indicated significant up-regulation of Fto expression in T4-treated atrial tissue (Figure 3A). This increase in Fto mRNA expression was further confirmed by qPCR (Figure 3B). Western blot analysis of atrial tissue methyltransferases (Mettl3, Mettl14, and Wtap) and m6A demethylases (Fto and Alkbh5) showed a significant increase in Fto protein levels in T4-treated mice, while Mettl3, Mettl14, Wtap, and Alkbh5 levels remained unchanged (Figure 3C and D). To determine cellular localization, we isolated atrial cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts. Western blotting demonstrated elevated Fto protein levels in both cell types from T4-treated mice. Notably, the increase in Fto expression was most prominent in cardiomyocytes compared with fibroblasts (Figure 3E and F). α-Smooth muscle actin was employed as a fibroblast marker to distinguish fibroblast populations for cell-type-specific protein analysis. Additionally, transcriptomic analysis revealed remodelling of multiple ion channel-related genes in atrial tissue following T4 administration. qPCR confirmed these alterations, and ion channel expression returned to baseline levels 4 weeks after T4 withdrawal, indicating that T4-induced ionic remodelling was reversible. In contrast, collagen I/III mRNA levels remained significantly elevated at the same time point, indicating irreversible structural remodelling (see Supplementary material online, Figure S2A–T).

Cardiomyocyte-specific deletion of Fto alleviated T4-induced AF

To investigate the role of Fto in AF development in T4-treated mice, we generated cardiomyocyte-specific Fto knockout mice by crossing Myh6-cre mice with Ftofl/fl mice. Successful deletion of Fto was confirmed by western blotting and qPCR (see Supplementary material online, Figure S3A–C). Following T4 treatment, Myh6-cre+; Ftofl/fl mice exhibited significantly reduced susceptibility to pacing-induced AF compared with control mice. This was evidenced by a significantly lower incidence and shorter duration of AF (Figure 4A–C). No significant differences were observed between the groups in heart rate or heart weight-to-tibial length ratio (HW/TL) (Figure 4D and Supplementary material online, Figure S3D and E). Furthermore, electrophysiological analysis revealed that Myh6-cre−; Ftofl/fl mice displayed reduced conduction velocity, a shorter ERP, prolonged activation time, and increased activation time dispersion compared with Fto knockout mice (Figure 4E–I) following subjected to T4. T4-induced atrial enlargement and fibrosis were significantly attenuated in Fto knockout mice compared with controls (Figure 4J–L).

Cardiomyocyte-specific Fto knockout attenuated Lox expression in T4-treated mice

Both cardiomyocyte-specific Fto knockout mice and their littermate controls were treated with T4. Transcriptomic sequencing was performed on atrial tissues from all four experimental groups. Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of genes up-regulated in T4-treated controls vs. saline-treated controls revealed significant associations with biological processes including ‘response to extracellular stimulus’, ‘extracellular matrix organization’, and ‘muscle organ development’. In T4-treated atria, GO analysis of genes down-regulated in Fto knockouts vs. controls showed significant enrichment in processes such as ‘extracellular matrix organization’ (Figure 5A and B). Targeted examination of the ‘extracellular structure organization’ category identified Lox as a top differentially expressed gene (Figure 5C). Both qPCR and immunoblotting confirmed significant increases in Fto and Lox mRNA and protein content in T4-treated control atria compared with saline-treated atria. However, Fto knockout significantly attenuated T4-induced up-regulation of Lox expression at both mRNA and protein levels (Figure 5D–G).

Cardiomyocyte-specific Fto overexpression promoted AF susceptibility via m6A-dependent mechanisms

To elucidate the functional relationship between Fto expression and its demethylase activity in AF pathogenesis, 2-month-old mice received i.v. injections of AAV9 driving either with wild-type Fto overexpression (AAV9-cTnT-Ftowt-oe) or a catalytically inactive Fto mutant (I367F; AAV9-cTnT-Ftomut-oe). Successful overexpression of Fto was validated at both the protein and mRNA levels (see Supplementary material online, Figure S4A–F). Wild-type Fto overexpression up-regulated Lox protein expression relative to both control and mutant groups (Figure 6A–C). Moreover, overexpression of wild-type Fto significantly increased AF incidence and duration compared with the mutant group (Figure 6D–F). Electrophysiological assessments revealed that only wild-type overexpression reduced conduction velocity, shortened the ERP, prolonged activation time, and increased activation time dispersion (Figure 6G–K). Structurally, mice overexpressing wild-type Fto exhibited significant atrial dilation and fibrosis vs. mutant-overexpressing mice and control mice (Figure 6L–N).

Fto modulated Lox mRNA transcription and translation through reduced m6A methylation

Neonatal rat atrial myocytes were infected with adenoviral vectors expressing either wild-type Fto (Ad-Ftowt), catalytically inactive Fto (Ad-Ftomut), or empty vector (Ad-vector). Wild-type Fto overexpression significantly increased Lox expression at both mRNA and protein levels vs. mutant and control groups (Figure 7A–C). Lox protein half-life remained unchanged across groups (Figure 7D and E), confirming that Fto does not regulate Lox protein stability. Mechanistically, wild-type Fto overexpression reduced Lox precursor mRNA while enhancing splicing efficiency, increased mature Lox mRNA abundance by inhibiting mRNA degradation (Figure 7F–I). Subcellular fractionation revealed unaltered nuclear-to-cytoplasmic Lox mRNA distribution (Figure 7J), indicating that m6A demethylation does not influence mRNA export. Thus, Fto-mediated m6A demethylation promotes Lox expression primarily through enhanced precursor mRNA splicing and mature transcript stabilization.

Pharmacological inhibition of Lox activity alleviated T4-induced AF

Guided by these findings, we investigated whether chemical Lox inhibition modulates T4-induced AF using BAPN, a potent Lox inhibitor. After 3 weeks of BAPN administration, T4-treated mice exhibited significantly reduced AF susceptibility compared with untreated T4 controls, evidenced by decreased AF incidence and shortened duration, without alterations in heart rate or HW/TL (Figure 8A–E and Supplementary material online, Figure S5A and B). BAPN treatment reversed T4-induced electrophysiological remodelling, normalizing conduction velocity, ERP, activation time, and activation time dispersion (Figure 8F–J). BAPN attenuated T4-induced up-regulation of collagen I/III mRNA (Figure 8K and L). Histologically, BAPN significantly reduced atrial fibrosis area and LAD vs. T4-only controls (Figure 8M–O).

Furthermore, in T4-injected mice, PV loop analysis revealed preserved systolic function, as evidenced by comparable EF and dP/dtmax relative to controls (see Supplementary material online, Figure S5C–E). Discussion

Main findings

In the present study, several novel findings have been made. First, patients with a history of hyperthyroidism showed an elevated risk of AF recurrence following RFCA, which was associated with significantly enlarged left atrial low-voltage zones. Secondly, T4 administration induced atrial electrical remodelling and fibrosis, along with a pronounced up-regulation of Fto expression in atrial tissue. Thirdly, cardiomyocyte-specific deletion of Fto down-regulated Lox expression at both mRNA and protein levels and reduced susceptibility to AF. In contrast, overexpression of wild-type Fto increased Lox expression and promoted AF susceptibility, through m6A demethylation-dependent enhancement of Lox mRNA splicing and translation efficiency. Finally, pharmacological inhibition of Lox significantly attenuated T4-induced atrial fibrosis and AF inducibility.

Clinical relevance of hyperthyroidism in AF recurrence

Observational studies demonstrated that approximately two-thirds of hyperthyroidism-associated AF patients achieved spontaneous conversion to SR following normalization of thyroid function.2–5,21 RFCA should therefore be reserved for patients demonstrating persistent AF after at least 3 months of euthyroid status. Notably, this selected population displayed over 40% post-RFCA recurrence rate,22,23 strongly suggesting that hyperthyroidism induces progressive and largely irreversible atrial structural remodelling. Consistently, our data showed a nearly three-fold increased risk of AF recurrence post-RFCA in patients with hyperthyroidism history, and significant expansion of left atrial low-voltage zones. These findings consistently support the paradigm that hyperthyroidism drives atrial fibrosis, creating a substrate for AF recurrence despite rhythm control interventions.

Electrical and structural remodelling in hyperthyroidism-induced AF

The pathogenesis and progression of AF involve a complex interplay between electrical and structural remodelling. Consistent with existing literature and confirmed in our murine model, hyperthyroidism induces significant remodelling of multiple ion channels, thereby creating a pro-arrhythmic substrate. Specifically, the up-regulation of Kcna5 corresponds to the well-documented action potential duration (APD) shortening in hyperthyroid models and its established role in AF pathogenesis.6,24,25 Similarly, increased Hcn2 expression aligns with known thyroxine-mediated enhancement of pacemaker current,26 while up-regulation of P2X purinoceptor 5 (P2rx5) is consistent with T3-induced expression changes reported in other tissue types.27 A key finding of our study was the distinct reversibility of electrical and structural remodelling following the restoration of normal thyroid function. Most T4-induced ion channel alterations returned to baseline levels 4 weeks after T4 withdrawal. In contrast, atrial fibrosis persisted. This lasting structural change likely contributes to the clinical recurrence of AF observed in hyperthyroid patients even after their thyroid function has normalized.

Clinical and animal studies demonstrate a strong link between inflammation and AF.28–30 The hypermetabolic state in hyperthyroidism, driven by tachycardia and increased myocardial contractility, elevates oxygen consumption and results in mitochondrial dysfunction with concomitant overproduction of reactive oxygen species,31,32 collectively triggering oxidative stress-related inflammatory pathways.33 Furthermore, excess thyroid hormone impairs autonomic nervous system balance. Evidence indicates that patients with hyperthyroidism exhibit markedly increased sympathetic activity,34,35 sympathetic overexcitation promotes inflammatory responses by activating T cells and facilitating the release of inflammatory factors.36,37 Compared with euthyroid individuals, patients with hyperthyroidism showed significantly elevated levels of pro-inflammatory biomarkers, including interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor-α, and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, indicating a systemic pro-inflammatory state. Fibrosis is the consequence of inflammation and contributes to AF.38 Prior research has shown that excess thyroid hormone increases myocardial collagen and interstitial fibrosis in T4-induced hyperthyroid rats, though the underlying mechanism remained unclear.39 Here, we extend this finding and demonstrate that T4-induced atrial fibrosis occurs via an m6A-demethylation-dependent pathway. This work establishes a direct molecular link between thyroid hormone excess and epigenetic dysregulation in atrial structural remodelling.

Epigenetic regulation of atrial remodelling in AF

AF pathogenesis involves epigenetic regulation, particularly m6A methylation. In murine AF models, decreased Mettl3 expression reduced m6A levels and enhanced mRNA stability of protocadherin gamma subfamily A, 10 (Pcdhga10), a calcium handling gene. Elevated Pcdhga10 increased Serca2a activity and Kcnj5 expression, disrupting calcium homeostasis, shortening APD, and establishing a proarrhythmic substrate.40 In human AF samples, up-regulated m6A reader YTHDF1 stabilized methyl-CpG-binding protein 2 (MECP2) transcripts and increased MECP2 protein, which suppressed solute carrier family 31 member 1 (SLC31A1) expression. This led to mitochondrial copper depletion, promoting glycolytic shift and fibroblast proliferation as key drivers of fibrotic remodelling.41 Our earlier work demonstrated age-related Fto up-regulation in atrial tissue, where it mediated Kcne1 mRNA demethylation, reduced Kcne1 protein, enhanced IKs, shortened APD, and increased AF susceptibility.18 We are the first to report altered m6A methylation in murine atrial tissue following T4 administration and identified Fto up-regulation as a significant contributor to hyperthyroidism-associated AF. Isolated cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts from mouse atrial tissue showed increased Fto content in both cell types from T4 treated animals, with more pronounced elevation in cardiomyocytes.

The observed cardiac Fto up-regulation in our hyperthyroidism model links endocrine signalling to epigenetic regulation. This likely occurs through canonical genomic actions of thyroid hormones, primarily T3 converted from T4, which binds thyroid hormone receptors (TRs) with higher affinity and genomic potency.42 Substantial evidence indicates the T3 TR complex binds to thyroid hormone response elements in target gene promoters to directly modulate transcription.43,44 This mechanism is well established in cardiac tissue, where T3 regulates calcium handling proteins including Serca2.45 We therefore hypothesize that Fto functions as a direct transcriptional target of thyroid hormone signalling, with the T3 TR complex recognizing specific response elements within the Fto promoter to initiate transcription, providing an efficient pathway for hyperthyroidism-induced Fto up-regulation.

Beyond m6A methylation, histone acetylation represents another fundamental epigenetic mechanism in AF pathogenesis.46–50 HDACs are critical mediators of AF-related pathological remodelling through multiple pathways. Structurally, HDAC6 activation promoted α-tubulin deacetylation across experimental models and human AF tissues, disrupting microtubule stability and impairing contractile function.51 Electrophysiologically, HDAC2 deficiency altered expression of potassium channel subunits including Kcnj3, Kcnj5, Kcnd3, and Kcnj2, leading to action potential prolongation and electrical instability.52 The therapeutic potential of HDAC inhibition has been consistently demonstrated. Valproic acid treatment attenuated structural remodelling, normalized APD, delayed AF onset in transgenic models, and ameliorated hypertrophy, fibrosis, and inflammation in hypertensive cardiomyopathy.53–55

Several critical questions regarding epigenetic regulation in hyperthyroidism-induced AF remain unanswered. Previous studies demonstrated that METTL3-mediated m6A modification stabilized HDAC5 mRNA and enhanced its expression,56 while FTO was activated by GCN5-mediated acetylation at lysine 88.57 These findings suggest potential crosstalk between acetylation and m6A methylation pathways. Determining whether histone modifications affect m6A regulatory components in hyperthyroidism, or conversely whether m6A modifications influence HDAC expression, could reveal novel integrative mechanisms in AF pathogenesis. These questions warrant investigation to clarify the epigenetic interplay in thyroid hormone mediated atrial remodelling.

Lox as a therapeutic target in hyperthyroidism-associated AF

Lox and Lox-like (LoxL) proteins are copper-dependent amine oxidases, play a pivotal role in the cross-linking of collagen and elastic fibres, which are essential for the proper assembly and structural integrity of extracellular matrix fibres.58–61 Substantial evidence supports the role for Lox in the pathogenesis of AF. Patients with AF exhibit higher Lox expression than those in SR.62 In a swine model, frequent atrial pacing induced structural and electrical remodelling along with significant Lox up-regulation.63 Reducing Lox expression prevents atrial fibrosis and decreases AF incidence.64 Lox inhibitors include primary amines (e.g. benzylamines, taurine, and BAPN) and hydrazine-based compounds (e.g. thiosemicarbazide and semicarbazide).58 BAPN, the most widely used pharmacological inhibitor, has been shown to suppress collagen expression and improve cardiac function in diabetic mice.65 In our study, Lox inhibitors effectively attenuated T4-induced atrial fibrosis in mice without disrupting pre-existing cardiac matrix architecture under physiological conditions. Notably, reported adverse effects such as aortic dissection occurred only in developing mice with active collagen remodelling and are not expected in mature organisms with stabilized collagen fibres.66,67 For patients undergoing treatment for hyperthyroidism, early implementation of adjunctive Lox inhibitor therapy may yield therapeutic benefits. It could enhance the likelihood of spontaneous rhythm conversion after achieving euthyroidism. Even in cases where spontaneous conversion does not occur, attenuation of fibrotic burden may significantly improve long-term ablation success rates.

Atrial cardiomyopathy as a guide for early detection and intervention

The concept of atrial cardiomyopathy offers a valuable framework for interpreting the structural changes observed in hyperthyroidism,68 suggesting that some patients may develop significant atrial fibrosis even in the absence of AF. This raises the possibility that the impact of hyperthyroidism on the atria is currently underestimated, as silent structural remodelling may go undetected without specific investigation. We propose that future management of hyperthyroid patients should consider routine cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to evaluate structural changes in the atria.

For patients found to have atrial fibrosis without AF, early intervention with anti-fibrotic therapies may be clinically meaningful. While such an approach remains investigational, it aligns with the evolving paradigm of treating substrate rather than waiting for clinical manifestations. This proactive strategy could potentially prevent the progression to overt atrial cardiomyopathy and its thrombo-embolic complications.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that hyperthyroidism independently predicts larger left atrial LVA in AF patients, correlating with higher recurrence after RFCA. We further identified the RNA demethylase Fto as a key mediator in hyperthyroidism-induced atrial remodelling. T4 up-regulated Fto, which reduced m6A methylation on Lox, enhancing its stability and translation. The subsequent rise in Lox content promoted atrial fibrosis, increasing AF susceptibility. Importantly, Lox inhibition with BAPN reversed these effects, supporting its therapeutic potential for hyperthyroidism-related AF.

Limitations

Our study has several inherent limitations. First, its single-centre, retrospective design and relatively small sample size limit the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, the use of left atrial LVAs as a surrogate for atrial fibrosis is suboptimal; future studies should employ more direct methods like MRI for validation. Thirdly, while the T4-induced mouse model effectively recapitulates the downstream hormonal milieu of hyperthyroidism and is thus suitable for studying direct cardiac effects, it notably lacks the upstream immunopathology, including TSH receptor antibodies and the autoimmune context seen in human Graves’ disease.

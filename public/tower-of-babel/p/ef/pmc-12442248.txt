Validation of the Arabic Version of Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire 35 (AVQ‐35) in Endoscopic Endonasal Resection
Authors: Ahlam Alamri, Abdulmajeed Alharbi, Hussain Albaharna, Ahmad Alsayed, Abdulrazag Ajlan, Ashwag Alqurashi, Basem Noor Elahi, Abdulaziz Alrasheed, Saud Alromaih, Saad Alsaleh, Ahmad Alroqi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundQuality of life (QoL) is a critical outcome measure in patients undergoing endoscopic endonasal resection of anterior skull base lesions. This study aimed to validate the Arabic version of the Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire 35 for endoscopic endonasal resections (AVQ35).MethodsThis study included patients who underwent endoscopic endonasal resection of anterior skull base lesions between January 2022 and December 2022 at King Saud University Medical City in Riyadh. QoL assessments were performed using standardized questionnaires at baseline and during the postoperative follow‐up visits. Statistical analyses, including paired t‐tests and regression analysis, were used to assess changes in QoL and to identify associated factors.ResultsAll individual components or items within the AVQ‐35 questionnaire were valuable and relevant when assessing a patient's QoL. The findings demonstrated a significant improvement in QoL scores following endoscopic endonasal resection of the anterior skull base lesions (p < 0.05). The AVQ‐35's validity was established by evaluating its internal consistency, test–retest reliability, capacity, and ability to detect clinical changes before and after surgery at two different time points. The internal consistency of the AVQ‐35 was excellent, with a Cronbach's alpha > 0.95 (0.980) across all time points for all domains.ConclusionThis study successfully validated the AVQ‐35 for use in patients undergoing endoscopic endonasal resection of anterior skull base lesions. The AVQ‐35 demonstrated excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha > 0.95) and responsiveness to clinical changes, as evidenced by significant improvements in QoL scores postoperatively. These findings establish the AVQ‐35 as a robust and culturally appropriate instrument for assessing QoL in Arabic‐speaking populations, filling a critical gap in patient‐reported outcome measures for anterior skull base surgery.

Introduction

Endoscopic endonasal resection (EER) has emerged as a transformative approach for managing anterior skull base lesions, offering advantages such as reduced morbidity, shorter hospital stays, and improved cosmetic outcomes compared to traditional open surgeries [1, 2]. However, for the patient, despite these advantages, the postoperative course often leaves the patient with substantial difficulties, including sinonasal dysfunction, pain, and psychosocial distress. These issues may influence their quality of life (QoL), which emphasizes the necessity of our work to build up a culturally adapted QoL questionnaire for Arabic‐speaking patients [3].

The Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire 35 (ASBQ‐35) is a well‐validated tool designed to assess QoL in patients undergoing skull base surgery [4, 5]. However, its application in Arabic‐speaking populations has been limited, possibly due to the absence of a culturally and linguistically adapted version. The urgency and importance of this issue are underscored by previous studies that have highlighted the importance of validated patient‐reported outcome measures (PROMs) in guiding clinical decision‐making and improving postoperative care [5, 6]. Nevertheless, despite these advantages, patients typically are faced with considerable postoperative adversity, including sinonasal morbidity, pain, and psychosocial stress. These are likely to affect their QoL significantly, demonstrating our work's importance in developing a culturally appropriate QoL tool in the Arabic‐speaking population [3].

Advancements in endoscopic techniques have revolutionized the field of skull base surgery, providing less invasive and more effective treatment options for various pathologies [7].

In recent years, EER has emerged as a promising approach for managing anterior skull base lesions, offering improved patient outcomes and an enhanced QoL [8]. QoL is a multifaceted and critical aspect of healthcare assessment encompassing various domains such as physical, emotional, and social functioning [1]. It serves as an essential measure of treatment success and offers valuable insights into the holistic impact of medical interventions [2].

The original English version of the ASBQ‐35 has been validated in multiple languages, demonstrating its cross‐cultural applicability. The Italian validation by Mattavelli et al. confirmed the ASBQ‐35's strong psychometric properties (Cronbach's α = 0.93), instilling confidence in its reliability, showing a significant correlation with sinonasal outcome measures [9]. Similarly, the German adaptation by Müller et al. reported excellent reliability (ICC = 0.91) and responsiveness to clinical changes following anterior skull base procedures [10]. A recent Japanese version by Tanaka et al. further reinforced the questionnaire's validity, with domain‐specific Cronbach's α values ranging from 0.82 to 0.94 [11]. These validations demonstrate the strength of the ASBQ‐35 across healthcare systems and cultural environments. Nevertheless, no Arabic translation was available before this study, which reduced its applicability for the Middle Eastern population, where ENDO‐Skull Base surgery is becoming more popular.

Recent studies highlight the critical role of disease‐specific QoL measures in skull base surgery outcomes. Patel et al. demonstrated sustained QoL improvements after endoscopic craniopharyngioma resection, particularly in physical and social domains, underscoring the need for longitudinal assessment [12]. Similarly, Khalafallah et al. developed the first suprasellar meningioma‐specific QoL tool, revealing unique postoperative challenges distinct from other skull base pathologies [13]. For pituitary surgery, Carmel Neiderman et al. reported long‐term QoL normalization in most domains except olfaction, while Ahmadipour et al. validated the SNOT‐NC for neurosurgical sinonasal morbidity, filling a critical gap in rhinological outcomes [14, 15]. These studies collectively emphasize that generic QoL instruments may overlook procedure‐specific sequelae, necessitating validated tools like the ASBQ‐35 for precise evaluation.

To the best of our knowledge, the ASBQ‐35 has not yet been validated in the Arabic language. Hence, there is a missing measure in postoperative QoL for Arabic patients. A tool for assessment of such phenomena is lacking for both clinical assessment of recovery and comparison of recovery in different linguistic and cultural contexts. The primary objective of this study was to fill this gap by translating, culturally adapting, and validating the AVQ‐35 for patients intended for EER. Through psychometric validation, we have developed a viable tool for researchers and practitioners in Arabic. This study aimed to validate the Arabic adaptation of the Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire 35 for EER (AVQ35).

Patients and Methods

Study Design

Between January 2022 and December 2022, a database of surgical cases involving the anterior skull base was established through prospective data collection. This database included patients who underwent consecutive surgeries and was a collaborative effort involving three high‐volume skull base teams at King Saud University Medical City in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, all of which were led by experts with post‐fellowship training in the field.

Population Selection

Patients were consecutively enrolled from the combined clinic of Skull Base Surgery before undergoing the endoscopic endonasal approach (EEA). We included individuals aged ≥ 18 years who had appointments for EEA. Exclusion criteria included patients who declined participation, those with more than 20% missing data on the ASBQ‐35, individuals who lacked reading or writing skills, and patients with mental conditions that might have hindered their ability to comprehend and follow instructions.

Data Collection

The translation of the ASBQ‐35 into Arabic was conducted using a rigorous forward–backward translation process to ensure both linguistic accuracy and cultural equivalence. Initially, two independent translators, both native Arabic speakers with expertise in medical terminology and fluent in English, performed separate forward translations of the English ASBQ‐35 into Arabic [5]. These translations were then reviewed and compared by a panel of four experienced rhinologists, all native Arabic speakers with strong English proficiency and extensive clinical experience in skull base surgery. This version was sent to another independent translator without experience with the original English version for back translation into English. No significant differences were found between the original English and the back‐translated version. Prospective patient‐reported QoL outcome data were collected longitudinally throughout the first postoperative year (preoperative baseline), at postoperative week 3.

Data Analysis

Numerical variables are expressed as mean and standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables were displayed in terms of frequencies and corresponding percentages. A normality test was performed to assess the data distribution. The results of data exploration indicated that the study data did not follow a normal distribution. Consequently, nonparametric statistical tests were used for analysis. Specifically, the Mann–Whitney test was used to compare the patients and controls.

The Wilcoxon signed‐rank test was used to assess the difference between preoperative and postoperative values. To evaluate internal consistency, Cronbach's alpha was utilized, where a value falling within the range of 0.7–0.8 was deemed “satisfactory,” a value between 0.8 and 0.9 was regarded as “good,” and a value exceeding 0.9 was considered “excellent.” The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was also employed to evaluate the test–retest reliability. Validity testing was performed by comparing cases and controls. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. SPSS version 28 was used for all statistical analyses.

Ethics Statement

The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of King Saud University (Ref. no. 16/0164/IRB) and informed consent was obtained from all participants included in the study.

Results

This study provides insights into validating the AVQ35 for EER. Although some aspects of QoL, particularly physical well‐being and specific symptom management, improved postoperatively, others remained relatively stable.

In this study, we enrolled 29 patients who had undergone EER of the anterior skull base. The average age of the patients was 51 ± 10 years. Most of them were female, 21 (72.4). The diagnosis varied; meningioma emerged as the most prevalent diagnosis, with approximately nine patients (31%) exhibiting this condition. Pituitary adenoma was diagnosed in 24.1% of the patients, making it the second most frequent. Other less common diagnoses included craniopharyngioma in four cases (13.8%), clival chordoma in three cases (10.3%), and epidermoid cysts in 6.9% of the patients. Additionally, cholesterol granuloma, clival fibrous dysplasia, right orbital apex hemangioma, and trigeminal schwannoma accounted for one case (3.4%) among the patients (Table 1).

The preoperative variable did not significantly affect (p = 0.123) the likelihood of enhanced QoL. With each one‐unit rise preoperatively, there was a 12% decrease in the odds of experiencing an improved QoL. Conversely, the first postoperative assessment variable significantly influenced (p = 0.008*) the probability of an enhanced QoL; for every one‐unit increase in postoperative, the odds of an improved QoL increased by 25%. The second postoperative assessment variable did not significantly impact (p = 0.689) the likelihood of enhanced QoL. For each one‐unit increase in the second postoperative assessment, there was a 3% reduction in the odds of experiencing improved QoL.

The AVQ‐35's validity was evaluated using internal consistency, test–retest reliability, and its ability to detect clinical changes at both follow‐up assessments. The internal consistency of the AVQ‐35 was excellent, with a Cronbach's alpha > 0.95 (0.980) across all time points for all domains. The AVQ‐35's validity was evaluated by examining its internal consistency, test–retest reliability, and ability to detect clinical changes before and after surgery at two different time points. The Cronbach's alpha values were 0.914, 0.929, and 0.948 for the preoperative and postoperative assessments, respectively. The scores significantly improved, indicating that the scale was responsive to the clinical changes.

Discussion

This study validated the AVQ35 for EER. Each aspect or question within the AVQ‐35 questionnaire contributes to the overall evaluation of how a patient's QoL is affected during the first 3 months of the first year after undergoing EER of the anterior skull base lesion. This implies that the AVQ‐35 is a well‐rounded tool that covers various aspects of a patient's life and well‐being, making it a valuable resource for healthcare professionals to assess the impact of surgery on the patient's overall QoL.

The AVQ‐35 questionnaire comprises 35 questions that assess various aspects including physical and psychosocial functioning, pain, and sinonasal and visual symptoms. Previous research has shown that both sinonasal‐specific and overall QoL tend to decrease during the first month following pituitary adenoma resection but gradually improve, surpassing preoperative levels by the third month [16]. In the context of this study, five of the six domains within the AVQ‐35 exhibited a significant decline in scores at the 3‐week postoperative mark. However, by the end of the first 3 months of the first year, all domains, except for the specific symptom domain, had significantly improved compared to baseline. The emotional well‐being domain remained relatively stable during the early postoperative period, likely due to anxiety reduction related to the tumor and impending surgery. Although certain domains and appearance scores showed significant improvements, they were offset by lower scores in smell‐ and taste‐related aspects. Consequently, the AVQ‐35 demonstrated its ability to effectively differentiate and assess various QoL domains relevant to endoscopic endonasal pituitary surgery.

Notably, the Anterior Skull Base Nasal Inventory‐12 has been criticized for its limited evaluation of sinonasal morbidity [17], prompting its supplementation with additional metrics [18]. This combination of assessment tools provides a more comprehensive and holistic evaluation of QoL after EER of anterior skull base lesions.

The study's findings indicate that surgery had a notable and statistically significant effect on the physical well‐being domain of life (QoL). This suggests that the surgical procedure substantially improved the patient's physical health and overall well‐being. In a broader context, such findings are significant because they emphasize the positive impact of surgery in enhancing a patient's physical health and, consequently, their QoL [19, 20]. This could be particularly relevant in medical contexts where surgery is a common treatment option, such as in cases of chronic illnesses, injuries, or conditions that significantly affect physical functioning. It is worth noting that the specific details of the surgery, patient demographics, and the measurement tools used to assess the physical well‐being domain would provide more context and depth for the study's implications. Nonetheless, the results suggest that surgery can be an effective intervention to improve the physical aspects of a patient's QoL.

This study revealed statistically significant correlations with preoperative baseline home activity performance after the first postoperative evaluation. This finding suggests a connection between the initial level of home activity performance before surgery and outcomes measured after the procedure. In other words, patients with better baseline home activity performance tended to show more positive results in their postoperative assessment. Furthermore, the study noted notable changes in the bending and standing positions during the first and second postoperative evaluations. These observations imply that surgical intervention had a meaningful impact on the patient's ability to perform specific movements or activities. Such changes could indicate improved physical function or reduced pain and discomfort associated with bending or standing. These findings correlate with studies highlighting the importance of assessing and considering a patient's preoperative baseline performance and tracking changes in specific activities or movements during postoperative evaluation [21, 22, 23]. This information can help healthcare professionals understand the effectiveness of surgical procedures and better tailor rehabilitation or follow‐up care.

This study showed that for each visit and assessment increase in the second postoperative assessment, the odds of improved QoL decreased by 3%. This suggests an association between the number of postoperative visits and assessments, and the odds of improved QoL. In other words, as the number of postoperative assessments increases, the likelihood of experiencing an improvement in QoL decreases by 3% for each additional visit and evaluation [24, 25]. This finding has potential implications for postoperative care and follow‐up protocols, and correlates with recent studies [26, 27, 28]. Additionally, it is crucial to consider potential confounding variables or factors that might influence this relationship.

The likelihood of an enhanced QoL can be influenced by factors such as the surgical procedure, the patient's general health, the quality of postoperative care, and other individual patient‐related factors.

This study revealed that the AVQ‐35 is a highly valid measurement tool for assessing clinical changes in a specific context, as evidenced by its strong internal consistency and ability to detect improvements in patients' conditions before and after surgery. The AVQ‐35, a measurement tool commonly used in a medical or clinical context, has been validated through various assessments [16, 29]. These assessments included evaluating internal consistency, test–retest reliability, and the ability to detect clinical changes over multiple follow‐up assessments. Hence, the AVQ‐35 demonstrates responsiveness to clinical improvements, with significant scores increasing over time, making it a valuable instrument for evaluating post‐surgery QOL.

The ASBQ‐35 has been shown to possess strong psychometric properties in various linguistic and cultural settings. The Italian validation by Mattavelli et al. confirmed the questionnaire's good reliability (Cronbach's α = 0.93) and its significant correlation with sinonasal outcome measures, supporting a comprehensive assessment of postoperative QoL [9]. Similarly, the German adaptation by Müller et al. reported excellent test–retest reliability (ICC = 0.91) and confirmed the tool's responsiveness to clinical changes following anterior skull base procedures [10]. Tanaka et al.'s Japanese version provided further empirical support for its cross‐cultural validity, with high internal consistency in all domains (Cronbach's α: 0.82–0.94) [11]. These successful international validations underscore the ASBQ‐35's reliability as a measure of health‐related QOL, particularly emphasizing its comprehensive assessment of postoperative QOL. Our validation of the Arabic AVQ‐35 adds to this body of evidence, extending the questionnaire's utility to Arabic‐speaking populations undergoing EER.

Conclusion

This study successfully validated the AVQ‐35 for use in patients undergoing EER of anterior skull base lesions. The rigorous translation and adaptation process ensured linguistic accuracy and cultural relevance, while the psychometric evaluation confirmed the tool's reliability and validity. The AVQ‐35 demonstrated excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha > 0.95) and responsiveness to clinical changes, as evidenced by significant improvements in QoL scores postoperatively. These findings establish the AVQ‐35 as a robust and culturally appropriate instrument for assessing QoL in Arabic‐speaking populations, filling a critical gap in PROMs for anterior skull base surgery. Future studies with larger, multicenter cohorts and extended follow‐up periods are recommended to further generalize these results.

Limitations

This study's limitations include a relatively small sample size (n = 29), which limits statistical power and generalizability; a single‐institutional setting, which potentially restricts the applicability of findings to other contexts; and a limited follow‐up period, which potentially hinders the assessment of long‐term QoL changes. Further research with more extensive, multicenter samples and extended follow‐up is warranted to confirm these preliminary findings.

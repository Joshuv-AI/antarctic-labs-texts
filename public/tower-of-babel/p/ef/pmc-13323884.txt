Ginsenoside Rh7 ameliorates myocardial injury in diabetic cardiomyopathy by promoting M2 polarization of macrophages via SIRT1/FOXO1/β-catenin
Authors: Chenyang Han, Shasha wu, Li Zha, Li Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo investigate the intervention effect of ginsenoside Rh7 on myocardial dysfunction in diabetic cardiomyopathy.MethodsA diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM) mouse model was established using a high-fat diet combined with streptozotocin (STZ), and treated with ginsenoside Rh7. Echocardiography was used to detect changes in cardiac function in mice. Mouse body weight, heart weight, and cardiac index were measured. H&E staining was used to detect pathological changes in the heart. Tissue chemistry and fluorescence staining were used to detect the expression of CD206 and SIRT1 in the heart. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to detect the levels of M1/M2 cell markers.ResultsRh7 can improve cardiac dysfunction in DCM mice by inhibiting the expression of SIRT1, thereby promoting the activation of the FOXO1/β-catenin complex and M2 polarization of macrophages.In vitro iBMDM experiments showed that Rh7 can promote CD206 expression and M2 polarization of macrophages. Small molecule-protein docking and pull-down experiments confirmed the binding relationship between Rh7 and SIRT1.ConclusionRh7 can ameliorate myocardial injury in DCM mice by inhibiting SIRT1 and activating FOXO1/β-catenin, thereby promoting M2 polarization of macrophages.Keywords: Ginsenoside Rh7, Diabetic cardiomyopathy, SIRT1, Macrophages, M2 polarization

Background

Ginseng, a member of the Araliaceae family, is a herbaceous plant with a wide range of pharmacological effects and medical uses. Many new bioactivities of ginseng have been discovered successively [1]. Ginsenosides are the main components of ginseng, and more than 30 monomers with identified structures have been determined. Ginsenosides belong to triterpenoid saponins and can be divided into three categories: diol-type, such as ginsenoside Rb1, Rb2, Rc, Rd, Rh2, etc.; triol-type, such as ginsenoside Re, Rf, Rg1, Rg2, Rh1, etc.; and oleanolic acid-type, such as ginsenoside R0, Rh3, etc. [2,3]. Studies over the years have shown that ginseng and ginsenosides have protective effects on myocardial reperfusion injury in animals and human extracorporeal circulation surgery [4]. Experiments have confirmed that ginsenosides and their main components, such as Rg1, Rb1, and Re, have significant protective effects on hypoxia-induced vascular endothelial cell damage, indirectly indicating that ginseng may have the potential to promote angiogenesis [5]. Coronary angiography also shows that ginseng can promote the formation of collateral circulation in experimental myocardial infarction areas, improve myocardial microcirculation, and increase coronary flow. This indicates that various ginsenosides have significant therapeutic effects on myocardial injury [[6], [7], [8]]. Rh7 is a novel ginsenoside belonging to the triol-type saponins. There are few reports on the pharmacological effects and mechanisms of Rh7. Given the role of other ginsenosides in cardiovascular diseases, it is necessary to conduct in-depth research on Rh7 [9].

Macrophages play an important role in cardiovascular diseases, especially in myocardial injury [10]. Macrophages exist in the body as immune cells, with functions such as immune response and antigen presentation, and their proliferation and differentiation are influenced by various factors. Macrophages are divided into M1 and M2 types, with the former having pro-inflammatory effects and the latter having anti-inflammatory effects [11]. During the process of myocardial injury, the dynamic balance of M1 and M2 macrophages is disrupted, and their abundance changes, a process known as polarization. In the progression of myocardial injury caused by various reasons, M1-type macrophages are activated early, mediating inflammatory responses and oxidative stress, and clearing harmful substances produced by stress. Later, M2-type macrophages are activated, increasing anti-inflammatory effects, maintaining autophagy balance, and delaying ventricular remodeling [12]. The activation of M2-type macrophages has a protective effect on cardiomyocytes, so regulating the polarization of macrophages is also one of the important means of treating myocardial injury [13]. SIRT1 has been proven to be an important regulatory protein for macrophage polarization. Overactivation of SIRT1 can inhibit the activation of FOXO1, a transcription factor that promotes M2 polarization through β-catenin. The regulatory relationship between SIRT1 and FOXO1 has been reported, but the specific mechanisms in myocardial injury need further clarification [14,15]. Therefore, we focused on the SIRT1-FOXO1/β-catenin pathway to study the effects of Rh7.

2Preparation and Intervention of DCM Mice

Preparation and Intervention of DCM Mice

A DCM mouse model was established using a high-fat diet and streptozotocin [16]. The procedure was as follows: 8-week-old C57BL/6 J mice were fed a high-fat diet after a 7-day acclimatization period. Four weeks after the high-fat diet, the model group mice were injected intraperitoneally with streptozotocin (STZ) solution prepared in 0.1 mol/L sodium citrate buffer (pH = 4.5) at a dose of 65 mg/kg. The injection was administered every other day for a total of 5 times. After the injection, blood glucose levels were measured from tail snips on days 3 and 7 to confirm the successful establishment of the diabetic mouse model (fasting blood glucose >11.1 mmol/L). The diabetic mice were continued on a high-fat diet to induce DCM. The model was considered successful when a decline in cardiac function and myocardial injury were observed.

4Detection of Fasting Blood Glucose in Mice

Detection of Fasting Blood Glucose in Mice

All mice were fasted for 12 h (water allowed) before measuring fasting blood glucose. The procedure was as follows: An alcohol swab was used to clean the mouse's tail and scissors. The mouse was held, and a small section of the tail was cut off using scissors. The glucose test strip was inserted into the glucometer slot, and the glucometer screen displayed the blood glucose value.

4Detection of Cardiac Function in Mice

Detection of Cardiac Function in Mice

The cardiac function of mice was detected using a high-resolution small-animal ultrasound imaging system 10 weeks after drug administration. The day before the detection, the hair on the left side of the mouse's chest was shaved clean to facilitate better contact between the probe and the skin, resulting in clearer images. The mice were anesthetized and placed supine on a heating pad with limbs secured for ultrasound examination. The machine was turned on, and the mouse ultrasound connector was inserted on the left side of the machine. A new case was created, with notes on mouse grouping and numbering. The mouse probe was tilted at the 11 o'clock position and gently pressed against the heart to obtain a clear B-mode image. After clicking the M key to obtain an M-mode image, the sampling line was placed to the left of the papillary muscles. The images were saved, and measurements were taken of the left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVID,d), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVID,s), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF%), left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS%), interventricular septal thickness in systole (IVS,s), and interventricular septal thickness in diastole (IVS,d).

5H&E Staining

H&E Staining

Tissue blocks were cut into 5-μm-thick sections using a microtome. The paraffin sections were placed in water to unfold and adhered to glass slides, which were then baked at 60 °C for 2 h in a slide dryer. The sections were sequentially immersed in xylene solution (twice, each for 10 min), anhydrous ethanol (twice, each for 5 min), and gradient ethanol solutions of 95%, 90%, 80%, and 70% for 2 min each to deparaffinize. The sections were then rinsed with distilled water for 2 min to remove residual ethanol. Hematoxylin was applied for staining for 20 min, followed by rinsing with distilled water for 20 min. The sections were differentiated with hydrochloric acid alcohol for 2-3 s and rinsed with distilled water until the tissue turned blue-violet. Eosin was applied for staining for 2 min. The sections were then dehydrated in gradient ethanol solutions of 70%, 80%, 90%, 95%, and 100% and xylene solution for 5 min each. After rinsing with distilled water for a few seconds, the sections were mounted with neutral resin. The mounted slides were observed and photographed under a microscope.

6Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

The expression levels of M1 and M2 cell markers in mouse cardiac tissue and cell culture supernatant were detected. The cell culture supernatant was centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 30 min, and the supernatant was reserved. Mouse cardiac tissue was ground in liquid nitrogen, lysed with NP-40, and the supernatant was collected. The cell culture supernatant was also reserved after cell separation. The detection was performed according to the ELISA kit instructions, and the results were expressed in pg/ml.

7Tissue Fluorescence Staining

Tissue Fluorescence Staining

Heart tissue was sectioned at a thickness of 4 μm using a Leica CM1500 cryostat and mounted on glass slides. The tissue slides were fixed with 40 g/L paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 15 min and washed three times with PBS solution for 5 min each. The slides were blocked with 100 mL/L horse serum for 1 h. The SIRT1 monoclonal antibody was applied to the sections and incubated overnight at 4 °C. After washing the primary antibody with PBS three times for 5 min each, the secondary antibody was applied to the tissue and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 2 h. The slides were then washed with PBS three times for 5 min each. Hoechst dilution (1:5000) was applied to the tissue and incubated at room temperature for 15 min, followed by mounting with 750 g/L glycerol. The mounted sections were stored in a −20 °C refrigerator in the dark.

8Western-Blotting

Western-Blotting

One hundred milligrams of cardiac tissue was weighed and homogenized in lysis buffer and protease inhibitors using a homogenizer. After incubation on ice for 15 min, the mixture was centrifuged at 12,000 r/min at 4 °C for 15 min to collect the supernatant. The protein concentration was measured using a BCA protein assay kit. The protein samples were mixed with loading buffer, denatured at 100 °C for 5 min, and subjected to electrophoresis (150 V, 50 min) and transferred to a membrane (wet transfer, 70 V, 70 min). The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk for 1-2 h and incubated with monoclonal antibodies overnight at 4 °C on a shaker. After washing with TBST six times for 5 min each, the membrane was incubated with IgG antibody at room temperature for 2 h. The membrane was washed again with TBST six times for 5 min each and developed by placing the PVDF membrane in a gel imaging analysis system and adding ECL working solution to capture the band images. The band images were analyzed using Image J software to calculate the grayscale values, and the relative protein expression levels were determined after normalization.

9Cell Model and Intervention

Cell Model and Intervention

iBMDM cells were used for the experiments. Isolated bone marrow cells were seeded at a concentration of 2×10^6/ml in IMDM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 μg/ml streptomycin, with the addition of 10 ng/ml M-CSF. The cells were co-incubated with 10 ng/ml IL-4 and 10 ng/ml IL-13 to induce M2 polarization of iBMDM. The iBMDM cells were divided into Control, IL-4/13, and Rh7 groups after seeding. The Rh7 group was pre-treated with 5 μM and 10 μM Rh7 for 6 h before inducing M2 polarization.

10Cell Fluorescence Staining

Cell Fluorescence Staining

The iBMDM cells were stained for CD206 using the coverslip method. After co-induction with IL-4/13, the cells were washed three times with PBS, fixed with 4% formaldehyde at room temperature for 0.5 h, and permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 5 min. The monoclonal antibody for CD206 was diluted 1:400 and incubated overnight at 4 °C. After washing twice with PBS, the cells were incubated with a fluorescent secondary antibody. The cells were mounted with 95% glycerol and observed under a fluorescence microscope.

11Small Molecule-Protein Docking and Molecular Dynamics Simulation

Small Molecule-Protein Docking and Molecular Dynamics Simulation

The 2D structure of the small molecule ligand was obtained from the PubChem database (http://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The 2D structure was input into Chem Office 20.0 software to generate its 3D structure and saved as a mol2 file. The sequence of the SIRT1 protein target was obtained from the Uniprot database (https://www.uniprot.org/) (Uniprotkb: Q923E4), and the protein crystal model was constructed using Alphafold (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk) and saved as a PDB file. The Molecular Operating Environment 2019 software was used to minimize the energy of the compound, preprocess the target protein, and identify the active pocket. Finally, molecular docking was performed using MOE 2019, with the number of runs set to 50. The binding activity between the two was assessed based on the binding energy, and the results were visualized using PyMOL 2.6.0 and Discovery Studio 2019 software. To investigate the interaction between the small molecule active component ginsenoside Rh7 and the SIRT1 protein target, molecular docking was performed using MOE 2019 software. The molecular docking energy was −9.4451 kcal/mol. Generally, a docking energy value of less than −4.25 kcal/mol indicates some binding activity between the two, less than −5.0 kcal/mol indicates good binding activity, and less than −7.0 kcal/mol indicates strong binding activity. The docking results showed that ginsenoside Rh7 has strong binding activity with SIRT1. The lower the binding energy, the stronger the potential binding activity between the small molecule active component and the target.

Molecular dynamics simulations of the complex formed by ginsenoside Rh7 and SIRT1 were performed using GROMACS 2020.3 software. The simulation box size was optimized to ensure a distance greater than 1.0 nm between protein atoms and the box. The system energy was optimized to reduce contacts or atomic overlaps. The NVT and NPT ensembles were used for simulation equilibration, with a total simulation time of 100 ns. The temperature and pressure were controlled using the V-rescale and Parrinello-Rahman methods, respectively. The van der Waals forces were calculated using the Lennard-Jones function, with a non-bonded cutoff distance of 1.4 nm. All bond lengths of atoms were constrained using the LINCS algorithm. The particle mesh Ewald method was used to calculate electrostatic interactions, with a Fourier spacing of 0.16 nm.

12Pull-down Assay

Pull-down Assay

Recombinant SIRT1 protein (15 μg) was incubated with biotin-labeled Rh7 (Biotin-Rh7). Recombinant G protein magnetic beads were used to capture the SIRT1-antibody complex. After washing with Tris buffer, the SIRT1 protein was detected using the same Western-Blot procedure described above, and biotin was detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated antibiotin antibody.

13Statistical Methods

Statistical Methods

All measurement data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (±s). The statistical software SPSS 17.0 was used for analysis and processing. After homogeneity of variance test, two independent sample t-tests were used for analysis between two groups of data; one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for three or more groups of data, followed by pairwise comparisons using the LSD method. All tests were two-tailed, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

1Rh7 Ameliorates Cardiac Dysfunction in DCM Mice

Rh7 Ameliorates Cardiac Dysfunction in DCM Mice

DCM mice exhibited significant myocardial injury and obvious cardiac dysfunction. Rh7 improved myocardial injury in mice, reduced the expression of inflammatory factors, and increased the proportion of CD206+ macrophages. The body weight of DCM mice decreased significantly due to diabetes, and Rh7 inhibited this weight loss, showing a significant difference compared with DCM [Fig. 1A]. The heart weight and cardiac index in DCM were significantly lower than those in the Control group, and the hearts showed obvious pathological changes. Rh7 inhibited these changes, significantly increasing heart weight and cardiac index compared with DCM [Fig. 1B and C]. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) test results showed that the FBG level in DCM mice was significantly higher than that in the Control group, and Rh7 reduced the FBG level [Fig. 1D]. Cardiac function test results showed that the LVEF, LVFS, IVS.s, and IVS.d values in DCM were significantly lower than those in the Control group, while the LVID.s and LVID.d values were higher. Rh7 significantly improved these parameters compared with DCM [Fig. 1E–J]. H&E staining results showed obvious myocardial cell injury and inflammatory reactions in DCM, which were significantly improved by Rh7 [Fig. 1K].

M1/M2 cytokine detection results showed that the levels of M1 cell markers IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α in DCM were higher than those in the Control group. Rh7 reduced the expression of inflammatory factors. The levels of M2 cell markers Arg-1, IL-10, and TGF-β1 in DCM were not significantly different from those in the Control group, but Rh7 increased the levels of these cytokines [Fig. 2A–F]. SIRT1 staining results showed that the positivity rate of SIRT1 in DCM was higher than that in the Control group, and Rh7 reduced the positivity rate of SIRT1 [Fig. 2G and H]. CD206 staining results showed no difference in the proportion of CD206+ positive cells between DCM and the Control group, but Rh7 increased the proportion of CD206+ positive cells [Fig. 2I and J]. The relative protein expression levels showed that the level of SIRT1 in DCM was significantly higher than that in the Control group, while the levels of FOXO1 and β-catenin were slightly increased. Rh7 reduced the level of SIRT1 and increased the levels of FOXO1 and β-catenin [Fig. 2K–P].

Small molecule-protein docking results showed that Lys 436, Val 404, Asn 338, and Ser 434 on the SIRT1 protein receptor formed hydrogen bond interactions with Rh7. Residues Val 258, Ala 254, Arg458, Ile 339, Val 437, and Phe 406 formed hydrophobic interactions with Rh7. The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) was used as a criterion to assess the stability of the compound-protein receptor binding system. During the simulation of ginsenoside Rh7 and SIRT1, equilibrium was reached around 40 ns, with an average RMSD of 21.47 Å. The root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) of the entire protein over 100 ns was calculated. The RMSF changes of the residues interacting with ginsenoside Rh7 and SIRT1 ranged from 1.43 to 24.08 Å, with an average of 8.95 Å. The radius of gyration (Rg) of SIRT1 fluctuated between 30.17 and 39.72 Å during the simulation, with an average Rg of 32.70 Å. Overall, the compound-protein complex system was relatively stable during the molecular dynamics simulation. The number of hydrogen bonds between Rh7 and SIRT1 fluctuated between 2 and 7, with an average of 4.40, which was consistent with the docking results. In the free energy landscape of the complex formed by ginsenoside Rh7 and the SIRT1 protein, several dispersed clusters with the lowest energy were observed, which may also be the reason for the high binding affinity and stability of the compound ginsenoside Rh7 with the SIRT1 protein receptor [Fig. S1C–H]. Pull-down experimental results showed that Rh7 and SIRT1 have a binding relationship. After point mutation, Rh7 lost its binding site and no longer had binding ability, which corroborated the docking results [Fig. S1I–L].

5Rh7 Promotes M2 Polarization of iBMDM Cells

Rh7 Promotes M2 Polarization of iBMDM Cells

IL-4/13 can induce M2 polarization of iBMDM cells, with significantly higher levels of Arg-1, IL-10, and TGF-β1 than in the Control group. Rh7 can further increase the expression of these cytokines, with significantly higher levels in the Rh7 group than in the IL-4/13 group [Fig. S2A–C]. Discussion

SIRT1 is an NAD + -dependent histone deacetylase involved in the regulation of metabolism, aging, cell survival, autophagy, and diabetes [17]. SIRT1 deacetylates and activates various proteins and transcription factors [18]. FOXO1 is one of the transcription factors deacetylated by SIRT1, involved in cellular resistance to oxidative stress, apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, insulin signaling, and metabolism [19]. It has been reported to shuttle between the nucleus and cytoplasm, acting as a histone deacetylase and targeting many non-histone proteins [20]. The acetylation status of the FOXO1 transcription factor regulated by SIRT1 selectively directs FOXO1 to specific targets, representing another regulatory pathway for metabolism and stress response [21]. Further studies have reported that FOXO1 is activated by deacetylation of lysines 242, 245, and 262 mediated by SIRT1. Since SIRT1 has been shown to prevent ROS production and oxidative stress by regulating FOXO1 expression, it has also been proposed that increased oxidative stress can, in turn, control SIRT1 activity, although the possibility of FOXO1 involvement in this activity is unclear [22]. SIRT1 plays an important role in regulating endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS), alleviating doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity by regulating ER-related molecules PERK and GRP78 [23]. As a transcription factor, FOXO1 regulates the activation and activity of β-catenin [24], which is an important protein promoting M2 polarization of macrophages. M2 cells play an important role in tissue repair, secreting large amounts of factors such as Arg-1, IL-10, and TGF-β1 to promote cell regeneration and repair [25].

Ginsenosides are small molecules with multiple activities. Many studies have shown that various ginsenosides have important effects on ischemic cardiomyopathy and other cardiac injuries, promoting angiogenesis, antioxidant stress damage, and anti-inflammatory responses. Rh7 is a novel saponin belonging to the triol-type, and its effects have rarely been reported. We found that Rh7 has a significant cardioprotective effect in the DCM model, and its effects are related to SIRT1. Inhibiting SIRT1 can activate FOXO1, thereby promoting β-catenin expression and M2 macrophage polarization. DCM mice exhibited significant myocardial injury and upregulation of inflammatory factors and SIRT1 expression, indicating the importance of SIRT1 in DCM. After Rh7 treatment, SIRT1 expression was inhibited, and M2 cell polarization was promoted, characterized by high expression of M2 cell markers such as Arg-1 and IL-10, and a significant increase in CD206-positive cells. CD206 is a membrane marker of M2 cells, and its upregulation indicates that M2 polarization is dominant in Rh7-treated mice. Myocardial injury was significantly alleviated, and the expression of FOXO1 and β-catenin was upregulated. When we used FOXO1 knockout mice or the β-catenin inhibitor IN-3, Rh7 inhibited SIRT1 expression, but M2 cell polarization was inhibited, with no significant changes in CD206-positive cells or Arg-1 and IL-10 levels. This indicates that Rh7 acts solely on SIRT1, not on FOXO1 and β-catenin, and also shows that downstream signaling is an important regulatory signal for M2 polarization, with SIRT1 being the upstream signal. To verify the interaction between Rh7 and SIRT1, we performed kinetic analysis and docking of Rh7-SIRT1. The results showed that Rh7 and SIRT1 have a stable binding conformation. Pull-down experiments confirmed their targeting relationship. When the binding site was mutated, Rh7 could no longer bind to SIRT1. iBMDM is a commonly used macrophage cell line. In vitro experiments showed that IL-4/13 induced M2 polarization of iBMDM, with upregulation of CD206 and SIRT1 during the polarization process, indicating that SIRT1 is a negative regulatory protein for M2 polarization. Rh7 further promoted the polarization process by inhibiting SIRT1 expression, consistent with the in vivo experimental results.

Conclusion

Our study found that Rh7, a novel triol-type ginsenoside, has therapeutic effects on myocardial injury. In the DCM model, Rh7 significantly ameliorated myocardial injury in mice, inhibited inflammatory damage, and bound to SIRT1 to inhibit its function, thereby promoting the activation of FOXO1/β-catenin and M2 polarization of macrophages. Of course, our study also has its limitations. We need to further investigate the pharmacokinetics and safety of Rh7, and study its pharmacological effects and mechanisms in other myocardial injury models to provide more comprehensive support for the application of Rh7.

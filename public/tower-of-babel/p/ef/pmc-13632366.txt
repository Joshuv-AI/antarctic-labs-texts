CBCT-based evaluation of the morphometric relationship between the nasopalatine canal, infraorbital foramen, and greater palatine foramen
Authors: Utku Cem Hasırcı, İsmail Çapar, Didem Dumanlı Kusay, Edanur Altın, Çiğdem Şeker, Gediz Geduk
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/ObjectivesThe nasopalatine canal (NPC) shows considerable morphological variability and may be related to adjacent anatomical structures. The aim of this study was to investigate the relationship between NPC diameter and the morphometric and morphological characteristics of the infraorbital foramen (IOF) and greater palatine foramen (GPF), and to evaluate the changes associated with enlarged and pathological NPC conditions.MethodsCBCT images of 180 individuals were retrospectively analyzed. Participants were classified into four groups based on NPC diameter: normal, enlarged, suspected pathology, and histopathologically confirmed NPDC. NPC morphology was evaluated in coronal, axial, and sagittal planes. IOF and GPF measurements and morphological types were assessed bilaterallyResultsNo significant associations were found between NPC diameter and age, sex, or coronal morphology. In the axial plane, round (52.9%) and heart-shaped (42.9%) forms predominated in normal and enlarged groups, while oval morphology was more frequent in pathology (60.0%) and cyst groups (75.0%). In the sagittal plane, cylindrical (34.3%) and banana-shaped (38.6%) forms were dominant in normal and enlarged groups, whereas funnel-shaped morphology was more common in pathology (40.0%) and cyst groups (55.0%). Increasing NPC diameter was associated with higher IOF and GPF measurements and morphological changes.ConclusionsNPC enlargement is associated with morphological and dimensional variations in the IOF and GPF. These findings support the presence of measurable morphometric associations between NPC diameter and adjacent maxillofacial anatomical structures, particularly in enlarged and pathological NPC conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-09184-8.

Introduction

The nasopalatine canal (NPC) is an anatomical structure located in the midline of the anterior maxilla, providing a connection between the oral and nasal cavities. It contains the nasopalatine nerve as well as branches of the sphenopalatine and greater palatine arteries, playing a crucial role in the innervation and vascularization of the anterior palate and nasal septum [1–3]. Clinically, the NPC serves as an important anatomical landmark, particularly in implant planning, local anesthesia administration, and maxillofacial surgical procedures. Inadequate evaluation of this structure may lead to complications such as hemorrhage, sensory disturbances, and implant failure [3, 4].

It is well established that the morphological and dimensional characteristics of the NPC vary among individuals. These variations may be influenced by factors such as age, sex, and tooth loss, leading to significant differences in canal diameter and shape [2–4]. Such variability, particularly in the anterior maxilla, may reduce anatomical predictability and complicate clinical decision-making during surgical planning. Therefore, accurate evaluation of pathologies associated with the NPC is of great importance. The nasopalatine duct cyst (NPDC) is the most common developmental non-odontogenic cyst of the maxilla, originating from epithelial remnants within the canal. It is often asymptomatic and typically detected incidentally during routine radiographic examinations. Radiographically, it appears as a well-defined radiolucent lesion located in the midline of the anterior maxilla; however, it may be confused with periapical pathologies or normal anatomical variations, leading to diagnostic challenges [5, 6].

The infraorbital foramen (IOF) and the greater palatine foramen (GPF), located in the maxillofacial region, are important anatomical structures that transmit neurovascular bundles responsible for the innervation of the face and palate [6]. Knowledge of the morphometric characteristics of these structures is essential for minimizing the risk of complications during surgical procedures and local anesthesia applications [7]. Additionally, the anatomical position of the GPF has been reported to vary among individuals [8]. However, the morphological and dimensional relationships between these structures and the NPC have been only limitedly investigated in the literature [6].

During maxillofacial development, neurovascular structures have been suggested to influence the morphology of surrounding bone tissues and contribute to regional anatomical variation [9]. However, the biological mechanisms underlying potential relationships among the NPC, IOF, and GPF remain incompletely understood. Previous morphometric observations have reported associations between NPC enlargement and IOF/GPF dimensions [6]. The infraorbital foramen was included in the present study not because of its direct anatomical proximity to the NPC, but because it represents one of the major neurovascular foramina of the maxilla and may reflect broader morphometric characteristics of maxillary anatomy. Evaluation of the IOF together with the NPC and GPF was intended to investigate whether NPC enlargement is associated solely with local anatomical changes or with more generalized morphometric variations within the maxilla. Therefore, rather than assuming a developmental or causal relationship, the present study was designed to investigate whether measurable morphometric associations exist among these structures in CBCT images.

Previous studies have primarily focused on the individual morphological and dimensional characteristics of the NPC, IOF, and GPF [2–5, 7, 10–13]. Among the limited studies evaluating these structures together, Cam et al. [6] reported significant associations between NPC enlargement and IOF/GPF dimensions and suggested that these structures may exhibit coordinated morphometric variations. However, that study did not include histopathologically confirmed NPDC cases and therefore could not evaluate whether similar relationships persist in confirmed pathological conditions. The present study addresses this limitation by including histopathologically confirmed NPDC cases and by comprehensively evaluating morphological and morphometric characteristics across normal, enlarged, suspected pathology, and confirmed NPDC groups.

Although the morphological and dimensional characteristics of the NPC have been extensively studied, its morphometric relationship with the infraorbital foramen and greater palatine foramen has been evaluated in only a limited number of studies [6]. Therefore, it is important to elucidate the potential relationship between the NPC and these adjacent anatomical structures. We hypothesized that enlargement or pathological alteration of the NPC may be accompanied by measurable morphometric variations in the IOF and GPF. The aim of this study was to investigate the relationship between NPC diameter and the morphometric and morphological characteristics of the infraorbital foramen (IOF) and greater palatine foramen (GPF), and to evaluate the changes associated with enlarged and pathological NPC conditions.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective study was conducted by evaluating cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) images of a total of 180 individuals obtained from the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Radiology, Faculty of Dentistry, Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University. The aim of this study was to investigate the relationship between NPC diameter and the morphometric and morphological characteristics of the infraorbital foramen (IOF) and greater palatine foramen (GPF), and to evaluate the changes associated with enlarged and pathological NPC conditions.

Ethical approval

Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University (Approval No: 2026/05). All procedures were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee.

Sample selection and grouping

Since the aim of the study was to compare morphometric and morphological parameters among different nasopalatine canal (NPC) diameter groups, study groups were formed from eligible cases that met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. A total of 180 individuals were included in the study.

Participants were categorized into four groups according to NPC diameter: the normal NPC group (≤ 6 mm; n = 70), the enlarged NPC group (> 6 mm and ≤ 10 mm; n = 70), the suspected pathology group (> 10 mm; n = 20), and the histopathologically confirmed nasopalatine duct cyst (NPDC) group (n = 20).

Due to the limited number of histopathologically confirmed NPDC cases, all eligible cases were included in this group. The normal and enlarged NPC groups were established to enable statistically balanced comparisons among the study groups. The inclusion criteria were as follows: CBCT images of individuals aged 18 years and older; images with diagnostic quality covering both anterior and posterior regions of the maxilla; and clear visualization of the nasopalatine canal (NPC), infraorbital foramen (IOF), and greater palatine foramen (GPF).

The exclusion criteria included the presence of cysts, tumors, trauma, severe resorption, or congenital anomalies in the maxilla; metal artifacts compromising image quality; a history of maxillary surgery; the presence of significant pathological lesions in the region of interest; advanced alveolar bone resorption; systemic diseases affecting maxillofacial bone morphology; insufficient imaging field; inability to clearly identify anatomical structures; and technical image quality issues preventing accurate measurements.

The included individuals were classified into four groups based on dentition status in the anterior maxillary region: fully dentate individuals, completely edentulous individuals, individuals missing both central incisors, and individuals missing a single central incisor [6].

The threshold values for nasopalatine canal (NPC) diameter were determined based on the anatomical and pathological limits reported in the literature. Previous studies have shown that the NPC diameter is typically less than 6 mm, that values exceeding 6 mm may be considered indicative of enlargement, and that diameters greater than 10 mm may raise suspicion of a nasopalatine duct cyst (NPDC). CBCT images of the patients were obtained at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Radiology, Faculty of Dentistry, Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University, using a Veraviewepocs 3D R100/F40 (J Morita Mfg. All CBCT images were evaluated using i-Dixel 2.0 software (J. Morita Corporation, Osaka, Japan).

Observer reliability

All measurements and morphological classifications were performed by an oral and maxillofacial radiologist with four years of experience. All evaluations were conducted according to a standardized measurement protocol. Due to the retrospective design of the study, observer blinding was not performed. To assess the reproducibility of the measurements, intraobserver agreement analyses were conducted.

Intra-observer reliability was assessed by calculating Cohen’s kappa coefficient for categorical variables and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for continuous measurements. Kappa values ranged between 0.76 and 0.84, indicating high agreement across all categorical variables (p < 0.001). ICC values ranged from 0.86 to 0.93, demonstrating good to excellent reliability for all continuous measurements (p < 0.001).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 22.0). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages (%).

For intergroup comparisons, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for continuous variables, and the chi-square test was applied for categorical variables. In addition, Pearson correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the relationships between NPC diameter and IOF/GPF measurements. Correlation coefficients (r) and corresponding p-values were calculated.

According to the power analysis, the sample size of 180 cases was determined to be sufficient to detect medium effect sizes for ANOVA (Cohen’s f ≈ 0.25). For categorical data, the sample size was considered adequate to detect medium effect sizes (Cohen’s w ≈ 0.30), although it was deemed limited for detecting small effect sizes [15].

A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. In addition to statistical significance, effect sizes and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) were also evaluated. To quantify the strength of associations between categorical variables, Cramer's V coefficients were calculated, whereas eta-squared (η2) coefficients were computed for continuous variables. All effect size estimates were reported together with their 95% confidence intervals.

Results

The mean age of the individuals included in the study was calculated as 43.53 ± 14.32 years. When age distribution was analyzed according to NPC diameter types, no significant differences were observed among the groups. The highest mean age was found in the suspected pathology NPC group (46.15 ± 14.28 years), whereas the lowest mean age was observed in the enlarged NPC group (42.73 ± 14.06 years).

In terms of sex distribution, 48.3% of the participants were female (n = 87) and 51.7% were male (n = 93). No significant association was found between NPC diameter types and sex.

When the relationship between dentition status in the anterior maxillary region and NPC diameter groups was evaluated, the distributions of fully dentate individuals, subjects missing a single central incisor, subjects missing both central incisors, and completely edentulous individuals were found to be comparable across the normal, enlarged, suspected pathology, and NPDC groups. No statistically significant difference was observed among the groups (p = 0.643).

Effect size analysis revealed a very weak association between coronal NPC morphology and NPC diameter groups (Cramer's V = 0.082; 95% CI: 0.043–0.247). In contrast, moderate associations were identified between NPC diameter groups and NPC morphology assessed in the axial and sagittal planes (Cramer's V = 0.323; 95% CI: 0.247–0.432 and Cramer's V = 0.365; 95% CI: 0.314–0.465, respectively).

The effect sizes for IOF and GPF morphologies ranged from small to moderate. Cramer's V values for left and right IOF morphologies were 0.112 (95% CI: 0.101–0.252) and 0.145 (95% CI: 0.123–0.284), respectively. For left and right GPF morphologies, Cramer's V values were calculated as 0.210 (95% CI: 0.170–0.333) and 0.274 (95% CI: 0.214–0.375), respectively.

For continuous variables, η2 values ranged from 0.048 to 0.244, indicating that the effect of NPC diameter groups on IOF and GPF measurements varied from small to large. The smallest effect size was observed for the left IOF vertical diameter (η2 = 0.048; 95% CI: 0.019–0.119), whereas the largest effect size was identified for the left GPF anteroposterior diameter (η2 = 0.244; 95% CI: 0.154–0.387).

The relationship between nasopalatine canal (NPC) morphology and diameter types in the coronal section was also evaluated, and no statistically significant differences were found among the groups (p = 0.752). The most commonly observed morphology across all NPC diameter groups was the single canal type, with a slight increasing trend from the normal group to the cyst-diagnosed group. In contrast, the Y-shaped canal morphology was observed at lower frequencies across all groups. Additional Pearson correlation analyses were performed to further evaluate the relationship between NPC diameter and IOF/GPF measurements. Statistically significant positive correlations were observed between NPC diameter and most IOF and GPF parameters (r = 0.228–0.475, p < 0.05). The strongest correlations were identified for the right GPF mediolateral (r = 0.475, p < 0.001) and right GPF anteroposterior (r = 0.474, p < 0.001) dimensions. No significant correlation was found between NPC diameter and the left GPF anteroposterior measurement (r = 0.020, p = 0.786). These findings further support the presence of measurable morphometric associations between NPC diameter and adjacent anatomical structures.

Discussion

The nasopalatine canal (NPC) is an important anatomical structure located in the anterior maxilla, connecting the nasal and oral cavities and containing neurovascular components [4, 16]. Accurate assessment of its morphology and differentiation between normal, enlarged, and pathological conditions are essential for treatment planning. Given that this region is frequently involved in implant procedures, comprehensive evaluation of the NPC is critical to prevent complications during implant surgery and local anesthesia applications [17, 18]. Currently, cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) is considered a reliable imaging modality for evaluating the NPC and surrounding anatomical structures due to its high-resolution three-dimensional imaging capabilities [19, 20]. The aim of this study was to investigate the morphometric relationship between NPC diameter and IOF/GPF dimensions and to evaluate the associated morphological variations observed in enlarged and pathological NPC conditions. Accordingly, the relationship between these three anatomical structures in the maxillofacial region was analyzed using CBCT. The morphological and dimensional characteristics of the NPC were comparatively evaluated in normal, enlarged, suspected pathology, and histopathologically confirmed NPDC groups. The findings suggest that an increase in NPC diameter may be associated with an increase in IOF and GPF dimensions.

Previous CBCT-based studies have reported that NPC morphology is highly variable and may be influenced by factors such as age, sex, dentition status, and ethnicity [4, 10, 14, 19–33]. Several studies have also indicated that NPC length and diameter may vary among populations, with higher measurements generally reported in males [4, 14, 32]. However, in the present study, no significant relationship was found between NPC diameter groups and age or sex, suggesting that these variables may not be determinative in all populations [25, 26].

Multiplanar evaluation plays a crucial role in the morphological assessment of the NPC [20]. In the literature, NPC morphology has predominantly been evaluated in sagittal Sects. [14, 26, 27, 29, 30, 33], while axial [14, 28] and coronal [19, 24, 28, 31, 33] evaluations are relatively limited. Moreover, most studies have focused on anatomical variations rather than inter-structural relationships. In contrast, the present study evaluated NPC morphology separately in sagittal, coronal, and axial planes.

No significant difference was observed in coronal morphology among the groups, which is consistent with previous reports suggesting that NPC morphology in the coronal plane tends to be relatively stable [27]. The single canal configuration was the predominant type across all groups, in agreement with the literature [19, 24, 28].

In the axial plane, the significant variation in NPC morphology with increasing diameter suggests that canal enlargement is not merely dimensional but also involves morphological transformation. Previous studies have reported oval and round shapes as the most common axial morphologies [14, 28]. In the present study, the increased prevalence of the oval form in suspected pathology and NPDC groups may indicate a morphological adaptation associated with canal expansion.

Similarly, sagittal findings support this observation. While the cylindrical form was predominant in the normal group, the funnel-shaped morphology was more frequent in enlarged and cyst groups, suggesting a progressive morphological change. Previous studies have reported cylindrical and conical forms as the most common sagittal morphologies [10, 14, 21, 30–33], although rare variations and multi-segmented canal structures have also been described [16].

The infraorbital foramen (IOF) is a critical anatomical structure transmitting the infraorbital nerve and vessels, and its morphology is of particular importance in surgical procedures [10, 34, 35]. Several studies have demonstrated variability in IOF size and position across populations [6, 36, 37]. Reported IOF diameters range between 1.78 mm and 3.9 mm [10, 34, 36]. In the present study, the mean horizontal IOF diameters were 3.61 mm (left) and 3.66 mm (right), and the mean vertical diameters were 3.89 mm bilaterally, consistent with the literature. Furthermore, both horizontal and vertical IOF diameters were significantly associated with NPC diameter groups, with higher values observed in enlarged and pathological groups (p < 0.05).

These findings are consistent with those reported by Cam et al. [6], who also found higher IOF measurements in enlarged and suspected pathology groups compared to normal individuals. This supports the existence of a measurable morphometric relationship between NPC diameter and IOF dimensions and may reflect broader anatomical variation within the maxillofacial region. The additional Pearson correlation analyses further supported these findings by demonstrating significant positive correlations between NPC diameter and most IOF and GPF measurements. These results indicate that the observed relationships are not limited to categorical group comparisons but are also present when NPC diameter is evaluated as a continuous variable. This finding provides additional evidence for a measurable morphometric association among these anatomical structures and further supports the consistency of the observed relationships across different statistical approaches.

Although the oval morphology is reported as the most common IOF shape in the literature [6, 10, 36], the present study identified the round type as the most frequent morphology across all groups. No significant association was found between NPC diameter and IOF morphology on the left side, whereas a significant association was observed on the right side, where non-round morphologies were more frequent in pathological groups. This suggests that IOF morphology may be influenced by NPC enlargement and pathological processes [6].

The observed right–left differences should be interpreted with caution. Previous studies have reported bilateral variations in the morphology and dimensions of maxillofacial foramina, and minor asymmetries may occur as part of normal anatomical variation [10, 36]. Since the present study was not specifically designed to investigate the causes of side-related differences, the biological significance of the observed asymmetry remains unclear and warrants further investigation.

The greater palatine foramen (GPF) also exhibits morphological and positional variability among populations, influenced by sex and ethnic factors [11–13]. In this study, GPF measurements increased with NPC diameter, and the cylindrical form became more prevalent in pathological groups, while the round form was absent. These findings suggest that measurable morphometric associations exist among the NPC, IOF, and GPF and that these associations become more evident in pathological conditions.

Previous studies have also demonstrated a relationship between the NPC and anterior maxillary bone and dentition, indicating that bone morphology may influence canal structure [29]. Additionally, accurate assessment of NPC dimensions has been shown to directly affect implant success [38].

One of the key findings of this study is that NPC enlargement is not limited to local morphological changes but is also associated with dimensional and morphological alterations in adjacent structures such as the IOF and GPF. The limited number of studies evaluating these structures together highlights the originality of the present study [6].

Cam et al. investigated the relationship between NPC enlargement and IOF/GPF dimensions. In the present study, this relationship was further evaluated by including histopathologically confirmed NPDC cases. The findings support the presence of a morphometric association between NPC enlargement and IOF/GPF dimensions, particularly in pathological NPC conditions. However, these findings should be interpreted as anatomical associations rather than evidence of predictive or diagnostic performance for NPDC. From a clinical perspective, recognition of these morphometric associations may contribute to a more comprehensive CBCT-based evaluation of the anterior maxilla. Because the NPC, IOF, and GPF are important anatomical landmarks in implant surgery, local anesthesia procedures, and other maxillofacial interventions, awareness of concurrent variations among these structures may assist clinicians during preoperative assessment and treatment planning [5, 7, 11, 12].

Limitations

This study has several limitations. It was conducted at a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Given that NPC morphology may be influenced by demographic and ethnic factors, multicenter studies are warranted. Additionally, subgroup analyses based on bone resorption levels were not performed, and their potential effects cannot be excluded. Furthermore, maxillary size and overall craniofacial morphology were not evaluated. Although dentition status was analyzed and cases with advanced alveolar bone resorption were excluded from the study, the potential influence of these anatomical variables on the measured parameters cannot be completely ruled out.

Another limitation is that all evaluations were performed by a single observer, and observer blinding could not be implemented due to the retrospective nature of the study. Nevertheless, the high kappa and intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) values demonstrated excellent measurement reliability and reproducibility.

Furthermore, the number of cases in the suspected pathology group and the histopathologically confirmed nasopalatine duct cyst (NPDC) group was relatively limited. Because NPDC is an uncommon lesion, assembling larger cohorts remains challenging. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are therefore needed to validate the present findings.

Finally, the direct relationship between morphological findings and clinical outcomes was not evaluated. Future prospective studies are needed to clarify these associations.

The present study was designed to evaluate morphometric relationships rather than diagnostic performance. Therefore, predictive modelling and diagnostic accuracy analyses were not performed.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that the morphology of the nasopalatine canal (NPC) is not merely a local anatomical variation but represents a complex structure associated with surrounding anatomical components. The findings indicate that increased NPC diameter is associated not only with morphological changes of the canal itself but also with dimensional and morphological variations in the IOF and GPF.

The significant morphological changes observed in axial and sagittal planes indicate that NPC enlargement is accompanied by measurable alterations in canal morphology. The pronounced morphological differences observed in pathological and cystic groups suggest that NPC morphology may vary according to both anatomical and pathological conditions.

From a clinical perspective, the observed morphometric associations between NPC diameter and IOF/GPF dimensions may be relevant during implant surgery, local anesthesia procedures, and other maxillofacial interventions. Therefore, comprehensive three-dimensional evaluation of these structures may assist clinicians during preoperative assessment and treatment planning.

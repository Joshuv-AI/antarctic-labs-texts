Time to recovery among tuberculosis patients receiving anti-Koch’s drug in Ibadan, Oyo State, Nigeria
Authors: Yusuff Akinkunmi Olasunkanmi, Rotimi Felix Afolabi, Olowolafe Tubosun Alex, Ayo S Adebowale, Adeniyi Francis Fagbamigbe
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNigeria is ranked seventh in the world and second among African countries with the highest burden of Tuberculosis (TB). This study aims to investigate the recovery time among TB patients receiving the first line of anti-Kochs (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide and Ethambutol) drugs at Chest Hospital Jericho, Ibadan.MethodologyThe study design was a facility-based retrospective cohort study that was conducted among patients on anti-Kochs drugs at a TB clinic from January 2015 to December 2019, Government Chest Hospital Jericho, Ibadan, Oyo state Nigeria. Six hundred and forty-four patient records were extracted from the hospital records. Kaplan-Meier survival method was used to describe the time to recovery, and Cox-proportional hazards model was used to determine factors associated with recovery time from TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs. Hazard ratios (HR) and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) were reported. All analyses were conducted at a 5% level of significance.ResultsThe median survival time (ST) of patients using anti-Kochs drugs for treatment was 61 (CI:59.6–62.4) days. About 72% of the patients recovered from TB after the first two months of having the disease. Patients who were female (aHR = 1.05; CI 0.89–1.25), HIV negative (aHR = 1.03, CI: 0.80–1.34) and lived in rural area (aHR = 0.97; CI 0.83–1.14) were more likely to have a shorter time to recovery from TB compared to their respective counterparts though statistically non-significant.ConclusionThis study showed a significant recovery after the use of anti-Kochs drugs. The recovery time of the participants was 61 days, which indicated that anti-Kochs drug is very effective for TB treatment. To increase adherence, it is suggested that the policy should be changed from the required time of 6 months (180 days) to 2 months (61 days), since the drugs require them to maintain a good diet during the time of taking the drugs. This finding is useful to the National TB Control Program and the hospital authorities.Keywords: Anti-Kochs drugs, Recovery time, Tuberculosis, Prognostic factors, Treatment

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is a contagious disease caused by bacteria known as mycobacterium tuberculosis, it mostly affects the lungs. It usually affects the carriers of the disease throughout the life period and determines the formation of tubercles in different parts of the affected body [1]. The existence of mycobacterium tuberculosis has been for a long time, which survived for over 70,000 years and has affected nearly 2 billion people worldwide (Global Tuberculosis Control), with about 10.4 million new cases annually [1]. Almost one-third of the world’s population are carriers of the TB bacillus and are at risk of developing the active disease [2]. TB is a bacterial disease that can affect virtually every body system but starts and predominates in the lungs [3].

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), in 2010 it was reported that about 9.4 million TB cases occurred globally [4]. In 2017, a treatment relapse of about 9.0 million was recorded among TB-HIV-negative, although about 6.3 million new cases of TB were recorded among people who were HIV-negative [1]. The African region accounts for a quarter of the world’s new cases of TB and 417,000 deaths out of 1.7 million global TB deaths [5].

Nigeria is ranked seventh among the 30 countries in the World with a high TB burden and second in Africa [6]. The prevalence of TB in Nigeria is estimated to be 616 cases per 100,000 [7]. About 407,000 individuals in Nigeria are projected to have TB yearly [6]. According to [8], Nigeria incidence rate on tuberculosis increased from 418 000 cases in 2017 to 429 000 cases in 2018, and deaths also increased from 155 000 to 157 000 during the same period.

Many TB patients have incomplete treatments even though free medication is available [9]. This may result in prolonged infection, and resistance to treatment, which may lead to increased morbidity and mortality [9]. Individuals diagnosed with a pulmonary type of TB and are not exposed to anti-tuberculosis drugs for more than one month (i.e. ‘new cases’ of tuberculosis) should be monitored for 6 months. Also, since antibiotics began to be used to fight TB and the body system is familiar with it, some microbes become resistant to it. The resistance of the microbes to antibiotics made the clinician invent another means of treatment called multi-drug resistant which will help to kill all the bacteria.

Several studies have reported that more than 95% of the patients observed sputum conversion after the use of the anti-Kochs drugs. However, there is no study focusing on the recovery time of TB patients after the use of anti-Kochs drugs using survival analysis. Information on the time to recovery among TB patients receiving the anti-Kochs medicines is needed to know the effectiveness of the drug. This will provide a useful guide in policymaking and program design for the National TB Control Program and the hospital authorities.

This study was conducted to assess the time to recovery from TB among patients receiving anti-Kochs drug in Chest Hospital Jericho Ibadan, Oyo state, Nigeria.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

A facility-based retrospective study was conducted among study participants who received treatment from January 2015 to December 2019, Government Chest Hospital Jericho, Ibadan, Oyo state Nigeria. This clinic was established in 2008 and managed by the State Ministry of Health to support the National TB response which is currently being supported by Damien Foundation. The clinic attends an average of 60–70 patients monthly and is open for services Mondays to Saturdays. It is the largest designated Direct Observed Treatment (DOTs) referral Centre in the State. Is one of the state TB managements centers a busy place, which provides comprehensive and specialized TB care, including initial and continuing counseling.

Study Population and Variables

The study participants consisted of 644 TB patients aged 18 years and above, receiving care at the government chest hospital, Jericho Ibadan, Oyo state. Participants’ information utilized for this study was extracted from the hospital’s records using a structured Excel spreadsheet. The extracted explanatory variables included age (< 24, 25–34, 35–49, ≥ 50 years), sex (male and female), marital status (never married and married), occupation (unemployed, professional and unprofessional), residence (urban and rural), HIV status (positive and negative), TB status after first line (positive and negative), patients status as at last seen (alive and dead), month of recovery after treatment(first, second, third and sixth). The outcome variable for this study was the recovery time of TB patients receiving anti-Kochs drug. The recovery time was estimated as the number of days elapsed between the confirmed date of sputum tested positive and the subsequent confirmed date the sputum converted to negative among patients who attended chest clinic hospitals. In this study, patients who did not recover from TB after six [6] months or those who died during the period of the study were right-censored.

Data analysis

Variables in the participants’ hospital record (secondary data) were extracted into Microsoft Excel which was exported from Microsoft Excel into Stata MP 18 for data cleaning and re-coding. Patients with the complete form of variables were included in the analysis. For analysis, categorical variables were presented using frequencies and percentages while mean and standard deviation were used to present continuous variables. Survival analysis methods were employed to investigate the duration (in days) of recovery from TB after the first use of anti-Kochs drugs. Kaplan-Meier survival method and log-rank test respectively were used to describe the time duration it takes a patient to recover from TB and examine the probability of time recovery of all the covariates. Semi-parametric Cox proportional hazard regression model was thereafter applied to identify the determinant of days to recover from TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs. STATA is a software package used for statistical analysis, data management and data visualization.

The analysis was carried out on Stata MP 18 using the following commands: {stset time-variable, failure(event_variable)} to define the survival data, ‘sts graph, survival’ to generate the Kaplan-Meier curve, ‘sts test variable’ for the log-rank test, and ‘stcox var1 var2 var3 … vari’ to perform the Cox regression model.

Survival analysis

In survival analysis the two most essential quantitative terms are the survivor function S(t) and hazard function h(t). In the present study, the survivor function gives the probability that TB patients survive longer than a specified time (t) after taking the anti-Kochs drugs, while the hazard function gives the instantaneous risk per unit time to recover from TB after time (t).

Unlike the survivor function s(t) which describes the likelihood of not failing before time (t), the hazard function h(t) deals with the instantaneous failure rate at time (t) among TB patients. In survival analysis, two variables are mandatory, they are survival time and the censorship index. It is believed that “survival time” is a discrete random variable that requires only a positive integer. In this study, all patients on anti-Koch’s drug for exactly 6 months are at risk since they are likely to recover from TB after taking the drugs during the period t. The survival time of TB patients is the recovery time for patients after the use of anti-Kochs drugs. However, those that either did not recover from TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs, loss to follow up and death during the time of study were censored in the current study.

A cox proportional hazard model was applied to the recovery time of patients from TB. The model presumed that, from time to time, the proportion of hazards was constant. The effect of a unit increase in covariates is multiplicative for the hazard rate in the proportional hazard model. For an individual with a given specification of a set of independent variables denoted by X to predict the hazard of individuals, the Cox model gives an expression of the hazard at time (t).

Regardless of group, the coefficients are presumed to be the same, but the baseline hazard may be group-specific. The exponential coefficient also known as hazard ratio (HR) is used to express that the exponentials of the coefficients show more exposure to an event of interest if > 1, but low exposure if HR < 1 while HR = 1 has no effect on the exposure. The statistical significance of the coefficient indicates whether or not these improvements would be statistically important over the predicted period. The covariates were plugged into the multiple Cox regression so as to control confounding.

Result

A total of 644 TB patients were identified. The patients’ average (mean ± SD) age was 34.45 ± 15.01 with one hundred and nighty (32.3%) of the patients aged 25–34 years, three hundred and seventy-three (62.6%) were male. More than half (65.2%) of the patients were ever married, three hundred and eighty (62%) were unprofessional, and three hundred and seventeen (51.9%) resided in rural areas. Five hundred and forty-one (89.8%) of the patient card extracted are HIV-negative. More than four-fifths of the patients were alive as at last seen according to the record extracted. Discussion

This study assessed the time to recovery among TB patients receiving anti-Kochs drugs in Ibadan, Oyo state, Nigeria. This study is essential as it contributes to the evidence on Nigeria’s progress toward achieving the global target of reducing TB deaths by 90% and TB incidence by 80% among people living with TB by 2030. The key findings of the study are the median recovery time on receiving treatment for the first-line drug called anti-Kochs.

This finding reflects the number of days it took the patients to recover from TB after the use of the first-line drugs. According to this study, majority of the patients’ sputum smear converted after 61 days (2 months) after the use of the anti-Kochs drugs. This finding is corroborated by a Switzerland study which found that sputum smear of TB patients converted from positive to negative after the second month of treatment using the first line drug (anti-Kochs) [10]. Furthermore, an African study observed that TB patients’ sputum mostly converted two months after passing through an observed therapy which shows the virus does not exist anymore [11].

The study shows the recovery rate of the first-line drug on TB patients registered in Chest Hospital Jericho Ibadan. The proportion of TB patients who became negative after the use of the first-line drug (anti-Kochs drugs) in this study was very high at 93%, this was in line with the study from [11]. An earlier study reported that patients have a 91% chance of sputum conversion after 2 months of severe treatment [12, 13]. A recent study conducted in the south-eastern part of Nigeria reported that patients being positive for TB after 2 months of treatment is as a result of ineffective use of the anti-Kochs drugs [14]. It was stated in an epidemiological study that the proportion of TB patients who remain positive after 2 months can be greater than 20% if patients are not consistent in taking their drugs [15].

The HIV-TB coinfection was not statistically significant in the time to recovery of TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs. Some studies [16] were not in support of this finding; the discrepancies may be due to the study site which primarily focused on TB patients, which potentially led to a lower proportion of TB-HIV coinfection in the study. Similarly [17], patients with HIV have more viral load which may affect the recovery time of patients after the use of anti-Kochs drug. However, a study conducted in Malawi corroborated the finding that HIV status does not have an influence on the recovery time of TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs [18].

Sex was not statistically significant in the recovery time of TB patients after the use of anti-Kochs drugs in this study, this was also similar to an earlier study [14]. This finding was in consonance with a Portuguese study where males were statistically significant in the recovery time of TB patients after the use of anti-Kochs drugs [19]. Similar outcomes have been reported in the literature [12, 20].

The age of patients was not statistically significant in the recovery time after the use of anti-Kochs drugs in this study. Several studies were in line with these findings [21, 22]. Although, patients within the age group of 65 years and more are likely to take more time in recovery from TB after the use of the anti-Kochs drugs, it was similar to a finding by [14] that there is always a delay in recovery time among older patients. This study is corroborated by a study in India, which found that it takes elderly TB patients more time to recover from TB [23].

In this study, there was no statistically significant relationship between the recovery time from TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs and the residence (urban and rural). These findings were similar to the outcome of an earlier study [24] but was a variant with another study which reported that the residence of patients was significant with TB patient recovery time [25] Also, in a similar study carried out in Norway, it was discovered that the residence of patients is not statistically significant with recovery time of TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs [26].

In this study, it was revealed that many of the patients were financially handicapped. Patients indicated that this problem had reduced their treatment adherence and had made them stop visitation to the clinic for the collection of the remaining treatment. Also, a similar result was reported in a qualitative study conducted in Asmara Eritrea, that the majority of the patients lost their jobs during the process of nursing the virus which now makes them financially dependent [27]. Furthermore, it was also discovered in a Ghanaian study that patients found it difficult to get money to transport themselves to the clinic due to the lack of jobs and money to transport themselves to the clinic [28, 29]. Financing is crucial for drug adherence, as many of these medications require adequate nutrition to be effective in the body. TB treatment adherence can improve if patients receive financial support or vocational training to help them generate income.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the recovery time for TB patients using anti-Koch’s drug was estimated to be 2 months. Also, there is a slight difference, but insignificant, in the time to recovery between the males and females. The recovery time from TB after the use of anti-Kochs drugs for sex, age, locality, ethnicity and marital status was 61 days, with those who are divorced recovering after 70 days.

Limitation

There are some limitations identified in the study. This retrospective study, as an observational study, cannot establish causality between TB treatment and other factors influencing sputum conversion. The study’s external validity is additionally impacted because it was only carried out in one location, which limits how far the results can be applied. Additionally, some important variables were not recorded on the patients’ clinic cards, limiting the scope of variables that could be analyzed. Incomplete information was removed to address the restriction of missing data. The fact that the study participants would have had several medical experts involved in their treatment during the study period may also have contributed to variation in the measurement of risk factors and results. Consequently, it is recommended that future research use a prospective cohort study should be done.

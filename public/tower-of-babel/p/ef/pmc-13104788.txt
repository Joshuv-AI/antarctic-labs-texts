Mean Apparent Propagator MRI: A Potential Imaging Biomarker of Brain Microstructural Alterations in Basic-Type Intermittent Exotropia
Authors: Qinglei Shi, Mengdi Zhou, Diwei Shi, Mengqi Su, Zongshu Wang, Shiqing Cong, Huixin Li, Haoran Zhang, Yangfan Sun, Ruijia Zhao, Jie Hong, Jing Fu, Hua Guo, Renzhi Wang, Xiang Wan, Zhaohui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to investigate brain microstructural alterations in children with basic-type intermittent exotropia (IXT) using mean apparent propagator magnetic resonance imaging (MAP-MRI) and to analyze their correlations with clinical signs.MethodsMRI was performed on individuals with basic-type IXT and healthy controls using a 3T Siemens scanner with a 64-channel head coil. Three-dimensional T1-weighted and diffusion-weighted images were acquired. MAP-MRI parametric maps were reconstructed using the Laplace-constrained algorithm, with diffusion tensor imaging maps serving as a reference. Voxel-wise analysis and deterministic fiber tractography were conducted to investigate microstructural changes of cortical regions and visual pathway in basic-type IXT. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was performed to assess the discriminative performance of the MAP-MRI metrics. The correlations between MAP-MRI metrics in significant brain regions and clinical signs were also analyzed.ResultsSeventy-eight individuals with basic-type IXT and 96 healthy controls were enrolled. MAP-MRI revealed extensive microstructural alterations in brain regions associated with binocular vision and oculomotor control, as well as cognition and emotion regulation (P < 0.01 at both the voxel and cluster levels). Receiver operating characteristic analysis demonstrated that MAP-MRI metrics exhibited excellent discriminative performance, with the highest area under the curve of 0.986. In addition, MAP-MRI metrics were correlated with disease severity and disease duration (P < 0.05).ConclusionsMAP-MRI identified widespread microstructural alterations and their associations with clinical signs, providing valuable insights into the neural correlates underlying impaired binocular vision and oculomotor control in children with basic-type IXT. MAP-MRI metrics have the potential to serve as noninvasive imaging biomarkers for detecting IXT-related microstructural changes.

Intermittent exotropia (IXT) is the most common type of childhood exotropia, with a global prevalence of approximately 0.12% to 3.90%, and a higher prevalence reported in Asia, reaching up to 3.9%.1–4 It encompasses four subtypes, including the basic, divergence excess, simulated divergence excess, and convergence insufficiency types, with the basic type being the most common.5 It is characterized by an intermittent exodeviation of either eye, often accompanied by binocular visual function impairment.6–8 If untreated, IXT may deteriorate over time or progress to constant exotropia in some cases.9–11 Cosmetic changes and loss of stereoscopic function may negatively affect the psychological well-being of children and their parents.12

Accumulating evidence indicates IXT is an abnormality of the central nervous system,13–16 yet its neural mechanisms remain unclear. Although functional neuroimaging studies have revealed abnormalities in visual and oculomotor areas in IXT in terms of spontaneous activity, task-related activation, and functional connectivity,17–25 investigations of structural brain alterations, particularly at the microstructural level, remain limited. Previous structural neuroimaging studies on IXT can be broadly categorized into two types. The first type focuses on macroscopic alterations using T1-weighted structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), with techniques such as voxel-based morphometry and source-based morphometry. Voxel-based morphometry analysis demonstrated that children with IXT had reduced grey matter density in oculomotor areas, including the right frontal eye field and bilateral inferior parietal lobule (IPL).21 Source-based morphometry analysis revealed altered cortical thickness and surface area in visual and oculomotor regions, such as left primary visual cortex, left inferior temporal cortex, right visual area, and bilateral IPL.21,26 However, these approaches primarily estimate late-stage macroscopic morphological changes and are insensitive to early microstructural abnormalities.27,28 The second type uses diffusion MRI to probe microstructural alterations. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) studies have reported microstructural changes (as reflected by reduced fractional anisotropy and mean diffusivity) in cortical regions and white matter tracts involved in visual processing and oculomotor control, including right precentral gyrus, right postcentral gyrus, right inferior longitudinal fasciculus, and left thalamic radiation.22,29 Although DTI could detect early microstructural changes, it is constrained by the assumption that water diffusion follows a Gaussian distribution.30 In complex biological tissues, water diffusion is often non-Gaussian owing to cell membranes and other barriers.31 Consequently, DTI has limited capacity to accurately characterize complex tissue microstructures, and its diffusion metrics may be biased. Therefore, to comprehensively characterize brain microstructural changes underlying visual and oculomotor deficits in IXT, a more advanced imaging methodology is needed.

Mean apparent propagator MRI (MAP-MRI), a recently developed diffusion model based on q-space sampling, may overcome these limitations.32 By measuring the probability density function of spin displacement, MAP-MRI can provide useful measures in complex tissue microstructures.33 Compared with DTI, it captures the non-Gaussian nature of the diffusion, enabling more precise quantification of diffusion anisotropy and offering more comprehensive, delicate, and accurate tissue characterization.32,34 MAP-MRI yields quantitative parameters, including mean squared displacement (MSD), q-space inverse variance (QIV), return-to-origin probability (RTOP), return-to-axis probability (RTAP), return-to-plane probability (RTPP), non-Gaussianity (NG), axial NG (NGAx), and radial NG (NGRad), which reflect tissue microstructural features, such as cellularity, diffusion restrictions, and heterogeneity.30 Specifically, MSD quantifies the average water diffusion and reflects the degree of diffusion restriction.35 QIV is sensitive to restricted diffusion and microstructural heterogeneity.35 Zero-displacement probabilities (RTOP, RTAP, and RTPP) provide information on compartment size, cellularity, cell size, and restricting barriers such as myelin.30,32,36 NG indices (NG, NGAx, and NGRad) quantify non-Gaussian diffusion and reflect microstructural complexity.32 Recently, MAP-MRI has proven more sensitive than DTI in detecting microstructural alterations in neurological diseases such as epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.37–40 Nevertheless, the potential of MAP-MRI to characterize brain microstructural changes in children with IXT has yet to be explored.

Therefore, this study applied both MAP-MRI and DTI to investigate brain microstructural alterations in children with basic-type IXT, with DTI serving as a conventional reference method. The correlations between altered MAP-MRI metrics and clinical characteristics were then analyzed. Additionally, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the potential of MAP-MRI metrics as objective imaging biomarkers for distinguishing children with basic-type IXT from healthy controls (HCs).

Methods

Participants

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee and Institutional Review Board of Capital Medical University, Beijing Tongren Hospital (TRECKY2020-139). A total of 78 individuals with IXT (mean age, 10.94 ± 1.69 years, 38 males) and 96 HCs (mean age, 11.14 ± 2.06 years, 48 males) were enrolled. Individuals with IXT were recruited from outpatient clinics of Beijing Tongren Hospital, and the age-, sex-, and education-matched HCs were enrolled from local community via advertisement. All participants included in this study were right handed, to reduce potential confounding effects arising from individual differences in hemispheric lateralization associated with handedness.41 Written informed consent was obtained from all participants and their guardians.

The inclusion criteria for individuals with IXT were (1) diagnosed with basic-type IXT based on medical history and clinical examination, (2) aged 9 to 16 years, and (3) best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) ≤ 0.0 logMAR. Exclusion criteria were (1) any ocular diseases, (2) history of any eye surgery, (3) systemic dysplasia or brain and nervous system diseases, and (4) MRI contraindications. Detailed information on subject recruitment is provided in Supplementary Materials 1.

Ophthalmic Examinations

All participants underwent detailed ophthalmological examinations, including BCVA, fundus examination, and stereoscopic vision. Near stereoacuity was measured with a random dot stereogram at a 40-cm distance. For individuals with IXT, further assessments were performed, including Worth four-dot test (detecting the presence of suppression), Newcastle Control Score (grading the ability of controlling exotropia), and prism alternate cover test (quantifying the angle of deviation). The angle of deviation was measured at near (33 cm) and distance (6 m), using a light source for near fixation and a small toy for distance fixation.

MRI Data Acquisition

MRI data were acquired using a 3T MRI scanner (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) equipped with a standard 64-channel head coil. Earplugs were provided to minimize scanner noise, and foam padding was used to restrict head motion. During the scanning session, participants were instructed to lie still and stay awake, and their state and head motion were continuously monitored via the scanner-room observation system.

Three-dimensional (3D) T1-weighted anatomical images were obtained using a 3D magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition gradient echo sequence: repetition time = 2000 ms, echo time = 2.25 ms, inversion time = 900 ms, matrix size = 256 × 256, field of view = 256 mm × 256 mm, slice thickness = 1.0 mm, flip angle = 8°, 192 slices, and voxel size = 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm. Diffusion-weighted imaging was acquired using an echo-planar imaging sequence with q-space Cartesian grid sampling: repetition time = 4300 ms, echo time = 81 ms, matrix size = 110 × 110, field of view = 220 mm × 220 mm, slice thickness = 2.0 mm, voxel size = 2 mm × 2 mm × 2 mm, b-value range = 0 to 3000 s/mm², echo spacing = 0.68 ms, and the number of acquisitions was 1.

Preprocessing and Reconstruction of MAP-MRI and DTI

To minimize susceptibility- and motion-induced artifacts, we applied a standardized postprocessing pipeline to the diffusion-weighted imaging data, including susceptibility-induced distortion correction and motion correction using the FMRIB Software Library (FSL; https://fsl.fmrib.ox.ac.uk). To enhance the robustness of MAP-MRI against data sparsity and elevated noise levels at high b-values, we used the MAP-MRI algorithm with Laplacian constraints for parametric map reconstruction, based on findings from our preliminary studies and previous studies.36 For DTI parametric map reconstruction, we adopted the widely used standard algorithm. The detailed principles and implementation procedures of both algorithms are provided in Supplementary Materials 2.

Voxel-wise Analysis

After reconstructing the MAP-MRI and DTI-derived parametric maps, normalization was performed to align these maps from individual space to the standard Montreal Neurological Institute space. To improve the accuracy of this transformation, the T1-weighted images were registered to the b = 0 s/mm² image, which served as the reference. Subsequently, the MAP-MRI and DTI parametric maps were normalized to Montreal Neurological Institute space using the transformation matrix obtained from T1-weighted image segmentation. To decrease noise and enhance statistical efficiency, a smoothing kernel with a full width at one-half maximum of 6 mm was applied to the parametric map. These steps were carried out using SPM12 (Wellcome Department of Imaging Neuroscience, London, UK) in conjunction with MATLAB R2020a (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA).

Brain regions were segmented using the Automated Anatomical Labeling and Brodmann area (BA) templates, and group comparisons of MAP-MRI and DTI metrics between the IXT and HC groups were subsequently conducted with RestPlus 1.2.42

ROC Analysis for Discriminative Performance

After voxel-wise comparisons of MAP-MRI parametric maps, masks of regions showing significant group differences for each metric were generated in standard space and inverse-transformed to each participant's native space. Mean and median values were extracted from these regions for each MAP-MRI metric.

The discriminative performance of each MAP-MRI metric was first evaluated individually using ROC curve analysis on mean or median values. Metrics with area under the curve (AUC) exceeding 0.70, 0.75, or 0.80 were then combined into three composite sets and reassessed by ROC curve analysis. A logistic regression model with bidirectional stepwise selection was also performed using all metrics as features to evaluate their joint discriminative performance. Performance measures including AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated. The PPV and NPV were calculated based on the case-control proportion in the study sample. The AUC was used to assess the overall performance. According to established criteria, AUC values of 0.90 to 1.00, 0.80 to 0.90, 0.70 to 0.80, 0.60 to 0.70, and 0.50 to 0.60 indicate excellent, good, fair, poor, and fail discrimination, respectively.43 ROC analysis was performed in Python 3.11.0 using NumPy and Pandas libraries.

Fiber Tractography Analysis

To investigate microstructural alterations in fiber bundle connections among visual and oculomotor cortices in individuals with IXT, deterministic fiber tractography was performed to reconstruct 3D streamlines based on selected regions of interest (ROIs) in the IXT and HC groups. The number and mean length of fiber bundles connecting the selected ROIs were subsequently computed and compared between the two groups. Detailed procedure is provided in Supplementary Materials 3.

Statistical Analysis

Demographic and clinical data were analyzed using SPSS 20.0 (IBM, Armon, NY, USA). Normally distributed data were compared using a two-sample t test, and non-normally distributed data using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as counts, and the comparison of sex was conducted using the χ2 test. Differences in MAP-MRI and DTI metrics between the IXT and HC groups were evaluated using two-sample t tests, controlling for age, sex, and education as covariates. A voxel-level and cluster-level threshold of P < 0.01, Gaussian random field corrected, was applied. Between-group differences in the number and mean length of fiber bundles connecting the selected ROIs were examined using two-sample t tests, with a significance threshold set at a P value of <0.05. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to correlate MAP-MRI parameters in significant brain regions with clinical measures (P < 0.05).

Results

Demographics and Clinical Signs

Demographic data and clinical signs of the IXT and HC groups were presented in Table 1. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in age, sex, handedness, education, or BCVA. Children with IXT showed prominent deficits in near stereoacuity compared with HCs (P < 0.001). Suppression was observed in 28 of the 78 children with IXT (35.9%). In the HC group, the mean near stereoacuity was 1.94 ± 0.27 log arcsec.

Differences in DTI Parameters

Voxel-wise comparisons of DTI parameters revealed significant differences between the two groups (Supplementary Table S2). Children with IXT showed reduced mean diffusivity values in occipital and frontal cortical regions, including the bilateral SMA, SFG, OFCmed, left MOG, IOG, and calcarine. Reduced fractional anisotropy values were also observed in the bilateral SMA. Detailed explanations of all identified brain regions and their functions, along with the microstructural interpretations of MAP-MRI and DTI parameters, are provided in Supplementary Materials 4.

Discriminative Performance of MAP-MRI Metrics

Voxel-based ROC analysis demonstrated that MAP-MRI parameters exhibited high discriminative performance for children with IXT (Fig. 2; Table 3).

(A) The AUC of the mean and median values for each parameter was analyzed using voxels within the binary masks obtained from voxel-wise comparisons between the IXT and HC groups. (B) Discriminative performance analysis for the combination of MAP-MRI parameters. The AUC of the mean and median values of the derived parameters was analyzed at thresholds of 0.70 (B1), 0.75 (B2), and 0.80 (B3). For comparison, the AUC of the mean and median values of the derived parameters was also evaluated using the logistic regression model with bidirectional stepwise selection (B4). Combined mean/median, MAP-MRI metrics with AUC values exceeding 0.70, 0.75, or 0.80 (based on mean or median values) in single-metric ROC analysis were combined and evaluated using ROC curve analysis.

In the single-metric analysis, RTAP demonstrated the best overall performance, with good discriminative ability for both mean (AUC, 0.823; sensitivity, 0.750; specificity, 0.774; NPV, 0.727; PPV, 0.794) and median values (AUC, 0.806; sensitivity, 0.556; specificity, 0.935; NPV, 0.644; PPV, 0.909). The RTOP also showed good performance for mean values (AUC, 0.803; sensitivity, 0.639; specificity, 0.903; NPV, 0.683; PPV, 0.885). Other metrics, including the MSD, QIV, and NGRad, exhibited fair performance with AUCs ranging from 0.72 to 0.78 for mean values. The NGAx showed poor performance, with AUCs around 0.67, whereas the NG demonstrated almost no discriminative ability, with AUCs near 0.54 and very low specificity (0.222), despite high sensitivity (1.000). Combined analysis using parameters selected by AUC thresholds (0.70, 0.75, and 0.80) further improved discriminative performance. The logistic regression model with bidirectional stepwise selection achieved excellent discriminative performance, with an AUC of 0.986 (sensitivity, 1.000; specificity, 0.966; NPV, 1.000; PPV, 0.972) for mean values, and an AUC of 0.932 (sensitivity, 0.971; specificity, 0.828; NPV, 0.960; PPV, 0.872) for median values.

Differences in Fiber Connections Among Visual and Oculomotor Cortices

Based on the voxel-wise MAP-MRI results showing that the between-group differences were mainly located in the dorsal visual pathway, we selected bilateral BA17, BA18, BA19, BA40, BA6, and BA8 were selected as ROIs. BA17, BA18, BA19, and BA40 are key components of the dorsal visual pathway, whereas BA6 and BA8 integrate visual input from this pathway and are involved in oculomotor control. As shown in Table 4, for the left hemisphere, no significant group differences were observed in the number and mean length of fiber bundles. Similar findings were observed in the right hemisphere, except for the reduced mean fiber length in connections between right BA40 and right BA6, and between right BA6 and right BA8 in the IXT group. Overall, these results indicate fiber bundle connections among visual and oculomotor cortices exhibited only limited microstructural alterations in the IXT group compared with HCs.

Discussion

This study applied MAP-MRI for the first time to investigate brain microstructural alterations in children with basic-type IXT, with DTI serving as a reference. MAP-MRI revealed widespread microstructural alterations across visual, oculomotor, and cognitive-affective regions in children with IXT, with pronounced involvement of the dorsal visual pathway. The correlation analysis showed MAP-MRI metrics were correlated with disease severity and disease duration. Compared with DTI, MAP-MRI detected a broader range of microstructural abnormalities, demonstrating its superior sensitivity to subtle microstructural changes. Furthermore, ROC analysis showed that MAP-MRI metrics effectively distinguished children with basic-type IXT from HCs, highlighting their potential as noninvasive imaging biomarkers for detecting microstructural abnormalities.

Children with basic-type IXT showed microstructural abnormalities in binocular vision–related regions, including bilateral MOG, IOG, and SOG, as well as left SPL, PCUN, calcarine, right MTG, and CUN. The calcarine accounts for nearly 67% surface area of V1 and, together with PCUN, plays a critical role in binocular fusion.44 The IOG, MOG, and CUN are implicated in spatial and face processing,45–47 the MTG is essential in 3D surface orientation and object localization, the SPL participates in discrimination of pure stereo-optic disparity information,48,49 and the SOG is involved in visuospatial information processing.50–54 Although the exact histopathological mechanism remains unknown, the reduced MAP-MRI metrics (e.g., MSD, RTOP, RTAP, RTPP, QIV, NG, NGRad, and NGAx) within these regions are generally considered associated with altered diffusion restriction patterns, impaired microstructural integrity, and reduced complexity.30,32 These findings align with previous studies reporting altered activation in the PCUN, IOG, MOG, and SPL in IXT.17,25,55 Binocular fusion comprises motor fusion and sensory fusion responsible for eye alignment and stereoscopic depth perception, which are partially or completely impaired in IXT and closely associated with disease duration.16,56–58 Therefore, abnormal microstructure in visual regions may be related to impaired binocular fusion and stereopsis in children with IXT. Correlation analysis further support this, and hint these abnormalities may progress with disease duration.

Microstructural alterations were also detected in oculomotor areas, including the bilateral IPL, SFG, SMA, and left PCUN. The IPL participates in eye movement initiation and attention modulation.21,59,60 The frontal eye field, located in the SFG, is involved in vergence, saccadic, and voluntary eye movements.61,62 The SMA participates in the saccadic task and controls eye movement,63,64 and the PCUN plays a role in visuomotor coordination.65,66 The observed reductions in MAP-MRI metrics (MSD, RTOP, RTAP, RTPP, NGRad, and NGAx) across these regions may reflect diminished microstructural integrity and complexity. Consistent with our findings, Fei et al.21 also found children with IXT showed reduced gray matter density in the frontal eye field and IPL. Clinically, children with IXT usually exhibit impaired oculomotor control and visuomotor coordination, manifested as reduced saccadic efficiency, abnormal smooth pursuit, and poor eye–hand coordination.67–69 Taken together, microstructural changes in these regions may be associated with clinically observed abnormal oculomotor control and visuomotor coordination.

Additionally, microstructural changes were found in cognitive–affective regions, including bilateral OFCmed, left insula, and ROL. The OFCmed is crucial for cognitive function and emotional control,70 and the insula and ROL are involved in emotion processing and social communication.71–74 Decreased RTAP, RTOP, and QIV in the OFCmed may reflect decreased neuronal density and microstructural complexity, whereas increased RTOP in the insula and ROL suggests greater diffusion restriction. Abnormal cortical thickness in the anterior insula, OFC, and PFC were also reported in concomitant exotropia.75 Abnormalities in these brain regions may be related to cognition and emotion regulation deficits in children with IXT.

Our results indicate that microstructural abnormalities in children with IXT extend beyond the visual and oculomotor regions to encompass cognitive–affective areas. Significant alterations were mainly located along the dorsal visual pathway, consistent with prior studies highlighting its critical role in eye movement and binocular disparity processing.19,21,76,77 Compared with six prior neuroimaging studies on children with IXT, the mean exodeviation in our cohort was largely comparable with three studies reporting severe exodeviation,21,23,25 and higher than in three studies reporting moderate exodeviation.22,26,29 As the degree of exodeviation reflects disease severity, greater exodeviation may be associated with more pronounced alterations in visual and oculomotor regions, as supported by correlation analyses in our study and previous studies.20,22,29 Beyond its impact on visual function, IXT could negatively affect children's daily life and mental health, leading to decreased reading ability, impaired motor skills, diminished eye-related quality of life, lower self-perception, and an increased risk of developing psychological problems such as anxiety and depression.78–81 Given that childhood is a critical period for visual development, the brain's neuronal connections are plastic.82–84 Therefore, timely and appropriate treatments and interventions may help restore binocular function at this stage.14

MAP-MRI revealed microstructural alterations in additional brain regions that were not detected by DTI, encompassing key nodes along the dorsal visual pathway (IPL and SPL), visual processing regions (SOG, CUN, PCUN, and MTG), and regions associated with cognitive–emotional functions (insula and ROL). These findings suggest that MAP-MRI is more sensitive than DTI in detecting subtle microstructural abnormalities.30,32,34 The excellent discriminative ability of MAP-MRI metrics, as demonstrated by ROC analysis, further supports their potential value as noninvasive imaging biomarkers. The advantages of MAP-MRI may stem from two factors: enhanced sensitivity to tissue microstructure beyond the Gaussian diffusion assumption, and when combined with Laplacian regularization, more robust and higher-fidelity modeling under noisy or sparsely sampled high b-value conditions (Supplementary Materials 2).36

This study has several limitations. First, owing to its cross-sectional design, the study could not assess how microstructural alterations evolve over time. Consequently, we cannot determine the temporal sequence of the observed alterations or infer whether they represent primary changes in IXT or secondary adaptations, nor can we establish causal relationships; longitudinal studies are therefore warranted. Second, only a single imaging modality was used; future work integrating MAP-MRI with other modalities and potential etiological factors, such as genetic markers, will be important for more comprehensively elucidating the pathophysiological mechanisms of IXT. Finally, children under 9 years were not included to ensure reliable compliance with the study procedures and adequate data quality.

Conclusions

MAP-MRI identified microstructural alterations in brain regions associated with binocular vison, oculomotor control, as well as cognition and emotion regulation in children with basic-type IXT, with pronounced changes observed in the dorsal visual pathway. These alterations are correlated with disease severity and disease duration. ROC analysis demonstrated MAP-MRI metrics had strong discriminatory performance, highlighting their potential value as noninvasive imaging biomarkers for detecting IXT-related microstructural changes. Collectively, these findings deepen our understanding of the neural mechanisms underlying impaired binocular vision and oculomotor control in children with basic-type IXT, and provides a basis for subsequent treatment and intervention.

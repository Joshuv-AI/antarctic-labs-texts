Improving forward identified hadron spectra in proton carbon collisions at 90 GeV/c using impact parameter dependent subcollision acceptance in PYTHIA 8 Angantyr
Authors: Nuha Felemban, Khaled Abdel-Waged
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe present a systematic study of identified–hadron production (protons, , ) in at over – using PYTHIA 8.315 with the Angantyr extension. To match the NA61/SHINE production-event definition, we apply a generator-level selection that suppresses events dominated by a single forward, beam-momentum proton at very small angle (elastic-/quasi-elastic–like “beam survivors”), while leaving hadron production in accepted inelastic events unchanged. We find that both the default Angantyr baseline and the non-suppressed fixed-radii geometry underestimate forward proton yields for , despite describing mid-angle production reasonably well. To address this, we introduce an impact–parameter–dependent suppression applied after the fixed–radii ND/SD/DD/EL classification, modifying only the relative fractions of ND/SD/DD/EL NN encounter types while leaving particle production within accepted encounters unchanged. Relative to a b–invariant (“flat”) alternative, the b–dependent scheme hardens the forward tail without introducing unphysical distortions outside the forward region. Including modest hadronic rescattering enhances soft yields at larger angles while preserving the very-forward high-p component; with a common strangeness retune, the combined setup gives the most consistent description across protons, pions, and kaons. These results show that a minimal impact-parameter–dependent change in the mixture of NN encounter types can improve forward yields at SPS energies without distorting large-angle production, once the NA61/SHINE production-event class is reproduced.Subject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

Precise hadron–production data for proton–carbon (C) interactions at fixed–target energies are indispensable for two broad arenas of contemporary physics: accelerator–based neutrino beams and cosmic–ray air–shower simulations. In long–baseline facilities, high–energy protons impinge on graphite targets and produce secondary mesons whose decays determine the flux; reinteractions of primary protons and of secondary hadrons inside the target and focusing elements further reshape the spectrum and flavor content. Measurements at (corresponding to ) directly constrain these secondary/tertiary components, thereby improving flux predictions for current and future programs (e.g., DUNE)1,2. The same hadronic channels govern the early stages of atmospheric cascades, so validated C production models are also critical inputs to air–shower frameworks (e.g., Corsika, Sibyll, Geant4, PYTHIA–based chains)3–6. Responding to these needs, NA61/SHINE at the CERN SPS has recently reported differential multiplicities of identified hadrons in C at 2, providing a stringent benchmark for event generators and a new handle on systematic uncertainties in neutrino–flux and air–shower modeling.

Despite this central role, widely used Geant4 10.7.0 physics lists show nontrivial tensions with the NA61/SHINE identified–hadron measurements for protons, charged pions, and charged kaons2. These lists couple a high–energy string–excitation model to an intranuclear cascade and nuclear de–excitation: the FRITIOF string model (FTF) with Precompound (P) and the Bertini cascade (BERT), FTFP_BERT; FTF with the Binary cascade (BIC), FTF_BIC; the Quark–Gluon String model (QGS) with P and BERT, QGSP_BERT; and the mixed “QBBC” tune8–12. Overlay comparisons of these lists to the NA61/SHINE spectra are shown in Ref. 7 (based on the measurements of Ref. 2). A consistent pattern emerges: no single list reproduces all bins simultaneously for p, , and 7, see Figs. 9.14–9.28. The largest discrepancies occur in the most forward angular bins and near spectrum edges, while mid–angle, mid–momentum regions are typically closer. Concretely, forward protons at –5 mrad are systematically underestimated for 7, Fig. 9.20; panels at – exhibit noticeable shifts of spectral peaks among lists relative to data7, Figs. 9.14 and 9.17; and examples display normalization and shape differences already at forward (–20 mrad) and at central angles (–80 mrad)7, Fig. 9.25–9.27. These residuals motivate targeted improvements to the forward, small–angle description.

We address these shortcomings using PYTHIA 8.31513 with the Angantyr extension14, which embeds Glauber geometry15,16 and Good–Walker diffraction17 into an event–by–event pA framework. In this picture, each admitted NN encounter seeds a pp-like partonic final state via PYTHIA’s multiparton interactions18,19 and Lund string fragmentation20, with soft diffractive features inherited from FRITIOF-like string excitation21,22. Building on our recent analysis at —where a flat (impact-parameter–independent) subcollision suppression combined with a near-beam selection and hadronic rescattering improved agreement with NA61/SHINE while revealing residual forward-angle tensions23—the present work (i) matches the NA61/SHINE production-event class on the generator side by suppressing events dominated by a single forward, beam-momentum proton at very small angle (elastic-/quasi-elastic–like “beam survivors”), thereby removing non-producing topologies from the comparison sample, and (ii) modifies only the relative fractions of geometrically identified non-diffractive (ND), single- and double-diffractive (SD, DD), and elastic NN encounter types as a function of impact parameter, without changing the particle-production dynamics within accepted encounters. This strategy improves the forward admixture—in particular the balance between non-diffractive and diffractive topologies—without requiring broad retunes of hadronization parameters; we validate it here at against the default Double–Strikman baseline in Angantyr, in which the “opacity/greyness” is an effective NN absorptivity (eikonal strength) of the elementary encounter that encodes configuration-by-configuration fluctuations and unitarization, rather than a nuclear initial-state shadowing effect.

Our impact-parameter–dependent acceptance factor can be interpreted as a generalized survival probability: it reduces the likelihood that a geometrically identifiedNN encounter contributes to the final state. Survival factors are widely used for diffractive topologies, for example in PYTHIA 8’s hard-diffraction/MPI framework24–27 and as fully differential soft-survival factors in SuperChic 2/328,29. Here the same idea is applied to all encounter types (ND/SD/DD/elastic) in pA and is imposed after the geometric classification, so that only the relative mixture and the number of accepted encounters are changed, not the particle production within an accepted encounter. This differs from approaches that implement nuclear screening by modifying the underlying interaction at the amplitude or cross-section level, typically through eikonal unitarization in impact-parameter space. Examples include QGSJET-II with enhanced multi–Pomeron diagrams and an opacity 30, SIBYLL 2.3d with a two-channel Good–Walker eikonal31, EPOS-LHC with Gribov–Regge multiple scattering and saturation32, and DPMJET-III/PHOJET with Regge-based absorptive corrections33. Herwig likewise treats soft diffraction within a cluster-hadronization + soft-MPI environment, where gap survival emerges from MPI interplay34. Our approach is complementary: it provides a minimal impact-parameter–dependent adjustment of the ND/SD/DD/elastic encounter mixture and of the number of accepted subcollisions, motivated by the NA61/SHINE systematics, while leaving the particle-production dynamics for accepted encounters unchanged.

Angantyr extends PYTHIA 8.315’s pp event generation to pA and AA by sampling the number and geometry of nucleon–nucleon (NN) encounters in a Glauber picture15,16 and incorporating diffractive excitations via the Good–Walker formalism17. Wounded nucleons—those participating in at least one inelastic interaction—act as independent particle–production sources in both soft and semi–hard regimes, in the spirit of wounded–nucleon phenomenology37,38 but embedded in a partonic event–generator framework. Concretely, Angantyr constructs the event as a superposition of pp–like subevents, each generated by the PYTHIA 8.315 MPI model18,19 and associated with an individual NN subcollision; diffractive and non–diffractive topologies are both included, with NN pairs classified as ND, SD, DD, or elastic (EL), which in turn determines which PYTHIA 8.315 subprocesses are invoked and how many particle–production sources contribute to a triggered event14,39. In our setup, after an NN pair has been assigned to ND/SD/DD/EL by the fixed–radius (Naive) geometric model, we accept or reject that already-classified encounter with a channel-dependent probability that depends on the effective impact parameter. This changes only the number and the relative mixture of accepted NN subcollisions (including both primary and secondary encounters), while leaving the subsequent PYTHIA 8.315 particle production for accepted encounters unchanged. The corresponding expressions are given in Eq. (12) and Eqs. (7)–(11); subsequent MPI, ISR/FSR, and Lund fragmentation proceed with the baseline tune, and hadronic rescattering and vertex settings follow Table 1. Nucleon positions in C are sampled from a harmonic–oscillator–based density tuned to the empirical charge profile40.

Role of subcollision models. A central ingredient in Angantyr is the subcollision model, which translates the sampled nuclear geometry into probabilities for an NN encounter to be elastic, non-diffractive, or diffractive, and thereby fixes (i) the interaction profile in impact parameter (how “black” or “transparent” the NN encounter is), (ii) the event-by-event fluctuation scheme (how the interaction strength varies from encounter to encounter, which controls diffraction), and (iii) the partition of NN encounters into ND/SD/DD/EL classes. Through these choices the subcollision model determines how many nucleons participate and how many particle-producing subevents are generated, so it directly affects per-event and per-trigger observables.

Double–Strikman model (Angantyr:CollisionModel=2). This model includes event-by-event fluctuations in the NN interaction strength (a Good–Walker–type picture), together with a semi-transparent interaction profile, which broadens the distribution of participating (wounded) nucleons and increases the diffractive contribution. CollisionModel = 2: Double–Strikman subcollision model (baseline). In Angantyr:CollisionModel=2, the Double–Strikman scheme provides a physically motivated description of nucleon–nucleon (NN) subcollisions with explicit event-by-event fluctuations of the effective transverse nucleon size14. In simple terms, different NN encounters are allowed to be more “compact” or more “extended” in the transverse plane, which changes their interaction probability and, consequently, the diffractive versus non-diffractive admixture. These quantum fluctuations (sometimes described as hot-spot–like variations of the transverse profile) are modeled by sampling an effective radius r from a gamma distribution,

Angantyr represents the encounter by a disk-like profile in : interactions are allowed only for separations smaller than a radius determined by , and the overall “greyness” of that disk is set by an opacity factor. It is therefore a property of the elementary NN amplitude (and its Good–Walker/color-fluctuation content), not a nuclear initial-state effect such as shadowing; the nuclear target enters separately through the Glauber geometry that samples how many such NN encounters occur in pA.

Channel-wise impact-parameter suppression on fixed–radii (Naive) geometry. Complementary to the fluctuating, semi-transparent Double–Strikman baseline (Angantyr:CollisionModel=2), we introduce a suppression layer that operates on the fixed–radii, black-disk Naive subcollision geometry (Angantyr:CollisionModel=0). The purpose is to adjust the relative fractions of non-diffractive (ND), single-diffractive (SD), double-diffractive (DD), and elastic (EL) NN encounter types in pA while keeping the particle-production model for an accepted encounter unchanged. Concretely, after the standard Naive geometric assignment has classified an NN pair as ND/SD/DD/EL, we accept or reject that already-classified pair with a channel-dependent probability that depends on an effective impact-parameter variable. In this way the black-disk classification boundaries remain intact; only the encounter admission probability is modified. This contrasts with the Double–Strikman baseline, where the relative class fractions follow from event-by-event fluctuations of the interaction strength and profile.

Conceptual relation to other generators. The encounter-level acceptance layer introduced here changes only how often each already-classified NN encounter type contributes to the triggered sample. It does not alter the particle-production dynamics of an accepted encounter (MPI, showering, fragmentation). This is analogous in spirit to rapidity–gap survival factors (often expressed as a survival probability that reduces diffractive contributions). In PYTHIA 8.315, hard diffraction realizes a dynamical gap survival through an MPI-based veto24–27, while SuperChic implements an eikonal survival factor at the amplitude level28,29. By contrast, generators such as HIJING modify particle production more directly through impact-parameter–dependent shadowing and in-medium energy loss48,49. In our framework, spectra change only through the post-filter channel composition and the mean number of admitted subcollisions per triggered event (see Eqs. (14)–(16)).

Results and discussion

This section validates PYTHIA 8.315 13 with Angantyr against identified–hadron data in proton–carbon (p+C) interactions at . Identified species include protons, charged pions, and charged kaons. Observables are analyzed per selected production event and binned in eight laboratory polar–angle intervals, . Model predictions are compared to NA61/SHINE at 2. Here “selected production event” refers to the generator-level emulation of the NA61/SHINE production sample described in Appendix A.

We confront four subcollision scenarios under identical shower/fragmentation and hadron–level settings (Table 1): (i) the Double–Strikman baseline (CollisionModel=2); and the fixed–radii (Naive, CollisionModel=0) geometry with (ii) Mode A impact-parameter–dependent encounter acceptance, (iii) Mode B flat (impact-parameter–independent) encounter acceptance, and (iv) Mode C with no additional encounter acceptance. In Modes A and B, the already-assigned ND/SD/DD encounter types are retained or rejected with channel-dependent probabilities, so that only the relative mixture of encounter types entering the triggered sample is changed. The particle-production model within an accepted encounter is unchanged. Inspection of the generator truth record shows that this edge is dominated by elastic and quasi-elastic topologies in which a leading proton exits with nearly full beam momentum at very small angle. These systematics indicate that the dominant modeling challenge at this beam momentum is the ultra-forward region, where (i) beam-survivor (quasi-elastic) contamination and (ii) the relative balance of encounter types both matter. Accordingly, our strategy is to (a) enforce a generator-level definition of the NA61/SHINE production event class (Appendix A) and then (b) adjust the relative acceptance of already-classified ND/SD/DD/EL encounters as a function of impact parameter (Table 1), without altering the particle-production dynamics within an accepted encounter. These comparisons reinforce the trends observed in our PYTHIA 8.315 study and motivate the combined use of the production-event selection proxy and the impact-parameter–dependent encounter acceptance as a complementary route to improve the small-angle, high-momentum description while keeping the mid-angle region essentially unchanged.

Summary and conclusions

We have analysed identified–hadron production (protons, , ) in at with PYTHIA 8.315+Angantyr, across –. As a first step, to match the NA61/SHINE production event class, we applied a generator-level suppression of “beam-like survivor” events in the near-beam corner of phase space (very small and ): events in this corner are down-weighted by a smooth selection weight with a tunable overall strength and an optional stronger suppression for more central encounters (Appendix A; Table 4). This removes elastic/quasi-elastic and ultra-forward survivor topologies that are rejected by the experimental trigger and offline selection, yields a smooth approach to in the forward spectra, and leaves the mid-angle/mid-momentum region essentially unchanged.

Building on this selection-matched baseline, we then introduced our physics extension: a channel-wise encounter-acceptance layer applied to the fixed–radii (Naive, CollisionModel=0) subcollision geometry. After Angantyr assigns each NN encounter to ND/SD/DD/EL, we accept or reject that already-classified encounter with a probability that depends on its channel and (in Mode A) on impact parameter; this changes only the relative mixture and number of accepted encounter types, while leaving the particle-production dynamics within an accepted encounter unchanged. We explored three configurations (Table 1): Mode A (b-dependent), Mode B (flat, b-independent), and Mode C (no additional acceptance), and we compared them to the Double–Strikman baseline (CollisionModel=2), where the “opacity/greyness” represents an effective NN absorptivity (eikonal strength) of the elementary encounter rather than a nuclear shadowing model. Finally, we assessed the effect of hadronic rescattering (HR) by enabling the optional final-state hadron–hadron rescattering stage.

Main findings. The generator-level suppression of beam-like survivors (Appendix A) removes the artificial enhancement as in the high-momentum corner (), while leaving the spectra at larger angles essentially unchanged. After this selection is applied, both the Double–Strikman baseline and the non-suppressed fixed–radii reference (Mode C) still underestimate the forward proton yield for most prominently at –. A flat, impact-parameter–independent encounter acceptance (Mode B) increases the forward proton yield above , but it simultaneously produces an overshoot that grows toward and reduces the low-p yield at larger angles (–), indicating that a single global reweighting can be too rigid. In contrast, the impact-parameter–dependent scheme (Mode A), which leaves small-b (more central) encounters largely unchanged and applies most of the reweighting at larger b (more peripheral) encounters, raises the forward tail in the shown comparisons without generating the Mode B peak. Enabling hadronic rescattering on top of Mode A (Mode A+HR) further improves agreement in the lowest-p region for –, with little impact on the very-forward high-p behaviour.

For pions, the Mode A+HR setup provides improved agreement in the shown comparisons: it retains Mode A’s forward description and increases the soft yield at intermediate and large angles through additional final-state interactions and resonance feed-down. For kaons, with a common strangeness retune (StringFlav:probStoUD), Mode A+HR likewise gives improved agreement across the displayed angle bins: Mode B suppresses the non-diffractive component that dominates associated strangeness production and therefore undershoots some bins, whereas Mode A retains a larger non-diffractive contribution and HR mainly redistributes yield toward low p without strongly altering the intermediate/high-p tail. Overall, in the set of comparisons shown here, an impact-parameter–dependent encounter acceptance applied after ND/SD/DD/EL classification (Mode A), supplemented by modest final-state rescattering, provides improved agreement relative to a flat acceptance (Mode B) and to the unsuppressed baselines.

Overall perspective and outlook. A central lesson of this study is that a like-for-like comparison to NA61/SHINE requires a clear separation between (i) a generator-level emulation of the published production-event class (Appendix A) and (ii) physics modelling of the interaction. Once the event class is matched, the remaining discrepancies are driven predominantly by the relative mixture of ND/SD/DD encounters rather than by modifications to hadronization or final-state particle production within an accepted encounter. The encounter-acceptance layer introduced here (Table 1) provides a controlled way to vary that mixture after geometric classification, improving the forward region while maintaining the already reasonable description at larger angles; modest hadronic rescattering further helps in the soft sector without changing the forward high-p trends.

A natural next step is to test portability across systems and energies where identified spectra and forward sensitivity exist, using the same two-step strategy (event-class emulation, then encounter-composition tuning). Candidate validation sets include RHIC small systems at , LHC at – (including multiplicity-differential measurements), and SPS light-ion and peripheral Pb+Pb data to probe the transition to many-subcollision regimes.

Appendix A: Generator-level implementation of the NA61/SHINE event selection at

A.1 Event-class proxy for the NA61/SHINE production sample

The NA61/SHINE spectra are normalized per production interaction, i.e. inelastic p+C collisions that produce secondary hadrons; purely elastic and quasi-elastic events (where the beam proton remains essentially intact and little or no secondary production occurs) are excluded by the experimental trigger and offline selection. To match this event class on the simulation side, we apply an event-level filter to the generated final state from PYTHIA 8 (Angantyr): we reject events that contain only a single forward, beam-momentum proton and no additional hadronic activity, and we retain events with clear secondary-hadron production. This requirement reproduces the net effect of the NA61/SHINE beam-definition and quality selection using the Beam Position Detectors (BPD, tracking and quality cuts on the incoming beam) together with the downstream S4 scintillator interaction trigger (used to suppress non-interacting beam particles), ensuring a consistent normalization for the forward-angle spectra comparison. We do not simulate detector response; the procedure in this appendix is an analysis-level selection applied to the generator output to match the published event class and fiducial phase space.

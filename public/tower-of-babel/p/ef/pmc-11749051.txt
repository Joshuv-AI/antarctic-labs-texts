Evaluation of the Changes in the Alveolar Bone and Root Length after Maxillary Anterior Tooth Retraction and the Factors Influencing Them
Authors: S. Harinarayanan, Sanju Somaiah, B. Goutham, Sunil Muddaiah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction:There is a scarcity of studies in which both alveolar bone remodeling and root resorption are simultaneously assessed and compared against possible influencing factors such as rate of retraction and change in inclination. The aim of our study was to assess the changes in the alveolar bone thickness and root length of the maxillary anterior teeth after retraction and correlate it to the potential influencing factors, namely rate of retraction and change in inclination.Subjects and Methods:Ten patients requiring upper premolar extraction as part of their treatment were included in the study. Alveolar bone and root length changes were measured using cone beam computed tomography and the rate of retraction and change in inclination were assessed using lateral cephalograms. Two readings: Preretraction and postretraction were taken. Statistical analysis was done using Paired rank t-test and Spearman’s rank correlation tests. Significance level was set at P < 0.05.Results and Conclusions:The labial alveolar bone thickness showed an increase, whereas the palatal and total alveolar bone thickness showed a decrease. Root resorption was observed in all teeth. Alveolar bone changes showed a positive correlation to the rate of retraction and a negative correlation to the change in inclination. The root length change showed a positive correlation to the change in inclination.

Introduction

Proclination of the anterior teeth is one of the most frequent malocclusions encountered by an orthodontist. Hence, retraction is one of the commonly used mechanics in orthodontics. Hence, a thorough understanding of the factors associated with it and its effects is most important in clinical practice. It is well established that orthodontic tooth movement occurs by alveolar bone resorption on the side of pressure and formation on the side of tension.[1]

Certain studies have found that the upper incisor retraction results in an increased bone deposition in the labial cortical plate. At present, the pattern of bone remodeling following anterior tooth retraction and the factors affecting it remain unclear, and there is a need for more quality studies to determine the same.[2] It is important to maintain the ideal force so as to prevent adverse effects on the alveolar bone. For instance, when the area involved is relatively small, too much force can cause cortical bone resorption and fenestration of the root.[3]

Orthodontically induced root resorption is an unavoidable pathological complication of orthodontic tooth movement. This resorption may be related to various factors, which include the biological variations in each individual, mechanical factors and genetics. Whilst the biological factors that are related to each individual patient cannot be controlled by the clinician, any mechanical factors involved with it must be managed.[4]

The popularization of cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) has made it possible to make very accurate analysis of the alveolar bone and root length. The most advantageous feature of CBCT is that it produces images with zero magnification.[5] It has been found that when compared to CBCT, the orthopantomogram consistently showed reduced values for the extent root resorption and hence cannot be relied upon for measuring the same.[6] The second major advantage is that the images are not affected by the orientation of the skull.[7] The utilization of a smaller field of view (FOV) minimizes radiation exposure of the patient. For the visualization of smaller areas, such as the maxillary anterior teeth, smaller FOVs are sufficient.[8]

Various factors have been said to affect the alveolar bone changes. Studies have found that the alveolar bone changes at various levels are correlated to the change in inclination, rate of retraction, and the extent of intrusion.[6] It has also been found that the pattern of bone remodeling varies at the different levels of the alveolar bone. Studies have found variations in the alveolar bone area and the vertical bone level at different areas of the tooth.[9]

The concept of repair of the root resorption postorthodontic treatment is still a controversial one. Studies have shown that even though there is evidence of some repair of the resorption post active orthodontic treatment, the extent of repair done remains unclear.[10] Hence, minimizing the extent of root resorption is most important.

Alveolar bone remodeling is an integral part of orthodontic tooth movement. Furthermore, root resorption during orthodontic treatment cannot be fully prevented. However, there is a scarcity of both studies in which both the parameters are assessed simultaneously and compared against potential influencing factors such as the rate of retraction and the change in inclination. Hence, the purpose of this study was to assess the alveolar bone and root length changes during maxillary anterior tooth retraction and their relation to the rate of retraction and root length changes.

Informed consent was obtained from all the participants of the study.

Cephalometric analysis

In the cephalogram, the SN line, palatal plane (PP), and the occlusal plane were traced. A line drawn from Sella (S) perpendicular to OL was constructed occlusal plane (OLp). The amount of space closure was measured as the change in the distance between the incisal edge of U1 (UIE) and the OLp before and after retraction. The change in inclination was calculated as the change in the angle between the PP and the long axis of U1 passing through the UIE and the root apex before and after retraction.

Cone beam computed tomography imaging

All the images were obtained using the same machine (Orthophos S, Dentsply Sirona) with the same patient positioning protocol and 50 mm × 50 mm FOV. Sidexis 4 X-ray image viewing software was used to measure all the parameters. The thickness of the labial and palatal alveolar plates was measured for each tooth, at the site adjacent to the widest point of the labiopalatal aspect of the root, in three slices separated by 3 mm (S1, S2, and S3, respectively). Labial and palatal alveolar bone thicknesses were assessed at the crestal level (S1; L1 and P1), midroot level (S2; L2 and P2), and apical level (S3; L3 and P3) to evaluate the bone thickness changes during retraction. The total alveolar bone thickness was taken as the sum of the labial and palatal alveolar bone thickness; T1 = L1 + P1, T2 = L2 + P2, T3 = L3 + P3.

Nine measurements were taken for each tooth from the radiographs: Three on the labial side, three on the palatal side, and three for the total thickness at the beginning of retraction [Figure 3]. The same measurements were repeated postspace closure (T1) [Figure 4]. T1 measurements were taken at the same slice levels as the T0 measurements. Similarly, root length change was measured using the same slices as the difference between the total tooth length at the beginning of retraction (R1) and the total tooth length postspace closure (R2), that is R1-R2. The total tooth length was taken as the distance between the incisal edge of the tooth and the most superior point of the root apex, along the long axis of the tooth. The rate of retraction and the change in inclination were measured from the lateral cephalogram and the rate of retraction calculated as the amount of retraction per month [Figure 5].

Statistical analysis

The data were collected, coded, and fed in SPSS Statistics for Windows (IBM, Ver 27.0, Armonk, NY, USA) for the statistical analysis. Paired rank t-test was used to analyze the alveolar bone and root length changes. The Spearman rank correlation test was used to evaluate the correlations between alveolar bone and root length changes, inclination change, and rate of retraction. The significance level of all tests was established at 0.05.

Discussion

Orthodontic tooth movement involves the movement of the tooth through a solid medium, namely the bone. This involves bone remodeling around the tooth being moved, with bone resorption on the leading side and bone formation on the lagging side. Several potential factors may affect bone remodeling. Of these, the rate of retraction and the change in inclination can be controlled by the treatment mechanics employed by the clinician and hence have to be studied thoroughly.

Root resorption following tooth movement is an unavoidable effect of orthodontic treatment. However, it should be controlled and within a reasonable limit. Hence, the evaluation of the normal root resorption following anterior tooth retraction is essential. Similarly, the study of the potential factors that may influence it, especially those factors that are under the control of the clinician, must be thoroughly studied.

The present study was done to assess the alveolar bone and root length changes after maxillary anterior retraction and the potential factors influencing them, namely the rate of retraction and the change in inclination.

The findings of this study showed an increase in the alveolar bone thickness in the labial aspect, whereas the alveolar bone thicknesses showed a reduction on the palatal aspect postspace closure. The root length showed a significant reduction postspace closure among all teeth, indicating root resorption [Table 2]. The lowest mean root resorption was seen in the two canines, whereas the highest mean reduction in root length was observed in the left lateral incisor. The change in alveolar bone thickness postspace closure showed a significant positive correlation with the rate of retraction indicating a degree of association between the alveolar bone change and the rate of retraction [Table 3]. Significant positive correlation was observed between the root length reduction and the inclination change indicating a possible association between the extent of root resorption and the inclination change [Table 4]. Significant negative correlation between the alveolar bone changes and inclination change was found indicating that the change in inclination inversely affects the change in alveolar bone thickness.

The results of our study are in accordance with the study done by Yodthong et al. to evaluate the factors related to the changes in alveolar bone thickness during upper incisor retraction. They found out that the labial bone thickness at the crestal level showed a significant increase. They also found that the changes in the alveolar bone were correlated to the rate of retraction and the change in inclination. These observations were made in our study also. However, they also observed an increase in the total bone thickness at the apical level postretraction, which was not found in our study.[2]

Our results are also in with the study done by Chen and Liu for the morphologic evaluation of root resorption after retraction using mini-implants in bimaxillary protrusion patients. They observed a reduction in the root length in the maxillary anteriors postretraction. Although the difference between the reduction in length of the lateral incisors and canines was not significant, the highest reduction in the root length was observed in the lateral incisor, which was also observed in our study.[4]

However, the results obtained from our study are not in accordance with the study done by Eksriwong and Thongudomporn to analyze the alterations in the alveolar bone change in relation to root position change after maxillary incisor retraction using stable skeletal structures as reference. Their results showed a significant reduction in the alveolar bone thickness in the labial aspect after retraction. No statistically significant changes were observed in the palatal alveolar bone. Their study also showed a positive correlation between the labial alveolar bone change and the inclination change, whereas our study showed a negative correlation between the two. The variation in the results may be due to the difference in the parameters used to make the alveolar bone measurements using CBCT.[1]

The study can be done for different anchorages used for space closure and comparing the changes observed between different amounts of retraction. The study can also be done to assess other potential factors that may influence the changes in the alveolar bone and root length, namely duration of treatment, method of retraction, and the amount of force used for retraction. Similarly, the measurements were taken immediately postretraction, whereas analysis of the measurements posttreatment and during follow-up visits will give us a better understanding about the long-term changes that may occur.

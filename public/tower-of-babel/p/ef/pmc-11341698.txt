Study the local metabolic changes of aneurysms through microcatheter sampling
Authors: Zejin Li, Tingbao Zhang, Yu Feng, Yihui Ma, Hao Chen, Xiaolin Wu, Jiayi Chen, Xuan Dai, Jincao Chen, Xiang Li, Wei Wei, Wenyuan Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Intracranial aneurysm is the primary cause of nontraumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage. To assess aneurysm metabolism, we present a method of intra-operatively collecting blood samples from the aneurysm neck, as well as the proximal and distal responsible vessels, using microcatheters. Through these paired comparisons, we can eliminate the interpatient variation usually observed in plasma samples taken from the peripheral vein. We utilized 39 plasma samples from 13 intracranial patients to characterize the metabolite profiles using untargeted liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry. Our findings revealed that l-tyrosine is upregulated at relatively high levels at the aneurysm neck than the proximal and distal aneurysm, whereas phenylpyruvic acid, l-cystine, and l-ornithine are downregulated. Based on this, there was also a significant decrease in arginine within small aneurysm of the internal carotid artery. The 6-month follow-up indicated that patients who experienced good recovery had lower levels of biliverdin, bilirubin, and metabolites of coenzyme Q within the aneurysm. In conclusion, our investigation provides a comprehensive overview of plasma metabolites in patients with intracranial aneurysms, shedding light on potential pathogenetic mechanisms in unruptured intracranial aneurysms. Moreover, the study proposes innovative ideas for establishing postoperative follow-up timelines for flow diverter devices.

Introduction

Intracranial aneurysms are local vascular wall abnormalities caused by congenital factors or hemodynamic changes in intracranial arteries1,2. When an aneurysm ruptures and bleeds, the main cause of subarachnoid hemorrhage, only about a third of people who receive timely treatment recover better3. Current methods of diagnosing aneurysms rely heavily on imaging techniques such as computed tomography angiography (CTA) and digital subtraction angiography (DSA). Although DSA is the gold standard for the diagnosis of aneurysms, due to its high cost and invasive nature, it cannot be used as a screening test for aneurysms. CTA is rapid and non-invasive, with diagnostic value for large and giant aneurysms, but there are doubts about the sensitivity and specificity of small aneurysms (< 5 mm) diagnosis. However, large and giant aneurysms only account for a small portion of intracranial aneurysms, as approximately 80% of unruptured intracranial aneurysms are small and medium. In addition, most ruptured intracranial aneurysms are less than 10 mm. Previous studies have looked for specific markers for the auxiliary diagnosis of aneurysms from various aspects, but no researches have been conducted on small aneurysms. Therefore, it is of great value to search for specific markers to aid in the diagnosis of aneurysms, especially small aneurysms.

Tubridge is a novel flow diverter medical device that is designed to treat intricate aneurysms, which are challenging to manage using clipping or conventional endovascular therapy4. The device is based on prior studies of the hemodynamics of intracranial aneurysms. Tubridge has a scaffold-like and container-remodeling structure with high metal coverage and low porosity. The main objective is to divert blood circulation from the aneurysm while maintaining regular flow to the branching artery5. The successful application of Tubridge therapy in managing small and medium-sized intracranial aneurysms has been proven4,6,7. However, there is currently a lack of studies predicting its efficacy.

There have been numerous previous studies on aneurysm metabolomics, however, all of them have gathered samples from peripheral veins or cerebrospinal fluid8–11. Due to the systemic circulation and the blood–brain barrier, these studies are not indicative of the genuine localized changes that take place around the aneurysm. Because of the large size of the microcatheter used to deliver the flow diverter device, microcatheters were used to collect blood samples from the aneurysm neck (in aneurysm, IA), the proximal (before aneurysm, BA) and distal (after aneurysm, AA) responsible vessel of the aneurysm during surgery. Plasma was isolated right after blood sample collection and were analyzed by a combination of ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS), or high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) technology. The changes of local metabolic microenvironment and the metabolic spectrum within the aneurysm were discussed in order to find the direct causes of aneurysm.

For the present report, we followed up with DSA results of aneurysm patients for six months after surgery, using O’Kelly-Marotta (OKM) grading scale to divide them into two groups based on their outcome. The OKM grading scale is a proposed method of assessing the degree of angiographic filling and contrast stasis in the setting of intracranial aneurysms treated by endovascular flow diversion12. Due to the dynamic nature of contrast stasis, it is designed to be used with cerebral angiography for the prediction of aneurysm closure over time. In cases where the aneurysm had no filling, it was rated D and classified as recovering well group (WG). The rest fell into the poorer group (PG). To explore specific metabolites for predicting postoperative outcome, Receiver Operating Characteristic curves (ROC) were plotted for specific metabolites to determine the value for clinical use.

Materials and methods

Sample population

In the current study, patients with aneurysms at Zhongnan Hospital of Wuhan University from April 2022 to April 2023 were prospectively enrolled for this study. We identified 147 patients with unruptured intracranial aneurysm, of whom 55 patients with intracranial aneurysm underwent endovascular treatment. Patients with a history of cardiovascular and other cerebrovascular diseases (arteriovenous malformation, subarachnoid hemorrhage), or those who participated in other studies and had incomplete information, were excluded, and 13 patients with intracranial aneurysm treated with the Tubridge flow diverter were finally included (Fig. 1). Relevant characteristics of all patients recruited in this study are summarized in Table 1. All participants were diagnosed and grouped according to DSA imaging and clinical conditions. Every technique was carried out in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1975, as amended in 2008, and the ethical guidelines set forth by the national and institutional committee responsible for human experimentation. The Ethics Committee of the Zhongnan Hospital of Wuhan University in China gave its approval to this study (Grant Number 2023280K). Every participant provided their informed agreement before being enrolled. Informed consent was obtained from all participants and their legal guardians. Blood sampling

During endovascular treatment all patients received 3 ml of IA, BA and AA (Fig. 2) through microcatheter extraction using vacutainers filled with lithium heparin as the anticoagulant (BD Bioscience). After centrifugation for 10 min at 1000g, the vacutainer was removed and the plasma supernatant was gathered and preserved in portions at − 80 °C until needed.Figure 2Sampling theoretical model: The sampling positions of the BA and AA are 2 cm away from the aneurysm.

Sampling theoretical model: The sampling positions of the BA and AA are 2 cm away from the aneurysm.

Extraction of metabolites and UHPLC-MS/MS analysis

Metabolites was extracted from 100 μL plasma samples, and a Vanquish UHPLC system (ThermoFisher, Germany) in conjunction with an Orbitrap Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Germany) were employed to conduct. The detail procedures were as mentioned before14,15.

Data processing and data analysis

The raw data files from UHPLC-MS/MS were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.1 (CD3.1) software to perform peak alignment, peak picking, and quantification. Parameters such as retention time tolerance, mass tolerance, signal intensity tolerance, and signal/noise ratio were set according to the instruction. The resulting peak intensities were adjusted, and normalized data were used to determine molecular formulas. Qualitative and relative quantitative results were obtained by matching peaks with databases. Statistical analysis was conducted using R, Python, and CentOS, and data standardization was performed. Metabolites with high variability in QC samples were eliminated, resulting in the identification and relative quantification of the metabolites.

The KEGG, HMDB, and LIPID Maps databases were used to annotate the metabolites. Data analysis was conducted using metaX software, employing techniques such as PLS-DA, univariate analysis with T-tests, volcano plots, heat maps, and correlation analysis16. The roles of the metabolites and metabolic pathways were investigated using the KEGG database, with statistically significant enrichment determined (P < 0.05).

Results

Plasma metabolic profiling of all patients

To search for changes in the metabolic profile after entering aneurysm, we noticed that IA was significantly more distinct from BA and AA. The PLS-DA model indicated that the metabolic profile of the plasma is significantly altered after flowing through the aneurysm neck (Fig. 3A,B). Additionally, we secured the VIP for the initial principal component in PLS-DA. The metabolites with VIP > 1 and P < 0.05 were deemed to have undergone significant alterations. The volcano plot clearly shows that there were 22 differential metabolites between IA and BA, while 37 between IA and AA (Fig. 3C,D). However, only 3 differential metabolites existed between BA and AA compared. The metabolites with similar characteristics were grouped together through hierarchical clustering analysis (Fig. 3E,F). At the aneurysm neck, l-Tyrosine, cortisol and hemolytic phosphatidylcholine products increased significantly, while amino acids such as phenylpyruvic acid, l-Cystine and l-Ornithine decreased considerably. KEGG pathway enrichment suggested significant enrichment in ABC transporters, Thiamine metabolism and phenylalanine metabolism (Fig. 3G). Metabolism of l-Tyrosine, cortisol and lipid were significantly reduced at the distal of the aneurysm, while that of amino acids such as dl-Arginine, phenylpyruvic acid, l-Cystine, l-Histidine and l-Glutamine were increased markedly. Plasma metabolic profiling of small internal carotid artery aneurysm

Furthermore, special attention was paid to the small internal carotid artery aneurysm. Aneurysms with a maximum diameter of less than 5 mm are classified as small aneurysms. A total of eight patients exhibited small internal carotid artery aneurysms in this study, with an equal distribution between males and females. (Table 1) We were pleasantly surprised to find that the metabolites within the plasma changed dramatically as they flowed through the small aneurysm in the internal carotid artery. Using the PLS-DA model, we found that IA was significantly different from BA and AA (Fig. 4A,B), with a good fit and the model was not overfitted. Metabolic profiling models using PLS-DA revealed notable differences in plasma metabolites across different locations of plasma, implying unique metabolic patterns at the molecular scale. The top20 VIP metabolites included amino acids such as phenylpyruvic acid, l-Cystine and dl-Arginine. By univariate analysis, 38 metabolites were significantly altered between the IA and BA (Fig. 4C), while 18 metabolites were changed markedly between the IA and AA, as demonstrated in the volcano plot (Fig. 4D). Each of these markedly changed metabolites grouped effectively within the heatmap (Fig. 4E,F). Comparison of plasma metabolic profiling between WG and PG

To further investigate whether there are specific metabolites that can predict postoperative efficacy, six months post-surgery DSA data was gathered from 10 patients. Based on subsequent cerebral angiography and OKM grading scale, the patients were categorized into the well group and the poorer group (Table 2) (Supplementary Information 1). Of the ten patients, five who had no aneurysm filling were rated D (WG), four who had aneurysm subtotal filling and persistent contrast at venous phase (significant stasis) were rated B3 (PG) and one who had only aneurysm entry remnant and significant stasis was rated C3 (PG). Employing the PLS-DA model, the categorization outcomes revealed a notable distinction in all plasma metabolites between WG and PG (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, a statistical analysis was performed on the metabolic fold changes between the groups. The results showed that 28 metabolites had a significant P-value of less than 0.05 (FDR < 0.1) and a VIP score greater than 1. Among these metabolites, 3 decreased while 25 increased in the WG group, as shown in the volcano plot (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, the heatmap distinctly separated through clustering analysis of the 28 metabolites that underwent significant changes (Fig. 5C). In contrast, the most significant fold change in metabolites indicated that the hemoglobin metabolites and coenzyme Q were downregulated. Analysis of KEGG pathway enrichment in various metabolites revealed that the metabolism of porphyrin and chlorophyll posed the greatest risk factor (Fig. 5D). To explore potential biomarkers that could predict postoperative recovery outcome, we plotted Receiver Operating Characteristic curve for the metabolites with significant differences. Combined with the results of KEGG enrichment analysis, we selected three specific metabolites containing biliverdin, bilirubin and coenzyme Q2, as shown in the boxplot (Fig. 6A). Discussion

Aneurysms have been the focus of research due to their high mortality and disability rates after rupture. Until now, the cause of aneurysms has not been clear, although it has been studied in depth. In our study, we hypothesized that the local metabolic microenvironment at the site of the aneurysm was altered, which caused the formation of aneurysms. In order to test our conjecture, microcatheters were used to collect plasma from the IA, BA and AA during surgery. When compared with each other, few metabolites were found to be altered in the BA compared to the AA. When the two groups were compared with IA, we were surprised to find that many metabolites were dramatically changed. This confirms our hypothesis that the local metabolic microenvironment at the aneurysm neck is altered and that the altered metabolites are of utmost importance in the progression of the aneurysm.

In this work, we conducted an untargeted metabolomics study to investigate local metabolic alterations in aneurysms. Previous studies such as Sun et al. collecting peripheral plasma for metabolomics analysis, and Ho et al. collecting cerebrospinal fluid, failed to truly reflect the local metabolic microenvironment of aneurysms10,11. In the latest publication by Xiong et al. in 202017, targeted metabolomics was used to analyze blood collected directly from supply arteries and drainage veins in 13 patients with gliomas during surgery. But when surgery was performed, the body was under stress, which drastically changed the metabolite composition of the plasma. So, we used microcatheter technology to collect samples that can both reflect the local metabolic microenvironment and minimize the interference of the stress response as possible compared with craniotomy. The safety of this sampling method has been corroborated by prior research18,19. Tutino et al. had used this method to determine gene expression but no study has yet employed this method for the identification of metabolites.

Analyzing the whole data, we found that tyrosine, cortisol and lipid metabolites increased in aneurysm neck, while phenylpyruvic acid, cystine, and ornithine decreased significantly. Previous studies had linked aneurysms to tyrosine-related modifiers such as tyrosine kinases, sulfotransferase and hydroxylases. These tyrosine-related enzymes have a significant impact on the functioning and behavior of different types of cells, including their survival, growth, aggregation, migration, and inflammatory response. To date, a high importance of epigenetic factors—microRNA—in the development of cerebral aneurysms has been revealed. A study of the circular RNA expression profile of intracranial aneurysms indicates that _circ_0135895 regulates aneurysm formation by modulating tyrosine kinase 220. Yan et al. using weighted gene co-expression network analysis found elevated tyrosine protein sulfotransferase enzymes in patients with ruptured aneurysms21. Laia et al. found that tyrosine hydroxylase promotes aneurysm development through Ang II. Although no studies have directly shown a relationship between tyrosine and aneurysms, these studies have demonstrated the significant impact of tyrosine on the formation of aneurysms. Phenylpyruvate acid is a product of phenylalanine metabolism. Under normal circumstances, phenylalanine is catalyzed by enzymes into phenylpyruvate acid. Studies have shown a decrease in phenylpyruvic acid in smokers with high blood pressure, which induces related vascular diseases by causing vascular endothelial damage22. Our study is aligned with this, but we have taken an additional step and investigated the local alterations in the aneurysm. The findings underscore the potential role of phenylpyruvate and tyrosine in the formation of aneurysms, however, the exact underlying mechanism remains unclear.

Significant changes in the metabolism of cystine within aneurysms were found in our study. Cysteine is a precursor of glutathione, and changes in its metabolism directly affect glutathione. The disruption of glutathione leads to the production of reactive oxygen species, which greatly contribute to the development of aneurysms by increasing the activation of vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2. (VEGFR2)23,24. NO, a mediator in pathophysiology of many vascular diseases, which participates in angiogenesis through maintaining vascular integrity, vasodilatation and platelet aggregation inhibition, may be an inflammatory agent affecting vascular wall remodeling. Studies conducted in a controlled laboratory setting have indicated that NO could potentially act as a secondary messenger, influencing the activity of VEGF11,25. The biosynthesis process primarily takes place via the arginine pathway26. The study by Maderna et al. pointed out that aneurysm formation is associated with VEGFR and nitrates23. Therefore, combining previous studies with our current data, arginine and cysteine may modulate VEGFR in a variety of ways to influence aneurysm initiation and progression.

Another goal of our study was to find specific metabolites to predict recovery after surgery in patients with Tubridge flow diverter device. Tubridge flow diverter device induces thrombosis in aneurysms while preserving the physiological flow of the parent artery. Since thrombosis is induced to occlude the aneurysm, here is no direct way to determine the outcome of the operation, only by follow-up DSA. Determining whether and when a review is needed is tricky because different patients recover differently. Scholars around the world are still inconclusive about the length of follow-up after flow diverter treatment27,28. When patients follow up, the aneurysm is not yet occluded, which means another follow-up is needed. In this study, Biliverdin, bilirubin and metabolites of coenzyme Q were significantly reduced in group WG. The metabolites of hemoglobin, biliverdin and bilirubin, have been pinpointed as primary triggers for cerebral vasospasm, as evidenced by studies conducted both in vitro and in vivo. Heme oxygenase-1 facilitates the conversion of free heme into equal molar quantities of ferrous iron, carbon monoxide (CO), and biliverdin29,30. The genesis of arterial thrombi is believed to stem from the attachment of platelets to the subendothelial matrix. CO might function in a manner akin to NO, stimulating soluble guanylate cyclase and increasing cGMP synthesis. This substance prevents the clumping of platelets, diminishes the attachment of leucocytes, lessens cell death, and reduces the generation of certain pro-inflammatory cytokines31. Therefore, due to the antiplatelet aggregation effect of CO, we think thrombus formation is faster in the WG group which may has lower CO. Incorporating coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), a fat-soluble compound present in various foods, can provide an extra layer of cardiovascular protection. Evidence shows that CoQ10 diminishes human platelet aggregation, clot retraction and granule secretion32. The WG group had lower levels of coenzyme Q, which accelerated platelet aggregation and thrombus formation. Our approach offers a new method for predicting follow-up time after flow diverter surgery. In our center, patients who receive treatment usually receive follow-up DSA 6 months after flow diverter treatment. Blood can be extracted from aneurysm neck using microcatheters during interventional therapy. If the aneurysm neck contains high levels of metabolites such as bilirubin or coenzyme Q, this may mean the aneurysm is slowly occluded and follow-up time needs to be appropriately postponed to avoid multiple invasive examinations. Considering the dual antiplatelet regimen, we suggest delaying for three months.

We also found something interesting in that three of the patients who were followed up developed stenosis at the distal end of the saccular aneurysm. We divided the three participants into a group and compared them to six other participants with saccular aneurysms to examine discrepancies in metabolites in the blood after aneurysm. Significant disparities in metabolites were observed between the two cohorts, with a notable decrease in Eicosapentaenoic acid ethyl ester levels in patients who manifested postoperative stenosis (Supplementary Information 3). Eicosapentaenoic acid ethyl ester is de-esterified to Eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) following oral administration. Reports suggest that EPA promotes endothelial activity, oxidative stress, the creation of foam cells, inflammation, the development or advancement of plaques, aggregation of platelets, thrombus development, and the rupture of plaques33,34. It is hypothesized that postoperative stenosis in these patients could be associated with EPA deficiency. Incorporating eicosapentaenoic acid ethyl ester in double antiplatelet therapy could potentially prevent stenotic events in these patients.

However, there are some limitations in our study. Our sample size is too small, resulting in the discovery of relatively few differential metabolites. In the present study, sample was taken from the neck of the aneurysm and replaced the aneurysm complex. The metabolic microenvironment may different in different regions within aneurysm35.

Conclusions

We demonstrated that patients with aneurysms exhibit an altered local metabolic microenvironment using untargeted metabolomics. This method can better capture metabolic changes at the lesion. We have established a connection between aneurysm development and amino acids. Moreover, the recovery following flow diverter surgery in aneurysm patients is linked to hemoglobin metabolites. These metabolites can serve as a tool for predicting the timing of postoperative assessment.

An auto-validation method for a complete IoT pivot irrigation model based on the Penman–Monteith equation
Authors: Abdelrahman Osman Elfaki, Saleh Ali Albelwi, Abderrahim Lakhouit, Osama Moh’d Alia, Mohamed Elsawy, Anas Bushnag, Raghad Mahmoud Alqobali, Mohammed Alotaibi, Ashraf Marei, Tareq Alhmiedat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pivot irrigation has been introduced as an engineering solution to cultivate large areas of farmland without increasing water usage. Although research studies have proposed different types of pivot irrigation systems, several challenges must be addressed to create a successful system. These challenges include determining crop water requirements, IoT components and architecture, and compatibility, as well as data interpretation, environmental factors, scalability, and result validation. This paper provides an auto-validation method for a complete IoT pivot irrigation model based on the Penman–Monteith equation. Auto-validation is crucial to protecting the pivot system from sensor errors caused by telecom network interference or natural factors. Furthermore, the proposed model effectively addresses all the aforementioned challenges, making it the first comprehensive IoT solution. This paper also develops a benchmark framework for evaluating pivot irrigation systems, providing a standardized basis for performance assessment in future research. As an initial experimental study, the evaluation was conducted using a single crop type (grass) at one field site over a period of 49 days, representing a proof-of-concept validation. Real-world experiments were performed to assess the accuracy and applicability of the proposed model. The results demonstrate significant improvements over traditional irrigation methods, highlighting the system’s effectiveness in optimizing water usage and enhancing agricultural productivity.

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) is often presented as the ideal solution to various problems1,2. Though the truth is more complicated than that, the development and implementation of a complete IoT model could enhance pivot irrigation systems; this would represent a significant advancement in modern agricultural engineering. By integrating the Penman–Monteith equation, this study proposes a new model that addresses critical challenges faced by current irrigation systems, such as accurately determining crop water requirements, optimizing resource use, ensuring environmental sustainability, and automatically validating the outputs from the Penman–Monteith equation to ensure result accuracy.

One of the most significant challenges in agriculture is the scarcity of water. As such, it is crucial to prioritize effective water management during irrigation. Ensuring that plants receive adequate water when needed is essential for maximizing plant growth and meeting a country’s food production goals. This is especially important in conjunction with climate change and environmental concerns, which impact productivity. It is essential to enhance food grain production and yield per hectare to address these challenges3. IoT has been applied across disciplines and domains4–6, highlighting the benefits of technologies such as remote sensors for soil moisture detection and automated irrigation systems. Therefore, leveraging IoT can offer effective solutions for addressing pivot irrigation challenges.

The Penman–Monteith equation is a widely used mathematical model in the fields of hydrology and agriculture for estimating evapotranspiration rates, which play a crucial role in water resource management, agricultural practices, and climate studies7. Despite wide research on the Penman–Monteith equation8,9, no study prior to this one has provided automatic validation of the outputs from the Penman–Monteith equation and IoT-based pivot irrigation systems. This limitation could be attributed to the following:There are a considerable number of parameters that need to be considered to accurately use the Penman–Monteith equation. The aforementioned points are challenging in any pivot irrigation system; however, in using the Penman–Monteith equation, this challenge is amplified due to the considerable number of sensors. In this paper, an auto-validation method for a complete IoT pivot irrigation model based on the Penman–Monteith equation is introduced. This model successfully addresses the four challenges mentioned above.

Moreover, the proposed model is designed to address the primary challenges of pivot irrigation systems, including determining crop water requirements, accounting for environmental factors, providing compatible solutions, offering data interpretation, and ensuring scalability. Moreover, this paper uses these parameters as a benchmark for evaluating the success of any proposed pivot irrigation system, which itself is another key contribution.

Related works

Research10 has emphasized that developing countries consume more water than developed countries to achieve equivalent yields due to the absence of cost-effective intelligent irrigation systems. The proposed system employs machine learning algorithms to forecast irrigation needs based on sensor data and weather forecasts. By analyzing information from ground sensors measuring soil moisture, soil temperature, air temperature, UV radiation, and humidity, along with weather forecast data, this system can enhance water-resource management in precision agriculture. AI algorithms aid in processing and analyzing the gathered data to offer real-time insights, facilitating efficient irrigation control.

Likewise, another study11 proposed an integration of microcontrollers and soil moisture sensors so that the water content in the soil can be detected and analyzed. This information was then processed by a Raspberry Pi system, which can control the irrigation process and send SMS alerts to farmers, providing them with timely information about irrigation needs and the status of different plots. The system included soil, humidity, and temperature sensors, which were deployed in the field to monitor the soil moisture, temperature, and humidity levels. These sensors provided real-time data, allowing for more accurate and efficient irrigation control. By analyzing the sensor data, farmers can determine the optimal irrigation schedule and avoid water wastage.

One study12 developed pivot irrigation by using an IoT system that allowed farmers to irrigate land in a circular pattern. It ensured that water was distributed evenly, delivering equal amounts of water across the entire farmland. Another work13 created an IoT solution to oversee the water levels within plants, sending this data wirelessly to a central unit for examination and decision-making purposes. This system was specifically engineered to gauge stem water potential (SWP) within plants. Other work14 utilized soil moisture sensors and a DHT-11 temperature sensor to collect data on the soil’s moisture level and temperature. This data was subsequently utilized by the system to determine when to activate or deactivate the water motor, thereby reducing water wastage and ensuring efficient irrigation. Further work15 addressed the challenge of accurately predicting irrigation requirements and crop yields in a timely manner. This system leveraged various sensors, including remote, proximity, temperature/humidity, and soil moisture sensors, to gather extensive agricultural data. These sensors offered insights into climate variations, weather predictions, nutrient deficiencies in plants, health monitoring, and other pertinent factors. Subsequently, the collected data underwent processing and analysis through AI techniques to generate accurate and dependable forecasts for irrigation needs.

Another study16 proposed a wirelessly controlled robot-based smart irrigation system that remotely managed the pivot irrigation system via an application to enhance irrigation efficiency. The system incorporated sensors that allowed for data collection on soil moisture, pH, temperature, and water levels. This data was subsequently utilized to make informed irrigation decisions. Likewise, other research3 designed a sensor-driven system to enhance irrigation practices. These sensors were capable of gauging a range of factors like soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and rainfall. By continuously collecting data, these sensors offered crucial insights for artificial intelligence algorithms to optimize irrigation strategies effectively. The data from these sensors played a vital role in assessing crop water requirements and facilitating precise irrigation scheduling, while also preventing both over- and under-watering.

To address the challenge of water scarcity in arid and semi-arid regions, a major obstacle to sustainable farming, one study17 introduced an IoT smart pivot irrigation system. This system featured an autonomous sensor network that gathered data on climate factors and soil moisture levels, offering up-to-date details on the irrigation needs of date palm trees. Leveraging sensor-derived insights, precise irrigation schedules were created, guaranteeing that date palms received the ideal water quantity directly at their roots.

Other researchers18 designed an irrigation system using control algorithms executed on an Arduino microcontroller. This system autonomously initiated and ceased water pumps according to soil moisture levels and water level readings. Moreover, it optimized irrigation activities by establishing the quantity and placement of pumps to be activated. The setup incorporated moisture sensors for gauging soil moisture and ultrasonic sensors for monitoring reservoir water levels. These sensor readings were transmitted to the microcontroller, which processed the data to configure the control algorithm and make well-informed choices regarding irrigation procedures. Likewise, other work19 introduced an irrigation system that incorporated sensors to gather data concerning soil moisture, temperature, and other pertinent parameters. This real-time data was transmitted to a cloud server via Narrowband Internet of Things (NB-IoT) technology. The sensor data allowed accurate field monitoring and supported informed irrigation decision making.

Other authors20 developed an IoT system that utilized innovative technologies to enhance pivot irrigation system monitoring in regions like Spain and Italy. The system integrated cloud functions, a Firestore real-time database, a Progressive Web App for user interaction, and voice assistants for data queries. An ESP32-CAM-based edge computing device monitored water consumption at hydrants, reducing users’ reliance on cloud connectivity by processing water meter images locally. Field tests conducted in maize fields demonstrated reliable monitoring with minimal data loss and accurate GPS sensor performance. Similarly, another study21 presented an IoT-based smart irrigation system designed to enhance agricultural efficiency through automated water management using center pivot irrigation technology. The system utilized the MQTT protocol for real-time data communication between sensors, a mobile application, and a web application, enabling farmers to monitor and control irrigation processes remotely. Key features included automated irrigation based on soil moisture levels and weather forecasts, as well as the ability to manually override the system when necessary.

Other work22 included the development of a smart irrigation system based on IoT and cloud architecture that measured soil moisture and humidity, utilizing machine learning for data processing. The system aimed to provide farmers with precise information for water management, increasing agricultural efficiency. The document highlighted the use of wireless sensor networks (WSNs) in agriculture, particularly in optimizing sensor node deployment for efficient data collection. Other researchers23 proposed an Edge-IoT-Cloud platform utilizing deep learning to monitor and predict crop water demands, collecting data on soil moisture, air temperature, humidity, water levels for irrigation solutions. The system employed sensors like thermal imaging for irrigation scheduling, weather and environmental sensors for real-time data, and underground sensors for monitoring soil conditions.

One study24 explored the need for innovative technologies to enhance irrigation practices for small-scale farmers in Pakistan due to decreasing water availability and increasing agricultural demands. This work designed a modified Center-Pivot Irrigation System (CPIS) for small-scale farmers, utilizing IrriExpress and SAP2000 software for hydraulic and structural design, respectively. The research highlighted the economic analysis of the CPIS modifications, showcasing improvements in efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Another team of researchers25 proposed an IoT system for monitoring irrigation, temperature, and water data in cotton fields. Their study emphasized the use of sensors for collecting data to improve agricultural intelligence. Yet another group26 developed an IoT system that enabled real-time monitoring and control of data related to crops, livestock, and agricultural assets. Their IoT platform utilized various sensors to monitor factors such as weather conditions, soil properties, crop growth, and livestock parameters.

A different study27 developed an IoT system for monitoring crucial environmental factors such as moisture, humidity, temperature, and water levels in real-time for smart agriculture practices. Sensors like the DHT22 module, water level sensor, and moisture sensors were connected to an ESP32-embedded system. The system leveraged the ThingSpeak cloud and ThingView app for wireless communication, enabling remote monitoring and control for farmers. This study demonstrated a significant 70% reduction in water consumption for soil irrigation compared to traditional methods. Finally, other work28 utilized an IoT-based sensor station, a user-friendly website, and a smartphone app for irrigation management, along with calibration experiments for soil moisture sensors. This research included case studies from corn, blueberry, and tomato fields in Michigan, USA, highlighting the benefits of IoT-based sensor technology in improving irrigation practices and crop yields. Crop water requirements

To determine crop water requirements, this paper used the Penman–Monteith equation, a widely accepted model for estimating evapotranspiration. It integrates multiple climatic variables, including temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and solar radiation, to provide accurate assessments of the water needs for various crops. It was employed to address two key parameters: crop water requirements, which ensure optimal growth and yield, and environmental factors, which influence water availability and usage efficiency. By incorporating these variables, the Penman–Monteith equation serves as a reliable tool for irrigation planning and water resource management. In agriculture, a crop coefficient is a numerical value that accounts for the unique water needs of various crops, taking into account their distinct growth patterns and characteristics31. According to one study32, the crop coefficient (Kc) typically varies within a range of 0.5 to 1.

The Penman–Monteith equation accounts for a range of variables such as sunlight exposure, temperature, moisture levels, wind velocity, and air pressure in computing the volume of water lost due to evapotranspiration from both the earth and plant life. It evaluates the thermal equilibrium on the ground surface and any impediments to water vapor transfer in the atmosphere, encompassing aerodynamic and radiative resistances. Essentially, this equation acts as a pivotal instrument in quantifying evapotranspiration processes, shedding light on water dynamics, agricultural water supervision, and environmental endurance. Its robust methodology and holistic strategy establish is a cornerstone in hydrological studies and a crucial tool for tackling water-related dilemmas amidst shifting climates.

A primary application of the Penman–Monteith equation lies in agriculture, where it can determine crop water necessities and schedule irrigation. Farmers and agricultural experts leverage this model to enhance water management, curtail soil salinity, and boost crop output by ensuring plants receive adequate water without wastage.

Experimental study

The developed system was employed in a real environment at the University of Tabuk, in a field used to grow grass. It utilized a cost-effective environmental monitoring unit by integrating a selection of budget-friendly sensors characterized by low power consumption.

Employment of sensing units

The developed system consists of two sensing units, as discussed earlier. For both sensing units, the researchers developed a robust and reliable design utilizing Fusion 360. This software enabled the creation of detailed 3D models and simulations, ensuring that each component met the necessary specifications for performance and durability. The design process involved rigorous testing and iteration to optimize functionality and reliability in various operational conditions. The decision to implement two separate stations rather than a single unit was primarily driven by the differing requirements for sensor data acquisition at specific heights and depths. For instance, air temperature measurements are conventionally obtained at approximately 2 m above ground level, whereas soil heat flux measurements must be collected from the soil surface. Likewise, wind speed monitoring is optimally performed at heights above 2 m. Therefore, to address these heterogeneous sensing requirements, two dedicated perception units were developed. Both units are equipped with appropriate sensors and are interconnected via a Wi-Fi communication protocol to enable seamless data exchange and integration.

Figure 5 presents the ground-station unit that was employed in the real field. The ground-station unit is responsible for gathering different environment values, including: soil temperature at two different ground levels, humidity, temperature, and air pressure. The collected data are processed and then transmitted to the base station for additional processing.Fig. 5The employed ground-station unit in the experiment testbed.

The employed ground-station unit in the experiment testbed.

The second unit, which is the turbine unit illustrated in Fig. 6, is equipped with both a wind-speed sensor and a temperature sensor. Using the data from Table 5, which covers 13 representative days within a 49-day monitoring period, we evaluated the performance of the IoT-based pivot irrigation system in predicting daily water requirements. Three common performance indicators, MAE, RMSE, and R2, were used to compare estimated Et0 values with irrigation volumes recorded in the field. The MAE was approximately 75 mm per day, the RMSE around 78 mm per day, and the R2 reached 0.67, indicating that the system captures the general trend in water demand while showing notable deviations from field-applied quantities. The observed R2 value of 0.67 reflects a moderate agreement between the estimated Et0 and the actual irrigation water applied in the field. This difference is expected in practical agricultural settings, where irrigation decisions are not based solely on atmospheric demand but also account for field conditions and management strategies. For example, Et0 represents the theoretical water demand under reference conditions, while actual irrigation often includes safety margins to prevent water stress, which can lead to higher applied volumes. Soil characteristics such as water-holding capacity, infiltration rate, and retention behavior also influence how water is stored and made available to plants, and these factors are not fully captured by Et0 estimation alone. Furthermore, variability in crop response and local microclimatic conditions may affect water consumption. Unmeasured field losses, including evaporation, deep percolation, and non-uniform distribution inherent to irrigation systems, contribute to the observed differences. Sensor measurement uncertainty and temporal fluctuations in environmental parameters also introduce variability into the Et0 calculations. These considerations indicate that the proposed model provides a reliable baseline for estimating irrigation demand rather than a direct representation of applied water volumes, and they help define the practical operating conditions of the system.

In most cases, predicted values were lower than the volumes applied in practice. This difference is expected, as Et0 represents atmospheric demand under reference conditions, whereas field irrigation reflects operational choices, safety margins, or unmeasured losses. The discrepancies observed do not necessarily indicate model errors but may reflect irrigation practices that exceed strictly required crop water needs.

The relatively high MAE and RMSE highlight the limitations of relying solely on Et0 for real-time irrigation scheduling. Factors such as crop characteristics, soil water-holding capacity, microclimatic variations, and equipment performance influence the actual water delivered in the field. Variations in soil texture, daily temperature fluctuations, wind speed, and sensor precision also contribute to the differences between predicted and applied irrigation.

Uncertainty analysis showed a standard deviation of residuals of about 34.2 mm per day, reflecting normal day-to-day variability in field conditions. A 95 percent confidence interval provided a more robust estimate of expected irrigation requirements. Sensor-related uncertainties, including small inaccuracies in temperature, humidity, solar radiation, or wind measurements, propagated into Et0 estimation.

The system provides a consistent baseline for estimating daily water demand. Integrating Et0 with crop-specific coefficients, local soil and climate information, and improved sensor performance could further enhance accuracy, support efficient irrigation management, and promote water conservation.

Conclusion

This paper proposed an auto-validation method for a complete IoT pivot irrigation model based on the Penman–Monteith equation. By integrating the Penman–Monteith equation, it accurately determines crop water requirements through real-time data collection and analysis. This improves resource optimization, reduces water waste, and supports environmental sustainability. Furthermore, the accuracy of these results is guaranteed by providing an auto-validation mechanism, which protects sensor reading data. The model’s scalability allows adaptation to various farm sizes and crop types, enhancing agricultural productivity across diverse regions.

This system also utilizes interconnected sensors for remote monitoring and control, reducing labor costs and improving accuracy. It offers both economic and environmental benefits, such as water conservation, cost savings, and improved crop yields, contributing to food security and sustainability.

This paper identifies six key factors essential for any successful IoT-based pivot irrigation system, some of which were previously noted35. These include accurately determining crop water requirements, specifying the IoT components employed, accounting for environmental conditions, testing system compatibility, ensuring proper data interpretation, and evaluating scalability.

Taken together, these six factors represent what this paper define as the completeness of an IoT pivot irrigation system. The proposed model not only ensures this completeness but also incorporates auto-validation of the results. To the best of the researchers’ knowledge, this is the first model to integrate both completeness and auto-validation simultaneously.

The originality of this study also stems from its pioneering scope, where it represents effective research, by considering auto-validation method for a complete IoT pivot irrigation model based on the Penman–Monteith equation. By deliberately focusing on a single traditional site, using grass as the reference crop, and limiting the monitoring period to 49 days, the study establishes a clear and reliable benchmark under well-defined conditions. This targeted validation not only safeguards the accuracy of the proposed auto-validation method but also provides a strong foundation for future extensions to diverse crops, climatic conditions, and longer seasonal cycles. Furthermore, the research aligns the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals on Zero Hunger, Clean Water and Sanitation, and Responsible Consumption and Production. The combination of methodological precision, regional novelty, and alignment with sustainability agendas underscores this study’s contribution as a cornerstone for advancing smart irrigation research in arid and semi-arid environments.

Overall, this IoT model offers a promising solution for sustainable and efficient water management in agriculture. However, challenges remain, including the need for reliable data collection, technical infrastructure, and affordability for small-scale farmers. In addition, the conducted study focuses on comparing and analyzing the actual irrigation water consumption with the estimated values derived from the Penman Monteith equation. Future research should aim to improve sensor accuracy, reduce overall system cost, and incorporate long-term controlled experiments to ensure greater reliability. Moreover, integrating advanced technologies such as machine learning and renewable energy solutions could further enhance system efficiency and provide deeper optimization insights.

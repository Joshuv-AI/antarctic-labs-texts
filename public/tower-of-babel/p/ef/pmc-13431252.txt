ABSTRACT
SummaryObjectives:To assess the effectiveness of structured-simulation learning with real-life scenarios on nurses’ knowledge and psychomotor skills in adult cardiopulmonary resuscitation.Methods:A quasi-experimental design was employed with 46 nurses from Al Nahdha Hospital. Participants were selected using stratified random sampling. The intervention consisted of a comprehensive simulation-based training program that incorporated real-life scenarios, hands-on practice, team dynamics, and structured debriefing. Data were collected at three time points: baseline (pre-test), immediately post-intervention, and six weeks post-intervention using a 25-item knowledge assessment tool and a 30-item CPR practical competency assessment tool.Results:The mean scores for baseline knowledge and psychomotor skills were 71.50 (SD = 12.14) and 62.29 (SD = 13.45), respectively. Significant improvements were observed post-intervention for both knowledge immediately (Mean Difference = 13.98, p < .001) and psychomotor skills (Mean Difference = 33.59, p < .001). At six weeks, knowledge scores remained significantly elevated compared to baseline (Mean Difference = 11.98, p < .001) with no significant decline from immediate post-test (p = .160). Psychomotor skills also remained significantly higher than baseline (Mean Difference = 29.30, p < .001), despite a small but significant decline from immediate post-test (Mean Difference = 4.29, p < .001).Conclusion:Structured simulation-based training with real-life scenarios significantly enhances nurses’ CPR knowledge and psychomotor skills with strong retention at six weeks post-intervention. Healthcare organizations should consider implementing comprehensive simulation-based training programs to improve CPR competency among nursing staff.

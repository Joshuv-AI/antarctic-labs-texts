Urine retention in elderly male due to hypo-contractile bladder treated successfully through Ayurveda – A Case Report
Authors: Sanjeev Rastogi, Shashwat Rastogi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Urine retention is a common problem in elderly people. In male it is often associated with prostatic hypertrophy and can also be associated with hypo contractile bladder. Such functional disorders of bladder do not respond well to conventional medicine and the only respite is given by catheterization either in situ or intermittent as per the requirement. Besides social stigma, catheterization has its own accompanying problems leading to its poor acceptance. Newer approaches like electrical stimulation, Sacral neuro-modulation and stem cell therapy for this clinical condition are still in the process of development. Ayurveda earlier has reported to have successfully treated such cases to the extent of complete recovery. We present here a case of 83 year old male who was not able to pass the urine due to bladder hypo contractility and was on self-retaining catheter for about 2 years, got clinically cured with the help of Ayurveda interventions taken for about a year. A follow up of the case was done for another two years to find him well without any medication. This case endorses the worth of Ayurveda interventions in such cases and warrants a serious enquiry about such interventions to generate more robust evidences.

Introduction

The incidence of hypo contractile or underactive bladder (UAB) causing urinary retention is increasing in the ageing population [1]. Therapeutic options for this disabling condition are however limited [2]. Underactive bladder is a prevalent but under-researched lower urinary tract symptom (LUTS) that occurs along with detrusor under activity (DU). Despite the high prevalence of UAB/DU, there are significant gaps in the understanding of its pathophysiological mechanisms, diagnosis, and treatment to the extent that it is largely considered an incurable condition. In recent years, however the understanding of UAB has increased. Clinical trials involving novel drugs, electrical stimulation, and stem cell therapy have shown promising results but still such treatments are in the process of development and are not commonly available [3].

Underactive bladder (UAB) clinical features are marked by voiding symptoms manifested as slow stream, intermittency, hesitancy, and feeling of incomplete emptying of the bladder [4]. These may be associated with storage related and voiding related symptoms. Detrusor under activity is usually identified by contraction of reduced strength and/or duration, resulting in prolonged bladder emptying and/or a failure to achieve complete bladder emptying within a normal time span. There are numerous causes of DU, including advanced age, neurological disease, low-level spinal cord injury, and bladder outlet obstruction (BOO). Up to 48 % of elderly men and 45 % of elderly women undergoing urodynamic evaluations are reported to have detrusor under-activity related bladder hypo contractility [2].

In light of limited therapeutic options to treat hypo contractile bladder and in view of associated high inconvenience and complications associated with catheterization [5], any alternative pharmacotherapy leading to clinical recovery seems to be important and demands a serious enquiry for its possibility of generalization after it getting thoroughly investigated. This case of an elderly male presenting with urine retention and upon catheter for 2 years, treated to the extent of clinical cure with the help of Ayurveda interventions is a lead in this regard.

De-identified patient specific information

An average built Hindu elderly male age about 83 years resident of Noida, New Delhi, Weight about 70 kg and height about 5 ft 6 inches.

Primary concerns and symptoms of the patient

Patient had recurrent episodes of urine retention since 2019. It was treated initially with spontaneous catheterization which was then shifted to indwelling silicon catheterization and was changed every 15 days.

Medical, family, and psychosocial history including relevant genetic information

Patient had an army background and was physically and mentally fit and strong. He had a history of hypertension and was on allopathic medicines for it since 2005. In 2013 he underwent Cholecystectomy. In August 2016 he underwent Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR) for his Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm. Since then he had been on blood thinning medicines. In August 2019, he complained of hematuria. Urinary bladder was found to contain blood clots. Cystoscopy and clot evaluation was done in the hospital. Timeline.

Relevant past interventions and their outcomes

On April 30, 2020 again the incident of Hematuria occurred. A catheter was placed and subsequently removed. Since June 16, 2020, patient started complaining of poor, painful and intermittent flow of urine. Folly's PUC was placed and removed later. On September 11, 2020, patient was not able to pass urine. Urologist diagnosed the problem as a case of severe Hypocontractile Detrusor. Voiding trials and detailed investigation confirmed the diagnosis. Patient was put on Tab Loxof 500 mg OD, Silofast 8 mg OD HS, Tab Urotone 25 mg for three months. Patient was able to recover and for about 2 months was able to pass urine naturally. On November 17, 2020 again the problem of urine retention recurred. Patient was put on a catheter again. Catheter was replaced every 2–3 weeks as the patient was not able to pass urine naturally. He faced a lot of distress physically and mentally. There was frequent pain and infection leading to fever etc.

Clinical findings

Patient had recurrent episodes of fever, pain in lower abdomen and difficulty in passing urine.

Diagnostic assessment

Patient was diagnosed through uroflometry as a case of severe hypo contractile bladder due to Detrusor under activity. Voiding trials have confirmed the diagnosis. The diagnosis was made at a tertiary care hospital in Delhi under the supervision of an Urologist.

Diagnostic challenges

In an elderly male the urinary retention may also be due to various other reasons. These have been ruled out by appropriate investigations and a diagnosis of hypo contractile bladder was established. The diagnosis was considered to have a no real cure and the patient was recommended to have regular catheterization.

Ayurvedic diagnosis

From Ayurveda perspective the patient was considered to have mutra saad due to derangement of apan vata which is responsible for evacuation of stool and urine. The treatment was focused upon palliation of apana vata in order to regularize it and to regularize urine flow [6].

Following the Ayurveda diagnosis of mutra saad the patient was recommended for following.

The above treatment started on February 1, 2022. Patient followed the whole Ayurveda intervention for about 6–7 months. He stopped the khanjanikari vati and gokshuradi guggulu after finding a complete relief to his urine related symptoms but continued with Varun Shigru Kwath for about 2 years. Since the start of ayurvedic medicine, the problem of urine retention has not resurfaced. Patient also continued with Tab Silofast 8 mg for about 2 years. Presently (Jan 2025) he is not taking any medicine for the urine related problems and is able to pass urine normally and naturally.

Discussion

Urine retention in elderly due to hypocontractile bladder is difficult to treat condition. The conventional treatment offered in such cases is not found to be very helpful in most cases. Diagnostic dilemma due to poor understanding about hypocontractile bladder in male elderly often misdirects the treating physician to look at prostatic hypertrophy as the possible cause of it [7]. More advanced therapies of hypocontractile bladder like sacral neuromodulation, InFlow Intraurethral Valve-Pump Medical Device, and stem cell therapy are still experimental and not in the common clinical use [8]. Once diagnosed urodynamically and not responding to the conventional therapy, the only option remains is to catheterize the patient either through clean intermittent catheterization (CIC) method or through retaining catheter. Regular catheterization has its own set of problems and besides becoming a permanent source of infection and possible trauma, it comes as a personal and social limitation affecting severely the quality of life of the person affected. In such state, it becomes miserable for patients to sustain the situation. So finding some remedies to improve the bladder functioning and normalizing the urinary flow related problems is imperative. Recently some case reports have been published related to successful Ayurveda interventions in diverse cases of urine retention [9,10]. This case comes as the further endorsement of the utility of Ayurveda interventions in cases of urinary retention. The strength of Ayurveda interventions in such cases is that it is devoid of any local or systemic adversity as is observed in the cases treated through Ayurveda. Moreover, it is found to offer a reasonably stable solution unrelated to continuation of therapy. The effects of interventions are found to be stable for long even once the active intervention is stopped.

From Ayurvedic perspectives, it might be easy to understand that once the apana vata is corrected which is responsible for bladder evacuation; the issue of urine retention may get solved. Medicines like varuna and shigru possibly take care of any urinary tracts inflammation which might have occurred due to repeated catheterization. Gokshuradi guggulu is an important ayurvedic medicine recommended in various urinary tract disorders [11]. Local hot and cold fomentation and cold water sprinkling possibly stimulates the bladder contractility due to similar dermatome innervation. How it actually stimulates the urinary bladder and how the effects remain stable even after the treatment is stopped is however yet to be understood and a lot is still required to be done to understand the mechanism of such interventions in hypo contractile bladder cases. More robust collaborative clinical trials involving Ayurveda physicians and urologist are also required to be done in this area to prove the real worth of such interventions and to make them available to a large segment of people suffering miserably due to their inability to pass the urine normally.

Conclusion

Hypo contractile bladder is still a difficult to treat condition and is often non responsive to conventional therapy. Catheterization offers a temporary solution at a very high cost of personal and social compromise. Newer treatments are still in infancy and far from the reach of real clinical application to common people. In such condition, Ayurveda interventions come as a hope of providing a safe and long term solution to treat hypo contractile bladder. The above case report is a preliminary observation in this direction which is needed to be further investigated for its possible inclusion in conventional treatment protocol of treating hypo contractile bladder.

Analysis of epidemiological characteristics of influenza viruses in Fengdu district, Chongqing, China, from 2022 to 2024
Authors: Ping Huang, Xiaoqin Jiang, Yuhong Liao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo analyze the epidemiological characteristics of influenza viruses in Fengdu, Chongqing, and provide a scientific basis for influenza prevention and control in the region.MethodsPharyngeal swab samples were collected from influenza-like illness cases at Fengdu Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital from 2022 to 2024 for etiological testing. Virus positivity rates across different years, genders, and occupations were compared, along with inflammatory indicators such as blood routine parameters between influenza A and B virus infections.ResultsA total of 19,576 nasopharyngeal swab samples were tested from 2022 to 2024, with 5073 positive for influenza viruses (detection rate: 25.91%). There were significant differences in positivity rates across different years (P < 0.001). The peak positivity rate for influenza A virus occurred in 2022 (27.15%), while influenza B virus had its highest detection rate in 2024 (5.67%). The epidemic peak for influenza A was from December to April of the following year, dominated by the H3N2 subtype; for influenza B, the peak was from December to March, with the Victoria lineage as the predominant strain. In terms of age distribution, the highest positivity rate for influenza A was observed in school-aged children aged 6 ~ 13 years (23.12%), while influenza B predominantly affected individuals aged 45 ~ 59 years (3.61%). Among different occupations, students had significantly higher positivity rates for both influenza A and B compared to other occupations (P < 0.05). The influenza A group had higher white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NC), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and serum amyloid A (SAA) levels than the influenza B group (P < 0.05).ConclusionIn Fengdu County, Chongqing, influenza epidemics exhibit distinct winter-spring seasonal patterns. Surveillance data indicate that influenza A (H3N2) constitutes the predominant subtype among type A viruses, while Victoria lineage dominates influenza B circulation. Epidemiological analysis reveals school-aged children demonstrate particular susceptibility to influenza A infections, whereas middle-aged adults show heightened vulnerability to influenza B. Public health recommendations emphasize: targeted vaccination campaigns for high-risk populations, enhanced health education initiatives, and utilization of inflammatory biomarkers to facilitate early differential diagnosis.

Introduction

Influenza, caused by influenza viruses, is an acute respiratory infectious disease characterized by rapid transmission, a short incubation period, and a propensity to trigger global pandemics [1–3]. Seasonal and geographical variations in influenza A viruses (such as H1N1 and H3N2) and influenza B viruses (Victoria/Yamagata lineages) are significant [4–6], posing a substantial burden on socioeconomics and public health [7, 8]. Studies have indicated that neutrophil count (NC), lymphocyte count (LC), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and serum amyloid A (SAA) serve as diagnostic biomarkers for infectious diseases and have certain value in the differential diagnosis of influenza virus infections [9, 10]. The Chinese Influenza Surveillance Network indicates [11] that the national seasonal peak occurs from December to March, with marked regional variations (e.g., the bimodal pattern observed in southern regions). However, Fengdu County previously lacked a systematic influenza surveillance system. As a mountainous county in Chongqing, its distinct climatic conditions and population mobility patterns (stemming from its role as a Yangtze River tourism hub) differ significantly from urban districts, necessitating localized epidemiological data. Based on this, the present study aims to systematically analyze the epidemiological characteristics and laboratory parameter variations of influenza viruses in Fengdu County, Chongqing from 2022 to 2024, thereby providing scientific evidence for precision influenza prevention and control in this region.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study spans from January 2022 to December 2024, encompassing two complete influenza epidemic seasons (2022–2023 and 2023–2024) to comprehensively characterize the annual and seasonal variations of influenza in Fengdu County. This study conducted a retrospective analysis of influenza-like illness (ILI) cases from 2022 to 2024 at Fengdu County Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital in Chongqing. Clinical data and peripheral blood test indicators from the medical record system were collected, including patient gender, age, WBC, NC, LC, NLR, CRP, SAA. Patients were categorized into age groups: 0 ~ 5 years, 6 ~ 13 years, 14 ~ 17 years, 18 ~ 44 years, 45 ~ 59 years, and ≥ 60 years. Inclusion criteria were: (1) meeting the relevant diagnostic criteria outlined in the “Diagnosis and Treatment Protocol for Influenza (2020 Edition)” [12] and confirmed through clinical examination; (2) not having received antiviral treatment prior to consultation; (3) presenting with symptoms of fever, sore throat, or cough, with fever duration ≤ 3 days; (4) having complete clinical data. The influenza-negative group comprised ILI cases with negative antigen test results and exclusion of other confirmed pathogen infections. Exclusion criteria included patients with co-infection by other pathogens (such as adenovirus, respiratory syncytial virus, etc.) or bacterial infections. All patients were required to sign an informed consent form, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fengdu County Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital.

Detection methods

Professionally trained medical staff collected nasopharyngeal swab samples from patients, which were immediately transported to the laboratory for testing. Virus antigen detection was performed using a colloidal gold test, which has high sensitivity and specificity in clinical practice [13]. Real-time fluorescent quantitative RT-PCR (ABI−7500) was employed for influenza virus subtype identification, using the Influenza A/B Virus Nucleic Acid Detection Kit (Real-Time PCR) manufactured by Shanghai BioGerm Medical Biotechnology Co., Ltd. with all operations strictly adhering to the requirements outlined in the “National Influenza Surveillance Program (2017 Edition)”. Blood routine tests and inflammatory markers were assayed using a Mindray Hematology Analyzer (BC−7500).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 20.0 software. Categorical variables were described using frequency and percentage n(%). For intergroup difference analysis, the chi-square test (χ²) was utilized when the proportion of cells with expected frequencies ≥ 5 was ≥ 80%. Continuous variables were first subjected to a test for homogeneity of variances. For continuous variables conforming to a normal distribution, they were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), and comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way ANOVA (F test). Non-normally distributed continuous variables were described using the median and quartiles M(P25, P75), and comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using the Kruskal-Wallis H test, with pairwise comparisons employing the Bonferroni correction method. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Influenza virus surveillance

During 2022–2024, a total of 19,576 nasopharyngeal swab specimens from ILI cases were tested using colloidal gold immunoassay. Influenza virus was detected in 5,073 cases (25.91%), with subtype distribution as follows: Influenza A virus: 4,174 cases (21.32%), Influenza B virus: 848 cases (4.33%), Co-infections with influenza A and B viruses: 91 cases (0.46%).There were significant differences in the positivity rates of different influenza viruses across different years (P < 0.001). The overall positivity rate for influenza and the positivity rate for influenza A virus peaked in 2022, which may be related to increased population mobility after the easing of COVID−19 restrictions. The positivity rates were 29.49% and 27.15%, respectively. The positivity rates for influenza B virus and mixed infections peaked in 2024, at 5.67% and 0.69%, respectively (see Table 1).

Temporal distribution of confirmed influenza cases

Influenza A virus activity was predominantly observed between December 2022 and April 2023, with the notable epidemiological pattern of the annual positivity rate peaking in 2022 while the seasonal epidemic peak occurred in March 2023, potentially associated with increased population mobility following the relaxation of COVID−19 containment measures. Influenza B virus cases were predominantly seen from December 2022 to February 2023. From 2023 to 2024, influenza A virus cases were mainly concentrated from December 2023 to March 2024, with two smaller peaks in December 2023 and February 2024, respectively. Distribution of influenza virus subtypes

Due to limitations in sample preservation conditions and constrained laboratory testing resources prior to 2023, this study exclusively conducted viral subtyping for influenza-positive specimens collected between December 2023 and December 2024. Further nucleic acid testing was conducted on colloidal gold test-positive cases from December 2023 to December 2024 to determine specific virus subtypes. Both H3N2 and H1N1 subtypes of influenza A virus were detected simultaneously from December 2023 to March, May, and December 2024, but the H3N2 subtype was the dominant strain. From December 2023 to April 2024, the Victoria lineage was the dominant strain for influenza B virus, with no detections in other months. Age distribution of influenza A and B viruses

There were significant differences in the positivity rates of influenza A virus across different age groups (χ²=282.49, P < 0.05). School-aged children aged 6 ~ 13 years had the highest proportion (23.12%), while the elderly aged 60 years and above had the lowest proportion (10.58%). Discussion

Influenza viruses are the primary pathogens causing acute respiratory infections, with reports indicating approximately 500,000 deaths globally each year due to these viruses [1, 14]. The transmission of influenza viruses is closely related to regional climatic conditions. Therefore, the present study aimed to analyze the epidemiological characteristics of influenza viruses in Fengdu, Chongqing, to provide a scientific basis for influenza prevention and control in the region. A total of 19,576 ILI specimens collected from 2022 to 2024 at Fengdu County Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital in Chongqing were tested for antigens, with 5073 positive specimens detected, yielding a positivity rate of 25.91%. significantly higher than the rates observed in Quzhou (18.5%) [15] and Jiaxing (19.1%) [16] Zhejiang Province, which may be related to the low temperatures and dry climate during winter and spring in Fengdu, favorable conditions for the survival and transmission of influenza viruses. Additionally, people tend to spend more time indoors in poorly ventilated environments during cold weather, increasing opportunities for human-to-human contact and thereby facilitating the spread of influenza.

Our study found significant differences in influenza positivity rates and the positivity rates of influenza A and B viruses across different years from 2022 to 2024 (χ²=350.74, 510.61, 121.67, P < 0.001). The highest influenza positivity rates were recorded in 2022 and 2023, predominantly characterized by influenza A virus predominance. Influenza B positivity rates were primarily concentrated in 2023 and 2024, demonstrating an overall increasing trend, which aligns with the latest 2023–2024 China CDC report [17], with the Victoria lineage accounting for 92% of influenza B cases nationally [6].The reasons for this trend may be related to the strict control measures implemented by health departments during the early stages of the COVID−19 pandemic, which restricted mobility and enhanced personal hygiene awareness, hindering the spread of influenza viruses. However, after the easing of pandemic restrictions in 2022, Fengdu, Chongqing, as an important domestic tourist city, experienced a sharp increase in tourist arrivals and frequent population mobility. Additionally, the annual Millennium Temple Fair held in spring may have further facilitated the rapid spread of influenza.

This study revealed a distinct seasonal pattern in the incidence of influenza in Fengdu, Chongqing. From 2022 to 2024, influenza A viruses were predominantly prevalent during the winter and spring seasons (December to March of the following year), with a second smaller peak occurring in 2024 (July-August). From December 2023 to December 2024, a total of 890 positive cases of influenza A and 550 positive cases of influenza B were detected. Notably, both H3N2 and H1N1 subtypes of influenza A were present in 2024 (January-March, May, and December), with H3N2 being the dominant subtype, while the H1N1 subtype was not detected in other months. Influenza B viruses were prevalent during the winter and spring of 2024 (December to March of the following year), with only the Victoria lineage detected throughout the year, and the Yamagata lineage was not detected. These findings are consistent with the results of studies by Wei-Juan Huang et al.[17] and Shanshan Li et al. [18]. The reasons for these observations may be attributed to the frequent antigenic drift of the H3N2 virus, which has strong immune escape capabilities, leading to its continued prevalence [17, 19]. Additionally, the novel influenza A H1N1 virus tends to be prevalent during seasonal transitions, demonstrating strong transmission and mutation capabilities.

This study found statistically significant differences in influenza positivity rates across different age groups (χ²=282.49, 40.353, P < 0.001). Among confirmed cases of influenza A virus infection, school-aged children aged 6 ~ 13 years were the most affected, followed by infants and young children aged 0 ~ 5 years. These findings are consistent with reports by Feifei Leng et al. [20], Gregory Hoy et al. [21], and María José Rivas et al. [22], which indicated the highest positivity rates of influenza A virus among individuals aged 6 ~ 14 years. The discrepancy with the study by Shanshan Li et al. [18] may be attributed to the higher coverage of influenza vaccination among the elderly population in our region, which provides a certain degree of protection against the occurrence of ILI. Additionally, the low willingness and rate of medical consultation among the elderly may result in negative test results by the time they seek medical attention. For infants aged 0 ~ 5 years, their relatively low immunity and for students aged 6 ~ 13 years, their weaker physical condition, poor hygiene awareness, high population density in schools, and frequent participation in collective activities may increase their susceptibility to cross-infection.

This study demonstrated no statistically significant difference in the positivity rates of influenza A and B viruses between different genders (P >0.05), This finding is consistent with reports from Xi’an [23] and Shanghai Jinshan District [24]. Key features of this translation: However, it is noteworthy that significant differences in the positivity rates of influenza A and B viruses were observed in the 0 ~ 5 years old and ≥ 60 years old age groups (χ²=7.482, 5.46, 8.139, 5.874, P = 0.006, 0.019, 0.004, 0.015), with higher positivity rates among females than males. This may be related to the gender ratio in Fengdu and the greater willingness of elderly females to participate in square dances and other activities. In this study, different occupations were categorized into student, staff, and other occupational groups. The results showed significant differences in the positivity rates of influenza A and B viruses among these three groups (χ²=86.79, 43.46, P < 0.001). Specifically, the positivity rates of influenza A and B viruses were higher in the student group than in the staff and other occupational groups, and the positivity rate of influenza A virus was higher in the staff group than in the other occupational group, consistent with reports from various regions [15, 20]. The reasons for these findings may be attributed to schools being dense environments that increase opportunities for influenza virus transmission. Additionally, teachers and medical personnel, who are major groups within the staff category, spend more time in public places, further increasing the risk of cross-infection.

The blood routine analysis in this study revealed significant differences in WBC, NC, LC, NLR, CRP, and SAA levels between different influenza groups and the negative group (χ²=9.574, 11.087, 14.545, 18.971, 15.322, 34.656, P < 0.001), consistent with the findings of multiple studies [4, 25]. Notably, both CRP and SAA levels were above the normal range, with a more pronounced increase observed in influenza A cases. Studies have shown [26] that CRP concentrations in the serum of healthy individuals are very low, but they significantly increase when the body is infected or injured. Due to the lower rate of antigenic variation in influenza B viruses, the fluctuations in CRP and SAA levels are less pronounced [4]. Additionally, influenza A patients had higher levels of WBC and NC compared to influenza B patients, indicating that influenza A cases are more prone to bacterial infections [27, 28]. Conversely, influenza A cases had lower LC levels than influenza B cases, resulting in a greater variation in NLR. NLR is a predictive marker associated with various malignancies and inflammatory responses [29, 30]. Some studies have reported a close correlation between NLR and influenza virus infection, suggesting that it can be used as a preliminary differential diagnostic indicator for influenza A and B [31]. This also suggests that early prevention of bacterial infections should be emphasized in influenza A cases to reduce the disease burden.

This study has several limitations. As a single-center retrospective study, it failed to collect and analyze data on patient comorbidities (e.g., asthma, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular diseases) and influenza vaccination history, which may influence infection susceptibility and clinical manifestations. Furthermore, subtype analysis was not performed for pre−2023 samples, nor were inflammatory biomarkers such as procalcitonin (PCT) and interleukins (ILs) assessed. Future prospective multicenter studies incorporating additional clinical variables are warranted to validate these findings.

To summarize, our epidemiological analysis of influenza viruses in Fengdu, Chongqing (2022–2024) reveals predominant winter-spring seasonality, with influenza A(H3N2) as the dominant subtype, concurrent circulation of A(H1N1)pdm09, and Victoria lineage as the primary influenza B strain. School-aged children and middle-aged adults emerged as susceptible populations, while notably, although surveillance data indicated lower influenza positivity rates among adults aged ≥ 60 years—potentially attributable to higher vaccination coverage, lower healthcare-seeking behavior, or delayed testing after viral clearance—this demographic remains a high-risk group due to their propensity for severe outcomes upon infection. Therefore, it is crucial to promptly implement influenza prevention and control measures, strengthen health education, promote handwashing, wear masks, and regularly vaccinate the main infected groups. Special attention should be paid to clinical symptoms such as fever and sore throat, as well as abnormal blood routine, CRP, and SAA indicators.These findings provide baseline data for precision prevention and control in this mountainous tourism county.

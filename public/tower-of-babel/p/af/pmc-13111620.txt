Clinical efficacy of Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide in the treatment of bronchial asthma and its impact on TNF-α and CD4+/CD8+
Authors: Ke Li, Xue Zhu, Shufen Cui, Yuanzhe Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis single-center randomized controlled trial evaluated whether Chaipu Decoction used as an adjunct to Seretide improves clinical and immunological outcomes in patients with bronchial asthma. A total of 300 adults treated between January 2023 and January 2025 were randomly assigned to receive either Chaipu Decoction plus Seretide or Seretide alone for 12 weeks. Clinical efficacy, adverse reactions, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) syndrome scores, pulmonary function indices (FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC and PEF), serum TNF-α, peripheral blood CD4+/CD8 + ratio, and Asthma Control Test (ACT) scores were assessed. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. SDTCM-EA2023-002), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Patients receiving the combined treatment achieved a higher total effective rate than those receiving Seretide alone (86.7% vs. 66.7%, p < 0.05), without a significant increase in adverse reactions. Compared with the control group, the combination group showed greater reductions in TCM syndrome scores, larger improvements in lung function, a more pronounced decrease in TNF-α, and a greater increase in the CD4+/CD8+ ratio after treatment, together with superior improvement in ACT scores. These findings indicate that Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide can provide additional clinical and immunological benefit in bronchial asthma over a 12-week treatment period while maintaining acceptable short-term tolerability.Keywords: Chaipu decoction, Seretide, TNF-α, CD4+/CD8+Subject terms: Cancer, Drug discovery, Immunology, Systems biology, Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

Bronchial asthma is a common long-lasting disease where the airways become inflamed, making them more sensitive and less open. Currently, around 300 million people across the world are affected by bronchial asthma., which affects the quality of life and health1, 2. Although modern medicine has made great strides in diagnosing and treating asthma, there are still some patients who have poor responses to traditional treatments, facing difficulties in treatment and recurrent attacks. Meanwhile, the application of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) in the treatment of asthma is gradually increasing, especially the combined use of TCM prescriptions, which has shown good clinical effects and safety. TCM has gradually received attention in treating bronchial asthma. Its unique theoretical system and rich clinical experience provide new ideas.

At present, the main treatment drugs for bronchial asthma include glucocorticoids, β2 receptor agonists, etc., which have significant effects in controlling symptoms and improving lung function. However, long-term use may lead to side effects, such as oral candidiasis infection, hoarseness, etc3, 4. In addition, some patients have poor reactions to these drugs and need to seek more effective treatment options. Recently, the application of TCM in asthma has gradually increased, especially the combined use of TCM formulas, which has shown good clinical efficacy and safety5, 6. Although TCM has shown great potential in treating bronchial asthma, it still faces some challenges. Firstly, the efficacy and mechanism of TCM are not yet fully understood, and there is a lack of large-scale, multi-center clinical research support7, 8. Secondly, the standardization and quality control of TCM still need to be further improved to ensure the stability and reliability of treatment9, 10. In addition, combining TCM with modern medical treatment to achieve synergistic effects is also an important direction of current research. Based on the current research status, the study combines Chaipu Decoction and Seretide to treat bronchial asthma. The study randomly divides patients with bronchial asthma into an Experimental Group (EG) treated with Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide and a Control Group (CG) treated with Seretide alone. Both groups are treated continuously for 12 weeks, and the clinical efficacy, adverse reactions, TCM syndrome scores, lung function indicators, TNF-α, and CD4+/CD8+ levels are observed in both groups. The research aims to analyze the clinical efficacy of Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide in bronchial asthma and its effects on the ratio of Tumor Necrosis Factor alpha (TNF-α) to CD4+/CD8+. The innovation lies in not only evaluating clinical efficacy and lung function, but also detecting inflammatory factors and immune function indicators to comprehensively evaluate the treatment effect. Simultaneously, the patient’s symptoms are observed continuously for 12 weeks to evaluate the long-term effectiveness and safety, providing more comprehensive data support for clinical application. The rationale for combining Chaipu Decoction with Seretide is pragmatic rather than ideological. Seretide (salmeterol/fluticasone) is widely used for symptom control and airway inflammation, yet a subset of patients continue to report incomplete control, recurrent symptoms, or poor tolerance to dose escalation. In TCM practice, Chaipu Decoction is commonly prescribed for patterns characterized by phlegm retention and dysregulated “qi” movement, which clinically maps onto persistent cough, chest tightness, and sputum-related symptom clusters in many asthma presentations. From a biomedical standpoint, several constituent herbs in Chaipu Decoction have been reported to show anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory activity, making the formula a plausible adjunct rather than a replacement. In this trial, we therefore tested an add-on strategy: Seretide remained the baseline therapy, while Chaipu Decoction was used as a structured co-intervention to determine whether symptom burden, lung function, and selected immune/inflammatory indices could be improved without compromising safety.

Materials and methods

General data

This trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant national regulations on human research. The protocol and procedures were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. SDTCM-EA2023-002). All participants (or their legal guardians where applicable) provided written informed consent after receiving a clear explanation of the study purpose, procedures, potential benefits, and foreseeable risks. To strengthen transparency and reduce selective reporting, the trial was prospectively registered on the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP). Participant confidentiality was protected through coded identifiers, and only authorized research staff had access to the study database.

A total of 300 patients with bronchial asthma who were admitted to the Department of Respiratory Medicine of the Affiliated Hospital of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2023 to January 2025 were selected as the research subjects. All patients met the diagnostic criteria for bronchial asthma and signed informed consent forms. Patients were randomly divided into an EG treated with Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide and a CG treated with Seretide alone, with 150 cases in each one. The randomization process uses a computer-generated random number table for grouping to ensure that each patient has the same probability of being assigned to the EG or the CG. To avoid selection bias, the study adopted the allocation hiding technique, sealing the randomized sequences in opaque envelopes, which were opened one by one by the study nurses when the patients were enrolled. In addition, the study adopted a single-blind design. Patients and medical staff were informed of the grouping, but the researchers responsible for data collection and analysis remained blind to the grouping to reduce measurement bias and reporting bias. To ensure the scientific nature and transparency of the research, the trial has undergone prospective registration on the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP). The research has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. All patients signed informed consent forms, indicating that they fully understood the research content, methods, potential risks, and benefits. Diagnostic criteria

The diagnostic criteria for bronchial asthma are mainly based on the “Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Bronchial Asthma (2020 Edition)”. The specific clinical manifestations of patients are: (1) Recurrent wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness or cough, which often occur or worsen at night and in the early morning, and are typical manifestations of bronchial asthma. Patients may experience symptoms after exposure to allergens, cold air, physical or chemical irritants, respiratory infections, exercise, and other triggers11, 12. (2) Episodic dyspnea: Patients may present with expiratory dyspnea accompanied by wheezing sounds during an episode. In severe cases, they may experience sitting breathing, cyanosis, and other symptoms13, 14. (3) Symptom relief: Symptoms can be relieved on their own or after treatment, and there may be no symptoms or signs during the relief period. In addition, in the physical examination, the main focus is on lung auscultation, which means that during the attack period, diffuse wheezing can be heard in both lungs, mainly in the expiratory phase, with prolonged expiratory phase. During severe asthma attacks, wheezing sounds may weaken or even disappear, indicating severe airway obstruction. In addition, some patients may experience auxiliary respiratory muscles participating in breathing, three concave signs, etc. during the attack period. In severe cases, they may experience increased heart rate, irregular pulse, etc15, 16.

Inclusion criteria

To ensure the scientific validity and reliability of research results, strict inclusion criteria have been established. (1) The patient must comply with the diagnostic guidelines mentioned above (2) The age is between 20 and 70 years old, which ensures a representative age distribution of subjects while avoiding other complications or differences in treatment response that may arise from being too old or too young. (3) Patients are included to cover different severity levels and improve the generalizability of results (4) The patient’s course of illness is at least 6 months to ensure that they have sufficient time to observe the treatment effect. (5) Patients or their legal guardians must sign an informed consent form, indicating a full understanding of the research content, methods, potential risks, and benefits. (6) The patient has no other serious respiratory diseases, cardiovascular diseases, liver diseases, kidney diseases, or malignant tumors. (7) The patient has no history of allergies to the TCM components and Seretide in Chaipu Decoction to ensure the safety.

Treatment method

In the study, the CG patients only receives conventional treatment with Seretide. In terms of dosage, adjust the dosage according to the condition. Mild asthma patients inhale once a day, once per inhalation (50 µg salmeterol + 250 µg fluticasone propionate). Moderate asthma patients inhale twice a day, once per inhalation. Severe asthma patients inhale twice a day, twice per inhalation. All patients receive continuous treatment for 12 weeks, with regular follow-up during the treatment period to monitor changes in their condition and adverse drug reactions17. The EG patients are supplemented with Chaipu Decoction based on routine treatment with Seretide. Same as the CG, adjust the dosage according to the condition, inhale 1–2 times a day, 1–2 times per inhalation, and continue treatment for 12 weeks. The ingredients of Chaipu Decoction include Bupleurum, Scutellaria baicalensis, Pinellia ternata, Magnolia officinalis, Poria cocos, dried tangerine peel, licorice, etc. The specific dosage is as follows: Bupleurum 10 g, Scutellaria 10 g, Pinellia 10 g, Magnolia officinalis 10 g, Poria 15 g, dried tangerine peel 10 g, and licorice 6 g. Soak the above-mentioned Chinese herbal medicines in water for 30 min, then bring to a boil over high heat, and simmer for another 30 min over low heat. Filter out the juice, add water and boil again. Combine the two decoctions and concentrate to 200 ml. Take one dose per day. Take it twice in the morning and evening, heated and taken. During treatment, the dosage of medication can be adjusted appropriately based on the specific condition and response.

Observed indicator

In terms of clinical efficacy, based on the improvement of symptoms, recovery of lung function, and improvement in quality of life, the clinical efficacy is divided into three levels: significant, effective, and invalid. Record in detail any adverse reactions that occur during the treatment period, including but not limited to dry mouth, bitter mouth, disgusting, vomiting, dizziness, palpitations, rash, etc. According to the severity of adverse reactions, they are classified into mild, moderate, and severe. Mild adverse reactions do not affect treatment, moderate adverse reactions need to adjust drug dosage or symptomatic treatment, and severe adverse reactions require suspension or termination of treatment. The TCM syndrome score reflects the severity of symptoms evaluated from the perspective of TCM. It is a set of standardized indicators used to assess the severity of symptoms from the TCM perspective. These scores cover different symptoms such as wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, coughing, phlegm volume, phlegm color, tongue appearance, and pulse. Each symptom is classified into 0 to 3 points based on its severity, with 0 indicating no symptoms, 1 indicating mild, 2 indicating moderate, and 3 indicating severe. The cumulative score can comprehensively assess the patient’s condition based on the principles of TCM. This scoring system can provide a detailed understanding of symptoms from a TCM perspective and supplement biomedical indicator assessments. Pulmonary function indicators mainly include Forced Expiratory Volume in the first second (FEV1), Forced Vital Capacity (FVC), FEV1/FVC ratio, Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF), etc. The inflammatory factor index mainly detects the TNF-α in serum. The immune function indicators mainly detect the CD4+/CD8+ ratio in peripheral blood. The Asthma Control Test (ACT) score is evaluated using the ACT questionnaire to evaluate the control status. The ACT questionnaire consists of 5 questions, each with a rating range of 1–5 points, and totaling 5–25 points. A total score of ≥ 20 signifies good asthma control, 16–19 indicates partial asthma control, and < 16 indicates uncontrolled asthma.

Statistical method

The data of all observation indicators were collected through standardized record forms and analyzed using SPSS 26.0 statistical software. For measurement data, the mean ± standard deviation (x ± s) was adopted, and the χ² test was also used. The significance level of the study is set at 0.05. When the p-value is less than 0.05, the difference is considered statistically significant. When the p-value is greater than 0.05, the difference is considered not statistically significant. In addition, to assess the effect size more accurately, all statistical analyses provide a 95% Confidence Interval (CI). When conducting statistical tests, all hypothesis tests were carried out using two-sided tests. For the missing data, the study adopted the Intention-to-Treat (ITT) method. Specifically, all patients randomly assigned to the EG and the CG were included in the final analysis, regardless of whether they had completed the entire research process. For the missing data points, the Multiple Imputation (MI) method is adopted for processing. This method can fully utilize all available data and improve the accuracy and reliability of the analysis. When conducting multiple comparisons, to control the Type I error rate, the Bonferroni correction method was adopted in the study. Specifically, when comparing multiple indicators at the same time point, the study divided the significance level by the number of comparisons to determine the adjusted significance level for each comparison. Given the pragmatic nature of add-on therapy and the reliance on symptom-based measures, we pre-specified core outcomes and applied two-sided testing with α = 0.05. For multiple comparisons across repeated time points, Bonferroni adjustment was used to reduce inflation of Type I error. Missing values were handled using an intention-to-treat framework with multiple imputation, aiming to reduce bias from dropout or incomplete follow-up. These choices were made to keep analysis conservative while preserving interpretability for clinical readers.

Discussion

To investigate the clinical efficacy of Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide in treating bronchial asthma and its effects on tumor necrosis factor and CD4+/CD8+ ratio, 300 patients with bronchial asthma admitted to the respiratory department of Affiliated Hospital of Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2023 to January 2025 were randomly divided into Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide treatment group (EG) and simple Seretide treatment group (CG). The clinical efficacy, adverse reactions, TCM symptom scores, lung function indicators, TNF-α, and CD4+/CD8+ levels were observed. In the research findings, the total effective rate of the EG was 86.7%, exceeding that of the CG (66.7%) (p < 0.05), indicating that Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide treatment had significant advantages in improving the clinical efficacy of bronchial asthma patients. The total incidence rates of adverse reactions in the EG and the CG were 25.3% and 33.3%, without clear difference (p > 0.05), indicating that both treatment regimens had good tolerability on safety. After treatment, the TCM syndrome score and lung function indicators of the EG were significantly improved, and the improvement was better than that of the CG (p < 0.05). A marked decline in TNF-α levels was evident in the EG (p < 0.05) and a clear increase in CD4+/CD8+ ratio after treatment appeared (p < 0.05), while without significant improvement in the CG on these indicators (p > 0.05). The combination of Chaipu Decoction and Seretide not only improved the clinical symptoms and lung function, but also regulated immune function, with high clinical application value. In the ACT score after 12 weeks of treatment, the ACT score of the EG significantly increased to (22.5 ± 1.8), and that of the CG increased to (20.3 ± 1.9). There was a significant difference in the scores between the two groups (p < 0.05). Although this difference was statistically significant, it only increased by about 2 points. According to the scoring criteria of the ACT, a total score of ≥ 20 indicates good asthma control, 16–19 indicates partial asthma control, and < 16 indicates no asthma control. The ACT scores of both the EG and the CG at 12 weeks after treatment reached good asthma control standards, but the score of the EG was higher, indicating that the treatment of Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide may have certain advantages in improving asthma control. In the study, patients in the group treated with Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide showed significant improvements in multiple indicators, indicating that the TCM components such as Bupleurum, Scutellaria, and Pinellia in Chaipu Decoction have anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and antioxidant effects, which can alleviate airway inflammation and improve lung function.

TNF-α is an important inflammatory mediator and plays a key role in the pathogenesis of bronchial asthma. TNF-α can activate and migrate inflammatory cells, increasing inflammatory factors, thereby exacerbating airway inflammation. In the study, the TNF-α in the EG significantly decreased after treatment (p < 0.05), indicating that the combination of Chaipu Decoction and Seretide can effectively inhibit the TNF-α and reduce inflammatory responses. This change is not only statistically significant, but from a biological perspective, the decrease in TNF-α levels helps alleviate airway inflammation and improve patients’ clinical symptoms. However, whether the reduction in TNF-α levels is sufficient to indicate immune regulation still needs to be comprehensively evaluated in combination with other inflammatory markers and clinical indicators. The CD4+/CD8+ ratio is an important indicator for evaluating the balance of the immune system. CD4+ cells mainly participate in regulating cellular immunity and humoral immunity, while CD8+ cells mainly engage in cytotoxic effects. In healthy individuals, the CD4+/CD8+ ratio is typically maintained within a certain range, approximately 1.5 to 2.0. In the study, the CD4+/CD8+ ratio in the EG significantly increased after treatment (p < 0.05), indicating that the combination of Chaipu Decoction and Seretide can balance the immune system, enhance the function of CD4+ cells, and thereby improve the body’s immune regulatory ability. This change is not only statistically significant, but from a biological perspective, an increase in the CD4+/CD8+ ratio helps to enhance the overall function of the immune system, reduce inflammatory reactions, and improve patients’ clinical symptoms.

Treatment compliance is one of the important factors affecting the treatment outcome. In the study, although the treatment compliance data are not specifically recorded, it can be inferred that the compliance in the EG treated with Chaipu Decoction combined with Seretide may be higher. This is because Chaipu Decoction, as a TCM decoction, has a mild nature and no serious adverse reactions have been observed in the research, which may help improve patients’ treatment compliance. In addition, the seretide further enhances the therapeutic effect and may have also improved patients’ treatment satisfaction and compliance. Patient-reported outcomes refer to the information directly reported by patients regarding their health status, functional status and treatment effects. These results are crucial for evaluating the clinical significance of the treatment, reflecting the actual feelings and quality of life. Although the study used the ACT score to assess asthma control, it did not involve other patient-reported results, such as the Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ), symptom diaries, patient satisfaction surveys, etc. The common AQLQ is a widely used tool for assessing the quality of life of asthma patients, including activity limitations, symptoms, and emotional functions.

Currently, some scholars also use classic TCM formulas to treat bronchial asthma. Wu Y et al. explored the Sanzi Yangqin Decoction in treating asthma through network pharmacology and experimental verification. SZYQD and its key component luteolin can alleviate asthma symptoms by inhibiting inflammation and excessive mucus secretion and controlling the NF-κB and AKT signaling pathways. Similar to the research results, both Sanzi Yangqin Decoction and Chaipu Decoction belong to a classic formula for warming the lungs and resolving phlegm. When used to treat bronchial asthma, they can significantly alleviate asthma symptoms and have a significant effect on relieving phlegm inflammatory factors18. Qin Z et al. explored the mechanism of Bushen Yiqi Decoction in treating asthma through network pharmacology and experimental verification. The results indicate that BYD exerts anti-asthma effects by controlling the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, inhibiting inflammatory cells and cytokines and reducing airway inflammation and remodeling. Similar to the research findings, when treating bronchial asthma, the additional Bushen Yiqi Decoction can significantly alleviate asthma symptoms. After treatment, the patient’s lung function indicators continue to improve19. Xiong X J explained the connotation of prescription and syndrome according to modern pathophysiological mechanisms, in response to the current application status of Chaipu Decoction and Maxing Shigan Decoction in emergency and critical care. Similar to the research results, the Chaipu Decoction combined with Maxing Shigan Decoction has a significant effect on bronchial asthma, mainly reflected in lung function indicators and sputum inflammatory factor indicators20. In summary, the combination of Chaipu Decoction and Seretide not only significantly improves the clinical symptoms and lung function, but also regulates immune function, which has high clinical application value.

Limitations

Several limitations should be stated plainly. First, this was a single-center trial based in one hospital setting, so external validity is not guaranteed; patient phenotype, allergen exposure, and care pathways may differ across regions. Second, the study used a single-blind approach rather than full double-blinding, which leaves room for expectancy effects on symptom reporting and clinician–patient interaction. Third, while we monitored common adverse reactions and did not observe a statistical safety signal, the follow-up window was limited to 12 weeks; rarer reactions and longer-term outcomes cannot be excluded on the basis of this dataset alone. Fourth, the trial focused on TNF-α and CD4+/CD8+ as representative indicators, but asthma inflammation is biologically heterogeneous; a broader panel (e.g., eosinophil counts, FeNO, IL-4/IL-5/IL-13 pathways) would better characterize mechanisms and may explain responder variability. Finally, adherence was not quantified using objective measures (such as inhaler counters or decoction compliance logs), which may have introduced unmeasured performance differences between groups. Future work should therefore prioritize multi-center recruitment, stronger blinding where feasible, extended observation, and a more mechanistically informative biomarker set.

Conclusion

This randomized controlled study indicates that adding Chaipu Decoction to standard Seretide therapy can offer measurable clinical advantages for patients with bronchial asthma. Over a 12-week treatment period, patients receiving the combined regimen showed higher overall response rates, greater improvement in lung function, and more pronounced reductions in TNF-α alongside increases in the CD4+/CD8+ ratio compared with those treated with Seretide alone. These findings suggest that the combined approach may not only relieve symptoms but also exert a modulatory effect on inflammatory and immune processes relevant to asthma. Importantly, the addition of Chaipu Decoction did not lead to a higher incidence of adverse reactions, supporting its short-term tolerability under monitored clinical conditions. While both groups achieved clinically meaningful improvement in asthma control, the consistently better outcomes observed in the combination group point to a potential additive or synergistic benefit. All these results support the use of Chaipu Decoction as an adjunct to inhaled corticosteroid/long-acting β₂-agonist therapy in selected patients with bronchial asthma. Larger, multi-center trials with extended follow-up and broader biomarker profiling will be required to confirm these effects and to better define the patient populations most likely to benefit from this integrative treatment strategy.

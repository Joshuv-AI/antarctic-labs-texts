Cervical cancer screening utilization and associated factors among women living with HIV in South West Ethiopia
Authors: Kenbon Bayisa, Samuel Ejeta Chibsa, Hana Tesfaye, Bilisumamulifna Tefera Kefeni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCervical cancer is a public health challenge among reproductive-age women in low and middle-income countries (LMICs). Primarily cervical cancer affects vulnerable group of HIV-positive women more than others. Despite overwhelming evidence that cervical cancer screening reduces maternal morbidity and mortality, there is limited study on the utilization of cervical cancer screening among HIV-positive women in Ethiopia. Therefore this study was conducted to assess the utilization and associated factors of cervical cancer screening among HIV- positive women in south west Ethiopia. A facility-based cross-sectional study was conducted among 387 HIV-positive women from July to August 2021. Data was collected through face-to-face interviews and checklists. After data were checked, it was entered into Epi-Data version 3.1 and then exported to SPSS version 23 for statistical analysis. Logistic regression was used to investigate the relationship between dependent and independent variables. The association was reported using the adjusted odds ratio (AOR) with a 95% confidence interval (CI), and the level of statistical significance was declared with a P-value less than 0.05. The study revealed that 41.3%; (95% CI: 36.6–46.6) of HIV-positive women underwent cervical cancer screening. More than half (57.6%) of study participant’ had heard about cervical cancer screening. Having multiple sexual partners (AOR = 3.13; 95%CI: 1.79–5.47), having a CD4 count less than 500cells/mm3 (AOR = 2.05; 95%CI: 1.29–2.23) and ever hearing about premalignant cervical cancer (AOR = 2.13; 95% CI: 1.35–3.37) were independent predictors of cervical cancer screening utilization among HIV- positive women. The study found that utilization of cervical cancer screenings among HIV-positive was relatively low. Those who have multiple sexual partners, aCD4 count less than 500cell/mm3 and have ever heard about premalignant cervical cancer were more likely use cervical cancer screening. All concerned bodies should improve awareness to increase utilization and integrating the cervical cancer screening services with ART clinics in all settings.Keywords: Utilization, Cervical cancer screening, HIV-positive women, Southwest Ethiopia.Subject terms: Cancer, Diseases, Health care, Medical research

Introduction

Cervical cancer occurs when cells in the cervix grow out of control, typically caused by human papillomavirus (HPV), and it poses a significant threat to women lives1,2. Globally, cervical cancer is the fourth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the fourth leading cause of mortality among women in the world with about 604,127 estimated new cases and over 341,831deaths every year3,4. This is mainly common among women in sub-Saharan Africa; due to a lack of information about the disease and prevention services3. About 7,445 new cervical cancer cases are diagnosed annually in Ethiopia ranking as the second leading cause of female cancer in the country4. In Africa, the overall prevalence was found to be 10.1% of those with positive visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA) results, and 1.7% of them had suspicious lesions on screening for cervical cancer investigation5. Human papillomavirus (HPV) has high-risk types that activate the progression of a normal cell into precancerous lesions leading to cervical cancer. HPV types 16 and 18 account for about 70% of cervical cancer cases globally1,2 Convincing evidence has confirmed that HPV vaccination programs for the most common high-risk HPV types would prevent about 87% of cases of cervical cancer worldwide. Since the approval of HPV vaccination in 2006, about 80 countries and territories have implemented national HPV vaccination programs, covering over 100 million women5,6. However, women living with HIV are more likely to develop persistent HPV infections at an earlier age and develop cancer sooner1. HIV/AIDS is a well-elucidated risk factor for cervical cancer, primarily through accelerating the development of pre-cancerous lesions in the cervix7. VIA of cervical cells with acetic acid was introduced in Ethiopia since 2009, followed by cryotherapy8. The human papillomavirus vaccination program introduced in Ethiopia was Quadri-valent Gardasil for 9 to 15 year old girls with a total dose of three, scheduled commonly at initial (0), 2month and 6months intervals for lower and high risk of genotypes8. Efforts have been made in Ethiopia in different directions, including the adoption of World Health Organization(WHO) guidelines for cervical cancer screening through visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA) or a Pap smear to decrease women mortality due to cervical cancer3 and the introduction of HPV vaccination for women younger than 15 years old9. The guidelines for cervical cancer screening in Ethiopia followed HIV-positive women, ages between 30 and 49 years were regularly included10–12. In contrast to HPV DNA testing, cytology, or Pap smear, cervical cancer screening techniques that use VIA examination are often recommended by WHO in resource-constrained regions4. Previous studies have shown that 17 to 32% of women have used cervical cancer screening (22). Yet, few studies have been done on cervical cancer screening utilization by HIV-positive women in this study area (10,22). Our current study assesses utilizations of cervical cancer screening among HIV-positive women that uses beyond having researchers and influencers, including contributing to the existing literature and scientific community. Therefore, this study was conducted to determine the prevalence of cervical cancer screening and associated factors among HIV-positive women in southwest Ethiopia.

Methods

Study setting and period

An institutional based-cross-sectional study design was employed at Mettu Karl Specialized and Bedelle General Hospitals from July to August; 2021. Both hospitals are located in the Oromia regional state south-west part of Ethiopia. They have antiretroviral therapy (ART) clinics and Visual Inspection with acetic acid (VIA) services. According to the registration book and health management and information statistics (HMIS) of adult ART clinic, 1553 of reproductive age women were being followed at both hospitals.

Population and study participants

All HIV- positive women followed ART clinic at the Mettu Karl Comprehensive Specialized and Bedelle General Hospitals were the source population. All randomly selected HIV- positive women attending the ART clinic in selected hospital during the study period were study participants. Critically ill women, those admitted, and women with previous history of cervicectomy were excluded from the study.

Sample size determination and sampling procedure

The sample size was determined using the single population proportion formula, considering the prevalence of cervical cancer screening service utilization from previous study conducted at Hawasa Hospital (P:40.1%11, a 95% level of confidence, a 5% margin of error and a 5% non-response rate. The calculated sample size was 387.

Sampling procedure

Among the five hospitals existing in Illu Aba Bor and Buno Bedelle Zone two hospitals were selected using a lottery method. The selected hospitals were Mettu Karl Specialized and Bedelle General Hospitals. The number of reproductive age women attending the ART clinics was 1553. The sample was proportionally allocated for both hospitals. Then, systematic random sampling technique was used to select the study units and the skip interval was calculated as (K = 1553/387 ≈ 4). The first client to be interviewed was selected randomly by a lottery method.

Operational definition

Cervical cancer screening utilization If women were assessed for premalignant lesions either through VIA or Pap smears by a health care provider9.

Data collection tool and procedure

A data collection tool was developed from various similar studies in different parts of the.

world and modified according to local contexts11–13. The questionnaire contained three parts: socio-demographic characteristics, medical and reproductive factors, and individuals’ knowledge and attitude towards cervical cancer screening and health system related factors. Data were collected using a structured face to face interview-administered questionnaire and check-list through client card reviewing.

Data quality control

Data were collected by trained nurses working in the health facility, and supervisors were public health officers working at each selected health facility. A supervisor observed and supervised daily data collectors. Data quality was ensured by giving training to data collectors and strict supervision during data collection. A pretest was conducted on 5% of the sample size at Darimu Primary Hospital before the actual data collection.

Data processing and analysis

Data were manually checked for completeness, coded, and cleaned before entering into a computer. Then, it was entered into Epi Data version 3.1 and exported into SPSS version 23 for statistical analysis. Descriptive statistics such as frequencies and percentage were calculated. Data were summarized using mean (SD) for normally distributed continuous variables and frequency table with percentage for categorical variables. The results were presented in narrations, tables, and graphs. A variable with a p-value less than 0.25 in bivariate analysis was entered into multiple logistic regressions. Multiple logistic regression analysis was performed to assess the association of each independent factor of cervical cancer screening service utilization among HIV positive women. The Hosmer and Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, with a p-value (p > 0.05) was checked to see good fitness. 95% Confidence intervals (CI), Odds ratios (OR), and a p-value of less than 0.05 were used to declare statistical association.

Results

Socio-demographic characteristics of study participants

A total of 380 participants were included with a response rate of 97.6%. The mean and standard deviation (SD) of the age respondents were 32.71 ± 7.42 respectively. One hundred seventy four (47.8%) of respondents were categorized in the age group between 25 and 34 years old and thirty four (8.9%) of respondents were 40 years old. About 303(79.7%) of participant were married. Two hundred fifty one (66.1%) of respondents were from urban resident. Almost two third (60.0%) of participants had formal education. One hundred forty seven (38.7%) and 112(29.5%) of the respondents occupational status are house wife and government employee respectively (Table 1).

Among respondents, two hundred seventy nine (73.4%) of them were multipara mothers. However, the mean and the standard deviation of respondents’ age at first sexual contact were 15.07 ± 5.26 respectively. The majority (70.0%) of respondents started first sexual intercourse at age greater than or equal to 15 years old. Regarding respondent’s sexual partners, 82 (21.6%) had multiple sexual partners. The mean duration of participants diagnosed with HIV and enrolled was 10.15 ± SD 3.91 years. More than half (64.2%) of them had been diagnosed and enrolled in HIV positive for greater than or equal to 10 years. Two hundred sixty (68.4%) of respondents were on ART regimen for greater than or equal to eight. Two hundred twenty-nine (60.3%) of respondents had a CD4 count greater than or equal to 500cells/mm3 (Table 2).

Awareness of women about cervical cancer screening of participants

Regarding the awareness status of cervical cancer screening, two hundred nineteen (57.6%) of participants had ever heard about cervical cancer disease. Among those who had ever heard about the disease of cervical cancer, 102(46.6%) and 75 (34.2%) of them had heard from health care workers and the media, respectively. Regarding having information on premalignant cervical cancer screening, two hundred six (54.2%) of respondents had ever have information on premalignant cervical cancer screening from different sources. More than half (51.9%) and 53(25.7%) of them were heard from health care workers and the media, respectively. More than half (52.9%) of participants were knew about pre malignant cervical cancer screening. Among participants who Knew about pre-malignant cervical cancer screening, 115(57.2%) and 86(42.8%) of them were knew about the screening methods VIA and Pap smear, respectively. More than half (51.8%) of respondents were counseled by health care providers on the uptake of cervical cancer screening (Table 3).

Factors associated with cervical cancer screening among ART clinic visiting women

In Bivariate logistic regression, educational status, residence, parity, duration on ART, CD4 count, history of STI, condom use during intercourse, having multiple partners and knowledge of pre-malignant cervical cancer screening identified as potential candidate variables for multiple logistic regression Therefore, having formal education were two times more likely to be screened for cervical cancer than were those without formal education (AOR = 2.06: 95% CI; 1.29,3.28). Additionally, women who used condoms during sexual intercourse were almost two times more likely be screened for cervical cancer than those who did not use condoms (AOR = 1.71:95%CI; 1.08, 2.71).

Furthermore, women’s with a CD4 count less than 500 cells/mm3 were two times more likely to be screened for cervical cancer than those with a count of 500 cells/mm3 or more (AOR = 2.05:95%CI:1.29, 2.23). Women with multiple sexual partners had 3.13 times higher odd of being screened for cervical cancer than those without multiple partners (AOR = 3.13: 95%CI: 1.79, 5.47). lastly, women who had heard about pre-malignant cervical cancer screening were 2.13 times more likely to be screened for cervical cancer than those who had not heard about it (AOR = 2.13:95%CI: 1.35, 3.37) (Table 4).

Discussion

This study revealed that the utilization of cervical cancer screening among HIV-positive women was 157 (41.3%) with a 95% CI of (36.6, 46.6). This finding was similar to a study conducted in Hawassa town (40.1%)11 but, higher than studies in Jimma Town (15.5%), southwestern Ethiopia13, Jos University Teaching Hospital Nigeria (15.5%)14, Gondar University, in northwest Ethiopia (10%)12 and Southern Ethiopia15. The difference could be attributed to the diverse socioeconomic and demographic backgrounds level of the study participants for example the level of education and awareness about cervical cancer and its screening program were higher in this study compared to others. However, this study’s findings were lower than studies in northern Tanzania (50.2%)16, Canada (58%)17 and Kenya (46%)18. These variations could be due to differences in socio-demographic characteristics of the study respondents, health promotion information, and prevention program and policies across countries. The study also revealed that women with formal education were two times more likely to be utilized cervical cancer screening compared to their counterparts. This finding is consistent with study in Nigeria14, Hawasa town11, Gondar12, and Addis Ababa19. It is possible that educated women have a better understanding of the causes, risk factors, prevention mechanisms, and treatment of the disease. It may be that those who are formally educated more efficient in processing of health and education information, as they easily understand health information from various sources and have the motivation and confidence to undergo cervical cancer screening.

This study demonstrated that HIV positive women with a CD4 count less than 500 cells/mm3 were twice as likely to be screened for cervical cancer as those with count greater than or equal to 500 cells/mm3. This finding is in consistent with the study done in Gondar12, which found that a CD4 count of </=200 cell/mm3 was significantly associated with cervical screening. The possible explanation could be that women with a lower CD4 count may have decreased immunity, making them more susceptible to the development of opportunistic infections, which can lead to more advanced signs and symptoms. Since they frequently visit health facilities, for opportunistic infection treatment they may be counseled on cervical cancer screening and decide to utilize the service. Biological plausibility evidence shows that immune-suppression and low CD4 counts caused by HIV infection predispose women living with HIV to an increased risk for cervical cancer and the development of squamous intraepithelial lesions20.

HIV positive women with multiple sexual partners were more than three times as likely to be screened for cervical cancer compared to their counter parts. This findings congruent with study done in southern Ethiopia15, Shabadino District, Southern Ethiopia21 and Tigray region, Ethiopia20. The reason for this congruent result might be that women with multiple sexual partners are at high risk of contracting sexually transmitted diseases (STDs). When women develop symptoms of any STDs, they are more likely to seek medical check-ups; making them available for screening services. HIV-positive women who have heard about premalignant cervical cancer screening were more than twice more likely to be screened for cervical cancer than women have not heard about it. This finding is consistent with a study done in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia19. The reason for this congruent may be due to similarities in methodology such as the study population, design and the area. A possible limitation is that since the studies were cross-sectional it may lead to there may be an overestimation of screening status due to social desirability bias, making it difficult to interpret cause-and-effect relationships.

Conclusion

The study found that the utilization of cervical cancer screenings among HIV-positive women was relatively low. Those who have multiple sexual partners, aCD4 count less than 500cells/mm3 and have heard about premalignant cervical cancer were more likely to utilize cervical cancer screening. All relevant authorities should work to improve awareness of utilization and integration the service with ART clinics in all settings.

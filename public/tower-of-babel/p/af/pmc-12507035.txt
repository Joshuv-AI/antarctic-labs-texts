Recurrent diabetic ketoacidosis in Saudi patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus: a retrospective study from a tertiary hospital
Authors: Muneera Alshareef, Samia A Bokhari, Arsalan Nawaz, Lama Arfaj, Al Hussein Alsharif, Haidar Alshamrani, Fahad Al-Bogami, Maram Altowairqi, Sally Aqabaw, Abdullah Alobisi, Yasir Al-Ard, Homam Talal Alsharifb
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUND: Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a potentially fatal complication of type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM). To our knowledge, limited studies have examined the risk factors of recurrent DKA in the Saudi T1DM population.OBJECTIVES: To identify factors contributing to recurrent DKA in Saudi patients with T1DM. By identifying the contributing factors for DKA recurrence the disease burden can be mitigated by making targeted interventions.DESIGN: RetrospectiveSETTING: Tertiary care center, Jeddah, Saudi ArabiaPATIENTS AND METHODS: Saudi patients with T1DM admitted with DKA between 2021 to 2023 were included. A comparative analysis was performed between patients with a single recurrence of DKA and those with multiple recurrent admissions.MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Association between multiple variables and recurrent DKA.SAMPLE SIZE: 245 Saudi patients with T1DMRESULTS: A total of 245 Saudi patients with T1DM were included. A significantly higher proportion of females were observed in the multiple recurrent DKA group (73.1% vs. 48.5%, P=.0001). Also, a higher proportion of single individuals were found among the multiple recurrent DKA group (P=.006). Longer duration of T1DM (≥5 years) and presence of microalbuminuria were more associated with multiple recurrent DKA than single recurrence of DKA. Among the precipitating factors, non-adherence to medications was associated with multiple recurrence of DKA (P=.02).CONCLUSION: This study has identified several risk factors contributing to recurrent DKA episodes among Saudi patients with T1DM. By increasing awareness of these factors among both healthcare providers and the community, we can develop more effective prevention strategies and ultimately reduce the incidence of recurrent DKA.LIMITATIONS: A retrospective study and single-center experience.Keywords: Readmission Diabetic ketoacidosis, Recurrent Diabetic ketoacidosis, Type 1 diabetes mellitus

INTRODUCTION

The prevalence of type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) is on the rise. It was estimated to be 8.75 million individuals with T1DM globally in 2022, including 1.52 million under the age of 20, although data on adults was limited.1 Saudi Arabia ranked eighth worldwide for the number of T1DM cases and the fourth in incidence rate (33.5 per 100 000), with approximately 35 000 children and adolescents affected.2 T1DM can lead to acute complications such as diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), which carries high morbidity and mortality risks if not promptly diagnosed and treated. Common participating reasons for DKA include recent diagnosis, infections, and noncompliance with insulin regimens.3,4 Shaka et al noted an increase in readmission rates for DKA among adults with T1DM in the United States over the past decade.5 A local study by Alotaibi et al indicated a 32.4% readmission rate for DKA, primarily among patients with T1DM.6 Besides the precipitating factors for recurrent DKA, its correlation with fragmented healthcare and increased mortality has been documented.7 Patients who have experienced more than two episodes of DKA are four times more likely to die within five years.8 This study aims to investigate the rates of DKA readmission and the contributing variables in Saudi patients with T1DM.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

We retrospectively analyzed Saudi patients with T1DM between 12 to 60 years who were admitted with DKA through the emergency room at King Fahad Armed Forces Hospital, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia from 2021 to 2023. Data was collected from electronic medical records using ICD-10 coding. The medical records of all patients were thoroughly reviewed to ensure the accurate selection of T1DM cases excluding other diabetes types and pregnant individuals. Ethical approval was taken from the Institutional Review Board of King Fahad Armed Forces Hospital, Jeddah (Ref: REC 669) dated 24th July 2024.

Variables

DKA was defined as blood glucose level exceeding 250 mg/dL, serum bicarbonate (HCO3) ≤18 mEq/L, pH <7.30, and the presence of serum ketones. Severe DKA was characterized by a pH <7.0. Multiple recurrent DKA was defined as two or more episodes within 12 months over the preceding three years. Detailed demographic and clinical data was collected, including the duration of diabetes (less than or more than five years), dietary compliance, medication affecting glucose levels, alcohol use, psychiatric conditions, chronic diabetes complications (such as retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy), self-monitoring of blood glucose practices, continuous glucose monitoring use, HbA1c levels, total insulin dosage, the use of multiple daily injections or insulin pumps, DKA severity, length of hospital stay, ICU admissions, and mortality rates. A comparative analysis was performed between patients with a single recurrence of DKA admission and those with multiple recurrent admissions. Patients with the first episode of DKA at the time of diagnosis were excluded. Only individuals with prior diagnosis of T1DM were included. Non-adherence to diet and insulin was evaluated by reviewing historical documentation and self-reported records, including the reasons for non-compliance.

Statistical analysis

Data was analyzed by Statistical Package for Social Sciences (IBM SPSS, IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) version 22. The values expressed as frequency (%) for categorized data and mean±standard deviation (minimum-maximum) for parametric data. Significance between groups was identified using Pearson chi-Square test for categorized data and unpaired Student T-test for parametric data. Multivariate logistic regression was made to find risk factors for readmission with DKA. A P value <.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 378 admissions with a diagnosis of DKA were evaluated, and only 245 patients with T1DM and DKA were included in the final analysis.

Table 1 summarizes the demographic features of the study population (n=245). The mean age of the participants was 22.1 (7.5) years, majority of whom were females (56.3%). Most participants were under the age of 25, single, and unemployed. A significant proportion had a BMI <25 kg/m² (69%) and education level up to high school (72.2%).

Table 5 shows the significant independent predictors of DKA readmission. Female patients were significantly more likely to experience multiple DKA recurrence than males (P=.001). Compared to single patients, married individuals had a significantly lower risk of DKA readmission (P=.019, OR=0.267, 95% CI: 0.089–0.806) as well as patients with a shorter diabetes duration (<5 years) had significantly lower odds of DKA readmission. Also, those with microalbuminuria and nonadherence to prescribed medications was a significant predictor of multiple recurrent DKA (P=.026, OR=2.218, 95% CI: 1.101–4.470).

DISCUSSION

DKA is one of the major complications of T1DM with increasing prevalence and is potentially a fatal disease.9,10 In various international studies, multiple risk factors associated with the recurrence of DKA have been identified including duration of diabetes, specific gender, uncontrolled diabetes, drug abuse, and socioeconomic status.11 Our study aimed to find the risk factors for the recurrence of DKA in our local population.

In our study, the mean age of the participants was 22.0 (7.5) years. A large study from Israel reported a higher mean age of 44 years, likely due to the inclusion of patients over 18 years of age, a longer study duration of 13 years compared to our 3-year study, and a larger sample size.12 Similarly, a study in Ethiopia observed a higher mean age of 52 years, since they included patients above 15 years of age and both DM types.13 In our study, there was no significant difference observed between the different age groups; however, a local study showed younger age a major risk factor for recurrence of DKA.14

Our study demonstrated that multiple recurrence of DKA is more common in females. A large Ethiopian study also demonstrated similar findings, where recurrence of DKA was 1.5 times greater in females than males. Similar findings have also been demonstrated in a German study. However, this observation contrasts with the findings of an Israeli study where no gender differences were identified.11–13

Elevated HbA1c levels emerged as a key predictor for recurrent DKA in the study by Golbets et al, a result mirrored in the findings of Ehrmann et al and Abulmagd et al, but not in the present study.11,12,15

Renal dysfunction is a well-known complication of DM and studies showed that patients with low estimated glomerular filtration rate (e-GFR) have severe DKA and prolonged hospitalization. Accordingly, an American study by Gangu et al mentioned that chronic kidney disease patients have recurrent hospitalizations due to DKA as compared to patients with normal kidney functions.16

As the duration of diabetes prolongs, it has established risks of micro- and macrovascular complications. Our data suggests that duration of diabetes is a strong predictor of multiple recurrence of DKA and this coincides with the other studies.13,17

The relationship between psychiatric illness and drug abuse with DKA recurrence has yielded inconsistent findings in the literature. While our study found no significant difference between the two groups, prior research by Leyden et al and Ehrmann et al identified psychiatric comorbidities and substance abuse as strong predictors of DKA recurrence. Conversely, an American study by Lyerla et al reported no significant association between depression and DKA recurrence which is aligning with our results.11,18,19

Our study found no difference in predicting recurrence of DKA between insulin pump users versus basal bolus regimen users. This finding is consistent with the study by Lyerla et al (P=0.1).19 The reason is that in our study, very few patients (2.8 %) were on insulin pumps similar to Lyerla et al where only 5% of the sample size were on insulin pumps.

We noted that marital status and education level are strong predictors of DKA. Multiple recurrence of DKA is more common in single and less educated patients. Data on these factors is scarce. However, an Italian study by Gesuita et al included a pediatric population found that paternal low level of education was strongly associated with DKA recurrence in children.20 A study by Lyerla at al also mentioned that education level, economic stability and social support in the form of family, and neighbors are crucial to managing DM and have a strong influence in the better management of diabetic patients.10 This mirrors our study's conclusions.

An interesting finding in our study was the lack of difference in DKA recurrence between patients using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and those employing self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG). We also did not find any difference between those who did and did not achieve time in range. However, A prior local study showed that patients using free-style Libre have a reduced risk of DKA recurrence which is contrary to our study.14 Our results reflect real-world data regarding CGM use in the community; however, factors such as delayed sensor prescriptions, limited sensor usage, and noncompliance with healthcare provider guidance may have influenced our findings. Nonadherence to medications was a major modifiable risk factor found in the studies by Ehrman et al and Tadlo et al This is consistent with our study where nonadherence to medication was found to be a significant predictor of recurrence.11,13

The main limitation of this study is its retrospective design, which relied on electronic medical records with incomplete or limited information on several relevant factors. Additionally, it was a single-center study, although conducted at a large tertiary care hospital in a major metropolitan city that serves a high volume of patients from diverse regions and backgrounds. Further multicenter, prospective studies are needed to better understand the factors contributing to DKA recurrence.

CONCLUSION

Multiple factors have been identified in this study which were associated with recurrence of DKA including gender, age, duration of diabetes mellitus, and associated microvascular complications especially nephropathy. Addressing these factors and enhancing awareness among the community and healthcare professionals can be helpful to mitigate this potentially life-threatening complication of diabetes mellitus.

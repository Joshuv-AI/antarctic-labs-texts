A multicenter phase II study of eribulin for BRAF V600E mutant metastatic colorectal cancer: the BRAVERY study (EPOC1701)
Authors: T Masuishi, H Taniguchi, D Kotani, H Bando, Y Komatsu, E Shinozaki, Y Sunakawa, T Satoh, T Nishina, T Esaki, M Wakabayashi, S Nomura, K Takahashi, H Ono, N Hirano, N Fujishiro, N Fuse, A Sato, Y Sakamoto, N Kuramoto, I Miki, H Ichiki, Y Nakamura, G Sharma, S Skrzypczak, E Schultz, C Kingsford, A Ohtsu, T Yoshino
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBased on the reported antitumor effects of microtubule inhibitors in xenograft models of BRAF V600E mutant colorectal cancer, this multicenter phase II study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of eribulin in patients with BRAF V600E mutant metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC).Patients and methodsPatients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC who received at least one prior regimen were administered intravenous eribulin. The primary endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR). Biomarker analyses were carried out using tumor and serial blood samples. Patients with reduced tumor size and/or progression-free survival (PFS) of >6 months were classified into the good response group, and the remaining patients were classified into the poor response group.ResultsAmong the 27 patients enrolled, the ORR was 0% (95% confidence interval 0.0% to 12.8%), the disease control rate was 41%, the median PFS was 1.4 months, and the median survival time was 5.3 months (median follow-up, 5.3 months). Of 26 patients for whom tumors for RNA sequencing were available, all four patients in the good response group had the BRAF mutant (BM)2 subtype on gene expression analysis of BM subtype, whereas 22 patients in the poor response group had the BM1 (n = 8) or BM2 (n = 14) subtypes (P = 0.07). The proportion of patients with decreasing BRAF V600E relative clonality at the start of the second cycle was 75% and 11% in the good response and poor response groups, respectively (P = 0.02). Grade 3 and 4 adverse events included neutropenia (63%) and febrile neutropenia (22%).ConclusionsThis phase II study of eribulin for patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC did not meet the primary endpoint. Although BM2 on gene expression analysis of BM subtype may be a predictive marker of eribulin efficacy, further studies are required to validate this hypothesis.Key words: BRAF V600E, eribulin, gene expression analysis, BRAF mutant subtype, metastatic colorectal cancer

Introduction

BRAF V600E mutations are identified in 4%-11% of patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) and are associated with poor prognosis. The BRAF inhibitor encorafenib plus cetuximab is superior to the standard of care as a second- or third-line treatment of BRAF V600E mutant mCRC1; however, the prognosis in these patients remains poor. Accordingly, the development of effective novel drugs is vital for improving the prognosis of this patient population.

BRAF V600E mutant and wild-type CRC have different gene expression profiles. RANBP2 increased microtubule outgrowth from kinetochores, and its knockdown impaired proliferation in a BRAF V600E mutant CRC cell line but not in BRAF and KRAS wild-type cell lines, indicating that BRAF V600E mutant CRC may be vulnerable to inhibitors of mitosis.2 They also observed greater sensitivity to microtubule inhibitors in the BRAF V600E mutant CRC cell line than in the other cell lines, suggesting an antitumor effect of microtubule inhibitors in xenograft models of BRAF V600E mutant CRC.

Eribulin is a microtubule inhibitor that has been used worldwide for the treatment of metastatic breast cancer or soft tissue tumors. After analyzing The Cancer Genome Atlas data, we found that the expression levels of ATP-binding cassette subfamily B member 1 (ABCB1), which is also known as MDR1, in CRC cells was significantly lower in those with BRAF V600E mutation than in those with BRAF V600E wild type.3 Interestingly, ABCB1 has been shown to contribute to eribulin resistance. In our prior clinical experience of treating four patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC with eribulin, one patient had a confirmed partial response, with a 39% decrease in tumor size on computed tomography (CT) from baseline, and another patient had stable disease, with a 7% decrease in tumor size from baseline and a progression-free survival (PFS) of 6 months.3 Based on these observations, we developed a phase II study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of eribulin in patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase II study that evaluated the efficacy and safety of eribulin monotherapy in patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC. Patients were enrolled between May 2018 and September 2019 at eight sites in Japan. We divided the patients into two study cohorts. The primary analysis part included patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC detected by next-generation sequencing (NGS) or PCR-based assays of tumor tissues. Patients who had BRAF V600E mutation detected by plasma-based NGS assay biopsy test according to the methods described in the GOZILA study4,5 and were negative for BRAF V600E mutation in tumor tissues or had unanalyzable tumor samples were included in the liquid biopsy part.

This study was carried out in accordance with the guidelines for Good Clinical Practice of the International Council on Harmonization of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use and the ethical guidelines for medical and health research involving human subjects. The study protocol is listed in the University Hospital Medical Information Network registry (UMIN000031221 and UMIN000031552). Patients provided written informed consent before participation. The protocol was reviewed and approved by the institutional review boards of all participating sites.

Eligibility criteria

The main inclusion and exclusion criteria of the present study were the following: (i) histologically or cytologically confirmed colorectal adenocarcinoma, (ii) refractoriness or intolerance to at least one regimen containing fluoropyrimidine with or without irinotecan or oxaliplatin, (iii) BRAF V600E mutant colorectal cancer diagnosed by NGS or PCR-based assays of tumor or blood samples, (iv) regardless of microsatellite instability (MSI)/mismatch repair (MMR) status, (v) at least one measurable lesion according to RECIST version 1.1, (vi) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0 or 1, (vii) no previous treatment with eribulin, and (viii) provision of written informed consent.

Treatment schedule and evaluation

Eribulin at a dose of 1.4 mg/m2 was intravenously administered over 2-5 min on days 1 and 8 of a 3-week cycle. We evaluated treatment efficacy according to RECIST version 1.1 using CT scans every 6 weeks for the first three evaluations and every 8 weeks from the fourth evaluation onward. Treatment was continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent. Toxicity was assessed according to the National Cancer Institute Common Toxicity Criteria version 4.0. The data collection cut-off date was 18 September 2020. Independent data and safety monitoring was conducted with access to all data.

Outcome measures and statistical analyses

The primary endpoint was confirmed objective response rate (ORR) according to the assessment of the investigator in the primary analysis part. The secondary endpoints were investigator-assessed PFS and disease control rate (DCR), overall survival (OS), and incidence of adverse events (AEs) in the primary analysis part. Patients with a reduction in tumor size and/or PFS of >6 months were classified into the good response group, and the remaining patients were classified into the poor response group as post hoc analysis. PFS was defined as the period from study registration to disease progression or death from any cause. OS was defined as the period from study registration to death from any cause.

We set a threshold of 5% ORR based on previously reported ORR of 0%-11% after second-line and later treatments in patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC. For the primary analysis, we calculated a sample size of 27 patients in the full analysis set (FAS), which was defined as the population of subjects who received the investigational drug at least once among all the enrolled subjects who satisfied all eligibility criteria and did not fall into any of the exclusion criteria, using an optimal two-stage design6 with an ORR of 25% deemed promising and 5% as unacceptable (one-sided α, 0.05; β, 0.1); promising DCR of 70%; threshold DCR of 50%; and Yule’s correlation coefficient of 0.5 between ORR and DCR. Our planned sample size for the liquid biopsy part was a maximum of 15 patients in an exploratory manner. For the primary analysis cohort, we planned an interim analysis when the response evaluation for the first 12 enrolled patients in the FAS became available in the first stage. If fewer than six patients had complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease (SD) (i.e. the point estimate of investigator-assessed DCR was <50%), we considered discontinuing the study because of futility; otherwise, we planned to continue the study to the second stage. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. All analyses were conducted using SAS Release 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Registry data analysis

In this study, individual patient-level data from the SCRUM-Japan Registry7 were used as a hypothetical control arm. The SCRUM-Japan Registry is a longitudinal observational study that collects real-world data on patients with advanced solid tumors harboring rare alterations identified during the SCRUM-Japan project. Radiographic imaging was prospectively carried out every 6-10 weeks according to the study protocol. The hypothetical control arm comprised patients who were not enrolled in the BRAVERY study and met the following criteria: (i) BRAF V600E mutant mCRC and (ii) refractory or intolerant to fluoropyrimidine, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin.

Translational research

Cancer-associated genomic alterations were analyzed in DNA extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) pretreatment tumor tissue specimens using the Oncomine Comprehensive Assay, which is an NGS-based, 161-gene, tissue-based panel. Plasma samples were analyzed using Guardant360, which is an NGS-based 74-gene circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) assay.8 We collected serial blood samples at three time points: before the initiation of treatment, on the first day of cycle 2, and after discontinuation of the protocol-specified treatment.

Whole transcriptome RNA sequencing of FFPE pretreatment tumor samples was carried out using NovaSeq 6000. Molecular features were extracted using the TxomeAI platform (Ocean Genomics Inc., Pittsburgh, PA), which included transcript- and gene-level expression quantification, expression-based clustering, and structural variant calling. Gene expression analysis was carried out according to the BRAF mutant (BM) subtype and consensus molecular subtype (CMS) classification using models developed by Barras et al.9 and Guinney et al.,10 respectively. DESeq2 was used to perform differential gene expression analysis between the good response and the poor response groups with a false discovery rate-adjusted P value of <0.05 using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Biomarker analyses were carried out in the good and poor response groups. The BM subtype, CMS subtype, and proportion of acquired gene alterations in ctDNA were compared between the two groups using Fisher’s exact test. The allelic frequency of BRAF V600E mutation in ctDNA and the proportion of patients with decreasing BRAF V600E relative clonality in ctDNA were compared between the two groups using Wilcoxon signed rank test.

Results

Patient characteristics in the primary analysis part

In the first stage of the primary analysis cohort, 6 of the first 12 enrolled patients had SD (50%). Therefore, the study was continued and patients were enrolled into the second stage. A total of 27 patients were enrolled in the primary analysis part and three patients were enrolled in the liquid biopsy part. All 30 patients who received at least one dose of the treatment protocol were included in the FAS and safety population (Figure 1).

The characteristics of the patients in the primary analysis part are summarized in Table 1. Briefly, 11 patients (40.7%) had an ECOG PS of 1; 11 patients (40.7%) had right-sided tumors (cecum, ascending colon, or transverse colon); and 22 patients (81.5%) received two or more previous treatment regimens. Only three patients (11.1%) had previously received a BRAF inhibitor in combination with an anti-epidermal growth factor receptor (anti-EGFR) antibody, because this combination treatment has not been approved. No patients had tumors with MSI-high.

Treatment exposure in the primary analysis part

Eribulin was administered at a median number of treatment cycles of 2 (range 1-12) and a median relative dose intensity of 76.6% (range 36.7%-100%). Dose modifications and interruptions were required in 16 (59%) and 20 (74%) patients, respectively. The median follow-up duration was 5.3 months (range 1.1-21.7 months). The median time to treatment failure was 43 days (range 18-295 days). All patients had discontinued eribulin at the time of final analysis because of disease progression. Subsequent chemotherapy was administered in 19 patients (70%) and included trifluridine/tipiracil in 8 patients, regorafenib in 5 patients, and an anti-EGFR antibody in 6 patients. No patients received a BRAF inhibitor.

Efficacy in the primary analysis part

Among the 27 patients in the final analysis, the ORR was 0% [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.0% to 12.8%]. Of these patients, 11 had stable disease with a DCR of 40.7% (95% CI 22.4% to 61.2%) and 3 (11%) had tumor shrinkage from baseline (Figure 1). The median PFS and OS were 1.4 months (95% CI 1.2-2.8 months) and 5.3 months (95% CI 3.6-8.9 months), respectively (Figure 2). Two patients had a PFS of ≥6 months (Figure 3). On subgroup analysis, previous treatment with a BRAF inhibitor in combination with an anti-EGFR antibody had no effect on the treatment outcomes [previous treatment versus no previous treatment: DCR 33% versus 42%; median PFS 1.5 months versus 1.4 months, hazard ratio (HR) 0.42; median OS 3.6 months versus 5.2 months, HR 1.02].

Adverse events

Among the 27 patients in the safety population, common grade 3 or 4 AEs included neutropenia, leukopenia, febrile neutropenia, and anorexia (Table 2). There were no treatment-related deaths during the study period.

Comparison of treatment efficacy with patients from the SCRUM-Japan Registry

The characteristics of the 12 eligible patients from the SCRUM-Japan Registry are summarized in Supplementary Table S1 available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105839. The treatment regimens were irinotecan-based with or without anti-EGFR antibodies or antiangiogenic agents in seven patients, trifluridine/tipiracil with or without bevacizumab in three patients, and regorafenib in two patients. The DCR was similar between the BRAVERY and registry cohorts (40.7% versus 41.7%, respectively; odds ratio 0.96, P = 0.96). No patients in either cohort achieved CR or PR. The PFS and OS in the BRAVERY cohort were similar to or slightly inferior to those in the registry cohort (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105839).

Biomarker analysis of eribulin efficacy

Of the 27 patients included in the primary analysis, four were in the good response group and 23 were in the poor response group. Of 27 patients, 26 tumor samples with 4 samples for the good response group and 22 from the poor response group were available for RNA sequencing and analysis. On gene expression analysis, all patients in the good response group had the BM2 subtype; of the 22 patients in the poor response group, 8 had the BM1 subtype and 14 had the BM2 subtype (P = 0.07; Figure 1). For the CMS, all patients in the good response group were classified as CMS4; among the 22 patients in the poor response group, the classification was CMS1 in 1, CMS2 in 1, and CMS4 in 20 (Figure 4). Differential gene expression analysis detected 52 differentially expressed genes with a false discovery rate-adjusted P value of <0.05. Of these genes, the top five highly expressed genes with the lowest P values in both groups are listed in Supplementary Tables S2 and S3 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105839).

On DNA sequencing of 23 tumor samples, the commonly identified genetic abnormalities included TP53 mutations (65%), RNF43 mutations (22%), PIK3CA mutations (22%), and RSPO2 or RSPO3 fusions (17%). No gene alterations were significantly associated with the classification into the good response or poor response groups (Figure 4).

On ctDNA analysis, BRAF V600E mutation was detected in all 25 patients at baseline. The allelic frequency of the BRAF V600E mutation at baseline was 5.3% (range 0.2%-26.1%) in the good response group and 3.1% (range 0.09%-42%) in the poor response group (P = 0.91). The proportion of patients with decreasing BRAF V600E relative clonality at the start of the second cycle was 75% in the good response group and 11% in the poor response group (P = 0.02). Acquired gene alterations at the start of the second cycle or disease progression were detected in one patient (25%) in the good response group and in nine patients (50%) in the poor response group (P = 0.59; Figure 4 and Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105839).

Results from the liquid biopsy part

Three patients (aged 65, 65, and 81 years) were enrolled based on detection of BRAF V600E mutation in plasma but not in tissue. All patients were men and had an ECOG PS of 0; left-sided tumors in the rectum (n = 2) and sigmoid colon (n = 1); and previous exposure to an anti-EGFR antibody. None achieved disease control at the first CT evaluation, with PFS of 1.4, 1.5, and 1.3 months, respectively, and OS of 5.6, 4.2, and 5.2 months, respectively.

Discussion

This phase II study demonstrated that eribulin monotherapy as second-line or later treatment achieved a reduction in tumor size but had limited antitumor activity in patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC and did not achieve its primary endpoint. Cremolini et al. reported that vinorelbine therapy in patients with BRAF V600E-mutant mCRC resulted in ORR of 0%, DCR of 5%, median PFS of 1.0 month, and median OS of 2.1 months.11 These studies in the clinical setting indicated that microtubule inhibitors were not effective for patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC. Although using a BRAF V600E mutant CRC cell line, Towle et al. reported greater growth inhibition by eribulin than by other microtubule inhibitors12; unfortunately, preclinical data were not replicated in clinical trial data. In the subgroup analysis of the RAISE and VELOUR studies, median PFS for fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin (folinic acid), and irinotecan (FOLFIRI) plus ramucirumab and aflibercept as second-line treatment was 2.7 months and 2.2 months, respectively, and median OS was 4.2 months and 5.5 months, respectively, for patients with BRAF V600E mutation.13,14 Although the OS in the present study was similar to that in the RAISE and VELOUR studies, the PFS was slightly inferior to that in these studies.

In this study, gene expression analysis demonstrated that all patients who had reduced tumor size and/or PFS of >6 months had the BM2 subtype. BRAF V600E mutant mCRC is known to have a varied prognosis. Barras et al. suggested that patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC should be classified into two groups using gene expression analysis, with the BM1 subtype characterized by KRAS/AKT pathway activation, mTOR/4EBP deregulation, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and the BM2 subtype characterized by cell cycle deregulation.9 In addition, BM1 subtype was significantly associated with the better outcome of combination dabrafenib, trametinib, and panitumumab therapy.15 In the present study, eribulin had greater efficacy in patients with the BM2 subtype than in those with the BM1 subtype. This finding may be attributed to inhibition of the G2-M cell cycle by eribulin. However, some patients with the BM2 subtype did not achieve tumor response after eribulin treatment and this analysis had a small sample size. This finding requires validation by further studies using genomic analyses.

In the BEACON CRC study, 86% of patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC were classified as CMS1 (49%) or CMS4 (37%)16; in the present study, almost all patients were classified as CMS4. This finding may be attributed to the reportedly worse prognosis of patients with CMS1 than of patients with CMS4. Furthermore, exposure to previous treatments was greater in this present cohort than in the BEACON CRC cohort. In the BEACON CRC study, because PFS and OS were similar between the doublet and triplet regimens, the doublet regimen was approved worldwide and the triplet regimen was also approved in Japan. On the other hand, efficacy was higher with the triplet regimen than with the doublet regimen in patients with CMS4. Although CMS1 was reported to elevate expression protein involved in cell cycle,10 CMS was not a predictor of response to eribulin treatment in the present study.

In the present study, the allelic frequency of the BRAF V600E mutation at baseline was not predictive marker of eribulin efficacy. However, the allelic frequency of the BRAF V600E mutation in both droplet digital PCR and whole-exome sequencing analyses was shown to be predictive marker of efficacy of BRAF inhibitor combination therapy.16,17 We attributed the discrepancy in results to differences in the efficacy of study drugs. Although decreasing BRAF V600E relative clonality was suggested to be predictor of response to eribulin treatment, further studies are needed to validate this result due to the small sample size of this analysis.

In all three patients included in the liquid biopsy part, BRAF V600E mutation was detected not in archival tumor tissues but in liquid biopsy samples. The concordance rate for BRAF V600E mutation between tumor tissue and ctDNA was reported to be high at 97%-100%.18 Accordingly, all three patients in the liquid biopsy part presumably acquired BRAF V600E mutations as a result of exposure to anti-EGFR antibody drugs. Consistent with the primary analysis findings, no patients in the liquid biopsy part achieved tumor response to eribulin. Development of BRAF inhibitors and other therapies targeting BRAF mutations as acquired resistance to anti-EGFR therapy is expected.

This study had several limitations. First was the single-arm, phase II design with a small sample size and the inclusion of Japanese patients only. Accordingly, the results of efficacy and safety analyses should be considered preliminary. Secondly, only three patients previously received the current standard of care (i.e. a BRAF inhibitor in combination with an anti-EGFR antibody). Although this study suggested that there was no association between prior use of a BRAF inhibitor and eribulin efficacy, studies with larger sample size are required to validate this finding. Thirdly, tumor samples were obtained from archival tissues rather than from tissues collected immediately before study participation. We evaluated BM subtype as a candidate biomarker of eribulin efficacy, but further studies using tissues collected immediately before treatment initiation are required to validate our results.

Conclusion

This phase II study of eribulin for patients with BRAF V600E mutant mCRC did not meet the primary endpoint. Although BM2 on gene expression analysis of BM subtype may be a predictive marker of eribulin efficacy, further studies are required to validate this hypothesis.

Protocol to measure protein half-life in cell culture using heavy water
Authors: Lorena Alamillo, Alexander Black, Matthew C Juber, Dominic CM Ng, Maggie PY Lam, Edward Lau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryProtein turnover, comprising the continued synthesis and clearance of proteins, is required for protein homeostasis and cell survival. Here, we present a protocol to measure protein turnover half-life in cultured cells using D2O labeling. We describe steps to determine the extent of deuterium exchange in amino acids, perform dynamic labeling, and collect protein samples, followed by mass spectrometry and kinetic analysis. The protocol is suitable for measuring protein half-life under steady-state conditions and perturbations in multiple cell types and culture media.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Alamillo et al.1Subject areas: Cell culture, Protein Biochemistry, Proteomics, Stem Cells, Mass Spectrometry

Before you begin

The protocol below describes the use of D2O labeling to calculate protein half-life in human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs). However, we have also successfully used the protocol in hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, transformed AC16 cells, and primary human fibroblasts.

Protein turnover rates, and the associated half-life, are typically measured in cultured cells using one of two methods. In the first, cycloheximide is used to inhibit new protein synthesis, after which the decay of the existing protein pool may be traced over time, using either immunoblots or mass spectrometry.2,3 In the second, via dynamic/pulsed stable isotope labeling of amino acids in cell culture (dynamic SILAC/pulsed SILAC), a stable isotope labeled essential amino acid, such as lysine, is introduced to a lysine-depleted medium. The rate of incorporation of heavy labeled lysine over time is then measured using mass spectrometry.4,5,6 Despite their wide adoption, these methods have limitations. For instance, cycloheximide inhibition of synthesis can cause cell stress and can suppress protein degradation and confound readout7; whereas the use of dynamic SILAC requires the experimenter to alter the basal medium of the cell culture to replace the supplemented amino acids.8

D2O labeling provides a convenient alternative to label newly synthesized proteins.9,10 In D2O labeling, the introduction of deuterium atoms into newly synthesized protein leads to telltale shifts in mass isotopomer ratios of peptides in mass spectrometry,11 which are a function of the excess deuterium enrichment, the fraction of newly synthesized proteins at the time of measurement, and the number of deuterium-labelable hydrogen atoms in a peptide sequence (Figure 1). Note that the hydrogen atoms under consideration here are primarily stable C–H groups that do not spontaneously back-exchange with the solvent, which is in contrast to the labile N–H groups in hydrogen-deuterium exchange (HDX) experiments. The number of labelable sites in a peptide (Spep) can be deconvoluted into the sum of individual labelable sites (Saa) in the amino acid residues that compose the peptide. In cell culture, this parameter depends on cellular biochemistry and medium composition.

Determined values for Saa for AC16, hiPSC, and hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes cultured in DMEM/F12+FBS, mTeSR Plus, and RPMI1640+B27 are provided in Alamillo et al.1 We find similar number of labelable sites in these cells, with notable labeling in alanine, aspartic acid, glutamic acid, proline, and other amino acids. Values from AC16 are used as the default under the in vitro experiment analysis option in the Riana software, as these Saa values correlated well with Saa values determined for hiPSCs and hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes with R2 over 0.9 when plotted against each other. Riana (Relative Isotope Abundance Analyzer) is a standalone Python software used to integrate mass isotopomer signals and calculate mass isotopomer distributions from mass spectrometry spectra and spectral ID files.12,13 The software uses Saa values to calculate Spep which determines the labeling plateau and ultimately affects protein turnover calulcations. If a different cell line or different culture condition (e.g., under amino acid starvation) is studied, it may be required to determine the cell-specific deuterium-incorporation sites to accurately calculate protein turnover rates. This protocol provides a general method to use multiple linear regression to learn Saa of each proteinogenic amino acids in a particular cell type, provided the cells can be passaged in ≥10 doublings to ensure complete turnover of all cellular proteins under the D2O enriched medium.

The protocol will passage cells in 6% D2O over multiple doublings to achieve full incorporation of the proportion of deuterium in the media. These cells are diluted with unlabeled cells at fixed amounts to create a set of calibration standards. The calibration standards are used to calculate number of accessible labeling sites of Spep from a linear fit model against the known proportion of fully labeled cells. The number of accessible labeling sites in each amino (Saa) acid can then be predicted using either a linear regression model or another machine learning strategy which functions by examining the number of exchangeable hydrogens on a peptide. The observation of individual amino acids being amenable to D2O labeling in cell culture can be confirmed using 16 to 18 h acid hydrolysis and direct-infusion high-resolution mass spectrometry analysis of the unlabeled (0% D2O) and fully labeled (6% D2O) cell lysates. The Saa values only need to be determined once for each cell type/culture condition. Using the learned Saa values, the protocol can then use dynamic D2O in multiple time points followed by mass spectrometry to measure protein half-life on a large scale.

Innovation

Dynamic SILAC is commonly utilized to examine protein turnover in cell culture. However, its use requires either commercially available or custom formulated SILAC media depleted of the amino acid label (e.g., lysine-free media). In contrast, this protocol is usable in virtually any culture media including proprietary commercial formulation whose compositions are not disclosed, provided the cells can tolerate a minor (∼6%) dilution of the medium. Previous applications of D2O labeling to determine protein turnover rates in cell culture were largely confined to the analysis of total protein-bound alanine14,15 or single proteins of interest.16,17 To derive kinetic information for many proteins simultaneously, the number of deuterium labeling sites in each peptide is required. Deuterium labeling sites have previously been examined for mammalian models but not thoroughly investigated for cell culture models. Prior calculations of turnover typically in cell culture largely assumed identical labelable atoms as in animal studies, namely four hydrogen atoms in alanine (at the alpha carbon C–H and the side chain CH3). This protocol introduces a method for proteome-scale protein turnover rate calculations of virtually any protein, by empirically determining the amino acid specific labelable sites (Saa) of each of 20 proteinogenic amino acids.

Prepare deuterium calibration standard cells to determine labelable sites

Timing: 2–3 weeks

This section of the protocol concerns culturing of cells in D2O for up to 10 doublings until all proteins are fully labeled. The expected outcome is fully labeled cell lysate that can be diluted in proportion with unlabeled cell lysate, allowing a calibration standard to be established for learning cell-specific amino acid labeling sites (Saa). It may be skipped if the labeling behavior of a cell type/sample is already known, or if preset values are to be used.

Note that throughout the protocol, standard proteomics reagents including LC-MS grade solvent is used. Acetonitrile is flammable. Observe safety precautions including the use of personal protective equipment (PPE) and safe chemical handling and disposal practice.

Determine Spep and Saa from calibration standard cells

Timing: 1 day

This section of the protocol uses the calibration standard cell experiments to determine Spep then Saa from fully labeled and fully unlabeled samples. The calibration standard samples are used to find peptides whose m0/mA values correlate tightly (R2 > 0.9) with the proportion of sample derived from D2O labeled cells. These well-fitted peptides are used to calculate peptide labeling site Spep from the natural isotopic distribugion, fully-labeled isotopic distribution, and the relative abundance of D2O in the experiment. The Spep and amino acid composition of each peptide is then used to find the amino acid specific labeling sites Saa. It may be skipped if the labeling behavior of a cell type/sample is already known, or if preset values are to be used.

The D1-amino acid signals are observed and distinguished from 13C1-amino acid signals in high-resolution mass spectrometry following the release of protein-bound amino acids. As with the Saa determination above, it may be skipped if the labeling behavior of a cell type/sample is already known, or if preset values are to be used.

Dynamic D2O labeling in cell culture and mass spectrometry analysis

Timing: 1–1.5 weeks

This section of the protocol performs in vitro D2O labeling to provide a time series with increasing incorporation of D2O in hiPSCs or the cell line of interest. During this step cells will be grown and exposed to D2O before harvesting at pre-determined time points. Samples will be processed via a typical proteomics workflow and then run on an LC-MS system. Upon completion, LC-MS data will be ready for analysis to determine global protein turnover kinetics.

Note: The selection of timepoints will have to be optimized for each cell line for proper kinetic curves. It is recommended for fast growing cells such as proliferating hiPSC or transformed cell lines that the time points should span 24 h, in line with the doubling time of the cells.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Mass spectrometry data is searched using Comet and post processed using Percolator. Peptide identifications are filtered to yield a false discovery rate of less than 1% based on the Percolator score q-value. A protein-level FDR filter may additionally be used. Riana v.0.8.3 is used to extract the intensities of the m0, m1, m2, m3, m4, and m5 isotopomers for each peptide-charge combination. Protein turnover calculations are based on peptides identified in at least four samples and having a peptide level R2 of ≥ 0.8.

Evaluation of data quality in protein half-life studies is hampered by the paucity of ground truth information for protein turnover in a particular cell type or system, as the magnitude of turnover rates is affected by proliferation rates and other culture conditions. A useful metric for evaluating data quality to consider the intra-protein variance of peptides.13 This follows the assumption that as the whole polypeptide chain is synthesized and degraded together, independent measurements of turnover rates from different peptide sequences within the protein should yield the same turnover rates. It should be noted however that peptides from the same proteins may have physiologically different turnover rates owing to post-translational modifications, processing, or alternative isoforms. Nevertheless, if the measurements are precise and stringent filtering is used, peptides originating from the same protein as well as peptides measured across independent time series should yield largely comparable turnover rates across slow and fast turnover proteins (Figure 6), with the dataset-median of intra-protein peptide median absolute deviations being 20%–30%.13 To convert the peptide-level protein turnover rates/half-life to protein-level summary, different methods may be used including taking a median or harmonic mean of turnover rates of qualifying peptides,13 or perform custom re-fitting of all data points grouped under a protein (Figure 6). In the latter method, a protein may be considered well-fitted if the protein level R2 is ≥ 0.8.

Limitations

This study uses D2O to examine protein turnover and, while no effects were seen on proliferation at the dosage used (6%), further studies would address whether D2O impacts cellular phenotypes. Furthermore, protein turnover calculations assume immediate and consistent incorporation of D2O, however, the incorporation of the deuterium may be delayed given the abundance of unlabeled amino acids in the media. The calculations also assume consistent protein abundance, therefore, treatments which alter protein abundance would interfere with our ability to purely measure changes in turnover. Correcting is limitation is still an active area of research.

The study may also be limited by the chosen cell type and equipment. The calibration experiments may not be feasible in cell types that grow too slowly or cannot be excessively passaged as this would interfere with their ability to fully incorporate deuterium. The chosen mass spectrometer must be capable of sufficient spectral resolution to measure the relative abundance between isotopomers. We recommend greater than 30,000 at 200 m/z.

Problem 1

Potential solution

•Deuterium is toxic in concentrations at ∼20% or greater concentration. Prior work has cultured cells in up to 15% D2O but different cell types may show different sensitivity. We recommend avoiding the use of deuterium concentrations greater than 10% for cell culture, and recommend 6% (v/v) for hiPSC cell culture.

Problem 4

Proteins have insufficient/excessive labels for calculation of turnover rates (Step 27).

Potential solution

•The labeling time points should be chosen to match the cell types used in the study and their proliferation rates such that the dynamic phase of label incorporation is captured. If only very short time points were chosen, the cells might not have accumulated sufficient label for turnover calculation.

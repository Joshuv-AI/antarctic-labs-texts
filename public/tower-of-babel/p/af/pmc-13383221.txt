Comparative evaluation of intralesional platelet-rich plasma and triamcinolone in management of oral submucous fibrosis: A prospective clinical study
Authors: Vipul Ughade, Sanyog Pathak, Sumit Asrani, Rajesh Dhirawani, Harshit Shrivastava
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOral Submucous Fibrosis (OSMF) is a chronic, progressive, potentially malignant disorder characterized by fibrosis of the oral mucosa, resulting in restricted mouth opening, burning sensation, mucosal blanching, and reduced cheek flexibility. Effective management remains challenging, and newer regenerative therapies such as platelet-rich plasma (PRP) have shown promising results.AimTo compare the efficacy of intralesional PRP and triamcinolone acetonide in improving mouth opening, burning sensation, cheek flexibility, and mucosal colour in patients with OSMF.Materials and methodsThis prospective comparative clinical study included 30 patients with OSMF (Group I–III according to the Ranganathan classification). Group I received intralesional PRP prepared using a standardized double-spin centrifugation technique, while Group II received intralesional triamcinolone acetonide. Six weekly injections were administered along with adjunct physiotherapy. Clinical parameters were assessed at baseline, 1 week, 6 weeks, and 3 months. PRP showed significantly greater reduction in burning sensation at 3 months (p < 0.001) and superior improvement in mucosal colour, whereas triamcinolone demonstrated greater improvement in cheek flexibility (p < 0.05).ConclusionBoth PRP and triamcinolone acetonide were effective in the management of OSMF. Introduction

Oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF) is a chronic, progressive, potentially malignant disorder characterized by fibrosis of the oral mucosa, resulting in restricted mouth opening, burning sensation, mucosal blanching, and impaired oral function. The condition is highly prevalent in South Asian countries and is strongly associated with areca nut consumption. Owing to its functional morbidity and risk of malignant transformation, early diagnosis and management are essential.1,2

Management of OSMF includes conservative and surgical approaches aimed at reducing symptoms and improving oral function. Intralesional triamcinolone acetonide is widely used because of its anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic properties; however, its effects may be temporary and prolonged use may be associated with adverse effects.3 Platelet-rich plasma (PRP), an autologous concentrate rich in growth factors, has emerged as a promising alternative because of its regenerative potential, ability to promote angiogenesis, and favourable safety profile.4

Although previous studies have compared PRP and triamcinolone in OSMF management, evidence regarding their relative effects on multiple clinical outcomes remains limited. Additional prospective studies are needed to evaluate these treatment modalities using a comprehensive outcome assessment.

We hypothesized that both therapies would improve clinical outcomes in OSMF, but that differences may exist in their effects on specific parameters. Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to compare changes in mouth opening and burning sensation following intralesional PRP and triamcinolone therapy. Secondary objectives were to compare changes in cheek flexibility and mucosal colour between the two treatment groups over a 3-month follow-up period.

Materials and Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This prospective comparative clinical study was conducted in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Hitkarini Dental College and Hospital, Jabalpur. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Hitkarini Dental College and Hospital. The trial was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry–India (CTRI/2026/04/109148). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment, and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was calculated using data from Hydri et al. Assuming an expected mean difference of 0.8 cm in mouth opening between groups, a pooled standard deviation of 0.7 cm, 80% power, and α = 0.05, a minimum of 12 participants per group was required. To compensate for possible attrition, 15 participants were enrolled in each group.

Participant selection

Thirty patients clinically diagnosed with oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF) (Group I–III according to Ranganathan classification) were included in the study. Patients presenting with reduced mouth opening and burning sensation who were willing to participate were enrolled. Pregnant and lactating women and patients with oral malignancy, temporomandibular joint disorders, bleeding disorders, systemic illnesses affecting healing, or other medically compromising conditions were excluded.

Randomization and allocation concealment

Participants were randomly assigned to either the PRP group or the Triamcinolone group using a random number table-generated allocation sequence. Allocation concealment was achieved using sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. Owing to the nature of the interventions, blinding was not feasible, and the study was conducted as an open-label trial. Each group comprised 15 patients. Group I received intralesional platelet-rich plasma (PRP), and Group II received intralesional triamcinolone acetonide (40 mg/mL).

PRP preparation protocol

Autologous platelet-rich plasma (PRP) was prepared under aseptic conditions using a standardized double-spin centrifugation technique.5 PRP was prepared using a Remi R-8C laboratory centrifuge with a rotor radius of 14.4 cm. Approximately 10 mL of venous blood was collected from the antecubital vein into sterile tubes containing acid citrate dextrose as an anticoagulant. The sample was centrifuged at 161 ×g for 12 min, followed by 645 ×g for 5 min. The platelet pellet was resuspended in 2–4 mL of plasma to obtain PRP.

Immediately before administration, PRP was activated using calcium gluconate and approximately 1 mL of activated PRP was injected intralesionally using a 30-gauge insulin syringe at multiple sites along the palpable fibrotic bands once weekly for six consecutive weeks.

Intervention protocol

Intralesional injections were administered once weekly for six weeks in both groups. Patients in Group I received PRP injections, whereas patients in Group II received 1 mL triamcinolone acetonide (40 mg/mL). The injection technique, number of injection sites, and treatment schedule were standardized between groups.

All participants were instructed to perform mouth-opening exercises three times daily for approximately 10 min per session and were counselled regarding cessation of areca nut and tobacco habits at every visit. Compliance was assessed through self-reporting during follow-up visits.

Outcome measures

Clinical outcomes were assessed at baseline, 1 week, 6 weeks, and 3 months. The primary outcome measure was change in maximum interincisal mouth opening. Secondary outcome measures included burning sensation, cheek flexibility, and mucosal colour. Clinical outcome measurements were performed by co-investigators who were not involved in administering the intralesional injections, thereby ensuring independence between treatment delivery and outcome assessment.

Mouth opening

Maximum interincisal mouth opening was measured using a calibrated Vernier caliper. The distance between the mesioincisal edges of the maxillary and mandibular central incisors was recorded in millimetres. Three readings were obtained and the mean value was used for analysis.

Burning sensation

Burning sensation was assessed using a visual analogue scale (VAS) ranging from 0 (no burning sensation) to 10 (severe burning sensation).

Cheek flexibility

Cheek flexibility was measured using a standardized technique. A point was marked vertically downward from the outer canthus of the eye and another point at the oral commissure on the affected side. The distance between these points was measured while the patient inflated the cheeks. The same side was assessed at all follow-up visits, and all measurements were performed by the same examiner throughout the study.6

Mucosal colour

Mucosal colour was evaluated using a study-specific four-point blanching score developed by the investigators for clinical assessment of mucosal changes. The scoring criteria were: Score 0 – normal mucosa; Score 1 – mild blanching; Score 2 – moderate blanching; and Score 3 – severe blanching. All assessments were performed by the same examiner throughout the study.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables showing normal distribution (mouth opening, burning sensation, and cheek flexibility) were analysed using two-way mixed ANOVA to assess the effects of treatment group and time. For repeated-measures analyses, sphericity was evaluated using Mauchly's test, and where violated, the Greenhouse–Geisser correction was applied. Post-hoc pairwise comparisons were performed using Bonferroni adjustment. Ordinal data (mucosal colour scores) were analysed using the Friedman test for intra-group comparisons and the Mann–Whitney U test for inter-group comparisons. Fisher's exact test was used for categorical variables. No participant was lost to follow-up; therefore, missing-data imputation was not required. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and observations

Demographic characteristics

A total of 30 patients with clinically diagnosed oral submucous fibrosis were included in the study and equally distributed into two groups, with 15 patients in the PRP group and 15 patients in the triamcinolone group. The overall mean age of the study population was 40.13 ± 11.61 years, with age ranging from 18 to 56 years. The PRP group had a mean age of 39.40 ± 12.24 years, while the triamcinolone group had a mean age of 40.87 ± 11.33 years. There was no statistically significant difference in age between the two groups (p = 0.736).

Among the study participants, 27 (90%) were males and 3 (10%) were females. The PRP group consisted of 13 males (86.67%) and 2 females (13.33%), whereas the triamcinolone group included 14 males (93.33%) and 1 female (6.67%). Gender distribution between the groups was statistically comparable (p = 1.000).

Discussion

The present study compared intralesional PRP and triamcinolone acetonide in improving mouth opening, burning sensation, cheek flexibility, and mucosal colour in patients with OSMF.

The mean age of participants was 40.13 ± 11.61 years with male predominance (90%). The age and gender distribution were comparable between groups.1,2

Both PRP and triamcinolone groups demonstrated significant improvement in mouth opening at all follow-up intervals. Maximum improvement occurred within the first six weeks, followed by a plateau phase at 3 months. However, intergroup comparison showed no statistically significant difference between the two treatment modalities. Similar observations were reported by Chandrakapure et al. and Sharma et al., who found significant improvement in mouth opening with both PRP and steroid therapy.4,6 The improvement in mouth opening following PRP may be attributed to enhanced angiogenesis, fibroblast modulation, and collagen remodeling, whereas triamcinolone improves mouth opening by reducing inflammation and inhibiting fibroblast proliferation.3

Burning sensation showed progressive reduction in both groups; however, PRP demonstrated significantly greater reduction at 3 months follow-up. The greater reduction in burning sensation observed in the PRP group may be related to the regenerative properties of platelet-rich plasma.5 Hydri et al. and Singh et al. also reported greater symptomatic relief following PRP therapy.7,8 Similar beneficial effects of PRP in the management of oral submucous fibrosis have also been reported in other clinical studies.9,10

Cheek flexibility improved significantly in both groups; however, triamcinolone demonstrated greater improvement at 6 weeks and 3 months. This finding may be attributable to the anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects of corticosteroids. Previous studies have similarly reported improved mucosal elasticity and functional outcomes following steroid therapy.11,12

Assessment of mucosal colour revealed significant improvement in both groups, with PRP demonstrating superior improvement at 6 weeks and 3 months. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies evaluating PRP in OSMF.6,13 The present findings support the regenerative effect of PRP in improving mucosal health.

Overall, both treatment modalities demonstrated significant clinical improvement in patients with OSMF. PRP showed superior improvement in burning sensation and mucosal colour, whereas triamcinolone demonstrated greater improvement in cheek flexibility. No significant difference in mouth opening was observed between the groups. Similar comparative outcomes have been reported previously.4,14

The present study has several strengths. A comprehensive outcome assessment including mouth opening, burning sensation, cheek flexibility, and mucosal colour was performed. Standardized measurement techniques and treatment protocols were used throughout the study, improving methodological consistency.

The present study has several limitations. The study was conducted at a single institution with a relatively small sample size, which may limit generalizability. Follow-up was limited to three months; therefore, long-term treatment outcomes, recurrence rates, and disease progression could not be evaluated. Biological markers and PRP characterization were not evaluated. Disease severity and habit-related variables were not analysed separately between groups. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted within the context of a short-term prospective clinical study.

Further multicentric studies with larger samples and longer follow-up are recommended.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this prospective single-centre study, both intralesional PRP and triamcinolone acetonide demonstrated improvement in clinical parameters of Oral Submucous Fibrosis over a 3-month follow-up period. PRP was associated with greater improvement in burning sensation and mucosal colour, whereas triamcinolone demonstrated greater improvement in cheek flexibility. No significant difference in mouth opening was observed between the groups. These findings should be considered preliminary, and larger multicentre studies with longer follow-up are required to confirm the relative benefits of these treatment modalities.

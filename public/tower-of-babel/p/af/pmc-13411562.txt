Assessment of Health and Wellness Practices among the Working Women in Western India
Authors: Prachi D. Sondankar, Khyati Kalra, Mayavati N. Nannaware, Parvinder S. Chawla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Context:In India, women bear a multitude of obligations, including caring for dependents, raising children, and managing the home, all while compromising their own health. Numerous studies have demonstrated that women are experiencing higher levels of stress.Aims and Objectives:(1) To assess health- and wellness-related practices among working women in India. (2) To determine demographic and work-related factors associated with wellness among the working women.Settings and Design:It was an online cross-sectional study conducted by investigators at a tertiary care hospital.Subjects and Methods:Snowball sampling method was used in the study to get the responses from the participants. Total 441 responses were received, which were analyzed.Statistical Analysis Used:Descriptive statistics was calculated based on the type of data. The Chi-square test of significance was used to show the association.Results:The mean age of the participant was 39.61 years with standard deviation ± 7.89 years. Most of the participants were married, had higher education, and belonged to a nuclear family. They were working in different occupational sectors. Though the majority, i.e., 70.75% of the women, had good health and wellness score, a notable portion of women showed poor score, which had significant association with younger age and long job hours.Conclusion:The findings indicate that younger age and long working hours are associated with poor scores. While a majority of women in this study report good health scores and satisfactory work–life balance, the significant percentage experiencing poor scores highlights critical areas for improvement.

INTRODUCTION

Women constitute about half of the human capital in India and about one-third of the total workforce. The ways in which the economy utilizes their potential is a matter of immense economic as well as social value for the country. Women’s experience in a certain phase in life varies widely from that of men. The challenges she faces are of multi-dimensional nature. Assessing the status of women in India does not have a simplistic analysis with clearly set conclusions.[1] Not unexpectedly, despite the overall increase in women’s workforce participation, the burden of care work and domestic work on women has not decreased. Women in India spend an average of 7.2 h on unpaid domestic work compared to 2.8 h spent by men, which has a direct bearing on their ability to participate in paid work. Women’s entry into the workforce needs to be supported with greater investment in care infrastructure and services, along with the reduction and redistribution of care work at home.[2]

Multiple role-playing has been found to have both positive and negative effects on the mental health and well-being of professional women.[3] Although economic development requires an integration of the male and female labor force, the dual-career families have witnessed deleterious effects on the life satisfaction of women and an enormous rise in their levels of stress.[4] Women’s health and working life constitute a complex connection with the potential for the development of new knowledge, and the scope of this connection should be considered from a broader perspective of living conditions and public health.[5] The Global Wellness Institute defines wellness as the active pursuit of activities, choices, and lifestyles that lead to a state of holistic health. Wellness is multidimensional and consists of physical, mental, emotional, and social dimensions.[6]

Different studies around the world have shown that there is complex relationship among women’s health and their work–life balance.[578910] There is lot of research focusing on the stress among working women and work–life balance;[489] however, none of the research studies have explored wellness practices among working women of India. So, the present study was planned to assess health- and wellness-related practices among working women in India and to determine demographic and work-related factors associated with wellness among the working women.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

It was an online cross-sectional study conducted by investigators at a tertiary care hospital. Ethical clearance was taken from the Institutional Ethics Committee (Ref.SKNMC/Ethics/App/2024/280 dated 30/08/2024).

The duration of data collection was from September 1st to September 30th, 2024. By taking the prevalence of 56%[11] of general well-being among working women and allowable error 5%, sample size was calculated by using the formula: sample size (n) = Z2 P(1-P)/d2. Minimum sample size required was 379; however, all the responses collected during one month period were included in the study.

All adult currently working women from various sectors and residing in India who have volunteered to take part in the study were included and their response to Google Form was taken as implied consent.

Data were collected by using a pre-formed and pre-structured questionnaire, which was prepared in the English and converted to local language which includes questions related to wellness practices among women. It was validated by expert faculty from the department. The questionnaire in the local language was validated by a language expert and back translated. This questionnaire was transformed into a Google Form and link for the same was circulated on various social media platforms like WhatsApp, Facebook, email, etc., among the known contacts of authors. Respondents were requested to forward it further to their known contacts. Thus, the snowball sampling method was used in the study to get the responses from the participants. Confidentiality and anonymity were maintained throughout the study period.

Questions were asked regarding various domains of health and wellness, like daily activities, exercise, diet, stress-related factors, and health-related factors. The scoring system was used and scores of 0, 1, 2, and 3 were given to each question based on the number of responses. A total health and wellness score was 20, and it was divided into two categories. Score between 0 and 9 was considered poor, and ≥10 was taken as good score. A good score indicates desirable healthy practices among the participants.

Data were collected and entered in MS Excel spreadsheet after coding. It was further processed and analyzed using EPI-INFO TM version 7.2.5 Software by CDC, Atlanta, Georgia. Descriptive statistics was calculated based on the type of data. The Chi-square test of significance was used to show the association, and P < 0.05 was considered significant at 95% confidence interval.

RESULTS

A total of 441 participants were included in the study. The findings of the study reveal significant insights into the health and wellness of women, particularly in relation to their work–life balance.

Maximum participants were from the age group of 31–40 years (50.11%), followed by 41–50 years (29.93%). The mean age of the participant was 39.61 years with standard deviation (SD) ±7.89 years. Most of the participants (50.57%) had higher education, i.e., postgraduate and above. All the respondents were from the urban area of western India.

55.78% participants belonged to a nuclear family, while 44.22% were from joint family. Approximately 90% of the participants were married. 48.17% women had marriage duration between 5 and 15 years, followed by 31.30% women who had duration of married life between 16 and 25 years. Most of the women, i.e., 58.85%, had two children, 40.36% were having one child, and 0.78% had three children. 41.49% women had school-going children, 23.12% women had children in the preschool age group, and 22.90% had adult children.

Figure 1 shows the distribution of participants as per their occupation. They were working in different occupational sectors like nursing professional (30.61%), medical professional (25.4%), government/private sector (20.63%), which includes engineers, clerks, and employees in government and private offices and companies, etc. 11.34% were in the teaching profession and 12.02% were in others category which includes women in different self-employed business. Figure 2 depicts the distribution of the type of job of participants, which shows that 65.99% were having full time job, 28.12% had shift duties, 3.4% were from work from home category, and 2.49% were having part time job.

DISCUSSION

The present study aimed to assess health- and wellness-related practices among working women in India and to determine some factors associated with wellness among the working women.

Demographic variables

The present study included a total of 441 participants across the India with the mean age of 39.61 years with SD ± 7.89 years. Most of the participants had higher education and belonged to a nuclear family. Approximately 90% of the participants were married. Most of the women, i.e., 58.85%, were mother of two children and 40.36% were having one child. 30.61% women were nursing professional, 25.4% were from medical professional, 20.63% had government/private sector job, and 11.34% were in the teaching profession.

In a study by Reddy et al., the mean age of the respondent was 38.70 (SD 8.66) years and 41.1% of the women had one child while 26.7% had two children.[3] In a study conducted among the married working women of Pondicherry, a sample of 180 were taken from academic, IT, and healthcare sectors. Sixty women from each sector were chosen for the study.[12] In another study, a sample of 180 married working women was selected using convenient sampling from academic, IT, and healthcare sectors.[13] Similar study population was taken in Bangalore study where the majority of them were in the age group of 35–45 years with one or two children. Most of them were postgraduates and were married. Four percent of the women were self-employed, 54% in the private sector, 12% in the public sector, 28% in academic institutions, and 2% were employed in other organizations.[14] Similar demographic profile is shared by other studies.[15161718]

Age and total health and wellness score

The data indicate that 70.75% of the participants achieved a good health and wellness score, while 29.25% reported a poor score. This disparity highlights a substantial portion of women who may be at risk for health issues, potentially linked to various demographic, socioeconomic factors, and personal circumstances. Statistically significant association was found in age and total score, as young age (<40 years) women were having a poor score than older women, where the majority had good score. Specifically, women under 40 years exhibited poorer health scores, suggesting that younger women may face unique challenges that adversely affect their health and wellness, potentially including higher stress levels, younger kids, greater work–life conflict, or insufficient coping mechanisms for managing responsibilities related to family and work.

In a study conducted among the married working women of Pondicherry,[12] out of the respondents in the age group under 30 years, 90% of them feel that they are not able to balance their work and family life while respondents in the age group over 40 years, 73% of them feel that they are not able to balance their work and family life indicating similar relationship with the present study. The study also highlighted that married elderly women were able to strike a balance between their personal and professional duties compared to young age married women, which might be due to the experience gained over the years in carrying out personal and professional duties.[12] Another study also mentions that married working women in the age group of under 30 years were found to have more work–life imbalance problems than those in the age group of 30–40 years, while married working women over 40 years were balancing work–life slightly better than the above mentioned age groups.[13] Similar findings were also shared by few other studies.[1619]

Impact of job hours and total health and wellness score

Total number of work hours per day also had an impact on the total health and wellness score; women who worked more than 9 h a day were had statistically significantly low score than those who worked less hours. Long working hours can significantly damages woman’s overall well-being and may have consequences like health issues, elevated stress levels, inactivity, and disruptions to daily routine. Flexible work schedules, job-sharing responsibilities, remote work choices, family support, and amenities like child care centers, gyms or recreation areas at work, paid maternity leave, and other things are all necessary to improve the wellness of women in both the public and private sectors.

The majority of the respondents agreed that work interfered with family life and more working hours attributed to very little time for family due to long hours spent in completing official work.[14] Similar findings were seen in a study done on nurses where more than 50 percent of nurses’ social life was disturbed due to their night shift duty.[15] Similar findings were also shared by a few other studies.[16172021]

Other factors

Statistically significant association was not found between the total score and variables like education, marital status, type of family, type of job of participants, occupations, their number of children, and number of daily activities. This lack of association may indicate that these factors do not have as direct an impact on total scores as age and work hours do, or it could reflect the complexity of interactions among these variables that were not fully captured in this analysis. Most of the women were educated and residing in the urban area; therefore, they may be well versed with wellness practices and coping mechanisms. The majority of the women were involved in more than three times and energy-consuming activities apart from their work.

Similarly, regardless of their job sector, age group, or number of children, married working women find it extremely difficult to reconcile their personal and academic lives, according to a study by Lakshmi and Sai Prasanth et al.[13] Another study found that 67% of the respondents suffered from the burden of excessive work confronted with tasks involving children, home, in-laws, parents, and their social circle. With the increase in demands on the job, the majority of them are burdened with excessive work in both their personal and work spaces contributing to work–life imbalance.[14] In a study by Reddy et al., women working in hospital setting reported higher work–family conflict compared to those working at school or industry setting and also high family–work conflict was found among those who were having one child, whereas it was less among those not having children.[3] In a study conducted in Pondicherry, the majority of respondents with two children were not able to balance their work and family life compared to respondents with no children, showing that the caring responsibilities that working women have toward their children definitely affect their work–life balance.[12] According to the findings of another research, married women who work, regardless of their age, marital status, number of children, or husband’s career, struggle to find a work–life balance.[16] In contrary, a study has noted that professionals in the IT sector had the most difficulty juggling their job and family obligations, followed by those in academia and finally those in the healthcare industry while current study could not establish any association between various careers and total scores.[16] Another study implies that employees from banking sector are relatively more satisfied in addition to employees from IT sector who are least satisfied.[18]

Work–life balance

In the present study, regarding work–life balance, 50.79% women were satisfied, 43.99% were partially satisfied, and 5.22% were not satisfied about their current work–life balance. The majority of the women agreed upon prioritizing their family over their work. The majority of the women also agreed upon having good work–life balance and having access to stress management resources. This can be attributed to majority being from health-related fields and having good level of education. Though results suggest that a majority feel content with their work–life integration, a notable portion remains either partially satisfied or dissatisfied, which could have implications for their overall well-being.

The findings from this study align with existing literature that emphasizes the importance of maintaining a healthy work–life balance as a means to enhance overall wellness among women. In a study conducted in Pondicherry, 82.2% of women felt that they were not able to balance their work–life while 70.56% of them felt that work–life balance was the biggest challenge that they faced.[12] The majority of the respondents agree that they experience high levels of stress and anxiety due to poor work–life balance in a study done in Bangalore.[14] Similar findings were shared by other researcher as well.[18] A study by Balaji also highlights that work and family have increasingly become antagonist spheres, equally greedy of energy and time and responsible for work–family conflict.[22] Author also stated that “these conflicts are intensified by the cultural contradictions of motherhood,” as women are increasingly encouraged to seek self-fulfillment in demanding careers, they also face intensified pressures to sacrifice themselves for their children by providing “intensive parenting,” highly involved childrearing and development with additional problems faced are those associated with finding adequate, affordable access to child and elderly care.”[22] A similar study concluded that striking an appropriate balance between one’s personal and professional lives is bit of an art and science too, i.e., leisure and personal time must complement one’s job passion.[19] Similar views are also expressed by other studies as well.[2123]

Strength and limitations

Strengths

The study focused upon a very crucial aspect of women’s health in today’s era, highlighting health and wellness practices among urban women and also their perspective on work–life balance and satisfaction. The study generates an evidence in highlighting complex relationship in various factors affecting women’s health and their work–life balance. It gives insight of challenges faced by women of urban India trying to balance their personal health, family life, and career.

Limitations

The study’s cross-sectional nature limits the ability to draw causal inferences between the identified factors and health outcomes. Longitudinal studies are needed to better understand how these relationships evolve over time. It was an online study using snowball sampling which can decrease reliability of the data. Additionally, because the survey was conducted online, only educated women with access to a device and the internet were able to participate, which decreased diversity and generalizability. The reliance on self-reported measures for health and wellness scores may introduce bias or inaccuracies due to subjective interpretations of health status. A broader range of variables could yield more comprehensive insights.

CONCLUSION

The study highlighted that nearly two-thirds of the women participants achieved a good health and wellness score, but one-third reported a poor score. This significant percentage experiencing poor scores highlights critical areas for improvement. The findings underscore the importance of considering age and work hours when evaluating health and wellness in women. The results suggest that interventions aimed at improving health outcomes should focus on younger women and those engaged in long working hours. Addressing these disparities through policy changes and community support could foster better health outcomes for women across various demographics. Encouraging women to prioritize self-care and their own health will help in a long term and they should be motivated for the same. Future research could explore additional dimensions of well-being, such as mental health support systems and community resources that could mitigate the negative effects associated with these demographic factors.

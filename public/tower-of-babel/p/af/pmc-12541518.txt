Machine learning-based screening of potential diagnostic markers for prostate cancer with immune cell infiltration analysis and experimental validation
Authors: Ya Zhao, Chaoqun Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProstate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) poses a significant global health burden. Apolipoprotein B mRNA editing enzyme catalytic polypeptide-like 3C (APOBEC3C) exhibits context-dependent roles in cancer but its function in PRAD remains unclear. This study aims to resolve APOBEC3C’s functional duality in PRAD and identify immune-related hub genes using integrated bioinformatics and experimental validation.MethodsScreening of PRAD differentially expressed genes (DEGs), Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and CIBERSORT immune infiltration assessment were performed based on The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Second, key genes in DEGs were screened using random forest (RF) analysis, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model, and the Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE). The diagnostic value of the characterized core gene was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis in both TCGA and external Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)-GSE29079 cohorts. The correlation between the core gene and tumor-infiltrating immune cells was further analyzed. In addition, in vitro PRAD models (PC-3/DU145 cell lines) were established to validate bioinformatics findings through functional assays, including quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), small interfering RNA (siRNA) knockdown, proliferation/migration tests, and chemokine profiling.ResultsDEG analysis identified 24,208 genes (2,269 up-regulated and 1,966 down-regulated). GO enrichment analysis showed that DEGs were mainly involved in biological processes such as regulation of vascular contraction, maintenance of epithelial homeostasis in the gastrointestinal tract, and intercellular adhesion; the KEGG pathway was enriched in vascular smooth muscle contraction, neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions, and cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) signaling pathways. Immune infiltration showed distinct distribution of 21 immune cells in PRAD vs. normal tissues (P=0.045). Machine learning (LASSO/RF/SVM-RFE) identified APOBEC3C as a core gene with high diagnostic accuracy [TCGA area under the curve (AUC) =0.859; GEO AUC =0.835]. Further analysis showed that APOBEC3C expression was significantly positively correlated with anti-tumor immune cells (activated dendritic cells: r=0.45, P=0.008; resting memory CD4+ T cells: r=0.38, P=0.015). The qRT-PCR results showed that APOBEC3C was expressed at significantly lower mRNA levels in PRAD cell lines (PC-3: 0.32±0.05-fold, P=0.003; DU145: 0.28±0.04-fold, P=0.001) than in normal prostate epithelial cells (RWPE-1). Functional validation confirmed that siRNA-mediated APOBEC3C knockdown (>75% efficiency) promoted malignant phenotypes: proliferation increased 1.8-fold (P<0.001) and migration accelerated 2.1-fold (P<0.001). Crucially, knockdown suppressed T-cell-recruiting chemokines C-X-C motif chemokine ligands 9 (CXCL9) (62% reduction) and C-X-C motif chemokine ligands 10 (CXCL10) (60% reduction) in PRAD cells (P<0.001).ConclusionsThis study revealed the regulatory role of APOBEC3C as an oncogene in PRAD progression, suggesting that it may become a potential molecular marker for PRAD diagnosis and targeted therapy and providing new ideas for clinical intervention.Keywords: Prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), machine learning, bioinformatics, immune microenvironment, APOBEC3C

Highlight box

Key findings

• Apolipoprotein B mRNA editing enzyme catalytic polypeptide-like 3C (APOBEC3C) diagnostic biomarker: multi-algorithm screening [least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)/random forest (RF)/Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE)] identified APOBEC3C as a core PRAD biomarker with high diagnostic accuracy [area under the curve (AUC): The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) =0.859, Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) =0.835].

• Immune-modulatory role: APOBEC3C downregulation correlates with immunosuppression (e.g., ↓dendritic cells/CD4+ T cells). Knockdown suppressed T-cell-recruiting chemokines CXCL9 (↓62%) and CXCL10 (↓60%), impairing T-cell recruitment (P<0.001).

• Tissue-specific tumor suppression: functional validation demonstrated that APOBEC3C is significantly downregulated in PRAD, and small interfering RNA (siRNA)-mediated knockdown mechanistically drives malignancy, increasing proliferation by 1.8-fold (P<0.001) and migration by 2.1-fold (P<0.001) in functional assays.

• Pathway links: DEGs enriched in cAMP signaling and neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions suggest APOBEC3C’s role in tumor-immune crosstalk.

What is known and what is new?

• APOBEC enzymes drive cancer mutagenesis, but APOBEC3C’s role in PRAD was undefined. Immune suppression is a hallmark of PRAD progression.

• This is the first study to (I) integrate multi-algorithm screening (TCGA cohort) with wet-lab validation; (II) link APOBEC3C to immune evasion via anti-tumor immune cell depletion; and (III) propose its cAMP pathway-mediated tumor suppression.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Diagnostic potential: APOBEC3C’s high AUC supports its clinical utility as a non-invasive biomarker for early PRAD detection.

• Therapeutic targeting: restoring APOBEC3C expression may reverse immune suppression; combinatorial strategies (e.g., immune checkpoint inhibitors) warrant exploration.

• Next steps: validate mechanisms (e.g., NF-κB/CXCL9 axis) in vivo and expand cohorts [GEO data + patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models] to address current limitations.

Introduction

Background

Prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) is one of the most common malignant tumors in men, and its incidence and mortality rates are continuously increasing worldwide (1). Although serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA)-based screening has significantly improved the early diagnosis rate, the high false-positive rate and the difficulty in identifying aggressive subtypes are still serious constraints to clinical decision-making (2). In recent years, the rapid development of high-throughput sequencing technology has revealed the high molecular heterogeneity of PRAD (3), but key driver genes and reliable diagnostic markers still need to be urgently explored. Therefore, integrating multi-omics data with machine learning algorithms (4) to systematically screen molecular targets with clinical translational potential has become an important direction for precision medicine research.

Human innate immune defense against exogenous viruses includes the apoB mRNA editing enzyme catalytic polypeptide-like (APOBEC) family of cytidine deaminases (5), which are capable of editing DNA or RNA by deamidating cytosine to uracil (6). The APOBEC family includes APOBEC1, APOBEC3 (including APOBEC3A–D and APOBEC3F–H), APOBEC4 and activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) (7). APOBEC3 proteins have been implicated in the natural defense against a variety of viruses associated with lymphomagenesis and play a dual role in the regulation of intrinsic immunity and genome stability (8).

Rationale and knowledge gap

Among them, APOBEC3C, as a core member of the family, has attracted much attention because of its “double-edged sword” properties in various cancers. Studies have shown that APOBEC3C promotes tumor progression in pancreatic and renal cell carcinomas by inducing DNA damage (9) or activating the NF-κB pathway (10), whereas APOBEC3C plays a positive role in the aggressiveness and prognosis of hepatocellular carcinomas (HCCs) (11), and exerts oncostatic effects by enhancing chemosensitivity (12). It was reported that transfection of human HepG2 HCC cells with a plasmid encoding APOBEC3C protein resulted in abundant G>A mutations in most of the newly formed hepatitis B virus (HBV) genomes (13). However, the specific functional role of APOBEC3C in PRAD pathogenesis and its interaction with the unique immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment remain poorly defined and lack consensus. Critically, no study has yet employed integrated machine learning approaches to identify APOBEC3C as a core biomarker within the PRAD transcriptome or comprehensively linked its expression dynamics to specific immune cell infiltration patterns in this context. Moreover, experimental validation of APOBEC3C expression in PRAD models and functional confirmation of its role (e.g., via knockdown migration/proliferation assays) are notably lacking. Notably, APOBEC family members can remodel the immune response by inducing tumor neoantigens or modulating chemokine secretion (14), and APOBEC3C may serve as a key node connecting genomic instability and anti-tumor immunity.

In recent years, machine learning-based biomarker screening strategies have demonstrated unique advantages in tumor research (15). Algorithms such as random forest (RF), least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) are able to effectively mine key genes in complex histological data through high-dimensional feature dimensionality reduction and pattern recognition (16). However, most of the existing studies focus on a single algorithm, lack joint validation of multiple models, and are less likely to combine with experimental data to confirm the prediction results (17-20) functionally. In addition, despite the initial progress of immune checkpoint inhibitors in PRAD treatment (21), therapeutic resistance due to the characteristics of the “cold tumor” microenvironment remains a major challenge (22).

Objective

In this study, we employed an integrated approach combining multi-algorithm machine learning screening of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-PRAD transcriptomic data, immune microenvironment deconvolution analysis (23), and in vitro functional validation to identify novel diagnostic biomarkers for PRAD, characterize the tumor-suppressive expression pattern and diagnostic potential of APOBEC3C specifically in PRAD, elucidate its correlation with immunosuppressive cell infiltration such as regulatory T cells (Tregs) and M2 macrophages as well as cytotoxic lymphocyte exclusion, experimentally validate APOBEC3C knockdown-induced malignant phenotype potentiation including proliferation and migration along with chemokine suppression like CXCL9 and CXCL10 leading to impaired T-cell recruitment. This study not only provides a new tool for molecular typing of PRAD but also provides an important theoretical basis for the background-dependent functional regulation mechanism of APOBEC family members. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2025-291/rc).

Methods

Data collection and cohort selection

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Clinical information and mRNA sequencing data of human PRAD cases were retrieved from the TCGA database (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga). Tumor samples (n=496) were selected based on the inclusion criteria: (I) histologically confirmed primary PRAD; (II) availability of normalized RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) data [fragments per kilobase of exon model per million mapped fragments (FPKM)]; (III) complete clinical records [Gleason score, tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, survival status]; (IV) treatment-naïve status (no prior radiotherapy/chemotherapy). Exclusion criteria: (I) metastatic or non-adenocarcinoma subtypes; (II) RNA-seq batch outliers [>3 standard deviation (SD) in principal component analysis]; (III) >20% missing transcriptomic data. Control samples (n=52) comprised matched adjacent non-tumor tissues from the same cohort meeting identical data completeness standards. Raw data underwent quality control (read cleaning and integration) to ensure consistency.

Data preprocessing and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) screening

Each dataset was background-corrected and normalized by the “limma” package of R software and converted to gene symbols in the probe annotation file that refer to the probe name in each dataset. The normalized data were subjected to intergroup differential expression analysis to screen out DEGs, and volcano maps and heat maps were drawn using the “ggord” and “pheatmap” packages, with the thresholds set at P<0.05 and |log20.05| and |log2fold change (FC)| >1.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis

The David (https://davidbioinformatics.nih.gov/#) database allows further understanding of the biological significance of DEG using GO enrichment analysis. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis is also available. The most important terms appearing in the statistical analysis were selected for visualization.

Machine learning algorithm screens for PRAD diagnostic biomarkers

In this study, three machine learning algorithms, RF, LASSO and SVM-RFE, are used to screen feature genes.

RF is an algorithm based on the decision tree algorithm. The “RandomForest” package is used for the analysis, and the parameter “ntree” is set to 500 to find the optimal number of trees. By calculating the error of cross-validation, the optimal number of trees is selected, and the RF model is reconstructed to obtain the importance score of the genes. Genes with an importance score greater than 0 were considered as PRAD significantly related genes.

LASSO was performed using the “glmnet” package in R, which represents a regression analysis algorithm that applies regularization to variable selection. Using LASSO, genes significantly associated with PRAD and normal samples were identified.

SVM-RFE represents a widely used supervised machine learning protocol for classification and regression, which uses the e1071 package to find the best variables and the kernlab and caret packages to select the minimum of the cross-validation error.

We applied these three algorithms in the TCGA-PRAD cohort and used the “venn” package to take the intersection genes of the results of the three algorithms further to validate the expression levels of the candidate diagnostic biomarkers.

APOBEC3C expression profiling and efficacy validation

Gene expression data for 496 PRAD tissues and 52 matched normal prostate tissues were obtained by querying the PRAD dataset (TCGA-PRAD) in the TCGA database. The “Gene_DE” module of TIMER 2.0 was used to analyze the differential expression of APOBEC3C between PRAD tissues and normal prostate tissues. TCGA RNA-seq data {log2[transcripts per million (TPM) + 1] normalized} were extracted using default parameters, and gene expression distribution was visualized using boxplots. Between-group comparisons were performed using the nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis test. To ensure robust validation, we additionally incorporated an independent Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset (GSE29079) for external verification. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis employed 10-fold cross-validation with 100 repeats to compute robust area under the curve (AUC) estimates and 95% CIs. Curves were generated using the pROC package (version 1.17.0.1) for each cohort separately (TCGA-PRAD and GSE29079). Diagnostic efficacy was quantified by calculating the AUC, with visualizations implemented using the ggplot2 package (version 3.3.3).

Evaluation of immune cell infiltration

In this study, the immune cell composition of PRAD gene expression profiles was quantified using the CIBERSORT deconvolution algorithm. The input data were RNA-seq expression profiles (TPM normalized) of the TCGA-PRAD cohort, referenced to the signature gene expression patterns of 21 immune cell subtypes in the LM22 feature matrix. Algorithm parameters were set to permutation test 20 times with disabling quantile normalization [QN = false (F)]. The final matrix of immune cell proportions was used for subsequent analysis. The results of immune cell infiltration were correlated and visualized using the R software package “tidyverse, reshape2, ggpubr, RColorBrewer, ggcorrplot”.

Correlation analysis of APOBEC3C with infiltrating immune cells

Pearson correlation analysis was used to assess the association between APOBEC3C expression levels and the abundance of tumor-infiltrating immune cells. Correlation coefficients (r) for two-sided tests and their significance P values were calculated by the cor.test function (stats package) in R language (v4.4.2), with a significance threshold defined as α=0.05. The results were structurally integrated using the tidyverse and reshape2 packages to generate a matrix of correlation coefficients containing gene-immune cell pairs. Visualization was achieved with the ggplot2 (v3.4.2) and RColorBrewer (v1.1-3) packages, and only correlation coefficients that reached significance (P<0.05) were labeled in the heatmaps.

Experimental validation of potential diagnostic markers

Cell lines and cell culture

All human cell lines utilized in this study were obtained from established, publicly available repositories with documented ethical compliance: RWPE-1 (normal prostate epithelial cells), acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, CRL-11609) and derived from non-malignant adult prostate tissue with donor consent under ATCC’s ethical guidelines. PC-3 (PRAD, androgen-independent): sourced from ATCC (CRL-1435) and originally isolated from a PRAD bone metastasis. DU145 (prostate carcinoma, castration-resistant): obtained from ATCC (HTB-81) and derived from a brain metastasis of PRAD. PC-3 cells were cultured in F-12k ham medium [10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% double antibody], and the DU145 cell line was cultured in Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) (10% FBS, 1% double antibody) at 95% humidity and 5% CO2 in a 37 ℃ constant temperature cell culture incubator.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA)-mediated APOBEC3C knockdown and functional validation

To functionally characterize the role of APOBEC3C in PRAD progression, loss-of-function experiments were performed using siRNA-mediated gene silencing. Two independent siRNA duplexes targeting human APOBEC3C (siAPOBEC3C-1: 5'-GCUGAAGGACUACGAUGAATT-3'; siAPOBEC3C-2: 5'-CCAGCUACUUCUGGACAATT-3') and non-targeting scrambled control (siNC: 5'-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3') were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). PC-3 and DU145 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 60% confluence and transfected with 50 nM siRNA using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After 48 hours of transfection, knockdown efficiency was validated by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) using the previously established APOBEC3C primers (Section 2.8.2). Cells showing >70% reduction in APOBEC3C mRNA expression relative to siNC-treated controls were subsequently used for functional assays.

A complete medium containing 10% FBS served as a chemoattractant in the lower chamber. After 24-hour incubation, migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.1% crystal violet, and counted in five random fields per insert.

The proliferative capacity of APOBEC3C-depleted cells was evaluated using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (CK04, Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan). Transfected PC-3 and DU145 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 2×103 cells/well. After 24 hours of adhesion (time-point 0), 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well containing 100 µL culture medium. Plates were incubated at 37 ℃ for 2 hours protected from light. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Synergy H1). Measurements were taken at 0, 24, 48, and 72-hour time points, with each experimental group containing three technical replicates. Background absorbance from cell-free wells was subtracted, and cell viability was calculated as: viability (%) = [(ODt − ODt0)sample/(ODt − ODt0)siNC] ×100% where ODt represents absorbance at time t, and ODt0 indicates baseline absorbance at 0 hours. Three independent experiments were performed.

qRT-PCR analysis of chemokine gene expression

Total RNA was isolated from PC-3 and DU145 cells using TRIzol® Reagent (15596026, Invitrogen) with purity verification (A260/A280 >1.8) via NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometry (Thermo Fisher). Reverse transcription was performed with PrimeScriptTM RT Master Mix (RR036A, Takara) using 1 µg RNA under standard cycling conditions (37 ℃ for 15 min, 85 ℃ for 5 s). Quantitative PCR utilized TB GreenTM Premix Ex TaqTM II (RR820A, Takara) on a QuantStudioTM 6 Flex system (Applied Biosystems) in 20 µL reactions containing 2 µL cDNA template and 0.8 µM gene-specific primers. Consistent primer sequences were employed throughout: APOBEC3C (F: 5'-CAGGTGCTGAAGGACTACGA-3', R: 5'-TCCAGGTAGGCGTTCTTGGT-3'), CXCL9 (F: 5'-AGAGTGTGAACGCAACCCAAG-3', R: 5'-TGGGATCTTCAGGGAGTTGAG-3'), CXCL10 (F: 5'-GTGGCATTCAAGGAGTACCTC-3', R: 5'-TGATGGCCTTCGATTCTGGA-3'), and reference gene GAPDH (F: 5'-GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3', R: 5'-GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG-3'). Amplification parameters included initial denaturation at 95 ℃ for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 5 s and 60 ℃ for 30 s, with subsequent melt curve analysis. All experiments incorporated triplicate technical replicates and three biological replicates, with relative quantification performed using the 2−ΔΔCt method and statistical significance (P<0.05) determined by a t-test in GraphPad Prism 9.0.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted using R 4.2.1, with data distribution rigorously assessed through normality testing via the Shapiro-Wilk test (α=0.05) and variance homogeneity evaluation via Bartlett’s test (α=0.05). For test selection, parametric data (meeting both normality and homogeneity assumptions) utilized Student’s t-test for two groups or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey post-hoc testing for three or more groups, while non-parametric data employed the Mann-Whitney U test for two groups or Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s post-hoc testing for three or more groups. Correlation analysis applied Pearson’s correlation for normally distributed pairs and Spearman’s correlation for non-normal pairs. Diagnostic power was assessed using ROC curves with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) calculated via the DeLong method. Statistical significance was defined as P<0.05, and all tests were two-sided.

Results

Differential expression analysis was performed on 496 PRAD samples and 52 normal samples from the TCGA cohort using the “limma” R package. The DEG analysis identified 24,207 genes, of which 2,269 were up-regulated and 1,966 were down-regulated (Figure 1A, volcano plot). In order to clearly demonstrate the key differential genes, the top 20 up-regulated genes and the top 20 down-regulated genes with the largest absolute value of log2FC were selected to draw heat maps (Figure 1B). The samples were clustered by tumor and normal prostate tissues, and the gene expressions were normalized by Z-score.

We evaluated the diagnostic potential of APOBEC3C through ROC curve analysis, revealing strong performance in the TCGA-PRAD cohort (AUC =0.859, 95% CI: 0.8–0.92) (Figure 4C) for distinguishing prostate tumors from normal prostate tissues, with this robust diagnostic capability further validated in the independent GEO cohort GSE29079 where APOBEC3C achieved an AUC of 0.835 (95% CI: 0.755–0.915) (Figure 4D), demonstrating consistent downregulation patterns across both datasets and confirming its significant clinical value as a diagnostic biomarker for PRAD.

Functional validation of APOBEC3C in PRAD progression

Efficient knockdown of APOBEC3C

Both siRNA duplexes achieved a significant reduction of APOBEC3C expression in PRAD cells (Figure 7A,7B). In PC-3 cells, siAPOBEC3C-1 and 2 reduced mRNA levels to 24.7%±3.0% (P<0.001) and 18.7%±2.5% (P<0.001) of siNC controls, respectively. Similar knockdown efficiency was observed in DU145 cells (siAPOBEC3C-1: 29.0%±2.0%; siAPOBEC3C-2: 20.3%±2.5%; both P<0.001). This successful silencing (>70% reduction) confirms the effectiveness of our siRNA design and validates subsequent functional assays.

CCK-8, Cell Counting Kit-8; qRT-PCR, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction.

APOBEC3C depletion enhances migration

Wound healing assays showed accelerated wound closure in silenced cells vs.siNC controls, with migration distance increasing 1.41-fold in PC-3 (P<0.001) (Figure 7C) and 2.06-fold in DU145 (P<0.001) at 48 hours (Figure 7D); Transwell migration further confirmed this effect, demonstrating 99% more migrated PC-3 cells (P=0.006) (Figure 7E) and 213% more DU145 cells (P<0.001) upon knockdown (Figure 7F).

APOBEC3C depletion enhances cellular proliferation

CCK-8 assays revealed time-dependent enhancement of viability in APOBEC3C-depleted cells. PC-3-siAPOBEC3C exhibited 1.8-fold higher viability than siNC controls at 72 hours (182.3%±8.7% vs. 100.0%±5.2%, P<0.001) (Figure 7G); DU145-siAPOBEC3C showed similar proliferation potentiation (176.5%±7.9% vs. 100.0%±4.6%, P<0.001) (Figure 7H). The proliferation curves demonstrated progressively divergent kinetics from 48 hours onward, indicating sustained growth advantage conferred by APOBEC3C loss.

APOBEC3C knockdown suppresses chemokine expression

To functionally validate APOBEC3C’s role in immune regulation, we quantified CXCL9 and CXCL10—key chemokines for recruiting CD8+ T cells and dendritic cells—in APOBEC3C-silenced cells. qRT-PCR analysis revealed that APOBEC3C knockdown significantly suppressed CXCL9 in PC-3 (0.45±0.07-fold, P<0.001) and DU145 (0.38±0.05-fold, P<0.001) cells vs. siNC controls (Figure 8A). Similarly, CXCL10 expression decreased by 59% in PC-3 (P<0.001) and 65% in DU145 (P<0.001) (Figure 8B). This chemokine downregulation mechanistically explains our CIBERSORT findings of reduced activated dendritic cells and CD4+ memory T cells in APOBEC3C-low tumors.

Discussion

The APOBEC family is well known for mediating DNA/RNA editing in genome stability regulation (24), but its members exhibit significant functional heterogeneity across cancers (25,26). Our study identifies APOBEC3C as a novel tumor-suppressive biomarker in PRAD, contrasting sharply with its documented oncogenic roles in other malignancies (27,28). This tissue-specific duality underscores how APOBEC3C may exert opposing effects through context-dependent molecular interactions.

To establish this context-dependent functionality, our multi-algorithm machine learning approach identified APOBEC3C as a key diagnostic biomarker for PRAD. This finding was robustly validated across clinical cohorts (TCGA-PRAD/GEO, AUC =0.859/0.835) and experimental models, where qRT-PCR confirmed significantly lower APOBEC3C expression in PC-3/DU145 cancer cells vs. normal RWPE-1 cells (P<0.001). Critically, functional validation demonstrated that siRNA-mediated APOBEC3C knockdown directly accelerated malignant progression, enhancing proliferation (1.8-fold viability increase, P<0.001) and migration (2.1-fold wound closure; 45% more Transwell invasion, P<0.001).

This tumor-suppressive role aligns with limited prior evidence in HCC, where APOBEC3C synergizes with genotoxic agents to induce cancer cell death (29). Similarly, APOBEC3B inhibits hepatitis B replication in hepatocytes (30), suggesting tissue-specific protective functions. However, APOBEC3C exhibits opposing oncogenic activities in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) (chemoresistance via genomic instability (9,31) and clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) (NF-κB-mediated invasion).

Mechanistically, we propose a PRAD-specific pathway: enrichment analyses linked low APOBEC3C to dysregulated cAMP signaling—a tumor-suppressive pathway known to inhibit proliferation via cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB) inactivation (32). We hypothesize that APOBEC3C downregulation disrupts cAMP signaling, representing an indirect mechanism distinct from its canonical DNA-editing function. Resolving the basis for this tissue-specific duality warrants further investigation (33).

Immune infiltration analysis showed that low APOBEC3C expression was significantly correlated with increased infiltration of immunosuppressive cells (e.g., M2-type macrophages, Tregs) and positively correlated with anti-tumor immune cells (activated dendritic cells, resting memory CD4+ T cells). Functionally, our qRT-PCR data confirmed that APOBEC3C knockdown significantly suppressed CXCL9 and CXCL10 expression in PRAD cells (Figure 8), which are critical chemokines for recruiting cytotoxic T cells and dendritic cells (34). It has been shown that activated dendritic cells activate initial T cells through high expression of MHC-I/II and co-stimulatory molecules (e.g., CD80/86) and are the central initiators of adaptive immune responses (35,36). In contrast, enrichment of Tregs may inhibit the function of effector T cells and promote immune tolerance in the tumor microenvironment through the secretion of factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β (37). APOBEC3C may remodel the tumor by regulating chemokine secretion (e.g., CXCL9/CXCL10) (38) or the expression of antigen presentation-associated molecules (e.g., MHC-I) (39), remodeling the tumor immune microenvironment. In addition, the positive correlation between APOBEC3C and neutrophil infiltration suggests that it may be involved in innate immune regulatory processes such as NETosis (neutrophil extracellular trap formation) (40,41).

In summary, the function of APOBEC3C as a novel oncogene of PRAD involves a multidimensional regulatory network. This study not only provides a new marker for molecular typing of PRAD but also opens up a new pathway for the development of immunoediting-based combination therapy strategies. Future studies need to further open up the complete chain of evidence from mechanism analysis to clinical translation to promote the development of precision medicine for PRAD.

Conclusions

This study identifies APOBEC3C as a novel diagnostic biomarker for PRAD through integrative machine learning screening (LASSO, RF, SVM-RFE) and experimental validation. APOBEC3C is significantly downregulated in PRAD tissues and cell lines, correlating with immunosuppressive microenvironment features (e.g., Tregs/M2 macrophage enrichment) and disrupted cAMP signaling pathways. External validation using an independent GEO cohort confirmed its robust diagnostic capability (AUC =0.859 TCGA cohort, 0.835 GEO cohort). While exhibiting tissue-specific duality (pro-tumorigenic in pancreatic/renal cancers vs. tumor-suppressive in PRAD/HCC), its PRAD-specific role likely involves chemokine-mediated immune editing. Current limitations include unvalidated in vivo mechanisms; future work should prioritize multi-center cohorts and therapeutic targeting of APOBEC3C-driven immunomodulation.

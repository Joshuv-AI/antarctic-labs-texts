Effectiveness of Buerger Allen Exercise as an Adjunct to Standard Pharmacotherapy in Reducing Peripheral Neuropathy Symptoms Among Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: A Randomized Controlled Pilot Study
Authors: Suganthakumari Francis, Anuratha Matipol Dharmalingam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is a common complication of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) with limited response to pharmacotherapy alone. Adjunct non-pharmacological interventions may enhance treatment outcomes.Aim:To evaluate the effectiveness of Buerger Allen Exercise as an adjunct to standard pharmacotherapy in reducing peripheral neuropathy symptoms among patients with T2DM.Materials and Methods:A pilot randomized controlled study with a pretest–posttest repeated measures design was conducted among 20 patients with T2DM and DPN. Participants were randomly allocated into experimental (n = 10) and control (n = 10) groups using a lottery method. Both groups received standard pharmacotherapy, while the experimental group additionally performed Buerger Allen Exercise for 15 days. Neuropathy symptoms were assessed at baseline, day 5, day 10, and day 15 using a structured neuropathy assessment scale. Data were analyzed using repeated measures ANOVA and t-tests.Results:The experimental group showed a statistically significant reduction in neuropathy scores compared to the control group (P < 0.05). A significant time–group interaction was observed, indicating progressive improvement with intervention.Conclusion:Buerger Allen Exercise is an effective short-term adjunct therapy in reducing symptoms of diabetic peripheral neuropathy. Larger studies are required to confirm long-term benefits.

INTRODUCTION

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is one of the most common complications of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM), affecting nearly half of diabetic patients and leading to pain, sensory loss, and increased risk of foot ulcers.[12] Pharmacological management primarily includes glycemic control using oral hypoglycemic agents and insulin, along with symptomatic treatment using agents such as pregabalin and duloxetine.[3] However, pharmacotherapy mainly provides symptomatic relief and has limited efficacy in reversing nerve damage.[4]

Exercise-based interventions have been shown to improve peripheral circulation and nerve function.[5] Buerger Allen Exercise, a simple postural exercise, enhances blood flow in the lower extremities and may reduce neuropathic symptoms. Integrating such non-pharmacological interventions with pharmacotherapy may improve outcomes, aligning with clinical pharmacy approaches to comprehensive care. Despite evidence supporting exercise-based interventions, limited randomized controlled trials have evaluated the short-term effectiveness of Buerger Allen Exercise specifically in patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy. Hence, this study was conducted to assess its effectiveness as an adjunct to pharmacotherapy.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A pilot randomized controlled trial with a pretest–posttest repeated measures design was conducted in a tertiary care hospital. A total of 20 patients diagnosed with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and peripheral neuropathy were enrolled.

Participants were selected using systematic random sampling and allocated into experimental (n = 10) and control (n = 10) groups using a simple randomization (lottery method). Allocation concealment and blinding were not implemented due to the nature of the intervention.

Inclusion criteria included patients aged 40–60 years, HbA1c >7%, and presence of peripheral neuropathy symptoms. Patients with foot ulcers, gangrene, critical illness, or those receiving anticoagulant therapy were excluded.

Both groups continued their prescribed pharmacotherapy (oral hypoglycemic agents and/or insulin). The experimental group additionally performed Buerger Allen Exercise consisting of:

Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics (mean ± standard deviation) and inferential statistics including paired t-test, independent t-test, and repeated measures ANOVA. Assumptions of normality were tested prior to analysis. The level of significance was set at P < 0.05. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee.

RESULTS

The experimental group demonstrated a progressive and statistically significant reduction in peripheral neuropathy scores compared to the control group over the 15-day intervention period. Repeated measures ANOVA showed a significant time–group interaction (P < 0.05), indicating the effectiveness of Buerger Allen Exercise. In the right leg [Table 1], the mean score decreased from 15.5 ± 5.4 at baseline to 3.8 ± 3.2 on day 15, whereas the control group showed minimal change (17.1 ± 3.3 to 16.9 ± 3.1). Similarly, for the left leg [Table 2], the experimental group showed a reduction from 20.6 ± 5.0 to 7.6 ± 4.0, while the control group scores remained relatively unchanged over time.

DISCUSSION

The findings of this study indicate that Buerger Allen Exercise significantly improves peripheral neuropathy symptoms when used alongside pharmacotherapy. Pharmacological management of DPN primarily targets glycemic control and symptomatic relief but does not adequately reverse neuropathic damage.[34] The observed improvement in the experimental group may be attributed to enhanced peripheral circulation and improved oxygen delivery to nerve tissues.

These results are consistent with previous studies demonstrating the beneficial effects of exercise on nerve function and circulation.[56] Improved blood flow may facilitate removal of metabolic waste and enhance nerve regeneration. From a clinical pharmacy perspective, adjunct non-pharmacological interventions such as exercise can optimize treatment outcomes, may complement pharmacological therapy and potentially reduce reliance on symptomatic medications.[7]

The integration of exercise therapy into routine diabetic care supports a multidisciplinary approach involving physicians, pharmacists, and allied health professionals. This aligns with current recommendations emphasizing holistic management of diabetes and its complications.[1]

The observed reduction in neuropathy scores suggests not only statistical significance but also potential clinical relevance, as improved symptoms may enhance mobility and reduce the risk of complications such as foot ulcers. However, given the short duration of the study, long-term clinical benefits remain uncertain.

CONCLUSION

Buerger Allen Exercise appears to be an effective short-term adjunct to standard pharmacotherapy in reducing symptoms of diabetic peripheral neuropathy. Incorporating such low-cost, non-pharmacological interventions may improve patient outcomes. Further large-scale and long-term studies are recommended.

Limitations

The study is limited by a small sample size, short duration of intervention, lack of blinding, and single-center design, which may affect generalizability. Additionally, long-term outcomes were not assessed.

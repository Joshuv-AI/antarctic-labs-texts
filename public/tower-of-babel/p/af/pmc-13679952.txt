Post-COVID-19 Condition has High Prevalence in Patients with Immune-mediated Inflammatory Diseases and Negatively Impacts Quality of Life
Authors: Tobias Schlosser, Marco Krasselt, Martin Hecker, Babett Holler, Albrecht Hoffmeister
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Pandemic situation by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 and the associated disease (coronavirus disease 2019 [COVID-19]) presents broad challenges. Post-COVID-19 condition represents a continuum of persistent symptoms following the acute course. The World Health Organization (WHO) proposes a definition with at least one symptom persisting for 3 months postonset. Negative affection toward quality of life (QoL) is frequently reported. Patients with immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (IMIDs), such as inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and rheumatic diseases (RDs) are of scientific interest as immunologic factors and immunosuppressive therapy might influence QoL in post-COVID-19 conditions.Methods:Adult IMID patients (≥18 years) were offered participation in this study at our university outpatient center. Data collection was questionnaire-based and recorded individual COVID-19 history and persisting symptoms. QoL has been assessed by several standardized and validated patient-reported outcome measures (Short IBD Questionnaire and Patient Health Questionnaire 4, 5Q-5D-5L).Results:A total of 156 patients were included (117 [75%] with IBD, 39 [25%] with RDs). Eighty-one patients (51%) reported a positive COVID-19 history, mostly with mild courses. Only two (2%) required hospitalization, one requiring intensive care. Seven (9%) reported no symptoms, while 20 (25%) met the WHO criterion for post-COVID-19 condition; 19 (23%) were still suffering from post-COVID-19 at the time of data acquisition. Mean/median global QoL scores (scale from 0 [=lowest] to 100 [=highest]) were 71.5/75 in patients without post-COVID-19, compared to 61.9/60 in those with the condition. A one-sided t-test showed a significant association between post-COVID-19 and lower QoL (P = 0.03).Conclusions:A relevant fraction of IMID patients suffered from post-COVID-19 condition. This condition negatively impacted the individual QoL.

INTRODUCTION

At the end of 2019, a series of atypical pneumonia cases had been detected in Wuhan (Hubei Province, People’s Republic of China), progressing into an epidemic throughout China, and finally into a worldwide pandemic. An underlying infectious pathogen was a novel coronavirus (severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 [SARS-CoV-2]). The World Health Organization (WHO) denominated the associated infection in February 2020 as coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19).[1]

The individual clinical course of COVID-19 varies highly: While about 33% of SARS-CoV-2-infected persons do not complain of any symptoms, 14% suffer from severe disease (i.e. dyspnea and/or hypoxia). In around 5% of cases, critical illness with respiratory failure occurs. In 2.3%, the disease ends fatally.[234] The severity and also the range of COVID-19 symptoms varies with cough, myalgias, and headache as most frequent complaints. Further the common symptoms include diarrhea, sore throat, anosmia/taste disorders, and joint pain. The understanding of COVID-19 as a multisystem illness resulted from the evident involvement of different organ systems.[5] An important factor in pathogenesis appears to be an imbalanced immune response, leading to hyperinflammation.[67]

Persisting symptoms following acute illness is an issue that was shown by observational studies of patients recovering from acute COVID-19 disease. Distinct names were proposed for that condition, such as “long COVID,” “postacute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection,” “postacute COVID-19,” “chronic COVID-19,” “long haul COVID,” and “post-COVID syndrome.” Broadly accepted is the terminology of WHO, which favors “post-COVID-19 condition” as a term for physical and mental symptoms, that had evolved within 3 months from onset of probable or confirmed COVID-19, last for ≥2 months, and cannot be explained otherwise.[89] Aspects of this COVID-19 aftermath may be unique, but many seem to be similar to other infectious diseases, severe illness, and/or sepsis.[1011] Establishing an exact prevalence for post-COVID-19 condition seems difficult due to a lack of clear definitions. An enormous meta-analysis of 54 studies totaling more than 1 million patients has calculated 6.2% of individuals with symptomatic COVID-19 between March 2020 and January 2022, who suffered from at least one ongoing symptom.[12] Estimations of the economic burden of post-COVID-19 condition are trending to multi-billion euros. Approximately 0.4% of the employed population are entirely or partially taken out of labor market because of post-COVID-19 condition.[13] Further financial and nonfinancial burden results from increased healthcare utilization of these chronic ill patients.[14]

Immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (IMIDs) present a group of chronic diseases with shared pathogenic aspects, that are represented by subclassification, even within the affected organ system (e.g., gastrointestinal: Crohn’s disease, ulcerative colitis; rheumatic: Rheumatoid arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, psoriatic arthritis, and granulomatosis with polyangiitis).[15] Around 4.5% of the world’s population suffers from IMID.[16] Prevalence, severity, and lethality of acute COVID-19 lies within general population’s level. Well-controlled IMID has been shown to be protective against severe course of COVID-19.[171819] With rheumatic and gastrointestinal IMIDs representing the most prevalent autoimmune diseases and rheumatoid arthritis, the most common (>500 per 105) by far, we focused on these two subspecialties.[16]

The impact of COVID-19 on IMID patients was a significant aspect throughout all phases of the pandemic: Due to the underlying condition on the one hand and immunosuppressive therapy on the other hand, a linkage of post-COVID-19 condition to IMID seems more than reasonable but has not been established yet. Because of the high prevalence of both IMID and post-COVID-19 condition, an association of these health conditions might therefore contribute to multiple large-scale factors. However, data on prevalence, symptoms, and QoL regarding post-COVID-19 condition in IMID have never been systematically investigated.

The purpose of this study was to gather information on the prevalence, clinical characteristics, and effects on quality of life of post-COVID-19 on individuals with IMIDs.

METHODS

Study design and population

We conducted an investigator-initiated trial at our university medical center. Adult outpatients (≥18 years) with an established diagnosis of IMID were screened. IMID had to be diagnosed by state-of-the-art criteria and documented by an experienced physician in the fields of gastroenterology and rheumatology, respectively. Three authors (TS, MH, and BH) from our Division of Gastroenterology screened for patients compared to one author (MK) from our rheumatology outpatient ward. There had been no preselection of IMIDs to include/exclude.

A self-filled German form was used to collect the data. Between April 12th, 2022 and September 26th, 2022, screening and recruitment (equivalent to questionnaire completion) were performed in a consecutive order to obtain as complete a sample as possible. Patients were not supervised within the completion of the provided pages. Help from the investigators themselves had been offered at the beginning and after finalization by asking for uncertainties during the process. Relevant support or interference was virtually not necessary. The printed forms were provided as one-sided paper pages (black text on white paper, 80 g/m2, DIN A4), which had been stapled in the upper left corner. Additionally, we handed out a wooden clipboard and a ballpoint pen as well. General instructions on how to fill in the survey forms were provided within the standardized questionnaires. Transfer from paper to electronic database was competed at the end of our study. Only clear answers were evaluated and entered into the full analysis dataset. If a patient´s questionnaire had been handed in for a second time during a separate appointment, only the latest issue was analyzed.

Patients incapable or unwilling to give information regarding their COVID-19 were excluded. All participants provided written consent.

For diagnosing post-COVID-19 condition, the above-mentioned definition of the WHO was used: Suffering from symptoms longer than 3 months (≥3 months) after proven or probable SARS-CoV-2 infection. These symptoms might be fluctuating or relapsing but have to last longer than 2 months (≥2 months) and cannot be explained otherwise.[89] Multiple-choice questions obtained the symptoms of post-COVID-19 condition. A collection of typical clinical features was made through the literature research.[2021] We asked for symptoms that occurred newly or worsened considerably with or after acute COVID-19 disease. More than one answer could be made. Additional features were entered in written form. A specific threshold for a symptom (e.g., measured body temperature regarding fever history) was not specified. Subjective estimation was sufficient.

Types of vaccination against SARS-CoV-2 had been requested by multiple choice questions. We preselected the four commercialized vaccine formulations, which had been authorized by the European Medicines Agency at the time of study conception (12/2021): Comirnaty® by BioNTech/Pfizer, Spikevax® by Moderna Biotech, Vaxzevria® by AstraZeneca and Jcovden® by JohnsonandJohnson/Janssen-Cilag International NV.[22] A single choice for the vaccine could be made from first to fourth round of vaccination. Free-text fields were given at each time.

The attending physician completed questions regarding the IMID condition such as time of diagnosis, or current pharmacological therapy based on their knowledge, and each patient’s individual medical record. Prednisone and prednisolone doses were considered equal.[23]

A report about the acute course of COVID-19 for our cohort had been published separately.[24] Small excerpts from our preliminary data were presented as poster presentation at the European Crohn´s and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) 2023 convention.[25]

Questionnaires

Self-assessment regarding quality of life (QoL) is a basic part of outcome analysis and gains importance in clinical studies.[26] Positively screened patients received the following set of health-related questionnaires.

QoL was assessed using the “European Quality of Life 5 Dimensions 5 Levels” (EQ-5D-5L), a commonly used patient outcome measure.[27] It contains a linear vertical Visual Analog Scale (VAS) with the endpoints “The best health you can imagine” (=100) and “The worst health you can imagine” (=0). The EQ‐5D‐5L evaluates patient’s QoL regarding five dimensions (mobility, self‐care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression). Categorical classification within each factor is divided into five levels ranging from “no problems” to “extreme problems.” Altogether, an EQ-5D-5L index can be calculated and normalized to a standardized value set for Germany.[28] This index ranges from 0 (worst possible QoL) to 1 (best possible QoL).

As mental health survey served the ultra-short form of the “Patient Health Questionnaire 4” (PHQ-4). Four answers give an overview for the individual level of anxiety/depression. Each point is associated with a value of 0–3 points, leading to a sum score of zero (lowest) to twelve points (highest level of anxiety/depression). PHQ is under free license.[29]

In case of an established diagnosis of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), the “Short Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire” (SIBDQ) was assessed in addition to the above listed questionnaires that had been handed out to all participants. The SIBDQ is an abbreviated version of the 32-item original IBDQ.[3031] The SIBDQ contains 10 questions, which are answered on a 7-level category. This totals on a sum from 10 to 70 with higher levels indicating better QoL.[32] Due to this additional point, the IBD patients had to fill in nine pages of questionnaires, whereas only six pages had been handed out to the rheumatic patient. To keep the entire cohort in our focus, we chose to omit the highly significant SIBDQ results, and present it solely within the Appendix.

Permission to use the mentioned questionnaires in their validated German version has been obtained from the respective authors.

Analysis

Statistical calculation/graphic visualization was performed by ”R” (Version 4.1.3, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna/Austria). Chi-squared testing with Yates’ continuity correction proofed independency of subgroups. One-sided t-test compared continuous variables. Welch modified t-test was used if Levene’s test had previously indicated inequality of variance.[33] Selection toward a one-sided t-test instead of a two-sided approach is justified by a solid a priori directional hypothesis (association of low QoL with post-COVID-19 condition[34]). In that way, we increase statistical power at the cost of only a slightly lowered specificity. P < 5% was considered statistically significant. One sample proportion test (z-test) of post-COVID-19 condition prevalence was performed as one-sided testing according to Newcombe[35] at a confidence level of 95% against alternative hypothesis that the rate of post-COVID-19 condition within our sample cohort is different compared to P0.

RESULTS

Study participants

One hundred and seventy questionnaires were included in the primary analysis. Removal of double entries left 156 patients with IMIDs eligible for further investigation. Most of the participants were female (n = 89; 57%). Median and mean age at time of our data acquisition were 42 and 46 years.

Most patients had a diagnosis of IBD (n = 117; 75%) representing 69 patients (44%) with Crohn’s disease and 48 patients (31%) with ulcerative colitis. Remaining 25% were patients with rheumatic diseases (RDs, n = 39). This heterogeneous group was built by 13 persons (8%) with rheumatoid arthritis, nine (6%) with axial spondyloarthritis, six (4%) with systemic lupus erythematosus, and six (4%) affected by psoriatic arthritis. Two patients (1%) had the preexisting condition of granulomatosis with polyangiitis. Sjögren’s syndrome, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, and dermatomyositis/polymyositis were diagnosed in one (0.6%) person each [Table 1].

Since we included patients with different IMIDs, pharmacological therapy was quite heterogenic in both patient groups [Table 2]: Seven patients with IBD (6% of IBD cohort) received symptomatic therapy, one (1%) the sphingosine receptor agonist ozanimod. Topical therapy as oral and/or rectal preparation has been established in 21 (18%) patients. Within the RD cohort, two patients (5% of RD cohort) had infusions of intravenous immunoglobulins, one patient (3%) used ibuprofen. Five RD patients (13%) were treated with mycophenolate-mofetil. Pharmacological principles were mostly overlapping but had some specific features: Systemic corticoid therapy was more frequent within the RD cohort (44% vs. 14%, P = 0.005). The prescribed doses were significantly lower among the IBD patients [mean 18 vs. 3.9 mg of prednisone, P = 0.009, Table 2]. Conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs ([cs] DMARD: e.g., methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, azathioprine, and sulfasalazine) were administered in 17 (44%) rheumatic patients. Azathioprine was used in 21 IBD patients (18%) and in two RD patients (5%, P = 0.09). Sulfasalazine therapy was reported in one RD case (3%), more common use was seen for the related aminosalicylate derivatives in IBD (n = 39; 33%, P = 0.0003). Biologic agents in general were established for RD therapy (n = 19; 49%) and IBD therapy (n = 53; 45%, P = 0.85). Distribution of monoclonal antibodies differs between patients with IBD and RD (P < 0.0001). Most frequently reported biologics were infliximab and adalimumab in both groups. Targeted-synthetic (ts) DMARDs such as Janus kinase inhibitors were only used in patients with RD [P = 0.003, Table 2].

Vaccination status

Vast majority had been vaccinated at least once against SARS-CoV-2 (n = 143; 92%). Only 10 patients (6%) did not receive any kind of vaccination against COVID-19. Three patients (2%) did not answer this specific question [Table 1]. Vaccines were mostly mRNA-based with 127 persons having received Comirnaty® or Spikevax®; twelve patients had been given with Vaxzevria®, and four patients with Jcovden® as their first course of SARS-CoV-2-vaccination. A second round of vaccination was stated by 136 IMID patients (87%; 4 x Vaxzevria®, 113 x Comirnaty®, 18 x Spikevax®, 1 x Unknown). Three vaccinations were declared by 113 persons (72%; 3 x Vaxzevria®, 83 x Comirnaty®, 25 x Spikevax®, 2 x Unknown). Finally, four vaccination shots were given to ten IMID patients (6%; 8 x Comirnaty®, 2 x Spikevax®).

Coronavirus disease 2019 history and postcoronavirus disease 2019 condition

Eighty-one (52%) patients with IMID stated an acute course of COVID-19 and the associated SARS-CoV-2 infection was mostly (n = 68) diagnosed by the polymerase chain reaction. Three patients did not provide an answer to this question; 72 persons (46%) had no proven and/or suspected COVID-19. Severity of COVID-19 seemed mild as solely one person had been hospitalized and another one had been in need for intensive care treatment. Most of the IMID patients with a history of COVID-19 (n = 64; 79%) had not received specific treatment, and a smaller fraction (n = 15; 19%) had been treated in an outpatient setting [e.g., by a general practitioner, Table 1]. Specific therapy (e.g., antiviral agent) or nonspecific therapy (e.g., medication to lower fever) and self-administrated and/or prescribed pharmacologic treatment has not been reported in further detail.

Of the 81 IMID patients with a positive COVID-19 history, n = 20 (25%) fulfilled the WHO criteria for a post-COVID-19 condition (Soriano et al. 2022).[8] No difference in post-COVID-19 prevalence was seen between patients with RD and IBD (29 and 23%, respectively). The predominant complaint in our cohort was ongoing fatigue (n = 15, 19%). Any other symptom was less common with memory disorder being the second most common (n = 6, 7%). Impact of postcoronavirus disease 2019 condition on quality of life

Overall, QoL was reduced in patients with post-COVID-19 condition. The VAS of the EQ-5D-5L questionnaire (EQ-VAS) was found to be significantly higher in IMID patients without post-COVID-19 condition [mean 62 vs. 71, P = 0.0257, Figure 2 and Appendix Table 1]. Interestingly, the actual EQ-5D-5L index did not differ between post-COVID-19 and non-post-COVID-19 patients [mean 0.81 vs. 0.85, P = 0.15, Appendix Figure 1]. When evaluating the distinct domains of the EQ-5D-5L though, we only saw significantly more anxiety/depression in the post-COVID-19 group (P < 0.05) while we found trends, but no significant differences, in the other domains [usual activities and pain/discomfort, Appendix Table 2].

DISCUSSION

The COVID-19 pandemic came along with tremendous challenges. To the best of our knowledge, a systematic description of patients with different IMIDs and their individual COVID-19 as well as the sequelae of post-COVID-19 condition (as defined by the WHO) has not been published yet. Therefore, we initialized an investigator-initiated trial to better understand and quantify the impact of post-COVID-19 condition on QoL in IMID patients. Effects of post-COVID-19 in this large group might therefore contribute to lots of large-scale topics regarding health care and socioeconomic factors. The actual prevalence of post-COVID-19 condition remains unknown due to a variety of definitions and methods of analysis (e.g., hospitalized vs. outward patients, with/without control group). Furthermore, we assume that this condition in general is underdiagnosed. For this investigation, we used the current WHO definition for post-COVID-19 condition.[8]

We report by the results of our study that the prevalence of post-COVID-19 condition is high in IMID patients and is associated with significantly reduced QoL. Given that preexisting comorbidities increase susceptibility and the post-COVID-19 condition impairs QoL, these findings reinforce specific strategies on how to handle IMID patients in the course of the COVID-19 pandemic, and upcoming infectious diseases of similar manner.

The proportion of post-COVID-19 in our study is substantially higher than described by a large meta-analysis using data from 55 individual studies and including 1.2 million people [25% vs. 6.2%, P < 0.001, Table 4].[12] While we can only speculate on the actual reasons for this finding, one explanation seems likely: Hanson et al.[12] pooled different studies from 22 countries. Most of these studies were not restricted to IMID patients, on the contrary: The included trials considered people from the general population after COVID-19 infection. Therefore, we believe that the prevalence of post-COVID-19 is increased in patients with IMID. Indeed, there are reports that postacute COVID-19 symptoms are common in patients with RDs.[36] Data from the COVID-19 Global Rheumatology Alliance Vaccine Survey suggest that one in four patients with systemic RDs shows prolonged COVID-19 symptoms (28+ days),[37] which is in line with our finding of 29% for RD patients. As for patients with IBD, up to 40% have been reported with having long-COVID.[38] Unfortunately, comparison and interpretation are difficult since different time frames have been used to define the post-COVID-19 condition. The named studies used 28 days or 1 month instead of at least 3 months after acute infection as defined by the WHO. The same post-COVID definition as in our study was applied for rheumatic patients in a matched control study from the Netherlands. The authors found that 21% (n = 77) of rheumatic patients had post-COVID-19 symptoms, compared to 13% (n = 23) of healthy control participants (unadjusted odds ratio 1.53, P = 0.03). The fraction of patients with post-COVID-19 was similar to our findings. However, more than 20% of the prospectively recruited patients did not return the follow-up questionnaires to report sequelae of acute COVID-19, and no survey regarding QoL was included. Furthermore, due to slight differences in recruitment periods, their study took place almost exclusively during the omicron period, and the participants had an “advanced” vaccination status (83% with ≥3 vaccinations). During recruitment, the dominant SARS-CoV-2 subvariants were BA.2, and BA.4/BA.5[39] In our opinion, this limits comparability.[40]

Our main finding adds to the growing knowledge on post-COVID-19, also showing that symptoms persist far beyond 4 weeks.

Of interest, the severity of the preceding COVID-19 disease (e.g., intensive care unit admission) is reported to be associated with the development of post-COVID-19 condition.[4142] In our cohort, diseases courses were mild with only two hospitalized patients. While this can be explained by the high vaccination rate of 92% among our patients, the high post-COVID-19 prevalence might be related to the underlying IMID.

In concordance with the already mentioned meta-analysis (54 studies and >1.2 million patients,[12]), ongoing fatigue was the most common complaint in our cohort. All other post-COVID-19 symptoms were less common. Importantly, some rheumatic as well as IBDs are associated with fatigue[4344] and fatigue is also a core symptom of fibromyalgia, a common co-morbidity in RDs.[45] However, the study was designed to ask for symptoms that occurred newly or worsened considerably with or after acute COVID-19 disease. The reported fatigue is therefore first and foremost attributable to the post-COVID-19 condition.

A crucial result of our study is the reduced QoL in our cohort of IMID patients. The persistence of post-COVID-19-associated symptoms such as fatigue or memory disorder particularly impacts mental health with an increase of anxiety and depression (respective domain of the EQ-5D-5L and the PHQ-4 questionnaire). Other domains (e.g., mobility) and the summarized 5Q-5D-5L index show no differences between IMID patients with and without post-COVID-19 condition. Independent of this important finding, global QoL evaluated using a VAS (EQ-VAS), was significantly reduced in patients with post-COVID-19 condition. The noticeable EQ-VAS results might therefore be directly influenced by the anxiety/depression of post-COVID-19 patients, but did not affect the summarizing 5Q-5D-5L index [Appendix Table 3].

QoL is also reduced among the general population with post-COVID-19 condition.[344647] However, several investigations show that preexisting psychiatric conditions (including depression and anxiety disorders) might be a risk factor for the development of a post-COVID-19 condition.[404849] In addition, comorbidities in general seem to increase the risk for post-COVID-19 conditions, especially in patients with autoimmune diseases.[50] At the same time, chronic diseases such as IMID are often accompanied by depressions.[5152] Both, a high prevalence of post-COVID-19 condition as well as an impaired QoL with regard on depression and anxiety seems therefore plausible for IMID patients. In this context, preexisting mental co-morbidities might contribute to the high post-COVID-19 prevalence among our IMID cohort. This assumption remains speculative though, since psychiatric co-morbidities have not been recorded systematically in this study.

Our findings emphasize the potentially enduring implications of COVID-19 infections in patients with IMIDs. The globally increasing COVID-19 vaccination rates (e.g., in patients with RDs in high-income countries) are therefore reassuring and contribute to the protection of this vulnerable patient group from COVID-19 and its sequelae with potentially tremendous impact on the healthcare systems.[53] In this context, vaccine inequity due to low vaccine access in low-income countries is an issue on a global scale.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, the absolute number in the post-COVID-19 condition group is rather small, possibly limiting the meaning of our findings. Second, this investigation is questionnaire-based and might therefore be biased by the subjective nature of patient-reported findings (recall and reporting bias). The study was not designed to assess the individual activity of the underlying immune-mediated disease at the time of fulfilling the questionnaires, so we cannot draw any conclusions on a possible impact of disease activity on the persistence of post-COVID-19 condition. The heterogeneity of scoring systems for specific IMIDs would have made a comparison extremely difficult. This might have been a relevant profounder, as health-related QoL is influenced by disease activity.[5455] Other possible confounders (anemia, comorbidities, psychosomatic illness, etc.) were not addressed.

Regarding the used questionnaires, the EQ-5D-5L refers to the present day, while both, the PHQ-4 and the SIBDQ, refer to the last 2 weeks.

The high ratio of IBD patients compared to RD patients results from imbalanced internal patient recruitment. That is also the main reason for differences regarding usual IMID epidemiology in our cohort and is a major boundary toward generalizability, as only two medical disciplines were involved.

Self-reporting surveys at certain time points always imply memory bias, especially when subjective symptoms such as fatigue and tiredness are questioned. However, a continuous longitudinal study seems hardly feasible, even though it would be desirable.

CONCLUSIONS

The prevalence of post-COVID-19 condition is high in patients with IMID, exceeding its occurrence in the general population. While this poorly understood COVID-19 sequelae generally impacts QoL negatively, it seems to particularly hamper mental health in IMID patients. As we know so far, preexisting comorbidities increase the risk for post-COVID-19 conditions, potentially predisposing IMID patients for both, acquiring post-COVID-19 condition and showing a reduced QoL with particular attention on mental health.

Noninvasive prediction of lymphovascular invasion in rectal cancer without lymph node metastasis using a SHAP-interpretable combined model integrating MRI radiomics features and clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers: a bicenter study
Authors: Leping Peng, Jingjing Niu, Gang Huang, Fan Zhang, Fang Ma, Xiuling Zhang, Yu Wang, Kai Ai, Xiaoyue Zhang, Yuqi He, Wei Cai, Xiaona Zuo, Yingmei Jia, Shuhong Gao, Yuan-Cheng Wang, Lili Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLymphovascular invasion (LVI) status in rectal cancer (RC) without lymph node metastasis (LNM) can significantly influence the patient’s treatment decisions. This study aims to develop and validate a combined model based on MRI radiomics features integrated with clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers for the prediction of LVI status in RC without LNM. The Shapley Additive Explanation (SHAP) method was employed to visualize the prediction process and enhance interpretability for clinical application.MethodsWe retrospectively collected data from 257 RC patients without LNM from two centers. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed on clinical data to identify independent predictors of LVI. Volumes of interest were manually delineated on T2WI and ADC sequences, and corresponding radiomic features were extracted. A combined model was constructed by combining rad-score and clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers, and the SHAP was used to visualize the prediction process.ResultsThe area under the curve (AUC) of the combined model was based on intratumoral features (training vs. testing vs. validation datasets: 0.813 vs. 0.854 vs. 0.807). The AUC of the combined model was based on both intra- and peritumoral features (training vs. testing vs. validation datasets: 0.855 vs. 0.841 vs. 0.860). After comparison, the combined model (C + Q) based on intra- and peritumoral MRI radiomics features integrated with clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers demonstrated better predictive performance.ConclusionThe combined model (C + Q) has great potential in the non-invasive prediction of LVI in RC without LNM, providing a basis for stratified management and individualized treatment decisions for RC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-025-00946-1.Keywords: Rectal cancer, Lymphovascular invasion, Magnetic resonance imaging, Radiomics, Immune-inflammatory

Introduction

According to data released by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the incidence of colorectal cancer (CRC) has been increasing globally every year in recent years, while the mortality rate has slightly decreased [1]. This trend may reflect changes in risk factors and the continuous efforts in cancer screening, genetic testing, precise staging, and rational treatment. However, even with negative lymph node metastasis (LNM), rectal cancer (RC) still has a certain recurrence rate and poor prognosis, and one of the significant recurrence factors is lymphovascular invasion (LVI) [2]. LVI is defined as the presence of tumor cells in the endodermal lumen or the outward penetration of lymphatic vessel wall destruction, which is the initial manifestation of lymph nodes and other organ metastasis [3, 4]. Previous studies have reported that the LNM rate in LVI-positive (LVI+) patients is as high as 65.7%, compared to 40% in LVI-negative (LVI−) patients [5]. However, once lymph node metastasis occurs, it is classified as stage III RC, and the treatment strategy is subsequently adjusted [6]. Several guidelines recommend that RC patients with negative LNM but LVI+ should receive the same treatment protocols as patients with positive LNM, including radical RC resection or adjuvant chemoradiotherapy since they have the same high risk of recurrence [7–9].

Currently, the diagnosis of LVI predominantly relies on post-operative pathological analysis. While computed tomography (CT) is widely employed to evaluate LNM and distant metastases in CRC, its utility in detecting LVI is limited due to inadequate soft tissue resolution. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) offers superior soft tissue contrast. However, its diagnostic accuracy for vessels smaller than 3 mm in diameter remains suboptimal [10]. As a result, the precise preoperative assessment of LVI in RC based on purely morphological criteria remains a significant challenge.

Radiomics, an emerging interdisciplinary field between medicine and engineering, utilizes high-throughput imaging data to noninvasively analyze tumor heterogeneity and microenvironmental characteristics that are invisible to the naked eye [11]. Recent studies have demonstrated that radiomic features from both the intra- and peritumoral regions can effectively evaluate tumor biological behavior [12, 13]. In RC research, radiomics based on CT or MRI has demonstrated promising predictive value for LVI in patients with RC [14–16]. However, these studies included patients with LNM+ or those who had undergone neoadjuvant therapy, leaving the potential of MRI radiomics for predicting LVI in RC patients without LNM still worth further investigation.

In recent years, the close association between inflammation and cancer has become a focal point in research. Increasing evidence suggests that cancer-elicited inflammation not only reduces the sensitivity of CRC patients to radiotherapy and chemotherapy but is also closely related to poor differentiation, microvascular invasion, and poor prognosis [17, 18]. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation combining clinical indicators and immune-inflammatory biomarkers can provide a more complete reflection of changes in the tumor microenvironment and the immune status of patients. Cao et al. demonstrated that the inclusion of MRI radiomics in a combined model significantly improved the ability to predict early rapid metastasis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, compared to a clinical model based solely on blood biomarkers [19]. Additionally, Zhong et al. demonstrated that integrating NLR and other risk factors with radiomics features significantly enhanced the accuracy of the combined model in predicting microvascular invasion in hepatocellular carcinoma [20]. Despite these advances, research on the use of combined models integrating clinical characteristics, radiomics, and immune-inflammatory biomarkers to predict LVI in RC without LNM has not yet been reported.

Therefore, this study aimed to develop and validate a combined model integrating radiomics features and clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers to predict the LVI status in RC without LNM. Additionally, the combined model will utilize SHAP to intuitively interpret the predicted process for clinical application.

Materials and methods

Patients selection and data collection

We retrospectively collected data from 257 RC patients without LNM who underwent curative resection. The data spans from February 2016 to January 2024 at Gansu Provincial Hospital (Center 1) and from June 2017 to July 2024 at Zhongda Hospital Southeast University (Center 2). Inclusion criteria were: ⑴ Patients with a postoperative pathological diagnosis of rectal adenocarcinoma without LNM; ⑵ LVI status was confirmed by postoperative pathology with complete data available. ⑶The interval between the MRI examination and surgery was less than 2 weeks. Exclusion criteria were: ⑴ incomplete or unavailable clinicopathological information; ⑵ received anti-tumor therapy before MRI examination; ⑶ incomplete or missing MRI images; ⑷ poor image quality or heavy artifacts; ⑸ The MRI scan did not fully cover the tumor region or was invisible on MRI images. Patients from Center 1 were randomly divided into training and testing datasets at an 8:2 ratio, while RC patients from Center 2 served as the external validation dataset. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria for RC patients in this study are provided in Fig. 1. Detailed descriptions of the methods for collecting baseline clinical data and immune-inflammatory biomarkers calculation are provided in Supplementary material 1 (Appendix E1-E2).

Detailed flowchart of the inclusion and exclusion criteria for the patients in this study

Pathological reference standard

The pathology reports for all RC patients were sourced from electronic medical records. Two pathologists from Center 1, with 5 and 8 years of experience in gastrointestinal pathology, respectively, independently evaluated the histological slides of all patients from both centers, while being blinded to clinical and imaging data. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus with a third senior pathologist who had 10 years of experience in gastrointestinal pathology. LVI + was defined as the detection of tumor cells within lymphatic or blood vessels, thrombus formation within the vascular endothelium, or tumor cell infiltration into the lymphatic vessel wall, as identified through postoperative pathological analysis following radical RC surgery [15].

MRI acquisition

Patients from Center 1 underwent routine pelvic MRI scans using one of the following three MRI scanners: two 3.0T MRI scanners (Elition, Philips; Skyra, Siemens) and one 1.5T MRI scanner (Amira, Siemens). Patients from Center 2 underwent routine pelvic MRI scans using one of three MRI scanners: three 3.0T MRI scanners (Ingenia II, Philips; Vida, Siemens; Prisma, Siemens). Both centers used a 16-channel body coil for the scans. Both small field-of-view high-resolution T2WI and ADC sequences were acquired in the oblique axial plane. ADC images were automatically generated from diffusion-weighted imaging (b-values of 0 and 1000 s/mm²). The MRI scanner parameters for both centers are detailed in the Supplementary material 2 _Table S1.

Image preprocessing and segmentation

All original MRI images were appropriately pre-processed to minimize the center effect from different centers and scanners. First, an N4 bias field correction was performed on the images to remove bias field artifacts [21]. Second, image intensity values were normalized by setting the mean to 0 and the standard deviation to 1 [22]. Third, B-spline interpolation was used to resample the images to a uniform voxel spacing of 1 × 1 × 1 mm³. Finally, gray-level discretization was standardized by applying an absolute discretization method.

Two radiologists with 6 (Reader 1, X.M.M) and 8 (Reader 2, Y.M.J) years of experience in abdominal diagnosis independently evaluated the MRI imaging features. They were blinded to all clinical and histopathological information of the patients. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus.Two radiologists used the ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8; http://www.itksnap.org) to manually segment the intratumoral and intratumoral plus peritumoral volumes of interest (VOIs) slice by slice, carefully avoiding the normal bowel wall, intraluminal air, and fecal matter. The intra- and peritumoral VOI included the tumor and the adjacent fat region extending to the medial border of the mesorectal fascia, encompassing vessels and lymph nodes within this region [16]. Reader 1 segmented the images of 130 patients, while Reader 2 segmented the images of the remaining 127 patients. After two months, two radiologists randomly selected 20 patients for repeat image segmentation. The reproducibility of the extracted features was evaluated by the inter-class correlation coefficient.

Radiomics feature Extraction, Standardization, and selection

The PyRadiomics software package (version 3.1.0; https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/en/latest) was used for feature extraction, which included shape, first-order, texture features, and wavelet-transformed features. All radiomics features were standardized using Z-scores. Considering the imbalance between LVI+ and LVI- patients, the synthetic minority over-sampling technique was applied to balance the dataset. We followed a four-step procedure to identify robust radiomics features in the training dataset. First, features with high stability in the test-retest setting [inter-class correlation coefficient (ICC) > 0.80] were retained for further analysis. Second, statistical analyses were performed using the chi-square test and Mann–Whitney U test, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05. Third, Pearson correlation analysis (PCC) (|r| >0.90) was applied to ensure low collinearity among features. Finally, to prevent model overfitting, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) was used to select the final radiomics risk factors for predicting LVI. The penalty parameter was optimized using 5-fold cross-validation. In addition, to address the potential selection bias introduced by univariate analysis, we conducted a sensitivity analysis by comparing the features selected using LASSO alone (Method B) with those derived from the combined approach of univariate analysis followed by LASSO (Method A). Clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers

Clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers with a P-value < 0.1 in univariate logistic regression analysis were further analyzed using multivariate logistic regression. Biomarkers with a P-value < 0.05 were identified as independent predictors of LVI status and were subsequently incorporated into the construction of the clinical model (C). The Chord and Sankey plots (Supplementary material 2_Figure S2) respectively illustrate the weights of the optimal independent predictive factors across the three datasets and their association with LVI status.

Model Development, Validation, and clinical application

We examined clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers, radiomics, and combined LVI prediction models to demonstrate the clinical value of the radiomics model. The combined model was constructed using logistic regression (LR) by integrating the Rad-score and clinical immune-inflammatory risk biomarkers, including the intratumoral combined model (C + T) and the intratumoral plus peritumoral combined model (C + Q). The predictive performance of the model was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, DeLong test, and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI). The goodness-of-fit of the model was assessed using calibration curves, while the clinical applicability and net benefit were evaluated through decision curve analysis (DCA) and clinical impact curves (CIC). Correlations between the optimal clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers and radiomic features, as well as among the optimal radiomic features, were visualized using cluster heatmaps (Supplementary material 2_Figure S3). Based on the study results, we used SHAP to visualize and analyze the prediction process of the LVI models. To enhance the robustness and credibility of the interpretability results, the key features identified by SHAP were further validated using bootstrapping analysis and multicollinearity testing.

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of the data distribution. For normally distributed continuous variables, intergroup comparisons were conducted using the independent samples t-test. For continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages, and group comparisons for count data were performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The reliability and robustness of the extracted features were assessed using the ICC. ICC ≥ 0.80 was considered to indicate good interobserver agreement and robustness [23]. The Cut-off value was determined by maximizing the Youden index. Statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.7.3; https://www.python.org/) and R software (version 4.0.4; https://www.r-project.org/). Other figures were generated using Origin software (version 2022). All analyses were two-tailed, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Radiomics feature extraction, standardisation, and selection

For feature extraction, a total of 851 radiomics features were extracted from each VOI, including 18 first-order statistical features, 14 shape features, 24 Gy-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features, 16 Gy-level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features, 16 Gy-level run-length matrix (GLRLM) features, 5 neighboring gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features, 14 Gy-level dependence matrix (GLDM) features, and 744 wavelet-transformed features. For each patient, 3404 features were extracted across T2WI and ADC sequences from both the intratumoral VOI and the combined intra- and peritumoral VOI. For feature selection, intratumoral and intratumoral plus peritumoral radiomics features were first filtered based on ICCs, resulting in the exclusion of 50 and 53 features, respectively. Subsequently, feature selection was performed using Method A (Chi-square test, Mann-Whitney U test, PCC, and RFE) and Method B (PCC and RFE). Ultimately, Method A and Method B identified 18 and 19 radiomic features from the intratumoral VOIs, and 21 and 22 features from the combined intra- and peritumoral VOIs, respectively. The ICC values and feature coefficients of the optimal radiomic features selected by both methods are provided in Supplementary Materials 2 (Tables S2-S4). Comparative analysis revealed that the two feature sets showed a 95% overlap, and the models constructed using Methods A and B demonstrated no significant differences in performance [AUCs of Radiomics model (T): 0.790 vs. 0.789, P = 0.867; AUCs of Radiomics model (Q): 0.817 vs. 0.834, P = 0.598], as detailed in Supplementary materials 2 (Figure S4). Considering that method A involves relatively fewer features, exhibits stable performance, and can effectively reduce the risk of overfitting while improving computational efficiency, we adopted method A for subsequent analyses with reference to previous studies [13, 24].

Prediction models development and validation

ROC and DeLong tests showed that the AUC values of the combined models (C + T) and (C + Q) in the training, testing, and external validation datasets were 0.813 vs. 0.855, 0.854 vs. 0.841, and 0.807 vs.0.860, respectively. The DeLong test P-values were 0.311, 0.893, and 0.439, respectively. Through IDI analysis (Supplementary material 2_Figure S5) and evaluation of each model’s accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV, the results demonstrated that the combined model (C + Q) demonstrated better performance (Fig. 3, Table 3). In the training, testing, and external validation datasets, the Rad-score (Q) was significantly higher in the LVI+ group than in the LVI− group (all P < 0.01), as shown in Supplementary material 2_Figure S6. The calibration curves of the models were highly consistent with the ideal curve (Fig. 4a and c). The cut-off values of the combined model (C + Q) were 0.288, 0.442, and 0.228 for the training, testing, and external validation sets, respectively. DCA (Fig. 4d-f) demonstrated that the combined model (C + Q) provided substantial net clinical benefit across threshold probability ranges of 0.09–0.91, 0.08–0.94, and 0.08–0.90 in the training, testing, and external validation sets, respectively. Consistent results were observed in the CIC (Fig. 4g-i), further showing the potential clinical utility of the combined model (C + Q).

Discussion

In this study, we developed radiomics features for predicting LVI based on T2WI and ADC images. A combined model integrating clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers and radiomics features was constructed, and five models were compared to identify the optimal predictive model for predicting the LVI status in RC patients without LNM. The model’s performance was successfully validated in an external validation dataset consisting of 53 patients. Finally, we used SHAP to visualize the entire model prediction process, from global to local interpretability levels. These results indicate that the combined model (C + Q), incorporating Rad-score (Q), MLR, and EMVI, was a feasible tool for evaluating LVI status. Furthermore, the DCA thresholds and model-specific cut-off values provide important reference points for clinical decision-making. Clinicians can apply the indicators derived from the model to perform individualized risk assessments for each RC patient, accurately estimate the likelihood of LVI positivity, and implement stratified management and tailored clinical interventions based on the cut-off values of the combined model (C + Q) and the DCA thresholds.

Currently, accurately identifying LVI in RC remains a significant challenge with traditional radiological methods. While histopathological examination can determine LVI status, detecting LVI in small post-capillary veins and lymphatic vessels remains difficult. Immunohistochemical staining for CD31 and D2-40 has been shown to improve the accuracy of LVI diagnosis [25]. Early LNM primarily spread through lymphatic vessels and small venous channels [26]. LVI was considered a high-risk factor for treatment and prognosis in CRC patients [27]. For patients with LNM+ RC, radical resection is typically the standard treatment. For those who are inoperable, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy is commonly used. Similarly, LVI+ is also considered a high-risk factor for both treatment and prognosis in RC patients. When both LVI and LNM are negative, endoscopic resection is usually the preferred treatment option. However, for RC patients who are LNM- but LVI+, salvage radical resection is still required, and for those ineligible for salvage surgery, adjuvant chemoradiotherapy should be considered [28, 29]. Therefore, in LVI+ RC patients, in addition to radical surgery, preoperative neoadjuvant radiotherapy may further improve survival rates and prognosis.

CT and MRI examinations have been widely applied in the clinical practice of RC patients. However, evaluating micrometastasis and invasion within the lymphovascular system remains a significant challenge. Wong et al. [30] and Tong et al. [15] analyzed LVI using intratumoral MRI radiomics, while Ge et al. [16] and Li et al. [14] employed CT-based radiomics from both intra- and peritumoral regions to predict LVI status in RC. These prior studies consistently demonstrated that combined models exhibit good predictive performance, which is consistent with our findings. In comparison, our study also has the following advantages: Firstly, we analyzed RC patients without lymph node metastasis before treatment, which is more rigorous and scientific than the above studies that included LNM+ and adjuvant therapy. Furthermore, the systematic evaluation of comprehensive clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers in RC patients provides additional added value for guiding potential immunotherapy strategies. Secondly, we used two-parameter MRI images for radiomic analysis, which made the analysis of tumor phenotype and microenvironment status more comprehensive. Finally, by employing the SHAP interpretability method, the traditional “black-box” limitation of radiomics research is overcome, making the model’s clinical application more transparent and comprehensible.

Due to the significant tumor heterogeneity in RC, a single MRI parameter is insufficient for comprehensive quantitative and qualitative characterization of the tumor. Therefore, we employed a dual-parameter MRI analysis. T2WI primarily provides morphological features of cancer, while the heterogeneity of ADC reflects intra- and peritumoral heterogeneity [31, 32]. In this study, we constructed a radiomics model using an LR classifier. Given that the number of radiomic features far exceeds the sample size, although univariate preselection may introduce some bias, this strategy effectively reduces dimensionality and mitigates the risk of overfitting, and it has been widely applied in radiomics research. We further validated the robustness of our results through a sensitivity analysis. In the specific radiomics model (Q), the features with the highest weights were ADC_wavelet-LLH_firstorder_MeanAbsoluteDeviation and T2WI_original_shape_Maximum2DDiameterRow, suggesting that these two features contribute significantly to the predictive task. The feature ADC_wavelet-LLH_firstorder_MeanAbsoluteDeviation quantifies the dispersion of signal intensity in the peritumoral region, capturing microenvironmental heterogeneity, which may be attributed to stromal remodeling or inflammatory cell infiltration resulting from tumor invasion into adjacent vasculature. The shape feature T2WI_original_shape_Maximum2DDiameterRow represents the largest horizontal extent of the tumor on T2-weighted imaging. Tumors with larger diameters often exhibit a more expansive or infiltrative growth pattern. Our study demonstrated that the radiomics model (Q) has superior predictive performance. This could be attributed to the peritumoral region containing substantial biological information related to angiogenesis and immunogenicity, which provides additional value in identifying LVI [14, 16]. In clinical practice, clinicians often perform multiple biopsies from the tumor and its adjacent areas to obtain a comprehensive histopathological assessment. These samples may harbor important biological characteristics that warrant further investigation.

Our study demonstrated that EMVI was a risk predictor for predicting LVI. EMVI refers to the direct invasion of venous structures beyond the muscularis propria by the tumor, which is typically assessed on MRI images. As the tumor progresses, the incidence of both LVI and EMVI significantly increases, indicating an enhanced ability of RC to invade the vascular system and spread via hematogenous or lymphatic pathways. Numerous studies have shown that LVI and EMVI in RC are significantly associated with local recurrence, distant metastasis, poor prognosis, and reduced overall survival [28, 33, 34]. MLR is a commonly used peripheral blood inflammatory marker. Inflammatory cells can release various bioactive substances within the tumor microenvironment, while tumors, during their multistage progression, acquire survival advantages through mechanisms such as resisting immune responses, evading growth suppression, and enhancing immune evasion. Immunoinflammatory biomarkers, as key components of the tumor microenvironment, are closely associated with tumor progression. Lymphocytes, through the induction of cytotoxic cell death and cytokine secretion, inhibit tumor cell proliferation and migration, thereby controlling tumor growth [35]. Monocytes influence the tumor microenvironment by promoting immune tolerance, angiogenesis, and enhancing tumor cell dissemination [36]. Our study demonstrated that patients with LVI positivity had a higher MLR (74.7%), suggesting that this subgroup may exhibit a higher malignant potential and a worse prognosis. Moreover, the analysis of clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers in RC patients may further assist clinicians in optimizing immunotherapeutic strategies and improving patient prognosis. These biomarkers also provide a new direction for the development of novel immunotherapeutic agents.

In recent years, the “black box” phenomenon has been a critical issue hindering the development and interpretability of radiomics. Moreover, due to advances in modern predictive modeling techniques and algorithms, traditional nomogram approaches should be replaced by more contemporary summarization methods [37]. SHAP, a highly practical machine learning interpretation tool, enables visualization of the overall and individual contributions of each feature, thereby enhancing the clinical applicability of models and increasing clinicians’ confidence in using predictive tools [24, 38]. In our study, the weights and effects of three independent predictive features in the combined model (C + Q) were illustrated using SHAP. Case-based analysis further demonstrated the contribution of these features in individual instances, calculating the final Shapley value to derive the predicted probability, thereby achieving personalized prediction. Moreover, the interpretability of SHAP is inherently dependent on the training data distribution and may be influenced by class imbalance and feature collinearity. In this study, the synthetic minority over-sampling technique, multicollinearity testing, and 1,000 bootstrap analyses were conducted to verify the robustness of key features, thereby improving the reliability of the interpretability results.

Nevertheless, our study has some limitations. First, although this study included data from two centers, the overall sample size was relatively small. In particular, the external validation cohort comprised only 53 patients, which may affect the accuracy and stability of model performance evaluation. The limited sample size may also restrict the generalizability of the model across different populations and multicenter settings. Currently, we are conducting a prospective multicenter study, which will help further validate these findings. Second, we excluded patients who received neoadjuvant therapy and those with poor image quality, as treated and untreated tumors may differ in intratumoral heterogeneity, LNM, and LVI status, which could lead to variations in radiomic features. In addition, low-quality images cannot ensure the reliability of radiomic analysis. However, this may to some extent limit the applicability of the model in real-world clinical settings. Therefore, future studies with larger sample sizes, multicenter, and prospective designs, including more diverse patient populations, are needed to further validate and extend the clinical utility of this model. Third, although the VOI delineated in our study reached the medial edge of the rectal mesenteric fascia, the accurate determination of the optimal dilatation distance of the ROI around the tumor needs to be further investigated. Fourth, the manual segmentation of RC tumor and tumor plus mesangial regions was time-consuming. However, ongoing research in automated segmentation is expected to substantially reduce this workload in the future. Fifth, due to the relatively low prevalence of RC with LVI-, we included patients from two centers using different MRI scanners (1.5T and 3.0T), and applied image preprocessing steps such as N4 bias field correction and voxel resampling to reduce variability. Future studies should aim to standardize acquisition protocols across centers and validate these findings in larger prospective cohorts. Sixth, there were no missing clinical or hematological variables for the included RC patients, as strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied. In future studies, we aim to establish a more comprehensive clinical information database to ensure the completeness of patient data and enhance the reproducibility of our findings. Finally, the biological interpretability of radiomics remains challenging and requires further research to uncover the potential connections between radiomics, genomics, and proteomics.

Conclusions

We developed and validated a combined model (C + Q) that integrates intra- and peritumoral MRI-based radiomic features with clinical immune-inflammatory biomarkers to determine the LVI status in RC patients. SHAP provides a bridge to personalized prediction, potentially aiding clinical decision-making for individualized treatment and stratified management of RC patients.

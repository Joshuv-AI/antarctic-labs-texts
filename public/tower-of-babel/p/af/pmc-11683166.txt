Long‐term outcomes after cleft lift surgery for pilonidal sinus disease in post‐pubertal adolescents: data from a prospective Danish cohort
Authors: J L Ankersen, I K Faurschou, H T Hougaard, D Doll, C Oetzmann von Sochaczewski, M Sørensen, A G Pedersen, S Haas
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimPilonidal sinus disease (PSD) is a common condition. Despite a relatively large proportion of patients presenting as post‐pubertal adolescents, only small cohorts focusing on this PSD subcategory have been published, and surgical treatment remains challenging.MethodThe study is based on a prospective database established at Randers Regional Hospital in 2016. All patients undergoing Bascom's cleft lift (BCL) surgery from June 2016 until June 2020 were included in this study, focusing on patients ≤18 years of age comparing them to adult patients. Indications for BCL surgery were primary extensive manifestation, non‐healing disease after previous elective surgery or disease recurrence.ResultsIn total, n = 380 PSD patients were operated, of which n = 79 were post‐pubertal adolescents with a median age at time of surgery of 17.2 years (interquartile range 16.3, 18.0). Sixty‐six (84%) were boys. Twenty‐seven (34%) presented with primary extensive manifestation, 34 (43%) due to non‐healing wounds and 18 (23%) due to recurrence. Fifty‐four patients (69%) healed uneventfully. There was no difference between the indications of surgery groups in terms of overall healing or time to healing if prolonged (P = 0.6). The median follow‐up was 62 months (interquartile range 48, 73), with a 5‐year recurrence rate of 19%, compared to 17% (P = 0.6) in the adult population.ConclusionOur study is the first to investigate long‐term clinical outcomes of BCL surgery for PSD in post‐pubertal adolescent patients. Recurrences seem to occur more frequently compared to adults. However, we demonstrate that post‐pubertal adolescent patients with advanced PSD can be treated with BCL surgery with acceptable outcomes.Keywords: adolescent, Bascom's cleft lift, cleft lift surgery, healing, pilonidal disease, pilonidal sinus, pilonidal sinus disease, post‐pubertal, recurrence

What does this paper add to the literature?

This paper is the largest mono‐therapeutic trial in post‐pubertal adolescent pilonidal sinus disease proving Bascom's cleft lift safe and with acceptable outcomes. With two‐thirds of patients undergoing surgery due to non‐healing wounds or recurrence, data suggest that post‐pubertal adolescent pilonidal sinus disease patients are a sub‐population at higher risk of treatment failure.

INTRODUCTION

Pilonidal sinus disease (PSD) is a common condition with an estimated incidence of 26–48 per 100 000 [1, 2, 3]. The disorder most often affects the young population [2, 4], particularly boys [5, 6]. The condition is associated with a complicated disease process, and its management remains controversial [7].

Evidence points to a causal agent identified and pictured by electron microscopy—sharp [8], short stiff [9], occipital [10] hair fragments [11]. These sharp hair fragments are able to inject themselves into the skin [12, 13] in the upper third of the intergluteal fold if scale direction is adequate [13, 14]. Movement of the gluteus leads to drilling the sharp free hair inwards, leading to the formation of tract and cavity (sinus) [1]. Approximately 40% of symptomatic cases may initially manifest as an acute abscess, with classic symptoms of infection, including swelling and pain [2, 15]. The treatment of these is conventional and is based on lateral incision and drainage with secondary open healing [16]. Chronic PSD is characterized by pain, unpleasant‐smelling discharge and bleeding, causing discomfort and thus having a significant impact on the patient's quality of life, affecting education, social activities, interpersonal relationships and sexuality [17, 18]. Hence, the disease incurs a significant socioeconomic cost, with the affected patients spending considerable time away from school or work [19, 20].

Due to a lack of evidence, a gold standard approach has yet to be established, and the management of PSD in post‐pubertal adolescent patients remains controversial. The treatment often depends on the department policy, surgeon's preference and training background [7, 21], and very few published studies have studied paediatric or post‐pubertal adolescent PSD separately [20].

Our aim was to study the subgroup of patients ≤18 years of age in our prospective database, hypothesizing that post‐pubertal adolescent PSD patients treated with Bascom's cleft lift (BCL) surgery have a greater risk of recurrence compared to the adult PSD population (≥19 years of age). We also aimed to investigate whether BCL surgery yields safe and satisfactory outcomes in terms of the risk of recurrence in a well‐defined population.

METHODS

Study design and participants

At the Department of Surgery, Randers Regional Hospital, Denmark, a single‐centre prospective database was established on consecutive PSD patients undergoing BCL surgery in 2016. Since September 2010, the treatment of complicated PSD has been centralized and standardized in the Central Danish Region (catchment area of 1.2 million citizens) to Randers Regional Hospital, where patients with recurrence after previous elective surgery, failure to heal >3 months after surgery and primary extensive manifestation are offered BCL surgery for the condition regardless of age [22]. PSD was considered primary extensive if unsuitable for less invasive surgery (typically pit‐picking). All patients undergoing BCL surgery from June 2016 until June 2020 were included in this study, focusing on patients aged ≤18 years, comparing them to adult patients. Smokers were required to abstain from smoking and nicotine for at least 6 weeks before BCL surgery. Postoperatively, patients were followed up in the outpatient clinic at 2 and 12 weeks post‐surgery or until complete wound healing. Additional wound care was offered if needed. Subsequently, a retrospective follow‐up was conducted in June 2023.

Data collection

The patients were referred to our outpatient clinic, and the patient was registered in our Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) database if the indication for BCL surgery was found. The REDCap system is a secure online browser‐based software solution for building and managing online surveys and databases. Background information such as name, civil registration number, sex, American Society of Anesthesiologists' score, comorbidities, smoking habits, body mass index (BMI), disease duration, preoperative symptoms, previous surgeries and indication for current surgery were registered. Surgical details were added on the day of surgery. Follow‐up included outpatient appointments 2 and 12 weeks postoperatively, or until complete healing.

In June 2023, a retrospective follow‐up was conducted. Data were acquired through medical notes. In Denmark, all citizens are assigned a unique civil registration number given upon birth or immigration [23]. The registration number is linked to the electronic patient records nationwide, and our patient record system covers all the Central Danish Region. Based on journal entries, the following retrospective data were noted on all patients: date of last contact, time to healing, number of clinical visits postoperatively, recurrence upon return after cleft lift surgery, time of recurrence and, if applicable, date of repeat surgery. As our journal system covers all the region, all clinical contacts and surgeries were registered in the journal, resulting in a low risk of loss to follow‐up. The date of last contact was defined as the date of patient discharge from the outpatient clinic. If a patient was lost to follow‐up before complete healing, the date of last patient contact was noted. Loss to follow‐up in healing was registered as missing. Our patients' files are national and, as our department is the referral centre for recurrent and complex PSD, all recurrences were referred to our department, keeping the risk of missing data in the recurrence to a minimum. Subsequently, the population of post‐pubertal adolescent patients was analysed separately from the adult population.

Surgical procedure

The surgical principles of the cleft lift procedure as described by John Bascom were applied, with the exception that a Penrose drain was placed and left in for 3 days postoperatively [24]. Patients were treated as day surgery cases, operated under general anaesthesia. Intra‐operatively, 1000 mg of metronidazole and 1500 mg of cephalexin sodium were administered intravenously. This was followed by metronidazole 500 mg three times daily and ciprofloxacin 250 mg twice daily for 3 days postoperatively. Hairs were removed by family members or a primary sector nurse one to two times weekly until full healing. Four dedicated surgeons performed all surgeries.

Surgical outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was to assess recurrence. Recurrence was defined as a new PSD manifestation after complete initial healing. Secondary outcomes included time to recurrence, time to healing and gender differences. Uneventful healing was defined as healing present at the time of the second clinical follow‐up (<4 months postoperatively), knowing that most patients would have healed before then. If the patients had not healed at the time of second follow‐up (3–4 months postoperatively), they were defined as experiencing prolonged healing and followed until complete healing.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses and illustrations were conducted in RStudio, version 2023.03.0 [25], and R Software, version 4.3.2 [26]. Descriptive data are reported as medians (interquartile range, IQR) and compared by Wilcoxon rank sum test, Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test or Fisher's exact test. A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Cumulated incidence proportion plots were used to compare the cumulative incidence of age‐specific recurrence in the adult and post‐pubertal adolescent populations, and to compare gender‐ and indication‐specific recurrence within the post‐pubertal adolescent population using a Grey's test and illustrated in plots.

Ethics

The study was conducted in conformity with the Declaration of Helsinki [27] and approved by the National Data Protection Agency (case number 1‐16‐02‐353‐15). In Denmark, databases established for the purpose of quality control do not need approval from the ethics committee.

Recurrence

Overall, 15/79 post‐pubertal adolescent patients (19%) had recurrent pilonidal disease after cleft lift surgery over a median follow‐up of 62 months (IQR 48, 73). The median time to recurrence in adolescent patients was 11 months (IQR 4, 21) (Table 2). Compared to the adult population, the cumulative incidence of recurrence in post‐pubertal adolescent patients indicated a higher risk of recurrence in this patient category (Figure 1; Table 3). The risk of recurrence after 5 years in the post‐pubertal adolescent population was 19% (IQR 11, 28), compared to 17% (IQR 13, 21) in the adult population (P = 0.6). Median time to recurrence in the adult population was 8 months (IQR 5, 17) (Table 3). The risk difference between the two groups was 2.7% (95% CI −6.9, 12.3).

Healing

Fifty‐four patients (69%) healed uneventfully and concluded their follow‐up at 3–4 months. Among the rest, median time to healing was 7.5 months (IQR 5.8, 9.5) (Table 2). Evaluating the indication, 20 (74%) patients with primary extensive manifestation healed uneventfully, and median time to healing if prolonged was 7 months (IQR 5.5, 8). Among patients with a lack of healing after previous surgery as an indication, 21 patients (64%) healed uneventfully and median time to healing if prolonged was 9 months (IQR 7, 12). Among patients with recurrence as an indication 13 patients (72%) healed uneventfully, and median time to healing if prolonged was 5 months (IQR 5, 9). There was no significant difference between the groups with different indications for surgery in terms of overall healing (P = 0.7) or time to healing if prolonged (P = 0.3) (Table S1).

Gender‐specific approach

Indications for surgery showed that 85% of girls underwent BCL surgery due to either recurrence or lack of healing after previous elective surgery (Table 1). In terms of recurrence, the risk among girls was 31% (8.8%, 56%) compared to a risk of 17% (8.8%, 27%) in boys, with a risk difference of 14.1% (95% CI −12.6, 40.8) (P = 0.2) (Table 3). Median time to recurrence in girls was 5 months (IQR 3, 8) compared to 12 months (IQR 5, 23) in boys (Figure 3; Table S1).

DISCUSSION

This, to our knowledge, is the largest single centre mono‐therapeutic trial to investigate long‐term clinical outcomes on BCL surgery for PSD in post‐pubertal adolescent patients. We found that one in five experienced recurrence of PSD within 5 years following BCL surgery, indicating a higher risk compared to the adult population. Additionally, girls had a higher and earlier risk of recurrence compared to boys.

Recurrence

Post‐pubertal adolescent PSD has traditionally been treated according to the same principles as adults, which has overall led to high recurrence and complication rates. Therefore, surgical treatment of this PSD subcategory remains challenging due to lack of evidence in the field of surgical outcomes. Several studies have attempted to investigate clinical outcomes of different treatment procedures in the hope of finding the right treatment for these young people. Recent studies in the literature have been reporting clinical outcomes of treatment methods such as excision and primary midline closure [16, 20, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33], marsupialization [20, 30], excision and healing by secondary intention [20, 28, 30, 31, 34], minimally invasive surgical treatment (pit‐picking and paediatric endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment) [16, 20, 28, 29, 35, 36], off‐midline primary closure (Karydakis, cleft lift, flap closure) [20, 28, 31] and conservative treatment (hair removal, improved perianal hygiene and abscess drainage) [7].

Our study population seems comparable to previously published literature in terms of age at the time of surgery and gender distribution. Notably, whereas post‐pubertal adolescent PSD has previously been viewed as a rare occurrence through lenses of curiosity, patients aged ≤18 years constitute 20.7% of this cohort. Our 5‐year recurrence rate of 19% is notably lower than the literature's reported rates of up to 42% with flap closure and 58.8% after excision with secondary healing [31]. Oliveira et al. reported similar findings, with recurrence rates of 40.9% and 41.4% for minimally invasive surgical treatment (endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment and pit‐picking) and excision and primary closure, respectively [16]. Judging only by recurrence, the cleft lift procedure does seem to be both a safe and efficient treatment option for this patient subcategory.

Healing

Treating this cohort with BCL surgery, we found that 69% of patients healed uneventfully and remained without recurrence over a median follow‐up of 62 months. Prolonged healing was observed in more than 31% of patients, with a median time to healing of 7.5 months (IQR 5.8, 9.5) in this group. Within the available literature, this subject is only sparsely mentioned but the continuous wound care most often requiring assistance interrupts and impacts these young people's lives, and the substantial risk of prolonged healing must be taken into consideration when planning surgery. However, a recent study of patients' preference in PSD treatment showed that younger patients strongly preferred treatments with a lower risk of infection/persistence, so they were only willing to accept a small risk in exchange for a treatment with faster recovery [37].

Gender differences

Our study further indicates that this is of relevance for girls as 85% were referred due to lack of healing or recurrence, and 31% experienced recurrence after a median of 4.5 months, although there are many unexplored and possibly contributing factors to this difference that could be related to hormones and secondary gender characteristics. However, the results may also simply be biased, as the surgeon(s) may perform the BCL procedure differently in boys and girls. With a hypothetical increased focus on cosmesis as an outcome in girls, the excision may have been too narrow; thus, the lateralization of the wound was not sufficient. This would result in a cosmetically more acceptable appearance but at the cost of a higher risk of healing problems and recurrence. However, this would still not explain the shorter time to recurrence. A study by Madenci and Uysal revealed that female gender and increasing BMI in children and adolescents with PSD were significant and interdependent risk factors for the development of recurrence after surgery [32]. This is somewhat consistent with our study, where girls had a higher BMI (27.9 vs. 25.5) and recurrence rate than boys; however, numbers are too small to correlate. Prospective trials of post‐pubertal adolescent pilonidal patients will perhaps shed some light on this issue in the future.

Distinct course of disease?

Maasewerd et al. and Sequeira et al. were among the first studies to publish findings on early recurrence among adolescent patients suggesting a distinct disease pattern. They reported a median time to recurrence of 5.8 months in children and adolescents and 6.2 months in patients aged ≤18 years [28, 29]. Thus, their findings helped to clarify that early‐onset PSD does not manifest as in adults, therefore stressing the need for specialized care.

Our results further indicate that when using the same therapeutic approach post‐pubertal adolescent PSD patients seem to have a slightly higher risk of recurrence (risk difference of 2.7%) compared to the adult population. We also observed that promotors of higher recurrence are previous PSD and lack of healing after elective surgery. Furthermore, it is worth noting that, despite the young age, two‐thirds of patients were referred due to recurrence or lack of healing after previous elective surgery. This again stresses the need for specialized care and perhaps a selective approach upfront, as suboptimal treatment seems to increase risk of failure or recurrence further down the line.

The pattern of early recurrences has been attempted to be clarified in previous studies. Doll et al. suggested that patients with a positive family history of PSD should have closer surgical monitoring as the disease will manifest earlier and recurrences are seen more often compared to patients with no family history of PSD. Further, the same study found recurrence rates were elevated if primary surgery was needed at a younger age in patients with a positive family history (P = 0.03) [38]. This may argue for delaying treatment or at least excisional surgery until patients are older.

Strengths and limitations

Our study has the major advantage of a large population size in a mono‐therapeutic treatment. As far as we know, our study provides the longest follow‐up of BCL surgery in post‐pubertal adolescent patients. Another strength is the elimination of recall bias, as our study can establish the temporal sequence between exposure and outcome. Moreover, there is no need for any recollection of information since data are being collected in a prospective manner at baseline.

Our study also has several limitations. First, the postoperative assessment of time to healing has been difficult to determine, as patients may have healed before their appointment in the outpatient clinic 2 weeks and 3 months postoperatively. Secondly, due to the prospective design of the study, some patients may have been lost to long‐term follow‐up if they have moved outside of Denmark, so we cannot exclude that some recurrence events were missed for patients treated elsewhere. Therefore, the loss of follow‐up theoretically may have impacted the validity and precision of our results. Nevertheless, due to the diminishing recurrence in these patients after long‐term follow‐up, the loss of recurrences is estimated to be minor, as movements within Denmark have been fully captured by our health data registry. Finally, the overall (small) size of the post‐pubertal adolescent pilonidal patient group limited subcategorizing.

Future research is needed to further investigate the optimum treatment of pilonidal disease in post‐pubertal adolescents. Due to the lack of true comparative studies, it has not been possible to provide recommendations on which treatment is preferable, in terms of lower recurrence rate or reduced anatomical changes to bridge time to adolescence. Therefore, stressing the need for further large prospective randomized studies with long‐term follow‐up is one of our aims.

CONCLUSION

Our study found that BCL surgery is a safe method for treating post‐pubertal adolescent PSD with acceptable outcomes. Nevertheless, recurrences seem to occur at a higher rate in post‐pubertal adolescent patients compared to adults. Girls seem to be the most vulnerable subgroup here. The composition of our cohort, with two‐thirds of patients already experiencing surgical failure or recurrence before being referred to us, stresses the need for specialist treatment upfront and emphasizes education and training of surgeons conducting PSD surgery and a selective approach in this PSD subcategory.

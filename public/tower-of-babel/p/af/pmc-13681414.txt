A copy-number variation generates a long-noncoding RNA that enhances ClBige1b expression to suppress cell expansion and seed growth in watermelon
Authors: Jianfei Song, Dan Zhou, Jianshu Zheng, Zhanpeng Tang, Nannan Li, Mengya Cao, Jianli Shang, Shuangwu Ma, Na Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Seed size is a key agronomic trait in watermelon: large seeds are favorable for sowing, whereas small-seeded watermelons are preferred for consumption. However, the causal genetic variations and underlying mechanisms that govern seed size in watermelon remain poorly understood. Here, we identified a copy-number variation (CNV) tightly linked to seed size; a single copy is present in large-seeded watermelons, whereas four copies are present in small-seeded lines. Compared with the single-copy CNV, the four-copy CNV contains two additional TATA boxes and activates the transcription of a downstream long-noncoding RNA (C. lanatus lncRNA seed size 1 [ClLNC-SS1]) on the antisense strand. Knockout of ClLNC-SS1 in the small-seeded line resulted in larger seeds and repressed the expression of ClBige1b, which is located downstream of the CNV on the sense strand. ClBige1b encodes a multidrug and toxic compound extrusion transporter, and its overexpression in a large-seeded line resulted in smaller seeds. By contrast, knockout of ClBige1b increased seed size in a small-seeded line, markedly increasing cell expansion in the exocarp, endocarp, and endosperm by reducing auxin contents in the developing seeds. Our findings demonstrate that the newly identified CNV regulates seed size via the ClLNC-SS1–ClBige1b module in watermelon, providing novel insight into the regulation of seed size and laying a foundation for its genetic improvement in watermelon and other crops.

Introduction

Seeds serve as both the reproductive organs of angiosperms and as a human food source. Given the adaptive and agronomic importance of seed size, clarifying its genetic and molecular regulatory mechanisms has long been a focus of plant science research. Hundreds of genes and regulatory factors that influence seed size have been identified in multiple species, especially Arabidopsis and rice, and their functional relationships have been summarized in excellent reviews (Orozco-Arroyo et al., 2015; Li and Li, 2016; Li et al., 2019). Nonetheless, although significant progress has been made in understanding the mechanisms that regulate seed size, these regulatory pathways have not been fully integrated, and the molecular mechanisms of many regulatory components remain unclear.

Copy-number variations (CNVs), a major type of genomic structural variation (SV), are an important source of the genetic variation that drives phenotypic diversity and contributes to adaptive traits (Tang and Amon, 2013; Li et al., 2025). However, only a small number of CNVs have been clearly demonstrated to regulate agronomically important traits in major crops (Lye and Purugganan, 2019). CNVs mediate nematode resistance in soybean through multiple genes at the Rhg1 locus (Cook et al., 2012), underlie natural diversity in rice grain size at the GL7 locus (Wang et al., 2015), and enhance maize aluminum tolerance by increasing multidrug and toxic compound extrusion transporter 1 (MATE1) copy number (Maron et al., 2013). They also regulate wheat plant height (Li et al., 2012), cotton architecture at the HPDA-D12 locus (Ji et al., 2021), and gynoecy in cucumber involving four genes at the female locus (Zhang et al., 2015). SV landscapes have been characterized on the basis of the Citrullus super-pangenome (Zhang et al., 2024), and we previously constructed a genome-wide SV map for watermelon, identifying a CNV that acts as a genetic switch to modulate red carotenoid accumulation and fruit flesh color (Li et al., 2025). However, the roles of most watermelon CNVs, especially in the regulation of seed size, remain to be characterized.

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), which are transcripts of >200 nt, have garnered considerable interest owing to their critical roles in diverse biological processes (Katayama et al., 2005; Kopp and Mendell, 2018). On the basis of their genomic locations, lncRNAs are classified into several subtypes, such as intergenic and enhancer lncRNAs (St Laurent et al., 2015; Hou et al., 2019). Although lncRNAs are ubiquitous in eukaryotic genomes (Quek et al., 2015; Palos et al., 2023), only a small fraction have documented biological roles, and fewer than 1% of human disease-associated lncRNA loci have been functionally characterized (Uszczynska-Ratajczak et al., 2018). Important discoveries related to plant lncRNAs over the past three decades have been reviewed previously (Palos et al., 2023). However, only two lncRNAs in soybean have been found to regulate seed weight (Wang et al., 2025), and the potential regulation of seed size by lncRNAs requires further study.

Watermelon is mainly consumed as a fresh fruit, valued for its juicy, sweet, and nutrient-dense flesh, but seeds in the pulp reduce its eating convenience, making the development of seedless or small-seeded watermelons a key breeding goal. By contrast, seed-oriented watermelon varieties, whose seeds are the edible product, require larger seeds. Several quantitative trait loci (QTLs) or genes associated with seed-size-related traits have been mapped on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, and 11 (Prothro et al., 2012; Meru and McGregor, 2013; Ren et al., 2014; Kim et al., 2015b; Li et al., 2018, 2021; Osae et al., 2021; Gong et al., 2022). These include a major QTL on chromosome 6 that has been detected consistently across multiple genetic backgrounds (Prothro et al., 2012; Meru and McGregor, 2013; Ren et al., 2014; Li et al., 2018; Osae et al., 2021) and a 13.96-kb deletion encompassing two genes on chromosome 2 that is associated with seed size (Li et al., 2021). To date, ClBG1 is the only seed-size-related gene in watermelon whose function has been confirmed by CRISPR–Cas9 gene editing (Wang et al., 2021), but its causal variant is unknown.

In a previous study, we identified the major QTL qSS6, which accounted for over 90% of the phenotypic variation in seed size in a segregating population (Li et al., 2018), but its key variant and causal gene remained to be identified. Here, we identified a CNV in the qSS6 genomic region that is strongly associated with seed-size-related traits and demonstrated that this CNV regulates seed size via C. lanatus lncRNA seed size 1 (ClLNC-SS1) and ClBige1b. Our results provide new information on seed size regulation and have implications for the improvement of seed size during watermelon breeding.

Results

A CNV is highly associated with seed size in watermelon

Our previous data suggested that watermelon seed size was strongly associated with a 55.5-kb narrow QTL region (Li et al., 2018). Here, using a published single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) and SV map (Li et al., 2025), we performed a genome-wide association study (GWAS) for seed size-related traits in watermelon: 1000-seed weight (TSW), seed length (SL), seed width (SW), and seed thickness (ST). SNP-GWAS identified one SNP signal (SNP6_5422090) on chromosome 6 that was highly associated with seed size in almost all datasets (Supplemental Table 1) and overlapped with a previously reported major QTL (Li et al., 2018). In addition, three SNP signals (SNP5_914783, SNP5_915383, and SNP5_915412) were associated with SW at Zhengzhou in 2018, and one SNP signal (SNP1_13808727) was associated with TSW at Sanya in 2019 (Supplemental Table 1).

An SV (SV6_5436179) within the major QTL interval was highly associated with all seed-size-related traits (Figures 1A and 1B; Supplemental Table 1). By analyzing re-sequencing data mapped to the reference genome of DR117 (a large-seeded line) (Li et al., 2025), we observed markedly higher sequencing depth in the SV region, whereas no significant change was detected in the large-seeded lines (Supplemental Figure 1). A comparison of the reference genomes of 97103v2 (a small-seeded line) (Guo et al., 2019) and DR117 revealed that SV6_5436179 was a CNV (CNV6_5436179; Figure 1C). The CNV comprised a single ∼230-bp repetitive unit in large-seeded lines, whereas small-seeded watermelons contained four tandem copies of this unit (Figure 1C). Although these minimal repetitive units were clearly homologous, their lengths varied from 220 to 239 bp owing to extensive differences at the 5′ terminus, an internal variable region, and the 3′ terminus (Supplemental Figure 2). These polymorphisms were mainly caused by widespread insertions and deletions, together with a single nucleotide substitution (Supplemental Figure 2). Given these differences, we designated the units Dup1, Dup2, and Dup3, respectively. Dup1 was present exclusively in large-seeded watermelons, whereas the tandem repeat Dup1231 occurred specifically in small-seeded lines (Figure 1C; Supplemental Figure 2). Because SNP6_5422090 was located in the 5′ UTR of Chr6.g12175 and did not alter its transcription, we next analyzed the CNV in 196 core watermelon accessions. Six of the seven watermelon (Citrullus) species contained only Dup1: Citrullus naudinianus, C. rehmii, C. ecirrhosus, C. colocynthis, C. amarus, and C. mucosospermus. However, the CNV differed in the seventh species, C. lanatus, which contained either Dup1 or Dup1231. The TSW, SL, SW, and ST of C. lanatus with Dup1 were 104.84 ± 24.39 g, 12.39 ± 1.04 mm, 7.54 ± 0.73 mm, and 2.22 ± 0.16 mm, respectively. By contrast, the values of these traits were significantly lower in C. lanatus with Dup1231 (49.08 ± 8.66 g, 8.87 ± 0.75 mm, 5.59 ± 0.45 mm, and 1.95 ± 0.10 mm) (Figure 1E). Sanger sequencing revealed that the ultrasmall-seeded germplasm CR113 had more than four copies of the CNV segment, as confirmed using a single PCR-based marker derived from the CNV (Supplemental Figure 3).

CNV6_5436179 influences upstream ClLNC-SS1 transcription to regulate seed size

To investigate how CNV6_5436179 influences seed size, we analyzed the sequences of Dup1 and Dup1231 using Promoter 2.0 and ElemeNT v.2023. The reverse complementary sequence of Dup1 (Dup1-R) contained two Inr elements and one BRE upstream (DRE) element. Although the Dup1231-R sequence contained the same number of Inr and DRE elements on each unit, it also harbored an additional TATA-box element on both Dup2-R and Dup3-R (Figure 2A) and contained more transcription factor binding sites than Dup1-R (Supplemental Figure 2). Assay for transposase-accessible chromatin using sequencing (ATAC-seq) revealed that the small-seeded line 14CB11, which carries Dup1231, exhibited higher spatial openness of chromatin than the large-seeded line ZXG01478, which carries Dup1 (Figure 2B). To test this hypothesis, we performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) of two recombinant inbred lines (RIL_L, large-seeded line; RIL_S, small-seeded line) derived from large- and small-seeded parents (Li et al., 2018). The two RILs differed in seed size but had comparable values of other traits and highly similar genetic backgrounds (Supplemental Tables 2 and 3). Notably, a transcript upstream of CNV6_5436179 was found only in RIL_S and had a full length of 1.6 kb as determined by Rapid Amplification of cDNA Ends assays (Figures 2C and 2D). Strand-specific RNA-seq (lncRNA-seq) (Supplemental Table 3) revealed that this transcript was transcribed in the reverse direction relative to the downstream gene and that the CNV was located immediately upstream of its transcription start site (Figure 2E). Four bioinformatics tools, PLEK, CNCI (Coding-Non-Coding Index), Pfam, and CPC (Coding Potential Calculator) (Supplemental Table 4), indicated that this transcript lacked coding potential, and we therefore concluded that it was an lncRNA. Because it was the first lncRNA found to modulate seed size in watermelon, we designated it ClLNC-SS1.

We next performed a dual-luciferase reporter (DLR) assay (Figure 2F), which showed that the Luciferase/Renilla (LUC/REN) ratio driven by Dup1231-R was higher than that driven by Dup1-R. Addition of a sequence containing a TATA box to Dup1-R (Dup1+13-R) significantly increased the LUC/REN ratio, whereas deletion of the TATA boxes in Dup2 and Dup3 of Dup1231-R greatly reduced the LUC/REN ratio. In addition, although Dup1111-R had a higher LUC/REN ratio than Dup1-R, it was still lower than that of Dup1+13-R (Figure 2F). These results indicated that Dup1231 has a stronger ability to activate ClLNC-SS1 transcription, owing to the presence of the Dup2/Dup3-specific TATA boxes.

To explore its function, we knocked out ClLNC-SS1 in the small-seeded germplasm 14CB11 by CRISPR–Cas9 editing and obtained two stable T2 lines with reduced ClLNC-SS1 expression (Figure 2G). Both of the knockout lines had larger seeds with a greater TSW, SL, and SW relative to wild-type (WT) 14CB11 seeds (Figures 2H and 2I), suggesting that ClLNC-SS1 suppresses seed growth in watermelon.

ClLNC-SS1 promotes downstream ClBige1b expression to regulate seed size

Intriguingly, the expression levels of ClBige1b and ClLNC-SS1 were significantly correlated (R2 = 0.8379) in 11 small-seeded and 5 large-seeded watermelons (Figure 3B), implying that ClLNC-SS1 may positively regulate ClBige1b expression. To assess this hypothesis, we performed a DLR experiment and observed that LUC activity was significantly higher when driven by a construct containing the ClBige1b promoter fused downstream of ClLNC-SS1–Dup1231 than by three other constructs containing the ClBige1b promoter (Figure 3C). In addition, knockout of ClLNC-SS1 significantly reduced ClBige1b expression (Figure 3D). Together, these findings suggest that ClLNC-SS1 promotes the expression of the downstream gene ClBige1b in watermelon.

ClBige1b expression was highest in seeds at 9 DAF and young leaves, followed by tender stems and roots (Figure 4A). To explore the effect of ClBige1b on seed growth, we knocked it out in the small-seeded watermelon germplasm Du1 and obtained 3 stable T2 lines with significant reductions in ClBige1b expression (Figure 4B). The ClBige1b knockout lines exhibited larger seed size, higher TSW (increased by 62.50%–120.83%), and greater SL and SW compared with WT Du1 (Figures 4C and 4D). We also obtained three ClBige1b-overexpressing lines in the larger-seeded ZXG01478 background. Both ClBige1b expression and ClBige1b protein levels were significantly higher in ClBige1b-overexpressing lines than in WT ZXG01478 (Figures 4E and 4F). ClBige1b restricts seed growth by inhibiting cell expansion

To investigate the cytological basis of seed-size regulation by ClBige1b, we observed transverse sections of WT and ClBige1b-knockout seeds. A comparative transcriptome analysis (Supplemental Table 3) of the WT and ClBige1b-knockout lines revealed 2580 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), 1290 downregulated and 1290 upregulated in the ClBige1b-knockout lines. Likewise, a similar analysis revealed 2344 DEGs between RIL_S and RIL_L, 1256 downregulated and 1088 upregulated in the large-seeded line RIL_L.

Four hundred and fifty-five DEGs were shared between the WT vs. ClBige1b-knockout and RIL_S vs. RIL_L comparisons (Figure 5D). Their top five COG functional classifications were “carbohydrate transport and metabolism,” “signal transduction mechanisms,” “defense mechanisms,” “general function prediction only,” and “lipid transport and metabolism,” and their top five KOG functional classifications were “general function prediction only,” “carbohydrate transport and metabolism,” “signal transduction mechanisms,” “secondary metabolite biosynthesis, transport and catabolism,” and “posttranslational modification, protein turnover, chaperones” (Supplemental Figure 4). RNA-seq and reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT–qPCR) showed that key genes associated with acid growth were significantly upregulated in the RIL_L and ClBige1b-knockout lines. These genes function in auxin signaling (ClIAA16 and ClIAA27), H+-ATPase activity (ClAHA11), and aquaporin transport (ClTIP1-1) (Figure 5E; Supplemental Table 5). Together, these results suggest that ClBige1b limits seed growth by inhibiting cell expansion.

Discussion

Seed size is an important trait in watermelon breeding because of its significant effect on fruit quality and marketability. Although several QTLs associated with seed-size-related traits have been identified (Prothro et al., 2012; Meru and McGregor, 2013; Ren et al., 2014; Kim et al., 2015b; Li et al., 2018, 2021; Osae et al., 2021), the mechanisms by which natural variations regulate seed size remain largely unexplored. Here, we identified a CNV associated with watermelon seed size using SV-GWAS (Figure 1) and demonstrated that its four-copy version activates ClLNC-SS1 transcription to promote ClBige1b expression, thereby inhibiting cell expansion and reducing seed size (Figures 2, 3, 4, and 5).

The effect of CNVs on gene expression can be either positively (Ji et al., 2021; Li et al., 2025) or negatively (Stranger et al., 2007; Wang et al., 2015) correlated with copy number. Triplication of a 1.2-kb segment in the ClREC2 promoter strongly enhances its expression in watermelon (Li et al., 2025), and tandem duplication of a 17.1-kb segment at the GL7 locus upregulates its expression while downregulating that of a nearby gene in rice (Wang et al., 2015). Here, ClLNC-SS1 transcription was detected only in small-seeded lines, owing to the ability of the four-copy CNV to activate ClLNC-SS1 transcription (Figure 2E). BRE, Inr, and the TATA box are essential core promoter elements that regulate transcription initiation. BRE modulates the efficiency of transcription complex assembly, Inr defines transcription start sites and drives basal transcription, and the TATA box initiates transcription by binding TATA-box binding protein. Sequence mutation of the TATA box greatly alters transcriptional activity, highlighting its crucial role in gene transcription (Roy and Singer, 2015; Sun et al., 2023). Dup1-R contained two Inr elements and one BRE element. Dup2-R and Dup3-R contained the same numbers of Inr and DRE elements as Dup1-R, but each also possessed an extra TATA-box element (Figure 2A). The addition of a TATA-box sequence significantly increased the transactivation activity of Dup1, whereas deletion of the TATA-box in Dup2 and Dup3 greatly reduced the transactivation activity of Dup1231 (Figure 2F). Combined with the enhanced chromatin accessibility within the CNV region and the markedly higher ClLNC-SS1 expression in small-seeded 14CB11 compared with large-seeded ZXG01478 (Figure 2B), these results suggest that Dup1231 activates the transcription of ClLNC-SS1, likely owing primarily to the presence of additional TATA boxes. An lncRNA that regulates gene expression and influences grain size has been identified in rice (Wang et al., 2018). Here, knockout of ClLNC-SS1 in small-seeded lines strongly downregulated ClLNC-SS1 expression and gave rise to larger seeds with increased TSW, SL, and SW (Figures 2G–2I). These findings suggest that the four-copy CNV confers a small-seeded phenotype by triggering ClLNC-SS1 transcription.

Plant MATE proteins participate in a wide range of physiological processes by extruding various substrates (Takanashi et al., 2014; Upadhyay et al., 2019), and the MATE protein Bige1 is involved in the regulation of seed size in rice (Qin et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2023), maize, and Arabidopsis (Suzuki et al., 2015). ClBige1b, a gene located downstream of the CNV region, shared high homology with Arabidopsis Bige1b and was highly expressed in developing seeds (Figures 3A and 4A). It also exhibited greater expression in the small-seeded line relative to the large-seeded line (Figure 2C). Knockout of ClBige1b in the small-seeded line resulted in larger seeds, whereas overexpression of ClBige1b in the large-seeded line produced smaller seeds (Figures 4B–4G), demonstrating that ClBige1b negatively modulates seed growth. Notably, the expression levels of ClBige1b and ClLNC-SS1 were significantly correlated in 11 small-seeded germplasm accessions (Figure 3B). Only the presence of both ClLNC-SS1 and Dup1231 activated a reporter gene driven by the ClBige1b promoter, and ClBige1b expression was markedly downregulated in ClLNC-SS1-knockout lines (Figures 3C and 3D), indicating that ClLNC-SS1 increases ClBige1b expression in small-seeded lines. Plant lncRNAs play diverse regulatory roles through distinct mechanisms, including mediating chromatin remodeling, serving as molecular scaffolds, acting as competing endogenous RNAs to sequester microRNAs and alleviate repression, and modulating mRNA posttranscriptional processes (Palos et al., 2023). For example, lincRNA6679 recruits FnWRKY50 and FnMYB59 to cis-activate downstream FnWRKY14 and improve resistance to Botrytis cinerea in strawberry (Guan et al., 2026), and lncRNA1 regulates branch number by sequestering miR6288b-3p in peach (Wang et al., 2024). Sequence alignment did not reveal any complementary sites between ClLNC-SS1 and the promoter or coding region of ClBige1b, and no microRNAs were predicted to target ClBige1b. In addition, their genomic distance was greater than 12 kb (Figure 2D). We therefore propose that ClLNC-SS1 may increase ClBige1b expression by facilitating chromatin loop formation or functioning as a molecular scaffold, possibilities that require further investigation. Because Dup1231 activates ClLNC-SS1 transcription and both ClLNC-SS1 and ClBige1b negatively modulate seed growth (Figures 2 and 4), it is reasonable to conclude that the transcription of ClLNC-SS1 is activated owing to the presence of Dup1231, thereby increasing ClBige1b expression and repressing seed growth.

Seed growth relies primarily on cell division and expansion (Sreenivasulu and Wobus, 2013). Knockout of ClBige1b greatly increased cell area in the exocarp, endocarp, and endosperm (Figures 5A and 5B), confirming that ClBige1b negatively modulates seed growth by inhibiting cell expansion. Cell expansion is governed by multiple factors, including hormones, H+-ATPase, aquaporins, and cell-wall-loosening enzymes. At present, its underlying mechanism can be largely explained by the acid growth model, in which auxin is a critical initiator (Du et al., 2020). Here, knockout of ClBige1b greatly increased levels of IAA and its derivatives in developing seeds (Figure 5C). Moreover, RNA-seq revealed that four auxin-related genes in the acid growth pathway, including auxin-inducible genes (ClIAA16 and ClIAA27), the H+-ATPase gene ClAHA11, and the aquaporin gene ClTIP1-1, were markedly upregulated in large-seeded lines and ClBige1b-knockout lines (Figures 5D and 5E). Because ClBige1b encodes a MATE protein (Figure 4A), we reasoned that it may restrict cell expansion by promoting auxin efflux, thus downregulating the expression of genes in the acid growth pathway.

On the basis of our findings, we propose a working model for seed-size regulation by ClLNC-SS1 in watermelon (Figure 6). In large-seeded watermelons, Dup1 is insufficient to activate ClLNC-SS1 transcription, resulting in low ClBige1b expression. This maintains appropriate auxin levels in developing seeds, driving cell expansion to form large seeds. By contrast, in small-seeded watermelons, extra TATA boxes enable Dup1231 to induce ClLNC-SS1 transcription and further upregulate ClBige1b. Increased ClBige1b expression reduces auxin levels in developing seeds, suppressing the auxin-mediated acid growth pathway and ultimately resulting in small seeds. Methods

Plant materials and phenotyping

A core collection of 196 watermelons (Li et al., 2025) was cultivated in Zhengzhou (Henan, China), Changji (Xinjiang, China), and Sanya (Hainan, China) in 2018 and 2019. Specifically, 129 C. lanatus accessions were evaluated at six locations, and the remaining accessions were evaluated in Zhengzhou in 2018. At each location, three replicates of 10 plants each were planted.

The parental lines (ZXG01478, large seeded; 14CB11, small seeded) and their derived RILs (RIL_L, large seeded; RIL_S, small seeded) were grown in Zhengzhou in 2018 and 2024. For ATAC-seq, ZXG01478 and 14CB11 seeds were collected at 9 DAF. For RNA-seq, RIL_L and RIL_S seeds were collected at 9 and 12 DAF. For lncRNA-seq, RIL_L and RIL_S seeds were collected at 9 DAF.

The small-seeded watermelon Du1 expressing the empty vector (EV1) or mutated ClBige1b (T2) and the large-seeded watermelon ZXG01478 expressing the empty vector (EV2) or mutated ClLNC-SS1 (T2) were planted in Zhengzhou (Henan, China) in 2024 and 2025. Seeds of EV1 and the ClBige1b-knockout mutant were collected at 9 DAF for RNA-seq.

For seed phenotyping, TSW was calculated from the average weight of 50 fully developed seeds per fruit. SL, SW, and ST were measured using vernier calipers on five randomly selected seeds per fruit, and data were recorded using SC-G software (Wanshen Detection Technology, Hangzhou, China).

GWAS analysis

GWASs were performed for seed size-related traits using the SNP and SV datasets. The association analysis for SNPs was performed using the mixed linear model (MLM) method in TASSEL software (Bradbury et al., 2007). The Q matrix was determined using STRUCTURE (Falush et al., 2003), and the pairwise kinship coefficients were estimated in TASSEL. The association analysis for SVs was performed using the MLM, Generalized Linear Model (GLM), Compressed Mixed Linear Mode (CMLM), and FarmCPU models in GAPIT (Lipka et al., 2012), which also integrates principal-component analysis and kinship analysis. A stringent Bonferroni correction was used to screen clear association signals based on p values (significance threshold: −log10(p) ≥ 7.35 for SNPs and 6.13 for SVs) divided by 0.05.

Total RNA isolation and RT–qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from watermelon flesh samples using the QIANEN RNase Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen). RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Qiagen), and RT–qPCR was performed as described previously (Li et al., 2018) using the watermelon actin gene as the reference gene. The primer sequences are listed in Supplemental Table 6.

RNA-seq

RNA concentration and purity were determined using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed using the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq platform to generate 150-bp paired-end reads. Raw reads were processed on the BMKCloud bioinformatics platform (www.biocloud.net). Clean reads were mapped to the watermelon 97103v2 reference genome (Guo et al., 2019) using HISAT2 (Kim et al., 2015a). DEGs between two groups were identified using DESeq2 (Love et al., 2014) with the criteria |log2(fold change)| > 1 and false discovery rate < 0.01. Gene functional annotation was performed using the Nr, Pfam, KOG/COG, Swiss-Prot, KO, and GO databases.

lncRNA-seq, full-length cloning, and bioinformatic analysis of ClLNC-SS1

Total RNA after rRNA removal was used to construct sequencing libraries with the NEBNext Ultra Directional RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, with index codes added to assign sequences to individual samples. Index-coded samples were clustered on the acBot Cluster Generation System using the TruSeq PE Cluster Kit v.3-cBot-HS (Illumina). After clustering, libraries were sequenced on the Illumina platform to generate paired-end reads.

Sequences were processed using the bioinformatics pipeline on the BMKCloud online platform (www.biocloud.net). The transcriptome was assembled with StringTie (Kim et al., 2015a) on the basis of reads mapped to the reference genome (Guo et al., 2019). Assembled transcripts were annotated using gffcompare, and unknown transcripts were screened to identify putative lncRNAs.

The full-length sequence of ClLNC-SS1 was obtained using RACE technology (Accurate Biotechnology [Hunan], ChangSha, China). Four computational tools (PLEK, CNCI, Pfam, and CPC) were combined to distinguish non-protein-coding RNA candidates from putative protein-coding RNAs in the unknown transcripts. PLEK (Li et al., 2014), which predicts lncRNAs via k-mer profiling, and CNCI (Sun et al., 2013), which analyzes adjoining nucleotide triplets, were used to differentiate lncRNAs from mRNAs. Transcripts were searched against the Pfam (Finn et al., 2015) and NCBI protein databases using BLASTP, and the protein-coding potential of the remaining transcripts was assessed using CPC (Kong et al., 2007).

Analysis of regulatory elements in CNV sequences

Core promoter elements in the CNVs were analyzed using the Elements Navigation Tool (https://www.juven-gershonlab.org/). Putative transcription factor binding sites were identified using the NSITE-PL tool (http://www.softberry.com/berry.phtml?topic=nsitep&group=programs&subgroup=promoter).

ATAC-seq

ATAC-seq was performed as described previously (Bajic et al., 2018). In brief, nuclei were isolated and tagmented with Tn5 transposase, and libraries were amplified and sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform to generate 150-bp paired-end reads. Raw reads were trimmed using Trimmomatic, aligned to the watermelon 97103v2 genome (Guo et al., 2019) with Bowtie 2 (Langmead and Salzberg, 2012), and processed with samtools (Li et al., 2009) to retain high-quality proper pairs. PCR duplicates and organelle reads were removed. Chromatin integrity and transcription start site enrichment were evaluated using R. Peak calling was performed with MACS2 (--nomodel --shift −75 --extsize 150), and peak reproducibility was assessed by Irreproducible Discovery Rate using pseudoreplicates.

Measurement of auxin contents

Endogenous auxins in seeds at 9 DAF were quantified by Ultra‑High Performance Liquid Chromatography–Multiple Reaction Monitoring–Mass Spectrometry (UHPLC-MRM-MS/MS). Samples were extracted in isopropanol:water (80:20, v/v) spiked with isotope internal standards, then homogenized, sonicated in an ice bath, concentrated, reconstituted in methanol:water (50:50, v/v), and filtered through 0.22-μm membranes. Serial standard dilutions with identical internal standards were prepared. Separation was performed on a Waters HSS T3 column using 0.1% formic acid in water/acetonitrile mobile phases. Analytes were detected on SCIEX QTRAP 6500+ MS/MS using Electrospray Ionization ± MRM mode. Calibration curves were established by least-squares regression, and method validity was confirmed by lower limit of detection, lower limit of quantitation, quality control recovery, and relative standard deviation. Final auxin concentrations (ng/g) were calculated from the raw values, dilution factor, and sample weight.

DLR assay

To examine how the CNV regulates ClLNC-SS1 expression, reverse complementary sequences of Dup1231 and Dup1 were isolated from the small-seeded watermelon line Du1 and the large-seeded line ZXG01478, respectively. A 13-bp fragment containing the TATA box (positions 141–153) from Dup2/Dup3 and its mutant allele was inserted into the corresponding region of the Dup1 reverse complementary sequence to generate Dup1R+13 and Dup1R+13mut. The same 13-bp fragment was deleted from the Dup2/Dup3 reverse complementary sequences in Dup1231 to produce Dup1231-26. All constructs were cloned into the pGreen II 0800-LUC vector, and DLR assays were performed as described previously (Song et al., 2025).

To assess the regulation of ClBige1b expression by ClLNC-SS1, ClLNC-SS1 and the ClBige1b promoter were isolated from Du1 and ZXG01478, respectively. Sequence combinations were inserted into the PUC57-StrigoQuant-like backbone (Zheng et al., 2020) by In-Fusion HD cloning. All constructs were used to transform watermelon protoplasts as described previously (Tian et al., 2017) with minor modifications. The LUC/REN ratio was measured using a dual-luciferase assay kit (Promega). The primers used are listed in Supplemental Table 6.

Vector construction and genetic transformation

For knockout of ClLNC-SS1 and ClBige1b, two specific single guide RNAs targeting their transcribed regions were designed using CRISPR-P 2.0 (http://cbi.hzau.edu.cn/cgi-bin/CRISPR2/CRISPR) and inserted into the pGH-rGRF-GIF-CR vector (Feng et al., 2021). Constructs were introduced into the small-seeded watermelons Du1 and 14CB11 by Agrobacterium-mediated transformation (Tian et al., 2017), and expression of ClLNC-SS1 and ClBige1b was detected to evaluate knockout efficiency.

For overexpression of ClBige1b, its full-length coding sequence was cloned into pBK-35S:attR-His (BioRun) by the Gateway method. Constructs were transformed into the large-seeded watermelon ZXG01478 by Agrobacterium-mediated transformation (Tian et al., 2017). All primers are listed in Supplemental Table 6. ClBige1b expression and ClBige1b protein levels were measured to identify ClBige1b-overexpressing lines.

Microscopy analysis

Seeds collected at 9 DAF were fixed overnight in FAA solution, then cleared by overnight incubation (12–24 h) in chloral hydrate solution. After sequential dehydration in an ethanol series (50%, 70%, 95%, and 100%), seeds were infiltrated and embedded in paraffin wax. Paraffin sections were prepared using a Leica RM 2016 microtome (Leica, Nanterre Cedex, France), stained with safranin-fast green, and observed under an Olympus IX-71 light/fluorescence microscope (Tokyo, Japan).

Statistical analysis

Data Processing System software was used to perform statistical analysis. Origin v.2025 (OriginLab, Northampton, MA, USA) and Tbtools-Ⅱ (Chen et al., 2023) were used to draw figures. Pairwise comparisons were performed using a two-tailed Student’s t-test (∗p < 0.05 and ∗∗p < 0.01) and Duncan’s multiple range test following one-way ANOVA (p < 0.05).

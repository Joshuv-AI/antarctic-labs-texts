Association of MHC class II β domain polymorphism in channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) with natural infection by Ligictaluridus floridanus
Authors: Guadalupe Moncerrat González Roldán, Gaspar Manuel Parra Bracamonte, Flaviano Benavides González, Isidro Otoniel Montelongo Alfaro, Jesús Di Carlo Quiroz Velásquez, Xochitl. F. De La Rosa-Reyna
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class II β genes are highly polymorphic and trigger the adaptive immune response in fish against parasitic infections. In channel catfish, exon 2 of the HLA (human leukocyte antigens) and H-2 genes encodes the MHC class II β domain. HLA and H-2 have not been documented in channel catfish populations or associated with parasite infections. We used Ligictaluridus floridanus, a common monogenean ectoparasite, to uncover SNPs in channel catfish. This study uses massive sequencing technology to find single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in a portion of the MHC class II β domain of the HLA and H-2 genes. Based on an adjusted parasite load, we ranked fish as resistant (RR) or susceptible (SS) to L. floridanus natural infection. We recovered SNPs from 58 fish, 32 classified as RR and 26 as SS. We found 13 SNPs, resulting in 12 non-synonymous changes (4 in HLA and 8 in H-2) and one synonymous change in the H-2 gene. The GG homozygote genotype for the alternative SNP HLA:1849444(C/G), which changes an aspartate (D) to glutamate (E), was significantly associated with susceptibility to L. floridanus. Our study identified 11 haplotypes in the HLA gene, with only one identical to the previously reported allele Icpu-DAB*03 (FKDN). We identified four non-synonymous SNPs in the HLA gene, with a significant association with susceptibility to L. floridanus observed in the genotype GG of SNP HLA:1849444(C/G). In the H-2 gene, the nine SNPs identified were non-significant in any test conducted.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00436-025-08575-0.

Introduction

The channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) is one of the most important members of Ictaluridae for its importance in aquaculture worldwide. The species naturally inhabits the southern part of Canada, the northern and eastern parts of the USA, and northern Mexico (Engle and Kumar 2022). The channel catfish farming industry started in the USA during the 1950 s and has since remained the most successful worldwide. In Mexico, this industry began by introducing 800 pairs of channel catfish broodfish imported from the USA in 1976 and 1981 (IMIPAS 2018). Since then, its farming predominantly occurs in ponds and floating cages within dams and rivers (Lara-Rivera et al. 2015).

Parasitic diseases, especially in farming conditions, can lead to several health problems and can cause chronic infections (Bouwmeester et al. 2021). Ligictaluridus floridanus (Mueller, 1936) has been identified as a prevalent parasite that infects wild and domestic channel catfish (Galaviz-Silva et al. 2013; Rábago‐Castro et al. 2011). Research indicates that L. floridanus adversely influences channel catfish’s body size, growth performance, and body weight under cultured and caged farming conditions (Rábago-Castro et al. 2014; Rábago‐Castro et al. 2011). L. floridanus is a monogenean parasite prevalent annually in channel catfish farms of Mexico and is a potential threat that can cause economic losses (Rábago‐Castro et al. 2011). Monogenean infections typically cause skin and gill irritation, local redness, overproduction of mucus, epithelial hyperplasia, and hemorrhages, ending as stressors (Whittington and Chisholm 2008). Nevertheless, L. floridanus rarely leads to mortality (Ilgová et al. 2021; Rábago‐Castro et al. 2011). In addition to L. floridanus, the channel catfish is susceptible to other monogeneans (Mendoza-Garfias et al. 2017). At least six known Ligictaluridus species can parasitize catfish that belong to the Ictaluridae (Beverley-Burton and Klassen 1990; Mendoza-Franco et al. 2023). Evidence shows that various catfish species have evolved to mitigate or withstand parasitism by Ligictaluridus spp. (Beverley-Burton and Klassen 1990). Consequently, these parasite species exhibit host specificity toward catfish species (Beverley-Burton and Klassen 1990). Monogeneans are highly host-specific, often at the genus or family level. For example, they may parasitize different species of Ictaluridae that share the same habitat or selectively parasitize members of Ictalurus clades (“punctatus” or “furcatus”) (Rosas-Valdez and Pérez-Ponce de León 2008). In Mexican freshwater fishes, a biogeographic core parasite fauna has been observed between monogeneans and Ictaluridae. At the species level, based on a standard taxonomic hierarchy index, L. floridanus was classified with low host specificity (Rosas-Valdez and Pérez-Ponce de León 2008, 2011). However, under experimental conditions, the prevalence of L. floridanus infection (calculated as the number of infected/examined fish) indicates a high susceptibility in I. punctatus and its hybrids (I. punctatus × I. furcatus), while the striped catfish (Pangasianodon hypophthalmus) showed no infection, suggesting resistance or immunity to L. floridanus (Benavides-González et al. 2024).

The adaptive immune system in fish is crucial in mediating the defense against parasites (Mokhtar et al. 2023). Three main types of molecules play a crucial role in responding to parasite infection: (1) immunoglobulins (Ig) or antibodies involved in identifying and binding to specific antigens, (2) T-cell receptors (TCRs) that are responsible for identifying peptide fragments that are bound to the major histocompatibility complex (MHC); (3) MHC class I α and class II β that are responsible for presenting endogenous (class I α) and exogenous (class II β) antigens.

At least three MHC class II β domains are located on chromosome 5 of the channel catfish genome, including class II histocompatibility antigen, B-L beta chain (LOC108264923), HLA class II histocompatibility antigen, DR beta 4 chain (LOC108265443), and H-2 class II histocompatibility antigen, I-E beta chain (LOC108265442) (Thompson et al. 2021).

HLA is a highly diverse gene, containing 42 alleles (Sasa-DAB) in Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) and 22 alleles (Onmy-DAB) in rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) (Grimholt 2016; Stosik et al. 2020). Channel catfish exhibit a significantly lower number of alleles, with only seven identified: Icpu-DAB*01 through Icpu-DAB*07. This designation is the only known allele diversity within this species (Godwin et al. 1997; Hogan et al. 1999). MHC class II β’s allelic diversity is well-known in a few fish species, and its association with adaptive immunity remains unclear (Grimholt 2016). Fish parasitology studies suggest that MHC class II β genes are candidates to be associated with parasite resistance, susceptibility, host range, specificity, and infection severity (Seifertová et al. 2016; Seifertová and Simková 2011). Several studies targeted exon 2 of the MHC class II β domain in the HLA gene as a leading candidate against exogenous parasites (Hofmann et al. 2017; Simkova et al. 2021). This region exhibits high variability with a significant non-synonymous substitution rate (dN), an observation primarily attributed to the peptide binding region (PBR), which tends to show increased dN changes when found in functional genes (Hughes and Nei 1989; Llaurens et al. 2012; Ono et al. 1993). Recently, massive sequencing technology has been used to uncover the diversity of highly diverse genes, including MHC (Sundaram et al. 2020). This study used massive sequencing to achieve the following objectives: (1) identify polymorphisms within a portion of the MHC class II β domain of the HLA and H-2 genes and (2) examine synonymous and non-synonymous changes and evaluate their association with susceptibility or resistance among farmed channel catfish naturally infected by L. floridanus.

Material and methods

Sampling and contrasting groups

Sampling was carried out by the Aquaculture Health Committee personnel from the State of Tamaulipas. L. floridanus was identified using taxonomic keys for monogenean (Hoffman 2019). Sixty specimens were collected in five commercial cage-reared channel catfish farms located in Jiménez (La Loba), Xicoténcatl (Ramiro Caballero), Abasolo (Puente Grande), and Jaumave (Jaumave) in Tamaulipas, México. The selected fish were euthanized following approved protocols (Committee of Ethics Permit No. CONBIOETICA-09-CEI-00–20190327). Each specimen was measured by furcal length (FL), total weight (TW), and number of parasites in gills (NPG). To detect and count parasites in fish gills, one of the four left-side gill arches from each specimen was removed with scissors, placed in a petri dish, and dissected with an anatomical needle to isolate the gill filaments. Twenty gill filaments were scraped and placed in a 0.65% saline solution on a glass slide. A coverslip was then placed over the sample, and the preparation was examined under a compound microscope. The number of parasites was calculated by multiplying the observed count by eight (4 gill arches × 2 sides) (Mladineo 2005; Rábago‐Castro et al. 2011; Hoffman 2019). The organisms sampled were collected from farms where animals are naturally exposed to L. floridanus. Contrasting groups with susceptibility (SS) or resistance (RR) to ectoparasite infestation were classified using the residuals of NPG (rNPG) fitting a generalized linear model (GLM) as: Yij = μ + i + Gi + β1F + εij, where Y = abundance of L. floridanus, μ = overall mean, i = individual random effect by specimen, G = effect of the ith farm, F = linear covariable of the FL, and ε = residual random effect, considered the random variable to adjust the L. floridanus abundance. The effect of some variables was not included in the model as they show collinearity with the FL. The statistical analysis was conducted with SAS 9.4 (SAS® Institute Inc., Cary, NC). Using the rNPG, organisms were categorized into 32 fish for RR and 26 for SS. The values for RR groups range from −1335.281 to −5.776, whereas those for SS range from 1.261 to 2391.533.

Sequencing of the MHC class II β domain

For molecular analysis, barbel clips were collected from 60 fish and preserved in vials with 95% ethanol at −20 °C. DNA was extracted from 20 mg of tissue using the DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit protocol (Qiagen® ID 69504). The quality and integrity of the DNA were visualized on 1% agarose gels and quantified using the NanoDrop 2000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Two individuals were excluded from the analysis due to low DNA quality. The remaining 58 individuals (32 RR and 26 SS) were sequenced.

Given the evolutionary relationship and 95.07% sequence identity between HLA and H-2 genes (Thompson et al. 2021), we designed a pair of primers targeting conserved regions in exon 2 using Primer-Blast (Ye et al. 2012). Exon 2, which is 273 base pairs (bp) long in both genes, shares 91.21% identity. We used the IcpuDAB*01 allele (U77597; Godwin et al. 1997) as a reference. The primers amplify a 220 bp fragment of the MHC class II β domain in the HLA gene. To confirm primer specificity, the region was mapped to the channel catfish genome assembly Coco_2.0 (GCF_001660625.3). The aligned region, specific to the MHC class II β domain in exon 2 of the HLA (LOC108265443) and H-2 (LOC108265442) genes, was amplified using primers: IpBe2n-F: 5’-GTGTATCTGGAGTAAAGAGGATCTCA-3’ and IpBe2n-R: 5’-GTAATTCCCAACATTATTCTTACAGA-3’. For sequencing, the final primer was synthesized by integrating the Illumina adapters, forward 5’-TCGTCGGCAGCGTCAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAG-3’, and reverse 5’-GTCTCGTGGGCTCGGAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAG-3’.

PCR touchdown conditions were as follows: 95 °C 5’ for 1 cycle, (95° 45″, 62° 45″, 72° 45″) for 5 cycles, decreasing 2 °C each cycle, (95° 45″, 55° 45″, 72° 45″) for 30 cycles, and 72 °C 10’ for 1 cycle. The Illumina MiniSeq platform was used for pair-end sequencing with a read length of 150 bp.

SNP detection

Illumina reads were filtered using FastQC (Andrews 2010) and Multiqc (Ewels et al. 2016). In the subsequent step, the reads were processed using Trimmomatic (Bolger et al. 2014) to eliminate adapter sequences and discard any read shorter than 150 bp. After filtering, reads were mapped to the channel catfish reference genome Coco_2.0 (GCA_001660625.3) using Bowtie2 (Langmead and Salzberg 2012). In SAMtools (Li et al. 2009), alignments were sorted and used as input to run the mpileup option in BCFtools (Li et al. 2009). A final dataset of SNP was created in VCFtools (Danecek et al. 2011) with no missing data allowed, a minimum of 3 for the less common allele (–mac 3), a quality of sites of 30 (–minQ 30), a minimum depth of 10 by genotype (–minDP 10), insertion/deletions were removed (–remove-indels), a minor allele frequency of 0.05 (–maf 0.05), and only biallelic sites (–min-alleles 2). SNPs were visualized in IGV (Thorvaldsdottir et al. 2013). SNPs identified were corroborated using the same procedure in BWA (Li and Durbin 2009), a second aligner. Genotype by sample was obtained by converting the VCF file to Genepop format using PDGSpider (Lischer and Excoffier 2012). SNPs arranged as haplotypes were counted in DnaSP v6 (Rozas et al. 2017). The Ensembl Variant Effect Predictor (VEP) identified amino acid changes caused by each SNP (McLaren et al. 2016). SNP positions were adjusted according to the channel catfish genome assembly ASM400665v3 available in the Ensembl database.

Statistical analysis

Calculations of allelic frequencies, linkage disequilibrium (LD), Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE), and genic differentiation were conducted using a Fisher exact probability test (Rousset 2008). Complementarily, univariate logistic analyses were used to estimate the odds ratio effect of the genotype (CI 95%) for identified SNPs using PROC LOGISTIC. Haplotype frequencies between groups were compared using a Fisher exact probability test. These analyses were conducted in SAS 9.4 (SAS® Institute Inc., Cary, NC).

Identification of HLA alleles

To identify differences with alleles previously reported, we used the four SNPs found in the HLA gene, arranged as haplotypes, and compared them with the seven sequences mined from NCBI, U77597.1, U77598.1 (Godwin et al. 1997), and AF044402.1 to AF044406.1 (Hogan et al. 1999). The reference sequence was XP_017323245.1. The alignment was conducted using MAFFT online (Katoh et al. 2019).

Arrangement of haplotypes

A weak LD was observed between the HLA and H-2 genes, indicating a limited correlation between these genetic regions. The intragenic LD in the HLA gene was moderate, while SNPs in the H-2 gene showed significant LD (P < 0.05). Given that all positions within the H-2 gene were strongly linked, we selected a single SNP to facilitate the organization of haplotypes. Accordingly, we formed 15 distinct haplotypes by integrating four SNPs from the HLA region with one SNP from the H-2 gene, five SNPs in total. The association analysis was conducted using the following SNPs in HLA: (1849407(A/T), 1849433(A/T), 1849444(C/G), 1867154(T/A), and the SNP 1867224(A/T) in the H-2 gene (Table 2). The results revealed ten haplotypes in the SS group and 14 in the RR group. One haplotype (AAGTT) was absent in the RR group, while five haplotypes were fixed. In the RR group, one haplotype was significant (ATCAT, P = 0.0294). The haplotype TTGAT, with a higher frequency in the SS, shows statistical significance (P = 0.0007). HLA alleles comparison

The analysis revealed that three of four changes found in the HLA gene’s MHC class II β domain coincided with proteins reported in channel catfish. Only the amino acid valine (V) found in IcpuDAB*01 (AAB67871.1) was not identified in this study (Fig. 1). A new amino acid change, K/N, was observed at position 43, replacing the K/Y found in the seven reported alleles (Fig. 1). Discussion

This study identified four non-synonymous variations in the HLA gene’s MHC class II β domain of channel catfish. Nine changes were found in the H-2 gene, with only one synonymous variation.

HLA is a candidate gene for the adaptive immune resistance to parasites in fish. In channel catfish, however, only a limited repertoire of alleles is known (Godwin et al. 1997; Hogan et al. 1999; Mokhtar et al. 2023). In this study, the non-synonymous changes identified were not validated through expression assays. Expression assays are essential for validating the functional impact of genetic variations and identifying pseudogenes (Grimholt 2016). Classifying such changes remains uncertain without these assays, requiring further investigation for accurate allele identification. Instead, our study was based on domains mapped to the channel catfish genome. Based on haplotype analysis, we have identified evidence of at least ten new possible alleles. Only one reported allele was verified, associated with the haplotype FKDN (Icpu-DAB*03). The haplotype corresponds to the variant AAC02095.1, isolated from a gynogenetic channel catfish of the Marion strain (Hogan et al. 1999). This variant was observed in two specimens (RR_46, RR_49) of channel catfish categorized within the resistant group, with no association to resistance or susceptibility. In the haplotype analysis, a significant association was found in both groups: the haplotype ATCA (YNDK) in the resistant group and haplotype TTGA (FNEK) in the susceptible group. These two haplotypes differ at positions HLA:1849407(A/T) and HLA:1849444(C/G). However, only the alternative genotype GG in the SNP HLA:1849444 (C/G), which changes an aspartate (D) to glutamate (E), was significantly linked to susceptibility. In HLA:1849407(A/T), its function is predicted as a weak binding peptide, as indicated by NetMHCIIpan 4.0 (Reynisson et al. 2020). The SNP changes a tyrosine (Y) to phenylalanine (F), showing no significance in the genotype test. A larger sample size could confirm or reject any significant association.

Phylogenetic studies strongly support a close evolutionary relationship between the nucleotide sequences of MHC class II β domain in the H-2 gene and HLA (Thompson et al. 2021). In this study, the H-2 gene exhibited greater nucleotide variability than the HLA gene, with no significant association with susceptibility or resistance to L. floridanus. Additionally, the H-2 gene segregates into homozygotes and heterozygotes, while the HLA gene consists entirely of homozygotes. Previous studies have observed signals of inbreeding in channel catfish farms (Parra-Bracamonte et al. 2011), which may contribute to a decline in heterozygosity. Both genes under study revealed a deficiency of heterozygotes. An alternative explanation is that the low-frequency or rare alleles were filtered out during data processing and undetected in the HLA gene. Additionally, although located physically close to the HLA gene, with a window of ~ 21,000 nucleotides apart, the LD between genes was weak. This is an observation not expected in inbred populations (Barría et al. 2019). Further evaluations are needed to determine if differential evolutionary forces influence the variation of HLA and H-2 genes.

The role of the MHC gene family in fish’s adaptive immune defense against parasites is poorly understood. MHC loci maintain high allele diversity to enhance parasite resistance through heterozygote advantage, rare alleles, and fluctuating selection (Spurgin and Richardson 2010). This complex dynamic involves spatial and temporal changes, affecting the frequencies of heterozygotes and rare alleles, leading to variations in allele frequencies, fixation, or loss due to selection or genetic drift. This study used the ectoparasite species L. floridanus to identify polymorphisms in the MHC class II β domain of channel catfish from northern Mexico. The host range and geographic distribution of L. floridanus are not completely known. A study reports that L. floridanus is an introduced species to northeastern Mexico (Cloutman et al. 2018). However, the evidence demonstrates that the species parasitizes freshwater fishes in northern and southern Mexico (Mendoza-Franco et al. 2023). Research suggests that the evolution of Ligictaluridus is shaped primarily by adaptation to new environmental conditions, rather than by host specificity (Beverley-Burton 1995). Besides Ictalurus, L. floridanus parasitizes the flathead catfish (Pylodictis olivaris) and common carp (Cyprinus carpio) (Cloutman et al. 2018; Garrido-Olvera et al. 2017). Other Ligictaluridus, including L. mirabilis and L. pricei, parasitized the channel catfish (Truong and Bullard 2021). Nowadays, Ligictaluridus has become an alien parasite, spreading with channel catfish and other Ictaluridae species used in aquaculture (Nitta and Nagasawa 2015; Wang et al. 2013).

While multiple biotic and abiotic factors can affect the prevalence of L. floridanus, this parasite strongly prefers channel catfish and persists remarkably well, with a 100% prevalence documented during the first 14 days following infection (Benavides-González et al. 2024). As members of the defense mechanism against parasites, the MHC class II β domains are candidates to be involved in the immune response to L. floridanus. In the host-parasite model of this study, we identify at least one specific polymorphism in the HLA gene’s MHC class II β domain significantly associated with susceptibility.

Conclusion

We applied next-generation sequencing to analyze allele diversity in HLA and H-2 genes. In this study, four non-synonymous SNPs were identified in the HLA gene, and eight non-synonymous SNPs, along with one synonymous SNP, were identified in the H-2 gene. A significant association was found in the HLA gene. The SNP HLA:1849444(C/G), particularly the GG genotype, showed a significant association with susceptibility to L. floridanus. Eleven alleles were identified, comprising one known and ten novel HLA alleles in channel catfish.

Late reoperative surgery after laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy: A novel pancreaticogastrostomy Based on the "Modified Blumgart" Philosophy (with video)
Authors: Yu Cai, Xiangjie Su, Xiaohong Liu, Yong Peng, Xingzhi Li, Ankang Wang, Yuan Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Modified Blumgart pancreaticogastrostomy for recurrence or anastomotic stricture.•Avoidance of jejunal mobilization.•Mucosa-to-mucosa pancreatic-gastric adaptation reduces hemorrhage.•Locking mattress suture ensures hemostasis, eliminates dead space.•Separate suturing of duct/gastric walls reduces pancreaticogastric anastomotic gap.

Introduction

Laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy (LPD) has been established as a standard procedure for tumors of the pancreatic head [1]. However, late local recurrence at the pancreaticojejunal anastomotic site or anastomotic stricture requiring reoperation is relatively uncommon, and only limited reports describe techniques for re-anastomosis of the pancreatic remnant [2,3]. In this article, we demonstrate a novel modified locking Blumgart pancreaticogastrostomy performed during re-laparoscopy for local tumor recurrence or anastomotic stricture at the previous pancreaticojejunal anastomosis. The technical rationale of this anastomotic approach include: (1) avoidance of resection of the original afferent jejunal limb, (2) a more physiological mucosa-to-mucosa adaptation between the pancreatic duct and gastric wall, and (3) use of a locking mattress suture to achieve more complete hemostasis, and (4) separate suturing of the anterior/posterior and lateral walls of both the pancreatic duct and gastric mucosa. Potential advantages include: (1) avoidance of jejunal mobilization; (2) reduced risk of hemorrhage from gastric acid erosion of the pancreatic stump; (3) elimination of dead space between the pancreatic stump and gastric wall, theoretically achieving a tighter anastomotic seal; and (4) a reduced anatomical gap at the pancreatogastric anastomosis, which may lower the risk of pancreatic fistula and anastomotic stricture. This novel anastomotic technique is particularly suitable for selected cases involving late local tumor recurrence or anastomotic stricture at the pancreaticojejunal anastomosis following prior pancreaticoduodenectomy.

Methods

A 70-year-old male with a history of LPD for pancreatic intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) three years prior was admitted to the Hepatobiliary-Pancreatic-Splenic Surgery Department of Nanchong Central Hospital for acute pancreatitis. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a 3.5 × 3 cm cystic-solid lesion with abnormal signal intensity at the previous pancreaticojejunal anastomotic site, closely associated with the main pancreatic duct, suggestive of IPMN recurrence (Fig. 1). Based on the patient’s history and imaging findings, local recurrence of IPMN was diagnosed. The decision was made to resect the previous pancreaticojejunal anastomosis while preserving the original hepaticojejunostomy and the distal afferent jejunal limb. To avoid jejunal mobilization, pancreaticogastrostomy was selected. To minimize the risk of pancreatic stump hemorrhage due to gastric acid erosion, a pancreatic duct-to-gastric mucosa anastomosis was performed, supplemented by a locking mattress suture technique to maintain anastomotic tension.Fig. 1Magnetic resonance imaging. Operative procedure

The patient was placed in a supine position with legs separated. A 1-cm incision was made above the umbilicus for the camera port, and four additional trocar ports (5 mm, 12 mm, 12 mm, and 10 mm) were placed in the upper abdomen. A firm, cystic-solid mass measuring 3.5 × 3 cm was identified posterior to the previous pancreaticojejunal anastomosis, located to the right of the portal vein–superior mesenteric vein axis, with ill-defined borders from the afferent jejunal limb.

After adhesiolysis, the prior pancreaticojejunal anastomosis and residual uncinate process tissue were resected. The afferent jejunal limb between the original hepaticojejunostomy and pancreaticojejunal anastomosis was divided using a linear stapler. Frozen section analysis of both the proximal jejunal stump and pancreatic resection margin confirmed no residual tumor.

The locking Blumgart pancreaticogastrostomy was performed in four layers: the posterior pancreatic stump-to-stomach layer, the posterior and lateral pancreatic duct-to-gastric mucosa layer, the anterior pancreatic duct-to-gastric mucosa layer, and the anterior pancreatic stump-to-stomach layer. The detailed procedure is described below:① Posterior Pancreaticogastrostomy Layer: A 3-0 Prolene suture was used to perform a figure-of-eight suture on the superior aspect of the pancreatic stump for hemostasis. From this point, the same suture was passed through the full thickness of the pancreas, then through the seromuscular layer of the posterior gastric wall in a longitudinal direction, and finally back through the posterior to anterior aspect of the pancreatic stump, forming a U-shaped stitch. Subsequent sutures were placed in a similar manner from the superior to the inferior border of the pancreas, usually requiring 2–3 stitches. The final suture was left long for subsequent use in the anterior pancreaticogastrostomy layer.② Posterior and Lateral Pancreatic Duct-to-Gastric Mucosa Layer: The posterior aspect of the pancreatic duct and gastric mucosa was anastomosed using a 4-0 PDS suture in a figure-of-eight fashion (if the pancreatic duct was sufficiently wide, a continuous suture was applied). A pancreatic stent was inserted and secured with the same suture. Additional sutures were placed at the 3 and 9 o'clock positions of the pancreatic duct–gastric mucosa anastomosis.③ Anterior Pancreatic Duct-to-Gastric Mucosa Layer: The anterior aspect of the pancreatic duct and gastric mucosa was sutured using 4-0 PDS in a figure-of-eight or continuous fashion, depending on the duct diameter.④ Anterior Pancreaticogastrostomy Layer: The long suture reserved from the inferior border of the posterior layer was used to run continuously from the inferior to the superior edge of the anterior pancreatic stump–stomach interface, and was finally tied to the tail end of the superior pancreatic suture (with video).

Definition

Postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) was defined according to the 2016 International Study Group of Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) criteria [4], as drain fluid amylase activity more than three times the upper limit of institutional normal serum amylase activity on or after postoperative day 3. The diagnostic criteria for acute pancreatitis followed the 2025 revised guidelines of the International Association of Pancreatology [5], which require the presence of at least two of the following three features: (1) clinical- acute upper abdominal pain; (2) biochemical- elevated pancreatic enzymes (serum lipase or amylase concentration >3 times upper limit of normal) and (3) imaging- features of acute pancreatic inflammation with or without necrosis on abdominal imaging, typically computed tomography (CT) or ultrasonography (USG). Pancreatic fistula risk scores (FRS) were assessed according to the FRS system recommended in the 2017 expert consensus on the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of postoperative pancreatic fistula issued by the Chinese Society of Surgery of the Chinese Medical Association [6].

Results

The total operative time was 150 min with an estimated blood loss of 50 mL. Final pathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of an intestinal-type IPMN with high-grade dysplasia. Involvement of the serosal surface and intramural layer of the intestinal wall by mucinous tumor was identified. The patient experienced no postoperative complications such as pancreatic fistula or hemorrhage. Between February 2024 and May 2025, three patients underwent this pancreatogastric anastomosis procedure. Indications included pancreaticojejunal anastomotic stricture in two cases and local tumor recurrence at the pancreaticojejunal anastomosis in one case. The pancreatic fistula risk scores for the three patients were 0, 1, and 3, respectively. No postoperative pancreatic fistula and delayed gastric empty occurred in any of the three patients. Follow-up to date has reached 7, 15, and 22 months postoperatively, respectively, with no incidence of pancreatitis. Conclusion

The modified locking Blumgart pancreaticogastrostomy is technically feasible and demonstrates preliminary safety in reoperative surgery for late local tumor recurrence or anastomotic stricture at the pancreaticojejunal site following laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy.

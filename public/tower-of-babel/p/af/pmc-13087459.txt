The Weight of Disease, the Loss Within: Rethinking Frailty Assessment in ADPKD
Authors: Michele Paolisi, Pierpaolo Bianca, Liliana Italia De Rosa, Martina Catania, Kristiana Kola, Matteo Brambilla Pisoni, Paola Maiucchi, Giuseppe Vezzoli, Maria Teresa Sciarrone Alibrandi
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We read with interest the study by Wang et al.,1 reporting no association between autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) and incident or worsening frailty compared with simple kidney cysts over 4.8 years of follow-up. Although the propensity score-matched design and comprehensive sensitivity analyses are commendable, we believe 2 underexplored sources of heterogeneity may have influenced the null findings.

First, ADPKD was analysed as a single entity despite well-established genotypic heterogeneity. Patients with PKD1 truncating mutations progress to end-stage kidney disease approximately 20 years earlier than PKD2 carriers and develop substantially greater organomegaly.2 As the authors acknowledge, genetic data were unavailable. Treating ADPKD as a monolithic entity may mask a true association with frailty in high-risk genotypic subgroups, where the signal is diluted by the inclusion of patients with milder disease courses.

Second, the absence of volumetric data (total kidney and liver volume) is a critical gap. Fadlallah et al.3 recently demonstrated that kidney volume is independently and nonlinearly associated with worse physical health and increased pain prevalence in ADPKD. In patients with severe organomegaly, gastrointestinal compression leads to early satiety, reduced intake, and progressive malnutrition, a recognized pathway to sarcopenia.4 Crucially, overall body weight in these patients may remain stable or even increase because of the mass of enlarged polycystic organs, masking concurrent loss of lean body mass.4,5 This paradox renders the weight loss component of the FRAIL scale unreliable, and the associated sarcopenia and nutritional depletion would affect fatigue and resistance; 3 of 5 FRAIL components potentially altered but undetected, particularly when operationalized through administrative codes.

Notably, the paradoxical finding of increased mortality without increased frailty in the ADPKD group may itself reflect disease-specific mechanisms, including occult nutritional decline driven by organomegaly, that escape detection by conventional frailty instruments. We therefore suggest that future studies investigating frailty in ADPKD should incorporate volumetric imaging data, genotypic stratification (at minimum, distinguishing PKD1 truncating from PKD2 mutations), direct body composition assessment to detect sarcopenia masked by organ mass, and multidimensional or disease-specific frailty instruments to unmask associations that may be concealed when this heterogeneous disease is analysed as a uniform entity.

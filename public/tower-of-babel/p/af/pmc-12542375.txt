Therapeutic value of dissecting stations 1 and 4Sb lymph nodes during radical distal gastrectomy for lower- and middle-third gastric cancer
Authors: Peng Liao, Ren-hao Hu, Xi-mao Cui, Shun Zhang, Xiao-hua Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe necessity of dissecting station 1 (right paracardial) and 4Sb (left gastroepiploic artery) lymph nodes (LNs) during radical distal gastrectomy for gastric cancer remains debated. This study investigated their therapeutic value for lower-third (LGC) and middle-third (MGC) gastric cancer.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed 897 patients undergoing radical distal gastrectomy (2013–2019). Metastasis rates, clinicopathological associations, multivariate analyses for risk factors and survival and therapeutic value index (TVI = metastasis frequency × 5-year survival of station-positive patients) for stations 1 and 4Sb were assessed, stratified by tumor location.ResultsFor LGC (n = 435), station 4Sb showed a 0.46% metastasis rate and a 0% TVI. In contrast, station 1 had a 5.06% metastasis rate and a TVI of 4.14%. Multivariate survival analysis for LGC revealed that after adjusting for T and N stage, station 1 metastasis was not an independent prognostic factor for overall survival (HR 1.60, 95% CI 0.95–2.71, p = 0.078). For MGC (n = 462), station 1 metastasis was an independent prognostic factor (HR 1.75, 95% CI 1.08–2.83, p = 0.023).ConclusionsRoutine dissection of station 4Sb LNs in LGC offers negligible survival benefit. Furthermore, given that station 1 metastasis is not an independent prognostic factor in LGC, its routine dissection also warrants reconsideration. These findings support a biology-driven refinement of lymphadenectomy guidelines, suggesting that omitting station 4Sb and selectively omitting station 1 in LGC may be oncologically safe. Prospective validation is crucial.Keywords: Gastric cancer, Lymph node dissection, Distal gastrectomy, Therapeutic value, Tumor location

Introduction

Gastric cancer remains one of the most aggressive malignancies globally, with lymph node (LN) metastasis being a critical determinant of prognosis [1]. While advances in diagnostic imaging and screening programs have improved the detection of early-stage gastric cancer (EGC), enabling curative resection, LN involvement persists as a key prognostic factor. This necessitates meticulous surgical strategies to achieve complete removal of the primary tumor and regional LNs [2]. Current clinical guidelines, such as those from the Japanese Gastric Cancer Association (JGCA), standardize the extent of lymphadenectomy (D1, D1+, or D2) based primarily on tumor depth and the type of surgical resection [2]. However, these guidelines may inadequately address anatomical variations in lymphatic drainage pathways intrinsically linked to the specific tumor location within the stomach [2–5].

The lymphatic system, embryologically derived from venous endothelial cells, generally follows venous pathways, directing metastatic spread first to perigastric nodes and subsequently to more distant nodal stations and systemic circulation [6]. For tumors located in the lower third of the stomach (LGC), lymphatic drainage predominantly occurs along the right gastric (station 5), right gastroepiploic (station 6), and suprapyloric nodes (station 4 d) within the gastropancreatic fold [7, 12]. Metastasis to station 4Sb – the LN group along the left gastroepiploic artery, which typically drains the upper stomach – is considered rare in LGC unless significant lymphatic obstruction induces retrograde flow [7, 8]. Despite this biological understanding, standard D2 dissection guidelines for distal gastrectomy often mandate the removal of station 4Sb nodes, seemingly prioritizing anatomical categorization over biological relevance based on tumor location [2, 10]. This discrepancy raises pertinent questions regarding the oncologic necessity and therapeutic benefit of resecting station 4Sb nodes, especially in EGC where reported metastasis rates to this station are often less than 2% [8, 9]. While existing studies frequently focus on advanced gastric cancer, a gap persists in the evidence regarding the value of dissecting these upper-station nodes in potentially lower-risk, distal tumors. Similarly, station 1 (right paracardial nodes), located superiorly near the cardia, is also routinely included in D2 dissection for distal gastrectomy. Its relevance for tumors confined to the lower third of the stomach is also debatable based on lymphatic drainage principles.

This study aims to investigate whether routine dissection of station 4Sb and station 1 LNs confers tangible clinical benefits in patients undergoing radical distal gastrectomy for LGC and MGC. By challenging guideline-driven anatomical mandates with a biology-driven approach focused on tumor location, and by analyzing metastatic patterns and therapeutic value indices (TVI), we seek to refine surgical precision, potentially reduce unnecessary dissection and associated morbidity, and ultimately optimize patient outcomes.

Methods

Patients

A retrospective review was conducted on all consecutive patients with histologically confirmed gastric adenocarcinoma who underwent curative-intent laparoscopic or open radical distal gastrectomy with D2 or D2 + lymphadenectomy between January 2013 and January 2019 at the Department of Gastrointestinal Surgery, Shanghai East Hospital, China. Clinical and pathological staging were performed according to the 8th Edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM Classification system.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) age 18 years or older; (2) histologically confirmed gastric adenocarcinoma; (3) primary tumor located in the middle or lower third of the stomach; (4) radical distal gastrectomy with D2/D2 + lymphadenectomy performed with curative intent; (5) clinical stage I-III disease prior to surgery. Patients with a history of other cancers, previous gastric resection, distant metastasis found before or during surgery (M1), or those who received preoperative chemotherapy or radiation therapy were excluded from the study. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai East Hospital (IRB Approval No: 2021 − 341). The requirement for informed consent was waived by the IRB due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified patient data.

Classification of LN station and range of LN dissection

The classification of LN stations and the definition of the range of LN dissection (D1, D1+, D2) were based on the Japanese Gastric Cancer Treatment Guidelines 2018 (5th edition) [10]. In our cohort, D2 + dissection was defined as a standard D2 lymphadenectomy plus the dissection of additional stations, most commonly including para-aortic nodes (No. 16a2/16b1), as determined by the surgeon’s assessment of advanced disease. Specifically relevant to this study:

No. 1 LN station: Defined as the nodes along the right side of the cardia, including those along the upper branch of the left gastric artery supplying the cardia.

No. 1 LN station: Defined as the nodes along the right side of the cardia, including those along the upper branch of the left gastric artery supplying the cardia.

No. 4Sb LN station: Defined as the nodes along the left gastroepiploic artery proximal to its first gastric branch.

No. 4Sb LN station: Defined as the nodes along the left gastroepiploic artery proximal to its first gastric branch.

Although stations 1 and 4Sb primarily drain the upper third of the stomach, current guidelines recommend their dissection as part of a standard D2 lymphadenectomy for distal gastrectomy [2, 10]. All patients included in this study underwent a procedure intended to be a D2 or D2 + dissection as per these guidelines.

Therapeutic value of lymph node dissection

The therapeutic value of dissecting each LN station was assessed using the Therapeutic Value Index (TVI). The TVI for a specific station was calculated by multiplying the frequency (percentage) of metastasis to that station by the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of patients who had metastasis specifically at that station[Calculated as: TVI (%) = Metastasis Frequency (%) × 5-year OS Rate (%) of station-positive patients]. The 5-year overall survival rates and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method. A higher TVI suggests a greater potential survival benefit derived from dissecting that station, while a low or zero TVI indicates limited or no improvement in prognosis attributable to the dissection of that specific station.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS statistical software version 24 (IBM). Continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical data were presented as numbers and percentages. Comparisons between groups were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and Student’s t-test for continuous variables. The median follow-up time was calculated using the reverse Kaplan-Meier method. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Multivariate logistic regression analysis, including variables with a p-value < 0.1 in univariate analysis, was used to identify independent risk factors for LN metastasis. A multivariate Cox proportional hazards model was used to identify independent prognostic factors for OS. Hazard ratios (HRs), odds ratios (ORs), and survival rates are reported with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 (two-sided). Patients with missing data on key variables required for a specific analysis, such as TNM stage or survival status, were excluded from that particular analysis. The cohort was complete regarding the primary variables for this study.

To further assess prognostic impact, a multivariate Cox proportional hazards analysis was performed. In the LGC cohort (Table 10), after adjusting for confounders, advanced age, T stage, and N stage were independent predictors of poor OS. Notably, metastasis to station 1 was not an independent prognostic factor (HR = 1.60, 95% CI 0.95–2.71, p = 0.078). Discussion

Lymph node dissection remains a cornerstone of curative surgery for gastric cancer, a malignancy with a particularly high incidence and mortality burden in Asian countries, including China, Japan, and Korea [1]. The optimization of lymphadenectomy extent, a topic of ongoing discussion in the literature [4, 5, 11, 12], is of paramount importance. Our study, conducted in a major Chinese center where D2 dissection is standard, investigated the value of dissecting two such controversial LN stations.

A significant finding is the negligible clinical benefit of dissecting station 4Sb nodes in LGC. The metastasis rate was extremely low (0.46%) and the TVI was 0%, consistent with reports from other studies [7–9]. More importantly, for station 1, our multivariate survival model revealed it was not an independent prognostic factor in LGC (p = 0.078). This suggests its involvement is more a reflection of overall tumor burden—driven by underlying risk factors such as advanced T and N stage [13]—rather than an independent driver of mortality. This finding directly challenges the routine inclusion of station 4Sb and 1 dissection in the D2 definition for LGC as specified in current guidelines [2, 10].

In stark contrast, for MGC, station 1 metastasis was an independent predictor of poor survival (p = 0.023). This highlights the critical role of tumor location. These divergent findings underscore the need for a location-specific, ‘biology-driven’ approach, adhering to principles of lymphatic drainage where LGC primarily drains to distal perigastric stations [14]. It is important to acknowledge that tumor location is one of several key determinants of lymphatic spread. A large body of literature has established the importance of morphological predictors such as lymphovascular invasion, histological subtype, and perineural invasion in assessing nodal metastasis risk [15]. Our study complements this knowledge by emphasizing the macroscopic, location-based patterns of spread, which can be assessed pre- and intraoperatively. A future, more integrated model might combine both morphological features and tumor location to create an even more precise, individualized approach to lymphadenectomy.

Our findings advocate for a potential refinement of lymphadenectomy guidelines currently prevalent in Asia. While comprehensive D2 dissection has been a hallmark of Asian gastric cancer surgery, leading to improved survival outcomes compared to less extensive procedures historically common in the West, our data support a more individualized strategy. The concept of TVI, first introduced by Sasako et al. [16], helps guide this refinement. Furthermore, our findings provide empirical data supporting recent reviews, such as by Nico et al. [17], which question the ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach. Omitting unnecessary dissection could offer tangible benefits. Given the high volume of gastric cancer surgeries in Asia, even small improvements in morbidity, especially as minimally invasive surgery becomes widespread, can have a significant impact [18].

Our study, based on a large cohort from a single Asian center, contributes to the growing body of evidence. However, it shares limitations common to retrospective studies. First, the 6-year study period may introduce temporal biases, as surgical techniques and adjuvant therapies could have evolved. Second, unmeasured surgeon-specific variability in performing D2 or D2 + dissections may exist. A key limitation is the extremely small number of positive cases for station 4Sb in LGC, which precluded any meaningful analysis for this subgroup. While we employed multivariate models to adjust for confounding, unmeasured selection biases may still exist. Furthermore, we did not perform adjustments for multiple comparisons across the numerous nodal stations analyzed, and did not calculate confidence intervals for the TVI values, which should be considered exploratory indices. Finally, our study lacks data on functional outcomes and quality of life, and its single-center design, based on a Chinese cohort, means that the findings’ generalizability to other populations with different genetic or epidemiological backgrounds must be considered with caution. These limitations underscore that our findings are hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that lymph node metastasis to station 4Sb in LGC is extremely rare and its dissection offers a TVI of zero. More importantly, our multivariate analysis suggests that station 1 metastasis is not an independent prognostic factor for survival in LGC. These findings collectively challenge the routine dissection of both stations 1 and 4Sb in LGC, supporting a biology-driven refinement of lymphadenectomy guidelines. Omitting these stations may reduce morbidity without compromising oncologic control. These retrospective findings require urgent validation in prospective, multicenter randomized trials to establish a new, individualized standard of care for distal gastric cancer.

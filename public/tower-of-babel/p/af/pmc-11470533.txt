Introduction and importance

Ureteric stump syndrome [USS] is a series of febrile recurrent lower abdominal pain, urinary tract infections, and hematuria that sometimes present with empyema as a rare complication [1]. The ureteric stump is left after ureteric re-implantation due to an impacted stone at the Vesical-ureteric junction [VUJ], or after nephrectomy of a non-functional kidney due to a distal ureterolithiasis; the ureteric stump forms a source of infection to the urinary bladder, due to obstructive stones left at the ureteric stamp [2]. Long-term, obstructive stones left at the ureteric stamp cause chronic irritation of the mucosa and can potentially cause metaplasia, dysplasia and malignancy [3]. In a standard protocol, in upper urinary tract, transition cell carcinoma [TCC], because of its multifocality, nephrectomy is performed along with ureterectomy to the level cuff of the urinary bladder en block resection, but not in the case of a non-functional kidney where the proximal ureterectomy is performed, and a potential ureteric stump is left in a patient, whom later on presents with recurrent febrile lower urinary tract symptoms [1]. It is important to exclude ureteric stump syndrome after nephrectomy or ureteric re-implantation, to the patients present with febrile Lower urinary tract symptoms, hematuria.

Case presentation

A patient aged 66 yrs., female presented with right flank pain for one year, colick in nature, radiating to the lower abdomen and genitalia and was associated with nausea and vomiting.

At first time, during ureteric re-implantation, we encounter adhesions during mobilization of the distal ureteric stump, because of long standing history of inflammation due to stasis and sepsis. Moreover, a distal segment of ureter was inflamed to the extent of easy tearing up on handling, that end up removing only a piece of fragment of stone, but the larger particle of stone was still stuck at VUJ, the ureteric re-implantation was carried out.

The patient was still experiencing a recurrence of lower abdominal pain and being diagnosed with recurrent urinary tract infection for the past 6 months after she had done ureteric re-implantation. Several blood tests showed leukocytosis and urine culture tests revealed E. coli and Pseudomonas. Serial urine analysis revealed a persistence WBC of 100/HPF and leucocyte of +3, and the patient was given antibiotics. Symptoms resolved after the administration of antibiotics, and symptoms subsequently recur again.

Due to persistent of febrile, lower abdominal pain, recurrence of being diagnosed with UTI following ureteric re-implantation. The patient was then scheduled for retrograde ureteroscopy of the native ureter and uretero-renoscope (URS) of the neo-ureterocystostomy (neo-reimplanted ureter). Intraoperative findings; an impacted distal ureterolithiasis at vuj of the native ureter, with debris that was subsequently fragmented with rigid URS and contact lithotripsy (Fig. 1). The re-implanted ureter was surveyed and found to have good patency (Fig. 2, Fig. 3). Postoperative recovery was uneventful, and the patient recovered (Fig. 4).

Clinical discussion

Recurrent febrile, lower urinary tract symptoms, hematuria, and lower abdominal pain are associated with ureteric stump syndrome in a patient post nephrectomy and proximal ureterectomy, or post-ureteric re-implantation due to distal ureterolithiasis [3,4]. In our case a potential risk factor for infection was the presence of an infected stone, which caused a stasis of urine that was trapped due to obstructions.

Some investigators have shown that the remaining ureteric stamp retains some peristaltic activity that prevents stasis, but in our case, due to stone obstruction, there has been urine stasis in the ureteric stump, which serves as a reservoir of infections [5].

Radiological investigations that can be used to diagnose ureteric stamp syndrome include retrograde ureterography, cystography, and CT IVU, which reveal the thickening of the ureteric stamp wall and enhancement and, if it contains calculi, hyperdense foci in the plain phase [1,6,7].

Complications including psoas muscle abscess or fistulalization of the ureteric stamp and nearby structures, e.g., the uterus. In addition, surgical excision of the ureteric stump or a laparoscopic approach for distal ureterectomy, also transurethral fulguration of the empyema of ureteric stump [8,9]. For our case involving an impacted stone, pneumatic contact lithotripsy was used. Since there was no reflux disease, the chance for reflux and stasis of urine was minimal to the ureteric stump; hence, clearance of the source of infection was successful [10].

Conclusion

Complete resection of the ureteric stamp due to stones at the vesical-ureteric junction is of paramount importance, especially when a foreign body is left in place, because of the potential reservoir of the infection, termed as ureteric stump syndrome. It is important to exclude ureteric stump syndrome after nephrectomy or ureteric re-implantation. Surveillance of the ureteral stump is of paramount important [11].

Complete response to pembrolizumab in a paediatric patient with Lynch syndrome-associated colorectal cancer: a case report and review of immunotherapy-related bowel obstruction
Authors: Antonio Ruggiero, Fernando Fuccillo, Palma Maurizi, Alberto Romano, Dario Talloa, Stefano Mastrangelo, Michele Basso, Vito Briganti, Maria Cristina Giustiniani, Roberto Persiani, Giorgio Attinà
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is extremely rare in paediatrics with aggressive histopathology and advanced presentation. Whilst pembrolizumab has shown efficacy in adult microsatellite instability-high or mismatch repair deficiency CRC, paediatric data remain scarce. A 12-year- old boy with Lynch syndrome-associated CRC developed severe cardiac toxicity after initial chemotherapy. Treatment was switched to off-label pembrolizumab, resulting in a complete response after 24 cycles. Despite good tolerability, the patient developed a delayed bowel stricture at the tumour site. After surgery, there was no pathological evidence of residual adenocarcinoma. This case demonstrates the potential of pembrolizumab in paediatric CRC, highlighting the importance of molecular-guided rather than age-restricted therapy selection.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is an exceptionally rare malignancy in the paediatric population, with an annual incidence of approximately 1 per 10 million adolescents under 20 years of age; this tumour accounts for 1% of paediatric cancers.1 However, compared to adults, children and adolescents tend to exhibit more aggressive disease due to unfavourable histology, with a higher incidence of signet ring and mucinous subtypes, and clinical presentation at later stages. Diagnosis is often delayed due to non-specific symptoms and the rarity of the disease.1,2

When CRC is diagnosed in children or adolescents, an underlying genetic predisposition should always be considered. Syndromic associations such as familial adenomatous polyposis and Lynch syndrome — previously referred to as hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer — must be investigated.3 In particular, Lynch syndrome is the most common hereditary colon cancer susceptibility syndrome and is characterized by a germline mutation in DNA mismatch repair genes (including MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PMS2). The syndrome is associated not only with gastrointestinal cancers but also with endometrial cancers. Notably, asymptomatic carriers of Lynch syndrome can also be identified through genetic screening.4

The diagnosis, staging and management of paediatric CRC are predominantly extrapolated from adult treatment guidelines, as no standardized international protocols exist for children and adolescents. Complete surgical excision remains the most crucial prognostic factor, even though most cases cannot undergo immediate intervention. In cases of inoperable or metastatic disease, chemotherapy is the mainstay of treatment. Standard regimens include 5-fluorouracil, folinic acid (leucovorin), oxaliplatin and irinotecan.5 These agents are often combined with targeted therapies, such as antivascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and antiepidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) monoclonal antibodies, based on the tumour’s KRAS, NRAS and BRAF mutation status.6

In recent years, immune-checkpoint inhibitors have revolutionized the management of metastatic CRC, particularly in tumours exhibiting microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR). These CRCs are more likely to respond to inhibitors targeting the immune-checkpoint proteins PD1 and CTLA4.6,7 PD1 blockade leads to changes in genes implicated in cytolysis and natural killer cell function, whilst CTLA4 blockade induces the proliferation of a subset of transitional memory T cells. When used in combination, these inhibitors modulate gene expression profiles associated with cellular proliferation and chemokine signalling.8

Pembrolizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting PD1, was the first tumour-agnostic immunotherapy approved by the FDA in 2017 for MSI-H/dMMR malignancies.9 The efficacy of pembrolizumab for MSI-H or dMMR metastatic CRC in adult patients was demonstrated by the pivotal phase III trial Keynote 177.10 This study compared pembrolizumab monotherapy to standard chemotherapy regimens (intravenous mFOLFOX6 or FOLFIRI, with or without targeted agents) in patients with MSI-H/dMMR metastatic CRC. With a median follow-up of 44.5 months, the final analysis revealed a median progression-free survival of 16.5 months in the pembrolizumab cohort compared with 8.2 months in the chemotherapy group (hazard ratio for progression-free survival 0.59). Patients with a complete response at the final analysis were 13.1% in the pembrolizumab group versus 3.9% with chemotherapy. Furthermore, pembrolizumab was associated with a more favourable safety profile, with grade ≥3 adverse events occurring in 22% of patients compared with 66% in the chemotherapy group.10

In addition to pembrolizumab, other immune-checkpoint blockade strategies have demonstrated efficacy in MSI-H metastatic CRC: the combination of nivolumab (anti-PD1) and ipilimumab (anti-CTLA4) has shown promising results, as evidenced by the phase III CheckMate 8HW trial.11

Although immune checkpoint inhibitors have demonstrated superior efficacy and an improved safety profile compared to conventional chemotherapy, long-term adverse effects remain incompletely understood. Pembrolizumab is generally well tolerated; however, it can induce immune-related adverse events, including pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies and nephritis.9,10

One emerging concern, particularly in patients with CRC treated with immunotherapy, is bowel obstruction secondary to treatment response. A recent international case series, compiled by the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO), described nine adult patients with CRC from eight European centres who developed unexpected intestinal obstruction following immune checkpoint blockade. This effect is hypothesized to result from tumour regression, leading to stricturing and fibrosis within the bowel. Although definitive predictor markers have not been established, certain radiological and endoscopic features, such as tumour location at the hepatic flexure, pre-existing strictures, endoscopic obstruction and annular tumour morphology, have been proposed as potential risk factors.12

Here, we present a case report to highlight the therapeutic potential of pembrolizumab in paediatric CRC, emphasizing the need for vigilant monitoring of both acute and delayed immune-related complications in these patients.

Ethical statement

Informed consent for publication was obtained from the parents of the patient. All data were anonymized, and access was restricted to the research team to maintain confidentiality.

Case description

A 12-year-old boy presented to the local Emergency Department with diarrhoea and haematochezia. CT scans of the abdomen revealed a large, coarse, oval-shaped mass in the pelvis, located in a left paramedian position, closely adjacent to the sigmoid colon and the posterior bladder wall. These imaging findings were accompanied by multiple lymphadenopathies involving the left common iliac region, loco-regional nodes, the left obturator region and bilateral inguinal areas. No distant metastases were found in the abdomen or the thorax. Serum tumour markers, including β-human chorionic gonadotropin, α-fetoprotein, cancer antigen 125 (CA125), carcinoembryonic antigen and CA19-9, were all within normal limits.

Due to the development of sub-occlusive symptoms, the patient was transferred to the Paediatric Surgery Department of San Camillo Hospital (Rome), where an MRI scan confirmed the findings (Figure 1), and he underwent median umbilical-pubic laparotomy and intraoperative proctoscopy with the placement of a colostomy. The histopathology report indicated the diagnosis of intestinal adenocarcinoma with necrosis foci. Additional molecular testing, including mutation of KRAS, NRAS and BRAF, revealed a KRAS mutation (G12D; p.Gly12Asp), whilst immunohistochemical profiling of MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PSM2 demonstrated dMMR. Subsequently, genetic analysis was performed using a panel comprising the mismatch repair genes MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PSM2 (SOPHIAGENETICS™), which revealed the germline variant MSH2:c.806C>T-p.(Ser269Leu), potentially pathogenic for Lynch syndrome.

Following oncological diagnosis, the patient was referred to the Paediatric Oncology Unit of Policlinico Universitario Agostino Gemelli (Rome), where his case was evaluated by the multidisciplinary CRC board. Since immunotherapy with pembrolizumab for CRC is only approved in Italy for adult patients, the initial treatment plan involved chemotherapy with the mFOLFOX6 regimen, consisting of oxaliplatin (85 mg/m2), leucovorin (400 mg/m2) and 5-fluorouracil (400 mg/m2) administered on day 1, followed by a continuous infusion of 5-fluorouracil (1200 mg/m2 per day for 2 days, for a total dose of 2400 mg/m2 over 46 hours). However, severe cardiac toxicity was observed immediately after the first chemotherapy cycle, characterized by increased troponin and pro-BNP levels and echocardiographic evidence of biventricular dysfunction (left ventricular ejection fraction 28%).

Two weeks after the resolution of the cardiac toxicity, the case was re-evaluated, leading to the decision to start immunotherapy treatment with pembrolizumab (2 mg/kg, administered every 3 weeks), in an off-label regimen.

The treatment led to a radiological partial response of the primary lesion during the first 12 cycles. Although the lymphadenopathies appeared radiologically stable, endoscopic ultrasound-guided biopsy confirmed they were negative for malignancy. Given the therapeutic response, treatment continuation was reassessed periodically by the multidisciplinary CRC board, leading to the administration of 24 cycles in total. At the completion of immunotherapy, MRI scans showed a residual 3 cm lesion at the recto-sigmoid junction and the lymphadenopathies were stable in size and number. Immunotherapy was well tolerated, except for immune-related pneumonitis (grade 1), which developed after the final dose of pembrolizumab and was successfully managed with oral corticosteroid therapy.

Radiological and endoscopic follow-up was subsequently initiated, with all evaluations including a PET-CT scan, showing no evidence of disease recurrence or progression. However, during the first year of follow-up, both MRI scans and endoscopy revealed the development of a severe colonic stricture at the site of the residual lesion. Notably, this stenosis was not observed at the end of immunotherapy but emerged later, indicating a possible long-term treatment-related effect of the immunotherapy (Figures 2 and 3). This finding led the multidisciplinary team to proceed with surgery, and the patient underwent a low anterior resection, using the trans-anal total mesorectal excision technique at the Policlinico Gemelli General Surgery Department. The definitive histological examination showed fibrosis, inflammation and sporadic microcalcifications without evidence of adenocarcinoma residue (Figure 4); 31 lymph nodes resulted free of metastasis.

The patient has now entered the third year of clinical and radiological check-ups.

Discussion

This report presents the exceptional case of a paediatric patient with MSI-H CRC who achieved a complete response to pembrolizumab. The patient’s young age, complex clinical history, unique treatment trajectory and long-term effects make this case particularly noteworthy. At the time of publication, no comparable cases encompassing all these variables have been identified in the paediatric literature.

Initial treatment followed standard adult CRC protocols, using fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy.5 However, severe cardiac toxicity, likely fluoropyrimidine-induced, necessitated treatment discontinuation.13,14 These exceptional circumstances, combined with the diagnostic finding of an MSI-H molecular pattern, presented pembrolizumab as a potential next-line therapy given its established efficacy in adult MSI-H/dMMR CRC.10 Pembrolizumab was administered off-label, with each cycle requiring multidisciplinary team review and approval. The choice of pembrolizumab over the combination of nivolumab and ipilimumab was dictated by the fact that pembrolizumab had already been approved by the Italian Medicines Agency (AIFA) for the treatment of CRC in adults since February 2022, whilst the nivolumab and ipilimumab combination was approved in November 2022, that is, after the patient had started second-line treatment (August 2022).

Radiological evaluations during the first 12 cycles demonstrated a partial response to treatment, with significant reduction of the primary lesion and dimensional stability of lymphadenopathies. Subsequently, all lesions remained stable until the 24th and final cycle. To confirm the extent of response, multiple biopsies of lymph node lesions were performed, all of which were negative for CRC. Based on these histological findings, a complete response was declared, and the patient transitioned to clinical, radiological and endoscopic surveillance.

Regarding safety, pembrolizumab was well tolerated, except for immunotherapy-induced pneumonitis. This adverse event is in line with those previously reported in adult patients.10,15 Notably, the patient developed a rare, delayed complication in the form of a grade 1 colonic obstruction. In fact, despite consistent radiological and clinical stability, endoscopy revealed progressive luminal narrowing at the primary tumour site, culminating in near-complete obstruction. Given the temporal relationship to pembrolizumab treatment and recent reports of immunotherapy-associated intestinal stenosis, this complication was attributed to the treatment’s complete response.12 This unexpected clinical finding expedited definitive surgical intervention, whereby the residual lesions (both primary and lymph node) were removed along with the entire stenotic colon segment.

At the time of publication, the patient is in the third year of follow-up after treatment completion and continues regular clinical, radiological and endoscopic assessments with no disease recurrence.

We acknowledge that the primary limitation of this study is its nature as a single-case report, which restricts the generalizability of our findings. Nevertheless, given the rarity of CRC in children and young adults, we believe that this report serves as a valuable reference for potential therapeutic management in the paediatric population, whilst also providing insights into potential treatment- related side-effects.

Conclusion

This case highlights the significant potential of pembrolizumab in the treatment of paediatric CRC and underscores the need for a paradigm shift in the therapeutic approach to this tumour type, regardless of patient age. In the absence of specific international guidelines, the management of paediatric patients with CRC is primarily extrapolated from adult protocols. In this context, it is crucial to transcend the conventional division between paediatric and adult oncology, as tumour biology — not chronological age — should guide therapeutic decision- making. Therefore, a more integrated therapeutic approach should be considered.

The successful off-label use of pembrolizumab in this case supports this agent as a therapeutic option for paediatric patients with metastatic MSI-H CRC, similar to its current application in adults. Consequently, we consider it urgent to facilitate the enrolment of paediatric patients in ongoing trials to expand therapeutic options and improve patient outcomes.

Pembrolizumab is generally well tolerated in the adult population. However, long-term side-effects remain poorly understood and require further investigation. Notably, this report also highlights a rare, delayed immune- related adverse event — colonic obstruction secondary to treatment response — previously described in adults but not characterized in children.

This work therefore, contributes valuable information to the currently available literature on this subject. Paediatric patients often lag behind in access to new treatments, depriving this population of all the therapeutic options that are available to adults. Bridging this age-based gap in oncology should be a priority for future research to ensure equitable access to emerging therapies and to optimize outcomes in younger populations.

Electronic community health information system usability evaluation in Ethiopia
Authors: Geletaw Sahle Tegenaw, Balew Ayalew Kassie, Asaye Birhanu Senay, Ketema Lemma Abdi, Woinshet Nigatu, Gemechis Melkamu, Dawit Wolde Daka, Muluemebet Abera Wordofa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundA community-level electronic health information system (eCHIS) was introduced in Ethiopia to support proper routine data management and use. Though large investments were made to implement this digital information system, the outcome was not as expected in terms of data quality and information use. Furthermore, the usability, effectiveness, efficiency, and overall satisfaction of the application’s users have remained unexplored. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the eCHIS usability factors in Ethiopia.MethodsWe followed a two-stage approach. First, we reviewed the existing literature to identify suitable protocols and usability metrics based on usability factors, criteria, and attributes. The Goal Question Metric approach was adopted to define the goal into several questions. A set of 26 questionnaires was developed and refined to encompass usability metrics across various dimensions. These dimensions included learnability (9/26), efficiency (1/26), effectiveness (3/26), trustfulness (4/26), and satisfaction (9/26). Each questionnaire was measured on a five-point Likert scale. Following that, a cross-sectional study was conducted among eCHIS users in purposefully selected districts of Oromia, Amhara, and Sidama regions of Ethiopia. The selection of study sites was based on their utilization and experience with eCHIS, in addition to considering their internet connectivity.ResultsA total of 81 participants completed the questionnaires. Overall, we observed a high level of satisfaction among health extension workers, midwives, and focal persons regarding the eCHIS application. However, we also identified some discrepancies in terms of learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustfulness, and satisfaction. We found that most health workers who casually utilize eCHIS applications had service experience greater than 10 years, eCHIS experience less than 2 years, and were in good connectivity areas, and were satisfied with the usability of eCHIS applications. Furthermore, the eCHIS usability was examined using the five metrics across the different eCHIS users (health extension workers, health professionals, focal persons, and staff at the Ministry of Health, regional, zonal, and woreda levels), which revealed relatively comparable mean score values for novice, casual, and expert users.ConclusionsThe usability evaluation of the eCHIS in Ethiopia revealed that users with experience and good internet connectivity were satisfied. Future generations of eCHIS will require standardizing the user interface, rules, and guidelines based on user experience, language translation consistency, and establishing a baseline standard for assessing user competency.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12911-025-03228-9.Keywords: Usability, eCHIS, HMIS, GQM approach, Learnability, Efficiency, Effectiveness, Trustfulness, Satisfaction, Ethiopia

Background

In Ethiopia, the community-level data management system, also called the community health information system (CHIS), was introduced in 2010 following the initiation of the routine health information system in 2008. This system is aimed at managing and using data generated as part of the Health Extension Program (HEP) of Ethiopia, which was started in 2003. The paper-based CHIS captures information about households and members, household characteristics information, and the packages of health services received by the population in the catchment area of the health posts by using tools including family folders and health cards [1–3]. A health post is the lowest health service delivery point in Ethiopia, supervised by the primary health care unit. Health posts provide a HEP package of services to a catchment population of 3000 to 5000 and were managed by at least two health extension workers (the Ethiopian form of community health workers) [2].

Despite the remarkable contributions of CHIS to data management practices at the community level of Ethiopia, its implementation has encountered various challenges. On the one hand, the manual management of community health data, combined with the workload of the HEWs and data burden, has resulted in the generation and reporting of data with poor quality and low information utilization. Though large investments are made in CHIS, the outcome gained was not as expected in terms of data quality and information use. This challenge was also evident from other low and middle-income countries that have adopted CHIS [6–8]. These and other reasons have called for the Government of Ethiopia to advance manual data management to electronic-based data management at the community level.

The development of the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS) application was started in 2016 following the introduction of the Information Revolution (IR) Roadmap, and actual implementation was started in 2018. The eCHIS is a high-priority initiative, demonstrating the Ethiopian Ministry of Health’s (MOH) intentions to further use technology and data to improve service delivery starting at the community level. The eCHIS digitizes the family folder and CHIS content into a mobile platform for use by Health Extension Workers (HEW) in the country. The mobile platform and corresponding clinical, reporting, and system management tools are expected to promote access to and use of data about community service delivery within HEP, supporting HEW responsibilities and equipping decision-makers with relevant, high-quality data to advance the health system in Ethiopia [4].

The eCHIS Application has three main components or independent applications, namely the HEW application, Health Center Referral Application, and the Focal Person application. The HEWs application is used for registering households and their members, guiding and documenting service provision, providing linkage for client referrals, and reporting service delivery data for use in program monitoring and performance improvement. The Health Center Referral Application is used for providing prenatal and postnatal care, family planning services, child vaccination, tetanus vaccination, nutrition service, linkage for pregnant and lactating women (PLW), and integrated management of childhood neonatal and childhood illness (IMNCI) referrals, and providing disease management services. The focal person application is used for completing supervision checklists, managing late visits, updating app settings, troubleshooting technical eCHIS issues, accessing service reports, and monitoring and reporting Health Post performance. eCHIS enables data recording, referral linkage, an automated reporting, monitoring, and performance analysis system, and brings efficiency in service delivery by allowing HEWs, their supervisors, health managers, and other healthcare providers to easily review household and individual data to deliver tailored services to households and individuals. It helps to provide real-time service delivery, serves as a job aid to HEWs, and facilitates the identification of households and individuals to avoid duplication of records [5–11]. Currently, eCHIS is under implementation in more than 7000 agrarian health posts in Ethiopia, including agrarian and pastoralist areas.

The usability of digital health systems is a critical factor in their acceptability and effective use by end-users of the system. Usability, in a more general term, is defined as the quality of a user’s experience when interacting with products or systems, including websites, software, devices, or applications. Usability is the extent to which users can use a system or application to achieve specified goals effectively and efficiently while promoting feelings of satisfaction in each context of use. It is about effectiveness, efficiency, and the overall satisfaction of the user [9–11]. The usability of mobile applications and technology is related to their adoption and acceptability by the end-users. People tend to use products that are easy to understand, work as expected, and eventually deliver value. In the context of software engineering, system usability plays a crucial role in shaping perceived quality in use by its users [12]. Evaluating the usability of the eCHIS application is important to know whether it achieves its intended goals efficiently or not, and it indicates the future direction of investments related to technology advancements. Currently, the eCHIS application is under implementation, and further developments are underway. Hence, the present usability evaluation is crucial to improving the application. Moreover, Carayon, P., & Hoonakker, P. (2019) emphasize the need for improved usability to enhance patient safety, care quality, and user satisfaction [13].

Previous literature has revealed that various usability evaluation models and standards, including ISO 9241-11, ISO 9126-1, ISO 9241 − 151, and the mobile Goal Question Model (mGQM), were used for testing technology usability [14]. In their comparison of two heuristic evaluation techniques for identifying usability issues in health information systems, Khajouei, R., Gohari, S.H., and Mirzaee, M. observed that the checklist method significantly outperformed the other in terms of the number of identified usability problems [15]. However, most of these models are based on desktop applications and are not specific to a particular mobile application. Additionally, these models are general and do not provide overall descriptions of how to select metrics corresponding to the usability factors or criteria of a specific application [16]. Moreover, the majority of usability testing is conducted in a controlled environment and doesn’t accurately show usability in the real context or operational environment. Usability testing with real participants is a fundamental evaluation method. It provides an evaluator with direct information about how people use systems or applications and what problems are with the application being tested. During usability testing, participants use the system or the application to complete a pre-defined set of tasks, and the evaluator records the results of users’ work. The evaluator then uses these results to identify the strengths and weaknesses of the system [17, 18].

Therefore, this research aimed to investigate the usability factors, attributes, criteria, and metrics surrounding the eCHIS application, and evaluate the application based on the identified factors, attributes, criteria, and metrics.

Methods

General approach

Model adoption and tool development

To develop our research protocol, we identified usability contextual factors (User, Task, Environment, Technology), usability attributes, and guidelines and criteria before establishing usability metrics based on [19–22]. Finstad, K., et al. 2010 [19] introduced usability metrics based on subjective metric evaluation that consider the subjective aspects of user experience. Similarly, Hussain and colleagues focused on subjective metrics for mobile evaluation. Meanwhile, Moumane, Karima, Ali Idri, and Alain Abran. 2016 [21] proposed a methodological approach for evaluating the usability of mobile applications using internationally recognized standards, specifically ISO 9241 and ISO 25,062. Then, we applied a Goal-Question-Metrics approach (GQM) to identify and develop usability metrics. Those metrics were directly used to develop the questionnaire for the data collection phase. Questions were developed to assess each described goal using the GQM approach (see more details in Supplementary Questionnaire I). Each question was designed carefully to refine the goal into several questions, and again, all the questions were refined into a set of usability metrics for the evaluation of the eCHIS application. In general, to assess the eCHIS usability, the data collection instrument was developed based on a literature review and an ISO usability evaluation tool for mobile applications.

Usability contextual factors for eCHIS application

We have developed usability metrics to measure the use of the eCHIS application. Usability factors include user, technology, task/activity, and environment. The usability of the eCHIS application was evaluated based on five usability attributes, i.e., learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustfulness, and satisfaction. More specifically, examine: (i) the learnability to explore how easy it is to learn and how easy it is to carry out basic tasks; (ii) the efficiency to understand the acceptable amount of time and effort required to utilize eCHIS; (iii) the effectiveness to explore whether the introduction of eCHIS speed up the workflow and displays the desired output as the user expect, (iv) the trustfulness of the eCHIS application performing tasks trustfully without any doubt of losing data or information; and (v) the satisfaction and attitudes of the users using eCHIS. A total of 26 survey questionnaires for the eCHIS application were used, and these attributes were adopted based on their relevance, importance, and impact on the usability of mobile applications to identify the usability issues of the eCHIS application. Furthermore, the study aimed to evaluate three eCHIS applications, such as the eCHIS application for HEW, the health center referral (HCR), and the focal person (FP).

Study design, setting, and period

A cross-sectional study was conducted among eCHIS users in purposefully selected districts of three regions of Ethiopia (Oromia, Amhara, and Sidama); zonal health departments (ZHDs) of the selected districts; regional health bureaus (RHBs) of the respective regions; and the Ministry of Health (MOH) from January to September 2022. The three districts were selected purposefully (one district per region) based on their eCHIS implementation status. The districts with at least 80% of the health posts that have started providing services using eCHIS (at least digital family folder, reproductive maternal newborn and child health (RMNCH) module), all of the health centers started referral services using eCHIS, the focal person at woreda health office (WorHO) started using focal person applications, and a district that had implemented eCHIS for at least 3 months before the assessment date were included in the study. In general, the three districts have completed household registration and started service using eCHIS. Furthermore, the identification of eCHIS users aligns with International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 9241-11 [23], which defines a user as someone who interacts with eCHIS. This encompasses individuals operating the eCHIS system, utilizing its outputs, and supporting it through eCHIS tasks such as maintenance and training. Following that, eCHIS user classification [24–26] was performed based on characteristics of the users, tasks, technology, or environments that could influence usability. In this study, the classification is based on skills and experiences (Novice, Casual, and Expert users), location (Oromia, Amhara, and Sidama, Ministry of Health), roles (HEW, Midwife, Focal Person, people from MoH), and internet connectivity (Poor and Good).

Study participants and selection procedure

eCHIS is used by HEWs, midwives, and the focal person. Based on their eCHIS use experience and level of expertise, users were divided into three groups: novice, casual, and expert. A novice is a user who has obtained eCHIS training but has not yet begun using eCHIS. A casual user is someone who has obtained eCHIS training but only uses it occasionally. An expert user is someone who has undergone eCHIS training, understands the majority of the system’s components, always uses eCHIS, has participated in system design and requirement analysis and/or development, and is an eCHIS technical expert. Additionally, we classified the study sites into those with good (allowing sync and referral of client data) and poor (delaying sync and referral of client information) internet connectivity.

Twelve HEWs (three novices with good connectivity, three novices with poor connectivity, three casuals with good connectivity, and three casuals with poor connectivity). Four midwives (one novice with good connectivity, one novice with poor connectivity, one casual with good connectivity, and one casual with poor connectivity). Additionally, four focal persons (one novice with good connectivity, one novice with poor connectivity, one casual with good connectivity, and one casual with poor connectivity) were selected and included in each district.

A total of two users were purposefully selected and included in the study from each of the Woreda health offices, zonal health departments, regional health bureau, and the MOH experts who are supporting eCHIS implementation. Also, a minimum of ten experts were planned from partners and developers of eCHIS. Eighty-one participants in total (60 from three districts and 21 from WorHO, ZHD, RHB, MoH, and partners) took part in this study. Overall, a total of 91 participants were planned for this study.

Data collection

Data were collected through an interviewer-based approach using standardized questionnaires. The questionnaire comprises two sections addressing respondents’ characteristics and usability metrics. The data collection was an interview (filling in a questionnaire based on their eCHIS experience and expertise). Before data collection, training in data collection tools was provided to data collectors. All data collectors included Bachelor of Science (BSc) and Master of Science (MSc) graduates with backgrounds in information technology, health informatics, public health, computer science, and software engineering.

Data quality control strategies were applied before, during, and after data collection. The tool was adapted from the evaluation metrics and uploaded to Open Data Kit (ODK), and the data were collected using tablets. The validation technique was employed when uploading the tool to ODK. The Data collectors and supervisors who had knowledge of HMIS/CHIS and previous experience in similar data collection were recruited outside of the study area. Data collectors were health information technology (HIT) professionals with a qualification of a diploma and a BSc, and supervisors were public health, computer science, and information science professionals with a qualification of a master’s degree. Prior to data collection, two days of training were provided to data collectors and supervisors with a focus on questionnaires, data collection techniques, and research ethics. The data collection process was closely monitored by supervisors at the field level as well as the data manager at the center. Daily feedback was given to data collectors, and measures were taken at the field level.

Data processing and analysis

After data collection, the completeness and consistency of data values were checked, cleaned, and exported to SPSS version 25 and Python for analysis. Also, Tableau Software was used for data visualization. The analysis was based on three eCHIS apps: HEW, HCR, and FP applications. A total of 26 usability survey questions were used to evaluate each application, classified into five categories for analysis: learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustworthiness, and satisfaction. The analysis and evaluation of eCHIS learnability were based on nine parameters, including home page layout, output dissemination, menu names and icons/buttons, overall user consistency, ease of use, ease of learning, graphic usage, language usage, and the availability of contents. Efficiency was based on the loading performance of eCHIS. Effectiveness was based on three parameters, including task performance, navigation structure, and information organization in eCHIS apps. Trustfulness was based on four parameters, including the access denial support of eCHIS, session time, user ID, password style and format, and authentication techniques. Nine parameters were used for analyzing the satisfaction of eCHIS, including action history, eCHIS stopping me from making errors, logical presentation of tags, current update request, UI style and format, help menu, alert message, easy error correction, and effective and consistent use of colors and backgrounds. Furthermore, the analysis was conducted based on internet connections and user profiles (casual, expert, and novice users). Finally, the usability triangulation was conducted between casual, expert, and novice users of eCHIS. In this study, the expert eCHIS user was utilized as a gold standard and as a reference for comparison against casual and novice users of eCHIS.

Results

Socio-demographic profile

Overall, a total of 81 participants completed the questionnaire across three regions in Ethiopia and the MoH and partner offices from Addis Ababa. There were ten non-respondents among the 91 planned participants. Most of the respondents were female (60.5%), with a mean age of 30 years, and 56.80% of the respondents had a diploma (Level 4 education). The distribution of respondent roles was as follows: HEW: 36 (44.44%), Focal Person (FP): 12 (14.81%), Midwife: 12 (14.81%), Health Information Technician (HIT): 7 (7.41%). The remaining respondents held various positions such as there were four participants from the Ministry of Health (MOH) working as senior software engineers, developers, project managers, and Health Information System (HIS) specialists (eCHIS implementation coordinators), one participant working as an Health Extension Program (HEP) focal person, one HMIS officer at the regional level, five participants from the planning department, and one participant from the Planning, Monitoring, and Evaluation Director. Forty-four (54.32%) respondents had less than 2 years of eCHIS experience, and 48(59.26%) had poor Internet connectivity. All the respondents (98.3%) except one had taken eCHIS training. eCHIS efficiency

On the loading performance of the eCHIS APP, 74%, 66%, and 58% of HEW, HCR, and FP agreed on eCHIS efficiency, respectively. However, 26% of HEW, 44% of HCR, and 42% of FP disagreed with the eCHIS efficiency app (Table 2).

eCHIS effectiveness

An agreement on HEW eCHIS effectiveness was observed among the three eCHIS parameters: HEW agreed with 85% of tasks performed by eCHIS and the observed information organization in eCHIS. Additionally, there was a 95% agreement on the consistent navigation structure of eCHIS. On the HCR eCHIS app, all three parameters scored an agreement score of 91% on the efficacy of eCHIS. Meanwhile, regarding the effectiveness of the FP eCHIS app, agreements of 82%, 100%, and 91% were observed for tasks performed by eCHIS, the consistent navigation structure of eCHIS, and the information organization of eCHIS, respectively. However, an overall disagreement rate of 9% was observed for the HCR eCHIS effectiveness. We also observed 15% disagreement on the information organization and task performed by the HEW application. Furthermore, 18% of disagreement were observed on the focal person application and 9% disagreement were observed on information organization of FP application (Table 2).

eCHIS trustfulness

Among the trustfulness aspects of HEW eCHIS, agreement scores of 65%, 55%, 88%, and 72% were observed in the following areas: access detail support provided by eCHIS, session time provided by eCHIS, user ID or password style and format of eCHIS, and authentication techniques provided by eCHIS, respectively. However, the disagreement in trustworthiness for HEW eCHIS ranged from 12% to 45%. An agreement scores of 60–91% was observed in the HCR eCHIS, and an agreement score of 25%–91% was observed in the FP eCHIS application. Whereas 9–44% were observed on HCR eCHIS trustfulness evaluation. Furthermore, 75% disagreement was observed on the session time provided by the FP application (Table 2).

eCHIS satisfaction

Among the nine parameters assessed, the HEW eCHIS application demonstrated an agreement score ranging from 82% to 94% in terms of user satisfaction. However, the help menu provided by HEW eCHIS received a lower score of 41%. In the cases of HCR-eCHIS satisfaction, the eight-parameter satisfaction score was between 74% and 99%. Whereas action history not provided by eCHIS scored an agreement of 26%. The FP eCHIS application, on the other hand, received an agreement score of 74% to 99%, except for an alert message if an error occurred by eCHIS, which was 66%. HEW eCHIS satisfaction disagreement was observed from 6 to 59%. The eCHIS has no action history feature yet, and we found that 22%, 74%, 26% of HEW, Midwife, and FP were in disagreement (Table 2).

eCHIS usability: comparison results

The eCHIS usability comparison was performed between expert, novice, and casual users, and we found that the expert evaluation of eCHIS learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustfulness, and satisfaction were 4.56, 4.00, 4.67, 3.92, and 4.28, respectively.

On the learnability of the eCHIS application, a maximum of 0.45 mean difference was observed between expert and causal users, while a value of 0.38 was observed between expert and novice users. On the efficiency of eCHIS application, the casual users’ maximum mean difference was observed among FP, and Regional, Zonal, and Woreda eCHIS experts, with a value of 0.89 and 1.12, respectively. Whereas no difference was observed between novice HCR (midwife) users and expert users, a 0.33 difference was observed between HEW and Midwife users.

On the Effectiveness eCHIS application, the casual user minimum and maximum mean difference from the expert users were 0.13 (i.e., HEW) and 0.57 (i.e., Regional, Zonal, and Woreda eCHIS experts), respectively. Novice Midwife users, on the other hand, had no difference with the expert users. However, the Novice HEW mean difference from the expert user was found to be 0.7.

There was no difference between expert and casual FP users on trustfulness of the eCHIS application, while there was a 0.42 difference between Regional, zonal, and woreda eCHIS support and eCHIS experts.

The satisfaction of the eCHIS application was 4.28 among expert users. Whereas, except for the HEW and Midwife (HCR), all casual users were scored below four. Discussion

The study’s findings, grounded in usability contextual factors such as user, task, environment, and technology, uncover intriguing insights and present opportunities for enhancing the usability of the next generation of eCHIS applications. On the one hand, we observed a high percentage of agreement scores among participants. The eCHIS usability evaluation agreement score indicates “how well the users learn and use eCHIS to achieve their goals and how satisfied they are with that process [27]. On the other hand, we observed neutrality or disagreement among respondents during the eCHIS evaluation. The disagreement score helps determine the features and functionality of the next generation of eCHIS in Ethiopia, including improving user experience and satisfaction [28–30], enhancing eCHIS functionality and performance [29–31], and increasing eCHIS adoption (intention to use and continue to use as well as recommend the app to others) [28, 30, 31] and retention, such as reducing user frustration, dissatisfaction, and withdrawal of eCHIS app usage [29, 31]. In this study, the agreement score was calculated based on ‘strongly agree’ and ‘agree’ responses, while the disagreement score was calculated based on ‘undecided,’ ‘disagree,’ and ‘strongly disagree’ responses. Overall, the HEW eCHIS app usability agreement ranges from 41% on the help menu provided by eCHIS to 99% on the home page of HEW eCHIS. HCR eCHIS app agreement ranges from action provided by eCHIS to 26% to 99% of the home page, easy-to-learn language, and available contents of HCR eCHIS. Focal Person eCHIS app usability agreement ranges from 25% session time to 100% agreement on the home page, easy to use, consistent navigation structure, error prevention (the application stops making errors), logical presentation of tasks, and UI style and format.

On the learnability of eCHIS based on nine parameters in the HEW eCHIS app, the highest agreement scores of greater than 90% were observed in the home page, menu names, icons, or buttons of eCHIS, the overall interface consistency of eCHIS, the ease of use of eCHIS, and the available content of eCHIS. In the HCR eCHIS app, except for the graphics of the eCHIS app (82%), all the other parameters scored an agreement of greater than 90%. Whereas, in the FP eCHIS app, all the parameters scored greater than 90%, and two parameters scored 100%, such as the home page and ease of use of the FP eCHIS app. However, the available content of the eCHIS FP app got an agreement score of 83%. Overall, except for the home page and easy-to-use eCHIS FP app, there was a disagreeable response. Specifically, a 15% disagreement score on the ‘easy-to-learn’ eCHIS HEW app, a 14% disagreement score on the graphics of the eCHIS HEW app, an 18% disagreement score on the graphics of the eCHIS HCR app, a 9% disagreement score on the graphics of the eCHIS FP app, and so on. To address this, the study findings emphasize that improving the learnability of eCHIS requires standardizing the user interface (UI), rules, and guidelines based on user experience, such as color, number of clicks, steps, etc., in the next generation of eCHIS applications. Addressing language translation consistency and refinement is also crucial. It is also good to include quick tips and assistance without affecting performance to improve usability. Overall, a well-designed eCHIS should be easy to learn and remember [23, 30].

Efficiency was based on one parameter: the loading performance of the eCHIS app agreement score was 74%, 66%, and 58% on the HEW, HCR, and FP of the eCHIS application, respectively. However, we observed a disagreement score of 26%, 44%, and 42% for HEW, HCR, and FP, respectively. This disagreement may be related to internet connectivity, but it requires further exploration and investigation. Resolving the disagreement regarding the efficiency of eCHIS will enhance the overall user experience. Ultimately, should allow HEW, midwives, and FP to complete activities swiftly while not interfering with their workflow, and it should become a central tool for Ethiopian health information systems [25, 32].

Effectiveness was based on three parameters, all of which scored higher than 90% agreement in the effectiveness of the eCHIS HCR app. The information organization of the eCHIS HEW app and the tasks performed scored an agreement of 85%, while the FP eCHIS task performed scored 82%. However, the 15% disagreement regarding task performance in eCHIS and the information organization in HEW eCHIS indicate that the next version of eCHIS will consider these parameters to enhance its effectiveness. Additionally, the 18% disagreement score in tasks performed by eCHIS on the FP eCHIS app will impact the overall performance of eCHIS. Moreover, minimizing the number of steps (clicks) will be crucial for improving the efficiency of eCHIS. A well-designed eCHIS facilitates the flow of clinical information from Kebele to the national level, resulting in better patient care and documentation. It should enable HEWs, Midwives, and FPs to carry out their duties appropriately and thoroughly [25].

Trustfulness is an important aspect of usability in eCHIS, and four parameters were used to evaluate the trustfulness of eCHIS. An agreement scores of 65%, 66%, and 75% was obtained in access detail support provided by eCHIS in HEW, HCR, and FP, respectively. HEW, HCR, and FP session time provided by the eCHIS agreement score were 55%, 60%, and 25%, respectively. The user ID or password style and format of the eCHIS agreement score were 88%, 74%, and 50% in HEW, HCR, and FP, respectively. Authentication techniques provided by eCHIS were 72%, 91%, and 91% in HEW, HCR, and FP, respectively. However, the disagreement in trustfulness is significantly higher in comparison with the other categories. Actions such as access support and password requirements (e.g., password age) were identified as crucial factors for enhancing the trustworthiness of eCHIS. Overall, enhancing data quality and integrity (including providing accurate, complete, and consistent data) [30], system performance and reliability (ensuring quick response times and minimizing errors and failures) [25], user interface design and feedback (ensuring a clear, intuitive, and user-friendly design) [33], and security and privacy measures (protecting data from unauthorized access and use) [25, 33] will improve the confidence and satisfaction of HEWs, midwives, and FPs with the system.

Finally, to evaluate the satisfaction of eCHIS, nine parameters were used. Among the nine satisfaction parameters, the agreement score was higher than 80% on seven parameters. Action history not provided by eCHIS and the help menu provided by eCHIS were scored at 78% and 41%, respectively. Similarly, action histories not provided by eCHIS scored an agreement of 26% and 74% on the HCR and FP apps, respectively. To enhance eCHIS user satisfaction, this study identifies automatic data synchronization (via push notifications) as crucial, dependent on internet connectivity or a set of predefined criteria. Conversely, for evaluating a user’s competency in using eCHIS, baseline standards must be established and introduced. In the end, users such as HEWs, midwives, and FPs should find the eCHIS easy to learn and remember, which often comes from intuitive design and a positive user experience [25].

This study also examined the usability of eCHIS based on participant type, total experience in years, eCHIS experiences, and internet connectivity. We observed that the following groups of health workers were satisfied with the learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustfulness, and satisfaction of eCHIS. First, compared to novice users, casual users agreed. This finding is evident, as a casual user refers to someone who has received eCHIS training but only uses it occasionally, while a novice is a user who has undergone eCHIS training but has not yet started using the system. Next, health workers who had service experience greater than 10 years and were in areas with good internet connectivity expressed their agreement in comparison with those who had less than 10 years of experience and were in areas with poor internet connectivity. Nevertheless, health workers in areas with poor internet connectivity express agreement with the effectiveness of eCHIS compared to those with good connectivity. This may need further investigation, as we are uncertain about the exact reasons. Finally, health workers with less than 2 years of eCHIS experience show a higher level of agreement, while the level of agreement among those with more than 2 years of experience is lower. While the exact reason is unknown, this may need further investigation and the formulation of hypotheses. Typically, under normal circumstances, higher experience with eCHIS correlates with greater agreement on its learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustworthiness, and satisfaction. Possible reasons may include the sample size of our study and the likelihood that, as health workers become familiar with eCHIS, they may demand new features and user experiences, among other factors.

Furthermore, comparison among novice, casual, and expert users based on twenty-six parameters revealed that, despite their different levels of experience, these user groups demonstrated comparable levels of usability when using the eCHIS application. For instance, a maximum of 0.45 mean difference was observed between expert and causal users, while a value of 0.38 was observed between expert and novice users on the learnability of eCHIS. More information on the learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustworthiness, and satisfaction of the eCHIS application is shown in Fig. 1.

However, this study has certain limitations. The study has a limited scale, and a large-scale usability study is required. For the future development and enhancement of eCHIS, follow user experience best practices and address the gaps identified by this study in the next generation of eCHIS to improve learnability, efficiency, effectiveness, trustworthiness, and satisfaction.

Conclusions

To precisely identify where eCHIS improvement is needed, this study intends to: (i) investigate the usability factors, attributes, criterion, and metrics; and (ii) evaluate the eCHIS application based on the identified factors, attributes, criteria, and metrics. eCHIS usability was evaluated by HEWs, Midwives, and FPs based on a five-point rating scale of five usability measurement attributes. Out of the total 26 parameters, learnability was based on nine parameters, Efficiency was based on one parameter, Effectiveness was based on three parameters, Trustfulness was based on four parameters, and satisfaction was based on nine parameters. We found that most health workers who casually utilize eCHIS applications had service experience greater than 10 years, eCHIS experience less than 2 years, and were in good connectivity areas, and were satisfied with the usability of eCHIS applications. Overall, the findings of the eCHIS usability study reveal opportunities to improve the usability of the next generation of eCHIS applications. Improving learnability will require (i) standardizing the user interface, rules, and guidelines based on user experience, e.g., color, number of clicks, steps, and (ii) language translation consistency and refinement. Incorporating quick tips and assistance without affecting performance will improve the efficiency of eCHIS. The future generation of eCHIS will minimize the number of steps (clicks). The infrastructure for deploying eCHIS must improve internet access and quality in addition to the application itself. e.g., the end-user device must meet certain performance and minimum criteria as well as be compatible with the latest internet technologies. Including access support and passwords, e.g., password age for eCHIS apparition, will improve trustworthiness. Automatic data synchronization (push notifications), depending on the availability of Internet connectivity or a set of predefined criteria, will improve satisfaction. Furthermore, to assess whether a user is competent to use eCHIS or not, baseline standards must be defined and introduced.

CDC42SE1 as a novel prognostic biomarker with immunomodulatory associations in breast invasive carcinoma
Authors: Yeyao Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aims to investigate the expression pattern, prognostic significance, and immune-related role of CDC42SE1 in breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA).MethodsRNA sequencing and clinical data from TCGA-BRCA and GEO datasets (GSE42568, GSE10810) were analyzed. CDC42SE1 expression was compared between tumor and normal tissues. Survival analyses, univariate and multivariate Cox regression, immune infiltration estimation (ssGSEA), and functional enrichment analyses were performed. A nomogram was constructed to predict patient survival.ResultsCDC42SE1 was significantly upregulated in BRCA tissues compared to normal controls (p < 0.001; AUC = 0.757), with validation in two independent GEO cohorts and at the protein level (CPTAC). High CDC42SE1 expression correlated with reduced overall survival (p = 0.014; HR = 1.43) and was identified as an independent prognostic factor in multivariate analysis (HR = 1.43, p = 0.033). Immune infiltration analysis revealed moderate positive associations with Tcm (R = 0.335), Th2, and Th17 cells, and negative associations with pDCs and cytotoxic cells. Functional enrichment linked CDC42SE1 to histone modification, ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, and mRNA surveillance pathways. A nomogram incorporating CDC42SE1 showed moderate accuracy in predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival (AUC: 0.580–0.585).ConclusionCDC42SE1 is overexpressed in BRCA and associated with poor prognosis and altered immune infiltration, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker with immunomodulatory relevance. Further mechanistic studies are warranted.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04960-y.Keywords: CDC42SE1, BRCA, Prognosis, Nomogram, Survival

Introduction

Breast Invasive Carcinoma (BRCA) is the leading cancer among women worldwide [1, 2]. According to worldwide cancer data from 2020, approximately 2.3 million new breast cancer instances are reported annually, leading to around 680,000 deaths each year. Females have an overall lifetime probability of roughly 12.9% to develop this type of cancer [1, 3]. In developed nations, the age-adjusted incidence can reach 90 cases per 100,000 population, whereas in underdeveloped areas like certain African regions, it is around 30 per 100,000 [4, 5]. Nevertheless, regions with limited resources experience elevated mortality burdens, primarily attributable to delays in diagnosis and constrained access to therapeutic interventions [6]. Common risk factors include age (significantly increased risk after > 50 years), family history, hormone exposure (e.g., long-term estrogen use), and lifestyle factors (such as obesity and alcohol consumption) [7]. Although early diagnosis and advances in treatment have reduced mortality, the heterogeneity and regional disparities of BRCA continue to pose challenges for public health [8]. The development of bioinformatics and high-throughput technologies has offered new insights into the molecular heterogeneity of BRCA [9–11].

CDC42SE1 (also known as SPEC1) is a small effector protein of CDC42 [12, 13] containing a conserved CRIB domain and key N-terminal residues. It collaborates with CDC42 to regulate cellular morphology, mediate F-actin assembly at the immunological synapse, and participate in phagocytosis [12, 14, 15]. The actin cytoskeleton dynamics it influences are fundamental for cell motility, proliferation, and neoplastic progression [16]. Despite these functions, research on CDC42SE1 remains limited. CDC42SE1 also regulates F-actin distribution via a non-canonical activin-BMP signaling pathway, highlighting its role in cellular morphology and neural development [17]. Additionally, it has been identified as one of 13 genes enriched in immune-related pathways in Alzheimer’s disease, suggesting potential involvement in immune regulation [18]. A genome-wide association study linked CDC42SE1 to 24-hour urinary sodium excretion, indicating a role in sodium homeostasis [19]. Research on CDC42SE1 in cancer remains limited; in A431 cutaneous carcinoma cells, low CDC42SE1 expression correlates with increased proliferation and migration, whereas ectopic expression inhibits these processes through negative regulation of CDC42 and the PAK1-Akt signaling pathway, associating low expression with poor prognosis [20]. Additionally, in gastric cancer cell lines, CDC42SE1 augments the synergistic action of taxanes and suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid [21]. Taken together, these findings suggest that CDC42SE1 may play context-dependent roles in tumor progression and drug response, motivating its systematic investigation in breast invasive carcinoma to assess expression patterns, prognostic significance, and immune associations.

CDC42SE1, a small effector protein of CDC42, has been implicated in cytoskeletal regulation, cellular proliferation, and immune-related pathways in non-cancer contexts [12, 18]. Although limited studies have explored its role in cancer, its potential involvement in tumor progression and immune modulation provides a rationale for investigating CDC42SE1 in BRCA. Therefore, our study aims to systematically analyze its expression, prognostic significance, and associations with immune infiltration in breast invasive carcinoma using public multi-omics datasets.

With progress in bioinformatics, researchers have utilized public databases to combine multi-omics data, facilitating comprehensive investigation of the molecular mechanisms driving BRCA. This study examined public databases to assess CDC42SE1 level in BRCA and its relationships with clinicopathological features, overall survival (OS), prognostic indicators, and immune infiltration. CDC42SE1 may contribute to the tumorigenesis and advancement of BRCA, underscoring its promise as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

Methods

Data acquisition

RNA-sequencing data and associated clinical details were sourced from the TCGA-BRCA project via the TCGA data portal. Gene expression matrices in TPM format were derived from STAR-aligned sequencing data (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). R software was utilized to conduct all data analysis and graphical visualization(version 4.2.1), during which samples lacking clinical information or containing duplicates were removed. Additionally, to validate the findings, two distinct datasets, GSE42568 and GSE10810, were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository [22, 23]. All data utilized in this study were obtained in accordance with the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and the TCGA guidelines. It is important to note that no direct experiments involving human participants or animals were conducted in this study. In addition, protein expression data from the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) were used for supplementary validation of CDC42SE1 expression at the protein level in BRCA.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis

We employed the pROC package in R for ROC curve analysis and used ggplot2 for visualization.

Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analysis

The proportional hazards assumption for all Cox models (univariate, multivariate, and nomogram components) was tested using the Schoenfeld residuals method (p > 0.05 for all covariates). Variables with non-significant univariate associations or indeterminate clinical status were excluded from the multivariate analysis. Patients were stratified into high and low CDC42SE1 expression groups based on the median value, with sensitivity analyses using quartiles to assess robustness. Internal validation was performed via bootstrap resampling. GEO datasets were used exclusively for expression validation, while survival analyses were conducted using TCGA-BRCA, with GEPIA providing independent platform-level confirmation [24].

Construction and evaluation of the prognostic nomogram

Prognostic factors identified through single-variable and multi-variable Cox regression models were utilized to construct a nomogram. This model incorporated variables significantly correlated with prognosis (multivariate p < 0.05) to predict 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS probabilities. The predictive performance of the nomogram was examined via calibration plots. R was employed for all statistical analyses. The survival package facilitated proportional hazards assumption tests and Cox regression, while the rms package was utilized for nomogram construction and visualization.

Enrichment analysis

mRNAs co-expressed with the CDC42SE1 gene were analyzed. We created all data plots using the ggplot2 library in R. Correlation between primary and secondary variables was analyzed, and results were presented as a co-expression heatmap (generated with ggplot2). To elucidate biological functions, significantly co-expressed genes underwent Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses in R. Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and adjusted p-values (FDR) were used to determine statistical significance. Enrichment results with adjusted p-values < 0.05 were considered significant. To improve interpretability, only the top enriched and biologically relevant GO terms and KEGG pathways were highlighted in the main text and figures.

Immune infiltration analysis

Data comprising the immune infiltration matrix were sourced from the Xiantao Academic platform (https://www.xiantao.love). Correlation between key variables in the preprocessed data and immune infiltration levels was analyzed using R, and outcomes were presented using the ggplot2 package. Immune infiltration levels were estimated using the Xiantao Academic platform, employing the ssGSEA algorithm with reference immune cell gene sets. Tumor purity adjustments were applied to account for stromal contamination, ensuring accurate quantification of immune cell fractions.

Statistical analysis

Violin plots illustrated CDC42SE1 expression levels across tumor and adjacent normal tissues. Using the median expression level as a cutoff, patients were categorized into high- and low-CDC42SE1 level subgroups. Baseline clinicopathological characteristics were summarized for the BRCA cohort, and correlations between CDC42SE1 level and clinical variables were evaluated. Prognostic factors were identified using both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models, with results visualized in a forest plot. Statistical significance was defined as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001. Subgroup analyses were exploratory; no multiple-testing correction (e.g., Bonferroni) was applied owing to the hypothesis-generating nature of the study.

Results

CDC42SE1 level in breast carcinoma and paired normal tissues

We collected gene expression data from 111 normal samples and 1069 BRCA patients from the TCGA database. Elevated CDC42SE1 expression level was detected in BRCA samples relative to normal controls (Fig. 1A). Marked overexpression of CDC42SE1 was detected in tumor tissues from 111 paired BRCA samples (Fig. 1B). The AUC for the CDC42SE1 gene was 0.757 (95% CI: 0.717–0.797), indicating moderate discriminative capacity for distinguishing BRCA from normal tissue (Fig. 1C). Additionally, CDC42SE1 level showed significant differences in 18 common cancer types, including BRCA, cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), and uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC) (Fig. 1D). To validate these findings, three independent datasets (TCGA-BRCA, GSE42568, and GSE10810) were analyzed using the thresholds |logFC| > 0.5 and adjusted p-value (p.adj) < 0.05. Results consistently showed significant up-regulation of CDC42SE1 across all three datasets (Fig. 1E and G). To further validate these findings at the protein level, CPTAC data were analyzed and showed that CDC42SE1 protein expression was also elevated in BRCA tissues compared with normal breast tissues (Supplementary Figure S1). Collectively, these results from both transcriptomic and proteomic datasets indicate that CDC42SE1 represents a potential biomarker for BRCA.

Association between CDC42SE1 level and clinicopathological features in BRCA

A group of 1069 BRCA patients was allocated to low (n = 534) and high (n = 535) CDC42SE1 level groups using the median expression level. A statistically significant difference was observed in PAM50 subtypes (p = 0.002), with CDC42SE1-high patients more frequently found in LumB (10.8% vs. 8.3% in CDC42SE1-low) and Basal subtypes (9.8% vs. 8%). Regarding menopausal status (p = 0.040), CDC42SE1-high expression was more common in postmenopausal patients (35.2%) compared to pre- (13.0%) and peri-menopausal (1.5%) patients. No statistical significances were detected in age, race, PR status, HER2 status, ER status, anatomic neoplasm subdivision, pathologic T stage, N stage, M stage, or overall pathologic stage (p > 0.05) (Table 1). Further analysis evaluated correlations between CDC42SE1 levels and nine key clinicopathological characteristics: PR status, ER status, HER2 status, PAM50 subtype, menopausal status, pathologic T stage, N stage, M stage, and overall pathologic stage (Fig. 2A and I). These findings indicated a moderate association between CDC42SE1 level and these features, suggesting potential links rather than a confirmed causal relationship.

CDC42SE1 as a prognostic biomarker in BRCA: correlations with survival across clinical subgroups

Analysis using the GEPIA database indicated that high CDC42SE1 level was correlated with significantly reduced OS in BRCA patients (p = 0.014) (Fig. 3A). The GEO datasets (GSE42568 and GSE10810) were used exclusively for validating CDC42SE1 expression levels, as these cohorts lacked comprehensive survival data compatible with the TCGA-BRCA analysis. Survival associations were therefore assessed using TCGA-BRCA as the primary dataset, with supplemental validation using the GEPIA platform. This finding was further validated using the Xiantao academic online platform ( (HR = 1.43, p = 0.033) (Fig. 3B). Subgroup analyses revealed that elevated CDC42SE1 expression level was markedly correlated with both overall survival (OS) and disease-specific survival (DSS) in patients with BRCA. Specifically, in the LumA subgroup (PAM50: LumA), high CDC42SE1 level correlated with poorer DFS (HR = 2.31, p = 0.028) (Fig. 3C). Moreover, elevated CDC42SE1 levels functioned as a key indicator of unfavorable OS in these specific subgroups: postmenopausal status (HR = 1.62, p = 0.029), right-sided anatomic subdivision (HR = 1.72, p = 0.031), HER2-positive status (HR = 2.67, p = 0.041), pathologic T2 stage (HR = 1.63, p = 0.047), M0 stage (HR = 1.64, p = 0.009), and stage II disease (HR = 1.89, p = 0.015) (Fig. 3D and I).

CDC42SE1 functions as an independent risk factor for OS in BRCA patients

Leveraging TCGA-BRCA data, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses examined the CDC42SE1 gene alongside critical clinicopathological factors.Univariate analysis identified age, ER status, menopause status, pathologic T stage, N stage, M stage, overall pathologic stage (all p < 0.05), and CDC42SE1 level (p = 0.033) as significant risk factors for OS (Fig. 4A). In the multivariate analysis forest plot for OS, Age (p < 0.001), ER status (p = 0.042), Pathologic N stage (p = 0.006), Pathologic M stage (p = 0.026), and CDC42SE1 level (p = 0.046) remained independent predictors (Fig. 4B). Although this p-value is at the threshold of statistical significance, the result remained consistent when using alternative stratification cutoffs (e.g., quartiles) and in sensitivity analyses, supporting its robustness as a prognostic factor. A nomogram was constructed to forecast 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS in patients with BRCA (Fig. 4C). The predictive accuracy was assessed using calibration curves (Fig. 4D). Time-dependent ROC curves were generated for 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival, with AUC values of 0.580, 0.551, and 0.585, respectively, indicating modest predictive ability (Supplementary Figure S2). Although the nomogram shows reasonable calibration, its clinical utility remains limited and requires further validation in larger, independent cohorts.

Functional analysis of CDC42SE1-associated genes

We carried out differential expression analysis for genes related to CDC42SE1 in BRCA. The filtering thresholds were defined based on |logFC| > 1, adjusted p-value < 0.05, and limited to protein-coding genes (Fig. 5A). The results revealed 32 upregulated and 192 downregulated genes. Subsequently, we further analyzed other genes co-expressed with CDC42SE1 in the TCGA-BRCA dataset. After applying the filters |correlation_spearman| > 0.3, p < 0.05, and retaining protein-coding genes, 5310 positively correlated genes and 8 genes with negative correlations were identified. Figure 5B shows the 20 most positively correlated genes. After Benjamini–Hochberg correction for multiple comparisons, the co-expressed genes of CDC42SE1 were significantly enriched in 1172 biological processes (BP), 282 cellular components (CC), 279 molecular functions (MF), and 75 KEGG pathways (adjusted p < 0.05, q value < 0.25). For clarity, only the top enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways with the lowest adjusted p-values and clear biological relevance were highlighted in figures and main text. To avoid overinterpretation of a large number of categories, for clarity, only the top enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways with the lowest adjusted p-values and clear biological relevance were highlighted in figures and main text (Fig. 5C and F). GO term annotation indicated these genes were predominantly associated with the following BPs: histone modification, mRNA processing, proteasome-mediated ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolic process, regulation of DNA metabolic process, and Golgi vesicle transport. The main CCs included: chromosomal region, nuclear speck, spindle, ubiquitin ligase complex, and nuclear envelope. The predominant MFs were: ATP hydrolysis activity, helicase activity, catalytic activity acting on RNA, transcription coregulator activity, and GTPase binding. Correlation between CDC42SE1 level and immune cells

We then evaluated the immune cell profiles in tumors exhibiting elevated versus reduced CDC42SE1 expression level. The analysis revealed that CDC42SE1 expression level was positively associated with central memory T cells (Tcm), T helper cells, gamma-delta T cells (Tgd), T helper type 2 (Th2), and T helper type 17 (Th17) cells, but negatively associated with plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDC), cytotoxic cells, and NK CD56bright cells. (Fig. 6A). Further analysis revealed that CDC42SE1 level showed a notable positive association with Tcm infiltration level (R = 0.335, p < 0.001) and T helper cells infiltration level (R = 0.237, p < 0.001), and markedly inversely associated with pDC infiltration level (R = -0.147, p < 0.001) and Cytotoxic cells infiltration level (R = -0.193, p < 0.001) (Fig. 6B–E). Correlations between CDC42SE1 and immune cell infiltration were moderate (e.g., Tcm R = 0.335, T helper R = 0.237), indicating potential associations but not strong causal effects. Then, we analyzed variations in immune cell populations between groups with elevated and reduced CDC42SE1 level. Significant differences (p < 0.05) were observed in 14 immune cells—such as CD8⁺ T cells, cytotoxic cells, NK cells, and pDCs—across the two groups (Fig. 6F). A heatmap was also constructed to visualize correlations between CDC42SE1 and immune infiltration levels in BRCA (Fig. 6G).

Discussion

CDC42SE1 has not been extensively investigated in the context of cancer pathogenesis. The relationship between CDC42SE1 and BRCA, along with its impact on prognosis, remains unclear. A notable increase in CDC42SE1 level was observed in BRCA patients, demonstrating a significant disparity with normal tissue. The AUC was 0.757, demonstrating moderate accuracy in discriminating tumor from non-tumor tissues. The AUC value of 0.757 indicates only moderate discriminative capacity, consistent with its role as a supportive rather than standalone diagnostic marker. These findings were validated across the TCGA-BRCA, GSE42568, and GSE10810 datasets [22, 23], and were further supported by CPTAC-based protein-level validation, which also showed increased CDC42SE1 expression in BRCA. Previous studies have reported low CDC42SE1 level in A431 cutaneous carcinoma cells, where its overexpression inhibited proliferation and migration [20]. In contrast, our findings indicate upregulated CDC42SE1 level in BRCA, associated with poorer prognosis. This apparent discrepancy may be attributable to tissue-specific roles of CDC42SE1 or context-dependent oncogenic functions. Further experimental validation in breast tissue is needed to clarify these mechanisms. Correlation analysis with clinicopathological characteristics demonstrated significant associations between CDC42SE1 level and nine clinical variables. Additionally, subgroup analyses were conducted. Collectively, these results support the potential utility of CDC42SE1 as a biomarker. Although subgroup analyses revealed consistent trends, they were not adjusted for multiple comparisons and should be interpreted as exploratory findings requiring validation in independent cohorts.

Based on KM analysis, patients with high CDC42SE1 expression exhibited significantly reduced survival relative to the low-expression cohort. Our analysis suggested that the CDC42SE1 gene may be an important biomarker for predicting prognosis in BRCA. Our data revealed that Age, ER status, Pathologic N stage, Pathologic M stage, and CDC42SE1 level significantly influenced OS and were identified as independent risk factors. Combining CDC42SE1 with clinicopathological variables may improve BRCA prognosis prediction.

We analyzed its correlation with immune infiltration levels in BRCA. Our analysis identified positive associations of CDC42SE1 level with Tcm, T helper, Tgd, and Th2 cells. Notably, elevated levels of Tcm cells have been previously linked to tumor advancement and poorer survival in cancers such as acute myeloid leukemia [25]. A predictive modeling study indicated that high levels of T helper cell infiltration (excluding Th2) are generally associated with favorable prognosis, although overall T helper cell infiltration may promote tumorigenesis by modulating inflammatory responses [26]. Highly infiltrated pro-tumoral γδ T cells promote angiogenesis, recruit myeloid-derived suppressor cells and neutrophils through IL-17 production, and contribute to an immunosuppressive microenvironment, which is positively correlated with progression in solid tumors including breast, colon, and ovarian cancers, and negatively linked to both OS and relapse-free survival [27]. Elevated Th2 levels have been linked to aggressive features in BRCA [28]. Our findings suggest that CDC42SE1 expression is associated with changes in immune cell infiltration that may be relevant to BRCA prognosis. However, these results are correlative in nature and do not establish a causal role for CDC42SE1 in tumor immune regulation. In addition, no mechanistic experiments were performed to clarify how CDC42SE1 may be linked to immune modulation, and immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-1, PD-L1, and CTLA4 were not analyzed in the present study. Therefore, CDC42SE1 should be regarded as a prognostic biomarker with potential immunomodulatory associations rather than a confirmed immunotherapeutic target. CDC42SE1 upregulation in BRCA correlated with multiple clinicopathological characteristics, suggesting its potential role in disease pathogenesis and severity. High CDC42SE1 level correlated with poorer survival, indicating its significance in BRCA progression. While the multivariate Cox analysis yielded a borderline p-value for CDC42SE1 (p = 0.046), its consistency across multiple statistical models and stratification methods supports its potential as a clinically relevant biomarker. Further validation in larger cohorts is warranted to confirm its prognostic strength.

The current study is not without limitations. First, it depended on bioinformatic analyses from public datasets without experimental validation (e.g., Western blot or IHC) to confirm CDC42SE1 level and function. Although CDC42SE1 showed statistical significance as a prognostic factor, the effect sizes were moderate. Therefore, conclusions should be interpreted cautiously. Lastly, the nomogram model requires further validation in larger, diverse cohorts to confirm its clinical applicability. In addition, time-dependent ROC analysis showed modest predictive performance of the nomogram, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUC values of 0.580, 0.551, and 0.585, respectively. These findings suggest that although the model may provide preliminary prognostic information, its predictive utility remains limited and should be further improved and validated in larger independent cohorts. Moreover, although supplementary protein-level validation using CPTAC supported the upregulation of CDC42SE1 in BRCA, the present study still lacked additional functional analyses, including associations with mutation status, proliferation markers, and pathway activity scoring. Therefore, further mechanistic and multi-dimensional validation in diverse patient cohorts is still warranted.

Conclusion

CDC42SE1 represents a potential diagnostic biomarker and an independent prognostic factor in BRCA, with expression patterns associated with alterations in the immune microenvironment. This study offers initial evidence indicating that CDC42SE1 may contribute to the pathogenesis of BRCA and lays a foundation for future research aimed at exploiting its utility as a therapeutic target or prognostic tool.

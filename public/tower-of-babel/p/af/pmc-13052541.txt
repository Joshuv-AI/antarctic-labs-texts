Adherence to ART medication refill pick-up in the regional hospital HIV clinics at the Sekhukhune district in Limpopo province, South Africa: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Mulisa Nepfumbada, Anam Nyembezi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSouth Africa has been reported to have a high burden of HIV and is currently running the largest anti-retroviral treatment (ART) programme globally which incorporates the test and treat policy. However, adherence to ART is particularly crucial for the optimal health outcomes of HIV patients. Evidence revealed that there are barriers to medication adherence in many parts of the country. Factors such as sociodemographic and patient’s characteristics have been found to be associated with ART adherence. The aim of this study was to measure the extent to which patients adhere to ART medication refill pick-up and to identify factors associated with adherence to ART medication refill pick-up among patients attending the regional hospital HIV clinics in Sekhukhune district, Limpopo province.MethodsA retrospective cohort study of patients who initiated ART between August 2020 and August 2021 was conducted. TIER.net electronic data were reviewed and extracted into Microsoft Excel 2010. The extracted data were analysed using EPI-Info V.7.2.5.0 software. Factors such as patients’ age, gender, pregnancy, co-morbidity, baseline CD4 count, ART regimen, tuberculosis (TB), WHO clinical HIV stage, treatment duration and number of missed pharmacy refill pick-up appointments were collected.ResultsAmong a total of 731 participants, only 30.5% of participants were found to be adherent to medication refill pick-up. Adherence to ART medication refill pick-up was the highest (56.0%) in facilities at Ephraim Mogale sub-district as compared with facilities in Elias Motsoaledi sub-district, with the OR of 0.359 and 95% CI of (0.243 to 0.532) with a significant p value (p=0.001). However, this study did not reveal a significant association between adherence to medication refill pick-up and other sociodemographic characteristics such as age, gender, pregnancy and TB.ConclusionsPatients collecting medication from sub-districts with increased HIV patient influxes are less likely to be adherent to ART medication refill pick-up as compared with participants attending care from sub-districts with reduced HIV patient volume.

Introduction

Globally, an estimate of about 37.7 million people were living with HIV in 2020.1 Eastern and Southern Africa carried the largest share of global HIV burden, which was estimated to be more than half of the global estimate in 2020.1 South Africa had the highest number of people affected by HIV, with the overall HIV prevalence of approximately 20.6% among the global estimate.1 Moreover, South Africa had the largest anti-retroviral treatment (ART) programmes globally, which were introduced in 2004 and incorporated the test and trea policy in September 2016.2 In 2004, the Limpopo Province rolled out the ART programme in the regional hospitals and gradually scaled it up to make it available in all government healthcare facilities.3

Since the introduction of ART, efforts have been made to ensure and to measure adherence to ART medication.4 Two simple methods have been used to measure adherence to ART medication; however, pharmacy adherence measures (PAMs) have been widely used due to their objective characteristics.4 Medication refill pick-up is a PAM which assesses pharmacy records to measure medication acquisition and distinct medication consumption.4 5 It is used to measure whether all or the majority of prescribed medication is picked up by the patients.4 5 In 2010, South Africa adopted the TIER.net system which is used to record HIV patients’ treatment and treatment status.6 This database generates reports for the patients’ treatments.6

Although ART does not cure HIV/AIDS, it is known to significantly maintain HIV suppression in plasma over the long term and drop the viral load to undetectable levels in the blood.7 To achieve ART maximum health benefits, patients’ adherence to ART regimens is one of the factors that improve clinical outcomes of HIV-infected patient.8 In addition, for the effective therapy and management of HIV, patients are required to commit to taking at least 95% of the treatment as prescribed.9

Non-adherence to ART may lead to multidrug resistance which may consequently allow HIV to multiply and progress to opportunistic infections.9 According to the WHO, non-adherence is the inability to follow treatment plans, take medication at prescribed times and frequencies and also follow restrictions regarding lifestyles, food and other medication.10 In addition, non-adherence may lead to a number of negative outcomes on healthcare systems and on individuals taking medication.9 10 These outcomes may include switching of patients treatment regimen from low to high due to treatment failure, viral mutation and the development of drug resistance.9 These higher regimens may be difficult to access due to availability in the country.2 10

The United Nations programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS) launched the 95-95-95 target in 2014, this target was aimed at diagnosing 95% of all HIV positive individuals, providing ART for 95% of diagnosed individuals and achieving viral suppression for 95% of those treated by 2030.11 Adherence to ART medication refill pick-up was found to increase chances of viral load suppression and improve the quality of life of patients.12 However, adherence to medication refill pick-up is widely affected by different factors such as age, gender, co-morbidity, duration on treatment and viral load suppression, which have been found to have been associated with ART medication refill pick-up adherence.13 14 In addition, the prevalence of ART medication adherence seems to be different across the regions. Studies conducted in Nigeria and South Africa reported the prevalence of ART medication refill pick-up of 21.1% and 56% respectively.15 16 In Southern African countries, high rates (ranging between 2.7%–55.4%) of loss to follow-up on medication refill pick up among those initiated on ART have been reported.17 18

The aim of this study was to measure the extent to which patients adhere to ART medication refill pick-up and to identify factors associated with adherence to ART medication refill pick-up among patients attending the regional hospital HIV clinics using electronic records, in the Sekhukhune district, Limpopo province, South Africa.

Methods

study site

The study was conducted at the regional hospital ARV clinics in the rural area of Sekhukhune district of the Limpopo Province in South Africa. The hospital is located under Elias Motsoaledi sub-district, which is categorised as one of many previously disadvantaged district municipalities.19 This hospital is one of the two regional hospitals in the Sekhukhune district and has feeder clinics which offer comprehensive HIV care, including ART.20 The hospital supports two sub-districts, namely Elias Motsoaledi sub-district, which covers four clinics and Ephraim Mogale, which covers six clinics.20 The selected study setting was based in a rural area where challenges such as lack of proper infrastructure, transportation and water scarcity still exist.19 The study compared two districts; therefore, the Sub-district burden was operationally defined based on the total patients volume during this study period. The sub-district with high patients volume was categorised as a high-burden sub-district relative to the other sub-district with lower patients volume.

Study design

In this quantitative retrospective cohort study, electronic databases were reviewed to measure the extent to which patients adhere to ART medication refill pick-up and determine factors associated with adherence to ART medication refill pick-up. The incidence of non-adherence and loss to follow-up, as well as the rate at which they were occurring, was uncovered. As illustrated in figure 1, the cohort in this study consisted of the TIER.net electronic database of adults aged 18 to 59 years with HIV who were initiated on ART between August 2020 and August 2021 at the regional hospital HIV clinics. Adherence was measured for twelve months after ART initiation.

Population and sampling

The study population included all eligible HIV positive adults aged 18 to 59 years who initiated HIV treatment between August 2020 and August 2021 in the regional hospital HIV clinics. Eight Clinics were selected and consisted of four clinics from Elias Motsoaledi sub-district and four clinics from Ephraim Mogale sub-district. Four clinics from Elias Motsoaledi sub-district had 532 eligible patients while four clinics from Ephraim Mogale sub-district had 192 eligible participants. Selection of the clinics was based on proximity to the supporting hospital, and only four clinics from each sub-district were selected. All eligible patients in the selected facilities were part of the study.

Inclusion: all living ART patients aged 18 to 59 years who initiated their treatment at the regional hospital HIV clinics between August 2020 and August 2021 were included in this study.

Exclusion: patients were excluded if they initiated ARV treatment but requested a transfer to another facility or died between August 2020 and August 2021. All ART patients aged 18 to 59 years who initiated their ART medication at the regional hospital HIV clinics prior to August 2020 and after August 2021 were excluded from this study.

Data collection procedure

The study used routine quantitative data collection from the patient’s electronic records database called TIER.net. TIER.net is an electronic data base for ART monitoring, evaluation and documentation of the number of people alive and on treatment.6 In this programme, patients’ demographics, laboratory test results and treatment are documented by the caregiver during patients’ visits. Data was extracted for all eligible patients and saved as Excel documents. As part of ensuring confidentiality, patients’ identifiers such as file numbers and patient’ names were removed and replaced with numbers in the Microsoft Excel tool. The study collected sociodemographic data and clinical characteristics from the TIER.net database.

The study collected the following variables: sociodemographic data (age, gender) and clinical characteristics (tuberculosis (TB) status at start of treatment, viral load, CD4 count, ART start date, last pharmacy ART refill, ART regimen, date of appointment kept, WHO clinical HIV stage, date of lost to follow-up).

Definition of outcomes and variables

Non-adherence to ART medication refill pick-up was defined as the delay in medication refill pick-up by seven or more days of the appointment day. Adherence to ART medication refill pick-up was defined as medication refill picked up within 7 days of their refill appointment date. Furthermore, patients who collected their medication refill seven or more days before the appointment date were also considered as non-adherent. The study was observed for 12 months after the initial treatment; therefore, overall adherence to medication refill pick-up was defined as adherence to medication refill pick-up for at least nine or more months. Lost to follow-up was defined as patients who lost contact with the HIV clinic for three consecutive months, not known to have died and did not return for medication refill pick-up.

Data validation

To ensure data accuracy, quality and completeness, data sets were double-extracted to assess entry discrepancies. Discrepancies were resolved by referring to the original system data. The final data extracted were subjected to a logical consistency check to identify and correct any inconsistent values before statistical analysis.

Data analysis

Quantitative secondary data of eligible patients were extracted from the patients’ electronic records, refined for quality and completeness and saved in an excel format. All data were cleaned and all missing data were recorded as “not recorded”. The primary outcome variable (the dependent variable) for the study was the adherence to ART medication refill pick-up. While the independent variables included the geographic location (Elias Motsoaledi and Ephraim Mogale sub-district), demographic characteristics (age and gender) and the clinical factors (pregnancy, CD4 count, viral load, TB and ART regimen).

Microsoft Excel was used for data entry of this study and Epi Info V.7.2.5.0 software was used to compute the descriptive and inferential statistics. Univariate analysis of all variables was conducted to describe the distribution and to find the patterns that exist within the variable while descriptive characteristics were used to characterise the participant characteristics including: age, gender, length on ART and co-morbidity. Descriptive statistics were presented as means, SD and median and expressed as percentages. For the descriptive analysis, data were compared between two sub-districts. Bivariate analysis was conducted to calculate the OR for the variable collected, the 95% CI and the p value to determine the statistical significance (the significant results reported at p values of <0.05 and <0.001).

The study participants were 731 in total, 539 (74%) were from Elias Motsoaledi sub-district, while 192 (26%) participants were from Ephraim Mogale sub-district. The age distribution in this study indicated the average (mean) age of 34 years with the most frequent age of 29 years. The minimum age was 18 years, and the maximum age was 59 years. The SD of the age was 9.66. The majority of the participants in this study were female and youth aged between 18 and 34 in both sub-districts. The youth in Elias Motsoaledi and Ephraim Mogale constituted 53.8% and 55.7% of participants, respectively. In both Elias Motsoaledi and Ephraim Mogale sub-districts, female participants made up 72.0% and 65.6% of the total participants, respectively.

Discussions

Results of the current study reveal sub-district as the only sociodemographic characteristic significantly associated with adherence to ART medication refill pick-up. Participants who collected medication from Ephraim Mogale sub-district were more likely to be adherent to ART medication refill pick-up. According to the 2011 Census, Elias Motsoaledi generally has a larger population (66.9%) as compared with Ephraim Mogale sub-district (33.1%).21 Elias Motsoaledi sub-district had a significantly higher number of patients (74%) as compared with Ephraim Mogale sub-district (26%). Ephraim Mogale sub-district, having a smaller population, has therefore resulted in facilities serving a smaller population as compared with Elias Motsoaledi.

The sociodemographic factors considered in the findings, such as age and gender, did not show a significant association and caries across literatures. Those who were adherent to medication refill pick-up were 1.02 times more likely to be adults as per the OR in this study. Though the influence was not statistically significant, the finding was consistent with a study conducted in Senegal whereby younger patients were more likely to not be adherent to ART medication refill pick-up.22 Moreover, the majority of the reviewed studies (77.1% and 71.4%) did not indicate an association with adherence relating to sex and age, respectively; only 14.3% of the studies showed that age per year increasing was positively associated with optimal adherence.14

Apart from age, gender was also found to not be significantly associated with adherence to medication refill pick-up. Adherent male participants in this study were more as compared with adherent female participants. HIV-infected females in developing countries demonstrated a better health-seeking behaviour as compared with males. This was not consistent with this study, as the results indicated that more females demonstrated a better health-seeking behaviour than males based on total male and female.2 Furthermore, in the rural setting of Zambia, adherence was associated with being female.23

Evidence suggests that pregnant women who receive one or more prescriptions turn out to be non-adherent. This study found adherence of 25.2% among pregnant women. Despite the benefits of ART medication in pregnancy, this study showed a high rate of non-adherence among pregnant women, which is not consistent with the Nigerian finding; Nigerian pregnant women showed a better adherence in pregnancy.24 In Southeastern Nigeria, only 37.1% where non-adherence and a much lower rate of non-adherence (19.4%) has been reported.24

Co-morbidity characteristic findings in this study were TB. In South Africa, HIV and TB co-infection rate is high.2 Nonetheless, only 2.6% of the HIV participants had TB during this study period. Furthermore, this study indicated no significant association between adherence and TB. This was consistent with other South African studies which found no significant changes in adherence on HIV and TB co-infected patients.2 Limpopo province is one of the most affected by TB, with approximately 55% TB patients also being HIV positive.25 According to the global TB report 2020, people living with HIV are likely to develop TB disease; therefore, early access to TB screening is a critical intervention to ensure timely TB treatment.26 In this study results, overall, 66.2% participants did not have TB status recorded on Tier.Net.

The primary goal of ART initiation and monitoring is viral load suppression.27 In this study, only 38.2% (22.6% suppressed and 15.9% undetectable) of participants had suppressed viral load. This is less than the UNAIDS target of 95% of patient on ART being virally suppressed by 2030.11 The unrecorded data on patients’ viral load and CD4 count constituted about 49.9% and 47.9% respectively. This is higher than a study in South Africa in which 36% of patient outcomes such as CD4 count, viral load and ART initiated reason were not recorded.6 This study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic and therefore missing data in this study might have been due to the COVID-19 pandemic as more focus was on the pandemic rather than the HIV programme.

The total study participants on first-line regimen were 99.6% and the total participants on second-line regimen were 0.4%. First-line ART regimen is the most recommended, simplified, less toxic and the most convenient regimen; hence, most of the participants were initiated on first-line regimen.28 Logistic regression analysis on ART regimen indicated no significant association with medication refill pick-up. Being in the first line regimen did not increase the chances of adherence in this study. It has been determined that the complexity and dosing of more than one dose per day may hinder adherence.2 Nonetheless, this study did not show a significant association.

An average of 82.9% of the participants were able to remain in care until the end of the study. The percentage of patients who retained care is higher than other findings in South Africa.17 In Ekurhuleni district, South Africa, 60% of the participants retained in care for the period of more than 12 month while 28% per cent were lost to follow-up.17 In this study, 17.1% of participants were lost to follow-up. This is inconsistent with a systematic review by Wilkinson et al, which indicated that the percentage lost to follow-up ranges from 2.7% to 55.4% in low and middle-income countries.29 This study has indicated a high number of non-adherence to medication refill pick-up.

Overall, the study results show that there is a low prevalence of medication refill pick-up. The results indicated that only 30.5% of the overall participants were able to collect their ART medication refill within 7 days of their appointment date for more than 90% of their time during the study period. In the US, researchers found the prevalence of 32.6% which is consistent with the prevalence in this study.15 The prevalence in this study is higher than the prevalence of 21.1% found in a study done in Nigeria.30 To further understand the pattern of ART adherence in this study, the results indicated that 60.3% of the participants were adherent in the first appointment. This adherence percentage increased to 72.6% in the second appointment and subsequently decreased with time until 44.9% in the last appointment over the ten-month period. Nonetheless, in another region of South Africa, the likelihood of achieving optimal adherence to ART improved with time.2

Research limitations

The study only assessed data from the electronic records. Some of the variables were incomplete in some of the patient’s electronic records. TIER.net database was found to have unrecorded information such as TB status and viral loads. Although random checks were done to ensure data validity, errors could not be completely ruled out because electronic data were not verified against original patient files. The primary limitation of the study was the reliance on unadjusted OR. Without the adjusted OR, the confounding variables could not be completely ruled out.

Conclusion

The bivariate findings have suggested an association between adherence to medication refill pick-up and facility patients’ volume. The study identified geographical disparity in ART adherence within the district. Patients in high volume sub-districts appeared less likely to be adherent as compared with patients in low volume facilities. The findings have suggested that adherence to medication refill pick-up is not primarily influenced by demographic factors, clinical characteristics or co-morbidity, but it is heavily influenced by the facility in which patients receive care. And therefore, the drivers of adherence in this region have pointed toward facility level factors such as service delivery, resource allocation and accessibility.

To improve on ART medication refill adherence, more emphasis should also be directed to health system factors such as high-volume patients. Facilities with a high volume of patients should be prioritised to improve adherence. Interventions such as moving stable patients to community-based pick-up points and resource allocation (to align staff ratios) should be prioritised in high volume patients’ facilities. Furthermore, the recording of full patient clinical test results after any consultation on the electronic system should be strengthened to avoid discrepancies. A follow-up study comparing treatment status on TIER.net versus outcomes on patient file to assess discrepancies and bias should be conducted.

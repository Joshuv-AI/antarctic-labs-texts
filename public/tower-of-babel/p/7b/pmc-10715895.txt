Chinese herbal medicines for prostate cancer therapy: From experimental research to clinical practice
Authors: Fanming Kong, Chaoran Wang, Jing Zhang, Xiaoqun Wang, Binxu Sun, Xian Xiao, Haojian Zhang, Yanqi Song, Yingjie Jia
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractProstate cancer remains the second most common malignancy in men worldwide, is a global health issue, and poses a huge health burden. Precision medicine provides more treatment options for prostate cancer patients, but its popularity, drug resistance, and adverse reactions still need to be focused on. Chinese herbal medicines (CHMs) have been widely accepted as an alternative therapy for cancer, with the advantages of multiple targets, multiple pathways, and low toxicity. We searched the experimental research and clinical practice of CHMs for prostate cancer treatment published in PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science in the last five years. We found five CHM formulas and six single CHM extracts as well as 12 CHM-derived compounds, which showed induction of apoptosis, autophagy, and cell cycle arrest, suppression of angiogenesis, proliferation, and migration of prostate cancer cells, reversal of drug resistance, and enhancement of anti-tumor immunity. The mechanisms of action include the PI3K/Akt/mTOR, AR, EGFR and Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathways, which are commonly implicated in the development of prostate cancer. We also summarized the advantages of CHMs in patients with hormone-sensitive and castration-resistant prostate cancer and provided ideas for their further experimental design and application.Keywords: Chinese herbal medicines, clinical practice, experimental research, prostate cancer, review

Introduction

Prostate cancer remains the second most common malignancy in men worldwide (Sung et al., 2021). Prostate cancer has caused a huge health burden on the Chinese population, with an age-standardized incidence rate of 17.3/100 000 in 2019, an increase of 95.2% compared to 1990 (Wang et al., 2022). The effect of age on morbidity and mortality in the elderly population showed a significant increasing trend (Liu, Yu, Bi, & Zhang, 2019). The majority of newly diagnosed cases in China are at advanced stages, and only 30% are localized (He et al., 2022). Totally, 10%–20% of metastatic patients develop castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) within five years (Kirby, Hirst, & Crawford, 2011), increasing the mortality burden in this population. With the development of precision therapy, a new generation of androgen receptor (AR) signaling inhibitors, bone-targeting agents, and poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors have made advances in the treatment of CRPC. There are still many limitations. Side effects associated with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) increase the risk of non-neoplastic mortality (Nguyen, Lairson, Swartz, & Du, 2018). ARV-mediated resistance to abiraterone and enzalutamide remains a problem (Antonarakis et al., 2014). Uneven economic levels and medical conditions in developing countries limit the use of precision therapy (Yi et al., 2020). More treatment options are urgently needed to reduce the burden of disease and improve the quality of life of people, especially older men.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) treatment of malignant tumors has a history of about 2000 years (Xiang et al., 2019) and is now integrated into the current healthcare system in China. An increasing number of Chinese medicine formulas, single extracts, and natural compounds have been found to inhibit the development of prostate cancer through various pathways (Wang et al., 2018). While inhibiting the growth, invasion, and metastasis of prostate cancer, Chinese herbal medicines (CHMs) reverse drug resistance and play a synergistic effect with drugs. CHMs can achieve the characteristics of multi-target therapy, and participate in the whole process of prostate cancer treatment. By retrieving relevant researches published in PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science in the past five years, we summarized the current experimental research progress and clinical practice of CHMs against prostate cancer, and provided ideas for its further experimental design and application. TCM has followed the individualized precision therapy concept for more than 2000 years (Liu, Wang, Zhang, Fan, & Lin, 2015). There is no name for prostate cancer in the ancient literature of TCM. It falls into the categories of “Longbi” or “Linzheng” based on its symptoms. Both internal (emotional disorders, poor lifestyle choices, etc.) and external (chemical and physical risks) elements can contribute to prostate cancer. These pathogenic elements affect organ's function and cause an accumulation of static blood, dampness-heat, and toxin. According to the different symptoms of the patients, it is divided into the pattern/syndrome of “internal obstruction of dampness-heat”, “binding of stasis and toxin”, “liver and kidney deficiency”. The clinical treatment principle is based on “reinforcing the healthy qi” combined with the methods of “resolving stasis, dispelling dampness, and detoxifying”. In classical theory, single TCMs which are used for prostate cancer treatment usually fall into four categories (Qi et al., 2015): Reinforcing healthy qi (e.g., Astragali Radix, Ganoderma, Psoraleae Fructus); Activating blood and resolving stasis (e.g., Curcumae Longae Rhizoma, Vaccariae Semen); Expelling dampness (e.g., Sophorae Flavescentis Radix, Pogostemonis Herba); Clearing heat and detoxifying (e.g., Paridis Rhizoma, Scutellariae Barbatae Herba, Tripterygium wilfordii Hook. f.). Clinical application should follow the principles of “syndrome differentiation and treatment” in the selection of core drugs.

Experimental research

CHM formulas

Many Chinese herbal formulas have anti-tumor activity, while individual extracts and compounds may not show biological activity, suggesting that CHMs play a synergistic role and contain the characteristics of syndrome differentiation (Hu et al., 2016). In recent years, some progress has been made in the application of modern medical methods to explore the targets of action of CHM formulas. The intervention of CHM-derived compounds in prostate cancer has been explored extensively but there are fewer studies on Chinese herbal formulas which can better reflect the unique advantages of TCM. We reviewed five CHM formulas that have been used in clinical practice for decades and provided preliminary experimental evidence and explanation of their anti-tumor mechanisms in prostate cancer. Pharmacological effects of CHM formulas in prostate cancer.

Qianlie Xiaozheng Decoction (QLXZ, water extract from a CHM compound) has been used in advanced prostate cancer for more than 10 years. In clinical practice, QLXZ could slow prostate specific antigen (PSA) progression, and enhance quality of life in patients with CRPC (Pang et al., 2015). Xu et al. (2018) demonstrated the mechanistic pathways of QLXZ in vitro and in xenografts. QLXZ inhibited the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway, induced excessive autophagy and accelerated cell death. QLXZ not only inhibited mTORC1 activity but also its upstream regulator, Akt, preventing compensatory feedback from its downstream regulator. These results provided the experimental basis for the further clinical application of QLXZ in androgen independent prostate cancer.

Qiling Decoction (QL, water extract from a CHM compound) was first composed by the famous Chinese medicine expert Zhiheng Zhou, with potent benefits against advanced prostate cancer in clinical trials. QL downregulated Bcl-2 and upregulated Caspase-3 and Bax2 to suppress prostate cancer progression and promoted apoptosis by promoting miR-143 upregulation, which could be a possible mechanism in prostate cancer patients (Cao et al., 2021). In castration-resistance prostate cancer, QL significantly decreased viability and increased apoptosis of docetaxel-resistant (DR) CRPC cells. The treatment inhibited docetaxel resistance and glycolysis of CRPC possibly via SNHG10/miR-12715p/ TRIM66 pathway (Cao et al., 2021). Feng et al (2021) highlighted the anti-tumor properties of QL in CRPC and unraveled the critical contribution of TRIM66/HP1γ/AR in this scenario.

Fuzheng Yiliu Decoction (FZYL, water extract from a compound CHM), consisting of Rehmanniae Radix, Anemarrhenae Rhizoma, Epimedii Folium, Morindae Officinalis Radix, Astragali Radix, Atractylodis Macrocephalae Rhizoma, Poria, Glycyrrhizae Radix et Rhizoma, Phellodendri Chinensis Cortex, Panacis Quinquefolii Radix, Testudinis Carapax et Plastrum, and Hedyotis diffusa. It has been used to treat hepatocellular cancer (Cao et al., 2011), breast cancer (Chen et al., 2021), and prostate cancer. The combination of FZYL and Docetaxel significantly enhanced apoptosis compared to Docetaxel alone. Combination therapy regulated the expression of 10 miRNAs which modify the PI3K-Akt pathway in CRPC (Fu et al., 2019).

CFF-1 (alcohol extract from a compound CHM) is a classic TCM-derived formula for several years. CFF-1 induced cell autophagy and apoptosis by targeting epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and acting competitively on EGFR with epidermal growth factor (EGF), inhibiting EGFR/PI3K/AKT and EGFR/PI3K/ERK signal pathways (Wu et al., 2018). However, PD-1 and PD-L1 inhibitors have not yet been employed in clinical therapy due to poor immunogenicity caused by low PD-L1 expression in prostate cancer (Rathi, Mcfarland, Nussenzveig, Agarwal, & Swami, 2021). CFF-1 inhibited EGFR/JAK1/STAT3 signaling to block PD-1/PD-L1 expression for enhancing T cell mediated antitumor immunity response. The treatment may be a promising strategy to resist immunosuppression (Zhang et al., 2022).

The clinical application of the First Affiliated Hospital of Hunan University of Traditional Chinese Medicine over the past 20 years has shown that Yishen Tonglong Decoction (YTD) can significantly relieve the clinical symptoms of prostate cancer patients such as difficulty in urination, loss of appetite and weakness. The mechanism of action may be related to the inhibition of the EMT process and the expression of the Ras/ERK signaling pathway in prostate cancer cells (Liu et al., 2021). Whether the Ras/ERK signalling pathway acts directly or indirectly still requires further experiments to be designed.

CHM formulas have achieved the certain curative effects in the clinical treatment of prostate cancer, following the principle of “Sovereign, minister, assistant and guide”, it plays the role of comprehensive treatment and has the unique advantages of the whole body and complementary effect. The above formulas follow the overall therapeutic principles of TCM through the combination of CHMs, acting on multiple targets to restore the balance of the body (So et al., 2019). However, due to the complexity of the ingredients, there are relatively few experimental studies on the formulas, and it is difficult to explain the interaction mechanism of their active ingredients. Chinese herbal formulas generate new ingredients during the formation of the prescription, and the methods and means of Western medicine should be used as appropriate to ensure thorough analysis of the active ingredients of the formulas. Serum pharmacochemistry, which represents the interaction between the body and drugs, has become an efficient method for exploring the effectiveness material base of TCMs (Ma, Xue, Wang, Wang, & Xue, 2017). The Traditional Chinese Medicine Integrated Database facilitates the investigation of the underlying biological processes that develop in response to the diverse pharmacological actions of TCM (Huang et al., 2018). In the future, with the help of research methods such as network pharmacology (Wang, Wang, Zhang, Hu, & Li, 2022), through the dynamic network and quantitative network (Luo, Wang, Sawadogo, Tan, & Yuan, 2020), a multi-dimensional research strategy can be provided for complex CHM formulas.

Single CHM extracts

With the innovation and development of TCM research technologies and methods, the pharmacological research of single CHM extracts has made remarkable progress. As a component of TCM formulas, the extracts of single CHMs have various anti-prostate cancer activities (Table 2), which provide more ideas for the further development of natural compounds. More and more research is being conducted to show the mechanism how single CHM extracts function on human prostate cancer.

The rhizoma of Paris polyphylla Smith var. yunnanensis (Franch.) Hand. -Mazz. is called “Chonglou” in Chinese, its extracts showed significant cytotoxic effects on various cancer cells (Su et al., 2022). Zhang et al. (2018) found that the IC50 of P. polyphylla ethanol extract (PPEE) on PC-3 cells was 3.98 µg/mL and the DU145 cells was 8 µg/mL. PPEE caused PC-3 cell cycle arrest in G0/G1, G2/M phases and induced apoptosis.

Ganoderma (Lingzhi in Chinese) is the dired fruits of Ganoderma lucidum (Leyyss. ex Fr.) Karst. or Ganoderma sinense Zhao, Xu et Zhang. Ganoderma and its extracts have anticancer bioactivities. The antiproliferative and apoptosis-inducing activities of Ganoderma and its extracts are closely related to cell cycle arrest, telomerase inhibition, autophagy and oxidative stress-related pathways (Sun and Sun, 2019). In metastatic prostate cancer cells, Ganoderma ethanol extract blocked the PI3K/Akt and MAPK/ERK signaling pathways, which was associated with cells growth, survival and apoptosis (Huang et al., 2019).

Trametes robiniophila Murr., a type of medicinal fungus, has been called “Huaier” in TCM for 1600 years. Huaier extracts are widely used in a range of tumors (Liu et al., 2021, Chen et al., 2021, Wang et al., 2017), and Huaier granule is approved by Chinese State Food and Drug Administration. Huaier extract targeted AR-FL and AR-V7 signaling pathways in both hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (HSPC) and castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) cells, subsequently resulting antiproliferative effects (Liu et al., 2021). Furthermore, extracts re-sensitized enzalutamide-resistant prostate cancer cells, downregulated Lamin B1 and inducted autophagy, thus suppressed proliferation and metastasis of PC-3 cells (Yang et al., 2018).

Scutellariae Barbatae Herba (Banzhilian in Chinese) is derived from Labiate family. In vitro and in vivo, Scutellariae Barbatae Herba induced cell apoptosis and cell cycle G2/M phase arrest by inactivating PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. It has the potential to exhibit anti-angiogenic properties (Sheng et al., 2022). As the core group of CHM for cancer patients, Hedyotis diffusa Willd. and Scutellariae Barbatae Herba reduced DNA content and accumulated prostate cancer cells at the G2/M phase (Hnit et al., 2020).

The ethanol extraction of Psoraleae Fructus (Buguzhi in Chinese) induced the apoptosis, autophagy, and altered a number of genes associated DNA damage in PC-3 cells. Psoraleae Fructus simultaneously decreased cell cycle-associated genes, including SKP2, CDCA3, CDCA2, GTSE1, CDCA7, CDCA7L, and CDC7, to regulate cell cycle (Lin et al., 2018).

Single CHMs are the main components of TCM, which can be used as single herbs or as part of the formulations. According to TCM theory, single CHMs also play a role in health-strengthening and pathogen-eliminating (Wang et al., 2020). On the basis of the treatment principle, CHMs are added or subtracted according to the patient's specific symptoms. The “effective body” in single CHMs can also be analyzed with the “assistant and guide” theory of TCM (Tang et al., 2018). Over the past few decades, CHMs have been recognized worldwide as a rich source of new drug discovery. Academician Boli Zhang suggested that “the development of new varieties of CHM should pay attention to component drugs, drug monomers with high technological content” (Zhang & Zhang, 2015). The mechanism research of single CHMs in prostate cancer is conducive to the development of more preparations including injections, tablets, capsules, and so on.

CHM-derived compounds

In 2020, Chinese scholars reported a total of 271 valuable natural compounds, and the pharmacological studies of these components showed great potential in the treatment of malignant tumors (Gu et al., 2022). Natural compounds including curcumin, matrine, quercetin, triptolide etc. inhibit prostate cancer development through various pathways in vivo and in vitro.

Curcumin

Curcumin, an extract from the turmeric rhizome, possesses biological activities which include anti-proliferative, anti-inflammatory, and anti-oxidative properties. Curcumin shows promise in several studies to overcome tumor resistance and re-sensitize cancer to chemotherapeutics (Shaikh et al., 2021). Curcumin could inhibit the proliferation and metastasis, and promote the apoptosis of PC‑3 and DU145 cells by inducing the expression of miR‑30a‑5p and inhibiting PCLAF (Pan et al., 2021). Zhao et al. (2018) demonstrated that curcumin inhibited JNK pathway in LNCaP cells, influenced transcription of genes by repressing H3K4me3. Curcumin induced ROS production, DNA damage and consequent mitochondrial dysfunction, and promoted apoptosis and necroptosis of PC-3 cells (Lee et al., 2021). Curcumin may induce chronic endoplasmic reticulum stress-mediated cell death, apoptosis, and activate the cell cycle arrest in PC-3, serving as a promising agent. In human prostate cancer stem cells, curcumin inhibited cells proliferation and invasion by modulating miR-770-5p and miR-1247 expression levels in the DLK1-DIO3 imprinted gene clusters (Zhang et al., 2018). Curcumin induced high miR-145 expression and caused ceRNA effect of lncRNA-ROR, suppressed the proliferation and in vitro invasion of human prostate cancer stem cells (Liu et al., 2017). Application of combined regimen may enhance the anti-tumor potential, curcumin in combination with carnosic acid (Ossikbayeva et al., 2021), arsenic trioxide (Mirzaei, Jahanshahi, Khatami, Reis, & Aghamir, 2022), 5-alpha reductase inhibitor (Nakayama et al., 2021), light irradiation (Rutz et al., 2021), deserve further exploration as an anti-tumor modality.

Poor water solubility, poor gastrointestinal absorption, and rapid metabolism affect its anti-tumor activity. Nanoemulsion is an effective drug delivery system to increase the solubilization of the encapsulated drug. Curcumin-based nanoemulsions could prolong cytotoxicity and cell cycle arrest in PC-3 cells (Guan et al., 2017). Al-Rabia, et al. (2022) prepared scorpion venom (SV) conjugated curcumin phytosomes which enhanced apoptosis and necrosis, reduced mitochondrial membrane permeability, increased Bax, caspase, and p53 levels, and reduced Bcl-2, NF-kB, TNF-a levels. A systematic review showed positive effects of nanocarrier-based drug delivery systems for enhancement of oral absorption of curcumin (Pan-On et al., 2022). Nanotechnology combined with curcumin makes it a novel treatment for prostate cancer.

Matrine

Matrine is an alkaloid extracted from the Chinese herb medicine Sophorae Flavescentis Radix (Kushen in Chinese). Matrine and its derivatives play a role in inhibiting proliferation and inducing apoptosis of various cancer cell lines (Chen et al., 2022, Rashid, Xu, Muhammad, Wang, & Jiang, 2019). Matrine promoted the expression of GADD45B, synergistically inhibited proliferation and promoted apoptosis of prostate cancer cells (Huang et al., 2018). Li et al. (2018) showed that the highest differential expression of FOXO1A, FOXO3A, FOXO4, FOXO6, and PI3K in castration-resistant prostate cancer cells. Matrine may inhibit cell proliferation, migration, invasion through FOXO and PI3K-AKT signaling pathways. Matrine inhibited the migration and invasion of CRPC by reducing the expression levels of MMP-2 and MMP-9 through inactivation of NF-κB pathway (Huang et al., 2017). In vivo and in vitro, matrine reversed EMT, induced cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis by activating the UPR/ER stress signaling pathway (Chang et al., 2018). Matrine combined with docetaxel could inhibit proliferation, migration and invasion of both DU145 and PC-3 cells in a dose- and time-dependent manner. Matrine have anti-inflammation effects on the DU145 and PC-3 cells (Li et al., 2019).

Quercetin

Quercetin is a polyphenolic flavonoid widely distributed in CHMs. Quercetin inhibited the growth of a panel of various cancer cell lines, had potential for therapeutic application in malignant tumor (Hashemzaei et al., 2017). Quercetin suppressed epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) process, downregulated MALA T1 expression in a dose- and time-dependent manner, promoted apoptosis and deactivated PI3K/Akt signaling pathway in human prostate cancer cell line PC-3 (Lu et al., 2020). Quercetin modulated ROS production and interfered with MAPK, Akt, and NF-κB signaling pathways, targeting prostate cancer cell with varied AR status (Ward et al., 2018). Furthermore, metformin combined with quercetin inhibited the VEGF/Akt/PI3K pathway, synergistically inhibiting the growth, migration and invasion of PC-3 and LNCaP cells (Sun et al., 2018). In vivo and in vitro, combined treatment with quercetin and paclitaxel enhanced the cancer cell-killing effects, with almost no side effects. The combination therapy inhibited cell proliferation and migration, arrested the cell cycle at the G2/M phase, induced endoplasmic reticulum stress to occur, and increased ROS generation (Zhang et al., 2020).

In overcoming drug resistance, quercetin had docetaxel-resistance reversal effect both in vitro and in vivo via androgen receptor and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways (Lu et al., 2020). Shu et al. (2018) established a PC-3 cell line (PC3/R) with acquired doxorubicin-resistance. In PC3/R cells, quercetin inhibited c-met expression and the PI3K/AKT pathway to reverse prostate cancer cell doxorubicin resistance. Quercetin downregulated hnRNPA1 and AR-V7 expression, antagonized androgen receptor signaling, and overcame enzalutamide resistance in mouse xenografts (Tummala, Lou, Gao, & Nadiminty, 2017).

This natural product is poorly absorbed and rapidly metabolized. Encapsulating quercetin into biodegradable nanoparticles may delay or prevent its metabolism, allowing long-term free levels in blood and other tissues (Hussain et al., 2021). Quercetin nanoparticles upregulate Su (Fu) mRNA expressions and downregulate gli mRNA expressions in the LNCaP cells via the Hedgehog signaling pathway (Mousavi et al., 2021).

Triptolide

Triptolide, active ingredient of CHM T. wilfordii (Leigongteng in Chinese). The anti-prostate cancer effect of triptolide may be related to its modulation of the androgen receptor (AR). AR plays an important role in the occurrence and development of prostate cancer in both hormone-sensitive and castration-resistant stages. Triptolide inhibited phosphorylation of AR and its CRPC-associated variant AR-V7 at Ser515 through XPB/CDK7, enhanced the anti-prostate cancer activities of enzalutamide on CRPC, even at a low dose (Han et al., 2017). Triptolide can activate calpain, decrease the stability of AR protein in LNCaP cells, and subsequently lead to the degradation the breakdown of the AR protein (Li et al., 2018).

Astragaloside

Astragaloside, an active ingredient extracted from CHM Astragali Radix (Huangqi in Chinese), has pharmacological effects on enhancement of immunity, attenuation of the migration and invasion and improvement of chemosensitivity (Zhang et al., 2020). Astragaloside suppressed AKT/NF-кB signaling, enhanced carboplatin sensitivity in prostate cancer cell lines LNCap and PC-3 by suppressing carboplatin-induced EMT (He et al., 2021). Expression of the endoplasmic reticulum stress genes BiP, CHOP and Caspase-12 mRNA was increased by astragaloside administration. Astragaloside demonstrated significant therapeutic effects on the proliferation of DU-145 cell (Tan et al., 2018).

Polyphyllin I

Polyphyllin I (PPI) is a major component extracted from P. polyphylla, significantly inhibits growth, invasion in various cancer cells (Wu et al., 2020, Han et al., 2020, You et al., 2021). PPI induced cell cycle arrest in G0/G1 phase in PC3 and DU145 cells by upregulating the expression of P21 and IL6 (Zhang et al., 2019). CIP2A/PP2A/ERK signaling plays an important role in prostate cancer growth, invasion, and EMT. PPI inhibited the signaling axis and exerted anti-tumor effects (Liu et al., 2018). Overexpression of HOTAIR enhanced cancer cell proliferation, and cell transition. Xiang et al. (2018) showed that PPI inhibited growth of CRPC cells through inhibition of HOTAIR. PPI inhibited the p65 protein levels and lncRNA HOTAIR, which subsequently led to the suppression of MUC1 gene expression. The combination of PPI and enzalutamide exhibits synergy.

Other natural compounds

Shikonin inhibited the growth and proliferation of parental and DX-resistant prostate cancer cells in a time- and dose-manner, arrested the cell cycle in the G2/M or S phase and modulated cell cycle regulatory proteins (Markowitsch et al., 2021). Artesunate induced cell-type-specific modes of action, leading to cell cycle arrest, apoptosis and ferroptosis, accompanied by G0/G1 phase arrest and down-regulation of cell cycle activating proteins in all DX-resistant prostate cancer cells and parental LNCaP (Vakhrusheva et al., 2022). Baicalin suppressed the CDK6/FOXM1 axis to suppress the cell cycle progression and proliferation in LNCaP and PC3 cells (Yu et al., 2020). In the low, medium and high dose brucine groups, the expression levels of HSP70 protein were significantly lower than those in the control group. Brucine inhibited the expression of HSP70 and promoted the apoptosis of PC-3 cells (Yan et al., 2022). Artemisinin inhibited c-Jun, p-c-Jun, NF-κB, p-Akt, caused PC-3 cell death via caspase-dependent intrinsic signaling cascade (Ahmad et al., 2020). Wogonoside inhibited PC-3 cell viability, migration, and invasion by suppressing Wnt/β-catenin pathway and reversing EMT (Wei et al., 2019). Patchouli alcohol inhibited DU145 and PC-3 cells growth and induced apoptosis via the NF-κB/Mcl-1 pathway (Cai et al., 2022). These findings may provide therapeutic targets for CRPC. Solamargine enhanced the efficacy of docetaxel by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt pathway in CRPC cells (Ge et al., 2022). Han et al. (2020) screened the anti-prostate cancer components of TCM. A total of 25 compounds were submitted to experimental biological activity tests and the small molecule MoL_11 was found to have the best activity which can be used as a lead compound used for potential anti-cancer drug discovery.

The main anti-cancer mechanisms of Chinese herbal formulas, extracts, and natural compounds mostly focuses on induction of apoptosis, autophagy, and cell cycle arrest, suppression of angiogenesis, proliferation, and migration of prostate cancer cells, reversal of drug resistance, and enhancement of anti-tumor immunity.(Fig. 2). So far, clinical studies of these extracts and compounds are still lacking, and it is hoped that more experimental results will be translated into clinical efficacy through high-quality clinical trials.

Mechanism of Chinese herbal medicines in treatment of prostate cancer.

Clinical practice

A systematic review showed that compared to endocrine therapy alone, a combination of TCM and endocrine therapy may be more effective in restraining the development of the disease (Cao et al., 2016). A retrospective cohort study of 1132 prostate cancer patients suggested that a lower death risk was observed with longer TCM use (Liu et al., 2016). In the last five years, a number of clinical trials have confirmed that Chinese herbal medicine has a certain effect on improving the quality of life, reducing PSA levels, and prolonging survival of prostate cancer patients (Table 3).

Hormone sensitive prostate cancer

A clinical study randomly divided 234 prostate cancer patients into observation group and control group, the control group was given oral bicalutamide tablets, and the observation group was combined with Jiawei Danggui Beimu Kushen Pills on this basis. The observation group could significantly reduce the serum PSA level, inhibit tumor vitality, and improve the clinical symptoms of patients. Long-term follow-up found that the observation group could improve the five-year cumulative survival rate, the incidence of adverse reactions was lower than the control group (Zhao et al., 2021). Gai et al. (2021) explored the effect of combining TCM with hormonal therapy for prostate cancer patients. The combination therapy group delayed the time to develop into the androgen-independent prostate cancer [(39.71 ± 8.13) vs (30.85 ± 7.24), P < 0.01], the number of patients with PSA level restored to normal was significantly higher than the control group. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found that oral curcumin had no effect on the duration of intermittent androgen deprivation. The curcumin group suppressed PSA elevation over six months, and 1440 mg/d was well tolerated and safe (Choi et al., 2019). Gu et al. (2021) randomly divided 76 patients with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer bone metastases into a control group and a modified Shenqi Dihuang Decoction (MSDD) group. Compared with the controls, the MSDD group showed significantly lower rates of castration resistance (71.87% vs 28.12%, P < 0.05) and new bone and visceral metastases (40.63% vs 18.75%, P < 0.05), reduced the pain associated with bone metastasis, and improved the patients' quality of life. In a randomized, controlled, double-blind clinical study, it was found that Qilan Capsules had a potentiating effect of androgen-deprivation therapy on patients with qi stagnation and blood stasis type of prostate cancer after castration (Chang et al., 2017). In hormone sensitive stage, combining CHM formulas slowed down the time for developing to CRPC, improved related symptoms.

Castration-resistance prostate cancer

Androgen deprivation therapy shows obvious effects at the beginning of treatment, but after 18–24 months of endocrine therapy, most patients eventually develop castration-resistant prostate cancer. The median survival time since development of castration resistance is approximately 14 months which poses a huge health burden (André, Daniela, Isabel, & Fernandes, 2018).

Zhang et al. (2022) recruited 53 metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) patients and randomised them into a control group (n = 27) and a CFF-1 (alcohol extract from a compound CHM) group (n = 26). After 3 and 6 months of CFF-1 treatment, PSA levels and the FACT-P scores were significantly decreased, KPS scores were obviously increased in comparison with control group. The result showed that CFF-1 inhibited PSA, improved fatigue status, and improved the quality of life in mCRPC patients. A total of 54 CRPC patients were randomly divided into a control and a trial group. All were treated by endocrine therapy and chemotherapy while the trial group combined with Fuyang Huayu Prescription in addition. Karnofsky, FACT-P and TCM symptom scores were all markedly improved in the trial group after 12 weeks of treatment (P < 0.05) and remained stable at 24 weeks. The serum PSA level was significantly decreased after 12 weeks of treatment both in the control [(25.9 ± 39.3) vs (20.0 ± 21.1) μg/L, P < 0.05] and in the trial group [(22.1 ± 33.9) vs (17.9 ± 19.1) μg/L, P < 0.05] (Zhang et al., 2017). A phase II pilot study demonstrated that docetaxel/curcumin had a high response rate without additional toxicity, and the treatment was well accepted by chemotherapy-naive metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer patients (Mahammedi et al., 2016). A double-blind, randomized, phase II study investigated the efficacy of docetaxel combined with curcumin among mCRPC patients. Due to the small sample size as a result of the early discontinuation at the interim analyses, the addition of curcumin to the treatment strategies of mCRPC patients was not efficacious (Passildas Jahanmohan et al., 2021).

Treatment-related side effects

At the same time of precise and standardized treatment of prostate cancer, treatment-related side effects may cause treatment interruption and affect patient survival. Side effects associated with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, osteoporosis, anemia, and fatigue, increase the risk of non-neoplastic mortality. Evidence of increased depressed mood in men who received ADT is also presented. Sexual dysfunction after radical prostatectomy and urinary incontinence seriously affect patients' quality of life. Combined treatment with TCM plays an important role in reducing postoperative complications and improving patients' mood.

Depression is associated with higher mortality in prostate cancer. Lin et al. (2019) enrolled 248 prostate cancer patients with depression in a retrospective cohort study. The patients were divided into TCM for depression group, TCM for other purposes group, and no TCM group. During a median follow-up period of 6.2 years, they found that TCM for depression may be associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality. Sun et al. (2021) found that psychological intervention combined with traditional Chinese and Western medicine can significantly improve the patient’s psychological state, and reduce the degree of pain. The two-year cumulative survival rate in the combination group was higher than in the control group (84.62% vs 63.46%).

Radical prostatectomy (RP) has been considered the best treatment for patients with localized and locally advanced prostate cancer. Although certain effects can be achieved, the complication seriously affects patients’ normal life. A 2022 retrospective clinical study consisting of 98 patients undergoing radical resection showed that Dahuang Zhechong Pills improved effective rate of complication treatment at different periods, reduced the biological recurrence rate and clinical recurrence rate (Hou and Bin, 2022).

Curcumin has demonstrated radioprotective properties in preclinical models (Kalman, Zhao, Anscher, & Urdaneta, 2017). Curcumin sensitizes prostate cancer cells to radiation partly via epigenetic activation of miR-143 and miR-143 mediated autophagy inhibition (Liu et al., 2017). Saadipoor et al. (2019) described the first clinical experience with nanocurcumin in prostate cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy. The single‐center design trial failed to demonstrate the efficacy of nanocurcumin and the relatively small number of patients recruited is a limitation.

Cancer pain greatly deteriorated the physical and psychological health of prostate cancer patients. Prostaglandins secreted by prostate cancer make patients extremely sensitive to pain. Prostate cancer has a strong propensity to metastasise to bone, which causes metastatic cancer-induced bone pain (Zajączkowska et al., 2019). Recent studies have shown that CHM can relieve pain and improve quality of life (Luo et al., 2020). Although the number of randomized controlled trials has been increasing in recent years, the quality of research related to the treatment of prostate cancer pain with TCM is low, and there is a lack of evidence-based exploration. Several systematic reviews of TCM for prostate cancer pain are underway (Gong et al., 2019, Wang et al., 2019). We expect that these studies will provide higher-quality evidence and enrich treatment options for pain caused by prostate cancer.

Discussion and conclusion

By reviewing the research of the past five years, we found that CHM formulas, single CHM extracts, and CHM-derived compounds play an important role in the anti-prostate cancer activity, inhibiting metastasis, reversing drug resistance, and enhancing immune activity. Mechanism exploration mainly focused on the compounds of CHMs. In clinical practice, Chinese herbal medicine works synergistically with chemotherapy and endocrine therapy to reduce PSA level, delay the occurrence of CRPC, alleviate adverse reactions, improve quality of life and prolong survival throughout the course of treatment. At present, there is a wide gap between experimental data and clinical application. For example, curcumin exerts a variety of anti-prostate cancer cell activities in basic experiments, but it has not shown a corresponding curative effect in randomized controlled studies (Passildas Jahanmohan et al., 2021). Analysis of the reasons may be due to the fact that the human body is a whole. The tumor environment may influence how cells respond to CHM. In vitro cell lines are in a relatively stable state, and tumor cells are a step-by-step growth process in vivo, which is difficult to simulate in vitro (Hu et al., 2016). Nanotechnology is beneficial in addressing the drawbacks that limit the clinical application of CHM extracts and compounds. The use of TCM as an adjuvant medicine loaded into nanoparticles for combination therapy may enhance its clinical application (Li et al., 2022, Wu et al., 2021).

Chemotherapy and new anti-androgens are key components in the management of advanced prostate cancer. However, the acquisition of chemoresistance is a common and critical problem, and studies have shown that CHMs inhibit docetaxel resistance through the SNHG10/miR-1271-5p/TRIM66 and c-met/PI3K/AKT pathway. Androgen axis-targeted therapies such as abiraterone and enzalutamide also have shorter efficacy due to primary or acquired resistance. The major mechanism is the generation of truncated AR splice variants that escape anti-androgen binding (Zhu et al., 2020). Enzalutamide resistance can be achieved by AR splice variants (Pinto et al., 2022). Quercetin and triptolide downregulated the AR/AR-V7 pathway, exhibited a synergistic effect with enzalutamide, and reversed its resistance. This may represent a potential strategy for reversing the resistance to anti-androgens.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have achieved fruitful results in the clinical treatment of various cancers, but prostate cancer remains unresponsive to ICIs. TCM compound CFF-1 alone, or combined with docetaxel inhibited EGFR/JAK1/STAT3 signaling to block PD-1 /PD-L1 expression for increasing T cell mediated antitumor immune response in prostate cancer patients (Zhang et al., 2022), suggesting that TCM maybe a promising treatment to resist tumor immunosuppression. The study provided ideas for the development of new immunotherapy drug combinations to improve the efficacy of prostate cancer immunotherapy.

In clinical practice, the treatment mode of integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine is recommended. We have summarized the advantages of CHMs at different stages of prostate cancer treatment. In patients with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, combined CHMs can delay the development of androgen-independent prostate cancer, reduce PSA levels and improve the 5-year survival rate. The addition of CHMs did not affect the course of androgen deprivation therapy. In castration-resistant prostate cancer, CHMs can improve patients' quality of life and improve PSA levels. In addition, CHM treatment can reduce postoperative complications and improve patients' anxiety. However, the current clinical research evidence on the treatment of prostate cancer with CHMs is still insufficient (Cao et al., 2016). These promising findings need to be interpreted with caution due to methodological problems in Chinese medicine trials. The primary outcomes of current clinical trials use different measurement tools and time points, and reporting is incomplete. At present, there are 14 related core outcome sets of prostate cancer included in the COMET database (https://www.comet-initiative.org/). Over the past decade, nine of them have been published (Meregaglia et al., 2020). We suggest further carrying out the multicenter, large sample, double-blind, randomized controlled trials and organize authoritative experts to formulate research inclusion criteria and outcome indicators at the trial design stage. According to the characteristics of prostate cancer, the follow-up period will be extended to 3–5 years to ensure long-term efficacy. TCM plays an important role in the overall management of prostate cancer. We should deeply study the etiology and pathogenesis characteristics of TCM at different stages to standardize the syndrome types and core treatment principles. We hope that the existing research can provide ideas for the transformation of high-quality evidence in the future.

The control mechanisms of clearance ratio on squeeze film dampers performance
Authors: Zhongyu Yang, Jiaqi Li, Yihang Shi, Yinli Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRotating machinery is a crucial component in modern industrial systems, offering high efficiency and reliable performance. However, vibration issues significantly shorten their operational lifespan. Squeeze film dampers (SFDs), due to their simple structure, low cost, and effective vibration reduction, are widely used in these systems. However, the strong nonlinearity of SFDs means existing designs often rely on trial-and-error methods. This study focuses on optimizing damper parameters, particularly the clearance ratio, which is a key design factor. A numerical model based on kinetic equations and elastic supporting vibration theory is developed to explore the relationship between damper transmissibility and speed coefficient across different clearance ratios. The impact of clearance ratio on squeeze film dynamics is further examined through experimental tests on a rotor-SFD system. Results show that at a clearance ratio of 0.3%, a stable squeeze film forms, minimizing two-phase flow effects and maximizing film carrying capacity. This research provides valuable insights into enhancing the efficiency of damper designs in rotating machinery.Keywords: Clearance ratio, Rotor-SFD system, Transmissibility, Two-phase flow effectSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The rotor system, as the core carrier of high-speed rotational power transmission in rotating machinery, plays a decisive role in determining the equipment’s lifespan, energy efficiency, and operational safety. However, under complex operating conditions, the rotor often experiences severe vibration due to accumulated imbalance. The imbalance arises from manufacturing and assembly errors, material inhomogeneity, or thermal deformation. The periodic centrifugal excitation force generated by this imbalance can lead to excessive vibration amplitude of the shaft system, increased bearing wear, and even induce resonance instability in the critical speed range, posing a significant threat to the safety of the unit. To suppress such vibration risks, the squeeze film damper (SFD), with its unique energy dissipation mechanism through the oil film squeeze, becomes a key component in ensuring the stable operation of the rotor system. By dynamically adjusting the damping and stiffness characteristics, it plays a critical role, especially during transient startup and shutdown, variable load impacts, and extreme temperature conditions. The SFD, through adaptive dissipation of vibrational energy and suppression of nonlinear instability, significantly enhances the rotor system’s tolerance to imbalance excitation, providing irreplaceable technical support for the long-term safe operation of high-performance gas turbines. In recent years, academia has conducted in-depth exploration into the dynamic characteristics of the rotor-SFD system.

Research on rotor imbalance is a core issue in the dynamics of rotating machinery, primarily involving the study of coupling effect mechanisms1, imbalance detection and fault diagnosis2–4, dynamic balancing and correction technologies5, and several other aspects. Zhang et al.6, based on the coupling tribology and dynamics, employed turbulence lubrication theory and finite difference methods to calculate the influence of imbalance on the nonlinear dynamic characteristics of turbocharger rotors. Tian et al.7 discretized the rotor system using finite element methods and studied the impact of imbalance on the rotor dynamics of turbocharger floating ring bearing systems in the 0–3500 Hz range. Rendl et al.8 used a numerical continuous method with an infinitely short bearing model to analyze the effects of rotational imbalance and oil film pressure gradients on the nonlinear dynamic characteristics of the rotor system. Kang et al.9, based on full-spectrum analysis theory, conducted simulation studies on the effects of imbalance excitation, rotational direction, and other parameters on the backward whirling phenomenon of the double-rotor system and validated the conclusions through experiments. Zhang et al.10 proposed a multi-1× fault rotor imbalance vibration suppression method based on the SP-based suppression method.

Research on SFDs mainly focuses on the analysis of dynamic characteristics and mechanisms11,12, structural design and optimization13,14, multi-field coupling vibration control, and intelligent control integration15. Wang et al.16, based on the Reynolds equation, employed a neural network model to study the oil film force, pressure, stiffness, and damping values under structural, lubrication, and operational parameters. Wang et al.17 combined SFDs with porous tilting pad gas bearings and studied the effects of supply pressure ratio, imbalance, installation, and damping liquid viscosity on the nonlinear dynamic characteristics of the squeeze film. Zhou et al.18 based their study on a hybrid multiphase flow model and a total energy model, analyzing the dynamic characteristics of SFDs considering two-phase flow phenomena and viscosity thermal effects, as well as the impact of thermal effects on two-phase flow. Zheng et al.19 proposed a controllable clearance SFD with hydraulic adjustment of the radial clearance, studying the critical amplitude under different imbalance and clearance conditions to determine the optimal radial clearance for the damper. Tian et al.20 conducted precise modeling of SFDs using component modal synthesis and computed the nonlinear oil film forces of SFDs via finite difference methods. They then experimentally studied the vibration damping effects of the SFD. Iacobellis et al.21 conducted experimental studies on the impact of squeeze film clearance, oil inlet quantity, oil inlet hole direction, and other parameters on the dynamic characteristics of SFDs.

Research on the SFD system focuses on dynamic modeling22,23, nonlinear vibration suppression24,25, and multi-physics coupling analysis26,27, aiming to improve vibration damping efficiency and operational reliability in high-speed rotating machinery, such as aircraft engines and gas turbines. Chen et al.28 used finite difference and numerical integration methods to develop a SFD model that takes fluid inertia into account, employing the Newmark-HHT method to analyze the dynamic characteristics of the rotor-SFD system under time-varying base motion excitation. Zheng et al.29 used a coefficient difference method to rapidly calculate the imbalance response of the rotor system supported by a SFD and optimized the numerical simulation method for the rotor-SFD system’s dynamic characteristics. Pan et al.30 solved the eccentric rotor system model with a SFD using the Newmark-β algorithm, analyzing the effect of oil film clearance on the rotor system’s dynamic characteristics under maneuvering loads. Luo et al.31 established a rotor-elastic ring SFD system model by coupling the energy equation, Reynolds equation, and viscosity-temperature equation, analyzing the impact of oil film temperature on the rotor system’s amplitude-frequency response curve and sudden imbalance response curve.

Some studies have further advanced the understanding of SFD dynamics under complex operating conditions. For instance, researchers have conducted extensive nonlinear analysis on bifurcation and chaos in SFD-supported rotors to identify stability boundaries32,33. The influence of key design parameters and fluid inertia on the dynamic response has also been rigorously investigated to improve modeling accuracy34,35. Furthermore, the vibration attenuation characteristics of SFDs under various excitation forms have been explored to validate their effectiveness in diverse engineering applications36.

The existing research mainly focuses on the dynamic characteristics analysis of the SFD and dynamic modeling of the rotor system, which provides a theoretical foundation for the application of SFDs. However, there has been no systematic analysis of parameter selection methods, and the current design of dampers largely relies on trial-and-error approaches. Therefore, conducting research on the optimal parameter selection range for SFDs, particularly on the clearance ratio, an important design parameter, is crucial for improving the design efficiency of gas turbines. By optimizing the design parameters of the damper, its vibration damping performance under high-speed conditions can be effectively enhanced, thereby improving the operational stability and reliability of gas turbines. To investigate the influence mechanism of the clearance ratio on the vibration damping performance of the SFD, this study adopts a combined research approach of theoretical derivation, numerical modeling, and experimental testing. The dynamic characteristics and vibration damping effects of the SFD under different clearance ratios are examined. In terms of theoretical research, a numerical calculation model is constructed based on the energy equation and the theory of elastic support vibration characteristics, and a program is developed to solve the relationship between the transfer rate of the elastic support and the rotational speed coefficient for different clearance ratios. A numerical model is also established to study the effect of clearance ratio on the dynamic characteristics of the squeeze film. Additionally, a rotor-SFD system dynamic characteristics test rig is set up to perform modal, steady-state, and transient dynamic tests, analyzing the nonlinear characteristics of the rotor system and the vibration damping effects of the damper under various clearance ratios.

Impact of clearance ratio on vibration characteristics

Although the actual rotor system possesses multiple degrees of freedom, the use of the Jeffcott model is justified in this context. As indicated by the modal analysis in Fig. 9, the rotor exhibits a symmetric fundamental bending mode near the first critical speed. In this frequency range, the gyroscopic effects and higher order mode couplings are minimal compared to the primary elastic and damping forces. Therefore, the Jeffcott model effectively captures the dominant dynamic behavior and force transmission characteristics required for parametric optimization, a standard approach supported by classical rotor dynamics literature41,42.

The variation of the transmission rate with respect to rotational speed for the elastic support-SFD system under different clearance ratios is illustrated in Fig. 14.

The transmission rate of the elastic support at different clearance ratios.

As shown in Fig. 14, as the clearance ratio decreases, the transmission rate reduces, and the damping effect of the SFD improves. However, when the clearance ratio becomes excessively small (c/R = 0.1%), the curve exhibits a ‘wave-like fluctuation,’ indicating strong nonlinearity of the squeeze oil film and an unstable damping effect. When the clearance ratio is 0.3%, the transmission curve is stable, with the peak clearly lower than those at 0.5% and 0.6%. At a clearance ratio of 0.2%, the peak is even lower, but the curve shows slight fluctuations. Therefore, the optimal clearance ratio should lie between 0.2% and 0.3%.

Numerical simulation of SFD

To investigate the influence of the clearance ratio of the damper on the dynamic characteristics of the SFD, while considering the effects of the specimen fabrication and the test rig, a dynamic model of the SFD was established. The model features a bearing radius of 46.4 mm, a damper width of 40 mm, and various clearance sizes of 0.05 mm, 0.08 mm, 0.1 mm, 0.12 mm, 0.14 mm, 0.16 mm, 0.18 mm, and 0.2 mm, corresponding to clearance ratios of 0.1%, 0.17%, 0.21%, 0.25%, 0.3%, 0.34%, 0.38%, and 0.43%, respectively. Numerical simulations were conducted based on this model.

Figure 15 illustrates the pressure distribution of the squeeze film under different clearance ratios, with the following characteristics: As the clearance ratio increases, the overall amplitude of the oil film pressure decreases, with a critical clearance ratio observed at 0.3%. At very low clearance ratios (c/R = 0.1% and 0.17%), the amplitude of the positive pressure region is significantly larger than that of the negative pressure region. In this case, the oil film is not fully formed, the load-bearing capacity is weak, and the damping effect is poor. When the clearance ratio is less than 0.3%, the pressure amplitude decreases rapidly. After the clearance ratio exceeds 0.3%, the rate of decrease in amplitude slows significantly.

Effect of Clearance Ratio on the Pressure Distribution of SFD

Figure 16 shows the distribution of gas phase volume fraction in the SFD under different clearance ratios. As the clearance ratio increases from 0.1% to the critical value of 0.3%, the region affected by the two-phase flow effect contracts, and the gas phase volume fraction decreases accordingly. When the clearance ratio exceeds 0.3%, the two-phase flow region expands again, and the gas phase volume fraction increases as well.

Effect of Clearance Ratio on the Gas Phase Volume Fraction of SFD. (a) c/R = 0.1%, (b) c/R = 0.17%, (c) c/R = 0.21%, (d) c/R = 0.25%, (e) c/R = 0.3%, (f) c/R = 0.34%, (g) c/R = 0.38%, (h) c/R = 0.43%.

This phenomenon arises from the two-phase flow, which is composed of cavitation and air entrainment effects. When the clearance ratio is less than 0.3%, cavitation dominates. As the clearance ratio increases, the oil film pressure amplitude decreases rapidly, and the range of saturation vapor pressure becomes smaller, weakening the cavitation effect. When the clearance ratio exceeds 0.3%, the air entrainment effect strengthens, leading to an expansion of the two-phase flow region.

Figure 22 illustrates the acceleration vibration amplitude of the rotor-SFD system under different damper clearance ratios. In the case of small damper clearance ratios, the squeeze film is unable to form a continuous and stable oil film layer. This results in a reduced load-bearing capacity of the damper, preventing it from effectively suppressing rotor system vibrations. As a consequence, the acceleration vibration amplitude remains at a relatively high level. Especially in the critical speed region, there is a significant difference in the acceleration vibration amplitude between the small and large damper clearance ratio cases, with the former being noticeably larger than the latter. As the damper clearance increases, the conditions for forming the squeeze film improve, enhancing the damping effect of the damper. This leads to a reduction in the rotor system’s acceleration vibration amplitude. However, once the critical value (c/R = 0.3%) is reached, the air intake phenomenon intensifies, the influence of two-phase flow effects increases, and the nonlinear effects of the squeeze film are enhanced, resulting in an increase in the acceleration vibration amplitude.

Effect of Damper Clearance Ratio on the Acceleration Vibration Amplitude of the Rotor-SFD System.

At high precession frequencies, the inertia effects of the squeeze film are enhanced, intensifying the two-phase flow effects. This increases the nonlinear degree of the squeeze film stiffness, which in turn leads to oscillations in the acceleration vibration amplitude of the rotor-SFD system. In the case of small damper clearance ratios, the squeeze film layer is thin, and the pressure amplitude is high, with the cavitation effect dominating. The two-phase flow effects of the squeeze film are strong, resulting in a large stiffness coefficient and intense oscillation phenomena. As the damper clearance increases, the oil film thickness increases, and the squeeze film tends to stabilize. This reduces the stiffness coefficient and lowers the degree of stiffness nonlinearity, thereby weakening the oscillation effects. However, once the damper clearance exceeds the critical value, the two-phase flow effects strengthen again, and the damping effect on the oscillations is correspondingly weakened.

It was found through theoretical derivation, numerical analysis, and experimental research that when the clearance ratio is 0.3%, the squeeze film can form a stable and continuous layer, the two-phase flow effect is weakest, and the oil film’s load-carrying capacity is highest.

Conclusion

This paper investigates the control mechanisms of clearance ratio on SFD performance through a combination of theoretical analysis, numerical simulation, and experimental research. Based on the energy equation and the theory of elastic support vibration characteristics, a numerical calculation model was developed, and a program was written to solve the relationship between the transmissibility and the speed coefficient of the SFD at different clearance ratios. A numerical model for the SFD was established to investigate the effect of the clearance ratio on the dynamic characteristics of the squeeze film. A rotor-SFD system dynamics test rig was built, and modal, steady-state, and transient dynamic tests were conducted to study the nonlinear characteristics of the rotor system and the vibration reduction performance of the SFD under different clearance ratios. The following conclusions were drawn:

The clearance ratio is a critical parameter governing vibration isolation efficiency. While increasing the clearance generally reduces transmissibility, an optimal interval of 0.2% to 0.3% is identified as the most effective design range. In this range, the system achieves minimal transmissibility without exhibiting the severe waveform fluctuations observed at smaller clearances, providing a deterministic target for engineering design to replace empirical trial-and-error methods.

While increasing the clearance generally reduces transmissibility, an optimal interval of 0.2% to 0.3% is identified as the most effective design range. In this range, the system achieves minimal transmissibility without exhibiting the severe waveform fluctuations observed at smaller clearances, providing a deterministic target for engineering design to replace empirical trial-and-error methods.

The study elucidates the microscopic physical mechanism responsible for the performance trends: a competitive evolution between cavitation and air entrainment. When the clearance ratio is below 0.3%, cavitation effects dominate due to rapid pressure drops; when it exceeds 0.3%, air ingestion intensifies, expanding the two-phase flow region. The optimal performance at 0.3% corresponds to the equilibrium point where the combined negative effects of these phenomena are minimized, ensuring maximum oil film continuity and load-carrying capacity.

Influenced by the flow phase evolution, the rotor system exhibits a non-monotonic vibration response. The 0.3% clearance ratio represents a critical turning point where the nonlinear vibration amplitude is minimized, and the system stability is maximized. This finding suggests that designing SFDs near this critical ratio can effectively suppress the transition to chaotic motion under high-speed conditions.

In summary, the clearance ratio significantly impacts SFD performance. A 0.3% clearance ratio optimizes vibration reduction performance, minimizes two - phase flow effects, and maximizes oil film bearing capacity for stable operation. This study provides a reliable basis for the optimal design of SFDs, helping to enhance system stability and efficiency while reducing trial - and - error in engineering applications, and contributing to the rational parameter selection of SFDs in rotor systems.

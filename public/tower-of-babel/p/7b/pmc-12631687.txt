Microfluidic All-Solid-State Electrodes for Real-Time Monitoring of Salivary Cations (Na+, K+, and Ca2 +)
Authors: Yoshihisa Ozeki, Tetsuya Yamada, Gen Mayanagi, Jumpei Washio, Nobuhiro Takahashi, Yasuko Yanagida
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Saliva is a valuable biofluid for noninvasive diagnostics, offering insights into oral and systemic health through ion composition analysis. Key electrolytes such as calcium (Ca2 +), sodium (Na+), and potassium (K+) are critical for tooth enamel integrity, remineralization, and caries resistance. Conventional detection methods such as atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS) and inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) are impractical for real-time or point-of-care applications. Here, we report the fabrication and integration of all-solid-state ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) into a microfluidic flow device for real-time, low-volume, and wireless salivary ion monitoring. The platform enables precise fluid handling, automated processing, and simultaneous multi-ion detection. The fabricated ISEs exhibited near-Nernstian responses for Ca2 + and Na+, while the K+ sensor showed a slightly sub-Nernstian behavior due to membrane deposition effects. Importantly, validation with real human saliva confirmed concentration-dependent responses, stable operation in complex biological matrices, and reproducibility across independent samples. The Na+ sensor demonstrated high selectivity (log10 KPot Na,K = –1.63 and log10 KPot Na,Ca = –4.63), while response times of 3–5 min supported near real-time monitoring. These results demonstrate that integrating solid-state ISEs with microfluidics provides a robust and noninvasive diagnostic platform for oral and systemic health assessment. The system represents a step toward miniaturized, cost-effective, and user-friendly point-of-care tools.

Introduction

Saliva is an essential biological fluid that plays a crucial role in both oral and systemic health. Composed of 99% water and 1% organic and inorganic molecules, it contains a variety of electrolytes, proteins, and biomolecules that make it a valuable medium for noninvasive diagnostics. , Among its key constituents, calcium (Ca2+), sodium (Na+), potassium (K+), and magnesium (Mg2+) are among the ions that are particularly significant for assessing oral health status. , Calcium and phosphate ions contribute to saliva supersaturation, preventing demineralization and promoting remineralization of tooth enamel. Elevated calcium levels in saliva are linked to greater protection against dental caries, while individuals with a higher risk of cavities often exhibit lower salivary calcium concentrations. Magnesium, which correlates with calcium levels, also plays a role in caries resistance. Additionally, the interaction between calcium, phosphate ions, and statherin strengthens enamel by enhancing remineralization and minimizing mineral loss. Given its dynamic composition, saliva provides critical insights into oral health and serves as a potential diagnostic tool for detecting early signs of oral and systemic diseases. −

Despite its diagnostic potential, current ion detection methods such as atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS) and inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), though highly sensitive, are impractical for real-time or point-of-care applications due to their reliance on complex sample preparation, specialized equipment, and trained personnel. , Salivary ion concentrations fluctuate rapidly due to factors such as diet, hydration, and health conditions, necessitating a portable and continuous monitoring system. , Traditional ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) have been explored for salivary ion sensing, but their dependence on liquid junctions and external reference solutions limits their usability in miniaturized, wearable, or in vivo applications. ,

To address these limitations, recent advancements in all-solid-state ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) and microfluidic technology offer a promising alternative by enabling portable, real-time, and multi-ion sensing in a noninvasive manner. , Solid-state ISEs eliminate the need for liquid junctions, improving stability, portability, and ease of use. Additionally, they can be fabricated as flexible film-based sensors, providing accurate sodium detection in saliva comparable to blood measurements. Integrating these sensors into a microfluidic platform further enhances functionality by enabling precise fluid handling, automated sample processing, and simultaneous multi-ion detection.

Microfluidic systems are particularly advantageous for saliva-based diagnostics, where sample availability is often limited. Studies have demonstrated that as little as 20 μL of saliva is sufficient for the rapid quantification of oral disease biomarkers, proving the feasibility of low-volume, high-sensitivity diagnostics. Additionally, microfluidic platforms streamline sample filtration, enrichment, and mixing, allowing for fully automated and hands-free analysis. A recent study has developed sensors to detect multiple ions simultaneously, including calcium (Ca2 +), magnesium (Mg2 +), potassium (K+), and sodium (Na+), using a single microfluidic platform. Since imbalances in these ions are often associated with dental caries, enamel demineralization, and systemic health issues, , multi-ion sensing enhances diagnostic precision and enables comprehensive oral health assessments.

The potential for real-time, wireless ion monitoring using miniaturized, portable microfluidic devices represents a transformative step in point-of-care (POC) diagnostics. These technologies provide rapid and actionable results, improving patient care, early disease detection, and personalized health monitoring. The integration of microfluidics, solid-state ISEs, and saliva-based diagnostics presents a cost-effective, accessible, and highly reliable solution for healthcare applications.

Therefore, this study aims to develop a microfluidic device integrated with all-solid-state ion-selective electrodes for real-time monitoring of multiple ion concentrations in saliva. By addressing the limitations of conventional ion sensing methods, the proposed system will enable low-volume, continuous, and wireless monitoring of salivary ions, contributing to the advancement of next-generation diagnostic tools for oral and systemic health monitoring.

Methodology

Materials and Reagents

All the materials and chemicals including “polyvinyl chloride (PVC), 2-Nitrophenyl octyl ether (NPOE), sodium tetrakis 3,5-bis­(trifluoromethyl)­phenyl borate (Na-TFPB), tetrahydrofuran (THF), Bis­[(benzo-15-crown-5)-4-methyl]­pimelate (Bis­(benzo-15-crown-5)), and Bis­[(12-crown-4)­methyl]­2-dodecyl2-methylmalonate (Bis­(12-crown-4)) were obtained from FUJIFILM Wako. Additional chemicals including calcium ionophore II (ETH 129), magnesium ionophore I (ETH 1117), and poly­(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-poly­(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS; 1.0 wt % in H2O)” were sourced from Sigma-Aldrich. Laminate film (100 μm, LZ-B620) was purchased from IRIS OHYAMA Inc. Deionized water (resistivity 18.2 MΩ·cm) was used throughout all experimental procedures, obtained from a Millipore Milli-Q Direct system (MeRCK). The reference electrode (Ag/AgCl) containing a 3.0 M NaCl solution was acquired from BAS Inc. Dimethylpolysiloxane (PDMS) was obtained from DuPont Toray.

All-Solid-State-Type Sensor Fabrication

A gold (Au) electrode on a polyethylene terephthalate (PET) film served as the conducting substrate. The fabrication process followed the schematic shown in Figure . The PET films underwent a cleaning process using isopropyl alcohol to remove surface contaminants. A thin layer of titanium (30 nm) and Initially gold (50 nm) was then deposited onto the PET substrate using a SVC-700TM deposition system (SANYU ELECTRON). Following deposition, a protective PET layer was laminated onto the film, leaving a 3 mm diameter sensing region exposed. PEDOT:PSS, employed as the conducting polymer, was deposited onto this exposed area by drop-casting 2.5 μL of PEDOT:PSS solution, followed by thermal curing at 140 °C for 5 min. Subsequently, ion-selective membrane solutions were drop-cast onto the PEDOT:PSS-coated sensing area and dried. These membranes were prepared by dissolving ionophore, polyvinyl chloride (PVC), nitrophenyl octyl ether (NPOE), and tetrakis­[3,5-bis­(trifluoromethyl)­phenyl]­borate (TFPB) in tetrahydrofuran (THF). The ionophores included Bis­(benzo-15-crown-5), Bis­(12-crown-4), ETH 129, and ETH 1117. Microchannel Flow Path Fabrication

To fabricate the flow path device, a glass substrate was first coated with gold (Au) and silver (Ag) using a sputtering process. The ion-selective electrode was then prepared by creating a 1 mm diameter sensor site on the gold electrode. This was achieved by laminating a protective film and applying a conductive polymer (PEDOT) followed by an ion-selective membrane (Figure a). Next, the flow path device was assembled by attaching a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) layer to the glass substrate. The channel was formed on the PDMS using a mold (Figure b), with dimensions of 3 mm in width and 0.5 mm in height.

Potentiometric Measurements

Electrochemical measurements were conducted at 25 °C, as illustrated in Figure . This system accompanied of ensuring a 16-bit analog-to-digital converter (ADS1115), a microcomputer board (Arduino Pro Mini), and a wireless communication module (Xbee), enabling real-time data acquisition. All electrochemical evaluations were conducted using standard aqueous solutions prepared with analytical-grade KCl, NaCl, and CaCl2 salts, not synthetic saliva. The Ag/AgCl electrode reference (3.0 M NaCl) was connected to the terminal ground, stable potential monitoring between the reference and ion-selective electrodes. Measurements were wirelessly transmitted and logged using a custom program written in Arduino IDE (ver. 1.8.5), with a data acquisition rate of one sample per second, and the activity coefficients of ions were estimated using the Debye–Hückel equation. The potentiometric system incorporated a 16-bit analog-to-digital converter (ADS1115), a microcontroller board (Arduino Pro Mini), and a wireless module (Xbee) for real-time data transmission and acquisition. This wireless monitoring configuration was based on a previously reported design, which provides detailed schematics and system integration strategies for portable, Arduino-based multi-ion sensing platforms. The limit of detection (LOD) for each ion-selective electrode was calculated as the ion activity corresponding to the electrode potential equal to the baseline potential plus three times the standard deviation of the baseline noise, consistent with previous work. All measurements involving error bars were performed in triplicate (n = 3), and results are presented as mean ± standard deviation.

Results and Discussion

Performance Evaluation of Ion-Selective Electrodes

The performance of the prepared Ca2 +, K+, and Na+ ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) was systematically evaluated to assess their electrochemical behavior. Each electrode was fabricated with a plasticized PVC membrane incorporating specific ionophores, plasticizers, and the ionic additive tetraphenylborate (TFPB), carefully optimized for selective ion recognition, as summarized in Table . The calibration curves for these electrodes, shown in Figure a–d, provide a comprehensive understanding of their response to varying ion concentrations.

Performance evaluation of (a) calibration curve for Ca2 +-ISE, (b) Ca2+ ion-selective electrodes, (c) K+ ion-selective electrodes, and (d) Na+ ion-selective electrodes.

Figure a illustrates the calibration curve for the Ca2 +-ISE, depicting the electrode potential (mV) as a function of time (min) in response to stepwise increases in CaCl2 concentration, ranging from 10–5 M to 10–1 M. The electrode potential increased consistently with rising Ca2 + concentration, demonstrating a clear and positive response. Notably, stable potential was achieved in within 5 min after changing concentration level, indicating the electrode’s stable responsiveness. A similar trend was observed for the K+ and Na+ electrodes.

The stability and precision of the ISEs were evident in their consistent response over multiple measurements, ensuring accuracy in ion quantification. Although long-term data are not shown in this manuscript, prior study has demonstrated the stable and reproducible performance of these ion-selective membranes in nonmicrofluidic systems, supporting their reliability for practical applications. The relationship between electrode potential and ion concentration followed the expected Nernstian behavior, as presented in Figure b–d, which depict the Nernstian response for Ca2 +, K+, and Na+ electrodes, respectively. The observed slopes were close to the theoretical Nernstian value for Ca2 + and Na+, confirming high selectivity and sensitivity, while K+ electrode showed a slightly lower slope. However, this deviation does not compromise the aim of the present study, which is to demonstrate the feasibility of integrating all-solid-state ion-selective electrodes into a miniaturized diagnostic platform. Previous studies have reported Nernstian behavior for the same K+-selective membrane over a concentration range of 10–3 to 10 ° M, covering the typical salivary range of 5–20 mM. The reduced performance observed in this study may be attributed to variability in membrane thickness during drop-casting. Optimization of fabrication procedures is currently underway to improve reproducibility and response accuracy in future work.

The results confirm that the prepared electrodes exhibit strong stability and reliable performance, making them suitable for accurate electrochemical measurements. The clear, stepwise response to different ion concentrations highlights their practical applicability in ion sensing. The consistent Nernstian response across all three ISEs indicates that these sensors provide robust and reproducible measurements, making them promising candidates for routine analytical applications.

The response of the fabricated reference electrode to chloride ion (Cl–) concentration was systematically investigated, as shown in Figure . Cl– was selected for concentration dependence testing because the electrochemical potential of an Ag/AgCl reference electrode is determined by the equilibrium reaction: AgCl(s)+e−⇌Ag(s)+Cl−(aq)

Evaluation of the concentration dependence of all-solid-state reference electrodes.

As a result, the activity of Cl– directly influences the electrode potential. Evaluating the response across a range of Cl– concentrations is a widely accepted method for characterizing the performance and stability of Ag/AgCl-based reference electrodes. This approach ensures that the electrode’s behavior under varying ionic conditions is accurately assessed. The electrode potential was measured across different chloride concentrations using agarose matrices with 10%, 20%, and 30% compositions. Among these, the 20% agarose electrode exhibited the most pronounced potential change, indicating the highest sensitivity to chloride concentration. The potential varied from approximately −6 mV at low chloride concentrations (−6 log [Cl–]) to around 5 mV at high concentrations (0 log [Cl–]), showing a well-defined response. The coefficient of variation for the 20% agarose electrode remained low at lower chloride concentrations (∼1.01) but increased to 1.89 at higher concentrations, suggesting a minor decline in measurement precision at elevated chloride concentrations.

In contrast, the 30% agarose-based electrode showed minimal response to chloride concentration, with relatively small variations in potential across the tested range. This suggests poor chloride sensitivity, likely due to the reduced ion mobility in the denser agarose matrix. The 10% agarose electrode exhibited a more negative potential at −1 log [Cl–] (0.1 M KCl), indicating a weaker linear dependence on chloride concentration compared to the 20% agarose electrode. The coefficient of variation for the 30% agarose electrode fluctuated significantly, particularly around −2 log [Cl–], indicating lower reproducibility and measurement accuracy at this concentration, implying lower reproducibility and measurement accuracy at this agarose concentration. Ideally, a reference electrode should exhibit minimal potential fluctuations with changes in analyte concentration. However, the observed chloride concentration dependence suggests that the agarose matrix plays a crucial role in ion mobility and interaction with the Ag/AgCl redox couple. The porosity and ion-exchange properties of agarose likely influence chloride ion movement within the electrode, altering its stability. Additionally, variations in matrix thickness may further impact ion diffusion, leading to differences in chloride sensitivity among the tested concentrations.

To assess temporal stability, the performance of the fabricated all-solid-state reference electrode was compared with a commercial reference electrode over a 16-h period, as shown in Figure . Initially, the fabricated electrode exhibited a stabilization phase lasting approximately 2 h, during which its potential fluctuated significantly. Following this period, the electrode attained a relatively stable potential; however, it remained slightly more sensitive to drift compared to the commercial reference electrode. This instability could be attributed to imperfect sealing during fabrication or the inherent characteristics of the agarose matrix, which may allow minor shifts in ion exchange over extended durations. Notably, the fabricated electrode exhibited greater susceptibility to drift, a factor that must be optimized for enhanced long-term performance. Previous studies indicate that high-quality all-solid-state reference electrodes should maintain a stable potential across extended measurement durations, , emphasizing the need for improved sealing and material refinement to reduce drift effects.

Evaluation of the time stability of an all-solid-state reference electrode.

The time stability evaluation in this study was focused solely on the reference electrode. In miniaturized electrochemical systems, the reference electrode often serves as the limiting factor for long-term stability due to its susceptibility to drift and environmental variations. In contrast, the ion-selective membranes used for Ca2 +, Na+, and K+ sensing are based on well-established compositions that have been previously validated for long-term stability in earlier studies. Therefore, the present work prioritized assessing the newly developed solid-state reference electrode to confirm its suitability for integration into miniaturized multi-ion sensing platforms.

The overall findings suggest that the fabricated all-solid-state reference electrode offers promising potential as an alternative to conventional liquid-junction reference electrodes. The chloride concentration dependence results indicate that the 20% agarose-based electrode provides the highest responsiveness, whereas the 30% agarose electrode demonstrates greater stability. The higher agarose concentration likely contributes to reduced ion mobility, resulting in less potential fluctuation but at the cost of sensitivity.

Fabrication of the Flow Channel Device Equipped with Each Sensor and Its Performance

To demonstrate the practical applicability of the fabricated all-solid-state reference electrode, it was integrated into a microfluidic flow channel device, as shown in Figure . The flow channel was constructed using PDMS, a transparent and chemically stable material that allows for precise control of ion solutions in real-time. The reference electrode was strategically embedded within the liquid flow channel, enabling continuous monitoring of ionic concentrations while maintaining high stability and reproducibility.

The microfluidic system was designed to introduce approximately 50 μL of solution into the flow channel, allowing for precise evaluation of ion concentrations. This setup provided an optimal platform for evaluating the performance of the reference electrode and ion-selective sensors under dynamic flow conditions. To further illustrate the fluid flow behavior and device operation, Movie S1 is included, demonstrating the seamless interaction between the sample solution and the sensors.

The performance of the reference electrode was evaluated by measuring the potential response of K+ sensors to varying concentrations of potassium chloride (KCl). As shown in Figure a, the potential exhibited a stepwise increase corresponding to increasing K+ concentration from 0.1 mM to 100 mM, demonstrating the sensor’s excellent sensitivity. Initially, at lower concentrations, the response showed some variation, but as the concentration increased, particularly above 0.5–10 mM, the potential increments became larger and more consistent. Figure b displays the sensor’s response plotted against log­[K+], where a clear linear relationship emerges at higher concentrations, aligning with the expected Nernstian behavior. This suggests that at concentrations above 0.5 mM, the sensor’s response becomes more accurate and stable, making it highly reliable for quantitative ion detection.

For the reference electrode mounted in the device: (a) calibration curve of K+ sensors, (b) potential response of K+ sensors, (c) potential response of Ca2 + sensors, and (d) potential response of Na+ sensors.

Beyond potassium detection, the microfluidic device was also tested with Ca2 + and Na+ sensors, and their responses are depicted in Figure c,d. Both sensors showed a well-defined stepwise potential change in response to increasing ion concentrations, mirroring the behavior observed for K+ sensors. The Na+ sensor demonstrated a smoother and more predictable response, while the Ca2 + sensor showed slight variations between readings, likely due to the nature of divalent ion interactions within the sensor membrane. These results confirm that they perform efficiently within the microfluidic flow channel, making them highly applicable for real-time multi-ion detection in various analytical settings.

To ensure accurate sodium ion detection, the selectivity of the Na+ sensor was evaluated against common interfering ions, K+ and Ca2 +, using solutions of 10–3 M NaCl, 10–3 M KCl, and 10–3 M CaCl2. The results, illustrated in Figure , confirm that the Na+ sensor showed strong selectivity (2 × 104 times more than Ca2+), as the measured potential remained stable when KCl or CaCl2 was introduced. The selectivity coefficients were determined to be log1 0 KPot Na,K = −1.63 and log1 0 KPot Na,Ca = −4.63, demonstrating that the sensor is significantly more responsive to Na+ than to K+ or Ca2 + (e.g., 8.7 times higher than the K+). The highly negative selectivity coefficient further confirms that the sensor effectively minimizes interference from other ions, making it highly reliable for applications requiring selective sodium ion detection.

The stable performance of K+, Ca2 +, and Na+ sensors within this setup further validates the reliability of the reference electrode in maintaining consistent and accurate measurements. Performance Evaluation in Human Saliva

To further validate the platform under clinically relevant conditions, we tested the electrodes using real human saliva samples. The experimental setup for saliva sampling and measurement is shown in Figure a. The Ca2 +-selective electrode exhibited a clear potential response to saliva samples diluted from 10–5 to 1×, as illustrated in Figure b. A calibration plot of electrode potential versus saliva concentration demonstrated a slope of 20.8 mV/dec (Figure c), consistent with the sensor’s performance in standard solutions. In this experiment, the saliva was diluted by water. These results confirm that the platform can reliably detect physiologically relevant Ca2 + concentrations in saliva, even in the presence of varying pH, viscosity, proteins, and enzymatic activity. The ability to operate effectively in a complex biological matrix indicates robustness against typical biological interferences and underscores the system’s suitability for noninvasive oral diagnostics.

Response of the Ca2 +-selective electrode in real human saliva. (a) Saliva sampling and measurement setup. (b) Potential response of the sensor to saliva dilutions (10–5 to 1×). (c) Calibration plot showing a slope of 20.8 mV/dec.

Real saliva inherently contains multiple ionic species, including chloride, phosphate, and bicarbonate, in addition to Na+, K+, and Ca2 +. By testing with undiluted saliva, the platform effectively undergoes interference validation in a complex ionic and protein-rich environment. The results in Figure confirm that the Ca2 + electrode responded consistently despite the presence of these coexisting ions, thereby demonstrating that the system performs reliably under physiologically relevant interference conditions.

In addition, the response of the Ca2 + electrode in saliva stabilized within 3–5 min following each dilution step (Figure ), confirming that the system is capable of real-time detection with practical response times. Although long-term continuous monitoring experiments remain ongoing, these saliva-based results provide direct evidence that the device can achieve rapid and stable readouts suitable for near real-time point-of-care testing.

Limitations and Future Perspectives

Although this study demonstrated reliable short-term performance of all-solid-state ion-selective electrodes, the long-term stability and durability of the ion-selective membranes were not evaluated. In real-world conditions, salivary proteins and biofilm accumulation may lead to sensor fouling, affecting accuracy and response consistency over time. Addressing these limitations is essential for clinical deployment. Ongoing research is focused on the development of antifouling protective coatings to enhance membrane robustness and minimize performance degradation during extended use. These aspects will be addressed in future investigations.

Conclusions

This study successfully demonstrated the fabrication and integration of a microfluidic device equipped with all-solid-state ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) for real-time salivary ion monitoring. The platform overcomes limitations of conventional liquid-junction electrodes by enhancing stability, portability, and reproducibility while requiring only small sample volumes. Experimental results confirmed high sensitivity and selectivity for Ca2 +, Na+, and K+ ions, with the Na+ sensor showing superior selectivity against potential interferents. The integrated solid-state reference electrode maintained stable performance within the microfluidic system, ensuring overall measurement reliability.

Crucially, validation with real human saliva demonstrated that the platform can operate effectively in physiologically complex environments containing multiple ions, proteins, and biomolecules. The Ca2 + sensor provided reproducible concentration-dependent responses across saliva dilutions, with stabilization times of 3–5 min, confirming suitability for near real-time diagnostics. These findings highlight the system’s potential for noninvasive oral health monitoring and early detection of systemic conditions.

Future efforts will focus on extending the operational lifetime of ion-selective membranes, developing antifouling strategies, and enabling fully continuous long-term monitoring. Together, these improvements will further advance the applicability of this platform for point-of-care diagnostics and broader biomedical uses.
